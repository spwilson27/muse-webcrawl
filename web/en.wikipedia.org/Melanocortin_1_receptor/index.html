Melanocortin 1 receptor

GeneMC1R
Protein classG protein-coupled receptor

The melanocortin 1 receptor (MC1R), also known as the melanocyte-stimulating hormone receptor (MSHR), is a G protein-coupled receptor expressed mainly on melanocytes, the pigment-producing cells of the skin and hair. It plays a central role in determining pigmentation in humans and other mammals.

Function

When activated by α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH) or ACTH, MC1R stimulates the production of brown-black eumelanin. When antagonised by the agouti signalling protein (ASIP), melanocytes switch to producing red-yellow pheomelanin. Activation also enhances DNA repair in melanocytes exposed to ultraviolet light.

Genetics and variation

Variations in the MC1R gene are a major cause of the red hair and fair skin phenotype; several loss-of-function variants are associated with red hair, freckling, poor tanning and increased risk of melanoma. In many other animals, MC1R variants underlie differences in coat colour, including melanism.
