The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) is an agency of the U.S. federal government, part of the Department of Transportation, entrusted with writing and enforcing the Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards and reducing deaths, injuries and economic losses from motor vehicle crashes.

History

NHTSA was established by the Highway Safety Act of 1970, building on earlier federal safety efforts spurred in part by Ralph Nader's 1965 book Unsafe at Any Speed. The agency took over vehicle safety standard-setting and created a uniform system for investigating defects and ordering recalls.

Responsibilities

The agency regulates vehicle performance standards, partners with state and local governments to reduce drunk driving and promote seat belt use, licenses vehicle manufacturers and importers, operates the five-star New Car Assessment Program crash-test ratings, administers the VIN numbering system, and sets corporate average fuel economy (CAFE) standards. It also collects crash data through systems such as the Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS).
