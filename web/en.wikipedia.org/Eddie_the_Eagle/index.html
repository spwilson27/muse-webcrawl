Eddie the Eagle

Michael David Edwards (born 5 December 1963), better known as Eddie the Eagle, is an English former ski jumper and Olympian. He represented Great Britain at the 1988 Winter Olympics in Calgary, becoming the first competitor to represent Great Britain in Olympic ski jumping since 1928. Despite finishing last in both the normal hill and large hill events, his enthusiastic amateurism made him an enduring folk hero.

Edwards became a symbol of the Olympic spirit at the 1988 Calgary Games.

Background

Edwards, a plasterer from Cheltenham, took up ski jumping because it was an inexpensive route to the Olympics and because Britain had no other jumpers to compete with for selection. He was significantly heavier than his rivals, wore glasses that fogged beneath his goggles, and trained largely self-funded, at times sleeping in his car and using borrowed equipment.

1988 Winter Olympics

At Calgary, Edwards finished last in both the 70 m and 90 m events, but set a British record of 73.5 metres on the large hill. His cheerful demeanour won over crowds and media worldwide. The president of the organising committee praised "some competitors who have won gold, some who have broken records, and some who have even soared like an eagle" at the closing ceremony — a remark widely taken to refer to Edwards.

Legacy

Partly in response to his case, the International Olympic Committee introduced the so-called "Eddie the Eagle rule", requiring aspiring Olympians to place within the top 30 per cent or top 50 competitors of international events. Edwards later worked in media and construction. His life story was dramatised in the 2016 film Eddie the Eagle, starring Taron Egerton and Hugh Jackman.
