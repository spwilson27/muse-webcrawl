Yagi–Uda antenna

InventorsShintaro Uda and Hidetsugu Yagi
First described1926 (Uda); 1928 English paper (Yagi)
TypeDirectional parasitic array
Typical useTelevision reception, amateur radio, radar

A Yagi–Uda antenna, commonly called a Yagi antenna, is a directional antenna consisting of a driven element (usually a dipole) mounted on a boom with several parasitic elements: a slightly longer reflector behind it and one or more slightly shorter directors in front. The parasitic elements are not connected to the transmitter or receiver but reradiate the signal so that the array beams radio waves preferentially in one direction, achieving high gain from a simple, cheap structure.

History

The antenna was invented in 1926 by Shintaro Uda of Tohoku Imperial University in Japan and publicized in English by his colleague Hidetsugu Yagi, whose name became attached to it abroad. It saw extensive military use in radar during the Second World War and became the standard rooftop television antenna of the postwar decades, still common for terrestrial TV and FM reception.

Operation

The reflector, about five percent longer than the driven dipole, pushes energy forward; directors, about five percent shorter, pull the beam along the boom. Adding directors raises gain — typical television Yagis reach 7–15 dBi — at the cost of a narrower bandwidth and beamwidth.
