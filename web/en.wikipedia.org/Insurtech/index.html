Insurtech

Insurtech (also spelled insuretech) is the use of technology innovations to improve the efficiency and customer experience of the insurance industry — a portmanteau of "insurance" and "technology" modelled on fintech. The term covers startups and new business models that apply data analytics, artificial intelligence, telematics, mobile apps and blockchain to insurance distribution, underwriting, pricing, policy administration and claims handling, as well as incumbent insurers' digital transformation programmes.

Applications

  • Digital distribution — apps and comparison sites that sell policies in minutes with minimal paperwork; "full-stack" digital insurers such as Lemonade (renters'/pet insurance), Root (usage- and behaviour-based car insurance) and ZhongAn (China's first online-only insurer, founded 2013) hold their own underwriting licences, while many startups act as brokers or managing general agents.
  • Usage-based and telematics insurance — premiums priced from actual behaviour, e.g. telematics devices or phone apps that score driving, or pay-per-mile cover.
  • Parametric insurance — policies that pay a fixed amount automatically when a measurable trigger occurs (e.g. a flight delay, earthquake magnitude or rainfall below a threshold), without loss adjustment.
  • Claims automation — AI chatbots, photo-based damage assessment and straight-through processing; Lemonade famously publicised a claim settled by its bot in three seconds.
  • Underwriting data — satellite imagery, IoT sensors, wearables (in life and health cover) and open data supplementing traditional actuarial tables.
  • Peer-to-peer and microinsurance — small groups pooling premiums, and low-cost, phone-delivered cover in emerging markets.

Development and reception

Investment in insurtech surged in the mid-2010s, peaking at roughly US$15 billion globally in 2021 before falling back sharply in 2022–2023 as valuations corrected and several listed insurtechs (whose combined ratios often exceeded those of incumbents) struggled with loss ratios. Industry analysis generally treats insurtech less as a replacement for insurers than as pressure to modernise legacy systems: many startups pivoted to selling technology to incumbents or partnering with them, and regulators in jurisdictions such as the UK (FCA regulatory sandbox) and Singapore have created innovation pathways while scrutinising algorithmic pricing for fairness — the EU and several US states restrict the use of certain variables and require explainability of automated decisions.
