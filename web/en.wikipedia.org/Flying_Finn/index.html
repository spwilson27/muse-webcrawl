Flying Finn

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"The Flying Finn" (Finnish: Lentävä suomalainen) is a nickname first given to Finnish distance runner Hannes Kolehmainen, whose victories at the 1912 Stockholm Olympics — three gold medals and a world record in the 5,000 metres — made Finland famous in international athletics. The name has since been applied to generations of Finnish sportspeople famed for speed and endurance.

Origin and the running era

The most celebrated bearer was Paavo Nurmi, who won nine Olympic gold medals in the 1920s and set 22 world records; journalists used "Flying Finn" for him throughout his career. Other runners given the epithet include Ville Ritola, Volmari Iso-Hollo, and, in the 1970s, Lasse Virén, who repeated the 5,000/10,000 metre Olympic double in 1972 and 1976.

Motorsport

Finland's extraordinary production of racing champions revived the nickname: rally drivers such as Timo Mäkinen, Rauno Aaltonen, Juha Kankkunen, Tommi Mäkinen, and Marcus Grönholm, and Formula One champions Keke Rosberg, Mika Häkkinen, and Kimi Räikkönen have all been called Flying Finns. In speedway and other sports the label is applied similarly, and it is also used commercially — for instance by the Finnish airline Finnair in its branding.
