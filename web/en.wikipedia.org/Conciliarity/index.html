Conciliarity

Conciliarity is the adherence of a church body to the principle that major decisions, especially in matters of doctrine, should be taken by a council or synod of bishops rather than by a single prelate alone. In the Eastern Orthodox Church, conciliarity is closely related to the concept of sobornost — the unity of the Church expressed through the consensus of the whole body of believers under the guidance of the Holy Spirit.

In the Orthodox Church

Orthodox ecclesiology holds that the highest earthly authority is an ecumenical council. Because no council has been universally received as ecumenical since the seventh (Nicaea II, 787), Orthodox governance operates through local and regional synods. The principle balances the role of the protos (first hierarch, such as a patriarch) against the synod of bishops he presides over.

In the Catholic Church

In Roman Catholic theology, conciliarity must be held together with the primacy of the Pope: an ecumenical council acts in union with, and subject to confirmation by, the Bishop of Rome. The Second Vatican Council (1962–1965) emphasised episcopal collegiality, a concept related to but distinct from conciliarism, the medieval theory — condemned by the Church — that a general council is superior to the pope.
