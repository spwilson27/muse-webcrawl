Quebec Sales Tax

The Quebec Sales Tax (QST; French: Taxe de vente du Québec, TVQ) is a provincial value-added tax charged in the Canadian province of Quebec. It is administered by Revenu Québec and applies to most sales of goods and services in the province alongside the federal Goods and Services Tax (GST).

Rate

Since 1 January 2013, the QST rate has been 9.975%, calculated on the selling price excluding GST. Together with the 5% GST, the combined tax burden in Quebec is 14.975%. The unusual rate is the result of harmonisation: the QST base was aligned with the GST base in 2013 and the tax ceased to be charged on top of the GST ("tax on tax"), with the rate set to keep provincial revenue neutral.

History

Quebec introduced a provincial retail sales tax in 1940; the QST in its value-added form dates from 1991, when Quebec became the first province to implement a VAT alongside the federal GST, administered provincially. For many years the QST was applied to the GST-inclusive price, an anomaly removed in the 2013 harmonisation agreement under which Ottawa compensated Quebec for aligning the tax bases.

Exemptions

As with the GST, basic groceries, prescription drugs and certain financial and residential services are zero-rated or exempt. Quebec also administers the GST within the province on Ottawa's behalf for most businesses, a unique arrangement in Canada.
