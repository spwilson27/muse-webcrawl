Edmund Barton

Sir Edmund Barton (18 January 1849 – 7 January 1920) was an Australian politician and judge who served as the first prime minister of Australia from 1901 to 1903. A leading figure of the federation movement, he is often called the "Father of Federation" for his tireless campaigning to unite the six British colonies into a single nation.

Edmund Barton, first Prime Minister of Australia

Early life and career

Born in Glebe, Sydney, Barton was educated at Sydney Grammar School and the University of Sydney, and was called to the bar in 1871. He entered the New South Wales Legislative Assembly in 1879 and later served in the Legislative Council, becoming Attorney-General of New South Wales on several occasions.

Federation

Barton led the federal convention process, presiding over the drafting committee that produced the Australian Constitution at the conventions of 1891 and 1897–98. His famous slogan — "a nation for a continent and a continent for a nation" — captured the movement's appeal. After referendums approved the constitution, the Commonwealth of Australia was proclaimed on 1 January 1901, and Barton formed its first government, leading the Protectionist Party to victory at the first federal election.

Prime minister

Barton's government established the machinery of the new Commonwealth, passing foundational legislation including the Immigration Restriction Act 1901, which inaugurated the White Australia policy, and the Audit Act. In 1903 he resigned to become a founding justice of the High Court of Australia, where he served until his death in 1920.

Legacy

The Canberra suburb of Barton and the Division of Barton in Sydney are named for him, and his portrait appears in Parliament House, Canberra.
