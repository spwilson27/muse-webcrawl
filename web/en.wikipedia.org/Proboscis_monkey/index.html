Proboscis monkey

The proboscis monkey (Nasalis larvatus), also called the long-nosed monkey, is an arboreal Old World monkey with an unusually large nose, a reddish-brown skin colour, and a long tail. It is endemic to the southeast Asian island of Borneo, where it lives in coastal mangrove forests and riverine lowlands.

Description

The species exhibits marked sexual dimorphism. Males weigh 16–22.5 kg and have a large, pendulous nose that may exceed 10 cm in length and hangs down over the mouth; the nose is thought to amplify vocalisations and may signal status. Females are about half the males' weight and have smaller, upturned noses. Both sexes have pot bellies, the result of a complex, multi-chambered stomach that ferments leaves.

Behaviour

Proboscis monkeys live in harems of one adult male with several females and their young, and in bachelor groups. They are strong swimmers — aided by partially webbed feet — and cross rivers to forage, sometimes falling prey to crocodiles. Their diet of leaves, unripe fruit, and seeds means they avoid ripe fruit: the sugars would ferment dangerously in their specialised stomachs.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Populations have declined sharply because of habitat loss from logging, oil palm expansion, and coastal development, as well as hunting. Protected populations survive in areas such as Bako National Park in Sarawak.
