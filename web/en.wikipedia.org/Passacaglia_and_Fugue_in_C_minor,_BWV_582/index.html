Passacaglia and Fugue in C minor, BWV 582

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The eight-bar ostinato on which the work is built

The Passacaglia and Fugue in C minor, BWV 582, is an organ work by Johann Sebastian Bach. Presumably composed early in his career, it is one of his most important and well-known compositions, and has been described as one of the greatest passacaglias ever written. The work was notably orchestrated by Leopold Stokowski and by Ottorino Respighi.

Structure

The passacaglia is built on an eight-bar ostinato theme in 3/4 time, first stated unaccompanied in the pedal. The theme is believed to be borrowed in part from a short passacaglia by the French composer André Raison. Over this unchanging bass, Bach unfolds twenty variations of extraordinary variety, exploring figuration, rhythm and registration while the harmony cycles relentlessly. The ostinato then supplies the subject of a massive double fugue: its first half is used as one subject, combined with a countersubject derived from the theme's second half, bringing the work to a monumental close.

Analysis and reception

Robert Schumann described the variations as "intertwined so ingeniously that one can never cease to be amazed". Musicologists have searched the work for numerological symbolism, including patterns some interpret as references to Bach's name or to Lutheran theology, though such readings remain conjectural. Because the passacaglia form was associated in Bach's time with French and north-German traditions alike, the piece is often cited as a synthesis of both schools.

Arrangements

Beyond the Stokowski and Respighi orchestrations, the work has been arranged for piano, for guitar and for many other combinations; its opening was also used to evoke solemn grandeur in films and recordings. The autograph manuscript is lost, and the work survives through copies made by Bach's pupils and contemporaries.
