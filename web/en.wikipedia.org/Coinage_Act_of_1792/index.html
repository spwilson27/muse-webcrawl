Coinage Act of 1792

The Coinage Act of 1792, passed April 2, 1792, established the United States Mint and regulated the coinage of the United States. It created the decimal dollar as the national unit of money, based on Alexander Hamilton's proposals.

Provisions

The act authorised gold eagles ($10), half eagles and quarter eagles; silver dollars, half dollars, quarter dollars, dismes and half dismes; and copper cents and half cents. It fixed a bimetallic ratio of 15:1 between silver and gold and prescribed the death penalty for debasing coins. The dollar was defined as 371.25 grains of pure silver.

Aftermath

The Philadelphia Mint began striking copper cents in 1793 and silver dollars in 1794. The act remained the foundation of US coinage law, with major revisions in 1834, 1849, 1853 and 1873.
