Karel Willem Frederik Marie Doorman (23 April 1889 – 28 February 1942) was a Dutch naval officer who commanded the Allied naval striking force in the Dutch East Indies during the early months of the Pacific War. He was killed in action at the Battle of the Java Sea, going down with his flagship, the light cruiser HNLMS De Ruyter.

Career

Doorman entered the Royal Netherlands Navy as a midshipman in 1906 and trained as a naval aviator, becoming one of the pioneers of Dutch naval aviation in the East Indies. By 1940 he was commander of the Naval Air Service in the colony, and in 1942 he took command of the Combined Striking Force of the American-British-Dutch-Australian (ABDA) Command.

Java Sea and death

His force fought the Battle of Badung Strait and, on 27 February 1942, engaged the Japanese invasion fleet in the Battle of the Java Sea. Hampered by poor communications, exhausted crews and Japanese superiority in training and torpedoes, the Allied force was defeated; De Ruyter and the cruiser Java were torpedoed and sunk on the night of 27–28 February, and Doorman chose to remain with his ship. His reported signal "I am attacking, follow me" became a rallying phrase in Dutch naval tradition, though its exact wording is uncertain. Several Royal Netherlands Navy ships, including two aircraft carriers and a frigate class, have borne his name.
