Labile verb

In linguistics, a labile verb (or ergative verb, or ambitransitive verb used in a particular pattern) is a verb that can occur either transitively or intransitively, with the subject of the intransitive use corresponding to the direct object of the transitive use, and no change in form.

English examples include break: "The storm broke the window" and "The window broke"; melt, open, sink, roll and change behave similarly. The transitive use is often analysed as causative ("cause to break") and the intransitive as anticausative or inchoative.

Labile verbs are common cross-linguistically, though languages differ in how freely the alternation applies and whether morphology marks it, as in languages with overt causative or anticausative affixes.
