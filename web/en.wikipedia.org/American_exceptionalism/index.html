American exceptionalism

American exceptionalism is the idea that the United States is inherently different from other nations, owing to its founding on ideals of liberty, equality before the law, individualism, populism, and laissez-faire markets. The term is often traced to Alexis de Tocqueville's observations in Democracy in America (1835–1840), and a related phrase was used by Joseph Stalin in 1929 to criticize American communists who believed their country was exempt from Marxist laws of history.

History and interpretations

Supporters point to the American Revolution, the absence of a feudal past, constitutionalism, and high social mobility. Critics argue the idea obscures slavery, the dispossession of Native Americans, and interventionist foreign policies, or that it overstates American uniqueness.

In political discourse

The concept has been invoked by presidents of both parties, notably becoming a prominent theme in debates between Barack Obama and Mitt Romney and in the rhetoric of Donald Trump, whose "America First" stance some commentators described as a distinct variant.
