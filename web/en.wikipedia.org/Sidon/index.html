Sidon (Arabic: صيدا, Ṣaydā; Phoenician: 𐤑𐤃𐤍, Ṣīdūn) is the third-largest city in Lebanon, located in the South Governorate of Lebanon on the Mediterranean coast, about 40 kilometres south of Beirut. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world and was one of the most important Phoenician city-states, famed for maritime trade, glassmaking and the production of purple dye from murex shells.

History

Sidon was inhabited from prehistoric times and rose to prominence in the Bronze Age. Together with Tyre and Byblos it formed the heartland of Phoenician civilization. The city was conquered in turn by Assyrians, Babylonians, Persians, Greeks under Alexander the Great, Romans and Arabs. It was a significant port during the Crusades, when it formed part of the Lordship of Sidon in the Kingdom of Jerusalem; the Crusader-era Sea Castle, built in the 13th century on an island connected to the shore, remains a landmark.

Modern city

Today Sidon is a port city and commercial centre for southern Lebanon, known for its old souks, the Debbane Palace, the Khan al-Franj caravanserai and the Eshmun Temple complex nearby, dedicated to the Phoenician god of healing. The Ain al-Hilweh Palestinian refugee camp lies on the city's southeastern edge.
