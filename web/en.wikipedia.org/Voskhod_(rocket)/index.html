Voskhod (rocket)

The Voskhod (Russian: Восход, "ascent" or "dawn"; GRAU index 11A57) was a Soviet expendable launch system derived from the R-7 Semyorka intercontinental ballistic missile family. It was an interim development between the Vostok and Soyuz rockets: a Vostok core fitted with a new, more powerful third stage (the RD-0110-powered Blok-E) and no escape tower, giving it the lift capacity needed for the heavier multi-crew Voskhod spacecraft and for Zenit reconnaissance satellites.

History

The Voskhod rocket first flew on 16 November 1963, launching a Zenit spy satellite. It carried the two crewed Voskhod missions: Voskhod 1 (October 1964), the first flight with a multi-person crew, and Voskhod 2 (March 1965), from which Alexei Leonov made the first spacewalk. The type flew 300 times in total — the vast majority launching Zenit reconnaissance satellites — before its retirement in 1976, when the Soyuz-U took over its roles.

The rocket stood about 44.6 m tall and could place roughly 5.9 tonnes into low Earth orbit. Because it lacked a launch escape system, crewed use was considered risky even by the standards of the time and was discontinued after the two missions.
