Musical quotation

Musical quotation is the practice of directly quoting another work — incorporating a recognisable portion of an existing composition, folk song, hymn or national anthem — within a new piece of music. Unlike allusion or stylistic modelling, quotation imports actual notes from a source the listener may be expected to recognise.

History and examples

Renaissance composers built masses on borrowed chant and chansons; later composers quoted for homage, irony, patriotism or structural symbolism. Tchaikovsky quotes "La Marseillaise" and the Russian imperial anthem in the 1812 Overture. Charles Ives made quotation a central technique, weaving hymns, marches and popular songs into works such as the Concord Sonata and his symphonies. In the 20th century, quotation fed into collage techniques (Berio's Sinfonia) and, in popular music, into sampling, where literal fragments of recordings are reused — raising new questions of copyright.
