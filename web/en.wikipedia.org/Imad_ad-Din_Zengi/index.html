Imad ad-Din Zengi

Imad ad-Din Zengi (Arabic: عماد الدین زنكي; also romanized as Zangi or Zengui; c. 1085 – 14 September 1146) was a Seljuk Turkish atabeg of Mosul, Aleppo, Hama, and Edessa. He was a son of Aq Sunqur al-Hajib, governor of Aleppo under Malik-Shah I. Zengi is remembered as the founder of the Zengid dynasty and as the first Muslim leader to mount a sustained, successful counteroffensive against the Crusader states, capturing Edessa in 1144.

Rise to power

After the death of his father, Zengi was raised at the court of Mosul. He served the Seljuk sultans in Iraq and in 1127 was appointed atabeg of Mosul; in 1128 he gained control of Aleppo, uniting northern Syria and the Jazira under his rule — a union that for the first time gave the Muslims of Syria a single strong leader against the Franks.

Wars against the Crusaders

Zengi campaigned repeatedly against the Principality of Antioch, the Kingdom of Jerusalem, and Damascus. His greatest triumph came in December 1144, when after a siege of 28 days he captured Edessa, capital of the first Crusader state, the County of Edessa. The fall of Edessa shocked Europe and was the direct cause of the Second Crusade.

Death and legacy

Zengi was assassinated by a Frankish slave in September 1146 while besieging Qal'at Ja'bar on the Euphrates. His territories were divided between his sons: Saif ad-Din Ghazi took Mosul and Nur ad-Din took Aleppo. Nur ad-Din continued his father's work of unifying Muslim Syria, a project completed by Saladin.
