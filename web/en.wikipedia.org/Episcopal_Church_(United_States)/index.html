Episcopal Church (United States)

The Episcopal Church is a mainline Protestant denomination in the United States and a member church of the worldwide Anglican Communion. Its full legal name is the Protestant Episcopal Church in the United States of America. It originated from the Church of England congregations of the American colonies and became an independent church after the American Revolution, organizing formally in 1789.

Washington National Cathedral, the cathedral of the Episcopal Diocese of Washington

Structure and worship

The church is governed by a General Convention, a bicameral body of bishops and elected deputies, and is led by a Presiding Bishop. It comprises about 100 dioceses in the United States and additional dioceses abroad. Worship follows the Book of Common Prayer, in its 1979 American edition, and ranges from Anglo-Catholic to evangelical styles.

History and positions

The Episcopal Church was the first Anglican province outside the British Isles. In the 20th and 21st centuries it became known for socially liberal positions relative to other Anglican provinces: it ordained women beginning in the 1970s and in 2003 consecrated Gene Robinson, its first openly gay bishop, a decision that strained relations within the Anglican Communion and contributed to the formation of the Anglican Church in North America by departing congregations. It has authorized rites for same-sex marriage since 2015. Membership has declined in recent decades and stands at roughly 1.5–2 million baptized members.
