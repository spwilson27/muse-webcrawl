Familial dysautonomia

Familial dysautonomia (FD), also called Riley–Day syndrome or hereditary sensory and autonomic neuropathy type III (HSAN-III), is a rare genetic disorder that affects the development and survival of certain nerve cells in the autonomic and sensory nervous systems.

Cause

The condition is caused by mutations in the IKBKAP gene (now often called ELP1), inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. It occurs almost exclusively among people of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry, where carrier screening has greatly reduced its incidence.

Symptoms

Symptoms include insensitivity to pain, inability to produce tears, poor temperature and blood-pressure regulation, episodes of severe vomiting (dysautonomic crises), and gastrointestinal and lung problems. There is no cure; treatment is supportive, and prognosis has improved with modern care, many patients now living into adulthood.
