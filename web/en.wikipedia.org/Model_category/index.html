Model category

In mathematics, a model category is a category equipped with three distinguished classes of morphisms — weak equivalences, fibrations, and cofibrations — satisfying axioms that allow one to do homotopy theory in an abstract setting. The notion was introduced by Daniel Quillen in his 1967 work Homotopical Algebra and is a foundation of modern homotopy theory.

Definition

A model category is a category with all small limits and colimits, together with three classes of morphisms (weak equivalences, fibrations, cofibrations) closed under composition and retracts, satisfying:

  • Lifting — trivial cofibrations have the left lifting property against fibrations, and cofibrations against trivial fibrations.
  • Factorization — every morphism factors as a cofibration followed by a trivial fibration, and as a trivial cofibration followed by a fibration.
  • Two-out-of-three — if two of f, g, and gf are weak equivalences, so is the third.

Examples and purpose

The prototypical example is the category of topological spaces, where weak equivalences are weak homotopy equivalences, fibrations are Serre fibrations, and cofibrations are retracts of relative cell complexes. Other examples include chain complexes of modules, simplicial sets (with weak equivalences detected by geometric realization), spectra, and categories of diagrams. By formally inverting the weak equivalences one obtains the category's homotopy category; objects can be replaced by cofibrant and fibrant approximations so that hom-sets in the homotopy category can be computed via homotopies. Model categories abstract the common structure behind these examples and support constructions such as derived functors, homotopy limits and colimits, and Quillen equivalences comparing different models of the same homotopy theory.
