Collaboration with Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy

Collaboration with Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy refers to the cooperation of governments, organizations, military formations, and individuals in countries occupied by or allied with the Axis powers during the Second World War. Collaboration ranged from the state policy of allied or satellite regimes to individual acts of assistance with administration, policing, deportations, and warfare. It coexisted with resistance movements in nearly every occupied country, and its memory remains politically sensitive.

Forms of collaboration

Historians distinguish forms and motives: ideological collaboration by fascist and nationalist movements that shared Axis aims; state collaboration by governments, such as Vichy France, seeking to preserve sovereignty; military collaboration, including foreign volunteers and units in the Waffen-SS recruited across Europe; economic collaboration by firms and workers supplying the German war economy; and administrative collaboration by civil servants and police who implemented occupation policies, including in some places participation in the arrest and deportation of Jews during the Holocaust.

By country

Patterns differed sharply. Denmark pursued a negotiated policy of cooperation until 1943. France's Vichy regime actively collaborated and French police carried out roundups such as the Vel' d'Hiv. Norway under Quisling — whose name became a synonym for traitor — and the puppet Independent State of Croatia under the Ustaše were ideologically aligned regimes. In Eastern Europe, local auxiliary police were implicated in mass shootings and ghetto clearances. Arab, Indian, and other anti-colonial collaborators sought Axis support against British rule.

Aftermath

After liberation, collaborators faced legal purges (épuration), trials, and sometimes extrajudicial reprisals; thousands were executed or imprisoned across Europe. Post-war national narratives often minimized collaboration in favour of resistance myths, which historians have since revised, examining the spectrum between collaboration and resistance and the "grey zones" of accommodation and survival.
