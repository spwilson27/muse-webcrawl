ARKit

ARKit
Developer: Apple Inc.
Initial release: June 2017 (with iOS 11)
Type: Augmented reality framework
Platforms: iOS, iPadOS

ARKit is an augmented reality (AR) development framework created by Apple for iOS and iPadOS. Announced at the Worldwide Developers Conference in June 2017 and released with iOS 11, ARKit lets developers build AR experiences using a device's camera and motion sensors, without specialized hardware.

Technology

ARKit uses visual-inertial odometry: it fuses camera imagery with accelerometer and gyroscope data to track the device's position in six degrees of freedom. It detects horizontal and vertical planes, estimates scene lighting, and (in later versions) builds depth maps and performs people occlusion. ARKit 2 (2018) added shared, persistent world maps enabling multiplayer AR. ARKit 3 added motion capture of people. ARKit 4 introduced a depth API exploiting the LiDAR scanner on iPad Pro and later Pro iPhones, enabling faster plane detection and instant placement of virtual objects.

Development

Developers use ARKit with Swift or Objective-C, typically together with SceneKit, SpriteKit, or Metal for rendering, or via frameworks such as RealityKit, which Apple introduced in 2019 for higher-level AR authoring. ARKit is frequently contrasted with Google's ARCore, its counterpart on Android.
