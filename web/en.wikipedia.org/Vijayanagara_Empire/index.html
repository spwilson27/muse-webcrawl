Vijayanagara Empire

The Vijayanagara Empire was a late medieval Hindu empire that ruled much of southern India, based in the Deccan Plateau region. It was established in 1336 by the brothers Harihara I and Bukka Raya I of the Sangama dynasty, reputedly under the guidance of the sage Vidyaranya. At its peak under Krishnadevaraya (r. 1509–1529), the empire covered most of the Indian peninsula south of the Krishna River and its capital was one of the largest cities in the world.

History

The empire arose amid the expansion of the Delhi Sultanate into the south and spent two centuries in rivalry with the Bahmani Sultanate and its successor Deccan sultanates, contesting the fertile Raichur Doab. Krishnadevaraya's reign brought military triumphs over the Gajapatis of Odisha and the Bijapur Sultanate, flourishing Telugu and Kannada literature, and monumental temple building at Hampi, Tirupati, and elsewhere.

At the Battle of Talikota in 1565, a confederacy of Deccan sultanates crushed the empire's army and sacked the capital, Vijayanagara — whose ruins at Hampi are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Aravidu successors lingered at Penukonda and Chandragiri into the 1640s as the south fragmented into Nayaka kingdoms.

Economy and culture

Foreign travellers such as Domingo Paes and Fernão Nuniz described a wealthy trading city importing horses from Arabia and Persia and exporting textiles, spices, and diamonds. The empire's hydraulic works, temple architecture, and court patronage left a lasting imprint on South Indian culture.
