Joshua Tree

The Joshua tree (Yucca brevifolia) is a plant species belonging to the genus Yucca. It is a monocotyledonous tree native to the arid southwestern United States, specifically California, Arizona, Utah, and Nevada, where it grows primarily in the Mojave Desert. The plant takes its name from the story of Joshua in the Bible, as early Mormon settlers passing through the Mojave Desert in the mid-19th century thought its outstretched limbs resembled the biblical figure raising his hands in prayer.

Description

Joshua trees grow slowly from seed and can reach heights of 15 metres (49 ft) or more, though most are considerably shorter. They have a thick, fibrous trunk and spiky, dagger-like evergreen leaves arranged in dense rosettes at the ends of the branches. In spring, mature trees produce large clusters of creamy white flowers, which are pollinated exclusively by the yucca moth (Tegeticula synthetica and related species), a classic example of obligate mutualism. The trees may live for several hundred years.

Distribution and habitat

The Joshua tree is largely confined to the Mojave Desert, at elevations between roughly 400 and 1,800 metres (1,300 and 5,900 ft). It typically grows in open grasslands and desert flats alongside other Mojave species such as blackbrush and creosote bush. Joshua Tree National Park in Southern California, established as a national monument in 1936 and elevated to national park status in 1994, protects extensive stands of the species.

Conservation

Because the species depends on a narrow climate envelope with cold winter temperatures and adequate winter rainfall, it is considered vulnerable to climate change. Studies project substantial contraction of its range under warming scenarios, and drought and wildfire pose additional threats. The Joshua tree has been the subject of petitions for listing under the U.S. Endangered Species Act and California state protections.

In popular culture

The tree lent its name to the 1987 album The Joshua Tree by the Irish rock band U2, whose cover photographs were taken in the Mojave Desert. The species is an iconic symbol of the American desert Southwest.
