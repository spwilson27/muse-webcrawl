Bean Boot

The Bean Boot (also known as the L.L.Bean Boot and originally the Maine Hunting Shoe) is a style of waterproof boot produced by the American retailer L.L.Bean. Introduced in 1912, it combines a rubber lower section with a leather upper and has become the company's signature product and an icon of New England outdoor style.

History

Leon Leonwood Bean, an avid hunter from Maine, designed the boot after returning from a hunting trip with wet, cold feet: he had rubber galoshes bottoms stitched to leather uppers, creating a lightweight, waterproof boot. He sold the first pairs by mail in 1912 with a money-back guarantee; when the stitching failed on most of the first hundred pairs, Bean refunded his customers, fixed the design, and built the company's reputation on the guarantee.

Design

The classic boot has a waterproof rubber bottom molded with a chain-tread sole, triple-stitched to a full-grain leather upper, and is offered in heights typically of 6, 8, and 10 inches, in lined, Thinsulate, and shearling variants. Boots in common sizes are still hand-stitched in Brunswick, Maine.

Cultural status and demand

The Bean Boot became a fashion staple of American "preppy" style, and from the late 2000s onward surges in demand produced months-long backorders and waitlists. A giant 16-foot replica of the boot, the "Bootmobile," serves as an L.L.Bean promotional vehicle.
