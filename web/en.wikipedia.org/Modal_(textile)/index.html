Modal (textile)

Modal is a type of rayon, a semi-synthetic fibre made from regenerated cellulose derived principally from beechwood pulp. Developed in Japan in the 1950s and commercialised by the Austrian company Lenzing AG, modal is a high-wet-modulus viscose fibre engineered to be stronger than standard rayon, especially when wet.

Properties

Modal fabrics are soft, smooth, and breathable, with a silky feel often compared to cotton or silk. The fibre absorbs about 50 percent more water than cotton, resists shrinking and pilling better than ordinary rayon, and retains its shape and finish through repeated washing. Modal is frequently blended with cotton and elastane (spandex) for underwear, T-shirts, activewear, pyjamas, and bed sheets.

Production

Modal is produced by a modified viscose process: beechwood chips are converted to pulp, the cellulose is dissolved and extruded through spinnerets into fibres, and the spinning conditions are adjusted to produce the fibre's characteristic high strength and softness. Modern production by Lenzing uses a largely closed-loop system that recovers process chemicals and solvents, and sources wood from certified sustainable forestry.

Environmental considerations

As a plant-based fibre, modal is biodegradable, but its environmental profile depends on forestry practices and chemical management. Older rayon production was notorious for carbon disulfide emissions; branded modal (such as TENCEL™ Modal) is marketed on improved sustainability credentials compared with conventional viscose.
