Western Sahara conflict

Western Sahara conflict
Status: Unresolved territorial dispute
Parties: Morocco vs Polisario Front / Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic
UN mission: MINURSO (since 1991)
Key division: The Berm (Moroccan Wall)

The Western Sahara conflict is an ongoing territorial dispute over Western Sahara, a territory on the northwest coast of Africa claimed and largely administered by Morocco but also claimed by the Polisario Front, which proclaimed the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR) in 1976. Western Sahara is listed by the United Nations as a non-self-governing territory.

Overview

Spain colonised the territory as Spanish Sahara from 1884 until 1975. An advisory opinion of the International Court of Justice in 1975 found no ties of territorial sovereignty sufficient to displace the Sahrawi people's right to self-determination, but Morocco and Mauritania partitioned the territory under the Madrid Accords, triggering the Western Sahara War (1975–1991). Mauritania withdrew in 1979; Morocco controls about 80 per cent of the territory west of the Berm, including all the coastline and the phosphate mines at Bou Craa, while the SADR controls the sparsely populated "Free Zone" to the east.

Diplomatic efforts

The 1991 Settlement Plan promised a referendum that never took place, deadlocking over voter eligibility. Later UN efforts, including the Baker Plans and rounds of talks at Manhasset and Geneva, failed to produce agreement. Morocco proposes autonomy under Moroccan sovereignty; the Polisario insists on a referendum including the option of independence. In 2020 the United States recognised Moroccan sovereignty over the territory, a position several other states have since echoed in varying forms.

Humanitarian dimension

Some 170,000 Sahrawi refugees live in camps near Tindouf, Algeria, administered by the Polisario and dependent on international aid. Fighting resumed at a low level after the 2020 collapse of the ceasefire at Guerguerat.
