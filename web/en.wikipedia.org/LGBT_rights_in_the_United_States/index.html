Lesbian, gay, bisexual, and transgender (LGBT) rights in the United States are among the most advanced in the world, having expanded substantially since the late 20th century. Legal developments have largely been driven by landmark decisions of the Supreme Court and by state-level legislation, while protections vary by state on some issues.

History

The modern movement is often dated to the Stonewall riots of 1969 in New York City. Through the 1970s activists campaigned against sodomy laws and discrimination; the AIDS crisis of the 1980s transformed organising and public visibility.

Landmark court decisions

In Lawrence v. Texas (2003) the Supreme Court struck down state sodomy laws nationwide. United States v. Windsor (2013) invalidated the federal Defense of Marriage Act's definition of marriage, and Obergefell v. Hodges (2015) established a nationwide right to same-sex marriage. In Bostock v. Clayton County (2020) the Court held that Title VII of the Civil Rights Act protects employees against discrimination because of sexual orientation or gender identity.

Federal legislation

The Matthew Shepard and James Byrd Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act (2009) extended federal hate-crime law to crimes motivated by sexual orientation and gender identity. The military's "Don't ask, don't tell" policy was repealed in 2011. The Respect for Marriage Act (2022) requires federal and interstate recognition of same-sex and interracial marriages.

State-level variation

States differ in areas such as adoption, public accommodations, conversion therapy bans, and policies affecting transgender people, including healthcare and participation in sports. These differences remain the subject of ongoing litigation and political debate.
