Emily Brontë

Emily Jane Brontë (30 July 1818 – 19 December 1848) was an English novelist and poet who is best known for her only novel, Wuthering Heights, now considered a classic of English literature. She also published a book of poetry with her sisters Charlotte and Anne titled Poems by Currer, Ellis, and Acton Bell, using the pen name Ellis Bell.

Life and work

The middle of the three surviving Brontë sisters, Emily lived most of her life at the parsonage in Haworth, Yorkshire, where her father was perpetual curate. Reserved and deeply attached to the moorland landscape, she worked as a teacher briefly and studied in Brussels with Charlotte in 1842. Wuthering Heights, published in 1847, initially baffled critics with its stark portrayal of passionate and destructive love, but was later recognised as a masterpiece. Emily died of tuberculosis at the age of 30, a year after the novel's publication.
