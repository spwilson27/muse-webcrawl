Single-cell sequencing

Single-cell sequencing examines the sequence information from individual cells with optimized next-generation sequencing technologies, providing a higher resolution of cellular differences and a better understanding of the function of an individual cell in its microenvironment. Unlike bulk sequencing, which averages signals across millions of cells, single-cell methods can reveal rare cell types, cell-to-cell variability in gene expression, and somatic mutations present in only a subset of cells.

Applications

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) profiles the transcriptome of individual cells and has transformed the classification of cell types in tissues, the mapping of developmental trajectories, and the study of tumour heterogeneity. Related methods sequence DNA from single cells (for copy-number and mutation analysis), profile chromatin accessibility (scATAC-seq), and measure proteins using sequencing-based antibody tags.

Workflow

A typical experiment involves isolating single cells — by microfluidic droplets, microwells or fluorescence-activated sorting — barcoding their nucleic acids so that reads can be traced back to a cell of origin, amplifying the tiny amounts of material, and sequencing. Computational pipelines then filter low-quality cells, normalize the data, and cluster cells by similarity.

Impact

Large consortia such as the Human Cell Atlas aim to map every cell type in the human body using single-cell technologies. The approach was named Method of the Year by the journal Nature Methods in 2013.
