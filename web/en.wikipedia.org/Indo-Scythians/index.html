Indo-Scythians

Indo-Scythian Kingdom
Periodc. 150 BCE – 400 CE
RegionCentral Asia, Afghanistan, northern India
First kingMaues (c. 85–60 BCE)
CapitalsSigal; Taxila; Mathura

The Indo-Scythians were a group of nomadic peoples of Scythian (Saka) origin who migrated from Central Asia into northwestern South Asia between the 2nd century BCE and the 4th century CE. In India they are known in Sanskrit sources as the Śaka. Their rule at various times covered parts of modern Afghanistan, Pakistan, and northern India, and they are known largely through their distinctive coinage.

Migration and early kings

Pressure from the Yuezhi pushed Saka tribes southward through Bactria into the Indus valley. Around 85 BCE the Saka king Maues (Moga) established a kingdom centred on Gandhara and Taxila. His successors, including Azes I—associated with the beginning of the Azes era in 58 BCE—extended Indo-Scythian rule over much of the northwest, displacing the Indo-Greek kingdoms.

Northern and Western Satraps

Indo-Scythian power later organized into satrapies. The Northern Satraps ruled around Mathura, while the Western Satraps of Gujarat and Malwa, founded by Chastana around 78 CE (the start of the Saka era still used in India's national calendar), endured for three centuries. The Western Satrap Rudradaman I (c. 130–150 CE) is celebrated for his Junagadh rock inscription, an early major Sanskrit inscription recording his military exploits and repairs to the Sudarshana lake.

Decline

The northern Indo-Scythian domains fell successively to the Indo-Parthians under Gondophares and then the Kushan Empire. The Western Satraps survived much longer, finally being annexed by the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II around 400 CE.

Legacy

Indo-Scythian coinage, which combined Greek and Kharoshthi legends, was a major vehicle of cultural exchange on the Silk Road, and the Saka era remains part of the Indian national calendar.
