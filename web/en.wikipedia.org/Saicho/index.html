Saichō

Saichō (最澄, 15 September 767 – 26 June 822) was a Japanese Buddhist monk credited with founding the Tendai school of Buddhism in Japan, based on the Chinese Tiantai tradition he studied during a voyage to Tang China. He is known posthumously as Dengyō Daishi.

Life

Born Mitsukuni in Ōmi Province, Saichō entered the priesthood at a young age. In 794 he established a hermitage on Mount Hiei, which grew into the great temple Enryaku-ji. In 804 he travelled to China as part of an official embassy, studied Tiantai (T'ien-t'ai) doctrine under Daosui and Xingman on Mount Tiantai, and returned in 805 with texts and teachings.

Tendai school

Saichō's Tendai school, centred on the Lotus Sutra, integrated esoteric (mikkyō) practice, meditation, precepts and Pure Land elements. His campaign for an independent Mahayana ordination platform (kaidan) on Mount Hiei was granted shortly after his death, a milestone in Japanese Buddhism. Enryaku-ji became one of the great monastic centres of Japan, and many later schools (Pure Land, Zen, Nichiren) trace their founders to monks trained there.
