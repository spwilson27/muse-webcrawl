Silbury Hill is a prehistoric artificial chalk mound near Avebury in the English county of Wiltshire. It is part of the Stonehenge, Avebury and Associated Sites UNESCO World Heritage Site. At 39.3 metres (129 ft) high, the hill is the tallest prehistoric human-made mound in Europe and one of the largest in the world; it is comparable in size to the smallest of the Egyptian pyramids.

Construction

Radiocarbon dating indicates construction began around 2400 BC, roughly contemporaneous with the later phases of Stonehenge. The mound was built in several stages from chalk, gravel, soil, and turf dug from a surrounding ditch, ultimately comprising some 324,000 cubic metres of material. Estimates suggest it may have required hundreds of workers and millions of person-hours.

Purpose

The purpose of Silbury Hill remains unknown. Excavations in the 18th and 19th centuries and a tunnel dug in 1968 found no burial at its centre. The flat summit and stepped profile have prompted theories ranging from a ceremonial platform to a symbolic representation of a primordial mound. Folklore associates the hill with a legendary King Sil and a golden statue said to be buried within.

Conservation

In 2000, heavy rain caused the collapse of a historic excavation shaft, and a major conservation project by English Heritage between 2007 and 2008 stabilized the mound. The hill is a Scheduled Monument, and climbing on it is prohibited.
