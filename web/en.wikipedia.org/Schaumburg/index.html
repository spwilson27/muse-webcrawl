Schaumburg is a district (Landkreis) in Lower Saxony, Germany, and the name of a historic county of the Holy Roman Empire. The modern district, formed in 1977, has its capital at Stadthagen and includes towns such as Bückeburg, Rinteln, Obernkirchen and Bad Nenndorf.

History

The County of Schaumburg emerged in the 12th century around Schaumburg Castle near Rinteln, ruled by the counts of Schaumburg, who later also became counts of Holstein. When Count Otto V died without heirs in 1640, the county was partitioned in 1647 between the Landgraviate of Hesse-Kassel, whose portion became the Hessian exclave of Schaumburg around Rinteln, and the counts of Lippe, whose share developed into the County and later Principality of Schaumburg-Lippe around Bückeburg.

Modern district

Schaumburg-Lippe survived as a small German state until 1946, when it joined Lower Saxony. The Hessian portion joined Prussia's Province of Hanover in the 19th century and later also became part of Lower Saxony. The present-day Landkreis Schaumburg unites most of the historic territory. The district is known for the Weser Hills (Wesergebirge), the spa town of Bad Nenndorf and Bückeburg Palace, still associated with the princely House of Schaumburg-Lippe.
