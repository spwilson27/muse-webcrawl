James Gibbs

James Gibbs (23 December 1682 – 5 August 1754) was a Scottish architect who trained in Rome and became one of the most influential British architects of the 18th century. His best-known buildings are the church of St Martin-in-the-Fields in London and the Radcliffe Camera in Oxford.

Life and work

Born in Aberdeen to a Catholic family, Gibbs studied architecture in Rome under Carlo Fontana. Returning to Britain, he was appointed to work on the new churches built under the 1711 Act, producing St Mary le Strand. His masterpiece, St Martin-in-the-Fields (1722–26) in Trafalgar Square, combined a classical temple portico with a tower and spire rising behind it — a formula copied in countless churches across Britain and the American colonies.

Other buildings and influence

Gibbs designed the Senate House in Cambridge, the Radcliffe Camera (1737–49), and numerous country houses. His Book of Architecture (1728) was one of the most widely used pattern books of the century, spreading his designs for churches, houses, doors and windows — including the rusticated, alternating-block door surround still called a "Gibbs surround" — throughout the English-speaking world.
