Hare quota

FieldElectoral systems
Named afterThomas Hare
FormulaTotal valid votes ÷ total seats
Used inLargest remainder method, STV

The Hare quota is a formula used in proportional representation systems to determine how many votes a party or candidate needs to win a seat. It is calculated by dividing the total number of valid votes by the number of seats to be filled.

Use

The Hare quota is used with the largest remainder method: parties first receive one seat for each full quota of votes, and remaining seats are distributed according to the largest leftover vote fractions. It is also one of the quotas historically used in the single transferable vote (STV), though the Droop quota is more common there today.

Properties

The Hare quota is the largest of the simple quotas and tends to be relatively favourable to smaller parties compared with the Droop quota, which sets a lower threshold. It is used in list-PR elections in countries including Bolivia, Costa Rica, and in Hong Kong's geographical constituencies.

Thomas Hare

The quota is named after Thomas Hare (1806–1891), the English political scientist who developed an early scheme of proportional representation by the single transferable vote.
