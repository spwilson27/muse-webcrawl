Haleakalā National Park

Haleakalā National Park is an American national park on the island of Maui in Hawaiʻi, encompassing the summit and slopes of Haleakalā, a massive shield volcano rising 10,023 feet (3,055 m) above sea level. The park covers about 33,000 acres and is famed for sunrise views above a sea of clouds from its summit.

Districts

The Summit District features the vast, colourful erosion valley at the volcano's top, sliding-sands cinder cones, and the endemic Haleakalā silversword plant. The coastal Kīpahulu District, reached by the Hana Highway, contains lush rainforest, waterfalls including Waimoku Falls, and the Pools of ʻOheʻo. The park is also prime habitat for endangered Hawaiian birds such as the nene goose.

History

Haleakalā was first protected within Hawaii National Park in 1916 and became a separate national park in 1976; it was designated an International Biosphere Reserve in 1980. In Hawaiian tradition, the summit basin is the place where the demigod Māui lassoed the sun to slow its passage.
