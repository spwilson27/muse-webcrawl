Harmonic (music)

In music, a harmonic is a tone produced by dividing a vibrating string or air column so that it sounds a pitch corresponding to one of the partials of the harmonic series, rather than its fundamental. Harmonics have a characteristic ethereal, flute-like quality and are used extensively on string instruments, guitar, and harp.

Natural harmonics

A player produces a natural harmonic by lightly touching a string at a node — most commonly at the midpoint (sounding an octave above the open string), at one-third of its length (an octave plus a perfect fifth), or at one-quarter (two octaves). The touch suppresses partials that do not have a node at that point, leaving the selected harmonic to ring. On guitar, harmonics at the 12th, 7th, and 5th frets are staples of both classical and rock playing, and "pinch harmonics" are a hallmark of electric guitar technique.

Artificial harmonics

On bowed strings, artificial harmonics are played by stopping the string firmly with one finger and touching it a fourth or fifth higher with another, allowing harmonics on any note, not only open strings. They feature prominently in virtuoso repertoire by Paganini, Sarasate, and many twentieth-century composers.

Notation

Harmonics are notated with a small circle above the note, with diamond-shaped noteheads, or by writing the sounding pitch with an indication of the string and node.
