Province of Massachusetts Bay

The Province of Massachusetts Bay was a Crown colony in British America, existing from 1691 to 1776. Created by a charter from William III and Mary II, it united the earlier Massachusetts Bay Colony, Plymouth Colony, and territories including Maine and (for a time) Nova Scotia. Its capital was Boston.

Government

The 1691 charter provided for a royally appointed governor, an elected assembly (the General Court), and a governor's council. Unlike the old theocratic charter, it mandated religious toleration for Protestants and based the franchise on property rather than church membership. Friction between royal governors and the assembly over salaries, taxation, and imperial policy grew through the 18th century.

Role in the Revolution

Massachusetts stood at the center of resistance to British measures such as the Stamp Act and the Townshend Acts. Events including the Boston Massacre (1770) and Boston Tea Party (1773) provoked the punitive Massachusetts Government Act of 1774, effectively ending charter government. The province's militia fought the first battles of the American Revolutionary War at Lexington and Concord in April 1775, and in 1780 the Commonwealth of Massachusetts adopted its own constitution.
