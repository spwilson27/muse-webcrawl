The New Palace (German: Neues Palais) is a palace situated on the western side of the Sanssouci park in Potsdam, Germany. Built by Frederick the Great between 1763 and 1769, it was the last great Prussian baroque palace and a deliberate demonstration of Prussia's undiminished power after the devastation of the Seven Years' War.

History

Construction began in 1763, the year the war ended, and finished in 1769. Unlike the intimate Sanssouci Palace, the New Palace was designed less as a residence than as a venue for royal receptions and apartments for guests, and as a "fanfaronade" — Frederick's own word — to display Prussian strength. It later became a favoured residence of Emperor Frederick III and of Wilhelm II, who modernized it and lived there until his abdication in 1918. The Potsdam Conference of 1945 was held at nearby Cecilienhof, not here, but the palace survived the Second World War largely intact. Today it is a museum administered by the Prussian Palaces and Gardens Foundation Berlin-Brandenburg, and the adjacent former palace buildings house parts of the University of Potsdam.

Architecture

The three-storey main block is over 200 metres long, crowned by a large central dome topped with the Prussian crown and three Graces. Its brick façades, painted to imitate more costly materials, carry more than 400 statues and dense sculptural ornament. Inside are some 200 rooms, including the vast Marble Hall, the concert rooms, and the celebrated Grotto Hall, decorated with shells, minerals and fossils. Facing the palace across the Mopke are the Communs, two enormous curved service buildings with colonnades — among the largest baroque structures of their kind.
