FamilyMuscicapidae

RangeEurope, Africa, Asia

The Old World flycatchers are a large family, the Muscicapidae, of small passerine birds restricted to the Old World (Europe, Africa and Asia), with a few recently introduced to Australia. The family includes more than 300 species, among them the chats, robins, forktails, and typical flycatchers such as the spotted flycatcher.

Description

Most are small, drab-plumaged insectivores, though some, such as the European robin and the redstarts, are more colourful. Many hunt by sallying out from a perch to snatch flying insects and returning to the same perch.

Taxonomy

Molecular studies have reshaped the family: many birds once classed as thrushes (chats and robins) and Old World warblers are now placed in Muscicapidae. The family is divided into several subfamilies, including Muscicapinae (typical flycatchers) and Saxicolinae (chats and stonechats).
