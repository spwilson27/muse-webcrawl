Jacksonville is a city in Pulaski County, Arkansas, United States, and a suburb of Little Rock, located roughly 15 miles north-east of the state capital. It is best known as the home of Little Rock Air Force Base, the largest employer in the area and the primary training base for the C-130 Hercules airlifter in the United States Air Force.

History

The community grew up around the Cairo and Fulton Railroad in the 1870s and was named for Nicholas Jackson, who deeded land for the railroad station. Jacksonville remained a small farming town until World War II and incorporated in 1941. Its modern growth began in 1955 with the construction of Little Rock Air Force Base, which transformed Jacksonville into a military community.

Near the city is the site of the Reed's Bridge battlefield, where in September 1863 Confederate forces delayed the Union advance on Little Rock during the American Civil War; the engagement is commemorated at Reed's Bridge Battlefield Heritage Park.

Geography and demographics

Jacksonville lies in central Arkansas within the Little Rock metropolitan statistical area. The population was about 29,477 at the 2010 census; more recent estimates place it near 27,000–28,000. The city is served by U.S. Route 67/167, a major corridor between Little Rock and the north-east of the state.

Education and community

In 2014 voters approved the creation of the Jacksonville North Pulaski School District, detaching the city's schools from the Pulaski County Special School District; the new district began operating in 2016 and opened a new Jacksonville High School in 2020. The Jacksonville Museum of Military History, housed in the former city hall, documents the area's military heritage, including the Arkansas Ordnance Plant of World War II and the Titan II missile programme.
