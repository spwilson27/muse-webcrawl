Tulcea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tulcea (Romanian pronunciation: [ˈtult͡ʃe̯a]; also rendered Tulcha in older sources) is a city in Northern Dobruja, Romania. It is the county seat of Tulcea County and the principal gateway to the Danube Delta. Situated on the right bank of the Danube, the city had a population of about 66,000 at the 2021 census.

History

The settlement was founded by the Greeks in the 7th century BC under the name Aegyssus, a name mentioned by Diodorus of Sicily and Ovid, who recorded in his Ex Ponto that it was named after its founder, a Dacian called Aegisos (Carpy Aegyssus). The Romans rebuilt and fortified it; it later passed through Byzantine, Bulgarian, Genoese and Ottoman hands, remaining an Ottoman port for roughly four centuries before joining Romania in 1878 after the Congress of Berlin.

Geography and role

Tulcea lies where the Danube begins to divide into its three main distributaries — the Chilia, Sulina and Sfântu Gheorghe arms — making it the natural departure point for boat excursions into the Danube Delta Biosphere Reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage Site renowned for its birdlife. Fishing, fish processing, shipbuilding and tourism are the city's main industries.

Landmarks

  • The Monument of Independence on Colnicul Hora hill, commemorating the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78
  • The Danube Delta Eco-Tourism Museum Centre and aquarium
  • The Art Museum and the Museum of History and Archaeology
  • The Aegyssus archaeological site and museum
  • The Azizyie Mosque, built in 1863

Demographics

Historically a cosmopolitan trading port, Tulcea has included Romanian, Lipovan Russian, Turkish, Tatar, Greek, Jewish, Bulgarian and Armenian communities; wooden and stone churches, mosques and a synagogue reflect this mixed heritage.
