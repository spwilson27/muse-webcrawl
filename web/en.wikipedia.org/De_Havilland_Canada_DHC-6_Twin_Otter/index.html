The de Havilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter is a Canadian 19-passenger twin-engined utility aircraft with short takeoff and landing (STOL) capability, developed by de Havilland Canada and produced from 1965 to 1988. Production was revived in 2010 by Viking Air as the Series 400.

Design and development

The Twin Otter was conceived as a twin-engined successor to the single-engined DHC-3 Otter, retaining its predecessor's rugged bush-plane character while adding turbine reliability. It is a high-wing monoplane powered by two Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6A turboprops, with a fixed tricycle undercarriage that can be fitted with floats, skis, or tundra tyres. Its large double doors and unpressurised cabin suit it to passenger, cargo, medevac, parachuting, and survey work.

Service history

The type has been used by airlines, air forces, and scientific organisations worldwide, from Caribbean island-hopping routes — including the famously short runway at Saba — to Antarctic resupply flights operated on skis. Production at Downsview, Ontario ran through Series 100, 200, and 300 aircraft until 1988, with 844 built. Viking Air acquired the type certificate, restarted production at Victoria, British Columbia as the Series 400 with modern avionics and more powerful engines, and delivered the first new aircraft in 2010.

Variants

  • Series 100/200/300 — original de Havilland Canada production, differing in engine power and nose length.
  • Series 400 — Viking Air production with PT6A-34 engines and glass cockpit.
  • Guardian 400 — maritime patrol and special-mission variant.

Specifications (Series 300)
