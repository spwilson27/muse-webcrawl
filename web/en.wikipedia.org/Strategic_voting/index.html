Strategic voting

Also calledTactical voting
FieldVoting theory, political science
Related lawDuverger's law

Strategic voting (or tactical voting) occurs when a voter casts a ballot for a candidate other than their sincere first preference in order to prevent an undesirable outcome — for example, supporting the strongest challenger to a disliked frontrunner rather than "wasting" a vote on a preferred candidate with little chance of winning.

Context

Strategic voting is most prominent under plurality voting systems such as first-past-the-post, where it underlies Duverger's law: voters desert smaller parties to avoid splitting the vote. The Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem shows that no non-dictatorial voting system for three or more candidates is wholly immune to strategic manipulation, though proportional and ranked systems change its frequency and form.
