Credit card fraud

Credit card fraud is the unauthorised use of a payment card, or of its number and security details, to obtain money or goods. Phishing is one of its principal supply chains: fake bank pages, bogus "verify your card" emails and counterfeit online shops exist to harvest card numbers, expiry dates and security codes directly from cardholders.

How phishing feeds card fraud

A victim who enters card details on a phishing page hands the attacker everything needed for "card-not-present" purchases online. The stolen data are typically sold in bulk on criminal marketplaces, combined with personal data from a data breach to defeat identity checks, and monetised within hours before the card is cancelled. Phishing kits often validate the number in real time and prompt for the victim's banking authentication codes as well, enabling transfers that bypass card controls entirely.

Countermeasures

Issuers respond with chip-and-PIN for in-person transactions, tokenisation for mobile wallets, behavioural fraud scoring, and strong customer authentication schemes such as 3-D Secure, which push a confirmation to the genuine cardholder. These measures reduce but do not eliminate losses, because the underlying weakness is the same one exploited by every confidence trick: a deceived cardholder who willingly supplies the secret.
