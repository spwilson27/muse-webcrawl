In the context of information security, social engineering is the psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or divulging confidential information. It differs from social engineering in the social sciences in that it is generally treated as a type of confidence trick for the purpose of information gathering, fraud, or system access.

Techniques

Phishing uses fraudulent messages that appear to come from reputable sources to induce recipients to reveal sensitive data. Pretexting involves inventing a scenario to engage the victim and obtain information under false pretences. Baiting tempts victims with something enticing — for example, infected USB drives left in public places. Vishing (voice phishing) and smishing (SMS phishing) apply the same principles by phone. Tailgating is a physical technique in which an unauthorized person follows an authorized one through a secured door.

Principles

Social engineers exploit human tendencies such as deference to authority, reciprocity, urgency, fear, and greed. Attackers often research targets using open-source intelligence before making contact, building a chain of small, plausible requests that culminate in the information or access sought.

Countermeasures

Defences include security awareness training, verification procedures for sensitive requests, multi-factor authentication, and organizational policies that normalize refusing or escalating suspicious demands. Because social engineering targets people rather than systems, purely technical controls are considered insufficient on their own.
