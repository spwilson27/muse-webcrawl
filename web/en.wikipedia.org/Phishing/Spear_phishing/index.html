Spear phishing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Spear phishing
TypeTargeted phishing attack
TargetSpecific individuals or organizations
MethodPersonalized fraudulent email or message using researched details
RelatedWhaling (targeting senior executives); business email compromise

Spear phishing is a targeted attempt to steal sensitive information such as account credentials or financial information from a specific victim or organization, often for malicious reasons. It is a form of phishing in which the attacker tailors the message using personal details about the target — such as their name, employer, job role, and colleagues — gathered from social media, company websites, or prior data breaches. This personalization makes spear phishing considerably more convincing than bulk phishing campaigns.

How it works

Attackers research a target, then craft a message that appears to come from a trusted sender — a manager, an IT department, or a known vendor — usually urging urgent action such as opening an attachment, following a link to a fake login page, or authorizing a payment. The related technique of whaling targets senior executives, and business email compromise (BEC) schemes impersonate executives to trick finance staff into fraudulent wire transfers.

Notable incidents

Spear phishing has been the entry vector in numerous high-profile intrusions, including the 2015 breach of the US Democratic National Committee and attacks on government agencies and defence contractors. The FBI has reported billions of dollars in losses attributed to business email compromise schemes.

Defences

Mitigations include phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication, email authentication protocols (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), staff training focused on verification habits rather than detection alone, and out-of-band confirmation for payment requests.
