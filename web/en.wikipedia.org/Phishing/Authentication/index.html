Authentication

Authentication is the process of verifying a claimed identity — of a person, device or service — before granting access to a system. It is the primary target of phishing: the attacker does not break the authentication mechanism but tricks the victim into surrendering the credentials it checks, usually by imitating a trusted login page.

Factors and phishing resistance

Authentication factors are conventionally grouped into something you know (passwords), something you have (a phone, a security token) and something you are (biometrics). Traditional multi-factor schemes that rely on one-time passwords remain phishable, because a real-time relay site can forward the code to the genuine service within its validity window. Modern phishing-resistant authentication, such as FIDO2 passkeys, binds the credential cryptographically to the legitimate origin, so a response produced for a look-alike domain is useless to the attacker.

Related risks

Stolen credentials and personal data harvested by phishing feed directly into account takeover, identity theft and downstream fraud such as credit card fraud. High-value victims may be individually targeted in whaling attacks, and corporate mailboxes are compromised through business email compromise.
