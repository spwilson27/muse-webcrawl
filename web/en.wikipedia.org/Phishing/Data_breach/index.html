Data breach

A data breach is a security incident in which sensitive, protected or confidential data is copied, viewed or stolen by an unauthorised party. Phishing stands on both sides of the phenomenon: it is one of the most common initial-access techniques that leads to a breach — an employee's stolen credentials open the door — and breaches in turn supply the raw material that makes later phishing campaigns convincing.

The phishing–breach cycle

When customer databases leak, the spilled names, email addresses, purchase histories and partial card numbers become targeting data. A phisher who knows which bank a victim uses, which parcel is genuinely in transit, or the name of the victim's manager can craft spear-phishing messages far more persuasive than generic spam; at the executive level this becomes whaling or business email compromise. Conversely, aggregated breach corpora containing billions of personal data records are traded to seed credential-stuffing attacks against services whose users reused passwords.

Consequences and defences

Major incidents — from the Yahoo breaches affecting three billion accounts to the Equifax breach of 2017 — have produced regulatory penalties, mandatory notification laws and a public expectation that organisations protect stored data with encryption, least-privilege access and phishing-resistant authentication for staff. Services such as breach-notification databases let individuals check whether their own addresses appear in known leaks and prompt password changes before phishers arrive.
