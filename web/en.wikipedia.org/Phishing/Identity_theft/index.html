Identity theft occurs when someone uses another person's personal identifying information — such as their name, identifying number, or credit card number — without permission, to commit fraud or other crimes. The term was coined in 1964. Identity theft is a major global problem, costing consumers and businesses billions of dollars annually.

Types

Identity theft takes several forms. Financial identity theft seeks economic benefit, for example opening credit accounts in the victim's name. Criminal identity theft occurs when a perpetrator gives a victim's name to police during an investigation. Medical identity theft involves using another's identity to obtain medical care or drugs. Synthetic identity theft combines real and fabricated information to create a new, fictitious identity.

Techniques

Common techniques include phishing and other forms of social engineering, malware, skimming devices on payment terminals, dumpster diving, and exploiting large-scale corporate data breaches. Stolen data is often sold on darknet marketplaces.

Effects and prevention

Victims may face damaged credit, debt collection for purchases they never made, and a lengthy remediation process. Prevention measures include using strong unique passwords, enabling multi-factor authentication, placing credit freezes, and monitoring financial statements. In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission provides recovery resources through IdentityTheft.gov.
