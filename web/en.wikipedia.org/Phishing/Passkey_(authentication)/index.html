Passkey (authentication)

A passkey is a FIDO2/WebAuthn credential: a cryptographic key pair in which the private key stays on the user's device (or in a synced keychain) and is released by a fingerprint, face scan or PIN. Passkeys are promoted as the phishing-resistant successor to passwords and one-time passwords, and as a direct countermeasure to phishing.

Why passkeys resist phishing

When a passkey is created, it is registered for a specific origin. During sign-in, the browser or operating system checks that the site requesting authentication matches that origin and only then lets the device sign the challenge. A phishing page on a look-alike domain therefore gets nothing: there is no shared secret to type, nothing to relay in real time, and no code that can be replayed at the genuine site. Unlike a password, the credential cannot be harvested by a fake form, leaked in a data breach of server-side hashes in a reusable form, or guessed.

Deployment

Major platforms — Apple, Google and Microsoft — synchronise passkeys across a user's devices, and most large consumer services now offer them. Hardware security tokens implement the same protocol for high-assurance use. Adoption is incomplete, and fallback methods such as SMS codes can reintroduce the phishing weakness; nevertheless, security agencies recommend passkeys as the strongest widely available defence against credential phishing, including the spear-phishing used in whaling attacks on executives.
