Personal data

Personal data is any information relating to an identified or identifiable living person: names, addresses, dates of birth, identification numbers, financial details, credentials and much else. It is the principal quarry of phishing — both what the phishing page extracts from its victim and what the phisher already knows in order to make the lure believable.

Personal data as bait and as loot

As bait, personal data personalises the attack. A message quoting the victim's real name, employer, recent order or manager's name — details bought from the proceeds of a data breach or scraped from social media — converts generic spam into spear-phishing, and at the top of an organisation into whaling. As loot, submitted personal data enables identity theft: account recovery flows are defeated with dates of birth and addresses, new credit is taken out in the victim's name, and payment details feed credit card fraud. A complete identity dossier assembled from several incidents is traded on criminal markets as a "fullz".

Protection

Data-protection law such as the European GDPR obliges organisations to minimise, secure and promptly report losses of personal data. For individuals, the practical defences are data minimisation (sharing less), unique passwords protected by phishing-resistant authentication, and scepticism toward any unsolicited request — the channel matters less than the fact that, as in any confidence trick, a stranger is asking for the data at all.
