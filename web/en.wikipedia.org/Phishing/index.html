Phishing

A phishing email impersonating a legitimate institution.

Phishing is a form of social engineering and a scam where attackers deceive people into revealing sensitive information or installing malware such as ransomware. Phishing attacks have become increasingly sophisticated and often transparently mirror the site being targeted, allowing the attacker to observe everything while the victim navigates the site and crosses any additional security boundaries with the victim.

As of 2020, phishing is by far the most common attack performed by cybercriminals; the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center recorded more incidents of phishing than any other type of computer crime.

Techniques

  • Email phishing — bulk fraudulent messages impersonating banks, services or employers, linking to credential-harvesting websites.
  • Spear phishing — messages targeted at specific individuals or organizations, using personal details to appear credible.
  • Whaling — spear phishing aimed at senior executives.
  • Clone phishing — a near-copy of a legitimate message the victim previously received, with links or attachments replaced by malicious ones.
  • Vishing and smishing — phishing by voice call or SMS text message.
  • Business email compromise — impersonation of executives or suppliers to induce fraudulent payments.

History

The term "phishing" — a variant of "fishing," spelled with the hacker convention of "ph" — emerged in the mid-1990s among hackers stealing AOL account credentials. Attacks shifted to online payment systems and banks in the early 2000s and have since spread to social media, cloud services and cryptocurrency users.

Countermeasures

Defenses include email authentication standards (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), spam and phishing filters, browser warnings, security awareness training, and — most robustly — phishing-resistant authentication such as FIDO2/WebAuthn security keys and passkeys, which bind credentials to the legitimate site's origin. Multi-factor authentication reduces, but does not eliminate, risk, as real-time phishing proxies can relay one-time codes.
