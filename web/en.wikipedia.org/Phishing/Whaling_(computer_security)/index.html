Whaling (computer security)

Whaling is a form of spear-phishing aimed at senior executives and other high-value targets — the "big fish" of an organisation. Where ordinary phishing casts a wide net, whaling crafts a bespoke lure for one person, using detailed personal data about the target's role, deals and habits.

Techniques

Whaling messages have impersonated courts (fake subpoenas), tax authorities, board members and regulators, and often arrive with researched references to genuine transactions. The goals include harvesting the executive's own credentials — a compromised C-suite mailbox powers business email compromise against the whole company — stealing material non-public information for trading fraud, and initiating fraudulent transfers directly. Because the target is senior, attackers exploit the reluctance of assistants and finance staff to question apparent executive instructions, a dynamic familiar from the classic confidence trick. Some campaigns begin with reconnaissance of leaked material from an earlier data breach.

Defences

Recommended countermeasures concentrate on the small population of likely targets: hardware security tokens or passkeys for their accounts, strict verification procedures for payments regardless of who appears to request them, and executive-specific awareness training. The term is a deliberate contrast with mass phishing: fewer casts, far larger catch per success.
