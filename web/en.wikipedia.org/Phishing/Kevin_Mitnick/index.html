Kevin Mitnick

Kevin Mitnick (1963–2023) was an American computer hacker and later security consultant, famous for a mid-1990s run from the FBI and for demonstrating that the weakest link in computer security is usually a person. His career is a foundational case study for phishing, because his signature technique — social engineering — is the same human manipulation on which phishing depends.

Social engineering before phishing

Mitnick's intrusions into companies such as Motorola, Nokia and Sun Microsystems relied less on technical exploits than on telephone calls: posing as an employee or technician, he persuaded staff to reveal passwords and internal information, or to run commands on his behalf. He later chronicled these techniques in The Art of Deception (2002), arguing that organisations armour their systems while leaving the human authentication channel — the helpful employee — wide open. Phishing is precisely this approach industrialised: the persuasive phone call replaced by millions of deceptive emails, each running the centuries-old script of the confidence trick.

Legacy

Arrested in 1995 after a widely publicised manhunt and released in 2000, Mitnick reinvented himself as a penetration tester and founded a security-training company whose simulated phishing campaigns test whether employees will surrender credentials. His story is routinely cited in awareness programmes: if a skilled talker could walk through the defences of major technology firms, an ordinary user facing a polished phishing page needs procedural defences — verification and phishing-resistant sign-in methods such as passkeys — rather than vigilance alone.
