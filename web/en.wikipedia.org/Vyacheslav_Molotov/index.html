Vyacheslav Mikhailovich Molotov (born Skryabin; 9 March 1890 – 8 November 1986) was a Soviet politician and diplomat who served as premier of the Soviet Union from 1930 to 1941 and as foreign minister under Joseph Stalin and again after Stalin's death. He was one of the few Old Bolsheviks to survive the Great Purge and remained a leading figure in Soviet politics for decades.

Early career

Molotov joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party in 1906 and adopted the pseudonym "Molotov" (from molot, "hammer"). He worked on the Bolshevik newspaper Pravda and was repeatedly arrested and exiled by the tsarist authorities. After the October Revolution of 1917 he rose through the party apparatus, becoming a close associate and loyal deputy of Joseph Stalin.

Premier and foreign minister

As chairman of the Council of People's Commissars (premier) from 1930, Molotov oversaw collectivization and the first Five-Year Plans. In May 1939 he replaced Maxim Litvinov as foreign minister, and in August of that year he negotiated the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, the non-aggression treaty with Nazi Germany that included secret protocols dividing Eastern Europe into spheres of influence. When Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, Molotov — not Stalin — announced the outbreak of war to the Soviet public.

During the Second World War he negotiated with the Western Allies, travelling to London and Washington in 1942, and represented the USSR at the Yalta and Potsdam conferences. The improvised petrol bomb used by Finnish troops against Soviet armour in the Winter War was mockingly dubbed the "Molotov cocktail" in his honour.

Later life

After Stalin's death in 1953 Molotov again served as foreign minister until 1956, when Nikita Khrushchev dismissed him for opposing de-Stalinization. In 1957 he joined the unsuccessful "Anti-Party Group" attempt to oust Khrushchev and was exiled to an ambassadorship in Mongolia, then expelled from the party in 1962. He lived in retirement in Moscow, unrepentant about his role under Stalin, and was quietly readmitted to the party in 1984, two years before his death.
