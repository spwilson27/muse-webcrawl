The Portuguese Air Force (Portuguese: Força Aérea Portuguesa, FAP) is the aerial warfare branch of the Portuguese Armed Forces. It was established as an independent service on 1 July 1952, uniting the earlier army and naval aviation arms. Its headquarters are at Alfragide, in the Lisbon metropolitan area, and its principal air bases include Monte Real (BA5), Beja (BA11), and Montijo (BA6).

History

Portuguese military aviation dates to 1914, when both the Army and the Navy created aeronautical services. Portuguese aviators participated in the First World War in Europe and in colonial campaigns. In 1922 Gago Coutinho and Sacadura Cabral made the first aerial crossing of the South Atlantic, from Lisbon to Rio de Janeiro.

The independent Air Force fought in the Portuguese Colonial War (1961–1974) in Angola, Mozambique, and Guinea, flying types such as the F-86 Sabre, Fiat G.91, and Alouette III helicopter. After 1974 the service was restructured for NATO air defence, maritime patrol, and transport roles.

Modern force

The FAP operates F-16 Fighting Falcon fighters for air defence, P-3C Orion maritime patrol aircraft, C-130 Hercules and C-295 transports, and EH101 Merlin and AW119 Koala helicopters. It also runs the Portuguese Air Force Academy and the Asas de Portugal aerobatic team legacy. Training is conducted on the Epsilon and Chipmunk fleets, and Portugal has ordered the Embraer KC-390 transports and A-29 Super Tucano aircraft.
