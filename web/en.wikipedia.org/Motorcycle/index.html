A motorcycle (motorbike, bike, or, if three-wheeled, trike) is a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle steered by handlebars from a saddle-style seat. Motorcycles are one of the most affordable forms of motorised transport worldwide, especially in Asia, where they serve as primary family vehicles, and are also used for sport, leisure and racing.

History

Experimenters attached steam and internal-combustion engines to bicycles in the 1860s–80s; the Daimler Reitwagen of 1885 is often considered the first true motorcycle. The Hildebrand & Wolfmüller (1894) was the first production model. Mass production by firms such as Indian and Harley-Davidson (both founded around 1901–03) followed, and motorcycles served extensively in both world wars. Post-war, Japanese manufacturers — Honda (whose Super Cub, launched 1958, became the best-selling motor vehicle in history), Yamaha, Suzuki and Kawasaki — came to dominate the world market.

Types

Categories include standard/naked bikes, cruisers, sport bikes, touring machines, dual-sport and off-road bikes (including motocross), scooters and mopeds. Engine sizes range from under 50 cc to over 2,000 cc.

Safety

Motorcycling carries a substantially higher risk of death or injury per distance travelled than driving a car, owing to the rider's exposure and the vehicle's instability. Helmets, protective clothing, anti-lock brakes and rider training significantly reduce the risk.

Culture

Motorcycles occupy a prominent place in popular culture, from post-war biker films to organised clubs and events such as the Sturgis rally and the Isle of Man TT races.
