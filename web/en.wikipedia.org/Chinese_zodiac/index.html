Chinese zodiac

The Chinese zodiac is a traditional classification scheme based on the Chinese calendar that assigns an animal and its reputed attributes to each year in a repeating twelve-year cycle. The twelve animals are the Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig.

Origins and the calendar

The cycle corresponds to the twelve Earthly Branches of the Chinese sexagenary system and to the orbit of Jupiter, once called the "Year Star." Animal signs were established by the Han dynasty and are combined with the five elements (wood, fire, earth, metal, water) to form a sixty-year cycle. A person's animal sign is determined by the lunar year of birth, changing at the Chinese New Year rather than January 1.

Legend of the Great Race

Folk legend tells that the Jade Emperor invited the animals to a great race to determine their order. The Rat, riding on the Ox and leaping ahead at the finish, took first place; the Pig arrived last. The Cat, tricked by the Rat in many versions of the tale, was excluded — explaining, in folklore, the enmity between cats and rats.

Astrological use

In Chinese astrology the year animal is said to influence personality and fortune, alongside the month ("inner animal"), day, and hour signs. The zodiac is widely used across East and Southeast Asia in New Year celebrations, fortune telling, and popular culture.
