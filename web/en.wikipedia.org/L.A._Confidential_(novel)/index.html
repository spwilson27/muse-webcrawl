L.A. Confidential (novel)

L.A. Confidential (1990), the third volume of James Ellroy's L.A. Quartet.

L.A. Confidential is a 1990 neo-noir crime novel by American author James Ellroy, the third book in his L.A. Quartet series, following The Black Dahlia (1987) and The Big Nowhere (1988) and preceding White Jazz (1992). Set in Los Angeles in the 1950s, it follows three very different LAPD officers whose lives converge on the investigation of a mass shooting at an all-night coffee shop, the fictional Nite Owl case.

Plot and characters

The novel interweaves the stories of Edmund Exley, an ambitious, by-the-book detective lieutenant building a political career; Wendell "Bud" White, a brutal plainclothes officer obsessed with violence against women; and Jack Vincennes, a celebrity narcotics detective who moonlights as a technical adviser on a television police show. Strand by strand, the Nite Owl investigation draws them into pornography rings, the fall of mob boss Mickey Cohen's empire, police corruption reaching the department's highest ranks, and the heroine — modelled on real crimes of the era, including the Bloody Christmas police-beating scandal of 1951. Ellroy's style in the novel alternates longer early chapters with the clipped, staccato prose for which he became famous.

Adaptation

The 1997 film adaptation, directed by Curtis Hanson and starring Kevin Spacey, Russell Crowe, Guy Pearce, and Kim Basinger, drastically condensed the novel's sprawling plot while preserving its central triangle of cops. It won two Academy Awards, including Best Adapted Screenplay and Best Supporting Actress for Basinger, and is frequently ranked among the greatest crime films ever made.
