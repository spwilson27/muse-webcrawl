Liverpool is a port city and metropolitan borough in Merseyside, North West England, on the eastern shore of the River Mersey estuary. Founded as a borough in 1207, it grew from a small trading port into one of the world's great maritime cities during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. It is famous as the home of The Beatles and of Liverpool Football Club and Everton Football Club, and for its waterfront skyline, the "Three Graces," and the Royal Liver Building.

History

King John granted Liverpool's first charter in 1207. The city prospered in the transatlantic trade of the eighteenth century — including, notoriously, a dominant share in Britain's slave trade — and in the nineteenth century handled vast flows of cotton, emigrants bound for North America and Australia, and ocean liners such as those of the Cunard and White Star lines. Its dock system and warehouses made it Britain's second port and one of the richest cities in the empire. Heavy bombing in the Liverpool Blitz of 1941, the decline of transatlantic shipping, and containerization brought severe economic decline in the postwar decades, accompanied by population loss; regeneration gathered pace from the 1990s onward.

Culture

Liverpool's musical heritage spans the Merseybeat boom of the 1960s led by The Beatles — who formed in the city in 1960 — through later acts from Echo & the Bunnymen to Frankie Goes to Hollywood. The city has more museums and galleries than any UK city outside London, including Tate Liverpool and the Walker Art Gallery. It was European Capital of Culture in 2008. Its maritime mercantile city was a UNESCO World Heritage Site from 2004 until its controversial removal from the list in 2021.

Sport

Liverpool F.C., based at Anfield, is one of England's most successful clubs, while Everton F.C. plays at Goodison Park and moved to a new stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock in 2025. The Grand National steeplechase is run at nearby Aintree.
