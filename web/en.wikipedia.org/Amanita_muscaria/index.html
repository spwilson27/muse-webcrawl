Amanita muscaria

Amanita muscaria, commonly known as the fly agaric, is a distinctive mushroom with a bright red cap dotted with white warts. Native throughout temperate and boreal regions of the Northern Hemisphere, it has been introduced widely in the Southern Hemisphere with plantation trees. It is the archetypal toadstool of fairy tales and folklore.

Toxicity and use

The mushroom is poisonous and psychoactive, containing ibotenic acid and muscimol; it was traditionally used as an insecticide (hence "fly agaric") and, in parts of Siberia, as an entheogen. It grows in mycorrhizal association with birches, pines, and other trees, and is generally not eaten except after careful detoxifying preparation in a few regional cuisines.
