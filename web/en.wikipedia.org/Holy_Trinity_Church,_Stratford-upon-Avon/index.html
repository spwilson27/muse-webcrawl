The Church of the Holy Trinity is a Grade I listed parish church of the Church of England in Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, England. Often called simply Holy Trinity Church or Shakespeare's Church, it stands on the banks of the River Avon in the Old Town and dates from 1210, although a church is recorded on the site from the Anglo-Saxon period. It is famous worldwide as the place where playwright William Shakespeare was baptised on 26 April 1564 and buried on 25 April 1616, two days after his death.

Architecture

The church is cruciform in plan, built of blue lias and ashlar stone, with a central tower and spire. Much of the present fabric is fourteenth- and fifteenth-century Perpendicular Gothic, including the chancel built by John de Stratford, later Archbishop of Canterbury, in the 1330s. The stained glass includes the Seven Sacraments window and windows by the Victorian workshop of Clayton and Bell. The churchyard, approached through an avenue of lime trees representing the twelve tribes of Israel and the twelve apostles, contains many historic graves.

Shakespeare connections

Shakespeare's baptism and burial are recorded in the parish registers displayed in the church. He is buried in the chancel, beneath a ledger stone bearing the famous curse epitaph: "Good friend for Jesus sake forbeare, / To dig the dust encloased heare. / Blessed be the man that spares these stones, / And curst be he that moves my bones." On the north wall nearby is the Shakespeare funerary monument, erected before 1623, showing the playwright with quill and paper; it is mentioned in the First Folio. Shakespeare's wife Anne Hathaway, his daughter Susanna Hall, and her husband John Hall are buried nearby.

The playwright retained a financial link to the church as a holder of a share in the Stratford tithes, which entitled him to burial in the chancel. Holy Trinity receives hundreds of thousands of visitors each year, making it one of the most visited parish churches in England.
