Geography of Asia

Asia is the largest continent on Earth, covering about 44.6 million square kilometres — roughly 30 percent of the planet's land area — and home to more than 4.7 billion people, over half the world population. Its geography spans arctic tundra in Siberia, the highest mountains on Earth, vast deserts, monsoon-fed river plains, and equatorial archipelagos.

Boundaries and regions

Asia is conventionally divided from Europe by the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caspian Sea, and the Caucasus, and from Africa by the Red Sea and the Isthmus of Suez. The United Nations geoscheme recognises regions including Northern Asia (Siberia), Central Asia, Western Asia, Southern Asia, Eastern Asia, and Southeast Asia. The continent borders the Arctic, Pacific, and Indian oceans.

Physical geography

The Himalayas, Karakoram, and Hindu Kush contain all of the world's peaks above 7,000 metres, including Mount Everest. Great river systems — the Yangtze, Yellow, Mekong, Ganges, Indus, Brahmaputra, Tigris–Euphrates, Ob, Yenisey, and Lena — drain to all three oceans. Deserts include the Arabian, Gobi, Taklamakan, and Thar, while Siberia contains the largest forest on Earth, the taiga, and Lake Baikal, the deepest and oldest lake in the world.

Climate

Asia exhibits every major climate type: polar in the far north, continental in Siberia (which records some of the Northern Hemisphere's coldest inhabited temperatures at Oymyakon), arid in the interior and southwest, and tropical in South and Southeast Asia. The Asian monsoon — seasonal wind reversals bringing summer rains to India, China, and Southeast Asia — shapes agriculture for billions of people.
