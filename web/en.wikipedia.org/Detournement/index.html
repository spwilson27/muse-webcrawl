Detournement

Détournement (French for "rerouting" or "hijacking") is a technique developed in the 1950s by the Letterist International and later the Situationist International, in which pre-existing artistic or media elements are reused in a new ensemble that reverses or subverts their original meaning. Guy Debord and Gil Wolman set out the method in their 1956 text "A User's Guide to Détournement".

Method

Détournement ranges from minor alterations — changing the captions of a comic strip or the dialogue of a film — to wholesale reuse of famous works in new contexts. The Situationists contrasted it with mere plagiarism: the detourned element gains its force from the collision between its familiar original meaning and the new, critical one. Debord's own films and the comic-strip collages published in the journal Internationale Situationniste are canonical examples.

Relation to recuperation

The Situationists paired détournement with its opposite, recuperation: the absorption of subversive ideas by the spectacle and their return as harmless commodities. They anticipated that advertising and mass media would themselves adopt détournement-like techniques, draining them of revolutionary force.

Legacy

Détournement is a direct ancestor of culture jamming, ad-busting, mashup and remix culture, and aspects of punk graphics, notably the work of Jamie Reid for the Sex Pistols. The practice remains a reference point in media studies and critical art practice.
