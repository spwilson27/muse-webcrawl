Cuban sandwich

A Cuban sandwich (Spanish: sándwich cubano) is a variation of a ham and cheese sandwich that originated in cafés catering to Cuban workers in Tampa and Key West, Florida, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It later became popular in Miami's Cuban exile community.

Composition

The sandwich is made with Cuban bread, sliced roast pork, glazed ham, Swiss cheese, dill pickles, and yellow mustard, then pressed and toasted in a plancha (sandwich press) until the bread is crisp and the cheese melts. The Tampa version traditionally includes Genoa salami, a legacy of Italian immigrants in Ybor City, while the Miami version omits it — a friendly rivalry between the two cities over the "authentic" recipe.

Recognition

In 2012, Tampa's city council designated the "Historic Tampa Cuban Sandwich" as the city's signature sandwich. Cuban sandwiches are now served throughout the United States and internationally.
