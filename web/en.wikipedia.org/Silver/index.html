Silver

Silver
Symbol: Ag · Atomic number: 47
Atomic mass: 107.8682
Appearance: lustrous white metal
Melting point: 961.78 °C
Density: 10.49 g/cm³
Group: 11 (transition metal)

Silver is a chemical element with the symbol Ag (from Latin argentum) and atomic number 47. A soft, white, lustrous transition metal, it exhibits the highest electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity and reflectivity of any metal. Silver occurs naturally in its pure, free form (native silver), as an alloy with gold and other metals, and in minerals such as argentite and chlorargyrite; most silver is produced as a byproduct of copper, gold, lead and zinc refining.

History and coinage

Silver has been valued since antiquity — mentioned in Genesis and mined in Anatolia and at Laurion in Attica, whose silver financed Athens' fleet. It formed the basis of many monetary systems (the pound sterling, the Spanish dollar and the name of currencies such as the franc and peso are linked to silver), and vast New World mines at Potosí and Zacatecas transformed the global economy after the 16th century. Most nations abandoned silver coinage in the 20th century, but silver retains a role as an investment and in commemorative coins.

Applications

Silver is used in electronics and electrical contacts, solar panels, catalysts, mirrors, jewellery and silverware (sterling silver is 92.5% silver alloyed with copper), and increasingly in antimicrobial coatings and textiles thanks to the oligodynamic effect of silver ions. Silver compounds include silver nitrate, silver halides once essential to photography, and silver oxide used in batteries.

Health and environment

Silver metal is not considered toxic, but soluble silver salts are poisonous, and chronic exposure can cause argyria, a permanent bluish-grey discolouration of the skin.
