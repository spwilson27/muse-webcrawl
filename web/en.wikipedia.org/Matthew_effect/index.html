Matthew effect

The Matthew effect of accumulated advantage is the principle that advantages tend to accumulate: "the rich get richer and the poor get poorer." The term was coined by sociologists Robert K. Merton and Harriet Zuckerman in 1968, taking its name from a parable in the Gospel of Matthew: "For to everyone who has, more will be given."

In science

Merton originally described how eminent scientists receive disproportionate credit for joint work, while less-known researchers are overlooked even when their contributions are comparable — a phenomenon also called the Matilda effect in the case of women scientists.

Other domains

The effect has been applied in education (early reading success compounding over time), economics, fame, and network science, where it is related to preferential attachment.
