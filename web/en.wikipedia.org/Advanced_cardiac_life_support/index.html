Advanced cardiac life support

Advanced cardiac life support (ACLS) or advanced cardiovascular life support is a set of evidence-based clinical interventions for the urgent treatment of cardiac arrest, stroke, myocardial infarction, and other life-threatening cardiovascular emergencies, provided by trained healthcare professionals. It builds on basic life support (BLS) — high-quality CPR and defibrillation — adding advanced airway management, intravenous access, cardiac rhythm monitoring, and pharmacotherapy.

Algorithms

Care follows algorithms published by bodies such as the American Heart Association (AHA), updated periodically from international consensus (ILCOR). Shockable rhythms (ventricular fibrillation and pulseless ventricular tachycardia) are treated with defibrillation and drugs such as epinephrine and amiodarone; non-shockable rhythms (asystole and pulseless electrical activity) centre on continuous CPR and epinephrine while reversible causes — the "Hs and Ts" — are sought and treated. Team dynamics, post-cardiac-arrest care, and targeted temperature management are integral parts of modern guidelines.

Training

ACLS certification courses are required for many physicians, nurses, paramedics, and other clinicians working in emergency and critical care settings, with renewal typically every two years.
