Brasília

Country: Brazil
Founded: 21 April 1960
District: Federal District
Design: Lúcio Costa (plan), Oscar Niemeyer (buildings)
The National Congress on the Monumental Axis

Brasília is the federal capital of Brazil and the seat of government of the Federal District. It was founded on 21 April 1960 under President Juscelino Kubitschek, who promoted the construction of a new capital in the country's interior to encourage development of the central region. The city replaced Rio de Janeiro as capital.

Planning and architecture

Brasília was planned by urbanist Lúcio Costa, whose "Plano Piloto" (Pilot Plan) gives the city a shape often compared to an aeroplane or a bird, organised along a Monumental Axis and a residential axis. Its principal buildings were designed by Oscar Niemeyer in a distinctive modernist style, with landscaping by Roberto Burle Marx. The Cathedral of Brasília, the Palácio da Alvorada, and the National Congress are landmarks. In 1987 the city was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its modernist urban planning and architecture.

Geography and population

The city sits on the Brazilian Highlands at about 1,100 metres elevation, beside the artificial Lake Paranoá. The Federal District's population is roughly three million, though only a fraction live in the planned central area; most live in surrounding satellite cities.
