Rabin fingerprint

Rabin fingerprint
Type: Rolling hash / fingerprinting scheme
Proposed by: Michael O. Rabin (1981)
Basis: Polynomial arithmetic over GF(2)

The Rabin fingerprint is a fingerprinting scheme proposed by Michael O. Rabin in 1981. It treats the input as a polynomial over the finite field GF(2) and computes its remainder modulo a randomly chosen irreducible polynomial. The resulting value serves as a compact fingerprint with provably low collision probability.

Method

An n-bit message is interpreted as a polynomial of degree less than n over GF(2). Dividing by a fixed irreducible polynomial of degree k yields a k-bit fingerprint. Because the computation is linear and incremental, the fingerprint can be computed as a rolling hash over a sliding window of data, similar in spirit to the Rabin–Karp string-search algorithm.

Applications

Rabin fingerprints underpin content-defined chunking in deduplication and backup systems (such as the approach used in LBFS and many modern storage systems), where rolling-hash boundaries split files into chunks whose average size is stable under insertions and deletions. They are also used in file-similarity detection and caching.
