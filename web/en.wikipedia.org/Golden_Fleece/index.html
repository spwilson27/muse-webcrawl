In Greek mythology, the Golden Fleece is the fleece of the golden-woolled, winged ram Chrysomallos, which was held in Colchis, on the eastern shore of the Black Sea. It is the object of the famous quest of Jason and the Argonauts, recounted most fully in Apollonius of Rhodes' epic Argonautica.

Origin

Chrysomallos was sent by the gods to rescue the children Phrixus and Helle from their stepmother Ino. Helle fell into the sea (the Hellespont) during the flight, but Phrixus reached Colchis, where he sacrificed the ram to Zeus and gave its fleece to King Aeëtes. The fleece was hung in a sacred grove of Ares and guarded by a sleepless dragon.

The quest

Jason, deprived of his rightful throne of Iolcus by his uncle Pelias, was sent to fetch the fleece as an impossible task. Sailing in the ship Argo with a company of heroes, the Argonauts reached Colchis, where, with the aid of Aeëtes' daughter Medea — who used her magic to lull the dragon — Jason seized the fleece and fled.

Interpretations

Scholars have proposed that the myth reflects real practices in the Caucasus region, where fleeces were used to strain gold dust from rivers, or early Greek voyages in search of wealth in the Black Sea. The fleece later gave its name to the Burgundian Order of the Golden Fleece, founded in 1430, one of Europe's most prestigious orders of chivalry.
