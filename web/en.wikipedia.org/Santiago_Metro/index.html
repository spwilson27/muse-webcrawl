The Santiago Metro (Spanish: Metro de Santiago) is a rapid transit system serving the city of Santiago, the capital of Chile. It is the second-oldest metro system in South America and one of the largest in Latin America, with seven lines and about 140 kilometres of track. It is operated by the state-owned Metro S.A. and carries roughly two million passengers per day.

History

The first section of Line 1 opened in 1975 during the military government of Augusto Pinochet, running beneath the Alameda, Santiago's main avenue. Subsequent decades saw steady expansion, including Lines 2, 4, 5, and 6, and fully automated Lines 3 and 6, which use driverless trains and platform screen doors. The system suffered extensive damage during the social unrest of October 2019 (the estallido social), when dozens of stations were burned or vandalised, prompting closures and a lengthy restoration programme.

Network and features

Lines 1, 2, 4, and 5 use rubber-tyred trains based on Paris Métro technology, while Lines 3 and 6 use conventional steel wheels with overhead power. Fares are integrated with the Red Metropolitana de Movilidad bus network through the bip! contactless card, and fares vary by time of day. Several stations display large-scale artworks under the MetroArte programme. New lines and extensions, including Line 7, are under construction or in planning.
