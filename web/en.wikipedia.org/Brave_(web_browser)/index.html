Brave (web browser)

Brave is a free and open-source web browser developed by Brave Software, Inc., based on the Chromium browser engine. First released in 2016, Brave distinguishes itself by blocking advertisements and website trackers by default, and by an optional advertising system that rewards users with the Basic Attention Token (BAT), a cryptocurrency, for viewing privacy-respecting ads. The company was co-founded by Brendan Eich, creator of JavaScript and co-founder of Mozilla, and Brian Bondy.

Features

Brave Shields blocks third-party ads, trackers, fingerprinting and cross-site cookies by default; users can adjust protections per site. The browser includes a built-in Tor mode ("Private Window with Tor"), native support for the IPFS decentralised protocol (added in 2021), a cryptocurrency wallet, the Brave Talk video-conferencing service, the Brave Search engine (launched 2021, built from the acquired Tailcat index), and an AI assistant named Leo (2023). Brave also upgrades connections to HTTPS where possible and strips known tracking parameters from URLs.

Brave Rewards and BAT

Users who opt in to Brave Rewards view notifications-style ads and receive a share of revenue in Basic Attention Tokens, which can be tipped to participating websites and creators or exchanged. The model aims to replace surveillance-based advertising: ad matching happens on-device from a downloaded catalogue, so browsing history need not leave the machine. The system has drawn both interest and criticism — publishers initially objected when Brave proposed replacing their ads with its own, and cryptocurrency aspects have attracted regulatory and press scrutiny.

History and reception

Brave Software was founded in 2015; the browser launched for desktop and mobile in 2016 and reached version 1.0 in November 2019, by which time it reported millions of monthly users, passing 50 million monthly active users in early 2022. Reviews generally praise its speed and out-of-the-box privacy, while controversies have included a 2020 incident in which affiliate referral codes were appended to some cryptocurrency exchange URLs (removed and apologised for by Eich) and periodic debate over its growing bundle of extra services. Brave remains one of the most prominent Chromium-based alternatives to Google Chrome that is not controlled by a large platform company.
