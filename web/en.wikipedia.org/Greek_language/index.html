Greek language

Greek has the longest documented history of any Indo-European language.

Greek (modern Greek: ελληνικά, elliniká) is an independent branch of the Indo-European language family, native to Greece, Cyprus, and other parts of the Eastern Mediterranean. It has the longest documented history of any living Indo-European language, spanning at least 3,400 years of written records. Today it is spoken by at least 13.5 million people.

History

Greek has been spoken in the Balkan peninsula since around the 3rd millennium BC. The earliest written evidence is the Linear B tablets of Mycenaean Greece (c. 1450 BC). After the Bronze Age collapse, writing was lost and then reinvented with the adoption of the Greek alphabet in the 8th century BC. Classical Greek — the language of Homer, Plato and Thucydides — flourished in the 5th and 4th centuries BC, with dialects including Attic, Ionic and Doric.

Through the conquests of Alexander the Great, a common form, Koine Greek, spread across the eastern Mediterranean and became the lingua franca of the Hellenistic world and the Roman East; it is the language of the New Testament. Medieval (Byzantine) Greek evolved over the empire's thousand-year history. Modern Greek emerged after 1453, and for much of the modern state's history an official diglossia existed between the archaizing Katharevousa and the vernacular Demotic; Demotic became the official standard in 1976.

Characteristics

Greek is a fusional language with a rich inflectional system: nouns decline in three genders and four cases, verbs conjugate for person, number, tense, aspect, mood and voice. The modern language has a five-vowel system and a pitch accent reduced to a single stress accent. Stress is marked with an acute accent (the tonos) in the monotonic orthography adopted in 1982.

Influence

Greek has supplied a vast vocabulary to other languages, especially in science, medicine, philosophy and politics — words such as democracy, theatre, biology and telephone are Greek in origin, often transmitted through Latin.
