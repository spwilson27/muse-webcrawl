Alexander Nevsky (film)

Alexander Nevsky (Russian: Александр Невский) is a 1938 Soviet historical drama film directed by Sergei Eisenstein, with Dmitri Vasilyev. It depicts the 13th-century struggle of Prince Alexander Nevsky of Novgorod against the invading Teutonic Knights, culminating in the Battle on the Ice of 1242. The film features a celebrated score by Sergei Prokofiev and stars Nikolay Cherkasov in the title role. It is widely considered Eisenstein's finest sound film and a classic of world cinema.

Production and themes

Made at Mosfilm, the film was shot in 1937–38, with the Battle on the Ice (filmed on a summer set using chalk, sand and glass for ice) forming its centrepiece. Made as Nazi Germany threatened the Soviet Union, the film carried an unmistakable anti-German message; after the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 1939 it was withdrawn, but it was re-released following the German invasion of 1941 and became a powerful patriotic rallying point.

Music

Prokofiev's score, later reworked into his cantata Alexander Nevsky, Op. 78, is a landmark of film music; Eisenstein and Prokofiev collaborated closely, sometimes matching music to the edited image and sometimes the image to the music — a hallmark of Eisenstein's theory of "vertical montage".

Legacy

The film regularly appears in polls of the greatest films ever made, and its imagery and score influenced later epics, including the works of John Williams and the battle scenes of many historical films.
