Nord Stream

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nord Stream is a system of offshore natural gas pipelines running under the Baltic Sea from Russia to Germany. It comprises the original Nord Stream 1, a pair of pipelines from Vyborg to Greifswald with an annual capacity of 55 billion cubic metres, in operation from 2011–2012, and Nord Stream 2, a parallel pair from Ust-Luga completed in 2021 but never certified for operation.

History

The project was led by Gazprom with European partners including Wintershall, E.ON, Gasunie, and Engie. It was controversial from the outset: Poland, the Baltic states, Ukraine, and the United States objected that it increased European dependence on Russian gas and bypassed transit countries, while Germany defended it as a commercial project.

2022 sabotage

Nord Stream 1 flows were halted by Russia in the summer of 2022 amid the confrontation over the invasion of Ukraine. On 26 September 2022, explosions damaged three of the four strings near the Danish island of Bornholm, releasing large quantities of methane. Investigations by Denmark, Sweden, and Germany followed; responsibility for the sabotage has been publicly attributed by various reports to a pro-Ukrainian group, though the full picture remains disputed, and one string of Nord Stream 2 remained intact but unused.
