The RCMP Musical Ride is a ceremonial performance by the Royal Canadian Mounted Police in which a troop of 32 riders on black horses performs intricate cavalry drills and formations, set to music. Dressed in the force's iconic scarlet serge tunic and carrying lances with red-and-white pennons, the riders execute figures such as the "dome," the "charge," and interweaving wheels and crosses at the trot and gallop. The Musical Ride is one of Canada's best-known national symbols and tours the country each summer, also performing internationally.

The Musical Ride performing one of its signature formations.

History

The Ride grew out of cavalry exercises practised by the North-West Mounted Police in the 1870s; the first recorded public performance dates to 1876 at Fort Macleod, and regular performances began at the Regina training depot in 1887. The unit toured Britain and the United States around the turn of the twentieth century and performed for royalty, cementing its ceremonial role. Today the Ride is based at the RCMP stables in Rockcliffe, Ottawa, where visitors can tour the facility and meet the horses.

The horses

The Musical Ride's horses are black Hanoverians bred at the RCMP's own breeding farm in Pakenham, Ontario. Horses begin training at age three and typically join the Ride at six, serving for many years. Riders are regular RCMP members who volunteer for the posting; they receive equitation training before joining the tour, and each rider cares for their own horse throughout the season.

Performances

Each summer the troop performs dozens of shows in communities across Canada, raising money for local charities, and it appears at state occasions at home and abroad — including state funerals, royal visits, and international tattoos. The Ride performed for Queen Elizabeth II, an honorary RCMP commissioner, on many occasions; in 2012 the RCMP presented her with a Musical Ride horse for the Diamond Jubilee.
