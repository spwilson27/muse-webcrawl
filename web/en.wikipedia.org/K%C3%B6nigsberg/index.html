Königsberg (German for "King's Mountain") was the historic German city on the Pregel River that is now Kaliningrad, Russia. Founded in 1255 by the Teutonic Knights during the Northern Crusades, it became the coronation city of the Prussian monarchy and the capital of East Prussia. The city was largely destroyed in the Second World War, was captured by the Soviet Union in 1945, and was renamed Kaliningrad in 1946 after its German population was expelled.

History

The site grew around a castle built on the advice of King Ottokar II of Bohemia. Königsberg joined the Hanseatic League and prospered as a Baltic port. In 1701 Elector Frederick III of Brandenburg was crowned there as Frederick I, first King in Prussia. The Albertina university, founded in 1544, made the city an intellectual center; the philosopher Immanuel Kant lived and worked in Königsberg his entire life.

The bridge problem

The city is famous in mathematics for the Seven Bridges of Königsberg: four land areas joined by seven bridges across the Pregel. Leonhard Euler's 1736 proof that no walk crossing each bridge exactly once exists founded the field of graph theory.

Destruction and aftermath

British bombing in 1944 and the Soviet assault of April 1945 left the city in ruins. Under the Potsdam Agreement northern East Prussia passed to the USSR; the surviving Germans were expelled westward and replaced by Soviet settlers. Ruins such as Königsberg Cathedral—with Kant's tomb—were later partly restored.
