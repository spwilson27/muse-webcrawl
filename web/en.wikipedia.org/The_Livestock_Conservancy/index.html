The Livestock Conservancy

The Livestock Conservancy is a nonprofit organisation based in Pittsboro, North Carolina, dedicated to protecting endangered livestock and poultry breeds from extinction. Founded in 1977 as the American Minor Breeds Conservancy — and later known as the American Livestock Breeds Conservancy — it is the pioneer organisation for heritage-breed conservation in North America.

Work

The Conservancy maintains a Conservation Priority List that classifies more than 150 breeds of cattle, horses, sheep, goats, pigs, donkeys, chickens, ducks, geese and turkeys as critical, threatened, watch or recovering, based on population surveys. It conducts breed censuses, supports breed associations, publishes husbandry and marketing resources, and promotes heritage breeds in the food system through directories and the "Shave 'Em to Save 'Em" initiative for rare-breed fibre.

Notable rescues

The organisation is credited with helping rescue breeds such as the Randall cattle of Vermont, Texas Longhorns in their distinct traditional types, Hog Island sheep from feral island populations, and the critically rare Santa Cruz sheep. Its annual priority-list updates are a standard reference for North American farm-animal genetic conservation.
