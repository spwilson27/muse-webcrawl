Dominant seventh chord

A dominant seventh chord, or major-minor seventh chord, is a seventh chord typically built on the dominant of a major or minor key: a major triad plus a minor seventh above the root. In C major, the dominant seventh chord is G–B–D–F, symbolized G7.

Function

The chord contains a tritone between the third and the seventh, giving it a strong tension that characteristically resolves to the tonic chord (V7–I). This resolution is a cornerstone of tonal harmony, and the chord is ubiquitous in classical music, jazz, blues, and popular music.

Resolution and voice leading

Traditionally the seventh of the chord resolves downward by step, while the leading note (the third) resolves upward to the tonic. Complete and incomplete forms of the chord are both common; in the incomplete form the fifth is omitted and the root doubled.
