Weimar National Assembly

The Weimar National Assembly (German: Verfassunggebende Deutsche Nationalversammlung) was Germany's first constituent assembly, elected on 19 January 1919 in the aftermath of the First World War and the November Revolution. It met in the town of Weimar rather than in turbulent Berlin, and it gave its name to the Weimar Republic.

Work

The election, held under proportional representation, was the first German national election in which women could vote; 37 of the 423 delegates were women. The assembly elected Friedrich Ebert as Reich President and Philipp Scheidemann as head of government, and on 31 July 1919 adopted the Weimar Constitution, which came into force on 11 August. The constitution established a democratic republic with universal suffrage, a strong president, and extensive civil rights, although its emergency powers (Article 48) later proved fateful.

Treaty of Versailles

The assembly reluctantly approved the Treaty of Versailles in June 1919, after an Allied ultimatum threatened renewed invasion. It completed its work and dissolved in 1920, when the first Reichstag of the republic was elected.
