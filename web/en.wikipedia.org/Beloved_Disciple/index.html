Beloved Disciple

The disciple whom Jesus loved (Greek: ὁ μαθητὴς ὃν ἠγάπα ὁ Ἰησοῦς), commonly called the Beloved Disciple, is a figure who appears several times in the Gospel of John but in none of the Synoptic Gospels. He reclines next to Jesus at the Last Supper and asks who will betray him; he stands at the foot of the cross and receives Jesus's mother into his care; he runs with Peter to the empty tomb and is the first to believe; and he appears in the final chapter at the Sea of Galilee. The Gospel's closing verses present him as the witness whose testimony underlies the book itself. His identity has been debated since antiquity: tradition identifies him with John the Apostle, son of Zebedee.

Appearances in John

The figure is introduced at the Last Supper (John 13:23), leaning on Jesus's breast. At the crucifixion (John 19:26–27), Jesus entrusts his mother to the disciple "from that hour." In John 20, the Beloved Disciple outruns Peter to the tomb, sees the linen cloths, and "believed." In John 21 he recognises the risen Jesus first ("It is the Lord!") and is the subject of Jesus's saying that prompts the rumour he would not die. The epilogue (21:24) declares: "This is the disciple who is bearing witness about these things... and we know that his testimony is true."

Identity debate

Church tradition from Irenaeus onward equated the Beloved Disciple with John of Zebedee, one of the Twelve, and hence with the evangelist. Many modern scholars accept a connection with a historical "John" — whether the apostle or a distinct "John the Elder" behind the Johannine community — while others argue for Lazarus (the only other man the Gospel says Jesus "loved"), for Mary Magdalene (a minority view popularised in fiction), or that the figure is a literary ideal representing the model believer and the community's founding witness. The anonymity itself may be deliberate, inviting readers to take the disciple's place.
