RoleAdjudicating disputes involving government agencies

An administrative law judge (ALJ) in the United States is an official who presides over trials and adjudicates claims and disputes involving administrative agencies. ALJs conduct hearings, rule on evidence, and issue initial or recommended decisions in matters such as benefits claims, regulatory enforcement, licenses, and workplace disputes.

Federal ALJs

Federal administrative law judges are appointed under the Administrative Procedure Act of 1946, which guarantees their independence from the agencies whose cases they hear through separate pay, tenure protections, and merit selection. The largest employer of ALJs is the Social Security Administration, whose judges decide disability benefit appeals; other major users include the Securities and Exchange Commission, the National Labor Relations Board, and the Department of Labor.

State ALJs

Many states maintain a central panel of administrative law judges who hear cases for multiple agencies, while others assign hearing officers within individual agencies. Titles and qualifications vary; some states require appointees to be licensed attorneys with litigation experience.
