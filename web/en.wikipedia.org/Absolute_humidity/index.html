Absolute humidity

Definition: Mass of water vapour per unit volume of air

Units: g/m³

Symbol: AH or ρw

Absolute humidity is the measure of water vapour in the air, defined as the mass of water vapour divided by the volume of the air-and-water-vapour mixture. It is usually expressed in grams of vapour per cubic metre of air (g/m³).

Definition and calculation

Mathematically, AH = mH2O / Vnet. Because the volume of a parcel of air changes with temperature and pressure, absolute humidity also changes when a parcel expands or contracts even if no water is added or removed — a drawback compared with measures such as specific humidity or mixing ratio. Absolute humidity can be computed from vapour pressure and temperature using the ideal gas law; saturated air at 30 °C holds about 30 g/m³, whereas saturated air at 0 °C holds only about 5 g/m³.

Relation to relative humidity

Relative humidity is the ratio of the actual vapour pressure to the saturation vapour pressure at the same temperature; for a given temperature it is proportional to absolute humidity. Weather reports normally give relative humidity because it better indicates comfort and the likelihood of precipitation, while absolute humidity is used in engineering applications such as drying, HVAC design and respiratory physiology.
