The Times is a British daily national newspaper based in London. It began in 1785 under the title The Daily Universal Register, adopting its present name on 1 January 1788. The Times and its sister paper The Sunday Times (founded in 1821) are published by Times Media, since 1981 a subsidiary of News UK, in turn wholly owned by News Corp.

The Times is generally regarded as Britain's "newspaper of record." It pioneered the use of war correspondents — most famously William Howard Russell during the Crimean War — and was the first newspaper to bear the name Times, lending it to numerous papers around the world, including The Times of India and The New York Times.

History

The paper was founded by John Walter I as The Daily Universal Register. Under nineteenth-century editors such as Thomas Barnes and John Thadeus Delane it became one of the most influential papers in the world, known for its independent editorial line and extensive foreign coverage. The newspaper's typeface, Times New Roman, was commissioned from Stanley Morison in 1931 and first issued by the paper in 1932, becoming one of the most widely used typefaces in history.

In the twentieth century the paper passed through the hands of the Astor family before being bought by Roy Thomson in 1966. After a year-long shutdown during an industrial dispute in 1978–79, it was acquired by Rupert Murdoch's News International in 1981. The Times switched from broadsheet to compact format in 2004 and introduced a metered paywall for its digital edition in 2010, one of the first major newspapers to do so.

Content and influence

The paper includes news, business, sport, arts coverage, the Times2 supplement, and a daily crossword. Its leading articles ("leaders") comment on the issues of the day, and its court pages traditionally recorded official and social announcements. Along with The Guardian and The Daily Telegraph, it is considered one of Britain's major quality newspapers.
