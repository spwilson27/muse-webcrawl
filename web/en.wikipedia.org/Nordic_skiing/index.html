Nordic skiing

Nordic skiing encompasses the various types of skiing in which the toe of the ski boot is fixed to the binding in a manner that allows the heel to rise off the ski, unlike alpine skiing, in which the boot is attached to the ski from toe to heel. Olympic events under the Nordic umbrella include cross-country skiing, ski jumping, and Nordic combined.

A cross-country skier using Nordic equipment

Disciplines

Cross-country skiing is practised recreationally and competitively in classic and skating (freestyle) techniques. Ski jumping involves launching from a ramp to achieve maximum distance with style points. Nordic combined pairs a ski jump with a cross-country race, scored by the Gundersen method. Telemark skiing, using free-heel bindings with a distinctive lunging turn, is a related discipline.

History

Skiing originated in Scandinavia and Siberia as a means of winter transport thousands of years ago; the word "ski" derives from Old Norse skíð. Sondre Norheim of Telemark, Norway, pioneered modern techniques and equipment in the 19th century. Nordic events have featured at the Winter Olympics since the inaugural 1924 Chamonix Games.
