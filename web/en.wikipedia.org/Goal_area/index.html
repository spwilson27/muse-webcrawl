Goal area

The goal area, colloquially the "six-yard box", is a rectangular marked area in front of the goal in association football, defined by Law 1 of the Laws of the Game. It lies inside the larger penalty area and is used chiefly to determine where goal kicks are taken and where the goalkeeper may be protected from challenges in certain competitions' interpretations.

Dimensions

Two lines are drawn at right angles to the goal line, 5.5 metres (6 yards) from the inside of each goalpost. These lines extend 5.5 metres into the field of play and are joined by a line drawn parallel to the goal line. The area enclosed by these lines and the goal line is the goal area, measuring 18.32 metres (20 yards) wide by 5.5 metres deep.

Function in the Laws

  • A goal kick is taken from any point within the goal area.
  • If play is stopped with the ball in the goal area for a dropped ball, the ball is dropped for the defending team's goalkeeper.
  • An indirect free kick awarded to the attacking team inside the goal area is taken from the nearest point on the goal-area line parallel to the goal line — so no attacking free kick can be closer than 5.5 metres to the goal.
  • An indirect free kick to the defending team inside its own goal area may be taken from anywhere within it.

Historically, the goal area once had a different shape: before 1902 the goal kicks were taken from within semicircular zones near each post, and the modern rectangular box dates from the early twentieth century standardisation of pitch markings.
