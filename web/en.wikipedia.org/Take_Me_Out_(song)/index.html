Take Me Out (song)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"Take Me Out" is a song by Scottish indie rock band Franz Ferdinand. It was released as the second single from their eponymous debut studio album in the United Kingdom on 12 January 2004 and in the United States on 12 April 2004, both through Domino Records. The song entered the UK Singles Chart at number three and reached number 66 on the US Billboard Hot 100. It was a number-one hit on the UK Indie Chart and the Canadian Singles Chart.

Composition

The song is notable for its abrupt tempo change: it begins at a fast 146 beats per minute before slowing, about a minute in, to a funkier 111 bpm groove that carries the rest of the track. Singer Alex Kapranos described the song as being about the tension of a standoff, likening it to two snipers aiming at each other — "take me out" as a plea to end the suspense.

Reception and legacy

At the 47th Grammy Awards, "Take Me Out" was nominated for Best Rock Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal. The song was ranked number 327 on Rolling Stone's 2021 list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time, and NME placed it among the greatest indie anthems. Its accompanying music video, directed by Jonas Odell, draws on Dada collage and the work of Russian avant-garde artists.

The song has been widely used in films, television programmes, advertisements and video games, and is regarded as one of the defining songs of the 2000s post-punk revival.
