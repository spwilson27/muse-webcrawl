Regulus

Regulus (α Leonis)
Constellation: Leo
Apparent magnitude: 1.35
Distance: about 79 light-years
Spectral type: B7 V (primary)

Regulus is the brightest object in the constellation Leo and one of the brightest stars in the night sky, lying approximately 79 light-years from the Sun. Its name, from Latin, means "little king" or "prince".

Characteristics

Regulus is a multiple star system composed of at least four stars. The primary, Regulus A, is a blue-white main-sequence star of spectral type B7 V with about 3.8 times the mass of the Sun. It rotates extremely rapidly — once every 15.9 hours — which distorts it into a pronounced oblate spheroid; if it rotated slightly faster it would break apart.

Companions

Regulus A is orbited closely by a white dwarf companion, and a more distant pair of small stars, Regulus B and C, lie about 5,000 astronomical units away. The system sits near the ecliptic and is regularly occulted by the Moon.
