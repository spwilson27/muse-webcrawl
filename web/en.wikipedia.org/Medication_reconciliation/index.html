Medication reconciliation

Medication reconciliation is the process of creating the most accurate list possible of all medications a patient is taking — including drug name, dosage, frequency, and route — and comparing that list against the physician's admission, transfer, and/or discharge orders, with the goal of providing correct medications to the patient at all transition points within the hospital and at discharge. It is a recognised patient safety practice aimed at preventing medication errors such as omissions, duplications, dosing errors, and drug interactions.

Process

Steps typically include developing a complete medication list (often called the Best Possible Medication History, gathered from the patient and other sources), developing discharge and transfer lists, comparing them, making clinical decisions based on the comparison, and communicating the new list to appropriate caregivers and to the patient. Pharmacists frequently lead or support the process.

Regulatory and safety context

The Joint Commission made medication reconciliation a National Patient Safety Goal in the United States, and the World Health Organization highlighted it in its "Medication Without Harm" challenge and the High 5s initiative. Electronic health records can support reconciliation but can also propagate inaccurate lists; transitions of care remain among the highest-risk points for adverse drug events.
