Symmetric group

In abstract algebra, the symmetric group defined over a set is the group whose elements are all the bijections from the set to itself, and whose group operation is the composition of functions. For a finite set of n elements, the symmetric group Sn contains n! permutations.

Definition and notation

Permutations are written in two-line notation, one-line notation, or as products of disjoint cycles. Every permutation decomposes uniquely into disjoint cycles and can be expressed as a product of transpositions; the parity (even or odd) of that decomposition is fixed, and the even permutations form the alternating group An, a normal subgroup of index 2.

Properties

Sn is non-abelian for n ≥ 3. By Cayley's theorem every group is isomorphic to a subgroup of some symmetric group, making these groups universal in group theory. Symmetric groups are generated by a single n-cycle together with any transposition, and their conjugacy classes correspond exactly to partitions of n (cycle types).

Applications

Symmetric groups arise wherever permutations act: in Galois theory (where the solvability of A5's simplicity explains why no general quintic formula exists), combinatorics, representation theory, and the physics of identical particles, whose states transform under representations of Sn.
