Robert Harley, 1st Earl of Oxford and Earl Mortimer

Robert Harley, statesman and patron of letters

Robert Harley, 1st Earl of Oxford and Earl Mortimer, KG PC FRS (5 December 1661 – 21 May 1724), was an English statesman and literary patron of the late Stuart and early Georgian periods. He was Speaker of the House of Commons and effectively Britain's chief minister from 1710 to 1714.

Political career

Harley entered Parliament in 1689 as a Whig but moved toward the Tories. As Secretary of State and then Chancellor of the Exchequer he managed Queen Anne's government; in 1711 he founded the South Sea Company and was created Earl of Oxford. Days later he survived an assassination attempt by Antoine de Guiscard, which increased his popularity.

Ministry and fall

As Lord High Treasurer, Harley negotiated the Peace of Utrecht (1713), ending British involvement in the War of the Spanish Succession. Losing the Queen's favour to Viscount Bolingbroke, he was dismissed days before Anne's death in 1714. The new Hanoverian regime impeached him for treason; he spent two years in the Tower of London before acquittal in 1717.

Patronage

Harley was a celebrated patron of writers including Jonathan Swift, Alexander Pope, Daniel Defoe and John Gay. His manuscript collection forms the Harleian Collection of the British Library.
