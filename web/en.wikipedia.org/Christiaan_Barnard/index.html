Christiaan Barnard

Christiaan Neethling Barnard (8 November 1922 – 2 September 2001) was a South African cardiac surgeon who performed the world's first successful human-to-human heart transplant. The operation, carried out at Groote Schuur Hospital in Cape Town on 3 December 1967, made Barnard an international celebrity and marked a turning point in surgery and medical ethics.

Early life and training

Barnard was born in Beaufort West in the Karoo, the son of a Dutch Reformed Church minister. He studied medicine at the University of Cape Town, graduating in 1945, and worked in general practice before returning to research. His doctoral work concerned intestinal atresia in newborns. In 1956 he went to the University of Minnesota, where he trained in cardiothoracic surgery under pioneers of open-heart surgery and encountered the transplant research then under way in the United States.

The first heart transplant

Returning to Cape Town, Barnard built a heart-surgery unit at Groote Schuur Hospital. On the night of 2–3 December 1967, his team of about thirty removed the heart of Denise Darvall, a young woman fatally injured in a road accident, and transplanted it into Louis Washkansky, a 53-year-old grocer dying of heart failure. Washkansky regained consciousness and could speak, but died eighteen days later of pneumonia brought on by immunosuppressive drugs. Barnard's second patient, Philip Blaiberg, transplanted in January 1968, survived nineteen months, demonstrating that the operation could sustain life.

Later career

Barnard continued transplant work and developed techniques including the heterotopic ("piggyback") transplant. Rheumatoid arthritis in his hands forced him to retire from surgery in 1983. He wrote books on health and an autobiography, became a prominent and sometimes controversial public figure, and died of an asthma attack while on holiday in Cyprus in 2001.
