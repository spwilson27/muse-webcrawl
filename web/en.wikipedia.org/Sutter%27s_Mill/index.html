Sutter's Mill

Sutter's Mill
Location: Coloma, California, on the American River
Owner: John Augustus Sutter
Famous for: Gold discovery, 24 January 1848

Sutter's Mill was a water-powered sawmill owned by the pioneer John Augustus Sutter, located in Coloma, California, on the South Fork of the American River. It is famous as the site of the first documented discovery of gold in California.

Discovery of gold

On 24 January 1848, James W. Marshall, a foreman building the mill, found flakes of gold in the mill's tailrace. Sutter tried to keep the find secret, but word spread — famously amplified by merchant Sam Brannan — and by 1849 the California Gold Rush had begun, bringing some 300,000 prospectors ("forty-niners") to California.

Legacy

The rush overwhelmed Sutter's lands and ruined his empire, while transforming California: statehood followed in 1850. The mill site is now part of Marshall Gold Discovery State Historic Park, where a replica mill stands near the original location.
