The National Bank of Ukraine (NBU; Ukrainian: Національний банк України) is the central bank of Ukraine, headquartered in Kyiv. Established in 1991 following Ukrainian independence, it issues the national currency, the hryvnia, conducts monetary policy, supervises the banking system and manages the country's international reserves.

History and functions

The NBU's independence is guaranteed by the Constitution of Ukraine and the 1999 Law on the National Bank. It introduced the hryvnia in 1996, replacing the temporary karbovanets. Since 2015 the bank has formally operated an inflation-targeting regime, aiming at 5 percent inflation over the medium term, and moved the hryvnia to a floating exchange rate.

Recent developments

Under Governor Valeria Gontareva (2014–2017), the NBU closed dozens of insolvent banks and oversaw the nationalisation of PrivatBank, the country's largest lender, in 2016. Following Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, the bank imposed capital controls, fixed the exchange rate temporarily, and sharply raised its key policy rate to 25 percent to stabilise the financial system, easing as inflation fell in 2023–2024.
