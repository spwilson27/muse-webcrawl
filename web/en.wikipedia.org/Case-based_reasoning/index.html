Case-based reasoning

Case-based reasoning (CBR), broadly construed, is the process of solving new problems based on the solutions of similar past problems. In artificial intelligence, CBR is a problem-solving paradigm in which a system stores experiences as cases in a case base and solves new problems by retrieving and adapting the most similar previous cases.

The CBR cycle

The classical model, described by Aamodt and Plaza (1994), has four steps: Retrieve the most similar case or cases; Reuse the information from those cases to solve the problem; Revise the proposed solution; and Retain the parts of this experience likely to be useful for future problems. Unlike rule-based systems, a CBR system learns incrementally simply by storing new cases.

History and applications

The approach drew on Roger Schank's work on dynamic memory and was embodied in Janet Kolodner's CYRUS system in the early 1980s. Applications include help-desk diagnosis, legal reasoning, design, medical decision support, and recommender systems. CBR appeals where problems are hard to formalise as rules but past examples are plentiful.
