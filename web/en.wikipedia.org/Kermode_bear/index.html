Kermode bear

The Kermode bear (Ursus americanus kermodei), also known as the spirit bear, is a subspecies of the American black bear living in the Central and North Coast regions of British Columbia, Canada. It is noted for the white- or cream-coloured fur found in a proportion of its population.

White fur

About one in ten Kermode bears is white. The colour is caused by a recessive mutation in the MC1R gene — the same gene associated with red hair and fair skin in humans — and is not a form of albinism. White bears may have a fishing advantage in daylight, as they are less visible to salmon from below.

Habitat and conservation

The subspecies is named after Frank Kermode of the Royal British Columbia Museum. It lives in the temperate rainforest of the BC coast, including Princess Royal Island, and is the official mammal of British Columbia. Much of its habitat lies within the Great Bear Rainforest, large parts of which received protection in 2016.
