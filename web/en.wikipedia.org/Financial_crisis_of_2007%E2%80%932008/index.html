Financial crisis of 2007–2008

Date2007 – 2008 (aftermath to ~2012)
Also known asGlobal financial crisis (GFC)
CauseUS housing bubble, subprime lending, excessive leverage

The financial crisis of 2007–2008, also known as the global financial crisis (GFC), was a severe worldwide economic crisis that is considered by many economists to have been the most serious financial crisis since the Great Depression of the 1930s. It was preceded by a bubble in the United States housing market and followed by widespread bank failures, government bailouts, and the Great Recession.

Causes

The crisis had multiple causes: predatory and subprime mortgage lending, the securitisation of risky loans into mortgage-backed securities and collateralised debt obligations, failures of credit rating agencies, excessive leverage by investment banks, and inadequate regulation of the shadow banking system.

Collapse and response

The crisis intensified in September 2008 with the bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers, the largest in US history. Governments and central banks responded with massive liquidity injections, bank recapitalisations, quantitative easing, and fiscal stimulus. In the United States, the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) authorised $700 billion to stabilise the financial system.

Aftermath

The crisis triggered the Great Recession, high unemployment, the European sovereign debt crisis, and long-term regulatory reforms such as the Dodd–Frank Act in the US and Basel III capital standards internationally.
