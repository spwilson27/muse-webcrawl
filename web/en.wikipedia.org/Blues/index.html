Blues is a music genre and musical form that originated among African Americans in the Deep South of the United States around the 1860s. Blues incorporated spirituals, work songs, field hollers, shouts, chants, and rhymed simple narrative ballads from the African-American culture. The blues form is ubiquitous in jazz, rhythm and blues, and rock and roll, and is characterised by the call-and-response pattern, the blues scale, and specific chord progressions, of which the twelve-bar blues is the most common.

Characteristics

The classic blues progression follows twelve bars of I–IV–V chords, with lyrics typically arranged in three-line AAB stanzas: a line is sung, repeated (often slightly varied), and answered by a concluding line. "Blue notes" — flattened third, fifth, and seventh degrees — give blues harmony its expressive character. Themes traditionally concern hardship, love, travel, and resilience, delivered with emotional directness.

History

Blues emerged in the Mississippi Delta and across the rural South in the decades after emancipation. Early country blues artists such as Charley Patton, Blind Lemon Jefferson, and Robert Johnson recorded in the 1920s–30s, while "classic blues" singers Ma Rainey and Bessie Smith popularised the form with jazz bands. W. C. Handy, the self-styled "Father of the Blues," published "Memphis Blues" (1912) and "St. Louis Blues" (1914), bringing blues structures to mainstream songwriting.

After the Second World War, migrating musicians electrified the blues in Chicago — Muddy Waters, Howlin' Wolf, and Little Walter defined the Chicago blues sound on Chess Records — while B. B. King, Albert King, and Freddie King ("the Three Kings") shaped modern blues guitar. In the 1960s British bands including the Rolling Stones and Led Zeppelin carried blues back to white American audiences, and Stevie Ray Vaughan led a blues-rock revival in the 1980s.

Influence

Blues is the foundation of virtually all subsequent American popular music: jazz, R&B, rock and roll, soul, funk, and hip-hop all draw on its forms, scales, and performance practices.
