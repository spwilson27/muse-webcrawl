Negligence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Negligence (Lat. negligentia) is a failure to exercise appropriate or required care, or a breach of a duty owed by one person to another, that causes injury or loss. It is a core concept in tort law, particularly in common law jurisdictions such as England and Wales, the United States, Canada and Australia. A successful negligence claim requires the claimant to prove, on the balance of probabilities, that the defendant owed them a duty of care, breached that duty, and that the breach caused damage that is not too remote.

Elements

The modern framework derives from the speech of Lord Atkin in Donoghue v Stevenson [1932], which established the "neighbour principle": persons must take reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions they can reasonably foresee will injure their neighbours. The elements of a negligence claim are generally:

  • Duty of care — a legal obligation to take care, assessed using foreseeability of harm, proximity between the parties, and whether it is fair, just and reasonable to impose a duty (the Caparo test in English law).
  • Breach — conduct falling below the standard of the reasonable person. In professional cases the standard is that of the reasonably competent practitioner (Bolam).
  • Causation — factual causation ("but for" the breach, would the harm have occurred?) and legal causation or remoteness (The Wagon Mound).
  • Damage — recognised loss, such as personal injury, property damage, or, in limited circumstances, pure economic loss and psychiatric injury.

Defences

Common defences include contributory negligence (partial fault of the claimant, reducing damages), volenti non fit injuria (voluntary assumption of risk), and illegality (ex turpi causa). Limitation periods also bar stale claims.

Related concepts

Negligence is distinguished from recklessness and gross negligence, which involve a higher degree of culpability, and from intentional torts such as battery. Criminal negligence may also constitute an offence, for example gross negligence manslaughter. See also duty of care, tort and damages.
