North Field (Qatar)

The North Field (also known as the North Dome field) is an offshore natural gas field in the Persian Gulf, northeast of Qatar. It is the world's largest known non-associated natural gas field, with recoverable reserves estimated at around 900 trillion cubic feet (25 trillion cubic metres). The same geological structure extends into Iranian waters, where it is known as the South Pars field; together the combined field is often called the North Dome/South Pars field.

Geology and discovery

The field was discovered in 1971 by Royal Dutch Shell. It lies in water about 65 metres deep and covers roughly 6,000 square kilometres in the Qatari sector. The reservoir is held in Permian–Triassic Khuff formation carbonates. Development was deferred for many years because of the field's size and the limited market for gas at the time of discovery.

Development

Production began in 1991, initially for domestic use. Qatar subsequently developed the field into the foundation of its liquefied natural gas (LNG) industry through the Qatargas and Ras Laffan projects, making Qatar one of the world's leading LNG exporters. Gas is piped to onshore processing facilities at Ras Laffan Industrial City. A moratorium on new projects imposed in 2005 to allow reservoir studies was lifted in 2017, and expansion projects known as North Field East, North Field South, and North Field West are planned to raise Qatar's LNG capacity substantially through the late 2020s.

Shared field with Iran

The field straddles the maritime border between Qatar and Iran. Qatar's share of the gas in place is generally estimated to be much larger than Iran's South Pars share, and Qatar has been able to develop its side far more rapidly than Iran, which has been constrained by international sanctions. The two countries have no formal joint development agreement.
