Macedonia naming dispute

The Macedonia naming dispute was a political dispute over the use of the name "Macedonia" between Greece and the Republic of Macedonia (now North Macedonia), lasting from 1991 until 2019. Greece objected to its northern neighbour's use of the name, arguing that it implied territorial claims on the Greek region of Macedonia and appropriated Greek history and symbols, particularly those of the ancient kingdom of Macedon and Alexander the Great. The dispute blocked the young republic's accession to NATO and the European Union for over a quarter of a century despite international mediation.

Background

The geographical region of Macedonia spans parts of six Balkan countries. After the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 it was divided among Serbia, Greece and Bulgaria. The southern, Vardar portion became a constituent republic of Yugoslavia in 1944 under the name Socialist Republic of Macedonia, a point Greece protested only mildly at the time. When the republic declared independence from Yugoslavia in September 1991 as the "Republic of Macedonia", Greece reacted strongly.

Course of the dispute

In 1992 the European Community required the new state to adopt a name not implying territorial claims. The republic was admitted to the United Nations in 1993 under the provisional reference "the former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia". A Greek trade embargo in 1994–95 was lifted after an Interim Accord of 1995, under which the republic changed its flag, removing the Vergina Sun, and both sides agreed to negotiate the name. At the 2008 NATO summit in Bucharest, Greece vetoed an invitation to the republic, prompting a case at the International Court of Justice, which ruled in 2011 that Greece had breached the Interim Accord.

The Prespa agreement

After the election of Prime Minister Zoran Zaev in 2017, negotiations accelerated under UN mediator Matthew Nimetz. On 17 June 2018, Greek prime minister Alexis Tsipras and Zaev signed the Prespa agreement at Lake Prespa, providing that the country would be renamed the Republic of North Macedonia, with its language recognised as Macedonian (of South Slavic origin) and its citizens' nationality "Macedonian/citizen of the Republic of North Macedonia". A referendum in September 2018 showed overwhelming support among voters but low turnout. The Macedonian parliament approved constitutional amendments in January 2019, and the Greek parliament ratified the agreement on 25 January 2019. The new name entered into force on 12 February 2019, ending the dispute and clearing the path for North Macedonia to join NATO in March 2020.
