Desilu Productions

Desilu Productions was an American television production company founded and co-owned by husband and wife Desi Arnaz and Lucille Ball. The company is best known for producing I Love Lucy and for pioneering production techniques that shaped the television industry.

History

Desilu was formed in 1950 to produce I Love Lucy (1951–1957). Ball and Arnaz insisted the sitcom be filmed before a live studio audience using multiple 35 mm cameras — Arnaz, with cinematographer Karl Freund, developed the synchronized three-camera setup that became the standard for sitcoms. Filming rather than broadcasting live preserved high-quality prints, enabling lucrative reruns and creating the model for syndication.

Productions

Beyond its own sitcoms such as The Lucy Show, Desilu produced or housed major series including The Andy Griffith Show, The Dick Van Dyke Show, Mission: Impossible, and the original Star Trek (1966–1969), which it greenlit under executive Herbert Solow. The company bought the RKO studio lot in 1957, expanding its production facilities.

Sale and legacy

After Ball and Arnaz divorced, Ball bought out Arnaz's share in 1962 and ran the studio herself. In 1967 she sold Desilu to Gulf+Western, where it became part of Paramount Television. Ball remains one of the earliest and most successful women to run a major Hollywood studio.
