Lough Gur

Lough Gur (Irish: Loch Gair) is a lake in County Limerick, Ireland, between the towns of Herbertstown and Bruff. The horseshoe-shaped lake, formed at the base of hills of limestone, is one of Ireland's most important archaeological sites: the area around it has been inhabited continuously for at least 6,000 years, since Neolithic times.

Archaeology

Around the lake stand stone circles, including the great Grange stone circle (the largest in Ireland, composed of 113 standing stones), wedge tombs, ring forts, crannogs and castle ruins. Excavations have revealed Neolithic house sites at Knockadoon, and the visitor centre displays finds from the area. The Lough Gur shield, a Bronze Age bronze shield now in the National Museum of Ireland, is among the artefacts associated with the wider region's hoards.

Folklore

Lough Gur is steeped in folklore: it is associated with the enchanted Earl of Desmond, Gearóid Iarla, said to ride around the lake every seven years on a white horse with silver shoes, and with the goddess Áine of nearby Knockainy. The lake is also said to conceal a submerged city beneath its waters.
