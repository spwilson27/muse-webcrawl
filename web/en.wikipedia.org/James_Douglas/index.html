James Douglas

James Douglas may refer to several notable people throughout history, including Scottish nobles, colonial administrators, and physicians.

Notable figures

James Douglas, Lord of Douglas (c. 1286–1330), known as "the Black Douglas", was a Scottish knight and feudal lord who became one of the chief commanders of Robert the Bruce during the Wars of Scottish Independence. He won fame in border warfare against England and died in Spain carrying Bruce's heart toward the Holy Land.

Sir James Douglas (1803–1877) was a Hudson's Bay Company fur trader and colonial governor on the Pacific coast of North America. As chief factor at Fort Vancouver and later governor of Vancouver Island and of British Columbia, he is often called the "Father of British Columbia".

James Douglas (1675–1742) was a Scottish physician and anatomist, a fellow of the Royal Society, whose name is attached to structures in anatomy such as the pouch of Douglas.

Other uses

The name is also borne by earls and dukes of Douglas and Morton, sportsmen, and the boxer James "Buster" Douglas, who famously defeated Mike Tyson in 1990.
