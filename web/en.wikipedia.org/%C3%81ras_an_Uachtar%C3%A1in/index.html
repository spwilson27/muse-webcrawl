Áras an Uachtaráin

Áras an Uachtaráin (Irish pronunciation: [ˈaːɾˠəsˠ ənˠ ˈuəxt̪ˠəɾˠaːnʲ]), formerly the Viceregal Lodge, is the official residence and principal workplace of the President of Ireland. It is located off Chesterfield Avenue in Phoenix Park in Dublin.

The building, which has ninety-two rooms, was designed by park ranger and amateur architect Nathaniel Clements in the mid-eighteenth century and was first occupied in 1751. It served as the summer residence of the Lord Lieutenant of Ireland and, after Irish independence, as the residence of the Governor-General of the Irish Free State before becoming the presidential residence in 1938.

History

The original house was built by Nathaniel Clements, Chief Ranger of Phoenix Park, around 1751. In the 1780s it was purchased by the British administration to serve as the summer residence of the Lord Lieutenant of Ireland. Following the establishment of the Irish Free State in 1922, it became the residence of the Governor-General. When the office of President of Ireland was created under the 1937 Constitution, Douglas Hyde, the first president, moved into the house in 1938, and it has been the presidential residence ever since.

The building and grounds

The house stands in an estate of about 130 acres within Phoenix Park. Its formal gardens include the President's Garden, designed with seasonal parterres. A window at the front of the house holds a lamp that is kept lit as a symbol of welcome to the Irish diaspora, a tradition begun by President Mary Robinson. Visitors can tour the state rooms on Saturdays by ticket.
