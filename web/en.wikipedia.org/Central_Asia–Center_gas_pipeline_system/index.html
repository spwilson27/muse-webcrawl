Central Asia–Center gas pipeline system

Central Asia–Center
Type: Natural gas pipeline system
Countries: Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Russia
Built: 1960s–1980s

The Central Asia–Center gas pipeline system (CAC) is a network of natural-gas pipelines running from Turkmenistan through Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan into Russia. Built in the Soviet era beginning in the 1960s, it historically carried Central Asian gas north to Russian and European markets under Gazprom's control.

Routes and capacity

The system comprises several parallel lines (CAC-1 through CAC-5) with a combined design capacity on the order of 80–90 billion cubic metres per year, though actual flows declined as Turkmenistan diversified exports, notably to China after 2009.

Incidents and significance

A 2009 explosion and pricing dispute halted Turkmen flows for months and accelerated export diversification. The pipelines remain strategically important for regional energy trade. See Gazprom and Turkmenistan.
