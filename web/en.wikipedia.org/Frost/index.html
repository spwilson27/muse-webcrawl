Frost

Frost is a thin layer of ice on a solid surface, formed from water vapour in air that is above freezing. It forms when a surface cools — typically by radiating heat to a clear night sky — to a temperature below the dew point of the surrounding air and below freezing, so that water vapour is deposited directly as ice crystals by deposition. The related term also describes the cold weather conditions that produce it.

Types

Hoar frost consists of feathery white crystals formed on calm, clear nights on grass, leaves, fences, and roofs. Rime forms when supercooled droplets of fog freeze on contact with a surface in wind. Window frost patterns form on cold glass. In agriculture and meteorology, a "frost" event is often defined simply by air temperature falling to 0 °C or below, even if no visible deposit forms.

Effects

Frost damages tender plants by freezing water inside their cells, and the dates of the last spring frost and first autumn frost define the growing season in temperate climates. Gardeners protect crops with covers, irrigation, smudge pots, and wind machines. Frost also drives frost heaving of soils and pavements and weathering of rock by freeze–thaw action.
