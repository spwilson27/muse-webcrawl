Nikolaas Tinbergen

Nikolaas "Niko" Tinbergen (15 April 1907 – 21 December 1988) was a Dutch biologist and ornithologist who shared the 1973 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Karl von Frisch and Konrad Lorenz for discoveries concerning the organisation of individual and social behaviour patterns. He is regarded as a founder of ethology, the study of animal behaviour.

Work

Tinbergen formulated the "four questions" that should be asked of any behaviour: its causation (mechanism), development (ontogeny), function (adaptation) and evolutionary history (phylogeny). His field experiments — on digger wasps' landmarks, stickleback aggression and courtship, and gull chick begging — set standards for testing behavioural hypotheses in nature. Books such as The Study of Instinct (1951) and The Herring Gull's World popularised the field. He taught at Oxford from 1949.

His brother Jan Tinbergen was an economist and Nobel laureate.
