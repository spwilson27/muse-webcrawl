10 Gigabit Ethernet

Networking standard
Speed10 Gbit/s
StandardIEEE 802.3ae (2002) and successors
MediaOptical fibre, twisted pair, twinax

10 Gigabit Ethernet (10GE, 10GbE, or 10 GigE) is a group of computer networking technologies for transmitting Ethernet frames at a rate of 10 gigabits per second. It was first defined by the IEEE 802.3ae-2002 standard.

Variants

Standards define physical layers for optical fibre (such as 10GBASE-SR, -LR and -ER), copper twinaxial cable (10GBASE-CR, used for short direct-attach links), backplanes, and twisted-pair cabling (10GBASE-T, IEEE 802.3an, up to 100 metres over Cat 6a). Unlike earlier Ethernet, 10 Gigabit Ethernet operates only in full duplex over point-to-point links.

Applications

The standard was adopted first in data centres and enterprise backbones, and progressively in servers, storage networks and high-performance computing.
