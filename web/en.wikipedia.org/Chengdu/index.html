Chengdu

Chengdu (Chinese: 成都) is the capital of Sichuan province in southwestern China and one of the country's most populous cities, with a metropolitan population exceeding 20 million. Located on the fertile Chengdu Plain in the Sichuan Basin, it has been an important political, economic and cultural centre for more than two thousand years.

History

Chengdu was founded as a capital of the Shu kingdom and was the site of the ancient Sanxingdui and Jinsha civilisations nearby. During the Three Kingdoms period it was the capital of Shu Han. The nearby Dujiangyan irrigation system, built in the 3rd century BC, still waters the plain and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Chengdu is famous for brocade weaving, tea culture, and Sichuan cuisine.

Economy and culture

Today Chengdu is a hub for electronics, automotive manufacturing, IT and finance in western China, home to many multinational companies and research institutions. The city is also known as the home of the giant panda: the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding attracts visitors from around the world. Its relaxed teahouse culture, spicy hot pot cuisine and mahjong parlours define a distinctive local lifestyle.

Transport

Chengdu is served by two international airports (Shuangliu and Tianfu), an extensive metro system, and high-speed rail connections across China.
