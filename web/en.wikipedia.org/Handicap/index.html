Handicap

ContextsSport, games, betting, disability (older usage)
Purpose (sport)To equalise competition between unequal contestants

Handicap most generally refers to a disadvantage imposed or an advantage adjusted in order to equalise conditions. The word is used in several distinct senses, most prominently in sport and games, where handicapping adjusts scores, weights, or starting positions so that competitors of different abilities can compete on more equal terms.

Etymology

The term derives from "hand in cap", a 17th-century English bartering and lottery game in which participants put forfeit money into a cap and an umpire judged the difference in value between exchanged items. The practice gave rise to handicapping in horse racing, where weights are assigned to horses to equalise their chances.

Sporting handicaps

In golf, a handicap index estimates a player's potential scoring ability so players of differing skill can compete; systems such as the World Handicap System govern its calculation. Handicap races in horse racing and motorsport, staggered starts, and weight classes or points adjustments in other sports serve similar levelling functions. In betting, a point spread is a form of handicap applied to a favoured team.

Other uses

"Handicap" was formerly a common term for disability, but in that sense it has largely been replaced by "disability" and related terms, and is now often considered dated or offensive when applied to people. The word also appears in phrases such as "handicap principle" in evolutionary biology, proposed by Amotz Zahavi, in which costly signals honestly advertise fitness.
