Creative nonfiction

Creative nonfiction (also called literary nonfiction or narrative nonfiction) is a genre of writing that uses literary styles and techniques to create factually accurate narratives. Unlike other forms of nonfiction, such as technical or journalistic writing, creative nonfiction aims to communicate information in the manner of a story.

Forms

The genre encompasses memoir, personal essay, travel writing, food writing, biography, and literary journalism, including the New Journalism associated with writers such as Tom Wolfe, Joan Didion, and Truman Capote, whose In Cold Blood (1966) is often cited as a landmark work.

Characteristics

Creative nonfiction employs scene, dialogue, character development, and narrative voice while remaining committed to factual accuracy. The balance between literary shaping and truthfulness has generated recurring debate about the ethics of reconstruction and embellishment.
