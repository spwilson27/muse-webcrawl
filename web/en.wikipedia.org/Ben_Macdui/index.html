Ben Macdui

Ben Macdui (Scottish Gaelic: Beinn MacDuibh, meaning "MacDuff's mountain" or "mountain of the black pig") is the second-highest mountain in Scotland and the United Kingdom, rising to 1,309 metres (4,295 ft) in the Cairngorms of the eastern Highlands, on the boundary between Aberdeenshire and Moray.

Geography

The mountain forms the highest point of the Cairngorm plateau, a vast area of arctic-alpine terrain. It lies within the Cairngorms National Park, south of Cairn Gorm itself. Long approaches from the Lairig Ghru pass, Deeside or the ski area at Cairn Gorm make an ascent of Ben Macdui a serious undertaking, and its plateau is notorious for sudden whiteouts; snow patches can persist through the summer in sheltered hollows.

History and folklore

Ben Macdui was long thought to be Scotland's highest mountain until precise surveying in the 19th century confirmed Ben Nevis (1,345 m) was higher. The mountain is associated with the legend of Am Fear Liath Mòr, the "Big Grey Man of Ben Macdui", a towering spectral figure said to stalk climbers on the summit plateau — a phenomenon sometimes attributed to the Brocken spectre effect in mist.

Flora and fauna

The plateau supports specialised montane wildlife including ptarmigan, snow bunting, dotterel and mountain hare, and Britain's only free-ranging herd of reindeer grazes the surrounding hills.
