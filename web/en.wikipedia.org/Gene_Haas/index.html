Eugene Francis "Gene" Haas (born 12 November 1952) is an American entrepreneur and founder of Haas Automation, one of the world's largest manufacturers of CNC machine tools, and the owner of the Haas F1 Team in Formula One and of the former NASCAR team Stewart-Haas Racing.

Haas Automation

After working as a machinist and programmer, Haas founded Haas Automation in 1983 in Sun Valley, California, to produce economical, fully programmable rotary tables and indexers for machine tools. The company built its first vertical machining centre, the VF-1, in 1988 and grew to become the largest machine-tool builder in the Western world, headquartered in Oxnard, California, with annual revenues exceeding $1 billion.

Motorsport

Haas entered NASCAR team ownership in 2002; in 2009 Tony Stewart joined as co-owner, forming Stewart-Haas Racing, which won Cup Series championships in 2011 (Stewart) and 2014 (Kevin Harvick) before closing at the end of the 2024 season. In 2014 Haas was granted a licence for a Formula One team — the first American F1 entrant in three decades. Haas F1 Team debuted in 2016, scoring points in its first race, and has since operated with technical partnerships with Ferrari and a base in Kannapolis, North Carolina, alongside facilities in England and Italy.

Legal issue and philanthropy

In 2007 Haas pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit tax fraud and served 16 months in federal prison; he paid about $75 million in restitution and fines. Through the Gene Haas Foundation (founded 1999), he has donated tens of millions of dollars to scholarships and manufacturing-education programmes (Haas Technical Education Centers) at schools and colleges.
