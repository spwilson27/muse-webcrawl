Ray Bradbury

Ray Douglas Bradbury (August 22, 1920 – June 5, 2012) was an American author who worked in fantasy, science fiction, horror, and mystery. He is best known for the dystopian novel Fahrenheit 451 (1953), the linked story collection The Martian Chronicles (1950), and the novel Something Wicked This Way Comes (1962).

Life and career

Born in Waukegan, Illinois — fictionalised as "Green Town" in his semi-autobiographical Dandelion Wine (1957) — Bradbury moved to Los Angeles in 1934 and educated himself in public libraries, crediting them in place of a university education. He sold his first story in 1941 and became a full-time writer by 1943. Over a 70-year career he wrote 27 novels and more than 600 short stories, plus screenplays including Moby Dick (1956) for John Huston, and episodes of The Twilight Zone. He adapted 65 of his stories for the television series The Ray Bradbury Theater.

Legacy

Bradbury received a Pulitzer Prize special citation in 2007 and the National Medal of Arts in 2004. Asteroid 9766 Bradbury and a crater on the Moon (in Dandelion crater, named by the Apollo 15 crew) honour him, as does Bradbury Landing, NASA's name for the Curiosity rover's Mars touchdown site.
