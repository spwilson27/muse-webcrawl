Broadway is a road in the U.S. state of New York. Broadway runs 13 miles (21 km) through Manhattan and 2 miles (3.2 km) through the Bronx, exiting north from New York City to run an additional 18 miles (29 km) through the municipalities of Yonkers, Hastings-on-Hudson, Dobbs Ferry, Irvington, and Tarrytown, and terminating north of Sleepy Hollow in Westchester County. It is the oldest north–south main thoroughfare in New York City, dating to the first New Amsterdam settlement.

History

Broadway follows a path originally traced by the Wickquasgeck Trail, an Algonquian route through the swamps and rocks of Manhattan Island. Dutch settlers widened it and named it the Breede Weg ("broad way"), from which the English name derives. Under British rule it became the principal road of the growing city, and by the 19th century it was lined with theatres, hotels, and department stores.

Theater District

Broadway is widely known as the heart of the American commercial theatre industry. The Theater District, concentrated around Times Square between West 40th and West 54th Streets, contains 41 professional theatres with 500 or more seats, whose productions are eligible for the Tony Awards. Together with London's West End, Broadway theatre is considered the highest level of commercial theatre in the English-speaking world.

Route

Broadway is unusual among Manhattan streets because it cuts diagonally across the Commissioners' Plan grid of 1811, creating famous intersections and squares including Union Square, Madison Square, Herald Square, Times Square, and Columbus Circle. Buildings such as the Flatiron Building occupy the triangular lots formed by these diagonal crossings.
