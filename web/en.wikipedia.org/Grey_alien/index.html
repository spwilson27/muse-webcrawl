Grey alien

Grey aliens, also referred to as Zeta Reticulans, Roswell Greys, or simply Greys, are purported extraterrestrial beings. They are frequent subjects of close encounter claims and alien abduction accounts. The details of such claims vary widely, but typically Greys are described as being human-like with small bodies, smooth grey-coloured skin, enlarged hairless heads, and large black eyes. The archetype became the most widely recognized depiction of an alien in popular culture during the late twentieth century.

Origins of the archetype

Descriptions resembling the Greys appear in earlier science fiction: H. G. Wells described future humans in "The Man of the Year Million" (1893) and grey-skinned Martians in The War of the Worlds (1898), and Gustav Sandgren's 1933 novel featured small grey humanoids. The modern image crystallized after the 1961 abduction account of Betty and Barney Hill, who described beings with large eyes; a drawing made under hypnosis by Betty Hill and broadcast coverage helped standardize the appearance. Claims linking Greys to the Zeta Reticuli star system derive from a star map Hill said she was shown, later publicized by Marjorie Fish's interpretation of it.

In abduction literature and media

Whitley Strieber's 1987 bestseller Communion, whose cover painting of a Grey's face became iconic, and the work of abduction researchers such as Budd Hopkins and David M. Jacobs, embedded the Grey in abduction narratives involving medical examinations and reproductive procedures. Greys feature prominently in television series such as The X-Files and films such as Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977) and Paul (2011). Folklorists and psychologists generally explain the reports through sleep paralysis, hypnagogic imagery, cultural influence, and the suggestibility of hypnotic regression.
