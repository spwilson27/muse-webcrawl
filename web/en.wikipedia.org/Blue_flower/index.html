Blue flower

The blue flower (German: Blaue Blume) is a central symbol of Romanticism, originating in Novalis's unfinished novel Heinrich von Ofterdingen (1802), in which the young hero dreams of a blue flower that calls to him and awakens a profound longing.

Meaning

The blue flower came to represent desire, love, and the metaphysical longing for the infinite and the unattainable—known in German as Sehnsucht. It influenced later writers and artists including Joseph von Eichendorff, George MacDonald, C. S. Lewis and Penelope Fitzgerald, whose novel The Blue Flower (1995) retells Novalis's life.

Natural blue flowers

True blue is rare among flowers; species such as cornflower, gentian, hydrangea and forget-me-not approximate it through pigments modified by soil acidity and metal ions.
