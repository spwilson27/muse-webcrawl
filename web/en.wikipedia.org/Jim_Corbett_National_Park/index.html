Jim Corbett National Park


Jim Corbett National Park is India's oldest national park, established in 1936 in Uttarakhand.

Jim Corbett National Park is a national park in the Nainital and Pauri Garhwal districts of Uttarakhand, India. It was established in 1936 as Hailey National Park — the first national park in India and in mainland Asia — and was renamed in 1957 in honour of Jim Corbett, the British hunter-turned-conservationist who had championed its creation and famously hunted man-eating tigers in the region.

Geography and wildlife

The park covers about 521 square kilometres of hills, riverine belts, marshes, grasslands (chaurs), and the reservoir of the Kalagarh Dam on the Ramganga River. It is part of the larger Corbett Tiger Reserve of about 1,288 square kilometres. The reserve protects one of India's densest populations of Bengal tigers, along with Asian elephants, leopards, sloth bears, gharials, otters, and more than 600 species of birds.

Project Tiger

Corbett was the site of the launch of Project Tiger in 1973, India's flagship tiger conservation programme, inaugurated on 1 April of that year. The park's management model, tourism zones (Dhikala, Bijrani, Jhirna, and others), and core-buffer strategy became templates for tiger reserves across India.

Tourism

The park is one of India's most popular ecotourism destinations, receiving hundreds of thousands of visitors a year. Safaris are conducted in open jeeps and canters, with the Dhikala grasslands zone especially prized for tiger and elephant sightings. Tourism is regulated by permits and seasonal closures during the monsoon.
