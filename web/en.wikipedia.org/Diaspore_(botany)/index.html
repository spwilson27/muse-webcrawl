Diaspore (botany)

In botany, a diaspore is a plant dispersal unit consisting of a seed or spore plus any additional tissues that assist dispersal. In other words, the diaspore is the part of the plant that is actually transported to a new site where it may germinate and establish. Depending on the species, the diaspore may be the seed alone, the whole fruit, or even a vegetative fragment such as a bulb, tuber, or broken-off branch.

Types and dispersal

Diaspores are adapted to different dispersal vectors. Winged fruits such as samaras are carried by wind (anemochory); fleshy fruits are eaten by animals and their seeds voided elsewhere (endozoochory); hooked or barbed fruits cling to fur (epizoochory); and buoyant fruits of coastal plants drift on water (hydrochory). Some plants, such as tumbleweeds, disperse as whole above-ground plants broken off at the stem base.

Significance

The size, shape, and dormancy properties of the diaspore strongly influence how far a plant can spread, which is central to island biogeography, invasion biology, and the recolonization of disturbed habitats.
