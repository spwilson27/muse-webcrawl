India Gate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The India Gate is a war memorial located astride the Kartavya Path (formerly Rajpath) in New Delhi, India. Designed by Sir Edwin Lutyens, it is a triumphal arch standing 42 metres (138 ft) high, commemorating the approximately 70,000 soldiers of the British Indian Army who died in the First World War and the Third Anglo-Afghan War.

The foundation stone was laid in 1921 by the Duke of Connaught, and the monument was unveiled in 1931 by the Viceroy, Lord Irwin. The names of more than 13,500 servicemen are inscribed on the arch itself.

Amar Jawan Jyoti and later additions

After the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971, a simple structure of black marble with an inverted rifle and soldier's helmet was added beneath the arch, housing the Amar Jawan Jyoti ("Flame of the Immortal Soldier"), an eternal flame honouring India's unknown soldier. In 2022 the flame was merged with the flame at the National War Memorial, which opened nearby in 2019 in the adjoining C-Hexagon.

A statue of Subhas Chandra Bose was installed in the canopy behind the arch in 2022; the canopy originally housed a statue of King George V, removed after independence.

Setting

India Gate anchors the eastern end of the ceremonial axis of New Delhi, designed as part of Lutyens's imperial capital. The surrounding lawns and boating ponds are a popular public gathering place, and the arch is floodlit at night. Annual Republic Day parades march past the memorial.
