Doetinchem (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈdutɪŋxəm]) is a city and municipality in the eastern Netherlands, in the province of Gelderland, in the Achterhoek region. Situated on the river Oude IJssel, it is the largest town in the Achterhoek and a regional centre for shopping, education, healthcare and employment. The municipality also includes the villages of Gaanderen, Wehl and Nieuw-Wehl.

History

Doetinchem is first mentioned in a document of 838 as Deutinkem, a settlement with a small church. It grew as a market town on the Oude IJssel and received city rights in 1236 from Count Otto II of Guelders. Defensive works included a town wall, moat and four gates, remnants of which include the Catharina Church and the former water tower. Much of the old centre was destroyed in March 1945, when Allied bombing—intended partly for nearby German targets—and fighting damaged the town shortly before its liberation by Canadian forces; the centre was rebuilt after the war in a sober reconstruction style.

Geography and economy

The town lies near the German border, on the Amsterdam–Arnhem–Ruhr corridor. Its economy includes manufacturing, logistics, healthcare and retail. Doetinchem has a railway station on the Arnhem–Winterswijk line and is connected by the A18 motorway.

Sport

Doetinchem is home to the professional football club De Graafschap, nicknamed de Superboeren ("the Super Farmers"), which plays at De Vijverberg stadium and has a passionate regional following, alternating between the Eredivisie and the Eerste Divisie. The town also has a strong tradition in volleyball and cycling.

Notable people

  • Guus Hiddink, football manager, born in nearby Varsseveld and a former De Graafschap player
  • Paul Bosvelt, former international footballer
  • Klaas-Jan Huntelaar began his professional career at De Graafschap on loan
