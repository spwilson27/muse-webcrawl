Data assimilation

Data assimilation blends observations with a model forecast

Data assimilation is the process by which observations of a physical system are incorporated into a numerical model of that system, combining the model forecast (the "background") with measured data, weighted by their respective uncertainties, to produce the best possible estimate of the system's state. It is the mathematical foundation of modern numerical weather prediction and is also used in oceanography, hydrology, atmospheric chemistry and space weather.

Methods

The central problem is statistical: find the state that best fits both the prior forecast and the observations. Operational weather centres solve it with variational methods — three-dimensional and four-dimensional variational assimilation (3D-Var and 4D-Var), pioneered operationally by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts, which adjusts a whole trajectory of model states over a time window. Ensemble methods, such as the ensemble Kalman filter, estimate uncertainties from a set of parallel forecasts and are used for probabilistic prediction and in many research systems. Simpler predecessors included optimal interpolation and nudging.

Applications

Beyond weather forecasting, data assimilation produces reanalyses — consistent historical records such as ERA5, built by running a fixed assimilation system over decades of observations — and underpins ocean state estimation, flood forecasting, air-quality prediction, ionospheric modelling and even epidemiology. The observations assimilated range from surface stations and radiosondes to satellite radiances, radar, aircraft reports and ocean floats, totalling tens of millions of measurements per day at major centres.
