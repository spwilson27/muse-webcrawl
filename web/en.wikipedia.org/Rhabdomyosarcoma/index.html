Rhabdomyosarcoma

Rhabdomyosarcoma (RMS) is a malignant tumour arising from skeletal muscle progenitor cells. It is the most common soft-tissue sarcoma of childhood, accounting for roughly 3–4 percent of paediatric cancers, with most cases diagnosed before the age of ten.

Types

The two main subtypes in children are embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, typically arising in the head, neck and genitourinary tract of young children, and alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, which occurs in older children and adolescents, often in the limbs and trunk, and is associated with PAX3–FOXO1 or PAX7–FOXO1 gene fusions. Pleomorphic RMS is the typical adult form.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on the site: a painless enlarging mass is common, while orbital tumours cause proptosis and genitourinary tumours may cause urinary obstruction or vaginal discharge.

Treatment

Treatment is multimodal, combining surgery, chemotherapy (classically vincristine, actinomycin D and cyclophosphamide) and radiotherapy, coordinated in cooperative trials such as those of the Children's Oncology Group. Prognosis depends on subtype, site, stage and fusion status; localized embryonal disease has survival rates above 70 percent.
