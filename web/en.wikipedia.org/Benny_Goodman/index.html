Benny Goodman

Benjamin David Goodman (May 30, 1909 – June 13, 1986) was an American clarinetist and bandleader, known as the "King of Swing". His orchestra, launched in 1934 with arrangements by Fletcher Henderson, ignited the swing era in 1935 with an electrifying performance at the Palomar Ballroom in Los Angeles, and in 1938 gave the first jazz concert ever presented at Carnegie Hall. Goodman's band was also notable for breaking racial barriers in American music: from 1935 he performed publicly in integrated small groups with pianist Teddy Wilson and vibraphonist Lionel Hampton, and later hired guitarist Charlie Christian.

Early life and career

Goodman was born in Chicago to poor Jewish immigrants from the Russian Empire, the ninth of twelve children. He took up the clarinet at a local synagogue and Hull House, studied with Chicago Symphony clarinettist Franz Schoepp, and was performing professionally in his teens. After playing with Ben Pollack's orchestra in the 1920s, he worked as a leading New York session musician in radio and recording studios during the Depression.

The swing era

In 1934 Goodman formed his own orchestra for NBC's Let's Dance radio programme, buying arrangements from Fletcher Henderson. A discouraging cross-country tour turned triumphant at the Palomar Ballroom in August 1935, when young dancers roared at the Henderson arrangements — a night often cited as the start of the swing era. On 16 January 1938 the band played Carnegie Hall, with guests from the Basie and Ellington bands; the concert, released as a live album in 1950, became one of the best-selling jazz records of all time and featured the famous "Sing, Sing, Sing" with Gene Krupa's drums and Jess Stacy's improvised piano solo.

Later life

Goodman maintained big bands and small groups for decades, toured internationally for the US State Department, and performed classical music, commissioning works from Bartók (Contrasts), Copland, and Hindemith. He received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award and Kennedy Center Honors. He died in New York in 1986, still practising the clarinet daily.
