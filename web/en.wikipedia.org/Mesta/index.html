Mesta

Mesta

Founded: 1273

Dissolved: 1836

Region: Kingdom of Castile / Spain

Purpose: Association of sheep ranchers

The Mesta (Spanish: Honrado Concejo de la Mesta) was a powerful association of sheep ranchers in the medieval Kingdom of Castile, formally established by Alfonso X in 1273 and abolished in 1836.

Transhumance

The Mesta organized the seasonal migration (transhumance) of millions of merino sheep between summer pastures in the northern mountains and winter pastures in the south, along protected drove roads called cañadas reales. The crown granted the Mesta sweeping privileges, including rights of passage and pasture, because wool exports were a major source of royal revenue.

Impact

Historians have long debated the Mesta's effects on Spanish agriculture: its privileges frequently conflicted with local farmers' enclosure of land, and some scholars blamed it for retarding arable farming, while others see its impact as more limited. The merino wool trade it sustained made Spain the leading source of fine wool in late medieval Europe.
