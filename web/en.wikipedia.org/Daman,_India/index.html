Daman is a city and the capital of the Indian union territory of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu. It lies on the Arabian Sea coast at the mouth of the Daman Ganga River, which divides the city into Nani Daman (Small Daman) in the north and Moti Daman (Big Daman) in the south.

History

Daman was a Portuguese colony for over 400 years, having been seized by the Portuguese in the 16th century (formally ceded in 1559). It remained under Portuguese rule until December 1961, when it was annexed by India during Operation Vijay, along with Goa and Diu. From 1961 to 2020 it formed part of the union territory of Daman and Diu; in January 2020 that territory was merged with Dadra and Nagar Haveli.

Geography and sights

The city is known for its beaches (Devka and Jampore), its Portuguese-era forts — notably the Fort of Moti Daman and the Fort of São Jerónimo in Nani Daman — and historic churches such as the Church of Bom Jesus. Its proximity to Gujarat, a dry state, has made Daman a popular destination for visitors from across the border.
