Warwick is a market town, civil parish and the county town of Warwickshire in the West Midlands of England, adjoining Leamington Spa to the east. It lies on the River Avon and is best known for Warwick Castle.

History

Warwick was fortified as a burh in 914 by Æthelflæd, Lady of the Mercians. The medieval castle was founded in 1068 by William the Conqueror and rebuilt in stone over following centuries. A great fire in 1694 destroyed much of the town centre, which was rebuilt in the Georgian style seen today.

Landmarks

Besides the castle, landmarks include the collegiate Church of St Mary, the Lord Leycester Hospital, and St John's Museum. The town gives its name to the University of Warwick, located near Coventry.
