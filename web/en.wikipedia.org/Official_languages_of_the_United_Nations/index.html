Official languages of the United Nations

The official languages of the United Nations are the six languages used in United Nations meetings and in which the UN writes all its official documents. They are Arabic, Chinese (Mandarin), English, French, Russian, and Spanish.

History

When the UN was founded in 1945–46, five languages were made official: Chinese, English, French, Russian, and Spanish. Arabic was added as an official language of the General Assembly in 1973 and subsequently of other bodies. English and French serve as the working languages of the UN Secretariat.

Use

Delegates may speak in any official language, and speeches are simultaneously interpreted into the other five. Official documents are published in all six languages. Each language has a designated Language Day at the UN, observed to promote multilingualism and cultural diversity.

Proposals and discussion

Additional languages such as Hindi, Portuguese, Bengali, and Turkish have periodically been proposed for inclusion, but expansion is resisted because of cost. Separately, the UN maintains services in languages like Hindi, Kiswahili, Portuguese, and Urdu for communications purposes without official status in deliberative bodies.
