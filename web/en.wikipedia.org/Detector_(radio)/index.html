Detector (radio)

A detector is an electronic device or circuit in a radio receiver that recovers the information content (demodulates) from a modulated radio wave. The term dates from the first three decades of radio (1888-1918), when receivers consisted of little more than a tuned circuit and a detector, with the audio signal fed directly to headphones.

Because the information-carrying signal must be extracted from the radio-frequency carrier, the detector performs a nonlinear operation. In an amplitude-modulated (AM) signal it recovers the envelope of the carrier; the most common circuit is the envelope detector, a diode rectifier followed by a low-pass filter.

History

Early detectors included the coherer, magnetic detectors, electrolytic detectors, and the cat's-whisker crystal detector, which used a crystal of galena contacted by a fine wire. The invention of the vacuum-tube diode and triode brought grid-leak and plate detectors, and later semiconductor diodes became standard.

Types of detectors

Beyond envelope detection, other demodulators include the product detector (used for single-sideband and CW reception), FM discriminators and phase-locked loops for frequency modulation, and synchronous detectors. In modern receivers, detection is often performed digitally in software-defined radio.
