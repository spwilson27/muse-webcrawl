European Council

European Council
CompositionHeads of state or government of the 27 EU member states, its President, and the President of the European Commission
SeatEuropa building, Brussels
Established1974 (informal); EU institution since 2009
RoleDefines the EU's general political direction and priorities

The European Council is a collegiate body of the European Union comprising the heads of state or government of the member states, together with its own president and the president of the European Commission. It defines the EU's overall political direction but does not legislate.

Functions

The European Council gives impetus to the Union's development, sets strategic guidelines (including foreign and security policy), nominates candidates for top EU posts such as Commission president, and acts as a final arbiter in sensitive areas like enlargement, treaty change, and eurozone crisis management. Its conclusions guide the work of the Council of the European Union and the Commission.

President

Since the Lisbon Treaty (2009) the European Council elects a full-time president for a 2.5-year term, renewable once. Herman Van Rompuy was the first permanent president, followed by Donald Tusk (2014–2019) and Charles Michel (2019–2024); António Costa took office in December 2024.

Meetings and distinctions

It meets at least four times a year in Brussels ("EU summits"). It should not be confused with the Council of the European Union (ministers) or the Council of Europe (a separate, non-EU body).
