Soft tissue sarcoma


Histopathology of an undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma

Soft tissue sarcomas are a broad and heterogeneous group of tumours (cancers) that originate in the connective tissues of the body—the tissues that connect, support, and surround other structures, including muscle, fat, blood vessels, nerves, tendons, and the lining of joints. They are rare, accounting for about 1% of adult cancers and around 7% of childhood cancers, and comprise more than 50 distinct histological subtypes.

Types

Subtypes are generally named for the tissue they most resemble: liposarcoma (fat), leiomyosarcoma (smooth muscle), rhabdomyosarcoma (skeletal muscle, the most common soft tissue sarcoma in children), synovial sarcoma, angiosarcoma (blood vessels), gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST), and undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma. Diagnosis relies on imaging, core-needle biopsy, and increasingly on molecular testing, since characteristic translocations and mutations define several subtypes and guide therapy.

Signs and symptoms

Soft tissue sarcomas often present as a painless, slowly enlarging lump, most commonly in the limbs, trunk, or retroperitoneum. Because they may grow large before causing symptoms, deep or large (>5 cm) masses are treated as suspicious and referred to specialist sarcoma centres for evaluation before biopsy or excision.

Treatment and prognosis

The mainstay of treatment for localised disease is wide surgical excision, often combined with radiotherapy to reduce local recurrence. Chemotherapy (typically doxorubicin-based) is used for some high-risk or metastatic cases; targeted drugs such as imatinib for GIST and pazopanib for several subtypes are also employed. Prognosis varies widely by subtype, grade, size, and stage; overall five-year survival for localised disease is roughly 80%, but falls substantially once distant metastases—most often to the lungs—develop. Known risk factors include prior radiation therapy, certain genetic syndromes (such as Li–Fraumeni syndrome and neurofibromatosis type 1), chronic lymphoedema, and exposure to some chemicals.
