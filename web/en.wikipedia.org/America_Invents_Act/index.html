Leahy–Smith America Invents Act

America Invents Act
Public Law 112-29
Enacted: 16 September 2011
Signed by: President Barack Obama

The Leahy–Smith America Invents Act (AIA) is a United States federal statute signed into law on 16 September 2011. It was the most significant reform of US patent law in decades, named for its lead sponsors, Senator Patrick Leahy and Representative Lamar Smith.

Key provisions

The Act switched the United States from a "first to invent" to a "first inventor to file" system for determining priority of invention, harmonising US practice with most of the world; the change took effect on 16 March 2013. It expanded prior-art definitions, created new post-grant proceedings at the Patent and Trademark Office — including inter partes review and post-grant review before the new Patent Trial and Appeal Board — and limited joinder of defendants in patent lawsuits. It also addressed fee-setting authority for the USPTO, satellite offices, and pro bono assistance for small inventors.

Effects and debate

Supporters argued the Act reduced litigation and uncertainty; critics contended that first-to-file favours large companies over independent inventors. Inter partes review became widely used to challenge patent validity and proved controversial, with the Board dubbed a "patent death squad" by one former Federal Circuit chief judge.
