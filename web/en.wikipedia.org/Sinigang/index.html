Sinigang

Sinigang na baboy (pork sinigang) served with rice.

TypeSoup or stew
Place of originPhilippines
Serving temperatureHot
Main ingredientsMeat or seafood, tamarind or other souring agent, tomatoes, onion, labanos, kangkong

Sinigang is a Filipino soup or stew characterized by its sour and savoury taste. It is most often associated with tamarind (sampalok), although it can use other sour fruits and leaves as the souring agent, such as unripe mango, calamansi, guava, santol, or bilimbi. It is one of the more popular dishes in Filipino cuisine and is traditionally served with steamed rice.

Preparation

The broth is soured and seasoned with patis (fish sauce), and cooked with tomatoes, onion, and vegetables such as labanos (radish), kangkong (water spinach), sitaw (long beans), talong (eggplant), okra, and siling haba (long green chillies). Common variants include sinigang na baboy (pork), sinigang na hipon (shrimp), sinigang na salmon, and sinigang na baka (beef). A modern variation, sinigang na baboy sa pakwan, uses watermelon to add sweetness alongside the sourness.

Cultural standing

Sinigang is frequently named among the Philippines' most beloved comfort foods; in 2021 the online guide TasteAtlas rated sinigang the world's best vegetable soup in its annual awards, and the dish is often cited alongside adobo as a candidate national dish of the Philippines. Commercial sour-soup mixes have made it a staple of Filipino home cooking and of the diaspora.
