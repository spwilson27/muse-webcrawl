Tucson is a city in and the county seat of Pima County, Arizona, United States, and is home to the University of Arizona. It is the second-largest city in Arizona after Phoenix. Located in the Sonoran Desert about 97 km north of the Mexican border, Tucson is one of the oldest continuously inhabited places in the United States, with settlement in the Santa Cruz River valley extending back thousands of years.

History

The name derives from the O'odham Cuk Ṣon, "at the base of the black [hill]". Spain founded the Presidio San Agustín del Tucson in 1775. Tucson passed to Mexico in 1821 and to the United States with the Gadsden Purchase of 1853; it served as capital of the Arizona Territory from 1867 to 1877. The arrival of the railroad in 1880 spurred growth, and the university was founded in 1885.

Geography and climate

Tucson sits in a valley ringed by five mountain ranges, including the Santa Catalinas to the north and the Rincons to the east, flanked by the two districts of Saguaro National Park. The climate is hot desert, with summers above 38 °C and a summer monsoon season bringing dramatic thunderstorms.

Economy and culture

Major employers include the university, Raytheon Missiles & Defense, and Davis–Monthan Air Force Base. In 2015 Tucson became the first US city designated a UNESCO City of Gastronomy. Attractions include the Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum, Mission San Xavier del Bac, the Pima Air & Space Museum and the annual Tucson Gem & Mineral Show.
