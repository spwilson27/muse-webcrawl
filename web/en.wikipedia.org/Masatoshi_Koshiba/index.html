Masatoshi Koshiba

Masatoshi Koshiba (1926–2020) was a Japanese physicist who shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Physics for pioneering contributions to astrophysics, in particular the detection of cosmic neutrinos.

Career

Born in Toyohashi and educated at the University of Tokyo and the University of Rochester, Koshiba built the Kamiokande detector in the Kamioka mine in Gifu Prefecture, originally to search for proton decay. Retuned for neutrino detection, Kamiokande in 1987 registered neutrinos from supernova SN 1987A in the Large Magellanic Cloud — the first detection of neutrinos from beyond the solar system — and confirmed the solar neutrino problem.

Legacy

Koshiba founded the Kamioka Observatory and mentored Takaaki Kajita, whose work with the successor detector Super-Kamiokande demonstrated neutrino oscillation and won the 2015 Nobel Prize. Koshiba's experiments effectively created the field of neutrino astronomy.
