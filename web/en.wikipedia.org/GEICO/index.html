GEICO (the Government Employees Insurance Company) is an American auto insurance company headquartered in Chevy Chase, Maryland. It is one of the largest auto insurers in the United States and a wholly owned subsidiary of Berkshire Hathaway. GEICO is known for its direct-to-consumer sales model and extensive advertising, featuring mascots such as the GEICO Gecko and the slogan "15 minutes could save you 15% or more on car insurance".

History

GEICO was founded in 1936 by Leo and Lillian Goodwin to provide auto insurance directly to federal government employees and military personnel, cutting out agents' commissions. The company moved its headquarters to Washington, D.C., and later to Maryland. In 1996, Berkshire Hathaway under Warren Buffett acquired full ownership; Buffett had first bought GEICO shares as a young investor in 1951 after visiting the company as a student.

Business

GEICO sells policies directly by phone and online in all 50 states and the District of Columbia. Beyond auto insurance it offers motorcycle, boat, homeowners and renters (through partners), commercial, and other lines. It is typically ranked among the top two American private passenger auto insurers by premiums written, competing closely with State Farm and Progressive.

Advertising

The company spends billions of dollars on advertising, with long-running campaigns including the Gecko (voiced originally by Kelsey Grammer and later by Jake Wood), the Cavemen, and "Did you know" spots. Its marketing is credited with making direct-response insurance mainstream in the United States.
