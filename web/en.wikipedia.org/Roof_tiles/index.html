Roof tiles

Roof tiles are coverings designed mainly to keep out rain, traditionally made from locally available materials such as fired clay (terracotta) or slate, and in modern times also from concrete, metal, bitumen, and plastic. Tiles are hung from the roof framework by fasteners through holes or by resting in battens, overlapping in rows to shed water.

History

Clay roof tiles were used in ancient China and Greece. The Romans spread the use of interlocking flat and curved tiles — the tegula and imbrex — throughout their empire, a combination echoed by later Mediterranean styles such as Spanish mission tiles and Italian pantiles.

Types

Common profiles include flat tiles, plain tiles, Roman tiles, pantiles, and interlocking single-lap tiles. Clay tiles are durable and fire-resistant but heavy, requiring a strong roof structure; concrete tiles offer similar profiles at lower cost.
