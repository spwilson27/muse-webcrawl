Henry II the Pious (Polish: Henryk II Pobożny; c. 1196 – 9 April 1241) was Duke of Silesia and High Duke of Poland from 1238 until his death. He was the son of Henry I the Bearded and Hedwig of Andechs. Henry II continued his father's work of consolidating the Piast lands and was the effective ruler of much of Poland when the Mongol invasion struck in 1241.

Early life and rule

Henry was associated with his father's government from an early age and served as co-ruler of Silesia. When Henry I died in 1238, Henry II inherited the Duchy of Silesia and the High Duchy of Kraków, and with them overlordship — contested but real — over Greater Poland and other Piast provinces. He maintained his father's alliance with the Přemyslid kings of Bohemia, sealed by his marriage to Anne, daughter of Ottokar I. Known for his piety, he supported monastic foundations including the Cistercian abbeys patronised by his parents, and completed the endowment of the convent at Trzebnica where his mother lived.

Mongol invasion and death

In early 1241 a Mongol army under Baidar and Kadan invaded Poland as a diversion from the main assault on Hungary. Henry gathered an army of Polish knights, Silesian levies, and military-order contingents near Legnica. On 9 April 1241, at the Battle of Legnica, his forces were destroyed and Henry was killed fighting. According to tradition his body was identified by his wife through a distinctive feature — six toes on one foot. He was buried in the Franciscan church in Wrocław.

Legacy

Henry's death at Legnica made him a hero of Polish and Christian resistance to the Mongol invasion, although the battle did not halt the Mongol advance into Hungary. His death also ended the brief reunification of the Polish lands achieved by his father: the kingdom fragmented again among his young sons, beginning a long period of division. His widow Anne founded a Franciscan friary in Wrocław in his memory.
