Bordeaux (French pronunciation: [bɔʁdo]) is a city on the Garonne River in southwestern France and the prefecture of the Gironde department and the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It is world-famous for its wine industry and its elegant 18th-century urban architecture; the historic Port of the Moon — the port area along the crescent-shaped bend of the Garonne — was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2007 as one of the largest protected urban ensembles in Europe.

History

The site was settled in antiquity as Burdigala, a port of the Bituriges Vivisci tribe that became prosperous under Roman rule as a trading post for wine and metals. In the Middle Ages, three centuries of rule by the English crown (from the marriage of Eleanor of Aquitaine to Henry II in 1152 until 1453) made Bordeaux wine a staple of the English market — the origin of the term "claret" for its red wines. The 18th century was a golden age: trade with the French West Indies enriched the city and produced its grand neoclassical facades, the Place de la Bourse, and the Grand Théâtre. The city served as a refuge for the French government in 1870, 1914, and 1940.

Wine

Bordeaux is the world's largest fine-wine region, with roughly 110,000 hectares of vineyards across appellations such as Médoc, Saint-Émilion, Pomerol, Graves, and Sauternes. Red blends dominated by Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, dry whites, and the sweet wines of Sauternes are produced under the appellation system; the official classification of 1855, drawn up for the Paris Exposition, still largely governs the prestige hierarchy of Médoc estates. The Cité du Vin, a museum devoted to wine culture, opened in 2016.

The modern city

Under mayor Alain Juppé from 1995, Bordeaux underwent extensive renovation: facades were cleaned, a modern tramway network was built, and the riverfront was reclaimed for pedestrians, including the famous Miroir d'eau (water mirror) before the Place de la Bourse. The LGV Sud Europe Atlantique high-speed line, opened in 2017, cut travel time to Paris to about two hours.
