North Wales

North Wales (Welsh: Gogledd Cymru) is a region of Wales, encompassing the northernmost principal areas: the Isle of Anglesey, Gwynedd, Conwy, Denbighshire, Flintshire, and Wrexham. It incorporates the historic counties of Anglesey, Caernarfonshire, Denbighshire, Flintshire, Merionethshire, and part of Montgomeryshire.

Yr Wyddfa (Snowdon), the highest mountain in Wales

Geography

The region is dominated by the mountains of Eryri (Snowdonia), including Yr Wyddfa, the highest peak in Wales at 1,085 metres, protected within Eryri National Park. The North Wales coast along the Irish Sea hosts resorts such as Llandudno, Rhyl, and Colwyn Bay, while the island of Anglesey (Ynys Môn) is linked to the mainland by the Menai Suspension Bridge and the Britannia Bridge.

History and culture

North Wales preserves the strongest Welsh-speaking communities in the country, especially in Gwynedd and Anglesey. In the Middle Ages the region formed the heartland of the principality of Gwynedd; Edward I's conquest in the 1280s left a ring of castles — Conwy, Caernarfon, Harlech, and Beaumaris — now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Slate quarrying in towns such as Blaenau Ffestiniog and Bethesda supplied roofing worldwide in the 19th century.
