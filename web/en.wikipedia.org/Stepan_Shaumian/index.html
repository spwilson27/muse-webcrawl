Stepan Shaumian

Born: 13 October 1878, Tiflis, Russian Empire
Died: 20 September 1918 (aged 39), near Krasnovodsk
Known for: Head of the Baku Commune; one of the 26 Baku Commissars

Stepan Georgevich Shaumian (13 October 1878 – 20 September 1918) was a Bolshevik revolutionary and politician of Armenian descent, active in the Caucasus. He was the head of the short-lived Baku Commune in 1918 and is remembered as the leader of the 26 Baku Commissars.

Life

Born in Tiflis (Tbilisi), Shaumian studied in Berlin and joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party, becoming a close associate of Vladimir Lenin and an editor of Bolshevik newspapers in the Caucasus. After the October Revolution he was appointed Extraordinary Commissar for Caucasian Affairs.

In April 1918 he became chairman of the Baku Soviet of People's Commissars, leading the Baku Commune, which attempted to govern the oil-rich city amid civil war and ethnic conflict. When Baku fell to Ottoman and Azerbaijani forces in September 1918, Shaumian and his fellow commissars fled by ship across the Caspian Sea but were arrested at Krasnovodsk and executed on 20 September 1918. The city of Stepanakert in Nagorno-Karabakh was named in his honour during the Soviet period.
