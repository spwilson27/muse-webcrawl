Wounded Knee occupation

The Wounded Knee occupation was a 71-day standoff in 1973 at Wounded Knee, South Dakota, on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation, led by members of the American Indian Movement (AIM) and Oglala Lakota activists protesting the tribal government of Richard Wilson and the United States government's treaty record.

Events

Beginning on 27 February 1973, about 200 occupiers seized the town — the site of the 1890 Wounded Knee Massacre — and declared the Independent Oglala Nation. Federal marshals, FBI agents and military advisers surrounded the site. Two occupiers were killed and a marshal was paralysed in exchanges of gunfire.

Outcome

The occupation ended on 8 May 1973 with a negotiated disarmament. It drew national attention to Native American civil rights, though promised treaty reviews produced limited change. Leaders Russell Means and Dennis Banks were tried and acquitted in 1974.
