The Olympic Oval in Calgary, Alberta, Canada, is a long-track speed skating facility built for the 1988 Winter Olympics, located on the campus of the University of Calgary. It was the first fully enclosed speed skating venue in North America and the first covered oval used at a Winter Games.

"The Fastest Ice in the World"

Thanks to Calgary's high altitude (about 1,100 metres), precise climate control, and purified water used to make the ice, the Oval gained a reputation as "the fastest ice in the world": the great majority of world records in long-track speed skating in the decades after 1988 were set there, and hundreds of national and personal records continue to be broken at the venue.

Facility and use

The building houses the 400-metre oval around two ice hockey rinks and an athletics track. It remains a national training centre for Canadian speed skaters, hosts World Cup events, and serves university and public skating and sport programs.
