576i

576i is a standard-definition video mode originally used for broadcast television in most of the world where the utility frequency is 50 Hz. The "576" denotes 576 horizontal lines of vertical resolution and the "i" stands for interlaced scanning; the frame rate is 25 frames (50 fields) per second.

Overview

The mode derives from the analogue PAL and SECAM systems, each of which used 625 total lines per frame, of which 576 carry picture information. When digitized, 576i pictures are commonly stored at 720 × 576 pixels (D1 resolution), with non-square pixels accommodating both 4:3 and 16:9 aspect ratios.

Digital broadcasting

576i at 25 frames per second (576i25) is one of the standard formats of DVB, ATSC-adjacent EDTV profiles, and DVD-Video in PAL regions. Its progressive-scan counterpart is 576p.

Comparison with 480i

The corresponding mode in 60 Hz countries is 480i, based on NTSC's 525 lines. The 576i mode offers about 20% more vertical resolution, while 480i provides a higher field rate, a trade-off visible in motion smoothness versus detail.
