Joseph Cinqué

Joseph Cinqué
Born: c. 1814, Sierra Leone
Died: c. 1879, Sierra Leone
Known for: Leader of the Amistad revolt

Joseph Cinqué (c. 1814 – c. 1879), also known as Sengbe Pieh, was a West African man of the Mende people who led a revolt of enslaved Africans aboard the Spanish slave ship La Amistad in 1839.

The Amistad revolt

Kidnapped in Sierra Leone and illegally transported to Cuba, Cinqué and others were sold and put aboard La Amistad for transport within Cuba. On 2 July 1839 Cinqué freed himself and led a revolt, killing the captain and cook and ordering the owners to sail back to Africa; instead the ship was steered north and seized by the US Navy off Long Island.

Legal case

The resulting case, United States v. The Amistad (1841), reached the US Supreme Court, where former president John Quincy Adams argued for the Africans. The Court ruled they had been illegally enslaved and were free. Cinqué and the survivors returned to Sierra Leone in 1842.

Legacy

Cinqué became a symbol of resistance to slavery; his story was dramatised in the 1997 film Amistad.
