English Heritage (officially the English Heritage Trust) is a charity that manages over 400 historic monuments, buildings and places in England. These include prehistoric sites, medieval castles, Roman forts, industrial sites, stately homes and Cold War bunkers. Among the best-known are Stonehenge, Dover Castle, Tintagel Castle, Hadrian's Wall, Osborne House and Kenwood House.

History

English Heritage was established in 1983 as the Historic Buildings and Monuments Commission for England, a non-departmental public body under the National Heritage Act. In April 2015 it was split in two: Historic England took over the statutory functions of designation, planning advice and research, while the new English Heritage Trust charity received a licence to manage the National Heritage Collection of properties in state care, together with a government grant of £80 million intended to make it self-financing from admissions, membership and donations.

Role

The charity cares for the collection on behalf of the nation, opening properties to millions of visitors a year, staging events such as historical re-enactments, and running a membership scheme. It also administers the London blue plaques scheme, commemorating notable figures at the buildings where they lived and worked. Its work sits alongside that of the National Trust, a separate and larger charity that owns many properties outright.
