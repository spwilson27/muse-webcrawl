Alternative hypothesis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In statistical hypothesis testing, the alternative hypothesis (denoted H1 or Ha) is the statement that contradicts the null hypothesis. It asserts that an observed effect or relationship exists — for example, that a treatment differs from control, or that two variables are associated. A test's outcome is reported in terms of whether the data give sufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis in favour of the alternative.

Directional and non-directional

An alternative hypothesis may be two-sided, stating only that a parameter differs from the null value, or one-sided, stating the direction of the difference. The choice affects the critical region and corresponds to one-tailed versus two-tailed tests.

Role in testing

Together with the null hypothesis, the significance level, and the test statistic, the alternative hypothesis determines the power of a test — the probability of correctly rejecting a false null hypothesis.
