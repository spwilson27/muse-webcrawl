Troodontidae is a family of bird-like theropod dinosaurs. They were mostly small- to medium-sized predators, ranging from the tiny Anchiornis-sized early forms to Troodon itself, which grew to about 2.5 metres long. Troodontids lived during the Jurassic and Cretaceous periods, mainly in Asia and North America, and are among the most bird-like of the non-avian dinosaurs. They are known for their unusually large brains (among the largest relative to body size of any dinosaurs), large forward-facing eyes suggesting good vision, and a retractable, sickle-shaped claw on the second toe of each foot, similar to but smaller than that of the related dromaeosaurids.

Anatomy and biology

Troodontids had long, slender legs adapted for running, serrated teeth with distinctive widely spaced denticles, and asymmetrical ears that may have given them acute hearing comparable to owls. Their large braincases and binocular vision support the idea that they were active, visually oriented hunters of small prey, possibly active at dawn, dusk or night. Fossil evidence of closely related paravians, and impressions associated with some troodontid specimens, indicate they were covered in feathers. Some troodontids are known from brooding specimens found curled over clutches of eggs, indicating parental care similar to that of modern birds.

Classification

Troodontidae belongs to the maniraptoran clade Deinonychosauria (or in some analyses forms the sister group to Dromaeosauridae + Avialae), making them close relatives of birds. Genera include Troodon, Stenonychosaurus, Saurornithoides, Zanabazar, Mei, Sinovenator, Byronosaurus, Gobivenator and Latenivenatrix — the latter, described in 2017 from Canadian material, is among the largest known troodontids. The taxonomy of North American troodontids was long complicated by the fact that Troodon formosus was originally named from a single tooth in 1856.
