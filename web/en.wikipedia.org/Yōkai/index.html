Yōkai (Japanese: 妖怪, "strange apparition") are a class of supernatural entities and spirits in Japanese folklore. They range from malevolent demons to mischievous tricksters and harmless oddities, and include animal-like creatures, shape-shifters, possessed objects and the ghosts of the dead. The word combines the characters for "bewitching/calamity" and "strange/mystery".

History

Belief in strange beings is ancient in Japan, but yōkai were systematized in the Edo period (1603–1868), when encyclopedias, picture books and the storytelling game Hyakumonogatari Kaidankai popularized them. The artist Toriyama Sekien published influential illustrated catalogues beginning with Gazu Hyakki Yakō (1776), inventing or codifying many famous designs. Folklorists such as Inoue Enryō and Yanagita Kunio later studied yōkai as cultural phenomena, and in the 20th century the manga artist Shigeru Mizuki revived popular interest.

Famous yōkai

Well-known examples include the oni, horned ogres; the kappa, water imps with a water-filled dish on their heads; the long-nosed tengu; shape-shifting kitsune (foxes) and tanuki; yūrei, ghosts of the dead; and tsukumogami, household tools that come alive after a hundred years. Animal, natural and human phenomena unexplained by science were often attributed to yōkai.

In modern culture

Yōkai remain pervasive in Japanese media, from Mizuki's GeGeGe no Kitarō to the Yo-kai Watch and Pokémon franchises, films such as Spirited Away, and countless anime, games and festivals.
