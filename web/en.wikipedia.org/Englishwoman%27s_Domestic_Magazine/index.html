The Englishwoman's Domestic Magazine was a monthly women's magazine published in London from 1852 to 1879 by Samuel Orchart Beeton, with his wife Isabella Beeton playing a major editorial role. Sold at two pence, it was aimed at young middle-class women and became one of the most successful and influential domestic periodicals of Victorian Britain.

Contents

The magazine offered fashion plates (supplied from Paris), needlework patterns, fiction, poetry, household advice, cookery and articles on domestic economy. It included paper patterns for dresses, an innovation that helped readers copy fashionable styles at home. Its correspondence pages allowed readers to exchange advice, and its articles addressed subjects such as household management, servants and child care.

The Beetons

Isabella Beeton joined the magazine's staff after her marriage in 1857, writing extensively on cookery and household management. The material developed in the magazine formed the basis of her celebrated Book of Household Management (1861), first issued in parts in the magazine from 1859.

The tight-lacing controversy

In 1867–68 the magazine hosted a famous, heated correspondence on tight lacing and corsetry, in which readers debated the health effects and fashionability of tightly laced corsets — a discussion still cited by historians of dress.
