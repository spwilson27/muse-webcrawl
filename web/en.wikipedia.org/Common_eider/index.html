Common eider

The common eider (pronounced /ˈaɪ.dər/) (Somateria mollissima) is a large sea duck that is distributed over the northern coasts of Europe, North America, and eastern Siberia. It breeds in Arctic and some northern temperate regions, but winters somewhat farther south in temperate zones, where it can form large flocks on coastal waters.

Description

The common eider is the largest of the four eider species and the largest duck found in Europe, measuring 50–71 cm (20–28 in) in length and weighing 0.81–3.04 kg (1.8–6.7 lb). The drake is unmistakable with its black and white plumage and green nape; females are brown but can be distinguished by size and shape. The species dives for molluscs and crustaceans, especially mussels, which it swallows whole and crushes in its large gizzard.

Eiderdown

The species is famous for eiderdown, the soft feathers the female plucks from her breast to line her nest. In Iceland and Norway the down has been harvested sustainably for centuries from protected nesting colonies, and eider farming remains an important tradition; the down is among the warmest natural insulation materials.
