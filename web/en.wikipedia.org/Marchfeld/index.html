Marchfeld

The Marchfeld is a sedimentary basin and plain in Lower Austria, northeast of Vienna, between the Danube River to the south and the Morava (March) River, forming the border with Slovakia, to the east. Covering some 900 square kilometres, it is one of Austria's most productive agricultural regions — a "granary" and vegetable-growing centre for Vienna — and is largely protected as part of the Donau-Auen National Park along the river.

History

The plain was the site of several significant battles. In 1260 the Battle of Kressenbrunn was fought nearby, and on 26 August 1278 the Battle on the Marchfeld (at Dürnkrut and Jedenspeigen) saw Rudolf I of Habsburg defeat King Ottokar II of Bohemia, who was killed — a turning point that established Habsburg rule in Austria. In 1809 the Marchfeld was the theatre of the battles of Aspern-Essling and Wagram during the Napoleonic Wars.

Geography

Baroque palaces such as Schloss Hof and Eckartsau are notable landmarks. The Marchfeld Canal, completed in the 1990s, supplies irrigation water and stabilises groundwater in the dry pannonian climate of the plain.
