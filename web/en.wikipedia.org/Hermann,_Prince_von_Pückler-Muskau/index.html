Prince Hermann von Pückler-Muskau

Hermann Ludwig Heinrich, Prince von Pückler-Muskau (30 October 1785 – 4 February 1871) was a German prince, landscape gardener, writer and traveller, best known for creating Muskau Park (Park Mużakowski), one of the finest English-style landscape parks in Europe, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Life

Pückler was born at Muskau Castle in Upper Lusatia, the eldest son of Count Ludwig Carl Hans Erdmann von Pückler and Countess Clementine von Callenberg. After a brief military career, he inherited the Muskau estate in 1811. Deeply influenced by the English landscape gardens he had seen in Britain, he began transforming the grounds around Muskau from 1815, creating a vast park in the English style along the Neisse river.

The enormous cost of the park brought him close to ruin. In 1826 he and his wife Lucie von Hardenberg, daughter of the Prussian chancellor Karl August von Hardenberg, agreed to a divorce so that he could seek a wealthy heiress in England whose fortune might save the estate; the plan failed, and the couple remained on close terms. In 1845 he sold Muskau and moved to Branitz near Cottbus, where he created another celebrated landscape park, including earth pyramids in a lake and a land pyramid in which he was eventually buried.

Works

Pückler won literary fame with his travel writings, published under the pseudonym "Semilasso". His Briefe eines Verstorbenen ("Letters of a Dead Man", 1830), describing travels through England, Wales and Ireland, was a bestseller and was translated into English by Sarah Austin. His Andeutungen über Landschaftsgärtnerei ("Hints on Landscape Gardening", 1834) became a classic of garden design, accompanied by an atlas of plans of Muskau Park.

Legacy

Muskau Park, divided between Germany and Poland since 1945, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2004. Pückler is also remembered in culinary lore: the tri-coloured ice cream dessert known in Germany as Fürst-Pückler-Eis (Neapolitan ice cream) was named after him.
