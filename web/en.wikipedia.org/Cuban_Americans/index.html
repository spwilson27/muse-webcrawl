Cuban Americans

Cuban Americans (Spanish: cubanoamericanos) are Americans of Cuban ancestry, including immigrants from Cuba and their descendants. They form the third-largest Hispanic group in the United States, numbering roughly 2.4 million people. The largest concentration is in South Florida, particularly Miami-Dade County, where the neighbourhood of Little Havana is the cultural heart of the community.

History

Cuban migration to the United States dates to the 19th century, when cigar workers settled in Key West and Tampa. Mass migration began after the Cuban Revolution of 1959, when hundreds of thousands fled the government of Fidel Castro. Later waves included the Freedom Flights of 1965–1973, the Mariel boatlift of 1980, and the balsero (rafter) crisis of the 1990s. The Cuban Adjustment Act of 1966 gave Cuban migrants a special path to permanent residency.

Culture and politics

Cuban Americans have had a major influence on the cuisine, music, and politics of South Florida. Historically the community leaned strongly Republican because of its anti-communist stance, though voting patterns have diversified among younger generations. Prominent Cuban Americans have served in the U.S. Congress and Cabinet.
