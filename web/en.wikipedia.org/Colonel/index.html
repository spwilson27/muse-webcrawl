Colonel

Colonel is a senior military officer rank, ranking above a lieutenant colonel and below a brigadier or brigadier general in most armies. Colonels typically command a regiment or brigade-sized formation, or hold senior staff appointments. The word comes from the Italian colonnello, "commander of a column."

History

The rank emerged in 16th- and 17th-century Europe, when colonels raised and led regiments, often as proprietary units. The pronunciation "kernel" in English arose from the older Spanish-influenced spelling coronel.

By country

In the United States, a colonel (O-6) wears a silver eagle insignia and commands brigades of several thousand soldiers. In the British Army, colonel is largely a staff and ceremonial rank, with brigades commanded by brigadiers. Many air forces and navies use equivalent ranks (group captain and captain, respectively). The title is also used ceremonially, as in the honorary Kentucky Colonel.
