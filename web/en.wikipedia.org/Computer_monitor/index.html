Computer monitor

A flat-panel LCD computer monitor

A computer monitor is an output device that displays information generated by a computer in pictorial or textual form. It comprises a visual display, supporting circuitry, a power supply, and usually an enclosure and controls. Monitors connect to the computer through interfaces such as HDMI, DisplayPort, USB-C, and (historically) VGA and DVI.

Technologies

Early monitors used cathode-ray tubes (CRTs), which were bulky but offered fast response and good color. From the 2000s, liquid-crystal displays (LCDs) — with TN, IPS, and VA panel types — displaced CRTs owing to their thin profile and low power use; LED backlighting then replaced fluorescent backlights. Organic light-emitting diode (OLED) monitors offer per-pixel emission, high contrast, and fast response, and are increasingly used in premium and gaming displays.

Performance measures

Key specifications include screen size (diagonal), resolution (for example 1920×1080 "Full HD", 2560×1440, and 3840×2160 "4K"), aspect ratio (commonly 16:9, with 16:10 and ultrawide 21:9 also available), refresh rate (60 Hz standard, 120–360 Hz for gaming), response time, brightness, contrast ratio, color gamut (such as sRGB, DCI-P3, Adobe RGB), and pixel density in pixels per inch.

History

Computer displays evolved from teletype terminals and vector displays in the 1960s through monochrome and color raster CRTs accompanying personal computers in the 1970s–80s, to the flat-panel era of the 2000s. Features once considered extras — integrated speakers, USB hubs, webcams, height-adjustable stands, and curved screens — are now common.
