Wyvern

A wyvern (from Old French wivre, from Latin vipera, "viper") is a legendary winged dragon with a reptilian body, two legs, a pair of wings, and a barbed tail. In heraldry the wyvern is distinguished from the four-legged dragon, and it appears frequently in Welsh and English heraldic and literary tradition.

Description

The wyvern's body is serpentine or draconic, with a single pair of hind legs (or, in some depictions, the forelegs fused with the wings), bat-like wings, an eagle's or serpent's head, and a tail ending in a barb or arrow-like sting. Unlike a dragon, it traditionally does not breathe fire in most heraldic usage, though modern fantasy frequently grants it venom or fiery breath.

Heraldry

Wyverns are common charges in British heraldry, appearing in the arms of families, towns, schools, and military units. A white (silver) wyvern was associated with Leicester and appeared in the arms of the Midland Railway, earning the nickname "the Wyvern" for the city. The golden wyvern on a red field was the banner of the medieval Kingdom of Wessex, and the Welsh dragon is sometimes classed with the wyvern type in heraldic discussion.

In fiction

Wyverns feature prominently in modern fantasy literature, film, and games, where the two-legged form is often used to distinguish wyverns from "true" four-legged dragons, as in the dragons of Game of Thrones and the monsters of the Monster Hunter series.
