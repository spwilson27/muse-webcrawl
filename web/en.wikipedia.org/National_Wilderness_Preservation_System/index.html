National Wilderness Preservation System

The National Wilderness Preservation System (NWPS) is a network of federally protected wilderness areas in the United States, created by the Wilderness Act of 1964. It is managed by four federal land agencies: the Bureau of Land Management, the United States Fish and Wildlife Service, the National Park Service, and the United States Forest Service.

Wilderness Act

Signed into law by President Lyndon B. Johnson on September 3, 1964, the Wilderness Act initially protected 9.1 million acres in 54 areas. The Act famously defines wilderness as "an area where the earth and its community of life are untrammeled by man, where man himself is a visitor who does not remain." Only Congress can designate wilderness areas.

Management and restrictions

Within designated wilderness, mechanized and motorized equipment is generally prohibited, as are roads, commercial enterprises, and permanent structures. Permitted activities include hiking, camping, hunting, fishing, and horseback riding. The system has grown to include more than 800 areas covering over 111 million acres — roughly 4.5 percent of the area of the United States — with the majority of acreage in Alaska.
