Badshahi Mosque

The Badshahi Mosque is a Mughal-era mosque in Lahore, Pakistan, located west of Lahore Fort on the outskirts of the Walled City. Commissioned by the emperor Aurangzeb in 1671 and completed in 1673, it was the largest mosque in the world at the time of its construction and remains one of the largest mosques in Pakistan and an iconic symbol of Lahore.

Architecture

Built of red sandstone with white marble domes and minarets, the mosque follows the classic Mughal congregational plan: a vast courtyard, reached by a monumental stepped gateway, faces a prayer hall divided into five bays. The courtyard can accommodate tens of thousands of worshippers. The prayer hall is richly decorated with carved marble, fresco work and inlay, and the four corner minarets are faced with marble inlay.

History

After the fall of the Mughal Empire, the mosque was used as a garrison by Sikh and later British forces, during which parts of the complex suffered damage and misuse. Repairs began in the 19th and 20th centuries, and extensive restoration in the decades after Pakistan's independence returned the building to religious use and to something close to its original appearance. The mosque houses a museum containing relics attributed to the Prophet Muhammad and his family, and it stands on UNESCO's tentative list for World Heritage status.
