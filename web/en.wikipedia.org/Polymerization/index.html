Polymerization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Monomers linking to form a polymer chain

In polymer chemistry, polymerization (American English) or polymerisation (British English) is a process of reacting monomer molecules together in a chemical reaction to form polymer chains or three-dimensional networks. Because of the large number of polymers in use, many distinct polymerization techniques have been developed for different monomer types, molecular weights and material properties.

Chain-growth polymerization

In chain-growth polymerization, monomers add one at a time to a small number of growing chains, each bearing an active centre — a radical, cation, anion or metal complex. The process has three stages: initiation, in which the active centre is created; propagation, in which the chain grows rapidly; and termination, in which the active centre is destroyed. Free-radical polymerization of ethylene and styrene, and Ziegler–Natta polymerization of olefins, are industrially dominant examples.

Step-growth polymerization

In step-growth polymerization, bifunctional or multifunctional monomers react with one another in steps, forming dimers, trimers and progressively longer oligomers that can combine at any stage. Molecular weight builds slowly, and very high conversion is needed to obtain long chains. Polycondensation is step-growth polymerization in which each bond formation releases a small molecule such as water; it is used to make polyesters, polyamides (nylons) and polycarbonates. Polyaddition, as in polyurethane formation, proceeds without a by-product.

Copolymerization

When two or more different monomers are polymerized together, the product is a copolymer. The sequence of units may be random, alternating, block-like or graft-like, and strongly affects properties such as toughness, elasticity and solubility. Styrene-butadiene rubber and ABS are well-known copolymers.

Techniques

Industrial methods include bulk (mass) polymerization, solution polymerization, suspension polymerization and emulsion polymerization, each balancing heat removal, viscosity and particle morphology differently. Living polymerization techniques, in which termination is suppressed, allow precise control of chain length and architecture.
