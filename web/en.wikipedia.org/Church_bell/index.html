Church bell

A church bell is a bell designed to be rung in a church, usually from a bell tower or belfry. Church bells summon worshippers to services, mark the hours and the Angelus, toll for funerals, and ring in celebration at weddings and festivals. They are traditionally cast in bronze (bell metal, an alloy of copper and tin) by specialist founders.

History

Bells were adopted by Christian churches in early medieval Europe; tradition credits Paulinus of Nola with their introduction around AD 400, and Irish missionaries spread small hand bells widely. Large tower bells developed with medieval bell-founding, and by the later Middle Ages most parish churches possessed at least one bell. In England the practice of change ringing on rings of tuned bells swung full circle developed in the seventeenth century.

Use

Bells may be swung, chimed by striking, or played as a carillon of tuned bells by a carillonneur at a keyboard. Famous bells include Big Ben in London, the Liberty Bell in Philadelphia (originally a state house bell), and the great bells of cathedrals such as Cologne and Notre-Dame de Paris. In Orthodox tradition, elaborate rhythmic ringing (zvon) is a developed art.
