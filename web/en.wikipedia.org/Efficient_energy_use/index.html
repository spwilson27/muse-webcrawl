Efficient energy use

Efficient energy use, sometimes simply called energy efficiency, is the process of reducing the amount of energy required to provide products and services. Improving energy efficiency reduces energy costs, energy imports and greenhouse gas emissions, and is regarded by bodies such as the International Energy Agency as a central element of climate policy, sometimes described as the 'first fuel'.

Examples

Efficiency improvements include insulating buildings, LED lighting, high-efficiency motors and appliances, heat pumps, and vehicles that travel further per unit of fuel. Appliance labelling schemes, such as the European Union energy label and the US Energy Star programme, help consumers identify efficient products, while building codes set minimum performance standards.

Economics

Although efficient technologies often cost more initially, lower running costs can recover the investment. The rebound effect describes how some of the expected savings are offset when lower operating costs encourage greater use of the service. Energy efficiency policy is frequently combined with demand-side management and building retrofit programmes.
