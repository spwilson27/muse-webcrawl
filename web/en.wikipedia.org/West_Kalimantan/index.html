West Kalimantan

West Kalimantan (Kalimantan Barat)
Province of Indonesia
Capital: Pontianak
Area: about 147,307 km²
Population (2020): 5,414,390

West Kalimantan (Indonesian: Kalimantan Barat) is a province of Indonesia on the island of Borneo. It is one of five Indonesian provinces in Kalimantan, the Indonesian part of Borneo. Its capital and largest city is Pontianak, which lies exactly on the Equator. The province shares land borders with the Malaysian state of Sarawak to the north, East Kalimantan to the north-east, and Central Kalimantan to the south-east.

Geography

West Kalimantan is dominated by lowland rainforest and is crossed by some of the longest rivers in Indonesia, most importantly the Kapuas River, at roughly 1,143 km the longest river in the country. The Schwaner and Müller mountain ranges rise along the eastern and northern borders. Large areas consist of peat swamp and mangrove along the coast of the Karimata Strait and the South China Sea.

History

The region was historically home to Malay sultanates, including Sambas, Mempawah and Pontianak, the last founded in 1771 by Syarif Abdurrahman Alkadrie. In the nineteenth century, Chinese gold miners organised in self-governing kongsi federations, including the Lanfang Republic, flourished in the interior before being suppressed by the Dutch. During the Second World War the area was occupied by Japan; the Pontianak massacres of 1943–44 claimed the lives of many members of the local elite. West Kalimantan became a province of the Republic of Indonesia in the 1950s.

Demographics

The population comprises Malay and Dayak peoples as the largest indigenous groups, together with a large ethnic Chinese community, Javanese, Bugis and Madurese. Languages spoken include Indonesian, Hakka and Teochew Chinese, and numerous Dayak languages. Religions include Islam, Catholicism, Protestantism, Buddhism and Confucianism.

Economy

The economy is based on agriculture and natural resources: oil palm, rubber, timber, pepper and other spices, gold and bauxite mining, and fisheries. Environmental concerns include deforestation, peatland fires and haze.
