To Kill a Mockingbird is a 1962 American drama film directed by Robert Mulligan, adapted by Horton Foote from Harper Lee's 1960 Pulitzer Prize-winning novel of the same name. Gregory Peck stars as Atticus Finch, a widowed lawyer in the racially divided Alabama town of Maycomb during the Great Depression, who defends Tom Robinson, a Black man falsely accused of raping a white woman. The story is told through the eyes of Atticus's young daughter Scout, played by Mary Badham.

Plot

In the fictional town of Maycomb, Alabama, in the 1930s, Scout Finch and her brother Jem pass their summers with their friend Dill and grow fascinated by their reclusive neighbour, Boo Radley. Their father Atticus, a principled small-town attorney, agrees to defend Tom Robinson, a Black field hand accused of raping Mayella Ewell. Despite presenting evidence that Robinson could not have committed the assault and that Mayella's father Bob Ewell is the likely aggressor, the all-white jury convicts Robinson, who is later shot while allegedly trying to escape. On Halloween night, Bob Ewell attacks Scout and Jem; they are saved by Boo Radley, who kills Ewell. The sheriff persuades Atticus to let the matter rest, to spare the shy Boo unwanted attention.

Cast

Gregory Peck won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of Atticus Finch. Mary Badham (Scout) and Phillip Alford (Jem) were cast from local children with no acting experience. Brock Peters played Tom Robinson, and Robert Duvall made his film debut as Boo Radley. James Anderson played Bob Ewell and Collin Wilcox played Mayella Ewell.

Production

Producer Alan J. Pakula and director Robert Mulligan shot the film in black and white at Universal Studios, where production designer Henry Bumstead recreated Maycomb on the backlot, modelling it on Harper Lee's hometown of Monroeville, Alabama, which was considered too modernised to film. Elmer Bernstein composed the score, noted for its gentle piano theme evoking childhood.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing more than ten times its budget. It was nominated for eight Academy Awards, winning Best Actor, Best Adapted Screenplay and Best Art Direction. In 1995 it was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry, and the American Film Institute ranked Atticus Finch the greatest hero of American cinema in its 2003 list "100 Years... 100 Heroes & Villains". Gregory Peck regarded the role as the favourite of his career, and Harper Lee praised his performance, remarking that Peck had given her a gift in his portrayal of her father.
