Fear and Trembling (Danish: Frygt og Bæven) is a philosophical work by Søren Kierkegaard, published in 1843 under the pseudonym Johannes de Silentio ("John of Silence"). One of Kierkegaard's most widely read books, it examines the biblical story of Abraham's willingness to sacrifice his son Isaac on Mount Moriah (Genesis 22) as a paradox of faith.

Content

The book contrasts Abraham, the "knight of faith", with the tragic hero who sacrifices for a universally understood ethical duty. Kierkegaard argues that Abraham's act cannot be justified by universal ethics — by ethical standards it is murder — and that Abraham acted instead by virtue of the absurd, believing that he would receive Isaac back. This involves a "teleological suspension of the ethical": the single individual, in an absolute relation to God, stands higher than the universal.

Influence

Fear and Trembling is a foundational text of Christian existentialism and has been widely influential in theology and philosophy, including on thinkers such as Jacques Derrida, who devoted The Gift of Death to its themes. Its concepts of the leap of faith and the knight of faith remain central to Kierkegaard scholarship. The title is taken from Philippians 2:12, "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling".
