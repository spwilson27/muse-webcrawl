Off-Off-Broadway refers to experimental and non-commercial theatre produced in New York City in venues with 99 or fewer seats, smaller and less formal than both Broadway and Off-Broadway theatres. The movement emerged in the late 1950s as a reaction to the rising costs and commercialism of Off-Broadway, and became a seedbed for new playwrights, performance art and avant-garde staging.

History

The term was coined around 1958–1960, when performances began appearing in coffeehouses and lofts in Greenwich Village and the East Village. Foundational spaces included Joe Cino's Caffe Cino (from 1958), Ellen Stewart's La MaMa Experimental Theatre Club (founded 1961), the Judson Poets' Theatre at Judson Memorial Church, and Joseph Chaikin's Open Theater. These venues launched or nurtured writers such as Sam Shepard, Lanford Wilson, María Irene Fornés and Edward Albee, and companies including the Wooster Group and Mabou Mines.

Characteristics

Off-Off-Broadway productions operate under union "showcase" codes that limit budgets and run lengths in exchange for exemption from full commercial contracts. Small scale allows radical experimentation with text, movement, multimedia and audience participation. The annual New York International Fringe Festival and venues such as PS 122 (Performance Space New York) and Dixon Place continue the tradition.
