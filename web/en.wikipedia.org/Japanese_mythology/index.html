Japanese mythology is a system of beliefs embracing Shinto and Buddhist traditions as well as agricultural folk religion. Its central narratives were recorded in the early 8th century in the Kojiki ("Record of Ancient Matters") and the Nihon Shoki, compiled partly to legitimise the imperial line, which claimed descent from the sun goddess Amaterasu.

Creation myths

In the beginning, heaven and earth emerged from chaos, and a succession of deities (kami) appeared. The last pair, the brother and sister Izanagi and Izanami, stood on the Floating Bridge of Heaven and stirred the sea with a jewelled spear, creating the first island. Their offspring included the islands of Japan and many kami. Izanami died giving birth to the fire god and descended to Yomi, the land of the dead; Izanagi's failed attempt to retrieve her is a foundational myth. From Izanagi's purification afterward were born Amaterasu (sun), Tsukuyomi (moon), and Susanoo (storm and sea).

Major cycles

Famous episodes include Susanoo's slaying of the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi and his discovery of the sword Kusanagi; Amaterasu's withdrawal into a cave, plunging the world into darkness until the other gods lured her out; and the descent of her grandson Ninigi to rule the earthly realm, bringing the Three Imperial Regalia—the sword Kusanagi, the mirror Yata no Kagami, and the jewel Yasakani no Magatama. His great-grandson Jimmu is counted the legendary first emperor of Japan.

Folklore

Beyond the court chronicles, Japanese tradition teems with yōkai (supernatural beings such as kappa, tengu, and kitsune), heroic legends, and local shrine myths, forming a rich folklore that continues to inspire literature, festivals, and popular culture.
