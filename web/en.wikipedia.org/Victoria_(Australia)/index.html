Victoria is a state in southeastern Australia. It is the second-smallest state by land area (227,444 km²) but the most densely populated, and the second-most populous state overall, with about 6.9 million inhabitants. Its capital and largest city is Melbourne, home to more than 70 per cent of Victorians. The state is bordered by New South Wales to the north, South Australia to the west, and the Bass Strait and Tasman Sea to the south.

History

Aboriginal Victorians occupied the area for tens of thousands of years before European settlement. The colony of Victoria separated from New South Wales in 1851, named in honour of Queen Victoria. Within months, gold was discovered at Ballarat and Bendigo, triggering one of the world's great gold rushes; by the 1860s Victoria was producing a large share of the world's gold, and Melbourne grew into one of the largest and richest cities of the British Empire. The Eureka Rebellion of 1854, centred on the Eureka Stockade at Ballarat, became a landmark in Australian democratic mythology. Victoria was one of the six colonies that federated to form the Commonwealth of Australia in 1901.

Geography and economy

The state spans coastal plains, the Great Dividing Range — including Mount Bogong (1,986 m), its highest point — and the semi-arid Mallee in the northwest. Landmarks include the Great Ocean Road and the Twelve Apostles limestone stacks. Melbourne is Australia's cultural and sporting capital, hosting the Australian Open tennis, the Melbourne Cup horse race, and numerous Australian rules football clubs; Victoria is the heartland of the AFL. The economy rests on services, finance, education, manufacturing, agriculture (notably dairy in Gippsland), and brown-coal electricity generation in the Latrobe Valley.
