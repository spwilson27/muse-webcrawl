Cornish–Fisher expansion

The Cornish–Fisher expansion (named for E. A. Cornish and Ronald Fisher) is an asymptotic expansion used in statistics to approximate the quantiles of a probability distribution in terms of its cumulants. It is the inverse counterpart of the Gram–Charlier and Edgeworth expansions, which approximate the density or distribution function; the Cornish–Fisher expansion instead directly adjusts the corresponding quantile of the normal distribution.

Formulation

For a random variable with mean μ, standard deviation σ, skewness γ₁ and excess kurtosis γ₂, the α-quantile can be approximated by starting from the standard normal quantile z and correcting it:

w = z + (z² − 1)·γ₁/6 + [ (z³ − 3z)·γ₂ − 2(z³ − 5z)·γ₁² ] / 24 − (z² − 1)·γ₁³ / 36 · ...

The quantile is then approximated as μ + σ·w. Higher-order terms involve higher cumulants.

Applications

The expansion is used in quantitative risk management to estimate Value at Risk and expected shortfall for portfolios whose returns are skewed or fat-tailed, adjusting the Gaussian VaR for higher moments of the return distribution. It is also used in constructing approximate confidence intervals and in quality control. Because it is an asymptotic series, the expansion can behave poorly for extreme quantiles or strongly non-normal distributions, and the approximated quantile function is not guaranteed to be monotonic.
