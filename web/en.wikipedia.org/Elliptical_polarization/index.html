Elliptical polarization

Elliptical polarization
FieldElectromagnetism, optics
Special casesLinear polarization, circular polarization
Described byJones vectors, Stokes parameters

In electrodynamics, elliptical polarization is the polarization of electromagnetic radiation such that the tip of the electric field vector at a fixed point in space describes an ellipse as a function of time. It is the most general form of polarization: the elliptically polarized wave may be decomposed into two linearly polarized waves in phase quadrature, with polarization planes perpendicular to each other.

Description

The electric field of a plane wave propagating in the +z direction can be written as the sum of two orthogonal components with amplitudes Ex and Ey and a phase difference δ between them. If δ is neither 0 nor a multiple of π, and the amplitudes differ, the tip of the resultant vector traces an ellipse. The shape of the ellipse is characterized by its axial ratio and the orientation of its major axis; the sense of rotation is described as right-handed or left-handed following conventions used in optics and radio engineering.

Special cases

When δ = 0 or π, the ellipse degenerates into a line, giving linear polarization. When the two amplitudes are equal and δ = ±π/2, the ellipse becomes a circle, giving circular polarization. Elliptical polarization is therefore the general case of which linear and circular are limiting forms.

Mathematical representation

Jones calculus represents fully polarized light with two-component Jones vectors; partially polarized light requires the Stokes parameters or the coherency matrix. Elliptical polarization states correspond to points on the surface of the Poincaré sphere, with circular states at the poles and linear states on the equator.

Applications

Elliptical polarization arises naturally in reflection from surfaces, in birefringent materials, and in radio propagation through the ionosphere. Circularly and elliptically polarized antennas are used in satellite communications because they are insensitive to the relative rotation of transmitter and receiver. In optics, ellipsometry measures the change in polarization state upon reflection to determine thin-film thickness and material properties.
