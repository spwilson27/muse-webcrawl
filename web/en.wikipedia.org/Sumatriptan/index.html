Sumatriptan

Sumatriptan, sold under the brand name Imitrex among others, is a medication used to treat migraine headaches and cluster headaches. It is taken orally, intranasally, or by subcutaneous injection. It is a member of the triptan class of drugs, developed by Glaxo (now GSK) and first approved in 1991, and is available as a generic medication.

Mechanism of action

Sumatriptan is a selective agonist of the serotonin 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptors. Activation of these receptors causes constriction of dilated cranial blood vessels and inhibits the release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides such as CGRP from trigeminal nerve endings, thereby aborting a migraine attack.

Medical use

Sumatriptan is used to abort migraine attacks once they have begun; it does not prevent future attacks. The subcutaneous injection works fastest and is particularly used for cluster headache. Effectiveness is greatest when the drug is taken early in an attack.

Side effects and contraindications

Common side effects include chest or throat tightness, tingling, dizziness, flushing, and fatigue. Because of its vasoconstrictor action, sumatriptan is contraindicated in people with ischaemic heart disease, previous stroke, and uncontrolled hypertension. Combining it with monoamine oxidase inhibitors, or with other serotonergic drugs (risking serotonin syndrome), requires caution. Overuse can lead to medication-overuse headache.
