Consistent estimator

In statistics, a consistent estimator or asymptotically consistent estimator is an estimator — a rule for computing estimates of a parameter θ0 — having the property that as the number of data points used increases indefinitely, the resulting sequence of estimates converges in probability to θ0. By definition, an estimator Tn of parameter θ is said to be consistent, if it converges in probability to the true value of the parameter.

Definition

Formally, Tn is (weakly) consistent for θ if for every ε > 0, the probability that |Tn − θ| exceeds ε tends to zero as the sample size n tends to infinity. Strong consistency means convergence almost surely. Consistency is typically proven using the law of large numbers — for example, the sample mean is a consistent estimator of the population mean.

Examples

The sample mean and the maximum likelihood estimator (under regularity conditions) are consistent. The (uncorrected) sample variance with denominator n is consistent though slightly biased in finite samples. An estimator can be unbiased for every finite n yet inconsistent in principle if its variance does not shrink, and conversely a consistent estimator may be biased for all finite sample sizes.

Relation to bias

A sufficient condition for consistency is that both the bias and the variance of the estimator tend to zero. Consistency is an asymptotic (large-sample) property, distinct from unbiasedness, which concerns a single fixed sample size.
