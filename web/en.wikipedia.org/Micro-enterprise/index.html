Micro-enterprise

Micro-enterprise
Definition (EU)Fewer than 10 employees; turnover or balance sheet ≤ €2 million
Definition (US SBA)Typically 5 or fewer employees
Related conceptsMicrofinance, small business, informal economy

A micro-enterprise is a very small business, generally defined by the number of employees and financial thresholds that vary by country and sector. In the European Union a micro-enterprise employs fewer than ten people and has annual turnover or a balance-sheet total not exceeding €2 million. In many development contexts the term refers to businesses with five or fewer employees, often run by a single owner-operator.

Role in economies

Micro-enterprises make up the great majority of firms worldwide and a large share of employment, particularly in developing countries, where many operate in the informal economy as street vendors, artisans, smallholder-linked traders and home-based producers. They provide livelihoods, especially for women and rural households, but often face limited access to formal credit, markets and business services.

Support and microfinance

Micro-enterprises are the target clients of microfinance institutions, which provide microloans, savings and insurance products too small for conventional banks. Institutions such as the Grameen Bank pioneered group lending to micro-entrepreneurs. Governments and NGOs also support micro-enterprises through training, simplified registration, and programmes intended to help them grow into small and medium-sized enterprises.

Challenges

Common challenges include low productivity, vulnerability to economic shocks, lack of collateral, regulatory burdens relative to size, and difficulty scaling. Nevertheless, micro-enterprises are widely regarded as a key engine of poverty reduction and local economic resilience.
