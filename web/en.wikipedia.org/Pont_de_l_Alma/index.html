Pont de l'Alma

The Pont de l'Alma ("Alma Bridge") is a road bridge over the River Seine in Paris, France. It was named to commemorate the Battle of the Alma of 1854, an early battle of the Crimean War, and was inaugurated by Napoleon III on 2 April 1856. The bridge links the Place de l'Alma on the Right Bank with the Quai Branly and the area near the Eiffel Tower on the Left Bank.

The Zouave

The original bridge's piers were decorated with statues of four soldiers of the Crimean War regiments. When the bridge was rebuilt between 1970 and 1974, only one figure was retained: the Zouave, sculpted by Georges Diebolt. The statue became a popular informal flood gauge for Parisians — when the Seine rose to the Zouave's feet, the riverside quays flooded; in the great flood of January 1910 the water reached his shoulders.

Flame of Liberty and Diana memorial

Above the bridge's eastern end stands the Flame of Liberty, a full-scale replica of the flame of the Statue of Liberty, donated in 1989 by the International Herald Tribune. The road tunnel beneath the Place de l'Alma is where Diana, Princess of Wales, was fatally injured in a car crash on 31 August 1997. The flame above the tunnel became an unofficial memorial to her, and a memorial garden and plaque were later established nearby.
