Perfect fifth

Semitones7
Just intonation ratio3:2
Equal temperament700 cents
InversePerfect fourth

In music theory, a perfect fifth is the musical interval corresponding to a pair of pitches with a frequency ratio of 3:2, or very nearly so. It spans seven semitones in equal temperament and five letter names (for example, C to G). The perfect fifth is one of the most consonant intervals, second only to the unison and octave in stability, and it is foundational to Western harmony.

Role in harmony

The perfect fifth is the defining component of the major and minor triad alongside the root and third, and a root-position chord built from a root and fifth alone — the "power chord" — is a staple of rock and metal guitar playing. Chord progressions moving by fifths, such as the ii–V–I progression in jazz and the circle of fifths, structure much of tonal harmony. Functional harmony treats the dominant chord, built on the fifth scale degree, as the principal tension chord resolving to the tonic.

Tuning

In just intonation the perfect fifth is exactly 3:2, or 701.955 cents. In twelve-tone equal temperament it is tempered slightly flat to 700 cents so that twelve fifths close the circle of keys. In meantone systems, fifths were tempered more strongly to sweeten the thirds. String instruments such as the violin, viola and cello are tuned in perfect fifths, and the interval figures prominently in the natural harmonic series, where the third partial sounds a fifth above the second.
