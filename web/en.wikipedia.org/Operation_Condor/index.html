Operation Condor

Operation Condor (Spanish: Operación Cóndor) was a campaign of political repression involving intelligence operations, coups and assassinations of left-wing sympathizers, carried out by South American right-wing dictatorships during the Cold War, formally agreed in 1975 and active into the 1980s.

Participating regimes included Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile under Augusto Pinochet, Paraguay, Peru and Uruguay, with technical and financial support and knowledge from the United States. The network shared intelligence, conducted cross-border abductions and renditions, and assassinated exiles abroad, including former Chilean foreign minister Orlando Letelier, killed by a car bomb in Washington, D.C. in 1976.

Victims and prosecutions

Estimates of deaths vary; human rights groups attribute tens of thousands of killings and disappearances to Condor-linked repression, amid a wider toll from the individual regimes. Declassified documents (including Paraguay's Archives of Terror, discovered 1992) and trials in Argentina, Italy and elsewhere have produced convictions of former officials decades later.
