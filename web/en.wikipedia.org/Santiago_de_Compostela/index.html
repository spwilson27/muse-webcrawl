Santiago de Compostela

Santiago de Compostela is the capital of the autonomous community of Galicia in northwestern Spain and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city is the culmination point of the Camino de Santiago pilgrimage routes and the traditional burial place of the apostle St. James the Great. Its population is about 100,000, including a large student population at the University of Santiago de Compostela, founded in 1495.

History

According to tradition, the tomb of St. James was rediscovered in the early ninth century by the hermit Pelayo and Bishop Theodemir of Iria Flavia. A shrine and then a great Romanesque cathedral were built over the site; the present cathedral, consecrated in 1211, later received Baroque additions including its famous Obradoiro façade. Pilgrims from across Europe made Santiago one of medieval Christendom's three great pilgrimage destinations, alongside Rome and Jerusalem.

The Camino today

The Way of St. James remains hugely popular: hundreds of thousands of pilgrims annually walk routes such as the Camino Francés to receive the Compostela certificate. The feast of St. James on 25 July is Galicia's regional holiday, and Holy Years (when 25 July falls on a Sunday) draw especially large crowds. The city's granite old town, arcaded streets, and gastronomy make it a major tourist destination.
