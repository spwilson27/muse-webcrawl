Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky (Russian: Петропавловск-Камчатский) is a city and the administrative centre of Kamchatka Krai, Russia. It is located in the far east of the country, on the southeastern coast of the Kamchatka Peninsula, on Avacha Bay by the Pacific Ocean. The city is one of the oldest settlements in the Russian Far East and is notable for having no road connection to the rest of Russia — it can be reached only by sea or air.

History

The city was founded on 17 October 1740 by the explorer Vitus Bering during the Second Kamchatka Expedition. Bering named the settlement Petropavlovsk ("of Peter and Paul") after his two ships, the Saint Peter and the Saint Paul. It served as a base for Bering's voyage to North America.

During the Crimean War, in August–September 1854, a Russian garrison of about 920 men under Vasily Zavoyko repelled an Anglo-French naval attack in the Siege of Petropavlovsk, one of the celebrated episodes of the war in the Pacific. In 1924 the city was renamed Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky to distinguish it from the city of Petropavlovsk in Kazakhstan.

Geography and economy

The city is surrounded by volcanoes, including Koryaksky and Avachinsky, whose proximity is both a scenic feature and a hazard; major eruptions and earthquakes have affected the region. Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky is a major fishing port — salmon and crab are central to its economy — and hosts a naval base of the Russian Pacific Fleet, including ballistic missile submarines based across the bay at Rybachy (Vilyuchinsk). Tourism, drawn by Kamchatka's volcanoes, geysers and wildlife, has grown in recent decades.
