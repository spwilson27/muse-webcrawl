The Sopranos

The Sopranos is an American crime drama television series created by David Chase. It aired on HBO from 10 January 1999 to 10 June 2007, for 86 episodes over six seasons, and is widely credited with launching the modern golden age of television.

Premise

New Jersey mob boss Tony Soprano (James Gandolfini) begins psychotherapy with Dr. Jennifer Melfi (Lorraine Bracco) after panic attacks. The series balances his two families — wife Carmela (Edie Falco), children Meadow and A.J., and his crime family, including Christopher Moltisanti, Paulie Gualtieri and Silvio Dante — while exploring American decline, therapy culture and violence.

Legacy

The show won 21 Primetime Emmy Awards and five Golden Globes. Its abruptly cut-to-black finale, "Made in America," remains fiercely debated. A prequel film, The Many Saints of Newark, followed in 2021. See also The Skatalites.
