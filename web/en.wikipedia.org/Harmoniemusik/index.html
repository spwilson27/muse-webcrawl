Harmoniemusik

Harmoniemusik is a term for music written for a Harmonie, a wind ensemble fashionable in German-speaking Europe during the second half of the eighteenth century and the early nineteenth century. Such ensembles provided background music — Tafelmusik — at aristocratic courts, as well as entertainment for outdoor occasions.

Harmoniemusik
Era: c. 1760–1830
Typical ensemble: pairs of oboes, clarinets, horns, bassoons
Centres: Vienna and other German-speaking courts

The ensemble

The standard Harmonie was an octet of pairs: two oboes, two clarinets, two horns, and two bassoons, sometimes augmented with additional instruments. Emperor Joseph II maintained a celebrated Kaiserliche Harmonie in Vienna drawn from the court opera orchestra.

Repertoire

Because full opera orchestras were not always available, much Harmoniemusik consisted of arrangements of popular operas, ballets, and symphonies. Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven all wrote original works for wind ensemble: Mozart's serenades in E-flat major (K. 375) and C minor (K. 388) and the Gran Partita (K. 361) are the most famous examples, while Beethoven contributed the Octet, Op. 103.
