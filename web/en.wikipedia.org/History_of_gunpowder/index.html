The history of gunpowder is the story of the invention and spread of the first chemical explosive. Gunpowder — a mixture of saltpetre (potassium nitrate), charcoal and sulfur — was invented in China during the Tang dynasty, probably by alchemists seeking an elixir of immortality, and it transformed warfare worldwide.

Invention in China

By the mid-9th century Chinese texts warn of a dangerous flammable mixture of sulfur, saltpetre and charcoal. The first printed formulas appear in the Song-dynasty military compendium Wujing Zongyao (1044). Military applications followed quickly: fire arrows, bombs thrown by trebuchet, the fire lance (a proto-gun), and by the 13th century true metal-barrel guns — the oldest surviving specimen, the Xanadu gun, dates to 1298. Chinese forces used gunpowder weapons extensively against the Jin and Mongols.

Transmission westward

Gunpowder technology reached the Islamic world and Europe in the 13th century, probably along Mongol-era trade and military routes. Roger Bacon described the mixture in the 1260s, and cannon appear in European records from the 1320s. Gunpowder artillery reshaped siegecraft — famously in the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople (1453) — and the "gunpowder empires" (Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal) built states around it. Saltpetre supply, often from India, became strategically vital, and states tightly controlled powder mills.

Improvements and decline

Corning (granulating) the powder in the 15th century made it more powerful and consistent; rifled firearms, cast cannon and naval broadsides extended its dominance for four centuries. In the 19th century, chemists analysed its combustion and states standardised the classic composition of about 75% saltpetre, 15% charcoal and 10% sulfur. From the 1880s, smokeless powders (nitrocellulose-based, e.g. Poudre B and cordite) replaced black powder in military small arms and artillery because they produced far more energy with little smoke or fouling. Black powder survives today in fireworks, fuses, quarrying, historical reenactment and muzzle-loading sport.
