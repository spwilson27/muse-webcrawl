Cardioid

TypePlane curve; epicycloid
Polar equationr = 2a(1 − cos θ)
Area6πa²
Arc length16a

A cardioid is a plane curve traced by a point on the perimeter of a circle that is rolling around a fixed circle of the same radius. It is therefore a type of epicycloid with a single cusp. Its name, from the Greek kardia (heart), reflects its heart-like shape, given by Étienne Pascal and popularized in the 18th century.

Equations

In polar coordinates with the cusp at the origin, the cardioid can be written r = 2a(1 − cos θ). It is also the curve generated as the envelope of circles whose centers lie on a fixed circle and which pass through a fixed point on it.

Properties

The cardioid encloses an area of 6πa² and has perimeter 16a. It appears as the caustic formed by light reflected in a circular cup, as the main bulb of the Mandelbrot set, and in the polar patterns of cardioid microphones, which are most sensitive to sound from the front.

Generation

Besides rolling circles, a cardioid arises as the inverse of a parabola with respect to its focus, and as a special case of a limaçon of Pascal.
