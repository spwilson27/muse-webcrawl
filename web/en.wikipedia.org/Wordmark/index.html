Wordmark

A wordmark, or logotype, is a distinct text-only typographic treatment of the name of a company, institution, product or brand used as a logo. Unlike a symbol or emblem, a wordmark relies entirely on lettering — the choice of typeface, letter spacing, case and colour — to be recognisable. Familiar examples include the logos of Coca-Cola, Google, Sony and FedEx.

Design

Designers may use an existing typeface with modifications, or draw custom letterforms, adjusting details such as the FedEx wordmark's hidden arrow formed by the negative space between the E and x. Effective wordmarks remain legible at small sizes, work in a single colour, and convey the personality of the organisation — conservative serifs for a law firm, rounded lowercase letters for a friendly technology brand.

Legal protection

Wordmarks are protected as trademarks. Trademark law distinguishes the standard character mark, which protects the wording itself in any style, from a stylised mark or special form mark, which protects a particular design. A wordmark may be used alone or combined with a symbol; together they form a combination mark. See also word.
