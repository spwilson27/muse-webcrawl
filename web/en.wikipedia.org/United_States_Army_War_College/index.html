United States Army War College

The United States Army War College (USAWC) is a senior service college of the U.S. Army located at Carlisle Barracks in Carlisle, Pennsylvania. It educates senior military officers and civilians for strategic-level leadership, awarding a master's degree in strategic studies.

History

Secretary of War Elihu Root founded the college in 1901 (it opened in Washington, D.C. in 1903) to address lessons of the Spanish–American War. Suspended during the world wars, it was re-established at Carlisle in 1951, where it has remained.

Curriculum

The resident course runs about ten months and covers strategic leadership, national security policy, theater strategy and joint warfighting, culminating in planning exercises. A distance-education program serves additional officers over two years. Graduates include many of the Army's senior generals.

Campus

Carlisle Barracks, dating to 1757, is among the oldest active Army posts. The college also hosts the Strategic Studies Institute and the U.S. Army Heritage and Education Center.
