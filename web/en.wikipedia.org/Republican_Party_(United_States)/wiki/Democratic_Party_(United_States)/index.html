The Democratic Party is one of the two major contemporary political parties in the United States, alongside its main rival, the Republican Party. Founded in 1828, it is the world's oldest active political party. Since the 1930s, the party has generally supported a socially liberal platform, including government regulation of the economy, social welfare programmes, labour unions, civil rights, and environmental protection.

History

The party traces its origins to the Democratic-Republican Party of Thomas Jefferson and James Madison, and took modern form under Andrew Jackson, whose presidency (1829–1837) championed expanded white male suffrage and a coalition of farmers, workers and slaveholders. In the 19th century the party defended slavery and states' rights, fracturing over the issue in 1860. After the Civil War, Democrats dominated the segregated South. Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal (1933 onwards) realigned the party around economic intervention and social insurance, creating a coalition of labour, minorities, intellectuals and Southern whites. Under Lyndon B. Johnson, the party enacted the Civil Rights Act and the Great Society, prompting a long realignment in which the South shifted to the Republicans.

Philosophy and platform

Modern Democrats favour a mixed economy with regulation, progressive taxation, expanded access to healthcare, action on climate change, protection of abortion rights, gun control measures, and a multilateral foreign policy. The party contains a broad range of factions, from centrist "New Democrats" to progressives and democratic socialists, and its electoral coalition is concentrated in urban areas, the coasts and among younger and minority voters.

Organisation

The Democratic National Committee (DNC), founded in 1848, coordinates strategy, fundraising and the Democratic National Convention, where the presidential nominee is chosen through a primary process involving pledged delegates and party leaders. Democratic presidents include Woodrow Wilson, Franklin D. Roosevelt, Harry S. Truman, John F. Kennedy, Lyndon B. Johnson, Jimmy Carter, Bill Clinton, Barack Obama and Joe Biden.
