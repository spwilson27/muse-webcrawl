The Republican Party, also known as the Grand Old Party (GOP), is a major American political party. It is, along with the Democratic Party, one of the two major contemporary parties in the United States.

The elephant is the long-standing symbol of the Republican Party.

The party was founded in 1854 by anti-slavery activists who opposed the Kansas–Nebraska Act, which allowed for the potential expansion of slavery into western territories. The party took its name from republicanism, the dominant ideology of the American Revolution. Abraham Lincoln, the party's first president, led the Union through the American Civil War and issued the Emancipation Proclamation.

History

Republicans dominated national politics for much of the period from 1860 to 1932, advocating protective tariffs, business interests, and sound money. The Great Depression brought Franklin D. Roosevelt's Democratic coalition to power, and the parties' coalitions reshuffled again during the civil rights era, when the Republicans adopted the "Southern strategy" and the once-solidly Democratic South became the party's base. Ronald Reagan's election in 1980 consolidated a coalition of fiscal conservatives, social conservatives, and defence hawks.

Platform and factions

The party generally favours lower taxes, deregulation, free markets, restrictions on immigration, gun rights, and a strong military, and it contains libertarian, populist, and establishment conservative factions. Its electoral coalition draws heavily on rural voters, white evangelical Christians, and business interests.

Presidents

Nineteen Republicans have served as president, including Abraham Lincoln, Theodore Roosevelt, Dwight D. Eisenhower, Richard Nixon, Ronald Reagan, George H. W. Bush, George W. Bush, and Donald Trump.
