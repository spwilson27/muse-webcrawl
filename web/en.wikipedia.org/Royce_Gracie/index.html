Royce Gracie (born 12 December 1966) is a Brazilian retired professional mixed martial artist. A member of the famous Gracie family, he is considered one of the most influential figures in the history of MMA, and is credited with popularising Brazilian jiu-jitsu around the world through his victories in the earliest Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) tournaments.

Early UFC career

Gracie was selected by his family to represent their style at UFC 1 in November 1993. Despite being one of the smallest competitors, he won the eight-man tournament, defeating Art Jimmerson, Ken Shamrock and Gerard Gordeau, all by submission. He repeated the feat at UFC 2 and UFC 4, compiling an 11–0 record in early UFC competition. His success against larger opponents from striking backgrounds demonstrated the effectiveness of grappling and ground fighting, transforming how martial artists trained.

At UFC 3 he withdrew after his quarter-final win over Kimo Leopoldo due to exhaustion and dehydration. His 1995 superfight with Ken Shamrock at UFC 5 ended in a draw after 36 minutes. Gracie later fought in Japan for PRIDE Fighting Championships and K-1, including a famous 90-minute draw with Hidehiko Yoshida's contemporary Kazushi Sakuraba in 2000, a bout that ended when Gracie's corner threw in the towel after his leg was injured.

Later career and legacy

Gracie returned to the UFC in 2006, losing to Matt Hughes at UFC 60. In 2016, at age 49, he defeated Ken Shamrock by TKO in their trilogy bout at Bellator 149. He was the first inductee, along with Shamrock, into the UFC Hall of Fame in 2003.

Beyond competition, Gracie has taught seminars worldwide, appeared in film and television, and is widely regarded as the man who introduced submission grappling to a global mainstream audience, paving the way for modern mixed martial arts.
