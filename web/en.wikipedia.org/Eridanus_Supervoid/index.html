Eridanus Supervoid

The Eridanus Supervoid is an extremely large region of the universe with a much lower density of galaxies than expected, located in the direction of the constellation Eridanus. It has been proposed as an explanation for the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) Cold Spot, an unusually cold and large region of the microwave sky.

Discovery

The Cold Spot was first noticed in data from the Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP) in 2004. Subsequent galaxy surveys in that direction suggested an underdense region — a cosmic void — possibly hundreds of millions of light-years across, which could cool the CMB photons passing through it via the integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect.

Significance

If confirmed as a single structure, the Eridanus Supervoid would be among the largest known structures in the universe, spanning roughly 1.8 billion light-years. However, later studies have debated whether the void is deep and large enough on its own to explain the Cold Spot's temperature decrement, and whether the structure is a single void or an alignment of smaller ones.
