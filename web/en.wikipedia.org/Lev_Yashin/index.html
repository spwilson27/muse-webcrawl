Lev Yashin

Lev Ivanovich Yashin (22 October 1929 – 20 March 1990) was a Soviet football goalkeeper, widely regarded as the greatest goalkeeper of all time. Nicknamed the "Black Spider" for his all-black kit and extraordinary reach, he is the only goalkeeper ever to win the Ballon d'Or (1963) and is credited with saving more than 150 penalty kicks in professional football.

Career

Yashin spent his entire club career, from 1950 to 1970, with Dynamo Moscow, winning five Soviet league titles and three Soviet Cups; he also played ice hockey as a goaltender, winning a Soviet Cup in 1953. For the USSR national team he earned 78 caps, winning gold at the 1956 Melbourne Olympics and the inaugural European Championship in 1960, and playing at four World Cups (1958, 1962, 1966 — when the USSR reached the semi-finals — and as a squad member in 1970). He revolutionised the position by commanding his penalty area, rushing out to meet attackers and organising his defence, rather than remaining on the goal line.

Honours and legacy

Yashin's farewell match in 1971 drew 100,000 spectators to Moscow's Lenin Stadium. FIFA named the World Cup's best-goalkeeper award after him from 1994 to 2010, and he was voted onto the FIFA World Team of the 20th Century. Postage stamps, statues and the Lev Yashin Club for Soviet and Russian goalkeepers commemorate his name.
