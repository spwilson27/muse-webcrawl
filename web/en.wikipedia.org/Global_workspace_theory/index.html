Global workspace theory

Global workspace theory (GWT) is a theory of consciousness developed by cognitive scientist Bernard Baars, first presented in his 1988 book A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness. It proposes that conscious experience arises when information is broadcast from a central "global workspace" to a wide array of specialised unconscious brain processes.

The theatre metaphor

Baars illustrates the theory with a theatre: consciousness is like a spotlight shining on a stage, selecting a small amount of information for attention, while the audience represents the vast number of unconscious processors that receive the broadcast and can respond. Competing unconscious processes vie for access to the stage; the winning content becomes globally available for report, memory and deliberate action.

Neuroscience and influence

Neuroscientists such as Stanislas Dehaene have developed the related "global neuronal workspace" model, associating conscious access with widespread activation and long-range synchrony involving prefrontal and parietal cortex, and with a late "ignition" of activity in perceptual tasks. GWT has influenced research on attention, working memory and disorders of consciousness, and has been implemented in computational and AI architectures; it remains one of several competing scientific theories of consciousness.
