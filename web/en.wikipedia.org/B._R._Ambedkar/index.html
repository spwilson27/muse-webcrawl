B. R. Ambedkar

Born 14 April 1891, Mhow, British India
Died 6 December 1956 (aged 65), Delhi, India
Known for Architect of the Constitution of India; campaign against caste discrimination

Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (1891–1956) was an Indian jurist, economist, social reformer, and political leader who headed the committee that drafted the Constitution of India. A lifelong campaigner against the caste system and the discrimination faced by Dalits ("untouchables"), he is regarded as one of the most influential figures in modern Indian history.

Early life and education

Born into a Mahar family treated as untouchable, Ambedkar overcame discrimination to become one of the first Dalits to obtain a university education in India. With scholarships he studied at Columbia University in New York, earning a PhD in economics, and at the London School of Economics, also qualifying as a barrister at Gray's Inn.

Activism and politics

Ambedkar led movements for Dalit rights, including the Mahad Satyagraha of 1927 asserting the right to draw water from a public tank, and campaigned for separate political representation for the Depressed Classes, an issue that brought him into conflict with Mahatma Gandhi and led to the Poona Pact of 1932. He founded political parties and newspapers championing the oppressed.

Constitution and later life

After independence, Ambedkar served as India's first Minister of Law and Justice and chaired the Drafting Committee of the Constitution, adopted in 1950, which abolished untouchability and guaranteed fundamental rights. In 1956, disillusioned with Hinduism's caste order, he converted to Buddhism with hundreds of thousands of followers, sparking the Dalit Buddhist movement. He died the same year and was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna in 1990.
