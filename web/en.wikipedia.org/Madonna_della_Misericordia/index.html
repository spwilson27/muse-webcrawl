Madonna della Misericordia

The Madonna della Misericordia, in English the Virgin of Mercy, is a Marian devotional image type in Roman Catholic art, especially popular in Italy from the 13th to the 16th centuries. It depicts the Virgin Mary standing with her cloak spread wide, sheltering beneath it a group of praying supplicants — often members of a confraternity, a religious order, a family, or the population of a town.

Iconography

The image expresses the idea of Mary's protective intercession: the faithful gather literally under her mantle. The supplicants are usually painted much smaller than the Virgin, in keeping with medieval conventions of hierarchical scale, and angels sometimes hold the edges of the cloak. The theme is related to the sheltering-cloak motif found in earlier Byzantine and Cistercian imagery and to the feast of Our Lady of Mercy.

Famous examples

The most celebrated version is Piero della Francesca's Madonna della Misericordia, the central panel of the polyptych commissioned in 1445 by the Compagnia di Santa Maria della Misericordia in Sansepolcro, in which the kneeling confraternity members shelter beneath the Virgin's majestically opened cloak. Other notable examples include works by Domenico Ghirlandaio in the Ognissanti, Florence, and by Enguerrand Quarton, whose Virgin of Mercy (1452) was painted for the Cadard family.

Devotion

Confraternities of the Misericordia, devoted to works of mercy such as burying the dead and caring for the sick, spread the image widely, and it remains a recognised symbol of charitable brotherhoods in Italy.
