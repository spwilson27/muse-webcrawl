Gwynedd (Welsh pronunciation: [ˈɡwɪnɛð]) is a county in the north-west of Wales. It was one of the historic kingdoms of Wales and, in its modern form, was created as a county in 1974 and reconstituted as a principal area in 1996. It is named after the medieval Kingdom of Gwynedd.

Geography

Gwynedd contains most of Eryri (Snowdonia) National Park, including Yr Wyddfa (Snowdon), the highest mountain in Wales. Its coastline runs along the Irish Sea and Cardigan Bay, and includes the Llŷn Peninsula. Principal towns include Bangor, Caernarfon, Porthmadog, Dolgellau and Pwllheli; Bangor is home to one of Wales's oldest universities.

Language

Gwynedd has the highest proportion of Welsh speakers of any Welsh county, with a majority of residents able to speak the language. The council operates bilingual services and Welsh-medium education is widespread.

History

The medieval kingdom was ruled by figures such as Llywelyn the Great and Llywelyn ap Gruffudd, the last native Prince of Wales, killed in 1282. Edward I's castles at Caernarfon, Conwy, Harlech and Beaumaris are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
