Milford Track

Length53.5 km (33.2 mi)
LocationFiordland National Park, New Zealand
Highest pointMackinnon Pass (1,154 m)
DesignationNew Zealand Great Walk

The Milford Track is a hiking route in Fiordland National Park, New Zealand, widely regarded as one of the finest walks in the world. It runs 53.5 km from the head of Lake Te Anau to Sandfly Point near Milford Sound, traversing rainforest valleys, the alpine Mackinnon Pass and passing the Sutherland Falls, among the tallest waterfalls in the world.

History

Māori used the valley routes for centuries to reach pounamu (greenstone) sources. In 1888 Quintin McKinnon and Ernest Mitchell found the pass now named Mackinnon Pass, opening the overland route to Milford Sound, and McKinnon became the first guide. A 1900 article in the London Spectator calling it "the finest walk in the world" established its fame.

Walking the track

During the Great Walks season (late October to April) the track is strictly regulated: walking is one-way only, numbers are limited by hut bookings, and there is no camping. Independent walkers stay in Department of Conservation huts while guided groups use separate private lodges. Outside the season facilities are reduced and alpine hazards greater. The four-day walk ends with a boat trip from Sandfly Point to Milford Sound.
