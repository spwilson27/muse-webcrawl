Culture of Saint Kitts and Nevis

The culture of Saint Kitts and Nevis, a two-island federation in the eastern Caribbean, blends African, British, and French influences shaped by the islands' history of sugar plantation agriculture and colonial rule. English is the official language, with a local Creole spoken informally.

Festivals

The country's premier celebration is the St. Kitts Carnival, known as "Sugar Mas," held from late December into early January, featuring calypso competitions, street parades, and the election of a Carnival Queen. The St. Kitts Music Festival, held each June, draws international performers. Nevis celebrates Culturama in late July and early August, commemorating emancipation with folk performances, pageants, and traditional food.

Music and folklore

Traditional music includes calypso, soca, and string band music. Folklore preserves masquerade troupes whose dances combine African, European, and indigenous elements; the moko jumbie stilt dancers are a familiar sight at festivals. Bull-playing and other Christmas-season folk theatre historically formed part of local celebrations.

Cuisine and sport

Local dishes feature goat water (a spicy goat stew, considered the national dish), saltfish, spiny lobster, johnnycakes, and tropical fruits. Cricket is the most popular sport, and the islands have produced international players; football (soccer) is also widely played.
