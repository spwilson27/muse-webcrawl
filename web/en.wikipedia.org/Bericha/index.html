Bericha

Bericha (also spelled berchha or barcha) is a traditional polearm of the Indian subcontinent. It consists of a long wooden shaft tipped with a broad spearhead, and was historically used by infantry and cavalry in parts of northern and central India.

Description

The bericha is characterised by its long shaft, often of bamboo or hardwood, and a leaf-shaped or lanceolate iron blade. Its length gave foot soldiers reach against mounted opponents, and it could be used for thrusting as well as for parrying. Regional variants differed in blade profile and in the fittings used where the head meets the shaft.

Use

Berichas appear in accounts of warfare in medieval and early modern India, where spears of various forms were among the most common weapons of both regular troops and militia. Long spears were valued because they were cheap to produce, required comparatively little training, and were effective in formation against cavalry.
