Lake Texcoco

Lake Texcoco (Spanish: Lago de Texcoco) was a natural lake in the Valley of Mexico, part of a system of interconnected lakes — Zumpango, Xaltocan, Xochimilco, and Chalco — that once covered much of the valley floor. The Aztec capital Tenochtitlan stood on an island in its western waters; today the lake bed underlies much of Mexico City and the municipalities east of it.

History

During the last glacial period the combined lake system was far deeper than in historical times. In the Aztec era, Lake Texcoco was the lowest and saltiest of the chain; the Mexica and their neighbours built dikes — most famously the 16-km dike of Nezahualcoyotl (c. 1449) — to separate its brackish water from the fresher lakes around Tenochtitlan and to support chinampa agriculture. After the Spanish conquest of 1521, colonial authorities, fearing floods, embarked on centuries of drainage works; by the mid-20th century the lake had largely vanished, leaving a vast seasonally flooded salt flat, the ex-Lago de Texcoco.

Modern era

The dry lake bed has been the site of repeated controversy. Mexico City's principal airport was planned for the site under President Enrique Peña Nieto, but the project was cancelled by referendum in 2018 under Andrés Manuel López Obrador and instead converted into the Lake Texcoco Ecological Park, one of the largest urban ecological restoration projects in the world, restoring wetlands for migratory birds. The lake bed's soft, waterlogged clays also contributed to the severe subsidence of Mexico City and amplified ground shaking in the 1985 and 2017 earthquakes.
