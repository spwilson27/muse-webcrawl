Pacific Decadal Oscillation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO) is a robust, recurring pattern of ocean–atmosphere climate variability centred over the mid-latitude Pacific basin. The PDO is detected as warm or cool surface waters in the Pacific Ocean north of about 20°N, and it shifts between "warm" and "cool" phases on timescales of roughly 20 to 30 years. It was named by Steven Hare in 1996 while studying connections between Alaskan salmon production and Pacific climate.

Phases and pattern

During a warm (positive) phase, the west Pacific becomes cooler and part of the eastern North Pacific warms; during a cool (negative) phase the opposite pattern occurs. Major twentieth-century regime shifts are generally dated to about 1925, 1947, and 1977, with the warm phase after 1977 associated with warmer coastal waters off Alaska and the western Americas and with pronounced effects on North American climate. The PDO index is defined as the leading principal component of North Pacific sea surface temperature anomalies.

Impacts

PDO phases modulate winter temperature and precipitation over North America — warm phases are typically linked to warmer, drier conditions in the Pacific Northwest and wetter conditions in the southwestern United States, with roughly the reverse in cool phases. Marine ecosystems track the oscillation: salmon catches in Alaska and sardine and anchovy regimes along the Pacific coasts of the Americas have historically risen and fallen with PDO phases, which is what led fisheries scientists to identify the pattern. The PDO also interacts with the shorter-lived El Niño–Southern Oscillation; when the two are in phase their combined effects on climate tend to be amplified.

Mechanisms

No single mechanism explains the PDO. Research implicates a combination of tropical Pacific forcing via the "atmospheric bridge", stochastic mid-latitude wind variability affecting the Aleutian Low, ocean dynamics including Rossby waves and the Kuroshio–Oyashio system, and the "reemergence" of winter temperature anomalies stored below the summer mixed layer. Because several processes contribute, the PDO's predictability beyond a few years is limited, and whether it is best regarded as a genuine oscillation or as the integrated response of the ocean to varied forcing remains debated.
