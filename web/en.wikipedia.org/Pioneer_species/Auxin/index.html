Auxin

Auxins are a class of plant hormones (phytohormones) that play a cardinal role in the coordination of growth and behavioural processes in plant life cycles. The best-known natural auxin is indole-3-acetic acid (IAA). Auxins are essential for plant body development, influencing cell division, elongation and differentiation.

Discovery

Charles Darwin and his son Francis observed in the 1880s that the tips of grass coleoptiles bend toward light, suggesting a transmitted signal. Frits Went isolated the growth-promoting substance in 1926 and named it auxin, from the Greek auxein, "to grow".

Functions

Auxin promotes cell elongation in shoots while inhibiting it in roots at higher concentrations; it regulates apical dominance, phototropism and gravitropism, initiates root formation and vascular tissue differentiation, and influences fruit development. Synthetic auxins such as 2,4-D are used as herbicides and in tissue culture.
