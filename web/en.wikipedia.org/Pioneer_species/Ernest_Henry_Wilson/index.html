Ernest Henry Wilson

Ernest Henry Wilson (15 February 1876 – 15 October 1930), known as E. H. Wilson, was an English plant explorer and collector who introduced large numbers of East Asian plants to Western horticulture. He undertook expeditions to China and Japan, first for the nursery firm Veitch and later for Harvard University's Arnold Arboretum.

Career

Wilson's first expedition to China (1899–1902) sought the handkerchief tree (Davidia involucrata); he went on to make several further collecting journeys, bringing back thousands of specimens and seeds, including lilies, rhododendrons, cherries and the kiwifruit's relatives. On a 1910 expedition he was seriously injured by a rockfall, leaving him with a limp he called his "lily limp".

Legacy

Many garden plants bear his name, and his photographs and diaries remain important records of early twentieth-century China. He died with his wife in a car accident in Worcester, Massachusetts, in 1930.
