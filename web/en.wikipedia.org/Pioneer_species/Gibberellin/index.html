Gibberellin

Gibberellins (GAs) are a large family of plant hormones, diterpenoid acids that regulate growth and influence developmental processes including stem elongation, germination, dormancy, flowering and fruit development.

Discovery

Japanese scientists studying the "foolish seedling" disease of rice — in which infected plants grew excessively tall and pale — traced the effect to a substance secreted by the fungus Gibberella fujikuroi. Eiichi Kurosawa demonstrated the effect in 1926, and gibberellic acid (GA3) was isolated and characterised in the following decades.

Role in agriculture

The semi-dwarf wheat and rice varieties of the Green Revolution carry mutations in gibberellin signalling or synthesis that shorten stems and resist lodging. Exogenous gibberellin is applied commercially to increase fruit size in seedless grapes and to promote uniform germination in brewing barley.
