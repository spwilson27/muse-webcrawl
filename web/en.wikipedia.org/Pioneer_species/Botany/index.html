Botany

Botany, also called plant science or phytology, is the branch of biology that studies plants, including their structure, properties, biochemical processes, classification and economic importance. A botanist or plant scientist is a scientist who specialises in this field.

Scope

Botany covers a wide range of disciplines, from plant anatomy and physiology to genetics, ecology, taxonomy and paleobotany. Its subjects range from microscopic algae and fungi-like organisms to the largest trees, and from single-celled forms to complex ecosystems.

History

The study of plants is ancient: Theophrastus, a pupil of Aristotle, wrote foundational works on plant classification in the fourth century BC. Carl Linnaeus established modern botanical nomenclature in the eighteenth century, and subsequent work in heredity, beginning with Gregor Mendel's pea experiments, made botany central to the development of genetics.

Importance

Plants supply food, oxygen, medicine, fuel and materials; botanical research underpins agriculture, horticulture, forestry and conservation biology.
