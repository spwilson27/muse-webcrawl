Ethylene

Ethylene (IUPAC name ethene) is a hydrocarbon gas that also acts as a plant hormone. It is produced by nearly all plant tissues and is best known for promoting the ripening of fruit, but it also influences germination, flowering, leaf abscission and responses to stress and wounding.

Discovery

In the nineteenth century it was noticed that illuminating gas leaking from street lamps caused plants to shed leaves. In 1901 Dimitry Neljubow identified ethylene as the active component. Its role in fruit ripening was established in the 1930s, when it was shown that ripening fruit emit the gas and that exposed fruit ripen faster.

Uses

Commercial fruit operations use controlled ethylene exposure to ripen bananas, tomatoes and other climacteric fruit uniformly after shipping. Conversely, storage technologies scrub ethylene or block its perception (for example with 1-methylcyclopropene) to delay ripening and extend shelf life.
