Herbarium

A herbarium is a collection of preserved plant specimens and associated data used for scientific study. Specimens — typically pressed, dried plants mounted on sheets of paper — are arranged systematically and serve as reference material for taxonomy, ecology and conservation.

History

The practice of pressing and mounting plants began in sixteenth-century Italy; Luca Ghini is credited with creating the first herbarium around 1544. Herbaria grew with botanical exploration, and major collections now hold millions of specimens; the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle in Paris and the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, hold among the largest.

Uses

Herbarium specimens anchor plant names (type specimens), document species distributions and flowering times, and increasingly provide DNA and chemical samples. Digitisation projects have made images and label data from many herbaria available online.
