Genetically modified crops

Genetically modified crops (GM crops) are plants used in agriculture whose DNA has been altered using genetic engineering methods. Commercialised traits include herbicide tolerance, insect resistance (notably Bt crops producing proteins from Bacillus thuringiensis), virus resistance and improved nutritional content.

History

The first genetically modified plant was produced in 1983, and the first commercial GM food crop, the Flavr Savr tomato, was approved in 1994. Plantings expanded rapidly, led by soybean, maize, cotton and canola; the largest producers include the United States, Brazil, Argentina, Canada and India.

Debate

GM crops are the subject of continuing public and scientific debate concerning food safety, environmental effects such as gene flow and effects on non-target organisms, corporate control of seed, and regulation. Major scientific bodies have concluded that approved GM foods are no riskier than conventional counterparts, while critics press for stricter labelling and assessment.
