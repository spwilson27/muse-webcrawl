Green Revolution

The Green Revolution was a period of technology transfer and research initiatives, beginning in the 1940s and peaking in the late 1960s, that dramatically increased agricultural production worldwide, particularly in the developing world. It centred on high-yielding, disease-resistant, semi-dwarf varieties of wheat and rice combined with synthetic fertilisers, pesticides and expanded irrigation.

Key figures and results

Norman Borlaug, working in Mexico with support from the Rockefeller Foundation, developed dwarf wheat varieties that helped Mexico achieve self-sufficiency and were later adopted in India and Pakistan, averting famine for hundreds of millions; Borlaug received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1970. The International Rice Research Institute's IR8 rice similarly transformed Asian production.

Criticism

Critics point to environmental costs — water depletion, soil degradation and chemical pollution — and to social effects, including increased inequality and dependence on purchased inputs. Later advocates have called for a more sustainable "second Green Revolution".
