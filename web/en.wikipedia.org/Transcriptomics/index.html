Transcriptomics

A heatmap visualising differential gene expression, a common output of transcriptomic studies.

Transcriptomics is the study of the transcriptome—the complete set of RNA transcripts produced by the genome of an organism, cell, or tissue under specific circumstances or in a specific cell—using high-throughput methods. Unlike the genome, which is largely fixed for a given cell line, the transcriptome varies with environmental conditions, developmental stage, and disease state, making it a key layer in understanding gene expression and regulation.

Methods

Microarrays

DNA microarrays, developed in the 1990s, measure the expression of pre-determined genes by hybridising labelled RNA-derived cDNA to probes fixed on a chip. They enabled the first genome-wide expression studies but can only detect known transcripts.

RNA sequencing

RNA-Seq uses next-generation sequencing of cDNA to count and characterise all RNA species present, including novel transcripts, splice variants, and non-coding RNAs. Since about 2008 it has largely superseded microarrays. Typical workflows involve RNA extraction, library preparation (often with poly-A selection or ribosomal RNA depletion), sequencing, read alignment or pseudo-alignment to a reference, quantification, and statistical testing for differential expression with tools such as DESeq2 and edgeR.

Single-cell transcriptomics

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-Seq) profiles gene expression in individual cells, revealing cell types and states hidden in bulk averages. Droplet-based platforms can process tens of thousands of cells per run. Related spatial transcriptomics methods preserve the tissue location of transcripts.

Applications

Transcriptomics is used to classify cancers and identify biomarkers, trace developmental trajectories, understand host–pathogen interactions, and assess drug responses. Large public repositories such as the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), the Sequence Read Archive, and the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project make millions of transcriptomic profiles available for reuse.
