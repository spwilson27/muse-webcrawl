Salary cap

In professional sports, a salary cap (or wage cap) is an agreement or rule that places a limit on the amount of money that a team can spend on players' salaries. It exists as a per-player limit or a total limit for the team's roster, or both. Several sports leagues have implemented salary caps, using them to keep overall costs down and to maintain a competitive balance by restricting richer clubs from entrenching dominance by signing many more top players than their rivals.

Hard and soft caps

Salary caps can be a major issue in negotiations between league management and players' unions, and were the focus of several sports strikes and lockouts. A hard cap, as in the NFL, cannot be exceeded for any reason; a soft cap, as in the NBA, contains exceptions that allow teams to exceed it under defined conditions, while a luxury tax system, as in Major League Baseball, penalises overspending without prohibiting it. A salary floor may also require a minimum team payroll.

Adoption

The NBA introduced the first modern salary cap in the 1984–85 season. The NFL adopted a hard cap in 1994, and the NHL adopted a cap after the 2004–05 lockout cancelled an entire season. Caps are rare in European football, which instead uses financial fair play rules limiting losses, though leagues such as Spain's La Liga enforce squad cost limits.
