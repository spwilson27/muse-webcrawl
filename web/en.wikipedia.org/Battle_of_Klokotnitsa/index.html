The Battle of Klokotnitsa (Bulgarian: Битка при Клокотница) was fought on 9 March 1230 between the armies of the Second Bulgarian Empire under Tsar Ivan Asen II and the Despotate of Epirus under Theodore Komnenos Doukas, near the village of Klokotnitsa on the Maritsa River in modern Bulgaria. The Bulgarian victory destroyed Epirote power and made Bulgaria the dominant state in the Balkans.

Background

Theodore of Epirus, having taken Thessalonica and had himself crowned emperor, broke his peace treaty with Bulgaria and invaded in early 1230 with a large army including western mercenaries. According to tradition, Ivan Asen II carried the broken treaty fixed to a spear as his standard as he marched to meet the invaders.

The battle

The Bulgarian army, smaller but including some 1,000 Cuman horsemen, engaged Theodore's host near Klokotnitsa. The Epirote army was crushed; Theodore himself was captured, along with his family and much of his court, and was later blinded after being implicated in a conspiracy. Ivan Asen treated the common prisoners generously, releasing most of the rank and file, which won him the loyalty of the territories he then occupied.

Aftermath

Bulgaria annexed most of Theodore's lands in Thrace, Macedonia and Albania, reaching its greatest medieval extent, with coasts on the Black, Aegean and Adriatic Seas. In gratitude Ivan Asen II built the Church of the Holy Forty Martyrs in his capital Tarnovo, whose inscription commemorates the victory on the feast day of the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste.
