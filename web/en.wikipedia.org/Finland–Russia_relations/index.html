Finland–Russia relations

Finland–Russia relations are the bilateral relations between Finland and Russia. The two countries share a land border of about 1,340 kilometres (830 miles), the longest of any European Union member state with Russia. Finland was part of the Russian Empire as the autonomous Grand Duchy of Finland from 1809 until declaring independence in 1917.

History

Following independence and a civil war in 1918, relations with Soviet Russia were tense. The Soviet Union attacked Finland in the Winter War of 1939–40, and the countries fought again in the Continuation War (1941–44), after which Finland ceded territory including much of Karelia but preserved its independence. During the Cold War Finland maintained neutrality under the 1948 Finno-Soviet Treaty of Friendship, Cooperation and Mutual Assistance — a policy of accommodation sometimes called "Finlandization" — while building strong trade ties with the USSR.

Post-Cold War

Finland joined the European Union in 1995, and trade and cross-border tourism with post-Soviet Russia grew substantially. Relations deteriorated sharply after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Finland abandoned military non-alignment and joined NATO in April 2023, doubling the alliance's border with Russia. In late 2023 Finland closed its entire eastern border, accusing Russia of funnelling asylum seekers to the frontier in a hybrid operation; trade and travel between the two countries largely ceased.
