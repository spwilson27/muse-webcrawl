Mona Lisa Overdrive


Author: William Gibson
Published: 1988
Genre: Science fiction, cyberpunk
Series: Sprawl trilogy (book 3)
Preceded by: Count Zero

Mona Lisa Overdrive is a science fiction novel by American-Canadian writer William Gibson, published in 1988. It is the final novel of the Sprawl trilogy, following Neuromancer (1984) and Count Zero (1986), and it takes place eight years after the events of the second book. The novel was a finalist for the Hugo Award for Best Novel.

Plot

The story interweaves several plot threads: Mona, a young runaway and sex worker in Cleveland who is surgically altered to resemble the global simstim superstar Angie Mitchell, and becomes entangled in a plot surrounding her; Angie herself, who can access cyberspace directly because of biochemical alterations made by her father; Kumiko, the daughter of a Yakuza boss, sent to London for safety with a holographic companion; and Slick Henry, an ex-convict artist who builds enormous kinetic sculptures in a derelict factory called the Factory. The threads converge around the "aleph", a portable biochip holding a vast virtual world inhabited by the Count (Bobby Newmark) and by entities descended from the AIs of the earlier novels.

Themes and reception

The novel develops Gibson's themes of artificial intelligence evolving beyond human comprehension, celebrity, corporate power, and the blurring of virtual and physical worlds. Reviewers generally regarded it as a satisfying, more character-driven conclusion to the trilogy.
