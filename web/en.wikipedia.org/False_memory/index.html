False memory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A false memory is a phenomenon in which someone recalls something that did not happen or recalls it differently from the way it actually happened. Suggestibility, activation of associated information, incorporation of contextual details from other sources, and misattribution of the source of one's memories are some of the mechanisms that can lead to false memories.

Research

Psychologist Elizabeth Loftus has conducted extensive research on false memories beginning in the 1970s. In the "lost in the mall" studies, about a quarter of participants came to remember a wholly fictitious childhood event — being lost in a shopping mall — after it was suggested by family members and interviewers. In the Deese–Roediger–McDermott (DRM) paradigm, participants confidently recall words that were never presented but are semantically associated with the studied list.

Post-event misinformation is another robust source of distortion: in Loftus's classic experiments, the wording of a question about a car accident ("smashed" versus "hit") altered witnesses' speed estimates and even their later memory of whether broken glass had been present.

False memory syndrome and recovered memories

During the 1980s and 1990s, some therapeutic practices aimed at recovering supposedly repressed memories of childhood abuse were implicated in producing vivid but false recollections, with devastating consequences for accused families. The controversy led to greater scrutiny of suggestive interviewing techniques and influenced standards for forensic interviewing of children and eyewitnesses.

Related phenomena

Confabulation, in which a person produces fabricated or distorted memories without intending to deceive, occurs in several neurological conditions. The "Mandela effect" is a popular term for widely shared false memories of public events. False memories are a normal feature of healthy cognition — memory is reconstructive rather than a literal recording — and they underscore the unreliability of uncorroborated eyewitness testimony.
