Ulysses S. Grant (born Hiram Ulysses Grant; 1822–1885) was an American military officer and statesman who served as the 18th president of the United States from 1869 to 1877. As commanding general of the United States Army during the American Civil War, he led the Union to victory over the Confederacy in 1865.

Early life and the Civil War

Grant graduated from West Point in 1843 and served with distinction in the Mexican–American War. He resigned from the army in 1854 and struggled in civilian life — working in farming and real estate and clerking in his father's leather shop in Galena, Illinois — until the Civil War began. Rejoining the army, he won early victories at Forts Henry and Donelson (where his demand for "unconditional surrender" made his initials famous), at Shiloh, and in the Vicksburg campaign of 1863, which split the Confederacy along the Mississippi.

Appointed general-in-chief in March 1864, Grant pursued a strategy of coordinated offensives and relentless attrition in the Overland Campaign and the Siege of Petersburg, while his subordinates Sherman and Sheridan devastated the Shenandoah Valley and the Deep South. He accepted Robert E. Lee's surrender at Appomattox Court House on 9 April 1865, offering generous terms.

Presidency

Elected president in 1868 and re-elected in 1872, Grant worked to protect the civil rights of African Americans during Reconstruction, using federal force against the Ku Klux Klan and prosecuting civil rights cases through the new Department of Justice. His administration also saw financial scandals involving associates — notably the Whiskey Ring and Crédit Mobilier — which tarnished his reputation despite his personal honesty. He stabilised the economy after the Panic of 1873's onset and pursued a peace policy toward Native Americans, though the era culminated in wars on the Plains.

Later life and legacy

After a two-year world tour, Grant lost his fortune to a fraudulent investment firm in 1884. Dying of throat cancer, he completed his Personal Memoirs — published by Mark Twain and widely regarded as among the finest military memoirs in English — days before his death on 23 July 1885, securing his family's finances. He is interred in Grant's Tomb in New York City. Historical assessments of Grant, long diminished by Lost Cause narratives, have risen sharply in recent decades.
