Gun politics in the United States

Debate over firearms regulation is a durable feature of American politics.

Gun politics in the United States is characterised by an enduring two-sided debate between advocates of gun control and advocates of gun rights. The United States has the highest rate of civilian firearm ownership in the world, and the Second Amendment — "A well regulated Militia, being necessary to the security of a free State, the right of the people to keep and bear Arms, shall not be infringed" — anchors the constitutional argument.

Legal framework

Federal law includes the National Firearms Act (1934), the Gun Control Act (1968), the Brady Act (1993), which introduced background checks, and the short-lived Federal Assault Weapons Ban (1994–2004). In District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) the Supreme Court held that the Second Amendment protects an individual right to possess a handgun in the home; New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen (2022) extended this to public carry and required regulations to be consistent with the nation's historical tradition. State laws vary widely, from permissive constitutional-carry regimes to strict licensing systems.

Organisations and debate

The National Rifle Association is the most prominent gun-rights organisation, while groups such as Everytown for Gun Safety and the Brady Campaign advocate stricter controls. Public opinion generally favours measures such as universal background checks while remaining divided on broader bans.
