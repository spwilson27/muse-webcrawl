Intelligence cycle

Intelligence cycle
Stages: Planning, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination, feedback
Used by: Intelligence agencies and analysts

The intelligence cycle is an idealized model of how intelligence is processed in civilian and military intelligence agencies and in law enforcement. It describes a repeating sequence of activities by which raw information is converted into finished intelligence for decision-makers.

Stages

In the classic model, the stages are: planning and direction (identifying what decision-makers need to know), collection (gathering data from human sources, signals interception, imagery, and open sources), processing (converting raw material into usable form, such as decryption or translation), analysis and production (evaluating and integrating material into assessments), and dissemination (delivering the product to consumers), whose feedback restarts the cycle.

Criticism

Scholars and practitioners criticise the cycle as too linear: in practice, stages overlap, analysts interact directly with collectors and consumers, and requirements often change mid-course. Alternative models portray intelligence as a network or a continuous targeting-and-exploitation process rather than a circle.

Use

Despite its limitations, the cycle remains the standard teaching model in intelligence training and is also applied in business competitive intelligence. See also Espionage and Intelligence agency.
