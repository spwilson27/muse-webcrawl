InheritanceAutosomal dominant
CauseGermline mutation of TP53 (chromosome 17p13.1)
OnsetOften childhood or young adulthood

Li–Fraumeni syndrome (LFS) is a rare inherited disorder that greatly increases the risk of developing several types of cancer, particularly in children and young adults. It is caused in most cases by a germline mutation in the TP53 tumour suppressor gene, which encodes the p53 protein sometimes called "the guardian of the genome".

Clinical features

The cancers most closely associated with LFS are soft-tissue sarcomas, osteosarcoma, premenopausal breast cancer, brain tumours, adrenocortical carcinoma and leukaemia — often remembered by the acronym SBLA. Lifetime cancer risk approaches 70% for men and 90% or more for women, and many patients develop multiple primary cancers.

Diagnosis and management

The syndrome was described in 1969 by Frederick Pei Li and Joseph F. Fraumeni Jr. Diagnosis combines family-history criteria (classic LFS and the broader Chompret criteria) with genetic testing for TP53. Because ionising radiation may induce further cancers, management emphasises intensive surveillance — including annual whole-body MRI under the "Toronto protocol" — and avoidance of radiotherapy where alternatives exist.
