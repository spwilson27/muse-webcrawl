Misery (novel)

AuthorStephen King
PublishedJune 8, 1987 (Viking Press)
GenrePsychological horror / thriller
Pages370 (first edition)

Misery is a psychological horror novel by American author Stephen King, published in 1987. It tells the story of romance novelist Paul Sheldon, who is rescued from a car crash by Annie Wilkes, a former nurse who describes herself as his "number one fan." When Annie discovers that Paul has killed off her beloved character Misery Chastain in his latest book, she holds him captive in her remote Colorado home and forces him to write a new novel resurrecting the character, subjecting him to psychological torment and escalating violence as his attempts at escape repeatedly fail.

Background

King has said the novel was partly a reaction to the hostile reception some fans and critics gave his fantasy novel The Eyes of the Dragon, and an allegory of his own struggle with drug and alcohol addiction—Annie representing the addiction that both nurtured and imprisoned him. Misery won the first Bram Stoker Award for Best Novel (tying with Robert McCammon's Swan Song) and was a bestseller.

Adaptations

The 1990 film adaptation, directed by Rob Reiner and written by William Goldman, starred James Caan as Paul and Kathy Bates as Annie; Bates won the Academy Award for Best Actress for the role, making it the only Oscar-winning performance in a film based on a King novel. A stage adaptation by Simon Moore premiered in 1992, and a Broadway production written by Goldman and directed by Will Frears, starring Bruce Willis and Laurie Metcalf, opened in 2015.
