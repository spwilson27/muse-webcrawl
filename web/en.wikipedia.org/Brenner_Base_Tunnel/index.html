Brenner Base Tunnel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Inside the exploratory tunnel

The Brenner Base Tunnel (German: Brennerbasistunnel; Italian: Galleria di base del Brennero) is a planned 55-kilometre-long (34 mi) railway tunnel through the base of the Eastern Alps beneath the Brenner Pass, linking Innsbruck in Austria with Fortezza in Italy. Including connections to the existing Innsbruck bypass, the tunnel system totals about 64 km, which would make it the longest rail tunnel in the world on completion, surpassing the Gotthard Base Tunnel.

Project

The tunnel forms the centrepiece of the Berlin–Palermo TEN-T corridor (Scandinavian–Mediterranean Corridor) and is intended to shift trans-Alpine freight from the congested Brenner motorway to rail. It consists of two single-track tubes 70 metres apart, connected by cross passages, with an exploratory tunnel below them used for geological investigation, drainage and, later, maintenance. Trains are expected to run at up to 250 km/h for passenger services, cutting the Innsbruck–Fortezza journey from about 80 minutes to about 25 minutes.

Construction

The project is managed by the Austrian-Italian company BBT SE and co-financed by the European Union. Construction of the main works began in the 2010s following completion of the exploratory tunnel sections; breakthrough and completion have been scheduled for the early 2030s, with opening planned around 2032. Together with upgraded approach lines in the Inn valley and in Italy, the tunnel will create a largely flat, high-capacity route across the Alps.
