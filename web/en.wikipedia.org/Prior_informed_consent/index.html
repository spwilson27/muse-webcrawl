Prior informed consent

Prior informed consent (PIC) is a legal process in which a person, community, or state is fully informed about an activity and given the opportunity to approve or decline it before it begins. The concept is used in environmental law, research ethics, medicine, and Indigenous rights.

In international environmental law

The Rotterdam Convention (1998) establishes a prior informed consent procedure for certain hazardous chemicals and pesticides in international trade, requiring exporting countries to obtain explicit consent from importing countries. The Basel Convention applies similar procedures to transboundary movements of hazardous waste, and the Nagoya Protocol requires PIC for access to genetic resources.

Free, prior and informed consent

The stronger standard of free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) is a right of Indigenous peoples, affirmed in the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007), to give or withhold consent to projects affecting their lands and resources.
