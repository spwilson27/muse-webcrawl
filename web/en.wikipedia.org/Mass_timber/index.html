Mass timber

Products: Cross-laminated timber (CLT), glulam, laminated veneer lumber, dowel-laminated timber
Uses: Mid- and high-rise buildings, schools, offices
Benefits: Carbon storage, prefabrication, speed

Mass timber is a category of engineered wood products made by bonding layers of lumber or veneers into large structural panels, beams and columns strong enough to serve as primary structure in large buildings.

Products

Cross-laminated timber (CLT) stacks boards in perpendicular layers; glued laminated timber (glulam) makes beams and columns; other products include nail- and dowel-laminated timber and laminated veneer lumber.

Advantages and challenges

Advocates cite stored biogenic carbon, lower embodied emissions than concrete and steel, off-site prefabrication and faster, quieter construction. Notable projects include Mjøstårnet in Norway and Ascent in Milwaukee. Challenges include fire performance regulation, moisture management, forestry sustainability and building-code limits, though codes increasingly permit tall wood buildings.
