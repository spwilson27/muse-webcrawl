Curvature form

In differential geometry, the curvature form describes the curvature of a connection on a principal bundle or vector bundle. Introduced in the framework of Élie Cartan, it is a Lie algebra-valued 2-form that measures the failure of covariant derivatives to commute and underlies the modern formulation of gauge theory.

Curvature form
Field: Differential geometry
Type: Lie algebra-valued 2-form
Key relation: Bianchi identity dΩ = [Ω, ω] − related structure equations
Applications: Riemannian geometry, Yang–Mills theory

Definition

Given a connection form ω on a principal G-bundle, the curvature form Ω is defined by Cartan's second structure equation, Ω = dω + ½[ω, ω], where d is the exterior derivative and the bracket is the Lie bracket combined with the wedge product. For a vector bundle with connection matrix A, the curvature is F = dA + A∧A.

Properties and applications

The curvature form satisfies the Bianchi identity, DΩ = 0, where D is the exterior covariant derivative. Applied to the Levi-Civita connection of a Riemannian manifold it yields the Riemann curvature tensor. In physics, the same construction gives the field strength tensor of Yang–Mills gauge fields, and Chern–Weil theory builds characteristic classes such as Chern and Pontryagin classes from invariant polynomials in the curvature form.
