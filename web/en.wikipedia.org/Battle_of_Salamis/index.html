The Battle of Salamis was a naval battle fought in September 480 BC between an alliance of Greek city-states and the Achaemenid Empire of Persia, in the straits between the island of Salamis and the mainland near Athens. It was the turning point of Xerxes I's invasion of Greece, the second Persian invasion during the Greco-Persian Wars.

Background

After the Greek defeat at Thermopylae and the naval standoff at Artemisium, Athens was evacuated — its population ferried to Salamis — and the Persians occupied and burned the city. The allied Greek fleet, about 370 triremes commanded nominally by the Spartan Eurybiades but guided strategically by the Athenian Themistocles, gathered in the Salamis straits. Themistocles argued that fighting in the narrow channel would negate the Persians' numerical superiority, and — according to tradition — sent a secret message to Xerxes falsely claiming the Greeks were about to flee, luring the Persian fleet into the straits.

Battle

The Persian fleet, numbering perhaps 600–800 ships from Phoenicia, Egypt, and Ionia, entered the confined waters and became congested. The Greek triremes attacked, ramming and boarding; the heavier Persian vessels could not manoeuvre, and the Egyptians sent around the island to block the western exit were too late. Xerxes watched the disaster from a throne on the slopes of Mount Aigaleos. Persian losses were heavy — perhaps 200–300 ships — against about 40 Greek.

Aftermath

Salamis destroyed the Persian fleet's ability to supply its huge land army in Greece. Xerxes withdrew to Asia with much of the army, leaving Mardonius to campaign the following year; his defeat at Plataea in 479 BC, together with the naval victory at Mycale, ended the invasion. The victory is often credited with preserving the independence in which classical Greek civilization flourished.
