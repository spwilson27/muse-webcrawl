Dnieper

The Dnieper at Kyiv, Ukraine

The Dnieper (Ukrainian: Dnipro) is one of the major transboundary rivers of Europe, rising in the Valdai Hills of Russia, flowing through Belarus and Ukraine, and emptying into the Black Sea. It is the longest river of Ukraine and Belarus and the fourth-longest river in Europe, after the Volga, Danube, and Ural, with a length of about 2,200 kilometres (1,400 miles).

Geography

The river's main tributaries include the Berezina, Sozh, Pripyat, Desna, and Inhulets. Major cities on its banks include Kyiv, Dnipro, Zaporizhzhia, and Kherson in Ukraine, and Mogilev in Belarus. A series of hydroelectric dams and reservoirs—Kyiv, Kaniv, Kremenchuk, Kamianske, Dnipro, and Kakhovka—were built along its Ukrainian course in the Soviet era.

History

The Dnieper formed part of the ancient Varangian trade route from the Baltic to the Byzantine Empire. In the 18th century, Ukraine's Cossack heartland lay along its lower reaches. In June 2023, during the Russo-Ukrainian War, the Kakhovka Dam was destroyed, draining its reservoir and causing widespread flooding downstream.

Navigation and economy

The river is navigable for much of its length and is important for transport, irrigation, and hydroelectric power generation in Ukraine.
