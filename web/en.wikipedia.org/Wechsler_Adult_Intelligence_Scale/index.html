Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale

Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale
Acronym: WAIS
Purpose: Measure intelligence in adults and older adolescents
Ages: 16–90
First published: 1955
Author: David Wechsler

The Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS) is an IQ test designed to measure intelligence and cognitive ability in adults and older adolescents. Developed by psychologist David Wechsler, it was first published in February 1955 as a revision of the Wechsler–Bellevue Intelligence Scale (1939). The current version is the WAIS-5, released in 2024; the widely used WAIS-IV appeared in 2008.

Structure

The WAIS-IV comprises core subtests grouped into four index scores: Verbal Comprehension (Similarities, Vocabulary, Information), Perceptual Reasoning (Block Design, Matrix Reasoning, Visual Puzzles), Working Memory (Digit Span, Arithmetic), and Processing Speed (Symbol Search, Coding). The indexes combine into a Full Scale IQ, normed to a mean of 100 and standard deviation of 15. The WAIS-5 restructured the test into five primary indexes, separating Visual Spatial and Fluid Reasoning.

Use

The WAIS is the most widely used individual intelligence test in clinical and neuropsychological practice. It is used to assess intellectual disability, giftedness, and cognitive profiles in conditions such as traumatic brain injury, dementia, and learning disorders. Its companion test for children is the Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children (WISC).
