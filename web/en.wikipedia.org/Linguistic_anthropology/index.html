Linguistic anthropology

Linguistic anthropology is the interdisciplinary study of how language influences social life. It is a branch of anthropology that explores how language shapes communication, forms social identity and cultural beliefs, and organises social action.

History

The field grew from the work of Franz Boas and Edward Sapir on Indigenous languages of North America, and from Dell Hymes's ethnography of communication, which argued that knowing a language includes knowing how to use it appropriately in social contexts.

Topics

Linguistic anthropologists study language socialisation, speech communities, code-switching, language ideology, ritual speech, literacy, multilingualism and endangered languages, typically through long-term ethnographic fieldwork and recordings of natural interaction.

Methods

Methods include participant observation, transcription of conversation, and analysis of discourse, gesture and participation frameworks.
