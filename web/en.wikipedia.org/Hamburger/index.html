A hamburger (or simply burger) is a food consisting of fillings — usually a patty of ground meat, typically beef — placed inside a sliced bun or bread roll. The patty may be pan-fried, grilled, smoked or flame-broiled, and is often served with cheese, lettuce, tomato, onion, pickles, bacon and condiments such as ketchup, mustard and mayonnaise. A hamburger topped with cheese is a cheeseburger.

History

The name derives from Hamburg, Germany, where minced beef dishes such as the Hamburg steak were traditional; German emigrants carried the dish to the United States in the nineteenth century. Several American claimants — including Louis Lassen of New Haven (1900), Charlie Nagreen of Wisconsin (1885), and Fletcher Davis of Texas — are credited with first serving the ground beef patty in a bun around the turn of the twentieth century. The hamburger became a defining fast food in the post-war United States through chains such as White Castle, McDonald's and Burger King, and spread worldwide.

Variations and culture

Regional and gourmet variations are endless, from sliders and double burgers to patties of lamb, chicken, fish or plant-based substitutes. The hamburger is a symbol of American cuisine and of globalised fast food, and record-breaking burgers weighing over a tonne have been cooked as publicity events.
