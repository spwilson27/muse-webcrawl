Emily Dickinson

Emily Elizabeth Dickinson (December 10, 1830 – May 15, 1886) was an American poet. Little-known during her life, she has since been regarded as one of the most important figures in American poetry. Dickinson was born in Amherst, Massachusetts, into a prominent family with strong ties to its community. She lived much of her later life in seclusion at the family Homestead.

Poetry

Dickinson wrote nearly 1,800 poems, almost none of which were published in her lifetime. Her poems are known for their brevity, unconventional capitalization and punctuation, extensive use of dashes, slant rhyme, and themes of death, immortality, nature and the inner life. She bound many of her poems into hand-sewn booklets called fascicles. After her death, her sister Lavinia discovered the cache of poems, and the first collection was published in 1890; complete, largely unaltered editions appeared only in the 20th century. Famous poems include "Because I could not stop for Death" and "'Hope' is the thing with feathers".
