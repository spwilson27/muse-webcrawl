Poplar

Poplar (Populus) is a genus of 25–30 species of deciduous flowering trees in the willow family, Salicaceae, native to much of the Northern Hemisphere. Species include the aspens, cottonwoods, and poplars proper; among the best known are the quaking aspen (Populus tremuloides), black poplar (P. nigra), eastern cottonwood (P. deltoides), and the white poplar (P. alba).

Description

Poplars are fast-growing, often large trees with alternate, triangular to circular leaves on flattened petioles that make them tremble in the wind. They are dioecious — male and female flowers, borne in catkins, appear on separate trees before the leaves emerge in spring. The seeds are tiny, carried on tufts of cottony hairs that aid wind dispersal. Many species reproduce clonally by root suckers; the Utah aspen clone "Pando" is a single genetic individual covering over 40 hectares and thousands of stems.

Uses

Poplar wood is soft, pale, and light, used for pulp and paper, plywood, pallets, matches, and oriented strand board. Hybrid poplars are grown in short-rotation plantations for timber and bioenergy, and poplars are planted as windbreaks, shade trees, and for phytoremediation of contaminated soils. The Lombardy poplar, a fastigiate clone of black poplar, is a classic avenue tree. Because of its small genome, Populus trichocarpa became the first tree to have its genome fully sequenced, serving as a model organism in plant biology.
