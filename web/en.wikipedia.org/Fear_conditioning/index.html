Fear conditioning

Fear conditioning is a form of classical (Pavlovian) learning in which an organism learns to associate a neutral stimulus, such as a tone or a context, with an aversive event such as an electric shock, and thereafter responds fearfully to the neutral stimulus alone.

It is a standard laboratory model for studying emotional learning and memory, and for disorders such as post-traumatic stress disorder and phobias. The amygdala is central to acquiring and storing fear memories; contextual conditioning also involves the hippocampus. Extinction — repeated exposure without the aversive event — gradually reduces the conditioned response and underlies exposure therapy.
