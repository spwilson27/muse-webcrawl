Trump–Ukraine scandal

Date2019–2020
Key figuresDonald Trump, Volodymyr Zelenskyy
OutcomeImpeachment of Donald Trump (2019); acquittal by the Senate (2020)

The Trump–Ukraine scandal was a political scandal in the United States that arose from a July 25, 2019 telephone call in which President Donald Trump asked Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to investigate former Vice President Joe Biden — Trump's political rival — and a discredited theory concerning Ukrainian interference in the 2016 US election. The call came while nearly $400 million in congressionally approved military aid to Ukraine had been withheld.

Disclosure and inquiry

A whistleblower complaint by an intelligence officer, filed in August 2019 and made public that September, triggered an impeachment inquiry by the House of Representatives. The White House released a memorandum of the call, and testimony from diplomats and officials — including Ambassador Gordon Sondland and Lt. Col. Alexander Vindman — described pressure on Ukraine to announce investigations in exchange for aid and a White House meeting. Trump maintained the call was "perfect" and denied any quid pro quo.

Impeachment and acquittal

On December 18, 2019, the House impeached Trump on two articles: abuse of power and obstruction of Congress, making him the third US president to be impeached. The Senate trial concluded on February 5, 2020 with acquittal on both articles, largely along party lines. The scandal shaped the run-up to the 2020 presidential election and US–Ukraine relations.
