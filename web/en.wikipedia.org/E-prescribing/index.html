E-prescribing

E-prescribing, or electronic prescribing, is the computer-based electronic generation, transmission, and filling of a medical prescription, replacing traditional paper and faxed prescriptions. It allows a physician, nurse practitioner, pharmacist, or other healthcare provider to use digital systems to write and send prescriptions to a participating pharmacy, usually integrated with an electronic health record (EHR).

Benefits

E-prescribing eliminates handwriting interpretation errors, enables automatic checks for drug–drug interactions, allergies, duplicate therapy, and correct dosing, and links prescribing to formularies so clinicians can see which drugs a patient's insurance covers. Studies associate e-prescribing systems with reduced medication error rates compared with paper prescribing, though poorly designed interfaces can introduce new error types, such as selecting the wrong drug or patient from drop-down lists.

Adoption

In the United States, adoption was driven by the Medicare Improvements for Patients and Providers Act (2008), the HITECH Act's meaningful-use incentives (2009), and state mandates requiring electronic prescribing of controlled substances (EPCS), which uses two-factor authentication and identity proofing. In England, the Electronic Prescription Service (EPS) transmits prescriptions from general practices to pharmacies via the NHS Spine; most primary care prescriptions are now issued electronically.

Standards

Transactions typically use standards such as NCPDP SCRIPT in the US and HL7 FHIR resources for medication requests, and prescriptions are routed through networks such as Surescripts between prescribers' systems and pharmacy software.
