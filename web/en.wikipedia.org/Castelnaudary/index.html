Castelnaudary (Occitan: Castèlnòu d'Arri) is a commune in the Aude department in the Occitanie region of southern France, situated between Toulouse and Carcassonne. It is the main town of the Lauragais plain and styles itself the "world capital of cassoulet".

The Grand Bassin, the largest open water on the Canal du Midi.

History

The town originated as a Roman staging post (Sostomagus) on the road between Narbonne and Toulouse. Its castle gave the town its name (Castellum Novum Arii, "Arius's new castle"). During the Albigensian Crusade it was besieged in 1211, when Simon de Montfort's small force withstood a much larger army, and the Battle of Castelnaudary in 1632 saw the capture of the rebel Duke of Montmorency, executed at Toulouse. The opening of the Canal du Midi in 1681 made the town a flourishing port; its Grand Bassin, 7 hectares in extent, is the largest open body of water on the canal.

Cassoulet

Castelnaudary claims to be the original home of cassoulet, the slow-cooked casserole of white haricot beans, pork, and confit of duck, traditionally served in a glazed earthenware cassole. Legend ties its invention to the Hundred Years' War siege of 1355. A local brotherhood, the Grande Confrérie du Cassoulet (founded 1970), promotes the dish, and the town hosts a Fête du Cassoulet each August.

Sights and institutions

Landmarks include the collegiate church of Saint-Michel, the 17th-century Présidenté (now a museum), the Cugarel windmill, and the Chapelle Notre-Dame de la Pitié. The town hosts the 4th Foreign Regiment of the French Foreign Legion at the Quartier Capitaine Danjou.
