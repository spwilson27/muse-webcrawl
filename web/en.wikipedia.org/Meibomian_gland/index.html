Meibomian gland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Meibomian glands (also called tarsal glands) are sebaceous glands arranged in a row along the margins of the eyelids, inside the tarsal plate. They are named after the German physician Heinrich Meibom, who first described them in detail. There are about 25 to 40 glands in the upper eyelid and 20 to 30 in the lower lid.

Function

The glands secrete an oily substance called meibum, which forms the outer lipid layer of the tear film. This layer slows evaporation of tears, keeps the tear film smooth and helps seal the eyelids during sleep.

Clinical significance

Blockage of a Meibomian gland can produce a chalazion, a painless lid lump, while acute infection leads to an internal hordeolum (stye). Meibomian gland dysfunction is a leading cause of evaporative dry eye disease.
