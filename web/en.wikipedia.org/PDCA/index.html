PDCA

PDCA (plan–do–check–act or plan–do–check–adjust) is an iterative design and management method used in business for the control and continual improvement of processes and products. It is also known as the Shewhart cycle, the Deming cycle, the control circle/cycle, or plan–do–study–act (PDSA).

The four phases

  • Plan — Establish the objectives and processes necessary to deliver results in accordance with the expected output. By establishing output expectations, the completeness and accuracy of the specification are checked.
  • Do — Implement the plan, execute the process, and collect data for charting and analysis in the following steps.
  • Check (or Study) — Study the actual results and compare them against the expected results to ascertain any differences. Look for deviations in implementation from the plan and for the appropriateness of the plan itself.
  • Act (or Adjust) — Take corrective action on significant differences between actual and planned results. If the change proves successful, standardise it; if not, work through the cycle again with a different plan.

Origins

The cycle derives from the work of Walter A. Shewhart, who in the 1920s and 1930s described a three-step process of specification, production and inspection as a dynamic scientific process of acquiring knowledge. W. Edwards Deming popularised the method in Japan in the 1950s, where it became a cornerstone of quality management and kaizen. Deming himself preferred the term "study" to "check" and referred to the method as the Shewhart cycle, noting that the model should be thought of as a spiral of continual improvement rather than a closed circle.

Use

PDCA underlies modern quality-management standards, including ISO 9001, and related frameworks such as lean manufacturing, Six Sigma (whose DMAIC cycle is a relative), and agile and clinical-improvement methodologies that use the PDSA variant.
