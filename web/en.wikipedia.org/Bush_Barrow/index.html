Bush Barrow

Bush Barrow is an early Bronze Age round barrow in Wiltshire, England, part of the Normanton Down barrow cemetery near Stonehenge. Excavated in 1808 by William Cunnington under the patronage of Sir Richard Colt Hoare, it contained one of the richest Bronze Age burials in Britain.

The burial

The grave held the skeleton of a tall man accompanied by exceptionally fine grave goods dating to around 1900 BCE, associated with the Wessex culture. They included a large gold lozenge decorated with precise geometric patterns, a gold belt hook, bronze daggers — one with a handle inlaid with thousands of tiny gold pins — and a ceremonial mace. The gold lozenge is often called the finest example of Bronze Age goldwork in England.

Significance

The richness of the burial led to its occupant being dubbed the "King of Stonehenge". The finds are held by the Wiltshire Museum in Devizes. The barrow forms part of the Stonehenge World Heritage Site and remains a key reference point for the study of early Bronze Age elites in Britain.
