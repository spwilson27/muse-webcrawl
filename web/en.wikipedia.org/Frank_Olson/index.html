Frank Olson

BornJuly 17, 1910, Hurley, Wisconsin, U.S.
DiedNovember 28, 1953 (aged 43), New York City, U.S.
OccupationBacteriologist; U.S. Army / CIA biological warfare researcher

Frank Rudolph Emmanuel Olson (July 17, 1910 – November 28, 1953) was an American bacteriologist and Army biochemist who worked on biological warfare research at Fort Detrick, Maryland, in association with the Central Intelligence Agency. His death in a fall from a New York hotel window in 1953, days after he was unwittingly given LSD in a CIA experiment, became one of the most controversial episodes of the Cold War intelligence era.

Background

Olson earned a Ph.D. in bacteriology from the University of Wisconsin and joined the Army's biological warfare program during the Second World War. He worked at Camp Detrick (later Fort Detrick) on aerosol delivery of biological agents and collaborated with the CIA's Technical Services Staff, including on projects related to interrogation and mind control, the constellation of programs later known under the name MKULTRA.

The LSD dosing and death

On November 19, 1953, at a retreat at Deep Creek Lake, Maryland, CIA officer Sidney Gottlieb had LSD slipped into Olson's after-dinner liqueur without his knowledge. Olson became agitated and paranoid in the following days. He was taken to New York for psychiatric treatment; in the early hours of November 28, 1953, he went through the closed window of his tenth-floor room at the Hotel Statler and died. The death was officially treated as a suicide or accident.

Revelations and aftermath

The circumstances were concealed from Olson's family until 1975, when the Rockefeller Commission report and press coverage revealed the LSD dosing. President Gerald Ford apologized to the family, and Congress voted them a $750,000 settlement. A later criminal investigation and a private inquiry commissioned by the family raised questions about the official account, but a 1994 exhumation and subsequent reviews did not produce a conclusive alternative finding, and the case remains debated.
