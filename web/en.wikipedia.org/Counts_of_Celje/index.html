The Counts of Celje (Slovene: Celjski grofje) were the most powerful noble dynasty in the late medieval Slovenian lands. Descended from the lords of Žovnek in Styria, they rose in the fourteenth century to become rivals of the Habsburgs and princes of the Holy Roman Empire.

Rise

The family took its name from Celje, which it acquired in the 1320s–1330s. In 1341 Emperor Louis IV raised Frederick I to the rank of count. Under Hermann I and his successors the counts accumulated extensive estates in Styria, Carniola, Croatia, and Slavonia, and intermarried with the royal houses of Bosnia, Serbia, Poland, and Hungary.

Peak and rivalry with the Habsburgs

Hermann II saved Emperor Sigismund's life at the Battle of Nicopolis (1396) and married his daughter Barbara of Celje to him, making her Holy Roman Empress. In 1436 the counts were elevated to Princes of the Empire, directly challenging Habsburg supremacy in the region, and Barbara's daughter's descendants shaped Hungarian politics for decades.

Fall

The last count, Ulrich II, a leading figure in Hungary as regent and captain-general, was assassinated in Belgrade in 1456 by followers of the Hunyadi faction. With his death the male line ended, and the counts' lands largely passed to the Habsburgs after a brief succession war.
