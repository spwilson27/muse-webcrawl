A floating market is a market where goods are sold from boats. Floating markets developed in regions where rivers and canals were the principal means of transport, so that farmers and traders could bring produce directly to customers by water. They are found today mainly in Thailand, Vietnam and Indonesia, where they function both as working markets and as major tourist attractions.

History

In the Chao Phraya basin of Thailand and the Mekong Delta of Vietnam, dense networks of natural waterways and dug canals (khlong) made boats the natural vehicles of commerce well into the twentieth century. Wholesale floating markets — such as Cai Rang near Can Tho, where buyers' boats carry a sample of their goods on a tall pole called a cây bẹo — operated at dawn, while smaller retail markets served canal-side villages.

Famous markets

Damnoen Saduak in Thailand's Ratchaburi province, on a canal dug in the 1860s under Rama IV, is the most photographed, with vendors in straw hats paddling wooden boats piled with tropical fruit; the weekend market at Amphawa is known for evening fireflies and grilled seafood. In Indonesia, Lok Baintan on the Martapura River in South Kalimantan sees traders in jukung canoes exchange goods by barter at sunrise; on Myanmar's Inle Lake, a rotating five-day market serves the lake's floating villages.

Decline

Road building, supermarkets and changing logistics have shrunk most floating markets since the late twentieth century; many survive largely on tourism, and several have been revived or relocated (as in Bangkok) as heritage attractions.
