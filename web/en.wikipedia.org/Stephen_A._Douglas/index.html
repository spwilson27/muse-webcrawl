Stephen Arnold Douglas (April 23, 1813 – June 3, 1861) was an American politician and lawyer from Illinois. A U.S. Senator, he was one of two nominees of the badly split Democratic Party in the 1860 presidential election, which was won by Republican Abraham Lincoln. Douglas had previously defeated Lincoln in the famous Lincoln–Douglas debates of 1858 for a Senate seat.

Early life and career

Born in Brandon, Vermont, Douglas migrated to Illinois in 1833, rose quickly as a lawyer and judge, and served in the U.S. House (1843–47) before entering the Senate.

Popular sovereignty

Douglas championed popular sovereignty — letting territorial settlers decide on slavery themselves. He engineered the Compromise of 1850, which kept the Union together temporarily, and the Kansas–Nebraska Act (1854), which repealed the Missouri Compromise and provoked violent conflict in Bleeding Kansas and the creation of the Republican Party.

Lincoln–Douglas debates

In 1858 Douglas debated Lincoln seven times across Illinois. At Freeport he stated the Freeport Doctrine: territories could effectively exclude slavery despite the Dred Scott decision. Douglas retained his seat but the doctrine split his party in 1860.

1860 election and death

The Democratic Party split into Northern and Southern tickets; Douglas won only Missouri outright, though he placed second in the popular vote. When the Civil War began in April 1861, Douglas rallied Northern Democrats to the Union cause before dying of typhoid fever in Chicago that June.
