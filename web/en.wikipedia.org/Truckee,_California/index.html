Truckee, California

Truckee is an incorporated town in Nevada County, California, in the Sierra Nevada mountains near Donner Pass. Its population was about 16,200 at the 2020 census. The town is named after a Paiute chief whose name sounded like "Tro-kay", said to mean "everything is all right", who guided emigrant parties in the 1840s.

History

Truckee grew up on the emigrant route over Donner Pass — the ill-fated Donner Party wintered nearby in 1846–47 — and boomed as a stop on the First Transcontinental Railroad, completed in 1869. Lumbering, ice harvesting, and railroading sustained the town through the 19th and early 20th centuries.

Geography and recreation

At an elevation of about 5,800 feet (1,770 m), Truckee is known for snowy winters and is a gateway to Lake Tahoe ski resorts including Northstar California, Palisades Tahoe, and Sugar Bowl. Donner Lake lies just west of town, and Interstate 80 passes through, making Truckee a popular year-round recreation base.
