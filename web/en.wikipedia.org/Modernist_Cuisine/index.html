Modernist Cuisine

Modernist Cuisine: The Art and Science of Cooking is a six-volume, 2,438-page cookbook published in 2011 by Nathan Myhrvold, Chris Young, and Maxime Bilet. Produced by a team of chefs, scientists, and photographers at Myhrvold's Cooking Lab in Bellevue, Washington, it documents the techniques and science of the modernist (or "molecular gastronomy") cooking movement associated with chefs such as Ferran Adrià and Heston Blumenthal.

Contents

The volumes cover: (1) history and fundamentals, including food microbiology and safety; (2) techniques and equipment, from sous-vide baths and centrifuges to freeze dryers; (3) animals and plants; (4) ingredients and preparations, including hydrocolloid gels, emulsions, and foams; (5) plated-dish recipes; and (6) a spiral-bound kitchen manual of recipes and tables. The book is famous for its dramatic cutaway photography of cooking equipment in cross-section.

Reception and impact

Modernist Cuisine won major cookbook awards, including the James Beard Foundation Award for Cookbook of the Year in 2012, and was praised for systematising the physics and chemistry of cooking for professional kitchens. At a launch price of about $625 for some 20 kilograms of books, it was also notable as a publishing venture. Spin-offs followed, including Modernist Cuisine at Home (2012), Modernist Bread (2017), and Modernist Pizza (2021).
