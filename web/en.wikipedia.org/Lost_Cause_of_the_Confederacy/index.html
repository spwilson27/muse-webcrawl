Lost Cause of the Confederacy

The Lost Cause is an American myth and historical negationist ideology that portrays the Confederate States of America and their cause in the Civil War in a romantic, heroic light. Emerging among former Confederates after 1865 — the term was popularised by Edward A. Pollard's 1866 book The Lost Cause — it holds that the South fought for states' rights rather than slavery, that slavery was benign, and that Confederate defeat resulted only from overwhelming Northern numbers and resources.

Historians overwhelmingly reject these claims, noting contemporary Confederate sources identifying slavery as central to secession. The Lost Cause was promoted through memorial associations, textbooks and monuments, and helped legitimise Jim Crow segregation; debates over Confederate monuments remain contentious today.
