Breathalyzer

The Breathalyzer is a device for estimating blood alcohol content (BAC) from a breath sample. The name, a blend of "breath" and "analyzer", was originally a trademark of inventor Robert Frank Borkenstein's device, patented in 1958, and is now often used generically for breath alcohol testers.

History

Early breath-testing devices included Rolla Harger's "Drunkometer" (1930s). Borkenstein, an Indiana State Police captain, developed the more practical Breathalyzer in 1954, using chemical oxidation and photometry to measure alcohol in the breath.

Technology

Breath alcohol concentration is related to blood concentration by a partition ratio, conventionally 2100:1. Modern evidential instruments use fuel-cell sensors or infrared spectroscopy. Preliminary screening devices are used at the roadside, while evidential results are typically confirmed at a police station.

Legal use

Breath tests underpin most drink-driving enforcement. Many jurisdictions criminalize driving above a set limit (for example 0.08 g/dL in most US states and 80 mg per 100 ml of blood in England and Wales) and penalize refusal to provide a sample.
