Seine fishing

Seine fishing (or seine-haul fishing) is a method of fishing that employs a fishing net called a seine, which hangs vertically in the water with its bottom edge held down by weights and its top edge buoyed by floats. Fish are encircled by the net and then hauled in.

Types

Beach seines are operated from shore, often by hand, to catch fish in shallow water. Boat seines are worked from a vessel in open water. The purse seine, in which a line drawn through rings at the bottom of the net closes it like a drawstring purse, is the dominant industrial method for schooling fish such as tuna, mackerel, and sardines. Danish (anchor) seines are towed along the seabed to herd flatfish.

History and regulation

Seine nets are among the oldest fishing gears, depicted in ancient Egyptian art. Because purse seines can take entire schools, their use — especially with fish aggregating devices — is regulated in many fisheries, and some jurisdictions restrict beach seining to protect juvenile fish and surf-zone ecosystems.
