HVAC — heating, ventilation, and air conditioning — is the technology of indoor environmental comfort. Its goal is to provide acceptable thermal comfort and indoor air quality in homes, offices, hospitals, vehicles and industrial facilities. HVAC design is a subdiscipline of mechanical engineering governed by the principles of thermodynamics, fluid mechanics and heat transfer.

Components

Heating is supplied by furnaces, boilers, district steam or heat pumps, which move heat rather than generate it and can also run in reverse for cooling. Air conditioning, invented by Willis Carrier in 1902 to control humidity in a printing plant, works on the vapour-compression refrigeration cycle. Ventilation dilutes indoor pollutants and supplies fresh air, increasingly through heat-recovery ventilators that reclaim energy from exhaust air.

Efficiency and environment

Because HVAC consumes a large share of building energy, efficiency ratings such as SEER and heat-pump COP, building insulation, and smart controls matter greatly. The industry is phasing down high-global-warming HFC refrigerants under the Kigali Amendment, and electrification with heat pumps is a central strategy for decarbonising buildings.
