Web3

Web3 (also known as Web 3.0) is an idea for a new iteration of the World Wide Web that incorporates concepts such as decentralization, blockchain technologies, and token-based economics. The term was coined in this sense in 2014 by Gavin Wood, a co-founder of Ethereum. Proponents envision users owning their data and digital assets rather than relying on large centralized platforms, contrasting Web3 with the static "read-only" Web 1.0 and the participatory, platform-dominated Web 2.0.

Concepts

Web3 applications, or dApps, run on peer-to-peer networks and blockchains rather than on servers controlled by a single company. Cryptocurrencies and tokens provide payment and incentive layers; non-fungible tokens (NFTs) represent ownership of digital items; decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs) coordinate collective decisions; and decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols replicate financial services without intermediaries. Identity is typically anchored to a cryptographic wallet rather than an account issued by a provider.

History

The term gained widespread attention in 2021 amid investment by venture capital firms and promotion by cryptocurrency advocates. Earlier, "Web 3.0" had been used by some writers to mean the Semantic Web, a different vision associated with Tim Berners-Lee.

Criticism

Critics argue that Web3 is a marketing buzzword, that its applications remain niche and difficult to use, that venture capital ownership undercuts the decentralization claim, and that blockchain systems carry environmental and scalability costs. Figures such as Jack Dorsey and Elon Musk publicly mocked the concept, and industry observers note that most users access "decentralized" services through a few centralized intermediaries.
