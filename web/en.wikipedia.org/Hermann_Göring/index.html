Hermann Wilhelm Göring (12 January 1893 – 15 October 1946) was a German politician, military leader and convicted war criminal. A leading member of the Nazi Party, he was one of the most powerful figures in Nazi Germany: founder of the Gestapo, commander-in-chief of the Luftwaffe, president of the Reichstag, and Adolf Hitler's designated successor from 1939 until his dismissal in April 1945.

Early life and First World War

Göring was born in Rosenheim, Bavaria, the son of Heinrich Göring, a judge and the first Reichskommissar of German South West Africa. He joined the Prussian Army in 1912 and transferred to the air service in the First World War, becoming a flying ace credited with 22 victories; he ended the war commanding Jagdgeschwader I, the "Flying Circus" formerly led by Manfred von Richthofen, and received the Pour le Mérite.

Rise in the Nazi Party

Göring joined the Nazi Party in 1922, commanded the SA, and was wounded in the Beer Hall Putsch of 1923, developing a morphine addiction during his treatment. Elected to the Reichstag in 1928, he became its president in 1932. After the Nazi seizure of power in 1933 he served as minister president of Prussia, created the Gestapo (later transferred to Heinrich Himmler), and as Reich aviation minister rebuilt German air power, becoming Luftwaffe commander-in-chief in 1935. In 1936 he took charge of the Four Year Plan, directing the regime's economic preparation for war; he also amassed a vast collection of looted art at his Carinhall estate.

Second World War

In 1940 Göring was promoted to the specially created rank of Reichsmarschall, making him the senior officer of the Wehrmacht. His prestige declined after the Luftwaffe's failures in the Battle of Britain, the Eastern Front and the defence of the Reich against Allied bombing. He issued the 31 July 1941 directive to Reinhard Heydrich ordering the organization of the "Final Solution". In April 1945, after he attempted to assume leadership of the Reich, Hitler stripped him of all offices and ordered his arrest.

Trial and death

Göring surrendered to American forces in May 1945 and was the highest-ranking Nazi tried at Nuremberg. He was convicted on all four counts — conspiracy, crimes against peace, war crimes and crimes against humanity — and sentenced to death by hanging. On the night of 15 October 1946, hours before his scheduled execution, he died by suicide using a cyanide capsule.
