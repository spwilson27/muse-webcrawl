George Wesley Bellows (August 12, 1882 – January 8, 1925) was an American realist painter, known for his bold depictions of urban life in New York City. He became, according to the Columbus Museum of Art, "the most acclaimed American artist of his generation."

Early life

Bellows was born and raised in Columbus, Ohio. He attended Ohio State University, where he played baseball and basketball, before leaving in 1904 to study art in New York under Robert Henri at the New York School of Art.

Career

Bellows became associated with the Ashcan School, a group of artists who painted scenes of everyday life in New York's poorer neighborhoods. His boxing paintings, including Stag at Sharkey's (1909) and Both Members of This Club (1909), are among his most famous works, capturing the raw energy of illegal prizefights. He also painted landscapes of Maine and California and produced a celebrated series of lithographs.

Death and legacy

Bellows died in 1925 of a ruptured appendix at the age of 42. His work is held by major museums including the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the National Gallery of Art, and the Whitney Museum of American Art.
