Engastration

Engastration is a cooking technique in which one animal is stuffed inside the gastric cavity of another animal. The method supposedly originated during the Middle Ages. Among the dishes that follow this method are the Yorkshire Christmas pie, containing pigeon, partridge, fowl, goose, and turkey in layers, and modern creations such as the turducken, in which a deboned chicken is stuffed into a deboned duck, which in turn is stuffed into a deboned turkey.

Examples

Historical examples include the elaborate "cockentrice" of medieval banquets (a pig and a capon sewn together to appear as a mythical beast) and Grimod de La Reynière's rôti sans pareil ("roast without equal"), described in the Almanach des Gourmands (1807), which nested seventeen birds, from a bustard down to a garden warbler, one inside another. The Algerian stuffed whole camel, often repeated in popular lists, is of doubtful authenticity.

Turducken

The turducken became widely known in the United States through Louisiana Cajun cooking and was popularised nationally by sports commentator John Madden, who praised and ceremonially carved one during Thanksgiving football broadcasts. Variations add stuffing between the layers and further nesting, sometimes jokingly extended to multi-bird roasts with a dozen or more birds.
