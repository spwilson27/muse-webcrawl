Qazi Muhammad (Kurdish: قازی محەممەد; 1 May 1893 – 31 March 1947) was an Iranian Kurdish religious scholar, judge (qadi), and political leader who served as president of the short-lived Republic of Mahabad in 1946. He remains one of the most revered figures in Kurdish national history.

Early life

Qazi Muhammad was born in Mahabad, in northwestern Iran, into a prominent family of judges and landowners of the Dehbokri tribe. He received a traditional religious education, became a judge and a respected community notable, and was fluent in Kurdish, Persian, Arabic, and several European languages.

Republic of Mahabad

After the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran in 1941, Soviet forces occupied the northwest, enabling Kurdish political organization. Qazi Muhammad became head of the nationalist party Komala and then founded the Kurdish Democratic Party of Iran (KDPI) in 1945. On 22 January 1946, in Mahabad's Chwar Chira Square, he proclaimed the Republic of Mahabad, a Soviet-backed Kurdish state encompassing a small area around Mahabad. His government introduced Kurdish-language education and publishing and granted women greater public roles.

Death and legacy

When Soviet troops withdrew from Iran under international pressure in 1946, Iranian forces reoccupied the region. Qazi Muhammad surrendered Mahabad without a battle to spare the city destruction, was tried by a military court, and was hanged on 31 March 1947 in the same square where the republic had been proclaimed. He is remembered by Kurds worldwide as a national martyr, and his plea for Kurdish unity remains a rallying point of Kurdish nationalism.
