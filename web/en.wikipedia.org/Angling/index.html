Angling

Angling is a fishing technique that uses a fish hook ("angle") attached to a fishing line to tether individual fish in the mouth. The fishing line is usually manipulated via a fishing rod, although rodless techniques such as handlining and longlining also exist. Angling is the principal method of sport fishing, but commercial fisheries also use angling methods such as longlining and trolling.

Techniques

In modern angling, the hook is usually baited with lures or bait and attached via a line to a rod and reel. When a fish bites, the angler sets the hook and reels in the line, playing the fish until it can be landed with a net or by hand. Major disciplines include fly fishing, in which an artificial fly is cast with a specialised weighted line; coarse fishing for freshwater species other than game fish; sea angling from shore or boats; and lure fishing with artificial plugs, spoons, and soft plastics.

History

Hooks of bone, shell, and bronze have been used for thousands of years. Angling as a pastime was popularised in England by works such as The Compleat Angler (1653) by Izaak Walton. The development of the fishing reel, split-cane and later carbon-fibre rods, and synthetic lines transformed the sport in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Regulation and conservation

Recreational angling is usually regulated through licensing, closed seasons, size limits, and bag limits. Catch and release is widely practised, particularly in game and specimen fishing, to conserve stocks. Competitive angling ranges from local club matches to international championships in coarse, game, and sea disciplines.

Tackle

Basic tackle comprises a rod, reel, line, hooks, and terminal tackle such as floats, weights, and swivels, together with bait or lures. Specialist branches use highly developed equipment, from centrepin reels and pole fishing rigs to big-game trolling outfits.
