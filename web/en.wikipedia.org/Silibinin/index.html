Silibinin

Silibinin (also known as silybin) is the principal active constituent of silymarin, a standardised extract of the seeds of the milk thistle plant (Silybum marianum). It is a flavonolignan used in supplements and, in intravenous form in parts of Europe, as an antidote for poisoning by the death cap mushroom (Amanita phalloides).

Chemistry

Silibinin is a roughly equimolar mixture of two diastereomers, silybin A and silybin B, formed from taxifolin and coniferyl alcohol. It is poorly soluble in water, which limits oral bioavailability; phospholipid complexes and intravenous formulations (as the succinate salt) were developed to overcome this.

Medical use

Intravenous silibinin is used in several European countries to treat amatoxin poisoning, where it is thought to block uptake of the toxin into hepatocytes. Oral silymarin preparations are widely sold as supplements for liver health, though clinical evidence for most claimed benefits remains limited.
