The Shōwa era (昭和時代, Shōwa jidai) was the period of Japanese history corresponding to the reign of Emperor Shōwa (Hirohito) from 25 December 1926 until his death on 7 January 1989. It was the longest reign of any Japanese emperor. The era name means "enlightened peace" or "radiant Japan".

Overview

The Shōwa period encompasses Japan's militarist expansion of the 1930s, the Second Sino-Japanese War and the Pacific War, defeat and Allied occupation (1945–1952), and the postwar "economic miracle" that made Japan the world's second-largest economy. Democratic reforms under the occupation produced the 1947 constitution, land reform, and women's suffrage; rapid growth from the 1950s to the 1970s transformed Japan into a leading industrial and technological power.

Legacy

The era's length and contrasts have made "Shōwa" a powerful cultural reference in Japan, evoking both wartime memory and nostalgia for postwar growth and popular culture.
