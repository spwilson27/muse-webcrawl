Fort Saint Elmo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Saint Elmo (Maltese: Forti Sant'Iermu) is a star fort in Valletta, Malta. It stands on the seaward shore of the Sciberras Peninsula that divides Marsamxett Harbour from Grand Harbour, and commands the entrances to both harbours along with Fort Tigné and Fort Ricasoli.

History

Construction and the Great Siege

The Order of Saint John built Fort Saint Elmo in 1552, to a four-pointed star design by the Spanish engineer Pietro Pardo, on the site of an earlier watchtower. In the Great Siege of Malta of 1565, the fort endured a month of heavy Ottoman bombardment and repeated assaults; its small garrison held out from 24 May until 23 June, buying time for relief forces to arrive from Sicily, at the cost of some 1,500 defenders, including the knight Dragut's opponent commander Luigi Broglia. The fort was rebuilt after the siege as part of the new fortified city of Valletta.

Later history

The fort was modified repeatedly under British rule in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. During the Second World War it was involved in repelling the Italian E-boat attack on Grand Harbour in July 1941. Members of the Malta Police Force staged part of the 1979 events surrounding the end of British military presence at the fort. Restoration in the 2010s returned the upper fort to public use.

Present day

Since 2015 the fort has housed Malta's National War Museum, relocated from the lower fort. It also hosts the Malta Police Academy. The fort is a scheduled Grade 1 monument and appears on Malta's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status as part of the Knights' Fortifications around the Harbours of Malta.
