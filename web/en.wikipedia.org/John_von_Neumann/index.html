John von Neumann

John von Neumann (28 December 1903 – 8 February 1957) was a Hungarian-American mathematician, physicist, computer scientist, engineer, and polymath. He made major contributions to many fields, including mathematics (foundations, functional analysis, ergodic theory, geometry, topology, numerical analysis), physics (quantum mechanics, hydrodynamics, quantum statistical mechanics), economics (game theory), computing, and statistics.

Early life

Born Neumann János Lajos in Budapest into a wealthy Jewish family, he was a child prodigy who earned a PhD in mathematics from Pázmány Péter University and a degree in chemical engineering from ETH Zürich. He taught in Berlin and Hamburg before moving to Princeton in 1930, becoming one of the first professors at the Institute for Advanced Study.

Work

Von Neumann axiomatized quantum mechanics and set theory, proved the minimax theorem, and co-authored Theory of Games and Economic Behavior (1944) with Oskar Morgenstern, founding modern game theory. In computing, the von Neumann architecture — a stored-program design described in his 1945 EDVAC report — underlies most computers today. He contributed to the Manhattan Project at Los Alamos, to the development of the hydrogen bomb, to cellular automata and self-reproducing machines, and to numerical weather prediction.

Later life

He served on the Atomic Energy Commission and advised the US government on defence policy. He died of cancer in Washington, D.C., at 53.
