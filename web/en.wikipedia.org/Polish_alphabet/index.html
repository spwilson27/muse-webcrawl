Polish alphabet

The Polish alphabet is the script of the Polish language, based on the Latin alphabet but with diacritics such as the kreska (acute accent), the kropka (dot above), the tail called the ogonek, and a stroke through the letter ł.

Letters

Standard Polish orthography uses 32 letters: a, ą, b, c, ć, d, e, ę, f, g, h, i, j, k, l, ł, m, n, ń, o, ó, p, r, s, ś, t, u, w, y, z, ź, ż. The letters q, v, and x do not occur in native words but appear in loanwords and foreign names. Several sounds are also written with digraphs: ch, cz, dz, dź, dż, rz, and sz.

Diacritics

The ogonek (ą, ę) marks nasal vowels; the acute accent (ć, ń, ś, ź) marks palatalized consonants; ó represents the vowel /u/ and ż/ź distinguish retroflex and alveolo-palatal fricatives. The stroked ł is now pronounced /w/ in standard Polish.

History

Polish began to be written in the Latin alphabet in the Middle Ages; early texts such as the Holy Cross Sermons used inconsistent spellings. Orthography was gradually standardized in the sixteenth century by printers in Kraków and grammarians such as Piotr Statorius, and repeatedly reformed, most recently in the twentieth century.
