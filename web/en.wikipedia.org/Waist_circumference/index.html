Waist circumference

Waist circumference is the measurement around the natural waistline, usually just above the belly button. It is a practical indicator of abdominal (visceral) fat and is used by the World Health Organization and other bodies as a measure of obesity-related health risk, complementing the body mass index (BMI).

PurposeEstimate abdominal obesity and cardiometabolic risk
WHO risk thresholdsIncreased: men ≥94 cm, women ≥80 cm; substantially increased: men ≥102 cm, women ≥88 cm
Related measuresBMI; waist–hip ratio; waist–height ratio

Measurement

Standard protocols (e.g. WHO STEPwise) measure the circumference midway between the lowest rib and the iliac crest, at the end of a normal breath, with the subject standing. Other protocols use the iliac crest or the umbilicus, so values across studies are not always strictly comparable.

Health significance

A large waist circumference indicates visceral adiposity and is a component of the metabolic syndrome. Elevated values are associated with increased risk of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and certain cancers, even in people with a normal BMI. Thresholds differ by sex and ethnicity; lower cut-offs are recommended for South and East Asian populations.
