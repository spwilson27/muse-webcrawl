Gion Matsuri

Gion Matsuri is a Japanese festival held annually in Kyoto throughout the month of July. It is one of Japan's most famous festivals and the country's largest traditional festival, centred on the Yasaka Shrine (formerly Gion Shrine) in the Gion district. Its highlight is the Yamaboko Junko, a grand procession of enormous decorated floats.

History

The festival originated in 869, when, during an epidemic, the emperor ordered a ritual in which 66 halberds — one for each province of Japan — were erected at Shinsen-en garden and portable shrines from Gion were carried out to purify the city and appease the gods of plague. The rite was repeated whenever epidemics struck and became an annual event by the late Heian period. The festival survived the Ōnin War (when it lapsed for several decades) and was revived in the sixteenth century under the patronage of Kyoto's wealthy merchant townspeople, whose neighbourhoods built and maintained the floats.

The floats and processions

The procession features two types of float: the tall hoko, weighing up to about 12 tonnes and pulled by teams of men, topped by a long pole or halberd; and the smaller yama, carried on men's shoulders, many decorated with pine trees and elaborate tableaux. The floats are adorned with precious tapestries — some imported centuries ago from Europe, Persia, and China — and are accompanied by musicians playing flutes, drums, and bells in the distinctive "kon-chiki-chin" rhythm of Gion-bayashi music.

The main processions (saki matsuri on 17 July and ato matsuri on 24 July) draw crowds of hundreds of thousands. In the evenings before them, the downtown streets close to traffic for yoiyama festivities, when the floats are displayed lit by lanterns and neighbourhoods open their homes to show family treasures. In 2009 the festival's float procession was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
