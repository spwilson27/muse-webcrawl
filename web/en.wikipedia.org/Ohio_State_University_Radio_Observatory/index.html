Ohio State University Radio Observatory

The Ohio State University Radio Observatory, home of the "Big Ear" radio telescope, was located on the grounds of Ohio Wesleyan University's Perkins Observatory near Delaware, Ohio. Designed and built under the direction of John D. Kraus, the Big Ear was one of the largest radio telescopes in the world and conducted the Ohio Sky Survey. It is best known for detecting the famous "Wow! signal" in 1977.

The Big Ear

The telescope was of a novel Kraus-type design: a flat, tiltable reflecting surface directed radio waves toward a large fixed spherical reflector, which focused them onto movable receivers. The collecting area measured roughly 110 metres by 21 metres. After early experiments with a smaller antenna, the Big Ear began the Ohio Sky Survey in 1965, cataloguing tens of thousands of radio sources.

The Wow! signal

From 1973 the telescope devoted much of its time to a search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI). On 15 August 1977, volunteer Jerry Ehman noticed a strong narrowband signal in the printouts and famously circled the characters "6EQUJ5" and wrote "Wow!" beside them. The signal, from the direction of the constellation Sagittarius, lasted 72 seconds and was never seen again despite repeated searches; its origin remains unexplained.

Closure

The land on which the telescope sat was sold to developers in 1983 and, after years of reprieves, the Big Ear was dismantled in 1998 to make way for a golf course and housing development. The observatory's SETI work continued in other forms, including the volunteer-run Argus project.
