Human body

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The human body is the structure of a human being. It is composed of many different types of cells that together create tissues and subsequently organ systems. They ensure homeostasis and the viability of the human body. It comprises a head, hair, neck, trunk (which includes the thorax and abdomen), arms and hands, legs and feet. The study of the human body involves anatomy, physiology, histology, and embryology.

Composition

The body consists of approximately 37 trillion cells and is composed by mass mainly of oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen, with smaller amounts of calcium, phosphorus, potassium, and other elements. Water accounts for roughly 60 per cent of body mass in adults. The body contains about 206 bones in adults, around 600 skeletal muscles, and a circulatory system with tens of thousands of kilometres of blood vessels.

Organ systems

Major systems include the integumentary, skeletal, muscular, nervous, endocrine, cardiovascular, lymphatic, respiratory, digestive, urinary, and reproductive systems. These systems work together to transport oxygen and nutrients, remove waste, regulate temperature, fight infection, and reproduce.

Development and variation

The body develops from a single fertilised egg through cell division and differentiation. Human bodies vary in height, build, and other characteristics by genetics, sex, age, nutrition, and environment; the scientific study of this variation is part of physical anthropology and medicine.
