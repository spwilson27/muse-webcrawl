Cochin International Airport is an international airport serving the city of Kochi, Kerala, in southwestern India. It is located at Nedumbassery, about 25 kilometres northeast of the city centre. It was the first airport in India to be built under a public–private partnership (PPP) model, developed with equity contributions from thousands of non-resident Indians and other investors, and is operated by Cochin International Airport Limited (CIAL), a company chaired by the Chief Minister of Kerala.

History and facilities

The airport opened in 1999 to replace the naval air station on Willingdon Island, which could not be expanded for large aircraft. Its terminal architecture draws on traditional Kerala design. The international Terminal 3, inaugurated in 2017, greatly expanded capacity. The airport handles large volumes of traffic to Gulf countries, reflecting the size of the Malayali diaspora there.

Cochin became notable as the world's first airport fully powered by solar energy: in 2015 CIAL commissioned a 12-megawatt solar plant whose output covers the airport's consumption, feeding surplus power to the grid. The model won the United Nations' Champion of the Earth award in 2018. The airport operates in the monsoon climate typical of the climate of India's southwest coast, and was temporarily closed during the Kerala floods of 2018 and 2019.
