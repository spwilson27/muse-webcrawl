The Catholic Church, also called the Roman Catholic Church, is the largest Christian church, with 1.4 billion baptised Catholics worldwide as of 2023. It is among the world's oldest and largest international institutions and has played a prominent role in the history and development of Western civilisation. The church consists of 24 sui iuris particular churches, including the Latin Church and 23 Eastern Catholic Churches, which comprise almost 3,500 dioceses and eparchies around the world.

The Pope, who is the Bishop of Rome, is the chief pastor of the church. The Diocese of Rome, known as the Holy See, is the central governing authority of the church.

Beliefs

Catholic doctrine is summarised in the Nicene Creed and detailed in the Catechism of the Catholic Church. Catholics believe in one God in three persons (the Trinity); in Jesus Christ as incarnate Son of God and saviour; and in salvation by grace through faith and works. The church teaches seven sacraments — Baptism, Confirmation, the Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony — among which the Eucharist, celebrated in the Mass, is the central act of worship, involving belief in transubstantiation. Veneration of Mary, the mother of Jesus, and of the saints, prayers for the dead, and the doctrine of purgatory are distinctive features of Catholic practice and teaching.

History

The church traces its origins to Jesus and the apostles, holding that the bishops are the successors of the apostles and the Pope the successor of Saint Peter. Christianity spread through the Roman Empire despite persecution, was legalised by Constantine (Edict of Milan, 313) and later became the state church of Rome. The East–West Schism of 1054 divided the church from Eastern Orthodoxy; the Western church was further divided by the Protestant Reformation of the 16th century, to which the church responded with the Counter-Reformation and the Council of Trent. The First Vatican Council (1869–70) defined papal infallibility; the Second Vatican Council (1962–65) introduced sweeping liturgical and ecumenical reforms.

Organisation

The church is governed as an episcopal hierarchy: the Pope, cardinals, patriarchs, archbishops, bishops, priests, and deacons. Religious orders and societies of apostolic life — such as the Jesuits, Franciscans, Dominicans, and Benedictines — form a major part of its life. Through parishes, schools, universities, hospitals, and charities the church is the largest non-governmental provider of education and health care in the world.

Social teaching

Catholic social teaching, developed in documents such as Rerum novarum (1891) and Laudato si' (2015), addresses the dignity of the human person, the common good, workers' rights, care for the poor, and stewardship of the environment. The church opposes abortion, euthanasia, and capital punishment, and teaches a sexual ethic centred on marriage.
