Ramones

The Ramones were an American punk rock band formed in the New York City neighborhood of Forest Hills, Queens, in 1974. They are often cited as the first band to define the punk rock sound and are considered one of the most influential acts in the genre. Despite achieving only limited commercial success during their career, the band's fast, stripped-down songs, uniform leather jackets and jeans, and relentless touring profoundly shaped punk in the United States and the United Kingdom.

History

The original lineup consisted of Joey Ramone (vocals), Johnny Ramone (guitar), Dee Dee Ramone (bass), and Tommy Ramone (drums), who later became the band's producer and was replaced on drums by Marky Ramone. Their debut album Ramones (1976) featured short, rapid songs such as "Blitzkrieg Bop" and "Beat on the Brat", recorded for just over $6,000. Later albums included Leave Home and Rocket to Russia (1977) and the Phil Spector-produced End of the Century (1980).

Touring and legacy

The Ramones played 2,263 concerts, touring virtually nonstop for 22 years until their farewell show in 1996. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2002 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2011. Their influence extends across punk, hardcore, pop-punk, and alternative rock; bands from the Sex Pistols and the Clash to Green Day and Metallica have cited them as a formative inspiration.

Deaths of the original members

Joey died of lymphoma in 2001, Dee Dee of a heroin overdose in 2002, Johnny of prostate cancer in 2004, and Tommy — the last surviving original member — of bile duct cancer in 2014.
