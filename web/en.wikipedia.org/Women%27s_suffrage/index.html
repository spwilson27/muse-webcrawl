Women's suffrage

Women's suffrage is the right of women to vote in elections. Beginning in the mid-19th century, women in many countries campaigned for this right, forming organisations such as the International Woman Suffrage Alliance (1904) and national suffrage associations.

First victories

New Zealand became the first self-governing country in which all women had the right to vote in parliamentary elections, in 1893, followed by South Australia in 1894 (the first jurisdiction also to let women stand for parliament). Finland in 1906 was the first European country to grant full suffrage with eligibility for office.

Twentieth century

After the First World War, many countries extended the vote: Germany, Austria, and Poland in 1918; the Netherlands in 1919; and the United States through the Nineteenth Amendment in 1920. France followed in 1944, Italy in 1946, Japan in 1945, and Switzerland at federal level only in 1971. Saudi Arabia was the last country to permit women to vote, in 2015.
