Primus pilus

The primus pilus ("first spear") was the senior centurion of a Roman legion. The title means "first file" or "first spear", reflecting the position of the first cohort's leading century at the head of the battle line in the earlier manipular legion. The primus pilus commanded the first century of the elite first cohort, which in the imperial legion was double-strength.

Role and status

The primus pilus was the most experienced soldier in the legion and sat in on the councils of war of the legate and the tribunes, effectively acting as the legion's senior professional adviser. His pay was far above that of an ordinary centurion — reportedly many times a legionary's wage — and the post was normally held for a single year, capping a long career of promotion through the centurionate ranks (primi ordines for the centurions of the first cohort).

Career after service

On completing his term, the primus pilus was discharged as a primipilaris and admitted to the equestrian order, opening the way to prestigious posts such as camp prefect (praefectus castrorum), procuratorships, and other senior appointments. A few exceptionally successful men rose even higher in imperial service. The position is attested in literary sources such as Caesar's commentaries and in numerous inscriptions recording the careers (cursus) of centurions.
