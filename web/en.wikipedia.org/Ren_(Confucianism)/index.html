Ren (Confucianism)

The character 仁 (ren) combines the radicals for "person" and "two", suggesting humaneness in relationships.

Ren (Chinese: 仁; pinyin: rén), meaning "co-humanity" or "humaneness", is a Confucian virtue denoting the good quality of a virtuous human when reaching for higher ideals or when being altruistic. Ren is exemplified by a normal adult's protective feelings for children and is considered the outward expression of Confucian ideals.

According to Confucius, ren is the highest virtue, encompassing all others. In the Analects, Confucius characterises ren through the Golden Rule in its negative form: "What you do not wish for yourself, do not do to others." He describes the person of ren as one who, "wishing to establish himself, seeks also to establish others; wishing to enlarge himself, he seeks also to enlarge others."

Interpretation

The character 仁 is a compound of the radical for person (人) and the number two (二), which scholars have read as indicating that humanity is realised in the relationship between people. Mencius later taught that the heart of compassion is the "sprout" of ren, present innately in all humans and requiring cultivation. Together with righteousness (yi), propriety (li), and wisdom (zhi), ren forms the core of Confucian ethics, and the junzi, or exemplary person, is one who has fully cultivated it.
