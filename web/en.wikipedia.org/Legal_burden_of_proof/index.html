Burden of proof (law)

In law, the burden of proof is the obligation on a party in a trial to produce evidence sufficient to support their case. It conventionally comprises two components: the burden of production (the duty to introduce enough evidence on an issue to reach the fact-finder) and the burden of persuasion (the duty to convince the fact-finder that one's account is true to the required standard).

Standards of proof

In criminal cases, the prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt, and the burden generally rests on the prosecution throughout, consistent with the presumption of innocence. In most civil cases, the claimant must prove their case on the balance of probabilities (a preponderance of the evidence). Intermediate standards, such as clear and convincing evidence, apply in some civil and quasi-criminal matters.

Shifting burdens

While the legal (persuasion) burden usually remains fixed, the evidential burden may shift during trial as evidence is presented; defences such as insanity or self-defence may place some burden on the defendant in certain jurisdictions.
