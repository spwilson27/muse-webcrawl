Historical memory

Related conceptsCollective memory, cultural memory, lieux de mémoire
Key scholarsMaurice Halbwachs, Pierre Nora, Jan Assmann

Historical memory refers to the way groups, institutions, and societies remember, represent, and use the past. It is closely related to the concept of collective memory introduced by Maurice Halbwachs, who argued that individual recollection is framed by social groups, and to Pierre Nora's notion of lieux de mémoire (sites of memory), places and symbols where memory crystallises.

Study and concepts

Scholars distinguish between communicative memory, carried by living witnesses, and cultural memory, preserved in texts, rituals, monuments, and archives. Historical memory is shaped by commemoration, education, museums, and media, and it can differ sharply from the findings of academic historiography.

Politics of memory

Because memory underpins identity and legitimacy, states and movements contest how events — wars, revolutions, atrocities, foundings — are remembered. "Memory laws", official apologies, truth commissions, and debates over monuments are all arenas in which historical memory is negotiated and fought over.
