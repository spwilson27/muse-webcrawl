Chicago house
Stylistic originsDisco, post-disco, funk, synth-pop
Cultural originsEarly 1980s, Chicago, Illinois
Key figuresFrankie Knuckles, Larry Heard, Marshall Jefferson
Derivative formsAcid house, deep house, techno

Chicago house is a style of electronic dance music that emerged in Chicago in the early 1980s, widely regarded as the first form of house music. It developed in clubs such as the Warehouse — where resident DJ Frankie Knuckles gave the genre its name — from disco, funk and European synth-pop, reworked with drum machines and synthesisers.

Sound and history

The classic sound rests on a four-on-the-floor kick drum, offbeat hi-hats, Roland TR-808 and TR-909 drum machines, TB-303 basslines, piano riffs and gospel-tinged vocals. Affordable equipment let producers like Jesse Saunders ("On and On", 1984), Marshall Jefferson ("Move Your Body", 1986) and Larry Heard, as Mr. Fingers ("Can You Feel It"), press records on labels such as Trax Records and DJ International. The 1986–87 acid house explosion, driven by Phuture's "Acid Tracks", carried the sound to Britain, where it ignited the rave era.

Legacy

Chicago house spawned deep house, garage house and influenced Detroit techno and most subsequent electronic dance music. The city honoured Knuckles with a street name, Frankie Knuckles Way, in 2004, and house music remains central to Chicago's cultural identity.
