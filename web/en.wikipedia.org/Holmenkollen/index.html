Holmenkollen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Holmenkollbakken ski jump, rebuilt in 2010, is a symbol of Oslo.

Holmenkollen is a mountain and a neighbourhood in the Vestre Aker borough of Oslo, Norway. It is famous worldwide as a venue for skiing competitions — above all the Holmenkollbakken ski jump and the annual Holmenkollen Ski Festival — and as the site of the world's oldest ski museum.

The ski venue

Skiing competitions have been held at Holmenkollen since 1892, making the Holmenkollen Ski Festival one of the oldest continuously held ski events in the world. The first ski jump on the site, the Holmenkollbakken, opened in 1892 and has been rebuilt many times; the present steel structure, designed by JDS Architects and opened in 2010 ahead of the 2011 FIS Nordic World Ski Championships, has a K-point of 120 metres and a distinctive cantilevered form. Holmenkollen has hosted the Winter Olympics of 1952 and the Nordic World Championships several times (1930, 1966, 1982, 2011), and the area also hosted biathlon World Cup events and the 2016 Biathlon World Championships.

Ski museum and other features

Inside the ski jump is the Holmenkollen Ski Museum, founded in 1923 and considered the world's oldest museum dedicated to skiing, with exhibits spanning some 4,000 years of skiing history. Nearby stand the Holmenkollen Chapel (rebuilt after a 1992 arson attack), the statue of the explorer Fridtjof Nansen, and the towering statue of King Olav V, a keen skier. The area, served by the Holmenkollen Line of the Oslo Metro, is also a popular recreation area with views over the city and the Oslofjord, and a starting point for the forests of Nordmarka.
