Complete graph

In the mathematical field of graph theory, a complete graph is a simple undirected graph in which every pair of distinct vertices is connected by a unique edge. The complete graph on n vertices is denoted Kn. It has n(n − 1)/2 edges — a triangular number — and is a regular graph of degree n − 1.

Properties

All complete graphs are their own maximal cliques. Kn is planar only for n ≤ 4; by Kuratowski's theorem, K5 and the complete bipartite graph K3,3 are the fundamental non-planar graphs. The complement of a complete graph is an empty graph. Complete graphs are highly symmetric: their automorphism group acts transitively on vertices and edges.

Examples and uses

K1 is a single vertex, K2 a single edge, K3 a triangle, and K4 the graph of the tetrahedron. Complete graphs arise in Ramsey theory — the Ramsey number R(3,3) = 6 states that any red–blue coloring of the edges of K6 contains a monochromatic triangle — and as models of fully connected networks in computer science and communications.

Counting

The number of matchings, spanning trees, and Hamiltonian cycles in complete graphs are classical results: by Cayley's formula, Kn has nn−2 spanning trees.
