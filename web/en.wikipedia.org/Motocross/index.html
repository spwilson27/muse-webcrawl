Motocross

Motocross is a form of off-road motorcycle racing held on enclosed circuits of dirt, mud, sand or grass featuring jumps, banked turns (berms), whoops and other obstacles. The name combines the French moto (motorcycle) with "cross country". Races ("motos") are run over a set time plus additional laps, and the combined results of two motos usually determine the overall winner.

History

Motocross evolved from British trials and scrambles competitions of the 1920s. After the Second World War it spread across Europe, and the FIM established a European championship in 1952, upgraded to a world championship in 1957. Belgian and Swedish riders dominated the early decades. Motocross was introduced to the United States in the late 1960s, where it spawned the stadium variant supercross from 1972, and American riders became dominant in the 1980s.

Equipment

Modern motocross bikes are lightweight machines with long-travel suspension, knobby tyres and engines of 125–250 cc (MX2) or 250–450 cc (MXGP/premier class), nowadays almost all four-stroke. Riders wear helmets, goggles, body armour, boots and neck braces.

Major competitions

Highlights of the calendar include the FIM Motocross World Championship, the AMA Pro Motocross Championship, the AMA Supercross series, and the annual Motocross des Nations, a national-team event contested since 1947.
