Banff National Park is Canada's oldest national park, established in 1885, and one of the world's most visited. Located in the Rocky Mountains of Alberta, about 110–180 km west of Calgary, the park covers 6,641 square kilometres of mountain terrain, glaciers, ice fields, alpine lakes and dense coniferous forest. Its best-known features include Lake Louise, Moraine Lake, the Icefields Parkway and the town of Banff itself.

History

The park originated in a dispute over ownership of the Cave and Basin hot springs, discovered by Canadian Pacific Railway workers in 1883. In 1885 the federal government reserved the area around the springs, and in 1887 the Rocky Mountains Park Act created a 673 km² reserve — Canada's first national park and the world's third, after Yellowstone and Australia's Royal National Park. The railway promoted the park as a tourist destination, building grand hotels such as the Banff Springs Hotel (1888) and Chateau Lake Louise. Indigenous peoples, including the Stoney Nakoda, had used the region for millennia before being excluded from the new park.

Geography and wildlife

The park spans the Continental Divide and includes part of the Columbia Icefield, source of rivers flowing to three oceans. Elevations range from about 1,383 m at the eastern boundary to 3,618 m at Mount Columbia's vicinity (Mount Forbes and other peaks exceed 3,300 m). Wildlife includes grizzly and black bears, elk, moose, bighorn sheep, mountain goats, wolves and cougars. The Trans-Canada Highway corridor through the park is fenced and provided with wildlife crossing structures, including the world's largest system of highway wildlife overpasses.

Tourism

Banff receives over four million visitors a year, supporting skiing at Sunshine Village, Lake Louise and Mount Norquay, hiking, and sightseeing along the Bow Valley and Icefields Parkways. Together with Jasper, Yoho and Kootenay national parks, it forms the Canadian Rocky Mountain Parks UNESCO World Heritage Site.
