Adalbert of Prague

Born: c. 956, Libice nad Cidlinou, Bohemia
Died: 23 April 997, Truso, Prussia
Venerated in: Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox Church
Feast: 23 April

Adalbert of Prague (Latin: Adalbertus; Polish: Wojciech; c. 956 – 23 April 997), known in the Czech lands as Vojtěch, was a Bishop of Prague and a missionary to the Hungarians, Poles, and Prussians, who was martyred in his efforts to convert the Baltic Prussians. He was later made the patron saint of Bohemia, Poland, Hungary, and Prussia.

Life

Born into the Slavník dynasty, Adalbert studied in Magdeburg under Adalbert of Magdeburg, whose name he took at confirmation. He became Bishop of Prague in 982, but conflicts over his reforming zeal and the massacre of his family by the rival Přemyslids drove him into exile in Rome. King Bolesław I of Poland supported his mission to Prussia.

Martyrdom and legacy

Adalbert was killed by pagan Prussians on 23 April 997 near Truso. Bolesław ransomed his body, reportedly for its weight in gold, and interred it at Gniezno Cathedral. He was canonized by Pope Sylvester II in 999, and his cult became central to Polish and Czech national identity.
