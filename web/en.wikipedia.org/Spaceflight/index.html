Spaceflight

Spaceflight (or space flight) is the application of astronautics to fly objects, usually spacecraft, into or through outer space, either with or without humans on board. Spaceflight is used for space exploration, Earth observation, communications, navigation, and increasingly commercial activities such as space tourism. Most spaceflight is uncrewed, carried out by satellites and robotic probes.

History

The theoretical foundations were laid by Konstantin Tsiolkovsky, Robert Goddard and Hermann Oberth in the early twentieth century. Spaceflight became practical after World War II, driven by military rocket development. The Soviet Union launched the first artificial satellite, Sputnik 1, in 1957, and the first human, Yuri Gagarin, in 1961. The United States landed the first humans on the Moon with Apollo 11 in 1969. Since then, space stations, reusable vehicles such as the Space Shuttle, and robotic missions throughout the Solar System have followed.

Principles

Reaching space requires a rocket capable of achieving orbital velocity — about 7.8 km/s for low Earth orbit — because, as Isaac Newton imagined with his cannonball thought experiment, an orbit is a state of continuous free fall around a planet. The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation governs the trade-offs between propellant, vehicle mass and velocity change (delta-v). Spacecraft navigate using celestial and inertial references and communicate by radio links such as NASA's Deep Space Network. Crewed flight additionally requires life support, radiation protection and safe re-entry through the atmosphere, where aerodynamic heating demands heat shields or thermal protection systems.

Applications and future

Thousands of satellites serve communications, weather forecasting, navigation (such as GPS) and Earth observation, while large constellations now provide global broadband. Commercial companies have reduced launch costs with reusable first stages. Current goals include returning humans to the Moon, eventual crewed missions to Mars, and expanding private spaceflight.
