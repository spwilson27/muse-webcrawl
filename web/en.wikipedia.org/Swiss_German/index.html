Swiss German

Swiss German (German: Schweizerdeutsch, Alemannic: Schwiizerdütsch) is any of the Alemannic dialects spoken in the German-speaking part of Switzerland, spoken natively by roughly two-thirds of the Swiss population. The dialects are the everyday spoken language, while Swiss Standard German is used for most writing, schooling, and formal contexts — a classic case of diglossia.

Dialects

Swiss German is not a single language but a group of dialects, including Zürich German, Bernese German, Lucerne German, and the highly distinctive Highest Alemannic dialects of the Valais and Bernese Oberland. Dialects differ in pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammar, though mutual intelligibility is generally high within Switzerland. Unlike in Germany and Austria, the dialects are spoken by all social classes.

Usage

Dialect dominates conversation, broadcasting entertainment, and much local media; Standard German is used in news broadcasts, education, and official writing. Swiss Standard German itself differs from German Standard German in vocabulary (e.g. Velo for bicycle), pronunciation, and the absence of ß (replaced by ss).

Writing

There is no standardised Swiss German orthography; informal writing (text messages, social media) spells words phonetically by dialect. A small body of dialect literature exists.
