Pen-y-ghent

Elevation694 m (2,277 ft)
LocationYorkshire Dales, England
Parent rangePennines

Pen-y-ghent is a fells in the Yorkshire Dales National Park in northern England. At 694 metres (2,277 ft) it is the lowest of the Yorkshire Three Peaks, the others being Whernside and Ingleborough, and it forms part of the gruelling Three Peaks Challenge walk of about 24 miles.

Geography

The hill rises above the village of Horton in Ribblesdale. Its name derives from Cumbric words generally interpreted as "hill on the border" or "hill of the winds". The summit shows a distinctive profile: stepped terraces of gritstone capping sloping beds of Carboniferous limestone, the result of differential erosion of alternating hard and soft rock layers.

Geology and features

Pen-y-ghent is notable for its limestone pavement and for Hull Pot, a large collapsed cavern on its flank, and Hunt Pot nearby. The Pennine Way long-distance footpath crosses the mountain, ascending from Horton in Ribblesdale — a famously steep section of the route.
