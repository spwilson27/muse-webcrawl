Jezero is a crater on Mars in the Syrtis Major quadrangle, about 49 km (30 mi) in diameter. It was selected as the landing site for NASA's Mars 2020 mission, whose rover Perseverance touched down there on 18 February 2021 at the site named Octavia E. Butler Landing.

Ancient lake

Orbital images show that Jezero once held a lake, fed by inlet valleys and drained by an outlet channel. At its western rim lies a prominent river delta rich in clays and carbonates, formed as sediment-laden water entered the crater lake roughly 3.6–3.8 billion years ago. Because such lakebed and delta deposits on Earth readily preserve signs of life, Jezero was judged an ideal place to search for evidence of ancient Martian microbial life and to collect samples of a once-habitable environment.

Mars 2020 exploration

Perseverance has explored the crater floor's igneous rocks, traversed the delta front, and drilled and sealed rock and regolith samples intended for eventual return to Earth by a future sample-return campaign. The crater's name, approved in 2007, comes from the town of Jezero in Bosnia and Herzegovina; jezero means "lake" in several Slavic languages.
