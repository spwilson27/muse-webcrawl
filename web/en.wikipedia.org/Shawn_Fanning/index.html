Shawn Fanning

Shawn Fanning (born November 22, 1980) is an American computer programmer and entrepreneur best known as the creator of Napster, the first widely used peer-to-peer file-sharing platform.

Napster

While a student at Northeastern University in Boston, Fanning wrote Napster in 1999 to let users easily share MP3 music files. Named after his hairstyle nickname, the service grew to tens of millions of users within a year and provoked landmark copyright litigation from the recording industry and artists including Metallica. Napster was shut down in 2001 following court orders, and its bankruptcy and brand sale followed.

Later ventures

Fanning subsequently co-founded SNOCAP, a digital rights and licensing venture; Rupture, a social network for gamers acquired by Electronic Arts; and Airtime, a video chat service launched with Sean Parker. He remains a notable figure in the history of digital music and internet entrepreneurship.
