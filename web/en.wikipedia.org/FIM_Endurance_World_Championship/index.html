The FIM Endurance World Championship (EWC) is the premier worldwide championship series for motorcycle endurance racing, sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM). Teams of riders share a single production-based motorcycle over long-distance races lasting 8 or 24 hours, combining speed with mechanical reliability and pit-stop efficiency.

History

The championship descends from the Coupe d'Endurance, founded in 1960, and gained world championship status in 1980. Following periods with different promoters, Warner Bros. Discovery Sports took over promotion of the series in 2015 and reshaped the calendar into a season spanning two calendar years before returning to a single-year format. Races have been staged across Europe, Japan, and the Middle East.

Races

The calendar is anchored by classic events: the 24 Heures Motos at Le Mans in France, the Bol d'Or 24-hour race at Circuit Paul Ricard, the 8 Hours of Spa Motos at Spa-Francorchamps, and the Suzuka 8 Hours in Japan — one of the most prestigious motorcycle races in the world, historically attracting factory MotoGP-calibre riders. Races award points based on finishing position, with bonus points at intermediate marks in the 24-hour events.

Classes and teams

The top Formula EWC class allows extensively developed factory machines, while the Superstock class runs near-production motorcycles. Factory-backed squads such as Suzuki Endurance Racing Team (SERT), YART Yamaha, F.C.C. TSR Honda France, and Yoshimura SERT Motul have dominated recent championships, with Suzuki's endurance outfit among the most successful teams in the sport's history.
