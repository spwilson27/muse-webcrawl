The University of California (UC) is a public land-grant research university system in the U.S. state of California. Headquartered in Oakland, the system is composed of ten campuses — Berkeley, Davis, Irvine, Los Angeles, Merced, Riverside, San Diego, San Francisco, Santa Barbara, and Santa Cruz — along with numerous research centers, medical centers, and affiliated national laboratories. The University of California enrolls roughly 295,000 students and employs about 25,000 faculty members, making it one of the largest university systems in the world.

The system was founded on March 23, 1868, when Governor Henry Haight signed the Organic Act creating the university from the merger of the private College of California and the public Agricultural, Mining, and Mechanical Arts College. Its first campus was established in Berkeley. Under the California Master Plan for Higher Education of 1960, the University of California was designated the state's primary research institution, drawing students from the top one-eighth of California high school graduates.

History

The university grew out of the College of California, founded in 1855, which transferred its land and assets to the new public institution. Benjamin Ide Wheeler, president from 1899 to 1919, greatly expanded the university's size and prestige. The Los Angeles campus, founded in 1919 as the Southern Branch, became UCLA. After the Second World War, the system expanded rapidly: Santa Barbara joined in 1944, Davis became a general campus in 1959, and Riverside, San Diego, Irvine, and Santa Cruz were founded or converted in the 1950s and 1960s. UC Merced, opened in 2005, is the first American research university founded in the 21st century.

Campuses

  • Berkeley (1868) — the flagship campus, known for its research across the sciences and humanities.
  • San Francisco (1864) — a graduate-only health sciences campus.
  • Los Angeles (1919) — UCLA, among the most applied-to universities in the United States.
  • Davis (1905) — noted for agriculture, veterinary medicine, and medicine.
  • San Diego (1960) — founded around the Scripps Institution of Oceanography.
  • Riverside (1954), Santa Barbara (1891), Santa Cruz (1965), Irvine (1965), and Merced (2005).

Governance

The university is governed by the Regents of the University of California, a 26-member board largely appointed by the governor of California. The president of the University of California serves as its chief executive. The system's shared governance model gives its Academic Senate substantial authority over admissions and curricula.

Research and impact

UC faculty and researchers have won dozens of Nobel Prizes, and the system manages or co-manages three U.S. Department of Energy national laboratories: Lawrence Berkeley, Lawrence Livermore, and Los Alamos. UC inventions generate hundreds of patents annually, and its five medical centers provide care across California. The system's motto, Fiat lux ("Let there be light"), appears on its seal alongside an open book and the date 1868.
