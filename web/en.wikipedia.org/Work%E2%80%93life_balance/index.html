Work–life balance

Work–life balance refers to the equilibrium individuals seek between the demands of paid work and the other parts of life, including family, leisure, and personal development. The concept gained currency from the 1970s and 1980s as dual-earner households became common.

History

Earlier campaigns for the eight-hour day framed the issue as "eight hours labour, eight hours recreation, eight hours rest." Modern discussion addresses long-hours cultures, unpaid care work (still disproportionately performed by women), and the blurring of boundaries by digital communication.

Policy and practice

Responses include flexible working, parental leave, limits on working time such as the European Working Time Directive, four-day-week trials, and "right to disconnect" laws adopted in France and elsewhere.

Criticism

Critics note that the metaphor of separate "work" and "life" spheres may obscure structural issues of workload and job design, and that balance norms vary strongly by culture and occupation.
