Sound design

Sound design is the art and practice of creating soundtracks for a variety of needs. It involves the specification, acquisition, or creation of auditory elements using audio production techniques and tools. It is employed in a variety of disciplines including filmmaking, television production, video game development, theatre, live performance, sound art, post-production, radio, and musical instrument development.

Film and television

In film, the sound designer works with the director and supervising sound editor to craft the sonic world of a production — dialogue, effects, Foley, ambience, and designed sounds that may have no natural source, such as lightsabers or dinosaur vocalisations. The title was first used prominently by Francis Ford Coppola for Walter Murch's work on Apocalypse Now (1979). Ben Burtt's work for Star Wars (1977) is a famous example of creating iconic sounds from manipulated recordings of the real world.

Games and interactive media

In video games, sound design must respond dynamically to player action, using middleware such as Wwise and FMOD to trigger, layer, and process audio in real time.

Theatre and product sound

Theatre sound designers compose effects and reinforce voices for live performance; industrial sound designers shape the sounds made by products, alarms, and user interfaces. The Academy Award for Best Sound and the former Best Sound Editing category have recognised the craft's importance to cinema.
