California Community Colleges

The California Community Colleges is a postsecondary education system in California, comprising 116 colleges organised into 73 districts, serving roughly 2 million students. It is the largest system of higher education in the United States.

History

The system traces to 1907 state legislation enabling high schools to offer postgraduate 'junior college' courses; Fresno City College (1910) is among the oldest. The 1960 Master Plan assigned community colleges open-access transfer and vocational missions, and the 1967 creation of a Board of Governors formalised the system.

Mission

Colleges provide transfer preparation for the University of California and California State University, career technical education, basic skills, workforce training, and lifelong learning. Many now offer bachelor's degrees in specific workforce fields under state pilot-turned-permanent programmes.

Governance and funding

A statewide Chancellor's Office coordinates the system; local boards govern districts. Proposition 98 guarantees a share of state revenue. Tuition for California residents is among the lowest in the nation, and the California College Promise Grant waives fees for many students.
