Wrestling mask

A wrestling mask is a fabric-based mask that some professional wrestlers wear as part of their in-ring persona or gimmick. In Mexico, masked wrestlers (known as enmascarados) are a central feature of lucha libre, where the mask holds deep cultural and personal significance. Masked wrestlers have also been prominent in Japan and appear in promotions worldwide.

In lucha libre

The mask is considered almost sacred in Mexican wrestling culture; removing an opponent's mask during a match is a serious offence that can result in disqualification. Famous masked luchadores include El Santo, Blue Demon, and Mil Máscaras. Masks often feature distinctive designs — wings, horns, crosses, or animal motifs — representing the wrestler's character.

Luchas de apuestas

In luchas de apuestas (betting matches), wrestlers wager their mask (máscara contra máscara) or their hair (máscara contra cabellera). The loser of a mask-versus-mask match must unmask publicly and is traditionally never permitted to wear that mask again; such matches are among the most prestigious in lucha libre and can draw record crowds.

Elsewhere

Masked wrestlers have also been popular in Japan (such as Tiger Mask and Jushin Thunder Liger) and in the United States, where masked performers include Rey Mysterio, who brought lucha libre traditions to a global audience.
