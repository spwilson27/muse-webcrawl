Lake Oroville is a reservoir formed by the Oroville Dam impounding the Feather River in the Sierra Nevada foothills of Butte County, California. It is the keystone reservoir of the California State Water Project, storing water for delivery to the Sacramento Valley, the San Francisco Bay Area, the San Joaquin Valley, and Southern California.

Dam and powerhouse

Oroville Dam, completed in 1968, is the tallest earthfill dam in the United States at 770 feet (235 m). The Edward Hyatt Powerplant at its base is one of the largest underground powerhouses in the country.

2017 spillway crisis

In February 2017, after heavy storms, erosion damage to the dam's main spillway and emergency spillway prompted the evacuation of nearly 190,000 downstream residents. The spillway was subsequently rebuilt at a cost of about $1.1 billion.

Recreation

The Lake Oroville State Recreation Area offers boating, fishing (notably for bass and Chinook salmon), camping, and houseboating; the lake's shoreline is about 167 miles at full pool.
