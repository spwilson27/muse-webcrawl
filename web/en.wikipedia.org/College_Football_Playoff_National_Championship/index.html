College Football Playoff National Championship

The College Football Playoff National Championship is a post-season college football game in the United States that determines the national champion of NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS). It is the final game of the College Football Playoff (CFP) and has been played annually since the 2014 season, when the first championship game was held in January 2015 at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas.

Format

From 2014 to 2023 the playoff involved four teams selected by a committee, playing two semi-final games hosted by rotating New Year's Six bowl games. Beginning with the 2024 season the playoff expanded to twelve teams, with the five highest-ranked conference champions and seven at-large teams; the top four seeds receive first-round byes. The championship game itself is held at a neutral site chosen by bid, separate from the bowl system.

History

The CFP replaced the Bowl Championship Series (BCS), which had matched the top two teams in a single title game from 1998 to 2013. Ohio State won the inaugural CFP championship, defeating Oregon. Alabama, under Nick Saban, won multiple titles in the four-team era, and other champions have included Clemson, LSU, Georgia and Michigan. The winner receives the College Football Playoff National Championship Trophy.
