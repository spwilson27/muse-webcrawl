The Indian general election of 1977 was held in March 1977 to elect the sixth Lok Sabha. It followed the Emergency (1975–1977) imposed by Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, and resulted in a decisive defeat for her Indian National Congress — the first time Congress lost power at the centre since independence.

Background

During the Emergency, civil liberties were suspended, opposition leaders jailed, and press censorship imposed. Excesses including forced sterilisation campaigns generated widespread resentment, especially in northern India. In January 1977 Gandhi unexpectedly called elections and released political prisoners.

Campaign and results

Opposition parties merged into the Janata Party, allied with the Congress for Democracy of Jagjivan Ram. Janata and its allies won about 330 seats; Congress fell to 154, and Indira Gandhi herself lost Rae Bareli to Raj Narain. Congress retained strength mainly in the south, where Emergency excesses had been milder.

Aftermath

Morarji Desai became India's first non-Congress prime minister. The Janata government restored civil liberties and amended constitutional provisions used during the Emergency, but internal rivalries led to its collapse in 1979, and Congress returned to power in the 1980 election.
