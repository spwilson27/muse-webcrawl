Choctawhatchee Bay

Choctawhatchee Bay is a bay in the Emerald Coast region of the Florida Panhandle, in the northwestern part of the U.S. state of Florida. The bay is located within Okaloosa and Walton counties, and has a surface area of approximately 129 square miles (334 km²). It is connected to the Gulf of Mexico through East Pass near Destin, and is fed principally by the Choctawhatchee River, which drains a large watershed in Alabama and Florida.

Geography

The bay stretches roughly 27 miles (43 km) from east to west. Communities along its shores include Destin, Fort Walton Beach, Niceville, Valparaiso, and Freeport. The Mid-Bay Bridge carries State Road 293 across the bay between Destin and Niceville. Santa Rosa Island and the Okaloosa Island barrier separate much of the bay's southern shore from the Gulf of Mexico.

Ecology

Choctawhatchee Bay is an estuary supporting seagrass beds, salt marshes, and oyster habitat. It is home to dolphins, manatees, sea turtles, and a wide variety of fish, and it supports endangered species such as the Gulf sturgeon and the Okaloosa darter. The bay's health is monitored by organisations including the Choctawhatchee Basin Alliance, which works on water quality, shoreline restoration, and oyster reef projects.

Recreation

The bay is popular for boating, fishing, kayaking, and paddleboarding. Its sheltered waters contrast with the surf of the nearby Gulf beaches, and marinas and public boat ramps line much of its shoreline.
