Rudolf, Crown Prince of Austria

Rudolf, Crown Prince of Austria (21 August 1858 – 30 January 1889) was the only son and third child of Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria and Empress Elisabeth, and heir apparent to the throne of Austria-Hungary. His death by suicide at his hunting lodge at Mayerling, where he shot his lover, the 17-year-old Baroness Mary Vetsera, and himself in 1889 — the Mayerling incident — made headlines worldwide and permanently altered the Habsburg succession.

Life

Raised under a harsh military-style education before his liberal-minded tutor Joseph Latour von Thurmburg softened his upbringing, Rudolf developed intellectual interests — natural history, ornithology and liberal politics — that set him at odds with his conservative father. He published anonymous articles criticising aristocratic privilege and travelled widely. In 1881 he married Princess Stéphanie of Belgium; the marriage was unhappy, though it produced a daughter, Archduchess Elisabeth Marie. Rudolf suffered from ill health and depression, compounded by alcohol and morphine use.

Mayerling and succession

On 30 January 1889 Rudolf and Mary Vetsera were found shot dead at Mayerling. The court first announced a cover story of heart failure, then conceded suicide; theories of murder have persisted ever since, though suicide remains the scholarly consensus. Rudolf's death left his cousin Archduke Franz Ferdinand as heir presumptive — whose assassination at Sarajevo in 1914 precipitated the First World War. The Mayerling story has inspired ballets, plays, operas and films, including the ballet Mayerling (1978) choreographed by Kenneth MacMillan.
