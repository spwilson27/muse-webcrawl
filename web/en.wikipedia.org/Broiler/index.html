Broiler

Broiler chickens near market weight

A broiler is any chicken raised specifically for meat production. Modern broilers, produced by specialised breeding companies, reach slaughter weight of about 2–3 kg in five to seven weeks — a growth rate achieved through decades of selective breeding for feed efficiency, breast yield, and rapid growth. Broilers are the most numerous farmed birds in the world, with tens of billions raised annually.

Breeding

The industry rests on crossbreeding: grandparent and parent stock supplied by a handful of breeding firms (whose strains include the Ross 308 and Cobb 500) produce the commercial birds. Breeding emphasises feed conversion ratio — modern broilers convert roughly 1.5–1.9 kg of feed per kg of live weight — but selection for growth has been criticised for associated health problems in breeder birds, which must be feed-restricted.

Production

Most broilers are raised in large climate-controlled barns on litter, free to roam the floor of the house, at stocking densities regulated in some jurisdictions; others are produced in free-range, organic, or cage-free systems with slower-growing breeds. Feed is typically a grain-and-soy diet phased from starter to finisher. Birds are caught, transported, and processed on automated lines.

Welfare and criticism

Rapid growth is linked to leg disorders, cardiovascular problems such as ascites, and contact dermatitis; stocking density, lighting, and transport are also welfare concerns. Industry and retailers have responded with assurance schemes and commitments — such as the Better Chicken Commitment — promoting slower-growing breeds and enriched environments, while per-capita chicken consumption continues to rise globally because of its low cost and low feed footprint relative to red meat.
