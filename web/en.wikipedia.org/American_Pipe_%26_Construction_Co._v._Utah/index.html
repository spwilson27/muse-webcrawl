American Pipe & Construction Co. v. Utah

American Pipe & Construction Co. v. Utah
Court: Supreme Court of the United States
Decided: 1974
Citation: 414 U.S. 538
Area of law: Class actions; statutes of limitations

American Pipe & Construction Co. v. Utah, 414 U.S. 538 (1974), is a United States Supreme Court decision concerning class action lawsuits. The Court held that the timely filing of a class action tolls the applicable statute of limitations for all persons encompassed by the class complaint — a rule known as American Pipe tolling.

Background

The State of Utah sued American Pipe and others under the Sherman Act, alleging a conspiracy in the sale of concrete and steel pipe. After class certification was denied, individuals who would have been class members sought to intervene, raising the question whether the limitations period had been suspended while the class action was pending.

Holding

The Court ruled that the commencement of a class action tolls the statute of limitations as to all asserted members of the class who would have been parties had the action been permitted to continue as a class action, reasoning that otherwise putative class members would be forced to file protective individual suits, defeating the efficiency purposes of Rule 23.

Significance

The doctrine was extended in later cases to plaintiffs who file separate actions after certification is denied, and it remains a frequently litigated feature of federal class action practice.
