Theology

Theology is the systematic study of the nature of the divine, or more broadly of religious belief. It is taught in universities, seminaries, and divinity schools, and draws on scripture, tradition, reason, and experience.

Branches

Sub-disciplines include biblical studies and exegesis, historical theology, systematic (or dogmatic) theology, moral theology, pastoral theology, and comparative theology across religions. Within Christianity, topics include the doctrine of God, Christology, soteriology, ecclesiology, and eschatology.

History

The term, from Greek theos (god) and logos (study), was used by Plato and Aristotle before being adopted by Christian writers. Medieval scholasticism — Anselm, Aquinas, Duns Scotus — sought to harmonise faith and reason; modern theology has been reshaped by historical criticism, liberation theology, and feminist and postcolonial approaches.
