John F. Kennedy Presidential Library and Museum

The John F. Kennedy Presidential Library and Museum is the presidential library and museum of John F. Kennedy, the 35th president of the United States (1961–1963). Located on Columbia Point in the Dorchester neighborhood of Boston, Massachusetts, the building was designed by architect I. M. Pei and dedicated on October 20, 1979. The library houses the papers and correspondence of President Kennedy and of other figures of his administration, and the museum exhibits trace his life and presidency.

Collections and exhibits

Holdings include the papers of Kennedy and Robert F. Kennedy, the Ernest Hemingway Collection of manuscripts and letters, and extensive audiovisual archives. Permanent exhibits cover the 1960 campaign, the space race, the Cuban Missile Crisis, the Peace Corps, and Jacqueline Kennedy's restoration of the White House; the museum displays items such as the coconut shell bearing Kennedy's PT-109 rescue message. The library presents the annual Profile in Courage Award and hosts public forums on history and politics.

History

Kennedy selected a site near Harvard in 1963, but after his assassination the project was delayed and the site changed to Columbia Point, next to the University of Massachusetts Boston. Construction, financed by private donations (many small contributions solicited after 1963), began in 1977.
