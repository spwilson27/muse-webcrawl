Yucca moths
FamilyProdoxidae
Key generaTegeticula, Prodoxus
Host plantsYucca and Joshua tree

Yucca moths are small moths of the family Prodoxidae, best known for their obligate mutualism with yucca plants of North America. Each species of pollinating yucca moth (genus Tegeticula) is associated with particular yucca species, and the plant depends entirely on the moth for pollination, while the moth's larvae feed on some of the plant's developing seeds.

The mutualism

Female moths possess specialised tentacle-like mouthparts used to gather a ball of pollen from a yucca flower. After laying eggs in the ovary of another flower, the female deliberately presses the pollen ball onto the stigma, fertilising the plant. The larvae then consume a fraction of the resulting seeds before burrowing out and pupating in the soil. Because neither partner can reproduce effectively without the other, the relationship is a classic textbook example of coevolution and obligate mutualism.

Cheaters

Some related moths, including "bogus yucca moths" (Prodoxus) and certain Tegeticula species, exploit the system by laying eggs in flowers or fruit without pollinating, and have been studied to understand how mutualisms remain stable against cheating.
