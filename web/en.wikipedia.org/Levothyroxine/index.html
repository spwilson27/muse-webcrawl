Levothyroxine

Levothyroxine
Trade names: Synthroid, Levoxyl, Euthyrox
Class: Thyroid hormone
Formula: C15H11I4NO4

Levothyroxine, also known as L-thyroxine, is a synthetic form of the thyroid hormone thyroxine (T4). It is used to treat thyroid hormone deficiency (hypothyroidism), including the severe form known as myxedema coma, and certain types of thyroid tumours. It is taken by mouth or by injection into a vein.

Medical uses

Levothyroxine is the standard treatment for hypothyroidism, replacing hormone the thyroid gland no longer produces in sufficient quantity. Dosing is individualised and typically monitored using thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) blood tests. It is also used after thyroidectomy and as part of suppressive therapy for some thyroid cancers.

Side effects

Side effects are generally those of excess thyroid hormone when the dose is too high, including weight loss, tremor, headache, trouble sleeping, heat intolerance and palpitations. Severe adverse effects may include chest pain and arrhythmias. Use in pregnancy is generally considered safe at appropriate replacement doses.

History and society

Levothyroxine was first made in 1927. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is among the most commonly prescribed medications in the United States.
