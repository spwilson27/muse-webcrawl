Aga Khan

The Aga Khan is the hereditary Imam of the Nizari Ismaili Muslims.

Aga Khan is the title held by the Imam of the Nizari branch of Isma'ilism, the second-largest branch of Shia Islam. The current holder, the Aga Khan V (Prince Rahim al-Hussaini), succeeded his father in February 2025 as the 50th hereditary Imam of the Nizari Ismailis. The title dates from the early nineteenth century, when it was conferred on Hasan Ali Shah, the 46th Imam, by the Shah of Persia.

History of the title

Hasan Ali Shah (Aga Khan I, 1804–1881) was governor of Kirman in Persia before conflict with the Qajar court led him to flee to Afghanistan and then British India, where he assisted the British in Sindh and received a pension and princely recognition. His successors were Aga Khan II (Aqa Ali Shah, Imam 1881–1885) and Aga Khan III (Sir Sultan Muhammad Shah, 1877–1957), a founding figure of the All-India Muslim League, president of the League of Nations in 1937, and a major figure in the politics of British India and in racehorse breeding.

Aga Khan IV

Aga Khan IV (Prince Karim al-Hussaini, 1936–2025) succeeded his grandfather in 1957 while a student at Harvard. Over nearly seven decades he built the Aga Khan Development Network (AKDN), a group of agencies working in health, education, culture, rural development and economic development, principally in Africa and Asia. He founded the Aga Khan Award for Architecture, the Aga Khan University, and the Aga Khan Trust for Culture, and was a prominent owner and breeder of Thoroughbred racehorses. He died in Lisbon on 4 February 2025.

Aga Khan V

Prince Rahim al-Hussaini (born 1971), the eldest son of Aga Khan IV, was named the 50th Imam in his father's will and took office as Aga Khan V in February 2025. Before his accession he chaired the AKDN's environment and climate committee and served on boards of several of its agencies. The Ismaili community he leads numbers several million people across more than 25 countries, with historic concentrations in Central and South Asia, East Africa, Iran, Syria and, through migration, North America and Europe.
