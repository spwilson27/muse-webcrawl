Siegfried Sassoon

Born: 8 September 1886, Kent
Died: 1 September 1967, Wiltshire
Known for: War poetry

Siegfried Loraine Sassoon (8 September 1886 – 1 September 1967) was an English war poet, writer and soldier, renowned for his stark poetry of the First World War trenches and his public protest against the war in 1917.

War service

Sassoon served as an officer in the Royal Welch Fusiliers on the Western Front, earning the Military Cross for gallantry and the nickname 'Mad Jack' for reckless bravery. His poems, collected in The Old Huntsman (1917) and Counter-Attack (1918), attacked patriotic rhetoric with bitter realism.

Protest

In 1917 his 'Soldier's Declaration' against the prolongation of the war was read in Parliament. Rather than court-martial him, the army sent him to Craiglockhart War Hospital in Edinburgh, where he met Wilfred Owen, whom he mentored. He returned to service and was wounded again in 1918.

Later work

After the war Sassoon wrote the semi-autobiographical Fox-Hunting Man trilogy and other memoirs, and late religious poetry after converting to Catholicism in 1957.
