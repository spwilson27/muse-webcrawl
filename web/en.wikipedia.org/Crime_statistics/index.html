Crime statistics

Crime statistics
Methods: Police records, victim surveys, self-report
US sources: FBI UCR/NIBRS, NCVS
Key problem: Dark figure of crime

Crime statistics are systematic measurements of crime, collected to describe its extent, trends, and distribution and to guide policy. No single measure is complete, so criminologists triangulate among several sources.

Methods

Official statistics derive from police records, such as the FBI's Uniform Crime Reports and its incident-based successor NIBRS. Victimization surveys, such as the US National Crime Victimization Survey, interview households directly and capture crimes never reported to police. Self-report studies ask people, often juveniles, about their own offending.

Limitations

The gap between actual and recorded crime is called the "dark figure." Reporting rates vary by offense and over time, police recording practices change, and definitions differ between jurisdictions, complicating comparisons.
