Formerly: Statoil
Industry: Petroleum, energy
Founded: 1972
Headquarters: Stavanger, Norway
Owner: Government of Norway (67%)

Equinor ASA (formerly Statoil) is a Norwegian state-owned multinational energy company headquartered in Stavanger, Norway. It is primarily a petroleum company operating in 36 countries with additional investments in renewable energy. Equinor is the largest operator on the Norwegian continental shelf and is ranked among the world's largest oil and gas companies by revenue.

History

The company was founded in 1972 as Den Norske Stats Oljeselskap A/S (Statoil) following Norway's oil discoveries in the North Sea. It merged with the oil and gas division of Norsk Hydro in 2007. In 2018 the company changed its name from Statoil to Equinor, combining "equi" (equality, equilibrium) with "nor" for Norway, reflecting a broader energy strategy that includes offshore wind, hydrogen and carbon capture and storage.

Operations

Equinor operates major fields including Troll, Johan Sverdrup and Oseberg. Internationally it has activities in the United States, Brazil, the United Kingdom and elsewhere, and it is a major supplier of natural gas to Europe. Its renewables portfolio includes the Hywind Scotland floating wind farm and shares in large offshore wind projects such as Dogger Bank.
