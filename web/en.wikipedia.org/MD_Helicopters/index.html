MD Helicopters, Inc. (formerly McDonnell Douglas Helicopter Systems) is an American aerospace manufacturer headquartered at Falcon Field in Mesa, Arizona. It produces light single-engine and twin-engine helicopters for commercial and military use, and is best known for the MD 500 series and the NOTAR (no tail rotor) designs.

History

The company's lineage runs from Hughes Helicopters, whose Model 369 — flown by the U.S. Army as the OH-6 Cayuse — became the civil Hughes 500. Hughes Helicopters was acquired by McDonnell Douglas in 1984. When Boeing merged with McDonnell Douglas in 1997, European regulators objected to Boeing holding both the Apache line and the civil helicopter business; Boeing sold the civil lines in 1999 to the Dutch company RDM, which formed MD Helicopters. The company later passed through financial difficulties and investment ownership, with investment firm Bardin Hill acquiring it out of bankruptcy in 2022.

Products

The range includes the single-engine MD 500E, MD 520N, and MD 530F (marketed for military use as the MD 530G and, in its armed scout form derived from the Little Bird family), and the twin-engine MD 900/902 Explorer. Several models use the NOTAR system, in which a fan-driven flow of air through slots in the tail boom replaces the conventional tail rotor, reducing noise and improving safety.
