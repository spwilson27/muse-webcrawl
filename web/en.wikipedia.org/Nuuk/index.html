Nuuk (Greenlandic pronunciation: [nuːk]; Danish: Nuuk, formerly Godthåb, "Good Hope") is the capital and largest city of Greenland, an autonomous territory within the Kingdom of Denmark. Located at the mouth of the Nuup Kangerlua fjord system on Greenland's southwest coast, about 240 km south of the Arctic Circle, it is the world's smallest capital city by population and the cultural, economic, and governmental centre of the territory.

History

The area has been inhabited for millennia by Paleo-Inuit and later Thule cultures, and Norse settlers farmed nearby fjords from the 10th century. The modern town was founded in 1728 by the Dano-Norwegian missionary Hans Egede, who had come seeking the lost Norse colonies and instead began mission work among the Inuit. Under the name Godthåb it became the administrative centre of Danish Greenland; with the introduction of home rule in 1979 the official name became Nuuk, Greenlandic for "cape".

Geography and climate

Nuuk sits on a rocky peninsula at the head of a large fjord system, with the distinctive Sermitsiaq mountain rising across the water. Its subarctic climate is moderated by the West Greenland Current; winters are long and snowy, summers cool, and the harbour is usually ice-free year-round.

Government and culture

Nuuk hosts the Inatsisartut (parliament) and the Government of Greenland (Naalakkersuisut), the University of Greenland (Ilisimatusarfik), the National Museum, and the Katuaq cultural centre, whose undulating facade evokes the northern lights. The city's population—around a third of Greenland's total—has grown steadily, with large modern districts such as Qinngorput built since the 2000s.

Transport

Nuuk Airport was expanded and opened as an international airport in 2024, enabling direct transatlantic flights. The city is not connected by road to any other settlement; travel is by air and sea.
