Identification friend or foe

Identification, friend or foe (IFF) is a combat identification system designed for command and control. It uses a transponder that listens for an interrogation signal and then sends a response identifying the platform, enabling military and civilian air traffic control interrogation systems to identify aircraft, vehicles, or forces as friendly or neutral, and to determine their bearing and range.

History

IFF was developed during the Second World War. British systems (IFF Mark I–III) were used alongside radar; the Mark III became the standard Allied system. Post-war civil secondary surveillance radar grew from IFF Mark X/XII technology.

Modes

Civil modes A and C (and Mode S) report identity and altitude; military modes include Mode 4 and the encrypted Mode 5, which provides secure identification, position, and data exchange. Mode 5 replaced Mode 4 in NATO service.

Limitations

IFF identifies only cooperative, correctly responding contacts: absence of a friendly reply does not by itself prove a contact is hostile, a factor in several friendly-fire incidents.
