The 2012 French presidential election was held on 22 April and 6 May 2012. Incumbent President Nicolas Sarkozy of the Union for a Popular Movement (UMP) sought a second term but was defeated in the second round by François Hollande of the Socialist Party (PS), who won with 51.64% of the vote. Hollande became the second Socialist president of the Fifth Republic, after François Mitterrand, and the first incumbent president to be denied re-election since Valéry Giscard d'Estaing in 1981.

Ten candidates stood in the first round. Hollande led with 28.63% of the vote, ahead of Sarkozy on 27.18%. Marine Le Pen of the National Front achieved the party's best-ever presidential result with 17.90%, while Jean-Luc Mélenchon of the Left Front took 11.10% and centrist François Bayrou 9.13%.

Background

Nicolas Sarkozy had been elected in 2007 promising reform and a "rupture" with the past. His presidency was dominated by the global financial crisis of 2008 and the subsequent eurozone sovereign debt crisis. By 2011 his approval ratings had fallen to historic lows for a Fifth Republic president, although he retained a loyal base within the UMP. The Socialist Party selected its candidate through an open primary in October 2011 — the first of its kind in France — in which François Hollande defeated Martine Aubry in the second ballot. The candidacy of Dominique Strauss-Kahn, long the front-runner, had collapsed in May 2011 following his arrest in New York.

Campaign

Hollande campaigned on a programme of growth over austerity, proposing to renegotiate the European fiscal compact, hire 60,000 additional teachers, introduce a 75% top rate of income tax on earnings above one million euros, and lower the retirement age back to 60 for some workers. His slogan was "Le changement, c'est maintenant" ("Change is now"). Sarkozy campaigned on his experience in crisis management, border security, and opposition to immigration, moving rightwards in the second round to court National Front voters. The single televised debate between the two finalists, held on 2 May, was watched by roughly 17.8 million viewers; Hollande's repeated refrain "Moi président de la République…" became the debate's defining moment.

First round

First-round results map: Hollande led in most departments of the west and south-west; Le Pen in parts of the north and Mediterranean coast.

Voting took place on Sunday 22 April 2012, with turnout of 79.48%. Hollande finished first, marking the first time an incumbent president had been beaten into second place in the first round. Marine Le Pen's 6.4 million votes surpassed her father Jean-Marie Le Pen's best result. Bayrou's score halved compared to 2007, and Mélenchon's Left Front achieved one of the best results for a candidate to the left of the Socialists.

Second round

The run-off was held on 6 May 2012. Most eliminated candidates' endorsements split along partisan lines: Mélenchon and Green candidate Eva Joly backed Hollande, while Bayrou announced a personal vote for Hollande, breaking with his party's neutrality. Le Pen refused to endorse either candidate. Hollande won with 18.0 million votes to Sarkozy's 16.9 million, and took office on 15 May 2012, appointing Jean-Marc Ayrault as Prime Minister.

Aftermath

The Socialists went on to win the legislative elections of June 2012, giving Hollande a parliamentary majority. Sarkozy retired from active politics for a time before returning to lead the UMP (renamed The Republicans) in 2014. The 2012 election confirmed the National Front's durable place as the third force in French politics and was the last presidential election contested under the pre-2017 party system, before Emmanuel Macron's victory disrupted both major parties.
