Ohrid

Ohrid (Macedonian: Охрид) is a city in North Macedonia on the shore of Lake Ohrid, and the seat of Ohrid Municipality. With a population of around 39,000, it is one of the country's largest cities and its best-known tourist destination. Ohrid is notable for once having had 365 churches, one for each day of the year, and has been called the "Jerusalem of the Balkans". The city and the lake were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979–1980.

History

The ancient city of Lychnidos stood on this site, probably founded by the Phoenicians or Illyrian tribes and later ruled by Macedon and Rome. In the late ninth and tenth centuries, Ohrid became a centre of Slavic learning under Saints Clement and Naum of Ohrid, disciples of Cyril and Methodius; the Ohrid Literary School produced thousands of students and helped spread the Cyrillic script. Samuel's Fortress, towering over the old town, recalls the era when Ohrid was capital of Samuel's empire. The city later passed through Byzantine, Serbian, and Ottoman rule.

Landmarks

Major sights include the Church of St. Sophia, the Church of St. John at Kaneo perched on a cliff over the lake, the ancient theatre, the Plaošnik archaeological site, and Samoil's Fortress. Lake Ohrid, shared with Albania, is one of Europe's oldest and deepest lakes, home to the prized Ohrid trout and the pearl-making tradition of local jewellers.
