Globus Toolkit

The Globus Toolkit is an open-source software toolkit used for building grid computing systems and applications. It was developed by the Globus Alliance — principally Argonne National Laboratory and the University of Southern California's Information Sciences Institute, with key figures including Ian Foster and Carl Kesselman — and became the de facto standard middleware for computational grids in science from the late 1990s.

Overview

The toolkit provided the core services a grid needs: security through the Grid Security Infrastructure (GSI), based on public-key certificates and proxy credentials; resource management through GRAM; high-performance, secure file transfer through GridFTP; and information and monitoring services. GT3 and GT4 aligned the architecture with web services standards (OGSA/WSRF).

Legacy

Globus underpinned major science infrastructures such as the Worldwide LHC Computing Grid and national research grids. In 2018 the open-source toolkit was end-of-lifed as the Globus project pivoted to a hosted, software-as-a-service platform for research data management operated by the University of Chicago.
