Palermo

Palermo (Sicilian: Palermu) is a city in southern Italy, the capital of both the autonomous region of Sicily and the Metropolitan City of Palermo. The city is noted for its history, culture, architecture and gastronomy, playing an important role throughout much of its existence; it is over 2,700 years old. Palermo is in the northwest of the island of Sicily, by the Gulf of Palermo in the Tyrrhenian Sea.

History

Palermo was founded by the Phoenicians around 736 BC under the name Zyz ("flower"). It passed to Carthage and then, after the First Punic War, to Rome. Under Arab rule from 831 to 1072 the city flourished as Balarm, an emirate capital famed for its agriculture, trade and scholarship. The Normans under Roger I conquered it in 1072, and under Roger II, crowned King of Sicily in 1130, Palermo became one of the wealthiest and most multicultural courts in Europe, blending Norman, Arab and Byzantine art and administration.

The city later passed through Angevin, Aragonese and Spanish rule, Bourbon rule, and finally Italian unification in 1860. Palermo suffered heavy bombing in the Second World War and was taken by Allied forces in July 1943 during the invasion of Sicily.

Main sights

The Arab-Norman Palermo and the Cathedral Churches of Cefalù and Monreale were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2015. Landmarks include the Palazzo dei Normanni with its Palatine Chapel, Palermo Cathedral, the churches of San Giovanni degli Eremiti and La Martorana, the Teatro Massimo (Italy's largest opera house), and the historic markets of Ballarò, Vucciria and Il Capo.

Culture and cuisine

Palermo is renowned for its street food, including arancine (fried rice balls), panelle (chickpea fritters), sfincione (thick Sicilian pizza) and pani câ meusa (spleen sandwich), as well as sweets such as cannoli and cassata. The city was Italian Capital of Culture in 2018.
