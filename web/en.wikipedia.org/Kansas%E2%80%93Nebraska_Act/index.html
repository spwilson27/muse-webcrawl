Kansas–Nebraska Act of 1854
Enacted30 May 1854
SponsorSenator Stephen A. Douglas
MechanismPopular sovereignty for the territories
RepealedMissouri Compromise line (36°30′)

The Kansas–Nebraska Act of 1854 was a territorial organic act that created the territories of Kansas and Nebraska. Drafted by Senator Stephen A. Douglas, it allowed settlers in those territories to determine through popular sovereignty whether they would allow slavery within their boundaries.

Background

The act effectively repealed the Missouri Compromise of 1820, which had prohibited slavery north of latitude 36°30′. Douglas sought Southern support for organizing the territory and for a transcontinental railroad with a Chicago terminus.

Consequences

Pro- and anti-slavery settlers rushed to Kansas, producing years of violence known as "Bleeding Kansas". The uproar destroyed the Whig Party, split the Democrats, and spurred the creation of the anti-slavery Republican Party in 1854 — major steps on the road to the Civil War.
