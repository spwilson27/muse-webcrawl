Otto Eduard Leopold, Prince of Bismarck, Duke of Lauenburg (1 April 1815 – 30 July 1898) was a German statesman who unified Germany in 1871 and served as its first chancellor until 1890. A conservative Prussian Junker, he mastered the politics of Realpolitik and dominated European diplomacy for two decades with his system of alliances.

Unification of Germany

Appointed Minister President of Prussia in 1862, Bismarck famously declared that the great questions of the day would be settled "by blood and iron". He engineered three wars that unified Germany under Prussian leadership: the Second Schleswig War against Denmark (1864), the Austro-Prussian War (1866), which expelled Austria from German affairs and created the North German Confederation, and the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71), after which the German Empire was proclaimed at Versailles on 18 January 1871 with Wilhelm I as Kaiser.

Chancellorship

As Chancellor, Bismarck fought the Catholic Church in the Kulturkampf, repressed the Social Democrats with the Anti-Socialist Laws while simultaneously creating Europe's first modern welfare state — health insurance (1883), accident insurance (1884), and old-age pensions (1889) — to undercut their appeal. Abroad he built a web of alliances (the League of the Three Emperors, the Triple Alliance of 1882, the Reinsurance Treaty with Russia) to isolate France and preserve peace.

Fall and legacy

After Wilhelm II ascended the throne in 1888, the young emperor clashed with Bismarck and forced his resignation in March 1890. Bismarck retired to Friedrichsruh, wrote his memoirs, and died in 1898. His alliance system collapsed after his dismissal, and historians debate how far his authoritarian unification shaped Germany's later path.
