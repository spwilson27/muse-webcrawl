East Sussex

East Sussex is a ceremonial county in South East England, bordered by Kent to the northeast, West Sussex to the west, Surrey to the northwest, and the English Channel to the south. Its county town is Lewes, and its largest settlement is the city of Brighton and Hove, a separate unitary authority.

Geography

The county's landscape is dominated by the chalk hills of the South Downs, which meet the sea in the dramatic white cliffs of the Seven Sisters and Beachy Head, and by the wooded High Weald in the north. Coastal towns include Eastbourne, Hastings, Bexhill-on-Sea, and Seaford.

History

Sussex was one of the kingdoms of Anglo-Saxon England and later a single county until it was divided into eastern and western divisions for administrative purposes, a split formalised with separate county councils in 1889. Near Hastings stands the site of the Battle of Hastings of 1066, commemorated at the town of Battle.
