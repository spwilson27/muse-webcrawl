Balochi (بلۏچی, romanised: Balòci) is a Northwestern Iranian language spoken primarily by the Baloch people in the Balochistan region of Pakistan, Iran and Afghanistan, with diaspora communities in the Gulf states, Turkmenistan and East Africa. It belongs to the Western Iranian branch of the Indo-Iranian languages and is most closely related to Kurdish and Parthian among its neighbours; standard estimates put the number of speakers at roughly 7–8 million.

Dialects

Balochi divides broadly into Western (Rakhshani), Southern (Makrani) and Eastern (Sulaimani) dialect groups, which differ in phonology and vocabulary and are not always mutually intelligible. Koroshi, spoken near Shiraz in Iran, is a distinct outlier. Efforts at standardisation have produced several competing standards, reflecting the absence of official status in any state: Balochi has no official recognition in Pakistan, Iran or Afghanistan, though it is used in media, poetry and primary schooling in parts of Balochistan.

Writing

Balochi is written mainly in an adapted Perso-Arabic script, standardised in its modern form in the mid-20th century, with a Latin-based alphabet also used by some writers and in diaspora. Balochi possesses a rich oral and written literary tradition, including heroic epic cycles (such as the tales of Hani and Sheh Mureed and Mir Chakar Rind), folk ballads, and modern prose and poetry; the earliest written works date to the 18th–19th centuries, though the oral tradition is far older.
