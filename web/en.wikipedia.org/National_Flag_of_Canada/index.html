National Flag of Canada

The National Flag of Canada, popularly known as the Maple Leaf Flag (French: l'Unifolié), consists of a red field with a white square at its centre bearing a stylised red maple leaf with eleven points. It was adopted in 1965, replacing the Canadian Red Ensign.

History and adoption

For much of its history Canada used the British Union Jack and various red ensigns bearing the Canadian coat of arms. In 1964 Prime Minister Lester B. Pearson initiated a search for a distinctive national flag, sparking the "Great Canadian Flag Debate" in Parliament. The design by George Stanley, inspired by the flag of the Royal Military College of Canada, was selected by committee and proclaimed by Queen Elizabeth II on 28 January 1965; it was first flown on Parliament Hill on 15 February 1965, a date now celebrated as National Flag of Canada Day.

Design and symbolism

Red and white are Canada's national colours, proclaimed by King George V in 1921. The maple leaf has been a Canadian emblem since the eighteenth century. The flag's proportions are two units long by one unit wide, and the white square is the same width as the flag's height.
