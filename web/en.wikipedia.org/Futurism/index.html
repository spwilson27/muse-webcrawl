Futurism

Futurism (Italian: Futurismo) was an artistic and social movement that originated in Italy in the early 20th century. It emphasised speed, technology, youth, industry, and violence, and sought to liberate Italy from the weight of its past. Filippo Tommaso Marinetti launched the movement with his "Manifesto of Futurism", published in 1909.

Art and ideas

Futurist painters such as Umberto Boccioni, Carlo Carrà, Luigi Russolo, and Gino Severini depicted motion through fragmentation, repetition, and force-lines. The movement extended into sculpture, architecture (notably Antonio Sant'Elia's visionary city drawings), literature, music, and even cuisine. Related movements arose in Russia and elsewhere.

Politics and legacy

Many Italian futurists embraced nationalism and later aligned with Fascism, which damaged the movement's reputation after World War II. Nevertheless, futurism influenced Art Deco, Vorticism, Surrealism, and modern graphic design, and its celebration of dynamism remains a landmark of the avant-garde.
