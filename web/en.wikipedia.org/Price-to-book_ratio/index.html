Price-to-book ratio

The price-to-book ratio (P/B ratio) is a financial ratio used to compare a company's current market value to its book value (where book value is the value of all assets minus liabilities). The calculation can be performed in two ways, but the result should be the same: dividing the market price per share by the book value per share, or dividing the company's total market capitalisation by the company's total book value.

P/B ratio = Market price per share ÷ Book value per share

Interpretation

A ratio above 1 indicates that the market values the company at more than the accounting value of its net assets, reflecting expectations of future earnings, intangible assets such as brands and intellectual property, or both. A ratio below 1 can indicate that a stock is undervalued, or that the market expects the company to destroy value or write down its assets.

The ratio is particularly useful for valuing companies with substantial tangible assets, such as banks and other financial institutions, and is less meaningful for firms whose value lies mainly in intangibles that accounting rules keep off the balance sheet. Value investors, following the approach popularised by Benjamin Graham, have historically looked for stocks trading at low price-to-book ratios as candidates for undervaluation.

Limitations

Book value is an accounting figure based on historical cost; it may diverge widely from the economic value of assets. Companies can also manipulate the ratio through share repurchases, which reduce book equity, or through asset write-downs. The related tangible price-to-book ratio excludes intangible assets and goodwill from the book value.
