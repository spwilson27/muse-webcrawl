National Air and Space Museum

Established: 1946 (as National Air Museum); opened 1976

Location: National Mall, Washington, D.C., United States

Part of: Smithsonian Institution

The National Air and Space Museum (NASM) of the Smithsonian Institution is a museum in Washington, D.C., United States, devoted to the history of aviation and spaceflight. It holds the world's largest and most significant collection of aviation and space artefacts, including the 1903 Wright Flyer, Charles Lindbergh's Spirit of St. Louis, the Bell X-1, and the Apollo 11 command module Columbia. It is among the most visited museums in the world.

History

The museum's origins lie in the Smithsonian's aeronautical collection, which began with kite acquisitions in the nineteenth century. Congress established the National Air Museum in 1946; it was renamed the National Air and Space Museum in 1966. The landmark building on the National Mall, designed by architect Gyo Obata, opened on 1 July 1976 as part of the United States Bicentennial celebrations. In 2003 the museum opened a second facility, the Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center near Washington Dulles International Airport in Virginia, which displays larger artefacts including the Space Shuttle Discovery and an SR-71 Blackbird.

Collection

The collection comprises tens of thousands of objects, including more than 68,000 aviation and space artefacts. Highlights include the Wright brothers' 1903 Flyer, the Apollo 11 command module, Friendship 7 (John Glenn's Mercury capsule), the lunar touchable moon rock, and Amelia Earhart's Lockheed Vega. The museum also operates the Center for Earth and Planetary Studies, a research unit in planetary geology and geophysics.

Renovation

Beginning in 2018, the Mall building underwent a comprehensive multi-year renovation that replaced its exterior stone cladding and renewed all of its exhibition galleries, reopening in phases through the mid-2020s.
