Localization (mathematics)

In commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, localization is a formal way to introduce multiplicative inverses for elements of a ring, generalising the construction of the field of fractions of an integral domain and of the rational numbers from the integers.

Construction

Given a commutative ring R and a multiplicatively closed subset S containing 1 and not 0, the localization S−1R consists of fractions r/s with r in R and s in S, modulo the natural equivalence relation. It is characterised by a universal property: it is the smallest ring receiving a map from R under which every element of S becomes a unit.

Important examples

  • Localizing the integers at the powers of a prime p gives the ring of rationals with denominator prime to p.
  • For a prime ideal 𝔭 of R, localizing at its complement gives the local ring R𝔭, central to the local study of schemes and varieties.
  • Related constructions exist for modules, categories and spectra in algebraic topology.
