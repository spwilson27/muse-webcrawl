The Battle of the Java Sea was a major naval battle of the Pacific War, fought on 27 February 1942 in the Java Sea between the Imperial Japanese Navy and the combined American-British-Dutch-Australian (ABDA) naval force under Dutch Rear-Admiral Karel Doorman. The Allied striking force was virtually annihilated trying to intercept the Japanese invasion convoy bound for Java, effectively ending organised Allied naval resistance in the Dutch East Indies.

Background

Japanese forces were overrunning Southeast Asia; in late February 1942 a large invasion convoy approached Java from the northeast. Doorman's scratch squadron—cruisers De Ruyter, Exeter, Houston, Perth and Java with nine destroyers—sailed from Surabaya to intercept, handicapped by no air cover, poor communications between four navies, and crews exhausted by weeks of operations.

The battle

Contact came in the afternoon of 27 February. In a long-range gunnery duel the Japanese heavy cruisers Nachi and Haguro outranged and outshot the Allies; HMS Exeter was crippled by an 8-inch hit in her boiler room and the destroyer Kortenaer was torpedoed and sunk. Doorman attempted to close the convoy after dark, but in the night action the cruisers De Ruyter and Java were struck by Japanese Type 93 "Long Lance" torpedoes and sank with heavy loss of life, Doorman going down with his flagship. His last signal is traditionally rendered as "Ik val aan, volg mij" ("I am attacking, follow me").

Aftermath

The surviving Allied ships were hunted down in the following days at the Battle of Sunda Strait and the Second Battle of the Java Sea; Perth, Houston and Exeter were all sunk. Java capitulated on 8–9 March 1942. The wrecks of the Java Sea battles became protected war graves; illegal salvage has since largely destroyed several of them, a matter of diplomatic concern.
