Turpentine

AppearanceColourless liquid
Main componentsα-pinene, β-pinene and other terpenes
SourcesPine resin; wood pulp by-products

Turpentine (also called spirit of turpentine, oil of turpentine or wood turpentine) is a fluid obtained by the distillation of resin harvested from living trees, mainly pines. It is composed principally of terpenes, chiefly the monoterpenes alpha-pinene and beta-pinene, with smaller amounts of carene, camphene, dipentene and terpinolene. Turpentine is used primarily as a solvent and as a source of materials for organic synthesis.

Production

Gum turpentine is distilled from crude oleoresin tapped from living pine trees. Large quantities are also produced as a by-product of the kraft process of papermaking, in which pinene-rich volatiles are recovered from wood pulp; this sulfate turpentine, after purification, supplies much of the industrial market.

Uses

Turpentine is a traditional solvent for oil-based paints and varnishes and is still favoured by some artists for thinning oil paint. Industrially it serves as a feedstock for synthesising fragrances, flavours, camphor and other fine chemicals. It was historically used in folk medicine and in early anaesthetic mixtures, though ingestion and prolonged exposure are hazardous: turpentine is an irritant and a sensitiser, and modern safety practice favours less toxic substitutes such as white spirit for most purposes.
