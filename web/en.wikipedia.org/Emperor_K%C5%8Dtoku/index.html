Emperor Kōtoku

Emperor Kōtoku (Japanese: 孝徳天皇, Kōtoku-tennō; 596 – 24 November 654) was the 36th emperor of Japan, according to the traditional order of succession. His reign spanned the years 645 to 654.

Reign:12 July 645 – 24 November 654
Predecessor:Empress Kōgyoku
Successor:Empress Saimei
Born:596
Died:24 November 654 (aged 57–58), Naniwa Nagara-Toyosaki Palace, Osaka

Reign

Kōtoku, born Prince Karu, ascended the throne after the Isshi Incident of 645, in which Prince Naka no Ōe and Nakatomi no Kamatari assassinated Soga no Iruka and forced the abdication of Empress Kōgyoku. Although the energetic reforms of the era are largely credited to Naka no Ōe and the crown prince's circle, Kōtoku's reign saw the proclamation of the Taika Reforms (645–646), which sought to centralize government on the Chinese Tang model, abolish private ownership of land, and reorganize administration.

His reign also saw the adoption of the first Japanese era name (nengō), Taika, followed by Hakuchi. Kōtoku moved the capital to Naniwa (modern Osaka), where he built the Naniwa Nagara-Toyosaki Palace. In his later years he was increasingly overshadowed by the crown prince, and after a falling-out, much of the court abandoned Naniwa to follow Naka no Ōe back to Asuka; Kōtoku died in 654, reportedly of illness and disappointment.
