Argostoli

Argostoli (Greek: Αργοστόλι) is a town and the capital and main port of the island of Kefalonia in the Ionian Islands of Greece. It has a population of roughly 10,000 and is the seat of the municipality of Argostoli. The town is built around the shore of a sheltered bay on the island's western side, the Gulf of Argostoli.

History

Argostoli became the island's capital in 1757, when the seat of administration was moved from the Castle of St. George during Venetian rule. The town grew prosperous as a trading port. Much of Argostoli was destroyed by the devastating Ionian earthquake of August 1953, which levelled most buildings on Kefalonia; the town was subsequently rebuilt. The De Bosset Bridge (Drapano Bridge), a stone bridge built in 1813 across the bay, is one of the town's landmarks, along with the lighthouse of Saint Theodori (Fanari).

Geography and sights

Near the town, the Koutavos Lagoon and the Katavothres sinkholes — a rare geological phenomenon in which seawater flows inland and disappears underground — attract visitors. Loggerhead sea turtles (Caretta caretta) are frequently seen in the harbour. Argostoli has an archaeological museum, a historical and folklore museum, and serves as a base for exploring the island's beaches and the Melissani Cave.
