Permian Basin (North America)

The Permian Basin is a large sedimentary basin in western Texas and southeastern New Mexico, named for the thick Permian-age rock deposits laid down when the region was a shallow sea. It is one of the world's most prolific petroleum-producing regions.

Permian Basin
Location: West Texas & SE New Mexico
Area: about 86,000 sq mi
Sub-basins: Midland, Delaware
Key cities: Midland, Odessa

Geology

The basin comprises the Midland Basin, the Delaware Basin and the Central Basin Platform. Its stacked shale, sandstone and carbonate reservoirs include the Spraberry, Wolfcamp and Bone Spring formations.

Petroleum production

Oil was first produced commercially in the 1920s. Horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing in shale from the 2010s made the Permian the highest-producing oil field in the United States and one of the largest in the world, transforming US energy output.
