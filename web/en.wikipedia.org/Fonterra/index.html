Fonterra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fonterra Co-operative Group Limited

Industry: Dairy

Founded: 16 October 2001

Headquarters: Auckland, New Zealand

Type: Farmer-owned co-operative

Fonterra Co-operative Group Limited is a New Zealand multinational publicly traded dairy co-operative owned by around 9,000 New Zealand dairy farmers. The company is responsible for approximately 30% of the world's dairy exports and is New Zealand's largest company, with revenues of over NZ$20 billion. It was formed in 2001 from the merger of the New Zealand Dairy Group, Kiwi Co-operative Dairies, and the New Zealand Dairy Board.

History

Fonterra was established under the Dairy Industry Restructuring Act 2001, which permitted the merger of the country's two largest dairy co-operatives and removed the Dairy Board's statutory export monopoly. In 2012 the company launched Trading Among Farmers, allowing outside investors to buy units in the Fonterra Shareholders' Fund while farmers retained control of the co-operative.

Operations

Fonterra collects milk from farms across New Zealand and operates processing plants in New Zealand, Australia, and other countries. Its brands include Anchor, Mainland, Tip Top, Anlene, and Anmum. Its ingredients business supplies milk powders, cheese, butter, and specialised nutritional products — including infant formula base — to food manufacturers in more than 130 countries.

Controversies

In 2008 Fonterra held a 43% stake in the Chinese company Sanlu, which was at the centre of the melamine-contaminated milk scandal. In 2013 the company issued a precautionary recall after the bacterium Clostridium botulinum was suspected in whey protein concentrate; later testing showed the organism was a harmless relative, but the incident temporarily disrupted exports and trade.
