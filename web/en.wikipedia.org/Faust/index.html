Faust

Faust and Mephistopheles, the scholar and the demon of the legend.

Faust is the protagonist of a classic German legend based on the historical Johann Georg Faust (c. 1480–1540), an alchemist and itinerant astrologer of the German Renaissance. In the legend, Faust, discontented with his life as a scholar, makes a pact with the Devil — represented by the demon Mephistopheles — exchanging his soul for unlimited knowledge and worldly pleasures.

The legend

The story first reached print in the anonymous Historia von D. Johann Fausten (1587), which portrayed Faust's bargain, his magical exploits and his eventual damnation as a warning against pride and sorcery. Christopher Marlowe adapted it for the English stage in The Tragical History of Doctor Faustus (c. 1592).

Goethe's Faust

The most influential treatment is Johann Wolfgang von Goethe's Faust, a closet drama published in two parts (1808 and 1832). Goethe transformed the tale: his Faust is a restless seeker who wagers that no experience will ever satisfy him, and in Part Two he is ultimately redeemed through striving and the intercession of Gretchen, the young woman he seduced and ruined in Part One.

Legacy

The Faust legend has inspired countless works, including Gounod's opera Faust, Berlioz's La Damnation de Faust, Liszt's Faust Symphony, and Thomas Mann's novel Doctor Faustus. The adjective "Faustian" describes any ambitious bargain made at the cost of one's integrity or soul.
