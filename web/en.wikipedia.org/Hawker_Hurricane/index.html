Hawker Hurricane

The Hawker Hurricane is a British single-seat fighter aircraft designed by Sydney Camm of Hawker Aircraft and powered by the Rolls-Royce Merlin engine. Entering Royal Air Force service in 1937, it was the RAF's first monoplane fighter and its first aircraft to exceed 300 mph. During the Battle of Britain in 1940 Hurricanes outnumbered the more famous Supermarine Spitfire and accounted for the majority of Luftwaffe aircraft destroyed. More than 14,000 were built, including Canadian production.

Design and development

Camm evolved the Hurricane from the Hawker Fury biplane, retaining a fabric-covered steel-tube fuselage that was rugged, easy to repair and tolerant of battle damage. Its thick wing provided a stable gun platform and room for eight machine guns. Early aircraft had fabric-covered wings, later replaced by stressed-skin metal wings, and two-blade fixed-pitch propellers gave way to constant-speed units.

Operational history

Hurricanes fought in France in 1940 and formed the backbone of Fighter Command during the Battle of Britain, where 32 Hurricane squadrons took part, destroying more enemy aircraft than all other defences combined. The type served in every major theatre: as a night fighter and intruder, in North Africa (including tropicalised Mk IIs with 12 machine guns or four 20 mm cannon), in Malta, the Soviet Union under Lend-Lease, and the Far East. The Sea Hurricane flew from carriers and CAM ships. The Mk IID "tank buster" carried two 40 mm Vickers S guns underwing, and Mk IVs fired rocket projectiles. Production ended in 1944, though Hurricanes remained in second-line service until the late 1940s.

Legacy

Often overshadowed by the Spitfire, the Hurricane is credited by historians with winning the greater share of the Battle of Britain. The RAF's Battle of Britain Memorial Flight still flies two Hurricanes, and dozens survive in museums worldwide.
