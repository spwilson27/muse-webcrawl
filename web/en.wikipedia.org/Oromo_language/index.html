Oromo language

Oromo (Afaan Oromoo) is an Afroasiatic language belonging to the Cushitic branch, spoken primarily by the Oromo people in Ethiopia and neighbouring parts of Kenya and Somalia. With roughly 37 million speakers, it is one of the most widely spoken languages in Africa and the most widely spoken language in Ethiopia.

Writing system

Oromo is written with a Latin-based alphabet called Qubee, officially adopted in 1991. Long vowels and consonants are indicated by doubling letters. Historically, the Ge'ez script and Arabic script were also used.

Grammar

Oromo is a subject–object–verb language with a system of grammatical cases marked by suffixes. It uses pitch and vowel length to distinguish meaning, and has a rich set of verbal derivations. Dialects include Borana, Eastern Oromo, and the Western/Central varieties such as Macca and Tulama.
