Quebec Bridge

The Quebec Bridge (French: Pont de Québec) is a riveted steel truss cantilever bridge crossing the lower St. Lawrence River between Sainte-Foy and Lévis in Quebec, Canada. Completed in 1919, it remains the longest cantilever bridge span in the world at 549 metres (1,801 ft), and its construction was marked by two catastrophic collapses.

Collapses

On 29 August 1907, during construction, the south arm collapsed into the river, killing 75 workers, including 33 Mohawk ironworkers from Kahnawake. A royal commission blamed design errors and inadequate oversight; consulting engineer Theodore Cooper's design had underestimated the structure's dead weight. On 11 September 1916, the new central span fell while being hoisted into place, killing 13 more men, when a casting supporting the span failed.

Completion and legacy

The bridge was finally completed in 1917 and opened to traffic in 1919, owned by Canadian National Railway and carrying rail, road, and pedestrian traffic. It was declared a National Historic Site of Canada in 1995 and an international historic civil engineering landmark. Ownership disputes over maintenance persisted until the federal government repurchased the bridge in 2024.
