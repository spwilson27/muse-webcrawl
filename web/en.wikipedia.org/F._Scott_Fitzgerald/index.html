F. Scott Fitzgerald

Francis Scott Key Fitzgerald (24 September 1896 – 21 December 1940) was an American novelist, essayist, and short story writer. He is best known for his novels depicting the flamboyance and excess of the Jazz Age, a term he popularised in his short story collection Tales of the Jazz Age. His most famous work is The Great Gatsby (1925), widely considered a masterpiece and one of the great American novels.

F. Scott Fitzgerald
Born: 24 September 1896, Saint Paul, Minnesota
Died: 21 December 1940, Los Angeles, California
Notable works: The Great Gatsby; Tender Is the Night

Career

Fitzgerald achieved success with his first novel, This Side of Paradise (1920), and became a celebrity with his wife Zelda. During his lifetime he published four novels, four story collections, and 164 short stories. Though initially only moderately successful, The Great Gatsby achieved a legendary reputation after his death.

Legacy

Fitzgerald is regarded as one of the greatest American writers of the twentieth century, a member of the "Lost Generation" of the 1920s.
