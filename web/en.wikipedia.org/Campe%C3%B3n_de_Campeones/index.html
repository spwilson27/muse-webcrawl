The Campeón de Campeones (Spanish: "Champion of Champions") is an annual Mexican football competition contested by the champions of the Liga MX Apertura and Clausura tournaments of the preceding season. It functions as the Mexican super cup.

History

The trophy was first contested in 1942, originally matching the league champion against the winner of the Copa México. The competition was held intermittently for decades and was discontinued after 1995 before being revived in 2003. With the adoption of the short Apertura/Clausura tournament format, the modern Campeón de Campeones pits the two seasonal champions against each other in a single match, often played at a neutral venue in the United States.

Format

If one club wins both the Apertura and Clausura in the same cycle, it may be awarded the trophy automatically or face a designated opponent under the competition's rules. The winner has also faced the MLS Cup champion in the Campeones Cup, a cross-league fixture inaugurated in 2018.
