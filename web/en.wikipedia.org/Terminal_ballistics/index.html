Terminal ballistics

Terminal ballistics is a sub-field of ballistics concerned with the behaviour and effects of a projectile when it hits and transfers its energy to a target. It is distinguished from internal ballistics (within the firearm) and external ballistics (in flight).

Concepts

Key topics include penetration, expansion and fragmentation of the projectile, and the permanent and temporary cavities produced in tissue or tissue simulants such as ballistic gelatin. In hunting and military contexts, terminal performance determines how effectively a projectile incapacitates or kills game or penetrates armour; in medicine, the same phenomena are studied as wound ballistics.

Testing

Standardized testing uses 10% ballistic gelatin at controlled temperature, high-speed photography and recovered-projectile measurement. Armour and vehicle protection are evaluated by whether a projectile perforates the target and by behind-armour effects such as spall and blunt trauma.
