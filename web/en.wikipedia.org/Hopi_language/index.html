Hopi language

Hopi (Hopilàvayi) is a Uto-Aztecan language spoken by the Hopi people of northeastern Arizona. It is the sole member of its branch of the family and has several dialects corresponding to the three mesas, of which Third Mesa Hopi is the basis of the standard orthography.

Features

Hopi has a relatively small consonant inventory including ejective and labialised velar stops, three tones on First Mesa, and a rich system of suffixation. Nouns distinguish animate and inanimate classes, and verbs inflect for aspect rather than tense in the Indo-European sense.

The Whorf controversy

Benjamin Lee Whorf famously claimed in the 1930s that Hopi lacked words and constructions referring to time, using it as the flagship example of linguistic relativity — the idea that language shapes thought. Later fieldwork, especially Ekkehart Malotki's 1983 study Hopi Time, documented extensive temporal vocabulary and devices, and Whorf's strong claims are now widely rejected, though the debate continues to shape discussion of the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis.

Vitality

Transmission to children declined during the twentieth century, and Hopi is considered endangered, but immersion preschools, a published dictionary, and tribal language programmes support revitalisation.
