Net protein utilization

Net protein utilization (NPU) is the ratio of the mass of amino acids converted to proteins to the mass of proteins supplied. It is used as a measure of protein quality, indicating how efficiently a dietary protein can be used by the body for growth and maintenance.

Calculation

NPU is calculated from the nitrogen retained by the body compared with the nitrogen consumed:

NPU = (nitrogen retained / nitrogen intake) × 100

Unlike biological value, which measures absorbed nitrogen, NPU takes into account the digestibility of the protein as well, making it a more complete measure of how much of an ingested protein is actually available to the body.

Values

Egg protein has an NPU of roughly 87, cow's milk about 81, and soy protein around 61. Wheat protein is considerably lower, at around 30. Values are typically determined from nitrogen balance studies, often using rats as model organisms.

Related measures

NPU is related to the protein efficiency ratio (PER), biological value (BV), and the more modern protein digestibility-corrected amino acid score (PDCAAS) and digestible indispensable amino acid score (DIAAS), which have largely supplanted it in regulatory contexts.
