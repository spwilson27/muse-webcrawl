Ernst Haeckel

Ernst Heinrich Philipp August Haeckel
Born: February 16, 1834, Potsdam, Prussia
Died: August 9, 1919, Jena, Germany
Fields: Zoology, natural history, philosophy
Known for: Monism, biogenetic law, Kunstformen der Natur

Ernst Haeckel was a German zoologist, naturalist, philosopher and artist who discovered, described and named thousands of new species and mapped a genealogical tree relating all life forms. He popularised Charles Darwin's work in Germany and coined many terms in biology, including ecology, phylum, phylogeny, ontogeny and Protista.

Scientific work

Haeckel was a professor of zoology at Jena for 47 years. His monograph on radiolarians drew on specimens from the Challenger expedition and reported over 3,500 new species. He proposed the "biogenetic law" — ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny — holding that an organism's embryonic development repeats its evolutionary history; the strong form of this doctrine is no longer accepted, though his embryo drawings remain famous and controversial.

Art and philosophy

His Kunstformen der Natur (1899–1904), a collection of one hundred detailed lithographs of organisms, influenced the Art Nouveau movement. Haeckel also developed monism, a philosophy identifying mind and nature as one substance, and founded the Monist League; his best-selling Die Welträthsel (The Riddle of the Universe, 1899) reached a mass readership.
