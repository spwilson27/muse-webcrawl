British Mandate for Palestine

Mandate for Palestine
Administering power: United Kingdom
League of Nations mandate confirmed: 1922; in force 1923
Ended: 14 May 1948
First High Commissioner: Sir Herbert Samuel

The Mandate for Palestine was a League of Nations mandate under which Britain administered the territory of Palestine — broadly modern Israel, the Palestinian territories and (initially) Jordan — following the Ottoman Empire's defeat in the First World War. British forces under General Allenby had conquered the territory in 1917–18, and the San Remo conference of 1920 assigned the mandate to Britain; the League Council approved its terms in July 1922 and it entered into force in September 1923.

Terms and administration

The mandate text incorporated the Balfour Declaration of 1917, requiring the establishment in Palestine of a "national home for the Jewish people" while safeguarding the civil and religious rights of the existing non-Jewish communities. In 1921–22 Britain separated the territory east of the Jordan River, creating the Emirate of Transjordan under Abdullah, which the League recognised as excluded from the Jewish national home provisions.

Britain governed through a High Commissioner in Jerusalem. Jewish immigration increased sharply in the 1930s as persecution in Europe intensified, fuelling Arab resentment that erupted in the 1929 riots and the Arab Revolt of 1936–39. Britain responded with commissions of inquiry — notably the Peel Commission (1937), which first proposed partition — and the 1939 White Paper, which sharply restricted Jewish immigration and land purchases just as the Holocaust began.

End of the Mandate

After the Second World War, Jewish insurgency against British rule, pressure over Holocaust survivors seeking entry, and escalating communal violence led Britain in 1947 to hand the problem to the United Nations. The UN Partition Plan (Resolution 181) proposed two states; it was accepted by the Jewish leadership and rejected by Arab leaders. Britain withdrew, the Mandate ended at midnight on 14 May 1948, and the State of Israel was proclaimed the same day — followed immediately by the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.
