Gulliver's Travels

Gulliver bound by the Lilliputians, from an early illustration.

Gulliver's Travels, or Travels into Several Remote Nations of the World (1726), is a prose satire by the Anglo-Irish writer and clergyman Jonathan Swift. It recounts the voyages of ship's surgeon Lemuel Gulliver to extraordinary lands and is both a parody of the travellers' tales popular in Swift's day and a savage satire on human nature, politics and the Royal Society.

Synopsis

Gulliver visits four realms: Lilliput, whose six-inch inhabitants are consumed by petty court factions and religious disputes (over which end of an egg to break); Brobdingnag, a land of giants whose king denounces European civilisation; the flying island of Laputa and the projectors of Lagado, satirising abstract science; and the country of the rational horses, the Houyhnhnms, whose brutish human-like Yahoos drive Gulliver to misanthropy. Terms from the book — Lilliputian, Yahoo, Brobdingnagian — entered the language.

Reception

Published anonymously, the book was an immediate bestseller and has never been out of print. Though often abridged as a children's adventure, it is regarded as a masterpiece of satire and a forerunner of the modern novel and of science fiction.
