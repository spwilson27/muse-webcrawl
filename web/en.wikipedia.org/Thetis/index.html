Thetis is a figure from Greek mythology with varying mythological roles. She is principally a sea nymph in the service of Hera, and is best known as the mother of the hero Achilles. As the daughter of the sea god Nereus and the Oceanid Doris, she was one of the fifty Nereids.

Mythology

Zeus and Poseidon both desired Thetis, but withdrew when the Titan Prometheus revealed a prophecy that her son would surpass his father in greatness; the gods therefore married her off to the mortal Peleus, king of the Myrmidons. Thetis, a shape-shifter, transformed into fire, water, and wild beasts to escape him, but Peleus, advised by the centaur Chiron, held on until she yielded. Their wedding on Mount Pelion was attended by the gods, and it was there that Eris threw the Apple of Discord — the spark of the chain of events leading to the Judgment of Paris and the Trojan War.

In Homer's Iliad, Thetis is the devoted, grieving mother of Achilles: she intercedes with Zeus on her son's behalf, and commissions Hephaestus to forge his new armor, including the famous shield. Later tradition (not in Homer) holds that she dipped the infant Achilles in the River Styx to make him invulnerable, holding him by the heel — the origin of the expression "Achilles' heel". After his death she emerges from the sea with the Nereids to mourn him.
