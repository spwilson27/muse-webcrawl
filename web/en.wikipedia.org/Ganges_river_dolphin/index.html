Ganges river dolphin

The Ganges river dolphin (Platanista gangetica) is a freshwater dolphin found in the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna and Karnaphuli–Sangu river systems of India, Bangladesh and Nepal. It is one of the few true river dolphins and, together with the related Indus river dolphin, belongs to the family Platanistidae. It is the national aquatic animal of India.

Description

Adults reach about 2–2.6 metres in length. The species has a long, narrow beak, a stocky body and very small eyes lacking a lens, rendering it functionally blind; it navigates and hunts by echolocation in turbid water, often swimming on its side. Females are larger than males.

Behaviour and diet

The dolphin feeds on fish and invertebrates, probing riverbeds with its beak. It surfaces briefly every half-minute or so and typically lives alone or in small groups. Calves are born mainly in the winter months.

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered. Barrages and dams fragment its habitat, while bycatch in gill nets, pollution and reduced river flows have reduced numbers to only a few thousand. India's Project Dolphin, announced in 2020, aims to conserve the species; the Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary in Bihar protects a stretch of the Ganges for it.
