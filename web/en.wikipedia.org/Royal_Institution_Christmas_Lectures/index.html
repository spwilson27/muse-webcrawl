Royal Institution Christmas Lectures

The Royal Institution Christmas Lectures are a series of public science lectures held annually at the Royal Institution of Great Britain in London during the Christmas holiday season. Established by Michael Faraday in 1825, they present scientific subjects to a general audience — originally young people — through demonstrations and experiments, in Faraday's words adapted "to the juvenile auditory".

History

Faraday, who gave the lectures nineteen times, initiated the series at a time when organised science education for children barely existed. His most famous course, The Chemical History of a Candle (1848 and again in 1860–61), remains in print as a classic of popular science writing. The lectures have continued nearly every year since 1825, pausing only during the Second World War.

Broadcast era

The lectures were first televised by the BBC in 1936, making them one of the world's oldest science television formats; from 1966 they became an annual fixture. Distinguished lecturers have included David Attenborough (1973, "The Language of Animals"), Carl Sagan (1977, "The Planets"), Richard Dawkins (1991, "Growing Up in the Universe"), Marcus du Sautoy, Hannah Fry and Chris Jackson. Demonstration-heavy formats, aided by a "lecture demonstrator" tradition, remain central; since 2010 the lectures have been broadcast on BBC Four. The Royal Institution has made archive recordings of past lectures freely available online.
