The Transbay Transit Center, officially the Salesforce Transit Center under a naming-rights agreement, is a transit station in downtown San Francisco, California, serving as the primary bus terminal for the San Francisco Bay Area. It replaced the seismically deficient Transbay Terminal, opened in 1939, which stood on the same site at First and Mission streets.

Design and construction

The centre was designed by Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects and developed by the Transbay Joint Powers Authority as the core of a new neighbourhood of towers around it, including the adjacent Salesforce Tower. The building is roughly four city blocks long and is topped by a 5.4-acre (2.2 ha) public rooftop park, Salesforce Park, with gardens, an amphitheatre and walking trails. Bus services run from an elevated bus deck with a direct ramp to the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge; street-level and concourse levels hold retail and a grand hall with a large light column. A below-grade rail level, built as a "train box", awaits a future extension of Caltrain and California High-Speed Rail into downtown (the Downtown Rail Extension, formerly Downtown Extension/DTX).

Opening and closure

The centre opened on 12 August 2018 but closed on 25 September 2018 after workers discovered a crack in one of the steel beams supporting the bus deck and rooftop park; a second crack was found nearby. Following installation of shoring and a permanent reinforcing fix, the centre reopened on 1 July 2019. The project's first phase cost about $2.4 billion.
