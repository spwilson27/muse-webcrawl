Delta wing

A delta wing is a wing shaped in the form of a triangle, named after the Greek letter delta (Δ). It is a planform notable for combining structural strength and stiffness with good supersonic aerodynamic performance, and it has been used on many high-speed aircraft, most famously the Concorde airliner and the Dassault Mirage family of fighters.

Characteristics

The delta's high sweep angle keeps the wing within the shock cone at supersonic speeds, reducing wave drag, while its deep root provides volume for fuel and landing gear and gives a strong, light structure. At low speeds and high angles of attack, a slender delta generates additional "vortex lift" from stable vortices that form over the upper surface along the leading edges. The same planform, however, produces relatively high induced drag at subsonic speeds, large trim changes, and — in tailless designs — high take-off and landing speeds. Many deltas therefore use elevons, combined elevator-ailerons along the trailing edge, in place of a separate horizontal tail.

Variants and history

Variants include the pure tailless delta (Mirage III), the tailed delta (MiG-21), the canard delta (Saab Viggen, Eurofighter Typhoon, Dassault Rafale), the cropped delta, and the ogival or ogee delta used by Concorde and the Avro Vulcan bomber. The concept was studied before the Second World War by designers such as Alexander Lippisch in Germany, and delta-wing aircraft became widespread in the 1950s as jet fighters pushed into sustained supersonic flight. Space vehicles have also used delta planforms: the Space Shuttle orbiter employed a double-delta wing to manage hypersonic re-entry and subsonic landing.
