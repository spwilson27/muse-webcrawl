Han River (Korea)

The Han River (Hangang) is a major river in South Korea and the fourth longest river on the Korean peninsula.

The Han River or Hangang (Korean: 한강; Hanja: 漢江) is a major river in South Korea, with some of its tributaries and drainage basin in North Korea. It is the fourth longest river on the Korean peninsula after the Yalu (Amnok), Tumen (Duman), and Nakdong rivers. The river begins as two smaller rivers in the eastern mountains of the Korean peninsula, which then converge near Seoul, the capital of the country.

The Han River and its surrounding area have played an important role in Korean history. The Three Kingdoms of Korea strove to take control of this land, where the river was used as a trade route to China (via the Yellow Sea). The river is no longer actively used for navigation because its estuary is located at the border of the two Koreas, which is heavily militarised; civilian entry is barred without special permission.

Course

The river is formed by the confluence of the Namhan River ("South Han"), which originates in Mount Daedeok in Gangwon Province, and the Bukhan River ("North Han"), which originates in North Korea near Mount Kumgang. The combined river flows westward through Seoul, passing the neighbourhoods of Gangnam and Yeouido island, and empties into the Yellow Sea (West Sea) near Incheon, reinforced in the lower reaches by the Imjin River. Its total length is commonly given as about 500 km (some 514 km including its longest headstream), and its drainage basin covers roughly 26,000–34,000 km² depending on how the lower basin is counted. The river is notable for its breadth in Seoul relative to its length; dozens of bridges cross it in the capital, including the Banpo Bridge with its Moonlight Rainbow Fountain.

The Han River parks and "Miracle on the Han"

The riverbanks in Seoul have been developed into extensive public parks (Hangang Park) with cycle paths, swimming pools, and camping areas, used heavily for recreation. The phrase "Miracle on the Han River" refers to South Korea's rapid post-war economic growth between 1961 and 1996, during which the country transformed from a war-devastated, aid-dependent state into one of the world's largest economies, symbolised by the development of Seoul along the river.
