Taekwondo

Origin: Korea
Focus: Striking, especially head-height and spinning kicks
Governing bodies: World Taekwondo; ITF
Olympic sport: Since 2000

Taekwondo is a Korean martial art and combat sport characterised by fast kicking techniques, including head-height kicks, spinning kicks and jumping kicks. Its name derives from Korean: tae (foot/leg), kwon (fist/hand) and do (the way or discipline).

History

Taekwondo was developed in the 1940s and 1950s by Korean martial artists combining indigenous traditions such as taekkyon with influences from karate learned during the Japanese occupation. The name "taekwondo" was adopted in 1955. Two main international federations emerged: the International Taekwon-do Federation (ITF), founded by Choi Hong-hi in 1966, and the Kukkiwon/World Taekwondo (WT) system established in the 1970s.

Practice and competition

Training includes patterns (poomsae or tul), sparring (kyorugi), breaking and self-defence. Ranks progress through coloured belts (geup) to black belt degrees (dan). WT-style sparring awards points for kicks and punches to the torso and head, with electronic scoring on protective gear. Taekwondo became an official Olympic sport at the Sydney 2000 Games and is practised by tens of millions of people worldwide.
