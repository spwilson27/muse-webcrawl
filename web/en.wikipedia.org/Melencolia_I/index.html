Melencolia I

Melencolia I is a large 1514 engraving by the German Renaissance artist Albrecht Dürer, one of his three "master engravings" alongside Knight, Death and the Devil and St. Jerome in His Study. It is one of the most famous and most interpreted prints in Western art.

Composition

The engraving shows a winged female figure, the personification of Melancholy, sitting in a dejected pose, head resting on her hand, surrounded by the unused tools of geometry, craft and measurement: a compass, scales, an hourglass, a sphere, a large geometric polyhedron (a truncated rhombohedron), a magic square on the wall, a bell, and a ladder. A bat-like creature flies across a dark sky carrying a banner with the title, while a comet or rainbow shines over a distant sea. The 4×4 magic square in the image is notable: its bottom row includes the numbers 15 and 14, giving the date of the work, and its central cells are said to allude to Dürer's mother, who died in 1514.

Interpretation

Scholars have linked the image to the Renaissance theory of melancholy as the temperament of genius, drawing on Marsilio Ficino's writings and Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa's occult philosophy; the "I" may denote the first of Agrippa's three kinds of melancholy, the melancholy of artists and artisans. Erwin Panofsky's influential reading saw the figure as a portrait of the creative mind stalled by its own limitations. The engraving's meaning remains debated, and it has inspired countless artists and writers.
