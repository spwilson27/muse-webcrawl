The Steyr-Mannlicher M1895 is a straight-pull bolt-action rifle designed by Ferdinand von Mannlicher and produced by Steyr and by FÉG in Budapest. Adopted as the standard infantry rifle of Austria-Hungary, it armed the Austro-Hungarian Army throughout the First World War.

Design

Its straight-pull action, in which the bolt handle is drawn directly back and pushed forward, allowed a rapid rate of fire; the bolt head unlocked via a rotating cam. The rifle used Mannlicher's en-bloc clip system: a five-round clip inserted into the magazine fell free through a slot in the bottom when empty.

Service

Nearly 3.5 million were produced. After 1918 the rifles passed to successor states and were widely used in the Second World War, many converted to the more powerful 8×56mmR cartridge as M95/30 and 31.M variants.
