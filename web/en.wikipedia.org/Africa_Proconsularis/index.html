Africa Proconsularis

Africa Proconsularis (also called Africa Vetus) was a Roman province on the north coast of Africa, roughly corresponding to modern Tunisia and adjoining parts of Libya and Algeria. Its capital was Carthage.

History

Rome created the province after destroying Carthage in the Third Punic War (146 BC). Famous for its grain exports, it became one of the empire's richest and most urbanised provinces, governed by a proconsul. Under Diocletian's reforms it was divided, with the core becoming Africa Proconsularis (Zeugitana) alongside Byzacena and Tripolitania. The Vandals conquered it in the 430s AD; Justinian's generals reconquered it for Byzantium in 533–534.

Legacy

The province was an early centre of Latin Christianity, home to Tertullian, Cyprian and Augustine.
