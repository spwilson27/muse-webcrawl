Clark Ashton Smith

Born: January 13, 1893, Long Valley, California
Died: August 14, 1961, Pacific Grove, California
Occupation: Writer, poet, artist
Genres: Fantasy, horror, science fiction, poetry

Smith, one of the "big three" of Weird Tales alongside Lovecraft and Howard.

Clark Ashton Smith (January 13, 1893 – August 14, 1961) was an American writer, poet and artist. He achieved early prominence as a poet in the tradition of the Decadents and George Sterling, then became — with H. P. Lovecraft and Robert E. Howard — one of the three leading contributors to the pulp magazine Weird Tales, writing fantasy, horror and science fiction stories noted for their lush, ornate prose and exotic invented worlds.

Life and poetry

Smith was born in Long Valley, California, and lived most of his life in a cabin near Auburn. Largely self-educated, he read voraciously, including the unabridged dictionary and the Encyclopædia Britannica. His first book of poems, The Star-Treader and Other Poems (1912), published when he was nineteen, brought praise from Ambrose Bierce and George Sterling, whose protégé he became. Volumes such as Odes and Sonnets (1918) and Ebony and Crystal (1922) established him as a leading West Coast poet.

Fiction

From 1929, driven partly by the need to support his ageing parents during the Depression, Smith wrote more than a hundred short stories for the pulps, inventing story cycles set in distinct worlds: Hyperborea, a primeval ice-age continent; Poseidonis, a remnant of Atlantis; Averoigne, a haunted medieval French province; Zothique, Earth's last continent under a dying sun; and Xiccarph, an alien planet. His tales — among them "The City of the Singing Flame", "The Empire of the Necromancers", "The Double Shadow" and "The Dark Eidolon" — combine cosmic horror, irony and decadent splendour. He corresponded extensively with Lovecraft, who mentioned him in "The Call of Cthulhu"'s wider circle of mythos writers.

Art and legacy

Smith also produced drawings, paintings and grotesque sculptures in soft stone. He largely stopped writing fiction after 1935 and died in 1961. Arkham House published his story collections beginning with Out of Space and Time (1942), and his work has been reissued in annotated editions, influencing generations of fantasy and horror writers.
