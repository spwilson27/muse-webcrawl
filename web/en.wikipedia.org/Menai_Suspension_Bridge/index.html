The Menai Suspension Bridge (Welsh: Pont Grog y Borth) is a suspension bridge spanning the Menai Strait between the island of Anglesey and the mainland of Wales. Designed by Thomas Telford and completed in 1826, it was one of the world's first great suspension bridges and a landmark of early 19th-century civil engineering, built to carry the London–Holyhead road (now the A5) and shorten the journey to Ireland via the port of Holyhead.

Design and construction

Before the bridge, crossing the strait depended on ferries and dangerous cattle swims. Telford suspended a deck 30 metres above high water — high enough for Royal Navy ships to pass — from sixteen massive wrought-iron chains anchored in rock tunnels on each shore. The main span of 176 metres (577 ft) was unprecedented, and the masonry towers stand 46 metres high. Construction began in 1819; the chains were hauled into place in 1825 in a celebrated operation, and the bridge opened on 30 January 1826, cutting the London–Holyhead journey by some nine hours.

Later history

The original wooden deck proved vulnerable to wind and was stiffened in 1840 and replaced in steel in 1893. In 1938–1940 the iron chains were replaced with steel ones without closing the bridge to traffic. Today the bridge carries the A55 local traffic and pedestrians alongside the larger Britannia Bridge (1850, rebuilt 1970s), and it is a Grade I listed building, admired as perhaps Telford's masterpiece.
