Aston Martin DB4 GT Zagato


The DB4 GT Zagato, introduced at the 1960 London Motor Show.

The Aston Martin DB4 GT Zagato is a grand tourer produced by Aston Martin from 1960 to 1963, combining the shortened, lightweight DB4 GT chassis with aluminium bodywork designed and built by the Italian coachbuilder Zagato in Milan. Only 19 original cars were built, making it one of the most valuable classic Aston Martins.

Design and performance

Zagato's Ercole Spada styled a curvaceous, pared-down body of thin aluminium that saved considerable weight over the standard DB4 GT. The 3.7-litre twin-cam straight-six engine produced around 314 horsepower, giving a top speed of about 154 mph (248 km/h) and acceleration competitive with contemporary Ferrari racing cars.

Racing

The cars were raced at Le Mans, the Goodwood Tourist Trophy and other events in the hands of drivers including Jim Clark and Roy Salvadori, though success against the Ferrari 250 GT SWB and 250 GTO proved elusive. Later "Sanction II" and "Sanction III" cars, built in the 1990s from unused chassis numbers, and a modern Continuation series have added to the original run.

Original examples have sold for well over ten million pounds at auction.
