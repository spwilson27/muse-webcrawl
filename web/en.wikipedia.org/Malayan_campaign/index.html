Malayan campaign

The Malayan campaign, frequently referred to as the Battle of Malaya, was a military campaign fought by Allied and Axis forces in Malaya, from 8 December 1941 to 15 February 1942 during the Second World War. It was dominated by land battles between British Commonwealth army units and the Imperial Japanese Army, with minor skirmishes at the beginning of the campaign between British Commonwealth and Royal Thai Armed Forces. The Japanese forces had a total air and naval supremacy in the campaign due to the low quality of British and Commonwealth air forces, as well as the sinking of the British capital ships Prince of Wales and Repulse on 10 December.

Background

Between the First and Second World Wars, British military strategy in the Far East was dominated by the "Singapore strategy": a plan to deter Japanese aggression by stationing a powerful fleet at the Singapore naval base. In practice, war in Europe meant the fleet could not be spared, and Malaya's landward defences were neglected in favour of air power that was never adequately provided. The British judged the jungle of the Malay Peninsula to be impassable to a large army, a judgement the Japanese would decisively overturn.

Invasion

The Japanese invasion began shortly after midnight on 8 December 1941 — before the attack on Pearl Harbor, local time — with landings at Kota Bharu in northern Malaya and at Songkhla and Pattani in Thailand. The invading force was the Japanese 25th Army under General Tomoyuki Yamashita, numbering only about 70,000 men but supported by superior tanks, aircraft and bicycle infantry. Japanese troops used the jungle and rubber plantations to outflank fixed British defensive positions such as the Jitra Line, which fell within days.

Advance down the peninsula

The Japanese advanced rapidly down both coasts of the peninsula. Key engagements included the Battle of Kampar, the disastrous Battle of Slim River on 7 January 1942, in which Japanese tanks shattered two Indian brigades, and the fighting around Muar and Gemas in Johore. On 31 January 1942 the last Allied forces withdrew across the Johore Strait to Singapore, blowing a gap in the causeway behind them.

Fall of Singapore

The campaign culminated in the Battle of Singapore. Japanese forces crossed the strait on 8 February 1942, and after a week of fighting Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival surrendered on 15 February. Some 80,000 British, Indian and Australian troops became prisoners of war, in addition to 50,000 taken in the rest of the campaign — the largest surrender of British-led forces in history. Prime Minister Winston Churchill called it "the worst disaster and largest capitulation in British history."

Aftermath

Malaya remained under Japanese occupation until September 1945. The defeat shattered British prestige in Asia and encouraged anti-colonial movements across the region. Yamashita earned the nickname "the Tiger of Malaya"; Percival spent the rest of the war as a prisoner and attended the Japanese surrender aboard USS Missouri in 1945.
