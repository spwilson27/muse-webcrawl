Amu Darya

The Amu Darya, also called the Oxus in ancient times, is a major river in Central Asia. It is formed by the junction of the Vakhsh and Panj rivers in the Pamir Mountains and flows northwest through Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. The river is about 2,400 kilometres (1,500 miles) long and historically emptied into the Aral Sea, though large-scale irrigation diversions have greatly reduced its flow.

History

The Oxus was a cradle of early civilisation: the Bronze Age Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex flourished along its upper course, and the river later formed the boundary between Iranian and Turanian lands in Persian tradition. In antiquity it marked stages of Alexander the Great's campaigns, and in the 19th century it became the frontier between the Russian and British spheres of influence in the "Great Game".

Water use and the Aral Sea

From the 1960s, Soviet irrigation projects diverted enormous volumes of Amu Darya water, primarily for cotton production. Together with diversions of the Syr Darya, this caused the Aral Sea to shrink dramatically, one of the worst environmental disasters of the 20th century. The river now frequently fails to reach the Aral Sea at all, ending in desert or in the Sarygamysh Lake.

Geography

The river's drainage basin covers around 534,700 square kilometres. Its principal tributaries include the Panj, Vakhsh, Kunduz, Surkhan Darya, Sherabad and Zeravshan. The Nurek Dam on the Vakhsh is among the tallest dams in the world.
