Internal fertilization

Internal fertilization is the union of an egg and sperm cell during sexual reproduction inside the female body. It is characteristic of most terrestrial animals, including mammals, birds, reptiles, and many insects, and contrasts with external fertilization, in which gametes meet outside the body, typically in water.

Methods

Insemination may occur through copulation, in which a male intromittent organ deposits sperm into the female reproductive tract; through a cloacal kiss, as in most birds; or through transfer of a spermatophore that the female takes up. Development of the fertilized egg may proceed by oviparity (egg-laying), viviparity (live birth), or ovoviviparity.

Advantages

Internal fertilization protects gametes from desiccation and predation, increases the likelihood that sperm reach the egg, and permits greater parental control over the environment of early development.
