Saint George's Cross

DesignA red cross on a white field
Associated withSaint George; England; Genoa; Georgia; several cities and orders

Saint George's Cross is a red cross on a white field, a heraldic symbol associated with Saint George since the time of the Crusades. It forms the national flag of England and appears in the flags and arms of many cities, regions and countries.

History

The red cross on white was worn by crusading soldiers and used by the Republic of Genoa, whose ships enjoyed English protection in return for a tribute, before becoming firmly associated with England. Saint George became England's patron saint in the Middle Ages.

Uses

The cross is a component of the Union Jack (combined with the crosses of Saints Andrew and Patrick), of the flag of Georgia (with four smaller crosses), of the White Ensign of the Royal Navy, and of the arms of cities including London, Milan and Barcelona.
