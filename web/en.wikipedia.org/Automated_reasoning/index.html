Automated reasoning
FieldComputer science, artificial intelligence, mathematical logic
SubareasAutomated theorem proving, model checking, SAT solving, logic programming

Automated reasoning is an area of computer science and mathematical logic concerned with developing computer programs that can reason completely, or nearly completely, automatically. It is usually considered a subfield of artificial intelligence, but it also has strong connections with theoretical computer science and philosophy.

Approaches

Major approaches include automated theorem proving, in which a system derives a formal proof of a conjecture from axioms; model checking, which verifies that a finite-state model satisfies a logical specification; satisfiability (SAT) and SMT solving; and term rewriting. Landmark developments included the resolution principle of J. Alan Robinson (1965) and systems such as Vampire, E, Otter and Prover9.

Applications

Automated reasoning is used in software and hardware verification, mathematics (including machine-checked proofs of the four colour theorem and the Kepler conjecture), program analysis, planning and the verification of safety-critical systems.
