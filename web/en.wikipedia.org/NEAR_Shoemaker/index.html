NEAR Shoemaker

NEAR Shoemaker
Mission type: Asteroid orbiter/lander
Operator: NASA / Johns Hopkins APL
Launch: February 17, 1996
Target: 433 Eros
Orbital insertion: February 14, 2000
Landing: February 12, 2001

NEAR Shoemaker, formally the Near Earth Asteroid Rendezvous mission, was a NASA robotic space probe launched in 1996 to study the near-Earth asteroid 433 Eros. Built and operated by the Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory as the first mission of NASA's Discovery Program, it became the first spacecraft to orbit an asteroid and the first to land on one.

Mission

NEAR launched on a Delta II rocket from Cape Canaveral on February 17, 1996. En route it flew by the main-belt asteroid 253 Mathilde in June 1997, returning the first close-up images of a C-type asteroid. A failed first rendezvous burn in December 1998 delayed arrival, but the spacecraft successfully entered orbit around Eros on February 14, 2000.

For a year NEAR mapped Eros from progressively lower orbits, measuring its shape, gravity field, composition, and magnetic environment. The asteroid proved to be a fractured, primitive body about 34 km long. The mission was renamed in honor of planetary scientist Eugene Shoemaker after his death in 1997.

Landing

Although not designed as a lander, on February 12, 2001 NEAR Shoemaker was guided to a soft touchdown in Eros's Himeros depression — the first landing on an asteroid. Its gamma-ray spectrometer continued returning data from the surface for about two weeks before the mission ended. See also 433 Eros.
