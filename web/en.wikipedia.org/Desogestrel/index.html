Desogestrel

Desogestrel is a progestin medication which is used in birth control pills for women. It is also used in the treatment of menopausal symptoms in women. The medication is available both alone and in combination with an estrogen. It is taken by mouth.

Desogestrel is a prodrug of etonogestrel, its active metabolite. It is a third-generation progestin derived from 19-nortestosterone (a gonane), developed by Organon and introduced for medical use in the early 1980s. It has weak androgenic activity compared with older progestins such as levonorgestrel.

Medical uses

Desogestrel is used in combined oral contraceptives together with ethinylestradiol (sold under brand names such as Marvelon and Mercilon) and as a progestogen-only pill ("mini-pill"). Some epidemiological studies have found a slightly higher relative risk of venous thromboembolism with third-generation pills compared with second-generation pills containing levonorgestrel, though the absolute risk remains low.

Pharmacology

After oral administration, desogestrel is rapidly converted in the liver and intestinal wall to etonogestrel (3-ketodesogestrel), which binds with high affinity to the progesterone receptor, suppressing ovulation and thickening cervical mucus. Etonogestrel is also the active progestin used in the contraceptive implant and vaginal ring.
