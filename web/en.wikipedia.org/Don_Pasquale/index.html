Don Pasquale

Don Pasquale is an opera buffa, or comic opera, in three acts by Gaetano Donizetti with an Italian libretto by Giovanni Ruffini (completed in part by the composer). It premiered at the Théâtre-Italien in Paris on 3 January 1843 and is considered the last great masterpiece of the opera buffa tradition.

Synopsis

The elderly bachelor Don Pasquale plans to marry a young bride and disinherit his nephew Ernesto, who loves the widow Norina. The family friend Dr. Malatesta devises a trick: Norina poses as the demure convent girl "Sofronia" in a sham marriage to Pasquale, then immediately becomes a spendthrift, domineering wife. Tormented, Pasquale agrees to end the "marriage" and gladly blesses the union of Ernesto and Norina.

Music

Notable numbers include Ernesto's aria "Com'è gentil," Norina's "Quel guardo il cavaliere," and the patter duet "Cheti, cheti, immantinente" for Pasquale and Malatesta. The opera remains a staple of the international repertoire.
