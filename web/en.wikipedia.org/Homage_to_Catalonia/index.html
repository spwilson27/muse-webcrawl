Homage to Catalonia

Homage to Catalonia
Author: George Orwell
Language: English
Subject: Spanish Civil War
Publisher: Secker & Warburg
Publication date: 25 April 1938

Homage to Catalonia is George Orwell's personal account of his experiences and observations fighting in the Spanish Civil War for the POUM militia of the Republican army. It was published in the United Kingdom on 25 April 1938. The book describes Orwell's admiration for the social revolution he found in Barcelona, his service on the Aragon front, his wounding by a sniper, and his disillusionment during the suppression of the POUM by Soviet-backed Communists, which forced him to flee Spain.

Background

Orwell arrived in Barcelona in December 1936 intending to write newspaper articles, but almost immediately enlisted in the militia of the POUM (Workers' Party of Marxist Unification), an anti-Stalinist Marxist party, because it was the militia open to him through his contacts with the Independent Labour Party. He served on the relatively quiet Aragon front near Huesca through the winter of 1936–37, experiencing the cold, lice, shortages and boredom of trench warfare, and came to admire the egalitarian spirit of the militia, in which officers and men drew the same pay and addressed each other as equals.

Synopsis

The book opens with Orwell's awed description of revolutionary Barcelona, where in late 1936 the working class appeared to be in control: militias ran the city, churches had been burned, waiters looked customers in the face, and formal address had vanished. After his time at the front, Orwell returned to Barcelona on leave in May 1937 and was caught in the "May Days" street fighting between anarchists and POUM on one side and Communist-led government forces on the other. Days after returning to the front he was shot through the throat by a sniper and nearly killed. While he recovered, the Spanish government, under Communist pressure, declared the POUM illegal; its leader Andreu Nin was arrested and executed, and Orwell's comrades were imprisoned. Orwell and his wife Eileen escaped across the French border in June 1937. Two chapters of political analysis — on the press coverage of the war and the internal politics of the Republic — were moved to appendices in later editions at Orwell's wish.

Publication and reception

The book was published by Secker & Warburg after Orwell's usual publisher, Victor Gollancz, refused it on political grounds. It sold poorly — only some 700 copies in Orwell's lifetime — and its criticism of the Soviet Union and praise of the revolutionary left were unwelcome to much of the British left. The first United States edition appeared only in 1952, with an introduction by Lionel Trilling, after the success of Animal Farm and Nineteen Eighty-Four had made Orwell famous; it was then widely praised.

Legacy

Homage to Catalonia is now regarded as a classic of war reportage and political witness, and as the formative experience behind Orwell's later works: his hatred of totalitarianism, his insistence on the manipulation of truth and language, and his democratic socialism were all sharpened in Spain, as he wrote in 1946 — "The Spanish war and other events in 1936–37 turned the scale and thereafter I knew where I stood." The book remains a key primary source on the Spanish Revolution and the Barcelona May Days.
