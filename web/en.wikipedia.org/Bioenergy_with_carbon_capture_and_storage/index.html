Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS) is the process of extracting bioenergy from biomass and capturing and storing the carbon, thereby removing it from the atmosphere. Because the carbon in biomass was drawn from the air as the plants grew, capturing and permanently storing the carbon dioxide released on combustion or fermentation can yield net negative emissions, making BECCS a proposed carbon dioxide removal technology.

Process

Biomass — energy crops, forestry and agricultural residues, or organic wastes — is converted to heat, electricity, or fuels such as bioethanol. The carbon dioxide produced is captured from the flue gas or fermentation stream and injected into deep geological formations for long-term storage. In ethanol production the fermentation CO2 stream is relatively pure and cheap to capture; a notable example is the ethanol plant in Decatur, Illinois, which has stored CO2 in a saline aquifer.

Role in climate scenarios

Many integrated assessment models used by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change rely on large-scale BECCS deployment in the second half of the century to meet 1.5 °C or 2 °C targets after overshoot. Critics note that these scenarios may assume land, water and biomass supplies that compete with food production and ecosystems.

Criticisms and constraints

The carbon negativity of BECCS depends on the full life cycle: emissions from growing, harvesting, transporting and processing biomass, and any land-use change, must be smaller than the amount stored. Sustainability of biomass supply, storage permanence, cost, and competition for land are the principal constraints on its scale.
