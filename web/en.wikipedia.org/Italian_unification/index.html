Italian unification

Italian unification
Italian: Risorgimento
Date: 1848–1871
Result: Kingdom of Italy proclaimed 1861; Rome capital 1871
Key figures: Victor Emmanuel II, Cavour, Garibaldi, Mazzini

Italian unification (Italian: Unità d'Italia), also known as the Risorgimento ('Resurgence'), was the 19th-century political and social movement that in 1861 resulted in the consolidation of different states of the Italian Peninsula into a single state, the Kingdom of Italy.

Background

After Napoleon's fall, Italy was divided among Austrian-influenced states, the Kingdom of Sardinia (Piedmont), the Papal States and the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies. Nationalist ideas spread, influenced by the French Revolution.

Course

Failed revolutions in 1848–49 were followed by Piedmontese diplomacy under Count Camillo di Cavour, alliance with France, and war against Austria in 1859. In 1860 Giuseppe Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand conquered Sicily and Naples. The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed on 17 March 1861 under Victor Emmanuel II. Venetia joined in 1866 and Rome in 1870, becoming capital in 1871.

Legacy

Unification left the 'Southern Question' of north–south economic disparity and conflict with the Papacy (the Roman Question, resolved only in 1929).
