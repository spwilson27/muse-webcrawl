Miracle

A miracle is an event that is inexplicable by natural or scientific laws and accordingly gets attributed to some supernatural or preternatural cause. Various religions claim reported miracles, which often concern healing, the control of nature, or the resurrection of the dead.

Definitions and philosophy

David Hume, in An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding (1748), defined a miracle as a "transgression of a law of nature by a particular volition of the Deity" and argued that testimony can never outweigh the uniform experience supporting natural laws. Philosophers continue to debate whether miracles are logically possible and how reported miracles should be evaluated.

In religions

In Christianity, miracles are attributed to Jesus and the saints; the Catholic Church investigates claimed miracles as part of the canonization process, requiring documented medical cures unexplained by science. Islam regards the Quran itself as the miracle of Muhammad, and miracles (mu'jizat) are attributed to prophets. Hinduism and Buddhism also contain extensive traditions of miraculous powers and events.
