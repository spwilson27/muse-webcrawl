The Tour of Flanders (Dutch: Ronde van Vlaanderen) is an annual one-day road cycling race held in Belgium each spring. It is one of the five "Monuments" of professional cycling and the most celebrated of the Flemish cobbled classics. The race starts in Antwerp (having earlier started in Ghent and Bruges) and finishes in Oudenaarde, covering roughly 270 kilometres of narrow roads, steep cobbled climbs known as hellingen, and flat cobbled sectors.

History

The race was founded in 1913 by Karel Van Wijnendaele, sports editor of the newspaper Sportwereld, partly to promote the paper and Flemish cycling. The first edition was won by Paul Deman. Racing was suspended during World War I and again briefly during World War II, but the Tour of Flanders has otherwise been held every year, becoming a defining symbol of Flemish identity and pride.

For decades the iconic climb of the Muur van Geraardsbergen, with its chapel at the summit, and the Bosberg formed the decisive finale. In 2012 the finish moved to Oudenaarde, and the route was redesigned around repeated circuits including the Oude Kwaremont and the Paterberg, which typically decide the race in its closing kilometres.

The climbs

Famous hellingen include:

  • Oude Kwaremont — a long, rough cobbled drag.
  • Paterberg — short and brutally steep, with gradients around 20 percent.
  • Koppenberg — notoriously steep and slippery when wet; ramps reach about 22 percent.
  • Muur van Geraardsbergen — the historic wall climb to the chapel.
  • Taaienberg, Kruisberg and Steenbeekdries.

Winners and records

Belgian riders dominated for much of the race's history. Achiel Buysse, Fiorenzo Magni, Eric Leman, Johan Museeuw, Tom Boonen and Fabian Cancellara are among those with three victories; Magni, an Italian, remains remarkable for winning three consecutive editions (1949–1951). In recent years Mathieu van der Poel and Tadej Pogačar have added editions to their palmarès. A women's race has been held since 2004, won multiple times by riders such as Lotte Kopecky and Elisa Longo Borghini.
