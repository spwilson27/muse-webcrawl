The 1997 Formula One World Championship was the 48th season of Formula One. It culminated in one of the most controversial finales in the sport's history at Jerez, where Jacques Villeneuve won the Drivers' Championship after a collision with Michael Schumacher, who was subsequently disqualified from the championship standings for unsportsmanlike conduct.

Season summary

Williams and Ferrari were the season's leading teams. Villeneuve won seven races, Schumacher five. Defending champion Damon Hill, now with Arrows, came within a lap of a famous victory in Hungary before a throttle problem dropped him to second behind Villeneuve. The Luxembourg Grand Prix at the Nürburgring gave Villeneuve a decisive points swing when both McLarens retired from the lead, and he arrived at the Jerez finale one point behind Schumacher.

The Jerez collision

Qualifying produced an unprecedented three-way dead heat, with Villeneuve, Schumacher and Heinz-Harald Frentzen all recording identical times; Villeneuve took pole as the first to set it. In the race, on lap 48, Villeneuve dived inside Schumacher at the Dry Sack hairpin. Schumacher turned in on the Williams; his Ferrari ended in the gravel and retired, while Villeneuve nursed his damaged car to third place — enough for the title. The FIA later excluded Schumacher from the 1997 championship (while allowing his wins and points records to stand in race statistics) and ordered him to participate in a road-safety campaign.

Legacy

Villeneuve became the first Canadian Formula One world champion, following in the footsteps of his father Gilles. The 1997 title was Williams' last Drivers' and Constructors' championship, and the last won by a Renault-powered car until 2005. The Jerez incident remains a central reference point in debates about Schumacher's career and about championship-deciding collisions.
