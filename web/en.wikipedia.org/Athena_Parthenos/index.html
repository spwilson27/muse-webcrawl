Athena Parthenos

The Athena Parthenos (Ancient Greek: Ἀθηνᾶ Παρθένος, "Athena the Virgin") is a lost massive chryselephantine sculpture of the Greek goddess Athena, made by Phidias and his assistants and housed in the Parthenon in Athens. Its epithet was an essential character of the goddess and gave the temple its name. Reproductions of it exist in smaller marble versions, coins, and other works, and a full-scale replica stands in the Parthenon replica in Nashville, Tennessee.

Description

The statue stood about 11.5 metres (38 feet) tall, not including its base. Athena stood upright, wearing a helmet adorned with a sphinx and two griffins, an aegis with the head of Medusa on her breast, and a long peplos. In her right hand she held a small figure of Nike (Victory), roughly 2 metres tall, and her left hand rested on a shield beside which coiled a great serpent, identified with Erichthonius. Her spear leaned against her shoulder.

The shield's exterior was decorated with reliefs of the Amazonomachy (the battle of the Athenians against the Amazons), while the interior showed the Gigantomachy, the war of the gods against the giants. The base depicted the birth of Pandora.

History

The statue was dedicated in 438 BC, though work on its decorative sculptures continued until 432 BC. The gold plates that formed the goddess's drapery were detachable and constituted a state treasury: they weighed, according to Thucydides, not less than forty talents of pure gold. Phidias was accused of embezzling some of this gold — and of impiety for allegedly including portraits of himself and Pericles on the shield — charges that ancient sources link to his exile or imprisonment.

The original statue was lost in late antiquity; it may have been removed to Constantinople and destroyed by fire, or perished with the Parthenon's own destruction. The Varvakeion Athena, a small Roman-era marble copy found in Athens, is considered the most faithful surviving reproduction.

Modern replica

In 1990, sculptor Alan LeQuire completed a full-scale recreation of the Athena Parthenos for the full-size Parthenon replica in Nashville's Centennial Park; in 2002 it was gilded, becoming the largest piece of indoor sculpture in the Western world.
