The Battle of Ponta Delgada, also called the Battle of Vila Franca do Campo or the Battle of Terceira, was a naval battle fought on 26 July 1582 off the island of São Miguel in the Azores. It was part of the War of the Portuguese Succession, in which Philip II of Spain claimed the Portuguese crown against the pretender António, Prior of Crato, who was backed by France.

A French expedition under Filippo di Piero Strozzi, carrying troops to sustain the Azores' resistance to Philip, met the Spanish fleet of Álvaro de Bazán, Marquis of Santa Cruz. Although the French had comparable numbers, the heavier Spanish galleons prevailed in a hard fight; Strozzi was killed and his fleet routed, with many prisoners later executed as pirates. It is often described as the first naval battle fought in the open mid-ocean.

Aftermath

The defeat doomed French intervention in the Azores. In 1583 the Marquis of Santa Cruz returned with an amphibious force and conquered Terceira Island, completing the Spanish conquest of the archipelago and consolidating the Iberian Union under Philip II.
