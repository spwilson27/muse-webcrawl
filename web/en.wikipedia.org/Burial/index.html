Burial

Burial
Funeral practice
Also called: Interment, inhumation
Earliest evidence: Middle Paleolithic

Burial, also known as interment or inhumation, is a method of final disposition whereby a dead body is placed into the ground, sometimes with objects. This is usually accomplished by excavating a pit or trench, placing the deceased and objects in it, and covering it over.

History

Intentional burial is among the earliest detectable forms of religious practice. Burials attributed to Neanderthals and early modern humans date back more than 100,000 years. Ancient Egyptian mummification, ship burials of the Vikings, and the terracotta army of Qin Shi Huang illustrate the cultural importance of grave goods.

Reasons for burial

Motives include respect for the dead, hygiene and sanitation, religious belief in an afterlife, and the needs of mourning and remembrance. Many cultures hold that the body must be treated with dignity and that burial grounds are sacred spaces.

Methods and locations

Bodies are usually buried in cemeteries or churchyards, often in coffins or shrouds. Alternatives include cremation, sea burial, sky burial, natural (green) burial and burial in mausoleums or catacombs. Laws in most countries regulate the depth of graves and the places where burial is permitted.
