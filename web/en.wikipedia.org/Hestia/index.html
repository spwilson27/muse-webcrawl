Hestia (Greek: Ἑστία, "hearth") is the ancient Greek goddess of the hearth, home, domesticity, and the sacrificial flame. The eldest child of Cronus and Rhea, she was the first swallowed by her father and the last disgorged, making her both oldest and youngest of the Olympians. Courts of Poseidon and Apollo, she swore perpetual virginity and received a place of honour at the centre of the house and of Olympus.

Cult and role

Because the hearth was the centre of Greek domestic and civic life, Hestia received the first and last portions of every sacrifice, and in prayer she was traditionally invoked first. Every city-state maintained a public hearth (prytaneion) with her undying flame, from which colonists carried fire to found new cities. In myth she is a still centre: she takes almost no part in the Olympians' quarrels, and in later lists of the twelve Olympians she is sometimes said to have yielded her seat to Dionysus. Her Roman equivalent, Vesta, was served in Rome by the Vestal Virgins.
