Shield volcano

Shield volcano
TypeVolcano morphology
LavaLow-viscosity mafic (basaltic), high-temperature
SlopeGentle, about 2–10°
EruptionsMostly effusive; Hawaiian-style fountains and flows
ExamplesMauna Loa, Kīlauea (Hawaii); Erta Ale (Ethiopia); Piton de la Fournaise (Réunion); Olympus Mons (Mars)

A shield volcano is a broad, gently sloping volcano built almost entirely from fluid lava flows. Named for its resemblance to a warrior's shield lying on the ground, it forms where low-viscosity basaltic magma erupts repeatedly: each thin flow travels far before solidifying, so the edifice grows wide rather than steep. Shield volcanoes are among the largest volcanoes on Earth — and include the largest known in the solar system, Olympus Mons on Mars.

Formation and structure

Shield volcanoes typically develop over mantle hotspots or at divergent plate boundaries. A summit caldera often forms where the magma chamber drains and the roof collapses; radial rift zones extend from the summit, channelling eruptions down the flanks. Hawaiian shields pass through growth stages — a submarine pillow-lava stage, an explosive subaerial transition, the main shield-building stage of voluminous fluid flows, and a waning post-shield stage of more viscous, gas-richer lavas that add a steeper cap.

Eruptive style

Eruptions are dominantly effusive rather than explosive: fire fountains feed pāhoehoe and ʻaʻā flows that can travel tens of kilometres, and lava lakes may persist in summit craters, as at Kīlauea and Erta Ale. Because flows move slowly and eruptions are comparatively predictable, shield eruptions are generally less lethal than stratovolcano eruptions, though they readily destroy property and can produce voluminous gas emissions ("vog").

Occurrence

The Hawaiian Islands are the archetype: Mauna Loa, rising more than 9 km from the ocean floor, is Earth's largest active shield, and Kīlauea has been in near-continuous eruption in recent decades. Other notable shields include the Galápagos volcanoes, Iceland's Skjaldbreiður (whose name gives the type), Piton de la Fournaise on Réunion, Erta Ale in Ethiopia, and the great Tharsis shields of Mars.
