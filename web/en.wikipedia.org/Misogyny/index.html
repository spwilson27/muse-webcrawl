Misogyny

Misogyny is hatred of, contempt for, or prejudice against women or girls. It is a form of sexism that can keep women at a lower social status than men, thus maintaining the social roles of patriarchy. Misogyny has been widely practised for thousands of years; it is found in sacred texts, mythology, philosophy, and in institutional practices that exclude or subordinate women.

Definitions and analysis

The word derives from the Greek misein (to hate) and gynē (woman). Sociologist Allan Johnson describes misogyny as a central part of sexist prejudice and ideology, manifested in forms ranging from disparaging jokes and pornography to violence and the teaching of self-contempt. Philosopher Kate Manne argues that misogyny is best understood not primarily as a psychological attitude of individual men but as the enforcement branch of a patriarchal order: the system that punishes women who violate patriarchal norms and rewards those who uphold them, distinct from sexism as the ideology that justifies those norms.

Historical examples

Woman-hating texts appear from Hesiod's account of Pandora onward; Aristotle described women as incomplete men, and medieval and early modern witch trials fell overwhelmingly on women. In the modern era, misogyny has been analysed in relation to violence against women, occupational segregation, online harassment and extremist communities such as the incel subculture.

Related concepts

Misogyny is contrasted with misandry (prejudice against men) and distinguished from internalised misogyny, in which women adopt hostile attitudes toward other women or themselves. Feminist movements have made challenging misogyny a central aim.
