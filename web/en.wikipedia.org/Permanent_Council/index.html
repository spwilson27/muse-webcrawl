Permanent Council

The Permanent Council is one of the two main political bodies of the Organization of American States (OAS), the other being the General Assembly. Composed of one ambassadorial representative from each member state, it keeps the organisation functioning between sessions of the General Assembly and serves as a standing forum for consultation on matters affecting the Americas.

Functions

The council meets regularly at OAS headquarters in Washington, D.C. Its responsibilities include:

  • Keeping vigilance over the maintenance of friendly relations among member states;
  • Assisting in the peaceful settlement of disputes between member states;
  • Acting as the preparatory committee for the General Assembly;
  • Carrying out decisions of the General Assembly and of the Meeting of Consultation of Ministers of Foreign Affairs;
  • Considering reports of OAS organs, including the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights.

Chairmanship

The chair of the Permanent Council rotates among the member states' representatives in order of precedence, typically every three months. The council's work is carried forward through standing committees, including committees on juridical and political affairs, hemispheric security, and administrative and budgetary matters.

Meetings of consultation

The Permanent Council may also convoke Meetings of Consultation of Ministers of Foreign Affairs to consider problems of an urgent nature and of common interest to the American states, a mechanism used in regional crises and in responses to interruptions of democratic order in member states.
