Naraka

Naraka (Sanskrit: नरक) is a term found in the dharmic religions — Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism — denoting a realm of torment and suffering, often compared to a hell or purgatory. The concept differs significantly among traditions: in some it is a temporary abode where the soul is purified through suffering for its misdeeds; in others it is a place of more prolonged punishment. Unlike the eternal hell of Abrahamic faiths, Naraka is generally not permanent — after the term of punishment is exhausted, the being is reborn according to its karma.

In Hinduism

In Hindu texts, Naraka is presided over by Yama, the god of death, whose record-keeper Chitragupta keeps account of every being's deeds. The Garuda Purana and other texts describe numerous distinct hells — lists commonly enumerate 28, including Tamisra, Andhatamisra, Raurava, Kumbhipaka and Kalasutra — each suited to particular sins such as theft, murder, cruelty or dishonesty.

In Buddhism

Buddhist cosmology describes Naraka as one of the six realms of rebirth (gati). Buddhist Narakas are divided into hot and cold hells, each with sub-levels; the deepest, Avici, is reserved for the gravest offences such as patricide or shedding the blood of a Buddha. Even there, residence is finite, though measured in immense spans of time.

In Jainism

Jain cosmology places Naraka-bhumi — seven hells of increasing suffering — beneath the earth, where hellish beings (naraki) endure torment inflicted by their environment and by demonic beings, until their inauspicious karma is exhausted.
