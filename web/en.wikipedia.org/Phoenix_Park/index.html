The Phoenix Park (Irish: Páirc an Fhionnuisce) is a large urban park in Dublin, Ireland, lying 2–4 km west of the city centre, north of the River Liffey. At 707 hectares (1,750 acres), it is one of the largest enclosed recreational spaces in any European capital. Its name derives not from the mythical bird but from the Irish fionn uisce, meaning "clear water".

History

The park began as a royal hunting ground in the 1660s under the Duke of Ormond and was walled and stocked with fallow deer — a herd of several hundred still roams the park today. The Earl of Chesterfield opened it to the public in 1747 and erected the Phoenix Monument. In 1882 the park was the scene of the Phoenix Park murders, the assassinations of the Chief Secretary for Ireland, Lord Frederick Cavendish, and his under-secretary Thomas Burke.

Attractions

The park contains Áras an Uachtaráin, the residence of the President of Ireland; Farmleigh House; the residence of the US ambassador; Dublin Zoo (founded 1830, one of the world's oldest zoos); the Wellington Monument, a 62-metre obelisk; the Papal Cross, erected for Pope John Paul II's 1979 visit, when a million people attended Mass in the park; and Ashtown Castle with its visitor centre. It is also a venue for concerts, running, cycling, polo, and cricket.
