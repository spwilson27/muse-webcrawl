Fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the FGFR3 gene, located on the short arm of chromosome 4 at band 16.3. It is a member of the fibroblast growth factor receptor family of receptor tyrosine kinases, which regulate cellular proliferation, differentiation, and survival in response to fibroblast growth factors (FGFs).

Structure and function

Like other FGFRs, FGFR3 consists of an extracellular ligand-binding region with three immunoglobulin-like domains, a single transmembrane helix, and a split intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. Binding of an FGF ligand, stabilized by heparan sulfate, dimerizes the receptor and triggers autophosphorylation, activating downstream pathways including MAPK/ERK, PI3K/AKT, PLCγ, and STAT signalling. In the growth plate, FGFR3 acts as a negative regulator of endochondral bone growth, restraining chondrocyte proliferation.

Clinical significance

Gain-of-function mutations in FGFR3 cause several skeletal dysplasias. The G380R substitution is responsible for about 98% of cases of achondroplasia, the most common form of disproportionate short stature. Other mutations cause hypochondroplasia, thanatophoric dysplasia types I and II, SADDAN syndrome, and the craniosynostosis of Muenke syndrome. Conversely, loss-of-function mutations produce tall stature with hearing loss (CATSHL syndrome).

FGFR3 is also implicated in cancer: activating mutations and FGFR3–TACC3 fusions are common in urothelial (bladder) carcinoma, and the FGFR inhibitor erdafitinib is approved for FGFR3-altered metastatic bladder cancer. A t(4;14) translocation in multiple myeloma places FGFR3 under a strong enhancer. The C-type natriuretic peptide analogue vosoritide, approved for achondroplasia, counteracts FGFR3 signalling downstream via the MAPK pathway.
