Bar association

A bar association is a professional association of lawyers, typically organized at the level of a jurisdiction such as a state, province, or country. Bar associations may be responsible for licensing and regulating the legal profession, disciplining members, providing continuing legal education, and offering services such as lawyer referral programs.

Types

In some jurisdictions the bar association is a mandatory ("integrated" or "unified") body: lawyers must belong to it in order to practice. In others, associations such as the American Bar Association in the United States are voluntary organizations that set model standards — the ABA accredits law schools and publishes the Model Rules of Professional Conduct — while licensing is handled by state bars and courts.

Functions

Bar associations commonly administer bar examinations, handle complaints against attorneys, publish journals, and promote access to justice and pro bono work.
