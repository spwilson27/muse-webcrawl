Historic Centre of Macao

The Historic Centre of Macao is a collection of over twenty historic sites in Macao, a Special Administrative Region of China, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2005. The sites reflect more than four centuries of cultural, architectural, and technological exchange between Portugal and China after the Portuguese established a trading settlement at Macao in the 1550s.

Principal sites

The inscribed area includes two zones. The first centres on the old city: the Ruins of St. Paul's (the surviving façade of the 17th-century Church of Mater Dei), St. Dominic's Church, the A-Ma Temple, Senado Square, the Leal Senado Building, the Moorish Barracks, and the churches of St. Lawrence, St. Joseph's Seminary, St. Augustine, and the Cathedral, along with the Dom Pedro V Theatre and the Sir Robert Ho Tung Library. The second zone contains the Guia Fortress, chapel, and lighthouse — the oldest modern lighthouse on the China coast.

Significance

UNESCO recognised the centre as testimony to the earliest and longest-lasting encounter between China and the West, visible in the fusion of Portuguese and Chinese architectural styles, street layouts, and religious and civic institutions that developed side by side in the city.
