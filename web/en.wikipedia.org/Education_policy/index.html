Education policy

Education policy or educational policy consists of the principles and government policies in the education sector that have an objective of enhancing educational outcomes. It involves laws, funding mechanisms, governance structures, curriculum decisions, and debates over what education is for — the preparation of citizens, the development of human capital for the economy, social mobility, and personal flourishing.

Key questions

Education policy addresses recurring questions: Who should provide education — the state, private providers, religious bodies, or a mixture? How should schools be funded and held accountable? What should be taught (the curriculum), and in which language? How selective should admissions be? Should higher education be free, subsidised, or fee-based? And how should teachers be trained, paid, and evaluated?

Approaches and debates

Major international trends include the expansion of compulsory schooling, standardised testing and accountability regimes (such as the former U.S. No Child Left Behind Act), school choice and charter schools, inclusive education for students with disabilities, early childhood education, and the growing role of international comparisons such as PISA in shaping national reforms — sometimes criticised as "policy borrowing."

Researchers study education policy through political science, economics, and sociology, examining how policies are formed, implemented, and resisted. Policy goals frequently conflict: equity versus excellence, autonomy versus accountability, and standardisation versus local adaptation recur across systems worldwide.
