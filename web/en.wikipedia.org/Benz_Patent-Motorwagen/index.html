Benz Patent-Motorwagen

The Benz Patent-Motorwagen, built by Carl Benz in 1885 and patented in January 1886 (DRP No. 37435), is widely regarded as the first practical automobile — the first vehicle designed from the outset to be propelled by an internal combustion engine.

Design

The three-wheeled vehicle had a lightweight tubular steel frame, a single-cylinder four-stroke engine of 954 cc producing about 0.75 horsepower mounted at the rear, electric ignition, a differential and rack-and-pinion steering of the front wheel.

The first long-distance journey

In August 1888 Benz's wife Bertha Benz, without his knowledge, drove a Patent-Motorwagen 106 km from Mannheim to Pforzheim with her sons, proving the automobile's practicality and generating enormous publicity.

Production

Twenty-five Patent-Motorwagens were built between 1886 and 1893, making it the first commercially available automobile.
