Medical evacuation

Medical evacuation, often abbreviated medevac or medivac, is the timely and efficient movement of casualties and seriously ill patients, usually by air, from the scene of an incident or a lower-level facility to definitive medical care, with en-route treatment by trained personnel.

History

Organised air evacuation began in the First World War and expanded greatly with helicopters in the Korean and Vietnam Wars, where rapid evacuation to surgical hospitals sharply improved survival — the origin of the "golden hour" concept.

Civilian and military use

Today medevac includes military casualty evacuation (CASEVAC), civilian air ambulances, helicopter emergency medical services, and international repatriation flights staffed by critical-care teams.
