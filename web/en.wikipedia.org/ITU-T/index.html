ITU-T

The ITU Telecommunication Standardization Sector (ITU-T) is one of the three sectors of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), a specialized agency of the United Nations. Based in Geneva, Switzerland, ITU-T coordinates standards for telecommunications and information and communication technologies. Its standards, called Recommendations, define much of the world's communications infrastructure.

History

ITU-T succeeded the International Telegraph and Telephone Consultative Committee (CCITT), which had coordinated international telegraph and telephone standards since 1956 (and whose predecessor bodies date to the 19th century). The current structure was adopted in 1993 as part of an ITU reorganization.

Standards

ITU-T Recommendations are organized in lettered series. Well-known examples include the G series for transmission systems (e.g. G.711 voice coding, GPON fibre access standards such as G.984), the H series for audiovisual systems (including the video coding standards H.264, developed jointly with ISO/IEC MPEG, and H.265), the X series for data networks and security (including X.509 public-key certificates), and the V series for modem communications. Most ITU-T Recommendations are freely available to download.

Organization

Work is carried out in study groups covering topics from network architecture and optical transport to multimedia and future networks. The World Telecommunication Standardization Assembly (WTSA), held every four years, sets the sector's work programme.
