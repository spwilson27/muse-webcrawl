Diet Coke, marketed in some countries as Coca-Cola Light, is a sugar-free and low-calorie soft drink produced and distributed by the Coca-Cola Company. It was first introduced in the United States on August 9, 1982, at a gala launch at Radio City Music Hall in New York City, and was the first new brand to use the Coca-Cola name since 1886. Diet Coke does not use a modified form of the Coca-Cola recipe; it is based on a separate formula (historically derived from the discontinued Tab formula lineage), sweetened with aspartame rather than sugar or high-fructose corn syrup.

History

Coca-Cola's first diet drink was Tab (1963), sold in distinctive pink cans. When the company decided that the flagship trademark itself could carry a diet product, it developed Diet Coke as a premium brand, famously launched with the Rockettes at Radio City Music Hall. It rapidly became the best-selling diet soft drink in the United States, overtaking Tab and Diet Pepsi; by the mid-1980s it ranked among the top-selling soft drinks of any kind. New Coke (1985) briefly changed the full-sugar line but not Diet Coke's formula.

Formulation and variants

Diet Coke contains carbonated water, caramel colour, aspartame, phosphoric acid, potassium benzoate, natural flavours, citric acid and caffeine (about 46 mg per 12 fl oz can — more than regular Coca-Cola). Spin-offs have included Diet Coke with Lemon, Diet Coke with Lime, Diet Cherry Coke, Diet Coke with Splenda (sucralose), and a 2018 relaunch in tall "sleek cans" with flavours such as Ginger Lime, Feisty Cherry, Zesty Blood Orange and Twisted Mango. Caffeine-Free Diet Coke debuted in 1983. Diet Coke differs from Coca-Cola Zero Sugar, which is formulated to taste like standard Coke.

Marketing and culture

Advertising campaigns have included the 1994 "Diet Coke Break" commercials featuring actor Lucky Vanous, the silver-can aesthetic, and collaborations with fashion designers such as Karl Lagerfeld, Jean Paul Gaultier and Marc Jacobs, who served as creative director in 2013. The brand developed a notably loyal, predominantly female consumer base and became one of Coca-Cola's flagship global brands, though overall soda-category volumes — and internal competition from Coca-Cola Zero Sugar — have reduced its sales since the 2010s.

Health discussions

As an aspartame-sweetened beverage, Diet Coke has periodically been discussed in debates over artificial sweeteners. In 2023 the WHO's IARC classified aspartame as "possibly carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2B) while the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee maintained its acceptable daily intake; regulators in the US and EU continue to regard aspartame as safe at permitted levels.
