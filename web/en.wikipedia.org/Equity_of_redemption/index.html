Equity of redemption

Equity of redemption

Field
English land law, mortgage law
Holder
Mortgagor
Maxim
"Once a mortgage, always a mortgage"

The equity of redemption is the right of a mortgagor in English law (and legal systems derived from it) to redeem mortgaged property — that is, to recover it free of the mortgage — by paying the debt in full, even after the contractual date for repayment has passed. It arose in the Court of Chancery, which intervened against the harsh common-law rule that missing the repayment date forfeited the land to the mortgagee.

Nature of the right

The equity of redemption is itself an equitable interest in the land, which the mortgagor can sell, lease, or mortgage further. Courts protect it jealously: any term that "clogs" or fetters the right to redeem is void, under the doctrine summarised as "once a mortgage, always a mortgage". Collateral advantages stipulated by the lender may be struck down if they endure after redemption or are unconscionable.

Foreclosure

The right is extinguished by a court order of foreclosure, by sale of the property under the lender's power of sale, or after long adverse possession. Modern mortgage law in many jurisdictions has supplemented it with statutory rights of reinstatement and redemption periods after sale.
