Red Terror (Ethiopia)

The Ethiopian Red Terror, known in Amharic as Qey Shibir, was a violent political campaign carried out by the Derg — the communist military junta led by Mengistu Haile Mariam — against rival political groups in Ethiopia and Eritrea, principally the Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Party (EPRP) and the All-Ethiopia Socialist Movement (MEISON). It followed the 1974 Ethiopian revolution and peaked in 1977–1978.

Background

After the Derg deposed Emperor Haile Selassie in 1974, leftist student-movement parties competed violently over the direction of the revolution. The EPRP accused the regime of betraying the revolution and began assassinating Derg supporters (the "White Terror"). In early 1977 Mengistu consolidated power within the Derg, and in March–April 1977 the regime officially unleashed the Red Terror: urban dwellers' associations (kebeles) and defence squads were armed and authorised to identify and kill "counter-revolutionaries".

Course and toll

Thousands of young people — students, teachers and suspected sympathisers — were rounded up, tortured and summarily executed; bodies were frequently left in the streets or displayed, and families were charged for the bullets and for permission to bury their dead. Estimates of the death toll vary widely, from tens of thousands to as many as several hundred thousand when combined with the concurrent civil wars and purges. Victims are commemorated by the Red Terror Martyrs' Memorial Museum in Addis Ababa, opened in 2010.

Aftermath

The Derg fell in 1991. Mengistu fled to Zimbabwe, where he remains in exile; in 2007 an Ethiopian court sentenced him to death in absentia for genocide. Other former officials were tried in lengthy proceedings in Addis Ababa.
