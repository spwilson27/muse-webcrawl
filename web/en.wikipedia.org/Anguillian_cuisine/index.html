Anguillian cuisine

Grilled crayfish and seafood are staples of Anguillian cooking

Anguillian cuisine is the cuisine of Anguilla, a British overseas territory in the Caribbean. It is influenced by native Arawak, African, Spanish, French and English cooking traditions, and centres heavily on the abundant seafood of the surrounding waters.

Seafood

Seafood is the foundation of the Anguillian table. Lobster, crayfish (spiny lobster), conch, mahi-mahi, red snapper, swordfish and tuna feature prominently. Anguilla is sometimes called the "culinary capital of the Caribbean" for its many restaurants serving freshly caught fish. Salt fish is used in traditional breakfasts, and conch appears in soups, fritters and salads.

Staples and traditional dishes

Pigeon peas and rice is a signature side dish, often regarded as part of the island's national dish alongside stewed goat or grilled lobster. Cornmeal preparations, Johnny cakes, and dumplings reflect African and British influences. Goat is the most commonly eaten terrestrial meat and is often prepared in curries and stews, a legacy of South Asian influence across the Caribbean. Local produce includes sweet potatoes, yams, squash, callaloo and tropical fruits such as mangoes and tamarind.

Beverages and desserts

Rum-based drinks, mauby (a bitter bark beverage), sorrel drink and bush teas are popular. Desserts make use of coconut, banana and local fruits, and include tarts and puddings descended from British recipes adapted to island ingredients.
