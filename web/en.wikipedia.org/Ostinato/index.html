Ostinato

An ostinato (Italian for "obstinate") is a motif or phrase that persistently repeats in the same musical voice, frequently at the same pitch. The repeating pattern may be a melodic figure, a rhythmic pattern, or a harmonic progression.

History

Repeated bass lines, known as ground bass or basso ostinato, underpinned Baroque forms such as the passacaglia and chaconne. Pachelbel's Canon is built over a two-bar ostinato; Ravel's Boléro repeats a single rhythmic-melodic figure throughout. In the 20th century, minimalists such as Steve Reich and Philip Glass made the ostinato a central structural device.

Related terms

In popular music, short repeated figures are called riffs or vamps; in electronic music, loops serve the same function. The ostinato is also fundamental in many African and Afro-Cuban musical traditions.
