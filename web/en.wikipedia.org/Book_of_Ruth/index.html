Book of Ruth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Book of Ruth (Hebrew: מְגִלַּת רוּת, Megillat Rut, "the Scroll of Ruth") is one of the books of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament. It is a short book of four chapters recounting the story of Ruth, a Moabite woman who, after the death of her Israelite husband, pledges loyalty to her mother-in-law Naomi and accompanies her to Bethlehem. There Ruth gleans in the fields of Boaz, a wealthy relative of Naomi's late husband, whom she eventually marries. Their son Obed becomes the grandfather of King David.

Summary

During the time of the judges, a famine drives Elimelech of Bethlehem to Moab with his wife Naomi and their two sons. The sons marry Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth, but Elimelech and both sons die. Naomi decides to return to Bethlehem; Orpah remains in Moab, but Ruth famously declares, "Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay. Your people will be my people and your God my God."

In Bethlehem, Ruth gleans grain in the fields of Boaz, who shows her kindness. Acting on Naomi's plan, Ruth approaches Boaz at the threshing floor, and Boaz, as a close relative with the right of redemption, marries her after a nearer relative declines. Ruth bears a son, Obed, and the book closes with a genealogy from Perez to David.

Themes

The book is a story of loyalty (hesed), kindness, and redemption. It highlights the inclusion of a foreigner — a Moabite widow — in the lineage of Israel's greatest king, and it is traditionally read during the Jewish festival of Shavuot, the season of the barley harvest in which the story is set.

Dating and authorship

Tradition attributes the book to the prophet Samuel, but modern scholarship generally dates its composition to the Persian period or later, though the story is set "in the days when the judges ruled," centuries earlier. Its polished prose and artistry have made it a celebrated example of the Hebrew short story.
