Fingerstyle guitar

Fingerstyle guitar (also fingerpicking) is the technique of playing the guitar or bass guitar by plucking the strings directly with the fingertips, fingernails, or picks attached to the fingers, as opposed to flatpicking, which uses a single plectrum. Because individual notes can be sounded simultaneously and independently, fingerstyle playing allows one guitarist to combine bass line, harmony, and melody at once.

Technique

In the most common approach, the thumb plays the bass notes on the lower strings — often in a steady alternating pattern, as in the "Travis picking" style named after Merle Travis — while the index, middle, and ring fingers play melody and chords on the higher strings. Classical guitar technique uses the nails and fingertips together, with standardised finger designations (p, i, m, a) derived from Spanish.

Styles and practitioners

Fingerstyle is fundamental to classical and flamenco guitar, and central to folk, country blues, ragtime, and much acoustic music. Noted fingerstyle players include Andrés Segovia, Chet Atkins, Merle Travis, Mississippi John Hurt, Leo Kottke, Tommy Emmanuel, and, in a modern percussive style, Andy McKee and Michael Hedges, who incorporate tapping and body percussion. Brazilian bossa nova and West African guitar traditions also rely on the fingers.
