Ogdoad (Egyptian)

The Ogdoad (Greek: ὀγδοάς, "the eightfold") were eight primordial deities in ancient Egyptian religion, worshipped principally at Hermopolis (Egyptian Khmunu, "Eight-Town"). They represented the state of the world before creation.

The eight deities

The Ogdoad consisted of four male-female pairs, the males depicted as frogs and the females as snakes: Nun and Naunet (primeval water), Heh and Hauhet (infinity), Kek and Kauket (darkness), and Amun and Amaunet (hiddenness). Together they embodied the chaotic conditions from which the primeval mound arose.

Mythology

In the Hermopolitan cosmogony, the interaction of the Ogdoad produced the egg or lotus from which the sun god emerged, beginning creation. After their creative work the eight were said to have died and retired to the underworld, from where they continued to make the Nile flow and the sun rise.
