Masjid al-Haram (Arabic: ٱلْمَسْجِدُ ٱلْحَرَام‎, "The Sacred Mosque"), also known as the Grand Mosque of Mecca, is a mosque that surrounds the Kaaba in Mecca, in the Hejazi region of Saudi Arabia. It is the largest mosque in the world and the holiest site in Islam: Muslims face the Kaaba within it when praying (the qibla), and it is the destination of the Hajj pilgrimage, which every able Muslim is expected to perform at least once in a lifetime, as well as the Umrah.

Structures within the mosque

At the centre of the mosque's courtyard stands the Kaaba, a cuboid granite shrine draped in the black-and-gold kiswah cloth, which pilgrims circumambulate seven times in the rite of tawaf. Set into its eastern corner is the Black Stone. Nearby are the Maqam Ibrahim, a glass enclosure containing a stone bearing footprints attributed to Abraham; the Zamzam Well, whose water pilgrims drink; and the hills of Safa and Marwah, now enclosed within the mosque's gallery, between which pilgrims walk seven times in the sa'i, commemorating Hagar's search for water.

History

Islamic tradition attributes the Kaaba's foundation to Abraham (Ibrahim) and his son Ishmael. The sanctuary was rebuilt and enlarged repeatedly: under the Umayyad, Abbasid, and Ottoman caliphates it acquired arcades, minarets, and a defined courtyard. Under Saudi rule the mosque has undergone massive expansions, especially under Kings Fahd and Abdullah, adding vast prayer halls, escalators, air conditioning, and the enlargements of the mataf (circumambulation area), making it able to accommodate millions during the Hajj. The mosque complex is overlooked by the Abraj Al Bait clock tower. Pivotal events in its history include the 1979 Grand Mosque seizure by Juhayman al-Otaybi's militants and the fatal 2015 crane collapse during expansion works.

Administration

The mosque, together with the Prophet's Mosque in Medina, is administered under the title of the Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques held by the King of Saudi Arabia. A permanent imam leads the five daily prayers, which are broadcast worldwide, and entry to Mecca, and hence the mosque, is restricted to Muslims.
