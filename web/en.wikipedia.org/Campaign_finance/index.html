Campaign finance

Campaign finance refers to the raising and spending of money intended to influence political campaigns and elections. Because modern campaigns require large sums for advertising, staff, travel, and data, the sources of campaign money — individuals, political parties, political action committees, and public funds — are a persistent subject of regulation and controversy in democracies.

Sources of funds

Candidates raise money from individual donors, party committees, and political action committees (PACs), and in some systems receive public financing. "Hard money" contributions are made directly to candidates and are typically limited and disclosed, while "soft money" and independent expenditures are spent by outside groups. Self-funding by wealthy candidates is significant in some jurisdictions.

Regulation

Many democracies limit contributions, require disclosure, cap spending, or subsidize campaigns to reduce corruption and unequal influence. Approaches vary widely: France and Canada ban or tightly restrict corporate and union donations, the United Kingdom caps spending, and Israel and Germany provide substantial public funding tied to votes or membership.

United States

In the United States, the Federal Election Campaign Act (1971, amended 1974) created the Federal Election Commission and contribution limits after Watergate. The Supreme Court's decisions in Buckley v. Valeo (1976) treated spending as protected speech, and Citizens United v. FEC (2010) allowed unlimited independent expenditures by corporations and unions, spurring the rise of Super PACs. The Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act of 2002 (McCain–Feingold) restricted soft money and electioneering communications.

Criticism and reform

Critics argue that large donors gain disproportionate access and that fundraising consumes lawmakers' time; defenders respond that spending enables political communication and that restrictions burden free speech. Reform proposals include small-donor matching systems, vouchers, stricter disclosure of "dark money," and constitutional amendments.
