Calcium hydroxide

Calcium hydroxide
Formula: Ca(OH)2
Molar mass: 74.09 g/mol
Appearance: white powder or colourless crystals
Common names: slaked lime, hydrated lime, pickling lime

Calcium hydroxide (traditionally called slaked lime) is an inorganic compound with the chemical formula Ca(OH)2. It is a colourless crystal or white powder obtained when calcium oxide (quicklime) is mixed, or "slaked", with water. Its aqueous solution is known as limewater, and a suspension of fine particles in water is called milk of lime.

Properties

Calcium hydroxide is moderately soluble in water and is a moderately strong base. On heating to about 853 K it decomposes back to calcium oxide and water. It reacts readily with carbon dioxide in air to form calcium carbonate — the process of carbonation that hardens lime mortars — and limewater turning milky in the presence of CO2 is a classic laboratory test for the gas.

Production

It is produced commercially by treating quicklime with water: CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2. The slaking reaction is strongly exothermic.

Uses

  • In construction, as an ingredient in mortar, plaster and whitewash;
  • In food preparation, as "pickling lime" and in the nixtamalization of maize, where it improves nutritional value and flavour;
  • In water and sewage treatment as a flocculant and for pH adjustment;
  • In agriculture, to neutralise acidic soils;
  • In dentistry, as an antimicrobial dressing in root canal treatment;
  • In the paper industry (Kraft process) and in leather tanning for removing hair from hides.

Calcium hydroxide is caustic and can cause severe skin and eye irritation and chemical burns.
