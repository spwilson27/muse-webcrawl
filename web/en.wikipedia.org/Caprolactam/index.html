Caprolactam (CPL) is an organic compound with the formula (CH2)5C(O)NH. It is a cyclic amide — a lactam — consisting of a seven-membered ring containing one nitrogen atom and a carbonyl group. This colourless, hygroscopic solid is the precursor to nylon 6, a widely used synthetic polymer; its name reflects that it can be viewed as the lactam of caproic (hexanoic) acid, more precisely of 6-aminohexanoic acid.

Production

Caprolactam is produced industrially on a scale of several million tonnes per year. The classical route starts from cyclohexanone, which is converted to its oxime with hydroxylamine; treatment of the oxime with sulfuric acid triggers the Beckmann rearrangement to give caprolactam, with ammonium sulfate as a major co-product. Alternative and newer processes — including routes via cyclohexane photonitrosation, ammoximation of cyclohexanone, and gas-phase Beckmann rearrangement — aim to reduce or avoid the large volumes of ammonium sulfate generated.

Uses

Almost all caprolactam produced is used to make nylon 6 filaments, fibres, and resins by hydrolytic ring-opening polymerisation. Nylon 6 is used in textiles, carpets, tire cord, engineering plastics, and films. A small fraction of caprolactam is used directly as a cross-linking agent and in other chemical syntheses.

Safety

Caprolactam is an irritant to the skin, eyes, and respiratory tract, and has relatively low acute toxicity; it is not classified as a human carcinogen (IARC Group 4, "probably not carcinogenic to humans").
