Sambar (dish)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sambar

Type: Stew / soup

Place of origin: India (Tamil Nadu)

Main ingredients: Pigeon pea (toor dal), tamarind, vegetables, sambar powder

Served with: Rice, idli, dosa, vada

Sambar (Tamil: சாம்பார்) is a lentil-based vegetable stew cooked with pigeon pea and a sour tamarind broth, popular throughout South India and in Sri Lankan Tamil cuisine. It is flavoured with sambar powder — a blend of ground spices typically including coriander seed, cumin, fenugreek, mustard seed, dried red chillies, turmeric and asafoetida — and finished with a tempering of mustard seeds, curry leaves and chillies fried in oil or ghee.

Preparation

Toor dal (split pigeon peas) is cooked until soft and mashed. Vegetables such as drumsticks (moringa pods), shallots, tomatoes, okra, carrots, pumpkin or brinjal are simmered in water with tamarind extract, turmeric and salt; sambar powder is added, the dal is stirred in, and the stew is brought briefly to the boil. A final tempering (tadka) of mustard seeds, cumin, dried red chillies, curry leaves and asafoetida in hot oil is poured over the top.

Serving

Sambar is a staple accompaniment to steamed rice and is also served with breakfast dishes such as idli, dosa, pongal and medu vada (which is soaked in sambar as sambar-vada). Regional variants differ in their spice blends and vegetables; Karnataka versions often include a little jaggery and grated coconut, while Kerala and Tamil versions emphasise different local vegetables.

Origins

Popular tradition attributes sambar to the Maratha court of Thanjavur in the 17th or 18th century: when the ruler Shahuji’s expected ingredient (amti dal, in one telling made with kokum) was unavailable, cooks substituted tamarind and local vegetables, and the dish was named, according to the story, after Sambhaji, the Maratha prince for whom it was served. Whatever its precise origin, tamarind-based lentil stews have older roots in Tamil cuisine, and dishes resembling sambar are described in early modern South Indian cookery.
