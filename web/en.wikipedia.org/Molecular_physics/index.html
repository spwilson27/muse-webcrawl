Molecular physics

Molecular physics is the study of the physical properties of molecules, the chemical bonds between atoms, and molecular dynamics. It overlaps significantly with physical chemistry, chemical physics, and quantum chemistry, and draws on techniques from atomic physics and spectroscopy.

Scope

Molecular physics investigates the structure of molecules — bond lengths, angles, and energies — and the energy levels associated with electronic, vibrational, and rotational motion. Molecular spectra (in the microwave, infrared, visible, and ultraviolet regions) provide the principal experimental window into these levels: rotational spectra give moments of inertia and bond lengths, vibrational spectra give force constants, and electronic spectra give information about excited states.

Methods

Theoretical treatment typically begins with the Schrödinger equation for nuclei and electrons, simplified by the Born–Oppenheimer approximation, which separates electronic motion from the much slower nuclear motion and yields potential energy surfaces on which nuclei move. Computational methods include ab initio quantum chemistry, density functional theory, and molecular dynamics simulation. Experimental methods include microwave and laser spectroscopy, molecular beam scattering, photoelectron spectroscopy, and diffraction techniques.

Applications

Understanding molecular structure and dynamics underlies atmospheric and astrochemistry (identifying molecules in interstellar clouds by their spectra), combustion and reaction kinetics, laser physics, and the design of new materials and pharmaceuticals.
