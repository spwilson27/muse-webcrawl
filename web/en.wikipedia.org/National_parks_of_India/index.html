National parks of India

There are more than 100 national parks in India, covering roughly 44,000 square kilometres — about 1.3 percent of the country's land area. National parks in India are IUCN category II protected areas, governed under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, which permits no human activity except for management and research purposes.

History

India's first national park was established in 1936 as Hailey National Park, now Jim Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand. The network expanded substantially after the launch of Project Tiger in 1973, which created tiger reserves centred on key national parks to conserve the Bengal tiger.

Notable parks

Notable parks include Ranthambore and Bandhavgarh, famous for tiger sightings; Kaziranga in Assam, home to the largest population of Indian rhinoceroses; Sundarbans, a mangrove park shared with Bangladesh; Gir, the only home of the Asiatic lion; and Great Himalayan National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. India's protected-area network also includes over 500 wildlife sanctuaries.
