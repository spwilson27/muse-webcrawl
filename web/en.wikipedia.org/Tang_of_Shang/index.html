King Tang of Shang (Chinese: 湯; pinyin: Tāng), born Zi Lü, was the founder of the Shang dynasty of China. A leader of the Shang tribe, he overthrew Jie, the last king of the Xia dynasty, at the Battle of Mingtiao, traditionally dated to around 1600 BCE.

Reign

Tang was celebrated in later Chinese tradition as a virtuous ruler who lowered taxes, forgave enemies, and prayed for rain during a great drought by offering himself at the altar — a story used by Confucian writers as an exemplar of benevolent kingship. His victory was later cited by Mencius as a justified overthrow of a tyrant, an early statement of the Mandate of Heaven doctrine.
