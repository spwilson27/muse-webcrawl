Childbirth, also known as labour, parturition or delivery, is the completion of pregnancy, where one or more babies exit the mother's internal environment via the vagina or a caesarean section. In 2019, there were about 140.11 million births globally. In developed countries most deliveries occur in hospitals, while in developing countries most are home births.

The most common childbirth method worldwide is vaginal delivery. It involves four stages of labour: the shortening and opening of the cervix, descent and birth of the baby, delivery of the placenta, and recovery of the mother and infant during the postpartum period, which is the first few hours following the delivery. The first stage is characterised by abdominal cramping and lower back pain that may last up to 19 hours. The second stage ends when the last infant is fully expelled. The third stage is the expulsion of the placenta, and the fourth is the first few hours of postpartum recovery.

Signs and symptoms

The most prominent sign of labour is strong repetitive uterine contractions. The distress levels reported by labouring women vary widely. They appear to be influenced by fear and anxiety levels, experience with prior childbirth, cultural ideas of childbirth pain, mobility during labour, and the support received during labour.

Other signs may include a mucus plug discharge, a rupture of membranes ("water breaking"), and light spotting known as bloody show. Oxytocin is involved in the onset of labour and is sometimes administered to induce labour medically.

Stages of labour

First stage

The first stage begins with the onset of contractions and ends when the cervix is fully dilated to 10 cm. It is divided into a latent phase, in which the cervix effaces and dilates to about 4–6 cm, and an active phase, during which dilation proceeds more rapidly.

Second stage

The second stage begins when the cervix is completely dilated and ends with the birth of the baby. Under the influence of contractions and maternal pushing, the baby descends through the pelvis and is born.

Third stage

The third stage is the delivery of the placenta, usually within 30 minutes of the baby's birth. Active management with oxytocin reduces the risk of severe postpartum haemorrhage.

Management

Care providers include obstetricians, midwives, family physicians, and doulas. Pain relief options range from relaxation techniques and immersion in water to opioids and epidural anaesthesia. The World Health Organization recommends a caesarean section rate of between 10 and 15 per cent of births at population level.

Complications

Complications of childbirth include obstructed labour, postpartum bleeding, eclampsia, and postpartum infection. Each year, about 287,000 women die due to complications of pregnancy and childbirth, many of them in low-resource settings.
