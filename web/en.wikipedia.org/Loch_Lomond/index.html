Loch Lomond

Loch Lomond (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Laomainn) is a freshwater loch in southern Scotland, lying on the Highland Boundary Fault between the Lowlands and Highlands. It is the largest lake in Great Britain by surface area.

Geography

The loch is about 36 kilometres (22 miles) long and up to 8 kilometres wide, covering around 71 square kilometres, with a maximum depth of about 190 metres. It contains more than 30 islands, including Inchmurrin, the largest freshwater island in the British Isles, and Inchconnachan, known for its introduced wallabies. Ben Lomond, 974 metres high, rises on its eastern shore.

Surroundings

Since 2002 the loch has formed the centrepiece of Loch Lomond and The Trossachs National Park, Scotland's first national park. Towns and villages on its shores include Balloch at the southern end, Luss and Tarbet on the west, and Balmaha on the east; the West Highland Way and A82 road follow its banks.

Culture

The loch is famous worldwide through the traditional song "The Bonnie Banks o' Loch Lomond", with its refrain about taking the high road and the low road. It is a popular site for boating, watersports, fishing and walking, close to Glasgow.
