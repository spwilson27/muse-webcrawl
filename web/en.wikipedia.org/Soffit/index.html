Soffit

A soffit is an architectural feature typically consisting of an exposed underside surface of a roof overhang, arch, balcony, or stairway. In residential construction the term most commonly refers to the board under the eaves that runs from the top of the exterior wall to the outer edge of the roof, closing the gap between the edge of the roof and the wall.

Function

Soffits protect the underside of roof overhangs from the weather, discourage birds and insects from nesting in the rafters, and provide ventilation intakes for the attic. Vented soffit panels allow air to flow into the attic, balancing the ridge vents and reducing moisture build-up.

Materials

Soffits are made from wood, aluminium, vinyl (uPVC), fibre cement, and composite materials. Aluminium and vinyl predominated in North America from the late twentieth century because of their low maintenance.

Etymology

The word derives from the French soffite, from Italian soffitto, ultimately from Latin suffixus, "fixed underneath". It is also the term for the underside of an arch.
