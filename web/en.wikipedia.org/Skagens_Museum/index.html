Skagens Museum

Skagens Museum is an art museum in Skagen, Denmark, that exhibits an extensive collection of works by members of the colony of Scandinavian artists known as the Skagen Painters, who lived and worked in the area in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It was founded on 20 October 1908 in the dining room at Brøndum's Hotel.

The museum's collection includes works by Michael Ancher, Anna Ancher, Peder Severin Krøyer, Holger Drachmann, Viggo Johansen, Marie Krøyer, Karl Madsen, Laurits Tuxen, Christian Krohg, and others, depicting the fishermen, landscape and distinctive light of the Skagen peninsula. In 1928 the collection moved to its present building, designed by Ulrik Plesner.

Associated sites

The museum also administers the artists' homes Anchers Hus (the Ancher family home) and Drachmanns Hus. In 2014 Skagens Museum merged with the local heritage museums to form the Museum Skagen organisation. The museum holds well over a thousand paintings, drawings and sculptures, and is one of Denmark's most visited art museums outside Copenhagen.
