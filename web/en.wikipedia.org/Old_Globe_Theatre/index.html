Old Globe Theatre

The Old Globe Theatre is a professional theatre company located in Balboa Park in San Diego, California. Built in 1935 as part of the California Pacific International Exposition, its main building is a replica of Shakespeare's Old Globe Theatre in London. The company, commonly called The Old Globe, is one of the oldest professional regional theatres in the United States.

History

The theatre was constructed in 1935, modelled on the London Globe of Shakespeare's time, to present abbreviated versions of Shakespeare plays during the exposition. After the fair closed, a group of local citizens preserved the building and formed a theatre company, which produced full-length productions from 1937 onward.

In 1978 an arson fire destroyed the original outdoor festival stage; it was rebuilt as the Lowell Davies Festival Theatre. A second major fire in 1984 damaged the main theatre. The complex grew to include three performance spaces: the Old Globe Theatre itself, the Sheryl and Harvey White Theatre, and the outdoor Lowell Davies Festival Theatre.

Work

The Old Globe produces a year-round season of around fifteen productions, including Shakespeare, classics, contemporary plays and musicals. Many Broadway productions have premiered or been workshopped there, among them Into the Woods and Dirty Rotten Scoundrels. The theatre has received the Tony Award for Outstanding Regional Theatre. It also runs extensive education and community programmes across San Diego County.
