Prosthetic leg

A prosthetic leg is an artificial limb that replaces a missing leg, typically after amputation due to trauma, disease, or congenital conditions. As part of the broader field of prosthetics, modern leg prostheses range from simple peg legs to computer-controlled bionic devices that restore near-natural walking and running.

Components

A lower-limb prosthesis typically comprises a custom-moulded socket that fits over the residual limb, a pylon providing structure, and a prosthetic foot. Above-knee (transfemoral) prostheses also include a prosthetic knee joint, which may be mechanical, hydraulic, or microprocessor-controlled. Specialized designs include carbon-fibre running blades used by para-athletes.

Types by amputation level

  • Transtibial — below the knee
  • Transfemoral — above the knee
  • Knee disarticulation and hip disarticulation
  • Foot and partial foot prostheses

History

Artificial legs date to antiquity — an Egyptian toe prosthesis is among the oldest known — and wooden peg legs were common for centuries. Wars drove major advances, and the late 20th century brought lightweight materials, energy-storing feet, and myoelectric and osseointegrated systems.
