Catherine of Aragon

Queen consort of England: 11 June 1509 – 23 May 1533
Born: 16 December 1485, Alcalá de Henares, Spain
Died: 7 January 1536, Kimbolton Castle, England
Spouses: Arthur, Prince of Wales; Henry VIII
Issue: Mary I of England

Catherine of Aragon (Spanish: Catalina de Aragón; 16 December 1485 – 7 January 1536) was Queen of England as the first wife of King Henry VIII from their marriage on 11 June 1509 until their marriage was annulled on 23 May 1533. She had previously been Princess of Wales while married to Henry's elder brother, Arthur, Prince of Wales. The youngest surviving child of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile, Catherine was highly educated and served as ambassador of Aragon to England in 1507, the first female ambassador in European history.

Marriages

Catherine married Arthur in 1501, but he died five months later; she maintained that the marriage was never consummated, and a papal dispensation allowed her to marry Henry VIII in 1509. As queen she was popular, acted as regent in 1513 while Henry campaigned in France — during which the English defeated the Scots at Flodden — and patronised humanist learning. Of her pregnancies only one child survived infancy: the future Mary I (born 1516). Several sons, including a prince named Henry who lived seven weeks in 1511, died young.

The King's Great Matter

By the mid-1520s Henry, lacking a male heir and enamoured of Anne Boleyn, sought an annulment, arguing that his marriage violated the biblical prohibition on marrying a brother's widow. Catherine refused to accept that her first marriage had been consummated or to retire to a convent, and appealed to Rome and to her nephew, Emperor Charles V. Pope Clement VII would not grant the annulment; Henry's response — breaking with Rome, declaring himself head of the Church of England, and having Archbishop Thomas Cranmer annul the marriage in 1533 — set the English Reformation in motion. Catherine was thereafter styled Dowager Princess of Wales, separated from her daughter Mary, and confined successively to Buckden and Kimbolton, where she died in January 1536, probably of cancer. She was buried in Peterborough Cathedral; to the end she signed herself "Katherine the Queen".

Legacy

Catherine's steadfastness won her lasting sympathy in England and in Catholic tradition, and her treatment became a central episode in accounts of the Tudor period; she is a prominent character in Shakespeare's Henry VIII.
