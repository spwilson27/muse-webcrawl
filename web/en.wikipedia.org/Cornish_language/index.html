Cornish (Kernewek) is a Brittonic Celtic language native to Cornwall in southwestern England. It is a sister language of Welsh and Breton, descending from the Common Brittonic spoken across much of Britain.

Decline and revival

Cornish retreated westward under pressure from English over many centuries and ceased to be a community vernacular by the end of the 18th century; Dolly Pentreath (d. 1777) is often cited as among its last fluent native speakers. A revival began in the early 20th century, led by Henry Jenner's handbook of 1904. Several competing spelling systems emerged — Unified Cornish, Kernewek Kemmyn and others — until a Standard Written Form was agreed in 2008 for official use.

Today

Cornish was recognised under the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages in 2002, and UNESCO reclassified it from "extinct" to "critically endangered". Several hundred people speak it fluently, it is taught in schools and adult classes, and it appears on public signage across Cornwall.
