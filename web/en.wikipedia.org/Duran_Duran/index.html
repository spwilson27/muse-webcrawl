Duran Duran

OriginBirmingham, England
Formed1978
GenresNew wave, synth-pop, pop rock
Classic line-upSimon Le Bon, Nick Rhodes, John Taylor, Roger Taylor, Andy Taylor
Notable albumsRio (1982), Seven and the Ragged Tiger (1983)

Duran Duran are an English pop rock band formed in Birmingham in 1978 by keyboardist Nick Rhodes and bassist John Taylor. Named after the villain Dr. Durand Durand in the film Barbarella, the band became leading figures of the New Romantic scene and one of the most commercially successful groups of the 1980s, selling more than 100 million records worldwide.

Career

With singer Simon Le Bon, drummer Roger Taylor and guitarist Andy Taylor completing the classic line-up, Duran Duran released their self-titled debut album in 1981, featuring the singles "Planet Earth" and "Girls on Film". Their second album, Rio (1982), with "Hungry Like the Wolf", "Save a Prayer" and the title track, made them international stars. Pioneering glamorous, cinematic music videos, they were among the first bands to exploit MTV, earning the nickname "the Fab Five" from the American press.

Seven and the Ragged Tiger (1983) produced "The Reflex", a US and UK number one, and the band contributed "A View to a Kill" (1985), the only James Bond theme to top the US charts. Side projects — Arcadia and The Power Station — and line-up changes followed; guitarist Warren Cuccurullo was a full member through the 1990s, when "Ordinary World" and "Come Undone" (1993) revived their chart fortunes.

Later years

The five classic members reunited for the album Astronaut (2004). Subsequent albums include Red Carpet Massacre (2007), All You Need Is Now (2010), Paper Gods (2015) and Future Past (2021). The band were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2022.

Legacy

Duran Duran are credited with fusing dance rhythms, electronic textures and rock guitar, and with helping to define the visual style of 1980s pop. Their influence is acknowledged by numerous later pop and electronic acts.
