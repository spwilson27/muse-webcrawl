Somerled (Old Norse: Sumarliði; Scottish Gaelic: Somhairle; died 1164) was a 12th-century Norse-Gaelic warlord who, by conquest, marriage, and diplomacy, created a maritime lordship embracing Argyll, Kintyre, and the Hebrides. He is the eponymous ancestor of Clann Somhairle — Clan MacDougall, Clan Donald, and Clan MacRory — and a foundational figure in the history of western Scotland.

Rise

Somerled's ancestry is given by later clan tradition as Gaelic, while his name and milieu were Norse-Gaelic. In the 1130s–1150s he rose as regulator (lord) of Argyll amid Scottish royal expansion under David I. In 1156 he defeated his brother-in-law Guðrøð Óláfsson, King of Man, in a naval battle off Islay, and by a subsequent settlement the Kingdom of the Isles was partitioned: the southern Hebrides went to Somerled, while Man and the northern isles remained with Guðrøð's line.

Rule and church

Somerled ruled a sea-kingdom held together by galley fleets, patronised the church — refounding Saddell Abbey (Cistercian) in Kintyre — and attempted, unsuccessfully, to reform the monastery of Iona. In 1164 he invaded the Scottish mainland with a great fleet, traditionally of 164 ships, landing near Renfrew, where he was killed — by assassination according to some sources — and his army dispersed.

Legacy

After his death his territories were divided among his sons, from whom descend the great West Highland clans. Historians regard Somerled as a pivotal figure in the Gaelic resurgence and partial de-Norseification of the Hebrides and western seaboard.
