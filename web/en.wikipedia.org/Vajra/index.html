Vajra

The vajra (Sanskrit: वज्र, "thunderbolt" or "diamond") is a ritual weapon and spiritual implement symbolising the properties of a diamond — indestructibility — and a thunderbolt — irresistible force. It is central to Vajrayana Buddhism and also appears in Hinduism and Jainism.

Hindu origins

In the Rigveda the vajra is the weapon of Indra, king of the gods, fashioned from the bones of the sage Dadhichi and used to slay the serpent Vritra, releasing the waters. Early vajras were depicted as clubs or sceptres with open prongs.

In Buddhism

In tantric Buddhism the vajra, held in the right hand, is paired with the bell (ghanta) in the left; together they symbolise compassion or method and wisdom. In Tibetan Buddhism it is called the dorje and features in countless rituals, mantras and meditations, including the name of the tradition itself — Vajrayana, the "Diamond Vehicle". The Buddha figure Vajrasattva holds a vajra at his heart as a symbol of purity.

Form and symbolism

A typical vajra has a central sphere with two sets of prongs — commonly five at each end, representing the five wisdoms or five elements — curving inward to meet at points. It symbolises the union of the absolute and relative truths and the indestructible nature of awakened mind.
