Superintelligence

A superintelligence is a hypothetical agent that possesses intelligence far surpassing that of the brightest and most gifted human minds. The term was popularized by philosopher Nick Bostrom, whose 2014 book Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies defines it as "any intellect that greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest."

Paths to superintelligence

Proposed routes include advanced artificial general intelligence in software, whole-brain emulation, brain–computer interfaces, and genetic enhancement. Bostrom distinguishes speed superintelligence (a human-equivalent mind running much faster), collective superintelligence (many minds networked), and quality superintelligence (qualitatively smarter cognition).

Risks and the control problem

Bostrom and others argue that a superintelligent system's goals might not align with human values, and that a decisive strategic advantage could make its actions difficult to reverse — the AI control problem. Concepts such as the intelligence explosion (I. J. Good, 1965) and instrumental convergence underpin much of the debate, which spans the AI safety research community and sceptics who question the timelines and assumptions.
