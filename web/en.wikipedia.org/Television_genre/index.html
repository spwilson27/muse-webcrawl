Television genre

A television genre is a category of television programs that share similar narrative, stylistic, or thematic conventions. As with film and literature genres, television genres help producers develop programs, help networks schedule and market them, and help audiences choose what to watch. Programs often combine several genres, and genre boundaries shift over time.

Major genres

Fiction genres include drama (with subgenres such as crime, medical, legal, and teen drama), comedy (including sitcoms, sketch comedy, and dramedy), soap operas and telenovelas, science fiction and fantasy, horror, westerns, and children's programming. Non-fiction genres include news, documentary, reality television, talk shows, game shows, sports coverage, variety shows, and educational programming.

Development

Television inherited many genres from radio, which had itself adapted forms from theater, vaudeville, and film. Early television adapted radio dramas, comedies, and quiz shows directly. Over time, formats specific to television developed, including the episodic procedural, the serialized prime-time soap, the reality competition format, and the limited series. The growth of cable and streaming services encouraged niche genres and hybrid forms aimed at specific audiences.

Genre analysis

Media scholars study television genres as systems of expectations: recurring settings, character types, plot patterns, and visual styles that audiences learn to recognize. Genre classifications also influence industry practice, including program commissioning, time-slot scheduling, awards categories, and audience measurement.
