Compressed-air energy storage

Compressed-air energy storage (CAES) is a way to store energy for later use using compressed air. Off-peak electricity drives compressors that pump air into underground caverns or vessels; the air is later released to generate electricity through turbines.

Plants

The first utility-scale plant opened at Huntorf, Germany in 1978, followed by McIntosh, Alabama in 1991. Conventional systems burn some natural gas when expanding the air; adiabatic designs aim to store and reuse the heat of compression.

Challenges

Suitable geology, round-trip efficiency below that of batteries, and heat management are the main constraints on wider deployment.
