Marc Chagall

Born7 July 1887, Vitebsk, Russian Empire
Died28 March 1985, Saint-Paul-de-Vence, France
MovementModernism, Surrealism (associated)

Marc Chagall (born Moishe Shagal; 7 July 1887 – 28 March 1985) was a Belarusian and French artist of Jewish origin. One of the most successful artists of the 20th century, he worked in painting, drawing, stained glass, stage design, ceramics and tapestry, developing a distinctive dreamlike style rooted in the folklore of his native Vitebsk.

Life

Chagall moved to Paris in 1910, where paintings such as I and the Village (1911) established his reputation. He returned to Russia during the First World War, founded an art school in Vitebsk after the Revolution, then emigrated to France in 1923. Fleeing the Nazi occupation, he spent the war years in the United States before settling in the south of France.

Major works

Among his celebrated public works are the ceiling of the Paris Opéra (1964), the stained-glass "Peace Window" at the United Nations, windows for the Hadassah Medical Center in Jerusalem, and large murals at New York's Metropolitan Opera.
