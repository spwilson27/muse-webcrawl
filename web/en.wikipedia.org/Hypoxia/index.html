Hypoxia

Hypoxia is a condition in which the body or a region of the body is deprived of adequate oxygen supply at the tissue level. Hypoxia may be generalized, affecting the whole body, or local, affecting a region of the body. Although hypoxia is often a pathological condition, variations in arterial oxygen concentrations can be part of normal physiology, for example during strenuous physical exercise.

Classification

Hypoxia is classically divided into hypoxemic hypoxia (low arterial oxygen, from lung disease or high altitude), anemic hypoxia (reduced oxygen-carrying capacity of blood), ischemic or stagnant hypoxia (inadequate blood flow), and histotoxic hypoxia (cells unable to use oxygen, as in cyanide poisoning). Signs include confusion, restlessness, rapid breathing and heart rate, and in severe cases cyanosis, coma and death.

Causes and treatment

Causes include pneumonia, asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, pulmonary embolism, heart failure, severe anemia, carbon monoxide poisoning and ascent to high altitude, where hypoxia underlies altitude sickness. Treatment is directed at the cause together with supplemental oxygen, ventilation support and, for altitude illness, descent and acclimatization. Cells respond to hypoxia partly through hypoxia-inducible factors (HIFs), which switch on genes such as erythropoietin.
