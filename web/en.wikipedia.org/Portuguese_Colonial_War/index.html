Portuguese Colonial War

The Portuguese Colonial War (Portuguese: Guerra Colonial Portuguesa), also known in Portugal as the Overseas War (Guerra do Ultramar), was a 13-year-long conflict fought between Portugal's military and the emerging nationalist movements in Portugal's African colonies between 1961 and 1974.

Background

The Estado Novo regime of António de Oliveira Salazar and Marcelo Caetano refused to decolonise, unlike other European powers that granted independence to their colonies after the Second World War. Guerrilla movements emerged in Angola (MPLA, FNLA, UNITA), Mozambique (FRELIMO) and Portuguese Guinea (PAIGC).

Outcome

The war strained Portugal's economy and military, contributing to the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, in which the regime was overthrown. The new government promptly ended the war and granted independence to the African colonies in 1975.
