The Virunga Mountains (also known as Mufumbiro) are a chain of volcanoes in East Africa, along the northern border of Rwanda, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda. The mountain range is a branch of the Albertine Rift Mountains, which border the western branch of the East African Rift. They are located between Lake Edward and Lake Kivu.

The range consists of eight major volcanoes. Most of them are dormant, except Mount Nyiragongo (3,470 m) and Mount Nyamuragira (3,063 m) in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Recent eruptions from those two volcanoes occurred in 2006, 2010 and 2021. Mount Karisimbi is the highest volcano at 4,507 m (14,787 ft). The oldest mountain is Mount Sabyinyo, which dates back to the beginning of the Pleistocene epoch.

List of mountains

The principal peaks, from west to east, are Nyamuragira, Nyiragongo, Mikeno, Karisimbi, Visoke, Sabyinyo, Gahinga and Muhabura.

National parks

The mountains are protected by three national parks: Virunga National Park in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Volcanoes National Park in Rwanda, and Mgahinga Gorilla National Park in Uganda. Together they form the core of the Virunga Conservation Area, established to protect the endangered mountain gorilla.

Mountain gorillas

The Virunga Mountains are one of only two remaining habitats of the mountain gorilla (Gorilla beringei beringei), the other being the Bwindi Impenetrable Forest in Uganda. The primatologist Dian Fossey established the Karisoke Research Center between Mount Karisimbi and Mount Visoke in 1967 and lived among the gorillas until her death in 1985. Her work, popularised in the book and film Gorillas in the Mist, drew worldwide attention to the species and to the conservation challenges facing the region.
