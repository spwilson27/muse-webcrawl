The Italian War of 1542–1546 was a conflict in the long series of Italian Wars between the Valois kings of France and the Habsburgs. Francis I of France, allied with the Ottoman sultan Suleiman the Magnificent, fought the emperor Charles V, who was joined from 1543 by Henry VIII of England.

Course of the war

French armies advanced in Piedmont and the Low Countries, winning the Battle of Ceresole (1544) in Italy. A joint Franco-Ottoman fleet attacked Nice in 1543, and the Ottoman fleet wintered at Toulon. Meanwhile Imperial and English forces invaded northern France in 1544; Henry VIII captured Boulogne, while Charles V advanced toward Paris before supply problems forced negotiations.

Settlement

The Treaty of Crépy (September 1544) largely restored the status quo between France and the Empire, with Francis renouncing claims to Naples and Flanders and Charles renouncing claims to Burgundy. Fighting with England continued until the Treaty of Ardres (1546), under which England kept Boulogne for a time in return for a French payment.
