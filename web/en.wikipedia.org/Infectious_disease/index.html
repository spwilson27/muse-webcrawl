Infectious disease

SpecialtyInfectious diseases (medical specialty)
CausesBacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, prions
TransmissionContact, droplets, airborne, vectors, fomites, food and water

An infectious disease, also known as a communicable disease or transmissible disease, is an illness caused by the invasion of a host organism by a pathogenic microorganism — such as a bacterium, virus, fungus, or parasite — or by an abnormal protein called a prion. Infection occurs when the pathogen multiplies within the host and damages tissues or disrupts normal function.

Transmission

Pathogens spread between hosts by many routes: direct physical contact, respiratory droplets and aerosols, contaminated food and water, surfaces (fomites), body fluids, and vectors such as mosquitoes and ticks. The chain of infection can be broken by hygiene, sanitation, vaccination, vector control, isolation of cases, and antimicrobial treatment.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on clinical signs and symptoms combined with laboratory methods such as culture, microscopy, antigen and antibody tests, and nucleic-acid amplification (PCR). Treatments include antibiotics for bacterial infections, antivirals, antifungals, and antiparasitic drugs, together with supportive care. The rise of antimicrobial resistance is a major global health concern.

Prevention

Vaccination is among the most effective preventive measures and has led to the eradication of smallpox and the near-eradication of poliomyelitis. Clean water, sanitation, safe food handling, hand hygiene, and public-health surveillance further reduce the burden of infectious disease, which remains a leading cause of death worldwide, particularly in low-income settings.
