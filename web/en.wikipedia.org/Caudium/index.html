Caudium

Caudium (Greek: Καύδιον) was an ancient city of Samnium in southern Italy, located in the Caudine Valley near the modern town of Montesarchio in Campania. It stood on the Appian Way between Capua and Beneventum. The surrounding region, the territory of the Caudini tribe of Samnites, is best known from the Battle of the Caudine Forks.

History

Caudium is famous for the events of 321 BC during the Second Samnite War, when a Roman army under the consuls Spurius Postumius Albinus and Titus Veturius Calvinus was trapped by the Samnite commander Gaius Pontius in the narrow passes of the Caudine Forks near the city. The Romans were compelled to surrender and pass under the yoke, a humiliation that became a byword in Roman memory — the "Caudine yoke" — for a disgraceful capitulation.

The city itself submitted to Rome and later appears as a posting station on the Via Appia. Little of the ancient town survives above ground; remains including tombs, inscriptions, and stretches of the Roman road have been found near Montesarchio.

Geography

Caudium lay in a broad valley (the Valle Caudina) enclosed by the mountains of Samnium, a natural corridor on the route from Campania into the Samnite interior — the strategic position that made it the scene of the famous ambush.
