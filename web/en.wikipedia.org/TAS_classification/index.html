TAS classification

The TAS classification (total alkali–silica classification) is a system for assigning names to many common types of volcanic rocks, based on the relationships between their combined alkali content (Na₂O + K₂O) and their silica (SiO₂) content. The system, formalised by Le Bas and colleagues in 1986 under the International Union of Geological Sciences, is widely used when a rock's mineralogical composition cannot be determined, as is common in fine-grained or glassy volcanic rocks.

The diagram

A sample's chemical analysis is plotted on a graph with weight percent SiO₂ on the horizontal axis and weight percent (Na₂O + K₂O) on the vertical axis. The diagram is divided into fifteen fields, each corresponding to a rock name — basalt, basaltic andesite, andesite, dacite, rhyolite, trachyte, phonolite, basanite, picrobasalt and others.

Use

Because it relies on chemical analysis rather than modal mineralogy, the TAS system complements the QAPF classification used for plutonic rocks. It does not distinguish some chemically similar rocks of differing mineralogy, and rocks altered after eruption may plot outside their original fields, so petrologists use it with care.
