Mont Ventoux
Elevation1,910 m (6,266 ft)
LocationVaucluse, Provence, France

Mont Ventoux is a mountain in the Provence region of southern France, about 25 km northeast of Carpentras in the department of Vaucluse. Rising to 1,910 metres, it is the highest mountain in the region and, standing isolated from other peaks, dominates the Rhône valley landscape — earning it the name "Giant of Provence". Its upper slopes are bare white limestone, giving the summit the appearance of being snow-capped much of the year.

Name and nature

The name Ventoux is thought to derive from a pre-Roman word for a windy or prominent height; the mistral wind at the summit has been recorded at over 300 km/h. The mountain is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, home to an unusual range of flora and fauna — species typical of both the Mediterranean and the high Alps — as well as reintroduced ibex and mouflon. Its forests were largely felled in past centuries for shipbuilding and charcoal and have since been replanted with cedar and beech.

Petrarch and culture

The Italian poet Petrarch famously climbed the mountain on 26 April 1336 and described the ascent in a letter, an event often cited as a symbol of Renaissance humanism — climbing a mountain for the view itself. Philosophers and writers from Burckhardt onward have retold the story.

Cycling

Mont Ventoux is one of the most feared climbs in the Tour de France, first included in 1951. The classic ascent from Bédoin climbs 1,610 m over 21.5 km. British rider Tom Simpson collapsed and died near the summit during the 1967 Tour, a combination of heat, exhaustion, alcohol and amphetamines; a memorial to him stands by the road. Epic stage finishes — including Chris Froome's victory in 2013 and his brief run up the mountain after a crash in 2016 — have cemented its legend. A meteorological observatory, built in 1882, crowns the summit.
