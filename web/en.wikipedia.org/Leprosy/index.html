Leprosy

Leprosy, also known as Hansen's disease, is a long-term infectious disease caused by the bacteria Mycobacterium leprae and Mycobacterium lepromatosis. It primarily affects the skin, peripheral nerves, upper respiratory tract and eyes.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms may take years to appear because the bacterium grows very slowly. Early signs include pale or reddish skin patches with loss of sensation. Untreated, nerve damage can lead to weakness, clawed hands, foot drop and injuries that go unnoticed, sometimes causing permanent disability.

Transmission and diagnosis

Leprosy spreads between people through droplets from the nose and mouth during prolonged close contact with untreated cases; it is not highly contagious. Diagnosis is usually clinical, supported by skin biopsy and laboratory tests.

Treatment and history

Leprosy is curable with multidrug therapy, supplied free by the World Health Organization since 1995. Known since antiquity and long surrounded by stigma, the disease is now rare in most countries, with around 200,000 new cases reported globally each year, mainly in India, Brazil and Indonesia.
