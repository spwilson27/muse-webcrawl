Humidex

Summary

Introduced: 1979, Environment Canada

Inputs: Air temperature and dew point

Used in: Canada

Similar index: Heat index (United States)

The humidex (short for humidity index) is an index number used by Canadian meteorologists to describe how hot the weather feels to the average person, by combining the effect of heat and humidity. It was introduced by Environment Canada in 1979, based on work by J. M. Masterton and F. A. Richardson.

Formula

The humidex is calculated from the air temperature and the dew point. It is a dimensionless number, but it is usually reported as if it were in degrees Celsius, and it is generally higher than the actual air temperature when humidity is significant. The formula uses the vapour pressure derived from the dew point to estimate the additional perceived warmth caused by humid air inhibiting the evaporation of sweat.

Interpretation

A humidex of 20 to 29 is generally comfortable; 30 to 39 causes some discomfort; 40 to 45 causes great discomfort and exertion should be avoided; above 45 is considered dangerous, with heat stroke possible. One of the highest humidex values recorded in Canada was 53.4 at Carman, Manitoba, in July 2007.

Comparison

The humidex serves the same purpose as the heat index used in the United States, though the formulas differ and the humidex is anchored to the dew point whereas the heat index uses relative humidity. Both aim to communicate the risk of heat-related illness more effectively than temperature alone.
