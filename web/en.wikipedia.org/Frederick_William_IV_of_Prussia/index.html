Frederick William IV of Prussia

BornOctober 15, 1795, Berlin, Prussia
DiedJanuary 2, 1861 (aged 65), Potsdam, Prussia
ReignJune 7, 1840 – January 2, 1861
SpouseElisabeth Ludovika of Bavaria

Frederick William IV (German: Friedrich Wilhelm IV.; October 15, 1795 – January 2, 1861) was King of Prussia from 1840 until his death in 1861. A deeply religious and artistic monarch — sometimes called "the romantic on the throne" — his reign was defined by the revolutions of 1848 and his refusal of a German imperial crown from the Frankfurt Parliament.

Reign

The eldest son of Frederick William III, he grew up in the era of the Napoleonic Wars and developed strong interests in architecture, landscape design, and medieval art; he patronised architects such as Karl Friedrich Schinkel and helped drive the completion of Cologne Cathedral. On succeeding his father in 1840 he raised liberal hopes by easing censorship and summoning a united diet, but disappointed constitutionalists by insisting on a corporative, Christian-monarchical state.

1848 and after

When revolution broke out in Berlin in March 1848, the king alternately conceded and resisted; after royal troops withdrew, he famously rode through Berlin wearing the revolutionary colours. Later that year the army recovered the capital, and in 1849 Frederick William refused the imperial crown offered by the Frankfurt Parliament — he would not accept a crown "from the gutter," offered by popular representatives rather than by his fellow princes. He instead promulgated a Prussian constitution (revised 1850) that endured until 1918, and pursued a conservative "Union" policy for Germany that collapsed in the humiliation of Olmütz (1850) under Austrian and Russian pressure.

Final years

In 1857 a series of strokes incapacitated him, and his brother Wilhelm became regent in 1858. Frederick William died childless at Sanssouci in 1861 and was succeeded by Wilhelm as Wilhelm I.
