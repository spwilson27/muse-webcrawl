Vasili III of Russia

Vasili III Ivanovich (Russian: Василий III Иванович; 25 March 1479 – 3 December 1533) was the Grand Prince of Moscow from 1505 to 1533, continuing the work of his father, Ivan III, in gathering the Russian lands. Sigismund von Herberstein, the Habsburg ambassador, recorded his reign in detail in his Rerum Moscoviticarum Commentarii.

Reign

Vasili III completed the annexation of the remaining semi-independent Russian principalities: Pskov in 1510 and Ryazan in 1521, along with the Novgorod-Seversk lands. He waged three wars against the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, capturing Smolensk in 1514, a prized gain that Lithuania never accepted. Relations with the Crimean and Kazan khanates fluctuated between alliance and raiding, and in 1521 Crimean Tatars reached the outskirts of Moscow.

Family and succession

After twenty years of childless marriage to Solomonia Saburova, Vasili controversially divorced her in 1525, confined her to a convent, and married Elena Glinskaya, who bore him two sons, including the future Ivan IV ("the Terrible"). Contemporary rumours suggested Vasili's unusually decision to divorce contributed to tensions with the church, which officially disapproved. He died in 1533 after an abscess on his leg turned septic, leaving a regency under Elena for the three-year-old Ivan.

Legacy

Vasili consolidated Muscovite autocracy, expanded its territory, and presided over a flourishing of architecture, including the Church of the Ascension at Kolomenskoye, built to commemorate the birth of his heir.
