Elite theory

Elite theory is a theory of the state and society in political science holding that a small minority — an elite — consisting of members of the economically, politically or intellectually dominant group inevitably rules, regardless of a country's formal democratic arrangements.

Classical theorists

The classical elite theorists were the Italians Gaetano Mosca and Vilfredo Pareto and the German-Italian Robert Michels. Mosca argued every society is divided into rulers and ruled; Pareto described the "circulation of elites"; and Michels formulated the "iron law of oligarchy" (1911), claiming that even democratic organisations develop oligarchic leadership.

Later development

In the United States, C. Wright Mills's The Power Elite (1956) described an interlocking directorate of corporate, military and political leaders. Elite theory contrasts with pluralism, which holds that power is dispersed among competing groups.
