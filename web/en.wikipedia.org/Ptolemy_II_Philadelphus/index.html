Ptolemy II Philadelphus (308 – 28 January 246 BC) was the pharaoh of Ptolemaic Egypt from 283 to 246 BC. The son of Ptolemy I Soter, he consolidated the kingdom his father founded and made Alexandria a leading centre of commerce, learning and culture in the Hellenistic world.

Reign

Ptolemy II expanded Ptolemaic influence in the Aegean and eastern Mediterranean, fought the First Syrian War against the Seleucid Empire, and intervened in Greek affairs during the Chremonidean War. He developed Egypt's administration, agriculture and Red Sea trade, founding ports for the elephant trade and expeditions into Nubia.

Court and culture

Under Ptolemy II the Library of Alexandria and the Mouseion flourished, attracting scholars such as Callimachus, Theocritus and Eratosthenes. He promoted the cult of the deified Ptolemaic dynasty and married his full sister Arsinoe II, adopting the epithet Philadelphus ("sibling-loving"); together they were worshipped as the Theoi Adelphoi.

Legacy

Ptolemy II completed or advanced great works including the Pharos lighthouse and the canal linking the Nile to the Red Sea. His reign is generally seen as the height of early Ptolemaic wealth and prestige.
