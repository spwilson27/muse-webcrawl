The history of Croatia spans from Roman Pannonia and Dalmatia through a medieval Croatian kingdom, centuries within Hungarian, Habsburg, Ottoman-frontier and Venetian spheres, the Yugoslav states of the 20th century, and independence in 1991.

Medieval kingdom

Croat Slavs settled the eastern Adriatic hinterland in the 7th century. Duchies coalesced into a kingdom crowned in 925 under Tomislav, reaching its height under Petar Krešimir IV and Dmitar Zvonimir. In 1102, by the arrangement remembered as the Pacta Conventa, Croatia entered a personal union with the Crown of Hungary while keeping its own Sabor (parliament) and ban (viceroy).

Frontier centuries

After the Ottoman victory at Krbava (1493) and Mohács (1526), the Croatian estates elected Ferdinand of Habsburg in 1527. Much of Croatia became the Military Frontier against the Ottomans; the coast and islands (including the Republic of Ragusa/Dubrovnik until 1808) were largely Venetian. Croatia was called the "remnants of the remnants" of its former kingdom.

Nation-building and Yugoslavia

The 19th-century Illyrian movement revived Croatian language and national consciousness within Austria-Hungary. In 1918 Croatia joined the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes; in 1941 the fascist Ustaše ran the Independent State of Croatia, a Nazi puppet state responsible for mass crimes, while Tito's Partisans — many of them Croats — won the war and built socialist Yugoslavia, in which Croatia was a constituent republic.

Independence

Multi-party elections in 1990 brought Franjo Tuđman's HDZ to power; Croatia declared independence in June 1991, triggering the Croatian War of Independence against the Yugoslav People's Army and Serb rebels. Operation Storm (1995) restored most territory, and Eastern Slavonia was peacefully reintegrated by 1998. Croatia joined NATO in 2009 and the European Union in 2013, adopting the euro and entering Schengen in 2023.
