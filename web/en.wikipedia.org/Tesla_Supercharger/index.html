The Tesla Supercharger network is an electric vehicle DC fast-charging network built and operated by American vehicle and energy company Tesla, Inc. First deployed in 2012 along highways in California, it grew into one of the largest fast-charging networks in the world, with stations across North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific.

Technology

Superchargers supply direct current directly to the vehicle's battery, bypassing the onboard AC charger. First-generation stations delivered up to 120 kW shared between paired stalls; the V3 Supercharger, introduced in 2019, provides up to 250 kW per vehicle with liquid-cooled cables, enabling roughly 75 miles (120 km) of range in five minutes for suitable models. V4 cabinets announced in 2024 raise output to 500 kW for cars and support the Tesla Semi.

Connector and opening to other brands

In North America Superchargers use Tesla's connector, which the company opened in November 2022 as the North American Charging Standard (NACS). From 2023, Ford, General Motors, Rivian and most other major manufacturers announced adoption of NACS and access agreements for the Supercharger network; the connector was standardized by SAE as J3400. In Europe, stations use the CCS2 connector and Tesla has progressively opened them to other brands through its pilot programme.

Usage and pricing

Early Model S and Model X cars included free unlimited Supercharging; later vehicles pay per kilowatt-hour or per minute, billed automatically through the owner's Tesla account, with idle fees to discourage occupying stalls after charging completes.
