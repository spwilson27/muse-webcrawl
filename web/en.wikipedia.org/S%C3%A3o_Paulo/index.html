São Paulo is the capital of the state of São Paulo in southeastern Brazil and the most populous city in the country, as well as one of the largest cities in the world. It is Brazil's foremost financial, corporate and cultural centre, and a major hub for Latin America.

History

The city was founded in 1554 by Jesuit missionaries, including Manuel da Nóbrega and José de Anchieta, at a site inland from the port of Santos. It grew slowly until the coffee boom of the late 19th century, which brought railways, industry and mass immigration from Italy, Japan, Lebanon and many other countries. By the mid-20th century São Paulo had become Brazil's industrial engine.

Geography and climate

São Paulo sits on a plateau around 760 metres above sea level, roughly 70 km from the Atlantic coast. It has a subtropical highland climate with mild winters and warm, rainy summers.

Economy and culture

The city hosts the São Paulo Stock Exchange (B3), headquarters of major banks and corporations, and a large services sector. Cultural landmarks include the São Paulo Museum of Art (MASP) on Avenida Paulista, Ibirapuera Park, the Municipal Theatre, and the São Paulo Biennial, one of the world's oldest art biennials. The city is also famous for its gastronomy, nightlife and the São Paulo Fashion Week.

Transport

São Paulo is served by Guarulhos International Airport, Congonhas Airport and an extensive metro and commuter rail network, though traffic congestion remains notorious.
