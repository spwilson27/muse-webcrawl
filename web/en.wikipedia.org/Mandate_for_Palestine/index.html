Mandate for Palestine

The Mandate for Palestine was a League of Nations mandate entrusting administration of the former Ottoman territory of Palestine to the United Kingdom. Drafted in 1920, confirmed by the League Council on 24 July 1922, it came into effect in September 1923 and lasted until 15 May 1948.

Terms

The mandate incorporated the Balfour Declaration of 1917, obliging Britain to facilitate a "national home for the Jewish people" while protecting the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities. It covered Palestine west of the Jordan and, initially in the same instrument, Transjordan, which Britain administered separately and which became independent in 1946.

Administration and conflict

British rule saw waves of Jewish immigration, Arab opposition, and escalating communal violence, including the 1929 riots, the 1936–39 Arab revolt, and the Jewish insurgency after World War II. Britain referred the question to the United Nations in 1947.

End

The UN Partition Plan of November 1947 proposed two states. Britain withdrew in May 1948; Israel declared independence on 14 May 1948, followed by the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.
