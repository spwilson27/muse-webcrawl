National nature reserve (United Kingdom)

National nature reserve
Designation: United Kingdom
Administered by: Natural England, NatureScot, Natural Resources Wales, NIEA
Established under: National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949

National nature reserves (NNRs) in the United Kingdom are areas of national importance for wildlife and geology that are protected and managed for conservation and research. They were established under the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949 and are declared by the statutory nature conservation bodies of each country: Natural England in England, NatureScot in Scotland, Natural Resources Wales in Wales, and the Northern Ireland Environment Agency in Northern Ireland.

Designation and management

NNRs are selected as the finest examples of habitats and sites of geological significance. Many are owned or leased by the conservation agencies themselves, while others are managed by approved bodies such as the National Trust, the RSPB, the Wildlife Trusts, or private landowners under management agreements. Most NNRs in England and Wales are also Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSIs), and many in Scotland carry equivalent designations.

Scale

There are more than 220 NNRs in England, over 40 in Scotland (including large sites such as Beinn Eighe, the first British NNR declared, in 1951), more than 70 in Wales, and a number in Northern Ireland. Reserves range from tiny sites protecting a single rare species to vast upland and coastal areas. Scotland's NNRs were reviewed in the early 21st century, leading to a smaller network of flagship sites.

Purpose

Beyond conservation, NNRs provide opportunities for scientific study and public access and enjoyment of nature. They form a key part of the UK's contribution to international conservation networks, with many reserves also designated as Special Areas of Conservation or Special Protection Areas.
