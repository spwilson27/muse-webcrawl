Domesticated animals
Livestock species domesticated in the Neolithic

This is a list of domesticated animals — species that have been selectively bred over many generations in captivity, changing them genetically from their wild ancestors, a process called domestication. Animals were domesticated for food, fibre, labour, transport, hunting, companionship and research. The earliest was the dog, domesticated from the wolf by hunter-gatherers well before agriculture; most livestock followed in and around the Fertile Crescent during the Neolithic Revolution, roughly 10,000 years ago.

Birds

  • Chicken — from the red junglefowl, Southeast Asia, c. 6000 BC; eggs and meat.
  • Domestic duck — from the mallard, China, c. 2000 BC.
  • Domestic goose — from the greylag (Europe) and swan goose (China).
  • Turkey — from the wild turkey, Mexico, c. 200 BC.
  • Domestic pigeon — from the rock dove; messengers, racing, food.
  • Budgerigar, canary and other cage birds kept as pets.

Fish and invertebrates

  • Common carp and goldfish (from Prussian carp, China), koi varieties bred for ornament.
  • Atlantic salmon, rainbow trout and tilapia farmed in aquaculture.
  • Western honey bee — managed for honey and pollination since antiquity.
  • Silkworm (Bombyx mori) — domesticated in China c. 3000 BC; fully dependent on humans.
