Ampex is an American electronics company founded in 1944 by Alexander M. Poniatoff as a subsidiary of Dalmo-Victor. The name AMPEX is a portmanteau, created by its founder, which stands for Alexander M. Poniatoff Excellence. Ampex pioneered magnetic tape recording in the United States and revolutionised the radio, television, and recording industries.

Audio recording

After World War II, working from German Magnetophon technology brought to America by Jack Mullin, Ampex produced the Model 200, the first commercially successful reel-to-reel tape recorder in the United States (1948). Bing Crosby famously adopted the machines to pre-record his radio shows, investing in the company and distributing its recorders. Ampex tape decks became the recording industry standard, leading to multitrack recording — the Ampex eight-track machines used by Les Paul and later studios — and Ampex also became a leading manufacturer of magnetic tape itself.

Videotape

In 1956 Ampex introduced the VRX-1000, the first commercially successful videotape recorder, using two-inch quadruplex tape and a rotating head assembly. The machine transformed television by allowing programmes to be recorded, time-shifted between time zones, and replayed — work for which the development team, including Charles Ginsburg and the young Ray Dolby, received major industry awards. Later Ampex innovations included the helical-scan recorder and the HS-100 slow-motion disc system.

Decline and legacy

Ampex declined from the 1970s as Japanese competitors dominated video equipment, and its data storage businesses were progressively sold off; the company passed through bankruptcy in 2008. Its contributions to recording technology are commemorated by Grammy and Emmy awards, and the Ampex name survives in data-storage and archival ventures.
