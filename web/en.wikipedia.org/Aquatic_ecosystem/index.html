Aquatic ecosystem

An aquatic ecosystem is an ecosystem found in and around a body of water. It contrasts with land-based ecosystems, which are terrestrial. Communities of organisms that are dependent on each other and on their environment live in aquatic ecosystems. The two main types of aquatic ecosystems are marine ecosystems and freshwater ecosystems.

Types

Marine ecosystems cover roughly 71% of Earth's surface and include oceans, estuaries, salt marshes, lagoons, mangrove forests, intertidal zones, and coral reefs. Freshwater ecosystems occupy a much smaller fraction of the surface and include lakes, ponds, rivers, streams, springs, bogs, and wetlands; limnology is the study of inland aquatic ecosystems. Aquatic systems are often classified by salinity, depth, light penetration (photic versus aphotic zones), and whether the water is flowing (lotic) or still (lentic).

Function

Primary production by phytoplankton and aquatic plants supports food webs that range from zooplankton to top predators. Aquatic ecosystems cycle nutrients, sequester carbon, and generate a large share of atmospheric oxygen through photosynthesis. Decomposers recycle organic matter, while benthic organisms anchor many food webs.

Threats

Aquatic ecosystems face pressures from pollution, eutrophication, overfishing, habitat destruction, invasive species, and climate change, including ocean warming and acidification.
