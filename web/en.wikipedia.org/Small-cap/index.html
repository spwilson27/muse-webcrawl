Small-cap

The term small-cap describes companies with a relatively small market capitalization. In finance, it generally refers to stocks of publicly traded companies with market capitalisations typically between about US$300 million and US$2 billion, sitting between micro-cap companies below them and mid-cap and large-cap companies above them.

Characteristics

Small-cap stocks are often younger, faster-growing companies. They tend to be more volatile and less liquid than large caps, but have historically offered periods of outperformance, a phenomenon studied as the "size effect" in asset pricing. Because analyst coverage is thinner, small-cap markets are considered less informationally efficient.

Indices

Major small-cap benchmarks include the Russell 2000 Index and the S&P SmallCap 600 in the United States. Many index funds and exchange-traded funds track such benchmarks, giving investors diversified exposure to the segment.
