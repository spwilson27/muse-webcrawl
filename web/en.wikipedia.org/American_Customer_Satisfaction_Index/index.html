American Customer Satisfaction Index

The American Customer Satisfaction Index (ACSI) is an economic indicator that measures the satisfaction of consumers across the U.S. economy. It was developed at the University of Michigan's Ross School of Business and launched in 1994.

ACSI
Launched: October 1994
Developed at: University of Michigan
Founder: Claes Fornell
Coverage: hundreds of companies across dozens of industries in the U.S.

Methodology

The ACSI is based on surveys of customers of individual companies, with scores reported on a 0–100 scale. Its cause-and-effect model links customer expectations, perceived quality, and perceived value to customer satisfaction, which in turn is linked to customer complaints and loyalty. Results are published quarterly and aggregated to sector, industry, and national levels.

Use

Researchers have studied the relationship between ACSI scores and business and stock-market performance, and the index is used by companies for benchmarking and by analysts as a gauge of consumer sentiment.
