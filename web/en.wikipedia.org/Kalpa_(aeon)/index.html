Kalpa (aeon)

A kalpa (Sanskrit: कल्प, "aeon") is an immense period of time in Hindu and Buddhist cosmology. In Hindu reckoning a kalpa is a single day of Brahmā, lasting 4.32 billion years, and is followed by a night of equal length during which the universe is dissolved.

Hindu cosmology

One kalpa is divided into 1,000 cycles of the four yugas (mahāyugas), or equivalently into 14 manvantaras, each presided over by a Manu. Each mahāyuga of 4,320,000 years consists of the Satya, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali yugas in descending order of virtue. Fifty years of Brahmā's life — 100 years in total, each of 360 days and nights — make up the lifespan of one Brahmā; the present Brahmā is said to be in his 51st year, in the śveta-vārāha kalpa.

Buddhist cosmology

In Buddhism a kalpa (kappa in Pali) is likewise an inconceivably long age through which the universe expands and contracts. Texts illustrate its length with similes: if a mountain were wiped once a century with a silk cloth, the mountain would wear away before a kalpa ended. Buddhist kalpas are subdivided into periods of formation, abiding, destruction and void, and the scriptures name past and future Buddhas by the kalpas in which they appear.

Usage

The concept allowed Indian thinkers to contemplate vast, cyclical timescales millennia before modern geology and cosmology established the great age of the universe.
