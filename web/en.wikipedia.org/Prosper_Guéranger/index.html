Prosper Louis Pascal Guéranger (4 April 1805 – 30 January 1875) was a French Benedictine monk and priest, generally regarded as the founder of the modern liturgical movement. He refounded the Abbey of Saint-Pierre in Solesmes after the suppression of monastic life during the French Revolution, becoming its first abbot, and worked to restore the Benedictine order in France and to revive the Roman liturgy and Gregorian chant.

Life and work

Ordained a priest in 1827, Guéranger purchased the former priory of Solesmes in 1832 and established a community there; in 1837 Pope Gregory XVI raised Solesmes to an abbey and named him its abbot and superior general of the new French Benedictine congregation. His Institutions liturgiques (1840–1851) attacked the neo-Gallican diocesan liturgies then used in France and argued for the restoration of the Roman rite, a campaign that largely succeeded within his lifetime. His most popular work, L'année liturgique (The Liturgical Year), offered commentaries on the Mass texts of every day of the church year and shaped Catholic devotional life for generations.

Chant restoration

Guéranger set the monks of Solesmes to the comparative study of medieval chant manuscripts, laying the groundwork for the restoration of Gregorian chant that culminated in the Vatican edition published under Pope Pius X. The Solesmes school of chant performance and scholarship, carried forward by Dom Joseph Pothier and Dom André Mocquereau, became internationally influential. Guéranger was also a prominent ultramontane, defending papal authority in writings that contributed to the climate surrounding the definition of papal infallibility at the First Vatican Council in 1870.
