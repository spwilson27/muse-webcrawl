Library of Congress Classification

The Library of Congress Classification (LCC) is a system of library classification developed by the Library of Congress in the United States. It is used by most research and academic libraries in the US and several other countries to organise their collections.

Structure

LCC divides knowledge into 21 main classes, each identified by a letter of the alphabet (for example A General Works, B Philosophy, Psychology, Religion; H Social Sciences; P Language and Literature; Q Science; T Technology), subdivided by further letters and numbers. Call numbers combine the class notation with book numbers (often following the Cutter system) to order items uniquely on the shelf.

History

The system was devised in the early 20th century, largely by Herbert Putnam and Charles Martel, to suit the Library of Congress's own rapidly growing collections rather than as a universal scheme. It is maintained and published by the Library of Congress and is now available through Classification Web.
