Jura (Scottish Gaelic: Diùra) is an island in the Inner Hebrides of Scotland, adjacent to and northeast of Islay. With an area of about 367 square kilometres and only around 200 residents, it is one of the least densely populated islands in Scotland, and its human population is vastly outnumbered by its roughly 5,000 red deer, from which the island's name — possibly from the Old Norse for "deer island" — may derive.

Geography

The island is dominated by three steep, conical quartzite mountains known as the Paps of Jura: Beinn an Òir ("mountain of gold", 785 m), Beinn Shiantaidh ("sacred mountain", 757 m), and Beinn a' Chaolais ("mountain of the sound", 733 m). Between Jura and the neighbouring island of Scarba lies the Gulf of Corryvreckan, famous for its whirlpool, one of the largest permanent whirlpools in the world, caused by strong tidal currents over an underwater pinnacle.

History

Jura formed part of the territory of the Lords of the Isles and later came under Campbell influence. Like other Hebridean islands it experienced emigration in the 19th century, though less severe clearances than elsewhere. The island's economy has long centred on deer stalking estates, farming, and its single whisky distillery.

George Orwell

From 1946 until his death in January 1950, the writer George Orwell lived for periods at Barnhill, a remote farmhouse at the northern end of Jura, where he wrote most of his dystopian novel Nineteen Eighty-Four. In 1947 he narrowly escaped drowning when his boat was overturned in the Gulf of Corryvreckan while returning from a picnic.

Whisky and economy

The Isle of Jura distillery, founded in 1810 and rebuilt in 1963, stands in the island's only village, Craighouse, which also hosts the island's hotel, shop, and church. A single road, the A846, runs along the east coast. Access is usually via the ferry from Islay's Port Askaig across the Sound of Islay to Feolin.

Wildlife

Besides red deer, Jura supports otters, seals, wild goats, and a diversity of birds; golden eagles and sea eagles may be seen. Most of the island is designated for its upland habitats.
