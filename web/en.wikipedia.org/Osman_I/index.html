Osman I or Osman Gazi (Ottoman Turkish: عثمان غازى, romanized: ʿOsmān Ġāzī; Turkish: I. Osman; c. 1258 – 1323/4) was the founder and first ruler of the Ottoman Empire. He established a small frontier principality (beylik) in northwestern Anatolia around the town of Söğüt, from which his descendants expanded into one of the largest empires in world history. The empire and the dynasty that ruled it took their name from Osman.

Life

Osman was the son of Ertuğrul, leader of the Kayı tribe of Oghuz Turks, who had settled in Söğüt under the patronage of the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum. After his father's death around 1280, Osman became chief (bey) of the tribe. Taking advantage of the decline of both the Seljuks and the Byzantine Empire, he steadily expanded his territory westward into Byzantine Bithynia, capturing strongholds such as Karacahisar, Bilecik, Yarhisar and İnegöl.

Tradition dates the founding of the Ottoman state to 1299, when Osman is said to have declared independence from the Seljuks. In 1302 he defeated a Byzantine force at the Battle of Bapheus, and his warriors went on to raid the environs of Nicaea and Nicomedia. Osman died in 1323 or 1324, reportedly just as news arrived that his son Orhan had captured Bursa, which became the first Ottoman capital.

Legacy

Later Ottoman tradition celebrated Osman as a just and pious warrior and attached numerous legends to his life, including the famous "Dream of Osman", in which a tree growing from his body shaded the whole world — a prophecy of the empire's future extent. Historians note that contemporary sources for his reign are extremely scarce and much of the narrative was written down more than a century after his death.
