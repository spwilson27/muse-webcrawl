Possession (law)

In law, possession is the control a person intentionally exercises toward a thing, whether or not they are its owner. Legal possession is usually combined with two elements derived from Roman law: physical control (corpus possessionis) and the intention to possess (animus possidendi). Possession is distinct from ownership, though the two frequently coincide and the law strongly protects possessors even against all but the true owner.

Concepts

Common distinctions include actual versus constructive possession (control through another, such as a bailee or an agent, or control of premises where a thing is kept), exclusive versus shared possession, and lawful versus wrongful possession. A finder of lost property generally acquires a possessory title good against everyone except the true owner. Long-continued possession of land can mature into title by adverse possession.

Significance

Possession serves as prima facie evidence of ownership, grounds actions such as trespass and conversion, and determines who may transfer interests (for example, delivery in the sale of goods). In criminal law, "possession" of prohibited items — drugs, weapons, stolen goods — is frequently an offence in itself, and courts distinguish actual from constructive possession, often requiring knowledge of the item's presence and character.
