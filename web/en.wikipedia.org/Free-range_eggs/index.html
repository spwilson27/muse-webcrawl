Free-range eggs

Free-range laying hens with outdoor access

Free-range eggs are eggs produced from birds that are permitted access to the outdoors, as opposed to eggs from hens kept in battery cages or confined indoors. The term "free range" is regulated differently in different jurisdictions, and the actual conditions hens experience under the label vary widely.

Definitions and regulation

In the European Union, free-range egg production requires that hens have continuous daytime access to open-air runs, mainly covered with vegetation, subject to maximum stocking densities, and the eggs are stamped with the farming method code "1". In the United States, the USDA requires only that free-range hens have some access to the outdoors; there is no minimum duration or stocking-density standard, so in practice "access" may mean a small door opening onto a screened porch.

Comparison with other systems

Free-range sits between cage production and pasture-raised production in both price and the amount of outdoor space afforded to birds. Barn (cage-free) systems keep hens indoors but uncaged; organic certification adds feed and antibiotic requirements on top of outdoor access rules.

Welfare and market

Advocates argue outdoor access allows natural behaviors such as foraging, dust bathing, and perching, while critics note higher risks of predation, disease, and feather pecking in large flocks. Free-range eggs command a price premium, and demand grew steadily as retailers and food companies adopted cage-free and free-range sourcing commitments in the 2010s and 2020s.
