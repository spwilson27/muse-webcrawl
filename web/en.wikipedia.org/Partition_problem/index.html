Partition problem

In number theory and computer science, the partition problem, or number partitioning, is the task of deciding whether a given multiset S of positive integers can be partitioned into two subsets S1 and S2 such that the sum of the numbers in S1 equals the sum of the numbers in S2. Although the partition problem is NP-complete, there are heuristics and pseudopolynomial-time algorithms that can solve it in many cases.

A multiset of numbers split into two subsets with equal sums.

Algorithms

The decision problem can be solved in pseudopolynomial time by dynamic programming: one computes which subset sums are achievable, and the answer is affirmative exactly when half of the total sum is achievable. The running time is polynomial in the number of elements and the total sum, but exponential in the number of bits needed to encode the input, which is why the problem is only weakly NP-complete. When exact solutions are unnecessary, the greedy Karmarkar–Karp differencing heuristic and its refinements produce partitions with very small discrepancies.

Related problems

The partition problem is closely related to the subset sum problem, which asks whether any subset sums to a given target, and to the bin packing and scheduling problems, where partitioning generalizes to balancing loads across many machines or bins. The optimization version, minimizing the difference between the two subset sums, is a classic problem in heuristic algorithm design and statistical physics, where random instances exhibit a well-known phase transition between easy and hard instances.
