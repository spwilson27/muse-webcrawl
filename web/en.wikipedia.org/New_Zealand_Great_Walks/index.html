New Zealand Great Walks

The New Zealand Great Walks are a set of premier tramping (hiking) tracks administered by the Department of Conservation (DOC). The walks pass through some of New Zealand's most scenic and iconic landscapes, are maintained to a high standard, and are served by comfortable huts that must be booked in advance during the Great Walks season (roughly late October to April). The network was established in 1992 to manage heavy visitor demand and protect the tracks from overuse.

The walks

  • Lake Waikaremoana Track — 46 km, Te Urewera, North Island
  • Tongariro Northern Circuit — 43 km loop around Mount Ngauruhoe in Tongariro National Park
  • Whanganui Journey — a 145 km river journey by canoe or kayak (a Great Walk though not a walk)
  • Abel Tasman Coast Track — 60 km along the coast of Abel Tasman National Park
  • Heaphy Track — 78.4 km through Kahurangi National Park
  • Milford Track — 53.5 km in Fiordland, often called "the finest walk in the world"
  • Kepler Track — 60 km loop in Fiordland National Park
  • Routeburn Track — 32 km linking Fiordland and Mount Aspiring national parks
  • Rakiura Track — 32 km loop on Stewart Island / Rakiura
  • Paparoa Track — 55 km, opened in 2019, also open to mountain bikes
  • Hump Ridge Track — added as the eleventh Great Walk in 2024

Booking system

During the peak season, walkers must book bunks in Great Walks huts or campsites in advance, with separate pricing for New Zealand residents and international visitors. Popular tracks such as the Milford Track sell out quickly when bookings open. Outside the season, facilities are cheaper and operate on a first-come, first-served basis, but conditions can be more hazardous.
