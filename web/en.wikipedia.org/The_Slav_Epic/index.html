The Slav Epic

ArtistAlphonse Mucha
Year1910–1928
MediumOil and tempera on canvas (20 canvases)
Largest dimensionsUp to 610 cm × 810 cm

The Slav Epic (Czech: Slovanská epopej) is a cycle of 20 large-format canvases by the Czech painter Alphonse Mucha, depicting the mythology and history of the Slavic peoples. Painted between 1910 and 1928, it is considered Mucha's life's masterwork, even though his popular fame rests on his Art Nouveau posters.

Creation and patronage

Mucha conceived the project around the 1900 Paris Exposition, where decorating the Bosnia-Herzegovina pavilion awakened his ambition to paint Slavic history. The cycle was financed by the American industrialist and Slavophile Charles Richard Crane, and Mucha spent 18 years — mostly at Zbiroh castle in Bohemia — on research and painting, travelling through Russia, Poland, Serbia, Bulgaria and Mount Athos. Scenes include the ancient Slavs between the "Turanian Whip and the Sword of the Goths," the introduction of the Slavonic liturgy, Jan Hus preaching, the defence of Szigetvár, the abolition of serfdom in Russia, and the final "Apotheosis of the Slavs."

Gift and wanderings

Mucha presented the completed cycle to the city of Prague in 1928, on the condition that a dedicated pavilion be built — which never happened. The canvases were rolled up and hidden from the Nazis during the Second World War, then stored and later displayed from 1963 in the castle of Moravský Krumlov. After decades of dispute, the cycle was transferred to Prague in 2012 and exhibited at the Veletržní Palace; litigation over ownership and display continued, and in 2021 the works returned on loan to Moravský Krumlov while Prague plans a permanent home.

Reception

Czech critics between the wars were cool toward the cycle's academic style, but popular reverence has grown steadily. The Slav Epic is now regarded as a national treasure and has toured internationally, drawing enormous crowds in Japan.
