Cyrus Hermann Kotzschmar Curtis (June 18, 1850 – June 7, 1933) was an American publisher of magazines and newspapers, founder of the Curtis Publishing Company. He built one of the most successful publishing empires in the United States around The Saturday Evening Post and the Ladies' Home Journal.

Career

After early ventures in Boston and Philadelphia newspapers, Curtis founded the Ladies' Home Journal in 1883, edited by his wife Louisa Knapp Curtis, and it became the first American magazine to reach one million subscribers. In 1897 he bought the struggling Saturday Evening Post for ,000 and rebuilt it into a mass-circulation weekly famous for its covers by Norman Rockwell and fiction by leading authors. His business model sold magazines cheaply and profited from advertising.

Philanthropy

Curtis was a notable philanthropist and music patron: he founded the Curtis Institute of Music in Philadelphia in 1924, endowing it so students attended tuition-free, and supported hospitals, museums, and the Curtis Arboretum at his Wyncote estate.
