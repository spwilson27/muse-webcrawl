Revolution of Dignity

Date18–23 February 2014 (peak)
LocationKyiv, Ukraine
ResultRemoval of President Viktor Yanukovych

The Revolution of Dignity (Ukrainian: Революція гідності), also known as the Maidan revolution, took place in Ukraine in February 2014 at the culmination of the Euromaidan protest movement. It resulted in the ousting of President Viktor Yanukovych and the restoration of the 2004 constitution.

Background

Protests began in November 2013 after Yanukovych suspended preparations to sign an association agreement with the European Union under Russian pressure. The protests, centred on Kyiv's Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Independence Square), grew after a violent police crackdown on students on 30 November 2013.

February events

Between 18 and 20 February 2014, clashes between protesters and security forces escalated into gunfire; around 100 protesters — remembered as the Heavenly Hundred — and 13 police officers were killed. On 21 February an EU-brokered agreement was signed, but Yanukovych fled Kyiv that night; the Verkhovna Rada voted to remove him on 22 February and set new elections.

Consequences

Russia condemned the change of government as a coup and responded by annexing Crimea and supporting armed separatists in the Donbas, beginning the Russo-Ukrainian War. Petro Poroshenko won the May 2014 presidential election.
