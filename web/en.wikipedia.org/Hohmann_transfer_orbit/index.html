Hohmann transfer orbit

In orbital mechanics, the Hohmann transfer orbit is an elliptical orbit used to transfer between two circular orbits of different radii around the same central body. It is generally the most fuel-efficient two-burn maneuver for such a transfer, though it may take longer than alternatives.

Description

The maneuver uses two engine impulses: one to move the spacecraft from its initial circular orbit onto the transfer ellipse, and a second at the far point to circularize into the target orbit. The transfer ellipse is tangent to both circular orbits.

History

The maneuver is named for German scientist Walter Hohmann, who described it in 1925. It is widely used for interplanetary missions and satellite orbit raising.

Limitations

Hohmann transfers are inefficient for very large radius ratios where a bi-elliptic transfer can use less total delta-v, and they require favorable planetary alignment for interplanetary travel.
