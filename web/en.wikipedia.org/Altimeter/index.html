Altimeter

An altimeter or altitude meter is an instrument used to measure the altitude of an object above a fixed level. The measurement of altitude is called altimetry, which is related to the term bathymetry, the measurement of depth underwater.

Pressure altimeter

The most common type is the barometric altimeter used in aircraft. It is an aneroid barometer that measures atmospheric pressure, which decreases predictably with altitude, and displays the result as height above a reference pressure datum. Pilots set the altimeter to local sea-level pressure (QNH) or standard pressure (1013.25 hPa) when flying at flight levels. Because pressure varies with weather and temperature, barometric altimeters are subject to errors that procedures and corrections must manage.

Other types

Radar (radio) altimeters measure height above the ground directly by timing reflected radio pulses and are used during aircraft approach and landing. Satellite altimetry uses radar or laser pulses from orbiting satellites to measure sea surface height, ice-sheet elevation, and terrain. Consumer devices such as watches and handheld GPS units combine barometric sensors with satellite positioning.
