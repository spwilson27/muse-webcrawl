Reflection coefficient

The reflection coefficient measures how much of a wave is reflected by a discontinuity in its transmission medium. In electrical engineering, for a transmission line of characteristic impedance Z₀ terminated in a load ZL, the voltage reflection coefficient is Γ = (ZL − Z₀)/(ZL + Z₀), a complex number whose magnitude ranges from 0 (perfectly matched, no reflection) to 1 (total reflection).

Special cases

An open circuit gives Γ = +1 and a short circuit Γ = −1, reflecting the wave with preserved or inverted polarity respectively. A matched load gives Γ = 0. The reflection coefficient is closely related to the standing wave ratio (SWR = (1 + |Γ|)/(1 − |Γ|)) and to return loss.

Other fields

Analogous coefficients describe reflected pressure waves in acoustics (from acoustic impedance contrasts), electromagnetic waves at dielectric boundaries (the Fresnel coefficients), and seismic waves at rock interfaces, where reflection coefficients underpin reflection seismology used in oil exploration.
