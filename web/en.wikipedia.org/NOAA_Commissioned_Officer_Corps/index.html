The NOAA Commissioned Officer Corps (NOAA Corps) is one of the eight uniformed services of the United States and the commissioned corps of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration. Its officers operate NOAA's fleet of research and survey ships and aircraft and hold scientific and administrative positions throughout the agency.

History

The corps traces its origins to 1917, when the commissioned corps of the U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey was established so that survey officers working near combat zones in the First World War would be treated as combatants rather than spies if captured. It became the ESSA Corps in 1965 and took its present form when NOAA was created in 1970.

Role and service

With only about 300 officers, the NOAA Corps is the smallest of the U.S. uniformed services. It has no enlisted personnel; its officers are all graduates with degrees in science, engineering, or related fields. Typical assignments include commanding hydrographic survey vessels, flying "hurricane hunter" reconnaissance aircraft, and leading environmental monitoring programs. Officers serve under the authority of the President and can be militarized in wartime.

Ranks

The corps uses naval rank structure, from ensign through vice admiral, and its officers are entitled to the same pay and benefits as members of the armed forces under Title 37.
