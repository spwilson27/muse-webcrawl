Zork is one of the earliest interactive fiction computer games, with roots in the influential Colossal Cave Adventure. The first version was written between 1977 and 1979 using the MDL programming language by Tim Anderson, Marc Blank, Bruce Daniels and Dave Lebling, members of the MIT Dynamic Modelling Group. When the group founded Infocom in 1979, Zork became the new company's first product, split into a trilogy — Zork I (1980), Zork II (1981) and Zork III (1982) — to fit on personal computers.

Gameplay

The player explores the ruins of the Great Underground Empire, typing natural-language commands such as "take lamp" or "go north". The game opens with its famous first line: "You are standing in an open field west of a white house." The parser was unusually sophisticated for its time, understanding complex sentences, pronouns and multiple objects. The dark is dangerous: "It is pitch black. You are likely to be eaten by a grue."

Technology

To port the game widely, Infocom created the Z-machine, a virtual machine whose story files could run on many platforms unchanged. The Z-machine architecture later became a standard for interactive fiction and is still supported by interpreters today.

Legacy

Zork was a major commercial success, selling over a million copies across the trilogy, and it spawned sequels including Enchanter, Beyond Zork and the graphical Return to Zork. It is frequently cited among the greatest video games ever made, and its name came from hacker slang for an unfinished program.
