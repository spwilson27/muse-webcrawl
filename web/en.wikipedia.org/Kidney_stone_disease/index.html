Kidney stone disease

Other namesNephrolithiasis, urolithiasis
SymptomsSevere flank pain, blood in urine, nausea
TypesCalcium oxalate, calcium phosphate, uric acid, struvite, cystine
Risk factorsDehydration, diet, obesity, certain metabolic disorders

Kidney stone disease, also known as nephrolithiasis, is a condition in which solid pieces of material (kidney stones) form in the urinary tract. Stones form when urine becomes concentrated, allowing minerals such as calcium oxalate to crystallise and stick together.

Signs and symptoms

Small stones may pass unnoticed, but a stone obstructing the ureter causes renal colic: waves of severe pain in the flank and lower abdomen, often radiating to the groin, accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and blood in the urine. Infection with obstruction is a medical emergency.

Treatment and prevention

Most small stones pass spontaneously with hydration and pain relief; alpha blockers may help passage. Larger or stuck stones may need shock wave lithotripsy, ureteroscopy, or percutaneous nephrolithotomy. Prevention centres on drinking enough fluid to produce over two litres of urine daily, dietary adjustment, and medications such as thiazide diuretics or potassium citrate in recurrent cases.
