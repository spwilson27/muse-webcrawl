Traffic congestion

Traffic congestion is a condition in transport that is characterised by slower speeds, longer trip times and increased vehicular queueing. It occurs when traffic demand exceeds the available capacity of a road network, most familiarly during rush hours in large cities.

Causes

Congestion arises from bottlenecks, traffic incidents, roadworks, adverse weather and traffic signals, layered on top of demand that concentrates travel into peak periods. Economists note the phenomenon of induced demand: adding road capacity can attract additional trips, returning congestion to previous levels.

Impacts and mitigation

Costs include lost time, fuel waste, air pollution and unreliable journeys. Mitigation strategies include public transport investment, congestion pricing (as in Singapore, London and Stockholm), traffic signal optimisation, ramp metering and policies that spread or reduce peak demand, such as flexible working hours.
