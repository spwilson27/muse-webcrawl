Establishment Clause

The Establishment Clause is the first of several pronouncements in the First Amendment to the United States Constitution: "Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion." Together with the Free Exercise Clause, it forms the religion clauses of the First Amendment.

Interpretation

The clause prohibits the federal government — and, through incorporation under the Fourteenth Amendment, state governments — from establishing an official religion or favouring one religion over others. Landmark cases include Everson v. Board of Education (1947), Engel v. Vitale (1962) on school prayer, and Lemon v. Kurtzman (1971), which produced the Lemon test. In Kennedy v. Bremerton School District (2022) the Court moved toward an analysis based on history and tradition.
