Fortnite Battle Royale

Fortnite Battle Royale is a free-to-play battle royale video game developed and published by Epic Games. Released in September 2017 as a mode of Fortnite, it drops up to 100 players onto an island where they scavenge for weapons and resources, build defensive structures, and fight to be the last player or squad standing as a damaging storm shrinks the playable area. It became a global cultural phenomenon, surpassing 350 million registered players by 2020.

Gameplay

Players skydive from a flying "Battle Bus" onto the island and land unarmed, then loot weapons, shields, and materials from buildings and chests. Fortnite's distinctive mechanic is building: players harvest wood, stone, and metal and instantly construct walls, ramps, and forts, which skilled players use offensively and defensively in "build battles." Matches last roughly 20 minutes. Modes include Solo, Duo, and Squads, and limited-time modes; in 2022 Epic added a permanent "Zero Build" mode that removes construction for players who prefer pure gunplay.

Seasons and live events

The game is organised into seasons and chapters, each reshaping the island map and storyline, with a purchasable Battle Pass of cosmetic rewards. Fortnite is famous for large in-game live events — concerts by artists such as Marshmello, Travis Scott, and Ariana Grande, film tie-ins, and brand collaborations spanning Marvel, Star Wars, and numerous musicians and athletes. A 2019 event ended with the map being consumed by a black hole before the launch of Chapter 2. In 2023, "Fortnite OG" temporarily restored the original 2018 map and drew record player counts.

Business and impact

Revenue comes from cosmetic microtransactions purchased with the in-game currency V-Bucks; the game generated billions of dollars in its first years. Fortnite helped popularise cross-platform play across consoles and PC. A legal dispute between Epic and Apple over in-app payment rules led to Fortnite's removal from the iOS App Store in 2020 and a landmark antitrust trial (Epic Games v. Apple); the game returned to iOS in the European Union in 2024 under new digital-market rules, and to the US App Store in 2025.
