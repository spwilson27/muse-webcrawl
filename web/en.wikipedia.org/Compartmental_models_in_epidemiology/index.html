Compartmental models in epidemiology

Compartmental models are a framework for modelling the spread of infectious disease in mathematical epidemiology. The population is divided into compartments according to disease status, and people progress between compartments at defined rates. The models are usually deterministic systems of ordinary differential equations, assuming a large, homogeneously mixing population.

The SIR model

The classic model, developed by Kermack and McKendrick in 1927, divides the population into Susceptible (S), Infectious (I), and Recovered/Removed (R). Susceptibles become infected at a rate proportional to contact with infectious individuals (the transmission rate β), and infectious individuals recover at rate γ. The basic reproduction number R₀ = β/γ determines whether an epidemic takes off.

Variants

Common extensions include SIS (no lasting immunity), SEIR (adding an Exposed latent class), SEIRS (immunity wanes), MSIR (maternal immunity), and models with births, deaths, vaccination compartments, or age and spatial structure. During the COVID-19 pandemic, SEIR-type models were widely used for forecasting and evaluating interventions.

Limitations

Basic compartmental models ignore heterogeneity in contact patterns, superspreading, and behaviour change; agent-based and network models address these at the cost of complexity and data demands.
