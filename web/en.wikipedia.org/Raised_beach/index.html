Raised beach

A raised beach: a former shoreline now standing above the reach of the sea.

A raised beach, coastal terrace, or perched coastline is a relatively flat, wave-cut or depositional platform that was formerly a beach or shore zone but is now above water level. Raised beaches are formed either by a fall in relative sea level, by uplift of the land (tectonic or isostatic), or by a combination of the two.

Because they preserve the position of a former shoreline, raised beaches are important records of past sea levels and of vertical land movement. A staircase of raised beaches at different heights documents successive high sea-level stands or progressive uplift.

Formation

Following the last glacial period, the melting of the great ice sheets caused both a global rise in sea level and the isostatic rebound of land depressed by the ice. In formerly glaciated areas such as Scotland, Scandinavia, Canada and Alaska, rebound outpaced the rise, leaving sequences of raised beaches — in parts of Sweden the highest post-glacial shoreline now stands over 280 metres above present sea level. On tectonically active coasts, such as the Huon Peninsula in Papua New Guinea, uplifted coral reef terraces form staircases dated by uranium-series methods.

Scientific value

By dating raised beaches — using shells, corals, radiocarbon or optically stimulated luminescence — geologists reconstruct relative sea-level change and rates of crustal movement, which informs understanding of glacial history, earthquake cycles and future coastal change.
