Adam

Adam
Spouse: Eve
Children: Cain, Abel, Seth (in Genesis)
Venerated in: Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Druze, Baháʼí Faith

Adam is the name given in Genesis 1–5 to the first human. Adam is the first human being aware of God, and features as such in various belief systems, including Judaism, Christianity, Gnosticism and Islam. According to the creation myth of the Abrahamic religions, he was the first man, created by God from the dust of the ground, and placed in the Garden of Eden with Eve, the first woman, formed from his rib.

In Genesis

In the Book of Genesis, God forms Adam from the dust (the Hebrew adam is related to adamah, "ground") and breathes life into him. Adam names the animals, eats with Eve of the forbidden fruit of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and is expelled from Eden. His sons include Cain, Abel and Seth; Genesis 5 credits him with a lifespan of 930 years.

In Judaism

In Jewish tradition Adam is the progenitor of humanity and the first to receive the divine breath; rabbinic literature discusses his original glory, his sin, and his repentance. A midrashic tradition holds that his burial place is the Cave of Machpelah or, in other accounts, near the Temple Mount.

In Christianity

In Christian theology Adam's disobedience — the Fall — introduced original sin into the world. The Apostle Paul contrasts Adam, through whom death came, with Christ, called the "last Adam" or "second Adam", through whom comes resurrection and life (Romans 5; 1 Corinthians 15).

In Islam

Adam (Arabic: آدم, ʾĀdam) is honoured in Islam as the first prophet and the father of humanity. The Qur'an relates his creation from clay, the angels' prostration before him, his dwelling in Paradise with his wife, his lapse and subsequent forgiveness by God. Unlike in Christian doctrine, Adam's error does not transmit inherited sin.

Historicity

Modern scholarship treats the Genesis Adam narratives as theological myth rather than history, and human origins are explained scientifically by evolution. Within genetics, the terms "Y-chromosomal Adam" and "mitochondrial Eve" denote the most recent common patrilineal and matrilineal ancestors of living humans — unrelated to the biblical figures.
