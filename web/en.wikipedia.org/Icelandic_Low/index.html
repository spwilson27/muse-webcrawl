Icelandic Low

Type: Semi-permanent low-pressure system
Location: North Atlantic, near Iceland and southern Greenland
Strongest: Winter

The Icelandic Low is a semi-permanent centre of low atmospheric pressure found between Iceland and southern Greenland in the North Atlantic. It is one of the atmosphere's "centres of action", most intense in winter, when the contrast between the cold continents and the relatively warm ocean drives frequent cyclones along the North Atlantic storm track.

Behaviour

The Low is not a single storm but a statistical average: a succession of extratropical cyclones deepens near Iceland as they travel northeastward. In summer it weakens and shifts position. Together with the Azores High, its strength defines the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO): when the pressure difference between the two is large (positive NAO), westerlies strengthen, bringing mild, wet winters to northern Europe and cold, dry conditions to Greenland; a negative NAO reverses the pattern and can bring severe cold to Europe.

Effects

The Icelandic Low steers storms toward northwestern Europe, shapes precipitation from Newfoundland to Scandinavia, and interacts with sea ice, ocean circulation, and fisheries in the northern Atlantic. Long-term shifts in its behaviour are a research topic in climate science.
