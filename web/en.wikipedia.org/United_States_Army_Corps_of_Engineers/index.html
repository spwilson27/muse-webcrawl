United States Army Corps of Engineers

The United States Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) is a U.S. federal agency and Army formation of engineers, soldiers and civilians responsible for much of the nation's public engineering: navigation, flood risk management, hydropower, water supply, environmental restoration and military construction. Its headquarters is in Washington, D.C.

Missions

The Corps operates and maintains hundreds of locks and dams, thousands of miles of navigable waterways and many of America's busiest ports. It is a major provider of hydroelectric power, manages flood-risk projects on rivers such as the Mississippi and Missouri, and issues permits for work in wetlands and navigable waters under the Clean Water Act and Rivers and Harbors Act.

History

The Corps traces its origins to the Continental Army's engineers of 1775 and was made a permanent separate branch in 1802, the same year it founded the U.S. Military Academy at West Point, which it long supervised. Its civil works role expanded through the 19th and 20th centuries with river and harbor legislation.

Organization

USACE is organized into divisions and districts aligned largely with major watersheds, plus research and support centers including the Engineer Research and Development Center (ERDC) in Vicksburg, Mississippi.
