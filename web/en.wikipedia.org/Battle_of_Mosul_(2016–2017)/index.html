Battle of Mosul (2016–2017)

Date16 October 2016 – 20 July 2017
LocationMosul, Nineveh Governorate, Iraq
ResultIraqi and coalition victory
BelligerentsIraq and allies · Islamic State (ISIL)

The Battle of Mosul was a major military campaign launched by the Iraqi government forces and their allies on 16 October 2016 to retake the city of Mosul from the Islamic State (ISIL), which had seized it in June 2014 and made it the capital of its self-declared caliphate in Iraq. At its conclusion it was among the largest and deadliest urban battles since the Second World War.

Forces and plan

The attacking coalition numbered around 100,000 personnel, including the Iraqi Army, the elite Counter Terrorism Service, federal police, Kurdish Peshmerga, and Popular Mobilization Forces, supported by US-led coalition air power and advisers. ISIL defended the city with an estimated 3,000–5,000 fighters, using tunnels, snipers, car bombs, and human shields.

The fighting

Iraqi special forces entered eastern Mosul in November 2016 and secured the east bank of the Tigris by January 2017. In February the offensive resumed against western Mosul, where the narrow streets of the Old City slowed the advance to a crawl. The Great Mosque of al-Nuri, from whose minaret ISIL's leader had proclaimed the caliphate, was destroyed in June 2017. On 10 July 2017 Prime Minister Haider al-Abadi declared Mosul liberated, with final pockets cleared by 20 July.

Human cost

Thousands of civilians were killed — estimates for civilian deaths commonly range from about 9,000 to 11,000 — and close to a million people were displaced. Much of western Mosul, including most of the Old City, was left in ruins.
