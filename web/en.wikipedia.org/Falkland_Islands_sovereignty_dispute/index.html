The Falkland Islands sovereignty dispute is a territorial conflict between the United Kingdom and Argentina over the Falkland Islands (Spanish: Islas Malvinas), an archipelago in the South Atlantic administered by Britain since 1833 and claimed by Argentina as part of its national territory.

Background

Sovereignty over the uninhabited islands was contested in the 18th century by France, Spain, and Britain, each of which maintained settlements. After independence, the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata asserted succession to Spain's rights and appointed governors in the 1820s. In January 1833 a British naval force required the Argentine garrison at Puerto Soledad to leave; Argentina regards this as a usurpation, while Britain maintains its claim rests on earlier settlement and continuous, peaceful administration thereafter, coupled with the islanders' right of self-determination.

Twentieth century and war

Argentina pressed its claim at the United Nations, whose General Assembly Resolution 2065 (1965) recognized a sovereignty dispute and invited negotiations; talks from the 1960s to the early 1980s failed to produce agreement. On 2 April 1982 Argentina's military junta invaded the islands. Britain dispatched a naval task force; after a 74-day conflict in which 255 British and 649 Argentine service personnel were killed, Argentine forces surrendered on 14 June 1982. Britain subsequently strengthened its garrison (RAF Mount Pleasant) and excluded sovereignty from the agenda in restored diplomatic relations under the 1989–90 Madrid "sovereignty umbrella" formula.

Modern positions

Argentina's 1994 constitution declares recovery of the Malvinas a permanent national objective, and Argentina seeks bilateral negotiations, rejecting the islanders as a party to the dispute. The UK maintains it will not negotiate sovereignty against the islanders' wishes; in a March 2013 referendum, 99.8% of participating Falkland Islanders (turnout 92%) voted to remain a British Overseas Territory. The dispute periodically intensifies over offshore oil exploration, fisheries, and commemorative anniversaries, while practical cooperation on flights, trade, and war-graves identification has continued.
