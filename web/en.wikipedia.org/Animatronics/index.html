Animatronics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Animatronics is the use of mechatronic puppets and figures that are animated by mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic or electronic means. Animatronic characters are used in theme parks, films, television, museums and exhibitions to simulate living creatures or people.

History

The field has roots in ancient automata and clockwork figures, such as those built by Jacques de Vaucanson in the 18th century. The modern era began with Walt Disney's WED Enterprises, which created the singing birds of the Enchanted Tiki Room (1963) and the Audio-Animatronic Abraham Lincoln for the 1964 New York World's Fair. Disney's figures were programmed with reel-to-reel tape controlling pneumatic and hydraulic actuators.

Film and theme parks

In cinema, animatronics enabled realistic creatures that could interact with actors on set, as in Jurassic Park (1993), whose life-size dinosaurs were built by Stan Winston's studio, and the films of Jim Henson's Creature Shop. Although computer-generated imagery has replaced animatronics in many roles, physical figures are still used for close-ups and practical effects. Theme park attractions worldwide feature increasingly sophisticated autonomous and free-roaming animatronic characters.

Technology

An animatronic figure consists of an armature or skeleton, actuators (servo motors, pneumatics or hydraulics), skin of foam latex or silicone, and a control system that synchronises movement — and often dialogue — to a programmed performance.
