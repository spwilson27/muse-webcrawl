The European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) is an independent intergovernmental organisation supported by most of the nations of Europe. Founded in 1975, it is headquartered at Shinfield Park, Reading, United Kingdom, with additional sites in Bologna, Italy (data centre) and Bonn, Germany. ECMWF operates one of the world's most powerful supercomputer facilities dedicated to weather prediction and produces global numerical weather forecasts widely regarded as among the most skilful available.

Products

The centre's Integrated Forecasting System (IFS) produces medium-range forecasts out to about two weeks, monthly and seasonal outlooks, and atmospheric composition forecasts through the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS). Its ensemble prediction system provides probabilistic forecasts used by insurers, energy traders, and governments. ECMWF also produces the ERA5 reanalysis, a global reconstruction of past weather widely used in climate research, and it is a partner in the EU's Destination Earth initiative.

The "European model"

In the United States media the IFS is often called the "European model" and its outputs are compared with the American GFS. The European model's reputation was cemented by events such as its early and accurate prediction of Hurricane Sandy's landfall in 2012. Verification statistics consistently rank the IFS at or near the top of global forecasting systems in the medium range.

History

ECMWF was established under a convention signed in 1973 and began operating in 1975, issuing its first operational forecasts in 1979. It has steadily increased model resolution and computing power over the decades; in the 2020s its Bologna site hosts the Atos BullSequana supercomputers used for operational runs.
