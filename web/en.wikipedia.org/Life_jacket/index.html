Life jacket

A life jacket, also called a life vest, life preserver, or personal flotation device (PFD), is a garment or device worn to keep a person's head and body afloat in water and prevent drowning. Life jackets provide buoyancy through inherently buoyant materials such as foam, or through inflation by gas cartridges or oral inflation.

Types

Regulatory systems, including those of the United States Coast Guard, classify PFDs by performance: offshore life jackets designed to turn an unconscious wearer face-up in rough water, near-shore buoyant vests, flotation aids for calm water activities, throwable devices, and special-use devices. Modern classifications increasingly use buoyancy measured in newtons under international standards.

History

Early life preservers used cork, kapok, and balsa wood. The inflatable life vest became famous in the Second World War as the "Mae West", named for the actress. Foam-filled vests became standard for recreational boating in the later 20th century.

Use and regulation

Many jurisdictions require children and all boaters in certain conditions to wear life jackets, and commercial vessels must carry approved jackets for every person aboard. Proper fit is essential; a jacket that is too loose can ride up and fail to keep the airway clear of the water.
