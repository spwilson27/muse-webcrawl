Giovanni da Verrazzano

Giovanni da Verrazzano (c. 1485 – 1528), often rendered in English as Verrazano, was a Florentine explorer in the service of the French crown. He is renowned as the first European since Norse colonisation to explore the Atlantic coast of North America between Florida and New Brunswick, including New York Bay and Narragansett Bay, in 1524.

Verrazzano was born at Castello di Verrazzano near Greve in Chianti, south of Florence, and spent part of his life in Dieppe, France. In 1524, funded by Lyon bankers and commissioned by King Francis I to find a westward route to Asia, he sailed with the carrack La Dauphine. He made landfall near Cape Fear, then sailed north, exploring Pamlico Sound (which he mistook for the Pacific), the mouth of the Hudson River, and New England as far as Maine and Nova Scotia.

Voyages and death

His letter to Francis I, describing the voyage, is a key early European account of the North American coast and its peoples. Verrazzano made further voyages to Brazil and the Caribbean; he died in 1528, reportedly killed and eaten by Carib natives on Guadeloupe, according to his brother Girolamo's account — though the circumstances remain debated.

Legacy

The Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge in New York City (spelled with a single "z" until officially corrected in 2018), the Jamestown Verrazzano Bridge in Rhode Island, and numerous other places bear his name. His account, transmitted through the map of his brother Girolamo (1529) and the writings of Richard Hakluyt, helped shape European maps of North America, even though his mistaken "Sea of Verrazzano" (Pamlico Sound) misled cartographers for a century.
