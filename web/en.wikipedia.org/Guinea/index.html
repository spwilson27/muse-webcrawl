Guinea (French: Guinée), officially the Republic of Guinea, is a coastal country in West Africa. It borders Guinea-Bissau and Senegal to the northwest, Mali to the northeast, Côte d'Ivoire to the southeast, and Liberia and Sierra Leone to the south, with the Atlantic Ocean to the west. The country is sometimes called Guinea-Conakry, after its capital, to distinguish it from Guinea-Bissau and Equatorial Guinea. It has a population of around 14 million and an area of 245,857 km².

Geography

Guinea is known as the "water tower of West Africa": the Niger, Senegal, and Gambia rivers all rise in its Fouta Djallon highlands. The country divides into four regions — coastal Lower Guinea (Maritime Guinea), the pastoral Fouta Djallon plateau, the savanna of Upper Guinea, and the forested southeast (Forest Guinea), which includes Mount Nimba, the country's highest point at 1,752 m and a UNESCO-listed strict nature reserve shared with Côte d'Ivoire.

History

The territory formed part of successive Sahelian empires, including the Mali and Songhai empires, and later the Imamate of Futa Jallon, an Islamic theocratic state founded in the 1720s. France colonized the region in the late nineteenth century. In 1958 Guinea, under Ahmed Sékou Touré, was the only French colony to vote against joining the new French Community, becoming independent on 2 October 1958; France withdrew abruptly, and Touré ruled an increasingly authoritarian one-party state until his death in 1984. A military coup followed, bringing Lansana Conté to power until 2008. Guinea held its first democratic presidential election in 2010, won by Alpha Condé, whose disputed third term ended with another military coup in September 2021 led by Mamady Doumbouya.

Economy

Guinea possesses some of the world's largest reserves of bauxite — roughly a quarter of the global total — and is a leading exporter of the aluminium ore. It also has significant deposits of iron ore (including the vast Simandou range), gold, and diamonds, alongside substantial hydroelectric potential, though poverty remains widespread. The country was severely affected by the West African Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016, which began in Guinea's forest region.
