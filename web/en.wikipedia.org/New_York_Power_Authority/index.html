The New York Power Authority (NYPA) is the largest state public power organization in the United States, operating 16 generating facilities and more than 1,400 circuit-miles of transmission lines. It is a non-profit, public-benefit corporation created by the New York State Legislature in 1931, and it sells power to government agencies, community-owned electric systems and rural electric cooperatives, job-producing companies, and private utilities for resale to their customers.

NYPA does not use tax money or state credit in its operations. It finances its projects through bond sales and earns revenue primarily through sales of electricity. Roughly 70 percent or more of the electricity it produces comes from clean, renewable hydroelectric power.

History

Governor Franklin D. Roosevelt championed the creation of a public authority to develop the hydroelectric resources of the St. Lawrence River for the benefit of the state's residents. The Power Authority Act of 1931 established the agency, and Roosevelt's successor, Herbert H. Lehman, oversaw its early development.

The authority's flagship facilities include the Niagara Power Project near Niagara Falls, dedicated in 1961 and one of the largest hydroelectric plants in the United States, and the St. Lawrence–Franklin D. Roosevelt Power Project, built jointly with Ontario in the 1950s. It also operates the Blenheim-Gilboa Pumped Storage Power Project in the Catskills and several smaller hydroelectric, natural gas, and clean energy facilities.

Modern role

In recent decades NYPA has taken a central role in New York State's clean energy transition, supporting the goals of the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act. Its programs include energy efficiency services for public buildings, development of transmission infrastructure for renewable energy, electric vehicle charging networks such as EVolve NY, and large-scale renewable generation projects. Under legislation enacted in 2023, NYPA was given expanded authority to build renewable energy generation to help meet the state's climate targets.
