Voltage standing wave ratio

In radio engineering and telecommunications, standing wave ratio (SWR) is a measure of impedance matching of loads to the characteristic impedance of a transmission line or waveguide. The voltage standing wave ratio (VSWR) is defined as the ratio of the maximum to the minimum voltage amplitude along the line.

Definition

When a transmission line is terminated in a load whose impedance differs from the line's characteristic impedance, part of the signal is reflected. The incident and reflected waves interfere to form a standing wave pattern. VSWR = (1 + |Γ|) / (1 − |Γ|), where Γ is the reflection coefficient. A perfect match gives VSWR = 1:1; higher values indicate greater mismatch and power loss.

Measurement and use

VSWR is commonly measured with an SWR meter inserted in the feedline of an antenna system. Antenna designers aim for low VSWR across the operating band; transmitter protection circuits often reduce power if VSWR exceeds a safe threshold, commonly 2:1 or 3:1. Related quantities include return loss and the power standing wave ratio (PSWR).
