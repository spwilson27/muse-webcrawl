Abraham–Lorentz force

In electrodynamics, the Abraham–Lorentz force (also called the Lorentz–Abraham force) is the recoil force on an accelerating charged particle caused by the particle's own emission of electromagnetic radiation. It is a manifestation of radiation reaction: a charge that accelerates radiates energy, so an additional force must act on it beyond the applied external force.

History and formula

The force was derived in the framework of classical electron theory by Max Abraham and Hendrik Lorentz around 1902–1904. Non-relativistically, it is proportional to the time derivative of the particle's acceleration (the "jerk"), with a coefficient proportional to q2/(6πε0c3), where q is the charge and c the speed of light; the characteristic time for an electron is of order 10−23 seconds. Paul Dirac derived the relativistic generalization in 1938, known as the Abraham–Lorentz–Dirac force.

Pathologies

The resulting equation of motion is third order in time and admits unphysical solutions, including "runaway" solutions in which a particle accelerates without limit after the external force is removed, and "pre-acceleration", in which the particle begins to move slightly before a force is applied. These difficulties are standard illustrations of the limits of classical electrodynamics and motivated alternatives such as the Landau–Lifshitz equation of radiation reaction.
