Venice is a neighbourhood in the Westside region of Los Angeles, California. Founded in 1905 by tobacco millionaire Abbot Kinney as a beach resort town modelled on Venice, Italy, it is known for its canals, its bohemian culture, and the lively Ocean Front Walk along Venice Beach.

History

Kinney built a network of canals, a pier, and resort attractions; "Venice of America" opened on 4 July 1905. The city was annexed by Los Angeles in 1926, and most canals were filled in during the 1930s, though a small canal district survives. Venice declined mid-century and became a centre of the Beat and hippie countercultures, later experiencing rapid gentrification.

Sights

The Venice Beach Boardwalk (Ocean Front Walk) is one of Los Angeles's most visited attractions, lined with street performers, vendors, and the outdoor weightlifting area known as Muscle Beach. The Venice Canals, the Venice Fishing Pier, and the skate park built into the beach are other landmarks. The neighbourhood is also home to a growing technology sector sometimes nicknamed "Silicon Beach".

Arts and culture

Venice has long attracted artists and musicians; The Doors formed there in 1965. The Venice Art Walls near the skate park are a legal graffiti venue, and the neighbourhood hosts the Abbot Kinney Festival and the Venice Art Crawl.
