Roberto Unger

Roberto Mangabeira Unger (born 24 March 1947) is a Brazilian philosopher, legal scholar, and politician. A longtime professor at Harvard Law School, he was a founder of the critical legal studies movement and served twice as Brazil's Minister of Strategic Affairs.

Thought

Unger's work attacks the idea that social arrangements are necessary or natural. In False Necessity (1987) and other writings he argues for "institutional imagination" — redesigning markets, democracy, and law to empower ordinary people and deepen democracy. His philosophy combines pragmatism with a belief in human beings' capacity to transcend context, a theme developed in The Self Awakened (2007) and The Religion of the Future (2014).

Politics

Returning repeatedly to Brazilian politics, Unger advised opposition movements during the military dictatorship, ran for office, and served in the governments of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Dilma Rousseff, working on long-term development strategy for the Amazon and national innovation policy.
