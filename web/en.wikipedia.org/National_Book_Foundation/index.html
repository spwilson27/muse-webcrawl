National Book Foundation

The National Book Foundation is an American nonprofit organisation founded in 1989 to administer the National Book Awards and to promote literature and reading in the United States. It is based in New York City.

National Book Awards

The Awards, first presented in 1950 by the book industry, honour American literature in five categories: Fiction, Nonfiction, Poetry, Translated Literature (added in 2018) and Young People's Literature. Panels of judges select longlists, shortlists and winners announced each November; past fiction winners include William Faulkner's Collected Stories, Ralph Ellison's Invisible Man, and works by Susan Sontag, William T. Vollmann, Colson Whitehead and Jesmyn Ward. The Foundation also bestows the Medal for Distinguished Contribution to American Letters and the Literarian Award for service to the literary community.

Programmes

Beyond the awards, the Foundation runs the 5 Under 35 celebration of emerging writers, BookUp reading programmes for underserved youth, and public events connecting readers with National Book Award authors — reflecting its mission "to celebrate the best literature in America, expand its audience, and ensure that books have a prominent place in American culture."
