Shamash

Shamash (Akkadian: Šamaš; Sumerian: Utu) was the ancient Mesopotamian god of the sun, justice, morality, and truth. As the sun crosses the sky daily, Shamash was believed to see everything that happens in the world, making him the divine judge who oversees both human and divine conduct.

Mythology and cult

Utu was the son of the moon god Nanna and the goddess Ningal, and twin brother of Inanna (Ishtar). His main temples, both called É-babbar ("Shining House"), were in Sippar and Larsa. Shamash is depicted on the upper part of the stele of Hammurabi's law code, handing the symbols of royal authority — the rod and ring — to the Babylonian king, an image that expresses the idea that the king's laws derive their legitimacy from the god of justice.

Roles

Shamash was invoked in incantations against evil, consulted in divination (especially extispicy and omens from the sun), and appealed to in prayers and hymns by the wronged. In the Epic of Gilgamesh he aids the hero against the monster Humbaba. His symbol is the solar disk, often shown with wavy rays or a saw-tooth emblem.
