Critical discourse analysis

Critical discourse analysis (CDA) is an interdisciplinary approach to the study of discourse that views language as a form of social practice and examines how power, ideology, and inequality are produced and reproduced in text and talk. Prominent figures include Norman Fairclough, Ruth Wodak, and Teun A. van Dijk.

Approach

CDA holds that discourse — especially in media, politics, and institutions — both reflects and shapes social structures. Fairclough's framework analyses texts at three levels: the text itself, the discursive practices of production and consumption, and the wider social practice. Unlike purely descriptive discourse analysis, CDA is explicitly committed to exposing hidden power relations and supporting social change.

Criticism

Critics argue that CDA can be politically biased, interpreting texts to confirm the analyst's prior commitments, and that it sometimes lacks methodological rigour compared with corpus-based approaches.
