Minié ball

The Minié ball (or Minie ball) is a type of muzzle-loading spin-stabilised bullet named after its inventor, French army officer Claude-Étienne Minié. It came to prominence during the Crimean War and especially the American Civil War, where it caused the great majority of battlefield wounds.

Design

The Minié ball is a conical-cylindrical soft lead bullet with a hollow, skirted base. It is slightly smaller than the rifle bore, so it drops quickly down the barrel when loading; on firing, expanding gases force the skirt outward into the rifling grooves, sealing the bore and imparting spin. This combined the rapid loading of a smoothbore musket with the accuracy of a rifle, extending effective aimed range to several hundred yards.

Impact

Because generals continued to use close-order Napoleonic tactics, the long-range accuracy of rifled muskets firing Minié balls made Civil War battles exceptionally bloody. The heavy soft-lead bullet also shattered bone on impact, frequently making amputation necessary.
