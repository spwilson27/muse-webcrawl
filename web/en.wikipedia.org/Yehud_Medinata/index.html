Yehud Medinata (Aramaic for "the province of Judah") was an administrative province of the Achaemenid Persian Empire in the region of the former Kingdom of Judah, established after Cyrus the Great's conquest of Babylon in 539 BCE and lasting until Alexander the Great's conquests in 332 BCE.

A small silver Yehud coin of the Persian period, inscribed with the province's name in Aramaic.

History

Following the Babylonian exile, Cyrus's decree permitted Jewish exiles to return and rebuild the Temple in Jerusalem, completed around 516/515 BCE. Under governors such as Zerubbabel and, in the fifth century, Nehemiah — who rebuilt Jerusalem's walls — and the scribe Ezra, the province developed a temple-centered administration. Persian-period Yehud was small and sparsely populated compared with the earlier kingdom.

Administration and coinage

The province was governed by officials bearing the title peḥah, subordinate to the satrap of Eber-Nari. Yehud minted small silver coins inscribed "YHD" (Yehud), among the earliest Jewish coinage, and is known archaeologically from stamp impressions on jar handles.
