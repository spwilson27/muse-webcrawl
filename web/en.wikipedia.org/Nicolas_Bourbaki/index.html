Nicolas Bourbaki

Nicolas Bourbaki is the collective pseudonym of a group of mathematicians, mostly French, who since the 1930s have written a series of influential textbooks presenting modern mathematics in a rigorous, axiomatic and highly abstract style. Their multi-volume Éléments de mathématique reshaped the presentation of algebra, topology and analysis.

Founding and members

The group was founded in 1934–35 by young mathematicians including André Weil, Henri Cartan, Claude Chevalley, Jean Dieudonné and Jean Delsarte, dissatisfied with existing textbooks. Membership is secret and rotating, with members traditionally retiring at 50. The fictional Bourbaki was given a biography and even a membership application to learned societies, producing famous jokes and controversies.

Influence

Bourbaki emphasised mathematical structures — groups, rings, topological spaces — and precise definitions. Their approach influenced university curricula worldwide and the "New Math" school reforms, though critics found the style austere and detached from applications and intuition.
