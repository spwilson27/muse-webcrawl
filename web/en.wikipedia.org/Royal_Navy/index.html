Royal Navy

The Royal Navy (RN) is the naval warfare force of the United Kingdom, British Overseas Territories and Crown Dependencies. It was established formally as the Navy Royal in the 16th century under Henry VIII, though English naval forces date back much further. From the mid-18th century until the Second World War, it was the world's largest and most powerful navy, playing a central role in establishing and sustaining the British Empire.

History

Victories against the Spanish Armada (1588), in the Anglo-Dutch Wars, and at Trafalgar (1805) under Admiral Nelson secured British command of the seas for over a century. The Royal Navy imposed the blockade of Germany in the First World War and fought the Battle of the Atlantic in the Second.

Modern navy

Today the Royal Navy operates two Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers, ballistic missile submarines carrying the UK's nuclear deterrent (Trident), Astute-class attack submarines, Type 45 destroyers and Type 23 frigates (being replaced by Type 26 and Type 31). It works jointly with the Royal Marines, the Fleet Air Arm, and the Royal Fleet Auxiliary, and is classed as a blue-water navy with global reach.
