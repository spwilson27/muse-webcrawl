Enhanced-definition television

Enhanced-definition television, or extended-definition television (EDTV), is a marketing term for certain television formats and devices that provide a picture superior to standard-definition television (SDTV) but not as detailed as high-definition television (HDTV). The term refers principally to formats that offer progressive scanning at standard-definition resolution.

Formats

The common EDTV formats are 480p (852×480 or 720×480 progressive), used in NTSC regions, and 576p (720×576 progressive), used in PAL and SECAM regions. Unlike interlaced 480i or 576i broadcasts, progressive signals display each frame in full, reducing flicker and making the formats well suited to DVD players, games consoles and plasma displays of the early 2000s marketed as "EDTV-ready" or "EDTV-compatible."

Broadcasting

In the United States, the Fox network broadcast in 480p widescreen — marketed as "Fox Widescreen" — before moving to 720p HDTV, and some affiliates of The WB and UPN also aired EDTV signals. EDTV signals use the same ATSC and DVB infrastructure as SDTV and HDTV broadcasts and can carry widescreen 16:9 pictures with Dolby Digital audio.

Relation to other formats

EDTV sits between SDTV (480i/576i) and HDTV (720p, 1080i, 1080p). As HDTV displays became inexpensive, the EDTV label largely disappeared from consumer marketing, though 480p and 576p remain widely used for streaming and secondary channels.
