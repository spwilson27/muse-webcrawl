Advocate

Occupation typeProfession (law)
Activity sectorsCourts, legal practice
DescriptionA legal practitioner who pleads cases in court on behalf of clients
Related rolesBarrister, solicitor, counsel, attorney

An advocate is a professional in the field of law. The term derives from the Latin advocatus, "one called to aid". Different countries' legal systems use the term with somewhat different meanings. In many civil-law and mixed jurisdictions, an advocate is the standard title for a lawyer qualified to represent clients in court — for example in India, Pakistan, and much of continental Europe (cf. French avocat, German Rechtsanwalt, Italian avvocato, Dutch advocaat).

In Scotland, advocates form a distinct branch of the legal profession, organised in the Faculty of Advocates. Like English barristers, they are specialists in courtroom pleading and legal opinions, are self-employed, work from shared chambers (stables), and are instructed — usually by solicitors rather than directly by clients — to argue cases, especially in the higher courts (the Court of Session and High Court of Justiciary). Senior Scottish advocates are appointed King's Counsel. The head of the Faculty is the Dean of Faculty.

Other uses

In South Africa, advocates similarly constitute the "Bar" branch of a divided profession. More generally, the word is used for anyone who publicly supports or recommends a cause — as in "consumer advocate" or "devil's advocate" — and in historical usage an "advocate" was the lay protector of a church or abbey (German Vogt).
