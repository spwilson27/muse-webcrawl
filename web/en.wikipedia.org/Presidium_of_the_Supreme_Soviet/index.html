Presidium of the Supreme Soviet

The Presidium of the Supreme Soviet (Russian: Президиум Верховного Совета) was the standing body of the Supreme Soviet of the Soviet Union, empowered to act in its name between its brief sessions. Established under the 1936 Constitution of the USSR, it exercised many functions of the head of state, and its chairman was often described abroad as the "President of the USSR," although real power rested with the leadership of the Communist Party.

Functions

The Presidium issued decrees (ukases) with the force of law, interpreted existing laws, ratified and denounced international treaties, received ambassadors, conferred state awards and military ranks, granted pardons, declared states of war or mobilization when the Supreme Soviet was not in session, and convoked the Supreme Soviet's sessions. Its decrees technically required ratification at the next session.

Chairmen

The first chairman was Mikhail Kalinin (1938–1946). Later chairmen included Nikolay Shvernik, Kliment Voroshilov, Leonid Brezhnev (twice), Anastas Mikoyan, Nikolai Podgorny, Vasily Kuznetsov (acting), Yuri Andropov, Konstantin Chernenko, Andrei Gromyko, and finally Mikhail Gorbachev (1988–1989), before the office was reorganized by the creation of an executive presidency in 1990.

Union republics

Each union republic also had its own Supreme Soviet and Presidium, with parallel structure and functions, headed by a chairman of the republican Presidium.
