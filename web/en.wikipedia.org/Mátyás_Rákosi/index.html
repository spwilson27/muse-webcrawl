Born: 9 March 1892, Ada, Kingdom of Hungary, Austria-Hungary
Died: 5 February 1971 (aged 78), Gorky, Soviet Union
Office: General Secretary of the Hungarian Working People's Party (1948–1956); Chairman of the Council of Ministers (1952–1953)

Mátyás Rákosi (9 March 1892 – 5 February 1971) was a Hungarian communist politician who led Hungary's Stalinist regime after the Second World War. As general secretary of the ruling Hungarian Working People's Party he headed a dictatorship marked by show trials, a vast secret police (the ÁVH), forced industrialisation and collectivisation, and an intense cult of personality as "Stalin's best pupil".

Rise to power

Rákosi had served in Béla Kun's short-lived Hungarian Soviet Republic of 1919, spent the interwar years as a Comintern official and sixteen years in Horthy's prisons before being exchanged to the Soviet Union in 1940. Returning with the Red Army in 1945, he eliminated rival parties by what he called "salami tactics" — slicing away the opposition piece by piece — and by 1949 Hungary was a one-party people's republic. The show trial of Cardinal Mindszenty (1949) and of his own comrade László Rajk (1949) symbolised the terror, in which hundreds were executed and tens of thousands imprisoned or interned.

Fall

After Stalin's death in 1953, Moscow forced Rákosi to yield the premiership to Imre Nagy while he retained the party leadership. Khrushchev's secret speech in 1956 destroyed his authority; he was removed in July 1956 and exiled to the Soviet Union. His successors' attempt to manage de-Stalinisation helped ignite the Hungarian Revolution of October 1956. Rákosi died in exile in Gorky in 1971.
