Boy Scouts of America

The Boy Scouts of America (BSA), officially rebranded as Scouting America in 2025, is one of the largest youth organizations in the United States. Founded on February 8, 1910, by William D. Boyce, it was inspired by Robert Baden-Powell's Scouting movement in Britain.

Its programs include Cub Scouts for younger children, Scouts BSA for ages 11–17, Venturing, Sea Scouts, and Exploring. Scouts progress through ranks from Scout to Eagle Scout, the highest achievement, earning merit badges and performing community service. The Scout Oath and Law emphasize character, citizenship, and fitness.

Membership policies changed substantially in the 2010s: openly gay youth were admitted from 2014, gay adult leaders from 2015, and girls were welcomed into Cub Scouts and Scouts BSA from 2018–2019. The organization filed for bankruptcy in 2020 amid thousands of sexual abuse claims, emerging in 2023 with a settlement trust of over $2.4 billion.
