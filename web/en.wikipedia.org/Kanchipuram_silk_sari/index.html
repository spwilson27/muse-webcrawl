Kanchipuram silk sari

The Kanchipuram silk sari, also known as the Kanjivaram sari, is a type of silk sari traditionally handwoven in Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu, India. Regarded as among the finest and most durable of Indian saris, it is distinguished by its heavy pure mulberry silk, contrasting borders, and zari (gold- and silver-wrapped thread) brocade depicting temple architecture, checks, stripes, and floral or mythological motifs.

Weaving

Weaving a Kanjivaram traditionally involves three shuttles: the weaver sits on one side and two assistants on the other, and the border and body are often woven in contrasting colours and joined by a strong interlocking weave called korvai; the decorative end piece (pallu) is joined by the petni technique. Pure zari is made of silk thread wrapped with silver wire and gold-dipped. A genuine sari may take weeks to complete, and its authenticity can be tested — among other ways — by burning a few threads of the zari.

Status and use

Kanchipuram saris are worn at weddings and festivals throughout South India and are treasured as heirlooms. The craft supports thousands of weaver households in and around Kanchipuram, many organised through cooperative societies, and the "Kancheepuram Silk" name has received Geographical Indication protection in India. Challenges include rising silk and gold prices and competition from powerloom imitations.
