Kurukshetra War

The great battle of the Mahābhārata, fought between the Pandavas and the Kauravas.

The Kurukshetra War, also called the Mahabharata War, is the central conflict of the Hindu epic Mahābhārata. It is a dynastic struggle between two groups of cousins, the Kauravas and the Pandavas, for the throne of Hastinapura, fought on the battlefield of Kurukshetra (in present-day Haryana, India). According to the epic, the war lasted eighteen days and claimed the lives of millions of warriors.

Background

The conflict grew from the rivalry between the blind king Dhritarashtra's sons, the hundred Kauravas led by Duryodhana, and the five Pandava brothers led by Yudhishthira. After the Pandavas were cheated of their kingdom in a rigged dice game and completed thirteen years of exile, Duryodhana refused to return even five villages. Peace missions, including Krishna's embassy, failed, and both sides gathered vast allied armies at Kurukshetra.

The battle

The Pandava army of seven akshauhinis faced eleven Kaurava divisions. The Kaurava command passed successively to Bhishma, Drona, Karna, and Shalya, all of whom were slain. Key episodes include Arjuna's crisis of conscience on the first day — answered by Krishna's discourse, the Bhagavad Gita — the fall of Bhishma, the killing of Abhimanyu, Arjuna's vow and the death of Jayadratha, the death of Ghatotkacha, Drona's fall after a stratagem concerning his son Ashvatthama, and the duel between Arjuna and Karna. On the eighteenth day Bhima killed Duryodhana in a mace duel, striking below the waist. That night Ashvatthama raided the Pandava camp and slaughtered the sleeping survivors, including the five sons of Draupadi.

Aftermath and significance

Of the millions said to have fought, only a handful of named warriors survived, including the five Pandavas, Krishna, Yuyutsu, Satyaki, Ashvatthama, Kripa, and Kritavarma. Yudhishthira was crowned at Hastinapura. Traditional Hindu chronology dates the war to the dawn of the Kali Yuga, around 3102 BCE, while modern scholars who seek a historical kernel generally place any such conflict in the early first millennium BCE, during the Painted Grey Ware period. The war remains a foundational narrative of dharma, duty, and the costs of conflict in Indian culture.
