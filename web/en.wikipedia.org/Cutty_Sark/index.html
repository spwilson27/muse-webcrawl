Cutty Sark

Cutty Sark is a British clipper ship, launched at Dumbarton, Scotland, in 1869. She was one of the last tea clippers to be built and one of the fastest, coming at the end of the era of sailing merchant ships before steamships took over the long-distance trades. Her name comes from the Scots term for a short chemise, worn by the witch Nannie in Robert Burns's poem "Tam o' Shanter"; her figurehead depicts Nannie holding a grey horse's tail.

Career

Cutty Sark carried tea from China to London, racing rival clippers such as Thermopylae. Her most famous voyage was a celebrated race against Thermopylae in 1872, which she lost after her rudder was damaged in a gale, though her captain's improvised repair at sea was widely admired. As steamships and the Suez Canal captured the tea trade, she switched to the Australian wool trade, in which she set record passages, once sailing from Sydney to London in 67 days.

Preservation

After service under Portuguese ownership as Ferreira, she was bought in 1922 and restored to her original name and appearance. Since 1954 she has been preserved in a dry dock at Greenwich, London, as a museum ship. A serious fire in 2007 damaged her during restoration, but she was repaired and reopened in 2012, raised three metres above the dock within a canopy structure that allows visitors to walk beneath her hull.
