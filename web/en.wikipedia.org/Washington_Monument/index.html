Washington Monument

The Washington Monument is an obelisk on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., built to commemorate George Washington, a Founding Father and the first president of the United States. Standing 169.046 metres (554 feet 7⅟ inches) tall, it was the world’s tallest structure when completed in 1884, and remains the world’s tallest predominately stone structure and the world’s tallest obelisk.

Design

Architect Robert Mills designed a tapered marble shaft rising from a Greek temple-like base, though the ornate colonnade was never built. The monument is an Egyptian-style obelisk with a small pyramidion at its apex, its tip capped with aluminium — then a precious metal — which also serves as a lightning rod. Inside, iron stairs and an elevator ascend past 193 commemorative stones donated by states, cities, countries and organisations.

Construction

The cornerstone was laid on 4 July 1848, but funding disputes and the upheaval of the Know Nothing party’s seizure of the monument society halted work in 1854 with the shaft at about 46 metres (152 feet). The unfinished stump stood through the Civil War. Congress finally appropriated funds in 1876, and the Army Corps of Engineers under Lt. Col. Thomas Lincoln Casey completed the shaft in December 1884, using marble from a slightly different quarry seam — the change of source leaves a visible colour break at the 152-foot level. The monument was dedicated on 21 February 1885 and opened to the public in 1888.

The monument today

The monument stands east of the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, aligned with the Capitol along the Mall. It sustained cracking in the 2011 Virginia earthquake and was closed for repairs until 2014; further elevator work closed it until 2019. Visitors ride the elevator to an observation level near the top for views over the capital.
