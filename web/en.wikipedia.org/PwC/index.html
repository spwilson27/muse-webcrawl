PwC

PwC (legally PricewaterhouseCoopers) is a British multinational professional services network and one of the "Big Four" accounting firms, alongside Deloitte, EY, and KPMG. PwC firms provide assurance (audit), tax, and advisory (consulting and deals) services in more than 150 countries, employing over 370,000 people and reporting global revenues of roughly $55 billion in recent fiscal years. The network is coordinated by PricewaterhouseCoopers International Limited, based in London, though each member firm is a separate legal entity.

History

PwC was created in 1998 by the merger of Price Waterhouse and Coopers & Lybrand, both with roots in nineteenth-century London accountancy: Samuel Lowell Price founded his practice in 1849, while William Cooper began in 1854. Price Waterhouse became known for auditing major industrial and entertainment clients—including administering the Academy Awards ballot count since 1935—while Coopers & Lybrand grew through international mergers. In 2010 the network shortened its trading name to PwC.

Services

The firm's assurance practice audits many of the world's largest companies. Its advisory arm covers strategy (including the Strategy& unit), mergers and acquisitions, restructuring, risk, and technology consulting; the tax practice advises on compliance, transfer pricing, and international structuring. PwC publishes widely cited research such as its Global CEO Survey.

Controversies

PwC has faced regulatory sanctions and litigation in several countries, including failures connected to the collapses of Satyam Computer Services in India, Banco Espírito Santo in Portugal, and the British retailer BHS, and a major tax-leak scandal in Australia in 2023 involving misuse of confidential government information, which led to partner departures, divestment of its government consulting business, and governance reforms.
