The Hampton Court Maze is a hedge maze planted at Hampton Court Palace in the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames, England. It is the oldest surviving hedge maze in the United Kingdom and one of the most famous mazes in the world.

History

The maze was planted around 1690–1695 by royal gardeners George London and Henry Wise for William III and Mary II, as part of the Wilderness garden adjoining the palace. It was originally planted in hornbeam; the current hedges are yew, replanted over the centuries after the original plantings wore out. The maze covers roughly a third of an acre and contains about half a mile of paths in a trapezoidal plot.

Design

Despite its fame, the maze is a multicursal puzzle maze of modest complexity, and a visitor following the right-hand rule can navigate it reliably. On average it takes about twenty minutes to reach the centre. The maze has featured in literature, most famously in Jerome K. Jerome's comic novel Three Men in a Boat (1889), in which Harris leads a party hopelessly astray inside it.

Cultural references

The maze has appeared in numerous works of fiction and is referenced in discussions of maze design as an archetype of the early modern pleasure-garden maze. A full-size replica sensory maze for children exists in the palace's Magic Garden play area.
