The Goodyear Tire and Rubber Company is an American multinational tire manufacturing company founded in 1898 by Frank Seiberling and based in Akron, Ohio. Goodyear manufactures tires for automobiles, commercial trucks, light trucks, motorcycles, SUVs, race cars, airplanes, farm equipment, and heavy earth-mover machinery. It also produced woven belts, hoses, and other rubber products.

History

The company was named after Charles Goodyear, the inventor of vulcanized rubber. Early Goodyear tires became popular because they were easily detachable and required little maintenance. The company grew with the automobile industry and became one of the largest tire makers in the world.

The Wingfoot and the blimp

Goodyear's Wingfoot logo, based on the winged foot of the Greek god Hermes, became one of the most recognizable trademarks in America. The company is also famous for its fleet of Goodyear Blimps, which have provided aerial coverage of sporting events since the 1920s.

Modern era

In 2021 Goodyear acquired Cooper Tire & Rubber Company in a deal valued at about $2.8 billion. The company remains one of the world's largest tire manufacturers alongside Michelin and Bridgestone.
