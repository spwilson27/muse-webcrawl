Valvular heart disease

Valvular heart disease is any cardiovascular disease process involving one or more of the four valves of the heart: the aortic and mitral valves on the left side, and the pulmonary and tricuspid valves on the right. Valve disorders generally take two forms: stenosis, a narrowing that obstructs blood flow, and regurgitation (insufficiency), in which a valve leaks and allows blood to flow backwards.

Types

Aortic stenosis, usually caused by age-related calcification, is the most common valve lesion in developed countries. Mitral regurgitation may result from degenerative disease or mitral valve prolapse. Rheumatic heart disease, following untreated streptococcal infection, remains a major cause of valve damage in the developing world. Endocarditis — infection of the valve leaflets — is another important cause.

Diagnosis and symptoms

Symptoms include breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations, fatigue, and fainting. Clinicians may detect a heart murmur on auscultation, but echocardiography is the principal diagnostic tool, allowing assessment of valve anatomy and the severity of dysfunction.

Treatment

Mild disease is monitored; severe disease typically requires surgery, either valve repair or replacement with a mechanical or bioprosthetic valve. Catheter-based procedures such as transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) have become an alternative for patients at high surgical risk.
