RNA polymerase

RNA polymerase
Enzyme
Catalyses transcription of RNA from a DNA template

In molecular biology, RNA polymerase (abbreviated RNAP or RNApol), or more specifically DNA-directed/dependent RNA polymerase (DdRP), is an enzyme that catalyzes the chemical reactions that synthesize RNA from a DNA template. Using the enzyme helicase, RNAP locally opens the double-stranded DNA so that one strand of the exposed nucleotides acts as a template for the synthesis of RNA, a process called transcription.

Structure

The 2006 Nobel Prize in Chemistry was awarded to Roger D. Kornberg for creating detailed molecular images of RNA polymerase during various stages of the transcription process. In most prokaryotes, a single RNA polymerase species transcribes all types of RNA. The bacterial core enzyme consists of five subunits (α, α, β, β′ and ω); a sigma (σ) factor joins to form the holoenzyme needed to initiate transcription at promoters.

Eukaryotic polymerases

Eukaryotes have multiple types of nuclear RNA polymerase. RNA polymerase I transcribes most ribosomal RNA genes; RNA polymerase II transcribes messenger RNA and most small nuclear RNA; RNA polymerase III transcribes transfer RNA, 5S rRNA and other small RNAs. Plants additionally have polymerases IV and V involved in gene silencing.

Mechanism

Transcription proceeds through initiation, elongation and termination. RNAP synthesizes RNA in the 5′ to 3′ direction, reading the template DNA 3′ to 5′, and includes proofreading mechanisms that remove misincorporated nucleotides. Unlike DNA polymerase, RNAP can start a chain without a primer. See also RNA splicing, a later step in gene expression.
