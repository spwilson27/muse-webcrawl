Block universe

The block universe theory of time, also known as eternalism, is a philosophical and physical view according to which all events in past, present and future are equally real. Time is treated as another dimension, like the three dimensions of space, so that the universe is a four-dimensional block of spacetime in which nothing fundamentally “flows”; what we call the passage of time is a feature of conscious experience within the block.

Relation to relativity

Eternalism draws strong support from the theory of special relativity. Because simultaneity is relative — different observers slice spacetime into different sets of simultaneous events — there is no frame-independent, universal “now”. Arguments such as the Rietdijk–Putnam argument conclude that if events are real for one observer, consistency pushes toward all events, past and future, being equally real. Hermann Minkowski's formulation of spacetime and Albert Einstein's own remarks are often cited in this connection.

Contrast with other views

The block universe contrasts with presentism, which holds that only the present exists, and with the growing block universe theory, in which the past and present exist but the future does not yet. Debates concern free will, the apparent direction of time, and whether the block picture can accommodate quantum mechanics.
