The Old Town (Polish: Stare Miasto) in Kraków, Poland, is the historic central district of the city, laid out around the Main Market Square (Rynek Główny), the largest medieval town square in Europe. It was one of the first sites chosen for the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1978.

History

Kraków was the capital of the Kingdom of Poland until 1596. After the Mongol invasion of 1241 destroyed the earlier settlement, the city was rebuilt in 1257 on a regular grid plan that survives today. The defensive walls were later replaced by the Planty, a ring of gardens encircling the Old Town.

Landmarks

  • St Mary's Basilica, with its hourly hejnał bugle call
  • The Cloth Hall (Sukiennice)
  • Town Hall Tower and the Adam Mickiewicz Monument
  • St Florian's Gate and the Barbican
  • Wawel Castle and Cathedral at the southern edge
