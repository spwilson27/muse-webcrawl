Learning with errors

Learning with errors (LWE)
Field: Lattice-based cryptography
Introduced: Oded Regev, 2005
Foundation for: Kyber, Dilithium (post-quantum standards)

Learning with errors (LWE) is a mathematical problem central to lattice-based cryptography, introduced by Oded Regev in 2005. Given samples of the form (a, ⟨a, s⟩ + e mod q), where s is a secret vector and e is a small random error, the task is to recover s; the noise makes the problem computationally hard even though the underlying system would be easy without errors.

Hardness

Regev proved a worst-case to average-case reduction: solving LWE on average is at least as hard as approximating certain worst-case lattice problems, and the problem is believed resistant to quantum algorithms. Variants include Ring-LWE and Module-LWE, which add algebraic structure for efficiency.

Applications

LWE underlies fully homomorphic encryption and the post-quantum cryptographic standards selected by NIST, including the Kyber key-encapsulation mechanism and the Dilithium signature scheme.
