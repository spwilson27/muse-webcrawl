The Telangana movement was the decades-long campaign for the creation of a separate state of Telangana from the Telugu-speaking region of the former princely state of Hyderabad, which had been merged into Andhra Pradesh in 1956. The movement culminated in 2014, when Telangana became the 29th state of India.

Background

When India's states were reorganised on linguistic lines in 1956, the Telangana region was merged with the Andhra region despite the States Reorganisation Commission's reservations, under a "Gentlemen's Agreement" promising safeguards for Telangana in government jobs, education, and development spending. Grievances that these safeguards were violated — over irrigation water, jobs, and funds — fuelled recurring demands for separation.

The 1969 agitation and later phases

In 1969 a mass agitation, led largely by students and employees, demanded a separate Telangana; more than 300 people died in police firing, and the movement subsided after political concessions and the countervailing Jai Andhra movement of 1972. The demand revived strongly after 2001, when K. Chandrashekar Rao founded the Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS) solely to pursue statehood. A fast-unto-death by Rao in late 2009 prompted the Union government to announce the process of forming Telangana, and years of protests, strikes (including the 42-day Sakala Janula Samme general strike of 2011), and political negotiation followed.

Statehood

Parliament passed the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act in February 2014, and Telangana formally came into being on 2 June 2014 with Hyderabad as the shared capital for up to ten years. K. Chandrashekar Rao became its first chief minister.
