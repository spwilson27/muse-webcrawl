Edwin of Northumbria
Reignc. 616 – 633
Bornc. 586, Deira
Died12 October 633, Battle of Hatfield Chase

Edwin (Old English: Ēadwine; c. 586 – 12 October 633), also known as Saint Edwin, was King of Deira and Bernicia — together known as Northumbria — from about 616 until his death. He converted to Christianity and was baptised in 627.

Reign

Son of Ælle of Deira, Edwin spent years in exile before defeating and killing his predecessor Æthelfrith at the Battle of the River Idle (616) with the support of Rædwald of East Anglia. Bede presents him as the most powerful English king of his day, exercising overlordship over most of Britain south of the Forth. He married Æthelburh of Kent, whose chaplain Paulinus accompanied her north; after lengthy deliberation Edwin accepted baptism at York at Easter 627, and Paulinus became the first Bishop of York.

Death and veneration

Edwin was killed at the Battle of Hatfield Chase in 633 by an alliance of Cadwallon of Gwynedd and Penda of Mercia. He was venerated as a saint and martyr, with his feast day on 12 October.
