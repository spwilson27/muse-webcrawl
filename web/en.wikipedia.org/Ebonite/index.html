Ebonite

Ebonite is a brand name for a material known generically as hard rubber, produced by vulcanising natural rubber with a high proportion of sulfur (typically 25–80 percent) for prolonged periods. The result is a hard, durable, usually black material that can be machined and polished to a glossy finish, resembling ebony wood — hence the name.

Properties and uses

Ebonite is an excellent electrical insulator and is resistant to many chemicals, which made it popular for early electrical components, battery cases, and chemical plant equipment. It has been widely used for bowling balls, fountain pens, tobacco pipe mouthpieces, and the mouthpieces of clarinets and saxophones, which are still often made of it today.

History

The process was developed in the 1840s–1850s, notably by Charles Goodyear in the United States, who patented vulcanite, and by Thomas Hancock in Britain. Ebonite was one of the earliest industrial plastics-like materials and remained important until largely replaced by modern thermoplastics such as Bakelite and later polymers.
