Addition of angular momenta

Addition of angular momenta is a central procedure in quantum mechanics for combining two (or more) angular momentum operators — for example the spin and orbital angular momentum of an electron, or the spins of two particles — into a total angular momentum. Because the individual angular momenta do not commute with each other in the combined basis, the states of the composite system must be re-expressed in a coupled basis of eigenstates of the total angular momentum operators J² and Jz.

Triangle rule

Given angular momentum quantum numbers j1 and j2, the total quantum number j can take values from |j1 − j2| to j1 + j2 in integer steps, and the total magnetic quantum number is simply m = m1 + m2. For example, coupling two spin-½ particles yields a spin-1 triplet and a spin-0 singlet; coupling spin ½ with orbital angular momentum l gives j = l ± ½.

Clebsch–Gordan coefficients

The overlap coefficients between the uncoupled basis states |j1 m1>|j2 m2> and the coupled basis states |j m> are the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. They are tabulated for common cases and are closely related to the Wigner 3-j symbols; coupling three angular momenta leads to the Wigner 6-j symbols and Racah coefficients.

Applications

The procedure underlies the term symbols of atomic spectroscopy (LS coupling and jj coupling), the classification of particle states in nuclear and particle physics, and selection rules for radiative transitions.
