Sir Michael Philip Jagger (born 26 July 1943) is an English singer and songwriter, the frontman and one of the founder members of the rock band the Rolling Stones. With Keith Richards, his songwriting partnership is one of the most successful in history, and his energetic stage performances and distinctive voice have made him one of the most popular and influential frontmen in the history of rock music.

Life and career

Jagger was born in Dartford, Kent, and was studying at the London School of Economics when he reconnected with childhood friend Keith Richards in 1961; the two formed the Rolling Stones with Brian Jones in 1962. Managed by Andrew Loog Oldham, the Stones cultivated a rebellious image that contrasted with the Beatles, and the Jagger–Richards team wrote classics including "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction", "Paint It Black", "Jumpin' Jack Flash", "Sympathy for the Devil", "Gimme Shelter" and "Brown Sugar".

In parallel, Jagger pursued film acting — notably Performance (1970) and Ned Kelly (1970) — and later film production through Jagged Films. His solo albums include She's the Boss (1985) and Wandering Spirit (1993); in 2011 he formed the supergroup SuperHeavy with Dave Stewart, Joss Stone, Damian Marley and A. R. Rahman.

Honours and personal life

Jagger was knighted in 2003 for services to popular music. He has eight children with five women and has been in a relationship with ballet dancer Melanie Hamrick since 2014. In 2019 he underwent successful heart valve replacement surgery, after which the Stones resumed touring; the band released the album Hackney Diamonds in 2023, their first studio album of original material in 18 years.
