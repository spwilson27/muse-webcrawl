Djibouti, officially the Republic of Djibouti, is a country in the Horn of Africa, bordered by Somalia to the south, Ethiopia to the southwest, Eritrea to the north, and the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden to the east. It lies at the Bab el-Mandeb strait, one of the world's busiest shipping lanes, and hosts military bases of several powers, including the only permanent United States base in Africa (Camp Lemonnier) and China's first overseas naval base, opened in 2017.

Geography

Djibouti is mostly stony desert with scattered plateaus and highlands. Lake Assal, at 155 metres below sea level, is the lowest point in Africa and one of the saltiest bodies of water in the world. The climate is hot and arid; the capital, Djibouti City, is among the warmest cities on Earth.

History

The region was part of the medieval sultanates of Ifat and Adal and a centre of trade between the Ethiopian highlands and the Red Sea. France established the colony of French Somaliland in the late nineteenth century, later renamed the French Territory of the Afars and the Issas. A referendum in 1977 led to independence, with Hassan Gouled Aptidon as first president. A civil war with the Afar-led FRUD movement in the 1990s ended in a peace accord. Ismail Omar Guelleh has been president since 1999.

Economy

The economy rests heavily on the Port of Djibouti, which handles the great majority of Ethiopia's imports and exports, and on services to foreign militaries. The population is divided mainly between the Somali Issa and the Afar ethnic groups; Somali and Afar are the most widely spoken languages alongside official French and Arabic.
