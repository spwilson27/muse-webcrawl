Doll

A doll is a model typically of a human or humanoid character, often used as a toy for children. Dolls have also been used in traditional religious rituals throughout the world. Traditional dolls made of materials such as clay and wood are found in the Americas, Asia, Africa and Europe. The earliest documented dolls go back to the ancient civilisations of Egypt, Greece and Rome.

History

Archaeologists have found Egyptian paddle dolls dating to roughly 2000 BCE, and Greek dolls with articulated limbs from the 5th century BCE. In Europe, doll-making centres grew in Germany (notably the Thuringian toy towns) and France from the 15th century; bisque (porcelain) dolls became fashionable in the 1860s–1900s, with French firms such as Jumeau and Bru producing highly prized examples. The 20th century brought composition, hard plastic and vinyl dolls; Mattel's Barbie (1959) transformed the fashion-doll market, and action figures for boys followed in the 1960s.

Types

  • Rag dolls of cloth, among the oldest children's toys.
  • Fashion dolls such as Barbie and Bratz.
  • Paper dolls, two-dimensional figures with cut-out clothing.
  • Nesting dolls (Russian matryoshka, from 1890).
  • Ceremonial and ritual figures, including Hopi katsina dolls and Japanese hina dolls displayed at Hinamatsuri.
  • Collector and art dolls, and modern reborn dolls.

Cultural significance

Dolls serve in play as tools for socialisation and storytelling; anthropologists study them as material culture. Debates continue over body image and gender roles in doll design, prompting more diverse product lines in recent decades.
