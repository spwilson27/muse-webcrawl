Day of Arafah

The Day of Arafah (Arabic: يوم عرفة, Yawm 'Arafah) is an Islamic holy day falling on the 9th day of Dhu al-Hijja, the twelfth and final month of the Islamic calendar. It is the second day of the Hajj pilgrimage and the day preceding Eid al-Adha. For pilgrims, standing in prayer on the plain of Arafat — the wuquf — is the essential rite of the Hajj.

The standing at Arafat

On this day, Hajj pilgrims travel from Mina to the plain of Arafat, about 20 kilometres east of Mecca, where they spend the afternoon in prayer and supplication near the Mount of Mercy (Jabal al-Rahmah). The Prophet Muhammad delivered his Farewell Sermon on this plain during his final pilgrimage. Pilgrims who miss the standing at Arafat are considered to have missed the Hajj itself.

Significance and observance

Muslims believe that on the Day of Arafah God forgives the sins of those who stand at Arafat and frees more people from the Fire than on any other day. Muslims not performing Hajj are encouraged to fast on this day; according to a well-known hadith, fasting it expiates the sins of the previous year and the coming year. At sunset pilgrims proceed to Muzdalifah, where they spend the night before the following day's rituals in Mina.
