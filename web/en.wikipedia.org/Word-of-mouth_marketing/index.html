Word-of-mouth marketing

Word-of-mouth marketing (WOMM) is the practice of encouraging consumers to pass information about a product, service or brand to other consumers. It relies on unpaid or incentivised recommendations — conversations, reviews, referrals and social media shares — rather than conventional advertising, and is consistently rated in surveys as one of the most trusted sources of product information.

Forms

Organic word of mouth arises naturally from product quality and customer experience. Amplified word of mouth is deliberately stimulated by campaigns: referral programmes offering rewards, seeding products with influential users, buzz marketing designed to generate talk, and viral content engineered for sharing online. Electronic word of mouth (eWOM) — reviews on retail and travel sites, ratings and social posts — extends the audience from an individual's acquaintances to thousands of strangers, and persists in searchable form.

Effectiveness and risks

Because recommendations come from peers rather than sellers, they carry credibility, but the same channel spreads complaints quickly, and negative word of mouth is often more influential than positive. Ethical guidelines require disclosure of paid or incentivised endorsements, and regulators in several countries treat undisclosed astroturfing — fake grassroots praise — as deceptive advertising. Measurement typically tracks referral rates, review scores and net promoter scores.
