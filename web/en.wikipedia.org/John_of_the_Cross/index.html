John of the Cross

John of the Cross (Spanish: Juan de la Cruz; 24 June 1542 – 14 December 1591) was a Spanish Catholic priest, mystic, and Carmelite friar of converso origin. He is a major figure of the Counter-Reformation in Spain, and he is one of the thirty-seven Doctors of the Church. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest poets in the Spanish language, and his writings on the soul's journey to union with God are classics of Western spirituality and mysticism.

Life

Born Juan de Yepes y Álvarez in Fontiveros, he joined the Carmelite Order and was ordained in 1567. Meeting Teresa of Ávila, he joined her reform of the order, founding the first house of Discalced Carmelite friars in 1568. Opposition from the unreformed Carmelites led to his imprisonment in Toledo in 1577–78, where, in a dark cell, he composed much of his poetry before escaping.

Writings

His major prose works — Ascent of Mount Carmel, Dark Night of the Soul, The Spiritual Canticle, and The Living Flame of Love — are commentaries on his poems. The phrase "dark night of the soul," from his description of the soul's purification on its way to union with God, has entered common usage. Canonized in 1726, he was declared a Doctor of the Church in 1926.
