Longest increasing subsequence

In computer science, the longest increasing subsequence problem finds a subsequence of a given sequence in which the elements are in sorted order, lowest to highest, and the subsequence is as long as possible. The subsequence is not necessarily contiguous.

Algorithms

A dynamic programming solution runs in O(n²) time, but a more efficient algorithm using binary search on auxiliary “pile” arrays — related to patience sorting — solves it in O(n log n) time. The patience sorting connection, developed by C. L. Mallows and others, also links the problem to the Robinson–Schensted correspondence and Young tableaux.

Related theory

The Erdős–Szekeres theorem guarantees that any sequence of (r−1)(s−1)+1 distinct numbers contains an increasing subsequence of length r or a decreasing subsequence of length s. For random permutations, the expected length of the longest increasing subsequence grows as about 2√n, a result associated with Ulam's problem and the work of Vershik, Kerov, Logan and Shepp.
