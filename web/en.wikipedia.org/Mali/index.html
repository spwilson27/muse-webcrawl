Mali, officially the Republic of Mali, is a landlocked country in West Africa. It is the eighth-largest country in Africa, with an area of over 1,240,000 square kilometres, most of it in the Sahara Desert. Mali borders Algeria to the north, Niger to the east, Burkina Faso and Ivory Coast to the south, Guinea to the southwest, and Senegal and Mauritania to the west. Its capital and largest city is Bamako, on the River Niger.

The country has a rich precolonial history as the heartland of a succession of great Sudanic empires — Ghana, the Mali Empire under Sundiata Keita and Mansa Musa, and the Songhai Empire — whose trading cities of Timbuktu and Djenné were famed centres of Islamic scholarship and trans-Saharan commerce in gold and salt. Colonised by France as French Sudan, Mali became independent in 1960 under Modibo Keïta.

Geography

Mali stretches from the Sahara in the north through the Sahel to the Sudanian savanna in the south. The Niger River arcs through the country, flooding seasonally into the Inner Niger Delta, one of Africa's great wetlands and a zone of fishing, rice cultivation, and pastoralism. Most of the population lives in the more fertile south. Natural resources include gold — Mali is one of Africa's largest gold producers — and cotton, its principal agricultural export.

History since independence

After Keïta's socialist governments, a 1968 coup brought Moussa Traoré to power; his regime fell to popular protest and a coup in 1991, leading to a democratic constitution and the presidency of Alpha Oumar Konaré, then Amadou Toumani Touré. In 2012, a military coup coincided with a Tuareg rebellion and jihadist seizure of the north, prompting the Mali War: French forces intervened in January 2013 (Operation Serval), and a UN peacekeeping mission (MINUSMA) followed. Instability persisted, with coups in 2020 and 2021 bringing a military junta under Assimi Goïta to power; MINUSMA withdrew in 2023 at the junta's demand, and Mali moved toward withdrawal from ECOWAS alongside Burkina Faso and Niger.

Culture

Mali is renowned for its music — artists such as Ali Farka Touré, Salif Keita, and Tinariwen have brought Malian blues, griot traditions, and desert rock to world audiences. The griot (jeli) tradition of hereditary praise-singers and historians remains central to Mandé society. Bamako hosts major photography and music festivals, and the manuscripts of Timbuktu are a celebrated intellectual heritage.
