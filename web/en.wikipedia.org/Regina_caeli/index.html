Regina caeli

Regina caeli (Latin for "Queen of Heaven") is one of the four seasonal Marian antiphons of the Roman Catholic Church, sung or recited during Eastertide, from Compline on Holy Saturday until Pentecost. During this period it replaces the Angelus, and it is traditionally prayed standing, as a sign of resurrection joy.

The antiphon greets Mary as Queen of Heaven.

Text

Regina caeli, laetare, alleluia.
Quia quem meruisti portare, alleluia.
Resurrexit, sicut dixit, alleluia.
Ora pro nobis Deum, alleluia.

"Queen of Heaven, rejoice, alleluia. For He whom you did merit to bear, alleluia. Has risen, as He said, alleluia. Pray for us to God, alleluia."

A versicle and collect follow: "Rejoice and be glad, O Virgin Mary, alleluia. For the Lord has truly risen, alleluia."

History and use

The antiphon's author is unknown; legend attributes it to Pope Gregory the Great, who was said to have heard angels singing its first lines during a procession in Rome in 596. It is one of the four antiphons sung at the end of Compline across the liturgical year, the others being Alma Redemptoris Mater, Ave Regina caelorum, and Salve Regina. The brief Regina Caeli address by the Pope at noon on Easter-season Sundays continues the tradition publicly in St. Peter's Square.

Musical settings

Composers across the centuries have set the antiphon, from Gregorian chant settings to polyphonic versions by composers such as Tomás Luis de Victoria, and festive settings for choir and orchestra by Mozart (K. 108 and K. 127) and Brahms (Op. 37 No. 3 for women's voices).
