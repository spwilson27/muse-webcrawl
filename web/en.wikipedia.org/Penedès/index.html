Penedès

The Penedès is a historic comarca (county) and natural region in Catalonia, Spain, lying roughly between the pre-coastal mountain range and the Mediterranean Sea, southwest of Barcelona. It is one of Spain's most important wine-producing areas, internationally renowned as the heartland of cava, the traditional-method sparkling wine.

Penedès
Country: Spain
Autonomous community: Catalonia
Provinces: Barcelona, Tarragona
Main towns: Vilafranca del Penedès, Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, Vilanova i la Geltrú

Geography

The region is a broad depression between the Catalan Pre-Coastal Range and the coast, drained by rivers such as the Foix and the Anoia. Its landscape is dominated by vineyards, interspersed with almond, olive and cereal cultivation. The Mediterranean climate, with mild winters and warm dry summers, together with varied soils, makes it well suited to viticulture.

Wine

The Penedès has a winemaking tradition stretching back to Roman times. The Denominació d'Origen Penedès covers still wines, while the separate DO Cava covers sparkling wines made mainly from the local white grape varieties Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada. Sant Sadurní d'Anoia is the centre of cava production, home to producers such as Freixenet and Codorníu. Vilafranca del Penedès hosts a wine museum and is famous for its castells (human towers) festival.

History

In the early medieval period the Penedès formed part of the frontier march of the County of Barcelona against al-Andalus. The town of Vilafranca was founded in the 12th century and became an important market centre. Today the historic comarca is divided among the modern comarques of Alt Penedès, Baix Penedès and Garraf, together with part of Anoia.
