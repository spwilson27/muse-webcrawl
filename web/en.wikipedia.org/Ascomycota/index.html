Ascomycota

Ascomycota is a phylum of the kingdom Fungi that, together with the Basidiomycota, forms the subkingdom Dikarya. Its members are commonly known as the sac fungi or ascomycetes. It is the largest phylum of fungi, with over 64,000 described species. The defining feature of this fungal group is the ascus, a microscopic sexual structure in which nonmotile spores, called ascospores, are formed.

Taxonomy and diversity

The phylum is divided into three subphyla: Pezizomycotina, which includes most macroscopic forms such as morels, truffles, and cup fungi; Saccharomycotina, the true yeasts including Saccharomyces cerevisiae; and Taphrinomycotina, a diverse basal group. Many ascomycetes are known only from asexual stages and were historically classified as Deuteromycota.

Ecology

Ascomycetes occupy a wide range of ecological niches. They form the fungal partner in most lichens, act as decomposers of plant material, and include important plant pathogens such as the agents of Dutch elm disease, chestnut blight, apple scab, and ergot of rye.

Importance to humans

Members of the phylum are used in the production of bread, beer, wine, cheese and antibiotics such as penicillin, and many are edible, notably truffles and morels. Others, such as Aspergillus and Candida species, can cause disease in humans.
