Folksonomy

A folksonomy is a classification system in which end users apply public tags to online items, typically to make those items easier for themselves or others to find later. This process is known as collaborative tagging, social classification, social indexing, and social tagging. Folksonomy was originally "the result of personal free tagging of information and objects (anything with a URL) for one's own retrieval", but the concept has since been broadened.

Origin

The term is a blend of folk and taxonomy, coined by information architect Thomas Vander Wal in 2004 in response to a question about the user-generated classifications emerging on sites such as the bookmarking service Delicious and the photo-sharing site Flickr. Unlike formal taxonomies and controlled vocabularies, a folksonomy has no hierarchy imposed in advance; structure emerges from the aggregate of individual tagging choices.

Advantages and criticisms

Supporters argue that folksonomies capture users' own language, scale cheaply, and reveal how a community understands a subject. Critics note ambiguity (synonyms, polysemy, singular/plural variants), lack of precision, and susceptibility to spam or idiosyncratic tags. Many systems blend the two approaches, combining user tags with curated vocabularies, and tag clouds are a common way of visualising the resulting distribution.
