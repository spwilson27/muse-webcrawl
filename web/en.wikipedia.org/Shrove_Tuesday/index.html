Shrove Tuesday (also known in Commonwealth countries as Pancake Day or Pancake Tuesday) is the feast day before the beginning of Lent on Ash Wednesday. Shrove Tuesday is determined by Easter, and as such, like Easter, is a moveable feast. It is observed by many Christians, including Anglicans, Lutherans, Methodists, Roman Catholics and Orthodox Christians, who "make a special point of self-examination, of considering what wrongs they need to repent, and what amendments of life or areas of spiritual growth they especially need to ask God's help in dealing with."

The word shrove is a form of the English word shrive, which means to obtain absolution for one's sins by way of confession and penance. Shrove Tuesday was named after the custom of Christians to be "shriven" before the start of Lent.

Terminology

In France and francophone regions, as well as in the United States, the day is widely known as Mardi Gras ("Fat Tuesday"), referring to the practice of eating rich, fatty foods before the fasting of Lent. In Iceland it is sprengidagur ("bursting day"); in Germany it is part of Fastnacht; in several countries it concludes the season of Shrovetide or carnival, which may begin as early as Epiphany or the week before Lent.

Traditions

In the United Kingdom, Ireland, and parts of the Commonwealth, Shrove Tuesday is popularly known as Pancake Day. Pancakes are associated with the day because they provide a way to use up rich foods such as eggs, milk, and sugar before the fasting season of Lent. Pancake races, in which participants run while flipping a pancake in a pan, are held in villages and towns; the race at Olney, Buckinghamshire, is traditionally dated to 1445. The day is also associated with "mob football" games in some English towns and with the ringing of a "pancake bell" in churches.

In Roman Catholic tradition, the faithful confess their sins and may burn the palms distributed during the previous year's Palm Sunday to make the ashes used on Ash Wednesday. The Shrove Tuesday tradition of confession and the "Shrove Tuesday confession bell" persisted in many Anglican and Lutheran parishes as well.

Dates

Shrove Tuesday falls 47 days before Easter Sunday, including the 40 days of Lent and the intervening Sundays. It can fall on any date between 3 February and 9 March, depending on the date of Easter. The following day, Ash Wednesday, begins the Lenten fast.
