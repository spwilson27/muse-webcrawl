The United States Third Fleet is one of the numbered fleets of the United States Navy, responsible for naval operations in the eastern and northern Pacific Ocean, including the U.S. West Coast, Alaska, Hawaii and the Bering Sea. Its headquarters are at Naval Base Point Loma in San Diego, California. It is a component of U.S. Pacific Fleet.

Second World War

The Third Fleet was formed on 15 March 1943 under Admiral William F. Halsey Jr. It conducted the major carrier operations of the later Pacific War, including the Philippine Sea, Leyte Gulf (where Halsey's decisions during the Battle off Samar became controversial), the strikes on Formosa and the Japanese home islands, and the final operations of 1945. Unusually, the same fleet staff and ships alternated designations: when Admiral Raymond Spruance commanded, the formation was called the Fifth Fleet, and when Halsey commanded, the Third Fleet. Japanese intelligence reportedly did not immediately grasp that the two designations referred to the same forces. The Third Fleet was disestablished on 7 October 1945.

Modern role

The Third Fleet was reactivated on 1 February 1973 at Ford Island, Hawaii, and later moved its headquarters to San Diego. Its traditional role is to train and certify naval forces deploying to the western Pacific (Seventh Fleet area), and to defend the approaches to the United States. Since 2015, under the "Third Fleet Forward" initiative, it has also deployed forces west of the International Date Line in coordination with the Seventh Fleet, and it regularly provides command for the biennial RIMPAC exercise, the world's largest international maritime exercise.
