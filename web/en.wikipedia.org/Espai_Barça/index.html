Espai Barça (Catalan for "Barça Space") is the large-scale redevelopment project of FC Barcelona centred on the club's Les Corts campus in Barcelona. Its centrepiece is the comprehensive remodelling of the Camp Nou stadium, alongside a new Palau Blaugrana arena for the club's basketball and handball sections, new offices, a club museum and shop, and reorganization of the surrounding public space.

History

The project grew out of earlier, unrealized schemes, including the Norman Foster design of 2007 that was abandoned during the club's financial difficulties. In April 2014 the club's members (socis) approved the Espai Barça proposal in a referendum. The final design for the new Camp Nou, by the Japanese firm Nikken Sekkei with Catalan partners, envisions a fully roofed stadium with an open, Mediterranean character, expanded to about 105,000 seats — remaining Europe's largest stadium.

Financing and construction

After delays, the project was restructured under president Joan Laporta. In 2023 the club closed a financing package of about €1.45–1.5 billion from a group of investors and lenders, to be repaid from future stadium revenues. Construction on the Camp Nou began in earnest in 2023, with the first team playing home matches at the Estadi Olímpic Lluís Companys during the works. The rebuilt stadium is planned to reopen in stages, with full completion targeted later in the decade. The campus also includes the Estadi Johan Cruyff at the club's training ground in Sant Joan Despí, home to the women's and reserve teams.
