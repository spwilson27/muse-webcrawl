Sea of Okhotsk

The Sea of Okhotsk is a marginal sea of the western Pacific Ocean, lying between Russia's Kamchatka Peninsula to the east, the Kuril Islands to the southeast, Japan's island of Hokkaido to the south, the island of Sakhalin to the west, and the Siberian coast to the north and west. It covers about 1,583,000 km² and is named after Okhotsk, the first Russian settlement in the region.

Geography and climate

The sea is connected to the Pacific through the straits of the Kuril chain and to the Sea of Japan by the Strait of Tartary and La Pérouse Strait. Siberian high pressure drives bitter winter winds across it, and from about November to June much of the sea freezes; its seasonal sea ice is the southernmost regular sea ice in the Northern Hemisphere and is vital to the regional ecosystem, fertilising spring plankton blooms. The Amur River discharges into the sea near Sakhalin, freshening its surface layer.

Biology and economy

The cold, nutrient-rich waters support major fisheries for pollock, salmon, herring, crab and other species, making the sea one of the world's most productive fishing grounds. The shelves off Sakhalin hold significant oil and natural gas fields (the Sakhalin projects). Ports include Magadan and Okhotsk in Russia and Abashiri in Japan, where drift-ice tourism is popular.

History

Russian explorers Ivan Moskvitin and Vassili Poyarkov reached the sea in the 1640s. In the 19th century it was a major ground for American whalers, and during the Cold War it became a bastion for Soviet ballistic-missile submarines.
