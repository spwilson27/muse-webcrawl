Thimble Theatre is an American comic strip created by E. C. Segar, first published on December 19, 1919, in the New York Journal. The strip is best remembered as the origin of Popeye the Sailor, who made his debut appearance in the strip on January 17, 1929, and soon eclipsed the original cast in popularity.

History

The strip was Segar's third published work and was distributed by King Features Syndicate. Its early years revolved around the Oyl family — Castor Oyl, his sister Olive Oyl, and her boyfriend Harold Hamgravy (later shortened to Ham Gravy). The stories were initially gentle domestic comedies before Segar shifted toward longer adventure serials.

Popeye

Popeye was introduced as a minor character hired to crew a ship in a 1929 storyline, but readers responded so enthusiastically that he became the strip's central figure. Other enduring characters include J. Wellington Wimpy, the hamburger-loving moocher; Eugene the Jeep; and the Sea Hag. The strip continued under various artists after Segar's death in 1938.

Legacy

Thimble Theatre is regarded as one of the most influential American comic strips, credited with pioneering the continuing adventure format in humor strips and with popularizing words such as "goon" and "jeep" in everyday English.
