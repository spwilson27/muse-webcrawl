Pitch (music)

Pitch is a perceptual property of sounds that allows their ordering on a frequency-related scale, or more commonly, pitch is the quality that makes it possible to judge sounds as "higher" and "lower" in the sense associated with musical melodies. Pitch is a major auditory attribute of musical tones, along with duration, loudness, and timbre.

Pitch can be determined only in sounds that have a frequency that is clear and stable enough to be distinguished from noise. The perception of pitch arises mainly from the fundamental frequency of a sound; doubling the frequency raises the pitch by an octave. In Western music, pitches are named by letters (A through G) modified by accidentals, and organised into octaves.

Pitch perception

Humans typically hear frequencies from about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, but pitch discrimination is finest in the mid-range. Listeners with absolute pitch (perfect pitch) can identify or produce a named pitch without a reference tone — an ability that is rare and partly dependent on early musical training. Relative pitch, the ability to judge intervals between pitches, is the more commonly trained skill.

Standards and notation

The most widely used tuning standard sets the A above middle C at 440 Hz (A440), adopted internationally in the twentieth century, though orchestras often tune slightly higher. On the staff, pitch is represented by the vertical position of note heads, with clefs fixing the mapping between position and pitch.
