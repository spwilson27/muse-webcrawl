Twill

Twill is one of the three fundamental textile weaves, alongside plain weave and satin. In twill, the weft passes over and under the warp in a regular progression — for example over two, under one — and the pattern steps by one thread in each row, producing visible diagonal lines called wales on the face of the cloth.

Characteristics

The diagonal structure packs more threads into a given area, making twill fabrics dense, durable, drapable, and resistant to showing soil. Common twills include denim (a warp-faced 3/1 twill dyed with indigo warp), gabardine, drill, chino, tweed, flannel in some constructions, and herringbone, a twill whose diagonal reverses direction regularly. Tartan is traditionally woven as a 2/2 twill.

Notation

Twills are described by the ratio of warp floats to weft floats in one repeat — a "2/1 twill" passes over two warp threads and under one — and by the direction of the diagonal, right-hand (Z) or left-hand (S). Even-sided twills such as 2/2 resemble plain weave in balance and can be woven with four shafts on a simple loom.
