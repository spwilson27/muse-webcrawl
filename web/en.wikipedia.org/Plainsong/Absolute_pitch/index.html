Absolute pitch

Absolute pitch (AP), often called perfect pitch, is the ability to identify or re-create a given musical note without the benefit of a reference tone. A person with absolute pitch can name the pitch of an isolated sound — a piano key, a doorbell, a car horn — as a note of the chromatic scale.

Causes

Absolute pitch is rare, on the order of one in ten thousand people in Western populations, but far commoner among speakers of tonal languages such as Mandarin and Vietnamese, and among musicians who began training in early childhood. Research suggests a critical period in development plus a genetic predisposition: early musical training before about age six strongly predicts the ability, and it clusters in families.

Characteristics and limits

Possessors differ in speed and accuracy; many identify white-key notes faster than black-key notes, reflecting the order of early learning. Absolute pitch can be a hindrance when music is transposed or performed at non-standard concert pitch, since notes are heard under fixed labels. It is distinct from relative pitch — the ability to identify intervals — which is trainable in nearly all musicians.
