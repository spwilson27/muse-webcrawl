The Football Association

The Football Association (the FA) is the governing body of association football in England and the Crown dependencies of Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man. Formed in 1863, it is the oldest football association in the world and was responsible for the first codified Laws of the Game. It is a member of UEFA and FIFA and holds a special permanent seat on the International Football Association Board, which maintains the Laws of the Game.

History

The FA was founded on 26 October 1863 at the Freemasons' Tavern in London, where representatives of eleven clubs and schools, led by Ebenezer Cobb Morley, met to agree a common set of rules for football. Morley drafted the first Laws of the Game; disagreement over hacking and carrying the ball led the rugby-playing clubs to withdraw and later form the Rugby Football Union. In 1871 the FA secretary Charles W. Alcock proposed the FA Cup, the world's first national football competition, and England played Scotland in the first international match in 1872.

Responsibilities

The FA organises the England national football teams at all levels, runs the FA Cup, FA Women's Cup and other competitions, and oversees the game from grassroots to the professional level in England, including discipline, refereeing, coaching and safeguarding. The Premier League and English Football League are run by separate bodies, though the FA retains regulatory authority and a special share in the Premier League.

Wembley and St George's Park

The FA owns Wembley Stadium, opened in 2007 on the site of the old Empire Stadium, which hosts England home matches and cup finals. In 2012 it opened St George's Park, the national football centre near Burton upon Trent, as the training base for all England teams.
