Gampopa

Gampopa Sönam Rinchen (1079–1153), also known as Dakpo Lhaje ("the physician from Dakpo") and Dakpo Rinpoche, was a Tibetan Buddhist teacher, physician and monk who founded the Dagpo Kagyu school. He was the principal student of the yogi Milarepa and is credited with merging the Kadam tradition's graduated path (lamrim) teachings with the Mahāmudrā and tantric instructions of the Kagyu lineage.

Life

Born in Nyal, in south-eastern Tibet, Gampopa trained as a physician and married young. After his wife and children died in an epidemic, he renounced household life, was ordained as a monk, and studied with Kadampa masters. In his early thirties he met Milarepa, under whom he attained recognition of the nature of mind. He later founded Daklha Gampo monastery in 1121, which became the seat of his tradition.

Teachings and writings

Gampopa's best-known work, The Jewel Ornament of Liberation, presents the complete Mahāyāna path in a systematic sequence from Buddha-nature through enlightenment, and remains a standard textbook in Kagyu monasteries. His synthesis — sometimes called the "four Dharmas of Gampopa" — shaped all later Kagyu schools, which trace their monastic lines through his disciples.

Legacy

His four main disciples founded the "four greater" Kagyu schools (Barom, Phagdru, Karma and Tsalpa), from which the later Kagyu sub-schools descend. The Karmapa line begins with his student Düsum Khyenpa.
