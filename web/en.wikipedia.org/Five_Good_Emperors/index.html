Five Good Emperors

The Five Good Emperors were the sequence of Roman emperors—Nerva, Trajan, Hadrian, Antoninus Pius, and Marcus Aurelius—who ruled the Roman Empire from 96 to 180 AD, during the Nerva–Antonine dynasty.

The term

The label derives from Niccolò Machiavelli's Discourses on Livy. Edward Gibbon called the period "the period in the history of the world during which the condition of the human race was most happy and prosperous."

The emperors

Nerva (96–98) restored liberty after Domitian; Trajan (98–117), Optimus Princeps, expanded the empire to its greatest extent; Hadrian (117–138) consolidated the frontiers, including Hadrian's Wall; Antoninus Pius (138–161) reigned in peace; Marcus Aurelius (161–180), the philosopher emperor, wrote the Meditations.

End of the sequence

Marcus Aurelius broke the adoptive pattern by naming his biological son Commodus as successor, whose disastrous reign (180–192) ended the era.
