Illusion of transparency

Illusion of transparency
FieldSocial psychology
Key researchersThomas Gilovich, Victoria Medvec, Kenneth Savitsky
Related effectsSpotlight effect; curse of knowledge
First described1998 (Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky)

The illusion of transparency is a cognitive bias in which people overestimate the degree to which their personal mental state is known by others. It leads individuals to believe that their emotions, thoughts and intentions "leak out" and are far more apparent to observers than they actually are. A closely related phenomenon is the illusion of asymmetric insight, in which people believe they know others better than others know them.

Research

The effect was named and demonstrated by Thomas Gilovich, Victoria Medvec and Kenneth Savitsky in 1998. In their experiments, speakers asked to give impromptu speeches overestimated how obvious their nervousness was to the audience; tasters of disgusting drinks overestimated how clearly their facial expressions betrayed their disgust to observers. Negotiators likewise overestimate how transparent their interests and intentions are to the other side, which can distort bargaining behaviour.

The illusion is thought to arise from anchoring on one's own vivid internal experience and adjusting insufficiently for the limited information available to others — the same egocentric bias underlying the spotlight effect, in which people overestimate how much others notice their appearance and behaviour.

Practical consequences

Believing one's anxiety is obvious can intensify public-speaking anxiety; experiments show that simply informing speakers about the illusion of transparency reduces both their perceived nervousness and the nervousness rated by audiences. In emergencies, the illusion can contribute to the bystander effect: victims assume their need for help is transparent, while onlookers wait for clear signals. The bias is also implicated in "tapping" studies, where people tapping out a melody vastly overestimate how recognisable it will be to listeners.
