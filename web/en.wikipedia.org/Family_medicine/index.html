Family medicine

FocusComprehensive primary care for all ages
SpecialistFamily physician

Family medicine is a medical specialty within primary care that provides continuing and comprehensive health care for the individual and family across all ages, genders, diseases, and parts of the body. The specialist, generally termed a family physician, is often the first point of contact in the health system.

Scope of practice

Family physicians diagnose and treat acute and chronic illnesses, provide preventive care and health education, manage chronic diseases such as diabetes and hypertension, and coordinate referrals to specialists. Care is delivered in offices, hospitals, and patients' homes, with an emphasis on continuity and the family and community context of health.

Training

In the United States, family medicine residency lasts three years following medical school; the specialty was formally recognised in 1969 with the founding of the American Board of Family Practice. Equivalent general practice training exists in most health systems worldwide, overseen internationally by WONCA, the World Organisation of Family Doctors.
