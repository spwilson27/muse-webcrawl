Box-spring

A box-spring (or divan in the United Kingdom) is a type of bed base: a sturdy wooden frame covered in cloth and containing steel springs or a metal grid, placed under a mattress to support it, absorb shock, and raise the height of the bed. Box-springs typically sit within a bed frame or on a simple set of legs or casters.

Construction and function

A traditional box-spring contains coil springs mounted on wooden slats inside the frame, upholstered with padding and fabric, with a thin non-slip sheet (the dust cover) underneath. Working with the mattress, it prolongs mattress life by absorbing a large share of the stress and wear of body weight, and it adds a modest amount of extra "give" to the sleep surface.

Modern developments

Many contemporary "box-springs" are actually rigid box foundations containing no springs at all — simply a wooden or metal support structure — because modern foam and pocket-coil mattresses require firm, non-flexing support, and using a flexible base can void warranties. Platform beds and adjustable bases have also displaced the box-spring in many markets. Standard heights are around 9 inches (full profile) with low-profile versions around 4–5 inches.
