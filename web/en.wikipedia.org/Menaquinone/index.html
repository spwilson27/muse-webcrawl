Menaquinone

Menaquinones are a class of molecules comprising a family of vitamin K compounds, designated vitamin K2. They share a 2-methyl-1,4-naphthoquinone core and differ in the length of their isoprenoid side chain, designated MK-4 through MK-13 according to the number of isoprene units.

Sources

Menaquinones are produced by bacteria. MK-4 is formed in animal tissues from dietary vitamin K1 and is found in meat, eggs, and dairy; longer-chain menaquinones occur in fermented foods, most notably Japanese nattō, which is exceptionally rich in MK-7, and in some cheeses. Gut bacteria also produce menaquinones, though the extent of their contribution to human vitamin K status is debated.

Function

Like all vitamin K forms, menaquinones act as cofactors for the enzyme gamma-glutamyl carboxylase, which activates proteins involved in blood coagulation and in the regulation of calcium in bones and blood vessels, including osteocalcin and matrix Gla protein.

Health

Vitamin K deficiency is rare in adults but can impair clotting. Research is ongoing into possible roles of vitamin K2 in bone and cardiovascular health; dietary recommendations generally treat all forms of vitamin K together.
