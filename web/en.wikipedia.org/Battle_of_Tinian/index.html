The Battle of Tinian was fought on the island of Tinian in the Mariana Islands from July 24 to August 1, 1944, during the Pacific War. U.S. Marines captured the island from its Japanese garrison, in an operation often praised as one of the most skillfully executed amphibious landings of the war.

Background

Tinian lies just south of Saipan, which American forces had just captured. The island's flat terrain and airfields made it a prime objective: it could support the heavy bombers that would later attack the Japanese home islands.

The battle

Instead of landing at the obvious beaches near Tinian Town, the Marines landed across two small, lightly defended beaches on the northwest coast, supported by an artillery barrage from Saipan and a feint toward Tinian Town. The surprise succeeded; the landing force got ashore with light losses, defeated a Japanese night counterattack, and advanced steadily south. Tinian Town fell within days, and organized resistance ended on August 1.

Aftermath

Tinian was rapidly developed into one of the largest air bases of the war. Its North Field became the departure point for the atomic bomb missions against Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945.
