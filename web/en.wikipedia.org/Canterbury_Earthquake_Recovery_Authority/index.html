The Canterbury Earthquake Recovery Authority (CERA) was a New Zealand public service department established in March 2011 to lead and coordinate the recovery of Canterbury following the September 2010 and February 2011 earthquakes. It operated under the Canterbury Earthquake Recovery Act 2011, which gave the government sweeping powers — subject to criticism as overly centralised — to suspend or amend other laws for recovery purposes.

Role and work

CERA oversaw the demolition of damaged buildings in central Christchurch, coordinated infrastructure repair through the Stronger Christchurch Infrastructure Rebuild Team (SCIRT), and produced the Christchurch Central Recovery Plan (the "Blueprint"), which defined anchor projects such as the convention centre precinct, the stadium, and the Avon River precinct. It also managed the residential red zone programme, under which the Crown offered to purchase nearly 8,000 homes in areas where land damage made rebuilding impractical — a process that drew praise for its speed and criticism over fairness, insurance disputes, and the treatment of uninsured and commercial properties.

Transition and legacy

As the recovery moved from emergency to regeneration, CERA's functions were progressively transferred. In February 2016 many powers passed to the new Crown company Ōtākaro Limited and the development agency Regenerate Christchurch; CERA itself was wound up in April 2016, with remaining responsibilities passing to the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet and to Land Information New Zealand. Assessments of CERA remain mixed: credited with restoring essential services and coordinating a vast rebuild, but faulted for slow decision-making, strained relations with the Christchurch City Council, and delays in the city's anchor projects.
