Governor of Puerto Rico

The governor of Puerto Rico (Spanish: gobernador de Puerto Rico) is the head of government of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, an unincorporated territory of the United States. The governor is the head of the executive branch and is responsible for administering the government, signing or vetoing legislation passed by the Legislative Assembly, appointing executive officials, and commanding the Puerto Rico National Guard. The official residence is La Fortaleza in Old San Juan, the oldest executive mansion in continuous use in the Americas.

Incumbent: Jenniffer González-Colón (since 2 January 2025)

Term: 4 years, no term limit

Residence: La Fortaleza, San Juan

First elected holder: Luis Muñoz Marín (1949)

History

Under Spanish rule Puerto Rico was governed by appointed captains general. After the Spanish–American War of 1898, the United States appointed civilian governors under the Foraker Act of 1900 and the Jones Act of 1917. The Elective Governor Act of 1947 allowed Puerto Ricans to elect their own governor; Luis Muñoz Marín, of the Popular Democratic Party, won the first election in 1948 and oversaw the drafting of the Commonwealth constitution of 1952.

Election and duties

The governor is elected by direct popular vote every four years on the same day as the Legislative Assembly elections, with no term limits. Candidates must be at least 35 years old, a U.S. citizen, and a resident of Puerto Rico for five consecutive years before the election. The governor delivers an annual address, proposes the budget, grants pardons, and may call special sessions of the legislature.

Recent history

In 2019, Governor Ricardo Rosselló resigned following mass protests (the "Ricky Renuncia" movement) triggered by leaked private chats and corruption arrests; after a brief contested succession, Wanda Vázquez Garced became governor. Pedro Pierluisi served as governor from 2021 to 2025. Jenniffer González-Colón, previously the island's resident commissioner in Washington, took office in January 2025.
