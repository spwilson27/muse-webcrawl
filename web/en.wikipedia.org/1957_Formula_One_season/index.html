The 1957 Formula One season was the 11th season of FIA Formula One motor racing. It featured the 8th World Championship of Drivers, which commenced on 13 January 1957 and ended on 8 September after eight races. The season also included several non-championship races for Formula One cars. Argentine driver Juan Manuel Fangio won his fifth and final World Drivers' Championship, driving for Maserati.

Summary

Fangio, who had won the 1956 title with Ferrari, returned to Maserati for 1957 and dominated the early part of the season, winning in Argentina, Monaco and France. His victory at the German Grand Prix at the Nürburgring is widely regarded as one of the greatest drives in the history of the sport: after a slow pit stop dropped him nearly a minute behind the Ferraris of Mike Hawthorn and Peter Collins, Fangio broke the lap record repeatedly, caught and passed both cars on the penultimate lap, and won.

The season was the last full year for the iconic Maserati 250F and the final championship season before the introduction of the International Cup for F1 Manufacturers in 1958. It also marked the last Formula One appearance of several pre-war style front-engined cars before rear-engined designs took over.

Drivers' Championship standings

Fangio finished the season with 40 points (46 gross), ahead of Stirling Moss on 25. The British Grand Prix saw the first World Championship win for a British-built car, the Vanwall shared by Tony Brooks and Stirling Moss.
