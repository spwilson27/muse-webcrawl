An electronic health record (EHR) is the systematized collection of patient and population electronically stored health information in a digital format. These records can be shared across different health care settings through network-connected, enterprise-wide information systems. EHRs typically include demographics, medical history, diagnoses, medications, immunisation status, laboratory results, radiology images and clinical notes.

EHR versus EMR

The term electronic medical record (EMR) is often used for the digital record within a single practice or hospital, while an EHR is conceived as a broader, longitudinal record designed to be shared between organisations and to follow the patient. In everyday usage the terms are frequently interchanged.

Adoption

Adoption accelerated in the twenty-first century, driven by government programmes such as the United States HITECH Act of 2009, which paid incentives for "meaningful use" of certified systems, and by national programmes in countries such as Estonia, Denmark and the United Kingdom. By the 2020s the large majority of hospitals in the United States and many other developed health systems used EHRs, with a small number of large vendors supplying much of the market.

Benefits and criticisms

Supporters cite legibility, instant availability, clinical decision support (alerts for drug interactions and allergies), e-prescribing, and the ability to analyse data for quality improvement and research. Critics point to high costs, poor usability, documentation burden and clinician burnout, limited interoperability between vendors, privacy and security risks from data breaches, and the persistence of "copy-forward" errors in notes.

Standards and interoperability

Interoperability relies on standards such as HL7 version 2 and the newer FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) API, and on terminologies including ICD for diagnoses, LOINC for laboratory observations and SNOMED CT for clinical concepts. Regulations in several jurisdictions now require vendors to support standardised data exchange and prohibit information blocking.
