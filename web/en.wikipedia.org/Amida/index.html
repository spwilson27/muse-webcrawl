Amida

Amida is the Japanese name of Amitābha, the Buddha of Infinite Light, the central Buddha of Pure Land Buddhism. (The word "Amida" also names other topics — including a 1984 arcade/NES-style game, a genus of lady beetles, and various places — but the dominant encyclopedic sense is Amitābha; this root-path page treats that primary topic.)

Amida (Amitābha)
Sanskrit: Amitābha / Amitāyus
Realm: Sukhāvatī, the Western Pure Land
Practice: Nembutsu — "Namu Amida Butsu"

Doctrine

According to the Longer Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, the bodhisattva Dharmākara made 48 vows and, after eons of practice, became Amitābha, establishing the Pure Land Sukhāvatī, where beings who sincerely call on his name — even ten times — may be reborn and attain enlightenment without retrogression. In East Asia this became the most widely practised form of Buddhism.

Pure Land practice

In China, devotion to Amituofo blended with other practices; in Japan, Hōnen founded Jōdo-shū and his disciple Shinran founded Jōdo Shinshū, teaching exclusive reliance on Amida's "Other Power" (tariki) through the nembutsu, "Namu Amida Butsu" ("I take refuge in Amida Buddha"). The Great Buddha of Kamakura (Kamakura Daibutsu, c. 1252) is a famous bronze Amida.

Iconography

Amida is usually depicted seated in meditation or with hands in the raigō-in welcoming mudra, often flanked by Kannon (Avalokiteśvara) and Seishi (Mahāsthāmaprāpta), descending to greet the dying believer (raigō).
