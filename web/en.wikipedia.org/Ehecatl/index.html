Ehecatl

Ehecatl (Classical Nahuatl: Ehēcatl, "Wind") is a pre-Columbian deity of the wind in Aztec and broader Mesoamerican mythology, most often understood as an aspect or manifestation of the feathered serpent god Quetzalcoatl — hence the compound name Quetzalcoatl-Ehecatl.

Role in myth

As the god of wind, Ehecatl was believed to sweep the road ahead of the rain god Tlaloc, clearing the way for the rains. In the Aztec creation myth of the Five Suns, Ehecatl helps bring the current world into being: he blows on the bones of the dead retrieved from the underworld Mictlan, and in the myth of the birth of the Fifth Sun, he breathes life into the sun and sets it moving. He is also credited with bringing love to humanity, in a story in which he fell in love with the mortal maiden Mayahuel.

Iconography and temples

Ehecatl is typically depicted wearing a distinctive buccal mask shaped like a duck's bill or a wind jewel (the cut conch shell, ehecailacacozcatl) on his chest. Temples dedicated to him were characteristically round — circular plans offered less resistance to the wind — the best-known example being the circular temple excavated at the Calixtlahuaca site and the temple platform discovered during construction of the Pino Suárez metro station in Mexico City.

Calendar

Ehecatl is associated with the day sign "Wind" (2 Ehecatl in the tonalpohualli calendar) and with the second day of the thirteen-day week.
