The mustang is a free-roaming horse of the Western United States, descended from horses originally brought to the Americas by the Spanish. Mustangs are often referred to as "wild horses", but because they are descended from once-domesticated animals, they are actually feral horses.

History

Horses first reached the Americas with Spanish explorers in the 16th century. Escaped, traded, or released animals spread across the Great Plains, where Native American nations adopted them and transformed their cultures. By the early 20th century an estimated two million mustangs roamed the West, but numbers fell drastically through slaughter and roundup until federal protection arrived in 1971.

Characteristics

Mustangs are typically small — about 14 to 15 hands high — with strong bones, hard hooves, and great endurance. They occur in every coat colour and pattern. Living in herds led by a dominant mare and protected by a stallion, they survive on sparse forage and little water.

Management and adoption

In the United States, the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) manages mustang populations on public land, periodically rounding up excess animals and offering them for public adoption.
