Port of Beirut

The Port of Beirut (Arabic: مرفأ بيروت) is the principal seaport of Lebanon, located on the northern coast of Beirut at the eastern end of Saint George Bay. Historically one of the busiest ports of the eastern Mediterranean and the gateway for most of Lebanon's imports, it is administered by the state-owned Gestion et Exploitation du Port de Beyrouth, with its container terminal long operated under concession by the Beirut Container Terminal Consortium.

History

Beirut has been a trading port since Phoenician times. The modern harbor was developed under Ottoman and French Mandate administrations, expanded with new basins and quays in the mid-twentieth century, and rebuilt after the Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990), during which the port area lay on the Green Line. Before 2020 it handled roughly 1.2 million TEU of containers annually and housed Lebanon's main grain silos, holding much of the country's strategic wheat reserve.

2020 explosion

On 4 August 2020, a fire ignited approximately 2,750 tonnes of ammonium nitrate that had been stored in Warehouse 12 since being impounded from the cargo ship MV Rhosus in 2014. The resulting explosion — among the largest non-nuclear blasts ever recorded — killed at least 218 people, injured more than 7,000, destroyed the silos and port facilities, and devastated large parts of the city. Operations were partially diverted to Tripoli and other ports; reconstruction of the port and accountability for the disaster remained subjects of national and international dispute in the years that followed.
