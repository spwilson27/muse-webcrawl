Leigh Van Valen

Leigh Van Valen (1935–2010) was an American evolutionary biologist at the University of Chicago, best known for the Red Queen hypothesis and the law of constant extinction that bears his name.

Work

In 1973 Van Valen proposed that extinction probability for a group of organisms is roughly constant over time, implying species must continually adapt just to persist — an idea he named for the Red Queen in Lewis Carroll, who runs to stay in place. He founded the journal Evolutionary Theory and wrote on everything from mammalian phylogeny to the definition of species.

Legacy

Independent-minded and prolific, Van Valen described fossil mammals from the early Cenozoic of North America and influenced macroevolutionary thinking for decades.
