Oxford English Dictionary

The Oxford English Dictionary (OED) is the principal historical dictionary of the English language, published by Oxford University Press. It traces the historical development of the English lexicon, providing for each word its meanings, pronunciations, spellings, etymology, and a chronological series of illustrative quotations.

History

The project began in 1857 with the Philological Society's call for a new dictionary to supersede Johnson's. James Murray became editor in 1879 and worked from the famous Scriptorium in Oxford. The first fascicle appeared in 1884 under the title A New English Dictionary on Historical Principles; the first edition was completed in 1928 in ten volumes.

Editions

The second edition (1989) ran to 20 volumes and some 290,000 entries. Since 2000 the dictionary has been continuously revised online as OED Online, with quarterly updates adding and revising entries; a full third edition is being prepared online rather than in print.

Method

Entries rest on millions of quotations gathered by volunteer readers and, today, large text corpora, documenting each sense's first known use and evolution.
