Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington

Born: 1 May 1769, Dublin, Ireland

Died: 14 September 1852, Walmer Castle, Kent, England

Allegiance: United Kingdom

Service: British Army; Prime Minister of the United Kingdom (1828–1830, 1834)

Known for: Victory at the Battle of Waterloo (1815); Peninsular War; Anglo-Mysore and Anglo-Maratha wars in India

Nickname: The Iron Duke

Field Marshal Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington (1 May 1769 – 14 September 1852) was an Anglo-Irish statesman, soldier and Tory politician who was one of the leading British military and political figures of the 19th century, serving twice as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. His defeat of Napoleon at the Battle of Waterloo in 1815 is remembered as among the most decisive victories in military history.

Early life and India

Born in Dublin into the Protestant Ascendancy as the third son of the Earl of Mornington, Wellesley was educated at Eton and the Royal Academy of Equitation at Angers, France. Commissioned as an ensign in 1787, he rose through purchase of commissions and served in Flanders. In India he distinguished himself in the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War, winning the Battle of Assaye (1803) — which he later considered his finest victory — and in the Second Anglo-Maratha War.

Peninsular War

From 1808 Wellesley commanded British forces in Portugal and Spain against Napoleonic France. He constructed the Lines of Torres Vedras to defend Lisbon, won battles including Talavera, Salamanca (1812), and Vitoria (1813), and by 1814 had driven French forces from the Iberian Peninsula into France.

Waterloo

After Napoleon's return from Elba, Wellington commanded the Allied army in Belgium. On 18 June 1815, together with Blücher's Prussian army, he defeated Napoleon at the Battle of Waterloo, ending the Napoleonic Wars.

Political career

Created Duke of Wellington in 1814, he entered politics as a leading Tory, serving as Prime Minister from 1828 to 1830 and briefly in 1834. His government passed Catholic emancipation in 1829 to avert civil unrest in Ireland, despite his earlier opposition. He later served as Foreign Secretary and as Commander-in-Chief of the Forces. Nicknamed the "Iron Duke", he remained an elder statesman until his death in 1852, when he received a state funeral.
