Parsimony

Parsimony is the principle that, all else being equal, explanations that posit the fewest entities or assumptions are to be preferred. It is most famously expressed as Occam's razor.

In science

In philosophy of science, parsimony is a heuristic guiding theory choice rather than a guarantee of truth. Quantitative relatives include minimum description length and statistical model-selection criteria such as the Akaike information criterion, which penalise overfitting.

In biology

In phylogenetics, maximum parsimony is a widely used optimality criterion that selects the evolutionary tree requiring the fewest character-state changes. It is also applied in cladistics and in historical linguistics.
