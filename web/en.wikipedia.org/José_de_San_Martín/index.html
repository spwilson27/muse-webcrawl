José de San Martín

José Francisco de San Martín y Matorras (25 February 1778 – 17 August 1850) was an Argentine general and the prime leader of the southern and central parts of South America's successful struggle for independence from the Spanish Empire, serving as the Protector of Peru.

Early life and career in Spain

Born in Yapeyú, in the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata, San Martín was taken to Spain as a child and educated in Madrid. He joined the Spanish army and fought against Napoleon in the Peninsular War, distinguishing himself at the Battle of Bailén. In 1812 he returned to South America to join the independence cause.

Crossing of the Andes

As commander of the Army of the Andes, in January 1817 San Martín led one of the greatest military feats of the age: the crossing of the Andes into Chile. Victories at Chacabuco (1817) and Maipú (1818) secured Chilean independence, proclaimed under Bernardo O'Higgins.

Peru and exile

San Martín then sailed north and proclaimed the independence of Peru on 28 July 1821, becoming its Protector. After a famous private meeting with Simón Bolívar at Guayaquil in 1822, he resigned his powers and withdrew from public life, living his remaining decades in exile in Europe. He died at Boulogne-sur-Mer, France. San Martín is a national hero of Argentina, Chile, and Peru.
