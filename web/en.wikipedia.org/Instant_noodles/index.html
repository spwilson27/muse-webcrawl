Instant noodles

Instant noodles, also called instant ramen, are a type of noodle dish consisting of pre-cooked and dried noodle blocks, usually sold with a packet of flavouring powder and/or seasoning oil. The dried noodles are designed to be rehydrated in hot water within a few minutes. Instant noodles are consumed worldwide — more than 100 billion servings per year — and are especially popular in East and Southeast Asia.

History

Instant noodles were invented by Momofuku Ando, founder of Nissin Foods in Japan. In 1958, after experimenting in a backyard shed, Ando developed "flash frying" — steaming noodles and then dehydrating them in hot oil — and launched Chikin Ramen, the first instant noodle product. In 1971 Nissin introduced Cup Noodles, packaged in a foam cup with dried vegetables, which proved a global success. Ando's invention was recognised in Japan as one of the country's most important post-war innovations.

Production and varieties

Commercial production involves mixing flour (usually wheat) with water, alkaline salts (kansui) in ramen-style products, sheeting and cutting the dough, steaming, and then dehydrating by deep-frying or hot-air drying. Regional varieties abound: Korean ramyeon is known for spicy flavours, Indonesia's Indomie for mi goreng (fried noodle) style, and Thailand's Mama for tom yum flavour. Premium and health-oriented versions, including non-fried and whole-grain noodles, have grown in recent decades.

Nutrition and criticism

Instand noodles are inexpensive and filling but are frequently criticised as high in sodium and saturated fat and low in fibre and micronutrients. Frequent consumption has been associated in some studies with cardiometabolic risk factors, prompting some manufacturers to reduce salt and fat content.
