Beta distribution

In probability theory and statistics, the beta distribution is a family of continuous probability distributions defined on the interval [0, 1] and parameterised by two positive shape parameters, denoted α and β. It is widely used to model random variables limited to intervals of finite length — probabilities, proportions, and percentages — and is the conjugate prior distribution for the Bernoulli, binomial, negative binomial, and geometric distributions in Bayesian inference.

Definition

The probability density function is proportional to xα−1(1−x)β−1, normalised by the beta function B(α, β) = Γ(α)Γ(β)/Γ(α+β). The mean is α/(α+β) and the mode, for α, β > 1, is (α−1)/(α+β−2). By choosing α and β the density can be uniform (both equal to 1), symmetric and bell-shaped, U-shaped, or skewed in either direction.

Applications

Because of its flexibility on a bounded interval, the beta distribution is used in Bayesian statistics (as the prior for a probability), in project management (PERT time estimates), in modelling allele frequencies in population genetics (the Balding–Nichols model), and in machine learning and A/B testing for conversion rates. The incomplete beta function gives the cumulative distribution and underlies the F and Student's t distributions.
