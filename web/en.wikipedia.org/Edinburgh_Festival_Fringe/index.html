Edinburgh Festival Fringe

The Edinburgh Festival Fringe (also known as The Fringe) is the world's largest performing arts festival, held annually in Edinburgh, Scotland, each August. It originated in 1947, when eight theatre companies turned up uninvited to perform on the margins — the "fringe" — of the inaugural Edinburgh International Festival. The Fringe is open-access: anyone with a show and a venue may take part.

History

The uninvited companies of 1947 grew year by year. The Festival Fringe Society was formed in 1959 to provide a central box office and programme. The Fringe became a celebrated launching pad for new writing and comedy: the satire revue Beyond the Fringe (1960) helped ignite the British satire boom, and the Perrier/Edinburgh Comedy Awards, founded in 1981, became a career-making prize for stand-up comedians.

Character

The programme spans theatre, comedy, dance, musicals, opera, cabaret, circus, children's shows, and spoken word, performed in venues ranging from major theatres to pubs, church halls, and shipping containers. In 2019 the festival staged over 3,800 shows. The 2020 festival was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic, returning in reduced form in 2021.

Influence

The Edinburgh model inspired fringe festivals worldwide, including the Adelaide Fringe, now the Southern Hemisphere's largest open-access arts festival.
