Nicholas John Spykman (October 13, 1893 – June 26, 1943) was a Dutch-American geostrategist and professor at Yale University. He is considered one of the founders of classical realism in American foreign policy thinking and is best known for the Rimland theory, a modification of Halford Mackinder's Heartland theory.

Spykman argued that the coastal fringes of Eurasia—the 'Rimland', encompassing Western Europe, the Middle East, South Asia and East Asia—were more strategically important than the interior Heartland, famously writing that 'who controls the Rimland rules Eurasia; who rules Eurasia controls the destinies of the world.'

Career and works

Born in Amsterdam, Spykman worked as a journalist and diplomat before earning his PhD at the University of California, Berkeley. He joined Yale in 1925 and founded its Institute of International Studies in 1935. His books include America's Strategy in World Politics (1942) and the posthumously published The Geography of the Peace (1944).

Spykman's ideas influenced the American policy of containment during the Cold War, which sought to prevent Soviet expansion into the Rimland regions of Europe and Asia.
