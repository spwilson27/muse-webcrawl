Mani (prophet)

Mani (c. 216 – 274 or 277 CE) was an Iranian prophet and the founder of Manichaeism, a religion that spread widely across the Roman and Persian worlds and eastward along the Silk Road into China. Born near Ctesiphon in Mesopotamia, then part of the Parthian Empire, Mani grew up among a Jewish-Christian baptist sect (the Elcesaites) to which his father belonged. He reported receiving revelations from his divine "Twin" (the Syzygos), first at age twelve and again at twenty-four, commanding him to proclaim a universal religion completing and superseding the revelations of Buddha, Zoroaster and Jesus.

Teaching

Mani taught a dualist cosmology in which the universe arises from the invasion of the Kingdom of Light by the Kingdom of Darkness; particles of divine light became trapped in matter, and salvation consists in their liberation through knowledge (gnosis), ascetic practice, prayer and the ritual meals of the community. He presented himself as the "Apostle of Light" and the promised Paraclete, the final prophet in a succession of revealers. His church was divided between the celibate "Elect" and lay "Hearers" who supported them.

Mission and death

Mani organised missions with remarkable energy, travelling to India and gaining the patronage of the Sasanian king Shapur I, for whom he composed the Shabuhragan, the only work he wrote in Middle Persian; his other scriptures were written in Syriac, and he himself illustrated the Arzhang, a picture book explaining his doctrine. Under Shapur's successors, at the instigation of the Zoroastrian priest Kartir, Mani fell from favour; he was imprisoned by Bahram I and died in chains at Gundeshapur.

Legacy

Manichaeism survived for a millennium, becoming for a time the state religion of the Uyghur Khaganate and leaving communities from North Africa to Fujian. Though later condemned as a heresy by Christians, Zoroastrians and Muslims, Mani's system influenced religious thought across Eurasia; Saint Augustine was a Manichaean "hearer" for nine years before his conversion to Christianity.
