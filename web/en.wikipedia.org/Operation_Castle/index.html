Operation Castle was a United States series of high-yield (high-energy) nuclear tests conducted by Joint Task Force 7 at Bikini Atoll beginning in March 1954. It followed Operation Ivy and preceded Operation Teapot. The series tested early designs for practical, deliverable thermonuclear weapons ("dry" hydrogen bombs using lithium deuteride fuel).

The Castle Bravo shot, 1 March 1954, was the largest US test ever conducted.

Castle Bravo

The first and most famous shot, Castle Bravo (1 March 1954), produced 15 megatons — two and a half times its predicted yield — because lithium-7 in the fuel, thought inert, contributed unexpectedly to the reaction. The explosion produced intense radioactive fallout that contaminated the Japanese fishing vessel Daigo Fukuryū Maru (Lucky Dragon No. 5) and the inhabited atolls of Rongelap and Utirik, whose residents were evacuated only after significant exposure. One Japanese crewman died, and the incident became an international scandal that galvanised opposition to atmospheric testing.

Other tests

The series also included Romeo (11 Mt), Koon (a fizzle of 110 kt), Union (6.9 Mt), Yankee (13.5 Mt) and Nectar (1.69 Mt). Castle demonstrated that dry-fuel thermonuclear weapons were practical; several designs were rapidly weaponised in the Mark 17, Mark 21 and Mark 24 bombs.

Legacy

The Bravo fallout disaster contributed to the public pressure that led to the Partial Test Ban Treaty of 1963 and remains a touchstone in discussions of nuclear safety and the health effects of fallout.
