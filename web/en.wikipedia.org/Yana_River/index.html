The Yana (Russian: Я́на) is a river in the Sakha Republic of northeastern Siberia, Russia. Formed by the confluence of the Dulgalakh and Sartang rivers on the northern slopes of the Verkhoyansk Range, it flows north for about 872 km to Yana Bay in the Laptev Sea, draining a basin of some 238,000 km² of permafrost tundra and taiga.

Geography

The river passes the town of Verkhoyansk — famous as one of the coldest inhabited places on Earth — and Batagay, then crosses the Yana-Indigirka Lowland before splitting into a broad delta below the settlement of Nizhneyansk. The Yana is frozen for much of the year, typically from October to late May or June, and is navigable in its lower reaches during the brief summer.

Palaeontology and archaeology

The Yana basin preserves rich Pleistocene fauna. The Yana Rhinoceros Horn Site (Yana RHS), dated to about 32,000 years ago, is among the earliest known traces of human settlement in the Arctic, yielding stone tools, ivory artefacts and faunal remains. Frozen mummies of woolly mammoths, bison and cave lions, including the famous Yukagir mammoth finds from the wider region, have been recovered from the river's permafrost banks. More recent studies of ancient DNA from Yana sites have identified the "Ancient North Siberians", a population ancestral in part to Native Americans.
