Clause

In language, a clause is a constituent that comprises a semantic predicating expression — typically a verb — and its dependents. A clause can form part of a sentence or be the whole sentence. Clauses are often the smallest grammatical unit that can express a complete proposition.

Types of clause

An independent (or main) clause can stand alone as a simple sentence, whereas a dependent (or subordinate) clause cannot stand alone and functions as a noun, adjective or adverb within a larger sentence. Dependent clauses are introduced by subordinating conjunctions or relative pronouns, as in "I know that she left" or "the book that I read". Coordinate clauses are joined by coordinating conjunctions such as and, but and or.

Clause structure

In English, clauses are commonly analysed around a subject and a predicate, with elements such as objects, complements and adverbials. The basic patterns are traditionally listed as SV, SVO, SVC, SVA, SVOO, SVOC and SVOA. Non-finite clauses (using infinitives, gerunds or participles) lack a tensed verb and often lack an overt subject.

Other uses

In law, a clause is a distinct numbered provision or article of a contract, statute or constitution — for example, the Commerce Clause of the United States Constitution.
