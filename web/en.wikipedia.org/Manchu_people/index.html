The Manchu people are a Tungusic ethnic group native to Manchuria in Northeast Asia. In the 17th century, under Nurhaci and his successors, they unified the Jurchen tribes, founded the Qing dynasty, and conquered China proper in 1644, ruling China until 1912.

History

The Manchus organised their society into the Eight Banners, a military and administrative system. During the Qing period, Manchus formed a privileged military caste garrisoned across the empire, though over time most adopted Chinese language and customs.

Today

Manchus form one of the largest recognised ethnic minorities in the People's Republic of China, numbering around ten million, mainly in Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang, and Hebei. The Manchu language is critically endangered, though revival efforts exist.
