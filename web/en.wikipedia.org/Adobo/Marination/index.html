Adobo as a marinade

In Spanish and Latin American usage, adobo is fundamentally a marinade: a seasoned liquid — typically vinegar or citrus juice, oil, salt, garlic, oregano, and paprika or ground dried chiles — in which meat is soaked before cooking. The word comes from the Spanish verb adobar, meaning to marinate or season.

Purpose

Marination in adobo serves several purposes: the acid of vinegar or lime slightly tenderizes the surface of the meat and acts as a preservative, the salt seasons throughout, and the spices and aromatics form a flavorful crust when the meat is grilled or roasted. Meats may rest in adobo from under an hour to overnight.

Examples

Mexican pork adobo and adobada use chile-based marinades; Canarian conejo en salmorejo uses a wine-and-herb adobo for rabbit; and the Filipino braising liquid also functions as a marinade when meat is soaked in the vinegar-soy mixture before simmering in it.
