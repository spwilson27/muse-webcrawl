Adobo in Spanish cuisine

In Spanish cuisine, adobo is a marinade of vinegar, olive oil, garlic, bay leaf, oregano, and sweet or smoked paprika (pimentón) used to flavor and preserve meats and fish. The technique dates to the days before refrigeration, when vinegar's acidity inhibited spoilage of slaughtered meat.

Classic preparations

Well-known examples include cazón en adobo, dogfish marinated in a paprika-vinegar adobo, floured and deep-fried — a signature tapa of Cádiz and Seville, also called bienmesabe; lomo en adobo, marinated pork loin related to the seasoning of cured sausages; and the adobo mixes applied to pork destined for chorizo. In the Canary Islands, adobo-style sauces accompany rabbit and goat.

Legacy

Spanish adobo traveled with colonization to Latin America and the Philippines, evolving into the chile-based adobos of Mexico, the dry rubs of Puerto Rico, and — by name, though by a largely indigenous method — Filipino adobo.
