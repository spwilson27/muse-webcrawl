Adobo in Manila

In Manila and the surrounding Tagalog region, adobo is most commonly prepared as chicken, pork, or a combination of the two simmered in a mixture of vinegar, soy sauce, crushed garlic, bay leaves and whole black peppercorns. The Metro Manila version is often regarded as the reference style against which regional Philippine variants are described: browned from the soy sauce, balanced between sour and salty, and served with steamed white rice.

Characteristics

Manila-style adobo is typically cooked until the liquid reduces to a glossy sauce that coats the meat; some households finish with a brief fry of the meat for crisp edges (adobong tuyo leaves almost no sauce at all). Pickled accompaniments such as atchara and sliced tomatoes are common on the side.

Cultural role

Because vinegar preserves the dish for days without refrigeration, adobo is a staple of Manila home cooking, carinderias (small eateries), and packed lunches. In 2021 the Philippine Department of Trade and Industry moved to develop a national standard for adobo preparation, prompting public debate about regional diversity.
