Paksiw

Paksiw is a Filipino cooking method and family of dishes in which fish or meat is simmered in vinegar, water, garlic, salt, and spices such as peppercorns and bay leaves. The name derives from the Tagalog verb paksiwin, "to cook in vinegar". The acidic broth both flavours and preserves the food.

Variants

The most common version is paksiw na isda, made with fish such as milkfish (bangus) or sardines, often with bitter gourd (ampalaya), eggplant, ginger, and chili. Paksiw na lechon recooks leftover roast pig in vinegar and sweet liver sauce, while paksiw na pata uses pork hock, sometimes with banana blossoms and sugar. In the Visayas, a related vinegar-based fish preparation is called inun-unan.

Characteristics

Paksiw belongs to the broad Filipino tradition of vinegar cookery that also includes adobo and kinilaw. Its sour, savoury flavour pairs with plain steamed rice, and like adobo it keeps well, often tasting better the day after cooking.
