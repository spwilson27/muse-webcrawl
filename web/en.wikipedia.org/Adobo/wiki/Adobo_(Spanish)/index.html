Adobo (Spanish)

Adobo or adobar in Spanish cuisine is a marinade and method of preserving and flavouring food, typically meat, in a mixture of vinegar or wine, olive oil, garlic, salt, and spices — most characteristically pimentón (Spanish paprika). The word derives from the Old Spanish adobar, "to dress, season or marinate".

History and use

Before refrigeration, adobo served as a preservative technique: the acidity and oil helped meat keep longer. Classic preparations include lomo en adobo (marinated pork loin), pinchos morunos, and bienmesabe, a fried fish marinated in adobo typical of Cádiz in Andalusia. Spanish colonisation carried the technique and the term to Latin America and the Philippines, where it evolved into distinct dishes such as Filipino adobo, which uses vinegar and soy sauce.

Characteristics

The defining flavour is usually the smoky, sweet or hot pimentón de la Vera, combined with oregano, cumin, bay leaf and garlic. Food is marinated for several hours or overnight before being grilled, roasted or fried.
