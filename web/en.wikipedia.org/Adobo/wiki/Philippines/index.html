Philippines

The Philippines, officially the Republic of the Philippines, is an archipelagic country in Southeast Asia comprising about 7,641 islands, with a population of over 110 million, making it one of the world's most populous countries. Its capital is Manila and its most populous city is Quezon City. The country is bordered by the South China Sea to the west, the Philippine Sea to the east, and the Celebes Sea to the south.

History

The archipelago was settled by Austronesian peoples and developed trading polities before Ferdinand Magellan's arrival in 1521 began Spanish colonisation, which lasted until 1898. After the Spanish–American War the islands passed to the United States; the Commonwealth period ended with independence recognised in 1946. The Ferdinand Marcos era, martial law, and the 1986 People Power Revolution that restored democracy are central to modern Philippine history.

Geography and culture

The Philippines lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire and is prone to typhoons, earthquakes, and volcanic eruptions; its megadiverse ecosystems include extensive coral reefs. More than 180 languages are spoken; Filipino and English are official. Culture blends indigenous, Spanish, and American influences, visible in the country's cuisine, festivals, and predominantly Roman Catholic faith.
