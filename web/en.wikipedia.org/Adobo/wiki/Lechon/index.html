Lechon

Lechon (from Spanish lechón, "suckling pig") is a whole roasted pig dish popular in the Philippines and in other former Spanish colonies including Cuba, Puerto Rico, and parts of Spain. In the Philippines it is considered a centrepiece of festive celebrations such as fiestas, weddings, birthdays, and Christmas.

Preparation

A whole pig is seasoned inside and out — commonly stuffed with lemongrass, garlic, onions, bay leaves, and spices — sewn closed, and roasted slowly on a bamboo spit over charcoal, turned continuously for several hours. The result is crisp, mahogany-coloured skin and tender meat. It is typically served with a liver-based sauce or spiced vinegar. Cebu is especially famed for its lechon, often seasoned so richly that it is eaten without sauce.

Variants and leftovers

Related dishes include lechon kawali (deep-fried pork belly) and lechon manok (roast chicken). Leftover lechon is commonly cooked as paksiw na lechon, simmered in vinegar and liver sauce. Roast pig dishes of the same name are found across the Spanish-speaking world and among Filipino diaspora communities.
