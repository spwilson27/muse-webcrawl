Filipino cuisine

Filipino cuisine is composed of the cuisines of more than a hundred distinct ethnolinguistic groups across the Philippine archipelago. It developed from Austronesian foundations with strong influences from Chinese, Spanish (via more than three centuries of colonisation), Mexican (via the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade), and American cuisines.

Characteristics

Meals are built around rice, eaten at breakfast, lunch and dinner. Flavour profiles favour combinations of sour (from vinegar, calamansi, tamarind and green mango), salty (soy sauce, fish sauce or patis, shrimp paste or bagoong), and sweet. Dishes are commonly served family-style, with several ulam (main dishes) shared alongside rice. Characteristic preparations include adobo, sinigang (sour soup), kare-kare (peanut stew), lechon (roast pig), pancit (noodles), and lumpia (spring rolls).

Eating customs

Traditional eating may be by hand (kamayan), and the day often includes merienda snacks. Street food and carinderias (small eateries) are central to everyday food culture, while festive occasions feature whole roast pig and elaborate rice cakes (kakanin).
