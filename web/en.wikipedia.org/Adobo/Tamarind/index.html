Tamarind adobo

Tamarind (sampalok) is one of the souring agents used in regional Philippine variants of adobo. Although the standard adobo relies on vinegar for acidity, cooks in some provinces — particularly where tamarind is plentiful — simmer meat in a broth soured with tamarind pulp, producing a dish that overlaps with sinigang-style souring while retaining adobo's garlic, peppercorns and bay leaf.

Preparation

Fresh or block tamarind is boiled and pressed to extract its sour juice, which replaces part or all of the vinegar in the braising liquid. Pork is the most common meat for tamarind adobo; the result is a lighter, fruitier sourness compared with vinegar, and the sauce is usually thinner and less reduced.

Context

The use of tamarind reflects the diversity of souring agents in Philippine cooking — calamansi, kamias (bilimbi), guava, and unripe mango are also used regionally. Tamarind-based souring predates the introduction of some vinegar styles and connects adobo to the broader Southeast Asian tradition of tamarind-soured dishes.
