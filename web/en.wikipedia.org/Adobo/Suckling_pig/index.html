Suckling pig adobo

Suckling pig prepared adobo-style combines the young pig — prized for tender meat and crisp skin — with the marinade traditions of Spain and the braising method of the Philippines. In Filipino cooking, leftover roast pig (lechon) is frequently simmered in adobo sauce of vinegar, soy sauce, garlic and bay leaves; a sweeter relative using liver sauce is lechon paksiw, while the unsweetened vinegar version follows the adobo pattern.

Preparations

In Spain, suckling pig (cochinillo) is most famously roasted Segovia-style, but regional recipes marinate the pig in an adobo of wine vinegar, paprika, and herbs before roasting, seasoning the meat and helping crisp the skin. In the Philippines, whole suckling pig adobo appears at fiestas: the pig is cut into pieces and braised in the adobo liquid until tender.

Characteristics

The adobo treatment balances the richness of suckling pig with acidity, and the vinegar bath also extends the keeping quality of the cooked meat — one reason the pairing arose in households that cooked a whole pig for celebrations and consumed it over several days.
