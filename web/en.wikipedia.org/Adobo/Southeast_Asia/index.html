Adobo in Southeast Asia

In Southeast Asia, adobo is identified almost entirely with the Philippines, where the vinegar-braised dish became a defining element of the national cuisine. The cooking method — simmering meat in vinegar with salt and spices — has regional parallels in dishes such as Indonesian and Malaysian slow braises and Vietnamese thịt kho, though these developed independently or through different trade influences.

Philippine spread

Across the Philippine archipelago, adobo took on local forms: in the Bicol region coconut milk and chiles are added; in Batangas, achuete oil colors the dish; in parts of the Visayas, a "white" adobo omits soy sauce. Filipino migrant communities have carried adobo throughout Southeast Asia and worldwide, where it is served in Filipino restaurants in Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, and Bangkok.

Colonial exchanges

The name itself arrived with Spanish colonization via Mexico and the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade, which also brought New World ingredients such as chiles and tomatoes to Southeast Asian kitchens. The underlying practice of vinegar preservation, however, is indigenous to the region's tropical climate.
