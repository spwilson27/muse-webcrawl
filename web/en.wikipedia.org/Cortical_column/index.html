Cortical column

A cortical column is a hypothesised functional unit of the cerebral cortex: a vertical group of neurons, spanning the cortical layers, that respond to similar stimuli and process related information.

Evidence

The concept arose from Vernon Mountcastle's work on somatosensory cortex (1957) and from Hubel and Wiesel's discovery of orientation columns in visual cortex. Ocular dominance columns and barrel columns in rodent cortex are anatomical examples.

Debate

Whether the column is a universal canonical circuit or merely a feature of some areas remains contested, with implications for models of cortical computation and projects such as the Blue Brain simulation.
