The New York State Thruway is a system of controlled-access toll highways in the U.S. state of New York, operated by the New York State Thruway Authority. Its mainline runs from the New York City line at Yonkers north to Albany and then west across the state through Syracuse, Rochester, and Buffalo to the Pennsylvania border near Lake Erie. At about 570 miles, the system is one of the largest toll highway networks in the United States.

Route

The mainline is signed as Interstate 87 between New York City and Albany and as Interstate 90 between Albany and the Pennsylvania line, roughly paralleling the Hudson River, the Erie Canal, and the Mohawk River. Connecting routes include the Berkshire Connector to Massachusetts, the Garden State Parkway Connector, the New England Thruway (I-95), the Niagara Thruway (I-190), and the Cross-Westchester Expressway (I-287). The Thruway crosses the Hudson River on the Governor Mario M. Cuomo Bridge (opened 2017–2018, replacing the Tappan Zee Bridge) and on the Castleton-on-Hudson Bridge.

History

A cross-state superhighway was first proposed in the 1940s under Governor Thomas E. Dewey, for whom the Thruway is officially named. Construction began in 1950, and the first sections opened in 1954; the mainline was substantially complete by 1956, before the creation of the Interstate Highway System, into which it was later incorporated. Tolls, originally expected to be removed once construction bonds were repaid, were retained to fund maintenance and the Authority's operations.

Tolls

The Thruway historically used a ticket system on the mainline, with tolls calculated by distance traveled. In 2020–2021 the system converted entirely to cashless tolling, with gantries reading E-ZPass transponders or billing by license plate. The system carries hundreds of millions of vehicle trips per year and is a principal freight and travel corridor for the northeastern United States.
