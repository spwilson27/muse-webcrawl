26 Baku Commissars

The 26 Baku Commissars were Bolshevik and Left Socialist Revolutionary members of the Baku Soviet Commune, which governed the city of Baku on the Caspian Sea briefly in 1918. The commune, led by Stepan Shahumyan, was established in the aftermath of the Russian Revolution and lasted from the spring of 1918 until July of that year, when it was overthrown by a coalition of Dashnaks, Socialist Revolutionaries, and Mensheviks known as the Centro-Caspian Dictatorship.

After Baku fell to Ottoman and Azerbaijani forces in September 1918, the commissars fled across the Caspian Sea to Krasnovodsk. There they were arrested by the anti-Bolshevik Transcaspian Government and, on the night of 20 September 1918, were executed by firing squad in the desert between the stations of Pereval and Akhcha-Kuyma on the Trans-Caspian railway.

Background

Baku, a major oil-producing city in the Russian Empire, became a centre of revolutionary activity after the February Revolution of 1917. The Baku Soviet, dominated by Bolsheviks and their allies, proclaimed the Baku Commune in April 1918 under the chairmanship of Stepan Shahumyan, a close associate of Vladimir Lenin. The commune nationalised the oil industry, banks, and shipping, and attempted to hold the city against advancing Ottoman forces and local opponents.

The execution

Following the collapse of the commune, the commissars attempted to escape by ship but were detained at Krasnovodsk. The Transcaspian Government, fearing a Bolshevik return and under pressure from British representatives in the region, ordered their execution. The twenty-six men were taken by train into the Karakum Desert and shot. The circumstances of the killings, and the degree of British involvement, became a long-running controversy in Soviet and British historiography.

Legacy

In Soviet historiography the commissars were celebrated as martyrs of the revolution. Their remains were reburied in Baku in 1920, and a prominent memorial with an eternal flame was erected in the city. Streets, districts, a metro station, and ships were named in their honour. After the collapse of the Soviet Union, the Baku memorial was demolished in 2009 and the remains reburied elsewhere.
