Pony car

Pony car is an American car classification for affordable, compact, highly styled automobiles with a sporty or performance-oriented image. The class was launched by the Ford Mustang in April 1964, whose name inspired the term.

Characteristics

Classic pony cars share a formula: long hood, short rear deck, rear-wheel drive, a wide range of factory options to individualize each car, parts shared with economical compact sedans to keep prices low, and available six-cylinder base engines with optional powerful V8s. The target buyers were young drivers wanting style and performance at a moderate price.

History

The Mustang's record first-year sales inspired competitors: the Chevrolet Camaro and Pontiac Firebird (1967), the AMC Javelin (1968), and the Plymouth Barracuda (actually launched two weeks before the Mustang in 1964), along with the Dodge Challenger and Mercury Cougar. The class declined in the 1970s under fuel-economy and emissions pressure, though the Mustang, Camaro and Challenger lines survived or were revived into the 21st century.

Pony car vs. muscle car

Pony cars are compact and style-led; muscle cars, such as the Pontiac GTO, were larger intermediate cars built around big engines. Some models blur the line, as performance versions of pony cars could outperform many muscle cars.
