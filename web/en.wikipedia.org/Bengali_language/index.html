Bengali language

Bengali (বাংলা, Bangla) is an Indo-Aryan language native to the Bengal region of South Asia. It is the official language of Bangladesh and the second most spoken language in India, with over 270 million speakers, making it among the most spoken languages in the world.

History

Bengali developed from Magadhi Prakrit and Sanskrit. Its literature flourished from the medieval period, and figures such as Rabindranath Tagore — the first non-European Nobel laureate in Literature — wrote in Bengali.

Language movement

The Bengali Language Movement in East Pakistan culminated on 21 February 1952, when students were killed protesting for recognition of Bengali; the date is now UNESCO's International Mother Language Day.

Script and features

Bengali is written in the Bengali-Assamese script, an abugida. It has a rich system of honourifics and a large loanword vocabulary from Persian, Arabic, Portuguese and English.
