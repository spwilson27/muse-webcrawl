Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch

LocationGrand Army Plaza, Brooklyn, New York City
ArchitectJohn H. Duncan
Dedicated21 October 1892
Height80 ft (24 m)
The arch at Grand Army Plaza, Brooklyn

The Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch is a triumphal arch at Grand Army Plaza in Brooklyn, New York City, just north of Prospect Park. Built from 1889 to 1892, it commemorates the Union defenders who fought in the American Civil War. It is one of the largest triumphal arches in the United States.

Design and sculpture

The arch was designed by John H. Duncan, who later designed Grant's Tomb. It measures 80 feet (24 m) high and 80 feet wide. The granite structure is crowned by a bronze quadriga — a four-horse chariot carrying Columbia, the personification of the United States — sculpted by Frederick William MacMonnies and added in 1898. MacMonnies also created the arch's major sculptural groups representing the Army and the Navy. Bas-relief panels by Philip Martiny depict Presidents Abraham Lincoln and Ulysses S. Grant on horseback.

History

The cornerstone was laid on 30 May 1889, and the arch was dedicated by President Grover Cleveland on 21 October 1892. Interior stairs once allowed visitors to reach a viewing platform at the top; the interior has been closed to the public since 2001 due to deterioration, though restoration campaigns have periodically been undertaken. The arch was designated a New York City landmark in 1973.
