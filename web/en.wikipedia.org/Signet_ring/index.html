Signet ring

A signet ring is a ring bearing a design, monogram, or coat of arms engraved in intaglio on its face, historically used to stamp a seal into wax or clay to authenticate documents. Signet rings have been worn since ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia and became a mark of authority and identity in Europe.

History

Seal rings were used by pharaohs and officials in the ancient Near East, where the impression of the ring served as a signature. In medieval Europe, nobles and merchants sealed charters and letters with their signets; destroying a man's ring at death prevented its misuse. The Fisherman's Ring of the Pope, used to seal papal documents, is a famous surviving example.

Modern use

Today signet rings are worn chiefly as jewellery, heirlooms, and class or association rings. Heraldic signets are conventionally engraved in reverse so that the wax impression reads correctly, and are often worn on the little finger of the non-dominant hand.
