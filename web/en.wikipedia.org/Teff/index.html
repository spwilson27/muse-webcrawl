Teff

Teff (Eragrostis tef), also known as Williams lovegrass or annual bunch grass, is an annual grass, a species of lovegrass native to the Horn of Africa, notably to modern-day Ethiopia and Eritrea. It is cultivated for its edible seeds and also for its straw to feed livestock. The seeds are very small, about 0.3 mm in diameter.

Cultivation

Teff is adapted to a wide range of environments, from drought-stressed to waterlogged soil conditions, and is a staple crop in Ethiopia, where its flour is used to make injera, a spongy fermented flatbread. Because the grains are so tiny, a handful suffices to sow a large area, and the crop can be harvested within a few months of planting.

Nutrition

Teff is naturally gluten-free and rich in iron, calcium, fiber, and resistant starch. Its nutritional profile and gluten-free status have driven growing international demand as a health food in recent decades.

History

Teff was domesticated in the Ethiopian highlands several thousand years ago and remains central to Ethiopian agriculture and cuisine. Ethiopia has at times restricted teff exports to protect domestic food supply, while cultivation has expanded to other countries including the United States, Australia, and the Netherlands.
