Information security

Information security (often abbreviated InfoSec) is the practice of protecting information by mitigating information risks. It involves preventing or reducing the probability of unauthorised access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, inspection, recording, or destruction of information, in whatever form it takes.

The CIA triad

The field is traditionally organised around three core principles, known as the CIA triad:

  • Confidentiality — ensuring information is accessible only to those authorised to have access.
  • Integrity — safeguarding the accuracy and completeness of information and processing methods.
  • Availability — ensuring that authorised users have access to information and associated assets when required.

Some frameworks extend the model with authenticity, non-repudiation, and accountability.

Risk management

Information security is fundamentally a risk management discipline. Organisations identify assets and threats, assess vulnerabilities and potential impacts, and select controls proportionate to the risk. Controls may be administrative (policies, training), technical (encryption, access control, firewalls, intrusion detection), or physical (locks, secure facilities).

Standards and governance

Widely adopted standards include the ISO/IEC 27000 family (notably ISO/IEC 27001 for information security management systems), the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, and COBIT. Many jurisdictions impose legal requirements for protecting personal data, such as the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Incidents and threats

Common threats include malware, phishing and social engineering, insider threats, ransomware, and distributed denial-of-service attacks. Major data breaches at large organisations have made information security a board-level concern, and the discipline continues to expand with the growth of cloud computing, mobile devices, and the Internet of Things.
