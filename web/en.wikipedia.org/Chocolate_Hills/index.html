Chocolate Hills

The Chocolate Hills are a geological formation in the province of Bohol in the Philippines. At least 1,260 hills — some counts list more than 1,700 — are spread over an area of more than 50 square kilometres. Their grass cover turns chocolate brown in the dry season, giving the formation its name.

Geology

The hills are conical karst mounds of marine limestone, roughly 30 to 50 metres high (the largest about 120 metres), formed by the dissolution of limestone uplifted above sea level and shaped by rainwater erosion over millions of years. Unusually uniform in shape, they are often compared to giant molehills or chocolate truffles.

Legend

Local legends explain the hills in various ways: as the droppings of a giant water buffalo, as the battleground leavings of feuding giants who hurled boulders at one another, or as the dried tears of a giant grieving for a lost mortal lover.

Tourism and protection

The Chocolate Hills are one of the Philippines' most famous tourist attractions and appear on the Bohol provincial flag and seal. Main viewing points include the Chocolate Hills Complex in Carmen and Sagbayan Peak. The formation was declared a National Geological Monument in 1988 and has been proposed for UNESCO World Heritage status.
