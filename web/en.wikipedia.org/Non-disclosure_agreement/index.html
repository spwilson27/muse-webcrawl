Non-disclosure agreement

A non-disclosure agreement (NDA), also known as a confidentiality agreement, is a legal contract between at least two parties that outlines confidential material, knowledge or information that the parties wish to share with one another for certain purposes, but wish to restrict access to or by third parties.

[Image placeholder: a contract being signed]

Types

An NDA may be unilateral, where only one party discloses information and the other party agrees to keep it secret, or mutual (bilateral), where both parties disclose confidential information to each other. NDAs are commonly signed when two companies or individuals are considering doing business together, during employment relationships, in settlement agreements, and in the protection of trade secrets.

Contents

Typical provisions define what counts as confidential information, the obligations of the receiving party, exclusions (such as information already publicly known), the duration of the obligations, and the consequences of breach. Courts may refuse to enforce provisions that are unreasonable in scope or duration, and NDAs cannot lawfully prevent the reporting of crimes or certain disclosures to regulators; whistleblower protections limit their reach in many jurisdictions.

Criticism

The use of NDAs in settlements of sexual harassment and misconduct cases has drawn criticism and, in several jurisdictions, legislative restriction, on the grounds that they can silence victims and conceal wrongdoing.
