Mountain man

A mountain man is an explorer who lives in the wilderness of the Rocky Mountains and the North American West. Mountain men were most common in the Rocky Mountains from about 1810 through to the 1880s (with a peak population in the early 1840s). Approximately 3,000 mountain men ranged the mountains between 1820 and 1840, the peak era of beaver trapping. They were mainly fur trappers and traders, working for companies such as the Rocky Mountain Fur Company and the American Fur Company, or independently.

History

The era began in the wake of the Lewis and Clark Expedition (1804–1806), whose member John Colter is often regarded as the first mountain man. In 1822 William Henry Ashley advertised for "enterprising young men" to ascend the Missouri River and trap in the Rockies, inaugurating the rendezvous system: instead of permanent forts, trappers gathered each summer at a prearranged valley to sell furs, buy supplies, and carouse. The first rendezvous was held in 1825 on the Green River; the last major one in 1840.

Life and work

Mountain men lived off the land, trapping beaver for their pelts, which were made into felt for hats. They wore buckskin clothing, carried muzzle-loading rifles such as the Hawken, and often married into Native American families, learning indigenous languages and survival skills. Notable mountain men include Jim Bridger, Jedediah Smith, Kit Carson, Hugh Glass — who famously survived a grizzly bear mauling — and John "Liver-Eating" Johnson.

Decline

By the 1840s changes in fashion (silk replacing beaver felt in hats) and the near-extinction of beaver in trapped watersheds collapsed the market. Many mountain men became scouts and guides for emigrant wagon trains on the Oregon and California trails, or served with the US Army in the Mexican–American War and the Civil War.

Legacy

The mountain men became romantic figures in American folklore, celebrated in dime novels, paintings by Alfred Jacob Miller and Frederic Remington, and modern films and rendezvous re-enactments held annually across the West.
