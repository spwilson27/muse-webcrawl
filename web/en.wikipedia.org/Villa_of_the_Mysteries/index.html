Villa of the Mysteries

The Villa of the Mysteries (Italian: Villa dei Misteri) is a well-preserved suburban ancient Roman villa on the outskirts of Pompeii, Italy, famous for the series of exquisite frescos in one room, which appear to depict a girl's initiation into the Dionysian Mysteries.

The frescoes

The large continuous frieze, painted around 60–70 BCE in the Second Pompeian Style against a vivid red background, shows life-size figures across several scenes: reading of rituals, flagellation, dancing and the unveiling of a sacred object — interpreted as stages of initiation into the cult of Dionysus, or possibly bridal preparations.

History

The villa, a substantial estate with wine-making facilities including a torcularium (wine press), was buried by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 CE and excavated in 1909–1910.
