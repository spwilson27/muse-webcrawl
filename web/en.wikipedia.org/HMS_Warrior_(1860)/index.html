HMS Warrior (1860)

Launched29 December 1860
TypeArmoured frigate (ironclad)
Displacement9,137 tons
Length418 ft (127.4 m)
StatusMuseum ship, Portsmouth Historic Dockyard

HMS Warrior is a 40-gun steam-powered armoured frigate built for the Royal Navy in 1859–1861. She was the name ship of the Warrior-class ironclads and, together with her sister Black Prince, the first armour-plated, iron-hulled warships, built in response to the French ironclad Gloire. Her revolutionary design — iron hull, 4.5-inch armour backed by teak, breech-loading guns, and both sail and steam propulsion — rendered every other warship in the world obsolete at a stroke.

Service

Warrior never fought in battle; her deterrent power and rapid technological advance meant she was overtaken within a decade by mastless turret ships. She served with the Channel Squadron until 1871, then in coastguard and reserve roles, later becoming a depot and hulk. Her hull survived as an oil jetty pontoon at Pembroke Dock for nearly fifty years.

Restoration

Recognised as the sole surviving 19th-century capital ship hull, Warrior was restored at Hartlepool between 1979 and 1987 at a cost of some £8 million, refitted to her 1862 appearance. Since 1987 she has been a museum ship at Portsmouth Historic Dockyard, alongside HMS Victory and the Mary Rose, and is part of the National Historic Fleet.
