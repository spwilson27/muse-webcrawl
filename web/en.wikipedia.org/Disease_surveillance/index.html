Disease surveillance

Disease surveillance is an epidemiological practice by which the spread of disease is monitored in order to establish patterns of progression. Its main role is to predict, observe, and minimize the harm caused by outbreak, epidemic, and pandemic situations, and to increase knowledge about which factors contribute to such circumstances.

Methods

Traditional surveillance relies on mandatory notification of specified diseases by clinicians and laboratories to public health authorities, supplemented by sentinel networks of reporting sites. Modern techniques include syndromic surveillance of emergency-department complaints, laboratory-based reporting, wastewater monitoring, digital surveillance of search and social-media signals, and genomic surveillance of pathogen variants. Data are analysed for clusters, trends, and aberrations that may signal an outbreak.

Institutions

National bodies such as the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control coordinate surveillance, while the World Health Organization operates global systems and the International Health Regulations (2005) oblige countries to report events that may constitute a public health emergency of international concern.
