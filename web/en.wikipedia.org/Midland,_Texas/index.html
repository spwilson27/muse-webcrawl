Midland is a city in western Texas, United States, and the county seat of Midland County. It lies in the Permian Basin, midway between Fort Worth and El Paso on the old Texas and Pacific Railway — a location that gave the city its original name, Midway, later changed to Midland. Together with neighbouring Odessa it forms the Midland–Odessa metropolitan area.

History and economy

Founded in 1881 as a railway stop and ranching centre, Midland was transformed by the discovery of oil in the Permian Basin in the 1920s, beginning with the Santa Rita No. 1 well in Reagan County in 1923. The city became the administrative headquarters of the West Texas oil industry, hosting the offices of major producers and oilfield service companies. Its skyline of office towers, unusual for a city of its size, earned it the nickname "The Tall City". The shale boom of the 2010s, driven by hydraulic fracturing in the Permian, made the region one of the most productive oil fields in the world and brought rapid population growth alongside boom-and-bust volatility.

Notable associations

Midland was the childhood home of President George W. Bush and the hometown of First Lady Laura Bush; the Permian Basin Petroleum Museum documents the region's industry. Midland International Air and Space Port serves the area and hosts a licensed spaceport.
