Toasting is a style of rhythmic, chant-like vocal delivery in Jamaican music, performed by a deejay (DJ) talking or rhyming over a rhythm track, usually at a sound system dance or on record. Developed in the 1950s and 1960s within Jamaica's sound system culture, toasting flourished in the reggae and dub era of the 1970s and became a central feature of dancehall. It is widely regarded as a direct precursor of American hip hop, which the Jamaican-born DJ Kool Herc carried to the Bronx in the 1970s.

Origins

Jamaican sound system operators such as Coxsone Dodd, Duke Reid and King Tubby competed for crowds with powerful mobile discos. Their deejays kept dancers entertained between records with jive talk, boasts, catchphrases and exhortations, a practice pioneered by Count Machuki with Coxsone's Downbeat system in the 1950s. King Stitt, "the Ugly One", was among the first to record toasting, with releases such as "Fire Corner" (1969).

Reggae and dub era

It was U-Roy who made the deejay a recording star. In 1970 his toasts over Duke Reid's rocksteady rhythms — "Wake the Town", "Rule the Nation" and "Wear You to the Ball" — held the top three positions on the Jamaican charts simultaneously, establishing the deejay album as a commercial force. Producers like Lee "Scratch" Perry and King Tubby used dub versions — stripped-down, echo-laden remixes of songs — as canvases for deejays such as Big Youth, I-Roy, Dillinger, Dennis Alcapone and Prince Jazzbo. Toasting styles ranged from Big Youth's cultural and Rastafari themes to I-Roy's witty, argumentative delivery.

Dancehall and influence

In the dancehall era of the late 1970s and 1980s, deejays such as Yellowman, Michigan & Smiley and Eek-a-Mouse moved from the margins to the centre of Jamaican music, and toasting evolved into the faster, more percussive chanting of ragga. Through emigrants such as DJ Kool Herc, the sound system tradition of talking over beats reached New York, where it was foundational to hip hop MCing. Toasting also influenced the British MC culture of jungle and UK garage.
