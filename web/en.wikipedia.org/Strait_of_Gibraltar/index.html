Strait of Gibraltar

LocationBetween Spain/Gibraltar and Morocco
ConnectsAtlantic Ocean to the Mediterranean Sea
Min. width13 km (8.1 mi)

The Strait of Gibraltar is a narrow strait that connects the Atlantic Ocean to the Mediterranean Sea and separates Europe from Africa — Spain and Gibraltar to the north, Morocco to the south. Its name derives from the Arabic Jabal Ṭāriq ("Tariq's mountain"), after the Berber commander Tariq ibn Ziyad, who crossed in 711 to begin the Muslim conquest of Iberia.

Geography

The strait is about 58 km long and narrows to about 13 km between Point Marroquí (Spain) and Point Cires (Morocco). Surface water flows in from the Atlantic while deeper, saltier Mediterranean water flows out below. In classical antiquity its shores held the Pillars of Hercules — the Rock of Gibraltar and a peak on the African side (Jebel Musa or Monte Hacho).

Importance

Gibraltar is one of the world's most strategic waterways: it is the only natural entrance to the Mediterranean and carries heavy shipping traffic to and from the Suez Canal. The British territory of Gibraltar commands the northern shore. The strait is also a major migration corridor for birds and whales, and a route — and graveyard — for migrants attempting the crossing from Africa to Europe. Proposals for a bridge or tunnel linking the continents have been studied for decades.
