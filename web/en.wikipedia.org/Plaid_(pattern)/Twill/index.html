Twill

Twill is one of the three fundamental types of textile weaves, along with plain weave and satin. It is characterised by a pattern of diagonal parallel ribs (called wales) produced by interlacing the weft (filling) yarn over one or more warp yarns and then under two or more warp yarns, with the interlacing point stepping sideways each row to form the diagonal.

Characteristics

Twill fabrics drape well, resist wrinkling, and hide soil better than plain weaves. The diagonal line may run upward to the right (a Z-twill) or to the left (an S-twill) as woven. Common notations such as 2/2 twill describe the number of warp yarns the weft passes over and under in each repeat.

Examples

Well-known twill fabrics include denim (a warp-faced 3/1 twill), gabardine, chino, drill, tweed, and serge. Combined with alternating colour sequences in warp and weft, the 2/2 twill structure produces patterns such as houndstooth and tartan. Herringbone is a variant in which the diagonal direction reverses periodically to form a zigzag.
