Placental abruption

Specialty: Obstetrics
Symptoms: Vaginal bleeding, abdominal pain, uterine tenderness
Frequency: approx. 1% of pregnancies

Placental abruption is a complication of pregnancy in which the placenta separates from the inner wall of the uterus before delivery. It occurs in about 1% of pregnancies worldwide and is a leading cause of maternal and perinatal mortality when severe. Separation deprives the baby of oxygen and nutrients and can cause heavy bleeding in the mother.

Signs and symptoms

Classic features are vaginal bleeding (which may be concealed if blood is trapped behind the placenta), abdominal or back pain, uterine tenderness, and frequent contractions. The severity ranges from a small, self-limited separation to complete abruption with fetal death and maternal shock and disseminated intravascular coagulation.

Risk factors

Risk factors include high blood pressure and preeclampsia, abdominal trauma, smoking and cocaine use, previous abruption, premature rupture of membranes, and advanced maternal age.

Management

Treatment depends on severity and gestational age. Mild cases near term usually prompt delivery; severe abruption with fetal compromise requires emergency caesarean section, while intrauterine fetal death with coagulopathy is usually managed by induction of labour, with intensive maternal resuscitation and blood transfusion as needed.
