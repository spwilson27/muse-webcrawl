Moment (mathematics)

In mathematics, the moments of a function are quantitative measures related to the shape of the function's graph. For a probability distribution, the n-th moment about a point summarises aspects of the distribution's shape: the first moment is the mean (expected value), the second central moment is the variance, and standardised higher moments give skewness (third) and kurtosis (fourth).

Definitions

The n-th raw moment of a random variable X is E[X^n]. Central moments are taken about the mean: the n-th central moment is E[(X − μ)^n]. Moments are computed about the origin (raw) or about other reference points in mechanics and image analysis, where the same formalism describes mass distributions and shapes; the moment of inertia in physics is an analogous second moment.

The moment problem

A classical question is whether a distribution is uniquely determined by its sequence of moments. The answer depends on growth conditions (Carleman's condition); some distributions, such as the log-normal, share their full moment sequence with other, different distributions. Method-of-moments estimation, one of the oldest techniques in statistics, estimates parameters by equating sample moments to theoretical ones.
