Heathcote River

Native nameŌpāwaho
LocationChristchurch, Canterbury, New Zealand
LengthAbout 16 km
MouthAvon Heathcote Estuary / Ihutai

The Heathcote River (Māori: Ōpāwaho) is a river in Christchurch, New Zealand. Spring-fed, it flows from the west of the city, skirting the foot of the Port Hills through southern suburbs including Hoon Hay, Somerfield, Beckenham, Opawa and Woolston, before joining the Avon River / Ōtākaro in the Avon Heathcote Estuary.

History

The river's Māori name, Ōpāwaho, refers to a pā (fortified settlement) that stood near its banks. In the nineteenth century the river was a working waterway: the Heathcote was navigable to wharves at Woolston (the "Steam Wharf"), making it an important early transport route for goods to Christchurch before rail and road took over. Woolston developed into an industrial area along its lower reaches.

Environment and earthquakes

The river suffered from industrial and stormwater pollution for much of the twentieth century, though water quality and riverside planting have improved through community and council restoration work. The 2010–11 Canterbury earthquakes caused liquefaction and bank slumping along parts of the river, and changed its course and drainage in places. A shared-use trail, Te Ara Ōpāwaho, follows sections of its banks.
