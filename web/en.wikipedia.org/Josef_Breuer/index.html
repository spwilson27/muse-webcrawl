Josef Breuer (January 15, 1842 – June 20, 1925) was a distinguished Austrian physician who made key discoveries in neurophysiology and, through his treatment of the patient known as "Anna O.", laid foundations for psychoanalysis. He was a mentor and elder collaborator of Sigmund Freud.

Physiology

Working with Ewald Hering, Breuer described the Hering–Breuer reflex, by which lung inflation inhibits inhalation via the vagus nerve. In the 1870s he also demonstrated that the semicircular canals of the inner ear function as an organ of balance, sensing angular acceleration — research foundational to vestibular physiology.

Anna O. and the cathartic method

From 1880 to 1882 Breuer treated Bertha Pappenheim ("Anna O."), who suffered from paralysis, disturbances of speech and vision and other symptoms diagnosed as hysteria. He found that symptoms eased when she narrated, under hypnosis or in conversation, the circumstances in which they had first appeared — a process she called the "talking cure" and "chimney sweeping". Breuer and Freud published the case in Studies on Hysteria (1895), the founding text of psychoanalysis. The two men later fell out, partly over Freud's insistence on the sexual aetiology of the neuroses, and Breuer withdrew from the field.
