Kraków (Polish: Kraków; also spelled Cracow) is the second-largest city in Poland and one of its oldest, situated on the Vistula River in the Lesser Poland region. It served as the capital of the Kingdom of Poland from 1038 until 1596 and remains the country's leading centre of academic, cultural and artistic life.

History

Legend attributes the city's founding to the mythical ruler Krakus, said to have slain the Wawel Dragon. Kraków became the seat of Polish kings, crowned at Wawel Cathedral, and flourished as a Hanseatic trading city. The Jagiellonian University, founded in 1364, is one of the oldest universities in the world and the alma mater of Nicolaus Copernicus. The capital moved to Warsaw in the late 16th century, but Kraków retained its role as coronation and burial place of monarchs.

During the Second World War the city was the seat of Nazi Germany's General Government; its Jewish population was confined to the Kraków Ghetto and largely murdered, while the nearby Płaszów camp operated outside the city. Unlike Warsaw, Kraków's historic fabric survived the war largely intact.

Sights

The Old Town, listed by UNESCO in 1978 among the first World Heritage Sites, centres on the vast Rynek Główny market square with St Mary's Basilica and the Cloth Hall (Sukiennice). Wawel Castle and Cathedral overlook the river, and the former Jewish quarter of Kazimierz is now a lively cultural district. Kraków attracts millions of visitors each year and was a European Capital of Culture in 2000.
