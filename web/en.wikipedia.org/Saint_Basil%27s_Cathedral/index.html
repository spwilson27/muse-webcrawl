Saint Basil's Cathedral

LocationRed Square, Moscow, Russia
Built1555–1561
Commissioned byIvan the Terrible
StatusMuseum; UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Cathedral of Vasily the Blessed, commonly known as Saint Basil's Cathedral, is an Orthodox church at the southern end of Red Square in Moscow. Built from 1555 to 1561 on the orders of Ivan the Terrible, it commemorates the capture of Kazan and Astrakhan.

Architecture

Famous for its brightly coloured onion domes, the cathedral actually comprises ten churches arranged around a central core, with a labyrinthine interior of narrow vaulted corridors. It was the tallest building in Moscow until the completion of the Ivan the Great Bell Tower in 1600.

History

Secularised in 1929, the cathedral has operated as a museum ever since, with occasional religious services resumed after 1991. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1990 as part of the Kremlin and Red Square complex.
