Mari Boine

Mari Boine
Born: Mari Boine Persen, 8 November 1956, Gámehisnjárga, Karasjok, Norway
Genres: Joik, folk, world, jazz
Occupation: Singer, musician
Years active: 1980s–present

Mari Boine (born 8 November 1956) is a Norwegian Sámi singer and musician. She is internationally known for combining the traditional Sámi vocal style joik with elements of jazz, rock and world music, and for songs that give voice to Sámi identity and the pressures of assimilation.

Early life

Boine grew up in a strict Laestadian Christian household in Karasjok, in Norway's far north, where joik was regarded as sinful and Sámi language and culture were suppressed. She trained and worked as a schoolteacher before turning to music in the 1980s, initially performing in Norwegian before reclaiming joik and her mother tongue.

Career

Her breakthrough album Gula Gula ("Hear the Voices of the Foremothers", 1989), released internationally on Peter Gabriel's Real World label, brought her global attention. Subsequent albums, including Goaskinviellja (1993) and See the Woman (2017), her first in English, explore shamanism, nature and women's experience. She has collaborated widely and tours extensively.

Recognition

Boine has received numerous honours, including Spellemann awards, the Nordic Council Music Prize (2003) and appointment as a knight of the Order of St. Olav. She is widely regarded as the most prominent musical ambassador of Sámi culture.
