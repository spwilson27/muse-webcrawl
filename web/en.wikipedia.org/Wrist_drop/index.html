Wrist drop

Wrist drop is a medical condition in which the wrist and the fingers cannot extend at the metacarpophalangeal joints, so the hand hangs flaccidly when the forearm is held horizontal. It is the classic sign of injury or dysfunction of the radial nerve, which innervates the extensor muscles of the forearm.

Causes

The radial nerve is most vulnerable where it winds around the humerus in the spiral groove. Classic causes include fractures of the humeral shaft; prolonged compression of the upper arm — "Saturday night palsy", when a person sleeps with the arm draped over a chair, or "honeymoon palsy" from another's head resting on the arm; improper use of crutches; and the motor neuropathy of lead poisoning, which classically presents with bilateral wrist drop. Posterior interosseous nerve lesions cause a partial picture (finger drop with preserved wrist extension, deviating radially).

Presentation and diagnosis

Patients cannot extend the wrist or fingers and lose grip strength, since effective gripping requires wrist extension. Sensory loss may occur over the dorsal hand and the anatomical snuffbox region (superficial radial nerve territory). Diagnosis is clinical, supported by nerve conduction studies and electromyography and imaging to find fractures or compressive lesions.

Treatment and prognosis

Most compressive palsies recover spontaneously over weeks to months. Management is supportive: a cock-up wrist splint holds the wrist in extension, physiotherapy maintains range of motion, and the underlying cause is treated (e.g., chelation for severe lead poisoning). If a nerve is transected or fails to recover, surgical exploration, repair, or tendon transfers may be needed.
