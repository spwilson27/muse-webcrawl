Minimally conscious state

Minimally conscious state
FieldNeurology
Defined2002 (Aspen Workgroup criteria)
DifferentialVegetative state, coma, locked-in syndrome

A minimally conscious state (MCS) is a condition of severely altered consciousness in which minimal but definite behavioral evidence of self-awareness or environmental awareness is demonstrated. It is distinguished from the vegetative state (unresponsive wakefulness syndrome), in which patients show no such evidence of awareness.

Diagnostic criteria

Patients in MCS inconsistently but reproducibly follow simple commands, gesture or verbalize yes/no responses, speak intelligibly, or show purposeful behaviors such as reaching for objects, visual pursuit, or appropriate emotional responses. Standardized tools such as the Coma Recovery Scale–Revised help differentiate MCS from vegetative state, since misdiagnosis rates with unstructured bedside examination have been reported around 40 percent.

Subcategories and prognosis

MCS is subdivided into MCS+ (with command-following or intelligible speech) and MCS− (only low-level purposeful behavior). Emergence from MCS is marked by reliable functional communication or functional object use. Patients in MCS generally have better prospects for recovery than those in a persistent vegetative state, and some respond to treatments such as amantadine, which accelerated recovery in a landmark 2012 trial after traumatic brain injury.
