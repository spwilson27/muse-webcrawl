Compagnie d'Aérostiers

Active1794–1799
CountryFrench Republic
RoleAerial reconnaissance
EquipmentHydrogen observation balloons (l'Entreprenant, l'Intrépide)

The Compagnie d'Aérostiers was the world's first air force unit: a French Revolutionary Army company of balloonists founded in 1794 to operate tethered hydrogen balloons for battlefield reconnaissance. Its debut at the Battle of Fleurus in June 1794 marks the first military use of an aircraft.

The balloon l'Entreprenant over the Battle of Fleurus

Formation

Impressed by demonstrations of reconnaissance balloons organized by the physicist Charles and Captain Jean-Marie Joseph Coutelle, the Committee of Public Safety decreed the creation of a balloon company on 2 April 1794. A balloon school and hydrogen-production establishment were set up at Meudon, where scientists including Nicolas-Jacques Conté developed practical methods for generating hydrogen and varnishing balloon envelopes.

Operations

The company's balloon l'Entreprenant observed Austrian positions during the siege of Charleroi and then at Fleurus on 26 June 1794, where Coutelle and an observer spent hours aloft signaling reports to the ground — contributing to the French victory and unsettling enemy observers who witnessed the novel apparatus. A second company was raised, balloons accompanied French armies in Germany, and one balloon, l'Intrépide, was captured by Austrian forces at Würzburg in 1796 and is preserved in Vienna's Museum of Military History — the oldest surviving military aircraft. Balloons were also sent with Napoleon's expedition to Egypt, though their equipment was lost when the French fleet was destroyed at Aboukir.

Disbandment and legacy

Napoleon, skeptical of the balloons' practical value, disbanded the companies in 1799. Military ballooning was revived in the nineteenth century — notably in the American Civil War and the Siege of Paris — and the Aérostiers stand at the head of the lineage of military aviation.
