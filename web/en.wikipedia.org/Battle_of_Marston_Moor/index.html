Battle of Marston Moor

The Battle of Marston Moor was fought on 2 July 1644 during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms. The combined forces of the English Parliamentarians under Lord Fairfax and the Earl of Manchester, together with their Scottish Covenanter allies under the Earl of Leven, defeated the Royalists commanded by Prince Rupert of the Rhine and the Marquess of Newcastle. It was one of the largest battles ever fought on English soil and cost Charles I control of the north of England.

Background

In 1644 Royalist York was besieged by Parliamentarian and Scottish armies. Prince Rupert marched north, skilfully outmanoeuvred the besiegers and relieved the city on 1 July. Against Newcastle's caution, Rupert insisted on giving battle the next day; the allied armies, which had begun to retreat, turned and deployed on Marston Moor.

The battle

Some 46,000 men were engaged. As evening approached, around 7:30 p.m. amid a thunderstorm, the allies attacked. On the allied left, Oliver Cromwell's cavalry, supported by Scottish horse under David Leslie, defeated Rupert's cavalry after a hard fight in which Cromwell was slightly wounded. On the other flank the Royalist cavalry under Lord Goring routed the Parliamentarian right and fell to looting the baggage. In the centre, the allied infantry recovered from initial setbacks. Cromwell regrouped, crossed the field, destroyed Goring's returning horse and then fell upon the Royalist infantry. Newcastle's Whitecoats refused quarter and died in place. The Royalists lost some 4,000 killed and 1,500 captured.

Aftermath

York surrendered on 16 July and the north was lost to the king. The battle made Cromwell's military reputation and deepened Parliamentarian debates about the conduct of the war, contributing to the creation of the New Model Army the following year. Rupert, previously unbeaten in pitched battle, called Marston Moor a disaster from which northern Royalism never recovered.
