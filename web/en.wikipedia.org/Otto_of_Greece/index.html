Otto of Greece


Born: 1 June 1815, Salzburg, Austrian Empire
Died: 26 July 1867 (aged 52), Bamberg, Bavaria
Reign: 6 February 1833 – 23 October 1862
House: Wittelsbach

Otto (Greek: Othon; 1 June 1815 – 26 July 1867) was a Bavarian prince who reigned as the first King of Greece from 1833 to 1862. The second son of King Ludwig I of Bavaria, he was chosen for the newly created throne by the Great Powers (Britain, France and Russia) after the Greek War of Independence, arriving in Greece as a seventeen-year-old with a Bavarian regency council and troops.

Reign

Otto's government moved the capital to Athens in 1834 and began building the institutions and neoclassical fabric of the modern Greek state, but the Bavarian-dominated administration, heavy taxation and the king's Roman Catholic refusal to convert to Orthodoxy bred resentment. The bloodless revolution of 3 September 1843 forced him to grant a constitution, yet he continued to intervene in politics and obstructed the Megali Idea — the ambition to liberate Greek lands still under Ottoman rule. His government's tacit support for Greek irregulars in the Crimean War led Britain and France to occupy Piraeus (1854–1857).

Deposition and exile

In October 1862, while Otto and Queen Amalia toured the provinces, a revolt deposed him; the couple left Greece aboard a British warship, famously with the king still wearing the Greek fustanella. He spent his remaining years in Bamberg, Bavaria, dying in 1867. Greece chose Prince William of Denmark as his successor, who reigned as George I.
