The Nose (opera)

The Nose
Composer: Dmitri Shostakovich
Librettist: Shostakovich, Yevgeny Zamyatin, Georgy Ionin, Alexander Preis
Language: Russian
Based on: "The Nose" by Nikolai Gogol
Premiere: 18 January 1930, Maly Opera Theatre, Leningrad

The Nose (Russian: Нос, Nos), Op. 15, is Dmitri Shostakovich's first opera, a satirical work completed in 1928 and premiered in Leningrad in 1930. Setting Nikolai Gogol's absurdist short story of 1836, the opera tells of a St. Petersburg civil servant whose nose leaves his face and develops a life of its own, attaining a rank higher than its owner.

Synopsis

Collegiate assessor Kovalyov wakes to find his nose gone — a smooth, flat surface in its place. His barber, Ivan Yakovlevich, discovers the nose in a loaf of bread and tries to dispose of it in the Neva. The nose, now dressed as a State Councillor, is seen praying in Kazan Cathedral and refuses to return to a subordinate's face. Despite police efforts, newspaper advertisements, and quack doctors, the nose cannot be reattached — until one morning Kovalyov wakes to find it mysteriously back in place, and life resumes its petty round.

Music and style

Shostakovich's score, written when he was in his early twenties, is a whirlwind of modernist experiment: montage-like scene changes, film music techniques, folk instruments including balalaikas and domras, and a famous percussion-only interlude. The opera requires a huge cast of mostly small roles and a tenor of exceptional range for the Nose's owner. Its grotesque comedy satirizes bureaucracy and social rank.

Performance history

After sixteen performances, hostile Soviet criticism led to the opera's withdrawal; Shostakovich revised nothing but the work vanished from the stage for decades. It was revived in the USSR in 1974 under Gennady Rozhdestvensky and has since entered the international repertory, with notable productions at the Metropolitan Opera (2010, designed by William Kentridge) and the Royal Opera House (2016).
