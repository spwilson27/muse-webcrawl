Hardcore punk (commonly abbreviated to hardcore or hXc) is a punk rock subgenre and subculture that originated in the late 1970s. It is generally faster, harder, and more aggressive than other forms of punk rock. Its roots lie partly in the earlier punk movement and partly in a reaction against it.

Characteristics

Hardcore songs are typically short (often under two minutes), very fast, loud, and played with shouted or screamed vocals, distorted guitars, and emphatic drum beats. Lyrics frequently address politics, personal freedom, anti-authoritarianism, social alienation and the scene itself. The do-it-yourself (DIY) ethic — self-released records, independent labels, cheap all-ages shows and fanzines — is central to the culture.

History

Pioneering scenes emerged around 1978–1981 in Washington, D.C. (Bad Brains, Minor Threat, whose song "Straight Edge" named the abstinent straight edge movement), Los Angeles and Orange County (Black Flag, Circle Jerks), San Francisco (Dead Kennedys), New York, Boston, and in Britain (Discharge, whose D-beat style spawned its own subgenre). Through the 1980s and 1990s hardcore diversified into crossover thrash, youth crew, powerviolence, emo and screamo, metalcore, post-hardcore and melodic hardcore, and influenced grunge, thrash metal and alternative rock.
