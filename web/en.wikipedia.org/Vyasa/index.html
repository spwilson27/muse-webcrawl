Vyasa

Vyasa (Sanskrit: व्यास, "compiler"), also known as Krishna Dvaipayana or Veda Vyasa, is a revered sage in Hindu tradition, credited with compiling the four Vedas, authoring the epic Mahābhārata, and composing the eighteen major Purāṇas and the Brahma Sūtras. He appears as a character in the Mahābhārata itself, as the grandfather of the Pāṇḍava and Kaurava princes whose war the epic narrates.

Life in tradition

According to the Mahābhārata, Vyasa was the son of the sage Parāśara and the fisherwoman Satyavatī, born on an island in the Yamuna river — hence his name Dvaipāyana, "island-born". He was called Krishna ("dark") for his complexion. When Satyavatī's sons by King Śantanu died childless, Vyasa fathered the next generation of the Kuru line: Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu and Vidura. In legend he dictated the Mahābhārata to the god Gaṇeśa, who served as his scribe on condition that the recitation never pause.

Works and role

Tradition holds that at the start of the present age Vyasa arranged the single Veda into the four collections — Ṛg, Yajur, Sāma and Atharva — and taught them to four disciples, earning the name Veda Vyasa. The Bhagavad Gītā forms part of his epic. Hindu tradition often regards "Vyasa" as a recurring office or title held by different sages in successive ages rather than a single historical individual, and modern scholarship generally treats the texts attributed to him as the work of many hands over centuries.

Veneration

The full-moon day of the month of Āṣāḍha is celebrated as Guru Pūrṇimā or Vyāsa Pūrṇimā in his honour, when teachers are venerated. The Vyasa temple at Ramnagar near Varanasi and the cave of Vyas Gufa in the Himalayas are associated with him.
