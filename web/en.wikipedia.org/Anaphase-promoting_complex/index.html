Anaphase-promoting complex

The anaphase-promoting complex (APC, or APC/C for anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome) is a large multi-subunit E3 ubiquitin ligase that controls progression through mitosis and the G1 phase of the cell cycle. By attaching chains of ubiquitin to specific regulatory proteins, it targets them for destruction by the proteasome, thereby making cell-cycle transitions irreversible.

Function

APC/C activity is switched on at the metaphase-to-anaphase transition by its activator subunit Cdc20. Its first key targets are securin and the mitotic cyclins. Destruction of securin frees the protease separase, which cleaves cohesin and allows sister chromatids to separate in anaphase; destruction of cyclin B inactivates CDK1 and allows mitotic exit. Later, with the activator Cdh1, APC/C maintains G1 by keeping mitotic cyclins and other regulators (such as geminin) low.

Regulation

The spindle assembly checkpoint restrains APC/CCdc20 while chromosomes remain unattached, by generating a mitotic checkpoint complex that inhibits it. APC/C is itself regulated by phosphorylation and by the inhibitor Emi1 in S and G2 phases. With roughly a dozen core subunits, it is among the largest and most intricate of the ubiquitin ligases, and its dysfunction is implicated in chromosome missegregation and cancer.
