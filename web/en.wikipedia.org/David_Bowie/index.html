David Robert Jones (1947–2016), known professionally as David Bowie, was an English singer, songwriter, and actor. Regarded as one of the most influential musicians of the 20th century, he was acclaimed for his innovative work, visual presentation, and constant reinvention across a career of more than five decades, selling over 100 million records worldwide.

Career

Bowie first achieved fame with the single "Space Oddity" (1969). He reached stardom in 1972 with the glam rock album The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars, fronting the persona of the bisexual alien rock star Ziggy Stardust. Subsequent reinventions included the soul-influenced Young Americans (1975), featuring "Fame", co-written with John Lennon, and the experimental "Berlin Trilogy" — Low, "Heroes" (both 1977), and Lodger (1979) — recorded with Brian Eno and Tony Visconti.

Later work

The 1980s brought his greatest commercial success with Let's Dance (1983). He fronted the band Tin Machine, reunited with Visconti for acclaimed later albums including Heathen and The Next Day (2013), and acted in films such as The Man Who Fell to Earth (1976), Labyrinth (1986), and The Prestige (2006). His final album, Blackstar, was released two days before his death from liver cancer in January 2016, and was widely read as a farewell.

Legacy

Bowie was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1996. His shifting personae and genre-crossing music influenced punk, new wave, electronic music, and pop stars for generations.
