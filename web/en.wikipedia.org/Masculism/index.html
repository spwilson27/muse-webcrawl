Masculism

Movement and ideology concerning men's interests

Masculism or masculinism is a term used for a range of movements and ideologies concerned with men's issues, gender roles and the status of men in society. Depending on the writer, it may describe advocacy for men's rights and interests, efforts to change cultural constructions of masculinity, or — in some critical uses — ideologies that reinforce male dominance. The term is thus used in several distinct, sometimes opposing senses.

Senses of the term

In one common usage, masculism refers to the men's rights movement and related advocacy: campaigning on issues such as fathers' custody rights, conscription and military service, male rates of suicide and homelessness, workplace deaths, boys' educational attainment, and circumcision. Writers in this tradition, such as Warren Farrell, argue that rigid male gender roles harm men as well as women.

In academic gender studies, "masculinism" is often used differently, for the ideological privileging of masculine traits and the naturalisation of male authority. Some pro-feminist men's movements distinguish themselves from men's rights activism, supporting feminist goals while addressing the costs of traditional masculinity to men themselves.

History

The word appeared in French as masculinisme by the early 20th century, initially for women displaying masculine traits and for early advocacy of men's interests. It gained wider currency from the 1970s alongside the men's liberation movement, which later split into men's rights and pro-feminist branches.
