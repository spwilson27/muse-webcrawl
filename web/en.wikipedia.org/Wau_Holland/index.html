Wau Holland

Wau Holland (born Herwart Holland-Moritz; December 20, 1951 – July 29, 2001) was a German computer activist and journalist who in 1981 co-founded the Chaos Computer Club (CCC), one of the world's oldest and largest associations of hackers. He is regarded as a founding figure of the German hacker scene and an early advocate of freedom of information and digital civil rights.

Life and work

Holland-Moritz grew up in Kassel and studied electrical engineering and philosophy in Marburg without completing a degree. Working as a computer consultant and technical writer, he became convinced that networked computing would transform society and that access to information should be free. His pseudonym "Wau" is said to derive from a dog-like bark he used.

In September 1981 he co-founded the Chaos Computer Club in Berlin. In 1984 the CCC demonstrated the insecurity of Germany's Bildschirmtext (Btx) videotex system by extracting money from a Hamburg savings bank's account in a widely publicized demonstration hack, returning the money the next day. Holland wrote for the club's magazine Die Datenschleuder and helped articulate an early "hacker ethic" adapted from Steven Levy: access to computers should be unlimited, all information should be free, authorities should be mistrusted and decentralization promoted, and computers can change life for the better.

Death and legacy

Holland died in Bielefeld in 2001 from complications of a stroke. The Wau Holland Stiftung (Wau Holland Foundation), founded in his memory, supports projects related to freedom of information, including archival work on hacker history and, for a time, donation processing for whistleblowing platforms.
