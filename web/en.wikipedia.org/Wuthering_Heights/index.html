Wuthering Heights

The moors of Yorkshire, the novel's wild setting

Wuthering Heights is the only novel by the English author Emily Brontë, initially published in 1847 under her pen name "Ellis Bell". It concerns two families of the landed gentry living on the West Yorkshire moors, the Earnshaws and the Lintons, and their turbulent relationships with the Earnshaws' foster son, Heathcliff. The novel, influenced by Romanticism and Gothic fiction, is considered a classic of English literature.

Wuthering Heights was accepted by publisher Thomas Newby along with Anne Brontë's Agnes Grey before the success of their sister Charlotte Brontë's novel Jane Eyre, but they were published later. The first American edition was published in April 1848 by Harper & Brothers. After Emily's death, Charlotte edited a second edition in 1850.

Although Wuthering Heights is now widely considered a classic of English literature, it was met with mixed reviews when first published, with many critics finding it savage and inappropriate due to what they viewed as its depiction of mental and physical cruelty, challenged by its unusual structure. Though Charlotte Brontë's Jane Eyre was originally considered the best of the Brontë sisters' works, many subsequent critics have argued that Wuthering Heights is superior. Wuthering Heights has also inspired many adaptations across film, radio, and television, including the 1939 film starring Merle Oberon and Laurence Olivier.

Plot

In 1801, Lockwood, a wealthy man from the South of England, rents Thrushcross Grange in the north for peace and recuperation, but is greeted coldly by his landlord, Heathcliff, at the nearby Wuthering Heights. After an unpleasant stay involving strange events and a ghostly visitation, Lockwood asks his housekeeper, Nelly Dean, to relate the history of Heathcliff and the two households.

Nelly recounts how Mr Earnshaw brought home an orphan boy, Heathcliff, from Liverpool, who grew inseparable from Earnshaw's daughter Catherine, while her brother Hindley came to resent him. Catherine later becomes engaged to the wealthy Edgar Linton, confessing to Nelly that she loves Heathcliff but considers him socially beneath her. Heathcliff overhears only part of the conversation, and leaves Wuthering Heights, returning years later as a wealthy man intent on revenge. The destructive passion between Catherine and Heathcliff, and the vengeance Heathcliff exacts on both families, carries the story into the next generation, until the younger Catherine and Hareton Earnshaw finally bring reconciliation.

Themes and style

The novel is notable for its unconventional narrative structure, told largely through the frame narratives of Lockwood and Nelly Dean, and for its exploration of class, revenge, obsession, and the supernatural against the wild landscape of the Yorkshire moors.
