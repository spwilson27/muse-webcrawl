Kandy (Sinhala: මහනුවර Maha Nuvara, "Great City"; Tamil: கண்டி) is a major city in the Central Province of Sri Lanka, set in the island's hill country around an artificial lake. It was the last capital of the ancient kings of Ceylon and is a sacred city for Buddhists worldwide.

History

Founded in the 14th century, Kandy became the capital of a kingdom that resisted Portuguese, Dutch and British colonial expansion for some three centuries, protected by its difficult terrain. The British finally took the city in 1815 under the Kandyan Convention, ending more than two millennia of Sinhalese monarchy. Kandy was also a centre of resistance in the Uva Rebellion of 1817–18.

Temple of the Tooth

The city's heart is the Sri Dalada Maligawa, the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, which houses a relic believed to be a tooth of the Buddha. The relic has played a central role in Sinhalese politics, since possession of it traditionally conferred the right to rule. Each year it is honoured by the Esala Perahera, a grand procession of drummers, dancers, fire-jugglers and richly caparisoned elephants.

Geography and sights

Kandy Lake, built in 1807 by the last king, Sri Wickrama Rajasinghe, fronts the temple complex. Nearby are the Royal Botanical Gardens at Peradeniya, the Udawatta Kele sanctuary and the University of Peradeniya. UNESCO inscribed the "Sacred City of Kandy" as a World Heritage Site in 1988.
