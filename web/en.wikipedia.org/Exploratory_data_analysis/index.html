Exploratory data analysis

Exploratory data analysis (EDA) is an approach of analyzing data sets to summarize their main characteristics, often using statistical graphics and other data visualization methods. A statistical model can be used or not, but primarily EDA is for seeing what the data can tell us beyond the formal modeling and thereby contrasts traditional hypothesis testing.

History

EDA was promoted by John Tukey, who encouraged statisticians to explore the data, and possibly formulate hypotheses that could lead to new data collection and experiments. Tukey's 1977 landmark book Exploratory Data Analysis systematized the approach, introducing tools such as the box plot, stem-and-leaf display, and five-number summary.

Approach

In EDA, the analyst seeks to understand the data's structure before committing to a model. Typical steps include computing summary statistics, checking distributions, detecting outliers and anomalies, testing underlying assumptions, and identifying relationships between variables. Tukey distinguished EDA from confirmatory data analysis (CDA), in which hypotheses are formally tested.

Techniques

Common graphical techniques include histograms, box plots, scatter plots, Q–Q plots, and stem-and-leaf displays. Numerical summaries include the mean, median, standard deviation, and Tukey's five-number summary: minimum, first quartile, median, third quartile, and maximum.
