Christmastide

Christmastide, also known as Yuletide or the Twelve Days of Christmas, is a season of the liturgical year in most Christian churches, coinciding with the winter period around Christmas. In Western Christianity it traditionally begins on Christmas Eve (24 December) or Christmas Day (25 December) and continues until the feast of the Epiphany on 6 January or, in some traditions, until Candlemas on 2 February.

The Twelve Days

The twelve days from Christmas to Epiphany include several major feasts: St Stephen's Day (26 December, Boxing Day in many countries), the feast of St John the Evangelist (27 December), Holy Innocents' Day (28 December), and the Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God (1 January). Twelfth Night, the eve of Epiphany, traditionally marked the end of the festivities with feasting, wassailing and entertainments such as those depicted in Shakespeare's play Twelfth Night.

Liturgical practice

In the Roman Catholic Church, Christmas Time runs from First Vespers of Christmas Eve to the Sunday after Epiphany (the Baptism of the Lord). The Church of England's season likewise extends past Epiphany to Candlemas in its modern calendar. In older English usage, "Christmastide" could include the whole period of winter merriment, with decorations removed by Twelfth Night lest bad luck follow.
