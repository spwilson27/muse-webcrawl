Nepotism

Nepotism is the practice among those with power or influence of favouring relatives, friends, or associates, especially by giving them jobs. The term originates from Italian nepotismo, based on the Latin root nepos, meaning nephew, and refers historically to the practice of popes granting positions and privileges to their nephews and other relatives.

History

From the Middle Ages until the late 17th century, some Catholic popes and bishops, having taken vows of chastity, raised their nephews as children and gave them positions of authority; several popes were themselves nephews or grandnephews of previous popes. Pope Innocent XII's bull Romanum decet Pontificem (1692) formally banned the practice of transferring property and offices to relatives.

Modern contexts

Nepotism is discussed in politics, business, academia, and entertainment, where family connections may provide advantages in hiring, promotion, and admissions. Many jurisdictions and organisations have anti-nepotism rules intended to protect meritocratic standards and prevent conflicts of interest. The related term cronyism describes favouritism toward friends and associates rather than family.
