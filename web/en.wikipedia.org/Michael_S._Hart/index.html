Michael S. Hart

Born8 March 1947, Tacoma, Washington, US
Died6 September 2011 (aged 64), Urbana, Illinois
Known forFounding Project Gutenberg; inventing the e-book

Michael Stern Hart (8 March 1947 – 6 September 2011) was an American author and futurist best known as the inventor of the electronic book (e-book) and the founder of Project Gutenberg, the first and one of the largest free digital libraries.

Project Gutenberg

On 4 July 1971, as a student at the University of Illinois, Hart gained access to a Xerox Sigma V mainframe computer at the Materials Research Laboratory. Given a generous account of computer time, he decided the greatest value would come not from computation but from storing and sharing information. He typed in the text of the United States Declaration of Independence — and made it available to other users on the ARPANET-era network. That text became e-text No. 1 of Project Gutenberg, named for the inventor of movable-type printing.

Hart's vision was that anything that could be entered into a computer should be freely copied and shared. He predicted that one day the public would carry libraries in portable devices. Working with volunteers, the project digitised the classics — the Bible, Shakespeare, the US Constitution and other public-domain works — first typed by hand, later scanned and proofread. By Hart's death in 2011 the collection had grown to more than 36,000 titles; it now offers tens of thousands of free e-books in plain-text and modern e-book formats.

Legacy

Hart lived modestly in Urbana, Illinois, devoting his life to the project and to advocacy of public-domain access and limited copyright terms. He is widely credited with launching the digital library movement decades before the commercial e-book era of the Kindle; obituaries in major newspapers hailed him as an information-age pioneer whose 1971 experiment anticipated the idea that digital copies should be free.
