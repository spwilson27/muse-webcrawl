Glenorchy is a small settlement in the Queenstown-Lakes District of Otago, New Zealand, at the northern end of Lake Wakatipu, about 45 kilometres (28 mi) from Queenstown by road. Nestled beneath the Humboldt and Richardson mountain ranges near the mouths of the Dart and Rees rivers, it has a resident population of only a few hundred but serves as a gateway to Mount Aspiring National Park and several of New Zealand's Great Walks.

Tourism and tramping

Glenorchy is the starting point for the Routeburn Track, one of New Zealand's most popular multi-day walks, as well as access to the Greenstone and Caples tracks and the Dart River valley. The nearby settlement of Paradise and the surrounding landscapes were used extensively as filming locations for The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit film trilogies, where the area stood in for parts of Middle-earth including Lothlórien and Isengard.

History

The settlement grew in the 1860s with the discovery of gold and later supported scheelite mining — the area was a significant source of the tungsten ore — as well as farming and sawmilling. It is named after Glenorchy in Argyll, Scotland.
