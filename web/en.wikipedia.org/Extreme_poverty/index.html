Extreme poverty

Extreme poverty is the most severe type of poverty, defined by the World Bank since October 2022 as living on less than US$2.15 per day in 2017 purchasing-power-parity terms. People in extreme poverty typically lack access to adequate food, safe drinking water, sanitation, shelter, health care and education.

Trends

The global extreme poverty rate fell dramatically over recent decades — from about 36% of the world's population in 1990 (roughly 1.9 billion people) to under 9% by 2019 — driven largely by economic growth in China and India. The COVID-19 pandemic reversed progress in 2020, pushing tens of millions back into extreme poverty, the first increase in a generation, and recovery has been uneven. Most of the remaining extreme poor are concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa and in fragile and conflict-affected states; on current trends the majority of the world's extreme poor are projected to be African by 2030.

Causes and policy

Extreme poverty is associated with conflict, weak institutions, climate shocks, disease burdens and exclusion by gender, ethnicity or disability. The first of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals is to end poverty in all its forms everywhere, with a target of eradicating extreme poverty by 2030. Policies discussed by researchers include economic growth, social protection and cash transfers (such as Brazil's Bolsa Família), microfinance, health and education investment, and direct "graduation" programmes combining assets with coaching for the ultra-poor.

Measurement debates

Critics argue that a single global income line misses deprivations in health, nutrition and dignity; multidimensional measures, such as the global Multidimensional Poverty Index published by the UNDP and the Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative, count overlapping disadvantages directly.
