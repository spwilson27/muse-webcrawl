The Ten Commandments is a 1956 American epic religious drama film produced, directed, and narrated by Cecil B. DeMille, shot in VistaVision (color by Technicolor), and released by Paramount Pictures. Based on the biblical story of the Book of Exodus, the film dramatizes the life of Moses, from his infancy and adoption by the Egyptian royal family, through his years as a prince, to his role as leader of the enslaved Hebrews and recipient of the Ten Commandments on Mount Sinai.

The film stars Charlton Heston in the lead role as Moses and Yul Brynner as the pharaoh Rameses II. It was DeMille's final and most successful film, and a remake of his own 1923 silent film of the same title. Adjusted for inflation, it remains among the highest-grossing films of all time.

Plot

Pharaoh Rameses I orders the death of all newborn Hebrew males after his soothsayer warns that a deliverer will be born to them. The infant Moses is set adrift on the Nile by his mother Yochabel and is found and adopted by Bithiah, the pharaoh's daughter. Raised as a prince alongside Rameses II, Moses grows into a successful general but discovers his Hebrew heritage and is exiled when he kills an Egyptian overseer. In Midian he marries Sephora, and from the burning bush on Mount Horeb receives God's command to free the Hebrews. Returning to Egypt, Moses confronts Rameses with a series of plagues, culminating in the death of the firstborn and the Exodus across the parted Red Sea. At Mount Sinai, while Moses receives the Ten Commandments, the people fall into idolatry with the Golden Calf; Moses shatters the first tablets in anger before receiving a second set, and leads the Israelites onward toward the Promised Land, which he himself may view but not enter.

Production

Filmed on location in Egypt and the Sinai Peninsula as well as at Paramount Studios in Hollywood, the production was one of the most expensive of its era, with enormous sets, thousands of extras, and elaborate special effects supervised by John P. Fulton, including the parting of the Red Sea sequence. The film was shot in VistaVision and Technicolor. Heston was cast in part because DeMille thought he bore a resemblance to Michelangelo's statue of Moses.

Reception and legacy

The film was nominated for seven Academy Awards, winning for Best Special Effects. It has become a perennial of American television, traditionally broadcast around Passover and Easter. In 1999 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant."
