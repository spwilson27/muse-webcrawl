Grumman

Grumman's F-14 Tomcat became one of the most famous naval fighters.

The Grumman Aircraft Engineering Corporation, later Grumman Aerospace Corporation, was a leading 20th-century American producer of military and civil aircraft and spacecraft. Founded on Long Island, New York, in 1929 by Leroy Grumman and others, the company became famous as the principal supplier of carrier-based fighters to the United States Navy and as builder of the Apollo Lunar Module. In 1994 it merged with Northrop to form Northrop Grumman.

History

Grumman began by repairing seaplanes and quickly won Navy contracts with the FF-1, the service's first retractable-gear fighter. During the Second World War, from its factories at Bethpage, it produced the F4F Wildcat, the F6F Hellcat — credited with destroying more enemy aircraft than any other Allied naval fighter — and the TBF Avenger torpedo bomber. Postwar jets included the F9F Panther, the swept-wing Cougar, and later the A-6 Intruder attack aircraft, the EA-6B Prowler electronic-warfare aircraft, the E-2 Hawkeye airborne early warning aircraft, and the swing-wing F-14 Tomcat.

Space and civil work

Grumman was the prime contractor for the Apollo Lunar Module (LM) that landed astronauts on the Moon from 1969, one of its most celebrated achievements. Its civil products included the Grumman Ag Cat agricultural biplane, the Gulfstream I business turboprop, and — through the Grumman American division — light aircraft. The company also built the ubiquitous LLV postal delivery truck body and canoes, a reminder of its origins in aluminium fabrication.

Merger

After the Cold War defence consolidation, Northrop acquired Grumman in 1994 for about $2.17 billion, creating Northrop Grumman, which remained headquartered for a time on Long Island before relocating its corporate headquarters. Grumman's Bethpage site was progressively wound down, ending nearly a century of large-scale aircraft production on Long Island.
