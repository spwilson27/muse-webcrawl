Mini (marque)

Owner: BMW

Founded: 1969 (marque); original car 1959

Headquarters: Munich, Germany

Products: Automobiles

Mini (stylised as MINI) is a British automotive marque owned by German manufacturer BMW since 1994. The word Mini has been used in car model names since 1959, and in 1969 it became a marque in its own right when the name "Mini" replaced the separate "Austin Mini" and "Morris Mini" car models.

History

The original Mini, designed by Sir Alec Issigonis for the British Motor Corporation, was a revolutionary small car with a transverse engine and front-wheel drive that maximised passenger space. It became a cultural icon of 1960s Britain and won the Monte Carlo Rally several times in tuned Cooper form. Production of the classic design continued until 2000.

The BMW era

BMW acquired the marque with its purchase of the Rover Group in 1994 and launched an all-new, larger MINI hatch in 2001, followed by models including the Clubman, Countryman crossover, and electric Cooper SE. Production centres on Plant Oxford in England, with additional models built in the Netherlands and, for electric models, in China.
