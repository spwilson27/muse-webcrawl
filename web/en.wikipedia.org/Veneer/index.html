Veneer

In woodworking, a veneer is a very thin slice of wood, usually thinner than 3 mm, glued onto core panels of wood, particle board, or medium-density fibreboard to produce flat panels such as doors, tops, and cabinet panels. Veneer allows the appearance of richly grained or rare wood to cover large surfaces while using far less of the material than solid timber.

Production and types

Logs are sliced, peeled on a rotary lathe, or sawn to produce veneer leaves, which are dried, graded, and often book-matched — mirrored consecutively — for decorative effect. Types include raw veneer, paper-backed and phenolic-backed veneer, reconstituted (engineered) veneer, and laid-up veneer joined into larger sheets.

Other uses

The term is also applied outside woodworking: masonry veneer is a non-structural brick or stone facing; dental veneers are thin porcelain or composite shells bonded to teeth; and "veneer" is used metaphorically for a superficial appearance concealing a different reality.
