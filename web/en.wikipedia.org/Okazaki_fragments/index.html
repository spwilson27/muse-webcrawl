Okazaki fragments
DefinitionShort DNA fragments synthesised discontinuously on the lagging strand during DNA replication
Discovered byReiji and Tsuneko Okazaki and colleagues (1968)
Length~100–200 nucleotides (eukaryotes); 1,000–2,000 (prokaryotes)
Joined byDNA ligase, after primer removal

Okazaki fragments are short sequences of DNA nucleotides that are synthesised discontinuously and later joined together by the enzyme DNA ligase to form the lagging strand during DNA replication. They are named after the Japanese researchers Reiji Okazaki, Tsuneko Okazaki and their colleagues, who discovered them in 1968.

Why fragments form

DNA polymerase can only synthesise DNA in the 5′ to 3′ direction and requires a primer. At the replication fork, the leading strand can be copied continuously toward the fork, but the lagging strand runs the "wrong" way. It is therefore built in short pieces, each begun by an RNA primer laid down by primase as the fork opens new template, and extended away from the fork until it reaches the previous fragment.

Processing

When a fragment is complete, its RNA primer is removed (by RNase H and flap endonuclease 1 in eukaryotes, or DNA polymerase I in bacteria), the gap is filled with DNA, and DNA ligase seals the nick between adjacent fragments, producing one continuous strand. Defects in this maturation process cause genome instability and are implicated in some human diseases.
