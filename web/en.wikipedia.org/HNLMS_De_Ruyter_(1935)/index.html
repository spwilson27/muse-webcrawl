HNLMS De Ruyter (Dutch: Hr.Ms. De Ruyter) was a light cruiser of the Royal Netherlands Navy, named after Admiral Michiel de Ruyter. Built during the Great Depression under severe budget restraint, she was intended to be the most powerful ship of the Dutch East Indies fleet, armed with seven 150 mm guns. Originally to be named Celebes, she was renamed before launching in 1935.

Design

De Ruyter incorporated lessons from the earlier Java-class cruisers, with improved armour, modern fire control supplied by Hazemeyer, and an aircraft catapult for a Fokker C.XI-W floatplane. Financial austerity limited her size and protection, but she was considered a well-balanced design and the pride of the interwar Dutch navy.

Wartime service and loss

After the German invasion of the Netherlands in 1940 she remained in the Dutch East Indies. Following the Japanese attack in December 1941 she became flagship of Rear Admiral Karel Doorman, commander of the Allied ABDA naval strike force. On 27–28 February 1942, at the Battle of the Java Sea, De Ruyter was torpedoed by the Japanese heavy cruiser Haguro and sank in the early hours of 28 February with the loss of 367 men, including Admiral Doorman, who reportedly chose to go down with his flagship.

Wreck

The wreck of De Ruyter was discovered in 2002 and designated a war grave. In 2016 it was found that large parts of the wreck had been illegally salvaged and removed from the seabed, prompting diplomatic protests and investigations by the Netherlands and Indonesia.
