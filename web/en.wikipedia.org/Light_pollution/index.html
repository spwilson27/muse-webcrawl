Light pollution

Light pollution is the presence of excessive or misdirected artificial light in the night environment. It includes sky glow over inhabited areas, light trespass onto neighbouring property, glare, and clutter from excessive groupings of bright lights. Light pollution is a side effect of industrialisation and urbanisation, and studies estimate that more than 80% of the world's population lives under light-polluted skies; the Milky Way is hidden from about one third of humanity.

Effects

Astronomically, sky glow washes out faint stars and galaxies, hampering both amateur observing and professional observatories, which are increasingly sited in remote locations. Ecologically, artificial light at night disrupts nocturnal animals: migrating birds are drawn off course, sea turtle hatchlings are misdirected away from the sea, and insect populations are affected. In humans, intrusive light and disrupted circadian rhythms have been linked to sleep disorders, though research continues. Poorly directed lighting also wastes energy; much outdoor light escapes unused into the sky.

Measurement and mitigation

The Bortle scale rates sky darkness from class 1 (excellent dark sky) to class 9 (inner-city sky). Mitigation measures include fully shielded fixtures that direct light downward, motion sensors and curfews, warm-coloured LEDs with reduced blue emission, and lighting ordinances. The International Dark-Sky Association (now DarkSky International) certifies dark-sky places and promotes responsible outdoor lighting.
