Cardiogenic shock

Cardiogenic shock is a medical condition resulting from the heart's inability to pump an adequate amount of blood to meet the body's needs, despite adequate intravascular volume. It is most commonly caused by acute myocardial infarction (heart attack) and is a leading cause of death in that setting.

Signs and symptoms

Signs include low blood pressure, rapid or weak pulse, cold and clammy skin, rapid breathing, confusion or altered mental status, and decreased urine output. Pulmonary congestion may cause shortness of breath and crackles on examination.

Causes

The most frequent cause is severe heart muscle damage following a large myocardial infarction. Other causes include mechanical complications of infarction (such as papillary muscle rupture or ventricular septal defect), myocarditis, end-stage cardiomyopathy, severe arrhythmias, and valvular dysfunction.

Treatment

Management focuses on restoring perfusion and treating the underlying cause: early revascularisation (typically percutaneous coronary intervention) for infarction-related shock, vasopressor and inotropic medications such as norepinephrine and dobutamine, and in some cases mechanical circulatory support such as intra-aortic balloon pumps, percutaneous ventricular assist devices, or veno-arterial ECMO. Mortality remains high, historically around 40–50% even with modern therapy.
