The Kansai region (関西地方, Kansai-chihō), also called the Kinki region (近畿地方), is the west-central region of Japan's main island of Honshu. It encompasses the prefectures of Nara, Wakayama, Kyoto, Osaka, Hyōgo and Shiga, often also including Mie. Its metropolitan core, the Keihanshin area of Osaka–Kobe–Kyoto, is Japan's second-largest urban concentration after Greater Tokyo.

History

Kansai is the historical heartland of Japan. Nara was the country's first permanent capital (710–784), followed by Kyoto, the imperial capital for over a thousand years until 1868. The region contains Japan's oldest shrines and temples, the great burial mounds (kofun) of early kings, and the cities of Sakai and Osaka, which grew into great merchant centres in the Edo period. The name Kansai, "west of the barrier", refers to the mountain checkpoints east of which lay the Kantō region around Edo (Tokyo).

Culture

The region is known for its Kansai dialect, its distinctive food culture — takoyaki, okonomiyaki, kitsune udon and the broader washoku heritage of Kyoto — and for comedy traditions such as manzai. Major attractions include Kyoto's temples and geisha districts, Nara's Great Buddha and deer park, Himeji Castle, Mount Kōya, the Ise Grand Shrine and Lake Biwa, Japan's largest lake.

Economy

Kansai's economy rivals that of many countries, centred on Osaka's commerce, Kobe's port, and electronics and pharmaceutical industries. Kansai International Airport, built on an artificial island in Osaka Bay, is the region's main international gateway.
