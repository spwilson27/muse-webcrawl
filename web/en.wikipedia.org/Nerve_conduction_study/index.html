Nerve conduction study

A nerve conduction study (NCS) is a medical diagnostic test commonly used to evaluate the function, especially the ability of electrical conduction, of the motor and sensory nerves of the human body. It is usually performed together with electromyography (EMG) and interpreted by a neurologist or physiatrist.

Method

Surface electrodes are placed on the skin over a nerve at several points. One electrode stimulates the nerve with a small electrical impulse while others record the resulting electrical activity. The speed of conduction (nerve conduction velocity) and the amplitude of the response are calculated from the distances and latencies measured.

Clinical uses

Nerve conduction studies help diagnose and localise conditions such as carpal tunnel syndrome and other entrapment neuropathies, Guillain–Barré syndrome, Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease, and generalised peripheral neuropathy. Results distinguish axonal loss from demyelination, each of which points to different underlying causes and treatments.
