The Chōshū Domain (長州藩, Chōshū-han), also known as the Hagi Domain, was a domain (han) of the Tokugawa shogunate of Japan during the Edo period from 1600 to 1871. Ruled by the Mōri clan from the castle town of Hagi, it comprised the provinces of Nagato and Suō.

History

After the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, the Mōri were reduced from a vast territory to the two western provinces. Resentment of the Tokugawa endured for over two centuries, and in the 1860s Chōshū became a center of the sonnō jōi ("revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians") movement.

Role in the Meiji Restoration

Chōshū allied with its former rival Satsuma in 1866, and their combined forces defeated the shogunate in the Boshin War, enabling the Meiji Restoration of 1868. Many Meiji leaders, including Itō Hirobumi, Japan's first prime minister, came from Chōshū.
