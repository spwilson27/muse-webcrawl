Protein structure

The four levels of protein structure, from sequence to assembly.

Protein structure is the three-dimensional arrangement of atoms in an amino acid-chain molecule. It is conventionally described at four levels: primary structure (the linear amino acid sequence), secondary structure (local folding patterns such as α-helices and β-sheets stabilized by hydrogen bonds), tertiary structure (the overall fold of a single polypeptide chain), and quaternary structure (the assembly of multiple chains into a complex).

Determination

Experimental structures are determined mainly by X-ray crystallography, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and cryo-electron microscopy, and deposited in the Protein Data Bank. A protein's structure dictates its function, and misfolding is implicated in diseases including Alzheimer's and Parkinson's.

Prediction

Predicting structure from sequence alone was a grand challenge of computational biology. Deep learning systems, most notably AlphaFold 2, achieved near-experimental accuracy for many single chains at the CASP14 assessment in 2020, and predicted structures for over 200 million proteins have since been released in a public database.
