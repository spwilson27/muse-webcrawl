Imperial War Museum

The Imperial War Museum (IWM) is a British national museum with branches at five locations in England, three of which are in London. Founded as the Imperial War Museum in 1917, it was intended to record the civil and military war effort and sacrifice of the United Kingdom and its Empire during the First World War. The museum's remit has since expanded to include all conflicts in which British or Commonwealth forces have been involved since 1914, and to encourage and facilitate the study of modern warfare and wartime experience.

History

The museum was established during the First World War, following a proposal by Sir Alfred Mond, and was opened by King George V at the Crystal Palace in 1920. It moved to the Imperial Institute in South Kensington in 1924, and in 1936 to its present main site in Lambeth, South London — the former Bethlem Royal Hospital, whose central portion with its distinctive dome was retained.

Branches

  • IWM London — the flagship branch in Lambeth, extensively redeveloped and reopened in 2014 for the First World War centenary.
  • IWM Duxford — a historic airfield near Cambridge, Europe's largest air museum.
  • IWM North — in Trafford, Greater Manchester, opened in 2002 in a building designed by Daniel Libeskind.
  • Churchill War Rooms — the underground Cabinet War Rooms in Whitehall, including the Churchill Museum.
  • HMS Belfast — a Royal Navy light cruiser moored on the River Thames.

Collections

The IWM's collections include more than ten million items: official documents, photographs, film and video recordings, oral history recordings, weapons and vehicles, aircraft, and works of art, including one of the most important collections of 20th-century British art. Its photograph and film archives are among the largest of their kind in the world.
