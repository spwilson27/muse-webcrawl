Nautical mile

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A nautical mile is a unit of length used in air, marine, and space navigation, and for the definition of territorial waters. Historically, it was defined as the meridian arc length corresponding to one minute (1/60 of a degree) of latitude at the equator, so that Earth's polar circumference is very near to 21,600 nautical miles. Today the international nautical mile is defined as exactly 1,852 metres (about 6,076 ft; 1.151 mi). The derived unit of speed is the knot, one nautical mile per hour.

History

The word mile is from the Latin word for a thousand paces: mille passus. Navigation at sea was done by eye until around 1500 when navigational instruments were developed and cartographers began using a coordinate system with parallels of latitude and meridians of longitude. The concept of a distance based on the circumference of the Earth had been understood since antiquity; by the late 16th century English geographers and navigators knew that the ratio of distances at sea to degrees was roughly constant along any great circle. In 1623 Edmund Gunter suggested that the nautical mile could be a convenient unit, and in 1635 Gerardus Mercator's maps made the relationship practical.

The international nautical mile was defined by the First International Extraordinary Hydrographic Conference, Monaco, in 1929, as exactly 1,852 metres. The United States adopted it in 1954 and the United Kingdom in 1970. Before 1929, slightly different definitions were used in different countries.

Use

The nautical mile is used worldwide for maritime and aviation purposes and is the unit of distance in the territorial sea and other zones defined by the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea. See also knot and sea mile.
