Framing (construction)

Framing, in construction, is the fitting together of pieces to give a structure support and shape. Framing materials are usually wood, engineered wood, or structural steel. The alternative to framed construction is generally called mass wall construction, where horizontal layers of stacked materials such as log building, masonry, rammed earth, and adobe are used without framing.

Light-frame construction

Building framing is divided into two broad categories: heavy-frame construction (heavy framing) if the vertical supports are few and heavy, such as in timber framing, pole building framing, or steel framing; and light-frame construction (light framing) if the supports are more numerous and smaller, such as in balloon framing, platform framing, or light-gauge steel framing. Light-frame construction using standardized dimensional lumber has become the dominant construction method in North America and Australia because of its economy and versatility.

Components

In platform framing, each floor is framed separately, creating a platform on which the next level is built. Walls consist of vertical studs on horizontal sill plates and top plates, with headers spanning openings for doors and windows. Floors are supported by joists, and roofs by rafters or prefabricated trusses. Structural sheathing of plywood or oriented strand board braces the frame against lateral loads.

History

Balloon framing emerged in the American Midwest in the 1830s, enabled by cheap machine-made nails and steam-sawn lumber, and largely replaced heavy timber framing for houses. Platform framing superseded balloon framing in the twentieth century, partly because balloon framing's long studs created fire channels in wall cavities.
