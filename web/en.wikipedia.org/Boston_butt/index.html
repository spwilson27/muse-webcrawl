Boston butt

A Boston butt is a cut of pork that comes from the upper part of the pig's shoulder, above the front leg. Despite its name, it has nothing to do with the rear end of the animal; together with the lower picnic shoulder it makes up the pork shoulder primal cut.

Characteristics

The Boston butt is a relatively tough but well-marbled cut with abundant intramuscular fat and connective tissue, typically weighing between four and eight pounds with the blade bone in. Its fat content makes it forgiving of long cooking and keeps the meat moist.

Use

It is the cut most commonly used for pulled pork barbecue, where it is seasoned with a dry rub and slow-smoked until the collagen breaks down and the meat can be shredded by hand. It is also used for sausages, carnitas, and braised dishes, and can be cut into pork steaks. The name is said to derive from the barrels, called "butts," in which the cuts were packed for shipment in colonial New England, where Boston butchers developed the style of cutting.
