Sea lion

Sea lions are marine mammals of the subfamily Otariinae within the eared seal family Otariidae, characterised by external ear flaps, long foreflippers, the ability to walk on all fours on land, and short, thick fur. Together with the fur seals they make up the otariids, one of three groups of pinnipeds. Six species survive today; a seventh, the Japanese sea lion, became extinct in the twentieth century.

Species and range

The living species are the Steller (or northern) sea lion of the North Pacific, the Australian sea lion, the South American sea lion, the New Zealand sea lion, the California sea lion, and the Galápagos sea lion. Sea lions are found from subarctic waters to the tropics in both hemispheres, though they are absent from the North Atlantic. The California sea lion, intelligent and readily trained, is the species most often seen in zoos, aquariums and marine shows, and has been trained by the United States Navy for underwater retrieval tasks.

Biology and behaviour

Males are much larger than females and may weigh several hundred kilograms; Steller bulls can exceed a tonne. Sea lions swim mainly with powerful strokes of their long foreflippers, steering with the hind flippers, a contrast with the "true" seals, which propel themselves with their hindquarters. They are social animals, hauling out in large groups on beaches and rocks, and during the breeding season bulls defend territories and gather harems of females. A single pup is born after a gestation of about eleven months, and mothers alternate foraging trips with nursing bouts.

Relationship with humans

Sea lions were historically hunted for meat, hides and oil, and several populations remain threatened by entanglement in fishing gear, depletion of prey, disease and conflicts with fisheries. The New Zealand and Australian sea lions are endangered; Steller sea lion populations in Alaska declined sharply in the late twentieth century for reasons still debated. Protected in many countries, sea lions are popular wildlife-watching attractions, notably the animals that colonise piers and marinas in ports such as San Francisco.
