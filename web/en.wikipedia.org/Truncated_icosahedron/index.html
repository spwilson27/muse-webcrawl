Truncated icosahedron

The truncated icosahedron is familiar as the pattern of the traditional soccer ball.

Faces32 (12 regular pentagons, 20 regular hexagons)
Edges90
Vertices60
Symmetry groupIcosahedral (Ih)
TypeArchimedean solid

In geometry, the truncated icosahedron is a polyhedron that can be constructed by truncating all of the regular icosahedron's vertices, creating 12 pentagonal faces from the original triangular ones, and 20 regular hexagonal faces from the original's triangular faces. It is a member of the Archimedean solids and has icosahedral symmetry. Every vertex is identical: one pentagon and two hexagons meet at each of the 60 vertices, and no two pentagons share an edge.

Applications

The solid is famous as the shape of the traditional association football (soccer ball), such as the Adidas Telstar introduced at the 1970 World Cup, whose black pentagons and white hexagons make the panels easy to see. In chemistry, the carbon molecule buckminsterfullerene (C60) has its 60 atoms arranged at the vertices of a truncated icosahedron, giving the "buckyball" its nickname. The shape also appears in architecture as geodesic-like domes, in the lenses of some lighthouse and projector optics historically, and in molecular and viral structures.

Construction

Truncating each vertex of a regular icosahedron at one third of each edge length converts the 12 vertices into pentagons and the 20 triangular faces into hexagons. The resulting solid, with edge length a, has surface area approximately 72.6 a² and volume approximately 55.3 a³. Its dual polyhedron is the pentakis dodecahedron.
