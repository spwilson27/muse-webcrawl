Horezu Monastery

The Monastery of Horezu (Romanian: Mănăstirea Horezu) is a Romanian Orthodox monastery in the town of Horezu, Vâlcea County, Romania. It was founded between 1690 and 1693 by Prince Constantin Brâncoveanu and is considered a masterpiece of the Brâncovenesc style, the only purely Romanian architectural style, combining local traditions with Byzantine and late Renaissance elements.

Architecture

The monastery's church, dedicated to Saints Constantine and Helena, is noted for its rich sculptural decoration, carved stone columns and balustrades, and frescoes painted by the masters Constantinos and Ioan. The complex includes the prince's residence, monks' cells, a refectory, and the chapel of the Holy Archangels. The school of painting and sculpture established at Horezu in the 18th century influenced religious art throughout Wallachia and Transylvania.

Recognition

Horezu Monastery was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993. It remains an active convent of nuns and a major pilgrimage and tourist destination. The nearby town of Horezu is also famous for its traditional pottery, which was inscribed on UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2012.
