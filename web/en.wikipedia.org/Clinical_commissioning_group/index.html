Clinical commissioning group

Clinical commissioning groups (CCGs) were National Health Service organisations in England that commissioned — that is, planned and purchased — most hospital and community health services for their local populations. Created by the Health and Social Care Act 2012, they took over commissioning responsibilities from primary care trusts in April 2013. All general practices in England were required to belong to a CCG, and the groups were led by elected GP representatives working with other clinicians and managers. CCGs were abolished on 1 July 2022 under the Health and Care Act 2022 and replaced by integrated care boards.

Responsibilities

CCGs commissioned elective hospital care, rehabilitation, urgent and emergency care including out-of-hours services, most community health services, maternity and children's healthcare, mental health and learning disability services, and continuing healthcare. Together they controlled roughly two-thirds of the NHS England budget. Primary care itself, dentistry, and specialised services remained the responsibility of NHS England.

Structure and mergers

Initially 211 CCGs were established. Their numbers fell steadily through mergers intended to align them with sustainability and transformation partnerships, reaching 135 by 2020 and 106 by 2021. Each CCG had a governing body with an accountable officer, lay members, and a chief finance officer, and was held to account by NHS England through an annual assurance process.

Abolition

The Health and Care Act 2022 dissolved CCGs and transferred their functions, staff, and assets to 42 integrated care boards within integrated care systems, intended to promote collaboration rather than competition between NHS organisations.
