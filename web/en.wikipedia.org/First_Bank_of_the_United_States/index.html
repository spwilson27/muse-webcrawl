First Bank of the United States

The First Bank of the United States, formally the Bank of the United States, was a national bank and mint chartered by the United States Congress in 1791, based on Alexander Hamilton's financial plan.

Background

As first Secretary of the Treasury, Hamilton proposed a national bank to stabilize credit, handle government funds, and issue a uniform currency.

Charter debate

Opponents such as Thomas Jefferson and James Madison argued the Constitution gave Congress no such power; Hamilton's doctrine of implied powers persuaded President George Washington to sign the bill on 25 February 1791.

History and closure

The bank was capitalized at $10 million and operated branches in major ports. In 1811 its charter was not renewed (by one vote in each house), leading to the Second Bank of the United States in 1816.
