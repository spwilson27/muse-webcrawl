Cost of capital

The cost of capital is the rate of return that a company must earn on its investment projects to maintain its market value and attract necessary funds. It is the minimum return investors expect for providing capital to the company, thus setting a benchmark that a new investment has to meet.

Components

Capital can come from debt, preferred stock, and common equity. The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) weights each source by its proportion in the target capital structure. Cost of debt is the after-tax interest rate; cost of equity is often estimated with the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM), which relates expected return to risk-free rate, beta, and market risk premium.

Uses

Firms use cost of capital as a hurdle rate in capital budgeting, a discount rate for net present value, and in valuation. It reflects opportunity cost and risk.
