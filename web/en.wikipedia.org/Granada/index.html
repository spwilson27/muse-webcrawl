Granada is the capital city of the province of Granada, in the autonomous community of Andalusia, Spain. It lies at the foot of the Sierra Nevada mountains, at the confluence of four rivers — the Darro, Genil, Monachil, and Beiro — at an elevation of about 738 metres above sea level. The city is world-famous for the Alhambra, the vast Moorish palace and fortress complex that overlooks it, and for having been the last stronghold of Muslim rule in Iberia, surrendered to the Catholic Monarchs in 1492.

History

An Iberian and then Roman settlement (Iliberis) stood in the area. After the Muslim conquest of Iberia in 711, settlement shifted to the Albaicín hill. Granada rose to prominence as the capital of the Zirid taifa in the 11th century and, from 1238, of the Nasrid Emirate of Granada — the last Muslim state in Iberia, which survived for over 250 years as a tributary of Castile and produced the Alhambra and Generalife palaces. On 2 January 1492, Emir Muhammad XII (Boabdil) surrendered the city to Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile, completing the Reconquista; the same year, the Alhambra Decree expelled Spain's Jews. Morisco revolts in the Albaicín (1499) and the Alpujarras (1568) followed, ending in the expulsion of the Moriscos.

Sights and culture

The Alhambra, Generalife, and the Albaicín quarter are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Other landmarks include the Renaissance cathedral and the Royal Chapel, burial place of the Catholic Monarchs. The University of Granada, founded in 1531, is one of Spain's largest, and the city has a major student population. Federico García Lorca, born in the province, is its most celebrated modern cultural figure.
