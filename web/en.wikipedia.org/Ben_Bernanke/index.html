Ben Shalom Bernanke (born 13 December 1953) is an American economist who served as the 14th chairman of the Federal Reserve from 2006 to 2014. A scholar of the Great Depression, he guided US monetary policy through the 2007–2008 financial crisis and the Great Recession.

Career

Bernanke taught at Stanford and then Princeton, where he chaired the economics department, before serving as a Federal Reserve governor (2002–2005) and chairman of President George W. Bush's Council of Economic Advisers. As governor he gave the influential 2002 speech on deflation prevention that earned him the nickname "Helicopter Ben".

Chairmanship and legacy

As chair, Bernanke cut interest rates to near zero, launched quantitative easing, and coordinated emergency lending during the crisis — policies credited with preventing a second depression but criticised for rescuing financial institutions. His memoir, The Courage to Act (2015), defends those decisions. In 2022 he shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Douglas Diamond and Philip Dybvig for research on banks and financial crises. See also Federal Reserve and 2008 financial crisis.
