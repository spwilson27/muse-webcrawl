The Rugby Football League (RFL) is the governing body for professional rugby league in England. Based in Manchester, it administers the England national rugby league team, the Challenge Cup, Super League, and the professional league pyramid that also includes the Championship and League 1. The organisation was founded in 1895 as the Northern Rugby Football Union at the George Hotel in Huddersfield, when 21 clubs resigned from the Rugby Football Union in protest at its refusal to allow "broken-time" payments to compensate working-class players for time lost from work.

History

The 1895 schism split English rugby in two and gave birth to the code that became rugby league, with 13-a-side teams, play-the-ball restarts and other rule changes intended to make the game faster and more spectator-friendly. The body renamed itself the Rugby Football League in 1922. The sport spread from its heartlands in Lancashire, Yorkshire and Cumberland to Australia, New Zealand, France and elsewhere, and the RFL became a founding member of the Rugby League International Federation.

Competitions and role

The RFL runs the Challenge Cup, first contested in 1896–97 and famous for its Wembley finals, and oversees the professional men's, women's and wheelchair games in England, including the Women's Super League and the Wheelchair Super League. In 1996 the top division was rebranded as Super League and switched to a summer season. Since 2023 the commercial and strategic direction of the elite game has been developed in partnership with IMG under a long-term agreement. The RFL is a member of the European Rugby League and Rugby League International Federation and is recognised by Sport England as the national governing body.
