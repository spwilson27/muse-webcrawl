Ellipsis

The ellipsis (plural: ellipses; from the Ancient Greek ἔλλειψις, "omission" or "falling short") is a series of dots that indicates an intentional omission of a word, sentence, or whole section from a text without altering its original meaning. The plural of this word is formed by replacing the s with e. Depending on their context and placement in a sentence, ellipses can indicate an unfinished thought, a leading statement, a slight pause, an echoing voice, or a nervous or awkward silence. Aposiopesis is the use of an ellipsis to trail off into silence, for example: "But I thought he was..."

Usage in English

In quoted material, an ellipsis marks omitted words; in dialogue and informal writing it can signal hesitation or a thought trailing off. Style guides differ on whether the ellipsis should be three spaced full stops (. . .) or the single precomposed character (…), and on whether a fourth dot is needed when the omission includes the end of a sentence.

In other contexts

In mathematics and computing, ellipses appear in sequences and ranges, such as 1, 2, 3, …, and in user-interface design three dots conventionally indicate "more options" or an action in progress.
