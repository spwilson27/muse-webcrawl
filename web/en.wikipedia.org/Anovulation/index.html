Anovulation

Anovulation is when the ovaries do not release an oocyte during a menstrual cycle. Therefore, ovulation does not take place. However, a woman who does not ovulate at each menstrual cycle is not necessarily going through menopause. Chronic anovulation is a common cause of infertility.

Causes

In addition to puberty, pregnancy, lactation and menopause, causes of anovulation include polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), hypothalamic dysfunction from stress, weight loss or intense exercise, hyperprolactinemia, thyroid disorders, premature ovarian insufficiency and certain medications such as hormonal contraceptives.

Signs and diagnosis

Anovulatory cycles may present as irregular or absent menstrual periods, or, confusingly, as apparently regular bleeding that is not preceded by ovulation. Diagnosis may involve tracking basal body temperature, urinary luteinizing hormone testing, serum progesterone measurement in the mid-luteal phase, and ultrasound monitoring of follicular development.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause: weight management and metformin for PCOS, dopamine agonists for hyperprolactinemia, and ovulation-induction agents such as letrozole or clomifene when pregnancy is desired.
