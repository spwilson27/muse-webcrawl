Simonides of Ceos

Bornc. 556 BC, Ioulis, Ceos
Diedc. 468 BC, Acragas, Sicily
OccupationLyric poet

Simonides of Ceos (Greek: Σιμωνίδης ὁ Κεῖος; c. 556 – 468 BC) was a Greek lyric poet, later included in the canonical list of nine lyric poets by the scholars of Hellenistic Alexandria. His conventional biography, assembled from later anecdotes, portrays a long and well-travelled life at the courts of rulers across the Greek world, including Hipparchus in Athens, the Scopadae of Thessaly, and Hiero I of Syracuse.

The banquet at Pharsalus

Cicero tells the famous story that Simonides was reciting a poem in praise of the Thessalian noble Scopas when the roof of the banquet hall collapsed, killing everyone inside except the poet, who had been called outside moments before. Simonides was able to identify the crushed bodies by remembering where each guest had been sitting — an experience said to have inspired his invention of the method of loci, the memory technique of the "memory palace".

Epitaphs and the Persian Wars

Simonides composed epitaphs for the Greek dead of the Persian Wars. The most famous, attributed to him, commemorates the Spartans who fell at Thermopylae:

Go tell the Spartans, passerby,
That here by Spartan law we lie.

Only fragments of his poetry survive, quoted by later authors or preserved on papyrus, but they show a poet noted for pathos and emotional directness; his lament for the dead and his "Danae" fragment were admired throughout antiquity.
