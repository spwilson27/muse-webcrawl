The Royal Navy Police (RNP) is the service police branch of the Royal Navy and Royal Marines. It was formerly known as the Royal Navy Regulating Branch. Members, traditionally known as "Regulators", enforce law and discipline, prevent crime and investigate offences within the Naval Service.

History

Policing in the Royal Navy was historically carried out by Masters-at-Arms, whose role dated back centuries. The Regulating Branch was formalised in the 20th century, and in 2007 the branch was renamed the Royal Navy Police, aligning it with the other service police forces.

Role

RNP personnel provide general policing on ships and shore establishments, conduct criminal investigations through their Service Police Crime Bureau, and undertake close protection and other specialist duties. Co-sworn personnel also serve with the Special Investigation Branch capability alongside the other services.

Organisation

The branch is led by the Provost Marshal (Navy). RNP units are based at the main naval bases of Portsmouth, Plymouth (Devonport) and Clyde (Faslane), and personnel deploy at sea in larger warships.
