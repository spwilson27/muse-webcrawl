Kurdistan Workers' Party

The Kurdistan Workers' Party (Kurdish: Partiya Karkerên Kurdistanê, PKK) is a Kurdish militant political organisation and armed guerrilla movement founded on 27 November 1978 by Abdullah Öcalan. It has waged an insurgency against the Turkish state since 1984, initially seeking an independent Kurdish state and later advocating autonomy and Kurdish rights within Turkey under the ideology of democratic confederalism.

History

The PKK emerged from the radical Turkish left of the 1970s. After the 1980 military coup in Turkey, much of its leadership fled to Syria and Lebanon's Bekaa Valley. Its armed campaign began in August 1984 with attacks in Eruh and Şemdinli. Öcalan was expelled from Syria in 1998 and captured in Nairobi in 1999; he remains imprisoned on İmralı Island. Ceasefires and a peace process (2013–2015) alternated with renewed fighting.

Designation

The PKK is designated a terrorist organisation by Turkey, the United States, the European Union and several other states and organisations.
