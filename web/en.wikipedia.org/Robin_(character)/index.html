Robin is the alias of several fictional superheroes appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. The character was created by Bob Kane, Bill Finger, and Jerry Robinson as Batman's youthful sidekick and crime-fighting partner. The first Robin, Dick Grayson, debuted in Detective Comics #38 in April 1940, and the identity — popularly known as "the Boy Wonder" — became one of the most enduring in comics.

Creation and concept

Robin was introduced a year after Batman's own debut, partly to soften the Dark Knight's grim tone and partly to give Batman someone to talk to, in the manner of Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson. Artist Jerry Robinson is generally credited with the name and the medieval-page inspired costume, drawing on illustrations of Robin Hood by N. C. Wyeth. The addition of a young partner hugely increased Batman's popularity, and for decades Robin shared equal billing in the feature.

Dick Grayson

The original Robin, Dick Grayson, was a young circus acrobat whose parents, the Flying Graysons, were murdered by a gangster. Bruce Wayne took him in as his ward, and Grayson became Robin, fighting alongside Batman while also co-founding the Teen Titans. As an adult, Grayson gave up the Robin identity and became the independent hero Nightwing. He has also served as Batman himself during periods when Bruce Wayne was absent or presumed dead.

Jason Todd

Jason Todd, a street orphan whom Batman caught trying to steal the Batmobile's tyres, became the second Robin in the 1980s. A more rebellious and unpopular incarnation, he was killed by the Joker in the famous storyline "A Death in the Family" (1988), after readers voted by telephone for his death. Todd was resurrected decades later and returned as the violent antihero the Red Hood.

Tim Drake

Tim Drake, introduced in 1989, deduced Batman and Robin's secret identities as a boy and earned the Robin role after Todd's death, arguing that "Batman needs a Robin." Drake was characterised as the most intellectual of the Robins and later adopted the identity Red Robin. He starred in the first ongoing Robin solo series and was a founding member of Young Justice.

Stephanie Brown and Damian Wayne

Stephanie Brown, formerly the Spoiler, briefly served as Robin in 2004, the first female Robin in mainstream continuity. Damian Wayne — the son of Bruce Wayne and Talia al Ghul, raised by the League of Assassins — became Robin in 2009 and has remained the principal holder of the mantle in the modern era, his arrogance and lethal training contrasting with his father's code.

In other media

Robin was famously portrayed by Burt Ward opposite Adam West in the 1966 Batman television series, which fixed the exclamations of "Holy —, Batman!" in popular culture. Chris O'Donnell played Dick Grayson in the 1990s films Batman Forever and Batman & Robin. The character has appeared extensively in animation, including Batman: The Animated Series, Teen Titans, and Young Justice, and in the live-action series Titans, where several Robins feature.
