Structure gauge

A structure gauge, also called the minimum structure outline, is a diagram or physical structure that defines the maximum size of a railway vehicle and its load relative to the space that must be kept clear of lineside structures. It is closely related to the loading gauge, which specifies the maximum dimensions of the vehicle itself, and to the kinematic envelope, which accounts for the movement and sway of vehicles in motion.

Definition

The structure gauge is set out by the civil engineering requirements of the railway: tunnels, bridges, platforms, signal gantries and other structures must all lie outside it, with allowances made for track curvature, cant (superelevation), and the dynamic behaviour of passing trains. The difference between the loading gauge and the structure gauge provides the safety clearance between a moving train and fixed infrastructure.

Application

Different railways adopt different structure gauges. In Great Britain, a series of standard gauges (such as W6, W8, W10 and W12) govern freight routes, with larger gauges progressively allowing taller and wider intermodal containers. On high-speed and newly built lines, such as those constructed to the European loading gauge standards (for example the GC gauge used for much of continental Europe's high-speed network), considerably more generous clearances apply.

When an out-of-gauge load must travel, engineers check its dimensions against the structure gauge of every section of the route, sometimes using physical gauging runs or laser-based measuring trains.
