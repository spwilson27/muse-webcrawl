Siyi

Siyi (Four Counties) Region: Pearl River Delta, Guangdong, China
Counties: Xinhui, Taishan, Kaiping, Enping
Dialect: Siyi Yue (Taishanese)

Siyi (Chinese: 四邑; "Four Counties") is a region in the Pearl River Delta of Guangdong province, China, comprising the four county-level divisions of Xinhui, Taishan, Kaiping and Enping, all administered by the prefecture-level city of Jiangmen. The region is sometimes expanded to a fifth county, Heshan, in which case it is known as Wuyi, the "Five Counties".

Language

The Siyi region is known for its distinctive variety of Yue Chinese, Siyi dialect, of which the best-known form is Taishanese (Hoisanese). Once the predominant Chinese variety spoken in North American Chinatowns owing to the region's heavy emigration, Taishanese differs markedly from standard Cantonese and is only partially mutually intelligible with it.

Emigration

From the nineteenth century, large numbers of people from Siyi emigrated overseas, particularly to the United States, Canada and Southeast Asia, driven by poverty, land pressure and the upheavals of the Taiping era. Remittances from emigrants funded the construction of the region's famous diaolou — multi-storey fortified watchtowers blending Chinese and Western architectural styles — most notably in Kaiping, where the Kaiping Diaolou and Villages were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007.
