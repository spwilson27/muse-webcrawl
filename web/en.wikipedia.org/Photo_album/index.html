Photo album

A photo album is a book or folder used to store, organise, and display a collection of photographs. Photo albums range from handmade scrapbooks with handwritten captions to commercially produced volumes with plastic sleeves or self-adhesive pages. They are commonly used to preserve family memories, document travel, and commemorate events such as weddings and birthdays.

A traditional photo album with photographs mounted in clear sleeves.

History

Photo albums became popular shortly after the invention of photography in the 19th century. The carte de visite portrait format, patented in 1854, led to albums with slots designed to hold the small cards. In the 20th century, roll film and inexpensive prints made family albums a household staple. The introduction of "magnetic" self-adhesive albums in the 1970s later proved damaging to prints, as the adhesives yellowed and became difficult to remove.

Types

Common types include slip-in albums with clear plastic pockets, ring-binder albums, post-bound albums, and traditional paper-page albums in which prints are fixed with photo corners or dry mounting. Scrapbooking combines photographs with decorative papers, journaling, and memorabilia.

Digital albums

With digital photography, physical albums have been partly replaced by online galleries, photo-sharing services, and printed photo books produced from digital files. Nevertheless, printed albums remain valued for their durability, tactile quality, and independence from changing file formats.

Preservation

Archivists recommend acid-free, lignin-free materials for long-term storage, since acidic papers and adhesives accelerate the fading and yellowing of photographs. Albums should be stored in cool, dry, dark conditions.
