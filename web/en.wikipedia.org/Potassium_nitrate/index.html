Potassium nitrate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Potassium nitrate crystals, historically known as saltpetre.

Potassium nitrate is a chemical compound with the formula KNO3. It is the nitrate salt of potassium, and is known historically as saltpetre (or saltpeter in American English). It is a colourless crystalline solid that dissolves readily in water. Potassium nitrate is a strong oxidiser and is best known as the principal oxidising ingredient of gunpowder (black powder), in which it is combined with charcoal and sulfur.

Potassium nitrate

Formula: KNO3

Molar mass: 101.10 g/mol

Appearance: White to colourless crystals

Melting point: 334 °C

Other names: Saltpetre, niter (nitre)

History

Saltpetre was scraped from cave walls, stables, and manure-rich soils for centuries, and was produced deliberately in "nitre beds" where nitrogenous waste was composted and leached. Its role in gunpowder made it a strategic material: governments established saltpetre monopolies and, in Britain, officials could legally dig in private cellars and dovecotes for nitrous earth. Major mineral deposits occur in the Atacama Desert (as part of caliche, mostly sodium nitrate) and elsewhere. Modern production largely converts sodium nitrate with potassium chloride, exploiting their different solubility behaviour.

Uses

Today the largest uses are in fertilisers, where potassium nitrate supplies nitrogen and potassium without chloride. It is also used in fireworks and rocket propellants, as a food preservative (E252) in cured meats, in toothpastes for sensitive teeth, in glass and ceramics, and in stump-removal products. In pyrotechnics it gives a lilac flame. Mixed with sugar it forms "rocket candy", a hobbyist solid propellant.
