Cockpit

The flight deck of an Airbus airliner, a modern "glass cockpit"

A cockpit or flight deck is the area, on the front part of an aircraft or spacecraft, from which a pilot controls the vehicle. The cockpit of an aircraft contains flight instruments on an instrument panel, and the controls that enable the pilot to fly the aircraft. By extension the term is used for the driver's compartment of racing cars and for control spaces in other vehicles and simulators.

Terminology

The word cockpit originally meant a pit for fighting cocks, and was applied by the eighteenth century to any small crowded enclosure, including the cramped quarters of junior officers on warships. It was transferred to aircraft during the First World War. Most airline pilots and aviation professionals use "flight deck", while "cockpit" remains common for smaller and military aircraft.

Design

A modern airliner cockpit seats two pilots side by side, with dual control yokes or sidesticks, throttles, and panels of instruments. Since the late 1970s the analogue dials of older flight decks have progressively been replaced by electronic flight instrument systems — the "glass cockpit", in which multifunction displays present attitude, navigation, engine and systems information. Design emphasizes ergonomics and crew resource management: controls are standardized, checklists are used, and since the hijackings of 11 September 2001, cockpit doors on airliners are reinforced and locked.

Safety

Accident investigations have driven changes in cockpit procedures and automation philosophy: the Tenerife disaster of 1977 and later accidents highlighted communication failures, while losses of control in flight have renewed attention to manual flying skills. Flight recorders — the "black boxes" — include the cockpit voice recorder, which preserves the last hours of flight-deck audio for investigators.
