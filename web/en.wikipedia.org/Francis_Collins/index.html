Francis Collins

Francis Collins (born 1950), geneticist and former NIH director

Francis Sellers Collins (born April 14, 1950) is an American physician-geneticist who discovered the genes associated with a number of diseases and led the Human Genome Project. He served as director of the National Institutes of Health (NIH) in Bethesda, Maryland, from 2009 to 2021, under Presidents Obama, Trump, and Biden.

Research career

Collins developed techniques of positional cloning to hunt disease genes; his teams identified the gene for cystic fibrosis in 1989, followed by genes for neurofibromatosis, Huntington's disease, and Hutchinson–Gilford progeria syndrome. In 1993 he became director of the National Human Genome Research Institute, leading the public consortium that produced a draft sequence of the human genome in 2000 and its finished version in 2003, racing the private Celera effort.

NIH director and other work

As NIH director — one of the longest-serving — Collins launched initiatives including the BRAIN Initiative, the All of Us precision-medicine cohort, and efforts against the opioid crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic. An evangelical Christian, he wrote the bestselling The Language of God (2006), arguing for the compatibility of science and faith, and founded the BioLogos Foundation to promote that dialogue. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2007 and the Templeton Prize in 2020.
