Chilaquiles
CourseBreakfast
Place of originMexico
Main ingredientsTortilla chips, salsa verde or roja
Common toppingsCrema, queso fresco, onion, egg, chicken

Chilaquiles are a traditional Mexican breakfast dish consisting of lightly fried corn tortilla quarters simmered in salsa — green (verdes) or red (rojos) — and garnished with crema, crumbled queso fresco, sliced onion and cilantro. The name comes from the Nahuatl chīlāquilitl, "chillies and greens".

Preparation and variations

The tortilla pieces (totopos) are fried until crisp, then briefly cooked in hot salsa so the edges soften while the centres retain some texture. Chilaquiles are commonly served with refried beans and topped with a fried or scrambled egg, shredded chicken, or steak; in Mexico City a torta de chilaquiles sandwiches them in a bolillo roll. Regional versions include chilaquiles divorciados (half red, half green sauce) and mole variants.

Culture

A classic use for leftover tortillas and salsa, chilaquiles are also reputed as a hangover remedy (cruda) and are a fixture of fondas, hotels and home kitchens across Mexico and the south-western United States.
