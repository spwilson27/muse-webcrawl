OECD

Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development
Founded: 30 September 1961
Headquarters: Paris, France
Members: 38 countries
Secretary-General: Mathias Cormann (from 2021)
Predecessor: OEEC

The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) is an intergovernmental organization with 38 member countries, founded in 1961 to stimulate economic progress and world trade. It is a forum whose member countries describe themselves as committed to democracy and the market economy, providing a platform to compare policy experiences, seek answers to common problems, identify good practices, and coordinate domestic and international policies of its members.

History

The OECD grew out of the Organisation for European Economic Co-operation (OEEC), formed in 1948 to administer the Marshall Plan for post-war reconstruction. The OECD Convention was signed in 1960 and entered into force in 1961. Membership has expanded beyond Europe to include countries such as Japan, Australia, Mexico, South Korea, Chile, Israel, and Colombia.

Work

The OECD publishes economic data and analysis, including the Economic Outlook, education comparisons through PISA, and standards on taxation — including the base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS) project and the global minimum tax agreement of 2021 — anti-corruption, and development assistance statistics through its Development Assistance Committee.
