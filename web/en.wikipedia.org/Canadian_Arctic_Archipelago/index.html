The Canadian Arctic Archipelago (French: Archipel arctique canadien), also known as the Arctic Archipelago, is a group of 36,563 islands in the Arctic Ocean north of mainland Canada. Covering about 1.42 million km², it contains much of the territory of Nunavut and part of the Northwest Territories, and includes three of the world's ten largest islands.

The islands are separated by a network of channels collectively forming the Northwest Passage, the sea route between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans sought by European explorers for centuries.

Geography

The archipelago extends some 2,400 km longitudinally and 1,900 km from the mainland to Cape Columbia, the northernmost point of Ellesmere Island and of Canada. Major islands include Baffin Island (the world's fifth-largest), Victoria Island, Ellesmere Island, Banks Island, Devon Island (the largest uninhabited island on Earth), Southampton Island, and Prince of Wales Island.

The eastern islands are mountainous, with peaks above 2,000 m — Mount Barbeau on Ellesmere Island reaches 2,616 m — while the western islands are lower sedimentary plains. Glaciers and ice caps cover parts of the eastern islands; elsewhere the land is polar desert and tundra.

Climate and wildlife

The climate is among the coldest and driest in Canada, with long, dark winters and brief cool summers. Much of the sea is locked in ice for most of the year, though Arctic sea-ice extent has declined sharply in recent decades. Wildlife includes polar bears, muskoxen, caribou, narwhals, beluga whales, walrus, and migratory birds.

History and sovereignty

Inuit and their Paleo-Eskimo predecessors have inhabited the region for roughly 4,000 years. European exploration intensified in the 19th century with the search for the Northwest Passage and for Franklin's lost expedition of 1845. Britain transferred its Arctic island claims to Canada in 1880. Canada's sovereignty over the archipelago — and its claim that the Northwest Passage constitutes internal waters, disputed by the United States and others — remains an important policy issue, sharpened by increased shipping as sea ice retreats.

Communities

Fewer than 15,000 people live in the archipelago, mostly Inuit, in small coastal communities such as Iqaluit (the capital of Nunavut, on Baffin Island), Rankin Inlet, Cambridge Bay, Resolute, and Grise Fiord, Canada's northernmost civilian settlement.
