Schwarz lemma


Image placeholder: Schwarz lemma

In mathematics, the Schwarz lemma, named after Hermann Amandus Schwarz, is a result in complex analysis about the maximum modulus of holomorphic functions defined on the open unit disk in the complex plane that take the value zero at the origin.

Statement

Let D be the open unit disk centred at the origin, and let f be a holomorphic function on D with f(0) = 0 and |f(z)| ≤ 1 for all z in D. Then |f(z)| ≤ |z| for all z in D, and |f′(0)| ≤ 1. Moreover, if equality holds for any nonzero z, or if |f′(0)| = 1, then f is a rotation: f(z) = eiθz for some real θ.

Proof and consequences

The classical proof applies the maximum modulus principle to the auxiliary function g(z) = f(z)/z (with g(0) = f′(0)), which is holomorphic on the disk. The lemma underlies the Schwarz–Pick theorem, which recasts the result in terms of the Poincaré metric and shows that holomorphic maps are contractions in hyperbolic geometry; it is also a key step toward the Riemann mapping theorem and the theory of conformal maps. Ahlfors generalized the lemma using curvature in the Ahlfors–Schwarz lemma.
