Satoshi Tajiri (born 1965) is a Japanese video game designer best known as the creator of the Pokémon franchise and a co-founder of the development studio Game Freak.

Early life and Game Freak

As a boy, Tajiri was an avid insect collector — nicknamed "Dr. Bug" — and later became fascinated by arcade games and the fanzine culture around them. In 1982 he founded Game Freak as a self-published gaming magazine with artist Ken Sugimori; in 1989 they re-established it as a video game development company. Its first title was Mendel Palace (Quinty) for the Famicom/NES.

Pokémon

Tajiri conceived Pokémon after seeing the Game Boy's link cable, imagining creatures travelling between machines as insects had crossed between the plants of his childhood. Developed over six years, Pocket Monsters Red and Green were released in Japan in 1996 and grew into one of the highest-grossing media franchises of all time. Tajiri remains executive producer on the series.
