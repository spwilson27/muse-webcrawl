Tony Oxley (15 June 1938 – 26 December 2023) was an English free improvising drummer and percussionist, one of the founders of British free improvisation and a co-founder of Incus Records. He was noted for a highly individual approach to the drum kit, augmenting it with found objects, contact microphones and electronics, and for long collaborations with musicians such as Derek Bailey, Cecil Taylor and Bill Dixon.

Early life and career

Oxley was born in Sheffield and studied piano from the age of eight before taking up drums at seventeen. While serving in a military band during national service he studied percussion and music theory, and formed his first groups in Sheffield in the early 1960s. In 1963 he formed the Joseph Holbrooke Trio with guitarist Derek Bailey and bassist Gavin Bryars, initially playing jazz standards before the group moved toward total improvisation by 1965–66; it is regarded as a foundational ensemble of the British free improvisation scene.

London years and Incus

After moving to London in 1966, Oxley became the house drummer at Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club, accompanying visiting American musicians, and in 1969–70 recorded his first albums as a leader, including The Baptised Traveller and 4 Compositions for Sextet. In 1970, with Bailey and Evan Parker, he co-founded Incus Records, the first British musician-run independent label devoted to improvised music. He was also a member of the Spontaneous Music Ensemble circle, Barry Guy's London Jazz Composers' Orchestra, and groups led by Howard Riley and others.

Later work

From 1978 Oxley played in a trio with pianist Cecil Taylor and cellist Tristan Honsinger, and he formed a celebrated duo partnership with Taylor, documented on numerous live recordings. He also worked extensively with trumpeter Bill Dixon, violinist Philipp Wachsmann and the Anglo-German groups he led from his later base in Viersen, Germany. His playing combined conventional kit work with steel bowls, springs, chains, electric toothbrushes and amplified objects, producing a shimmering, metallic sound-world. Oxley was also a painter, and his artwork appeared on many of his album covers. He died on 26 December 2023 at the age of 85.
