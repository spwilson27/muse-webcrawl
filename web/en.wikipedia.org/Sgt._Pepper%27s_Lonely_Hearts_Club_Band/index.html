Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band is the eighth studio album by the English rock band the Beatles, released in May 1967. It spent 27 weeks at number one on the UK Albums Chart and 15 weeks at number one in the United States.

Concept and recording

Paul McCartney proposed that the Beatles record as an alter-ego band, the fictional Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band. The album broke new ground with its elaborate production, orchestral arrangements, Indian instrumentation, sound effects, and innovative tape techniques, and its iconic cover by Peter Blake and Jann Haworth was among the first to print song lyrics on the sleeve.

Legacy

Featuring songs such as "With a Little Help from My Friends", "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds", and the closing "A Day in the Life", the album is widely regarded as one of the greatest of all time and a landmark of popular culture and psychedelic music.
