Kenosis

Kenosis: the "self-emptying" of Christ described in Philippians 2.

In Christian theology, kenosis (from the Greek κένωσις, kénōsis, "emptying") is the "self-emptying" of Jesus — the renunciation of his divine attributes in becoming human. The concept is drawn from Philippians 2:7, which says that Christ "emptied himself" (Greek heauton ekenōsen), "taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men."

The Philippians hymn

The passage in Philippians 2:5–11, often called the Carmen Christi, is thought to be an early Christian hymn. It presents the incarnation as a voluntary act of humility: Christ, though existing in the form of God, did not regard equality with God as something to be grasped, but emptied himself, and in consequence was exalted by God. Paul presents this as a pattern for Christian humility and mutual service.

Kenotic theology

From the 19th century onward, "kenotic" theologies in Lutheran and Anglican traditions explored in what sense the incarnate Christ could be limited in knowledge or power while remaining fully God. Versions of the doctrine have differed: some hold that Christ gave up only the independent exercise of certain divine attributes; others, that he surrendered some attributes altogether during the incarnation. Critics, including much of the Reformed tradition, charge that strong versions of kenosis compromise the immutability of God and the full divinity of Christ, while defenders argue the doctrine safeguards a genuinely human Jesus. In Eastern Orthodox theology the concept overlaps with the broader teaching of theosis and divine condescension.
