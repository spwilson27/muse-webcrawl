Cross-dressing

Cross-dressing is the act of wearing items of clothing not commonly associated with one's sex. Cross-dressing has been practiced throughout much of recorded history, in many societies, and for many reasons, including disguise, comfort, entertainment, and self-expression. The term was coined by the German sexologist Magnus Hirschfeld in 1910 (as "transvestism"), though cross-dressing itself is far older, appearing in mythology, theatre, and literature from antiquity onward.

Cross-dressing has a long history in theatre and performance.

History

In Greek and Norse mythology, gods and heroes such as Heracles, Thor, and Loki are depicted wearing women's clothing. In the theatre of classical Greece, Shakespeare's England, and Japanese kabuki, male actors traditionally played female roles, and in some periods women performed as men ("breeches roles" in opera). Historical figures such as Joan of Arc, who wore male military dress, and the Chevalier d'Éon, who lived part of life as a woman, are famous examples. Many societies also had practical cross-dressing: women who dressed as men to serve as soldiers or sailors, or to access male occupations.

Terminology

Hirschfeld distinguished cross-dressers from other gender-variant people, arguing that cross-dressing was an independent phenomenon. The older clinical term "transvestism" is now generally avoided in everyday usage in favour of "cross-dressing". People who cross-dress do so for varied reasons: as a hobby, for performance (including drag), for comfort, or as an expression of identity; cross-dressing by itself says nothing definite about a person's sexual orientation or gender identity.

Law and social attitudes

Sumptuary laws and local ordinances in various places historically restricted clothing by sex; in some 19th- and 20th-century American cities, cross-dressing was prosecuted under anti-disguise statutes. Attitudes have liberalised in many countries, and cross-dressing is now a visible part of popular culture through drag performance, cosplay, and fashion, though it remains stigmatised or illegal in some jurisdictions.
