The history of Iran, also known historically as Persia, is among the longest continuous histories of any country. The Iranian plateau was home to early civilisations such as Elam, and in the 6th century BC Cyrus the Great founded the Achaemenid Empire, one of the largest empires of the ancient world, which fell to Alexander the Great in 330 BC.

Classical and medieval periods

Successor states included the Seleucid, Parthian, and Sasanian empires; the Sasanians rivalled Rome and Byzantium for four centuries. The Muslim conquest of Persia (633–651) brought Islam, and over subsequent centuries Persian culture, language, and scholarship flourished under dynasties such as the Samanids, Seljuks, and the Mongol Ilkhanate.

Early modern to present

The Safavid Empire (1501–1736) established Twelver Shi'a Islam as the state religion, shaping modern Iranian identity. After the Qajar era and the Constitutional Revolution of 1905–1911, Reza Shah founded the Pahlavi dynasty in 1925. His son Mohammad Reza Shah was overthrown in the Iranian Revolution of 1979, which established the Islamic Republic under Ayatollah Khomeini, followed by the Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988).
