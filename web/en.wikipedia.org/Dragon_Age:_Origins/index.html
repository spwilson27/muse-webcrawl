Dragon Age: Origins is a 2009 role-playing video game developed by BioWare and published by Electronic Arts. It is the first game in the Dragon Age franchise and was described by its creators as a spiritual successor to the Baldur's Gate series.

Plot

Set in the kingdom of Ferelden, the game casts the player as a new recruit of the Grey Wardens, an order sworn to fight the darkspawn. When a Blight — a darkspawn invasion led by an Archdemon — threatens the land, the Warden must unite Ferelden's divided factions amid civil war following the disaster at the Battle of Ostagar. Each playable character begins with one of six "origin stories" determined by race and class.

Gameplay

The game uses real-time combat that can be paused to issue orders, party-based tactics with programmable "tactics" slots, and a dialogue system in which silent protagonist choices shape companion approval and story outcomes.

Reception and legacy

Origins was critically acclaimed for its story, characters, and scope, won numerous RPG of the Year awards, and received the expansion Awakening (2010). It was followed by Dragon Age II (2011) and Dragon Age: Inquisition (2014).
