Francis Galton

Sir Francis Galton (1822–1911), polymath and founder of eugenics

Sir Francis Galton (16 February 1822 – 17 January 1911) was an English polymath and the originator of eugenics during the Victorian era. A cousin of Charles Darwin, Galton made contributions to statistics, psychology, anthropology, geography, meteorology, and criminology, publishing over 340 papers and books.

Contributions

Galton pioneered the statistical concepts of correlation and regression to the mean, invented the quincunx ("Galton board") to demonstrate the normal distribution, and devised the first weather map and a theory of anticyclones. He established the scientific use of fingerprints for identification, introduced twin studies and questionnaires into the study of heredity and human ability, and founded differential psychology. His Hereditary Genius (1869) argued that talent runs in families.

Eugenics and legacy

Galton coined the term eugenics in 1883, advocating the improvement of the human population through selective breeding — ideas enthusiastically adopted in many countries with consequences now widely regarded as pernicious, including forced sterilisation programmes. His endowment founded a chair in eugenics at University College London, later renamed in the field of human genetics. Modern reassessments credit his lasting statistical innovations while condemning the social programme he championed, and several institutions have removed his name from buildings.
