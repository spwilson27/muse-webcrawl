Genus (mathematics)

Genus
FieldTopology, algebraic geometry
Informal definitionNumber of "holes" in a surface
ExamplesSphere: 0; torus: 1; double torus: 2

In mathematics, the genus is an invariant that classifies objects such as surfaces and curves. Most intuitively, the genus of a connected orientable surface is the number of "holes" or "handles" it has: a sphere has genus 0, a torus genus 1, and a pretzel with two holes genus 2.

Topology

For a closed orientable surface, the genus g is related to the Euler characteristic χ by χ = 2 − 2g. Such surfaces are completely classified up to homeomorphism by their genus. For non-orientable surfaces, the genus (or demigenus) counts cross-caps: the projective plane has non-orientable genus 1 and the Klein bottle 2, with χ = 2 − k. The genus can also be defined algebraically as half the rank of the first homology group of the surface.

Algebraic geometry

For a smooth projective curve, the genus is the dimension of the space of holomorphic one-forms, and it governs the geometry of the curve: genus 0 curves are rational, genus 1 curves are elliptic curves, and higher-genus curves are of general type. The genus appears in the Riemann–Roch theorem, one of the foundational results of the subject. Related notions include the arithmetic genus of varieties and the genus of a knot, defined via the minimal genus of a Seifert surface it bounds.
