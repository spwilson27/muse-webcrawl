Descender (climbing)

A descender is a piece of climbing equipment used to control a climber's descent on a fixed rope during abseiling (rappelling). Descenders work by generating friction against the rope, allowing the user to regulate speed by hand while the device is attached to the harness with a locking carabiner.

Types

The classic figure-eight descender is a metal ring shaped like an 8 through which a bight of rope is passed. Other designs include sticht plates, tube-style devices (also usable as belay devices), racks used in caving for long descents, and self-braking descenders used by rope-access technicians. The traditional body rappel and the Dülfersitz are improvised methods without dedicated devices.

Technique and safety

The user keeps a braking hand on the rope below the device; letting go or losing control can result in an uncontrolled fall, so backup knots such as a prusik are often used. Excess friction can overheat devices on long descents, glazing rope sheaths.
