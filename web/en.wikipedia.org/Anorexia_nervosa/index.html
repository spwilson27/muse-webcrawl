Anorexia nervosa

Distorted body image is a core feature of anorexia nervosa
SpecialtyPsychiatry
SymptomsRestriction of food intake, intense fear of weight gain, low body weight
OnsetUsually adolescence

Anorexia nervosa (AN), often referred to simply as anorexia, is an eating disorder characterised by food restriction, body image disturbance, and an intense fear of gaining weight. Individuals with anorexia usually have a low body weight for their height and build. The disorder typically begins in adolescence and affects females far more often than males, although it occurs in all sexes and ages.

Signs and symptoms

Signs include deliberate restriction of energy intake, significant weight loss, preoccupation with food, calories and body shape, excessive exercise, and denial of the seriousness of low weight. Physical complications of starvation include bradycardia, hypotension, loss of bone density (osteoporosis), amenorrhea, hair loss, and electrolyte disturbances that can cause cardiac arrest. Anorexia has one of the highest mortality rates of any psychiatric disorder.

Causes

The cause is multifactorial: twin studies indicate substantial genetic heritability, and psychological traits such as perfectionism and obsessive-compulsiveness are risk factors. Cultural emphasis on thinness, dieting, and certain occupations or sports that stress leanness also contribute. Neurobiological studies implicate altered serotonin and dopamine signalling.

Treatment

Treatment involves restoring a healthy weight, treating medical complications, and psychotherapy. Family-based treatment is the leading approach for adolescents; cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT-E) is widely used for adults. Severe cases may require hospitalisation and careful refeeding to avoid refeeding syndrome. Recovery is often prolonged, and relapse is common.
