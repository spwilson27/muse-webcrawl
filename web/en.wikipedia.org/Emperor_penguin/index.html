Emperor penguin

The emperor penguin (Aptenodytes forsteri) is the tallest and heaviest of all living penguin species and is endemic to Antarctica. Adults stand 110–122 cm (43–48 in) tall and weigh 22.7–45.4 kg (50–100 lb). Like all penguins it is flightless, with a streamlined body and stiff, flattened flippers for swimming.

The species is famous for its breeding cycle, the only one among penguins conducted during the Antarctic winter. After courtship, the female lays a single egg, which the male incubates on his feet under a brood pouch for about two months while the female feeds at sea; males huddle together fasting through temperatures below −60 °C (−76 °F).

Emperor penguins feed on fish, krill and squid and are preyed upon by leopard seals and orcas. The species is listed as near threatened, with climate change-driven loss of sea ice threatening breeding colonies; some colonies, such as that at Halley Bay, have suffered catastrophic breeding failures.
