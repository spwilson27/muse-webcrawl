Sputnik 2

Sputnik 2 was the second spacecraft launched into Earth orbit, on 3 November 1957, and the first to carry an animal into orbit — a Soviet space dog named Laika. It was a 4-metre-high cone-shaped capsule with a base diameter of 2 metres, weighing about 508 kg, built in under four weeks on the orders of Nikita Khrushchev to mark the 40th anniversary of the October Revolution.

Laika

Laika, a stray dog from Moscow, was selected and trained for confinement in small spaces. The spacecraft's life-support system was designed to keep her alive for about a week, but no recovery was planned; she died within hours of launch from overheating, a fact not fully acknowledged until 2002.

Mission

Sputnik 2 carried instruments measuring solar radiation and cosmic rays, along with sensors for Laika's vital signs. Its telemetry system failed partway through the mission. The spacecraft re-entered the atmosphere and burned up on 14 April 1958, after 162 days in orbit.

Legacy

The flight demonstrated that a living being could survive launch and weightlessness, paving the way for human spaceflight, though Laika's fate has remained a focus of debate about the ethics of animal testing.
