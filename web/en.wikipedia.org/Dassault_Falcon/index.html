Dassault Falcon

A Falcon 7X, the flagship trijet of the Falcon family

The Dassault Falcon is a family of business jets manufactured by Dassault Aviation of France. The line began with the Falcon 20, first flown in 1963, and is noted for sharing technology with Dassault's military aircraft, including fighter-style flight controls and, in later models, fly-by-wire. Unusually among business jets, several Falcons use a three-engine S-duct layout.

History

The original Mystère 20/Falcon 20 was developed at the instigation of Pan American World Airways, which promoted it in the United States; the type also served with the US Coast Guard as the HU-25 Guardian. Successive models included the Falcon 10, the stretched Falcon 50 — the first business jet with a supercritical wing and a trijet layout — and the Falcon 900 and Falcon 2000 families of the 1980s and 1990s.

Current models

The flagship Falcon 7X (in service from 2007) and its stretched derivative the Falcon 8X introduced digital flight controls adapted from the Rafale fighter. The twin-engine Falcon 2000LXS and the wide-cabin Falcon 6X fill the mid-range, and the Falcon 10X, announced in 2021, is designed as an ultra-long-range flagship able to fly routes such as New York–Shanghai. More than 2,700 Falcons have been delivered, serving corporate, government and head-of-state operators in some 90 countries.
