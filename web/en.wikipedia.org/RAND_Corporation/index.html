RAND Corporation

The RAND Corporation is an American nonprofit global policy think tank, research institute, and public sector consulting firm. RAND Corporation engages in research and development (R&D) across several fields and industries. Since the 1960s RAND research has influenced the United States' policymaking process and changed the operations and conduct of various US public services, from healthcare and economics to military strategy. The name RAND is a contraction of "research and development".

History

RAND began in 1946 as "Project RAND" within the Douglas Aircraft Company, set up under a special contract with the US Army Air Forces at the urging of General Henry H. "Hap" Arnold. In 1948, with funding from the Ford Foundation, it was spun off as an independent nonprofit corporation. Its early work focused on nuclear strategy, systems analysis and intercontinental missile design; its analysts included figures such as Herman Kahn, Bernard Brodie, John von Neumann (as consultant), and Albert Wohlstetter.

RAND became a prototype of the modern think tank: it pioneered game theory applications, the Delphi method of expert forecasting, linear programming applications, and Paul Baran's work on packet switching — a foundation of the Internet — as well as key contributions to artificial intelligence and computer science at its computing division. The RAND Health Insurance Experiment (1974–1982) remains a landmark in health economics.

Organisation and reach

RAND operates several federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs) for the US Department of Defense and Department of Homeland Security, and runs the Frederick S. Pardee RAND Graduate School in Santa Monica. It has offices across the United States and in Europe (RAND Europe) and Australia. Alumni include numerous Nobel laureates and senior officials; the organisation's name is sometimes used generically for the military–industrial "think tank" culture of the Cold War.
