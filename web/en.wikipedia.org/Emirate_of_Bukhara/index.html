Emirate of Bukhara

The Emirate of Bukhara (Persian: امارت بخارا; Uzbek: Buxoro amirligi) was a Central Asian polity that existed from 1785 to 1920 in the region now occupied by Uzbekistan and parts of Tajikistan and Turkmenistan, with its capital at Bukhara.

The emirate was founded by the Manghit dynasty after the decline of the Khanate of Bukhara. It was a significant centre of Islamic learning and of the Silk Road trade, but was marked by internal stagnation, slavery and conflict with the neighbouring khanates of Khiva and Kokand.

Russian expansion under the "Great Game" led to defeats in 1866–1868, after which Bukhara became a Russian protectorate, ceding Samarkand and territory along the Amu Darya. The last emir, Mohammed Alim Khan, was overthrown by the Red Army in 1920, and the emirate was replaced by the Bukharan People's Soviet Republic.
