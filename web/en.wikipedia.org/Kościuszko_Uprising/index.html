The Kościuszko Uprising was a national insurrection in 1794 led by Tadeusz Kościuszko against the Russian Empire and Prussia, seeking to preserve the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth after the Second Partition of Poland (1793) and to uphold the reforms of the Constitution of 3 May 1791.

Outbreak

Kościuszko, a veteran of the American Revolutionary War, proclaimed the uprising in Kraków's market square on 24 March 1794 and assumed supreme command. On 4 April his forces — including peasant scythemen (kosynierzy) — defeated a Russian corps at the Battle of Racławice, a victory of great symbolic importance.

Course of the war

The insurrection spread to Warsaw and Vilnius, where Russian garrisons were expelled. Kościuszko's Połaniec Manifesto (May 1794) promised personal freedom and reduced labour dues to peasants who joined the cause. However, combined Russo-Prussian pressure mounted: Kościuszko was wounded and captured at the Battle of Maciejowice (10 October), and on 4 November Russian forces under Alexander Suvorov stormed Warsaw's Praga district, massacring thousands of civilians. Warsaw capitulated soon after, ending the uprising.

Consequences

The defeat led directly to the Third Partition of Poland in 1795, erasing the Commonwealth from the map for 123 years. Kościuszko was released from Russian captivity in 1796 and died in Switzerland in 1817. The uprising became a central symbol of Polish national resistance.
