Duke of Richelieu (French: duc de Richelieu) was a title of French nobility in the Peerage of France, created on 26 November 1629 for Armand Jean du Plessis de Richelieu (1585–1642), the chief minister of Louis XIII, universally known as Cardinal Richelieu. The dukedom took its name from the family's estate of Richelieu in Poitou, where the cardinal built a celebrated palace and a model town.

History

Cardinal Richelieu had no direct male heirs, and the title passed by special arrangement to the descendants of his elder brother Henri du Plessis de Richelieu through the female line, on condition that they bear the arms of Richelieu. Successive holders included Armand de Vignerot du Plessis, a naval commander, and his grandson Louis François Armand de Vignerot du Plessis, 3rd Duke of Richelieu (1696–1788), Marshal of France, courtier and notorious libertine whose memoirs chronicled the court of Louis XV.

One of the most notable later holders was Armand Emmanuel de Vignerot du Plessis, 5th Duke of Richelieu (1766–1822). An émigré during the French Revolution, he entered Russian service, became governor of Odessa — where he is honoured as a founder of the city — and after the Bourbon Restoration twice served as Prime Minister of France (1815–1818 and 1820–1821).

The male line of the family became extinct in 1952 with the death of the 8th duke, Marie Odet Jean Armand de La Chapelle de Saint-Jean de Jumilhac. The name survives in the town of Richelieu in Indre-et-Loire, in the Palais-Royal connection of the cardinal's legacy, and in countless cultural depictions of the first duke, notably in Alexandre Dumas's The Three Musketeers.
