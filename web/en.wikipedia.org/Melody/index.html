Melody

A melody (from Greek μελῳδία, melōidía, "singing, chanting"), also called a tune, voice or line, is a linear succession of musical tones that the listener perceives as a single entity. In its most literal sense, a melody is a combination of pitch and rhythm, while more figuratively it can include other musical elements such as tonal colour.

Elements

Melodies are often built from phrases and motifs, and typically move by a mixture of stepwise motion and leaps. A melody's contour — its shape of rising and falling pitch — is a key part of its identity. Melodies usually rest on a scale or mode, gravitate towards a tonal centre, and are organised by phrasing and cadences analogous to sentences in speech.

Melody in Western music

In folk and popular music the melody is usually the most memorable element of a song and carries the lyrics in vocal music. In classical music, melody interacts with harmony and counterpoint: a theme may be developed, sequenced, inverted and combined with other voices. Theorists distinguish conjunct (stepwise) from disjunct (leaping) melodic motion, and analyse melodies in terms of range, climax and balance.

Cultural aspects

Conceptions of melody differ across cultures: some traditions, such as Indian classical music, prize elaborate melodic improvisation within a rāga, while many West African traditions subordinate melody to rhythm. Timbre and ornamentation are essential to melodic identity in many styles.
