Norton Winfred Simon (February 5, 1907 – June 2, 1993) was an American billionaire industrialist and art collector. He built a major food conglomerate around Hunt Foods and amassed one of the finest private art collections in the United States, now housed in the Norton Simon Museum in Pasadena, California.

Business career

Born in Portland, Oregon, Simon briefly attended the University of California, Berkeley, before leaving to work in business. In the 1930s he acquired control of the Val Vita Food Products company, a cannery, and merged it with Hunt's, building Hunt Foods into a leading brand. Through his holding company, Norton Simon Inc., he later acquired interests in McCall Publishing, the Saturday Review, and Canada Dry. Known as a shrewd and demanding dealmaker, he was among the pioneers of the modern corporate conglomerate and aggressive takeover tactics.

Art collecting

Simon began collecting art seriously in the 1950s, acquiring Old Master paintings, Impressionist and Post-Impressionist works, and one of the world's great collections of South and Southeast Asian sculpture. In 1974, facing financial difficulty at the Pasadena Art Museum, he assumed control of it and installed his collection; the institution was renamed the Norton Simon Museum. He famously favoured lending his works and bargaining hard on prices, and his collection includes works by Raphael, Rembrandt, Degas, Van Gogh, and Picasso, including the purchase of Rembrandt's portrait of his son Titus and Duveen's stock of Old Masters.

Personal life

In 1971 Simon married the actress Jennifer Jones, who after his death became chair of the museum's board. He died in 1993 at his home in Beverly Hills.
