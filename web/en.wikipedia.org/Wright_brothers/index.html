Wright brothers

The Wright brothers, Orville Wright (1871–1948) and Wilbur Wright (1867–1912), were American aviation pioneers credited with inventing, building and flying the world's first successful motor-operated aeroplane.

First flights

On 17 December 1903 at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, their Wright Flyer made four flights, the longest lasting 59 seconds and covering 852 feet, with Orville at the controls for the first flight. Key innovations included three-axis control — wing-warping for roll, a forward elevator for pitch and a rear rudder for yaw — developed through their glider experiments and wind-tunnel work in Dayton, Ohio.

Later years

The brothers refined the Flyer into a practical aeroplane by 1905 and demonstrated it in the United States and Europe in 1908. Patent litigation, Wilbur's early death from typhoid in 1912, and Orville's sale of their company in 1915 closed their active role, but their priority is now widely recognised.
