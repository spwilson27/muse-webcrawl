Helianthus

Helianthus is a genus of about 70 species of annual and perennial flowering plants in the aster family, Asteraceae, commonly known as sunflowers. The name derives from the Greek helios ("sun") and anthos ("flower"). The genus is native to North and Central America, with a few species in South America.

Description

Sunflowers are typically tall, rough-haired herbs with large flower heads (capitula) made up of many small florets: sterile yellow (sometimes red or purple) ray florets around the rim and fertile disc florets in the centre, whose arrangement in spirals follows mathematical patterns related to the Fibonacci sequence. Young flower heads exhibit heliotropism, tracking the sun across the sky.

Cultivation and uses

The common sunflower, Helianthus annuus, was domesticated by Indigenous peoples of North America and is now grown worldwide for its edible seeds and for sunflower oil, one of the world's major vegetable oils. The Jerusalem artichoke (Helianthus tuberosus) is cultivated for its edible tubers. Sunflowers are also popular ornamentals, are used in phytoremediation of contaminated soils, and have become a national symbol of Ukraine, one of the largest producers of sunflower oil.
