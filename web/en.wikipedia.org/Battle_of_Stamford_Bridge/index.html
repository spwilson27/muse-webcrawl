The Battle of Stamford Bridge took place at the village of Stamford Bridge, East Riding of Yorkshire, on 25 September 1066, between an English army under King Harold Godwinson and an invading Norwegian force led by King Harald Hardrada and Harold's brother Tostig. The Norwegians were decisively defeated, and both Hardrada and Tostig were killed. The battle is traditionally seen as marking the end of the Viking Age.

Background

After the death of Edward the Confessor in January 1066, Harold was crowned king, but Harald of Norway pressed his own claim and invaded northern England with a fleet of some 300 ships. After defeating the northern earls at the Battle of Fulford on 20 September, the Norwegians awaited hostages and supplies at Stamford Bridge.

The battle

Harold Godwinson marched north at remarkable speed, covering roughly 185 miles in four days, and caught the Norwegians unprepared — many had left their armour with the ships in the heat. Tradition tells of a lone Norse axeman holding the bridge against the English until he was killed from beneath the planks. The English then broke the Norse shield wall in hard fighting.

Aftermath

The survivors were allowed to sail home in only 24 ships. Harold's triumph was short-lived: within days he had to march south to face William of Normandy's invasion, and he was killed at the Battle of Hastings on 14 October 1066.
