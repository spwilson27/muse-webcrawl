Adaptation (stylised as Adaptation.) is a 2002 American comedy-drama film directed by Spike Jonze from a screenplay by Charlie Kaufman. It is a metafictional work about Kaufman's own struggle to adapt Susan Orlean's 1998 nonfiction book The Orchid Thief into a screenplay, and it blends that struggle with a dramatised version of the book's story. Nicolas Cage plays both Charlie Kaufman and his fictional twin brother Donald; Meryl Streep plays Orlean and Chris Cooper plays orchid poacher John Laroche.

Plot

Hired to adapt The Orchid Thief, the self-loathing Charlie suffers writer's block, unable to turn a book he admires — about flowers, obsession and Florida history — into a conventional movie. His carefree twin Donald, a hack screenwriter following formulaic screenwriting guru Robert McKee's rules, succeeds effortlessly. Desperate, Charlie writes himself into the script, then follows Orlean to Florida, where he discovers that she and Laroche are involved in producing drugs from rare ghost orchids. The film's third act deliberately collapses into the clichés Charlie despised — chases, guns and revelations — as the adaptation takes over the adapter.

Reception and awards

The film was acclaimed for its originality and performances. Chris Cooper won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor; Cage, Streep and the screenplay were also Oscar-nominated. In a famous quirk, the fictional Donald Kaufman received a co-writing credit, making him the first wholly fictional person nominated for an Academy Award. The film frequently appears on lists of the best films of the 2000s.
