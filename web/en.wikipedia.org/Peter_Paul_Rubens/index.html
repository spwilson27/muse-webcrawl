Peter Paul Rubens

Sir Peter Paul Rubens (28 June 1577 – 30 May 1640) was a Flemish artist and diplomat, the most influential exponent of the Baroque in Northern Europe. His dynamic compositions, sensuous colour and vigorous brushwork shaped seventeenth-century painting, and his large Antwerp workshop produced altarpieces, mythologies, portraits and tapestry designs for courts across Europe.

Life and career

Born in exile in Siegen, Rubens grew up in Antwerp and spent 1600–1608 in Italy, absorbing the work of Titian, Caravaggio and the classical tradition while serving the Duke of Mantua. Returning to Antwerp, he became court painter to the Archdukes Albert and Isabella and built a grand house-studio, employing assistants who executed works from his designs; pupils and collaborators included Anthony van Dyck and Jacob Jordaens. His diplomatic missions for the Spanish Netherlands took him to Madrid, Paris and London, where Charles I knighted him.

Major works

Masterpieces include The Raising of the Cross and The Descent from the Cross in Antwerp Cathedral, the vast Marie de' Medici cycle in the Louvre, The Judgment of Paris, The Garden of Love, and the ceiling of the Banqueting House in Whitehall — his only surviving ceiling painting in situ. His voluptuous female nudes gave rise to the term "Rubenesque".
