Killing form

In mathematics, the Killing form, named after the German mathematician Wilhelm Killing, is a symmetric bilinear form that plays a fundamental role in the theories of Lie groups and Lie algebras. It provides a canonical way of measuring structure on a Lie algebra and underlies the classification of semisimple Lie algebras.

Definition

Let g be a Lie algebra over a field, and let ad(x) denote the linear map y ↦ [x, y] given by the adjoint representation. The Killing form is the bilinear form

B(x, y) = tr(ad(x) ad(y)).

It is symmetric, bilinear, and invariant: B([x, y], z) = B(x, [y, z]) for all x, y, z in g. It is also preserved by every automorphism of the algebra.

Properties

Cartan's criterion states that a Lie algebra over a field of characteristic zero is semisimple if and only if its Killing form is nondegenerate. Similarly, a Lie algebra is solvable if and only if the Killing form vanishes on its derived algebra. For a compact semisimple Lie group, the Killing form is negative definite, so its negative defines an invariant inner product on the algebra, making the geometry of the corresponding group Riemannian.

Examples

For the algebra sl(n, C) of traceless n×n matrices, the Killing form is B(x, y) = 2n tr(xy). For the rotation algebra so(3), it is a negative multiple of the ordinary dot product on three-dimensional space. In theoretical physics, the Killing form supplies the natural metric on the Lie algebra of a gauge group and appears in the kinetic terms of Yang–Mills theory.
