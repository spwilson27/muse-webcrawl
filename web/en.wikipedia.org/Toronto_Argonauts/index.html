The Toronto Argonauts (officially the Toronto Argonaut Football Club and colloquially known as the Argos) are a professional Canadian football team competing in the East Division of the Canadian Football League (CFL). Based in Toronto, Ontario, the Argonauts were founded in 1873 by the Argonaut Rowing Club, making them the oldest professional sports franchise in North America still using its original name, and one of the oldest in the world. They play their home games at BMO Field.

History

The club was formed on October 4, 1873, by members of the Argonaut Rowing Club who wanted an autumn activity to stay fit; the name references the Argonauts of Greek mythology and the rowing club's Jason-and-the-Argonauts emblem. The Argos were founding members of the Ontario Rugby Football Union (1883) and later the Interprovincial Rugby Football Union (the "Big Four"). They won their first Grey Cup in 1914 and built dynasties in the 1930s and 1945–1952, when they won five championships in eight years behind stars such as Joe Krol and Royal Copeland, "the Gold Dust Twins".

After a long drought, the 1971 team — led by Leon McQuay and coached by Leo Cahill — reached the Grey Cup but lost the famous "rain game" to Calgary. The drought ended in 1983 under coach Bob O'Billovich. In 1991, under flamboyant new owners Bruce McNall, Wayne Gretzky, and John Candy, the Argos signed Raghib "Rocket" Ismail and won the Grey Cup at Winnipeg in a frigid classic. Doug Flutie led back-to-back championships in 1996 and 1997. Further titles came in 2004, in the 100th Grey Cup in 2012 at home, in 2017, 2022, and 2024. With 19 Grey Cup victories, the Argonauts are the most successful team in the championship's history.

Rivalries and identity

The Argonauts' fiercest rival is the Hamilton Tiger-Cats, met annually in the Labour Day Classic. Their traditional fight song is "Pull Together", reflecting the rowing heritage. Since 2018 the club has been owned by Maple Leaf Sports & Entertainment, alongside the Maple Leafs, Raptors, and Toronto FC.
