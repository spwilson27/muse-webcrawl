International aid

International aid, also called foreign aid or development aid, is financial, technical or material assistance given by governments, multilateral institutions and private organisations of one country or group of countries to support the economic, environmental, social and political development of other countries, or to relieve suffering during emergencies.

Types of aid

Aid is commonly divided into development aid, intended to promote long-term economic growth and poverty reduction; humanitarian aid, given in response to natural disasters and conflicts; and military aid. Aid may be bilateral (government to government), multilateral (channelled through bodies such as the World Bank or United Nations agencies), or provided by non-governmental organisations and charities.

Official development assistance (ODA) is the standard measure monitored by the Development Assistance Committee of the OECD. Donor countries have committed in principle to a target of giving 0.7 per cent of gross national income as ODA, a goal set by the United Nations in 1970 and met by only a handful of countries.

History

Large-scale modern aid programmes began after the Second World War with the Marshall Plan, under which the United States funded the reconstruction of Western Europe. During the Cold War, aid flows expanded to Asia, Africa and Latin America, often shaped by strategic as well as developmental motives. From the 1990s, attention shifted towards aid effectiveness, debt relief and internationally agreed goals such as the Millennium Development Goals and their successors, the Sustainable Development Goals.

Criticism and debate

The effectiveness of aid is widely debated. Supporters argue that aid has contributed to major gains in health, education and infrastructure. Critics contend that aid can foster dependency, prop up corrupt governments, or be tied to the commercial and political interests of donors. Research findings remain mixed and depend heavily on the type of aid and the governance of the recipient country.
