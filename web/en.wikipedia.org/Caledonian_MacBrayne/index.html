Caledonian MacBrayne, usually shortened to CalMac, is the major operator of passenger and vehicle ferries to the Hebridean and Clyde islands of Scotland. It operates more than 30 routes between the Scottish mainland and 22 islands, carrying several million passengers annually, and is a lifeline service for island communities. The company traces its origins to 1851 and the shipping firm of David Hutcheson & Co., later David MacBrayne.

History

The MacBrayne company built up mail, passenger and cargo steamer services throughout the Highlands and Islands during the 19th century. The modern Caledonian MacBrayne was formed in 1973 by the merger of the Caledonian Steam Packet Company and David MacBrayne, both state-owned. In 2006 the undertaking was split: vessels and piers passed to Caledonian Maritime Assets Ltd (CMAL), while Caledonian MacBrayne became the operating company, which must re-bid for its routes under public-service contracts tendered by Transport Scotland. The company has won the Clyde and Hebrides Ferry Services contract in successive tender rounds.

Fleet and livery

CalMac's fleet ranges from small passenger-only craft to large ro-ro ferries such as MV Loch Seaforth and MV Finlaggan. Since 1973 the ships have worn black hulls with red funnels bearing the yellow lion rampant of the MacBrayne tradition. Fleet renewal — including delayed dual-fuel vessels built at Ferguson Marine — has been a major political issue in Scotland.
