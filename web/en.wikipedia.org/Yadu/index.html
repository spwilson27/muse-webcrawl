Yadu is a legendary king in Hindu tradition, the eldest son of King Yayati and Devayani, and the progenitor of the Yadu lineage (Yaduvansha), one of the principal branches of the Chandravansha or Lunar dynasty. The god Krishna is described in the epics and Puranas as a descendant of Yadu, and the Yadava clans of the Mahabharata trace their ancestry to him.

The curse of Yayati

According to the Mahabharata and the Bhagavata Purana, Yayati was cursed with premature old age by the sage Shukra. He asked each of his sons to exchange their youth for his age. Yadu and his elder brothers refused, while the youngest, Puru, accepted. In consequence Yayati cursed Yadu's line: his descendants would never hold sovereign kingship in the royal line of succession, while Puru's descendants — the Pauravas, ancestors of the Kauravas and Pandavas — inherited the throne. Yadu's progeny nevertheless became a populous and powerful confederacy.

The Yadavas

Yadu's sons founded branches known as the Sahasrajit and Krostu lines; from Krostu descended the Vrishnis, Andhakas and Bhojas who settled around Mathura and later Dvaraka. Krishna, of the Vrishni branch, is the most famous Yadava. After Krishna's death the Yadavas destroyed themselves in a fratricidal brawl at Prabhasa, as narrated in the Mausala Parva.

Historical communities

Several modern communities, notably the Yadavs of northern India, claim descent from Yadu and Krishna; historically, dynasties such as the Seunas (Yadavas) of Devagiri adopted the lineage name.
