James Edward Cleveland (5 December 1931 – 9 February 1991) was an American gospel singer, composer, and arranger widely regarded as the "King of Gospel". He is credited with creating the modern gospel choir sound, and his 1963 recording "Peace Be Still" with the Angelic Choir became one of the best-selling gospel albums of its era.

Early life

Born in Chicago, Cleveland grew up singing at Pilgrim Baptist Church, where the minister of music was Thomas A. Dorsey, the father of gospel music. As a boy soprano he sang with Dorsey's choir; when his voice changed abruptly as a teenager it developed the gravelly, powerful baritone that became his trademark. He began composing while still young and formed his first group, the Gospel Chimes, in the late 1940s.

Career

Cleveland worked with the Caravans in the 1950s and developed a style that fused traditional gospel with the rhythms of rhythm and blues and the massed-voice arrangements of the church choir. After moving to Detroit and then Los Angeles, he recorded the live album Peace Be Still (1963) with the Angelic Choir of Nutley, New Jersey, which sold in the hundreds of thousands and made him gospel's dominant recording artist. In 1968 he founded the Gospel Music Workshop of America, an annual convention that trained generations of gospel musicians, and in 1970 he founded the Southern California Community Choir. He also pastored the Cornerstone Institutional Baptist Church in Los Angeles.

Legacy

Cleveland won four Grammy Awards and recorded dozens of albums with choirs across the United States, recording more than a hundred albums in total. His songs, including "Peace Be Still", "I Don't Feel No Ways Tired", and "Lord, Help Me to Hold Out", remain standards of the gospel repertoire, and he mentored artists including Aretha Franklin, who recorded her landmark Amazing Grace album (1972) with Cleveland and the Southern California Community Choir. He was posthumously given a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
