Kharosthi (also spelled Kharoshthi or Kharoṣṭhī) was an ancient script used in the northwestern Indian subcontinent, principally in Gandhara, from about the 3rd century BCE to the 3rd century CE. Unlike most scripts of India, it was written from right to left, a feature inherited from its partial derivation from Imperial Aramaic.

Use and decipherment

Kharosthi was used to write Gandhari Prakrit and Sanskrit, and appears on coins, inscriptions (including edicts of Ashoka in the northwest), and documents on wood, leather and birch bark from Central Asian sites along the Silk Road such as Niya. The script was deciphered in the 19th century principally by James Prinsep, using bilingual Indo-Greek coins. It died out around the 3rd century CE in India, lingering somewhat longer in Central Asia. The script is encoded in Unicode (block U+10A00–U+10A5F).
