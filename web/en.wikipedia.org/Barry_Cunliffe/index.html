Barry Cunliffe

Sir Barrington Windsor Cunliffe (born 10 December 1939), commonly known as Barry Cunliffe, is a British archaeologist and academic. He was Professor of European Archaeology at the University of Oxford from 1972 to 2007 and is now an emeritus professor. He specialises in the archaeology of Iron Age Britain and the wider Atlantic-facing world of prehistoric Europe.

Barry Cunliffe, archaeologist of Iron Age Europe.

Career

Cunliffe studied archaeology and anthropology at the University of Cambridge. He became a lecturer at the University of Bristol in 1963 and professor at the University of Southampton in 1966, where he developed a major school of archaeology. In 1972 he moved to Oxford as Professor of European Archaeology, a post he held until his retirement in 2007.

His excavations include the Iron Age hillfort at Danbury in Essex, the Roman palace at Fishbourne in West Sussex, Hengistbury Head in Dorset, and Mount Batten in Devon. His long-running project at the hillfort of Danebury in Hampshire, excavated over two decades, produced landmark volumes on Iron Age communities. Later work on the site of Le Câtillon II in Jersey and excavations on Scilly extended his interests to the Atlantic seaways.

Works

Cunliffe is the author of many widely read books, including Iron Age Communities in Britain (1974), Facing the Ocean: The Atlantic and Its Peoples (2001), The Celts: A Very Short Introduction (2003), Europe Between the Oceans: 9000 BC – AD 1000 (2008), Britain Begins (2012), and On the Ocean: The Mediterranean and the Atlantic from Prehistory to AD 1500 (2017). He was knighted in 2006 for services to archaeology.
