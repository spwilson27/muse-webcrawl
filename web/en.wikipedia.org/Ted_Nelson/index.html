Ted Nelson

Theodor Holm Nelson (born June 17, 1937) is an American pioneer of information technology, philosopher, and sociologist. He coined the terms hypertext and hypermedia in 1963 and published them in 1965. Nelson also originated terms such as transclusion, virtuality, intertwingularity, and micropayment, and has spent decades developing his vision of a deep, parallel hypertext system.

Project Xanadu

In 1960 Nelson founded Project Xanadu, an ambitious design for a networked hypertext system with visible two-way links, version management, transclusion (quotation by reference with full context), and built-in micropayments for authors. Conceived long before the World Wide Web, Xanadu influenced generations of hypertext researchers, though repeated attempts to ship a finished product became famous for their delays. Nelson contrasted his design with the Web's one-way, breakable links, which he criticised sharply.

Works and life

Nelson is the author of Computer Lib/Dream Machines (1974), a countercultural classic of early personal computing, and Literary Machines (1981), which sets out the Xanadu design. He studied philosophy at Swarthmore College and sociology at Harvard. His later systems include ZigZag, a multidimensional information structure. He is the son of actress Celeste Holm and director Ralph Nelson.
