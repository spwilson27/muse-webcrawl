Symphony No. 5 (Shostakovich)

The Symphony No. 5 in D minor, Op. 47, by Dmitri Shostakovich, was composed between April and July 1937 and premiered in Leningrad on November 21, 1937, by the Leningrad Philharmonic Orchestra conducted by Yevgeny Mravinsky. The work was composed under intense political pressure during Stalin's Great Terror, shortly after Shostakovich's opera Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District had been denounced in Pravda, an event that placed the composer's career—and possibly his life—in danger.

Background

Following the January 1936 Pravda editorial "Muddle Instead of Music", Shostakovich withdrew his Fourth Symphony from rehearsal and sought a work that could rehabilitate him officially. The Fifth was presented in the press—apparently with official prompting—as "a Soviet artist's creative response to just criticism". Its classical four-movement design, audible lyricism, and apparently triumphant finale satisfied the authorities while allowing listeners to hear something more ambivalent.

Music

The symphony lasts around 45 minutes and is scored for a large orchestra. Its movements are:

  1. Moderato: a sombre, searching movement built from angular string figures and a lamenting theme, ending in quiet, desolate high strings over celesta.
  2. Allegretto: a heavy, sardonic scherzo with Mahlerian overtones.
  3. Largo: an intense slow movement for strings and winds without brass, often heard as a memorial to the victims of the purges; audience members reportedly wept at the premiere.
  4. Allegro non troppo: a finale whose blazing D-major conclusion has been interpreted both as sincere triumph and as forced rejoicing.

Reception and interpretation

The premiere received an ovation lasting at least half an hour, and the symphony restored Shostakovich's standing. Ever since, audiences and scholars have debated whether the finale represents genuine optimism or, as claimed in the controversial memoir Testimony, "rejoicing that is forced, created under threat... as if someone were beating you with a stick". The Fifth remains Shostakovich's most frequently performed symphony.
