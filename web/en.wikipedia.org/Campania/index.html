Campania is an administrative region of Italy, located in the south of the country along the Tyrrhenian Sea. Its capital and largest city is Naples. With around 5.6 million inhabitants, it is one of Italy's most populous regions and the most densely populated.

Geography

Campania borders Lazio to the northwest, Molise to the north, Apulia (Puglia) to the northeast, and Basilicata to the east. Its coastline includes the Gulf of Naples, dominated by Mount Vesuvius, and the celebrated Amalfi Coast. The islands of Capri, Ischia, and Procida lie just offshore.

History

In antiquity the region was settled by Italic peoples, colonised by the Greeks (who founded Naples and Paestum), and later became a rich province of the Roman Empire — the emperor Augustus and the poet Virgil were associated with it. The Roman towns of Pompeii and Herculaneum, destroyed by the eruption of Vesuvius in AD 79, are among the world's most famous archaeological sites. In the Middle Ages and early modern period Campania formed the mainland core of the Kingdom of Naples and later the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies, before Italian unification in 1861.

Economy and cuisine

Campania is famous for its agriculture — San Marzano tomatoes, buffalo mozzarella (mozzarella di bufala campana), lemons, and wines such as Taurasi and Fiano di Avellino — and as the birthplace of pizza, first made in Naples. Tourism centres on Naples, Pompeii, Vesuvius, the Amalfi Coast, and Capri.
