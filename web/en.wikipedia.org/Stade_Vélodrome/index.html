The Stade Vélodrome is a multi-purpose stadium in Marseille, France, and the home ground of the football club Olympique de Marseille. With a capacity of 67,394 it is the largest club football stadium in France. It has hosted matches at two FIFA World Cups, three UEFA European Championships and the 2023 Rugby World Cup final rounds, as well as the final of the 2024 Summer Olympics football tournaments. Since 2016 it has been known commercially as the Orange Vélodrome.

History

The stadium opened in 1937, its name reflecting the cycling track (vélodrome) that originally surrounded the pitch; it hosted stages of the Tour de France for decades, with the finish line on the track. It was a venue for the 1938 FIFA World Cup. The cycling track was progressively removed in the 1980s as the ground was converted for football, and the stadium was extensively rebuilt for the 1998 FIFA World Cup.

Renovation for Euro 2016

Ahead of UEFA Euro 2016 the stadium was comprehensively remodelled: capacity rose from about 60,000 to 67,394 and a vast undulating roof — the largest ever built in France — was added to cover all spectators, improving the famous atmosphere created by the home support, particularly the virages (curved ends) behind each goal.

Other events

Beyond Olympique de Marseille's home matches, the Vélodrome regularly hosts the France national football and rugby union teams, and has staged rugby league and rugby union World Cup matches and major concerts.
