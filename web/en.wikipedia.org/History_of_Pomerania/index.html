History of Pomerania

The history of Pomerania covers the Baltic coastal region between the Oder and Vistula rivers, inhabited in turn by Germanic and Slavic peoples, ruled by the Griffin dukes of Pomerania, contested by Poland, Denmark, Brandenburg and Sweden, long a Prussian province, and divided between Germany and Poland since 1945.

Early history

The region takes its name from the Slavic po more, "by the sea". After Germanic tribes migrated away in late antiquity, West Slavic tribes including the Pomeranians settled the coast from the sixth century. Attempts by the early Polish Piast rulers to conquer and Christianise Pomerania in the tenth to twelfth centuries were only partly successful; the missions of Otto of Bamberg (1124, 1128) established Christianity.

Duchy of Pomerania

The Griffin dynasty, beginning with Wartislaw I, ruled Pomerania for nearly 500 years. From the twelfth century German settlers arrived in the Ostsiedlung, founding towns under German law; Szczecin (Stettin), Gdańsk (Danzig) and other ports joined the Hanseatic League. Pomerania was repeatedly partitioned among Griffin lines and threatened by Brandenburg, the Teutonic Order and Denmark. Pomerania's dukes became imperial princes, and the eastern part (Pomerelia, around Gdańsk) passed between Poland and the Teutonic Order before returning to Poland in 1466.

Reformation and Swedish Pomerania

The Reformation was adopted in 1534. The last Griffin duke, Bogislaw XIV, died in 1637 during the Thirty Years' War, and by the Peace of Westphalia (1648) western Pomerania went to Sweden (Swedish Pomerania, including Stettin) and the east to Brandenburg-Prussia. Sweden ceded Stettin and the Oder mouth to Prussia in 1720 and its remaining Pomeranian territory in 1815.

Prussian province and the twentieth century

From 1815 a unified Prussian Province of Pomerania existed, an agricultural region of Junker estates east of the Oder. After the First World War most of Pomerelia became Poland's "Corridor" to the sea, while the rest remained German. In 1945 the Potsdam settlement placed Pomerania east of the Oder–Neisse line under Polish administration; the German population fled or was expelled and the region was resettled by Poles, many themselves displaced from territories annexed by the USSR. Western Pomerania became part of East Germany and today forms part of the German state of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern.
