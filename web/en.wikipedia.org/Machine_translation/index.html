Machine translation

Machine translation (MT) is the use of software to translate text or speech from one natural language to another. It is a subfield of computational linguistics and natural language processing.

History

Early systems of the 1950s, such as the Georgetown–IBM experiment of 1954, used rules and bilingual dictionaries. The 1966 ALPAC report cut US funding, but rule-based systems continued commercially. Statistical machine translation, pioneered at IBM around 1990, learned phrase correspondences from parallel corpora and dominated with services such as Google Translate (2006).

Neural machine translation

From about 2016, neural machine translation using encoder–decoder networks and later Transformer architectures largely replaced statistical methods, producing major gains in fluency. Large language models have since further improved quality, though challenges remain with low-resource languages, idiom, and factual fidelity.

Evaluation

Automatic metrics such as BLEU compare system output with reference translations; human evaluation remains the gold standard. Applications range from web browsing and commerce to diplomacy and accessibility.
