Goma is a city in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, the capital of North Kivu province. It stands on the northern shore of Lake Kivu, immediately across the border from the Rwandan city of Gisenyi (Rubavu), in the shadow of the active volcano Mount Nyiragongo.

Volcanic hazards

Goma is built on old lava flows from Nyiragongo, one of Africa's most dangerous volcanoes. In January 2002 an eruption sent rivers of lava through the city centre, destroying a large share of its buildings and displacing hundreds of thousands; a further eruption in May 2021 forced another mass evacuation. Dissolved gas in Lake Kivu itself is regarded as a long-term limnic hazard.

Conflict and humanitarian role

After the 1994 Rwandan genocide, Goma hosted vast refugee camps, and it has repeatedly been a prize in Congo's eastern wars: it was taken by Rwandan-backed RCD rebels in 1998, by the M23 movement in 2012, and again by M23 in January 2025. The city is the principal hub for the UN peacekeeping mission MONUSCO and for humanitarian agencies serving the Kivus, and its airport links the region to Kinshasa.

Economy

Goma is a trading centre for the agricultural and mining hinterland of North Kivu, with cross-border commerce with Rwanda a mainstay. Tourism to nearby Virunga National Park, famous for mountain gorillas, has been repeatedly interrupted by insecurity.
