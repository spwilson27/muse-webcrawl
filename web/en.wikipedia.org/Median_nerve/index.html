Median nerve

Median nerve
FromLateral and medial cords of the brachial plexus (C5–T1)
InnervatesForearm flexors, thenar muscles, lateral hand
Key branchesAnterior interosseous nerve, palmar cutaneous branch, recurrent thenar branch

The median nerve is a major peripheral nerve of the upper limb. It is formed in the axilla by the union of the lateral and medial cords of the brachial plexus, descends through the arm and forearm, and enters the hand through the carpal tunnel. It supplies most of the flexor muscles of the forearm, the muscles of the thenar eminence, and sensation to the palm and the lateral three and a half fingers.

Course

The nerve forms around the third part of the axillary artery and runs down the arm alongside the brachial artery, giving off no branches in the arm. In the forearm it passes between the heads of pronator teres, descends beneath flexor digitorum superficialis, and gives off the anterior interosseous nerve, which supplies the deep flexors. Just above the wrist its palmar cutaneous branch crosses superficial to the flexor retinaculum, while the main trunk passes beneath it through the carpal tunnel with the flexor tendons.

Function

Motor branches supply the forearm flexors and pronators (except flexor carpi ulnaris and the ulnar half of flexor digitorum profundus, supplied by the ulnar nerve), the thenar muscles that move the thumb, and the two lateral lumbricals. Sensory fibres serve the palmar skin of the thumb, index, middle, and half of the ring finger, and their fingertips.

Clinical significance

Compression of the nerve in the carpal tunnel causes carpal tunnel syndrome — the commonest entrapment neuropathy — with nocturnal tingling and numbness in the median distribution and, in advanced cases, wasting of the thenar muscles. Injury higher in the forearm (pronator syndrome) or at the elbow produces weakness of flexion and the characteristic "hand of benediction" when the patient tries to make a fist. Damage to the anterior interosseous nerve alone prevents the "OK" pinch sign.
