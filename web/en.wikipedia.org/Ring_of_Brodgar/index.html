Ring of Brodgar

The Ring of Brodgar is a Neolithic stone circle and henge in the Orkney Islands of Scotland. It stands on a narrow isthmus between the Loch of Stenness and the Loch of Harray on Mainland, and forms part of the Heart of Neolithic Orkney UNESCO World Heritage Site, together with Maeshowe, the Stones of Stenness and Skara Brae.

The Ring of Brodgar is the third-largest stone circle in the British Isles.

Description

The circle has a diameter of about 104 metres, exceeded in Britain only by Avebury and Stanton Drew. It was originally composed of some 60 stones, of which 27 survive, standing up to about 4.7 metres high. Uniquely among British stone circles, the stones are surrounded by a rock-cut circular ditch up to 10 metres wide and 3.4 metres deep, dug out of solid sandstone—an estimated 12,000 cubic metres of rock removed. Unlike other henges, Brodgar has no encircling bank.

History and purpose

The monument is generally dated to between 2500 and 2000 BC, among the last of the great Orkney monuments built. Its precise purpose is unknown; like other stone circles it is thought to have served ceremonial or ritual functions, possibly with astronomical associations. Folklore later linked the stones with dancing giants turned to stone and with Norse sun and moon temples. Excavations in 2008 dated nearby structures, including the huge walled complex at the Ness of Brodgar between the ring and the Stones of Stenness.
