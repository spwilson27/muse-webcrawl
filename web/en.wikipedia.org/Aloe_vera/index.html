A cultivated Aloe vera plant.

Aloe vera is a succulent plant species of the genus Aloe. It originates from the Arabian Peninsula but is widely cultivated around the world and has become naturalised in warm climates. The plant is stemless or very short-stemmed, with thick, fleshy, green lance-shaped leaves containing a clear gel.

Description

Aloe vera grows to about 60–100 cm tall, spreading by offsets. Its leaves are thick and fleshy with serrated white-toothed margins, and its yellow tubular flowers are produced on a spike up to 90 cm tall. The inner leaf tissue contains a mucilaginous gel, while the outer rind produces a bitter yellow latex containing aloin.

Cultivation and uses

The species has been used for thousands of years in traditional medicine; references appear in the Egyptian Ebers Papyrus. Today the gel is widely used in cosmetics, skin lotions, ointments and beverages. Scientific evidence for most claimed health benefits is limited: topical aloe gel may help heal burns and skin abrasions, while oral use of the latex is a strong laxative and can be toxic in large doses.

Distribution

Although the wild origin is thought to be the Arabian Peninsula, centuries of cultivation have spread the plant throughout tropical and subtropical regions, including the Mediterranean, India, China, Mexico and the Caribbean.
