Bertram Kostant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bertram Kostant (May 24, 1928 – February 2, 2017) was an American mathematician who worked in representation theory, differential geometry and mathematical physics. He was a professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology for nearly four decades and is known for contributions including the Kostant multiplicity formula, the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem work, and the Kostant partition function.

Life and career

Kostant was born in Brooklyn, New York, and attended Stuyvesant High School. He studied mathematics at Purdue University and earned his PhD from the University of Chicago in 1954 under Irving Segal, with a dissertation on representations of Lie groups. After positions at the Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton, and Berkeley, he joined the MIT faculty in 1962 and remained there until his retirement.

Mathematics

Kostant's work linked algebra, geometry and physics. His formula for the multiplicities of weights in representations of semisimple Lie groups, the Kostant multiplicity formula, became a cornerstone of representation theory. He introduced the Kostant partition function and, with Shlomo Sternberg, the BRST formalism in physics; the cubic Dirac operator, geometric quantization and the theory of prequantization also bear his influence. In 1990 he received the American Mathematical Society's Leroy P. Steele Prize, and he was a member of the National Academy of Sciences.
