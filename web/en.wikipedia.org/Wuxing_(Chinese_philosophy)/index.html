Wuxing (Chinese philosophy)

The generation (sheng) and control (ke) cycles of the five phases.

Wuxing (Chinese: 五行; pinyin: wǔxíng), usually translated as five phases or five elements, is a fivefold conceptual scheme in Chinese philosophy used to describe interactions and relationships between phenomena. The five phases are Wood (木), Fire (火), Earth (土), Metal (金), and Water (水). Rather than static constituents of matter (as in the Greek theory of elements), they represent dynamic processes and qualities in continual transformation.

Cycles

The phases are linked by two cycles. In the generating cycle (sheng 生), Wood feeds Fire, Fire produces Earth (ash), Earth bears Metal, Metal enriches Water, and Water nourishes Wood. In the controlling cycle (ke 克), Wood parts Earth (roots), Earth dams Water, Water extinguishes Fire, Fire melts Metal, and Metal chops Wood. Balance between the cycles models harmony in the body, the state, and the cosmos.

History and applications

The scheme was systematized during the Warring States period by thinkers such as Zou Yan (305–240 BCE) and was integrated into Han dynasty cosmology, where it underpinned theories of dynastic succession: each dynasty ruled by virtue of a phase and was succeeded by the phase that conquers it. Wuxing correlations extend across Chinese thought — the five phases are matched with the five directions, seasons, colours (green-blue, red, yellow, white, black), tastes, tones, planets, and yin organs (liver, heart, spleen, lungs, kidneys).

The scheme remains foundational in traditional Chinese medicine, where diagnosis and acupuncture treatment aim to rebalance the phases; in feng shui; in Chinese astrology; and in martial arts such as xingyiquan, whose five fists correspond to the phases.
