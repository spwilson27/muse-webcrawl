Immunoglobulin A

Immunoglobulin A (IgA)
Type: Antibody (immunoglobulin)
Forms: Monomer (serum); dimer (secretory IgA)
Main sites: Mucosal surfaces, saliva, tears, breast milk

Immunoglobulin A (IgA) is an antibody that plays a crucial role in the immune function of mucous membranes. It is the most abundant immunoglobulin produced in the body and the second most common in serum, after IgG.

Forms

IgA exists in two major forms. Serum IgA circulates mainly as a monomer, produced by plasma cells in the bone marrow. Secretory IgA (sIgA) is a dimer joined by a J chain and protected by a secretory component; it is transported across mucosal epithelia into secretions such as saliva, tears, colostrum, and the fluids of the gut and respiratory tract.

Function

At mucosal surfaces—the body's main interface with the environment—IgA neutralises pathogens and toxins and prevents microbes from adhering to epithelial cells, a process called immune exclusion. It does so largely without triggering inflammation.

Clinical aspects

Selective IgA deficiency is the most common primary immunodeficiency; many affected people are asymptomatic, though some have recurrent infections or allergies. IgA nephropathy, the commonest glomerulonephritis worldwide, involves deposition of IgA in the kidneys. See also Antibody and Immune system.
