Second Barons' War

The Second Barons' War (1264–1267) was a civil war in England between a group of rebellious barons, led by Simon de Montfort, and the royal forces of King Henry III. De Montfort's victory at Lewes in 1264 made him ruler of England in all but name and led to his famous parliament of 1265, but he was killed at the Battle of Evesham in 1265 and royal authority was restored.

Causes

Grievances against Henry III included his costly foreign projects, favouritism toward French relatives, and refusal to abide by the Provisions of Oxford (1258), by which a council of barons had constrained the crown. Papal release of the king from his oath to the Provisions drove the parties to arms.

Course of the war

De Montfort captured Henry and Prince Edward at the Battle of Lewes (14 May 1264). Governing through the Mise of Lewes, he summoned the 1265 parliament, notable for including burgesses from the towns. Edward escaped captivity in May 1265 and destroyed de Montfort's army at Evesham (4 August 1265), where de Montfort was killed. Remaining rebels held out at Kenilworth and the Isle of Ely until 1267, when the Statute of Marlborough closed the conflict.

Legacy

The war is remembered for de Montfort's parliament, an important step in the development of representative government, even though his motives were largely baronial rather than democratic.
