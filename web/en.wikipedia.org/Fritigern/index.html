Fritigern (Gothic: Frithugairns) was a chieftain of the Thervingi Goths who led his people across the Danube into the Roman Empire in 376 AD and defeated the Romans at the Battle of Adrianople in 378, one of the most consequential defeats in Roman history.

Crossing the Danube

Pressed by the advancing Huns, Fritigern petitioned the Eastern emperor Valens for permission to settle his people in Thrace as foederati. Valens agreed, hoping to gain recruits and tax revenue. The crossing of 376 brought perhaps 200,000 Goths over the Danube, but Roman officials, notably the corrupt commander Lupicinus, profiteered from the refugees' food supply and drove them toward starvation.

Gothic War (376–382)

After a failed Roman attempt to assassinate the Gothic leaders at a banquet in Marcianople, Fritigern led a full revolt. On 9 August 378, at Adrianople, his combined Gothic cavalry and infantry destroyed the Eastern Roman field army; Emperor Valens himself was killed on the field — the worst Roman military disaster since Cannae. Fritigern continued campaigning in the Balkans until about 380, after which he disappears from the sources. The war ended in 382 with a treaty under Theodosius I that settled the Goths in the empire as autonomous federates, a precedent that shaped the following century.
