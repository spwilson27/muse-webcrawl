Tulip Revolution

The Tulip Revolution, also called the 2005 Kyrgyz Revolution, was the overthrow of President Askar Akayev of Kyrgyzstan in March 2005 following disputed parliamentary elections. It was one of the "colour revolutions" that swept parts of the post-Soviet world in the 2000s.

Background

Akayev had ruled Kyrgyzstan since 1990. Dissatisfaction with corruption, nepotism, and economic stagnation grew, and the parliamentary elections of February–March 2005 were widely criticized as rigged in favour of the president's family and allies.

Events

Protests began in the southern cities of Osh and Jalal-Abad and spread to the capital, Bishkek. On 24 March 2005, demonstrators stormed the presidential palace (the White House), and Akayev fled the country, eventually resigning in Moscow on 4 April.

Aftermath

Opposition leader Kurmanbek Bakiyev became acting president and won the July 2005 presidential election. Bakiyev himself was ousted in a second revolution in April 2010.
