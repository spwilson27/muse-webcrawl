The National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) is a United States federal agency responsible for conducting research and making recommendations for the prevention of work-related injury and illness. It is part of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention within the Department of Health and Human Services.

History

NIOSH was created by the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970, the same statute that established the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) in the Department of Labor. While OSHA sets and enforces workplace standards, NIOSH serves as the research arm, providing the scientific basis for standards and guidance.

Activities

The institute conducts laboratory and field research on hazards ranging from chemical exposures and respirable dust to ergonomics and workplace violence. It certifies respirators used in occupational settings, publishes recommended exposure limits, operates health hazard evaluation programs that investigate specific workplaces on request, and funds extramural research and training through programs such as its Education and Research Centers.

Relationship to OSHA

NIOSH recommendations are advisory rather than regulatory; OSHA decides whether to adopt them as enforceable permissible exposure limits. The two agencies were deliberately placed in separate departments so that research conclusions would remain independent of enforcement politics.
