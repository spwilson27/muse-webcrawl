Lanark (novel)

Lanark
AuthorAlasdair Gray
Published1981 (Canongate)
GenreNovel; dystopian / realist
Full titleLanark: A Life in Four Books

Lanark: A Life in Four Books (1981) is the first novel by the Scottish writer and artist Alasdair Gray. Written over nearly thirty years, it is widely regarded as a landmark of modern Scottish literature and a catalyst for the Scottish literary renaissance of the 1980s.

Structure and plot

The novel interweaves two narratives. Books One and Two form a realist Bildungsroman following Duncan Thaw, a Glasgow art student modelled in part on Gray himself. Books Three and Four present the dystopian fantasy of Lanark, an amnesiac who wakes in Unthank, a nightmarish shadow-city resembling Glasgow. Unusually, Book Three comes first in the published order, and the volume includes an "Index of Plagiarisms" in which Gray cheerfully catalogues his borrowings.

Reception

Anthony Burgess praised it as a work of genius, and it has been credited with transforming the possibilities of the Scottish novel. The book's themes of creativity, institutional power, and the divided self, together with Gray's own typography and illustrations, made it a cult classic that continues to be reprinted and staged in adaptation.
