Go-kart

A go-kart, also written as go-cart (often simply kart), is a small, open-wheel, four-wheeled vehicle used for recreation and racing. Go-karts come in many forms, from simple pedal- or motor-powered fun karts to high-performance racing karts capable of speeds over 130 km/h (80 mph); shifter karts with gearboxes can exceed 160 km/h. Karting is widely regarded as the first step in a professional racing driver's career.

History

The first kart is credited to Art Ingels, a race car fabricator in California who in 1956 built a small vehicle from scrap metal powered by a surplus lawnmower engine. The idea spread rapidly; by the late 1950s manufacturers such as Go Kart Manufacturing Co. were producing karts commercially, and karting clubs and tracks appeared across the United States and Europe.

Design and racing

A kart consists of a tubular steel chassis, engine mounted beside or behind the driver, a solid rear axle, and simple steering and braking. Racing karts are divided into classes by age, engine type and whether they have gearboxes. Many Formula One champions — including Ayrton Senna, Michael Schumacher, Lewis Hamilton and Max Verstappen — began their careers in karting, and Senna called it the purest form of motor racing. Rental ("arrive and drive") karting is a popular leisure activity at indoor and outdoor tracks worldwide.
