Hans Hahn (mathematician)

Hans Hahn
27 September 1879 – 24 July 1934
Austrian mathematician and philosopher

Hans Hahn (1879–1934) was an Austrian mathematician and philosopher who made fundamental contributions to functional analysis, measure theory, and the philosophy of science. He spent most of his career at the University of Vienna.

Mathematics

Hahn is best known for the Hahn–Banach theorem (1927), one of the cornerstones of functional analysis, which guarantees the extension of bounded linear functionals. With Stefan Banach he also proved the uniform boundedness principle, known as the Banach–Steinhaus theorem. His other results include the Hahn decomposition theorem in measure theory, the Hahn embedding theorem for ordered groups, and work on curves and set theory. He supervised the doctoral work of Karl Menger and taught Kurt Gödel.

Philosophy

Hahn was a founding member of the Vienna Circle of logical positivists. He helped bring Gödel and Menger into the Circle's orbit, read the manuscript of Wittgenstein's Tractatus with colleagues, and worked to popularise the scientific world-conception before his early death in 1934.
