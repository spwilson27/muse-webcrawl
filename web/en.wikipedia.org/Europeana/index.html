Europeana is the European Union's digital platform for cultural heritage, providing access to tens of millions of digitised items — books, paintings, photographs, films, sound recordings, and archival documents — contributed by thousands of galleries, libraries, archives, and museums across Europe.

History

The initiative grew from a 2005 letter by President Jacques Chirac and other leaders proposing a European digital library, partly in response to Google Books. A prototype launched on 20 November 2008; demand crashed the site on its first day. Europeana is operated by the Europeana Foundation (Europese Bibliotheek) in the National Library of the Netherlands and funded mainly by the EU.

Model and data

Europeana does not host most content: it aggregates standardised metadata (using the Europeana Data Model) and links to objects held by contributing institutions. Metadata are released under CC0, and content carries rights labels from the RightsStatements.org framework. APIs and thematic collections, exhibitions, and campaigns such as Europeana 1914–1918 (crowdsourced First World War memorabilia) and Europeana Migration support reuse in education, research, and creative industries.

Role

The platform is the centrepiece of the EU's common European data space for cultural heritage, promoting digitisation standards and multilingual access to Europe's shared memory.
