Eric S. Raymond

Eric Steven Raymond
Born: December 4, 1957, Boston, Massachusetts
Known for: The Cathedral and the Bazaar, Jargon File, fetchmail
Movement: Open-source software

Eric Steven Raymond (often referred to by his initials, ESR) is an American software developer, open-source software advocate, and author of the 1997 essay and 1999 book The Cathedral and the Bazaar. He is a leading figure and spokesperson of the open-source movement.

Work

Raymond wrote the fetchmail email client, maintained the Jargon File (published in print as The New Hacker's Dictionary), and contributed to software including the GIF library, GNU Emacs documentation and the gpsd project. His essay compared the "cathedral" model of centrally planned software development with the "bazaar" model of open, decentralised development, formulating "Linus's law": given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow.

Advocacy

He co-founded the Open Source Initiative in 1998 and served as its president until 2005. His writings, including the Halloween Documents commentary and The Art of Unix Programming (2003), have been influential in promoting open-source development to businesses.
