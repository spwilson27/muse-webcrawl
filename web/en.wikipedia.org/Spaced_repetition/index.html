Spaced repetition

FieldCognitive psychology, education
Key conceptExpanding review intervals
RelatedForgetting curve, flashcards, Anki, SuperMemo

Spaced repetition is a learning technique that incorporates increasing intervals of time between subsequent reviews of previously learned material in order to exploit the psychological spacing effect. Newly introduced and more difficult material is shown more frequently, while older and less difficult material is shown less frequently.

History

The spacing effect was first described by the German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus in his 1885 work on memory, in which he charted his own forgetting curves. In the 1930s the idea was applied to vocabulary learning, and in the 1980s Sebastian Leitner's flashcard box system and Piotr Woźniak's SuperMemo software popularised algorithmic scheduling.

Algorithms

Modern spaced repetition software schedules reviews based on the user's self-graded recall. Early algorithms such as SM-2 computed an easiness factor for each item; later systems, including Anki's scheduler and FSRS, model memory stability and retrievability statistically.

Applications

The technique is widely used for language learning, medical education, and examination preparation, where large bodies of factual knowledge must be retained over long periods.
