UKUSA Agreement

The UKUSA Agreement is a multilateral treaty for joint cooperation in signals intelligence between Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States. The alliance of these five countries is commonly known as the Five Eyes. Signed originally by Britain and the United States shortly after the Second World War, the agreement formalised the wartime code-breaking partnership built around Bletchley Park and its American counterparts.

Under the agreement, the signatory agencies share intercepted communications, traffic analysis, cryptanalysis resources and acquired communications intelligence, and they divide global collection responsibilities geographically. The participating agencies include the NSA (United States), GCHQ (United Kingdom), CSE (Canada), ASD (Australia) and GCSB (New Zealand).

History

The agreement grew out of the 1943 BRUSA Agreement and was signed in secret on 5 March 1946. Canada joined in 1948, and Australia and New Zealand in 1956. The existence of the agreement was not officially acknowledged for decades; it was publicly confirmed in 2010 when the full text was released by the UK National Archives and the NSA.

Scope

Participating agencies cooperate on the collection and dissemination of signals intelligence (SIGINT), communications security, and the protection of shared intelligence material. The agreement also governs relationships with "third party" partner nations that cooperate with the Five Eyes without full membership.
