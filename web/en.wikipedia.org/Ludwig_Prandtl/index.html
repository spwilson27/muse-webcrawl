Ludwig Prandtl

Born4 February 1875, Freising, Germany
Died15 August 1953, Göttingen, Germany
FieldFluid mechanics, aerodynamics

Ludwig Prandtl (1875–1953) was a German fluid dynamicist and aerospace scientist widely regarded as the founder of modern aerodynamics.

Contributions

In 1904 Prandtl introduced the concept of the boundary layer — the thin layer of fluid near a surface where viscosity matters — which made it possible to reconcile theory with the observed drag on wings and bodies. At Göttingen he built a school of fluid mechanics, developed the lifting-line theory of wings, the Prandtl–Glauert compressibility rule, and wind tunnels that advanced aircraft design. The dimensionless Prandtl number, relating momentum and thermal diffusivity, is named after him. His students included Theodore von Kármán.
