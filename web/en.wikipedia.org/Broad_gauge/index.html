Broad-gauge railway

A broad-gauge railway is a railway with a track gauge (the distance between the rails) broader than the 1,435 mm (4 ft 8½ in) used by standard-gauge railways. Broad gauge was widely used historically and remains dominant on several national networks.

Major broad gauges

  • 1,676 mm (5 ft 6 in) — Indian gauge: used in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Argentina and Chile; the most extensive broad gauge in the world by route length.
  • 1,600 mm (5 ft 3 in) — Irish gauge: Ireland, Northern Ireland, parts of Brazil and Victoria, Australia.
  • 1,668 mm (5 ft 5⅔ in) — Iberian gauge: Spain and Portugal.
  • 1,520 mm (4 ft 11⅞ in) — Russian gauge: Russia and other former Soviet states, Finland (1,524 mm), Mongolia.

History

Isambard Kingdom Brunel chose 7 ft ¼ in (2,140 mm) for Britain's Great Western Railway, arguing for greater speed and stability, but the "gauge war" ended in favour of standard gauge for interoperability. Many early American railways used gauges from 4 ft 10 in to 6 ft before standardisation. Broad gauges generally permit wider vehicles, higher capacity and stability, at the cost of higher construction expense and incompatibility at break-of-gauge points.
