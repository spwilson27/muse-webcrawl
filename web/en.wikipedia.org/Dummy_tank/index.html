Dummy tank

Dummy tanks are decoy military vehicles made to resemble real tanks, used to deceive enemy reconnaissance about the strength and location of armoured forces. They have been made of wood, canvas, and, most famously, inflatable rubber.

History

Decoy tanks were used in the First World War and extensively in the Second World War. British forces used dummy armour in North Africa, and in 1944 the American "Ghost Army" (the 23rd Headquarters Special Troops) deployed inflatable tanks, complete with sound and radio deception, to mislead German forces in Europe, including around the Normandy and Rhine operations. Dummy tanks remained in use in later conflicts, sometimes fitted with heaters to mimic a real vehicle's thermal signature against infrared sensors.
