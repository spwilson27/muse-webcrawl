Ecofemminism
Term coined: 1974, Françoise d'Eaubonne
Fields: Feminism, environmentalism, philosophy
Key figures: Vandana Shiva, Maria Mies, Val Plumwood, Carolyn Merchant

Ecofemminism is a philosophical and political movement that links the oppression of women with the degradation of the natural environment, arguing that both arise from patriarchal structures that treat women and nature as resources to be dominated. The term was coined by French writer Françoise d'Eaubonne in her 1974 book Le Féminisme ou la Mort.

Ideas

Ecofeminist thinkers such as Carolyn Merchant (The Death of Nature, 1980) traced how the scientific revolution reframed nature as a passive machine to be exploited, in parallel with the subordination of women. Vandana Shiva and Maria Mies connected environmental destruction to global capitalism and its impact on women in the Global South, who often bear the greatest burden of ecological harm as food producers and water gatherers.

Branches and criticism

The movement includes spiritual, materialist and constructivist strands. Critics — including some feminists — have challenged strands of ecofeminism that appear to essentialise an equation between women and nature, while materialist ecofeminists emphasise political economy over spirituality. The movement has influenced environmental justice activism and feminist political ecology.
