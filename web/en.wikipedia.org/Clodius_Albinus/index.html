Decimus Clodius Septimius Albinus (c. 150 – 19 February 197) was a Roman general and usurper who proclaimed himself emperor during the civil wars that followed the murder of Pertinax in 193, the Year of the Five Emperors. As governor of Britain he commanded three legions and the allegiance of the armies of the northwest; after years of uneasy cooperation with Septimius Severus, he was defeated and killed at the Battle of Lugdunum, one of the largest battles ever fought between Roman armies.

Early career

Albinus was born at Hadrumetum in North Africa into a senatorial family. He served with distinction under Marcus Aurelius on the Danube and in the east, governed provinces including Dacia and Bithynia, and was consul around 187. By 191 he was governor of Britain, the empire's largest provincial army command.

Bid for the throne

When the Praetorian Guard auctioned the throne to Didius Julianus in 193, the legions of Britain, Gaul, and Spain acclaimed Albinus emperor, while Severus marched from Pannonia and Pescennius Niger was proclaimed in Syria. Severus, needing to deal with Niger first, bought Albinus's neutrality by granting him the title of Caesar and a consulship. Once Niger was defeated, Severus moved west, and in 196 Albinus was declared a public enemy; he crossed to Gaul and proclaimed himself Augustus.

Defeat and death

The rival armies met near Lugdunum (modern Lyon) on 19 February 197. Ancient sources claim some 150,000 men fought; after a hard and initially successful attack, Albinus's forces were routed. He fled into the city and died there — by suicide or capture and execution — and Severus had his body abused and his family killed, eliminating the last rival and founding the Severan dynasty.
