German battleship Tirpitz

Tirpitz
Class: Bismarck-class battleship
Launched: 1 April 1939
Commissioned: 25 February 1941
Displacement: about 42,900 tonnes standard
Fate: Sunk by RAF bombers, 12 November 1944

Tirpitz was the second of two Bismarck-class battleships built for Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine during the Second World War. Named after Grand Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz, she was the sister ship of Bismarck and the heaviest battleship ever built by a European navy.

Service

After commissioning in 1941, Tirpitz was deployed to Norway, where her mere presence as a fleet in being forced the Royal Navy to keep powerful forces in home waters and heavily escort the Arctic convoys to the Soviet Union. She rarely fired her main guns in anger — notably bombarding Spitzbergen in Operation Zitronella (1943) — but was repeatedly attacked, including by British midget submarines in Operation Source (September 1943) and by carrier aircraft in Operation Tungsten (April 1944).

Sinking

On 12 November 1944, RAF Lancaster bombers of Nos. 9 and 617 Squadrons attacked with 12,000-pound Tallboy bombs near Tromsø. Tirpitz capsized with heavy loss of life, with around a thousand of her crew killed. The wreck was salvaged and broken up after the war.
