Immersion (virtual reality)

Immersion into virtual reality (VR) is the perception of being physically present in a non-physical world. The perception is created by surrounding the user of the VR system in images, sound or other stimuli that provide an engrossing total environment. The term is closely related to the psychological state of presence — the subjective feeling of "being there" in the virtual environment.

Types of immersion

Researchers commonly distinguish three kinds of immersion. Sensory immersion occurs when the virtual environment fills the user's field of view and engages multiple senses, as in head-mounted displays. Challenge-based immersion arises when users are absorbed by balanced challenges and rewards, as in games. Imaginative immersion occurs when users become absorbed in a narrative or identify with characters, in the way readers are drawn into a novel.

Contributing factors

Immersion is strengthened by a wide field of view, high display resolution and refresh rate, accurate head and body tracking with low latency, spatialized audio, haptic feedback, and consistent interaction. Breaks in immersion — the moments when technical failures or inconsistencies remind the user of the real world — undermine presence. Motion sickness from latency or mismatched motion cues is a major barrier.

Applications

Beyond gaming and entertainment, immersive VR is used in flight and surgical simulation, psychotherapy (including exposure therapy for phobias and PTSD), architectural visualization, education, and industrial training.
