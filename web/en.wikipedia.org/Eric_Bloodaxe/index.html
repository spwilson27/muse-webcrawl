Eric Haraldsson (Old Norse: Eiríkr blóðøx; c. 885–954), nicknamed Eric Bloodaxe, was a tenth-century Norwegian king, the favoured son of Harald Fairhair. He ruled Norway briefly before being driven out by his half-brother Haakon the Good, and twice became king of Northumbria in northern England, where he was killed at the Battle of Stainmore in 954 — the last independent Scandinavian king of York.

Life

According to the sagas, Eric won his epithet by murdering several of his brothers to secure the throne; modern historians treat parts of the saga account cautiously but accept the broad outline. After Harald Fairhair's death, Eric ruled Norway for about two years before Haakon, raised at the court of Æthelstan of England, returned and displaced him with popular support.

Eric then took to the sea and, in 947, was accepted as king at York, whose Norse-Irish rulers sought a counterweight to English expansion. He was expelled in 948, restored in 952 after the murder of his rival Olaf Sihtricson, and finally defeated and killed in 954, when Northumbria was permanently absorbed into the kingdom of England. Coins struck in his name at York name him Eric rex.

Reputation and sources

Eric's memory survives in Icelandic sagas, skaldic verse (including the death-song Eiríksmál, said to have been commissioned by his widow Gunnhild), and English chronicles. Saga authors painted him as ruthless but also as a ruler wronged by Gunnhild's sorcery and ambition; the thirteenth-century Egil's Saga dramatises his feud with the skald Egil Skallagrímsson, whom Eric spared after Egil composed a praise-poem overnight — the famous "head-ransom" episode at York.
