Wang Xizhi (王羲之, 303–361) was a Chinese calligrapher of the Eastern Jin dynasty, traditionally honoured as the Sage of Calligraphy (書聖). Coming from the eminent Wang clan of Langya, he served as a general and administrator but is remembered entirely for his art, which set the standard for Chinese calligraphy — especially the running (semi-cursive) script — for the next sixteen centuries.

The Orchid Pavilion Preface

Wang's masterpiece is the Lantingji Xu (蘭亭集序), the Preface to the Poems Composed at the Orchid Pavilion, written in 353 at a gathering of 42 literati at the Orchid Pavilion near Shaoxing for the Spring Purification Festival. Composed in a single inspired draft — tradition says Wang tried and failed to improve on it the next day — the preface meditates on the pleasures of the gathering and the brevity of life. No original survives; the work is known from faithful Tang-dynasty traced copies, the finest being the "Shenlong version" attributed to Feng Chengsu, now in the Palace Museum, Beijing.

Style and legacy

Wang mastered regular, running, and cursive scripts, freeing calligraphy from archaic stiffness and endowing it with natural grace and rhythmic vitality. The Tang emperor Taizong was so devoted a collector of his works that, legend holds, the original Lantingji Xu was buried with the emperor in his tomb. Countless later calligraphers, including his seventh son Wang Xianzhi, studied the "Two Wangs" as the supreme model.
