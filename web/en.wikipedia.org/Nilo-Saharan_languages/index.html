Nilo-Saharan languages

The Nilo-Saharan languages are a proposed language family spoken in northern and eastern Africa, from the Nile Valley and the Sahara to the Great Lakes region. The grouping, introduced by Joseph Greenberg, would unite branches such as Saharan (including Kanuri), Songhai, Fur, Maban, Central Sudanic, Eastern Sudanic (including the Nilotic languages and Nubian), Kunama and Kadu.

Status of the proposal

Nilo-Saharan is controversial: many specialists accept some internal groupings but regard the overall family as undemonstrated, because the shared features adduced may reflect contact rather than common descent. It would include on the order of 200 languages, among them Dinka, Nuer, Maasai, Luo, Kanuri and the ancient Nubian languages of the middle Nile.
