The Pamir Mountains are a mountain range in Central Asia, centred on the Gorno-Badakhshan autonomous region of eastern Tajikistan and extending into Afghanistan's Wakhan Corridor, China's Xinjiang, and the borderlands of Kyrgyzstan and Pakistan. Often called the "Roof of the World", the Pamirs form the knot from which radiate the Himalayas, Karakoram, Hindu Kush, Kunlun and Tian Shan ranges.

Geography

Much of the range is a high, cold desert plateau above 3,500 m, crossed by broad valleys. Its highest summit is Kongur Tagh (7,649 m) in China; the highest in Tajikistan is Ismoil Somoni Peak (7,495 m), known in Soviet times as Communism Peak. The Fedchenko Glacier, about 77 km long, is the longest glacier in the world outside the polar regions. Large high-altitude lakes include Lake Karakul and Lake Sarez, the latter formed by a landslide in 1911.

History

Branches of the Silk Road crossed the Pamirs, and Marco Polo described the region's wild sheep — the Marco Polo sheep — after traversing it in the thirteenth century. In the nineteenth century the mountains became a theatre of the "Great Game" between the British and Russian empires, and their frontiers were fixed by treaty in the 1890s, creating the Wakhan Corridor as a buffer. The Soviet-built Pamir Highway (M41), one of the world's highest international roads, links Khorog with Dushanbe and Osh across passes exceeding 4,600 m.

People

The valleys are home to the Pamiri peoples, speakers of Eastern Iranian languages such as Shughni and Wakhi, many of them followers of Ismaili Islam, alongside ethnic Kyrgyz pastoralists on the northern and eastern high plateaus.
