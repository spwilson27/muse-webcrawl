Kurt Waldemar Tank (1898–1983) was a German aeronautical engineer and test pilot who led aircraft design at Focke-Wulf from 1931 to 1945. His Fw 190 became one of the Luftwaffe's principal fighters of the Second World War, and after the war he designed early jet aircraft for Argentina and India.

Focke-Wulf years

Tank joined Focke-Wulf in 1931 as head of the design department and flight testing, personally test-flying many of his creations. His designs included the Fw 200 Condor airliner and, most famously, the Fw 190 Würger (1939), whose radial-engine design complemented the Messerschmitt Bf 109. Later high-altitude developments bore his initials as the "Ta" series (Ta 152, Ta 154).

Postwar career

In 1947 Tank emigrated to Argentina, where for Juan Perón's government he developed the FMA IAe 33 Pulqui II jet fighter, first flown in 1950. After Perón's fall he moved to India in 1956 and led the design of the HAL HF-24 Marut, the first jet fighter designed in India, which entered service in the 1960s. He returned to Germany in 1969 as a consultant to MBB.
