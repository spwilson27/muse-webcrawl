Landnámabók

Landnámabók (Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈlantˌnauːmaˌpouːk], "Book of Settlements"), often shortened to Landnáma, is a medieval Icelandic written work which describes in considerable detail the settlement (landnám) of Iceland by the Norse in the 9th and 10th centuries AD.

Contents

Landnámabók is divided into five parts and over 100 chapters. The first part tells of how the island of Iceland was found. The book lists the names of more than 400 settlements and over 3,000 people. It describes the settlers (landnámsmenn, singular landnámsmaður) who arrived in Iceland, where they settled, and their descendants down to the 11th century. It also mentions over 400 places by name, many of which retain the names given by the original settlers to this day.

Versions

The work survives in several versions, of which the principal ones are Sturlubók, compiled by Sturla Þórðarson in the 13th century, and Hauksbók, compiled by Haukr Erlendsson in the early 14th century. A third version, Melabók, survives only in fragments. All of these are thought to derive from an earlier lost original, possibly compiled in the 12th century, sometimes attributed in part to Ari Þorgilsson the Learned.

Historical value

Landnámabók is one of the most important sources for the early history of Iceland and for the study of Icelandic genealogy. Although it was written down centuries after the events it describes, much of its material is believed to rest on oral tradition. It is closely related to Íslendingabók, Ari's brief history of Iceland. Together with the Icelandic sagas, it forms the foundation of the national historical tradition of Iceland.
