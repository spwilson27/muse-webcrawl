Monuments (cycling)

The cobbled sectors of Paris–Roubaix, the "Hell of the North", one of the five Monuments.

The Monuments are five classic one-day bicycle races considered the most prestigious and demanding events in professional road cycling outside the Grand Tours. All were founded before the First World War except Liège–Bastogne–Liège, which is older still, and all are characterised by great length (generally over 240 km), historic routes and iconic terrain such as cobblestones and short steep climbs.

The races

Milan–San Remo opens Monument season in March and is the longest, at around 290 km; long a sprinters' classic, it is decided on the Cipressa and Poggio climbs near the finish. The Tour of Flanders (Ronde van Vlaanderen) follows two weeks before Roubaix, over the cobbled bergs of East Flanders such as the Oude Kwaremont and Paterberg. Paris–Roubaix is defined by its brutal pavé sectors, including the Trouée d'Arenberg, and finishes in the Roubaix velodrome. Liège–Bastogne–Liège, the oldest of the five, rolls through the Ardennes over climbs like La Redoute. The Giro di Lombardia (Il Lombardia) closes the season in October amid the hills around Lake Como, traditionally climbing the Madonna del Ghisallo.

Records

Only three riders have won all five Monuments: Belgians Rik Van Looy, Eddy Merckx and Roger De Vlaeminck. Merckx holds the record with 19 Monument victories across his career. Winning all five remains one of the rarest career achievements in the sport; in the women's peloton, the corresponding races have progressively gained Monument-equivalent status though the designation is informal.
