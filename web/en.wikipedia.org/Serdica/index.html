Serdica

Serdica (Bulgarian: Сердика) is the historical name of Sofia, the capital of Bulgaria. Named for the Thracian tribe of the Serdi, the city grew under Roman rule into an important administrative and commercial centre of the province of Dacia Mediterranea. Constantine the Great is said to have called it "my Rome".

History

Serdica was fortified under Trajan and Marcus Aurelius, adorned with theatres, baths and temples, and hosted the imperial court of Galerius, whose Edict of Serdica (311) ended the Diocletianic persecution of Christians. In 343 the city hosted the Council of Serdica, an ecumenical council of bishops. The rotunda church of St George, amphitheatre remains and other ruins survive in modern Sofia, many displayed in the Serdika metro-station archaeological complex.
