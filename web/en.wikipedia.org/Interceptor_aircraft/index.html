Interceptor aircraft

An interceptor aircraft is a type of fighter aircraft designed specifically to intercept and destroy enemy aircraft, especially bombers and reconnaissance aircraft, usually relying on high speed and powerful armament. The type emerged during the Second World War and matured in the early Cold War.

Design

Interceptors emphasise climb rate, top speed and radar-guided missiles over manoeuvrability and endurance. Point-defence interceptors such as the Soviet MiG-21 defended fixed targets, while long-range types like the American F-106 Delta Dart and the Soviet Tu-128 patrolled vast airspaces.

Decline of the type

As intercontinental ballistic missiles reduced the manned-bomber threat and multirole fighters gained capable radars and missiles, the dedicated interceptor largely disappeared; the MiG-31 remains a notable late example.
