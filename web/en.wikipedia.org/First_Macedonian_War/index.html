First Macedonian War

Date214–205 BC
LocationMacedon, Illyria, and Greece
ResultPeace of Phoenice; status quo ante bellum
BelligerentsRoman Republic and allies vs. Macedon

The First Macedonian War (214–205 BC) was fought by Rome, allied with the Aetolian League and Pergamon, against Philip V of Macedon, contemporaneously with the Second Punic War against Carthage.

Background

Philip V, alarmed and opportunistic in the face of Rome's difficulties against Hannibal, concluded an alliance with Carthage in 215 BC after Hannibal's victory at Cannae. Rome responded by sending forces across the Adriatic to contain Macedon and prevent any junction between Philip and Hannibal.

Course of the war

Roman operations centred on the Illyrian coast and, from 211 BC, on an alliance with the Aetolian League, which bore the brunt of the land fighting in Greece along with Pergamon under Attalus I. Macedonian successes against the Aetolians led the league to make a separate peace in 206 BC, and Rome, still preoccupied with Hannibal, soon followed.

Peace of Phoenice

The war ended with the Peace of Phoenice in 205 BC, which broadly restored the status quo. The inconclusive settlement left Greek affairs unsettled and set the stage for the Second Macedonian War a few years later.
