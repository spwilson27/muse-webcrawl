Schönbrunn Palace

Schönbrunn Palace (German: Schloss Schönbrunn) is a former imperial summer residence located in the Hietzing district of Vienna, Austria. The Baroque palace is one of the most important architectural, cultural, and historical monuments in the country. The 1,441-room Rococo palace and its extensive gardens were the main summer residence of the Habsburg rulers. Since the mid-20th century it has been a major tourist attraction; the palace and park were designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996.

History

The property came into Habsburg hands in 1569. According to legend, Emperor Matthias discovered a spring (schöner Brunnen, "beautiful spring") on the estate in 1612, giving the palace its name. The present palace was built under Leopold I to designs by Johann Bernhard Fischer von Erlach beginning in the 1690s, and was substantially remodeled in the mid-18th century under Maria Theresa by Nikolaus Pacassi, acquiring its distinctive yellow façade.

The palace

State rooms include the Great Gallery, where court banquets were held, the Millions Room paneled in rosewood and decorated with Persian and Indian miniatures, and the Blue Chinese Salon where Emperor Charles I signed his abdication of participation in government affairs in 1918. Mozart performed in the Mirror Room as a six-year-old in 1762. Napoleon headquartered at Schönbrunn in 1805 and 1809, and the Congress of Vienna era saw the palace at the center of European diplomacy. Emperor Franz Joseph I was born at Schönbrunn in 1830 and died there in 1916.

Gardens

The gardens, designed in French Baroque style, include the Gloriette triumphal structure on the hill (1775), the Neptune Fountain, Roman ruins, an obelisk fountain, a maze, and the Palm House. The Tiergarten Schönbrunn, founded in 1752, is the world's oldest continuously operating zoo.
