Prince Marko

Prince Marko (Serbian: Краљевић Марко, Kraljević Marko; Bulgarian: Крали Марко; Macedonian: Крале Марко) was a Serbian prince, the son of King Vukašin Mrnjavčević, who ruled a principality centred on Prilep in western Macedonia from 1371 until his death in 1395. Though historically an Ottoman vassal, he became the most celebrated hero of South Slavic epic poetry, remembered as a defender of the weak and an opponent of Ottoman rule.

Prince Marko, as depicted in a fresco at Marko's Monastery.

Life

Marko succeeded his father Vukašin, who was killed at the Battle of Maritsa in 1371. His realm, one of several fragments of the collapsed Serbian Empire, lay in western Macedonia, and like other regional lords he accepted Ottoman suzerainty, supplying troops to the sultans. He died at the Battle of Rovine in 1395 fighting for the Ottomans against the Wallachians — tradition holds that before the battle he prayed that God help the Christians, and that he himself might be among the first to fall.

Epic tradition

In Serbian, Bulgarian, and Macedonian folklore, Marko is transformed into a superhuman hero of the age before his own: he shares wine with fairies (vile), defeats the Turkish champion Musa Kesedžija, drinks with and defies the sultan, and rides his miraculous speaking horse Šarac for 150 years. The epic cycle about him was recorded by Vuk Karadžić and others in the 19th century and remains central to South Slavic literary heritage. Marko's Monastery near Skopje, completed under his patronage, preserves his portrait in fresco.
