The 7.62×39mm (also called 7.62 Soviet or .30 Russian Short) is a rimless bottlenecked intermediate cartridge of Soviet origin. It was designed during the Second World War and first used in the SKS carbine, before becoming world famous as the cartridge of the AK-47 assault rifle and the RPD light machine gun.

History

Soviet designers sought a round intermediate between pistol and full-power rifle cartridges, influenced by the German 7.92×33mm Kurz. The M43 cartridge was finalized in 1943–44, and weapons chambered for it — the SKS (1945), AK-47 (1949) and RPD — armed the Warsaw Pact and were exported and copied worldwide, making 7.62×39mm one of the most widely used military cartridges of all time.

Characteristics

The cartridge's pronounced case taper aids reliable extraction in adverse conditions. Standard ball ammunition fires a bullet of about 7.9 g (122 gr) at around 715 m/s. Although the Soviet Union replaced it in front-line service with the 5.45×39mm from 1974, 7.62×39mm remains in widespread military, hunting and sporting use around the world.
