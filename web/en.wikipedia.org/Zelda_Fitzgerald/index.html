Zelda Fitzgerald

Zelda Fitzgerald (née Sayre; 1900–1948) was an American novelist, painter, and socialite. The wife of writer F. Scott Fitzgerald, she became an icon of the Jazz Age and was dubbed by her husband "the first American flapper".

Life

Zelda Sayre, daughter of an Alabama Supreme Court justice, met Scott Fitzgerald at a country club dance in 1918; they married in 1920 after the success of his first novel. The couple became celebrities of the 1920s, living in New York, Paris, and the French Riviera, and Zelda's image and writings — including material Scott drew from her diaries and letters — shaped his fiction.

Later years

From 1930 Zelda experienced repeated mental-health crises and was diagnosed (in the terms of the time) with schizophrenia; she spent much of her remaining life in psychiatric clinics. She wrote the semi-autobiographical novel Save Me the Waltz (1932), painted, and worked on a second novel. She died in a fire at Highland Hospital in Asheville in 1948.

Legacy

Modern scholarship has rehabilitated Zelda Fitzgerald as a creative artist in her own right, and her life has inspired numerous biographies, novels, and screen portrayals.
