Menthol

Menthol
Formula: C10H20O
Molar mass: 156.27 g/mol
Appearance: white crystalline solid
Melting point: 36–38 °C (levomenthol)

Menthol is an organic compound, more specifically a monoterpenoid alcohol, made synthetically or obtained from the oils of corn mint, peppermint, or other mints. It is a waxy, crystalline substance, clear or white in colour, solid at room temperature and melting slightly above it.

Properties and sensation

The main form occurring in nature is (−)-menthol, called levomenthol, with the (1R,2S,5R) configuration. Menthol produces its characteristic cooling sensation by activating the TRPM8 ion channel in sensory neurons, the same receptor that responds to cold temperatures — an effect exploited in lozenges, balms and inhalants, quite apart from any actual change in temperature.

Production

Natural menthol is obtained by freezing peppermint and corn mint (Mentha arvensis) oils and filtering out the crystals. Synthetic menthol is produced on a large scale, for example by hydrogenation of thymol or through the Haarmann–Reimer process starting from myrcene. World production runs to many thousands of tonnes annually.

Uses

Menthol is used in oral hygiene products, cosmetics, shaving creams, lip balms, throat lozenges and cough medicines, topical analgesics, chewing gum, confectionery and cigarettes, where it masks harshness and produces a cooling taste. In medicine it serves as a mild local anaesthetic and counterirritant for itching and muscle pain. It is also an intermediate in organic synthesis and a chiral auxiliary.

Safety

Menthol is generally regarded as safe at flavouring doses. Concentrated menthol can irritate skin and eyes, and ingestion of large quantities may cause abdominal pain, nausea and, rarely, more serious symptoms.
