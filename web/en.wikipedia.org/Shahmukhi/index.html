Shahmukhi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shahmukhi (Punjabi: شاہ مکھی, literally "from the mouth of the Shah") is the Perso-Arabic script used to write the Punjabi language in Pakistan, particularly in the province of Punjab, where it is the standard script of newspapers, books, and official use. In India, Punjabi is written in the Gurmukhi script, so the same language is written in two scripts across the border — a classic example of a biscriptal language.

History

The script developed from the 13th century onward as Punjabi-speaking Muslims adapted the Persian variant of the Arabic script to their language, and it became the vehicle of the classical Punjabi Sufi tradition: the poetry of Baba Farid (whose verses entered the Guru Granth Sahib), Shah Hussain, Sultan Bahu, Bulleh Shah, and Waris Shah, author of the epic Heer Ranjha, was composed and transmitted in Shahmukhi.

Alphabet

Shahmukhi is written right to left in the Nastaʿlīq style and is closely related to the script of Urdu, from which it is distinguished by additional letters for Punjabi sounds — notably retroflex and tonal features — using digraphs and the letter he with superscript strokes, and by adaptations such as the letter for /ɳ/ and /ɭ/. Like Urdu, it normally omits short vowels in ordinary text, marking them, when needed, with diacritics.
