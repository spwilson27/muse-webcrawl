Sprouting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Freshly sprouted mung beans

Sprouting is the practice of germinating seeds to be eaten raw or cooked. Sprouts can be germinated at home or produced industrially. Common seeds for sprouting include mung beans, alfalfa, lentils, chickpeas, soybeans, radish, broccoli, and various grains. A sprout consists of the seed together with the emerging root (radicle), stem, and first leaves, harvested before true leaves develop substantially.

Nutrition and safety

Germination mobilises stored nutrients, increasing some vitamins and reducing some antinutrients, and sprouts are eaten for their texture and freshness in salads, sandwiches, and Asian dishes (bean sprouts are stir-fried or used in soups). However, the warm, humid conditions ideal for sprouting also favour bacterial growth; outbreaks of Salmonella and E. coli linked to raw sprouts have led health authorities to recommend cooking sprouts for vulnerable people.

Methods

Home sprouting typically uses a jar with a mesh lid: seeds are soaked, then rinsed and drained two or three times daily for several days until the desired length is reached.
