Apprenticeship

An apprenticeship is a system of training a new generation of practitioners of a trade or profession, combining on-the-job training with accompanying study. Apprenticeships enable learners to earn a wage while mastering practical skills under the supervision of experienced workers, and they have historically been the main route into skilled crafts.

History

The system dates to the late Middle Ages, when guilds regulated training: a master craftsman would take on a young apprentice, usually bound by indenture for a term of years, providing instruction, food and lodging in return for labour. On completing the term and producing a "masterpiece", the apprentice became a journeyman and eventually a master.

Modern apprenticeships

Today apprenticeships are prominent in countries such as Germany and Switzerland, where the "dual system" combines workplace training with part-time vocational school. Many other countries have expanded apprenticeship programmes into fields beyond the traditional trades, including healthcare, information technology, finance and engineering, often leading to recognised qualifications.
