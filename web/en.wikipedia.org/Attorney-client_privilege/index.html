Attorney–client privilege

Attorney–client privilege, or legal advice privilege, is a legal privilege that protects confidential communications between a lawyer and their client made for the purpose of giving or receiving legal advice. It is one of the oldest privileges in common law.

Scope

The privilege belongs to the client, who alone may waive it. It generally covers communications, not underlying facts, and survives the end of the representation and the client's death in most jurisdictions.

Exceptions

Recognised exceptions include the crime-fraud exception, where legal advice is sought in furtherance of wrongdoing, and situations involving disputes between lawyer and client or threats of serious harm. Related but distinct doctrines include litigation privilege and the work-product doctrine in the United States.
