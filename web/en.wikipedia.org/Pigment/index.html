Pigment

A pigment is a coloured material that is completely or nearly insoluble in water and in the medium in which it is used; it colours surfaces by selective absorption and scattering of light. Unlike dyes, which dissolve, pigments are dispersed as fine particles in paints, inks, plastics and cosmetics.

Types and history

Prehistoric art used earth pigments such as ochre and charcoal. Historic pigments include ultramarine (from lapis lazuli), vermilion, lead white and madder lake; many traditional pigments were toxic. Modern synthetic organic and inorganic pigments — phthalocyanine blue and green, quinacridones, titanium white — offer brilliance and permanence. Biological pigments include chlorophyll, haemoglobin, carotenoids and melanin.

Properties

Important qualities are tinting strength, opacity, lightfastness, and chemical stability. The Colour Index International assigns generic names and constitution numbers to pigments used in industry.
