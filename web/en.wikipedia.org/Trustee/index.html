Trustee


A trustee administers property held in trust

A trustee is a person or institution given legal title to property held in trust, with a fiduciary duty to manage it solely for the benefit of the trust's beneficiaries, in accordance with the terms of the trust instrument and the law. The trustee may be an individual, such as a family member, or a professional or corporate trustee such as a bank.

Duties

Trustees owe beneficiaries duties of loyalty, prudence, impartiality, and accounting. They must invest trust assets as a prudent investor would, keep accurate records, avoid conflicts of interest and self-dealing, and distribute income or principal as the trust directs. Breach of these duties can make a trustee personally liable to restore losses.

Other uses

The term is used more broadly for members of governing boards—trustees of universities, museums, and charities—and for officials such as bankruptcy trustees, who administer a debtor's estate for creditors under court supervision. In some legal systems a trustee in bankruptcy or a public trustee holds property for those unable to manage it themselves.
