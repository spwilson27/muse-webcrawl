Cost–benefit analysis

Cost–benefit analysis (CBA) is a systematic approach to estimating the strengths and weaknesses of alternatives by assigning monetary values to their costs and benefits, used to determine the option providing the best approach while preserving savings.

Method

Analysts enumerate costs and benefits, monetise them (including intangibles such as time and statistical lives), discount future values to present values, and compare net present value or benefit–cost ratios, with sensitivity analysis for uncertainty.

Use in policy

CBA is required for major regulations in the United States and widely used for transport, environmental and health policy. Criticisms concern monetisation of life and nature, distributional effects and discounting of future generations.
