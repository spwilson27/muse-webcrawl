The Russky Bridge (Russian: Русский мост, "Russian Bridge") is a cable-stayed bridge in Vladivostok, Russia, spanning the Eastern Bosphorus strait between the Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula and Russky Island. With a main span of 1,104 metres, it is the longest cable-stayed bridge in the world; its M-shaped pylons rise 321 metres, among the tallest bridge towers anywhere.

Construction

The bridge was built as part of the infrastructure programme for the 2012 APEC summit, held at the new Far Eastern Federal University campus on Russky Island. Construction ran from 2008 to 2012 in one of the harshest climates of any major bridge: temperatures range from about −31 °C to +37 °C, with strong winds and ice formation in the strait. The stayed-cable system uses parallel strands with special damping to handle wind and rain vibration.

Design

The bridge carries four lanes of traffic and has a total length of 3,100 metres including approaches. The cable stays, coloured in the white, blue and red of the Russian flag, are the longest in the world on such a structure. A second major bridge, the Zolotoy Bridge across Zolotoy Rog Bay, opened in the same period, transforming Vladivostok's transport geography.

Significance

The bridge is a symbol of Russia's "pivot to Asia" and of Vladivostok's development, and it features on the 2,000-rouble banknote issued in 2017.
