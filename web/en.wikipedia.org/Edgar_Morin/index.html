Edgar Morin (born Edgar Nahoum, 8 July 1921) is a French philosopher and sociologist known for his work on complexity and transdisciplinarity. He is a research director emeritus at the French National Centre for Scientific Research (CNRS) and one of France's most prominent public intellectuals, whose career has spanned the study of cinema, mass culture, politics and systems theory.

Life and career

Born in Paris to a family of Sephardic Jewish origin from Salonica, Morin joined the French Resistance during the Second World War, adopting the pseudonym "Morin" which he later kept. After the war he was briefly active in communist circles before breaking with the French Communist Party in 1951. At the CNRS he founded the Centre for the Study of Mass Communication and co-founded the journal Arguments. His early sociological works included Le cinéma ou l'homme imaginaire (1956) and studies of contemporary culture and rumour.

Complex thought

Morin's central project is "complex thought" (pensée complexe), set out in the six volumes of La Méthode (1977–2004). Rejecting reductionism, he argues that knowledge must connect what disciplinary specialisation separates, embracing uncertainty, contradiction and the interdependence of observer and observed. His UNESCO-commissioned Seven Complex Lessons in Education for the Future (1999) advocates reforming education to teach the human condition and planetary interdependence. He has also written extensively on Europe, ecology and politics, notably in Terre-Patrie (1993, with Anne Brigitte Kern).
