Raška (Serbian Cyrillic: Рашка) is a geographical and historical region in southwestern Serbia, centred on the valleys of the Raška and Ibar rivers. It takes its name from Ras, a medieval fortress and town near modern Novi Pazar, which was one of the earliest capitals of the Serbian medieval state.

History

In the early Middle Ages Raška was the core territory of the Serbian principality ruled by the Vlastimirović dynasty, and later the heartland of the Grand Principality of Serbia under the Nemanjić dynasty from the 12th century. Great Župan Stefan Nemanja ruled from Ras, and the region's monasteries — including Đurđevi Stupovi, Sopoćani, and nearby Studenica — became centres of Serbian church and culture. The name "Raška" was used in Latin and Hungarian sources as a synonym for Serbia itself, and Serbian rulers were sometimes styled kings of Rascia.

Geography

The region is mountainous, bordered by Kopaonik to the east and the Pešter plateau to the west. Its principal towns are Novi Pazar, Raška, and Tutin. Today its territory overlaps largely with the modern Raška District and forms part of the wider Sandžak area.
