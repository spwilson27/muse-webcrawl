X-ray optics is the branch of optics that manipulates X-rays instead of visible light. Because X-rays interact only weakly with matter and are strongly absorbed by most materials, conventional lenses and mirrors used at normal incidence generally do not work; instead, X-ray optical systems rely on reflection at grazing incidence, diffraction, or refraction by very small amounts.

Principles

The refractive index of all materials for X-rays is slightly less than one, so X-rays incident on a surface from vacuum or air undergo total external reflection if the grazing angle is below a critical angle, typically a fraction of a degree to a few degrees depending on photon energy and the reflecting material. Dense, high-atomic-number coatings such as gold, platinum or iridium are used to maximize reflectivity.

Grazing-incidence mirrors

The most widely used focusing geometry is the Wolter telescope, described by Hans Wolter in 1952, in which X-rays reflect successively from a paraboloid and a hyperboloid (or ellipsoid). Nested Wolter mirror shells form the heart of X-ray observatories such as Chandra, XMM-Newton and NuSTAR. The Kirkpatrick–Baez geometry uses two orthogonal curved mirrors to focus in two dimensions and is common at synchrotron sources.

Diffractive and refractive optics

Fresnel zone plates focus X-rays by diffraction and are widely used in scanning transmission X-ray microscopes, reaching spatial resolutions of tens of nanometres. Compound refractive lenses, stacks of many weakly refracting lenslets, are used to focus hard X-rays at synchrotrons and X-ray free-electron lasers. Multilayer mirrors, alternating nanometre-scale layers of two materials, act as Bragg reflectors at larger angles and are essential for extreme ultraviolet lithography.

Applications

X-ray optics enable X-ray astronomy, materials characterization at synchrotron facilities, phase-contrast imaging in medicine and industry, and X-ray microscopy of biological specimens. Capillary (polycapillary) optics collect and redirect X-rays in laboratory instruments such as X-ray fluorescence spectrometers.
