Lorient

Lorient (Breton: An Oriant) is a commune and seaport in the Morbihan department of Brittany in western France, with a population of roughly 58,000. Its name derives from L'Orient ("the East"), recalling its origins as a base of the French East India Company trading with Asia.

History

The Compagnie des Indes established shipyards at the site from 1666, building up a settlement around the confluence of the Scorff and Blavet rivers. After the company's fortunes declined, the French Navy took over, and Lorient grew into one of France's principal naval bases. In the Second World War the German Kriegsmarine built the massive Keroman submarine pens there; because the concrete U-boat base could not readily be destroyed, the Allies bombed the surrounding city flat, destroying most of Lorient in 1943, though the pens survived and remain standing today.

The post-war city was rebuilt in a functional modern style. The submarine base has been redeveloped as a centre for offshore racing and maritime heritage (the Cité de la Voile Éric Tabarly), and Lorient is a major fishing port — one of France's largest — as well as a shipbuilding and composite-materials centre.

Culture and sport

Lorient hosts the Festival Interceltique de Lorient each August, one of the world's largest Celtic cultural festivals, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors. The city's football club, FC Lorient, nicknamed Les Merlus (the Hakes), plays at the Stade du Moustoir and has spent multiple seasons in Ligue 1.
