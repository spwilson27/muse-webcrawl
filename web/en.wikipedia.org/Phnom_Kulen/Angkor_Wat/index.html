Angkor Wat

Angkor Wat is a temple complex in Cambodia, located on a site measuring 162.6 hectares within the ancient Khmer capital city of Angkor. The Guinness World Records considers it as the largest religious structure in the world. Originally constructed as a Hindu temple dedicated to the god Vishnu for the Khmer Empire by King Suryavarman II during the 12th century, it was gradually transformed into a Buddhist temple towards the end of the century.

Architecture

Angkor Wat combines the temple mountain and galleried temple plans of Khmer architecture. Its five towers represent the five peaks of Mount Meru, home of the Hindu gods, and its outer wall and moat represent the surrounding mountain ranges and ocean. The temple is famed for its extensive bas-reliefs depicting Hindu epics and historical scenes, and for the devata carvings on its walls.

History

Construction is estimated to have taken some 30 years in the early 12th century. Unlike most Khmer temples, Angkor Wat faces west. After the Khmer Empire's decline it was maintained by Theravada Buddhist monks and never entirely abandoned. It appears on Cambodia's national flag.

Conservation

The temple complex is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of Angkor Archaeological Park and has undergone extensive conservation, notably by the Archaeological Survey of India and international teams.
