Phnom Kulen


The Kbal Spean riverbed near Phnom Kulen is carved with lingas and images of Hindu gods.

Phnom Kulen is a mountain range and national park in Siem Reap Province, Cambodia, rising to about 487 metres roughly 48 kilometres north-east of the city of Siem Reap. Its name means "Mountain of the Lychees." The range is held sacred by Cambodians as the birthplace of the Khmer Empire: it was here, on ancient Mahendraparvata, that Jayavarman II is said to have proclaimed himself chakravartin (universal ruler) in 802 CE, in the ceremony traditionally taken to found the empire that later built Angkor.

Archaeology

The plateau carries numerous brick temples and carvings of the early Angkorian period. At Kbal Spean, the bed of the Stung Kbal Spean river is carved over long stretches with lingas, yonis, and images of Vishnu, Brahma, and Shiva, so that the water flowing down to Angkor was ritually purified — the site is popularly called the "River of a Thousand Lingas." In 2012–2013, airborne lidar surveys led by Damian Evans and colleagues revealed beneath the jungle a vast, formally planned urban landscape, Mahendraparvata, one of the earliest Angkorian cities, with gridded roads, ponds, and a huge reservoir. Phnom Kulen's sandstone quarries also supplied much of the stone for Angkor's temples.

Religious significance and tourism

The mountain remains an active pilgrimage site. Near the summit, the temple of Preah Ang Thom houses a large reclining Buddha carved from a single sandstone boulder in the 16th century. Pilgrims and tourists also visit the two-level Kulen waterfall and the linga carvings. Phnom Kulen National Park, established in 1993, protects some 375 square kilometres of forest, though it faces pressure from logging, quarrying, and settlement.
