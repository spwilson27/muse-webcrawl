Phnom Kulen (Khmer: ភ្នំគូលែន, "Mountain of Lychees") is a mountain range and a part of Phnom Kulen National Park in Siem Reap Province, Cambodia. Rather than a single mountain, it is a chain of small mountain plateaux of moderate height, running about 40 km (25 mi) north of Angkor. Phnom Kulen is considered a holy mountain in Cambodia, of special religious significance to Hindus and Buddhists.

Mahendraparvata

Phnom Kulen is the site of Mahendraparvata, the capital city of King Jayavarman II, founder of the Khmer Empire. In 802 AD, according to later inscriptions, Jayavarman II proclaimed himself chakravartin (universal ruler) on the mountain in a ceremony declaring Cambodia's independence from "Java," conventionally regarded as the founding act of the Khmer Empire. Long known from inscriptions, the city was mapped in detail in 2013, when airborne lidar surveys revealed a vast planned urban landscape of roads, canals, ponds and temple foundations beneath the forest.

Sacred sites

The plateau contains dozens of ruined temples and carvings. At Kbal Spean, the "River of a Thousand Lingas," the riverbed is carved with lingams and images of Hindu gods, sanctifying the water flowing down to Angkor's reservoirs. The mountaintop pagoda of Preah Ang Thom houses a large reclining Buddha carved from a sandstone boulder in the 16th century. Sandstone quarries on the mountain supplied stone for the temples of Angkor.

National park

Phnom Kulen National Park, established in 1993, protects the watershed forests feeding the Siem Reap River, along with waterfalls popular for bathing and picnics. The area was among the last Khmer Rouge strongholds, held until 1998, and landmines long restricted access.
