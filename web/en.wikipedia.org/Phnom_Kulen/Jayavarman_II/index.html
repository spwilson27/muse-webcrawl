Jayavarman II (Khmer: ជ័យវរ្ម័នទី២; c. 770 – 850) is widely regarded as the founder of the Khmer Empire, the powerful Hindu-Buddhist empire that dominated Southeast Asia from the 9th to the 15th century. Cambodian tradition and later inscriptions credit him with unifying the divided Chenla polities and declaring independence from "Java" in a famous ceremony in 802 CE.

The 802 ceremony

According to the Sdok Kak Thom inscription, carved some 250 years after his death, Jayavarman II performed a ritual on the summit of Phnom Kulen (Mount Mahendra) in 802 in which a Brahmin priest declared him a chakravartin, or universal ruler, and instituted the devaraja ("god-king") cult, identifying the king with the Hindu god Shiva. The date 802 is conventionally taken as the beginning of the Angkorian period.

Reign

Jayavarman II established a series of capitals, including the recently identified city of Mahendraparvata on Phnom Kulen — revealed in detail by lidar surveys beginning in 2012 — before settling at Hariharalaya, near present-day Roluos southeast of Angkor. His reign laid the political and religious foundations — divine kingship, state temples, and centralized administration — on which his successors built the empire.

Legacy

Although few inscriptions from his own reign survive and much of the narrative rests on later sources, Jayavarman II occupies a foundational place in Cambodian national history, comparable to that of a founding monarch in other traditions.
