Pumpkin pie

Pumpkin pie is a dessert pie with a spiced, pumpkin-based custard filling, traditionally served in the United States and Canada during the autumn and early winter, especially at Thanksgiving and Christmas. The pumpkin is native to North America, and the pie has become an emblematic part of the Thanksgiving meal.

Description

The filling consists of cooked, puréed pumpkin (often canned pumpkin, which may be made from Dickinson squash) blended with eggs, milk or evaporated milk, sugar, and a characteristic mixture of spices — usually cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, and cloves, sold premixed as "pumpkin pie spice." The filling ranges in color from orange to brown and is baked in a single pastry shell, typically without a top crust, until set. It is commonly served with whipped cream.

History

Early American settlers made pumpkin-based dishes by hollowing out the squash, filling it with milk, honey, and spices, and baking it in hot ashes. Recipes resembling the modern pie appeared in American cookbooks by the late eighteenth century; Amelia Simmons included a pumpkin pudding baked in a crust in American Cookery (1796). Libby's began selling canned pumpkin in 1929, and its recipe printed on the can label — the "Famous Pumpkin Pie" — became the standard home version.

Cultural significance

Pumpkin pie is so strongly associated with Thanksgiving that the holiday is sometimes called "Turkey Day," with the pie as its signature dessert. A giant pumpkin pie baked in New Bremen, Ohio, in 2010 weighed 3,699 pounds and holds the record as the world's largest. Variations include sweet potato pie in the American South and pumpkin tarts in Canada.
