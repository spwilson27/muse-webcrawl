Die Walküre

Die Walküre (The Valkyrie)
Opera by Richard Wagner
Second of the four operas of Der Ring des Nibelungen
Premiere: 26 June 1870, Munich

Die Walküre (The Valkyrie), WWV 86B, is the second of the four music dramas that constitute Richard Wagner's cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen. It was first performed on 26 June 1870 at the National Theatre Munich, against Wagner's wishes, at the insistence of King Ludwig II.

Plot

The opera tells of the twins Siegmund and Sieglinde, separated children of the god Wotan, who fall in love; of Wotan's conflict with his wife Fricka over his support of Siegmund; and of the Valkyrie Brünnhilde, Wotan's favourite daughter, who defies her father to protect Siegmund and is punished by being stripped of her divinity and put to sleep on a rock surrounded by magic fire.

Music

The most famous passage is the "Ride of the Valkyries" opening Act 3, one of the best-known excerpts in all opera. The opera also contains the lyrical "Winterstürme" aria and the closing "Magic Fire Music".
