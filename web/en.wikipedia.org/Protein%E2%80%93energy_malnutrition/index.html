Protein–energy malnutrition

Protein–energy malnutrition
Specialty: Endocrinology, nutrition
Forms: marasmus, kwashiorkor
Risk factors: poverty, famine, illness

Protein–energy malnutrition (PEM), sometimes called protein–energy undernutrition, is a form of malnutrition defined by a deficiency of dietary protein and/or energy (calories). It is a major cause of illness and death in children in low-income settings and also affects hospital patients and the elderly worldwide.

Classification

Severe acute malnutrition in children presents principally as marasmus, a wasting syndrome driven mainly by energy deficiency, and kwashiorkor, characterized by edema, skin and hair changes, and fatty liver, historically attributed to protein deficiency with relatively higher energy intake. Intermediate and combined forms (marasmic kwashiorkor) are recognized, and assessment uses weight-for-height, mid-upper-arm circumference, and the presence of bilateral pitting edema.

Treatment

Management follows staged protocols: initial stabilization treating hypoglycemia, hypothermia, dehydration, and infection, followed by nutritional rehabilitation with therapeutic feeds such as F-75 and F-100 or ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF).
