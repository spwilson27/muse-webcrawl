First Barbary War

The First Barbary War (1801–1805), also known as the Tripolitan War and the Barbary Coast War, was the first of two Barbary Wars, in which the United States and Sweden fought against the four North African states known collectively as the Barbary States — the Sultanate of Morocco and the Regencies of Algiers, Tunis and Tripoli. Three of these were nominal provinces of the Ottoman Empire, but in practice autonomous.

Causes

Barbary corsairs seized American merchant ships and enslaved crews, demanding tribute in exchange for safe passage. After independence, American shipping lost British protection, and the United States paid substantial tributes and ransoms through the 1790s. When the Pasha of Tripoli, Yusuf Karamanli, demanded an increased payment and received none, he declared war in May 1801 by cutting down the flagstaff of the U.S. consulate.

Course of the war

President Thomas Jefferson dispatched a naval squadron to the Mediterranean. The war's most famous episodes include the grounding and capture of the frigate USS Philadelphia in Tripoli harbour in 1803, and Lieutenant Stephen Decatur's daring raid in February 1804 to burn her so she could not be used by the enemy — an exploit Admiral Nelson is said to have called the most bold of its age. In 1805, William Eaton led a mixed force of Marines and mercenaries across the desert from Egypt to capture Derna — the source of the Marine Corps hymn line "to the shores of Tripoli". The threat to Tripoli itself induced the Pasha to sign a peace treaty in June 1805.

Aftermath

The treaty ended the enslavement of American captives, though disputes over tribute continued until the Second Barbary War in 1815 finally ended American payments.
