Virtual Light is a science fiction novel by American-Canadian writer William Gibson, published in 1993. It is the first book of the Bridge trilogy, followed by Idoru (1996) and All Tomorrow's Parties (1999). The novel was a finalist for the Nebula Award and the Hugo Award for Best Novel.

Plot

Set in a post-earthquake California of 2005, the novel follows Chevette Washington, a bicycle courier in San Francisco who impulsively steals a pair of high-tech sunglasses — "virtual light" glasses containing valuable data — from a man at a party, and Barry Rydell, a former police officer turned rent-a-cop sent to retrieve them. Their story plays out against the setting of the Bridge: the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge, closed after an earthquake and colonised by a shantytown community, which becomes central to the plot's conflict between the homeless community and a corporate plan to rebuild the city.

Themes

The novel explores gentrification, privatised policing, media saturation and the improvisational repurposing of technology. Gibson coined the glasses' name from research into a real display technology at the University of Washington's Human Interface Technology Lab.
