Johannes Diderik van der Waals (1837–1923) was a Dutch theoretical physicist who founded the modern theory of the continuity of gaseous and liquid states.

Work

His 1873 doctoral thesis introduced the van der Waals equation, which improves the ideal gas law by accounting for the finite size of molecules and the attractive forces between them — forces now called van der Waals forces. He also formulated the law of corresponding states, allowing different gases and liquids to be compared on a common scale.

Career and honours

Largely self-educated, van der Waals became the first professor of physics at the University of Amsterdam in 1877 and received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1910. The van der Waals radius and the craters and institutions bearing his name commemorate his influence on physical chemistry.
