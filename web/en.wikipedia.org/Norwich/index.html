Norwich is a cathedral city and district of Norfolk, England, on the River Wensum. It was England's second city after London for much of the medieval and early modern period, enriched by the wool and worsted cloth trade.

History

The Anglo-Saxon settlement grew into a major trading centre; after the Norman Conquest, Norwich Castle and the cathedral (begun 1096) were built. Flemish "Strangers" in the 16th century revitalised its textile industry. In 2012 Norwich became England's first UNESCO City of Literature.

Landmarks

The city preserves an exceptional collection of medieval churches — 31 survive within the old walls — along with the Norman castle keep, the Guildhall, and Elm Hill's Tudor street. Norwich Market is one of the oldest and largest open-air markets in England.

Modern Norwich

Today the city is a regional centre for insurance and financial services, retail and higher education, home to the University of East Anglia and its Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts, and to Norwich City Football Club at Carrow Road.
