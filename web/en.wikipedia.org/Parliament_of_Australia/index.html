The Parliament of Australia (also called the Federal Parliament or Commonwealth Parliament) is the federal legislature of Australia. It consists of three elements: the monarch (represented by the governor-general), the Senate (the upper house) and the House of Representatives (the lower house). The combination of two elected houses, in which the members of the Senate represent the six states and two territories, and the members of the House represent electoral divisions according to population, follows the model of the United States Congress, a feature sometimes called the "Washminster system".

Parliament was opened in Melbourne on 9 May 1901, following the federation of the six Australian colonies on 1 January 1901. It sat in Melbourne until 1927, when it moved to Canberra, first to the Provisional Parliament House (now Old Parliament House) and from 1988 to the present Parliament House on Capital Hill.

Composition

Under section 1 of the Constitution of Australia, the Parliament is the third of the three elements that together constitute the legislative power of the Commonwealth: the monarch, the Senate and the House of Representatives. In practice, executive power is exercised by ministers drawn from the Parliament and responsible to it, headed by the prime minister.

Senate

The upper house, the Senate, consists of 76 senators: twelve for each of the six states, and two each for the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory. Senators are elected by proportional representation using the single transferable vote, normally for six-year terms, with half the state senators facing election every three years.

House of Representatives

The lower house, the House of Representatives, consists of 151 members elected from single-member electoral divisions of roughly equal population, using preferential (instant-runoff) voting, for terms of up to three years. The party or coalition with a majority in the House forms government.

Functions

The Parliament makes and amends laws, authorises the raising and spending of public money, scrutinises the executive government through question time and committees, and provides a forum for debate on public policy. Most legislation is initiated by the government, though private members may also introduce bills.
