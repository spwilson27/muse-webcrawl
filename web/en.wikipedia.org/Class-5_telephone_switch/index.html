Class-5 telephone switch

A Class-5 telephone switch is a telephone switch or telephone exchange in the public switched telephone network (PSTN) that directly serves subscribers and manages subscriber lines. It sits at the edge of the network, in a local exchange or central office, and connects subscriber loops to the rest of the telephone network through higher-class tandem switches (classes 4 through 1).

Functions

A Class-5 switch provides dial tone, collects dialled digits, supplies ringing and battery feed (part of the BORSCHT functions for analogue lines), routes calls between local subscribers, and hands off long-distance traffic to a Class-4 tandem switch. It also supports features such as call waiting, caller ID and three-way calling, as well as emergency (911) routing.

Examples

Historic and widely deployed Class-5 systems include the Western Electric 5ESS, the Nortel DMS-100, and the Siemens EWSD, originally electromechanical (such as the No. 5 Crossbar) and later fully digital stored-program-control systems. In modern networks their role is increasingly performed by softswitches and media gateways in Voice over IP architectures.

Telephone switch hierarchy

The five-level hierarchy was formalised in the North American Bell System: Class 5 (local/end office), Class 4 (toll/tandem), Class 3 (primary), Class 2 (sectional) and Class 1 (regional) centres.
