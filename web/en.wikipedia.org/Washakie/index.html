Washakie

Born: c. 1804
Died: February 20, 1900
Known for: Chief of the Eastern Shoshone

Chief Washakie (c. 1804 – February 20, 1900) was a prominent leader of the Eastern Shoshone people of the Rocky Mountain region, in what is now Wyoming. Renowned as both a warrior and a diplomat, he is remembered for his efforts to secure land and rights for his people while generally maintaining peaceful relations with the United States.

Leadership

Washakie became the principal chief of the Eastern Shoshone in the mid-19th century. He allied with the U.S. Army against traditional enemies of the Shoshone, including the Sioux, Cheyenne, and Arapaho, and served as a scout and negotiator. He negotiated the Fort Bridger Treaties of 1863 and 1868, the second of which established the Wind River Reservation in present-day Wyoming, which he chose for its sheltered valley and resources.

Later life and legacy

Washakie advocated for education and agriculture among his people while resisting further loss of tribal land. When he died in 1900 he was given a full military funeral at Fort Washakie, an honor reflecting his standing with the United States. Washakie County, Wyoming, and various schools and statues commemorate him. He was among the first Native Americans inducted into the National Cowboy & Western Heritage Museum's Hall of Great Westerners.
