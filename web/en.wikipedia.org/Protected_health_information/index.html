Protected health information

Protected health information (PHI) is any information in a medical record or other health data that can be used to identify an individual and that was created, used, or disclosed in the course of providing healthcare. In the United States, PHI is regulated under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) of 1996.

Identifiers

HIPAA lists 18 types of identifiers that make health information protected, including names, addresses, dates (such as birth and admission dates), telephone and fax numbers, email addresses, Social Security numbers, medical record numbers, account and license numbers, biometric identifiers, full-face photographs, and any other unique identifying number or code.

Rules and safeguards

The HIPAA Privacy Rule limits the use and disclosure of PHI by covered entities (health plans, healthcare clearinghouses, and most healthcare providers) and grants patients rights to access and amend their records. The Security Rule requires administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for electronic PHI, and the Breach Notification Rule requires notification when unsecured PHI is improperly accessed or disclosed.

De-identification

Health information that has been de-identified — by removing the specified identifiers or by expert statistical certification — is no longer considered PHI and may be used more freely, for example in research.
