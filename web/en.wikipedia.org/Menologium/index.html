Menologium

A menologium (Greek: μηνολόγιον, mēnologion; also spelled menologion, plural menologia) is a service book used in the Eastern Orthodox Church and in Eastern Catholic churches following the Byzantine Rite. It contains the lives of the saints arranged in calendar order, month by month, for readings at meals in monasteries, at matins, or for private devotion.

Contents and structure

The menologium collects brief biographies, accounts of martyrdoms, miracles and feasts for each day of the church year, beginning in September with the start of the Byzantine ecclesiastical year. Longer versions include full vitae, while the shorter synaxarion gives condensed notices read liturgically. The texts draw on earlier martyrologies, the writings of Symeon Metaphrastes in the tenth century, and numerous local traditions.

Menologium of Basil II

The most famous example is the Menologium of Basil II, an illuminated manuscript produced in Constantinople around the year 1000 for the emperor Basil II. It contains some 430 miniatures painted by eight artists and covers half the liturgical year, from September to February. It is now held by the Vatican Library (Vat. gr. 1613) and is celebrated as a masterpiece of Byzantine book painting.

Use

Selections were traditionally read aloud during monastic meals and at the sixth hour or matins on a saint's feast day. In parish practice, abbreviated lives from menologia inform sermons and devotional reading. Western analogues include the Roman Martyrology and medieval legendaries such as Jacobus de Voragine's Golden Legend.
