Escaramuza charra

An escaramuza team performing during a charreada

Escaramuza charra is the only female equestrian event in the Mexican charreada. The escaramuza means "skirmish" and consists of a team of eight women riding horses sidesaddle in synchronised, choreographed routines at high speed, performed to traditional Mexican music.

Description

Teams perform a series of manoeuvres (ejercicios) in the charro arena (lienzo charro), including crosses, circles, fans, and combinations in which the riders pass between each other at a gallop with inches to spare. A routine normally lasts about eight minutes and contains twelve exercises. The performances are judged on precision, timing, synchronisation, and difficulty; deductions are made for loss of formation, collisions, and changes of pace.

Dress

Riders wear the colourful adelita dress, named after the women soldiers of the Mexican Revolution, consisting of a wide, full skirt, blouse, and sash, together with a traditional sombrero charro. Dresses of a team must match, and the ensemble, along with the tack, must comply with the regulations of the Federación Mexicana de Charrería. Historically, women rode in the charreada only in exhibition until escaramuza was officially recognised as a competitive event in 1992.
