Kinkaku-ji

Kinkaku-ji (Temple of the Golden Pavilion), formally Rokuon-ji, is a Zen Buddhist temple in northern Kyoto, Japan. Its top two floors are completely covered in gold leaf, and it overlooks a large pond garden.

History

The site was the retirement villa of the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu, acquired in 1397; on his death in 1408 it became a Zen temple as he had willed. The pavilion was burned down in 1950 by a novice monk—an event fictionalised in Yukio Mishima's novel The Temple of the Golden Pavilion—and rebuilt in 1955.

Design

Each of the three floors represents a different style: palace style, samurai-house style and Zen temple style. Kinkaku-ji is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto and one of Japan's most visited temples.
