Aspic

Aspic or meat jelly is a savoury gelatin dish made with a meat stock or broth, set in a mold to encase other ingredients. These often include pieces of meat, seafood, eggs, or vegetables.

A traditional aspic with eggs and vegetables suspended in clear jelly.

Preparation

Almost any type of meat stock can be made into aspic, though stocks rich in natural gelatin — from veal bones, pigs' trotters, or poultry — set most reliably; commercial gelatin may be added for clarity and firmness. The stock is clarified, poured over the arranged ingredients in a mould, and chilled until set, then unmoulded for serving.

History and variations

Meat jellies appeared in medieval European cuisine as a way of preserving cooked meat from air and spoilage. Regional versions include Russian kholodets, Polish galareta, and Ukrainian kholodets. Tomato aspic became popular in the United States in the early 20th century, and moulded aspic dishes were a hallmark of mid-century entertaining before falling out of fashion.
