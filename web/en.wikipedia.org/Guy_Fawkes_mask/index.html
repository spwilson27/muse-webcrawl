Guy Fawkes mask

The Guy Fawkes mask, popularised by V for Vendetta and adopted by protest movements.

The Guy Fawkes mask is a stylised depiction of Guy Fawkes, the best-known member of the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, which attempted to blow up the English House of Lords. The mask, with its smiling face, rosy cheeks, upturned moustache, and thin pointed beard, was designed by illustrator David Lloyd for the comic series V for Vendetta (1982–1989), written by Alan Moore.

Popularisation

The 2005 film adaptation of V for Vendetta, in which the anarchist protagonist "V" wears the mask, brought the design to a worldwide audience. In 2008 the hacktivist collective Anonymous adopted the mask during its protests against the Church of Scientology, and it subsequently became a generic symbol of protest against governments and powerful institutions, appearing in the Occupy movement and demonstrations around the world.

Symbolism and use

Wearing the mask serves both to conceal identity and to signal resistance to authority; its mass-produced uniformity is itself part of the message. The masks are typically made of thin plastic and sold widely; a notable irony is that official merchandise royalties flow to the media conglomerate that owns the film rights. In some countries, notably in parts of the Middle East, wearing the mask at protests has been restricted or banned.

Historical background

Effigies of Fawkes had been burned on bonfires on 5 November (Guy Fawkes Night) for centuries before the mask existed, and earlier Fawkes masks were crude carnival items. The Lloyd design, however, fixed the modern image: an enigmatic, faintly sinister smile.
