Polymath Project

The Polymath Project is a collaboration among mathematicians to solve important and difficult mathematical problems by coordinating many mathematicians to communicate with each other on finding a solution. The project began in January 2009 on the blog of Timothy Gowers, a Fields Medalist, who posed the problem of finding a combinatorial proof of the density Hales–Jewett theorem.

History and results

The first project, Polymath1, succeeded within weeks: dozens of contributors, from research mathematicians to high-school students, produced a new proof of a special case of the density Hales–Jewett theorem, published under the pseudonym D. H. J. Polymath. Subsequent projects tackled problems including the Erdős discrepancy problem (solved by Terence Tao in 2015, drawing on Polymath5 discussions) and bounded gaps between primes, where Polymath8 reduced the gap bound substantially following Yitang Zhang's breakthrough.

Method

Discussion takes place openly on blogs and a wiki, with rules encouraging incremental ideas, partial results and clear exposition so that many participants can contribute. Papers resulting from the collaborations are published under the collective pseudonym "D. H. J. Polymath".
