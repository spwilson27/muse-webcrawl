Uniform Monday Holiday Act

Uniform Monday Holiday Act
Enacted: June 28, 1968
Effective: January 1, 1971
Effect: Moved several US federal holidays to Mondays

The Uniform Monday Holiday Act is an Act of the United States Congress, signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson in 1968 and effective from 1971, that moved several federal holidays to predetermined Mondays in order to create regular three-day weekends for federal employees and to encourage travel and recreation.

Holidays affected

The Act fixed Washington's Birthday (Presidents Day) on the third Monday in February, Memorial Day on the last Monday in May, Columbus Day on the second Monday in October, and Veterans Day on the fourth Monday in October; Veterans Day was later returned to its traditional November 11 date in 1978. Martin Luther King Jr. Day and Labor Day are also observed on Mondays under related legislation.
