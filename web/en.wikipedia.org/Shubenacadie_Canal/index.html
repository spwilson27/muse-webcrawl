Shubenacadie Canal

Location: Nova Scotia, Canada
Route: Halifax Harbour to the Bay of Fundy
Construction: 1826–1861 (intermittent)
Status: Historic site; partially restored

The Shubenacadie Canal is a canal in central Nova Scotia, Canada, linking Halifax Harbour at Dartmouth with the Shubenacadie River and ultimately the Bay of Fundy. Built to move goods across the province's interior by water, it follows a chain of lakes and rivers long used as a Mi'kmaq transportation route.

Construction

Work began in 1826 under the Shubenacadie Canal Company but stalled amid financial difficulties; construction resumed in the 1850s and the canal opened to traffic in 1861. Its most distinctive feature is a marine railway — an inclined plane at Dartmouth that hauled barges between the harbour and Lake Banook.

Decline and preservation

The canal struggled against railway competition and closed commercially in 1870. Surviving locks, the inclined plane site and canal sections are preserved as heritage, notably at Shubie Park in Dartmouth, and parts of the waterway are used for recreation and paddling.
