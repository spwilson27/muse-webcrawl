Human nose

Human nose
SystemRespiratory system
FunctionsOlfaction (smell); filtering, warming and moistening air
PartsNasal cavity, nostrils, septum, turbinates, olfactory epithelium

The human nose is the most protruding part of the face and the primary organ of smell and the main entrance of the respiratory system. It filters, warms and moistens inhaled air, houses the olfactory receptors, and shapes the resonance of the voice. Its external form, supported by bone and cartilage, varies considerably between individuals and populations.

Structure

The visible nose consists of the root, bridge, dorsum, tip and nostrils (nares), separated by the columella; its upper framework is the nasal bones and the lower part cartilage. Inside, the nasal septum divides two nasal cavities, each lined with mucous membrane and scrolled turbinates (conchae) that greatly increase surface area. The paranasal sinuses open into the cavities, and the olfactory epithelium at the roof contains millions of receptor neurons for smell. At the back, the cavities join the nasopharynx.

Function

Cilia and mucus trap particles and pathogens; rich blood flow warms air toward body temperature; and olfactory receptors send signals via the olfactory bulb to the brain, contributing most of what is perceived as flavour. The nose also participates in the nasal cycle of alternating congestion between sides.

Clinical significance

Conditions include rhinitis, sinusitis, deviated septum, nasal polyps and nosebleeds (epistaxis); surgery of the nose is rhinoplasty. Loss of smell (anosmia) can follow infection, including COVID-19.
