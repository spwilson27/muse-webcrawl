United States Secretary of Agriculture

The United States secretary of agriculture is the head of the United States Department of Agriculture. The position carries similar responsibilities to those of agriculture ministers in other governments. The secretary is a member of the president's Cabinet and is appointed by the president with the advice and consent of the Senate.

Duties

The Department of Agriculture carries out government policy on farming, agriculture, forestry, rural economic development, and food. Its agencies include the Forest Service, the Food Safety and Inspection Service, and the agencies administering the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) and federal crop insurance. The secretary oversees roughly 100,000 employees across these programs.

History

The Department of Agriculture was created in 1862 under President Abraham Lincoln, who called it "the People's Department," but its head was a commissioner without Cabinet rank. President Grover Cleveland signed the law elevating the department to Cabinet status in 1889, and Norman Jay Coleman, the last commissioner, became the first secretary. Henry A. Wallace, secretary under Franklin D. Roosevelt, shaped New Deal farm policy, including the Agricultural Adjustment Act.
