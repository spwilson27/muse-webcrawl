Kigali Amendment

The Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol is an international agreement adopted on 15 October 2016 in Kigali, Rwanda, to phase down the production and consumption of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) — potent greenhouse gases widely used as refrigerants in air conditioners and refrigerators. It entered into force on 1 January 2019.

Background

HFCs were introduced as substitutes for ozone-depleting chlorofluorocarbons under the original Montreal Protocol, but although they do not harm the ozone layer, many have global warming potentials hundreds to thousands of times greater than carbon dioxide. Rapid growth in cooling demand, especially in developing economies, made HFC emissions one of the fastest-growing climate threats.

Provisions

Developed countries began reducing HFC use in 2019 and target an 85% cut by 2036 relative to baseline. Most developing countries freeze consumption in 2024 and aim for an 80% reduction by 2045; a second group, including India, follows a later schedule with a 2028 freeze. Full implementation is projected to avoid up to 0.4–0.5 °C of global warming by 2100, with additional benefits from improved energy efficiency of cooling equipment.

Ratification

The amendment has been ratified by the large majority of Montreal Protocol parties. The United States ratified it in October 2022 following Senate approval, and China accepted it in 2021.
