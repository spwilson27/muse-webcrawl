Haruki Murakami

Haruki Murakami (born January 12, 1949) is a Japanese writer whose novels and stories, blending popular culture, surrealism, and melancholic themes, have become international bestsellers and have been translated into some 50 languages. He is widely regarded as one of the most important contemporary novelists.

Life and career

Murakami was born in Kyoto and grew up near Kobe. Before becoming a writer he ran a jazz bar in Tokyo called Peter Cat. He began writing at 29 after deciding, while watching a baseball game in 1978, that he could write a novel; his debut, Hear the Wind Sing (1979), won a prize for new writers. Norwegian Wood (1987) made him a literary celebrity in Japan.

Style and themes

Murakami's fiction combines realistic narration with dreamlike and fantastical elements — wells, cats, ears, and parallel worlds recur — and is steeped in Western literature, jazz, and pop music. He has also translated F. Scott Fitzgerald, Raymond Carver, and J. D. Salinger into Japanese, and written nonfiction about running and the 1995 Tokyo subway sarin attack.
