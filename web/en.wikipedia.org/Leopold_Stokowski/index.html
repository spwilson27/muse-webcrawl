Leopold Anthony Stokowski (18 April 1882 – 13 September 1977) was a British-born American conductor, one of the leading conductors of the early and mid-20th century. He is best known for his long association with the Philadelphia Orchestra, which he transformed into one of the world's finest ensembles, and for his appearance in Walt Disney's film Fantasia (1940).

Career

Born in London to a Polish father and an Irish mother, Stokowski studied at the Royal College of Music and at Oxford. After posts as an organist and choirmaster in London and New York, he made his conducting debut in 1908 and became music director of the Cincinnati Symphony Orchestra in 1909. In 1912 he took over the Philadelphia Orchestra, remaining until 1941 and building its lush, free-bowing "Stokowski sound." He conducted without a baton, shaping phrases with expressive hand gestures.

Stokowski was a tireless champion of new music, giving the American premieres of works by Mahler, Schoenberg, Stravinsky, Varèse and many others, and he made numerous orchestral transcriptions, most famously of Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor, heard at the opening of Fantasia. He later led the NBC Symphony, co-founded and conducted the All-American Youth Orchestra, and served with the Houston Symphony and other ensembles. In 1962, at the age of 80, he founded the American Symphony Orchestra in New York.

Recording pioneer

Stokowski embraced recording technology from the acoustic era onward, experimenting with electrical recording, stereophony and multi-channel sound, and he continued making records into his nineties; his final recording sessions took place in 1977, the year of his death at 95.

Personal life

He was married three times, including to the pianist Olga Samaroff and to the heiress Gloria Vanderbilt. Stokowski cultivated an air of mystery around his origins and accent, which changed noticeably over his career.
