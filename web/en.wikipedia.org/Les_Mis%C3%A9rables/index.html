Les Misérables

Author: Victor Hugo
Published: 1862
Language: French
Genre: Historical novel

Les Misérables is a French historical novel by Victor Hugo, first published in 1862, widely regarded as one of the greatest novels of the 19th century. It follows the ex-convict Jean Valjean and his pursuit by the police inspector Javert in post-Napoleonic France.

Plot

Valjean, imprisoned for stealing bread, is transformed by the mercy of Bishop Myriel and reinvents himself as a factory owner and mayor. His story intertwines with those of Fantine, her daughter Cosette, the student Marius, and the street urchin Gavroche, culminating in the June Rebellion of 1832.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times for stage, film and television, most famously in the stage musical by Boublil and Schönberg, which opened in Paris in 1980 and in London in 1985.
