7.1 surround sound

TypeMultichannel audio format
Channels8 (7 full-range + 1 LFE)
Predecessor5.1 surround sound
UsesHome theatre, digital cinema, Blu-ray, gaming

7.1 surround sound is the common name for an eight-channel surround sound audio system. It adds two additional speakers to the more conventional 5.1 surround sound configuration: a pair of rear surround (or surround back) channels positioned behind the listener, complementing the side surround channels. The ".1" denotes a dedicated low-frequency effects (LFE) channel reproduced by a subwoofer.

Channel configuration

A standard 7.1 layout consists of the following channels:

  • Front left (FL) and front right (FR) — the main stereo pair, flanking the screen.
  • Center (C) — placed above or below the screen, carrying most dialogue.
  • Side surround left (SL) and side surround right (SR) — placed to the sides of the listener.
  • Rear surround left (SBL) and rear surround right (SBR) — placed behind the listener, the addition that distinguishes 7.1 from 5.1.
  • LFE — the low-frequency effects channel, reproduced by one or more subwoofers.

The extra rear channels improve the sense of envelopment and allow sound effects to pan smoothly in a full circle around the listener, an effect used extensively in action films and video games.

History and use

While 5.1 became the dominant home format with the arrival of DVD in the late 1990s, 7.1 was adopted first in digital cinema, where formats such as Dolby Digital Surround EX and later Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio on Blu-ray discs carried discrete 7.1 soundtracks. Home theatre receivers with 7.1 amplification became common in the 2000s, and the format was widely used in high-end home cinema installations and PC gaming setups.

Because most source material is mixed in 5.1 or stereo, receivers often apply matrix upmixing technologies — such as Dolby Pro Logic IIx or DTS Neo:6 — to derive the two rear channels from material that does not contain them natively. With the rise of object-based audio formats such as Dolby Atmos and DTS:X, which add height channels (for example 5.1.2 or 7.1.4 configurations), the 7.1 bed layout remains the foundation on which those immersive systems are built.
