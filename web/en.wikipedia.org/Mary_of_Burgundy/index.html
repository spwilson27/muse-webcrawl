Mary of Burgundy

Mary of Burgundy (1457–1482), nicknamed "Mary the Rich," was Duchess of Burgundy from 1477 until her death, the only child of Duke Charles the Bold. Her inheritance of the wealthy Burgundian lands in the Low Countries made her one of the most sought-after brides in Europe.

Reign

When Charles was killed at the Battle of Nancy in January 1477, France invaded the duchy, and the cities of the Netherlands forced the young duchess to sign the Great Privilege, restoring many local liberties. She married Archduke Maximilian of Austria (later Emperor Maximilian I) in August 1477, bringing the Burgundian inheritance into the House of Habsburg — a union foundational to the Habsburg Netherlands and to two centuries of Franco-Habsburg rivalry.

Death and legacy

Mary died at age 25 in March 1482 from injuries suffered in a fall from her horse while falconing near Bruges. She is buried in the Church of Our Lady in Bruges beside her father. Her children were Philip the Handsome, whose marriage brought Spain to the Habsburgs, and Margaret of Austria, later regent of the Netherlands.
