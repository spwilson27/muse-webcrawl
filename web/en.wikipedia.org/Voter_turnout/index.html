Voter turnout


Definition: Percentage of eligible voters who cast a ballot in an election
Measures: VAP turnout; VEP turnout; turnout of registered voters

Voter turnout is the percentage of registered voters — or, in other measures, of the voting-age or voting-eligible population — who actually cast a ballot in an election. It is one of the most studied quantities in political science, because it is taken as an indicator of the health and legitimacy of a democracy.

Measurement

Turnout figures differ depending on the denominator used. Voting-age population (VAP) turnout divides ballots cast by all residents of voting age, including non-citizens and ineligible felons. Voting-eligible population (VEP) turnout excludes ineligible groups and generally gives a higher figure. Some countries report turnout only among registered voters, which can approach 100% where registration is automatic or compulsory.

Factors affecting turnout

Research associates higher turnout with proportional electoral systems, compulsory voting laws (as in Australia and Belgium), competitive elections, automatic or same-day voter registration, weekend or holiday voting, and higher levels of education and income. Turnout tends to be lower in second-order elections such as midterms, local elections and European Parliament elections, and declines have been documented in many established democracies since the mid-twentieth century.

Consequences

Low or unequal turnout can skew representation toward older, wealthier and more educated citizens. Political scientists debate whether high turnout itself changes election outcomes; its clearest effects are on the perceived legitimacy of the result and on which groups' preferences policy responds to.
