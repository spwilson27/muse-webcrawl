Itō Hirobumi

Born16 October 1841, Chōshū Domain, Japan
Died26 October 1909, Harbin, Manchuria
Office1st Prime Minister of Japan (served four terms)

Prince Itō Hirobumi (伊藤 博文, 16 October 1841 – 26 October 1909) was a Japanese politician, statesman and member of the Meiji oligarchy. As the first Prime Minister of Japan, he oversaw the creation of the Meiji Constitution of 1889 and the establishment of the Imperial Diet, and he remains one of the principal architects of modern Japan.

Early life

Born in Chōshū Domain, Itō was educated as a samurai retainer and studied in London as one of the Chōshū Five. He became involved in the movement to overthrow the Tokugawa shogunate that led to the Meiji Restoration of 1868.

Career

Itō served as Prime Minister four times (1885–1888, 1892–1896, 1898, and 1900–1901). He drafted Japan's first modern constitution after studying constitutional systems in Europe, and founded the Rikken Seiyūkai political party. From 1905 he was the first Resident-General of Korea.

Assassination

Itō was assassinated at Harbin railway station in 1909 by Korean independence activist An Jung-geun.
