Vestment

Used byChristian clergy and servers
ExamplesChasuble, stole, alb, cope, dalmatic
ColoursVary by liturgical season

Vestments are the distinctive garments worn by clergy and other ministers during Christian worship. Originating in the everyday dress of the late Roman world, vestments became specialised liturgical clothing, varying by denomination, office, and season, and often richly ornamented.

Principal garments

The alb is a long white tunic worn by servers and ministers alike. Over it, a priest celebrating the Eucharist wears the chasuble, a poncho-like outer garment, with a stole — a long band worn around the neck — beneath it. Deacons wear the dalmatic and a stole over one shoulder. The cope is a processional cloak, and bishops add the mitre and crosier as insignia of office.

Liturgical colours

In the Western tradition, vestment colours follow the church year: white for feasts of Christ and Easter, red for Pentecost and martyrs, green for Ordinary Time, and violet for the penitential seasons of Advent and Lent, with black, rose, and gold used on particular occasions. The Eastern churches use their own colour customs, often favouring gold and bright tones more broadly.

Modern usage

Vestments remain standard in Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, and Lutheran worship, while many other Protestant churches favour academic gowns, simple robes, or no special dress, reflecting differing theologies of ministry. Historic vestments are also prized as works of textile art and embroidery.
