Lu Xun

Born: Zhou Shuren, 25 September 1881, Shaoxing, Zhejiang, Qing China
Died: 19 October 1936 (aged 55), Shanghai
Occupation: Writer, essayist, critic
Notable works: Diary of a Madman, The True Story of Ah Q, Call to Arms, Wandering

Lu Xun (25 September 1881 – 19 October 1936), born Zhou Shuren, was a Chinese writer, essayist, poet, and literary critic. He was a leading figure of modern Chinese literature and is widely regarded as one of China's greatest writers of the 20th century.

Life

Born to a declining scholar-gentry family in Shaoxing, Lu Xun studied mining and medicine in Japan, where exposure to Chinese students' apathy during the Russo-Japanese War led him to abandon medicine for literature, believing cultural reform was more urgent than physical healing. He returned to China in 1909.

Writing

His first major story, "Diary of a Madman" (1918), published in New Culture, used vernacular Chinese (baihua) and satirised cannibalistic traditional society. "The True Story of Ah Q" (1921–22) created an enduring symbol of self-deception. Collections Call to Arms (1923) and Wandering (1926) established his reputation. Later he turned mainly to sharp, polemical essays (zawen).

Legacy

Lu Xun influenced the May Fourth Movement, modern Chinese prose, and writers across Asia. Museums and memorials in Shanghai, Beijing and Shaoxing preserve his homes and manuscripts.
