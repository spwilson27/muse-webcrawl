Sergio Marchionne

Sergio Marchionne (17 June 1952 – 25 July 2018) was an Italian-Canadian businessman, widely credited with the turnaround of the Italian automotive group Fiat and with engineering its merger with the American automaker Chrysler. He served as chief executive officer of Fiat (2004–2014) and then of Fiat Chrysler Automobiles (FCA) from 2014 until days before his death in 2018, and was also chairman of Ferrari and of the industrial vehicles group CNH Industrial.

Early life and career

Marchionne was born in Chieti, Abruzzo, and emigrated with his family to Toronto, Canada, at age 14. He studied philosophy, commerce, and law, qualifying as a chartered accountant and barrister, and worked in finance and law before moving into industry. He rose through the Swiss-based SGS (where he led a celebrated turnaround) and Lonza Group, before being recruited to Fiat's board and then, in June 2004, to the chief executive role when the company was close to collapse.

Fiat turnaround and the Chrysler merger

At Fiat, Marchionne cut layers of management, renegotiated a costly put option with General Motors — extracting a US$2 billion settlement in 2005 — and returned the group to profit. In 2009, in the depths of the financial crisis, he persuaded the US government to hand Fiat a controlling stake in the bankrupt Chrysler without cash payment, in exchange for Fiat's small-car technology and management. Fiat took full ownership in 2014 and merged the two into Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, headquartered legally in the Netherlands. Under Marchionne FCA spun off Ferrari (2016), eliminated net industrial debt, and concentrated on high-margin brands — Jeep, Ram, Maserati, and Alfa Romeo.

Style and legacy

Famous for his blunt manner, all-black wardrobe, and workaholism, Marchionne was admired for hard-nosed deal-making and candour about the industry's overcapacity — notably his 2015 presentation "Confessions of a Capital Junkie" urging consolidation — and criticised for underinvestment in electrification and for FCA's emissions scandals. He was forced to step down on 21 July 2018 because of complications following shoulder surgery, and died four days later in Zurich; Mike Manley succeeded him at FCA. In 2021 FCA merged with PSA Group to form Stellantis.
