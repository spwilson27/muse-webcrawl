Stoma (medicine)

In anatomy, a stoma (plural: stomata or stomas) is any opening in the body. In surgical usage, the term most often refers to an opening created on the abdomen that connects the inside of the body to the outside, allowing waste to leave through an artificial route — a procedure called an ostomy. The word comes from the Greek στόμα, "mouth".

Types

Common stomas include the colostomy (from the colon), ileostomy (from the ileum), and urostomy (for urine diversion). A tracheostomy creates an opening in the windpipe for breathing. Stomas may be temporary, allowing an injured or operated section of bowel to heal, or permanent, for example after removal of the rectum or bladder.

Care

Effluent from an abdominal stoma is collected in a replaceable pouch worn over the opening. The stoma itself is made of the moist red lining of the bowel, has no nerve endings, and requires skin protection and regular appliance changes. Stomas are created in the treatment of colorectal cancer, inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis), trauma, and congenital conditions.
