Impulse response

Impulse response
Field: Signal processing, control theory
Input: Dirac delta function δ(t)
Notation: h(t)

In signal processing, the impulse response of a dynamic system is its output when presented with a brief input signal called an impulse. More generally, it is the reaction of any dynamic system in response to some external change.

Linear time-invariant systems

For a linear time-invariant (LTI) system, the impulse response completely characterises the system: the output for any input is the convolution of that input with the impulse response. In continuous time, the impulse is the Dirac delta function; in discrete time it is the Kronecker delta.

Frequency domain

The Fourier transform of the impulse response is the system's transfer function (or frequency response), which describes how each frequency is scaled and phase-shifted. Practical engineers often approximate an impulse with a short pulse, such as a hammer strike in structural testing or a click in room acoustics.

Applications

Impulse responses are used to model loudspeakers and concert-hall reverberation (for convolution reverb in audio), radar and sonar systems, economic responses to shocks, and the spread of signals in control systems. Filters are commonly classified as finite impulse response (FIR) or infinite impulse response (IIR) according to the length of their impulse response. See also Convolution and Transfer function.
