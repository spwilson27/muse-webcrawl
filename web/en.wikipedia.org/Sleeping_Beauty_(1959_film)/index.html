Sleeping Beauty (1959 film)

Sleeping Beauty is a 1959 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Productions, based on Charles Perrault's fairy tale. It was the sixteenth Disney animated feature film and the last to be produced while Walt Disney was personally overseeing the animation studio's fairy-tale features.

Plot

At the christening of Princess Aurora, the evil fairy Maleficent curses her to prick her finger on a spinning wheel and die before sunset on her sixteenth birthday. The good fairy Merryweather softens the curse to a deep sleep, breakable by true love's kiss. Aurora is raised in hiding as Briar Rose by the three good fairies, falls in love with Prince Phillip, but pricks her finger and falls asleep; Phillip battles Maleficent, who transforms into a dragon, and defeats her, waking Aurora with a kiss.

Production and music

The film is celebrated for its distinctive, angular, tapestry-like art direction by Eyvind Earle, inspired by medieval manuscripts. Its score, adapted by George Bruns from Tchaikovsky's 1890 ballet The Sleeping Beauty, includes the song “Once Upon a Dream.” Voiced by Mary Costa (Aurora), Bill Shirley (Phillip), and Eleanor Audley (Maleficent), the film was expensive to make and initially underperformed, but grew in reputation through reissues and is now considered one of Disney's finest animated works.
