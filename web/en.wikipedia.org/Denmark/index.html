Denmark is a Nordic country in Northern Europe. It is the metropolitan part of the Kingdom of Denmark, which also includes the autonomous territories of the Faroe Islands and Greenland in the North Atlantic Ocean. Metropolitan Denmark consists of the northern part of the Jutland peninsula and an archipelago of 443 named islands, and is the southernmost of the Scandinavian countries, lying southwest of Sweden and south of Norway, with Germany as its only land neighbour.

Denmark has a population of roughly 5.9 million and its capital and most populous city is Copenhagen. The country is a constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary system; executive power is exercised by the government, headed by the prime minister, and legislative power is vested in the Folketing.

History

A unified Kingdom of Denmark emerged in the eighth century as a competent seaborne trading power amid the struggle for control of the Baltic Sea. During the Viking Age, Danish kings ruled an extensive North Sea empire at times. Denmark joined the Kalmar Union with Norway and Sweden in 1397. In 1849 it became a constitutional monarchy. Denmark remained neutral in the First World War, was occupied by Nazi Germany in the Second, and became a founding member of NATO in 1949 and joined the European Economic Community (now the European Union) in 1973, though it retains its own currency, the krone.

Geography and society

Denmark is a low-lying country with a temperate climate. It consistently ranks highly in measures of happiness, income equality, education, and low corruption, and is known for its comprehensive welfare state and the concept of hygge. Major exports include pharmaceuticals, agricultural products, and wind turbines.
