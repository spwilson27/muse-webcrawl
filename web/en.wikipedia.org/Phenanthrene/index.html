Phenanthrene

Chemical formulaC14H10
Molar mass178.23 g/mol
Melting point101 °C
Boiling point332 °C
AppearanceColourless crystals, blue fluorescence

Phenanthrene is a polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) consisting of three fused benzene rings arranged in an angular fashion. It is isomeric with anthracene, in which the three rings are fused linearly. The name derives from "phenyl" and "anthracene". Phenanthrene occurs naturally in coal tar and in cigarette smoke, and appears as colourless crystals that fluoresce blue under ultraviolet light.

Phenanthrene: three benzene rings fused at an angle

The phenanthrene skeleton is a key structural motif in natural products: it underlies the steroids, many alkaloids such as morphine and codeine, and cannabinoids. The 9,10-bond of phenanthrene is especially reactive — oxidation gives phenanthrenequinone, and the region between C-9 and C-10 is often described as having olefin-like character. Classic laboratory syntheses include the Haworth synthesis and the Bardhan–Sengupta synthesis.

Occurrence and significance

Phenanthrene is one of the most abundant PAHs in the environment, arising from incomplete combustion of fossil fuels and biomass. It is used as a model compound in studies of PAH biodegradation and environmental fate. Unlike its isomer benzo-fused relatives, phenanthrene itself is not classed as a probable carcinogen, though many substituted phenanthrenes and larger PAHs are of toxicological concern.
