Germanicus Julius Caesar (24 May 15 BC – 10 October AD 19) was an ancient Roman general and politician most famously known for his successful campaigns in Germania. A favourite of the Roman public and of the emperor Augustus, who adopted his uncle Tiberius with the condition that Tiberius adopt him, Germanicus stood as a likely successor to the throne until his sudden death at Antioch amid rumours of poisoning.

Germanicus, nephew and adopted son of Tiberius and father of Caligula.

Career

The son of Nero Claudius Drusus — from whom he inherited the honorific Germanicus, awarded for his father's German victories — he served in Illyricum and on the Rhine. After Augustus's death in AD 14, Germanicus faced down the mutiny of the Rhine legions, refusing their offer to proclaim him emperor. In AD 14–16 he led major campaigns across the Rhine to avenge the Teutoburg Forest disaster: he visited the battlefield and buried Varus's dead, defeated Arminius's coalition at Idistaviso and the Angrivarian Wall, and recovered two of the three lost legionary eagles before Tiberius recalled him, judging the Rhine frontier sufficient. He celebrated a triumph at Rome in AD 17.

Death and legacy

Sent to reorganise the eastern provinces with overriding authority (imperium maius), Germanicus clashed with Gnaeus Calpurnius Piso, the governor of Syria. Falling ill at Antioch, he died on 10 October AD 19, convinced — according to Tacitus and Suetonius — that Piso and his wife Plancina had poisoned him, possibly with Tiberius's connivance. Rome mourned extravagantly; Piso's trial ended with his suicide. Germanicus's widow Agrippina the Elder and their children were later persecuted by Tiberius and Sejanus; his son Gaius (Caligula) and grandson Nero eventually became emperors. Tacitus's portrait in the Annals made Germanicus the lost hero of the early Principate.
