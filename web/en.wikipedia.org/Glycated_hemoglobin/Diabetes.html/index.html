Diabetes

Diabetes mellitus, commonly known simply as diabetes, is a group of metabolic diseases characterised by chronically elevated blood sugar (hyperglycaemia) resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both. The main types are type 1 diabetes (autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing beta cells), type 2 diabetes (insulin resistance combined with inadequate insulin secretion, strongly associated with obesity and lifestyle), and gestational diabetes in pregnancy.

Diagnosis is by blood glucose testing or by glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), which reflects average blood sugar over the preceding two to three months; an HbA1c of 6.5% or higher indicates diabetes. Long-term complications include cardiovascular disease, kidney failure, retinopathy, neuropathy and foot ulceration. Management involves lifestyle measures, oral glucose-lowering drugs, insulin, and monitoring of blood sugar and complications. Diabetes affected more than 500 million adults worldwide in the early 2020s.
