Ogasawara Islands

The Ogasawara Islands (小笠原群島), also known in English as the Bonin Islands, are a Japanese archipelago of over 30 subtropical and tropical islands located around 1,000 kilometres (620 mi) south of Tokyo in the Pacific Ocean. Administratively they form Ogasawara Village, part of Tokyo Metropolis. Only two islands are inhabited: Chichijima (Father Island), the seat of the village government, and Hahajima (Mother Island).

Geography and ecology

The islands are oceanic in origin and were never connected to a continent, so they host many endemic species and are sometimes called the "Galápagos of the Orient". The archipelago's ecosystems led UNESCO to inscribe the islands as a World Heritage Site in 2011. The climate is subtropical, and the surrounding waters support coral reefs, dolphins, and whales, making the islands a centre for whale watching and eco-tourism.

History

The islands were uninhabited when claimed by Japan in the seventeenth century; the name "Bonin" is thought to derive from a French cartographer's rendering of the Japanese bunin ("no people"). A multinational settler community arrived from 1830, and the islands became strategically important in the Second World War — nearby Iwo Jima was the site of a famous battle in 1945. The United States administered the islands after the war and returned them to Japan in 1968, when the islanders of Western descent were allowed to return.
