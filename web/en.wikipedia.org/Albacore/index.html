Albacore

The albacore (Thunnus alalunga), known also as the longfin tuna, is a species of tuna in the family Scombridae found in temperate and tropical waters of all the world's oceans. Distinguished by its exceptionally long pectoral fins, it reaches about 1.2 metres in length and up to roughly 40 kg. Albacore is one of the most commercially valuable tunas, sold fresh and as the premium "white meat" canned tuna.

Biology

Albacore are fast, migratory predators feeding on fish, squid, and crustaceans, and are themselves preyed on by sharks and larger billfish. Six distinct stocks are recognized, in the North and South Atlantic, the Indian Ocean, and the North and South Pacific, with seasonal migrations across ocean basins. Like other tunas they are regionally warm-bodied, which powers their sustained swimming.

Fishery

Fleets from Spain, France, Japan, Taiwan, and the United States catch albacore with trolling lines, pole-and-line gear, longlines, and driftnets. It supports famous seasonal fisheries such as the Bay of Biscay "bonito del norte" season. Management is shared among regional fisheries bodies; the North Atlantic stock was long overfished but has rebuilt under quotas, while assessments differ for other stocks. Because albacore accumulate mercury, health agencies advise limiting consumption, particularly for children and pregnant women.
