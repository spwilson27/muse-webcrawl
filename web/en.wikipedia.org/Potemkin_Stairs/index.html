Potemkin Stairs

The stairs, a giant staircase in Odesa, Ukraine, designed as a formal entrance to the port.

The Potemkin Stairs (Ukrainian: Потьомкінські сходи, Potiomkinski skhody), or Potemkin Steps, is a giant stairway in Odesa, Ukraine. They are considered a formal entrance into the city from the direction of the sea and are the best known symbol of Odesa. The stairs were originally known as the Boulevard Steps, the Giant Staircase, or the Richelieu Steps.

Design and history

Designed in 1837 by the architects Francesco Boffo and Avraam Melnikov and built between 1837 and 1841, the staircase originally had 200 steps (now 192, after eight were buried when the harbour road was raised). It is 142 metres long and rises through ten landings. The stairs exhibit a deliberate optical effect: viewed from above, only the landings are visible and the steps disappear; viewed from below, only the steps appear. The stairway took its present name after Sergei Eisenstein's 1925 film Battleship Potemkin, whose celebrated "Odessa Steps" sequence depicts a massacre of civilians on the staircase during the 1905 revolution — an event that did not in fact occur there as filmed, though unrest and killings took place elsewhere in the city.

The Eisenstein sequence

The film sequence, in which a baby carriage rolls unattended down the steps amid fleeing crowds and Cossack troops, is one of the most famous and frequently quoted passages in cinema history, and it fixed the staircase's identity in world culture. The steps have since been widely imitated in films and appear in countless homages.
