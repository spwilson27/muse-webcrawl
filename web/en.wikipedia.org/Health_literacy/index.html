Health literacy

Health literacy is the degree to which individuals can find, understand and use information and services to inform health-related decisions and actions for themselves and others. It encompasses reading and numeracy skills applied to health tasks: reading a medicine label, understanding a consent form, interpreting test results, or weighing risks and benefits of treatments.

Importance

Limited health literacy is common — studies in the United States have found that a large share of adults struggle with basic health information — and is associated with worse health outcomes, more hospital admissions, poorer management of chronic diseases, and lower use of preventive services such as vaccination and screening. It affects people of all education levels but is more common among older adults, people with limited English proficiency, and those with lower incomes.

Improving health literacy

Approaches include plain-language writing, the teach-back method (asking patients to repeat instructions in their own words), visual aids, and designing health systems that do not assume high literacy. The expanded definition adopted by Healthy People 2030 also stresses "organizational health literacy" — the responsibility of institutions to make information accessible.
