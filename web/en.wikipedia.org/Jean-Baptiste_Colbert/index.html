Jean-Baptiste Colbert

Born: 29 August 1619, Reims, France

Died: 6 September 1683, Paris, France

Office: Controller-General of Finances (1665–1683)

Monarch: Louis XIV

Jean-Baptiste Colbert (29 August 1619 – 6 September 1683) was a French statesman who served as First Minister of State and Controller-General of Finances under King Louis XIV from 1665 until his death in 1683. His economic programme, known as Colbertism, was a form of mercantilism aimed at making France economically self-sufficient and dominant in European trade.

Career

Born into a merchant family of Reims, Colbert rose through the service of Cardinal Mazarin and, after Mazarin's death in 1661, won the confidence of Louis XIV by discrediting the finance minister Nicolas Fouquet. He accumulated offices: intendant of finances, controller-general, secretary of state for the navy and for the royal household.

Policies

Colbert reformed taxation, codified commercial law, founded state manufactures such as the Gobelins tapestry works, subsidized export industries, founded the French East India Company, expanded the navy from a handful of ships to one of Europe's largest fleets, and patronized science and the arts, founding the Academy of Sciences and the Paris Observatory. His system of industrial regulation and inspection aimed at uniform quality, though critics then and since have noted its rigidity and the burden of taxation on the peasantry.
