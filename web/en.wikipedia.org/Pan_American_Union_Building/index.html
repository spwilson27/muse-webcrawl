Pan American Union Building

The Pan American Union Building is the headquarters of the Organization of American States (OAS), located at 17th Street and Constitution Avenue NW in Washington, D.C. It was built between 1908 and 1910 for the Pan American Union, the predecessor secretariat of the OAS, with funding from the United States government, the republics of the Americas, and a gift from steel magnate and philanthropist Andrew Carnegie.

Architecture

The building was designed by architects Albert Kelsey and Paul Philippe Cret in a Beaux-Arts style combining Spanish colonial and classical elements, intended to symbolise the cultures of North and South America. Constructed of white Vermont marble, it features sculpture and decorative work representing the member nations, a rear patio and tropical garden (the Aztec Garden), and the Hall of the Americas, a grand ceremonial space used for signings and official events.

History

The cornerstone was laid on 11 May 1908 and the building was dedicated on 26 April 1910. The Charter of the Organization of American States was signed in its environs in 1948 era diplomacy, and the building continues to house the OAS General Secretariat and its Permanent Council. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
