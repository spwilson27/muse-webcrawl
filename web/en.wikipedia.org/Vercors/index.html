Vercors

The Vercors is a massif of limestone mountains and plateaus in the départements of Isère and Drôme in southeastern France, forming part of the French Prealps west of Grenoble. Surrounded by steep cliffs and cut by dramatic gorges and caves, the high plateau — the largest in the northern French Alps — rises to 2,341 m at the Grand Veymont. Since 1970 much of the massif has been protected as the Vercors Regional Natural Park.

The Maquis du Vercors

During the Second World War the remote plateau became a stronghold of the French Resistance, the Maquis du Vercors. In June–July 1944, after the Allied landings in Normandy, the maquisards proclaimed the Free Republic of the Vercors and awaited Allied support. German forces, including airborne troops landing by glider at Vassieux-en-Vercors, crushed the maquis in a brutal assault in which some 840 French — fighters and civilians — were killed. The destroyed villages and memorials on the plateau commemorate the episode.

Geography and activities

The Vercors is noted for karst scenery, extensive cave systems, and the Combe Laval road carved into the cliffs. It is a popular area for cross-country skiing (Villard-de-Lans hosted events at the 1968 Grenoble Winter Olympics), hiking, caving and climbing.
