Carbon offset

A carbon offset is a reduction or removal of emissions of carbon dioxide or other greenhouse gases made in order to compensate for emissions made elsewhere. Offsets are measured in tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO2e) and are commonly traded as carbon credits.

Types of projects

Offset projects include renewable energy generation, reforestation and avoided deforestation (REDD+), methane capture from landfills and livestock, energy efficiency improvements, and carbon dioxide removal projects such as biochar and direct air capture.

Markets

Offsets are traded in compliance markets, created by regulation such as the Kyoto Protocol's Clean Development Mechanism, and in voluntary markets, where companies and individuals buy offsets to meet self-declared climate goals. Certification standards seek to ensure offsets are real, measurable, permanent, additional, and independently verified.

Controversies

Investigations have raised concerns that some offsets — particularly avoided-deforestation and certain renewable-energy credits — overstate their climate benefit. Critics also argue that offsetting can act as a "licence to pollute," while supporters view well-governed offsets as a useful complement to direct emissions reductions.
