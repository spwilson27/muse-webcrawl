Dmitri Shostakovich

Born25 September 1906, Saint Petersburg, Russian Empire
Died9 August 1975, Moscow, Soviet Union
Works15 symphonies, 15 string quartets, operas, concertos

Dmitri Dmitriyevich Shostakovich (1906–1975) was a Soviet composer and pianist, widely regarded as one of the major composers of the 20th century. His output includes 15 symphonies, 15 string quartets, six concertos, two operas, and a large body of chamber and film music. His career unfolded under the shadow of Joseph Stalin's cultural policies, and his music has been the subject of enduring debate over its relationship to Soviet ideology.

Early life

Shostakovich was born in Saint Petersburg and entered the Petrograd Conservatory at thirteen, studying piano with Leonid Nikolayev and composition with Maximilian Steinberg. His First Symphony, written as a graduation piece at nineteen, brought him international attention.

Success and denunciation

The opera Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District (1934) was initially a triumph, but in 1936 an editorial in Pravda, "Muddle Instead of Music," denounced it after Stalin attended a performance. Shostakovich responded with his Fifth Symphony (1937), subtitled "A Soviet artist's reply to just criticism," which restored him to official favour. He was denounced again in 1948 under the Zhdanov doctrine, along with Prokofiev and Khachaturian.

War and later years

His Seventh Symphony, the "Leningrad," was composed during the siege of that city in 1941 and became a symbol of resistance. After Stalin's death in 1953, works such as the Tenth Symphony and, later, the autobiographical Eighth String Quartet (1960) explored darker themes. His final years were marked by ill health and a preoccupation with death in works including the Fourteenth Symphony and the Viola Sonata.

Legacy

Shostakovich's music combines tonal accessibility with irony, grotesquerie, and emotional depth. The posthumous memoir Testimony (1979), edited by Solomon Volkov, sparked controversy over the composer's private views that continues among scholars.
