Joseph Saddler (born 1 January 1958), better known by his stage name Grandmaster Flash, is a Barbadian-American disc jockey and rapper. He is one of the pioneers of hip hop DJing, cutting and mixing, and is credited with inventing or perfecting foundational turntable techniques including the backspin, punch phrasing and the use of headphones to cue records with precision.

Early career and innovations

Raised in the Bronx, Saddler studied electronics and was fascinated by the mechanics of turntables. Dissatisfied with existing DJ methods, he developed the "Quick Mix Theory": using two copies of the same record, a mixer with a cueing system, and marks on the vinyl, he could isolate and extend the drum breaks that dancers loved, effectively turning the turntable into a musical instrument.

Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five

With the Furious Five — Melle Mel, Kidd Creole, Keef Cowboy, Scorpio and Rahiem — Flash recorded for Sugar Hill Records. Their 1982 single "The Message", driven by Melle Mel's vivid depiction of inner-city life, is regarded as one of the most important records in hip hop history and helped establish rap as a vehicle for social commentary. Other notable tracks include "White Lines (Don't Don't Do It)" and "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel" (1981), a live-mixed collage showcasing his turntable skills.

Recognition

In 2007 Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five became the first hip hop act inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. Flash also appears as a central figure in the Netflix series The Get Down (portrayed by Mamoudou Athie) and served as host of the documentary series Hip Hop Family Tree-related programming; his memoir, The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash: My Life, My Beats, was published in 2008.
