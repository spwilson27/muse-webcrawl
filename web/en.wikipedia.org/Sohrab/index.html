Sohrab

Sohrab or Suhrab (Persian: سهراب) is a legendary warrior from the Shahnameh, the national epic of Greater Iran, which was completed in about 1010 CE by the poet Ferdowsi. He is the son of the great hero Rostam and of Tahmina, daughter of the king of Samangan.

The story

Conceived during Rostam's brief stay in Samangan while searching for his lost horse Rakhsh, Sohrab grew up in Turan without knowing his father's identity; Tahmina gave him an amulet of Rostam to wear. Seeking fame and hoping to find his father, the young Sohrab joined the army of Afrasiab, the Turanian enemy of Iran, intending to overthrow the Iranian shah Kay Kavus and place Rostam on the throne.

When the armies met, Sohrab and Rostam fought in single combat without recognising one another, Rostam concealing his name. After a prolonged struggle, Rostam dealt Sohrab a mortal wound, and only then discovered the amulet and realised he had killed his own son. Sohrab died in his father's arms, and Rostam's grief became one of the most famous tragedies of Persian literature.

Cultural impact

The tale of Rostam and Sohrab has inspired countless retellings, paintings and operas; in English it is best known through Matthew Arnold's narrative poem Sohrab and Rustum (1853). The story is a central theme of Iranian cultural identity and of the tragic conflict between the generations.
