The French Football Federation (French: Fédération Française de Football, FFF) is the governing body of football in France. It was founded in 1919 and is based in the capital, Paris. The FFF is a founding member of both FIFA and UEFA and is responsible for all aspects of French football, from the national teams to the administration of the country's club competitions outside the professional leagues (which are run by the Ligue de Football Professionnel under its authority).

Responsibilities

The federation organises the France national football team and the France women's national football team, as well as the national youth teams. It runs the Coupe de France, France's premier knockout cup competition (founded in 1917 and open to thousands of amateur and professional clubs), the Coupe de France Féminine, and the national amateur leagues (National, National 2, National 3) below Ligue 2. It also oversees futsal, beach soccer, refereeing, coaching education, and disciplinary matters. The elite national training centre at Clairefontaine (the Institut national du football de Clairefontaine, opened in 1988) is the FFF's flagship academy and the base of the national teams.

History

French football was originally governed by the Union des Sociétés Françaises de Sports Athlétiques (USFSA), which was a founding member of FIFA in 1904; after years of rivalry between competing bodies, the Comité Français Interfédéral unified the sport and was transformed into the Fédération Française de Football Association on 7 April 1919, with Jules Rimet — later FIFA president and creator of the World Cup — as its first president. Under the FFF, France won the FIFA World Cup in 1998 (hosted at home, at the new Stade de France) and again in 2018, and the UEFA European Championship in 1984 and 2000. The federation counts more than two million licensed players among its affiliated clubs, making it one of the largest sports federations in France.
