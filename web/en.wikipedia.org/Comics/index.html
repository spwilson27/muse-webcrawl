Comics

Comics is a medium that uses images, often combined with text, to express ideas and tell stories. The imagery typically takes the form of a sequence of panels, with dialogue and narration conveyed through speech balloons, captions, and sound-effect lettering. Major traditions include American comic books and comic strips, Japanese manga, and Franco-Belgian albums.

History

Sequential art has antecedents in Egyptian painting, medieval tapestries such as the Bayeux Tapestry, and the satirical prints of the nineteenth century. The modern comic strip emerged in American newspapers in the 1890s with characters such as the Yellow Kid. Comic books became a mass medium in the 1930s; the debut of Superman in Action Comics in 1938 launched the superhero genre and the so-called Golden Age of Comic Books.

Forms and genres

Comics appear as newspaper strips, monthly pamphlet-style issues, collected graphic novels, and webcomics published online. Beyond superheroes, the medium embraces memoir, journalism, history, fantasy, and literary fiction, and works such as Maus and Persepolis have won major literary honours. In Japan, manga accounts for a large share of all books and magazines sold and addresses every demographic and genre.
