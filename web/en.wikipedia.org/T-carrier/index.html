T-carrier

The T-carrier system, developed by Bell Labs, is a digital transmission system used in North America and Japan to multiplex multiple voice and data channels over a single physical line. Its best-known level is the T1 line.

Levels

A T1 carries 24 digitised voice channels at a total rate of 1.544 Mbit/s. Higher levels include T2 (6.312 Mbit/s), T3 (44.736 Mbit/s, 672 channels), and T4. The corresponding European system is the E-carrier.

Technology

T-carrier uses pulse-code modulation: each voice channel is sampled 8,000 times per second and encoded in 8 bits, giving 64 kbit/s per channel, with framing bits synchronising the stream.

Use

T1 lines were widely used for business telephone trunks and early Internet connections before being largely superseded by fibre-optic and DSL services.
