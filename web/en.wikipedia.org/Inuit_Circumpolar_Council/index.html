The Inuit Circumpolar Council (ICC) is a major international non-governmental organisation representing the Inuit of four countries — Alaska (United States), Canada, Greenland (Denmark), and Chukotka (Russia) — a population of roughly 180,000 people across the circumpolar Arctic homeland known as Inuit Nunaat.

History

The organisation grew out of a 1973 Arctic peoples' conference and was formally founded in 1977 in Barrow (Utqiagvik), Alaska, largely at the initiative of Eben Hopson, amid concern over oil development in the Arctic. It adopted the name Inuit Circumpolar Council in 2006. The ICC holds a general assembly every four years, rotating among member countries, and elects an international chair.

Work

The ICC is a Permanent Participant in the Arctic Council and holds consultative status at the United Nations. It advocates on climate change — Arctic warming proceeds several times faster than the global average — as well as contaminants transported to the Arctic, wildlife management (including polar bears and seals), language revitalisation, food security, and Inuit self-determination. Its 2009 Circumpolar Inuit Declaration on Sovereignty in the Arctic asserted Inuit rights in a rapidly changing region.
