Brain tumor

TypesPrimary and metastatic; benign and malignant
Common primary typesGliomas, meningiomas, pituitary adenomas
SymptomsHeadache, seizures, focal deficits, raised intracranial pressure

A brain tumor is an abnormal growth of cells within the brain or the central spinal canal. Tumors may be primary, arising from brain tissue or its coverings, or metastatic, spreading from cancers elsewhere in the body; metastatic tumors are more common overall. They may be benign or malignant, but even benign tumors can be dangerous because the skull cannot expand.

Types and presentation

Gliomas, arising from glial cells, include the aggressive glioblastoma; meningiomas arise from the meninges and are usually benign; pituitary adenomas affect hormone production. Symptoms depend on location and growth rate and include headaches (often worse in the morning), seizures, weakness, speech or vision changes, and personality changes, frequently from raised intracranial pressure.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis relies on neurological examination and imaging, usually MRI, with biopsy or surgical sampling to classify the tumor molecularly. Treatment combines surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, tailored to type and grade; modern classification also uses genetic markers such as IDH mutation status. Prognosis varies enormously, from cure for many benign tumors to limited survival for glioblastoma.
