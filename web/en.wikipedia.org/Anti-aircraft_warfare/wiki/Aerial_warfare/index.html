Aerial warfare

Fighter aircraft engaged in aerial combat during the Second World War era

Aerial warfare is the use of military aircraft and other flying machines in warfare. It includes air combat between aircraft, bombing of ground and naval targets, reconnaissance, air transport of troops and supplies, and the defence against enemy air attack. Modern aerial warfare is conducted by air forces and by naval and army aviation arms.

History

Military use of the air began with observation balloons in the late 18th and 19th centuries. Powered flight transformed warfare in the First World War, when aeroplanes progressed from reconnaissance to fighter combat, tactical bombing and the first strategic bombing raids by Zeppelins and heavy bombers. The interwar theorists Giulio Douhet, Hugh Trenchard and Billy Mitchell argued that air power could be independently decisive.

The Second World War saw aerial warfare on a vast scale: the Battle of Britain, carrier battles in the Pacific, strategic bombing campaigns over Germany and Japan, airborne assaults, and finally the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. In the jet age, Korea, Vietnam and the Gulf War demonstrated guided weapons, suppression of air defences, and precision bombing, while unmanned aerial vehicles have increasingly taken over reconnaissance and strike missions in the 21st century.

Roles

  • Air superiority — defeating enemy air forces to control the sky
  • Strategic bombing — attacking industry, infrastructure and morale deep behind lines
  • Close air support — attacking ground targets in support of friendly troops
  • Interdiction — striking supply lines and reinforcements
  • Reconnaissance and surveillance — gathering intelligence from the air
  • Airlift — transporting troops, equipment and relief supplies
  • Air defence — fighters, anti-aircraft artillery and surface-to-air missiles countering enemy air attack
