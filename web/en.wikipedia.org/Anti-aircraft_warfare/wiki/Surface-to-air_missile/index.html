Surface-to-air missile

A surface-to-air missile being launched

A surface-to-air missile (SAM), also known as a ground-to-air missile (GTAM) or surface-to-air guided weapon (SAGW), is a missile designed to be launched from the ground (or from ships) to destroy aircraft or other missiles. SAMs range from shoulder-fired man-portable systems to huge long-range weapons defending cities and fleets, and they form the backbone of modern integrated air defence systems.

History

Development began during the Second World War in Germany (the Wasserfall and Enzian projects) but the first operational systems appeared in the 1950s, including the American Nike Ajax and the Soviet S-25 Berkut and S-75 Dvina (SA-2 Guideline). The S-75 gained fame for shooting down a U-2 spyplane piloted by Francis Gary Powers over the Soviet Union in 1960 and was exported widely, seeing heavy use in Vietnam and the Middle East. Later generations — the American Patriot, the Soviet/Russian S-300 and S-400, the European Aster and the Israeli Arrow — added multi-target engagement, anti-ballistic-missile capability and high mobility.

Guidance

SAM guidance methods include:

  • Command guidance — the ground station tracks both missile and target and radios commands
  • Semi-active radar homing — the missile homes on radar energy reflected from the target
  • Active radar homing — the missile carries its own radar
  • Infrared homing — the missile seeks the target's heat, typical of short-range systems
  • Track-via-missile — target data is relayed through the missile to the ground station

Organisation

SAMs are rarely used alone; they are deployed in systems combining early-warning and fire-control radars, command posts, launchers and reload vehicles, layered with fighters and anti-aircraft guns to defend against attack profiles from terrain-following jets to ballistic missiles.
