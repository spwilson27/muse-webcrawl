Tart

A tart is a baked dish consisting of a filling over a pastry base with an open top not covered with pastry. The pastry is usually shortcrust, and the filling may be sweet or savoury. Tarts are typically made in shallow pans with straight, fluted sides and removable bottoms, and are distinguished from pies, which in common usage are deeper and often have a top crust.

History

Tarts descend from medieval European cookery: French tarte and Italian torta/crostata were among the earliest pastry preparations, initially savoury meat dishes and later showcases for fruit and custard. Medieval pastry cases ("coffins") were often inedible containers; by the early modern period, enriched shortcrust made the whole dish edible, and tarts became centrepieces of patisserie.

Varieties

Famous sweet tarts include the French tarte Tatin (caramelized upside-down apple tart), tarte au citron (lemon tart), Bakewell tart from Derbyshire, the Portuguese pastel de nata (custard tart), Hong Kong egg tarts, and fruit tarts glazed with apricot jam over crème pâtissière. Savoury examples include quiche Lorraine and onion tart. Sizes range from individual tartelettes to large sharing tarts.

Preparation

The crust is commonly blind-baked — lined with weights to prevent rising — before filling, to keep the base crisp ("no soggy bottom"). Fillings such as lemon curd, ganache, or frangipane may be baked again or set cold.
