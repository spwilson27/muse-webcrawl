Sierra Nevada (U.S.)

Highest pointMount Whitney, 14,505 ft (4,421 m)
Length~400 mi (640 km), north–south
LocationCalifornia (and Nevada), United States

The Sierra Nevada is a mountain range in the Western United States, between the Central Valley of California and the Great Basin. The vast majority of the range lies in the state of California, although the Carson Range spur lies primarily in Nevada. Its name is Spanish for "snowy mountain range". The Sierra Nevada is part of the American Cordillera, an almost continuous chain of mountain ranges forming the western backbone of the Americas.

Geography

The range runs about 400 miles (640 km) from the Tehachapi Pass in the south to the Fredonyer Pass in the north, where it meets the Cascade Range. Its width ranges from about 50 to 80 miles. Notable features include Lake Tahoe, the largest alpine lake in North America; Mount Whitney, at 14,505 feet (4,421 m) the highest point in the contiguous United States; and Yosemite Valley, carved by glaciers from granite.

Geology and ecology

The Sierra is a tilted fault block: a long gentle slope rises from the Central Valley in the west, while the eastern escarpment drops steeply to the Great Basin. The core of the range is granitic rock of the Sierra Nevada Batholith, formed deep underground in the Mesozoic era and uplifted over the last several million years, then sculpted by Ice Age glaciers. The range is home to three national parks — Yosemite, Sequoia, and Kings Canyon — and to the giant sequoia groves, the largest trees on Earth by volume.

The Sierra snowpack supplies water to much of California through the Sacramento and San Joaquin river systems, and its western foothills were the centre of the California Gold Rush of 1848–49, which began at Sutter's Mill on the American River.
