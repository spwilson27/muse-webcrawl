Hypnotherapy

Hypnotherapy is a type of complementary therapy that uses guided relaxation, intense concentration and focused attention to achieve a heightened state of awareness, sometimes called a trance, in which therapeutic suggestions are offered. It is used to help people manage problems such as anxiety, phobias, chronic pain, irritable bowel syndrome, and habits such as smoking.

History

The practice descends from the work of Franz Mesmer in the 18th century; James Braid coined the term "hypnosis" in the 1840s, and figures such as Jean-Martin Charcot, Sigmund Freud and Milton Erickson influenced its development. Modern hypnotherapy is often combined with cognitive behavioural therapy.

Evidence and criticism

There is reasonable evidence supporting gut-directed hypnotherapy for irritable bowel syndrome and hypnosis as an adjunct for pain management and procedural anxiety; evidence for many other indications is weaker and studies are often small. Critics note the risk of false memory creation when hypnosis is used to "recover" supposedly repressed memories, a practice now widely discouraged.
