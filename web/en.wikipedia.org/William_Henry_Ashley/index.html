William Henry Ashley

William Henry Ashley (c. 1778 – March 26, 1838) was an American fur trader, land speculator, manufacturer, and politician. He co-founded the Rocky Mountain Fur Company and devised the mountain man "rendezvous" system that transformed the western fur trade, before serving Missouri in the U.S. House of Representatives.

Fur trade career

Born in Virginia and raised in Missouri, Ashley served as lieutenant governor of Missouri before entering the fur trade. In 1822 he and Andrew Henry placed their famous newspaper advertisement for "enterprising young men" to ascend the Missouri River — recruiting the legendary first class of mountain men, including Jedediah Smith, Jim Bridger, and Thomas Fitzpatrick. After harrowing encounters including the Arikara War of 1823 and the Leavenworth Expedition, Ashley in 1825 replaced the fixed-post supply model with an annual rendezvous at a prearranged mountain valley, where trappers traded a year's furs for supplies. He sold the company to Smith, David Jackson, and William Sublette in 1826, wealthy and famous.

Politics

Ashley was elected to Congress in 1831 and served three terms, chairing the Committee on Indian Affairs and advocating for western interests. He died in Boonville, Missouri, in 1838.
