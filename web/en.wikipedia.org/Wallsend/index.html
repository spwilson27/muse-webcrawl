Wallsend is a town in the metropolitan borough of North Tyneside, Tyne and Wear, England, at the eastern end of Hadrian's Wall. It is situated on the north bank of the River Tyne, about 3.5 miles (5.6 km) east of Newcastle upon Tyne. Historically part of Northumberland, Wallsend takes its name from its location at the end of the Roman wall.

History

The Romans built the fort of Segedunum at Wallsend around AD 122, garrisoning the eastern terminus of Hadrian's Wall, which ran from the fort down to the Tyne. The excavated fort is now a museum with a viewing tower and a reconstructed bath house.

In the industrial era Wallsend became famous for coal mining — the Wallsend colliery gave its name to "Wallsend coal", a prized household fuel — and for shipbuilding. The Swan Hunter shipyard (later Swan Hunter & Wigham Richardson) at Wallsend built many famous vessels, including RMS Mauretania (launched 1906), then the largest ship in the world, and HMS Ark Royal. Shipbuilding declined in the later 20th century; part of the yard now supports offshore engineering.

Transport and community

Wallsend is served by the Tyne and Wear Metro (Wallsend station, whose signs are uniquely bilingual in English and Latin), and lies near the northern entrance of the Tyne Tunnel. Famous people born in Wallsend include the musician Sting (Gordon Sumner), who worked as a teacher in the area and set his musical The Last Ship in the town's shipyards.
