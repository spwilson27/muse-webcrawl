Kavad II

Kavad II (Middle Persian: Kawād), regnal name Shiruya (or Siroe), was King of Kings of the Sasanian Empire for a few months in 628. He came to power by overthrowing and executing his father, Khosrow II, ended the long Byzantine–Sasanian war, and died within the year — traditionally of plague — leaving the empire in turmoil shortly before the Arab conquests.

Coup against Khosrow II

Kavad was the eldest son of Khosrow II by Maria, a Byzantine princess according to some traditions. Khosrow's favour toward another son, Mardanshah, by his favourite wife Shirin, drove Kavad and disaffected nobles and generals to act. On 23 February 628 the conspirators stormed the palace at Ctesiphon, freed Kavad from confinement and proclaimed him king; Khosrow was tried on a list of charges and executed days later, along with many of Kavad's brothers.

Peace with Byzantium

The coup ended the devastating war of 602–628, in which the emperor Heraclius had campaigned deep into Persia. Kavad opened negotiations and concluded peace on the basis of the pre-war frontiers: Persian forces evacuated occupied Byzantine territory, prisoners were exchanged, and the relic of the True Cross, captured at Jerusalem in 614, was returned to Heraclius.

Death and succession

Kavad died after a reign of only a few months, in an outbreak of plague remembered in Persian tradition as the "Plague of Shiruya", which also devastated western Iran. He was succeeded by his young son Ardashir III, but effective power passed to rival generals and factions, beginning a period of rapid coups and civil war that fatally weakened the Sasanian state before the Muslim conquest of Persia.
