Radcliffe College

Radcliffe Yard, the historic centre of the college.

Radcliffe College was a women's liberal arts college in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and functioned as the female coordinate institution for Harvard University. Founded in 1879 as the Society for the Collegiate Instruction of Women — popularly known as the "Harvard Annex" — it was chartered as Radcliffe College in 1894, named in honour of Ann Radcliffe (née Mowlson), the first woman to give a substantial donation to Harvard.

History

In its early years, Radcliffe students were taught by Harvard professors in separate classes; from 1943, classes were fully coeducational. Undergraduate admissions were merged with Harvard in 1977 under a "non-merger merger", and in 1999 Radcliffe College was formally dissolved, becoming the Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study at Harvard University. From 1963 onward, Radcliffe students received Harvard diplomas signed by the presidents of both institutions.

Notable alumnae

Alumnae include author and activist Helen Keller (class of 1904), writer Gertrude Stein, poet Adrienne Rich, and former Barclays and publishing figures; the college's Schlesinger Library is a leading repository on the history of women in America.
