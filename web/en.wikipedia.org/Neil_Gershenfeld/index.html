Neil Gershenfeld

Neil Adam Gershenfeld (born 1959/1960) is an American physicist and computer scientist, best known as the director of the MIT Center for Bits and Atoms (CBA) and as the founder of the global fab lab movement of community digital-fabrication workshops.

Career

Gershenfeld studied physics at Swarthmore College and earned his PhD at Cornell University in 1990, working on neural networks and quantum computing. After research at Bell Labs he joined the MIT Media Lab faculty. His 1998 class "How to Make (Almost) Anything" — created so students could use a suite of computer-controlled fabrication tools — unexpectedly drew students from across MIT and became the seed of the fab lab concept.

Fab labs

The first fab labs were set up in the early 2000s in places including inner-city Boston, rural India, South Africa, Norway and Ghana, each equipped with a standard inventory of laser cutters, CNC mills, 3D printers, electronics benches and programming tools. The network has grown to well over a thousand labs worldwide, coordinated through the Fab Foundation and an annual gathering, and trained through the Fab Academy programme, which Gershenfeld teaches from MIT to students distributed across the network.

Research and writing

At the Center for Bits and Atoms, Gershenfeld's research spans digital materials, programmable matter, quantum computation and the Internet of Things. He is the author of When Things Start to Think (1999) and FAB (2005), and co-author of Designing Reality (2017) with his brothers Alan Gershenfeld and Joel Cutcher-Gershenfeld. He has been recognised as one of the pioneers of personal fabrication and was listed among the "Scientific American 50" and Popular Mechanics' breakout innovators.
