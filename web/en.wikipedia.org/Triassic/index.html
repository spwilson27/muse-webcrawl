The Triassic is a geologic period and system which spans 50.5 million years from the end of the Permian Period 251.902 million years ago (Mya), to the beginning of the Jurassic Period 201.4 Mya. The Triassic is the first and shortest period of the Mesozoic Era. Both the start and end of the period are marked by major extinction events. The Triassic Period is subdivided into three epochs: Early Triassic, Middle Triassic and Late Triassic.

History of study

The Triassic was named in 1834 by Friedrich von Alberti, after the three distinct rock layers (from the Latin trias meaning a triad) found in Germany and northwest Europe: red beds, capped by marine limestone, followed by continental red beds — the so-called "Trias".

Climate and geography

During the Triassic, almost all the Earth's land mass was concentrated into a single supercontinent known as Pangaea. The climate was generally hot and dry, with extensive deserts. The interior of Pangaea was likely arid, while coastal regions experienced seasonal monsoonal conditions. There were no polar ice caps.

Life

The Triassic began in the wake of the Permian–Triassic extinction event, the largest mass extinction in Earth's history, which devastated marine and terrestrial life. Recovery took millions of years. Three categories of organisms can be distinguished: survivors from the extinction, new groups that flourished briefly, and other new groups that went on to dominate the Mesozoic. In the oceans, modern-type corals and teleost fish appeared. On land, the first dinosaurs, pterosaurs and true mammals evolved, while archosaurs such as phytosaurs and rauisuchians became dominant predators. The first frogs and turtles also appeared.

End of the period

The Triassic ended with the Triassic–Jurassic extinction event, which wiped out many groups including most non-dinosaurian archosaurs and many large amphibians, clearing ecological niches for dinosaurs to dominate the Jurassic.
