Henry Dunant (born Jean-Henri Dunant; 8 May 1828 – 30 October 1910) was a Swiss humanitarian, businessman, and social activist. He was the founder of the Red Cross and inspired the creation of the Geneva Conventions. In 1901 he received the first Nobel Peace Prize together with Frédéric Passy, making him the first Swiss Nobel laureate.

Solferino and A Memory of Solferino

Dunant was raised in Geneva in a devout Calvinist family active in social work. While travelling in Italy in June 1859 on business — he hoped to secure land and water concessions in French-occupied Algeria and sought an audience with Napoleon III — he witnessed the aftermath of the Battle of Solferino, in which tens of thousands of wounded soldiers were left largely untended on the battlefield. Dunant organised local civilians, especially women, to care for the wounded of all sides under the motto tutti fratelli ("all are brothers").

In 1862 he published A Memory of Solferino, proposing the creation of permanent voluntary relief societies in every country and an international legal agreement guaranteeing protection for the wounded and for those caring for them.

Founding the Red Cross and the Geneva Convention

Dunant's book led in February 1863 to the formation in Geneva of the International Committee for Relief to the Wounded — the future International Committee of the Red Cross — by Dunant, Gustave Moynier, and three others. A diplomatic conference followed, and on 22 August 1864 twelve states signed the First Geneva Convention. In October 1863 an international conference had adopted the red cross on a white background — the Swiss flag reversed — as the protective emblem.

Later life

Dunant's Algerian business ventures collapsed, and in 1867 he was declared bankrupt; the scandal forced his resignation from the Committee he had founded, and he spent decades in poverty and obscurity, living in London, Paris, and finally the village of Heiden in Appenzell, Switzerland. Public recognition returned in the 1890s, crowned by the 1901 Nobel Peace Prize, whose money he left largely to charity. He died in Heiden in 1910. His birthday, 8 May, is commemorated as World Red Cross and Red Crescent Day.
