Eggs Benedict

Eggs Benedict
Course: Breakfast, brunch
Place of origin: United States
Main ingredients: English muffin, poached egg, Canadian bacon or ham, hollandaise sauce

Eggs Benedict is an American breakfast or brunch dish, consisting of an open-faced English muffin topped with a poached egg, Canadian bacon or ham, and hollandaise sauce. The dish was popularized in New York City in the late 19th century and is named after a patron or patrons named Benedict, though several competing origin stories exist.

Origin

One account credits Delmonico's Restaurant in Manhattan, where in the 1860s a regular patron, Mrs. LeGrand Benedict, asked the chef for something new. Another claims that in 1894 a hungover Wall Street broker named Lemuel Benedict ordered the combination at the Waldorf Hotel, where maître d' Oscar Tschirky adapted it and put it on the menu. A third claim comes from Commodore E. C. Benedict, a banker and yachtsman, whose recipe was later published by a relative.

Preparation

A muffin half is toasted and topped with a slice of Canadian bacon or ham, a poached egg, and a generous spoonful of hollandaise sauce — an emulsion of egg yolk, melted butter, and lemon juice or vinegar. The dish is traditionally served as two halves on one plate.

Variations

Many variants exist, including eggs Florentine (with spinach instead of ham), eggs Royale (with smoked salmon), eggs Sardou (with artichoke hearts and anchovy), and eggs Blackstone (with bacon and tomato). In the Pacific Northwest, Dungeness crab is sometimes used.
