Fort William, Highland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort William (Scottish Gaelic: An Gearasdan, "The Garrison") is a town in Lochaber in the Scottish Highlands, in the council area of Highland. It sits on the eastern shore of Loch Linnhe, at the foot of Ben Nevis, the highest mountain in the British Isles, and is a major centre for hillwalking, climbing and outdoor tourism.

History

The first fortification on the site was a wooden fort built by Oliver Cromwell's forces in 1654, during the Commonwealth, intended to pacify the Highlands; it was known as the Garrison of Inverlochy. After the Glorious Revolution it was rebuilt in stone in 1690 and named Fort William after William of Orange. The fort withstood a Jacobite siege in the rising of 1746 but was demolished in stages in the nineteenth century to make way for the railway.

The town grew around the fort and was for part of its history called Maryburgh, then Gordonsburgh and Duncansburgh, before settling on Fort William. In the twentieth century an aluminium smelter opened nearby at Lochaber, powered by hydroelectricity, and tourism became the dominant industry.

Geography and tourism

Fort William marks the southern end of the Great Glen and of the Caledonian Canal, whose locks at nearby Corpach are known as Neptune's Staircase. The town is the start or finish of the West Highland Way and the Great Glen Way, and the starting point for most ascents of Ben Nevis. The Jacobite steam train to Mallaig, the Nevis Range gondola and the Commando Memorial at Spean Bridge are among its visitor attractions.

Transport

The town is served by the A82 trunk road and by Fort William railway station on the West Highland Line, famous for the Glenfinnan Viaduct. The Corran Ferry crosses Loch Linnhe south of the town.
