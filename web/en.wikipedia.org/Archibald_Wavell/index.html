Field Marshal Archibald Percival Wavell, 1st Earl Wavell (5 May 1883 – 24 May 1950) was a senior officer of the British Army. He served in the Second Boer War, the Bazar Valley Campaign and the First World War, during which he was wounded and lost an eye. During the Second World War he served as Commander-in-Chief Middle East, in which role he led British forces to victory over the Italian Army in Eritrea, Abyssinia, western Egypt and eastern Libya during Operation Compass in January 1941, only to be beaten back by the Afrika Korps under Erwin Rommel in May 1941. He served as Commander-in-Chief, India, from July 1941 until June 1943 and then served as Viceroy of India until his retirement in February 1947.

Early life and First World War

Wavell was born at Colchester, the son of a major-general, and educated at Winchester College and Sandhurst. Commissioned into the Black Watch in 1901, he served in South Africa and on the North-West Frontier of India, and attended the Staff College at Camberley. In the First World War, after service in France — where he lost his left eye at the Second Battle of Ypres in 1915 — he was posted to the Caucasus front with the Russians and then to Allenby's staff in Palestine, serving through the successful campaigns of 1917–1918. Between the wars he held senior commands and in 1937–1938 commanded British forces in Palestine during the Arab revolt.

Second World War

As C-in-Chief Middle East from 1939, Wavell directed an enormous and under-resourced theatre encompassing Egypt, Palestine, Iraq, East Africa, and Greece. His Western Desert Force under Richard O'Connor destroyed the Italian Tenth Army in Operation Compass (December 1940 – February 1941), taking some 130,000 prisoners. British forces also conquered Italian East Africa. However, the diversion of troops to Greece, the arrival of Rommel's Afrika Korps, a revolt in Iraq, and the Vichy French campaign in Syria stretched his command beyond its means; after the failure of Operation Battleaxe, Churchill replaced him in July 1941, exchanging posts with Claude Auchinleck. As C-in-Chief India and then ABDACOM commander in early 1942, Wavell presided over the disasters of Malaya, Singapore, and Burma, for which historians judge his resources hopelessly inadequate rather than his generalship deficient. He was made a viscount in 1943.

Viceroy of India

Appointed Viceroy in June 1943, Wavell took office amid the Bengal famine, whose relief he pressed with the military resources at his command. He attempted to advance political settlement through the Wavell Plan and the Simla Conference of 1945, which failed over the Muslim League's claim to nominate all Muslim members of the executive council. His relations with Churchill were poor, and in February 1947 Attlee's government replaced him with Lord Mountbatten, who oversaw partition and independence. Wavell was created Earl Wavell on his return and devoted his final years to literature — he was a noted anthologist, editor of Other Men's Flowers (1944) — before dying in 1950.
