Iridaceae

Iridaceae is a family of perennial, herbaceous and bulbous plants in the order Asparagales, taking its name from the genus Iris. The family includes roughly 66–80 accepted genera and around 2,000 species distributed worldwide, with notable centres of diversity in southern Africa, the Mediterranean region, and South and Central America.

Description

Members of the family are mostly perennial herbs arising from rhizomes, corms or bulbs. The leaves are typically sword-shaped (ensiform), unifacial, and arranged in a fan-like distichous pattern at the base. Flowers are often large and showy, with three outer and three inner tepals, three stamens opposite the outer tepals, and an inferior ovary. The fruit is a loculicidal capsule.

Genera and uses

Well-known genera include Iris, Gladiolus, Crocus, Freesia, Crocosmia, Sisyrinchium, Watsonia, Moraea and Dietes. Many are grown as garden ornamentals and cut flowers. The stigmas of Crocus sativus yield the spice saffron, the most expensive spice by weight, and orris root, from iris rhizomes, is used in perfumery.

Taxonomy

The family is divided into several subfamilies, including Iridoideae, Crocoideae, Nivenioideae, Isophysidoideae and Aristeoideae. Southern Africa is especially rich in species, many adapted to Mediterranean-type winter-rainfall climates.
