Metabolomics

Metabolomics is the scientific study of chemical processes involving metabolites — the small-molecule intermediates and products of cell metabolism. It involves the systematic identification and quantification of the metabolome, the complete set of metabolites in a biological sample, using techniques such as mass spectrometry and nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy.

Scope

Unlike genes and proteins, metabolites are the direct end products of cellular processes and reflect both genetic and environmental influences, making metabolomics a powerful approach in systems biology. It is one of the "omics" sciences alongside genomics, transcriptomics and proteomics, but the metabolome is far more chemically diverse, and no single analytical method can detect all metabolites.

Methods

Samples (blood, urine, tissue, cell cultures) are typically separated by gas or liquid chromatography coupled to mass spectrometry, or analysed directly by NMR. Experiments may be untargeted — profiling as many features as possible — or targeted at specific compounds. Data analysis relies on multivariate statistics and pathway databases such as KEGG and the Human Metabolome Database.

Applications

Applications include biomarker discovery for disease diagnosis, toxicology, drug development, nutrition research, plant science and environmental monitoring.
