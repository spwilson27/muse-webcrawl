Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany

The Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany (German: Grundgesetz für die Bundesrepublik Deutschland) is the constitution of the Federal Republic of Germany. It was approved on 8 May 1949 in Bonn, and, with the signature of the western Allies, came into effect on 23 May 1949 as the de facto constitution of West Germany. The word "Basic Law" was used deliberately to indicate that it was a provisional arrangement pending German reunification; when reunification came in 1990, the Basic Law was retained and extended to the whole country.

Contents

The Basic Law opens with an inviolable guarantee of human dignity (Article 1) followed by a catalogue of fundamental rights binding on all state authority. It establishes a federal parliamentary republic in which legislative power rests with the Bundestag and the Bundesrat, executive power with the Federal Government under the Chancellor, and judicial review with the Federal Constitutional Court. Articles 1 and 20 — protecting human dignity, democracy, the rule of law, federalism and the social state — are shielded from amendment by the "eternity clause" (Article 79(3)).

History

The Basic Law was drafted by the Parliamentary Council (Parlamentarischer Rat), a body of 65 delegates from the western Länder, which convened in Bonn in September 1948 under the chairmanship of Konrad Adenauer. Influenced by the failure of the Weimar Constitution, the framers strengthened the position of the Chancellor (including the constructive vote of no confidence) and entrenches fundamental rights directly enforceable by the courts. The document has been amended many times, notably to permit rearmament in the 1950s and on reunification in 1990.
