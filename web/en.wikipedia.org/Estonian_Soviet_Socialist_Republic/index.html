Estonian Soviet Socialist Republic

The Estonian Soviet Socialist Republic (Estonian: Eesti Nõukogude Sotsialistlik Vabariik, abbreviated Eesti NSV) was a constituent republic of the Soviet Union from 1940 to 1941 and again from 1944 to 1991. It was established following the Soviet occupation of the independent Republic of Estonia in June 1940 under the terms of the secret protocols of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact. The annexation was never recognised by most Western states, which maintained Estonian diplomatic missions throughout the Cold War, and Estonia regards its statehood as having continued de jure throughout the period.

History

After ultimata and a staged "people's government" under Johannes Vares, the Estonian SSR was proclaimed on 21 July 1940 and formally admitted to the USSR on 6 August 1940. The first Soviet year brought nationalisation, arrests, and the mass deportation of some 10,000 Estonians to Siberia in June 1941 — days before Nazi Germany invaded. Under German occupation (1941–1944) Estonia was administered in the Reichskommissariat Ostland. Soviet forces reconquered the country in 1944 after battles including the long defence at the Narva front and the Battle of Tannenberg Line; some 70,000 Estonians fled westward. A second, larger deportation in March 1949 removed over 20,000 people, chiefly to break resistance to collectivisation, which was completed that year. Forest Brothers guerrillas resisted into the 1950s.

Soviet Estonia

Within the USSR, Estonia was the most prosperous republic, with the highest standard of living, helped by oil-shale energy and industry, large-scale immigration of Russian-speaking workers (which reduced ethnic Estonians from 88 per cent of the population in 1934 to about 61.5 per cent by 1989), and relative cultural contact with Finland via television. From 1987 the "Singing Revolution" mobilised mass peaceful protest; the Estonian Supreme Soviet declared sovereignty in November 1988 and, on 20 August 1991, during the Moscow coup attempt, Estonia declared its independence restored, recognised by the USSR on 6 September 1991.
