Karuta

Karuta (Japanese: カルタ, from Portuguese carta ["card"]) are Japanese playing cards. Playing cards were introduced to Japan by Portuguese traders during the mid-16th century. These early decks were used for trick-taking games. Karuta decks fall into two broad families: those derived from Portuguese-suited decks, and the e-awase picture-matching games native to Japan.

Types

The best-known variety is uta-garuta, based on the Hyakunin Isshu anthology of one hundred classical waka poems. In competitive karuta (kyōgi karuta), players memorise the poems, listen to a reader recite the opening lines, and race to slap the corresponding card bearing the poem's closing lines; national championships are held annually in Japan, and the game was popularised internationally by the manga and anime Chihayafuru.

Other popular decks include iroha karuta, in which cards match proverbs to pictures — used traditionally to teach children the kana syllabary — and hanafuda, the "flower cards" used for games such as koi-koi.
