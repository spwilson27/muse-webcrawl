Jogaila

Jogaila (Lithuanian: Jogaila; c. 1352/1362 – 1 June 1434), later Władysław II Jagiełło, was Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1377 and, by marriage to Queen Jadwiga, King of Poland from 1386 — a joint reign lasting until his death, the longest of any Polish monarch. His baptism and marriage founded the Polish–Lithuanian union and the Jagiellonian dynasty.

Rise and union with Poland

Son of Algirdas and nephew of Kęstutis, Jogaila fought a civil war with Kęstutis and then Vytautas. By the Union of Krewo (1385) he promised to accept Catholic baptism, marry Jadwiga, and attach Lithuania to the Polish Crown. He was baptised Władysław in Kraków in February 1386, married Jadwiga, and was crowned king.

Reign

Jogaila oversaw the Christianisation of Lithuania (1387) and, with Vytautas as Grand Duke in Lithuania, the great victory over the Teutonic Order at the Battle of Grunwald (1410), followed by the Treaty of Thorn (1411). The Union of Horodło (1413) deepened Polish–Lithuanian ties.

Death and legacy

He died in 1434 and was buried in Wawel Cathedral. His sons by Sophia of Halshany, Władysław III and Casimir IV, succeeded him, founding a dynasty that ruled Poland-Lithuania and at times Bohemia and Hungary.
