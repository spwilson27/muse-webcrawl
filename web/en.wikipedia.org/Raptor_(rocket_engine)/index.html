Raptor (rocket engine)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Raptor is a family of reusable, methalox (methane/oxygen) rocket engines developed and manufactured by SpaceX for use on the Starship launch system. It uses a full-flow staged combustion cycle, in which all of the propellant passes through preburners driving separate fuel and oxidiser turbopumps — a configuration offering high efficiency and reduced coking and wear. Production versions stand about 3.1 metres tall; the sea-level Raptor powers both the Super Heavy booster (33 engines) and the Starship upper stage (3 sea-level and 3 vacuum-optimised Raptor Vacuum engines).

Design

Burning liquid methane rather than kerosene allows the propellant to be manufactured on Mars from water and carbon dioxide via the Sabatier reaction (in-situ resource utilisation), avoids the soot buildup that complicates kerosene engine reuse, and yields good specific impulse. In the full-flow cycle, oxygen-rich and fuel-rich preburners each drive one turbopump; the hot gases are injected into the main chamber so no propellant is dumped overboard. Raptor 2 (flown from 2022) simplified the design and raised thrust; Raptor 3 (unveiled 2024) further increased thrust and chamber pressure while integrating cooling channels and secondary plumbing into the engine structure, eliminating many external parts and the need for a separate heat shield around each engine.

Development

SpaceX announced the Raptor concept in 2012–2014 as a methane engine for its Mars architecture. Component and subscale testing took place at NASA's Stennis Space Center and SpaceX's McGregor, Texas test site. Full-scale engines powered the Starhopper test vehicle in 2019 and the series of Starship high-altitude test flights of 2020–2021, before the first integrated Starship test flight in April 2023. Engines are mass-produced at the "Raptor Factory" at McGregor and at Starbase, Texas, with the company aiming for production rates of one or more engines per day to support rapid, fully reusable operations.
