Circular motion

FieldClassical mechanics
Key quantitiesAngular velocity, centripetal acceleration

In physics, circular motion is a movement of an object along the circumference of a circle or rotation along a circular arc. It can be uniform, with a constant rate of rotation and constant speed, or non-uniform, with a changing rate of rotation.

Uniform circular motion

Although the speed is constant in uniform circular motion, the velocity is continually changing direction, so the object is always accelerating toward the centre of the circle. This centripetal acceleration has magnitude a = v²/r, where v is the speed and r the radius; equivalently, a = ω²r, where ω is the angular velocity. The net force required is the centripetal force F = mv²/r, directed toward the centre.

The period T (time for one revolution) and frequency f are related by T = 1/f, and ω = 2πf = 2π/T.

Non-uniform circular motion

If the speed also changes, the acceleration gains a tangential component equal to the rate of change of speed, in addition to the centripetal (radial) component. Vertical circular motion under gravity, as in a roller coaster loop, is a standard example.

Applications

Circular motion describes satellites in orbit, vehicles cornering, centrifuges, and particles in cyclotrons, where magnetic forces provide the centripetal force.
