Zero (complex analysis)

Zero of a function
FieldComplex analysis

In mathematics, and more specifically in complex analysis, a zero (or root) of a complex-valued function f is a point a in its domain at which f(a) = 0. Zeros are the counterparts of poles, where the function tends to infinity, and together they govern much of the behaviour of meromorphic functions.

Multiplicity

A zero a of a holomorphic function has multiplicity m if near a the function can be written as f(z) = (z − a)m g(z) with g holomorphic and g(a) ≠ 0; equivalently, the first m − 1 derivatives vanish at a while the m-th does not. A zero of multiplicity one is called simple. Zeros of a nonzero holomorphic function are isolated, a consequence of the identity theorem.

Counting zeros

The argument principle relates the number of zeros and poles inside a closed contour, counted with multiplicity, to the change in argument of f around the contour. Special cases include Rouché's theorem, used to localise zeros, and Jensen's formula. The fundamental theorem of algebra — that a polynomial of degree n has exactly n complex zeros counted with multiplicity — is a cornerstone result. The distribution of zeros of the Riemann zeta function is the subject of the Riemann hypothesis.
