Mentorship

Mentorship is the patronage, influence, guidance or direction given by a mentor: an expert who guides a less experienced person, the mentee, by building trust and modelling positive behaviours. The term derives from Mentor, the friend of Odysseus in Homer's Odyssey to whom the education of Telemachus was entrusted. Effective mentoring requires time and commitment from both parties.

Phases and functions

Research beginning with Kathy Kram's Mentoring at Work (1985) distinguishes career functions of mentoring — sponsorship, coaching, exposure, protection and challenging assignments — from psychosocial functions such as role modelling, acceptance, counselling and friendship. Mentoring relationships typically pass through phases of initiation, cultivation, separation and redefinition. Formal programmes match mentors and mentees within an organization, while informal relationships arise spontaneously; both are associated in studies with career advancement and satisfaction.

Contexts

Mentoring is used in business, academia, medicine, law, the arts and youth development, where organizations such as Big Brothers Big Sisters pair adult volunteers with young people. Mentoring differs from coaching, which focuses on specific skills and tasks, and from sponsorship, which involves active advocacy. Newer forms include peer mentoring, group mentoring, reverse mentoring (in which a junior person mentors a senior, often on technology) and e-mentoring conducted online.
