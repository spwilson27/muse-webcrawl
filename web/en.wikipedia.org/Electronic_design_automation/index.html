Electronic design automation (EDA) is a category of software tools for designing electronic systems such as integrated circuits and printed circuit boards. EDA tools carry a design from specification through logic design, simulation, physical layout and verification to the data used for manufacturing.

Design flow

A typical digital IC flow includes architectural design, register-transfer level (RTL) description in languages such as Verilog and VHDL, logic synthesis, functional verification, place and route, timing analysis and signoff checks. Analogue and mixed-signal design relies heavily on circuit simulation with SPICE.

Industry

The EDA industry is concentrated among a few large vendors — principally Synopsys, Cadence Design Systems and Siemens EDA (formerly Mentor Graphics). EDA tools are indispensable to the semiconductor industry, since modern chips with billions of transistors cannot be designed manually.

History

Computer-aided design for electronics began in the 1960s and 1970s at large electronics firms and universities; commercial EDA emerged in the early 1980s, with logic synthesis and hardware description languages transforming practice later that decade.
