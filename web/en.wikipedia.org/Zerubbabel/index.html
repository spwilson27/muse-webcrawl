Zerubbabel

Zerubbabel (Hebrew: זְרֻבָּבֶל, Zərubbāḇel) was a governor of the Achaemenid province of Yehud (Judah) in the late 6th century BCE and the grandson of Jeconiah, penultimate king of Judah. He is remembered in the Hebrew Bible as the leader of the first group of Jewish exiles returning from the Babylonian captivity and as the figure credited with laying the foundation of the Second Temple in Jerusalem.

According to the Book of Ezra, after Cyrus the Great conquered Babylon in 539 BCE and issued his decree permitting the exiles to return, Zerubbabel led some 42,360 people back to Jerusalem. Together with the high priest Joshua (Jeshua) son of Jozadak, he rebuilt the altar and, in the second year after the return, began work on the Temple. The work stalled amid opposition from neighbouring peoples and was resumed only around 520 BCE, spurred by the prophets Haggai and Zechariah, who hailed Zerubbabel as God's chosen "signet ring" and proclaimed messianic expectations around his Davidic lineage. The Second Temple was completed in 515 BCE. Zerubbabel then disappears from the biblical record; later tradition in 1 Chronicles names him father of Meshullam and Hananiah.

In later tradition

Zerubbabel appears in the genealogies of Jesus in the Gospels of Matthew and Luke as a descendant of David, reflecting his importance as a link in the royal line after the exile. Rabbinic literature also developed legends about his role at the Persian court.
