Wilhelm Killing

Born10 May 1847, Burbach, Prussia
Died11 February 1923, Münster, Germany
FieldsMathematics
Known forLie algebras; Killing form; Killing vector fields; Cartan matrix

Wilhelm Karl Joseph Killing (1847–1923) was a German mathematician who made important contributions to the theory of Lie algebras, Lie groups, and non-Euclidean geometry. He studied at Münster and Berlin, where he came under the influence of Karl Weierstrass, and spent much of his career as a professor at the Lyceum Hosianum in Braunsberg and later at the University of Münster.

Classification of Lie algebras

In a series of papers between 1888 and 1890, Killing carried out the first classification of the simple Lie algebras, introducing the concepts now known as the Cartan matrix, the Weyl group, and root systems. He identified the exceptional Lie algebras as part of his classification; Élie Cartan completed and rigorously reworked the classification in his 1894 thesis, acknowledging Killing's pioneering role. The bilinear form on a Lie algebra used in this theory is called the Killing form in his honour.

Other contributions

Killing made early contributions to non-Euclidean geometry and to the geometry of spaces of constant curvature, and his name is attached in differential geometry and general relativity to Killing vector fields — vector fields that generate isometries of a metric, corresponding to symmetries and conserved quantities of a spacetime.
