Kappa (folklore)

Kappa
Type: Yōkai (water spirit)
Origin: Japanese folklore
Habitat: Rivers, ponds and lakes
Traits: Water-filled dish on head; fond of cucumbers

A kappa is a reptiloid water spirit in Japanese folklore, a type of yōkai said to inhabit rivers and ponds. Typically depicted as roughly the size of a child, with scaly skin, webbed hands and feet, a turtle-like shell and a shallow dish on top of its head that holds water — the source of its power.

Behaviour

Kappa are said to be mischievous or dangerous, luring people and animals into the water, but they are also credited with teaching humans skills such as bone-setting and irrigation. They are famously fond of cucumbers, and cucumber rolls (kappa maki) bear their name.

Defences

According to legend, a kappa is unfailingly polite: if a person bows deeply, the kappa will bow back, spilling the water from its head-dish and losing its strength until the water is replaced. See also Yōkai.
