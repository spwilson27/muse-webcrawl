Kaitai Shinsho

Kaitai Shinsho (解体新書, "New Text on Anatomy") is a Japanese medical text published in 1774, the first complete Japanese translation of a Western anatomical work. It was translated from the Dutch edition of Johann Adam Kulmus's Anatomische Tabellen by a group of scholars led by Sugita Genpaku and Maeno Ryōtaku.

Background

In 1771 Sugita Genpaku observed the dissection of an executed criminal at the Kozukappara execution grounds and found the Dutch anatomical illustrations strikingly more accurate than those in Chinese texts. Encouraged, he and his colleagues spent several years translating the work, coining many new Japanese medical terms in the process.

Significance

The publication of Kaitai Shinsho in 1774 is regarded as a landmark in rangaku (Dutch learning) and in the history of Japanese medicine, symbolizing the beginning of Western-style empirical science in Japan. Sugita later recounted the project in his memoir Rangaku kotohajime (1815).
