Chiral symmetry breaking


The QCD vacuum and chiral symmetry

In particle physics, chiral symmetry breaking is the spontaneous symmetry breaking of the approximate chiral symmetry of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the theory of the strong interaction. In the limit where the light quarks (up, down and strange) are massless, the QCD Lagrangian is invariant under independent rotations of left-handed and right-handed quark fields, a symmetry denoted SU(3)L × SU(3)R for three flavours. In nature this symmetry is not realised in the spectrum of hadrons; instead the vacuum breaks it spontaneously to the diagonal subgroup SU(3)V, generating a quark condensate.

Consequences

By Goldstone's theorem, spontaneous breaking of the chiral symmetry implies the existence of massless pseudoscalar bosons. In QCD these are identified with the pions, kaons and eta meson, which are not exactly massless because the quark masses break the symmetry explicitly as well; they are therefore called pseudo-Goldstone bosons, and their small masses and interactions are described by chiral perturbation theory. The classic relation mπ2 fπ2 ≈ −(mu+md)⟨q̄q⟩ (the Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation) connects the pion mass to the quark condensate.

Chiral symmetry breaking also explains why most of the mass of ordinary matter arises from QCD dynamics rather than from the Higgs mechanism: the constituent quark masses generated in the broken phase, and the associated binding energy, account for most of the mass of protons and neutrons. At sufficiently high temperature or density, as in the early universe or in heavy-ion collisions, chiral symmetry is expected to be restored, in a phase transition explored experimentally at facilities such as RHIC and the LHC.

Historical development

The phenomenon was first understood through the work of Yoichiro Nambu and collaborators around 1960, who adapted ideas from superconductivity to particle physics; Nambu received the 2008 Nobel Prize in Physics in part for the discovery of the mechanism of spontaneous broken symmetry in subatomic physics.
