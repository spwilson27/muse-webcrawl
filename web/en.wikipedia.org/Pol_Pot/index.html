Pol Pot

Pol Pot (born Saloth Sâr; 19 May 1925 – 15 April 1998) was a Cambodian revolutionary, dictator, and politician who ruled Cambodia as Prime Minister of Democratic Kampuchea between 1976 and 1979. He was the leader of the Khmer Rouge from 1963 until his death in 1998. During his administration, Pol Pot attempted to create a Cambodian "master race" through social engineering, whose policies caused famine, forced labour, and the Cambodian genocide, in which an estimated 1.5 to 2 million people — roughly a quarter of the population — died.

Rise to power

Educated in Phnom Penh and Paris, where he joined the French Communist Party, Saloth Sâr returned to Cambodia in 1953 and rose through the communist underground, becoming party leader in 1963 and waging guerrilla war from the jungle. After the Khmer Republic of Lon Nol collapsed, the Khmer Rouge captured Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975 and forcibly evacuated its population to the countryside.

Democratic Kampuchea

Pol Pot's regime abolished money, private property, markets, religion, and formal education, declaring "Year Zero". Intellectuals, ethnic Vietnamese and Cham, former officials, and suspected enemies were murdered — notoriously at the Tuol Sleng (S-21) prison and the "Killing Fields" — while mass starvation resulted from radical collectivisation. Border clashes with Vietnam provoked an invasion in December 1978; the regime fell on 7 January 1979.

Later life and death

Pol Pot continued guerrilla resistance from the Thai border. In 1997 his own movement purged and imprisoned him; he died in April 1998, reportedly of heart failure, while awaiting transfer to an international tribunal. Decades later, the Khmer Rouge Tribunal convicted senior leaders including Nuon Chea and Khieu Samphan of genocide and crimes against humanity.
