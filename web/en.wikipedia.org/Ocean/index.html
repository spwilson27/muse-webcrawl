Ocean

The ocean is the body of salt water that covers approximately 70.8% of Earth's surface and contains 97% of Earth's water. In common usage, the planet's ocean is divided into several principal oceans: the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern (Antarctic), and Arctic Oceans. The ocean moderates Earth's climate, drives weather systems, and supports an enormous diversity of life.

The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest of Earth's oceanic divisions.

Physical characteristics

Ocean water has an average salinity of about 3.5%, dominated by sodium chloride. The ocean is vertically structured into zones by light and temperature: the sunlit euphotic zone (roughly the top 200 m) supports photosynthesis; below lie the twilight (mesopelagic), midnight (bathypelagic), abyssal, and hadal zones. The thermocline separates warm surface water from the cold deep ocean, most of which is near 0–3 °C.

Circulation

Wind-driven surface currents form large rotating gyres in each ocean basin, while the thermohaline circulation — driven by differences in temperature and salinity — moves deep water around the globe on timescales of centuries. Tides, caused mainly by the Moon's gravity, produce periodic rises and falls of sea level along coasts.

Life

Life is believed to have originated in the ocean more than 3.5 billion years ago. Marine habitats range from coral reefs and kelp forests to hydrothermal vents. Phytoplankton in the surface ocean produce a large share of Earth's oxygen through photosynthesis.

Human impact

The ocean absorbs much of the excess heat and about a quarter of the carbon dioxide generated by human activity, leading to ocean warming, acidification, and deoxygenation. Overfishing, plastic pollution, and coastal development further threaten marine ecosystems. The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea governs the use of the oceans.
