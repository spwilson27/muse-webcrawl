Shoe

Purpose: Foot protection and comfort
Parts: Sole, upper, heel, toe box
Materials: Leather, textiles, rubber, synthetics

A shoe is an item of footwear intended to protect and comfort the human foot. Shoes have been worn since prehistory — the oldest known examples, sagebrush bark sandals from Oregon, date to roughly 10,000 years ago — and over time they have also become items of fashion and status.

Construction

A typical shoe consists of a sole (including outsole, midsole and insole), an upper that holds the shoe to the foot, a heel, and fastening such as laces, straps or buckles. Traditional shoemaking centred on leather, while modern mass production uses textiles, rubber and synthetic materials.

Types

Shoes include athletic shoes (trainers/sneakers), boots, dress shoes such as Oxfords and loafers, sandals, high heels, and specialised footwear for occupations and sports. Sizing systems differ between countries, based on units such as the barleycorn in the UK and US or the Paris point in continental Europe.

Industry

Shoemaking was industrialised in the nineteenth century with machinery for lasting and stitching, and production is now global, with the majority of shoes manufactured in Asia.
