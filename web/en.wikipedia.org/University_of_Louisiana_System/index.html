The University of Louisiana System (UL System) is a public university system in the U.S. state of Louisiana. It is the largest higher education system in the state by enrolment, comprising nine universities coordinated by a Board of Supervisors and a system president headquartered in Baton Rouge. It is distinct from the Louisiana State University System and the Southern University System.

History

The system originated in 1974, when the new Louisiana Constitution created the Board of Trustees for State Colleges and Universities to govern most of the state's public four-year institutions outside the LSU and Southern systems. In 1998 the board adopted the name University of Louisiana System, and its institutions were permitted to rename themselves "University of Louisiana at" their city — a renaming first taken up by the universities at Lafayette and Monroe in 1999.

Member institutions

  • University of Louisiana at Monroe

Together the nine universities enrol more than 90,000 students and award a large share of Louisiana's bachelor's degrees, with particular strengths in nursing, education, engineering, and business.

Governance

The System is governed by a 16-member Board of Supervisors, 15 appointed by the governor and confirmed by the state senate, plus a student member. The board appoints the system president and each campus president (chancellor).
