Alexei Nikolaevich

Born: 12 August 1904, Peterhof, Russian Empire
Died: 17 July 1918, Yekaterinburg, Russia
Parents: Nicholas II and Alexandra Feodorovna
Title: Tsarevich (heir apparent) of Russia

Alexei Nikolaevich (12 August [O.S. 30 July] 1904 – 17 July 1918) was the last tsesarevich and heir apparent to the throne of the Russian Empire. He was the youngest child and only son of Emperor Nicholas II and Empress Alexandra. He was born with haemophilia, a condition his parents sought to treat through the influence of the mystic Grigori Rasputin — a reliance that badly damaged the monarchy's reputation.

Life

Alexei was born at Peterhof during the Russo-Japanese War and baptized amid national celebrations. His haemophilia, inherited from his mother (a granddaughter of Queen Victoria), emerged in infancy; minor injuries could cause life-threatening internal bleeding. Attacks at Spala in 1912, during which he nearly died, deepened his parents' dependence on Rasputin, whose calming presence seemed to aid the boy's recoveries. The secrecy surrounding the heir's illness fed rumour and scandal at court.

Revolution and death

After Nicholas II abdicated in March 1917 — renouncing the throne for Alexei as well as himself — the family was held under house arrest at Tsarskoe Selo, then exiled to Tobolsk and finally Yekaterinburg. On the night of 16–17 July 1918, Alexei, aged thirteen, was murdered with his parents and sisters by Bolshevik guards in the basement of the Ipatiev House.

Legacy

The family's remains, discovered near Yekaterinburg, were identified by DNA testing; two sets of remains found in 2007 were confirmed in 2008 to be Alexei and one of his sisters, closing claims by impostors such as Anna Anderson. The Russian Orthodox Church canonized Alexei and his family as passion bearers in 2000.
