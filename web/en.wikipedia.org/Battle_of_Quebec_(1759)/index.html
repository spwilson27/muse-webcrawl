The Battle of Quebec, also known as the Battle of the Plains of Abraham, was fought on 13 September 1759 during the Seven Years' War (the French and Indian War in North America). A British army under Major-General James Wolfe defeated the French forces of the Marquis de Montcalm outside Quebec City.

After a summer of frustration before the French defences along the Beauport shore, Wolfe landed his troops at night at Anse-au-Foulon and scaled the cliffs to reach the Plains of Abraham west of the city. Montcalm chose to give battle immediately rather than await reinforcements. The British regulars' disciplined volley fire broke the French advance in a fight lasting under an hour. Both commanders were mortally wounded: Wolfe died on the field, and Montcalm the following day.

Consequences

Quebec surrendered on 18 September. Although the French won a partial success at Sainte-Foy the following spring, British naval relief sealed the outcome, and Montreal capitulated in 1760. By the Treaty of Paris (1763) France ceded Canada to Britain, transforming the map of North America.
