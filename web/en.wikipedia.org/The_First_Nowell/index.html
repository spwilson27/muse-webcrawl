The First Nowell


The carol as printed in Carols Ancient and Modern (1823).

"The First Nowell", also known as "The First Noël", is a traditional English Christmas carol of Cornish origin. It recounts the story of the Nativity: the angel's announcement to the shepherds, the appearance of the Star of Bethlehem, and the visit of the Three Wise Men bearing gifts of gold, myrrh, and frankincense. Each verse ends with the refrain "Nowell, Nowell, Nowell, Nowell / Born is the King of Israel."

History

The word "Nowell" derives from the French Noël (Christmas), itself from the Latin natalis (birth). The carol is thought to date from the 16th or 17th century or earlier and may have begun as a Cornish song performed in church as a trope or processional piece; its melody in triple time is unlike most English folk tunes of its period. It first appeared in print in William Sandys's Christmas Carols Ancient and Modern (1833), having been included in Davies Gilbert's collection of 1822–23. The modern choral arrangement by John Stainer, published in 1871, standardized the nine-verse text in common use today.

Text

The carol begins: "The first Nowell the angel did say / Was to certain poor shepherds in fields as they lay; / In fields where they lay, keeping their sheep, / On a cold winter's night that was so deep." The narrative proceeds through the shepherds' journey to Bethlehem and the arrival of the Magi, concluding with an exhortation to praise God.

Recordings

The carol appears in standard collections such as the Oxford Book of Carols and Carols for Choirs, and the annual Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols at King's College, Cambridge. It has been recorded by numerous artists across classical, folk, and popular genres and is frequently sung in the form arranged by David Willcocks, with its soaring descant in the final verse.
