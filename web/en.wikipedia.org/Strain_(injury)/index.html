Strain (injury)

A strain is an acute or chronic injury to a muscle or tendon — the fibrous tissue connecting muscle to bone — caused by overstretching or overuse. It is commonly called a "pulled muscle". Strains are distinguished from sprains, which injure ligaments that connect bones to each other.

Signs and classification

Symptoms include sudden pain, muscle spasm, weakness, swelling and limited range of motion; a severe strain may produce bruising or a palpable gap in the muscle. Strains are graded by severity: grade I involves overstretching or micro-tears of a few fibres; grade II a partial tear; grade III a complete rupture, which may require surgical repair. Common sites are the hamstrings, the lower back, the calf (gastrocnemius) and the groin.

Causes and treatment

Risk factors include inadequate warm-up, fatigue, poor conditioning, previous injury and sudden eccentric loading, as in sprinting or lifting. Initial management follows the principles of protection, rest, ice, compression and elevation, followed by graded rehabilitation exercise to restore strength and flexibility. Imaging by ultrasound or MRI can confirm the grade when diagnosis is uncertain.
