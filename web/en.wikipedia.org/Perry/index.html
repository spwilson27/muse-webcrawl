Perry

Perry pears, from which the traditional fermented drink is made.

Perry is an alcoholic beverage made from fermented perry pears, similar to the way cider is made from apples. It has been common for centuries in England, particularly in the Three Counties (Gloucestershire, Herefordshire and Worcestershire), and in France it is produced in Lower Normandy. Perry may also refer to the surnames and places listed in indexes of articles.

Production

Perry pears are small, hard, astringent fruits, often inedible raw, belonging to distinctive cultivars such as Blakeney Red, Barland and Green Horse. The fruit is milled and pressed — traditionally through straw or hair cloths between oak boards — and the juice fermented slowly with wild or cultured yeasts. Like fine cider, traditional perry is a still or lightly sparkling drink of around 5–9% alcohol by volume; a single-variety, bottle-conditioned perry can be compared to a sparkling wine.

Modern perry

The mass-market pear-flavoured alcopop sold as "pear cider" in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries prompted renewed interest in, and definitional debates about, authentic perry made only from perry pears. The drink is protected within the Three Counties by a Protected Geographical Indication for "Gloucestershire Perry", "Herefordshire Perry" and "Worcestershire Perry".
