René II, Duke of Lorraine

René II
Duke of Lorraine: 1473–1508
Born: 2 May 1451, Angers
Died: 10 December 1508, Fains
Spouse: Philippa of Guelders

René II (2 May 1451 – 10 December 1508) was Duke of Lorraine from 1473 until his death. He is best known for defeating Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy, at the Battle of Nancy in 1477, in which Charles was killed.

Early life and accession

René was the son of Yolande of Anjou and Frederick II, Count of Vaudémont. Through his mother he inherited the Angevin claims, and in 1473 he succeeded his cousin Nicholas I as Duke of Lorraine, also becoming Count of Vaudémont on his father's death in 1470.

War with Burgundy

Charles the Bold occupied Lorraine in 1475 as part of his project to build a middle kingdom between France and the Empire. René allied with the Swiss Confederates and the Alsatian towns, recovered his duchy, and on 5 January 1477 won the decisive Battle of Nancy, securing Lorraine's independence from Burgundian absorption. He later pressed the Angevin claim to Naples without lasting success.
