Latimeria

Latimeria is a genus of coelacanth fish, famous as a "living fossil". Long thought extinct since the end of the Cretaceous, a living specimen was caught off South Africa in 1938 and identified by Marjorie Courtenay-Latimer and J. L. B. Smith.

Species

Two species are recognised: the West Indian Ocean coelacanth Latimeria chalumnae, found off the Comoros and South Africa, and the Indonesian coelacanth Latimeria menadoensis, discovered in 1997 off Sulawesi.

Biology

Latimeria are large, lobe-finned fish reaching about 2 m, living at depths of 150–700 m in submarine caves. They are live-bearers, slow-growing and long-lived, and are listed as critically endangered (L. chalumnae). As sarcopterygians they are more closely related to tetrapods than to most other fish.
