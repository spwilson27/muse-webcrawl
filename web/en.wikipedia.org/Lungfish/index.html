Lungfish

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Sarcopterygii
Subclass: Dipnoi

Lungfish are freshwater lobe-finned fishes belonging to the subclass Dipnoi. They are best known for retaining primitive characteristics, including the ability to breathe air, and are considered living fossils closely related to tetrapods.

Distribution

Six extant species survive: the Queensland lungfish in Australia, the South American lungfish, and four African species of Protopterus. African and South American lungfish can survive seasonal drought by aestivating in mud cocoons.

Anatomy

Lungfish possess one or two lungs derived from the swim bladder, elongated eel-like bodies in the African and South American forms, and fleshy paired fins supported by a single bone — a pattern homologous to tetrapod limbs.

Evolution

Dipnoans were diverse in the Devonian period. Genetic studies place lungfish as the closest living fish relatives of land vertebrates.
