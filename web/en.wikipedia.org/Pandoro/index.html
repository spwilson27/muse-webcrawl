Pandoro

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pandoro (Italian: [panˈdɔːro]) is a traditional Italian sweet bread, most popular around Christmas and New Year. Originating in Verona, it is made with a rich, eggy yeast dough and baked in a distinctive tall, eight-pointed star-shaped mould, giving the finished cake the form of a frustum with a star cross-section. It is traditionally served dusted with vanilla-scented icing sugar, said to evoke the snowy peaks of the Italian Alps in winter.

History

The name pandoro derives from pan d'oro, "golden bread", a reference to its golden colour from egg yolks, and possibly to rich breads served to Venetian nobility. The modern form is usually credited to the Verona pastry industry: in 1894 the pastry chef Domenico Melegatti obtained a patent for the soft, star-shaped cake and its mould, and his company industrialised its production. Together with Milan's panettone, pandoro is one of the two iconic Italian Christmas cakes.

Characteristics

Unlike panettone, pandoro contains no candied fruit or raisins; its crumb is soft, buttery and golden, flavoured with vanilla and lemon zest. When sliced horizontally, the pieces form star shapes, and the cake is sometimes cut and layered with cream or mascarpone. A packet of powdered sugar is customarily included in the box to be shaken over the cake before serving.
