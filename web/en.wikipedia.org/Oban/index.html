Oban (Scottish Gaelic: An t-Òban, meaning "the little bay") is a resort town in Argyll and Bute, on the west coast of Scotland. Despite its small size it is one of the busiest ferry ports in Scotland, serving as the gateway to the Inner and Outer Hebrides, with Caledonian MacBrayne ferries to Mull, Colonsay, Lismore, Barra and other islands.

History and landmarks

The town grew in the 19th century around its harbour, distillery (founded 1794) and the arrival of the railway in 1880. Above the town stands McCaig's Tower, an unfinished granite folly modelled on the Colosseum, built from 1897 by banker John Stuart McCaig. Dunollie Castle, seat of the MacDougall clan, overlooks the northern entrance to the bay.

Economy

Tourism, fishing and ferry traffic dominate the economy; Oban styles itself the "Seafood Capital of Scotland." The town is also a centre for marine science, hosting the Scottish Association for Marine Science at nearby Dunstaffnage.
