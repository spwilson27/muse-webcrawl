Disambiguation

Disambiguation is the process of resolving ambiguity — determining which meaning of a word or expression is intended when the same term refers to more than one thing. The term is widely used in linguistics, information retrieval, and reference works.

On Wikipedia

On Wikipedia, a disambiguation page is a non-article page that lists articles associated with a given title, helping readers find the topic they want when a name could refer to several subjects (for example, a place, a person, and a film sharing one name). Such pages are reached from the ambiguous title itself or via a hatnote on the article that occupies the primary title. Disambiguation complements the principle of using natural, recognisable article titles.

Word-sense disambiguation

In computational linguistics and natural language processing, word-sense disambiguation (WSD) is the task of identifying which sense of a polysemous word is used in a given context. Classic approaches include dictionary-based methods such as the Lesk algorithm and, more recently, supervised machine-learning and neural methods using contextual embeddings. WSD underlies applications such as machine translation, information retrieval, and question answering.

Other uses

In entity linking and knowledge bases, disambiguation assigns mentions in text to unique entities. Librarians and indexers similarly resolve ambiguous headings in catalogues.
