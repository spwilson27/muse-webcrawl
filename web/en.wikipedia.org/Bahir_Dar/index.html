Bahir Dar

Bahir Dar
Region: Amhara, Ethiopia
Elevation: ~1,800 m
Population: ~300,000+

Bahir Dar is a city in northwestern Ethiopia and the capital of the Amhara Region. It lies on the southern shore of Lake Tana, the largest lake in Ethiopia and the source of the Blue Nile, at the point where the river leaves the lake.

Geography

The city sits at an elevation of about 1,800 metres. Some 30 km to the southeast are the Tis Issat Falls, where the Blue Nile drops over a cliff — among Ethiopia's best-known tourist attractions. Lake Tana's islands hold historic Ethiopian Orthodox monasteries, some dating to the 14th century, housing illuminated manuscripts and religious treasures.

History

Bahir Dar grew around a Jesuit mission established in the early 17th century. It developed as a trading and administrative centre during the 20th century and was rebuilt with wide, palm-lined avenues under Italian occupation in the late 1930s. Emperor Haile Selassie built a palace overlooking the lake and at one point considered moving the capital here.

Economy and tourism

The city is a base for visiting Lake Tana's monasteries and the Blue Nile Falls, and hosts Bahir Dar University. Its airport connects it to Addis Ababa, and textile and agro-processing industries operate in the area.
