Pennsylvania Avenue

Pennsylvania Avenue is a diagonal avenue in Washington, D.C., and Prince George's County, Maryland, famous as the route connecting the White House with the United States Capitol. The section between the White House and the Capitol is often called "America's Main Street" and is the traditional route of presidential inaugural parades, state funerals, and public demonstrations.

History

The avenue was laid out in Pierre L'Enfant's 1791 plan for the federal city and named for the state of Pennsylvania. It was one of the earliest streets developed in Washington, and Thomas Jefferson had it planted with Lombardy poplars. By the nineteenth century it was the city's principal commercial street; the stretch nearest the Capitol was redeveloped in the twentieth century by the Pennsylvania Avenue Development Corporation, created in 1972, which produced the present mix of federal buildings, offices, Memorial parks, and the National Gallery of Art's surroundings.

Route

Northwest Pennsylvania Avenue runs from the Capitol grounds to the White House, continuing to Georgetown, while Southeast Pennsylvania Avenue extends from the Capitol across the Anacostia River into Maryland, where it continues as Maryland Route 4 toward Upper Marlboro. Landmarks along the route include the National Archives, the Department of Justice, the Federal Trade Commission, the Canadian Embassy, and Freedom Plaza.
