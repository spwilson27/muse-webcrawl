Victor Emmanuel II of Italy

Victor Emmanuel II (Italian: Vittorio Emanuele II; 14 March 1820 – 9 January 1878) was King of Sardinia from 23 March 1849 until 17 March 1861, when he assumed the title of King of Italy and became the first king of a united Italy since the 6th century. Italians gave him the epithet "Padre della Patria" (Father of the Fatherland).

Reign

Succeeding his father Charles Albert after defeat at the Battle of Novara, Victor Emmanuel preserved the liberal Statuto Albertino constitution. With his prime minister, the Count of Cavour, he pursued the unification of Italy (Risorgimento): alliance with France against Austria in 1859, Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand in 1860, the annexation of Venetia in 1866, and the capture of Rome in 1870, which he made the capital.

Legacy

The enormous Victor Emmanuel II Monument (Altare della Patria) in Rome commemorates him as the symbol of national unity.
