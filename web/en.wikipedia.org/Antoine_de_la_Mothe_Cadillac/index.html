Antoine Laumet de La Mothe, sieur de Cadillac (5 March 1658 – 16 October 1730) was a French explorer and adventurer in New France, northern North America. He was the founder of Detroit, Michigan, in 1701, where he established Fort Pontchartrain du Détroit, and he later served as governor of French Louisiana.

Career in New France

Arriving in Acadia around 1683, Cadillac (born Antoine Laumet) assumed a noble title and rose through colonial service, commanding Fort de Buade at Michilimackinac in the 1690s. Persuading the minister Pontchartrain of the strategic value of the strait between Lakes Erie and Huron, he founded Detroit in July 1701 to block English expansion and control the fur trade. His administration was controversial; accusations of profiteering and abuse of power led to his transfer.

Louisiana and later life

In 1710 Cadillac was appointed governor of Louisiana, arriving in 1713. He failed to develop profitable trade or mines and quarrelled with the colony's concessionaire, Antoine Crozat, and was recalled to France in 1716. After a spell of imprisonment in the Bastille he returned to his native Gascony, where he died in 1730.

Legacy

The Cadillac automobile brand, founded in Detroit in 1902, took its name and crest from him, and places including Cadillac Mountain in Maine and the town of Cadillac, Michigan, bear his name.
