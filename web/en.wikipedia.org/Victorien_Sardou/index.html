Victorien Sardou

Victorien Sardou (5 September 1831 – 8 November 1908) was a French dramatist. He is best remembered today for his development of the well-made play and for works such as Madame Sans-Gêne, La Tosca — the basis for Puccini's opera Tosca — and Fédora, written for Sarah Bernhardt.

Career

Born in Paris, Sardou initially studied medicine before turning to theatre. After early struggles and poverty, his comedy Les Pattes de mouche (1860) established his reputation. He became one of the most successful playwrights of the Belle Époque, known for meticulous plotting, historical dramas and satires of contemporary society.

He was elected to the Académie française in 1877. George Bernard Shaw coined the term "Sardoodledom" to mock his melodramatic contrivances, though his craftsmanship influenced many later dramatists and librettists.
