Dhauli is a hill and historic site on the banks of the Daya River, about 8 kilometres south of Bhubaneswar in Odisha, India. It is traditionally associated with the aftermath of the Kalinga War (c. 261 BCE), the bloody conquest that is said to have moved the Mauryan emperor Ashoka to embrace Buddhism and non-violence.

Ashokan edicts

Dhauli preserves one of Ashoka's major rock edict sites: two special edicts addressed to the officers of the newly conquered Kalinga province are inscribed on a rock face, replacing several of the standard major edicts found elsewhere. At the top of the inscribed rock is a celebrated early sculpture of the forepart of an elephant, among the oldest monumental sculptures in India, apparently emerging from the stone.

Shanti Stupa

In 1972 a Buddhist peace pagoda, the Shanti Stupa, was built on the hill by the Japanese Buddhist order Nipponzan-Myōhōji in collaboration with the Kalinga Nippon Buddha Sangha. Its white dome, lion statues, and reliefs illustrating the life of the Buddha and Ashoka's transformation overlook the plain of the Daya River.

Tradition

Local tradition holds that the waters of the Daya ran red with blood after the Kalinga battle, and that Ashoka, surveying the carnage from Dhauli, resolved to abandon conquest by force for "conquest by dharma." The site is now a popular pilgrimage and tourist destination near Bhubaneswar.
