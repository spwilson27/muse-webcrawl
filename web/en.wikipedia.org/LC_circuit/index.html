LC circuit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An LC circuit, also called a resonant circuit, tank circuit, or tuned circuit, is an electric circuit consisting of an inductor, represented by the letter L, and a capacitor, represented by the letter C, connected together. The circuit can act as an electrical resonator, an electrical analogue of a tuning fork, storing energy that oscillates at the circuit's resonant frequency.

Operation

Energy in the circuit sloshes back and forth between the electric field of the capacitor and the magnetic field of the inductor. In an ideal, lossless circuit the oscillation continues indefinitely; in practice, resistance (an RLC circuit) makes it decay. The resonant frequency is f = 1 / (2π√(LC)) hertz, where L is inductance in henries and C is capacitance in farads.

Applications

LC circuits are fundamental to radio technology: they select a narrow band of frequencies from a wider signal in tuners, act as filters, and form the frequency-determining element of oscillators. They are used in induction heating, RFID tags, oscillators, frequency mixers and impedance-matching networks.
