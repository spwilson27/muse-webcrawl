Shallot

The shallot is a culinary cultivar group of the onion (Allium cepa).

The shallot is a cultivar group of the onion. Until 2010, the (French red) shallot was classified as a separate species, Allium ascalonicum. The taxon was synonymised with Allium cepa (the common onion) in 2010, as the genetic difference between the two is slight.

Description

Shallots are grown from a bulb that divides into a cluster of smaller bulbs, or offsets, each with a thin coppery or greyish skin. Their flavour is milder, sweeter and more delicate than that of the common onion, with a hint of garlic, which makes them prized in fine cooking, especially in French cuisine, where finely minced shallots are a base for sauces such as beurre blanc and for vinaigrettes. In Southeast Asian cuisines, shallots are a fundamental ingredient, often fried crisp as a garnish.

Varieties and cultivation

Important types include the French red shallot, the grey shallot (griselle), and the banana shallot, an elongated cross between a shallot and an onion. True shallots are traditionally propagated vegetatively from bulbs rather than seed. They are thought to originate in Central or Southwest Asia, and the name derives from Ashkelon, the ancient city in the southern Levant — hence the former species name ascalonicum.
