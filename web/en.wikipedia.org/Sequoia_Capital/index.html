Sequoia Capital is an American venture capital firm headquartered in Menlo Park, California, on Sand Hill Road. Founded in 1972 by Don Valentine, it is widely regarded as one of the most successful venture firms in history, having backed many of the defining companies of the technology industry at early stages.

History

Don Valentine, a former sales and marketing executive at National Semiconductor and Fairchild Semiconductor, founded Sequoia in 1972 with a $3 million first fund. The firm took its name from the giant sequoia trees of California. Sequoia's early investments included Atari (1976) and Apple Computer (1978), where Valentine was an early backer of Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak. In 1974 Sequoia backed Tandem Computers, and in 1987 it led the founding investment in Cisco Systems.

Investments

Sequoia's portfolio has included Apple, Cisco, Oracle, Google, PayPal, YouTube, Instagram, WhatsApp, Airbnb, Stripe, DoorDash, Snowflake, Zoom, Nvidia (through an early investment), LinkedIn, Yahoo!, Klarna, ByteDance and OpenAI. The firm invests across stages, from seed to growth, and historically operated regional funds in China, India and Southeast Asia; in 2023 the China and India/Southeast Asia operations were split into independent firms (HongShan and Peak XV Partners, respectively).

RIP Good Times memo

Sequoia is also known for its widely circulated 2008 presentation "RIP Good Times", which warned portfolio companies about the financial crisis and urged them to cut costs and extend runways — an episode often cited as emblematic of the firm's role as an adviser to founders, not just a financier. In 2022, Sequoia restructured its U.S./Europe funds into an open-ended "evergreen" structure, allowing it to hold public shares longer.
