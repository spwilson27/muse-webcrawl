Haitian cuisine

Haitian cuisine consists of cooking traditions and practices from Haiti. It is a Creole cuisine that originates from a blend of several culinary styles that populated the western portion of the island of Hispaniola, namely African, French, indigenous Taíno, Spanish and Arab influences. Haitian cuisine is comparable to that of the rest of the Latin-Caribbean, though it differs in several ways from its regional counterparts.

Characteristics

Flavour is bold and spicy, anchored by epis, a blended seasoning base of herbs, peppers, garlic, scallions, and citrus that is used to marinate meats and flavour stews. Scotch bonnet peppers provide heat, and sour orange juice is a common marinade. Rice and beans — diri ak pwa — is a national staple, with regional variations such as diri djon djon, rice cooked with black mushrooms in the north.

Popular dishes

  • Griot – cubed pork shoulder marinated in citrus, boiled, and fried, typically served with pikliz.
  • Pikliz – a spicy pickled vegetable relish of cabbage, carrots, and Scotch bonnet peppers.
  • Soup joumou – a squash soup traditionally eaten on January 1, Haitian Independence Day; it was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2021.
  • Tassot – fried goat or beef.
  • Legim – a hearty vegetable stew.
  • Accra – fritters made from malanga root.
  • Mayi moulen – cornmeal, often served with bean sauce (sos pwa).

Beverages

Haiti is known for its rum, notably Rhum Barbancourt, and for clairin, an unaged cane spirit produced by hundreds of small distilleries. Kremas, a sweet creamy alcoholic drink made with coconut and condensed milk, is popular at holidays.
