Bench warrants are issued by a judge from the bench.

A bench warrant is a process issued by a court or judge ordering the arrest of a person and their production before the court. The name derives from the judge's bench: unlike an arrest warrant sought by police on suspicion of a crime, a bench warrant typically issues on the court's own initiative.

Grounds

The most common ground is failure to appear for a scheduled court date, but bench warrants also issue for contempt of court, failure to pay fines or child support, violation of probation, or a grand jury indictment. In some jurisdictions the equivalent order is called a capias.

Consequences

A person subject to a bench warrant may be arrested at any encounter with law enforcement, such as a traffic stop, and held until a hearing. Warrants are commonly cleared by voluntarily surrendering to the court, posting bail, and rescheduling the missed proceeding, sometimes with additional penalties for the failure to appear.
