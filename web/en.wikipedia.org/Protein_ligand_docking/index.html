Protein ligand docking

Protein–ligand docking is a computational technique that predicts the preferred orientation and binding mode of a small molecule (the ligand) when bound to a protein target, forming a stable complex. It is a central method of structure-based drug design, used to screen compound libraries and estimate binding affinity before laboratory testing.

Approach

A docking calculation has two main components: a search algorithm that explores possible positions, orientations, and conformations of the ligand within the protein's binding site, and a scoring function that ranks each pose by its estimated binding energy. Common programs include AutoDock, AutoDock Vina, GOLD, and Glide.

Types

  • Rigid docking — both partners treated as rigid bodies
  • Flexible ligand docking — ligand bonds may rotate while the protein is held fixed
  • Flexible docking — selected protein side chains or regions also move, approximating induced fit

Applications and limits

Docking enables virtual screening of millions of compounds and helps rationalize how known drugs bind their targets. Accuracy is limited by the treatment of solvent, protein flexibility, and the approximations in scoring functions, so docking predictions are normally followed by experimental validation and methods such as molecular dynamics.
