Phalaenopsis

Phalaenopsis, commonly known as moth orchids, is a genus of about seventy species of orchids native to Asia, Southeast Asia, and northern Australia. They are among the most popular orchids in cultivation because of their long-lasting, arching sprays of flowers and their tolerance of indoor conditions.

PhalaenopsisFamily: Orchidaceae
Native range: Asia to Australia
Common name: Moth orchid
Type: Mostly epiphytic

Description

Most species are epiphytes, growing on trees with thick, fleshy leaves and aerial roots covered in spongy velamen. The flowers, which in wild species are often white or pink, can remain open for several months. The genus name, given by Carl Ludwig Blume in 1825, derives from the Greek phalaina (moth), reflecting the resemblance of some flowers to large moths in flight.

Cultivation

Hybrid Phalaenopsis are mass-produced as houseplants and potted gifts worldwide. They prefer bright indirect light, warm temperatures, and a coarse bark medium, and can be induced to rebloom by a period of cooler night temperatures.
