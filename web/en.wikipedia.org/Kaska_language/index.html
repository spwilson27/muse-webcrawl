Kaska language

Kaska, also known as Dene Keh and Nahane, is a Northern Athabaskan language spoken by the Kaska Dena people in the southeastern Yukon and northern British Columbia, Canada, in communities including Ross River, Watson Lake, Upper Liard, Good Hope Lake, Lower Post and Dease River.

Status

Kaska is severely endangered. Most fluent speakers are elderly, and the number of speakers is estimated in the low hundreds or fewer out of an ethnic population of over a thousand. Language revitalisation efforts include documentation projects, community language courses, and teaching materials developed with the Yukon Native Language Centre and local First Nations governments such as the Liard First Nation and the Ross River Dena Council.

Dialects and relatives

Kaska forms part of a dialect continuum with the closely related Tahltan and Tagish languages, sometimes grouped together as Tahltan–Kaska. Its orthography uses an alphabet based on Latin script with diacritics marking nasalisation and tone; Kaska is a tonal language with a complex verb morphology typical of Athabaskan languages, including a classificatory verb system.
