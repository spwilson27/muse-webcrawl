Music of Senegal

Sabar drummers in Dakar

The music of Senegal is among the best known in West Africa, shaped by the griot tradition of hereditary praise-singers and drummers, the sabar drum ensembles of the Wolof people, and, since the 1970s, the internationally successful dance music known as mbalax.

Traditional music

Griots (géwël in Wolof) preserve genealogies and histories through song, accompanied by instruments such as the kora (a 21-string harp-lute), the xalam, and the balafon. Sabar drumming accompanies wrestling matches, weddings, and dance events, with intricate rhythms "spoken" on drums of different sizes.

Mbalax and modern music

Mbalax blends sabar percussion with Western instruments and influences from Cuban music, jazz, and funk. It was popularised above all by Youssou N'Dour and his band Étoile de Dakar / Super Étoile, who achieved worldwide fame from the 1980s. Other major figures include Baaba Maal, who sings in Pulaar and fuses folk with pop, and the hip-hop scene of Dakar, which became a significant voice in Senegalese politics and youth culture. Akon, raised partly in Senegal, carried its influence into global pop.
