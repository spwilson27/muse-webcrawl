Helen Frankenthaler

Helen Frankenthaler (December 12, 1928 – December 27, 2011) was an American abstract expressionist painter and a leading figure of post-war American art. Her career spanned six decades, and she is credited with pioneering the "soak-stain" technique that helped launch the Color Field movement.

Mountains and Sea and soak-stain

After graduating from Bennington College, where she studied with Paul Feeley, Frankenthaler settled in New York and was championed by the critic Clement Greenberg. In 1952 she painted Mountains and Sea, her breakthrough work, by pouring thinned paint onto raw, unprimed canvas so that the colour soaked into the fabric itself, creating luminous veils of colour with no visible brushwork. The painting deeply impressed Morris Louis and Kenneth Noland, who adopted and developed the method and became central figures of Color Field painting in Washington, D.C.

Later work and recognition

From the 1960s Frankenthaler worked with acrylic paints, achieving denser, brighter fields of colour, and she became an innovative printmaker, notably in woodcut. Her work was the subject of major retrospectives at the Whitney Museum and the Museum of Modern Art, and in 2001 she received the National Medal of Arts. She died in Darien, Connecticut, in 2011, at the age of 83.
