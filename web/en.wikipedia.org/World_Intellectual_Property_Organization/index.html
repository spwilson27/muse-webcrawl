The World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) is one of the 15 specialised agencies of the United Nations, dedicated to promoting the protection of intellectual property throughout the world. It was established by a convention signed in Stockholm in 1967 and became a UN specialised agency in 1974.

History and mandate

WIPO traces its origins to the bureaux administering the Paris Convention (1883) for industrial property and the Berne Convention (1886) for copyright, which merged in 1893. Today WIPO administers 26 international treaties covering patents, trademarks, designs, copyright and related rights, and promotes harmonisation of national IP laws.

Services

The agency operates global filing systems that let applicants seek protection in many countries with a single application: the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) for patents, the Madrid System for trademarks and the Hague System for industrial designs. It also runs an Arbitration and Mediation Center, which handles domain-name disputes under the UDRP, and publishes global IP statistics and indices.
