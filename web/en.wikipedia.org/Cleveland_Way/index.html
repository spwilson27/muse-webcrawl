The Cleveland Way is a National Trail in ancient Cleveland in North Yorkshire, England. It runs 109 miles (175 km) in a horseshoe from Helmsley, across the North York Moors and along the North Yorkshire and Cleveland Heritage Coast to finish at Filey. It was opened on 24 May 1969, the second National Trail to be designated in England and Wales.

Route

From Helmsley the trail crosses the southern edge of the moors past Rievaulx Abbey and Sutton Bank, then follows the Hambleton Hills and the Cleveland Hills escarpment through Osmotherley and past the Wain Stones to Guisborough. Beyond Saltburn-by-the-Sea it turns to the coast, following the cliffs south through Staithes, Runswick Bay, Whitby, Robin Hood's Bay and Scarborough to Filey Brigg. The inland moorland half and the coastal half offer a celebrated contrast of heather upland and cliff scenery.

Points of interest

Sights along the way include Rievaulx Abbey, the Kilburn White Horse, Mount Grace Priory, Roseberry Topping, the fishing villages of the heritage coast, Whitby Abbey, and Scarborough Castle. The route links at Whitby with Wainwright's Coast to Coast Walk, and at Filey with the Yorkshire Wolds Way.

Walking the trail

Most walkers complete the trail in about nine days. The terrain is moderate, with the stiffest climbs on the coastal cliffs and the ascent of Roseberry Topping. Waymarking uses the National Trail acorn symbol throughout.
