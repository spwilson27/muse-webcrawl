Alternative stable state

In ecology, alternative stable states (or alternative stable equilibria) are configurations in which an ecosystem can persist under the same environmental conditions, with the state it occupies depending on its history. A sufficiently large disturbance can push the system across a threshold into a different state, and the change may be difficult or impossible to reverse — a phenomenon known as hysteresis.

Examples

The best-documented examples come from shallow lakes, which can exist either in a clear-water state dominated by submerged plants or a turbid state dominated by phytoplankton. Similar regime shifts have been proposed for coral reefs (coral- versus algae-dominated), kelp forests, savannas, and rangelands.

Implications

Alternative stable states complicate ecosystem management: reducing the stressor that caused a shift may not restore the original state, because the new state is stabilized by internal feedbacks.
