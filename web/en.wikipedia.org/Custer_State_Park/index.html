Custer State Park

Custer State Park is a South Dakota state park and wildlife reserve in the Black Hills, United States. The park covers 71,000 acres (287 square kilometres) of varied terrain including rolling prairie grasslands and rugged granite mountains. It is named for Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer and was established in 1912.

Wildlife

The park is home to a herd of roughly 1,500 free-roaming American bison, one of the largest public herds in the world. Each autumn the park stages a Buffalo Roundup, in which the herd is gathered and surplus animals are auctioned to keep the population in balance with the available forage. Other wildlife includes elk, mule deer, white-tailed deer, mountain goats, bighorn sheep, pronghorn, mountain lions, and a band of feral burros descended from pack animals, known for approaching visitors' cars along the Wildlife Loop Road.

Scenic drives

The Needles Highway and Iron Mountain Road are noted for their pigtail bridges, single-lane tunnels, and views of granite spires and of Mount Rushmore. Sylvan Lake, created in 1881 by a dam on Sunday Gulch Creek, is a popular swimming and climbing area.

History

The area was originally part of the Custer State Forest. Governor Peter Norbeck championed the park's creation and helped design its scenic roads to harmonise with the landscape. The State Game Lodge served as the summer White House for President Calvin Coolidge in 1927.
