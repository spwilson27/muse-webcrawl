Deafness

Deafness is the partial or complete inability to hear. Hearing loss may be present at birth (congenital) or acquired at any time in life, may affect one or both ears, and ranges in severity from mild difficulty understanding speech to profound deafness, in which even very loud sounds are not detected. According to the World Health Organization, hundreds of millions of people worldwide live with disabling hearing loss, making it one of the most common sensory impairments.

Types and causes

Conductive hearing loss results from problems conducting sound through the outer or middle ear, such as earwax blockage, ear infections, or damage to the ossicles. Sensorineural hearing loss results from damage to the hair cells of the cochlea or to the auditory nerve; common causes include ageing (presbycusis), prolonged exposure to loud noise, certain infections and genetic conditions. Mixed losses combine both mechanisms.

Management and culture

Treatment depends on the cause: conductive losses can often be corrected medically or surgically, while sensorineural losses are usually managed with hearing aids or, in severe cases, cochlear implants. Many deaf people communicate through sign languages and identify with Deaf culture, regarding deafness as a difference rather than solely a medical condition. Preventive measures include vaccination, reducing occupational and recreational noise exposure, and prompt treatment of ear infections; newborn hearing screening allows early intervention that greatly improves language outcomes.
