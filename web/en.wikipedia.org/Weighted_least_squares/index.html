Weighted least squares

Weighted least squares (WLS) is a method of estimating the parameters of a regression model in which observations are given different weights, typically inversely proportional to the variance of each observation. It generalises ordinary least squares (OLS), in which all weights are equal.

Formulation

Given observations y = Xβ + ε, WLS minimises the weighted sum of squared residuals Σ wᵢ(yᵢ − xᵢβ)². With weights wᵢ = 1/σᵢ², the estimator is β̂ = (XᵀWX)⁻¹XᵀWy, where W is the diagonal matrix of weights. When the error variances are known up to a constant, this estimator is the best linear unbiased estimator under the Gauss–Markov assumptions extended to heteroscedastic errors.

Applications

WLS is used when data have unequal precision: combining measurements from different instruments, meta-analysis (weighting studies by inverse variance), survey sampling, and fitting models to grouped data. Feasible generalised least squares estimates the weights from the data when variances are unknown.
