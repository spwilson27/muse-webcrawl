2060 Chiron is a small Solar System body orbiting between Saturn and Uranus. Discovered by Charles Kowal in 1977, it was the first object identified as a centaur — a class of icy bodies on unstable orbits among the giant planets. It is named after the centaur Chiron of Greek mythology.

Dual nature

Chiron behaves as both an asteroid and a comet: in 1988 it unexpectedly brightened and developed a coma, showing that it contains volatile ices that sublimate when it nears the Sun. It therefore holds the dual designation 95P/Chiron as a comet as well as its minor-planet number 2060. At roughly 218 kilometres across, it is one of the largest known centaurs.

Rings

Observations of stellar occultations, first in 2011 and analysed in 2015, indicated that Chiron is probably surrounded by a system of two narrow rings, making it one of the few small bodies known to have rings. Its orbit is chaotic on million-year timescales, and centaurs like Chiron are thought to be former Kuiper belt objects that may eventually become short-period comets.
