War in North-West Pakistan

The War in North-West Pakistan, also known as the War in Waziristan, was an armed conflict between the Pakistan Armed Forces and armed militant groups such as the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP), al-Qaeda, and their foreign and local allies in the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province along the Afghan border. The conflict is generally dated from 2004, when tensions rooted in the army's search for al-Qaeda fighters in Waziristan's mountains escalated into armed resistance.

Course of the conflict

After early peace agreements failed, the conflict intensified with the 2007 siege of the Lal Masjid in Islamabad and the formation of the TTP under Baitullah Mehsud. Major Pakistani operations included Operation Rah-e-Rast in Swat (2009), Operation Rah-e-Nijat in South Waziristan (2009), and Operation Zarb-e-Azb, launched in North Waziristan in June 2014 after the attack on Karachi's Jinnah International Airport. The December 2014 massacre of schoolchildren at the Army Public School in Peshawar, in which some 149 people were killed, was a turning point, prompting a national counter-terrorism plan and a sharp decline in militant attacks in subsequent years.

Impact

The war displaced millions of people from the tribal districts at its height and caused tens of thousands of deaths among combatants and civilians. The United States conducted a controversial campaign of drone strikes in the region. In 2018 the FATA were merged into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province.
