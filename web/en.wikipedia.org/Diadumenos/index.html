Diadumenos

The Diadumenos ("diadem-bearer"), together with the Doryphoros, is one of the two most famous figural types of the Greek sculptor Polykleitos, forming a basic pattern of Ancient Greek sculpture that all present strictly idealised representations of young male athletes in a convincingly naturalistic manner.

The lost bronze original, made about 440-430 BC, showed a young athlete tying a fillet (diadem) around his head after a victory. Polykleitos ascribed the work in his treatise, the Canon, as a demonstration of his system of ideal proportions (symmetria), in which the parts of the body relate to one another in harmonious ratios.

Copies

The Diadumenos was widely copied in Roman times. The best-known marble copies include the example from Delos (now in the National Archaeological Museum of Athens) and the Farnese Diadumenos in the British Museum. The pose is closely related to, but more animated than, the Doryphoros: the weight leg and raised arms create a chiastic balance characteristic of Polykleitan contrapposto.
