Sati (Sanskrit: सती, Satī), also known as Dakshayani, is a Hindu goddess of marital felicity and longevity. In Hindu mythology she is the first consort of the god Shiva and the daughter of the priest-king Daksha. Sati is famous for her self-immolation at her father's sacrificial ceremony, an act of protest against his insult to her husband; she was later reborn as the goddess Parvati, who again married Shiva.

Legend

According to the Puranas, particularly the Shiva Purana and the Bhagavata Purana, Sati loved Shiva from childhood and won him through intense ascetic devotion, despite her father's disapproval. After their marriage, Daksha organized a great yajna (sacrifice) to which he deliberately did not invite Shiva. Sati attended against Shiva's advice and, humiliated by Daksha's public insults of her husband, invoked her yogic powers and immolated herself in the sacrificial fire.

Enraged, Shiva created the fierce beings Virabhadra and Bhadrakali, who destroyed Daksha's sacrifice and beheaded Daksha (he was later restored with a goat's head). Shiva then wandered the universe carrying Sati's corpse; to restore his equanimity, Vishnu dismembered the body, and the places where her body parts fell became the sacred Shakti Peethas.

Relationship to the sati practice

The goddess's name and story were later invoked in connection with sati, the historical practice in which a widow immolated herself on her husband's funeral pyre. Scholars note that the goddess Sati was not a widow—she died before her husband and in protest at her father's conduct—and debate how directly the myth legitimized the later custom, which was abolished in British India in 1829 and criminalized in modern India.
