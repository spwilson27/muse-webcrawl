Fort McMurray

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort McMurray is an urban service area in the Regional Municipality of Wood Buffalo in Alberta, Canada. It is located in northeastern Alberta, in the midst of the Athabasca oil sands, surrounded by boreal forest at the confluence of the Athabasca and Clearwater rivers. Its population, including a large "shadow population" of temporary workers, has fluctuated with the fortunes of the oil industry; the 2021 census recorded about 68,000 residents in the urban service area.

History

The area was long inhabited by Cree and Dene peoples. A North West Company fur trade post was established in 1790, and the Hudson's Bay Company post, named for William McMurray, dates to 1870. Fort McMurray remained a small transportation and trading centre — connected by rail in the 1920s and serving river traffic north — until commercial oil sands development transformed it from the 1960s, with the Great Canadian Oil Sands (later Suncor) plant opening in 1967 and Syncrude in 1978.

Oil sands economy

The city is the service centre for the Athabasca oil sands, among the world's largest petroleum reserves. Boom-and-bust cycles tied to oil prices have driven rapid population growth, high wages, housing shortages, and extensive work camps served by fly-in/fly-out labour.

2016 wildfire

In May 2016 the Horse River wildfire forced the evacuation of the entire city, some 88,000 people — the largest wildfire evacuation in Canadian history. The fire destroyed about 2,400 homes and buildings, roughly ten percent of the city, and disrupted oil sands production. Residents returned beginning in June 2016, and rebuilding continued for years. In 2020 the city also suffered a major flood from ice jams on the Athabasca River.
