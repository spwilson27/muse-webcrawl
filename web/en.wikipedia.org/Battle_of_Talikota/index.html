Battle of Talikota

Date23 January 1565
LocationNear Talikota, Deccan, India (present-day Karnataka)
ResultDecisive Deccan Sultanates victory
BelligerentsDeccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar) vs. Vijayanagara Empire
CommandersAli Adil Shah I; Hussain Nizam Shah I vs. Aliya Rama Raya (killed)

The Battle of Talikota, fought on 23 January 1565, was a watershed battle in the history of South India. A confederacy of the Deccan Sultanates inflicted a catastrophic defeat on the Vijayanagara Empire, then the dominant power of the southern Deccan. The battle ended with the capture and execution of the Vijayanagara regent Aliya Rama Raya and was followed by the sack and ruin of the great city of Vijayanagara (Hampi).

The plain near the Krishna river, where the fate of Vijayanagara was decided.

Background

Vijayanagara's power under the regent Rama Raya rested on aggressive interference in the quarrels of the Deccan Sultanates, playing Bijapur, Golconda and Ahmadnagar against one another. Resentment at this diplomacy, and at perceived insults to Islam, united the normally rival sultanates in a rare confederacy sealed by marriage alliances. Their combined armies marched south and crossed the Krishna river near Talikota.

The battle

The Vijayanagara army was enormous, with large contingents under Rama Raya's brothers Tirumala and Venkatadri. The fighting was hard and at first evenly matched. The turning point came when Rama Raya, an elderly man directing the battle from a litter, was captured by enemy troops (in some accounts after his litter-bearers fled) and brought before Hussain Nizam Shah, who had him beheaded on the spot. The sight of their leader's head displayed on a spear threw the Vijayanagara army into panic and rout, and the pursuit became a massacre.

Aftermath

The victorious sultans advanced on Vijayanagara itself. The capital, one of the largest cities in the world at the time, was systematically sacked and left in ruins — the celebrated ruins at Hampi today. The empire survived in diminished form under the Aravidu dynasty at Penukonda and later Chandragiri, but its era of supremacy in the south was over, altering the political balance of India for good.
