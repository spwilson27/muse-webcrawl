An orchestra is a large instrumental ensemble typical of classical music, which combines instruments from different families. There are typically four main sections of instruments: bowed string instruments, woodwinds, brass instruments and percussion instruments, led by a timpanist. The orchestra generally is led by a conductor who directs the ensemble with movements of the hands and arms, often made easier by the use of a conductor's baton.

The term orchestra derives from the Greek ὀρχήστρα (orchestra), the part of a Greek theatre where the chorus sang and danced. A full-size Western orchestra may sometimes be called a symphony orchestra or philharmonic orchestra.

Instrumentation

The modern symphony orchestra's core is the string section (violins I and II, violas, cellos, double basses), typically comprising more than half the players. Woodwinds (flutes, oboes, clarinets, bassoons) usually appear in pairs or triples; brass comprises horns, trumpets, trombones and tuba; and percussion is headed by timpani. Keyboard instruments and harp are added for particular works.

History

Orchestras developed during the Baroque era around ensembles of strings at courts such as Mannheim, whose disciplined playing set new standards. Joseph Haydn established the classical orchestra's proportions, and through the 19th century — with Beethoven, Berlioz, Wagner and Mahler — orchestras grew to the large ensembles required by Romantic and modernist scores. In the 20th century, recording and broadcasting shaped the professional orchestra, with notable ensembles including the Berlin Philharmonic, Vienna Philharmonic, and the major American and British orchestras.

Organisation

Players are organised in a hierarchy: the conductor (often also music director), the concertmaster, and section principals. Orchestras perform in concert halls on subscription series, and also play for opera and ballet, recording sessions and film scores. Smaller ensembles include the chamber orchestra and period-instrument ("historically informed") orchestras.
