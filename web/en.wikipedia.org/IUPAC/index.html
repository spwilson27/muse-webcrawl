International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry

AbbreviationIUPAC
Founded1919
HeadquartersResearch Triangle Park, North Carolina, US
PurposeChemical nomenclature and standards

The International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) is an international federation of chemists and national adhering organizations, founded in 1919. It is best known for standardizing chemical nomenclature, terminology, atomic weights, and measurement methods.

Work

IUPAC publishes the systematic nomenclature used to name organic and inorganic compounds, maintains the standard atomic weights of the elements through its commissions, and issues technical reports and recommendations. It also plays a role, together with other bodies, in validating claims of the discovery of new elements and approving their names.

Organization

The union works through divisions and committees covering fields from physical and biophysical chemistry to chemical education, and convenes a biennial General Assembly and World Chemistry Congress.
