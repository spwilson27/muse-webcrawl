The European Defence Agency (EDA) is an agency of the European Union that promotes and facilitates integration between member states within the EU's Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP). Established in 2004 and headquartered in Brussels, it works to improve European defence capabilities, foster collaborative armaments programmes, strengthen the European defence technological and industrial base, and coordinate research and technology.

Role

The Agency runs cooperative projects in areas such as military mobility, satellite communications, cyber defence, maritime surveillance and helicopter training, and it manages ad hoc programmes on behalf of participating states. It also plays a central role in the Coordinated Annual Review on Defence (CARD), which maps member states' defence plans to identify collaboration opportunities, and it supports the Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO) framework launched in 2017.

Governance

The EDA's steering board, composed of defence ministers or their representatives, is chaired by the EU's High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, who also serves as Head of the Agency. Its chief executive manages day-to-day operations, and the budget is comparatively small relative to national defence spending, since most project funding comes directly from participating states.
