2020 Beirut explosion

On 4 August 2020, a large amount of ammonium nitrate stored at the Port of Beirut in Lebanon exploded, causing at least 218 deaths, more than 7,000 injuries, and US$15 billion in property damage. The blast, one of the largest non-nuclear explosions in history, left an estimated 300,000 people homeless. A fire in Warehouse 12 at the port ignited roughly 2,750 tonnes of ammonium nitrate that had been confiscated from the abandoned ship MV Rhosus and stored unsafely since 2014. The explosion registered as a seismic event of magnitude 3.3 and was felt hundreds of kilometres away, including in Cyprus and Israel.

Background

The ammonium nitrate had arrived in Beirut in 2013 aboard the Moldovan-flagged Rhosus, en route from Georgia to Mozambique. After the ship was detained over port fees and its seaworthiness, the cargo was unloaded into a hangar at the port in 2014. Customs officials wrote repeatedly to judges over the following years requesting that the dangerous cargo be removed, re-exported, or handed to the army, but no action was taken.

The explosion

In the late afternoon of 4 August, a fire broke out in Warehouse 12, which stored the ammonium nitrate alongside fireworks and other materials. At 18:07 a first, smaller explosion sent a cloud of smoke over the port; about thirty seconds later, at 18:08, the bulk of the stockpile detonated in a massive second blast that produced a white mushroom cloud, a destructive shockwave, and an orange-red plume from nitrogen oxides. The explosion carved a crater roughly 120 metres across and devastated the port districts, damaging homes, hospitals, and the country's grain silos.

Aftermath

The blast overwhelmed hospitals already strained by the COVID-19 pandemic, destroyed much of Lebanon's wheat reserves, and intensified public anger at government corruption and negligence amid an ongoing economic crisis. Mass protests forced the resignation of Prime Minister Hassan Diab's government on 10 August. Investigations and judicial proceedings against officials were repeatedly obstructed and politically contested, and as of several years later no senior official had been convicted. International donors provided humanitarian aid, and reconstruction of the port and surrounding neighbourhoods proceeded slowly.
