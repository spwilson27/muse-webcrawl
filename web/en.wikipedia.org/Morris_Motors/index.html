Morris Motors Limited was a British privately owned motor vehicle manufacturing company formed in 1919 to take over the assets of William Morris's WRM Motors Limited and continue production of the same vehicles. By 1926 its production represented 42 per cent of British car manufacture, and Morris was the United Kingdom's best-selling car brand for much of the interwar period.

History

William Morris began building cars in Oxford in 1912 with the Morris Oxford "Bullnose." Buying components from outside suppliers and assembling them in a former military college at Cowley, Morris undercut rivals and, after the First World War, slashed prices to become Britain's largest car maker. The company absorbed several suppliers, acquired Wolseley Motors in 1927 and MG in 1935, and launched popular models including the Morris Eight and the Morris Ten.

Post-war and merger

After Morris's death, the Nuffield Organisation merged with Austin in 1952 to form the British Motor Corporation (BMC). Famous Morris-badged models of the BMC era included the Morris Minor (1948–1971), designed by Alec Issigonis, the Morris Oxford series, and the Morris Mini-Minor — the original Mini of 1959. The Morris name survived through British Leyland on cars such as the Marina and Ital, the last Morris-badged car, ending production in 1984.
