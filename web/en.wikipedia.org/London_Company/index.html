Jamestown, founded in 1607 under the London Company's charter

The London Company, officially the Virginia Company of London, was an English joint-stock company chartered by King James I on 10 April 1606 as one of the two divisions of the Virginia Company (the other being the Plymouth Company). Its charter granted it rights to settle the coast of North America between latitudes 34° and 41° N, overlapping in part with its sister company, with the intent of establishing profitable colonies and finding a route to the Pacific.

Jamestown

In December 1606 the company dispatched three ships — Susan Constant, Godspeed, and Discovery — under Captain Christopher Newport. In May 1607 the expedition founded Jamestown on the James River, the first permanent English settlement in North America. The colony endured extreme hardship, including the "Starving Time" of 1609–10, conflicts with the Powhatan Confederacy, and chronic disease, but survived, and after 1612 began prospering through the cultivation of tobacco introduced by John Rolfe.

Reform and dissolution

A new charter in 1609 made the company effectively the colony's government, and the 1619 reforms of Sir Edwin Sandys encouraged private settlement and created the House of Burgesses, the first elected assembly in English America. However, financial losses, factional disputes among shareholders, and the devastating Powhatan attack of 1622, which killed about a quarter of the colonists, undermined the company. Following an investigation, its charter was annulled by the Court of King's Bench in 1624, and Virginia became a royal colony.

The company also sponsored the settlement of Bermuda, administered from 1612 until the separate Somers Isles Company took over in 1615.
