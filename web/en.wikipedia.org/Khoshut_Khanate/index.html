Khoshut Khanate

The Khoshut Khanate was a Mongol khanate of the Oirat Khoshut tribe based on the Tibetan Plateau from 1642 to 1717. Founded by Güshi Khan, it controlled Qinghai (Kokonor) and exercised a protectorate over Tibet, where Güshi Khan installed the Fifth Dalai Lama, Ngawang Lobzang Gyatso, as the spiritual and temporal ruler of Tibet.

Founding

In 1636 Güshi Khan led the Khoshut Oirats from their pastures near Kokonor southward at the invitation of Gelug school authorities in Lhasa. After defeating rival Mongol and Tibetan factions, including the Tsangpa king, Güshi Khan enthroned the Fifth Dalai Lama in 1642 and was himself proclaimed Khan of Tibet. The khanate thus became the military guarantor of the Ganden Phodrang government.

Relations with the Qing

The Khoshut khans maintained tribute relations with the Qing dynasty and periodically intervened in Tibetan affairs, although day-to-day administration rested with the Dalai Lama's regents. Under Lha-bzang Khan, the last Khoshut ruler, relations with Lhasa deteriorated; Lha-bzang deposed the Sixth Dalai Lama in 1706.

Fall

In 1717 the Dzungar Khanate invaded Tibet and killed Lha-bzang Khan, ending the Khoshut Khanate. Qing imperial armies expelled the Dzungars in 1720, after which Qinghai was incorporated more directly into the empire and Amdo's Khoshut princes were reorganised into banners under Qing supervision.
