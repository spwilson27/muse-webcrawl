Bruno of Cologne

Bornc. 1030, Cologne, Germany
Died6 October 1101, Calabria, Italy
Feast day6 October
Known forFounder of the Carthusian Order

Bruno of Cologne (German: Bruno von Köln; c. 1030 – 6 October 1101), also called Saint Bruno, was a German monk and the founder of the Carthusian Order. Educated at Reims, he became a celebrated teacher and chancellor of the cathedral school there before renouncing worldly advancement and embracing the eremitical life. In 1084 he and six companions settled in the remote Chartreuse mountains near Grenoble, establishing the hermitage that became the Grande Chartreuse.

Early life

Bruno was born in Cologne around 1030. He studied at Reims, where he later taught for some twenty years and earned wide renown as a master of grammar and theology. Appointed chancellor of the archdiocese, he fell out with the corrupt archbishop Manasses I and left Reims, resolved to pursue a life of solitude and prayer.

Foundation of the Carthusians

After a period with the monks at Molesme, Bruno obtained permission from Bishop Hugh of Grenoble to settle in the Chartreuse desert. There his community combined solitary cells with common prayer, a pattern that defined Carthusian life. In 1090 Pope Urban II, Bruno's former pupil, summoned him to Rome as an adviser; unwilling to remain at court, Bruno eventually founded a second hermitage, Santa Maria della Torre, in Calabria, where he died in 1101.

Legacy

Bruno left few writings—commentaries on the Psalms and the letters of Paul, and two letters—but his order became one of the most austere and enduring in Western monasticism. He was never formally canonised by process, but his cult was approved in the 16th century; his feast is kept on 6 October.
