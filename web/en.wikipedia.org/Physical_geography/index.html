Physical geography

Physical geography (also known as physiography) is one of the three main branches of geography, alongside human geography and technical geography. It is the branch of natural science dealing with the study of processes and patterns in the natural environment, such as the atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere and geosphere.

Sub-disciplines

  • Geomorphology – the study of landforms and the processes that shape them.
  • Hydrology – the study of water on and below the Earth's surface.
  • Climatology – the study of climate and weather patterns.
  • Biogeography – the distribution of species and ecosystems.
  • Pedology – the study of soils.
  • Glaciology, oceanography and coastal geography – ice, oceans and shorelines.

Scope

Physical geographers study how landscapes evolve, how climate systems operate, and how natural hazards such as floods, landslides and volcanic eruptions arise. The discipline provides much of the scientific basis for environmental management and for understanding climate change.
