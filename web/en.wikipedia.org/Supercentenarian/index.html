Supercentenarian

A supercentenarian is a person who has reached the age of 110 years. It is estimated that only about one in a thousand centenarians reaches this age, and validated supercentenarians number in the low hundreds alive at any time.

Verification

Age claims at these extremes require rigorous documentary validation, historically performed by the Gerontology Research Group and, for records, Guinness World Records. Validation typically requires birth records, census entries, and marriage or identity documents forming a consistent chain.

Notable cases

The longest verified human lifespan is that of Jeanne Calment of France (1875–1997), who lived 122 years and 164 days. The longest verified male lifespan is that of Jiroemon Kimura of Japan (1897–2013), at 116 years and 54 days. Kane Tanaka (1903–2022) is the second-oldest verified person, at 119 years.

Research

Supercentenarians are studied in gerontology for clues about the genetics and lifestyle factors of extreme longevity and delayed morbidity; many remain comparatively healthy until shortly before death, a phenomenon known as compression of morbidity.
