Chervil

Chervil (Anthriscus cerefolium), sometimes called French parsley or garden chervil, is a delicate annual herb related to parsley and carrots. Native to the Caucasus and western Asia, it was spread through Europe by the Romans and is now a classic ingredient of French cuisine.

Chervil

Scientific name: Anthriscus cerefolium

Family: Apiaceae

Genus: Anthriscus

Description

Chervil grows to 40–70 cm tall, with finely divided, lacy, light-green leaves and slender hollow stems. It produces umbels of small white flowers followed by narrow oblong seeds. The plant bolts quickly in warm weather, so it is grown mainly as a cool-season crop.

Culinary use

The leaves have a mild flavour intermediate between parsley and anise. Chervil is one of the four traditional fines herbes of French cooking, together with tarragon, chives and parsley, and is used in omelettes, salads, soups and sauces; it is added at the end of cooking because its flavour dissipates with heat. It is also a traditional Lenten herb in parts of Europe.
