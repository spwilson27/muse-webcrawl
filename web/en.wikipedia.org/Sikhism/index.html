Sikhism, also known as Sikhi, is an Indian religion and philosophy that originated in the Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent around the end of the 15th century CE. It is one of the most recently founded major religions and among the largest in the world, with about 25–30 million adherents, known as Sikhs.

Beliefs

Sikhism developed from the teachings of Guru Nanak and the nine Sikh gurus who succeeded him. Its central teaching is devotion to and remembrance of one God (Ik Onkar), expressed in the opening words of the scripture, and the pursuit of liberation through honest living, selfless service (seva), and meditation on God's name (nam japna). The faith rejects caste distinctions, idol worship, and ritualism, and stresses equality of all humans, including full equality of women.

History

Guru Nanak (1469–1539) founded the faith after a transformative spiritual experience; his hymns form part of the Guru Granth Sahib. Guru Arjan compiled the scripture and built the Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) at Amritsar; Guru Hargobind added the doctrine of miri-piri (temporal and spiritual authority). In 1699 Guru Gobind Singh founded the Khalsa, the order of initiated Sikhs marked by the Five Ks, and declared the Guru Granth Sahib the eternal Guru. After periods of Mughal persecution, Sikhs built an empire under Maharaja Ranjit Singh (1799–1849), annexed by the British in 1849. The Partition of 1947 divided Punjab between India and Pakistan, and most Sikhs now live in Indian Punjab, with large diaspora communities in Canada, the United Kingdom, and the United States.

Practices

Worship centres on the gurdwara (temple), where the Guru Granth Sahib is enthroned and hymns (kirtan) are sung; every gurdwara maintains a langar, a free community kitchen serving all visitors regardless of faith. Sikhs observe festivals including Vaisakhi and Gurpurbs (gurus' anniversaries).
