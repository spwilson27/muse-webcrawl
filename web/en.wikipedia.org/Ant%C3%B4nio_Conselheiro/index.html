Antônio Vicente Mendes Maciel, better known as Antônio Conselheiro ("Antônio the Counselor"; 13 March 1830 – 22 September 1897), was a Brazilian religious leader who founded the village of Canudos in the northeastern state of Bahia. His settlement attracted thousands of poor followers and was destroyed by the Brazilian army in the War of Canudos (1896–1897).

Early life

Born in Quixeramobim, Ceará, Maciel worked as a teacher, clerk, and travelling salesman before a personal crisis led him to a wandering life as a lay preacher in the sertão, the arid backlands of northeastern Brazil. He preached an austere, apocalyptic Catholicism, repaired churches and cemeteries, and gathered a devoted following among the impoverished rural population.

Canudos

In 1893 Conselheiro and his followers settled at Canudos, an abandoned cattle ranch on the Vaza-Barris River. The community grew to perhaps 25,000 inhabitants. Viewing the young Brazilian Republic as ungodly, Conselheiro refused its authority, and after a series of clashes the federal government sent successive military expeditions against the settlement. The fourth expedition annihilated Canudos in October 1897; Conselheiro had died, probably of dysentery, shortly before the final assault.

Legacy

The war was immortalized by Euclides da Cunha in Os Sertões (1902; translated as Rebellion in the Backlands), one of the classics of Brazilian literature.
