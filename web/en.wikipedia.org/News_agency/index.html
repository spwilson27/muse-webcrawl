News agency

A news agency is an organisation that gathers news reports and sells them to subscribing news organisations, such as newspapers, magazines, and radio and television broadcasters. Also called wire services, news agencies supply much of the international and national news published by the world's media.

History

The model began with Agence Havas in Paris (1835), followed by the Associated Press in New York (1846), a cooperative of newspapers sharing the costs of war reporting, and Reuters, founded in London in 1851. Using telegraph networks, agencies could deliver rapid, factual reports in a distinctive inverted-pyramid style designed so any subscriber could use them.

Major agencies

The global "big three" are the Associated Press (AP), Reuters and Agence France-Presse (AFP). Other major agencies include the German dpa, Spain's EFE, China's Xinhua News Agency and Russia's TASS. Many national agencies are state-owned or organised as cooperatives of their member newspapers.

Services

Beyond text, agencies now supply photographs, video, graphics, audio and data feeds. Their business has been pressured by the decline of newspaper revenues and the rise of free online news and social media, prompting diversification into financial data and technology services; Reuters, for example, is part of Thomson Reuters.
