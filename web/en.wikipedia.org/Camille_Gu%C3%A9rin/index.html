Jean-Marie Camille Guérin (22 December 1872 – 9 June 1961) was a French veterinarian and bacteriologist who, together with Albert Calmette, developed the Bacillus Calmette–Guérin (BCG) vaccine against tuberculosis.

Life and work

Guérin studied veterinary medicine at Alfort and joined the Institut Pasteur de Lille in 1897, working under Albert Calmette. Beginning in 1908, Calmette and Guérin repeatedly subcultured a virulent strain of Mycobacterium bovis on a bile-containing medium; after some 230 passages over thirteen years the bacillus had lost its virulence while retaining immunogenicity.

The BCG vaccine

The strain was first administered to a human infant in Paris in 1921 and went on to become the world's most widely used vaccine against tuberculosis, especially protective against severe childhood forms of the disease. The vaccine's name preserves the initials of both men. Guérin later directed services at the Institut Pasteur in Paris and continued research on tuberculosis immunisation for the rest of his career.
