SawStop

SawStop is an American table saw manufacturer headquartered in Tualatin, Oregon. The company was founded in 2000 by Stephen Gass, a patent attorney and amateur woodworker, to commercialize his flesh-sensing safety technology for table saws. The technology detects contact between the spinning blade and human skin, then stops and retracts the blade within milliseconds, drastically reducing the severity of injuries. Since 2017, SawStop has been a subsidiary of TTS Tooltechnic Systems, the German parent company of Festool.

Technology

The SawStop system applies a small electrical signal to the blade and continuously monitors it. Because the human body is conductive and has capacitance, contact with skin changes the signal. When a change is detected, a high-speed brake—an aluminium block driven by a spring—jams into the blade, stopping it in about 3–5 milliseconds, while the blade's angular momentum drives it down below the table surface. The mechanism typically leaves the user with a minor nick instead of the amputation injuries common in table saw accidents, of which tens of thousands occur annually in the United States. Activation destroys the blade and a replaceable brake cartridge, costing the user roughly $100–$200 per incident. A bypass mode allows cutting conductive materials such as wet lumber or metal-laminated stock, which can otherwise trigger the brake.

History and regulation

After major manufacturers declined to license the invention, Gass founded SawStop to build its own saws, shipping its first cabinet saw in 2004 and later adding contractor, jobsite, and compact models. In 2003 Gass petitioned the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission to require flesh-detection technology on all table saws; the proposal advanced slowly for two decades, with the CPSC voting in 2023 to propose a performance standard that would effectively require blade-contact injury mitigation on new saws. SawStop's patent enforcement drew litigation and an International Trade Commission exclusion order against Bosch's competing REAXX saw in 2017. In February 2024, SawStop announced it would dedicate a key patent to the public should the CPSC rule take effect.

Reception

Woodworkers and safety advocates widely praise the technology's proven record of preventing amputations, though the saws carry a price premium and the company's lobbying for a mandate and its patent stance have drawn criticism from competitors and some consumer groups.
