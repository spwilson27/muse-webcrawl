This American Life (TAL) is an American weekly hour-long radio program produced by Chicago Public Media and hosted by Ira Glass. It is produced in collaboration with Chicago Public Media, delivered to more than 500 public radio stations in the United States by Public Radio Exchange (PRX), and is also one of the most popular podcasts in the United States. Primarily a journalistic non-fiction program, it has also featured essays, memoirs, field recordings, short fiction and found footage.

Format

Each episode has a theme, explored in several "acts" — usually three or four stories that offer different perspectives on the theme. Stories are often personal narratives, reported pieces and interviews, narrated and produced with an emphasis on character and plot unusual in radio journalism. The show premiered on November 17, 1995, on WBEZ Chicago under the original title Your Radio Playhouse, changing its name in 1996. Host Ira Glass had worked in public radio for nearly two decades before creating the programme.

Reception and influence

This American Life has won all of the major broadcasting awards, including multiple Peabody Awards and the first Pulitzer Prize ever awarded for audio journalism, in 2020, for the episode "The Out Crowd". Its podcast regularly draws millions of downloads per episode. The programme's narrative style has influenced a generation of audio producers; its producers helped launch or shaped influential podcasts including Serial (2014), a spin-off that became a cultural phenomenon, and S-Town (2017). A television adaptation ran on Showtime from 2007 to 2009 and won three Emmy Awards. The show's archive of more than 800 episodes is available online.
