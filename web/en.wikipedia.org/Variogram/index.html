Variogram

In spatial statistics, a variogram is a function describing the degree of spatial dependence of a spatial random field or stochastic process. The variogram, commonly denoted 2γ, is defined as the variance of the difference between field values at two locations across realizations of the field. Its square root counterpart, γ, is called the semivariogram.

Definition

For a random field Z(x), the variogram at separation vector h is 2γ(h) = Var[Z(x+h) − Z(x)]. Under the intrinsic stationarity assumption this equals the expected squared difference E[(Z(x+h) − Z(x))²]. The semivariogram γ(h) is half of this quantity. When the process is second-order stationary, the variogram is related to the covariance function C by 2γ(h) = 2C(0) − 2C(h).

Empirical variogram and models

In practice, an empirical (or experimental) variogram is computed from data by averaging squared differences between sample pairs falling into distance bins. A theoretical model — commonly spherical, exponential, Gaussian, or Matérn — is then fitted to it. Characterising features include the nugget (the intercept at zero distance, reflecting measurement error and microscale variation), the sill (the plateau value), and the range (the distance beyond which samples are effectively uncorrelated).

Applications

Variograms are the foundation of geostatistics and kriging interpolation. They originated in mining geology through the work of Georges Matheron in the 1960s, building on practical work by Danie Krige in South African gold mining, and are now used in hydrology, meteorology, environmental science, remote sensing and petroleum engineering.
