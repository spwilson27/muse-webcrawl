Circular saw

TypePower tool / machine tool
UsesCutting wood, metal, masonry, plastic
InventedLate 18th century (various claims)

A circular saw is a power-saw using a toothed or abrasive disc or blade to cut different materials using a rotary motion spinning around an arbor. Circular saws are used for cutting wood, masonry, metal, and plastic, and may be hand-held or mounted to a machine.

History

Circular saw blades are depicted in ancient contexts, but practical circular saws date from the late eighteenth century, when they entered use in sawmills in England and the Netherlands. In the United States, Shaker inventor Tabitha Babbitt is often credited with developing a circular saw for a sawmill around 1813, having observed the inefficiency of two-man pit saws.

Types

Common types include handheld sidewinder and worm-drive saws, table saws, radial arm saws, miter saws (with fixed circular blades), and cold saws for metal. Blades vary by tooth count, geometry, and material — high-speed steel, carbide-tipped, or abrasive discs — chosen for the material being cut and the finish required.

Safety

Circular saws are a significant source of workshop injuries. Hazards include contact with the blade and "kickback", where the workpiece is thrown back toward the operator. Guards, riving knives, and anti-kickback designs reduce these risks.
