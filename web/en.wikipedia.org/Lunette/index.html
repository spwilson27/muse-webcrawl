Lunette
In architecture: semicircular space above a door or window
In fortification: detached work with two faces and an open gorge

A lunette (French for "little moon") is a semicircular architectural space, often containing a window or painting, formed by the intersection of a vault with a vertical wall or located above a door or window.

In architecture

Lunettes are common in Renaissance and Baroque architecture, where the half-moon field above an architrave or within a pendentive-like space was frequently decorated with frescoes or reliefs. Michelangelo's lunettes in the Sistine Chapel depict the ancestors of Christ.

In fortification

In military engineering, a lunette is a small detached outwork in front of the main body of a fortress, with two faces forming a salient angle and flanks retreating toward the place, its rear (gorge) left open.
