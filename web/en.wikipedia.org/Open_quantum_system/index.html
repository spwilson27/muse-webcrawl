Open quantum system

A quantum system interacting with a surrounding environment (bath), leading to decoherence and dissipation.

In physics, an open quantum system is a quantum-mechanical system that interacts with an external quantum system, the environment or bath. In general, these interactions significantly change the dynamics of the system and result in quantum dissipation, such that the information contained in the system is lost to its surroundings. Because no quantum system is completely isolated from its surroundings, it is important to develop a theoretical framework for treating these interactions in order to obtain an accurate understanding of quantum systems.

Overview

Techniques developed in the context of open quantum systems have proved powerful in fields such as quantum optics, quantum measurement theory, quantum statistical mechanics, quantum information science, and quantum cosmology. In contrast to closed systems, where the state can be described by a wavefunction evolving unitarily under the Schrödinger equation, an open system is described by a density matrix whose evolution is generally non-unitary.

Markovian and non-Markovian dynamics

When the environment has no memory of past interactions, the dynamics are called Markovian and can often be described by a master equation in Lindblad form (the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad equation), which generates a completely positive, trace-preserving dynamical semigroup. When environmental correlations persist, the dynamics are non-Markovian and require more elaborate methods, such as the Nakajima–Zwanzig equation, hierarchical equations of motion, or influence-functional path integrals.

Decoherence

A central phenomenon in open quantum systems is decoherence: the loss of quantum coherence as the system becomes entangled with environmental degrees of freedom. Decoherence explains the suppression of interference and the emergence of classical behaviour, and it is a principal obstacle to building large-scale quantum computers.
