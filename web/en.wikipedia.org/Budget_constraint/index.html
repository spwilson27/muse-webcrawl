Budget constraint

Budget constraint
FieldMicroeconomics, consumer theory
RepresentsAll combinations of goods affordable at given prices and income
GraphBudget line (two goods)
Related conceptsIndifference curve, opportunity cost, utility maximization

In economics, a budget constraint represents all the combinations of goods and services that a consumer may purchase given current prices within their given income. In the two-good case it is expressed as:

pxx + pyy = m

where px and py are the prices of goods x and y, x and y are the quantities consumed, and m is the consumer's income. The set of affordable bundles (those costing no more than income) is called the budget set; its boundary is the budget line.

Properties

The slope of the budget line equals the negative ratio of prices, −px/py, which measures the opportunity cost of good x in terms of good y — the rate at which the market allows the consumer to substitute one good for another. An increase in income shifts the budget line outward in parallel; a change in the price of one good rotates it around the intercept of the other good. Consumer choice theory models the consumer as selecting the affordable bundle that reaches the highest indifference curve, which occurs at a tangency where the marginal rate of substitution equals the price ratio.

Extensions

Budget constraints are generalized in many settings: intertemporal choice (saving and borrowing across periods, giving the lifetime budget constraint), labour supply (the trade-off between consumption and leisure), rationing and quantity constraints, kinked constraints produced by taxes, subsidies, quantity discounts, and in-kind transfers such as food stamps, and "soft" budget constraints in the theory of socialist and state-owned enterprises, a term coined by János Kornai for organizations that expect to be bailed out rather than face bankruptcy.
