War in Abkhazia (1992–1993)
Date14 August 1992 – 27 September 1993
LocationAbkhazia, western Georgia
ResultAbkhaz victory; ethnic cleansing of Georgians from Abkhazia
BelligerentsGeorgia vs. Abkhaz separatists, supported by North Caucasian volunteers and elements of the Russian military

The War in Abkhazia from 1992 to 1993 was fought between the Georgian government and Abkhaz separatists in Abkhazia, a region on the eastern Black Sea coast that was an autonomous republic within Soviet Georgia. The war began on 14 August 1992, when Georgian forces under Defence Minister Tengiz Kitovani entered Abkhazia, officially to secure railways and free hostages, and escalated into a full-scale conflict.

Course of the war

Georgian troops quickly took the capital, Sukhumi, but Abkhaz forces regrouped in the north, aided by volunteers from the Confederation of Mountain Peoples of the Caucasus, including Chechen fighters led by Shamil Basayev, and by support from Russian military elements. A Russian-brokered ceasefire in July 1993 collapsed when Abkhaz forces launched an offensive in September, capturing Sukhumi on 27 September 1993. The fall of the city was followed by massacres of Georgian civilians.

Aftermath

More than 200,000 ethnic Georgians fled or were expelled from Abkhazia in what international observers and the OSCE have described as ethnic cleansing. Casualty estimates run to 20,000–30,000 killed. The May 1994 Moscow Agreement established a ceasefire monitored by CIS (effectively Russian) peacekeepers and the UNOMIG observer mission. Abkhazia has functioned as a de facto independent state since; Russia recognized its independence after the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, a step most of the world rejects, continuing to regard Abkhazia as part of Georgia.
