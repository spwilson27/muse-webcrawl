Order of the British Empire

The Most Excellent Order of the British Empire is a British order of chivalry established on 4 June 1917 by King George V, rewarding contributions to the arts and sciences, work with charitable and welfare organisations, and public service outside the civil service. It has five classes, in civil and military divisions.

Classes

From highest to lowest the classes are Knight or Dame Grand Cross (GBE), Knight or Dame Commander (KBE/DBE), Commander (CBE), Officer (OBE) and Member (MBE). Holders of the top two classes are entitled to the style Sir or Dame (for British citizens). The associated British Empire Medal (BEM) is awarded for meritorious local service.

History

Created during the First World War, the order was the first to be awarded on a mass scale and the first to include women on equal terms. It originally recognised wartime service and was divided into civil and military divisions in 1918. Appointments are announced in the New Year Honours and the monarch's official Birthday Honours lists.

Criticism and reform

The word "Empire" has drawn criticism since decolonisation, and some recipients have declined or returned honours in protest. Periodic reviews have considered renaming the order — for example to the "Order of British Excellence" — but its name remains unchanged, and it continues to be widely awarded for community and voluntary service.
