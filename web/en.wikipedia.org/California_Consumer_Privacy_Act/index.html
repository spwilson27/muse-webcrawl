California Consumer Privacy Act

The California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (CCPA) is a state statute intended to enhance privacy rights and consumer protection for residents of California, United States. It was signed into law on June 28, 2018, and took effect on January 1, 2020.

Background

The CCPA was passed rapidly by the California State Legislature to head off a stricter ballot initiative championed by real-estate developer Alastair Mactaggart. It was the first comprehensive consumer privacy law of its kind in the United States, frequently compared with the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Rights granted

The Act gives California consumers the right to know what personal information a business collects about them and how it is used; the right to delete personal information (subject to exceptions); the right to opt out of the "sale" of their personal information; and the right to non-discrimination for exercising these rights. Businesses meeting thresholds — for example, annual gross revenues above a statutory amount, or buying, selling, or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households (raised from 50,000 by later amendment) — are subject to the law.

Enforcement and the CPRA

The CCPA is enforced principally by the California Attorney General, with a limited private right of action for certain data breaches. In November 2020 voters approved Proposition 24, the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA), which substantially amended the CCPA effective January 1, 2023, adding rights to correct inaccurate data and to limit the use of sensitive personal information, and creating the California Privacy Protection Agency to enforce the law.
