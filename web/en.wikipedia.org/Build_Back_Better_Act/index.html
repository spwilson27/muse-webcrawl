Build Back Better Act

The Build Back Better Act was a bill introduced in the 117th United States Congress to fulfil aspects of President Joe Biden's Build Back Better Plan and the American Families Plan. The comprehensive social, climate and tax package passed the House of Representatives on 19 November 2021 but stalled in the Senate; many of its climate provisions were later enacted in reduced form by the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022.

Provisions

The House-passed bill, scored at roughly $1.7 trillion in spending over ten years, included universal pre-kindergarten, an extension of the expanded child tax credit, four weeks of paid family and medical leave, subsidies for child care and home care for the elderly, hearing coverage under Medicare, and some $555 billion for clean energy and climate action — the largest climate investment in U.S. history at that time. It was to be financed by a minimum tax on large corporations, surtaxes on very high incomes, and expanded IRS enforcement.

Legislative history

The bill was pursued through budget reconciliation to bypass the Senate filibuster. After months of negotiation with Senators Joe Manchin and Kyrsten Sinema, the House passed it 220–213. In December 2021 Manchin announced he could not support the bill, citing inflation concerns, and negotiations collapsed.

Aftermath

In July 2022, Manchin and Majority Leader Chuck Schumer announced a successor agreement, the Inflation Reduction Act, which carried forward most of the climate and energy provisions, prescription-drug pricing reform and corporate minimum tax, while dropping the child-care, pre-K and paid-leave elements. It was signed into law on 16 August 2022.
