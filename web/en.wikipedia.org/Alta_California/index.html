Alta California

Alta California ("Upper California"), also known as Nueva California ("New California") among other names, was a province of New Spain, formally established in 1804 after the region that included present-day California was split. It was later incorporated as a territory of the Mexican Republic in 1822, and finally became part of the United States following the Mexican–American War and the cession of California in 1848.

History

Spanish colonization of Alta California began in 1769 with the Portolá expedition and the founding of Mission San Diego de Alcalá, the first of 21 Franciscan missions stretching north to Sonoma. The missions, supported by military presidios at San Diego, Santa Barbara, Monterey and San Francisco, were intended to convert and acculturate the Indigenous peoples of California, whose populations declined catastrophically from disease and mission conditions.

Mexican era

After Mexican independence in 1821, Alta California became a territory of Mexico. In the 1830s the missions were secularized and their vast lands granted as ranchos to Californio families, giving rise to a pastoral cattle economy trading in hides and tallow. Governance was unstable, with frequent revolts against governors sent from Mexico City.

American conquest

During the Mexican–American War, U.S. forces occupied Monterey and other ports in 1846, while American settlers proclaimed the short-lived California Republic at Sonoma in the Bear Flag Revolt. The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848) ceded Alta California to the United States; the discovery of gold at Sutter's Mill the same year triggered the California Gold Rush and rapid statehood in 1850.
