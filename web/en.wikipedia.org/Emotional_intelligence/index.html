Emotional intelligence

Emotional intelligence (EI) is the ability to perceive, interpret, demonstrate, control, evaluate, and use emotions to communicate with and relate to others effectively and constructively.

History

The concept drew on earlier work on social intelligence. Peter Salovey and John Mayer coined the term in 1990 and developed an ability model. Daniel Goleman's 1995 best-selling book Emotional Intelligence popularized the idea for leadership, education, and work.

Models

The ability model treats EI as mental abilities: perceiving emotions, using emotions to facilitate thought, understanding emotions, and managing emotions. Trait EI and mixed models incorporate personality traits and competencies such as self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, and social skills. Measurement tools include the Mayer-Salovey-Caruso test and various self-report scales.

Criticism

Critics question whether EI is distinct from general intelligence and personality, and whether some measures predict outcomes beyond established traits.
