Ladakh
Union territory of India
Capital: Leh (summer), Kargil
Area: about 59,146 km²
Highest point region: Karakoram and Ladakh ranges

Ladakh is a region administered by India as a union territory, constituting a large portion of the larger Kashmir region, which has been disputed between India, Pakistan and China since 1947. It is a high-altitude cold desert between the Karakoram Range in the north and the Himalayas in the south, drained by the Indus River, whose valley holds Leh, the largest town.

Ladakh's population is roughly half Tibetan Buddhist (mainly in Leh district) and half Shia Muslim (mainly in Kargil district). Historically an independent kingdom influenced by Tibet, it was annexed by the Dogra rulers of Jammu in the 19th century. In 2019 the former state of Jammu and Kashmir was reorganised and Ladakh became a separate union territory without a legislature. It borders Tibet and Xinjiang (China) and Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan, and its eastern boundary at Aksai Chin is disputed with China.
