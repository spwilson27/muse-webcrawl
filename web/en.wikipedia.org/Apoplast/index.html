Apoplast

The apoplast is the network of extracellular spaces within a plant, comprising the cell walls and intercellular spaces, through which water and solutes can move freely without crossing a cell membrane. It is one of the two main pathways for transport in plant tissues, the other being the symplast (movement through connected cytoplasm via plasmodesmata).

Function

In roots, water and dissolved minerals travel through the apoplast until they reach the endodermis, where the waterproof Casparian strip blocks further apoplastic movement and forces substances through selectively permeable cell membranes — giving the plant control over uptake. The apoplast also plays roles in cell signalling, pH regulation, and interactions with pathogens, many of which colonise apoplastic spaces.

History of the term

The term was coined by the German plant physiologist Ernst Münch in 1930, from the Greek apo ("away from") and plastos ("formed").
