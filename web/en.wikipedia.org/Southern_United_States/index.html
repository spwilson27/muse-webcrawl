Southern United States


The Southern United States, as defined by the U.S. Census Bureau

The Southern United States, commonly referred to as the American South, the South, or the Dixie, is a geographic and cultural region of the United States. It is located between the Atlantic Ocean and the Western United States, with the Midwestern and Northeastern United States to its north and the Gulf of Mexico and Mexico to its south.

The U.S. Census Bureau defines the South as the South Atlantic, East South Central, and West South Central divisions, comprising sixteen states: Alabama, Arkansas, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maryland, Mississippi, North Carolina, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Virginia, and West Virginia, along with the District of Columbia. Historically, the region is associated with the states that seceded during the American Civil War to form the Confederate States of America, though the modern definition is broader.

History

The South was settled by British colonists beginning in the early 17th century, with Virginia founded in 1607. The plantation economy, based on cash crops such as tobacco, rice, indigo, and later cotton, relied on enslaved African labor, a system that shaped the region's society and politics for more than two centuries. After the American Revolution, the invention of the cotton gin in 1793 greatly expanded cotton production and the institution of slavery.

In 1861, eleven Southern states seceded from the Union, precipitating the American Civil War. The Confederacy's defeat in 1865 ended slavery through the Thirteenth Amendment, and Reconstruction reshaped the region's political order, though Jim Crow laws later imposed racial segregation that lasted into the mid-20th century. The Civil Rights Movement of the 1950s and 1960s dismantled legal segregation.

Culture

Southern culture is known for its distinctive cuisine, music, dialects, and customs. Southern cooking features dishes such as fried chicken, barbecue, collard greens, black-eyed peas, and cornbread. The region is the birthplace of musical genres including blues, jazz, country, bluegrass, rock and roll, and Southern hip hop. Southern American English is characterized by features such as the Southern drawl and monophthongization of the "ai" vowel.

Economy

Once overwhelmingly agricultural, the modern South has diversified into manufacturing, banking, energy, and technology. Major metropolitan areas such as Atlanta, Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, Charlotte, and Nashville have become national economic centers. The region has experienced rapid population growth since the late 20th century as part of the Sun Belt migration.
