Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) is an enzyme attached to the membrane of cells in the lungs, arteries, heart, kidney and intestines. It is a component of the renin–angiotensin system, where it counterbalances ACE by breaking down the vasoconstrictor angiotensin II into the vasodilator angiotensin (1–7), thereby helping to regulate blood pressure and fluid balance.

Role in coronavirus infection

ACE2 serves as the entry point into human cells for several coronaviruses. Its extracellular domain is bound by the spike (S) protein of SARS-CoV, the virus of the 2002–04 SARS outbreak, and of SARS-CoV-2, the virus that causes COVID-19; after binding, the host protease TMPRSS2 primes the spike for membrane fusion. Human coronavirus NL63 also uses ACE2. The enzyme's distribution helps explain the multi-organ character of severe COVID-19.

Research

Soluble recombinant ACE2 has been investigated as a decoy therapy that could neutralise circulating virus, and ACE2 expression is studied in connection with susceptibility differences between individuals and species. Despite early concerns, evidence indicates that common blood-pressure medicines that act on the renin–angiotensin system do not need to be stopped during COVID-19.
