Charles law

Charles law is an alternative title for the gas law more commonly written Charles's law. It states that the volume of a fixed amount of an ideal gas held at constant pressure is directly proportional to its absolute temperature: V ∝ T, or V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂.

Summary

The relationship is named after the French balloonist and scientist Jacques Charles, whose experiments in the 1780s established that gases expand uniformly with temperature. Published by Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac in 1802 with credit to Charles, the law is one of the empirical relationships combined in the ideal gas law, and it implies the existence of an absolute zero of temperature at −273.15 °C, the point at which a gas's extrapolated volume would vanish.
