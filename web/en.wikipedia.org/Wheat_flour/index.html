Wheat flour is a powder made from grinding wheat, used for human consumption. It is the principal flour in most of the world's baking — breads, cakes, pastries, noodles and pasta — because wheat proteins, chiefly glutenin and gliadin, combine to form gluten, the elastic network that gives dough its structure and chew.

Types

Flours are graded by extraction rate (how much of the grain is included) and protein content. White flour is milled from the starchy endosperm alone; whole wheat or wholemeal flour includes the bran and germ. Bread flour is made from hard wheat with 12–14% protein for strong gluten development; cake flour is finely milled from soft wheat with low protein for tender crumb. All-purpose or plain flour is a middle blend, and self-raising flour has baking powder and salt pre-mixed. Atta is a finely ground whole-wheat flour used for South Asian flatbreads such as chapati and paratha.

Production and enrichment

Modern flour is produced in roller mills, which replaced millstones in the late 19th century and allow separation of bran, germ and endosperm. In many countries white flour is enriched by law with iron and B vitamins (thiamine, niacin, riboflavin, folic acid) lost in milling, and may be bleached or treated with maturing agents to improve baking performance. Flour is the main ingredient in bread, the staple food for large parts of the world.
