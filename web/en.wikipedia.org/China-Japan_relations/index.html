China–Japan relations

China–Japan relations (Chinese: 中日关系; Japanese: 日中関係) are the international relations between China and Japan, two of the largest economies in the world. The countries are geographically separated by the East China Sea and share more than a millennium of cultural exchange, punctuated by periods of conflict.

History

China profoundly influenced Japanese culture: writing, Buddhism, law codes and architecture reached Japan through missions to Sui and Tang China from the 7th century. Relations turned violent in the late 19th and 20th centuries, with the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95), in which Japan took Taiwan, and the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–45), part of the Second World War, which caused immense loss of life in China and remains central to historical memory in both countries.

Modern relations

Japan and the People's Republic of China normalised relations in 1972 and signed a Treaty of Peace and Friendship in 1978. Trade and investment grew enormously, and China became Japan's largest trading partner. At the same time, tensions persist over historical issues, Japanese textbook controversies, visits to Yasukuni Shrine, and the territorial dispute over the Senkaku/Diaoyu Islands in the East China Sea, as well as competition for regional influence.
