Lake Victoria is one of the African Great Lakes. With a surface area of approximately 68,800 square kilometres, it is Africa's largest lake, the world's largest tropical lake, and the world's second-largest freshwater lake by surface area after Lake Superior in North America. It is bordered by Uganda to the north, Kenya to the northeast, and Tanzania to the south. The lake was named by the explorer John Hanning Speke, the first European to document it (in 1858), in honour of Queen Victoria.

Geology and hydrology

Geologically young — it dried out completely around 17,300 years ago and refilled about 14,700 years ago — the lake occupies a shallow depression between the eastern and western branches of the East African Rift. It receives most of its water from direct rainfall rather than its inflow rivers, the largest of which is the Kagera. Its only outflow is the Victoria Nile at Jinja, Uganda, now regulated by the Nalubaale and Kiira hydroelectric dams near the site of the former Ripon Falls and Owen Falls.

Ecology

Lake Victoria famously supported an extraordinary adaptive radiation of more than 500 endemic cichlid fish species. The introduction of the Nile perch in the 1950s, together with overfishing and eutrophication, drove an estimated 200 of these species to extinction — one of the largest vertebrate extinction episodes of modern times — though some cichlids have since partially recovered. The lake's fishery remains vital to the regional economy, and its basin is one of the most densely populated rural areas in Africa, with more than 40 million people.
