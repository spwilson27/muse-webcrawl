The Twelve Caesars

The Twelve Caesars (Latin: De vita Caesarum, "On the Life of the Caesars") is a set of twelve biographies of Julius Caesar and the first eleven emperors of the Roman Empire, written by the Roman historian Suetonius and published around AD 121, during the reign of Hadrian.

Contents

The work covers Julius Caesar, Augustus, Tiberius, Caligula, Claudius, Nero, Galba, Otho, Vitellius, Vespasian, Titus, and Domitian. Each life is organised not chronologically but by rubric — ancestry, public career, private habits, appearance, death, and omens — a structure that made the work enormously influential on later biography.

Sources and style

Suetonius served as Hadrian's secretary (ab epistulis), giving him access to imperial archives, letters, and edicts, which he quotes verbatim. His anecdotal, sometimes scandalous, manner preserves details of court life found nowhere else, though modern scholars treat his gossip with caution.

Legacy

The Lives shaped the Western image of the early emperors, inspired Einhard's life of Charlemagne, and underlie much of Robert Graves's novels I, Claudius and Claudius the God.
