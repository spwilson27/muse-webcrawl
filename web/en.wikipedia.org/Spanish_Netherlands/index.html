Spanish Netherlands
Period: 1556–1714
Capital: Brussels
Rulers: Habsburg Spain
Succeeded by: Austrian Netherlands

The Spanish Netherlands (Spanish: Países Bajos Españoles) were the Habsburg territories in the Low Countries ruled by the Spanish branch of the House of Habsburg from 1556 to 1714. At their greatest extent they comprised most of present-day Belgium, Luxembourg, the northern French department of Nord-Pas-de-Calais, and parts of the modern Netherlands and Germany.

History

The Seventeen Provinces came under Spanish rule when Charles V abdicated in 1555–56, leaving the Netherlands to his son Philip II. Spanish rule, heavy taxation, and religious persecution of Protestants provoked the Dutch Revolt (the Eighty Years' War). By the 1580s the northern provinces had effectively seceded — declaring independence in 1581 — while Alessandro Farnese, Duke of Parma, reconquered the south for Spain. The Peace of Münster (1648) recognised the independence of the Dutch Republic, leaving Spain with the southern provinces.

Archdukes and later rule

From 1598 to 1621 the Spanish Netherlands were ruled, nominally sovereign, by Archduke Albert VII and the Infanta Isabella Clara Eugenia, a period of reconstruction and Catholic revival. After Albert's death the territory reverted to direct Spanish rule, governed by governors-general from Brussels. Supplied and reinforced via the Spanish Road from Lombardy, the Army of Flanders remained Spain's most important field army. Wars with France under Louis XIV stripped away Artois and parts of Flanders and Hainaut.

Succession

In the War of the Spanish Succession the territory was occupied by the Grand Alliance; the Treaty of Utrecht (1713) and Treaty of Rastatt (1714) transferred the Spanish Netherlands to the Austrian Habsburgs, after which they were known as the Austrian Netherlands.
