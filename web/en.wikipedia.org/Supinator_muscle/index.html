Supinator muscle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Supinator
OriginLateral epicondyle of humerus, radial collateral and annular ligaments, supinator crest of ulna
InsertionLateral, posterior and anterior surfaces of proximal radius
NerveDeep branch of radial nerve (posterior interosseous nerve), C5–C6
ActionSupination of the forearm

The supinator is a broad muscle in the posterior compartment of the forearm, curved around the upper third of the radius. Together with the biceps brachii it supinates the forearm — rotating it so the palm faces upward (anteriorly) — and it is the primary supinator when the elbow is extended.

Structure

The muscle arises from the lateral epicondyle of the humerus, the radial collateral and annular ligaments and the supinator crest of the ulna. Its two layers enclose the deep branch of the radial nerve, which passes through the arcade of Frohse before emerging as the posterior interosseous nerve. Fibres wrap around the neck and shaft of the radius to insert on its proximal third.

Clinical significance

Entrapment of the posterior interosseous nerve within the supinator causes posterior interosseous nerve syndrome, with weakness of finger and wrist extension but preserved sensation; a related pain syndrome at the arcade of Frohse is termed radial tunnel syndrome. The supinator is also relevant in fractures of the radial head, which it surrounds.
