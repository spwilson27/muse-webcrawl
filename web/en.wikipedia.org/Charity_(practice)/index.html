Charity (practice)

Charity
DefinitionVoluntary giving of help to those in need
FormsMoney, goods, time, services
Related conceptsPhilanthropy, altruism, alms

Charity is the voluntary giving of help, typically in the form of money, food, goods, or time, to those in need. It is a practice found in virtually all cultures and religious traditions, where it is often regarded as a moral duty. The word derives through Old French charité from Latin caritas.

Practice across traditions

In Christianity, charity is a theological virtue expressed through almsgiving and works of mercy. In Islam, zakat is one of the Five Pillars, and voluntary giving (sadaqah) is highly praised. Judaism teaches tzedakah, regarded as an obligation of justice rather than optional generosity. Hindu, Buddhist, and Sikh traditions likewise commend dana (giving) and practices such as the Sikh langar free kitchen.

Modern charitable sector

Today, charitable giving is organised largely through nonprofit organisations and foundations, regulated in many countries with tax privileges for donors and registered charities. Giving encompasses disaster relief, medical research, education, poverty relief, and the arts. Effective altruism has popularised evaluating charities by measurable impact per donation.

Criticism

Critics have questioned whether charity can perpetuate dependency, substitute for state welfare, or favour donor preferences over recipients' needs, while supporters emphasise its flexibility, local knowledge, and moral significance.
