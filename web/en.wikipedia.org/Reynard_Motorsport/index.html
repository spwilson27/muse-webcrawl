Reynard Motorsport was a British manufacturer of racing cars founded in 1973 by Adrian Reynard. Starting from Formula Ford, it grew to become for a time the world's largest producer of racing cars, dominating Formula Three, Formula 3000 and North American Champ Car racing, before financial difficulties led to its collapse in 2002.

History

Adrian Reynard built his first car while studying engineering, founding his company (originally as Sabre, then Reynard) in Bicester. An early hallmark was designing cars that won on their debut — a feat the company repeated at successively higher levels. Reynard broke into Formula Three in 1985, displacing Ralt as the category's leading constructor, and won the first Formula 3000 race in 1985; its cars took the International F3000 championship in 1986, 1987 and 1988.

In 1994 Reynard entered CART/Champ Car with a debut victory, and its chassis won the series championship every year from 1995 to 2001, powering drivers such as Jacques Villeneuve, Michael Andretti and Juan Pablo Montoya, and multiple Indianapolis 500 wins. The company also engineered the first BAR Formula One car for 1999 under a partnership with British American Racing, though Reynard itself never competed in F1 as a constructor.

Decline

Expansion into road cars, an Indianapolis facility and the downturn after a failed stock-market flotation strained finances. When Champ Car teams switched toward rival Lola chassis in 2002, Reynard entered receivership; its assets and designs were sold, some passing to other constructors. A revived Reynard brand later produced low-volume track-day cars.
