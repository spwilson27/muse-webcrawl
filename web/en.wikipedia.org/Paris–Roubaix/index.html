Paris–Roubaix

Paris–Roubaix Nickname: The Hell of the North; Queen of the Classics
First held: 1896
Distance: approx. 250–260 km
Terrain: Flat, with ~55 km of cobbled sectors (pavé)
Finish: Roubaix Velodrome
Record wins: 4 — Roger De Vlaeminck, Tom Boonen

Paris–Roubaix is a one-day professional men's road bicycle race in northern France, starting near Paris (in Compiègne since 1977) and finishing in Roubaix, at the border with Belgium. It is one of cycling's five Monuments and the most famous of the cobbled classics. The race is nicknamed the "Hell of the North," "Queen of the Classics," and l'Enfer du Nord, and is known for its rough terrain and cobblestone sectors, including the notorious Trouée d'Arenberg (Forest of Arenberg) and the Carrefour de l'Arbre.

History

The race was created in 1896 by Théodore Vienne and Maurice Perez, Roubaix mill owners who had built a velodrome in the town, with the support of the newspaper Le Vélo. It has been held every year since except during the two world wars. The "Hell of the North" name originated after the First World War, when journalists describing the 1919 edition saw a landscape devastated by the fighting — trenches, craters, and shattered trees. The winner receives a trophy in the form of a cobblestone (a piece of pavé mounted on a plinth).

Course

The modern race covers roughly 257 kilometres, of which some 55 kilometres are ridden over cobbles arranged in around 29 numbered sectors, graded from one to five stars by difficulty. The five-star sectors are the Trouée d'Arenberg — a 2.3 km stretch of rough, badly worn cobbles through the Arenberg forest — Mons-en-Pévèle, and the Carrefour de l'Arbre, the last major obstacle, about 17 km from the finish. Rain turns the pavé treacherously slippery and coats riders in mud; the finish is contested with a lap and a half of the outdoor Roubaix velodrome, built in 1895.

Notable winners

Roger De Vlaeminck and Tom Boonen share the record of four victories. Belgian riders have dominated overall. Other celebrated winners include Eddy Merckx, Francesco Moser (three consecutive wins, 1978–1980), Fabian Cancellara, and Mathieu van der Poel. A women's race, Paris–Roubaix Femmes, was inaugurated in 2021, first won by Lizzie Deignan with a solo breakaway.
