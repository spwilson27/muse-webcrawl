New Bedford is a city in Bristol County, Massachusetts, United States, located on Buzzards Bay. During the 19th century it was one of the most important whaling ports in the world and, for a time, the richest city per capita in the United States.

History

The area was settled by Europeans in 1652 and was part of Dartmouth before incorporating as a town in 1787. New Bedford's whaling fleet grew to dominate the industry by the 1850s, with hundreds of vessels sailing worldwide; the city provides the setting for the opening chapters of Herman Melville's novel Moby-Dick (1851). After whaling declined, textile mills drove the economy, followed later by commercial fishing.

Economy

In the 21st century the Port of New Bedford has repeatedly ranked as the highest-grossing commercial fishing port in the United States by value, driven largely by the scallop fishery. The city is also a centre for the region's Portuguese-American community and hosts the New Bedford Whaling Museum and the New Bedford Whaling National Historical Park.
