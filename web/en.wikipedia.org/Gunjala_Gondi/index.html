Gunjala Gondi

Gunjala Gondi is a script used to write the Gondi language, a Dravidian language spoken by the Gond people of central India. The script is named after the village of Gunjala in the Adilabad district of Telangana, where manuscripts written in it were found in 2006. Dated documents suggest the script was in use by the Gond community from at least the 18th century into the early 20th century, before falling out of use. The script was added to the Unicode Standard in 2018.

Discovery

In 2006 a bundle of manuscripts was found in Gunjala village, preserved by a family of hereditary Gond record-keepers. The documents, written by local Gond scribes, included land records, genealogies and accounts, with some dated by internal evidence to as early as 1750. Their decipherment showed that the script had served the administrative needs of Gond rajas and village officials in the Adilabad region.

Characteristics

Gunjala Gondi is an abugida: consonant letters carry an inherent vowel that can be modified by vowel signs, and conjunct consonants are written with special ligature forms. It is one of two scripts developed specifically for Gondi, the other being the Masaram Gondi script invented in 1918 by Munshi Mangal Singh Masaram. Most Gondi speakers today who write the language use the Devanagari or Telugu scripts.

Revival and encoding

Scholars at the University of Hyderabad and community activists have worked to document and teach the script. It was encoded in the Unicode Standard in version 11.0 (June 2018), enabling its use in digital text.
