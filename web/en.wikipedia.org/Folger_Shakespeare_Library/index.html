Folger Shakespeare Library

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Folger Shakespeare Library is an independent research library on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., in the United States. It has the world's largest collection of the printed works of William Shakespeare, and is a primary repository for rare materials from the early modern period (1500–1750). The library was established by Henry Clay Folger in association with his wife, Emily Jordan Folger, and opened in 1932.

History

Henry Clay Folger, president of the Standard Oil Company of New York, began collecting Shakespeareana in 1889 with the purchase of a Fourth Folio (1685). With Emily Jordan Folger, he assembled an unmatched collection of First Folios, quartos, and promptbooks. The couple chose Washington, D.C., for their library, on a site near the Library of Congress and the Capitol. Architect Paul Philippe Cret designed the white marble building, whose Art Deco exterior is decorated with bas-relief scenes from Shakespeare's plays by sculptor John Gregory. The library was dedicated on April 23, 1932, shortly after Henry Folger's death, and is administered by the Trustees of Amherst College, the Folgers' alma mater.

Collections

The Folger holds 82 copies of the 1623 First Folio — the largest collection anywhere, roughly a third of the copies known to survive — along with many early quartos, the Titus Andronicus quarto of 1594, promptbooks, manuscripts, playbills, paintings, and an extensive collection of non-Shakespearean early English printed books. Its holdings total some 200,000 books, 60,000 manuscripts, and thousands of works of art.

Building and programs

Inside, the Tudor-style Great Hall and the Old Reading Room evoke Elizabethan England, while the Folger Theatre hosts productions of Shakespeare and other early modern drama. The library supports scholarly programs, the Folger Institute, exhibitions, education programs, the PEN/Faulkner Award readings, and publications including Shakespeare Quarterly. A major renovation completed in 2024 added new exhibition halls, in which all 82 First Folios are displayed together, and expanded visitor spaces.
