Observable

In physics, an observable is a physical property or quantity that can be measured. In classical mechanics, observables are real-valued functions on phase space. In quantum mechanics, observables are represented by self-adjoint (Hermitian) operators acting on the Hilbert space of states.

Quantum mechanics

Examples of quantum observables include position, momentum, energy, spin, and angular momentum. The possible outcomes of measuring an observable are the eigenvalues of its operator; the Born rule gives the probability of each outcome from the state vector. Observables whose operators do not commute, such as position and momentum, cannot be measured simultaneously with arbitrary precision — the content of the uncertainty principle.

Formalism

For an observable A with eigenstates |a⟩, a system in state |ψ⟩ yields the outcome a with probability |⟨a|ψ⟩|². The expectation value of A in state |ψ⟩ is ⟨ψ|A|ψ⟩.
