Biomedical engineering

[Image placeholder: MRI scanner]

Biomedical engineering (BME), also known as medical engineering, is the application of engineering principles and design concepts to medicine and biology for healthcare purposes — including diagnostics, monitoring, and therapy. The field combines the design and problem-solving skills of engineering with medical and biological sciences to advance healthcare treatment.

Subfields

  • Biomechanics — mechanical analysis of biological systems, from joint forces to blood flow.
  • Biomaterials — materials designed to interact with living tissue, used in implants and prosthetics.
  • Medical imaging — technologies such as MRI, CT, ultrasound, and PET that visualize the interior of the body.
  • Biomedical electronics and instrumentation — sensors, pacemakers, and patient-monitoring devices.
  • Tissue engineering and regenerative medicine — growing replacement tissues and organs.
  • Neural engineering — interfaces between machines and the nervous system.
  • Clinical engineering — management and maintenance of medical technology in hospitals.
  • Rehabilitation engineering — assistive devices for people with disabilities.

History

Although prosthetic devices date to antiquity, biomedical engineering emerged as a distinct discipline in the mid-20th century, driven by advances such as Willem Kolff's dialysis machine, the artificial heart valve, the cardiac pacemaker, and the development of medical imaging. Willem Einthoven's electrocardiogram and the later invention of computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging transformed diagnosis. The field professionalized with dedicated university programs, societies such as the Biomedical Engineering Society (founded 1968), and regulatory frameworks for medical devices.

Regulation

Medical devices are regulated in most jurisdictions — for example, by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and under the European Union Medical Device Regulation — with devices classified by risk and subject to approval processes before marketing. Biomedical engineers work in industry, hospitals, research laboratories, and regulatory agencies.
