Guided-missile destroyer

Modern guided-missile destroyers are among the largest and most capable surface combatants.

A guided-missile destroyer (hull classification symbol DDG in the United States Navy) is a destroyer designed to launch guided missiles. These warships combine the traditional destroyer roles of anti-submarine and anti-surface warfare with long-range anti-aircraft and missile-defence capabilities, typically provided by a phased-array radar and vertical launching systems.

Development

The type emerged in the 1950s as navies converted gun destroyers to carry early surface-to-air missiles such as Terrier and Tartar. The US Navy's Arleigh Burke class, built around the Aegis Combat System and SPY-1 radar, became the archetype of the modern DDG; over 70 have been built since 1991. Other major classes include the British Type 45, the Japanese Atago and Maya classes, the South Korean Sejong the Great class, the Chinese Type 052D and Type 055 (classified by NATO as a cruiser), and the Australian Hobart class.

Role

Guided-missile destroyers escort carrier and amphibious groups, provide area air defence, conduct ballistic missile defence patrols, and strike land targets with cruise missiles such as the Tomahawk.
