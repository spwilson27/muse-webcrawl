Balanced circuit

A balanced circuit is an electrical circuit whose two sides have identical impedances to ground, so that a signal carried as the difference between two conductors is largely immune to common-mode interference. Balanced lines, typically twisted pairs, are standard in professional audio and telecommunications.

Operation

The transmitter drives equal and opposite signals on the two conductors. Noise picked up equally by both is rejected by a differential receiver, which responds only to the difference — a property quantified as common-mode rejection ratio.

Applications

Balanced connections are used in microphone cables (XLR connectors), telephone lines, AES3 digital audio, and Ethernet over twisted pair.
