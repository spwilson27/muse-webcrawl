Crystallography

Crystallography is the experimental science of determining the arrangement of atoms in crystalline solids. It studies crystal structure, symmetry, growth, and properties, and is fundamental to mineralogy, materials science, chemistry, and structural biology. The word derives from the Greek krýstallos (clear ice, rock crystal) and gráphein (to write).

History

Early contributions came from Nicolaus Steno, who observed the constancy of interfacial angles (1669), and René Just Haüy, who proposed that crystals are built from repeating units. In 1912 Max von Laue demonstrated the diffraction of X-rays by crystals, and William Henry Bragg and William Lawrence Bragg developed X-ray diffraction analysis, founding modern crystallography. Their work made it possible to determine atomic positions precisely.

Methods

The principal technique is diffraction: a beam of X-rays, electrons, or neutrons is scattered by a crystal, producing a pattern from which the electron density (or nuclear positions) can be reconstructed. X-ray crystallography requires growing suitable crystals and solves structures from small molecules to huge macromolecular complexes. Electron diffraction and cryo-electron microscopy serve microcrystals and large assemblies, while neutron diffraction locates hydrogen atoms and probes magnetism.

Symmetry and notation

Crystals are classified into 7 crystal systems, 32 point groups, and 230 space groups, described in the International Tables for Crystallography. Lattice geometry is specified by unit-cell parameters and Miller indices. Beyond perfectly periodic crystals, the field also covers twinning, defects, modular structures, and quasicrystals, whose discovery by Dan Shechtman in 1982 overturned the strict definition of a crystal.

Applications

Crystallography underpins drug design, metallurgy, semiconductor engineering, and the determination of landmark structures such as DNA (using Rosalind Franklin's diffraction data), penicillin, insulin, and the ribosome. The International Union of Crystallography oversees the field, and 2014 was celebrated as the International Year of Crystallography.
