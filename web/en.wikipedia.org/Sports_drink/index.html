Sports drink

Sports drinks, also known as electrolyte drinks, are beverages whose stated purpose is to help athletes replace water, electrolytes, and energy before, during and after training or competition. Though sometimes in conflation with energy drinks, sports drinks are a distinct category of functional beverages.

Composition

Typical sports drinks contain water, carbohydrates (usually 6–8% sugar), and electrolytes such as sodium and potassium lost in sweat. They are classified by tonicity as hypotonic, isotonic or hypertonic relative to body fluids. Added flavourings, colours and sometimes B-vitamins are common.

History

The category began with Gatorade, developed in 1965 by researchers at the University of Florida to help the Gators football team cope with heat. The product's commercial success inspired competitors such as Powerade and Lucozade Sport, and sports drinks became heavily marketed worldwide, often through athlete endorsements.

Effectiveness and criticism

Evidence supports sports drinks for prolonged vigorous exercise lasting more than about an hour, particularly in the heat, where they aid fluid retention and carbohydrate supply. For shorter or moderate exercise, water is generally sufficient, and health researchers have criticised the sugar content of sports drinks when consumed as everyday soft drinks, linking them to obesity and dental erosion.
