Ministry of education

A ministry of education is a national or sub-national government agency responsible for education policy, planning, and administration. Such ministries typically oversee primary, secondary, and often higher education; set curricula and examinations; fund schools and universities; license and regulate teachers; and gather educational statistics.

The head of the ministry, usually styled minister of education (or secretary of education in some countries, such as the United States Department of Education and Mexico's Secretaría de Educación Pública), is a member of the national cabinet.

Functions

Depending on the country, a ministry of education may control education directly or share responsibility with regional and local authorities. In federal states, such as Germany, Canada, and the United States, much educational authority rests with states, provinces, or Länder, and the federal role concentrates on funding, research, and civil-rights enforcement. Unitary states more often run national curricula and examinations centrally.

History

Central education ministries are largely a 19th-century creation, emerging as states took over schooling from churches and local communities. Prussia established an early system of state supervision; France created a ministry of public instruction in 1828; Japan founded its Monbushō in 1871 during the Meiji Restoration. England and Wales acquired a central education department in 1839 and a ministry in 1944.

Notable national ministries

  • India — Ministry of Education, renamed from the Ministry of Human Resource Development in 2020 under the National Education Policy
  • United Kingdom — Department for Education (England); education is devolved in Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland
  • United States — Department of Education, established as a cabinet department in 1979
  • France — Ministère de l'Éducation nationale
  • Japan — Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT)
