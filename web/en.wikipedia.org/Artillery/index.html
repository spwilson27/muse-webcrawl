Artillery comprises ranged weapons that launch munitions far beyond the range and power of infantry firearms. Historically the term included siege engines such as catapults and trebuchets; in modern usage it refers chiefly to gunpowder weapons — cannons, howitzers, mortars and multiple rocket launchers — and to the army branch that operates them. Artillery has been called the "king of battle" and caused the majority of combat casualties in the world wars of the 20th century.

History

Gunpowder artillery appeared in China by the 13th century and spread to Europe, where cannon transformed siege warfare — notably in the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople in 1453. Standardisation under Gribeauval and the reforms of Napoleonic France made field artillery a decisive arm. Rifled barrels, breech loading, recoil mechanisms and smokeless powder revolutionised guns in the later 19th century; the French Canon de 75 modèle 1897 pioneered rapid indirect fire, which dominated the First World War.

Modern artillery

Modern systems include towed and self-propelled howitzers (common calibres 105 mm, 122 mm, 152 mm and 155 mm), mortars, and rocket systems such as the M270 MLRS and HIMARS. Precision-guided munitions, counter-battery radar, drones and digital fire control have greatly increased accuracy. Naval guns and anti-aircraft artillery are related branches.
