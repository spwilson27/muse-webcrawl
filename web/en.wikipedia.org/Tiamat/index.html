Tiamat, primordial goddess of the salt sea

Tiamat is a primordial goddess of the sea in ancient Mesopotamian religion, representing the salt waters and primeval chaos. In the Babylonian creation epic Enūma Eliš, she is the mother of the first generation of gods and the antagonist slain by the storm god Marduk, who forms the heavens and earth from her divided body.

Mythology

At the beginning, before heaven and earth had names, Tiamat (salt water) mingled with Apsu (fresh water), giving birth to the gods. When Apsu plotted to destroy the noisy younger gods and was killed by Ea, Tiamat raised an army of monsters — including eleven great creatures and dragons — led by her new consort Kingu, to whom she gave the Tablet of Destinies. The gods, in terror, empowered Marduk as their champion and king. Marduk defeated Tiamat in single combat, trapping her in a net and splitting her skull; from her body he made the sky and earth, and from her eyes flowed the Tigris and Euphrates.

Interpretation

Scholars relate Tiamat to the Semitic root tihām ("sea"), cognate with Hebrew tehom, the watery deep of Genesis. The myth dramatises the ordering of cosmos from chaos and legitimised Babylon's supremacy and Marduk's kingship of the gods; it was recited at the Babylonian New Year (Akitu) festival. In modern popular culture, Tiamat appears as a multi-headed dragon in the game Dungeons & Dragons.
