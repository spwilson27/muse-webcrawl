Aluminium oxide

Aluminium oxide (or aluminium(III) oxide) is a chemical compound of aluminium and oxygen with the chemical formula Al2O3. It is the most commonly occurring of several aluminium oxides, and specifically identified as aluminium oxide. It is commonly called alumina.

Aluminium oxide
Formula: Al2O3
Molar mass: 101.96 g/mol
Melting point: 2,072 °C
Mohs hardness: 9 (corundum)

Occurrence and production

Its most common crystalline form, corundum, forms the gemstones ruby (red, chromium-bearing) and sapphire (other colours, iron/titanium-bearing). Alumina is refined from bauxite by the Bayer process and is the feedstock for aluminium metal via the Hall–Héroult process.

Properties and uses

Al2O3 is hard, chemically inert, an electrical insulator with high thermal conductivity for a ceramic. Uses include abrasives and sandpaper, refractory linings, ceramics and spark plugs, catalyst supports, chromatography media, fillers in plastics and cosmetics, and synthetic corundum for watch bearings and lasers.

Structure

In corundum (α-Al2O3), oxygen ions form a hexagonal close-packed lattice with aluminium ions occupying two-thirds of the octahedral interstices. Metastable γ-alumina has a defective spinel structure and high surface area.
