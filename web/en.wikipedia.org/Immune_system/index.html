Immune system

The immune system is a network of biological systems that protects an organism from diseases. It detects and responds to pathogens — viruses, bacteria, fungi, parasites — as well as cancer cells and foreign substances, distinguishing them from the organism's own tissues.

Immune system
Components: White blood cells, antibodies, complement, lymphatic system, thymus, spleen
Functions: Defence against infection, immune surveillance

Innate and adaptive immunity

Innate immunity provides immediate, non-specific defence through barriers, inflammation, phagocytes and natural killer cells. Adaptive immunity, found in vertebrates, uses B and T lymphocytes to produce antibodies such as immunoglobulin E and to retain immunological memory, the basis of vaccination.

Disorders

Immunodeficiency, from genetic disorders or infections such as HIV, weakens defence. Overactivity causes allergy and anaphylaxis; loss of self-tolerance causes autoimmune disease. Glucocorticoids such as cortisol suppress immune activity and are widely used as anti-inflammatory drugs.
