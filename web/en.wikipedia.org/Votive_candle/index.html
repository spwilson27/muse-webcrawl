Votive candle

A votive candle or prayer candle is a small candle, typically white or beeswax yellow, intended to be burnt as a votive offering in a religious ceremony. Worshippers light votives in churches, shrines and at gravesides as a tangible sign of prayer — for a special intention, in thanksgiving, in memory of the dead, or before an image of Christ, the Virgin Mary or a saint.

Practice

In Roman Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican and some Lutheran churches, votives are placed in racks of sand or tiered metal stands, often inside coloured glass holders (red is traditionally associated with the sanctuary lamp and with the Holy Spirit). A small donation is customary. Large candles in tall glass cylinders, popular in Latin Catholic cultures as veladoras, may burn for several days; novena candles are associated with nine-day prayer cycles.

The practice has ancient parallels in oil lamps offered at temples and tombs, and similar offerings of light exist in Judaism (the yahrzeit candle), Hinduism (diya lamps) and Buddhism (butter lamps). The flame symbolises the light of faith, the presence of the divine, and the continuation of prayer after the worshipper has left.
