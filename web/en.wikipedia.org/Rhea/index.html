Rhea

For other uses, see Rhea (disambiguation). This article is about the Titaness in Greek mythology.

Rhea or Rheia (Ancient Greek: Ῥέα or Ῥεία) is a mother goddess figure in Greek mythology, the sister and wife of Cronus and the mother of the Olympian gods Demeter, Hades, Hera, Hestia, Poseidon and Zeus. In Roman mythology, she was identified with the goddess Ops.

Rhea, mother of the Olympians

Mythology

Rhea was a daughter of the sky god Uranus and the earth goddess Gaia; she was thus one of the Titans. She married her brother Cronus, who, fearing a prophecy that he would be overthrown by his own child, swallowed each of their children at birth. When Rhea bore Zeus, her youngest, she hid him in a cave on Crete — in the version told in Hesiod's Theogony, on Mount Lyctus, where he was raised by the nymphs and the goat Amalthea — and gave Cronus a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes (the Omphalos stone) in the baby's place, which he swallowed unwittingly.

When Zeus grew up, he forced Cronus to disgorge his siblings — by some accounts with the help of the Titaness Metis, who supplied an emetic — and then fulfilled the prophecy by overthrowing the Titans in the ten-year war known as the Titanomachy.

Cult and associations

Rhea was strongly associated with Crete, where her son was hidden, and with the mountain mother goddess Cybele of Phrygia, with whom she was often conflated in later antiquity. Her symbols included the lion, by which her chariot was drawn in some depictions, and the tympanum (hand drum). In Hesiod's Theogony her name has been connected since antiquity with the Greek word for "flow" and "ease".

Namesakes

Rhea's name has been given to Saturn's second-largest moon, Rhea, discovered by Giovanni Domenico Cassini in 1672, and to the flightless South American birds called rheas, and to the asteroid 577 Rhea.
