Annapurna Circuit

LocationAnnapurna region, Nepal
Length160–230 km (100–145 mi), depending on route
Highest pointThorong La pass, 5,416 m (17,769 ft)
UseTrekking
SeasonAutumn and spring

The Annapurna Circuit is a trek within the Annapurna mountain range of central Nepal. The trail circles the Annapurna massif, passing through subtropical forests, terraced farmland, alpine meadows and the high, arid landscape of Manang before crossing the Thorong La pass at 5,416 metres. It is widely regarded as one of the world's great long-distance treks.

Route

The classic route begins near Besisahar in the Marshyangdi valley, climbs through Manang, crosses Thorong La into the Kali Gandaki valley, and descends past Muktinath and Jomsom toward Pokhara, passing between the peaks of Annapurna and Dhaulagiri through one of the deepest gorges in the world. Side trips include the Tilicho Lake trail. Trekkers typically take 12 to 21 days and stay in village teahouses.

Development

The Annapurna region opened to foreign trekkers in 1977 and is managed as the Annapurna Conservation Area, Nepal's largest protected area. Road construction in the 2000s shortened parts of the route and prompted the development of alternative "natural" trekking trails to preserve the classic walking experience.
