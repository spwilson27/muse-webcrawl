Bankside

Bankside is a district of London, England, on the south bank of the River Thames in the London Borough of Southwark, between Blackfriars Bridge and London Bridge. The name derives from "banke syde" in early medieval documents. For centuries outside the jurisdiction of the City of London, it became famous as an entertainment district of theatres, brothels and bear-baiting pits.

History

Because it lay in the liberty of the Clink under the Bishop of Winchester rather than the City, Bankside hosted activities banned across the river. The Rose Theatre (1587), the Swan, the Hope and Shakespeare's Globe (1599) stood here, staging plays by Shakespeare, Marlowe and Jonson. The area later became industrial, dominated by wharves, breweries and the Bankside Power Station.

Modern Bankside

Regeneration transformed the district from the 1990s. Bankside Power Station was converted into Tate Modern (opened 2000), one of the world's most visited art museums. A reconstruction of Shakespeare's Globe opened in 1997, the Millennium Bridge (2000) links Bankside to St Paul's Cathedral, and Borough Market at its eastern edge is one of London's oldest food markets. The Thames Path runs along the riverfront past the Golden Hinde replica and the Clink Prison Museum.
