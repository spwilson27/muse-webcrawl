Geocaching

Geocaching is an outdoor recreational activity in which participants use a Global Positioning System (GPS) receiver or mobile device to hide and seek containers, called geocaches or caches, at specific locations marked by coordinates all over the world. A typical cache is a small waterproof container containing a logbook and sometimes small trinkets for trading. Millions of active caches are listed on platforms such as Geocaching.com, operated by Groundspeak.

History

Geocaching began on 3 May 2000, the day after the United States discontinued Selective Availability, which had deliberately degraded civilian GPS accuracy. Computer consultant Dave Ulmer of Beavercreek, Oregon, hid a bucket in the woods near Portland and posted its coordinates to a Usenet newsgroup as the "Great American GPS Stash Hunt". Within days it had been found and logged. The name "geocaching", suggested by Matt Stum, replaced "GPS stash hunt" later that year, and Jeremy Irish launched Geocaching.com in September 2000.

Cache types

Traditional caches are containers at the posted coordinates. Multi-caches require visiting stages to derive final coordinates; mystery or puzzle caches require solving a puzzle. EarthCaches, administered by the Geological Society of America, have no container and instead teach a geological lesson at the site. Event caches are gatherings of geocachers, and trackable items such as Travel Bugs and geocoins move from cache to cache toward a goal.
