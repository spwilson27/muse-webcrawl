Status symbol

A status symbol is a visible, external symbol of one's social position, an indicator of economic or social status. Many luxury goods are often considered status symbols. The term relates to sociologist Thorstein Veblen's concept of conspicuous consumption, introduced in The Theory of the Leisure Class (1899).

History

Societies have long regulated display: sumptuary laws in medieval and early modern Europe restricted fabrics, colours, and furs to particular ranks. Court dress, large households of servants, and heraldic arms functioned as status markers, as did duelling scars and academic dress.

Types

Status symbols vary by culture and era and include:

  • Possessions: luxury cars, watches, jewellery, large houses, private jets
  • Occupations and titles: noble titles, prestigious professions, corner offices
  • Memberships: exclusive clubs, alma maters, frequent-flyer tiers
  • Experiences and knowledge: travel, familiarity with fine wine or art

Signalling theory

Economists and evolutionary biologists analyse status symbols through signalling theory: a signal is credible when it is costly to fake, so expensive displays honestly advertise underlying wealth or ability — an idea related to the handicap principle in biology.

Counter-signalling

High-status individuals sometimes deliberately avoid display ("quiet luxury" or countersignalling), since only the insecure need to prove status — illustrated by billionaires wearing plain clothing.
