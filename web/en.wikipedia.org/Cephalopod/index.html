Cephalopods
Class: Cephalopoda
Phylum: Mollusca
Species: About 800 living

A cephalopod is a member of the molluscan class Cephalopoda, which includes octopuses, squid, cuttlefish and nautiluses. They are bilaterally symmetrical marine animals with a prominent head, arms or tentacles, and — in most living groups — an internal or absent shell.

Biology

Cephalopods move by jet propulsion, have highly developed eyes and the largest nervous systems among invertebrates, and can change colour and texture via chromatophores for camouflage and signalling. Octopuses and cuttlefish show notable learning and problem-solving.

Diversity and history

Fossil cephalopods such as ammonites and belemnites dominated ancient seas. Living species range from tiny pygmy squid to the giant and colossal squid. They are important predators and fisheries resources. See Octopus and Squid.
