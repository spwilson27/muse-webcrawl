Cube

In geometry, a cube is a three-dimensional solid object bounded by six square faces, with three faces meeting at each vertex. It is a regular polyhedron, specifically a regular hexahedron, and one of the five Platonic solids. It has 6 faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices.

Properties

For a cube with edge length a, the surface area is 6a², the volume is a³, the face diagonal is √2·a, and the space diagonal is √3·a. The cube is the only regular hexahedron and is one of the five Platonic solids. Its dual polyhedron is the regular octahedron.

Cartesian coordinates

A cube centred at the origin with edges parallel to the axes can be described by the vertices (±1, ±1, ±1) for edge length 2. The cube is also the three-dimensional case of the hypercube family; its four-dimensional analogue is the tesseract.

Applications

Cubes occur in everyday objects such as dice and boxes, and the cubic crystal system is named after the shape. The problem of doubling the cube was one of the classical problems of antiquity, proved impossible with compass and straightedge.
