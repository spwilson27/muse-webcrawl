The Kapuas River (Indonesian: Sungai Kapuas) is a river in the Indonesian part of Borneo, in the province of West Kalimantan. At about 1,143 kilometres (710 mi), it is the longest river in Indonesia and the longest river on the island of Borneo, as well as one of the world's longest island rivers. It is the principal waterway of western Borneo and a vital transport artery for the interior.

Course

The Kapuas rises in the Müller mountain range in the centre of Borneo and flows westward through broad, swampy lowlands, passing the Kapuas Lakes region and the towns of Putussibau, Sintang (where it is joined by the Melawi River), Sanggau, and Pontianak, the provincial capital, which sits at the confluence with the smaller Landak River near the river's mouth on the South China Sea. The river is navigable for much of its length, and tributaries such as the Melawi extend navigation deep into the interior.

Ecology

The basin supports extensive peat swamp and freshwater swamp forests and exceptionally rich fish fauna, including arowana, giant snakehead, and many endemic species. The Kapuas Lakes area, centred on Lake Sentarum, is a Ramsar-listed wetland of seasonal lakes that fill and empty with the river's flood pulse. Threats include illegal logging, mining pollution — notably from gold mining — and conversion of peatland to plantations.

Human use

For centuries the river has linked coastal Malay sultanates such as Pontianak with Dayak peoples of the interior, carrying trade in forest products, rattan, and gold. Today it remains a highway for passengers and freight, as road infrastructure in West Kalimantan is limited. Floating houses and markets line its banks in Pontianak, where the river is crossed by bridges and celebrated in local culture; the equator monument stands nearby.
