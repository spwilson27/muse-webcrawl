Tree of life (biblical)

In Judaism and Christianity, the tree of life (Hebrew: עֵץ הַחַיִּים, ʿêṣ haḥayyîm) is a tree planted by God in the Garden of Eden whose fruit gives everlasting life. It appears in the Book of Genesis and again in the Book of Revelation, and is a pervasive image in later religious and literary tradition.

In Genesis

According to Genesis 2:9, the tree of life stood "in the midst of the garden", alongside the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. Adam and Eve were forbidden only the fruit of the latter. After they ate it and were expelled from Eden, God stationed cherubim and a flaming sword "to guard the way to the tree of life" (Genesis 3:24), lest they eat of it and live forever in their fallen state.

In other biblical books

The Book of Proverbs uses "tree of life" as a metaphor for wisdom, righteousness, fulfilled desire, and a soothing tongue (Proverbs 3:18; 11:30; 13:12; 15:4). In the Book of Revelation, the tree of life reappears in the New Jerusalem, bearing twelve kinds of fruit and leaves "for the healing of the nations" (Revelation 22:2), with access restored to those who are faithful.

In Jewish tradition

In rabbinic and Kabbalistic thought, the Torah is called a tree of life to those who hold fast to it. In Kabbalah, the Tree of Life is a diagram of ten interconnected sefirot representing the process of divine emanation and creation, widely used in Jewish mysticism and later adopted by Hermetic and occult traditions.

In Christianity

Christian interpreters have frequently read the tree of life as a type of the Cross, and the Eucharist is sometimes described as fruit of the tree of life restoring the immortality lost in Eden.
