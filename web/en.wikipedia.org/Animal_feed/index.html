Animal feed

Compound feed pellets are formulated for specific species and life stages

Animal feed is food given to domestic animals, especially livestock, in the course of animal husbandry. There are two basic types: fodder and forage. Used alone, the word feed more often refers to fodder — commercially prepared mixtures of ingredients such as grains, soybean meal, vitamins and minerals, formulated to provide balanced nutrition.

Types

Forage refers to plant material eaten in the field, such as pasture grass and browse. Fodder includes harvested feeds: hay, straw, silage, and compound (mixed) feeds. Compound feeds may be complete (supplying all required nutrients), concentrates (supplementing forage), or premixes of micronutrients.

Feed production and ingredients

The global feed industry produces more than a billion tonnes of compound feed per year. Major ingredients include maize (corn), wheat, barley, oats, soybean meal and other oilseed cakes, fish meal, and by-products such as bran, molasses and distillers' grains. Feed formulation balances energy, protein, amino acids (notably lysine and methionine), minerals such as calcium and phosphorus, and vitamins for each species and growth stage.

Regulation and safety

Feed safety is regulated because contaminants can pass into human food. Concerns include mycotoxins, heavy metals, antibiotic residues, and — after the BSE crisis — prohibitions on feeding meat-and-bone meal to ruminants. The industry also faces scrutiny over land use, since feed crops occupy a large share of global cropland, and over the use of fish meal and soy linked to deforestation.
