The Zócalo (officially the Plaza de la Constitución) is the main square in the heart of the historic centre of Mexico City. It has been a centre for the inhabitants of the city since pre-Hispanic times, when the Aztec city of Tenochtitlan stood there; the Mexica ceremonial precinct, the Templo Mayor, lay just to its northeast. The square is one of the largest city squares in the world, measuring roughly 240 metres (790 ft) on each side.

The square's popular name derives from a base (zócalo meaning plinth or pedestal) built in 1843 for a planned monument to Mexican independence; the monument itself was never constructed, but the name stuck. A giant Mexican flag raised and lowered daily by soldiers stands at its centre.

History

After the Spanish conquest, the plaza became the Plaza Mayor of the colonial capital, flanked by the viceregal palace (now the National Palace), the cathedral, and market arcades. It was formally named Plaza de la Constitución in 1813 in honour of the Spanish Constitution of Cádiz. Over the centuries the square has hosted markets, bullfights, celebrations, protests and concerts.

Surroundings

The Zócalo is bordered by the Mexico City Metropolitan Cathedral to the north, the National Palace to the east, the buildings of the Government of Mexico City to the south, and the Old Portal de Mercaderes to the west. The Templo Mayor archaeological site and museum lie just behind the cathedral.
