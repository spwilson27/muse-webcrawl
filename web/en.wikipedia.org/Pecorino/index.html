Pecorino


Pecorino Romano, one of Italy's oldest cheeses.

Pecorino is the name given to Italian cheeses made from sheep's milk; the word derives from pecora, Italian for sheep. Most pecorinos are hard, salty cheeses suited to grating, though young versions are softer and milder.

Protected varieties

Several pecorinos hold protected designation of origin (PDO/DOP) status under European law. The best known is Pecorino Romano, made for over two thousand years and now produced mostly in Sardinia, as well as in Lazio and Grosseto province. Others include Pecorino Sardo, Pecorino Toscano, Pecorino Siciliano, Pecorino di Filiano, and Pecorino Crotonese. A distinctive Sardinian variant, casu martzu, is ripened with live cheese-fly larvae and is banned from commercial sale in the EU.

Use

Aged pecorino is the traditional grating cheese of central and southern Italian cooking; Pecorino Romano is essential to Roman dishes such as cacio e pepe, amatriciana, and carbonara, where it is sometimes combined with or substituted for Parmigiano-Reggiano.
