David is a bronze statue by the early Renaissance sculptor Donatello, depicting the biblical hero David after his victory over Goliath. It is famous as the first unsupported standing nude statue cast since classical antiquity, and the first freestanding nude produced in the Renaissance.

Description

The youthful David stands in contrapposto, wearing only a shepherd's hat and boots, holding Goliath's sword, with one foot resting on the giant's severed head. A wing from Goliath's helmet brushes up David's inner leg. The figure's smooth, androgynous physique and enigmatic expression have attracted extensive commentary, including readings of homoerotic symbolism and of civic symbolism for the Florentine Republic.

History

The statue's date and original location are uncertain. It is usually dated to the 1440s and was recorded in 1469 in the courtyard of the Palazzo Medici in Florence, where it stood as a symbol of the Medici and of republican virtue against tyranny. Donatello also made an earlier, clothed marble David (1408–1409). The bronze David passed eventually to the Bargello museum, where it remains.

Significance

Art historians regard the statue as a landmark in the revival of classical naturalism and in the history of sculpture: a life-size independent nude, conceived in the round, without architectural support. It precedes Michelangelo's David by roughly sixty years.
