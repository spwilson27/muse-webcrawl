Janus kinase inhibitor

A Janus kinase inhibitor, also known as a JAK inhibitor or jakinib, is a class of medication that blocks the activity of one or more enzymes of the Janus kinase family (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, TYK2). By interfering with the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, these small-molecule drugs dampen the signalling of many pro-inflammatory cytokines at once, and are used chiefly as immunosuppressants in autoimmune and inflammatory diseases and in certain blood cancers.

Approved drugs

  • Ruxolitinib (JAK1/JAK2) — the first JAK inhibitor approved (2011), for myelofibrosis and polycythemia vera; topical forms are used for atopic dermatitis and vitiligo.
  • Tofacitinib (mainly JAK1/JAK3) — approved for rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ulcerative colitis and other indications.
  • Baricitinib (JAK1/JAK2) — used in rheumatoid arthritis, severe alopecia areata and atopic dermatitis; also authorised in hospitalised COVID-19 patients.
  • Upadacitinib and abrocitinib (JAK1-selective) — used in rheumatoid arthritis, atopic dermatitis, ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease.
  • Deucravacitinib — a selective allosteric TYK2 inhibitor approved for plaque psoriasis.
  • Fedratinib and pacritinib — developed for myelofibrosis.

Mechanism of action

JAK inhibitors are small molecules taken orally (some also formulated as creams). They enter cells and occupy the ATP-binding pocket of the kinase domain, preventing phosphorylation of downstream targets. Unlike biologic drugs, which neutralise a single cytokine or receptor outside the cell, JAK inhibitors act intracellularly and simultaneously reduce signalling from many cytokines sharing the same JAK pairings.

Safety

Common adverse effects include upper respiratory infections, herpes zoster reactivation (shingles), raised blood lipids and cytopenias. A large safety trial of tofacitinib in older rheumatoid arthritis patients with cardiovascular risk factors found higher rates of major cardiovascular events and malignancy than with TNF inhibitors, prompting regulators in the United States and Europe to add boxed warnings across the class for serious heart events, cancer, blood clots and death. Patients are generally screened for tuberculosis and viral hepatitis before treatment and monitored with blood counts and lipids.
