Milestone

TypeRoad marker
PurposeMark distance along a route
Historical originRoman Empire (miliarium)
Modern equivalentsMilepost, kilometre marker, highway location marker

A milestone is one of a series of numbered markers placed along a road or boundary at intervals of one mile (or, in metric countries, one kilometre), used to indicate distance travelled or remaining. The term derives from the stone markers erected along Roman roads, the most famous being the Milliarium Aureum, the "golden milestone" in the Roman Forum from which distances in the Empire were measured.

History

Roman milestones were cylindrical stone columns inscribed with the distance to the nearest city and often the name of the reigning emperor. In Britain, milestones proliferated with the turnpike trusts of the 18th century, which were required to mark distances for toll and coaching purposes; many survive as listed structures. Similar traditions existed in other road-building cultures, including the Ottoman Empire and Japan's ichirizuka mounds marking one ri.

Modern usage

Modern highways use smaller mileposts or marker posts, valued for locating incidents and maintenance. Though physical milestones have largely given way to signs and GPS, they remain a cultural touchstone.

Figurative meaning

By extension, "milestone" denotes a significant stage or achievement in any process — in project management, a scheduled point marking the completion of a major deliverable, and in human development, expected markers such as a child's first steps or words.
