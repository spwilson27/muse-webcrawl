Maserati 250F

Maserati 250F
Manufacturer: Maserati
Production: 1954–1960
Class: Formula One
Engine: 2.5 L straight-six (later V12)
Power: approx. 240–270 hp

The Maserati 250F was a Formula One racing car built by the Italian manufacturer Maserati and raced from January 1954 to November 1960. Regarded as one of the finest front-engined Grand Prix cars ever made, it won eight World Championship Grands Prix and carried Juan Manuel Fangio to memorable victories, most famously at the 1957 German Grand Prix at the Nürburgring, widely considered one of the greatest drives in motor racing history.

Design

The 250F was designed principally by Gioacchino Colombo, Vittorio Bellentani and Valerio Colotti around a tubular spaceframe chassis with independent front suspension and a de Dion rear axle. Its 2,493 cc straight-six engine, fed by three Weber carburettors and running on a mixture of petrol, methanol, ether and castor oil, produced about 240 bhp at first, later rising with development. A five-speed transaxle mounted at the rear gave the car near-ideal balance, making its handling forgiving and progressive — a quality privateers particularly loved. In total, 26 examples were built, and the car remained competitive for years through continuous evolution, including a lightweight version and a 2.5-litre V12 in 1957.

Racing history

The 250F won on its debut at the 1954 Argentine Grand Prix in Fangio's hands. Fangio won the 1957 Drivers' Championship with Maserati, clinching the title with his comeback victory at the Nürburgring, where he broke the lap record repeatedly to overhaul the Ferraris of Mike Hawthorn and Peter Collins after a slow pit stop. Stirling Moss also raced 250Fs as a privateer and praised the car's balance. In non-championship racing the 250F was even more successful, and examples continued racing into the 1960s, long after front-engined cars had faded elsewhere.
