Tupi people

The Tupi people are an Indigenous people of Brazil who, before European colonisation, inhabited most of the Brazilian Atlantic coast and parts of the Amazon basin. They spoke languages of the Tupi–Guarani family, and the related Old Tupi language became a lingua franca of colonial Brazil.

Society and history

Coastal Tupi groups such as the Tupinambá lived in large palisaded villages, practised slash-and-burn agriculture centred on manioc, and engaged in ritual warfare and ceremonial cannibalism of captured enemies, described in famous captivity accounts by Hans Staden and Jean de Léry. From the 16th century, Portuguese colonisation, disease, enslavement, and missionisation devastated Tupi populations.

Legacy

Despite this, Tupi language and culture profoundly shaped Brazil: thousands of Portuguese words for plants, animals, and places derive from Tupi, and peoples such as the Potiguara, Tupinambá, and Guarani (a closely related group) maintain Tupi–Guarani languages today.
