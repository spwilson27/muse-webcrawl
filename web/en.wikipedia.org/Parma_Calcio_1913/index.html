Parma Calcio 1913 is an Italian professional football club based in Parma, Emilia-Romagna. Founded in 1913, the club spent most of its early history in the lower divisions before rising to Serie A in 1990 and enjoying a golden decade in which, funded by the Parmalat company of Calisto Tanzi, it became one of Europe's strongest sides.

History

The club began as Verdi Football Club in July 1913, adopting the name Parma that December. It took its cross emblem and colours from the city. After decades in Serie B and C, promotion to Serie A came in 1990 under coach Nevio Scala. Captained at various times by players such as Gianfranco Zola, Hernán Crespo, Fabio Cannavaro, Lilian Thuram, and Gianluigi Buffon, Parma won the Coppa Italia three times (1992, 1999, 2002), the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1993, and the UEFA Cup in 1995 and 1999, and finished Serie A runners-up in 1997. The collapse of Parmalat in 2003 triggered financial crisis; the club was declared bankrupt in 2015, demoted to Serie D, and refounded under the present name. Parma then achieved three consecutive promotions, returning to Serie A in 2018, and has since moved between the top two divisions, winning promotion again as Serie B champions in 2024.

Stadium and identity

Parma plays at the Stadio Ennio Tardini, opened in 1923 with a capacity of about 22,000. The home shirt traditionally bears a black cross on white, while the club is also known as the Gialloblu for its yellow-and-blue colours. Noted rivalries include the Derby dell'Enza with Reggiana.
