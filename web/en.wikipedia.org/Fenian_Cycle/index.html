Fenian Cycle

The Fenian Cycle (Irish: an Fhiannaíocht), also known as the Finn Cycle or the Ossianic Cycle, is one of the four major cycles of early Irish mythology. It centres on the exploits of the mythical hunter-warrior Fionn mac Cumhaill (Finn MacCool) and his band of warriors, the Fianna, and is set mainly in the third century AD, in the reign of the High King Cormac mac Airt.

Content

Unlike the kings and provincial heroes of the Ulster Cycle, the heroes of the Fenian Cycle are roving warriors who live outside settled society, hunting, fighting, and feasting in the wilds of Ireland. The central surviving text is the Acallam na Senórach ("The Colloquy of the Ancients"), a long Middle Irish prose work, probably compiled around 1200, in which Fionn's surviving companions Caílte and Oisín recount the lore of the Fianna to Saint Patrick. Other important tales include The Pursuit of Diarmuid and Gráinne, the story of Fionn's acquisition of knowledge by tasting the Salmon of Knowledge, and numerous ballads and lays (laoithe) preserved in later manuscripts such as the Book of the Dean of Lismore.

Characters

Key figures include Fionn himself; his son Oisín (Ossian), the poet of the band; his grandson Oscar; the swift runner Caílte; the handsome Diarmuid Ua Duibhne; and the treacherous Goll mac Morna, whose clan is in long-standing feud with Fionn's. The cycle ends with the destruction of the Fianna at the Battle of Gabhra.

Influence

Fenian tales remained popular in Irish and Scottish Gaelic oral tradition into the modern era. James Macpherson's eighteenth-century "Ossian" poems, purportedly translated from ancient Gaelic sources, drew loosely on the cycle and sparked both a European literary sensation and a lasting controversy over their authenticity.
