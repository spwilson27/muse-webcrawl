Luis Buñuel

Born22 February 1900, Calanda, Spain
Died29 July 1983, Mexico City, Mexico
OccupationFilm director
MovementSurrealism

Luis Buñuel Portolés (1900–1983) was a Spanish filmmaker, widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential directors in the history of cinema. Working in Spain, Mexico and France, he was a leading figure of surrealist cinema.

Career

Buñuel's first film, Un Chien Andalou (1929), made with Salvador Dalí, became a landmark of surrealism, followed by the scandalous L'Âge d'Or (1930). After the Spanish Civil War he rebuilt his career in Mexico with films such as Los Olvidados (1950) and El ángel exterminador (1962). Returning to Europe, he directed Viridiana (1961), Belle de Jour (1967), The Discreet Charm of the Bourgeoisie (1972), which won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film, and That Obscure Object of Desire (1977).

Style

His films combine dream logic, black comedy and sharp critiques of bourgeois morality and the Catholic Church.
