Tahini

Tahini (from Arabic ṭaḥīna, from the root "to grind") is a condiment made from toasted, ground sesame seeds. A staple of Levantine and Middle Eastern cooking for centuries, it is the essential ingredient of hummus and baba ghanoush and a sauce, dip and dessert base in its own right.

Production

Sesame seeds are soaked, hulled (in most commercial production), roasted and stone-ground into a pourable paste. Natural tahini separates on standing, with oil rising to the top, and is stirred before use. Hulled seeds give a lighter, smoother and less bitter paste than unhulled.

Culinary uses

Whisked with lemon juice, garlic and water, tahini becomes the sauce served over falafel, shawarma and grilled fish throughout the Levant. It enriches hummus, sweetens into halva with sugar syrup, flavours Chinese sesame noodles and Japanese goma dressings in related forms, and appears in modern baking and dressings worldwide as a nut-free, vegan staple.

Nutrition

Tahini is energy-dense, rich in unsaturated fats, protein, calcium, magnesium and B vitamins. Sesame is also a recognised allergen, subject to labelling rules in the US (under the FASTER Act, from 2023) and the EU.
