Rural area

A rural area or the countryside is a geographic area located outside towns and cities. Typical rural areas have a low population density and small settlements, and agricultural land and forests often predominate over built-up areas. What counts as rural varies between countries, and official definitions are used for planning, funding and statistical purposes.

Definitions

Definitions usually combine settlement size, population density and distance from urban centres. In the United States, the Census Bureau defines rural as all territory outside urban areas of 2,500 or more people (a threshold revised in 2020 to use housing units), while the Office of Management and Budget uses a county-based metropolitan classification. In the United Kingdom, settlements of fewer than 10,000 people are generally classed as rural. International bodies such as the OECD classify regions by the share of population living in rural communities.

Characteristics

Rural economies traditionally centre on agriculture, forestry, fishing and resource extraction, though tourism, commuting and remote work have diversified many rural areas. Residents often face longer distances to hospitals, secondary schools and other services, sparser public transport, and — historically — poorer broadband and mobile coverage, issues collectively described as the rural–urban digital divide.

Rural life and policy

Governments pursue rural development policies covering farm support, service provision, housing and infrastructure. Rural areas are also valued for landscape, biodiversity and recreation, and most protected landscapes and national parks are rural. In many high-income countries, counter-urbanisation has brought population growth to accessible rural districts even as remote areas continue to lose young people.
