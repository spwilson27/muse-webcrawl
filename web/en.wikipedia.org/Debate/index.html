Debate

Debate is a process of formal discourse in which opposing arguments are presented on a particular topic. Debates occur in public meetings, parliaments, courts and educational settings, and may be judged competitions with formal rules and timed speeches. Unlike ordinary argument, a debate typically assigns positions to participants, requires them to support claims with evidence and reasoning, and gives each side the opportunity to rebut the other.

Competitive debate

In schools and universities, debate is a popular extracurricular activity, particularly in the United States and the United Kingdom. Formats include British Parliamentary debate, used at the World Universities Debating Championship, with four teams of two; Lincoln–Douglas debate, a one-on-one format centred on values; and policy (cross-examination) debate, in which pairs argue for or against a policy resolution over a season. Judges evaluate argumentation, evidence, strategy and delivery.

Political and public debates

Legislative bodies such as parliaments and congresses conduct their business through structured debate governed by rules of procedure. Televised debates between election candidates — beginning most famously with the Kennedy–Nixon debates of 1960 in the United States — have become major events in democratic campaigns, and are credited with influencing voter perceptions even when they rarely change many minds directly.

Debate and education

Supporters argue that debate teaches research, critical thinking, public speaking and the ability to see multiple sides of an issue; studies associate participation with improved academic outcomes. Critics note that competitive formats can reward speed, aggression and tactical argument over accuracy and genuine understanding.
