Flora family

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Flora family (FIN: 402) is a family of stony asteroids in the inner asteroid belt, named after its largest member, 8 Flora. It is one of the most populous asteroid families, with thousands of identified members, and lies close to the ν6 secular resonance at the inner edge of the belt — a dynamical gateway that delivers family members into Earth-crossing orbits, making the Flora family a major source of near-Earth asteroids and, consequently, of meteorites.

Characteristics

Flora family members are S-type (silicaceous) asteroids with albedos around 0.2–0.3. The family formed from the catastrophic disruption of a parent body roughly 200 km in diameter; 8 Flora, at about 140 km across, is likely the largest remnant of that parent. Because the family is comparatively old and dynamically spread, distinguishing true family members from background interlopers is difficult, and membership estimates vary.

Connection to meteorites

Spectroscopic and dynamical studies link the Flora family to the L chondrite meteorites, among the most common meteorites falling to Earth; the Ordovician fossil meteorite record — a spike in L chondrite falls some 470 million years ago — is interpreted as the breakup of the L chondrite parent body, with the Flora family as a possible remnant population. The Itokawa asteroid visited by Hayabusa is also an S-type body from the inner belt with affinities to this population.
