Aqaba is the only coastal city in Jordan and the largest city on the Gulf of Aqaba, situated at the northeastern tip of the Red Sea between Eilat in Israel to the west and Saudi Arabia to the south. Its port is Jordan's sole maritime gateway, and the city is a popular resort known for warm weather, beaches, and coral-reef diving.

History

The area has been inhabited since about 4000 BC and was known in antiquity as Elath and later as Ayla, an important port under Roman, Byzantine, and early Islamic rule; excavations have revealed one of the world's oldest purpose-built churches (late 3rd century). The Mamluk-era Aqaba Fort guards the town. During the First World War, Aqaba was captured in July 1917 by Arab forces advised by T. E. Lawrence in the famous Battle of Aqaba, a turning point of the Arab Revolt.

Modern city

In 2001 Aqaba was designated a Special Economic Zone (ASEZ) with low taxes and customs duties to attract investment in tourism, logistics, and industry. The port handles Jordan's imports and exports, including phosphates and potash, and has been expanded southward toward the Saudi border. The city is served by King Hussein International Airport, and a giant flagpole flying the flag of the Arab Revolt—among the tallest in the world—dominates the waterfront.
