Lumbini (Sanskrit: लुम्बिनी, "the lovely") is a Buddhist pilgrimage site in the Rupandehi District of Lumbini Province, Nepal, near the Indian border. According to Buddhist tradition, it is the place where Queen Maya Devi gave birth to Siddhartha Gautama — who became the Buddha — in 563 BCE (some traditions place the date later, around 480 BCE). It is one of the four most holy sites of Buddhism, alongside Bodh Gaya, Sarnath and Kushinagar.

The birth tradition

Tradition holds that Maya Devi, travelling from her husband's capital Kapilavastu to her parents' home, stopped in the sal grove of Lumbini and gave birth standing, holding the branch of a sal tree, near a pond. The newborn was received by the gods Brahma and Indra. The Maya Devi Temple stands over the reputed exact spot, marked by a "marker stone" found during excavations, and beside it lies the sacred pond in which Maya is said to have bathed.

History

The emperor Ashoka visited Lumbini in the 3rd century BCE and erected a polished sandstone pillar; its Brahmi inscription records his pilgrimage and notes that "here the Buddha was born," making it the earliest epigraphic evidence identifying the site. Chinese pilgrims Faxian (5th century CE) and Xuanzang (7th century CE) described the site in their travel accounts. With the decline of Buddhism in the region, Lumbini was lost to memory until 1896, when the Ashoka pillar was rediscovered by General Khadga Shumsher Rana and the German archaeologist Alois Anton Führer.

Modern development

Lumbini was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997. A master plan designed by the Japanese architect Kenzō Tange in 1978 laid out a sacred garden, a monastic zone in which Buddhist countries and traditions have built monasteries in their national styles, and a central canal and eternal peace flame. Pilgrims and tourists visit throughout the year, with the largest gatherings on Buddha Jayanti (Vesak).
