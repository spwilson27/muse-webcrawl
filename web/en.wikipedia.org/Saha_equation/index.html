Saha ionization equation

The Saha ionization equation is a formula of statistical mechanics and thermodynamics that describes the degree of ionization of a gas in thermal equilibrium as a function of temperature, density and the ionization energies of its atoms. It was derived by the Indian astrophysicist Meghnad Saha in 1920.

Equation

The equation relates the number densities of successive ionization stages: the ratio of ions to neutral atoms grows with temperature (roughly as T3/2 times a Boltzmann factor e−I/kT, where I is the ionization energy) and falls as the electron density increases. Partition functions of the atom and ion weight the result.

Applications

Saha's equation explained the sequence of stellar spectral types as a temperature sequence in ionization, transforming stellar astrophysics, and it remains fundamental in plasma physics. It is valid for sufficiently dilute, hot gases; at high densities, corrections for particle interactions and pressure ionization are needed. It was historically important in Cecilia Payne's 1925 determination that stars are composed mainly of hydrogen.
