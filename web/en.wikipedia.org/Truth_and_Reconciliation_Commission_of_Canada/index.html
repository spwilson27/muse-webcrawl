Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada (TRC) was a truth and reconciliation commission active in Canada from 2008 to 2015, established as part of the Indian Residential Schools Settlement Agreement. Its mandate was to document the history and lasting impacts of the Canadian Indian residential school system on Indigenous children who were forcibly removed from their families, and to inform all Canadians about that history.

Work

The commission was chaired by Justice (later Senator) Murray Sinclair, with commissioners Marie Wilson and Chief Wilton Littlechild. It held seven national events across Canada, gathered statements from more than 6,750 survivors and others, and assembled millions of records from government and church archives, later transferred to the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation at the University of Manitoba.

Findings

The TRC's final report, released in 2015, concluded that the residential school system — which operated from the 1880s until the last school closed in 1996 and through which about 150,000 First Nations, Métis, and Inuit children passed — amounted to a policy of cultural genocide. It documented widespread physical and sexual abuse and the deaths of thousands of children, and issued 94 Calls to Action directed at governments, churches, and Canadian society, addressing child welfare, education, language, health, justice, and commemoration. Canada marks 30 September, the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, in response to the commission's work.
