Cross River gorilla

Cross River gorilla
Scientific name: Gorilla gorilla diehli
Conservation status: Critically Endangered (IUCN)
Population: ~200–300 mature individuals
Range: Nigeria–Cameroon border region
Diet: Herbivorous (fruit, leaves, stems)

The Cross River gorilla (Gorilla gorilla diehli) is a critically endangered subspecies of the western gorilla (Gorilla gorilla). It was named a new species in 1904 by Paul Matschie, a mammalian taxonomist working at the Humboldt University Zoological Museum in Berlin, but its populations were not systematically surveyed until 1987.

Habitat and behaviour

Only about 200 to 300 Cross River gorillas are thought to remain in the wild, spread across roughly 12,000 km² of mountainous forest along the headwaters of the Cross River on the border between Nigeria and Cameroon. They differ from western lowland gorillas in their smaller skulls, teeth and hands. Cross River gorillas are especially wary of humans and live in small groups, typically of fewer than 20 individuals, feeding mainly on fruit, leaves and lianas such as Aframomum.

Conservation

The subspecies is threatened by habitat loss to agriculture and logging, and by hunting for bushmeat. Conservation measures include the creation of protected areas such as Cameroon's Takamanda and Kagwene reserves and Nigeria's Afi Mountain Wildlife Sanctuary and Mbe Mountains sanctuary, community-based conservation programmes, and cross-border agreements between Nigeria and Cameroon. Camera-trap images obtained in 2012 provided rare photographic evidence of the animals in the wild.
