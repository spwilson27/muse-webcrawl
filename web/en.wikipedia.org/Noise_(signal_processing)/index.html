Noise (signal processing)

In signal processing, noise is a general term for all unwanted (and, in general, unknown) modifications that a signal may suffer during capture, storage, transmission, processing, or conversion.

Types of noise

Noise is often classified by its statistical properties or its spectrum. White noise has constant power across frequencies; pink (1/f) noise and thermal (Johnson–Nyquist) noise from resistive elements are common in electronics; shot noise arises from the discrete nature of charge carriers. Noise may also be described by its source — environmental interference, quantisation error in analogue-to-digital conversion, or sensor noise in images (displayed as random grain or "snow").

Measurement and reduction

The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) quantifies how much a signal stands out from background noise, usually expressed in decibels. Noise reduction techniques include filtering, shielding, averaging of repeated measurements, and digital noise reduction algorithms. Noise is not always detrimental: dithering exploits added noise to reduce quantisation artefacts, and stochastic resonance uses noise to amplify weak signals.
