Rainbow (also known as Ritchie Blackmore's Rainbow) is a British rock band formed in London in 1975 by guitarist Ritchie Blackmore after he left Deep Purple. The band's early albums with vocalist Ronnie James Dio combined hard rock with fantasy-themed lyrics and are considered classics of heavy metal; later line-ups moved toward a more commercial, radio-friendly sound.

History

Blackmore recorded Ritchie Blackmore's Rainbow (1975) with Dio and members of the band Elf. The albums Rising (1976) and Long Live Rock 'n' Roll (1978), featuring drummer Cozy Powell, cemented the band's reputation. After Dio's departure, vocalist Graham Bonnet sang on Down to Earth (1979), which produced hits "Since You Been Gone" and "All Night Long". With Joe Lynn Turner on vocals (1981–84) the band scored further hits including "Stone Cold". Blackmore disbanded Rainbow in 1984 to re-form Deep Purple, revived it in the 1990s with singer Doogie White (Stranger in Us All, 1995), and again from 2015 with Ronnie Romero for live shows.

Legacy

Rainbow's Dio-era music influenced power metal and neoclassical metal; "Man on the Silver Mountain", "Stargazer" and "Kill the King" remain staples of the genre.
