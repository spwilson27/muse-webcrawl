Gold King Mine waste water spill

The 2015 Gold King Mine waste water spill was an environmental incident that began on 5 August 2015, when United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) personnel, along with workers for a contracting firm, caused the release of toxic mine waste water into Cement Creek, a tributary of the Animas River in Colorado.

The spill

The crew had been excavating at the entrance of the abandoned Gold King Mine near Silverton, Colorado, intending to pump out and treat contaminated water ponded behind debris. The excavation breached the plug holding back the water, releasing approximately three million gallons of acidic mine water laden with heavy metals, including cadmium, lead, arsenic, beryllium, zinc, iron and copper. The plume turned the Animas River a vivid mustard-orange colour.

Impact

The spill flowed through the Animas into the San Juan River in New Mexico and onward toward Lake Powell, affecting communities in Colorado, New Mexico, Utah and the Navajo Nation. Water intakes, irrigation and recreation were temporarily shut down. The EPA was criticised for its delayed notification of downstream communities and officials. In September 2015 the EPA accepted responsibility for the spill.

Aftermath

The site was designated a Superfund site (the Bonita Peak Mining District) in 2016, and an interim water treatment plant was installed to treat the mine discharge. The incident drew national attention to the estimated tens of thousands of abandoned mines leaking contaminated water across the western United States and to the absence of a dedicated funding mechanism for their cleanup.
