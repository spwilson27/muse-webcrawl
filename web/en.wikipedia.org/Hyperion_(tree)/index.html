Hyperion (tree)

Hyperion is a coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens) in Northern California, in Redwood National Park, that is the world's tallest known living tree. It measures 116.07 metres (380.8 ft) tall and was discovered on 25 August 2006 by naturalists Chris Atkins and Michael Taylor. The tree was verified as the tallest living tree by Stephen Sillett, surpassing the previous record-holder, the Stratosphere Giant.

Hyperion is estimated to be 700 to 800 years old and to contain about 530 cubic metres (18,600 cu ft) of wood. Its exact location is kept secret to protect it from damage by visitors; in 2022 the National Park Service closed the surrounding area and made approaching the tree punishable by fines and imprisonment.

Context

The tree was named after Hyperion, a Titan of Greek mythology. Coast redwoods grow in a narrow fog belt along the Pacific coast and include several of the tallest trees on Earth; Hyperion grows on a hillside rather than in the more sheltered alluvial flats. Researchers study its canopy ecosystem, which supports other plants and animals high above the ground.
