Memoir

A memoir (from French mémoire, meaning memory or reminiscence) is a nonfiction narrative based on personal memories. Unlike an autobiography, which typically covers the author's entire life chronologically, a memoir usually focuses on a particular period, theme, or series of events in the author's life.

Characteristics

Memoirs are written in the first person and emphasize the author's emotional truth and personal experience rather than a strictly factual record. The genre often concentrates on a turning point, a relationship, a place, or a profession. Memoirists shape real events into a narrative arc, selecting detail for thematic effect.

History

The form has ancient roots. Julius Caesar's Commentarii de Bello Gallico is an early example of a leader's memoir. Saint Augustine's Confessions (c. 400 AD) is a landmark of spiritual autobiography. The modern literary memoir flourished in the 20th and 21st centuries, with works such as Maya Angelou's I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings and Frank McCourt's Angela's Ashes becoming bestsellers.

Memoir vs. autobiography

An autobiography generally spans a whole life and stresses factual, chronological completeness, while a memoir is more impressionistic and selective, organized around themes and memories.
