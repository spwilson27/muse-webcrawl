Plant milk

Plant milk is a plant-based drink produced by soaking and grinding plant material in water, yielding a milky liquid used as a substitute for dairy milk. Common varieties are made from soy, almond, oat, rice, coconut, hemp, pea, and cashew. Plant milks are consumed by people who are lactose-intolerant, allergic to cow's milk, vegan, or who prefer them for environmental, animal-welfare, or taste reasons.

History

Soy milk has been made in China since at least the Han dynasty era and was common across East Asia for centuries; almond milk appears in medieval European and Middle Eastern cookery, often for use during Lent. Commercial plant milks expanded rapidly from the 1990s onward, with oat milk surging in the 2010s following the success of Swedish brand Oatly.

Nutrition and regulation

Nutritional profiles vary widely: soy milk is closest to cow's milk in protein content, while almond and rice milks are much lower in protein. Most commercial products are fortified with calcium and vitamins D and B12. In the European Union the word "milk" is legally reserved for animal milk (with a few traditional exceptions), so products are marketed as "drinks"; the United States FDA permits the term "milk" with plant-source qualifiers.

Environmental impact

Life-cycle analyses generally find plant milks produce fewer greenhouse-gas emissions and use less land than dairy milk, though almond milk requires substantial irrigation water and rice milk generates methane.
