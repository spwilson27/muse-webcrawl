Glass transition

The glass transition is the gradual and reversible transition in amorphous materials — or in amorphous regions of partly crystalline materials — from a hard and relatively brittle "glassy" state into a viscous or rubbery state as the temperature is increased. The characteristic temperature of this transition is the glass transition temperature, denoted Tg.

Characteristics

Unlike melting, the glass transition is not a first-order phase transition: there is no latent heat and no abrupt change in volume, but properties such as the heat capacity, thermal expansion coefficient and viscosity change markedly over the transition range. The observed value of Tg depends on the measurement method and on the cooling or heating rate, reflecting the kinetic nature of the phenomenon as molecular motion effectively freezes on experimental timescales.

Importance

The glass transition governs the service behaviour of polymers, metallic glasses, and oxide glasses. For thermoplastics it marks the boundary between rigid use and rubbery flexibility; for amorphous pharmaceuticals and foods, storage below Tg slows crystallisation and degradation. Below its Tg of roughly 500–600 °C, window glass behaves as an elastic solid, while above it the melt can be worked and blown.
