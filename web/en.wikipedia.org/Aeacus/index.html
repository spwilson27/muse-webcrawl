Aeacus

Aeacus was a mythological king of the island of Aegina in the Saronic Gulf. He was a son of Zeus and the nymph Aegina, and the father of the heroes Peleus and Telamon. Renowned for his piety and justice, Aeacus became after death one of the three judges of the dead in the underworld, alongside Minos and Rhadamanthus.

Aeacus
Parents: Zeus and Aegina
Offspring: Peleus, Telamon, Phocus
Role: King of Aegina; judge of the dead

Mythology

According to myth, Hera, jealous of Aegina, sent a plague that destroyed the population of the island. Aeacus prayed to Zeus, who repopulated Aegina by transforming ants into people — the Myrmidons. Aeacus is also said to have helped build the walls of Troy and to have arbitrated disputes among the gods.

Judgeship in Hades

Because of his reputation for justice, Aeacus was appointed after death to judge the shades of Europeans entering the underworld and to hold the keys of Hades.
