Cabinet of Iceland

The Cabinet of Iceland (Icelandic: Ríkisstjórn Íslands) is the collective decision-making body of the government of Iceland, composed of the Prime Minister and the government's ministers. It is formally appointed by the President of Iceland following parliamentary elections and the formation of a governing coalition acceptable to the Althing, Iceland's parliament.

Because Iceland's party system is fragmented, cabinets are almost always coalitions of two or three parties. The Prime Minister chairs cabinet meetings and allocates portfolios; ministries have varied in number and structure, typically numbering around nine to twelve, and have been reorganized repeatedly to combine or split policy areas such as fisheries, environment, social affairs and finance.

History

The cabinet traces its origin to the establishment of home rule in 1904, when Hannes Hafstein became the first Minister for Iceland within the Danish government, and to full cabinet government under the Kingdom of Iceland after 1918 and the Republic proclaimed in 1944. Notable cabinets include the long-serving governments of the Independence Party and Progressive Party coalition era, the post-2008 financial-crisis government of Jóhanna Sigurðardóttir—the world's first openly lesbian head of government—and the broad three-party coalition led by Katrín Jakobsdóttir from 2017 to 2024.
