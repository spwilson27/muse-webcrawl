Building automation

Building automation (BAS) is the automatic centralized control of a building's essential systems — heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC), lighting, power systems, fire and security systems, and other interrelated functions — through a building management system (BMS) or building automation and control network. The objectives of building automation are improved occupant comfort, efficient operation of building systems, reduction in energy consumption and operating costs, and improved life cycle of utilities.

Overview

A BAS connects distributed controllers and sensors over networks using protocols such as BACnet, LonWorks, Modbus, and KNX. Direct digital control (DDC) systems schedule equipment, monitor temperatures and energy use, and alert operators to faults, allowing a facility team to manage a large building from a central workstation or remotely.

History

Automated controls began with pneumatic thermostats and controls in the early 20th century, followed by electromechanical and then electronic systems. The energy crises of the 1970s drove adoption of energy management systems; modern systems integrate with information technology networks and support analytics, demand response, and predictive maintenance.

Smart buildings

Contemporary building automation is a foundation of the "smart building" concept, combining automation with occupancy analytics, indoor environmental quality monitoring, and grid-interactive operation to reduce energy use and carbon emissions.
