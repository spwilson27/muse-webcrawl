Languages of Ireland

The island of Ireland has several languages. The main language spoken today is English — in the form of Hiberno-English — while the Irish language (Gaeilge), a Celtic language, is the national and first official language of the Republic of Ireland and is recognised in Northern Ireland.

Irish

Irish was the dominant language of Ireland for most of recorded history. It declined sharply in the 19th century through anglicisation, emigration and the Great Famine, surviving most strongly in western Gaeltacht areas. Census figures in the Republic show large numbers able to speak Irish, with a much smaller number of daily speakers outside education. Irish is compulsory in schools in the Republic.

English

English, introduced from the 12th century and spread especially from the 17th century, is the first language of the great majority. Hiberno-English retains influences from Irish in vocabulary, idiom and syntax.

Other languages

Ulster Scots is spoken by some communities in Northern Ireland and County Donegal. Shelta is a traditional language of Irish Travellers. Irish Sign Language (ISL) is recognised in the Republic, while British Sign Language is also used in Northern Ireland. Recent immigration has added Polish and many other languages as widely spoken home languages.
