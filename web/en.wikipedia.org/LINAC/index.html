LINAC

A medical linear accelerator
RedirectSee Linear particle accelerator

LINAC is an abbreviation most commonly used for a linear particle accelerator, a type of particle accelerator that propels charged particles to high speeds along a straight line using oscillating electric fields. The term "linac" is used generically in physics and specifically in medicine.

Physics linacs

In research physics, linear accelerators accelerate electrons, protons and ions for experiments and as injectors for synchrotron storage rings and colliders. The longest linac, at SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory in California, is about 3.2 km (2 mi) long and powered the SLAC linac collider experiments; large facilities such as the European XFEL and LCLS use superconducting linacs. Linacs are also used in neutron spallation sources such as the Spallation Neutron Source in Tennessee.

Medical linacs

In hospitals, "linac" usually means a medical linear accelerator used in external-beam radiation therapy for cancer. A medical linac accelerates electrons to energies of several MeV; the electrons either treat superficial tumours directly or strike a target to produce high-energy X-rays (photons) shaped by collimators into beams conforming to the tumour. Modern machines are mounted on rotating gantries and are used for techniques including IMRT, VMAT, stereotactic radiosurgery and image-guided radiotherapy.

Other meanings

The capitalisation LINAC may also appear as an acronym in other technical contexts, but in scientific literature it overwhelmingly denotes the linear accelerator.
