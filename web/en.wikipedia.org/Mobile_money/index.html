Mobile money

Mobile money is a technology that allows people to store, send, and receive money using a mobile phone, typically through an account issued by a mobile network operator or a partner bank. It is especially significant in developing countries, where it gives people without bank accounts access to financial services through basic handsets and networks of local agents who convert cash to and from electronic value.

History

The best-known mobile money service, M-Pesa, was launched in Kenya in 2007 by Safaricom and grew rapidly into the country's dominant payment system, used for remittances, bill payment, wages, and savings. Similar services followed across Africa, South Asia, and Latin America, including MTN Mobile Money, bKash in Bangladesh, and GCash in the Philippines.

Services

Mobile money accounts typically support person-to-person transfers, airtime purchases, merchant payments, bill payment, and cash-in and cash-out through agents. Many providers have added savings, microcredit, insurance, and international remittances. Transactions on basic phones are usually conducted through USSD or SMS menus, with smartphone apps increasingly common.

Impact and regulation

Research has credited mobile money with advancing financial inclusion and, in the case of M-Pesa, with lifting a measurable share of Kenyan households out of poverty by improving risk-sharing and occupational choice. Regulators generally require e-money issuers to hold customer funds in trust accounts and comply with anti-money-laundering rules, including tiered know-your-customer requirements.
