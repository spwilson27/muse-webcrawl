Dental prosthesis

A dental prosthesis is an intraoral (inside the mouth) prosthesis used to restore (reconstruct) intraoral defects such as missing teeth, missing parts of teeth, and missing soft or hard structures of the jaw and palate. Prosthodontics is the dental specialty that focuses on dental prostheses. Such prostheses are used to rehabilitate mastication (chewing), improve aesthetics, and aid speech.

Types

Dental prostheses include crowns, which cap an individual tooth; bridges, fixed restorations spanning the gap of one or more missing teeth supported by adjacent teeth; dentures, removable appliances replacing some (partial dentures) or all (complete dentures) of the teeth in an arch; and dental implants, titanium fixtures anchored in the jawbone that can support crowns, bridges, or dentures. Maxillofacial prostheses, such as obturators, additionally replace defects of the palate or other structures.

Materials and fabrication

Prostheses are fabricated in dental laboratories, increasingly with computer-aided design and manufacturing (CAD/CAM), from materials including acrylic resin, porcelain and other ceramics, zirconia, metal alloys, and combinations such as porcelain fused to metal. Design must account for occlusion, the condition of the supporting tissues, and the patient's hygiene and maintenance.
