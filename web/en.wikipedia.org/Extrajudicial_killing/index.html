Extrajudicial killing

An extrajudicial killing is the killing of a person by governmental authorities or their agents without the sanction of any judicial proceeding or due legal process. Such killings violate the right to life protected by the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, and include summary executions, deaths in custody caused by torture or neglect, and killings by death squads.

Forms and examples

Extrajudicial killings have been documented in many settings: the operations of death squads during the "dirty wars" in Latin America, in which thousands of suspected dissidents were also forcibly disappeared; police "encounter killings" staged as shoot-outs in South Asia; and mass killings of alleged drug offenders during the Philippine drug war under President Rodrigo Duterte, examined by the International Criminal Court. "Targeted killing" by states of suspected terrorists outside declared battlefields occupies a debated boundary, its defenders invoking self-defence and the laws of armed conflict, its critics charging that it substitutes executive suspicion for trial.

International response

The United Nations maintains a Special Rapporteur on extrajudicial, summary or arbitrary executions, and the Minnesota Protocol (revised 2016) sets international standards for investigating potentially unlawful deaths. Impunity for perpetrators is widely identified as the condition that allows such killings to continue.
