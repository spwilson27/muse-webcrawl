Musical mode

In music theory, a mode is a type of musical scale coupled with a set of characteristic melodic and harmonic behaviours. The term derives from the medieval system of eight church modes, itself adapted from ancient Greek theory, and is now applied to scales and melodic frameworks in many traditions.

The diatonic modes

The seven modes of the major scale are Ionian (identical to the major scale), Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian (the natural minor scale) and Locrian. Each begins on a different degree of the parent scale, producing a distinctive pattern of whole and half steps and a characteristic colour — Dorian, for example, is a minor scale with a raised sixth degree.

Usage

Modes governed Western music before the consolidation of major–minor tonality around 1600, organising plainchant and Renaissance polyphony. Modal thinking survives in folk music, and was revived in the 20th century by composers such as Debussy and Bartók and by jazz musicians — Miles Davis's Kind of Blue (1959) is the classic of modal jazz. Comparable modal frameworks include the Indian raga and the Arab maqam.
