BSA

BSA most commonly refers to the Birmingham Small Arms Company, a British industrial conglomerate founded in 1861 in the Gun Quarter of Birmingham, England. Originally a maker of firearms, BSA grew into a major manufacturer of bicycles, motorcycles, cars and military equipment.

History

The company was formed by a group of Birmingham gunsmiths who had supplied arms during the Crimean War and adopted mechanised production. BSA expanded into bicycles in the 1880s and motorcycles from 1910; by the 1950s BSA was the largest motorcycle producer in the world, with famous models including the Gold Star and the Bantam. It also owned the Daimler car company from 1910 to 1960.

Decline and legacy

Competition from Japanese manufacturers and poor management brought the group's motorcycle business to collapse in the early 1970s; its remnants were folded into Norton Villiers Triumph. The BSA name survives on airguns and, following a revival by India's Mahindra group, on a new generation of motorcycles launched in the 2020s.

Other meanings

The abbreviation BSA may also refer to the Boy Scouts of America, bovine serum albumin (a laboratory protein), the Business Software Alliance, or body surface area in medicine.
