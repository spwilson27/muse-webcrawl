The Pinaceae, or pine family, are conifer trees or shrubs, including many of the well-known conifers of commercial importance such as cedars, firs, hemlocks, larches, pines and spruces. The family is included in the order Pinales. It is the largest extant family of conifers, with around 220–250 species in 11 genera, found mostly in the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Members of the family are trees (rarely shrubs) growing from 2 to 100 metres tall, mostly evergreen (except the deciduous larches), resinous, monoecious, with subopposite or whorled branches, and spirally arranged needle-like leaves. The female cones are large and usually woody, with numerous spirally arranged scales; the seeds are mostly winged.

Genera

The family is commonly divided into four subfamilies, including Pinoideae (pines, Pinus), Piceoideae (spruces, Picea), Laricoideae (larches and Douglas-firs) and Abietoideae (firs, cedars and hemlocks).

Ecology and uses

Pinaceae dominate vast areas of boreal and montane forest. They are of major economic importance as sources of timber, pulpwood, resins, turpentine and edible seeds such as pine nuts. Many species are widely planted as ornamentals.
