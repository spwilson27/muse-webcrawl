The Ford Model A (also colloquially called the A-Model Ford or the A, and marketed as the Fordor in some body styles) was the Ford Motor Company's second market success, replacing the long-running Model T after 18 years. First produced on 20 October 1927 and sold from 2 December that year, it was available in a wide range of body styles and colours — a notable break from the Model T, which famously came in black only for much of its run.

Design

The Model A featured a conventional three-speed sliding-gear manual transmission (the Model T had used a planetary gearbox), four-wheel mechanical brakes, a safety-glass windshield, and a 201 cu in (3.3 L) flathead inline-four producing 40 hp (30 kW), giving a top speed of about 65 mph (105 km/h). Styling was overseen by Henry Ford's son Edsel, and prices ranged from about US$385 for a roadster to US$1,400 for the Town Car.

Production

Production totalled 4,858,644 cars across all variants when it ended in March 1932 (with 1931 the last full model year in the United States). The Model A was also built under licence in the Soviet Union as the basis for the GAZ-A. It remains one of the most popular collector cars of its era, supported by an extensive restoration community.

Legacy

The Model A established the template for Ford's subsequent V8 cars and its nameplate was later revived only informally. Hot rodders adopted Model A bodies extensively, and the car is a fixture of pre-war American motoring culture.
