Historic preservation

FocusConservation of historic places and buildings
Key instrumentsNational Register of Historic Places (U.S.), listed buildings (UK), UNESCO World Heritage
Origins19th century

Historic preservation (U.S.), built heritage preservation, or heritage conservation (UK) is an endeavour that seeks to preserve, conserve, and protect buildings, objects, landscapes, and other artifacts of historical significance. The movement emerged in the 19th century amid rapid industrialisation and urban redevelopment.

History

Early milestones include the protection of Mount Vernon by the Mount Vernon Ladies' Association in the 1850s and Britain's Ancient Monuments Protection Act 1882. In the United States, the demolition of New York's Pennsylvania Station in 1963 galvanised the modern movement, leading to the National Historic Preservation Act of 1966, which created the National Register of Historic Places.

Practice

Preservation practice encompasses documentation, stabilisation, restoration, rehabilitation, and adaptive reuse, guided in many jurisdictions by standards and tax incentives. Internationally, the 1972 UNESCO World Heritage Convention established a list of sites of outstanding universal value. Preservationists increasingly engage with intangible heritage, cultural landscapes, and issues of climate adaptation.
