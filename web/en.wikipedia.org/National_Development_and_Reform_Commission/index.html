National Development and Reform Commission

Formed: 2003 (as NDRC); origins in the State Planning Commission, 1952

Headquarters: Xicheng District, Beijing, China

Parent body: State Council of the People's Republic of China

Abbreviation: NDRC

The National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) is a macroeconomic management agency under the State Council of the People's Republic of China. Broadly responsible for formulating and implementing strategies of national economic and social development, it is among the most powerful bodies in the Chinese government, sometimes described as a "mini State Council." Its functions include drafting five-year plans, approving major investment projects, setting administered prices, coordinating industrial policy, and overseeing energy and climate policy.

History

The NDRC traces its origins to the State Planning Commission, founded in 1952 and modelled on Soviet central planning bodies. It was renamed the State Development Planning Commission in 1998, and in 2003 it absorbed functions of other agencies to become the National Development and Reform Commission. Although China's economy has marketised greatly, the NDRC retains significant authority over investment approvals, pricing of utilities and fuel, and strategic industries.

Functions

The commission drafts annual and five-year plans for national economic and social development for approval by the National People's Congress, monitors macroeconomic performance, coordinates policies on regional development (including the Belt and Road Initiative), and administers China's carbon emissions trading and climate commitments through its Department of Climate Change. It also houses the National Energy Administration and the National Bureau of Statistics was historically affiliated with the planning system.
