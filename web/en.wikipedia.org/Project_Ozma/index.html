Project Ozma

TypeSETI experiment
Year1960
DirectorFrank Drake
SiteNational Radio Astronomy Observatory, Green Bank, West Virginia

Project Ozma was a pioneering search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI) experiment conducted in 1960 by astronomer Frank Drake at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory in Green Bank, West Virginia. It was the first modern attempt to listen for radio signals from other civilizations, and it inaugurated the scientific field of SETI. The project was named after Princess Ozma, ruler of the fictional Land of Oz — a place Drake described as very far away, difficult to reach, and populated by exotic beings.

The search

Using the 26-metre (85-foot) Tatel radio telescope, Drake observed two nearby Sun-like stars, Tau Ceti and Epsilon Eridani, at a frequency of 1.42 gigahertz — the emission line of neutral hydrogen, which Drake and others reasoned any technologically advanced civilization would recognize as a natural "magic frequency." Roughly 150 hours of observations were recorded over several months. Apart from a false alarm later traced to a secret military experiment, no artificial signals were detected.

Legacy

Although Ozma found no signals, it demonstrated the feasibility of systematic SETI searches and inspired decades of subsequent programs, including Project Cyclops, the SETI Institute's Project Phoenix, and Breakthrough Listen. A follow-up, Ozma II, was conducted at Green Bank in the 1970s by Patrick Palmer and Ben Zuckerman, examining hundreds of nearby stars. Drake's related "Drake equation," presented in 1961, grew directly out of the planning surrounding Ozma and remains a framework for discussing the abundance of communicative civilizations.
