John Reith, 1st Baron Reith

Born: John Charles Walsham Reith, 20 July 1889, Stonehaven, Scotland
Died: 16 June 1971 (aged 81), Edinburgh, Scotland
Occupation: Broadcasting executive
Known for: First general manager and director-general of the BBC

John Charles Walsham Reith, 1st Baron Reith (20 July 1889 – 16 June 1971), was a Scottish broadcasting executive who established the tradition of independent public service broadcasting in the United Kingdom.

BBC

An engineer by training, wounded in the First World War, Reith was appointed general manager of the newly formed British Broadcasting Company in 1922, famously admitting he knew nothing of broadcasting. When the company became the British Broadcasting Corporation under royal charter in 1927, he became its first director-general, serving until 1938. His mission for the BBC — to "inform, educate and entertain" — and the values associated with him are still called "Reithian". He defended the BBC's editorial independence during the General Strike of 1926.

Later career

After the BBC, Reith chaired Imperial Airways and oversaw its merger into BOAC. Under Churchill he briefly served as Minister of Information, then Minister of Transport, and was raised to the peerage as Baron Reith in 1940. He later chaired the Colonial Development Corporation and published memoirs and diaries.

Legacy

The BBC's annual Reith Lectures, begun in 1948, are named in his honour. His imposing, principled management shaped the model of public service media worldwide.
