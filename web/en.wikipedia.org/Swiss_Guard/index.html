The Pontifical Swiss Guard is a military unit of Swiss soldiers responsible for the safety of the Pope and for security at the Apostolic Palace in Vatican City. Established by Pope Julius II in 1506, it is among the oldest military units in continuous operation and is famous for its distinctive blue, red, and orange-yellow striped Renaissance-style uniform. It is sometimes called "the world's smallest army."

History

Swiss mercenaries were prized throughout Renaissance Europe for their discipline and loyalty. The Guard's defining moment came during the Sack of Rome on 6 May 1527, when 147 of its 189 men died holding the steps of St Peter's Basilica and the Passetto corridor so that Pope Clement VII could escape to Castel Sant'Angelo. New recruits still swear their oath of allegiance each year on 6 May, the anniversary of the battle. The Guard's ceremonial weapons include the halberd and sword, though modern guards also train with modern firearms and unarmed combat.

Recruitment and service

Recruits must be unmarried Swiss Catholic men between 19 and 30 years old, at least 174 cm (5 ft 8.5 in) tall, and must have completed basic training with the Swiss Armed Forces. Minimum service is 26 months. In 2015 Pope Francis dismissed the Guard's commander Daniel Anrig, and in 2022 the Vatican announced plans to rebuild the Guard's barracks and to expand the corps; proposals to admit women have been discussed but not adopted.
