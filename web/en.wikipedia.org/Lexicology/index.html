Lexicology

Lexicology is the branch of linguistics that studies the lexicon — the vocabulary of a language — including the nature, structure, history and use of words and word meanings. It examines how words are formed, how they relate to one another semantically, and how vocabularies change over time.

Scope

Lexicology overlaps with morphology (the internal structure of words), semantics (meaning), etymology (word origins) and phraseology (fixed expressions and idioms). It is distinguished from lexicography, the practical craft of compiling dictionaries, although the two fields inform each other.

Approaches

Historical lexicology traces semantic change and borrowing between languages, while synchronic lexicology describes the vocabulary of a language at a given time, analysing features such as polysemy, synonymy, antonymy and semantic fields. Modern lexicology makes extensive use of large text corpora.
