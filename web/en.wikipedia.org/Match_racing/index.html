Match racing

Match racing is a form of sailing competition in which two identical or matched boats race against each other on a short course, rather than a whole fleet racing together. Because only the opponent matters, match racing is a tactical duel: crews try to gain control before the start, force penalties on the rival boat, and cover their opponent's moves around the course.

Format

Races are typically sailed in supplied, evenly matched keelboats over windward-leeward courses lasting about 20 minutes, officiated by on-water umpires who decide protests immediately, showing green, yellow or red penalty flags. Events usually use round-robin stages followed by knockout semi-finals and finals. The pre-start, in which boats circle and jockey for the favoured position in the minutes before the gun, is considered an art in itself.

Events

Match racing is the format of the America's Cup, first contested in 1851. The World Match Racing Tour, founded in 2000, crowns the match racing world champion under World Sailing, and classic events include the Congressional Cup in Long Beach, California, and the King Edward VII Gold Cup in Bermuda. Women's and youth match racing championships are also held, and the discipline was part of the Olympic sailing programme for women (Elliott 6m) at London 2012.
