Prusik

A Prusik (also spelled Prussik) is a friction hitch or knot used to attach a loop of cord around a rope. It grips when loaded in either direction along the rope but slides freely when unloaded, making it valuable in climbing, mountaineering, caving, rope rescue, and arboriculture. It is named after its inventor, the Austrian mountaineer Karl Prusik, who described it in 1931.

Use

The Prusik is tied with a loop of smaller-diameter cord wrapped two or three times around the host rope and passed through itself. Climbers use paired Prusiks to ascend a fixed rope, as a backup (autoblock) on rappels, and in crevasse-rescue hauling systems. The cord must be significantly thinner than the main rope for the hitch to grip reliably.

Related hitches

Related friction hitches include the Klemheist knot, the Bachmann hitch, and the French Prusik (autoblock). Because a loaded Prusik can jam tightly, it may be difficult to release under load, a limitation addressed by variants and by careful dressing of the knot.
