Latvian language

Latvian
Native to: Latvia
Speakers: about 1.5 million native
Family: Indo-European, Baltic
Official in: Latvia, European Union
ISO: lv, lav

Latvian (latviešu valoda) is an Eastern Baltic language in the Indo-European family, spoken natively by roughly 1.5 million people, primarily in Latvia, where it is the sole official language. Together with Lithuanian it is one of only two surviving Baltic languages.

History

Latvian emerged as a distinct language by the 16th century from the tribal dialects of the Latgalians, Curonians, Semigallians and Selonians. The first printed texts, religious works from the 1580s, used a German-influenced orthography; the modern spelling was standardised in the early 20th century under scholars such as Kārlis Mīlenbahs and Jānis Endzelīns.

Grammar and phonology

Latvian is a heavily inflected language with two genders, seven noun cases and a rich verb system. It has a pitch-accent system with three syllable intonations in standard Latvian, fixed stress on the first syllable, and phonemic vowel length. The alphabet has 33 letters, using diacritics such as ā, č, ē, ģ, ī, ķ, ļ, ņ, š, ū and ž.

Status

After decades of Russification during the Soviet occupation, language laws since 1988–1999 have restored Latvian as the dominant public language, though a large Russian-speaking minority remains.
