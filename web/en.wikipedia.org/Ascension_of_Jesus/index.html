Ascension of Jesus

The Ascension of Jesus (anglicized from the Vulgate Latin: Ascensio Domini Nostri Iesu Christi) is the Christian belief, embodied in the major creeds and confessions, that Jesus ascended to Heaven in bodily form in the presence of his disciples, following his resurrection. According to the Acts of the Apostles, the ascension occurred forty days after Easter on the Mount of Olives, near Bethany, after which Jesus was taken up and hidden by a cloud.

Biblical accounts

The ascension is described in Luke 24 and Acts 1, and is alluded to in Mark 16, John 20, and the letters of Paul and Peter. In Acts, the risen Jesus instructs the apostles to await the Holy Spirit in Jerusalem; two men in white then announce that Jesus will return in the same way he was seen to go. The event precedes Pentecost by ten days.

Feast day

The Feast of the Ascension, also called Ascension Day or Ascension Thursday, is one of the principal feasts of the Christian year and is celebrated on the fortieth day of Easter (the Thursday of the sixth week of Eastertide), though some provinces transfer it to the following Sunday. It is a public holiday in many countries. The traditional site of the event, the Mount of Olives, contains the Chapel of the Ascension, which preserves a slab said to bear Jesus's footprint.

Theology

In Christian theology the ascension completes the exaltation of Jesus begun at the resurrection: he is seated "at the right hand of the Father", reigns as king, intercedes as high priest, and will return in glory. The ascension is confessed in the Nicene and Apostles' Creeds.
