Ottoman–Persian Wars

The Ottoman–Persian Wars were a series of conflicts between the Ottoman Empire and various dynasties of Persia — the Safavids, Afsharids, Zands and Qajars — fought from the early 16th century to the early 19th century over control of the Caucasus, Mesopotamia, eastern Anatolia and western Iran.

Background

The rivalry began after the Safavid dynasty established Twelver Shi'a Islam as the state religion of Persia in 1501, challenging the Sunni Ottoman Empire. Shah Ismail I's expansion into eastern Anatolia, where many Turkmen tribes sympathised with the Safavids, provoked Sultan Selim I.

Major conflicts

The Battle of Chaldiran in 1514 was a decisive Ottoman victory that temporarily annexed eastern Anatolia and Upper Mesopotamia. Later wars included the Ottoman–Safavid War of 1532–1555, ended by the Peace of Amasya; the war of 1578–1590, which gave the Ottomans large gains in the Caucasus; and Abbas I's reconquests in the early 17th century, confirmed by the Treaty of Zuhab in 1639, which set a frontier that largely endured.

After the Safavid collapse, Nader Shah of the Afsharids fought the Ottomans in the 1730s and 1740s. The final Ottoman–Qajar war of 1821–1823 ended with the Treaty of Erzurum, reaffirming the Zuhab frontier.

Legacy

The wars helped define the modern borders between Turkey, Iraq and Iran and deepened the Sunni–Shi'a geopolitical divide in the region.
