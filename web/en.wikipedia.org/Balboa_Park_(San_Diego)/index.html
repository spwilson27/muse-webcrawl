Balboa Park is a 1,200-acre (490 ha) historic urban cultural park in San Diego, California, United States. In addition to open space areas, natural vegetation zones, green belts, gardens, and walking paths, it contains museums, several theaters, and the world-famous San Diego Zoo. There are also many recreational facilities and several gift shops and restaurants within the boundaries of the park.

Placed in reserve in 1835, the park's site is one of the oldest in the United States dedicated to public recreational use. Balboa Park is managed and maintained by the Parks and Recreation Department of the City of San Diego.

History

The land was set aside in 1868 by city leaders who designated 1,400 acres as "City Park." For decades it remained largely undeveloped scrubland. Horticulturist Kate Sessions, often called the "Mother of Balboa Park," leased nursery land within the park in the 1890s in exchange for planting trees throughout the city, and she introduced many of the park's characteristic plantings.

The park was renamed in 1910 for the Spanish explorer Vasco Núñez de Balboa, the first European to sight the Pacific Ocean from the Americas, in preparation for the Panama–California Exposition of 1915–1916, which celebrated the opening of the Panama Canal. The exposition produced the park's celebrated Spanish Colonial Revival architecture, including the California Building with its ornate tower, now an icon of San Diego. A second exposition, the California Pacific International Exposition of 1935–1936, added further buildings and gardens.

Attractions

Balboa Park hosts more than a dozen museums, including the San Diego Museum of Art, the San Diego Air and Space Museum, the San Diego Natural History Museum, the Museum of Us (formerly the Museum of Man), and the Fleet Science Center. The Old Globe Theatre, modeled on Shakespeare's Old Globe in London, anchors the park's performing arts institutions. The Botanical Building, built for the 1915 exposition, is one of the largest lath structures in the world and shelters thousands of plants beside the Lily Pond.

The park's gardens include the Inez Grant Parker Memorial Rose Garden, the Japanese Friendship Garden, and the desert and Alcazar gardens. The Spreckels Organ Pavilion houses one of the world's largest outdoor pipe organs, donated in 1914.

Geography and layout

The park lies northeast of downtown San Diego, bounded roughly by Sixth Avenue to the west, Upas Street to the north, 28th Street to the east, and Russ Boulevard to the south. It is traversed by the Cabrillo Bridge, completed in 1914, which carries El Prado across the Cabrillo Canyon. Much of the park's eastern portion is devoted to golf courses, the Morley Field sports complex, and natural canyons.
