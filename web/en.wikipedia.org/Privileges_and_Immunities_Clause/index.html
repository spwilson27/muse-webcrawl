Privileges and Immunities Clause

The Privileges and Immunities Clause (U.S. Constitution, Article IV, Section 2, Clause 1) provides that "The Citizens of each State shall be entitled to all Privileges and Immunities of Citizens in the several States." It is also known as the Comity Clause.

Purpose and interpretation

The clause prevents a state from treating citizens of other states in a discriminatory manner with respect to fundamental rights, subject to substantial justification. In Corfield v. Coryell (1823), Justice Bushrod Washington listed protected privileges including the right to travel, to pursue a livelihood, and to access courts. Later cases, such as United Building and Construction Trades Council v. Camden (1984) and Supreme Court of New Hampshire v. Piper (1985), applied the clause to municipal hiring preferences and bar admission residency rules.

Distinction

It is distinct from the Privileges or Immunities Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment, which protects rights of national citizenship against state abridgement and was narrowly construed in the Slaughter-House Cases (1873).
