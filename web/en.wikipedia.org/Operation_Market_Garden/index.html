Operation Market Garden was an Allied military operation during the Second World War fought in the German-occupied Netherlands from 17 to 25 September 1944. The airborne operation was undertaken by the First Allied Airborne Army with the land operation by XXX Corps of the British Second Army.

The objective was to create a 64 mi (103 km) salient into German territory with a bridgehead over the River Rhine, creating an Allied invasion route into northern Germany. This was to be achieved by two sub-operations: Market, an airborne assault to seize key bridges over rivers in the Netherlands, and Garden, a ground attack moving over the seized bridges.

The operation

The largest airborne operation of the war dropped some 35,000 men: the US 101st Airborne around Eindhoven, the US 82nd Airborne around Nijmegen, and the British 1st Airborne Division, with the Polish 1st Independent Parachute Brigade, around Arnhem — furthest behind German lines. XXX Corps advanced up a single narrow corridor ("Hell's Highway") repeatedly cut by German counterattacks. Unbeknownst to Allied planners, two SS Panzer divisions were refitting near Arnhem.

Outcome

The bridges at Eindhoven and Nijmegen were taken, but the British 1st Airborne was overwhelmed at Arnhem; only about 2,400 of some 10,000 men were evacuated across the Rhine on the night of 25–26 September. The operation failed in its ultimate aim — the road bridge at Arnhem became famous as "a bridge too far" — although the Allies retained a salient at Nijmegen. Allied casualties were roughly 15,000–17,000, exceeding German losses.
