Commonwealth Games

The Commonwealth Games is an international multi-sport event held every four years among athletes from the Commonwealth of Nations. First held in 1930 in Hamilton, Canada, as the British Empire Games, it has been staged under its present name since 1978. The Games are overseen by the Commonwealth Games Federation and are known as "the Friendly Games" for their emphasis on inclusiveness.

Features

Around 5,000 athletes from more than 70 nations and territories compete in about twenty sports. Unlike the Olympics, the programme includes sports popular mainly in the Commonwealth, such as lawn bowls, netball, and squash, and it fully integrates para-sport events into the same programme and medal table — a first among major multi-sport events. The King's Baton Relay carries a message from the Head of the Commonwealth around the world before each Games.

Host cities

Editions have been held in London, Sydney, Auckland, Edinburgh, Kuala Lumpur, Manchester, Melbourne, Delhi, Glasgow, the Gold Coast, and Birmingham (2022). Rising costs have strained recent hosting; Australia's state of Victoria withdrew as 2026 host in 2023, after which Glasgow agreed to stage a scaled-back Games.
