Bending

In solid mechanics, bending (also known as flexure) characterises the behaviour of a slender structural element subjected to an external load applied perpendicularly to its longitudinal axis. The load causes the element to curve, generating internal stresses: compressive stress on one side of the cross-section and tensile stress on the other, separated by a neutral axis where stress is zero.

Beam theory

The classical description is Euler–Bernoulli beam theory, which relates the applied bending moment to the curvature of the beam through the flexural rigidity (the product of Young's modulus and the second moment of area). Timoshenko beam theory extends the analysis by including shear deformation, important for short, deep beams.

Deflection and failure

Engineers calculate the deflection of beams under service loads and their ultimate strength in bending. Materials may fail by yielding, fracture, or elastic instability (buckling of the compression flange). In reinforced concrete and timber design, bending is usually the principal action considered for beams, slabs, and lintels.

Bending in manufacturing

The term also describes a metalworking process in which sheet metal is plastically deformed along an axis, typically with a press brake, to produce angles, channels, and other profiles.
