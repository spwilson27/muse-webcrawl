Indian reserve

In Canada, an Indian reserve (French: réserve indienne) is specified by the Indian Act as a "tract of land, the legal title to which is vested in Her Majesty, that has been set apart by Her Majesty for the use and benefit of a band". Reserves are held by the Crown in trust for a First Nation band, and band members have the right to live on and use the land, though the land itself cannot be privately owned or sold except to the Crown.

Legal status

Reserve land is governed by the Indian Act of 1876 and subsequent amendments. Under section 91(24) of the Constitution Act, 1867, "Indians, and Lands reserved for the Indians" fall under federal legislative authority, so reserves are a federal rather than provincial responsibility. Individuals may hold Certificates of Possession for particular lots. Reserves are distinct from traditional territories, which can be far larger and are subject to Aboriginal title claims.

Statistics

There are more than 3,000 Indian reserves in Canada, most of them small; the total area is roughly 28,000 square kilometres. Reserves are most numerous in British Columbia, where over 1,700 exist, a legacy of the colonial reserve-commission process rather than treaties. Not all First Nations people live on reserves; a large and growing proportion of the Registered Indian population lives off reserve, in cities and towns across the country.
