cdmaOne is the brand name for the family of second-generation (2G) mobile telecommunications standards based on the IS-95 standard, also known as TIA/EIA-95. Developed chiefly by Qualcomm, cdmaOne was the first commercial cellular system based on code-division multiple access (CDMA) technology.

Technology

IS-95 uses direct-sequence spread spectrum: all users share a 1.25 MHz channel, distinguished by unique spreading codes. Features include soft handoff between cells, precise power control, and variable-rate vocoders offering voice rates up to 14.4 kbps (Rate Set 2), with packet data at similar speeds.

Deployment

cdmaOne networks launched commercially in Hong Kong and South Korea in 1996 and spread widely in North America (notably through Verizon and Sprint), parts of Asia, and elsewhere, competing with GSM. South Korea became the largest cdmaOne market.

Evolution

cdmaOne was succeeded by the backward-compatible CDMA2000 family (1xRTT, EV-DO), and most cdmaOne/CDMA2000 networks were shut down in the early 2020s as carriers moved to LTE and 5G.
