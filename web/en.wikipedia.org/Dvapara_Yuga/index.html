The Dvapara Yuga (Sanskrit: द्वापर युग) is the third of the four yugas, or world ages, in Hindu cosmology, following the Treta Yuga and preceding the Kali Yuga. According to the Puranas, it lasts for 864,000 human years (2,400 divine years of 360 human years each, counting its dawn and twilight). In Dvapara Yuga, virtue (dharma) stands on only two of its four legs, and humanity declines further from the perfection of the first age, Satya Yuga.

Characteristics

Texts describe the age as one in which people become troubled by disputes, disease, and shortening lifespans — humans are said to live at most a thousand years. Knowledge of the Veda is divided, and rulers may become unjust. Nevertheless, devotion and ritual still flourish, and great teachers appear.

Mahabharata and Krishna

The events of the epic Mahabharata, including the Kurukshetra War, are traditionally placed at the end of the Dvapara Yuga. The god Krishna, an avatar of Vishnu, is said to have departed the earth at the age's close, an event marking the beginning of Kali Yuga, traditionally dated to 3102 BCE.

The yuga cycle

Together the four ages form a mahayuga of 4,320,000 years: Satya (1,728,000), Treta (1,296,000), Dvapara (864,000), and Kali (432,000), in the ratio 4:3:2:1, reflecting the progressive decline of dharma through the cycle before it begins again.
