Komeito

Komeito (公明党, Kōmeitō), formerly the Clean Government Party and New Komeito, is a Japanese political party founded in 1964 by members of the Buddhist lay organisation Soka Gakkai. It is a conservative, centrist party with a strong grassroots voter base, and was the junior coalition partner of the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) from 1999 until 2025.

History

Komeito was established in 1964 as the political arm of Soka Gakkai, the Nichiren Buddhist lay movement. In 1970, after a scandal over attempts to suppress a critical book, the party formally separated from Soka Gakkai and adopted a secular party constitution, though Soka Gakkai members remain its core electorate. The party joined governing coalitions in the 1990s and in 1999 entered a durable coalition with the LDP under the name New Komeito (reverting to Komeito in 2014).

Policies

Komeito emphasises welfare provision, education (including free textbooks, an early signature reform), consumer protection, and local government. It has traditionally been pacifist and cautious about revising Article 9 of the Constitution, acting as a moderating "brake" within coalitions, though it accepted the 2015 security legislation allowing limited collective self-defence. It supported the consumption tax's reduced rate for food.

Coalition and 2025 split

The LDP–Komeito coalition governed Japan almost continuously from 1999 to 2025. In October 2025, under leader Tetsuo Saito, Komeito left the coalition — its first departure in 26 years — citing the LDP's inadequate response to political funding scandals, a decision that reshaped Japanese politics.
