Lake McKerrow (Māori: Whakatipu Waitai) is a lake in Fiordland, in the southwestern corner of the South Island of New Zealand, lying near the Tasman Sea coast at the end of the Hollyford Valley. It is named after James McKerrow, a 19th-century surveyor and astronomer.

Geography

The lake is a former fiord that has been cut off from the sea by sediment deposited at its mouth, and it now drains southward via the Hollyford River. It is roughly 14 kilometres long and lies within Fiordland National Park. The Hollyford Track, a well-known tramping route, passes along its eastern shore.

History

The lake's outlet at Martins Bay was the site of a short-lived 1870s settlement, Jamestown, founded with high hopes as a port for a goldfield road over the Southern Alps; isolation and difficult conditions led to its abandonment. Māori knew the area as an important source of pounamu (greenstone) routes and food resources.
