Prasada

Prasada (Sanskrit: प्रसाद, romanized: prasāda), also rendered prasad or prasadam, is a religious offering in Hinduism and Sikhism—most often edible food first offered to a deity, saint, or guru, and then consumed by worshippers as a blessing. Literally, a gracious gift; anything, usually edible, given by a saint, guru, or god to a devotee that is believed to be imbued with divine grace. In its material sense, prasada grows to encompass the concept of receiving a gift from the divine after giving it to them in offering.

Practice

Food offered to the deity during puja—called naivedya or bhog before it is accepted—is believed to be blessed and sanctified by the deity, and is then distributed to devotees by the priest. Common items include fruit, sweets such as laddu, panchamrita (a mixture of milk, curd, honey, sugar, and ghee), and cooked dishes. Consumption of prasada is considered an essential part of worship, symbolizing that the divine returns the devotee's offering, suffused with grace. In many temples, large quantities are prepared daily; the Mahaprasada of the Jagannath Temple at Puri is particularly famous, and prasada is served to all regardless of caste.

In Sikh practice, karah parshad, a sweet flour-based food, is distributed to the congregation at the end of services in a gurdwara, and the community kitchen (langar) serves a full blessed meal to all visitors. Comparable offerings appear in Jain, Buddhist, and various bhakti traditions.

Meaning and etiquette

Prasada is to be received with reverence—typically with both hands and a bowed head—and consumed rather than discarded. The term also has broader meanings in Sanskrit and Indian philosophy: serenity or graciousness of mind, and the divine grace by which liberation is attained. In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna speaks of offering even "a leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water" with devotion, and prasada is described as purifying the mind.
