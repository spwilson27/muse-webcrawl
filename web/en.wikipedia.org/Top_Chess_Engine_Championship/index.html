Top Chess Engine Championship

Founded2010 (as Thoresen Chess Engine Championship)
FormatDivisions, cup, and superfinal

The Top Chess Engine Championship, formerly known as TCEC or (until 2013) the Thoresen Chess Engines Competition, is a computer chess tournament founded by Norwegian chess enthusiast Martin Thoresen. It is often regarded as the unofficial world computer chess championship because of its strong field of participants and long time controls on high-end hardware, giving a higher quality of tournament chess than other events.

Format

The championship is organized in seasons, with engines competing in a league system of divisions. Lower divisions qualify engines for higher ones, culminating in a Premier Division; the top two engines of the Premier Division meet in a superfinal of 100 games played from selected opening positions with both colors. Since Season 11, TCEC also runs a knockout cup event, and in later seasons additional events such as Swiss tournaments were added.

Notable winners

Dominant engines over the years have included Houdini in the early seasons, Stockfish, which became the most successful engine in the competition's history, and Komodo. The arrival of neural-network engines transformed the event: Leela Chess Zero, using a deep neural network evaluated on GPUs, reached the superfinal and won multiple championships, sparking the "neural era" alongside hybrid designs such as Stockfish's later adoption of NNUE evaluation.

Hardware and broadcast

Games are played on powerful shared hardware — CPUs for conventional engines and GPUs for neural engines — and are broadcast live on the TCEC website with live evaluations, making the event popular among chess fans.
