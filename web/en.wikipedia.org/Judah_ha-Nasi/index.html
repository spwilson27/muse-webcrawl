Judah ha-Nasi

Judah ha-Nasi (Hebrew: יְהוּדָה הַנָּשִׂיא, Yehudah ha-Nasi, "Judah the Prince"; c. 135 – c. 217 CE) was a Jewish scholar and leader of the Jewish community in Roman Palestine in the late 2nd and early 3rd centuries CE. He is best known as the chief redactor of the Mishnah, the foundational written compilation of Jewish oral law. In rabbinic literature he is often called simply "Rabbi" (Rebbi) — the Rabbi par excellence — or Rabbenu ha-Qadosh, "our holy teacher".

Life

Judah was a descendant of Hillel the Elder and the son of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel II. Born, according to tradition, on the day Rabbi Akiva died, he studied under the leading sages of the generation after the Bar Kokhba revolt. He succeeded his father as nasi (prince/patriarch) and presided over the Sanhedrin at Usha, then Beit She'arim, and finally Sepphoris, where he lived for many years. Wealthy and respected by the Roman authorities — tradition associates him with a Roman emperor called "Antoninus" — he used his position to stabilise Jewish religious life in the aftermath of the revolts.

The Mishnah

Around 200 CE Judah completed the redaction of the Mishnah, organising the accumulated oral traditions of Jewish law into six orders (sedarim) covering agriculture, festivals, family law, civil and criminal law, sacrifices, and ritual purity. Tradition explains the decision to commit the oral law to writing by the fear that persecution and dispersion would cause it to be forgotten. The Mishnah became the basis of the Talmud and of all subsequent rabbinic Judaism. Judah is also credited with rulings that relaxed earlier stringencies and with maintaining a Hebrew of exceptional purity in his household; the Talmud relates that his maidservant's usage was consulted on obscure words.

Death and legacy

Judah died at Sepphoris, according to tradition on the same day as the birth of Rav, and was buried at Beit She'arim, whose necropolis became a favoured burial place for Jews from across the Roman world. His sons Gamaliel and Simeon succeeded him in leadership and scholarship.
