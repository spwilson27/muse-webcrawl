Logistics

Container logistics at a major port

Logistics is the part of supply chain management that deals with the efficient forward and reverse flow of goods, services, and related information from the point of origin to the point of consumption according to the needs of customers. It involves the integration of information, transportation, inventory, warehousing, material handling, packaging, and sometimes security.

Origins

The term originates in military usage, from the French logistique, associated with the movement and supply of troops. Military logistics — ensuring that armies have food, ammunition, equipment and transport — has often determined the outcome of campaigns. In the 20th century the discipline was formalised and transferred to business, where it became central to manufacturing and retail distribution.

Activities

Business logistics includes inbound logistics (movement of materials from suppliers), internal logistics within production, and outbound logistics (distribution of finished goods). Key functions are transport management, warehousing, inventory control, order fulfilment, and reverse logistics for returns and recycling. Third-party logistics providers (3PLs) perform these functions under contract.

Modern developments

Containerisation, barcodes, electronic data interchange, GPS tracking, warehouse automation and e-commerce fulfilment networks have transformed the field. Modern supply chain managers optimise against objectives often summarised as delivering the right product, in the right quantity and condition, to the right place, at the right time, for the right cost.
