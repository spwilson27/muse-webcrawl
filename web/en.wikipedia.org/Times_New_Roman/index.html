Times New Roman

Times New Roman is a serif typeface commissioned by the British newspaper The Times in 1931 and released by the Monotype Corporation. Designed under the supervision of Stanley Morison, with letterforms drawn by Victor Lardent, it first appeared in the newspaper on 3 October 1932.

Design

The design was influenced by earlier serif faces such as Plantin, but was adapted with a larger x-height and narrower proportions so that more words could fit on a line of newspaper text. Its sturdy letterforms and moderate contrast made it highly legible at small sizes, and it rapidly became one of the most widely used typefaces in publishing.

Digital use

Times New Roman became one of the core fonts of the personal computer era. Versions were bundled with Microsoft Windows and Apple Macintosh operating systems, and it served for years as the default font of Microsoft Word and many web browsers. Although largely superseded as a default, it remains ubiquitous in academic and legal documents, where it is often a required typeface for submissions.
