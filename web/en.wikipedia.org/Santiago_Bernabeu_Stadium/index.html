Santiago Bernabéu Stadium

The Santiago Bernabéu Stadium (Spanish: Estadio Santiago Bernabéu) is a football stadium in Madrid, Spain, and the home ground of Real Madrid CF since its completion in 1947. With a current seating capacity of roughly 84,000 spectators, it is one of the largest and most famous football venues in the world. It is named after Santiago Bernabéu, the legendary club president.

History

The stadium opened on 14 December 1947 as the Nuevo Estadio Chamartín and was renamed for Bernabéu in 1955. It was expanded repeatedly, notably for the 1982 FIFA World Cup, which it hosted including the final. The Bernabéu has staged four European Cup/Champions League finals (1957, 1969, 1980, 2010), the 1964 European Championship final, and the second leg of the 2018 Copa Libertadores final, moved from Buenos Aires.

Renovation

A sweeping renovation begun in 2019 wrapped the stadium in a futuristic metallic shell with a fixed roof and a retractable pitch that can be stored in an underground greenhouse, allowing year-round concerts and events. Seating capacity rose to about 84,000 and new hospitality, museum, and commercial spaces were added, at a cost exceeding one billion euros.
