Indian mathematics

RegionIndian subcontinent
Periodc. 1200 BCE – 18th century CE
Key figuresAryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara II, Madhava

Indian mathematics developed in the Indian subcontinent from ancient times and produced foundational contributions including the decimal place-value system, the use of zero as a number, early trigonometry, and infinite series for π and trigonometric functions.

Early period

The Sulba Sutras (c. 800–200 BCE) contain geometric rules for altar construction, including statements related to the Pythagorean theorem. Jain mathematicians developed interests in large numbers, permutations and early ideas of infinity. By the classical period, the Bakhshali manuscript showed sophisticated arithmetic with a dot symbol for zero.

Classical period

Aryabhata (476–550) presented astronomy and mathematics in verse, giving an approximation of π and sine tables. Brahmagupta (598–668) gave rules for arithmetic with zero and negative numbers and methods for quadratic equations and cyclic quadrilaterals. Bhaskara II (1114–1185) advanced algebra, the chakrawala method for Pell-type equations, and early ideas resembling differential calculus.

Kerala school

Madhava of Sangamagrama (c. 1340–1425) and his successors in Kerala derived infinite series for π, sine, cosine and arctangent centuries before their European rediscovery, with proofs recorded in texts such as the Yuktibhasa. Indian numerals and methods transmitted through Arabic scholarship profoundly influenced world mathematics.
