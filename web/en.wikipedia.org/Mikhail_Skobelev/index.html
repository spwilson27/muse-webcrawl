Mikhail Skobelev

Mikhail Dmitrievich Skobelev
Born: 29 September 1843, Saint Petersburg, Russia
Died: 7 July 1882, Moscow, Russia
Allegiance: Russian Empire
Rank: General of the Infantry
Known as: "The White General"

Mikhail Dmitrievich Skobelev (29 September 1843 – 7 July 1882) was a Russian general famous for his campaigns in Central Asia and during the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78. His habit of riding into battle on a white horse and wearing a white uniform earned him the nickname "the White General"; the Turkmen called him "Bloody Eyes" for the severity of his reprisals.

Central Asian campaigns

Skobelev served in the Russian conquest of Central Asia, taking part in the Khivan campaign of 1873 and suppressing the Kokand rebellion of 1875–76. He became the first governor of the Fergana Valley region. In 1880–81 he commanded the expedition against the Teke Turkmen, storming the fortress of Geok Tepe in January 1881 after a methodical siege — an assault followed by a massacre of the defenders that remains controversial.

Russo-Turkish War

During the war of 1877–78 Skobelev distinguished himself at the siege of Plevna, the crossing of the Balkan mountains in winter, and the advance toward Constantinople. His daring and theatrical personal bravery made him enormously popular with Russian troops and the public.

Death and legacy

Skobelev died suddenly of heart failure in a Moscow hotel in 1882, aged only 38. His pan-Slavist speeches had made him a political figure as well as a soldier. Streets, squares and the city of Fergana (renamed Skobelev until 1924) bore his name.
