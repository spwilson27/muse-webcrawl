Jakob Nielsen (born October 5, 1957) is a Danish web usability consultant, human–computer interaction researcher, and co-founder of the Nielsen Norman Group. He has been called a leading guru of web page usability and was a prominent voice on user interface design from the early days of the World Wide Web.

Work

Nielsen earned a PhD in human–computer interaction from the Technical University of Denmark. He worked at Bellcore and Sun Microsystems before co-founding the Nielsen Norman Group with Donald Norman in 1998. He is known for his "10 usability heuristics" for interaction design, for advocating "discount usability engineering" — cheap, small-sample evaluation methods — and for his long-running Alertbox column on web usability.

Views and books

Nielsen argued that users scan rather than read web pages, popularizing findings on F-shaped reading patterns, and he famously criticized Flash-heavy design in his 2000 essay "Flash: 99% Bad". His books include Usability Engineering (1993), Designing Web Usability (2000), and, with Hoa Loranger, Prioritizing Web Usability (2006). Some of his pronouncements — such as on scrolling, PDFs, and mobile design — have drawn criticism from other designers, but his empirical, test-driven approach remains influential in user experience practice.
