Erythroplakia

Erythroplakia is a clinical term for a red, velvety patch or plaque in the mouth that cannot be attributed to any other condition. It is considered an oral potentially malignant disorder, with a much higher risk of containing dysplasia or carcinoma than the more common white lesion, leukoplakia.

Clinical features

Erythroplakia most often occurs on the floor of the mouth, the soft palate, and the lateral tongue in older adults, and is strongly associated with tobacco and alcohol use. It is usually asymptomatic and may be found incidentally during dental examination. Mixed red-and-white lesions are termed erythroleukoplakia or "speckled leukoplakia".

Diagnosis and management

Because a high proportion of erythroplakias show severe dysplasia, carcinoma in situ, or invasive squamous cell carcinoma at biopsy, any persistent red oral lesion requires prompt biopsy. Management generally involves complete surgical excision and long-term follow-up, along with cessation of tobacco and alcohol.
