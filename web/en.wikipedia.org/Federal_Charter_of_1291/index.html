Federal Charter of 1291

The Federal Charter or Letter of Alliance (German: Bundesbrief) is one of the earliest constitutional documents of Switzerland. Dated to the beginning of August 1291, it is a treaty of mutual assistance among the three Alpine cantons of Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden, traditionally regarded as the founding document of the Swiss Confederation.

CreatedEarly August 1291
SignatoriesUri, Schwyz, Unterwalden
PurposeMutual defence alliance
LocationMuseum of the Swiss Charters of Confederation, Schwyz

Contents

Written in Latin, the charter pledges the three valley communities to aid one another against attack, to accept no judge who had purchased his office or was not a native of the valleys, and to resolve disputes among themselves by arbitration. It closes with a provision that the agreement shall last, "if God wills, for ever".

Significance

Although Switzerland's confederation grew through many later alliances, the 1291 charter was elevated in the nineteenth century to the status of founding act, and Switzerland celebrates its national day on 1 August in its honour. The original parchment is preserved at Schwyz.
