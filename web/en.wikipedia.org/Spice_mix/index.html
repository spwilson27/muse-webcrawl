Spice mix

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Prepared spice blends at a market

A spice mix or spice blend is a mixture of ground or whole spices — often also including herbs, salt, sugar or other flavourings — prepared in advance so that a characteristic flavour profile can be added conveniently to a dish. Spice mixes are found in nearly every culinary tradition, whether prepared at home or manufactured commercially.

Examples

Well-known spice mixes include garam masala and curry powder from South Asia; ras el hanout and baharat from the Middle East and North Africa; Chinese five-spice powder; Jamaican jerk seasoning; Cajun seasoning and Old Bay from the United States; herbes de Provence and quatre épices from France; shichimi tōgarashi from Japan; and advieh from Iran. Chilli powder blends, barbecue rubs and mixed spice (used in British baking) are further examples.

Preparation

Whole spices are typically toasted to release aromatic oils, then ground and combined. Because ground spices lose potency over time, mixes are best stored in airtight containers away from light and heat. Commercial blends may also contain anti-caking agents and salt as a preservative and flavour carrier.
