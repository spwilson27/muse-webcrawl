The Chicago Bulls are an American professional basketball team based in Chicago, Illinois. Founded in 1966, the Bulls compete in the National Basketball Association (NBA) as a member of the Eastern Conference Central Division and play home games at the United Center.

The Jordan era

The Bulls rose to global prominence in the 1990s behind Michael Jordan, Scottie Pippen, and coach Phil Jackson. After drafting Jordan in 1984, the team eventually won three consecutive championships from 1991 to 1993, then, following Jordan's brief retirement and return, a second "three-peat" from 1996 to 1998. The 1995–96 team won a then-record 72 regular-season games and is widely regarded as one of the greatest teams in NBA history.

Later years

After the 1998 championship the dynasty was dismantled and the Bulls struggled through a long rebuilding period. The 2010–11 team, led by Derrick Rose — the youngest MVP in league history — reached the Eastern Conference finals, but injuries curtailed that era. The franchise has continued to rebuild around new cores while remaining one of the NBA's most commercially valuable and globally recognised teams.

Rivalries

Historic rivals include the Detroit Pistons, who repeatedly blocked the young Bulls in the late 1980s, the New York Knicks, and the Miami Heat. The Bulls' six titles rank them among the most successful franchises in league history.
