Stokes' theorem

A surface Σ with boundary curve ∂Σ, as in Stokes' theorem

In vector calculus, Stokes' theorem (also called the generalized Stokes theorem, after George Gabriel Stokes) relates the surface integral of the curl of a vector field over a surface to the line integral of the vector field around the boundary of the surface:

∬Σ (∇ × F) · dΣ = ∮∂Σ F · dr

The theorem is a special case of the generalized Stokes theorem on differential forms, which unifies the fundamental theorem of calculus, Green's theorem, and the divergence theorem.

History

The result was first communicated by Lord Kelvin to George Stokes in a letter dated 1850, and Stokes set it as an examination question at Cambridge in 1854. It was later published and generalized by mathematicians including Poincaré and Cartan, who gave the modern formulation in terms of exterior derivatives: ∫∂Ω ω = ∫Ω dω.

Applications

Stokes' theorem is foundational in electromagnetism, where it converts between the differential and integral forms of Maxwell's equations — in particular Faraday's law of induction and Ampère's circuital law — and in fluid dynamics, where it relates circulation to vorticity.
