The Story of My Life (Keller book)

The Story of My Life is the autobiography of American author and activist Helen Keller, published in 1903 when she was 22 years old and a student at Radcliffe College. Dedicated to telephone inventor Alexander Graham Bell, the book recounts her early life, the illness at 19 months that left her deaf and blind, and her education under her teacher Anne Sullivan.

Contents

The book's most celebrated passage describes the moment at a water pump in 1887 when Sullivan spelled "w-a-t-e-r" into one of Keller's hands while water ran over the other, and Keller suddenly grasped that things have names — the breakthrough that opened language to her. It goes on to describe her studies, her love of literature and nature, her controversial early story "The Frost King", and her entry into Radcliffe. The published volume also includes Keller's letters and a supplementary account by her editor, John Albert Macy.

Legacy

Translated into some fifty languages, The Story of My Life remains a classic of American autobiography and a foundational text in the public understanding of deafblind education. Its scenes were dramatised in William Gibson's play and film The Miracle Worker.
