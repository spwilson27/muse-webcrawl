Israeli cuisine

Israeli cuisine comprises both local dishes traditionally prepared in the region and foods brought to Israel by Jewish immigrants from around the world. Since the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, and particularly since the late 1970s, an Israeli Jewish fusion cuisine has developed, drawing on Mizrahi, Sephardi, and Ashkenazi traditions as well as Palestinian Arab and broader Levantine and Mediterranean cooking.

History

The cuisine of the Land of Israel has ancient roots, shaped by the agricultural products of the region — wheat, olives, grapes, figs, pomegranates, and dates — the "seven species" mentioned in the Bible. Jewish dietary laws (kashrut) influenced cooking practices, as did the cuisines of the many countries from which Jewish immigrants arrived, including Yemen, Iraq, Morocco, Poland, Russia, and Germany.

Characteristic dishes

Well-known dishes include falafel, hummus, shakshuka, sabich, bourekas, schnitzel, and Israeli salad of finely diced tomato and cucumber. Street food is a major part of the food culture, and meals often centre on fresh salads, grilled meats, and pita bread.

Modern developments

Since the 1990s, Israeli chefs have gained international recognition, and restaurants in cities such as London and New York have popularised modern Israeli cooking abroad. Tel Aviv is also noted for a large number of vegan restaurants.
