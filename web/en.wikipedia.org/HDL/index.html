High-density lipoprotein

HDL
Full nameHigh-density lipoprotein
Size5–17 nm (smallest lipoprotein)
Key proteinApolipoprotein A1

High-density lipoprotein (HDL) is one of the five major groups of lipoproteins and the smallest and densest, rich in protein relative to lipid. HDL particles carry cholesterol from peripheral tissues back to the liver in reverse cholesterol transport, and HDL cholesterol (HDL-C) is often called "good cholesterol".

Function

Nascent HDL, built around ApoA-I secreted by liver and intestine, acquires free cholesterol from cells via ABCA1; LCAT esterifies it, maturing the particle. CETP exchanges HDL cholesteryl esters for triglycerides in VLDL. HDL also has anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties.

Clinical significance

Low HDL-C is epidemiologically associated with increased coronary artery disease risk, but trials raising HDL-C with niacin or CETP inhibitors largely failed to reduce events, suggesting HDL-C is a marker rather than a causal target. HDL-C enters risk calculators and is subtracted in the Friedewald equation.
