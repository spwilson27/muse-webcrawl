Rhineland-Palatinate (German: Rheinland-Pfalz) is a western German state (Land) covering 19,854 km² with a population of about 4.1 million. Its capital and largest university city is Mainz; other major cities include Ludwigshafen, Koblenz, Trier, Kaiserslautern, and Worms.

Geography

The state is defined by the valley of the Rhine and its tributaries — the Moselle, Lahn, Nahe, and Ahr — incised through upland ranges including the Eifel, Hunsrück, Westerwald, Taunus, and Palatinate Forest, the largest contiguous forest area in Germany. The Upper Middle Rhine Valley, with its castles, vineyards, and the Lorelei rock, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Wine growing dominates the river slopes: six of Germany's thirteen wine regions (Ahr, Mosel, Nahe, Rheinhessen, Pfalz, Mittelrhein) lie wholly or largely within the state, and it produces about two-thirds of German wine.

History

The territory was historically fragmented among the Electorate of Mainz, the Electorate of Trier, the Electoral Palatinate, and Prussia's Rhine Province. After the Second World War the French occupation authorities created the state in 1946 from the Prussian Rhineland districts of Koblenz and Trier, the Hessian province of Rhenish Hesse, and the Bavarian Palatinate; a 1956 referendum confirmed its continuation within the Federal Republic. Trier, its oldest city, preserves major Roman monuments, including the Porta Nigra, and the cathedrals of Mainz, Worms, and Speyer are masterpieces of Romanesque architecture; the Speyer Cathedral and the ShUM cities' Jewish heritage sites hold UNESCO status.

Economy

The state hosts the Ludwigshafen chemical complex of BASF, the world's largest integrated chemical site, alongside pharmaceuticals (including BioNTech, headquartered in Mainz, developer of the first widely used mRNA COVID-19 vaccine), automotive supply, and a strong wine and tourism sector.
