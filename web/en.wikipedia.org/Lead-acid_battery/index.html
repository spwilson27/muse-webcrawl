Lead-acid battery

Lead-acid battery
Inventor: Gaston Planté (1859)
Cell voltage: 2.1 V
Electrolyte: Dilute sulfuric acid
Electrodes: Lead and lead dioxide

A lead-acid battery is a rechargeable battery that uses lead dioxide and sponge lead electrodes immersed in a sulfuric acid electrolyte. Invented by French physicist Gaston Planté in 1859, it was the first type of rechargeable battery created.

Chemistry

During discharge, both electrodes react with the acid to form lead sulfate and water, releasing electrical energy; charging reverses the reaction. A typical cell produces about 2.1 volts, so a 12-volt car battery contains six cells in series.

Uses

Despite its low energy density compared with newer chemistries, the lead-acid battery remains the dominant choice for automobile starter batteries because it can deliver very high surge currents cheaply. It is also widely used in uninterruptible power supplies, backup lighting, forklifts and off-grid storage. Lead-acid batteries are among the most recycled consumer products in the world.
