Marcel Riesz

Marcel Riesz (1886–1969)
Born:16 November 1886, Győr, Austria-Hungary
Died:4 September 1969, Lund, Sweden
Fields:Functional analysis, harmonic analysis, potential theory
Institutions:Lund University; University of Maryland

Marcel Riesz (Hungarian: Riesz Marcell, 16 November 1886 – 4 September 1969) was a Hungarian mathematician, the younger brother of Frigyes Riesz, known for his work in classical analysis, summability theory, potential theory, and mathematical physics.

Career

Riesz studied in Budapest, Göttingen, and Paris, taking his doctorate under Lipót Fejér in 1912. Invited by Gösta Mittag-Leffler, he moved to Sweden in 1911 and spent most of his career there, becoming professor at Lund University in 1926. After retiring from Lund in 1952, he spent a decade at universities in the United States, principally the University of Maryland, before returning to Lund.

Work

His name is attached to several fundamental results. The Riesz potential, a fractional integral generalizing the Newtonian potential, arose from his study of the wave equation and Riemann–Liouville integrals in the 1930s–40s. The M. Riesz extension theorem (1923) generalizes the Hahn–Banach theorem for positive linear functionals, and the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem (1926, with Olof Thorin), on the boundedness of linear operators between Lp spaces, is a cornerstone of harmonic analysis. With G. H. Hardy he wrote the monograph The General Theory of Dirichlet's Series (1915). Late in life he developed a theory of generalized Clifford algebras and spinors.

His doctoral students included Harald Cramér and Einar Hille, and his collected papers were published in 1988.
