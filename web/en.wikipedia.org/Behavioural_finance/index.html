Behavioural finance

Behavioural finance
FieldFinance, psychology, economics
Key figuresDaniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky, Richard Thaler, Robert Shiller
Core conceptsProspect theory, cognitive bias, market anomalies, limits to arbitrage

Investor psychology can move market prices.

Behavioural finance is the study of how psychological influences and cognitive biases affect the behaviour of investors and financial markets. It seeks to explain market anomalies—such as bubbles, crashes and excess volatility—that are difficult to reconcile with the efficient-market hypothesis and perfectly rational agents.

Foundations

Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory (1979) showed that people evaluate gains and losses asymmetrically and overweight small probabilities. Richard Thaler documented systematic departures from rationality such as mental accounting and the endowment effect, while Robert Shiller emphasised narratives and irrational exuberance in asset prices.

Key biases

Documented biases include overconfidence, herding, loss aversion, anchoring and the disposition effect—the tendency to sell winners too early and hold losers too long. Behavioural finance also invokes limits to arbitrage to explain why smart money cannot always eliminate mispricing.
