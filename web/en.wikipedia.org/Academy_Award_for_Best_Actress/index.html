Academy Award for Best Actress

The Academy Award for Best Actress is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS). It is given in honour of an actress who has delivered an outstanding performance in a leading role in a film released that year. The award was traditionally presented by the previous year's Best Actor winner.

History

The 1st Academy Awards were held in 1929, and Janet Gaynor won the award for her performances in three films — 7th Heaven, Street Angel and Sunrise: A Song of Two Humans — the only occasion on which the award was given for multiple roles. Performances were initially listed for all of an actor's work in the eligibility period; from the following year the award recognised a single named performance.

Records

  • Most wins: Katharine Hepburn, with four (for Morning Glory, Guess Who's Coming to Dinner, The Lion in Winter and On Golden Pond).
  • Most nominations: Meryl Streep, with 17 nominations in this category (and further nominations as supporting actress).
  • Youngest winner: Marlee Matlin, aged 21, for Children of a Lesser God (1986).
  • Oldest winner: Jessica Tandy, aged 80, for Driving Miss Daisy (1989).
