Kōchi Prefecture (高知県, Kōchi-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located on the south coast of the island of Shikoku. Its capital and largest city is Kōchi. The prefecture corresponds to the historic Tosa Province, famous as the home of the Meiji Restoration hero Sakamoto Ryōma and the revolutionary movement that helped topple the Tokugawa shogunate.

Geography

Kōchi is the largest of Shikoku's four prefectures by area but the least populous. About 84% of its land is forested mountain terrain, drained by rivers such as the Shimanto — often called "Japan's last clear stream." Its long Pacific coastline faces the Kuroshio Current and is exposed to typhoons and the tsunami risk of the Nankai Trough.

Culture and economy

The prefecture is known for the exuberant Yosakoi dance festival held each August, bonito (katsuo) fishing and seared bonito tataki, yuzu citrus, and Tosa washi paper. Forestry, fishing and greenhouse horticulture dominate the economy, while population decline and ageing are pressing concerns.
