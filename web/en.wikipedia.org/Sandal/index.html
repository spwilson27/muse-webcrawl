Sandal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sandals are an open type of footwear, consisting of a sole held to the wearer's foot by straps going over the instep and around the ankle. Sandals can also have a heel. While the distinction between sandals and other types of footwear can sometimes be blurry, the common understanding is that a sandal leaves all or most of the foot exposed. People may wear sandals for several reasons, among them comfort in warm weather, economy, and as a fashion choice.

History

Sandals are among the oldest known footwear. The oldest known shoes, sagebrush-bark sandals found in Fort Rock Cave in Oregon, date to roughly 10,000 years ago. Ancient Egyptians wore sandals of palm leaves and papyrus, and the Greeks and Romans made extensive use of them; Roman caligae were heavy sandals worn by soldiers. In many ancient cultures sandals carried social and religious significance: removing one's sandals was a customary act of reverence on holy ground, and wealthy wearers used elaborately decorated examples in gold and jewels.

Construction and varieties

Traditional sandals are made of leather, rope, woven plant fibres, or wood. Modern sandals commonly use rubber, foam, and synthetic fabrics. Well-known varieties include the flip-flop (thong sandal), the gladiator sandal, the huarache of Mexico, the Japanese zōri and geta, and cushioned athletic sandals designed for hiking. Contemporary fashion sandals range from simple flat designs to high-heeled evening wear.

Cultural aspects

Sandals are everyday footwear in much of the tropics and subtropics, where closed shoes are uncomfortable in the heat. They also hold symbolic roles in several religions: Hindu and Buddhist monks often wear simple sandals, and in Christianity sandals are associated with humility and with Franciscan and other mendicant orders.
