Costa Rican cuisine

Costa Rican cuisine is known for being flavourful yet relatively mild, built around fresh local ingredients such as rice, beans, plantains, corn, vegetables, and tropical fruits, together with coffee. It draws on indigenous, Spanish, African, and Caribbean influences, with notable regional variation between the Central Valley and the Caribbean coast.

Staple dishes

The most emblematic dish is gallo pinto, a mixture of rice and beans — usually black beans in the Central Valley and red beans on the Caribbean coast — seasoned with onion, sweet pepper, cilantro, and Salsa Lizano, and traditionally eaten at breakfast with eggs, cheese, plantains, or natilla (sour cream). The typical lunch is the casado, a plated meal of rice, beans, salad, fried plantains, and a choice of meat, fish, or cheese.

Other foods

Other common dishes include olla de carne (a beef and vegetable soup), tamales wrapped in plantain leaves, patacones (fried green plantains), and, on the Caribbean coast, rice and beans cooked in coconut milk and rondón seafood soup. Popular drinks include coffee, agua dulce made from raw sugar cane, and fresh fruit juices (refrescos naturales).
