Derek Chauvin

Derek Michael Chauvin (born March 19, 1976) is an American former police officer who murdered George Floyd, a 46-year-old African-American man, in Minneapolis, Minnesota, on May 25, 2020. During an arrest, Chauvin, then an officer with the Minneapolis Police Department, knelt on Floyd's neck for about nine minutes while Floyd was handcuffed and lying face down, as bystanders recorded the incident.

The Hennepin County Government Center, where Chauvin was tried in 2021

Murder of George Floyd

Floyd had been arrested on suspicion of using a counterfeit $20 bill. During the arrest, Chauvin pressed his knee into Floyd's neck for 9 minutes and 29 seconds while Floyd repeatedly said he could not breathe. Floyd became unresponsive and was pronounced dead at hospital. Video of the killing sparked worldwide protests against police brutality and racism, and Chauvin was fired the following day.

Trials and convictions

In April 2021, a Minnesota jury convicted Chauvin of second-degree unintentional murder, third-degree murder, and second-degree manslaughter; he was sentenced to 22.5 years in prison. In December 2021 he pleaded guilty in federal court to violating Floyd's civil rights and received a concurrent federal sentence of 21 years. He had served on the Minneapolis force from 2001 to 2020, accumulating numerous complaints but few disciplinary consequences. In November 2023 he survived being stabbed by another inmate at a federal prison in Arizona.

Reactions and legacy

The case became a landmark in debates over policing in the United States, prompting departmental bans on chokeholds and neck restraints, the federal George Floyd Justice in Policing Act proposals (which stalled in Congress), and a Justice Department pattern-or-practice investigation of the Minneapolis Police Department.
