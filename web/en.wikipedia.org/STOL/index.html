STOL

STOL is an acronym for short take-off and landing, describing aircraft with short runway requirements. A STOL aircraft can operate from runways far shorter than those needed by conventional aeroplanes of similar size, thanks to high-lift wings, large flaps, low stall speeds and sometimes powered lift.

Characteristics

There is no single universal definition, but STOL performance is generally associated with take-off and landing distances of a few hundred metres or less. Design features include large wing area, leading-edge slats, double-slotted flaps, and propellers or blown flaps that keep airflow attached at low speeds. Examples include the de Havilland Canada DHC-6 Twin Otter and the Antonov An-2.

Related concepts

Related categories include VTOL (vertical take-off and landing), STOVL (short take-off and vertical landing), and CTOL (conventional take-off and landing). STOLports are small airports built specifically for STOL operations, such as London City Airport in its early configuration.
