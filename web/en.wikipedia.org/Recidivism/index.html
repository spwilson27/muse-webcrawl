Recidivism

Recidivism rates vary widely between jurisdictions and depend heavily on how reoffending is measured.

Recidivism (from Latin recidivus, "recurring", from re- "again" and cado "I fall") is the tendency of a convicted criminal to reoffend. The term is used by criminologists and policymakers to describe the relapse of released prisoners into criminal behaviour, and recidivism rates are a principal measure of the effectiveness of a criminal justice system and of rehabilitation programmes.

Measurement

There is no single standard measure. Studies may count rearrest, reconviction or reincarceration, and may follow cohorts for periods of one to nine years, so figures are not always comparable. In the United States, a widely cited Bureau of Justice Statistics study of prisoners released in 2005 across 30 states found that about 68 per cent were rearrested within three years and about 77 per cent within five years, although a much smaller proportion returned to prison for a new offence. Rates in some Northern European countries are markedly lower; Norway, for example, reports two-year reconviction rates around 20–25 per cent.

Factors

Research associates higher recidivism with younger age at first arrest, extensive criminal history, substance abuse, unemployment, unstable housing and antisocial peers, while employment, stable relationships and completion of education or treatment programmes are associated with desistance. The "risk–need–responsivity" model holds that supervision and treatment reduce reoffending when they are matched to an offender's risk level and criminogenic needs.

Reduction efforts

Programmes shown in meta-analyses to reduce recidivism include cognitive-behavioural therapy, drug treatment, education and vocational training, and some forms of restorative justice. Conversely, purely punitive measures such as "scared straight" schemes and intensive surveillance without treatment have generally shown little or no benefit. Housing-first and re-entry support services aim to remove the practical obstacles that push released prisoners back into offending.
