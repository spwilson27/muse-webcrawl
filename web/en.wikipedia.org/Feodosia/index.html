Feodosia

Feodosia (Russian: Феодосия; Ukrainian: Феодосія; from Greek: Θεοδοσία, "gift of the gods") is a port city on the south-eastern coast of Crimea, on the Black Sea. Founded by Greek colonists from Miletus in the 6th century BC, it is one of the oldest cities in the region, with a recorded history of more than 2,500 years. Its population is roughly 70,000.

History

In antiquity, Theodosia was a Greek colony and later part of the Bosporan Kingdom, then passed under Roman and Byzantine influence. In the 13th century the Genoese acquired the city and rebuilt it as Caffa, which became one of the largest trading ports of the Black Sea and a major hub of the slave trade. In 1347, during the Mongol siege of Caffa, plague-infected corpses were reportedly catapulted into the city — an event often cited in accounts of the Black Death's arrival in Europe, as Genoese ships carried the disease westward. The Ottoman Empire captured Caffa in 1475. The Russian Empire annexed Crimea in 1783, and the city resumed the name Feodosia.

Modern city

Today Feodosia is a seaside resort and port, known for its beaches, mud baths, and mineral springs. It was the home of the marine painter Ivan Aivazovsky, whose gallery in the town remains a major attraction, and of the writer Alexander Grin. The remains of the Genoese fortress still overlook the bay. Since 2014 the city has been administered by Russia as part of the Republic of Crimea, a status disputed internationally, with Ukraine and most UN member states regarding Crimea as Ukrainian territory.
