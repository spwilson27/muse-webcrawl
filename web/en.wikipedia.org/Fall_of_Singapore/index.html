Fall of Singapore

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Battle of Singapore, also known as the Fall of Singapore, was fought in the South-East Asian theatre of the Second World War when the Empire of Japan captured the British-ruled island city of Singapore from 8 to 15 February 1942. The surrender of some 80,000 British, Indian and Australian troops was the largest capitulation of British-led forces in history, and Winston Churchill called it the "worst disaster" in British military history.

Background

Singapore was the linchpin of British strategy in the Far East — a major naval base guarding the sea routes between the Indian and Pacific Oceans. Its defences, however, were oriented toward attack from the sea. Japanese forces under General Tomoyuki Yamashita invaded Malaya on 8 December 1941 and advanced rapidly down the Malay Peninsula, using jungle tracks, bicycles and air superiority, defeating the naval Force Z (whose battleships Prince of Wales and Repulse were sunk on 10 December). By the end of January 1942, Commonwealth forces had withdrawn across the Johor Strait onto Singapore Island.

The battle

On the night of 8–9 February 1942, Japanese troops crossed the strait and landed on the island's north-western shore, overwhelming the Australian defenders there. Despite outnumbering the attackers, the defenders under Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival were outmanoeuvred, short of water after the loss of the reservoirs, and unable to concentrate their forces. On 15 February 1942 — Chinese New Year — Percival surrendered at the Ford Motor Factory in Bukit Timah.

Aftermath

Some 80,000 Allied soldiers joined 50,000 already captured in Malaya as prisoners of war; many died in captivity, including on the Burma Railway. Singapore was renamed Syonan-to and occupied until 1945; its Chinese population suffered massacres in the Sook Ching operation. The fall of the supposedly impregnable fortress shattered the prestige of the British Empire in Asia and accelerated demands for independence across the region.
