The Bahmani Sultanate was a Muslim state of the Deccan in South India and one of the major medieval Indian kingdoms. Founded in 1347 by Ala-ud-Din Bahman Shah in revolt against the Delhi Sultanate, it was the first independent Muslim kingdom in the Deccan.

History

The sultanate's first capital was Gulbarga (Ahsanabad); Ahmad Shah I moved the capital to Bidar in 1425. The Bahmanis fought a long series of wars with the Hindu Vijayanagara Empire to the south over the fertile Raichur Doab. Under the wazir Mahmud Gawan in the 1460s and 1470s the kingdom reached its greatest extent, stretching from coast to coast.

Decline and fragmentation

After Mahmud Gawan's execution in 1481 factional strife between Deccani and foreign (Afaqi) nobles weakened the court. By the early 16th century the sultanate had fragmented into the five Deccan sultanates: Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar and Berar. The last Bahmani sultan, Kalimullah, fled Bidar in 1527.

Culture

The Bahmanis patronized Persianate art and architecture, including the madrasa of Mahmud Gawan at Bidar and the Haft Gumbaz tombs at Gulbarga, and the sultanate played a key role in the development of the Deccani language.
