The West Indies men's cricket team, nicknamed the Windies, is a men's cricket team representing the mainly English-speaking countries and territories in the Caribbean region and administered by Cricket West Indies. As of 2024, the team has participated in every edition of the Cricket World Cup and won the first two editions held in 1975 and 1979. They were also the first team to win the ICC T20 World Cup twice, in 2012 and 2016.

Players such as George Headley, Everton Weekes, Clyde Walcott and Frank Worrell — the "three Ws" — laid the foundations of West Indian cricket in the post-war era. The team reached its zenith under the captaincy of Clive Lloyd and later Viv Richards in the late 1970s and 1980s, fielding a fearsome pace attack of Malcolm Marshall, Michael Holding, Andy Roberts, Joel Garner and Courtney Walsh, and going fifteen years without losing a Test series between 1980 and 1995.

History

Early years

A combined West Indies team first toured England in 1900, and the West Indies Cricket Board of Control was admitted to the Imperial Cricket Conference in 1926. The side played its first Test match at Lord's in June 1928, losing to England. George Headley, the great Jamaican batsman known as the "black Bradman", was the outstanding player of the inter-war period.

Post-war rise

Under John Goddard the West Indies won their first Test series in England in 1950, remembered for the spin bowling of Sonny Ramadhin and Alf Valentine and celebrated in the calypso "Victory Test Match". Frank Worrell became the first black man appointed captain for a full series in 1960–61, leading the celebrated tour of Australia that ended with the first tied Test in Brisbane. Garry Sobers, arguably the greatest all-rounder in the game's history, succeeded him.

Dominance: 1976–1995

Clive Lloyd moulded the side into the most dominant team in world cricket. The West Indies won the inaugural World Cup in 1975 and retained it in 1979, losing the 1983 final to India. With batsmen such as Viv Richards, Gordon Greenidge and Desmond Haynes, and an assembly line of fast bowlers, they set a record of 11 consecutive Test victories in 1984 and did not lose a Test series for fifteen years.

Decline and T20 success

The retirement of the greats, disputes between players and the board, and the rise of other nations saw a steep decline from the late 1990s, despite the individual brilliance of Brian Lara, who holds the world records for the highest individual Test score (400 not out) and first-class score (501 not out). In the shortest format, however, the West Indies flourished: captained by Darren Sammy, they won the World Twenty20 in 2012 and again in 2016, when Carlos Brathwaite hit four consecutive sixes in the final over against England.

Governing body

Cricket West Indies (CWI), formerly the West Indies Cricket Board, administers the team. Its member associations are Barbados, Guyana, Jamaica, the Leeward Islands, Trinidad and Tobago, and the Windward Islands. The team draws players from sovereign states including Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, Jamaica, Saint Lucia, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and Trinidad and Tobago, as well as territories such as Montserrat and Anguilla.

Tournament history

  • Cricket World Cup: champions 1975, 1979; runners-up 1983.
  • ICC T20 World Cup: champions 2012, 2016.
  • ICC Champions Trophy: champions 2004.
