Thromboxane A2

Thromboxane A2 (TXA2) is a type of thromboxane, a lipid in the eicosanoid family, that is produced in activated platelets during haemostasis. It has prothrombotic properties: it stimulates activation of new platelets and increases platelet aggregation, and it is also a potent vasoconstrictor.

Synthesis and function

Thromboxane A2 is generated from prostaglandin H2 by thromboxane-A synthase in platelets. It has a very short half-life of about 30 seconds in the blood, being rapidly hydrolysed to the inactive thromboxane B2. Its effects are balanced by prostacyclin (PGI2) released from the endothelium, which inhibits platelet aggregation and dilates vessels.

Clinical relevance

Low-dose aspirin irreversibly inhibits the enzyme cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) in platelets, suppressing thromboxane A2 production for the life of the platelet; this is the basis of aspirin's use in preventing heart attacks and strokes.
