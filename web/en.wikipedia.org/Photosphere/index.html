Photosphere

The photosphere is the lowest layer of a star's atmosphere and the surface from which most of its visible light is emitted. For the Sun, the photosphere has an effective temperature of about 5,772 K (5,499 °C) and a thickness of only a few hundred kilometres, making it effectively the visible "surface" of the Sun despite being composed of gas.

Properties

The photosphere marks the region where the plasma becomes transparent to photons: below it the gas is opaque. Observed with telescopes, the solar photosphere shows granulation — a pattern of convection cells roughly 1,000 km across — as well as darker sunspots, which are regions of intense magnetic activity some 1,500 K cooler than their surroundings, and bright faculae. Towards the edge of the solar disc the photosphere appears dimmer, an effect called limb darkening, because sight lines there sample higher, cooler layers.

Other layers

Above the photosphere lie the chromosphere and the corona, which are far hotter but much less dense and are normally visible only during total solar eclipses or with special instruments. The concept applies to all stars: a star's measured radius and temperature are essentially the properties of its photosphere.
