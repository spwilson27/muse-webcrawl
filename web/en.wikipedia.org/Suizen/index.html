Suizen

Suizen (吹禅, "blowing Zen") is the Zen Buddhist practice of meditation performed by playing the shakuhachi, a Japanese bamboo flute. It was practised especially by the komusō, mendicant monks of the Fuke sect of Zen Buddhism during the Edo period, who played solo pieces called honkyoku.

Practice

In suizen, the act of blowing the flute is itself the meditation: breath, sound, and attention are unified, and the music produced is a by-product rather than the goal. The difficult, breathy tones of the shakuhachi demand sustained concentration, making the instrument a tool for attaining enlightenment. Practitioners traditionally played while wearing a tengai, a woven basket hat covering the head, symbolising the abandonment of ego.

Honkyoku

The repertoire of suizen consists of honkyoku ("original pieces"), solo works transmitted within temples such as Myōan-ji in Kyoto. Pieces such as Kyorei, Mukaiji, and Kokū are traditionally attributed to the legendary flute-playing monk Kyochiku. The pieces are characterised by long phrases, pitch bending, and extensive use of silence (ma).

History

The Fuke sect traced its lineage to the Chinese Chan master Puhua (Fuke in Japanese). During the Edo period the komusō enjoyed special privileges from the Tokugawa shogunate, including freedom of travel. After the sect was abolished in 1871 during the Meiji Restoration, suizen survived through lay lineages and today is practised worldwide both as a spiritual discipline and as an art music tradition.
