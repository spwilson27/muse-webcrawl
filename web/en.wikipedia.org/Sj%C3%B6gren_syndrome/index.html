Sjögren syndrome

Sjögren syndrome is a long-term autoimmune disease that primarily affects the body's moisture-producing glands, especially the tear and salivary glands. It is named after the Swedish ophthalmologist Henrik Sjögren, who described it in 1933. Hallmark symptoms are dry eyes (keratoconjunctivitis sicca) and dry mouth (xerostomia), though the condition can also involve the joints, skin, lungs, kidneys and nervous system.

Sjögren syndrome

Specialty: Rheumatology, immunology
Symptoms: Dry eyes, dry mouth, fatigue
Named for: Henrik Sjögren
Treatment: Symptomatic; immunosuppressants in severe cases

Classification

The syndrome is classified as primary when it occurs alone and secondary when it accompanies another autoimmune disease such as rheumatoid arthritis or lupus. Diagnosis relies on clinical findings, anti-Ro/SSA and anti-La/SSB autoantibodies, and sometimes a labial salivary-gland biopsy.

Management

There is no cure; treatment targets symptoms and complications. Artificial tears, saliva substitutes, and drugs such as pilocarpine help dryness, while hydroxychloroquine and other immunomodulators may be used for systemic features. Patients have an increased risk of lymphoma and require long-term monitoring.
