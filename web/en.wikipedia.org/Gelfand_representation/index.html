Gelfand representation

Schematic of the Gelfand transform mapping algebra elements to functions on the character space.

In mathematics, the Gelfand representation, named after Israel Gelfand, associates to a commutative Banach algebra a topological space — its character space (or spectrum) of non-zero homomorphisms into the complex numbers — and represents elements of the algebra as continuous functions on that space vanishing at infinity. The assignment of values at each character is called the Gelfand transform.

Theory

For a commutative Banach algebra A, the Gelfand transform is an algebra homomorphism from A into the algebra of continuous functions on its character space equipped with the Gelfand topology. Its kernel is the Jacobson radical, so the transform is injective when the algebra is semisimple. In the special case of a commutative C*-algebra, the Gelfand–Naimark theorem states that the transform is an isometric isomorphism onto the continuous functions on the character space, which establishes a duality between commutative C*-algebras and locally compact Hausdorff spaces.

Applications

The theory underlies the spectral theory of normal operators, the proof of Wiener's theorem on Fourier series, and the modern approach to harmonic analysis on locally compact abelian groups, where the group algebra's character space recovers the dual group.
