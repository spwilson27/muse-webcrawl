Volkswagen Type 2

The Volkswagen Type 2, known officially as the Transporter, Kombi or Microbus and informally as the "VW Bus" or "Camper", is a light commercial vehicle introduced in 1950 by the German automaker Volkswagen as its second car model — following the Type 1 Beetle. Its forward-control, rear-engined layout placed the driver over the front wheels and maximised cargo space.

Generations

The first generation (T1, 1950–1967) is recognisable by its split windscreen and V-shaped front panel; deluxe "Samba" versions had up to 23 windows. The T2 "bay window" (1967–1979, and built in Brazil until 2013) became an icon of the 1960s counterculture and of surf and camping culture. Later generations — the boxier T3, and the front-engined, front-wheel-drive T4, T5, T6 and T7 — moved progressively toward modern van design; the electric ID. Buzz revives the classic silhouette.

Variants and use

Type 2s were produced as panel vans, pickups, ambulances, minibuses and camper conversions (notably by Westfalia). In Brazil the Kombi remained in production for 63 years, the last air-cooled model ending production in 2013 when safety regulations required airbags and ABS it could not accommodate.
