Dog sports

Dog sports are competitive or recreational activities in which dogs and their handlers participate, often under the sanction of kennel clubs and sporting organizations. Many sports derive from the original working purposes of dog breeds, such as herding, hunting, guarding and pulling.

Types of dog sports

  • Agility — dogs negotiate an obstacle course of jumps, tunnels, weave poles and contact obstacles against the clock.
  • Obedience and rally obedience — precision performance of commands and exercises.
  • Flyball — relay racing over hurdles to retrieve a ball.
  • Dock jumping — dogs leap for distance or height into water.
  • Herding trials, field trials and hunt tests — assessments of working herding and gundog ability.
  • Sled dog racing, skijoring, canicross and bikejoring — pulling and mushing sports.
  • Schutzhund/IGP — tracking, obedience and protection work.
  • Disc dog, lure coursing, nose work/scent work, barn hunt, weight pulling, carting and dog dancing (musical freestyle).

Organization

Events are sanctioned by bodies such as the American Kennel Club, the United Kennel Club, the Fédération Cynologique Internationale and sport-specific organizations. Many competitions are open to mixed-breed dogs as well as purebreds. Supporters value dog sports for exercise, training and the human–dog bond; animal-welfare critics focus on injury risks and the demands of some disciplines.
