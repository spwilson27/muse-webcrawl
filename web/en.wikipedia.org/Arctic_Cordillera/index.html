Arctic Cordillera

The Arctic Cordillera is a mountain range running along the northeastern flank of the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, from Ellesmere Island in the north to the Labrador Peninsula in the south. It is one of Canada's ecozones and physiographic regions, containing some of the country's highest and most heavily glaciated mountains, including Mount Barbeau on Ellesmere Island, at 2,616 m the highest point in eastern North America.

Geography

The cordillera consists of a series of individual ranges—such as the British Empire Range, the United States Range, the Torngat Mountains and the mountains of Baffin Island (including the Baffin Mountains of Auyuittuq National Park). It includes large ice caps such as the Penny and Barnes ice caps on Baffin Island and the Devon Ice Cap, remnants associated with the former Innuitian ice sheet. The range was uplifted during the Innuitian orogeny and later deeply dissected by glaciers, leaving fjords, U-shaped valleys and sharp alpine peaks.

Climate and ecology

The climate is polar desert and tundra: long, extremely cold winters, short cool summers and very low precipitation. Vegetation is sparse—mosses, lichens and dwarf shrubs—while wildlife includes muskoxen, Arctic hares, lemmings, Peary caribou and, along the coasts, polar bears and seals. Much of the range lies within protected areas such as Quttinirpaaq and Sirmilik national parks.
