The Rugby Football Union (RFU) is the national governing body for rugby union in England. Founded in 1871, it was the first rugby union governing body in the world, and it administers the game at all levels in England, including the England national team, whose home ground is Twickenham Stadium in London — the largest dedicated rugby stadium in the world.

History

The RFU was founded on 26 January 1871 at the Pall Mall Restaurant in London, when representatives of 21 clubs met to standardise the laws of the game. Later that year the first written laws were approved, and England played Scotland in the first rugby international in Edinburgh. In 1886 the RFU was a founding member of the International Rugby Football Board (now World Rugby).

The Union's insistence on strict amateurism — in particular its refusal to allow compensation for "broken time" lost at work — led in 1895 to the schism in which 22 northern clubs resigned and formed the Northern Union, the forerunner of rugby league. The RFU maintained its amateur ethos for a century, until the game was declared "open" in 1995 following the World Cup in South Africa.

Role

The RFU is a registered cooperative society owned by its member clubs. It organises the community game through county constituent bodies, runs the women's and age-grade pathways, and with Premiership Rugby shares management of the elite club game. Its income derives largely from England internationals at Twickenham, broadcasting and sponsorship, and is reinvested in the sport.
