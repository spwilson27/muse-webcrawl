Anthracene

Anthracene crystals

Formula: C14H10
Molar mass: 178.23 g/mol
Appearance: Colourless to pale yellow solid
Melting point: 216 °C

Anthracene is a solid polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) consisting of three fused benzene rings in a linear arrangement, with the formula C14H10. It is a component of coal tar, from which it is obtained commercially, and is an isomer of phenanthrene, in which the rings are arranged at an angle.

Properties and reactions

Anthracene is colourless when pure and shows a blue fluorescence under ultraviolet light. Chemically it is reactive at its central (9,10) positions: it readily undergoes Diels–Alder reactions across the middle ring, oxidation to anthraquinone, and photodimerisation in sunlight. Anthracene crystals were also historically important in physics as scintillators for detecting radiation and in early studies of organic semiconductors.

Uses

The principal industrial use of anthracene is its oxidation to anthraquinone, a feedstock for dyes and for the industrial production of hydrogen peroxide. Anthracene itself has limited direct uses; like other PAHs it is monitored as an environmental pollutant arising from incomplete combustion. See also polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon.
