E-Residency of Estonia

E-Residency of Estonia is a programme launched by the government of Estonia on 1 December 2014 that gives non-Estonians access to a government-issued digital identity, enabling them to use Estonian online services, open an Estonian company entirely online and sign documents digitally. It is often described as the first step toward a "country as a service" model and transnational digital identity.

The programme

Applicants apply online, undergo a background check by the Estonian Police and Border Guard Board, and collect a smart card containing digital certificates at an Estonian embassy or consulate. The card allows secure authentication and legally binding digital signatures recognised across the European Union under eIDAS.

Use by entrepreneurs

E-residents can register an EU-based company in Estonia, manage it remotely and access Estonian banking and payment services. The programme has attracted tens of thousands of e-residents from over 170 countries, who have founded thousands of companies. E-residency does not confer citizenship, physical residency, or tax residency, nor a right to travel to Estonia.

History and criticism

The initiative grew out of Estonia's advanced e-government infrastructure (the X-Road data exchange layer). A former Edward Snowden article competition in a business magazine helped publicise the idea. Critics have raised questions about security supervision and whether the benefits are mainly symbolic, while supporters point to soft power and business creation.
