Salvation

Salvation (from Latin salvatio, from salva, "safe, saved") is the saving of human beings from sin and its consequences, most centrally death and separation from God, in the theology of the Abrahamic religions. The academic study of salvation is called soteriology. In a broader sense, the term is applied to deliverance or liberation in other religions, such as liberation (moksha) from the cycle of rebirth in Indian traditions.

In Christianity

In Christianity, salvation is understood as deliverance from sin and its effects through the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian traditions differ over how salvation is received and mediated: Catholic and Orthodox teaching emphasizes faith, grace and the sacraments administered by the Church, while Protestant traditions arising from the Reformation emphasize justification by faith (sola fide), with internal divisions — for example between Calvinist doctrines of predestination and Arminian doctrines of free will — over whether salvation, once received, can be lost. Related concepts include atonement, grace, redemption and eternal life.

In other religions

In Judaism, salvation is primarily communal and historical — God's deliverance of Israel, as in the Exodus — alongside individual righteousness and repentance (teshuvah). In Islam, salvation depends on faith in God, good works and God's mercy, with final judgment on the Day of Resurrection. In Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism, the analogous goal is release from saṃsāra, the cycle of birth, death and rebirth, attained through knowledge, devotion or disciplined practice.
