Fundamental diagram of traffic flow

The fundamental diagram of traffic flow is a diagram that gives a relation between road traffic flow (vehicles per hour), density (vehicles per kilometre), and speed. It is the key relation of traffic flow theory, linking the three macroscopic variables by the identity flow = density × speed.

Description

Flow increases with density at low densities (free-flow conditions, near the free-flow speed) up to a maximum known as capacity, reached at the critical density. Beyond that point, traffic becomes congested: as density rises toward the jam density, both speed and flow fall. The curve's two regimes explain phenomena such as shock waves and stop-and-go traffic.

Models

Early forms were proposed by Greenshields (1935), who assumed a linear speed–density relationship, yielding a parabolic flow–density curve. Later models by Greenberg, Underwood, and others fit observed data better, and modern traffic engineering calibrates the diagram for each road using detector data.
