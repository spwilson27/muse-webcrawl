Hellhound

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A hellhound is a mythological hound that embodies the guardian of a hell, death, the underworld or the dead, similar to a demon. Hellhounds are typically embodied as huge black dogs, or sometimes as small demon-like creatures, with glowing eyes and fiery breath. They are often assigned to guard the entrances to the world of the dead, such as graveyards and burial grounds, or undertake other duties related to the afterlife or the supernatural, such as hunting lost souls or guarding a supernatural treasure.

In world mythology

The most famous hellhound is Cerberus of Greek mythology, the multi-headed dog (usually three-headed) who guards the gates of Hades, allowing the dead to enter but none to leave; he was overcome by Heracles as his twelfth labour. In Norse mythology, Garmr is the blood-stained hound chained at the entrance to Hel who breaks free at Ragnarök to fight the god Týr. Welsh tradition has the Cŵn Annwn, spectral hounds of the Otherworld (Annwn) whose hunting calls — the "Gabriel hounds" tradition also survives in northern England — are omens of death.

British folk tradition

Britain is rich in phantom black-dog lore closely related to the hellhound: the Black Shuck of East Anglia (said to have burst into churches at Bungay and Blythburgh in 1577, leaving scorch marks still pointed out on a door), the Moddey Dhoo of the Isle of Man, the Barghest of Yorkshire, the Padfoot, and the Gytrash. Such dogs are typically enormous, black, with saucer-like glowing eyes, appearing on lonely roads, churchyards and ancient tracks; some are omens of death, others relatively benign guides. Conan Doyle drew on Dartmoor legends of a spectral hound for The Hound of the Baskervilles (1902).

Other traditions

Analogues appear worldwide: the Nahua and Maya underworld dogs who guide the dead; in parts of Europe, the "Wild Hunt" is led by demonic hounds; in the Americas, folklore tells of the cadejo (Central America) and the hellhounds of the southern United States linked to crossroads legends.

In popular culture

Hellhounds appear throughout modern fantasy and horror — in the Harry Potter books (the three-headed Fluffy, after Cerberus), the television series Supernatural and Teen Wolf, video games such as Call of Duty (zombie hellhounds), and role-playing games, where they are often fire-breathing pack hunters serving devils.
