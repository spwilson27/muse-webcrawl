Birch syrup

Birch syrup is a savory mineral-tasting syrup made from birch sap, produced mainly in Alaska, Canada and Russia from the sap of birch trees such as paper birch and Alaska birch. Unlike maple syrup, its sugar is predominantly fructose rather than sucrose.

Production

Birch sap flows later in spring than maple sap, after maple season ends. The sap is much more dilute — typically 80 to 110 litres of sap are needed to make one litre of syrup, roughly twice the ratio required for maple — so producers rely heavily on reverse osmosis to remove water before boiling. The syrup is difficult to make and correspondingly expensive.

Flavour and use

Birch syrup is darker and less sweet than maple syrup, with molasses-like, caramel and slightly bitter or spicy notes. It is used as a gourmet condiment in glazes, marinades, sauces, desserts and drinks rather than primarily as a pancake syrup.
