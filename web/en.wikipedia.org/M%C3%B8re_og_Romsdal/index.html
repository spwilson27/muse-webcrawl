Møre og Romsdal is a county in the northernmost part of Western Norway. It borders the counties of Trøndelag, Innlandet, and Vestland, and faces the Norwegian Sea. The county seat is the town of Molde; the largest town is Ålesund.

Geography

The county is traditionally divided into three districts: Nordmøre in the north, Romsdal in the centre, and Sunnmøre in the south. The landscape is dominated by deep fjords — including the Storfjorden system, Romsdalsfjord, and the celebrated Geirangerfjord, a UNESCO World Heritage Site — cut into steep alpine mountains such as the Sunnmøre Alps and the Trolltindene range. The Trollstigen mountain road and the Atlantic Ocean Road are famous tourist routes.

Economy

Fishing, fish processing, and aquaculture are central to the coastal economy, and the county has a strong maritime and shipbuilding industry around Ålesund and Ulsteinvik. Petroleum-related supply industries, furniture manufacture, and tourism are also significant employers.

Transport

The county's many islands and fjords make ferries and subsea road tunnels essential; the E39 coastal highway and the Rauma railway line (ending at Åndalsnes) serve the region, and the Hurtigruten coastal steamer calls at several ports.
