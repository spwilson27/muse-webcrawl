Saadi Shirazi

Abū-Muhammad Muslih al-Dīn bin Abdallāh Shīrāzī (Persian: ابومحمد مصلح‌الدین بن عبدالله شیرازی), better known by his pen name Saadi (سعدی, romanized: Saʿdī, lit. '[the] fortunate'), also known as Saadi of Shiraz (سعدی شیرازی Saadi Shirāzi), (1210 – 1291 or 1292) was a Persian poet and prose writer of the medieval period. He is recognized for the quality of his writings and for the depth of his social thoughts. Saadi is widely recognized as one of the greatest poets of the classical literary tradition, earning him the nickname "the Master of Speech" (استاد سخن Ostād-e Sokhan) among Persian scholars.

Life

Saadi was born in Shiraz, Iran. After losing his father in childhood, he was educated in Shiraz and later studied at the famous Nizamiyya school in Baghdad, where he was influenced by Sufi teachers including Ibn al-Jawzi's grandson and Shihab al-Din Umar al-Suhrawardi. Following the Mongol invasions of Persia, Saadi spent roughly three decades travelling through Anatolia, Syria, Egypt, Arabia, Iraq, India, and possibly Central Asia, before returning to Shiraz around 1257, where he remained until his death.

Works

His two most famous works are the Bustan (The Orchard), completed in 1257, a book of poetry in the mathnawi form concerned with ethics, and the Gulistan (The Rose Garden), completed in 1258, a mixture of prose and verse containing stories, anecdotes, and personal reflections. His famous aphorism about human solidarity, Bani Adam ("Human beings are limbs of one body..."), is inscribed at the entrance of the United Nations headquarters in New York.
