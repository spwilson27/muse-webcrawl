The Yamal Peninsula (Russian: полуостров Ямал) is a large peninsula in the Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug of northwest Siberia, Russia. Extending roughly 700 kilometres (430 mi) northward into the Kara Sea between Baydaratskaya Bay to the west and the Gulf of Ob to the east, its name in the Nenets language means "the end of the land."

Geography and climate

The peninsula is a low, flat expanse of tundra dotted with lakes and underlain by permafrost. Winters are long and bitterly cold, summers brief and cool. The landscape supports large herds of reindeer herded by the indigenous Nenets people, whose traditional nomadic lifestyle has continued into the modern era. The peninsula is also famous for exceptionally preserved mammoth remains, including the baby mammoth "Lyuba," found by a Nenets reindeer herder in 2007, and for the large gas-release craters that have appeared in the permafrost since 2014.

Natural gas

Yamal holds some of the largest undeveloped natural gas reserves on Earth, estimated at more than 50 trillion cubic metres in total. Development by Gazprom and partners includes the giant Bovanenkovo field, put into production in 2012, and the Yamal LNG plant at Sabetta, built with Novatek and international partners, which ships liquefied natural gas along the Northern Sea Route using ice-class tankers. Gas extraction and infrastructure have raised concerns about effects on Nenets reindeer migration and the fragile tundra environment.
