Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act

The Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act (NAGPRA), enacted by the United States Congress on 16 November 1990, is a federal law requiring federal agencies and institutions that receive federal funding to return Native American human remains and cultural items — funerary objects, sacred objects and objects of cultural patrimony — to lineal descendants and culturally affiliated tribes and Native Hawaiian organizations.

Provisions

NAGPRA required museums and agencies to inventory human remains and associated funerary objects, summarise other cultural items, consult with tribes, and repatriate items on request where cultural affiliation could be shown. It also governs the excavation and inadvertent discovery of remains on federal and tribal lands, prohibits trafficking in Native American human remains and cultural items, and established a review committee to monitor the process.

Implementation and controversy

Implementation was slow for decades; major museums held large collections of remains. A long-running dispute concerned the Ancient One (Kennewick Man), whose remains were repatriated and reburied in 2017 after DNA evidence showed affiliation with modern tribes. Revised regulations that took effect in January 2024 strengthened consultation requirements and the duty of care, set deadlines for completing repatriation, and prompted many institutions to remove Native American items from display.
