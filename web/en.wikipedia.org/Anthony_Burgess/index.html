Anthony Burgess

Anthony Burgess

Born: 25 February 1917, Manchester, England
Died: 22 November 1993, London
Occupation: Novelist, composer, critic
Notable works: A Clockwork Orange, Earthly Powers

John Anthony Burgess Wilson (25 February 1917 – 22 November 1993), who published as Anthony Burgess, was an English writer and composer. Though he wrote more than thirty novels, he is best known for the dystopian satire A Clockwork Orange (1962), whose invented teenage slang, Nadsat, and meditation on free will were amplified by Stanley Kubrick's 1971 film adaptation.

Life

Born in Manchester, Burgess studied English at the University of Manchester, served in the army during the Second World War, and taught in Malaya and Brunei — experiences behind his Malayan Trilogy. Told (wrongly) in 1959 that he had a brain tumour and a year to live, he wrote prolifically to provide for his wife; he in fact lived for more than three further decades, settling in Monaco.

Works

His novels include the Enderby tetralogy, Nothing Like the Sun (a fictional life of Shakespeare), Napoleon Symphony and Earthly Powers (1980), often named his finest book. Burgess was also a prolific composer of symphonies and chamber music, a linguist, translator and critic, and wrote studies of Joyce, Hemingway and D. H. Lawrence as well as the autobiographies Little Wilson and Big God and You've Had Your Time.
