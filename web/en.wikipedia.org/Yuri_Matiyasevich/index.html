Yuri Matiyasevich

Yuri Vladimirovich Matiyasevich
Born: 2 March 1947, Leningrad, USSR
Field: Mathematical logic, number theory
Known for: Solving Hilbert's tenth problem (MRDP theorem)

Yuri Vladimirovich Matiyasevich (born 1947) is a Russian mathematician and computer scientist, celebrated for his 1970 solution of Hilbert's tenth problem, which proved that no general algorithm can decide whether arbitrary Diophantine equations have integer solutions.

The tenth problem

David Hilbert in 1900 asked for an algorithm that, given any polynomial equation with integer coefficients, determines whether it has an integer solution. Martin Davis, Hilary Putnam, and Julia Robinson had reduced the problem to showing that a single exponential-growth Diophantine relation is Diophantine. In January 1970, the 22-year-old Matiyasevich — using Fibonacci numbers — proved exactly that the relation y = F(2x) is Diophantine, completing the MRDP theorem: every recursively enumerable set is Diophantine, so Diophantine solvability is undecidable.

Career

Matiyasevich has spent his career at the St. Petersburg Department of the Steklov Institute of Mathematics (PDMI RAS), where he heads the Laboratory of Mathematical Logic. His monograph Hilbert's Tenth Problem (1993) is the standard account. He has also worked on the Riemann zeta function, computational complexity, and the combinatorics of word equations, and served repeated terms on the programme and organising side of Russian mathematics olympiads and logic conferences. He is a full member of the Russian Academy of Sciences.
