The Derby Stakes, popularly known as the Derby or the Epsom Derby, is a Group 1 flat horse race in England open to three-year-old colts and fillies. It is run at Epsom Downs Racecourse in Surrey over a distance of one mile, four furlongs and six yards (2,420 metres), usually on the first Saturday of June. It is Britain's richest flat race and the most prestigious of its five Classics.

History

The race was first run in 1780 and named, according to tradition, after Edward Smith-Stanley, 12th Earl of Derby, won a coin toss with Sir Charles Bunbury over the naming rights. The Derby became the model for similar races worldwide, including the Kentucky Derby. The course's undulating terrain, camber, and the downhill run into Tattenham Corner make it a demanding test of balance and stamina.

Notable winners

Famous winners include Sea Bird (1965), Nijinsky (1970), Shergar (1981, by a record ten lengths), Galileo (2001), Sea The Stars (2009), and Frankel's contemporary Camelot (2012). Lester Piggott rode a record nine Derby winners.
