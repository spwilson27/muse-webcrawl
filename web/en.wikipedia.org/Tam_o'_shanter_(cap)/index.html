Tam o' shanter (cap)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A tam o' shanter (in the British military often abbreviated to ToS) is a name given to the traditional Scottish bonnet worn by men. The tam o' shanter is named after Tam o' Shanter, the hero of the 1790 poem of that name by Robert Burns.

Description

The bonnet is made of wool, knitted or woven, and is characteristically worn flat on the head with a soft, full crown that flops over to one side. It is usually topped with a woollen pom-pom (a "toorie") at the centre. Traditionally the tam is worn with the crown pulled to the right side. Early bonnets were knitted in one piece, stretched on a wooden disc to give them their flat shape, and then felted.

History and military use

The style derives from the bonnets long worn in Scotland and became popularly associated with Burns's poem in the nineteenth century. During the First World War the British Army adopted a khaki version known as the "bonnet, trench" or Tam o' Shanter for Scottish regiments serving in the trenches, replacing the Glengarry as practical field dress. The ToS remains part of the uniform of the Royal Regiment of Scotland and of Canadian Scottish regiments, often bearing a regimental cap badge and tartan patch.
