National People's Congress

The National People's Congress (NPC) is the national legislature of the People's Republic of China. Under the Chinese constitution it is the highest organ of state power. It has nearly 3,000 deputies and meets in full session for roughly two weeks each spring in the Great Hall of the People in Beijing, alongside the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (the "Two Sessions").

Powers

The NPC formally elects the President and Vice President of China, the Premier (on the President's nomination), and other top state officials; it amends the constitution, enacts basic laws, approves the state budget and national economic plans, and decides questions of war and peace. Between sessions, its roughly 175-member Standing Committee exercises legislative power and interprets the constitution and laws, in practice functioning as the working legislature.

Composition and elections

Deputies are elected for five-year terms by provincial-level people's congresses and by the People's Liberation Army, under the dominant leadership of the Chinese Communist Party. The first NPC was convened in 1954, which adopted China's first constitution.
