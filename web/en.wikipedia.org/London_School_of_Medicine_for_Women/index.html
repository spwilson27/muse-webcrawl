The London School of Medicine for Women (LSMW) was the first medical school in Britain to train women as doctors. It was founded in 1874 by Sophia Jex-Blake, Elizabeth Garrett Anderson, Emily Blackwell and others, after women were refused entry to existing medical schools and to qualifying examinations.

History

The school opened at 30 Henrietta Street, Brunswick Square, with 14 students, and secured clinical teaching through an association with the Royal Free Hospital from 1877 — the first teaching hospital in London to admit women students. In 1876 an enabling act of Parliament allowed examining bodies to grant qualifications to women, and the University of London opened its degrees to women in 1878.

Later years

In 1898 the school became the London (Royal Free Hospital) School of Medicine for Women. It admitted men in 1947 and was renamed the Royal Free Hospital School of Medicine. In 1998 it merged with University College London's medical school to form the Royal Free and University College Medical School, now UCL Medical School.
