Eadie–Hofstee diagram

In biochemistry, an Eadie–Hofstee diagram (also known as an Eadie–Hofstee plot or Eadie–Augustinsson plot) is a graphical representation of enzyme kinetics in which reaction rate is plotted as a function of the ratio between rate and substrate concentration: v is plotted against v/[S].

Derivation

The plot is derived by rearranging the Michaelis–Menten equation. In the resulting straight line, the y-intercept equals Vmax, the slope equals −Km, and the x-intercept equals Vmax/Km. The diagram is named after George Eadie and Barend Hofstee (with a related form by Karl-Bertil Augustinsson).

Advantages and limitations

Because both axes depend on the measured velocity, deviations from Michaelis–Menten behaviour — such as curvature indicating multiple enzymes or allosteric effects — can be more apparent than in the Lineweaver–Burk plot. However, the shared variable on both axes means experimental errors are correlated, and modern practice generally favours nonlinear regression directly on the Michaelis–Menten equation.
