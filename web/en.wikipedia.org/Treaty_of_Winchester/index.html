Treaty of Winchester

SignedNovember 1153
LocationWinchester, England
PartiesStephen of England; Henry Curtmantle (Henry II)

The Treaty of Winchester, also known as the Treaty of Wallingford, was an agreement made in November 1153 that effectively ended the Anarchy, the civil war in England between King Stephen and the supporters of Empress Matilda. It was concluded at Winchester after negotiations conducted at Wallingford the previous summer.

Background

Stephen had seized the throne in 1135 on the death of Henry I, despite having sworn to support Henry's daughter, the Empress Matilda. The resulting war devastated much of England. By 1153 both sides were exhausted; Matilda's son Henry, Duke of Normandy, had invaded England, and the death of Stephen's son Eustace in August 1153 removed a major obstacle to a settlement, since Henry was now the obvious heir.

Terms

  • Stephen was to remain king for the rest of his life.
  • Henry was formally adopted as Stephen's son and recognised as his heir, succeeding to the throne on Stephen's death.
  • Stephen's surviving son, William, surrendered his claim to the throne but retained his father's lands in England and Normandy and did homage to Henry for them.
  • Hostages were exchanged and key castles were to be surrendered or held under agreed arrangements, and foreign mercenaries were to be dismissed.

Aftermath

Stephen died in October 1154, less than a year after the treaty, and Henry succeeded peacefully as Henry II, founding the Plantagenet dynasty and beginning a programme of restoring royal authority damaged by the civil war.
