Sump pump

A sump pump is a pump used to remove water that has accumulated in a water-collecting sump basin, commonly found in the basements of homes. The water may enter via the perimeter drains of a basement waterproofing system, funnelling into the basin, or because of rain or natural ground water, if the basement is below the water table level.

Operation

Sump pumps send water away from a house to a place where it no longer poses a problem, such as a municipal storm drain or a dry well. Most pumps are electric centrifugal pumps switched on automatically by a float or pressure sensor when the water in the pit reaches a set level. The two common designs are submersible pumps, sealed and immersed in the pit, and pedestal pumps, whose motor sits above the pit on a column. Discharge passes through a check valve to prevent backflow.

Backup systems

Because pump failure or a power outage during a storm can flood a basement, many installations add a battery-powered backup pump or a water-powered backup that uses municipal water pressure. Maintenance involves periodic testing, clearing the pit of debris, and verifying the float switch moves freely.
