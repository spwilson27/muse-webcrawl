Berbers

Berbers, or the Amazigh people (Berber languages: Imazighen), are a diverse grouping of distinct ethnic groups indigenous to North Africa who predate the arrival of Arabs in the Maghreb. Their main connections are identified by their usage of Berber languages, most of them mutually unintelligible, which are part of the Afroasiatic language family.

Distribution

Berbers are concentrated principally in Morocco, Algeria, Libya, and Tunisia, with smaller communities in Mauritania, Mali, Niger, Egypt (notably the Siwa Oasis), and Burkina Faso, as well as a large diaspora in Europe, particularly in France, Spain, Belgium, and the Netherlands. Estimates of the total Berber population range widely, from 25 million to 50 million people.

Language and identity

The Berber languages form a branch of the Afroasiatic family and have been spoken in North Africa since antiquity. They are written in several scripts, including the indigenous Tifinagh alphabet, the Latin alphabet, and the Arabic script. In 2001 Berber became a national language in Algeria, and in 2011 Tamazight was made an official language in Morocco; Algeria followed in 2016. The Amazigh flag — blue, green, and yellow bands with the red Yaz symbol (ⵣ) representing the "free man" — is a widely recognized cultural emblem.

History

Berber kingdoms such as Numidia and Mauretania flourished in antiquity, producing figures such as King Masinissa and the Roman-era writer Apuleius. Saint Augustine of Hippo was of Berber descent. After the Arab conquests of the 7th century, Berber dynasties — including the Almoravids, Almohads, Marinids, and Zayyanids — ruled large parts of the Maghreb and al-Andalus.

Culture

Traditional Berber culture is closely tied to the land of the Maghreb, encompassing village assemblies (jemaa), distinctive jewellery and weaving, henna art, and festivals such as Yennayer, the Amazigh New Year. Religious practice today is predominantly Sunni Islam, though pre-Islamic traditions, and historical Christian and Jewish Berber communities, form part of the historical record.
