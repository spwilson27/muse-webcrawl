Education economics

Education economics or the economics of education is the study of economic issues relating to education, including the demand for education, the financing and provision of education, and the comparative efficiency of various educational programmes and policies. From early works on the relationship between schooling and labour market outcomes, the field has grown to cover the economics of education at all levels and in informal learning.

Average earnings rise steeply with educational attainment

Human capital

The central framework is human capital theory, developed by Theodore Schultz and Gary Becker, which treats education as an investment: individuals and society incur costs (tuition, foregone earnings) in exchange for higher future productivity and wages. Estimated private returns to an additional year of schooling average around 9–10 per cent worldwide, and education is a significant component of national wealth and a driver of long-run economic growth.

Signalling and screening

An alternative view, associated with Michael Spence, holds that credentials may partly signal pre-existing ability rather than create skills. Distinguishing human capital from signalling effects — for example through "sheepskin effects" of diploma years — remains an active research question with implications for education subsidies.

Finance and production

Because education has positive externalities and capital markets cannot easily lend against future earnings, governments fund and provide most schooling. Economists study education production functions — how class size, teacher quality, and spending affect achievement — school choice and competition, teacher labour markets, and the design of student loans and vouchers. Natural experiments and randomised trials, such as the Tennessee STAR class-size experiment, have been influential in this literature.

Inequality

Education is both a response to and a driver of inequality: access and quality differ by family income, while skill-biased technological change raises the premium on higher education, shaping wage distributions across advanced economies.
