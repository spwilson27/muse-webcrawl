Vickers machine gun

The Vickers machine gun, formally the Vickers .303-inch machine gun, was a water-cooled medium machine gun produced by Vickers Limited, originally for the British Army. A development of Hiram Maxim's gun with an inverted toggle-lock action, it entered service in 1912 and remained in British service for more than fifty years, famed for its extraordinary reliability.

Design

The Vickers was lighter and more compact than the Maxim it derived from, with the barrel surrounded by a water jacket holding about four litres for cooling; steam was led to a condenser can so the water could be reused and the gun's position not betrayed by vapour. It was fired from a tripod with sophisticated elevating and traversing gear for sustained indirect fire, including "barrage" fire onto map references.

Service

The gun was the standard battalion support weapon of the British and Commonwealth armies in both world wars. In a celebrated action at High Wood in August 1916, ten Vickers guns of the 100th Machine Gun Company fired nearly one million rounds in twelve hours of continuous barrage fire without a single stoppage. The Vickers was also widely used in aircraft (synchronised and flexible mounts), tanks, and larger-calibre variants such as the .50-inch Vickers. It was declared obsolete in British service in 1968, among the longest-serving weapons in British history.
