Euro banknotes

Euro banknotes
Issued1 January 2002 (first series)
Denominations€5, €10, €20, €50, €100, €200 (and €500, discontinued)
IssuerEuropean Central Bank and national central banks

Euro banknotes are the physical currency of the euro area. Unlike euro coins, which have national designs, banknotes are identical throughout the eurozone. Seven denominations were introduced on 1 January 2002; the €500 note was dropped from the second series after issuance ended in 2019, though it remains legal tender.

Design

The first series, designed by Austrian artist Robert Kalina, depicts generic windows, gateways and bridges in European architectural styles of successive eras — Classical, Romanesque, Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque and Rococo, iron-and-glass, and modern — deliberately avoiding identifiable real structures or people. The Europa series, rolled out from 2013 beginning with the €5 note, added a portrait of the mythological Europa in the watermark and hologram, improved security features, and more saturated colours.

Features

Notes increase in size with denomination and carry distinct dominant colours (grey, red, blue, orange, green, yellow-brown, purple) to aid recognition. Security elements include watermarks, holograms, security threads, colour-changing ink and raised print.
