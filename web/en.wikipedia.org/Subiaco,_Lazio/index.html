Subiaco, Lazio

Subiaco is a town and comune in the Metropolitan City of Rome, in the Lazio region of central Italy. Situated in the valley of the Aniene river, it is famous as the place where Saint Benedict of Nursia founded his first monasteries in the 6th century, making it a cradle of Western monasticism.

History

The town takes its name from the artificial lakes (Sublaqueum, "below the lakes") created by the emperor Nero for his villa. Around 497 AD the young Benedict withdrew to a cave above the valley—the Sacro Speco—and after three years of hermit life founded twelve small monasteries in the area.

Monasteries

The Monastery of Saint Scholastica and the Monastery of Saint Benedict (Sacro Speco) remain active Benedictine houses, decorated with medieval frescoes. In 1465 two German printers established Italy's first printing press at Saint Scholastica.

The town

Subiaco's historic centre climbs a hillside crowned by the Rocca Abbaziale fortress. It is a member of the association of Italy's most beautiful villages and a gateway to the Monti Simbruini regional park.
