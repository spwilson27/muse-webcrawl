Fermat's little theorem

Fermat's little theorem states that if p is a prime number, then for any integer a, the number ap − a is an integer multiple of p. In modern notation:

ap ≡ a (mod p).

If a is not divisible by p, the theorem is equivalent to:

ap−1 ≡ 1 (mod p).

History

Pierre de Fermat first stated the theorem in a letter of 18 October 1640 to his friend Bernard Frénicle de Bessy, without proof. The first published proof was given by Leonhard Euler in 1736, using induction; Leibniz had produced an equivalent proof in an unpublished manuscript before 1683.

Applications

Fermat's little theorem is fundamental in number theory and is used in primality testing (the Fermat primality test), in the RSA cryptosystem, and in proofs throughout elementary number theory. It is a special case of Euler's theorem. Numbers that satisfy the congruence for composite bases, like 341 = 11 × 31, are called Fermat pseudoprimes; Carmichael numbers satisfy it for all coprime bases.
