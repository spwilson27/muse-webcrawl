Part of speech

Part of speech Also called: Word class; lexical category
Traditional English set: Noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection
Classification criteria: Morphology, syntax, semantics
Open classes: Nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs

In grammar, a part of speech or part-of-speech (abbreviated as POS or PoS, also known as a word class or lexical category) is a category of words (or, more generally, of lexical items) that have similar grammatical properties. Words that are assigned to the same part of speech generally display similar syntactic behaviour — they play similar roles within the grammatical structure of sentences — and sometimes similar morphology, in that they undergo inflection for similar properties.

The traditional English classes

Commonly listed English parts of speech are the noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition, conjunction, interjection, and sometimes numeral, article (determiner), and particle. This scheme descends from the eight parts of speech of Greek grammar described by Dionysius Thrax (noun, verb, participle, article, pronoun, preposition, adverb, conjunction), adapted by Latin grammarians — who dropped the article and added the interjection — and imposed on English from the Renaissance onward, despite imperfect fit.

Open and closed classes

Word classes divide into open classes, which readily accept new members (nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs in English — new words such as "selfie" or "to google" enter constantly), and closed classes, whose membership is essentially fixed (pronouns, determiners, prepositions, conjunctions). Languages differ in their inventories: Japanese has three adjective-like classes and a class of adjectival nouns; many languages lack a separate adjective class; Mandarin distinguishes measure words (classifiers). Modern linguistics therefore defines classes language-internally, using distributional tests rather than meaning-based definitions such as "a noun is a person, place, or thing," which fail for abstract nouns like "arrival" or "justice."

Applications

Part-of-speech tagging — assigning a class label to each word in running text — is a foundational task in computational linguistics and natural language processing, used in parsing, information retrieval, and speech synthesis. Standard tagsets, such as the 36-tag Penn Treebank set, refine the broad classes with distinctions of tense, number, and degree.
