Yangtze

Yangtze (Chang Jiang)
Length: 6,300 km (3,915 mi)
Source: Tanggula Mountains, Tibetan Plateau
Mouth: East China Sea, near Shanghai
Countries: China

The Yangtze or Yangzi (Chinese: 長江, Chang Jiang, "Long River") is the longest river in Eurasia, the third-longest in the world after the Nile and the Amazon, and the longest river entirely within one country. It flows 6,300 kilometres eastward from the glaciers of the Tibetan Plateau to the East China Sea.

Geography

The river rises in Qinghai province and passes through 11 provincial-level divisions, including Sichuan, Chongqing, Hubei, Hunan, Jiangxi, Anhui, and Jiangsu. Its drainage basin covers one-fifth of China's land area and is home to nearly one-third of the country's population. Major tributaries include the Min, Jialing, Han, Gan, and Huangpu rivers. Below Yichang the river cuts through the famous Three Gorges — Qutang, Wu, and Xiling.

History and economy

The Yangtze valley was a cradle of Chinese civilisation; Neolithic cultures such as Hemudu flourished along its lower reaches, and the river long divided northern and southern cultural spheres. Today the delta cities — Shanghai, Nanjing, Wuhan, Chongqing — form one of the world's great economic corridors. The river is a vital shipping artery: it is the busiest inland waterway on Earth.

Three Gorges Dam and environment

The Three Gorges Dam, completed in the 2000s, is the world's largest hydroelectric power station by installed capacity. Seasonal flooding has caused devastating disasters, notably in 1931 and 1954. The river's ecosystems suffered heavy pressure: the baiji (Yangtze river dolphin) was declared functionally extinct in 2006, and the Chinese paddlefish was declared extinct in 2022; a ten-year fishing ban was imposed in 2021 to aid recovery.
