Caber toss

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (recreated from memory)

The caber toss is a traditional Scottish athletic event in which competitors toss a large tapered wooden pole called a caber (from the Gaelic cabar, meaning beam). It is one of the signature events of the Highland games. The object is not distance but accuracy: the caber must flip end over end and land pointing as close as possible to the 12 o'clock position relative to the thrower.

The event

A typical caber is around 5–6 metres (16–20 ft) long and weighs 35–60 kg (80–130 lb), though specifications vary. The athlete cradles the tapered (narrow) end in cupped hands, lifts and balances the pole while running forward, then stops and heaves the bottom end upward so the caber rotates forward; the top end strikes the ground and, if the toss is successful, the pole topples over so the narrow end lands away from the thrower.

Scoring

Successful tosses are scored by the clock position where the caber comes to rest — 12:00, straight ahead, is perfect — while failed attempts are judged by the angle of rotation achieved (for example, "quarter to" positions). If no competitor turns the caber, a shorter or lighter one may be used; if all succeed, a heavier caber may be brought in. Other traditional events at the games include the stone put, Scottish hammer throw, weight throw, and sheaf toss.

Origins

The event's origins are uncertain; it has been attributed variously to lumberjacks tossing logs, soldiers bridging Highland streams and castle moats with felled trees, and centuries-old strength contests recorded at fairs since at least the 16th century.
