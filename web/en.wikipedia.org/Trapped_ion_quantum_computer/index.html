Trapped ion quantum computer

A trapped ion quantum computer is a quantum computer that uses ions — electrically charged atoms — suspended in free space by electromagnetic fields as its qubits. Quantum information is stored in stable electronic states of each ion, and quantum logic gates are performed with precisely aimed lasers that couple the ions' internal states to their shared motional modes. First proposed in 1995 by Ignacio Cirac and Peter Zoller, trapped ions have achieved some of the highest gate fidelities and coherence times of any quantum computing platform.

Architecture

Ions such as 40Ca+, 171Yb+, and 9Be+ are confined in Paul or Penning traps inside ultra-high vacuum chambers and laser-cooled to near their motional ground state. The Cirac–Zoller gate and later Mølmer–Sørensen gates entangle ions through collective vibrational modes. For scaling, the quantum charge-coupled device (QCCD) architecture shuttles ions between interaction zones in segmented traps, an approach pursued commercially by companies such as IonQ and Quantinuum (Honeywell).

Advantages and challenges

Ions offer identical natural qubits, long coherence, all-to-all connectivity within a chain, and high-fidelity readout. Challenges include slow gate speeds compared with solid-state platforms, the complexity of laser control systems, and scaling to millions of physical qubits needed for fault-tolerant computation.
