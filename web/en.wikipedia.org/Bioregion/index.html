Bioregion

A bioregion is an area defined by natural characteristics rather than political boundaries — by its geology, climate, watersheds, flora and fauna, and often by the human cultures adapted to those conditions. Related scientific terms include ecoregion and biogeographic realm.

Ecological usage

Conservation organizations such as WWF divide land and oceans into realms and ecoregions to plan protection of representative habitats. Australia's IBRA system and US EPA ecoregions similarly classify regions by ecosystem patterns for management and reporting.

Bioregionalism

In the 1970s, writers such as Peter Berg promoted bioregionalism: a social and political philosophy advocating governance, economies and identities organized around natural regions, local self-reliance and ecological stewardship, exemplified by Cascadia in the Pacific Northwest.

Defining boundaries

Bioregions are often delineated by watersheds (drainage basins), mountain ranges and vegetation transitions, but boundaries are gradients rather than sharp lines, and schemes differ by purpose and scale.
