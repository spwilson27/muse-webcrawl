Agreement (linguistics)

In linguistics, agreement or concord (abbreviated AGR) occurs when a word changes form depending on the other words to which it relates. It is an instance of inflection, and usually involves making the value of some grammatical category (such as gender or person) "agree" between varied words or parts of a sentence.

For example, in Standard English, one may say I am or he is, but not I is or he am. This is because English verbs inflect for agreement with the person and number of their subject.

Types of agreement

Subject–verb agreement

Many languages mark agreement between a verb and its subject in person and number, and sometimes gender. Spanish, for example, has a rich system of subject–verb agreement: yo hablo "I speak", ella habla "she speaks", ellos hablan "they speak".

Agreement within the noun phrase

In languages such as French and German, adjectives, articles and demonstratives agree with the noun they modify in gender, number, and (in German) case: French le petit chien "the little (masc.) dog" versus la petite maison "the little (fem.) house".

Anaphoric agreement

Pronouns typically agree with their antecedents in person, number and gender: Mary gave her book to herself.

Agreement in linguistic theory

Generative grammar treats agreement as a central syntactic relation, formalised in models such as government and binding theory and the minimalist program through feature checking or the operation Agree. Typologists note that agreement systems vary widely: some languages, such as Japanese and Mandarin Chinese, have little or no overt agreement morphology.
