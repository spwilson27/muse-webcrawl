Storm Shadow

Storm Shadow is a long-range, air-launched cruise missile developed jointly by the United Kingdom and France and manufactured by MBDA. The French version is designated SCALP-EG. Designed for precision strikes against hardened, high-value fixed targets, it entered service in 2003.

Design

The missile is powered by a turbojet engine, flies at high subsonic speed at low altitude, and has a range in excess of 250 kilometres (export figures vary). It navigates by inertial and GPS guidance matched to stored terrain profiles, and uses an imaging infrared seeker for terminal target recognition. Its BROACH warhead combines a precursor shaped charge that penetrates earth or concrete with a main follow-through bomb, allowing attack of bunkers and hardened aircraft shelters. Low-observable shaping reduces its radar cross-section.

Operational history

Storm Shadow was first used in combat by the Royal Air Force during the 2003 invasion of Iraq, and subsequently in Libya (2011) and against Islamic State targets in Syria and Iraq. In 2023 the United Kingdom supplied Storm Shadow missiles to Ukraine, which used them against Russian targets, including facilities in Crimea; France likewise supplied SCALP missiles. Carriers have included the Tornado, Typhoon, Rafale and Mirage 2000.
