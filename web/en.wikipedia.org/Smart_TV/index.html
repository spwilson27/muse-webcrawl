Smart TV

A smart TV, also known as a connected TV, is a television set with integrated Internet connectivity and interactive features that allow users to stream video and music, browse the web, view photos and install applications. Smart TVs run dedicated operating systems and provide access to over-the-top streaming services such as Netflix, YouTube and Amazon Prime Video, as well as features like voice control, screen mirroring from phones, and integration with smart home platforms.

History

The term "smart TV" was popularized by Samsung around 2010 as manufacturers added Ethernet ports, Wi-Fi and app stores to sets that previously displayed only broadcast signals. Early connected TVs offered fixed widgets for weather and news; competition with external streaming devices such as Roku, Apple TV and Chromecast pushed manufacturers to adopt full platforms. By the late 2010s, the large majority of televisions sold in developed markets were smart TVs.

Platforms

Major platforms include Samsung's Tizen, LG's webOS, Google TV/Android TV, Roku TV and Amazon Fire TV, alongside systems from Vizio and others. Platform operators have become significant businesses in their own right through advertising, subscription revenue shares and licensing — collecting viewing data to sell targeted advertising, a practice known as automatic content recognition (ACR).

Privacy and security

Smart TVs have drawn sustained criticism and regulatory attention over data collection. ACR systems can log what viewers watch, voice remotes transmit audio, and some manufacturers have been penalized for tracking viewing habits without adequate consent — including a 2017 settlement in which Vizio paid $2.2 million to US regulators. Security researchers have also demonstrated vulnerabilities in TV operating systems and bundled applications, and many budget models receive few software updates.
