Trinidad and Tobago

Republic of Trinidad and Tobago
Capital: Port of Spain
Languages: English
Population: about 1.5 million

Trinidad and Tobago, officially the Republic of Trinidad and Tobago, is the southernmost island country in the Caribbean. Consisting of the main islands Trinidad and Tobago and numerous smaller islands, it is situated 11 kilometres off the coast of northeastern Venezuela. The capital is Port of Spain.

The islands were inhabited by Indigenous peoples for centuries before Columbus encountered Trinidad in 1498. Trinidad was a Spanish colony until captured by Britain in 1797; Tobago changed hands repeatedly among European powers. The two were united in a single British colony in 1889, gained independence in 1962, and became a republic in 1976.

Economy and culture

The economy is based mainly on petroleum and natural gas, including one of the world's largest natural asphalt lakes, Pitch Lake. Trinidad is famous for Carnival, calypso and soca music, and the steelpan, the only acoustic musical instrument invented in the twentieth century. See also Trinidad and Tobago cuisine.
