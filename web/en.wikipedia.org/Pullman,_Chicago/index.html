Pullman, one of Chicago's 77 defined community areas, is a neighbourhood on the South Side of the city. It is centred on the first planned model industrial community in the United States, built by industrialist George Pullman from 1880 to house workers of his Pullman Palace Car Company.

The company town

Designed by architect Solon Spencer Beman and landscape architect Nathan Franklin Barrett, Pullman offered brick housing, indoor plumbing, parks, a church, theatre, hotel and shops — amenities far above typical workers' housing of the era, though rents were charged and rules were strict. The town was internationally praised as a model community and won honours at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition, held nearby.

Strike and annexation

When the company cut wages during the depression of 1893 without reducing rents, workers launched the bitter Pullman Strike of 1894, which spread nationwide under Eugene V. Debs and was broken by federal troops. The Illinois Supreme Court later ordered the company to divest its non-industrial property, and the neighbourhood had already been annexed to Chicago in 1889.

Historic district and national monument

Pullman was designated a National Historic Landmark District in 1970. In 2015 President Barack Obama proclaimed it the Pullman National Monument, the first National Park Service unit in Chicago, preserving the factory complex, the Hotel Florence and the planned streetscape of rowhouses.
