Super Mario Bros.

Super Mario Bros. is a platform video game developed and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES). Released in Japan and North America in 1985, it is one of the best-selling and most influential video games of all time, credited with helping revive the North American video game industry after the crash of 1983.

Gameplay

Players control Mario (and, in two-player mode, Luigi) through the Mushroom Kingdom to rescue Princess Toadstool (Peach) from Bowser, king of the Koopas. The game scrolls sideways through 32 levels across eight worlds, filled with enemies such as Goombas and Koopa Troopas, question-mark blocks, warp pipes, and power-ups — the Super Mushroom, Fire Flower, and Starman. Its precise jumping mechanics, designed by Shigeru Miyamoto and Takashi Tezuka, set the template for the platform genre. The overworld theme composed by Koji Kondo is among the most recognizable melodies in gaming.

Legacy

The game spawned the long-running Super Mario series, Nintendo's flagship franchise. It sold over 40 million copies on the NES, has been re-released on many platforms, and is a mainstay of speedrunning, including the famous "minus world" glitch. In 2005 IGN named it the greatest video game of all time.
