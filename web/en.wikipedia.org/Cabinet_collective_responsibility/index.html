Cabinet collective responsibility, also known as collective ministerial responsibility, is a constitutional convention in parliamentary systems that members of the cabinet must publicly support all governmental decisions made in cabinet, even if they do not privately agree with them. This support includes voting with the government in the legislature.

Overview

If a member of the cabinet wishes to openly object to a cabinet decision then they are obliged to resign from their position in the cabinet. The convention is central to the operation of the Westminster system and similar parliamentary democracies, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and New Zealand.

Rationale

The doctrine serves several purposes. It presents a united front to parliament and the public, so that the government can be held collectively accountable for its decisions. It also protects the confidentiality of cabinet deliberations, allowing ministers to argue candidly in private while defending the agreed position in public. Because the cabinet acts as a single body, the legislature can treat a vote against a major government measure as a vote of no confidence in the government as a whole.

Exceptions

Governments occasionally suspend collective responsibility on particular issues. Famous examples include the "agreement to differ" in the British National Government of 1932 over tariff policy, and the free votes allowed during the 1975 and 2016 referendums on membership of the European Communities and the European Union. Coalition governments also sometimes adopt formal arrangements permitting the partners to differ on specified questions.

Consequences of breach

Ministers who breach collective responsibility are normally expected to resign or face dismissal. Notable resignations over the principle include ministers who could not support government policy on Europe, defence or economic strategy. The convention extends in many systems to parliamentary private secretaries and, in practice, shapes the discipline of the entire ministerial payroll vote.
