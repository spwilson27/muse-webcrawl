Trauma center

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A trauma center (spelled trauma centre in British English) is a hospital equipped and staffed to provide comprehensive emergency medical services to patients suffering traumatic injuries. Trauma centers grew out of the recognition that seriously injured patients benefit from rapid, specialised treatment — the concept of the "golden hour" — and from organised trauma systems linking prehospital care, surgery, and rehabilitation.

Levels

In the United States, trauma centers are verified by the American College of Surgeons and designated by state authorities at levels I through V. A Level I center provides the highest level of care, with in-house trauma surgeons, anaesthesia, and a full range of specialists available around the clock, plus research and education programmes. Level II centers provide similar clinical care without the same research mandate; Levels III–V provide progressively more limited stabilisation and transfer capability. England operates a network of major trauma centres within regional trauma networks.

Care

Care follows structured protocols such as Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS), prioritising airway, breathing, circulation, disability and exposure (ABCDE). Damage-control surgery, massive transfusion protocols, and interventional radiology are central to modern practice. Outcomes for major trauma improve measurably when patients are taken directly to appropriately designated centers.
