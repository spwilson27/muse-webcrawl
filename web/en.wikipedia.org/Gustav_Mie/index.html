Gustav Adolf Feodor Wilhelm Ludwig Mie (29 September 1868 – 13 February 1957) was a German physicist best known for his 1908 solution of Maxwell's equations for the scattering of electromagnetic radiation by spherical particles, now called Mie scattering.

Career

Mie studied mathematics and physics at the University of Rostock and Heidelberg, completing his doctorate in 1891. He held professorships at Greifswald, where he published his scattering theory, and later at Halle and Freiburg, where he spent the remainder of his career.

Mie scattering

His 1908 paper, "Contributions to the optics of turbid media, particularly of colloidal metal solutions," gave an exact solution for the scattering and absorption of light by homogeneous spheres of arbitrary size and refractive index. The theory explains phenomena such as the colours of colloidal gold and is fundamental to modern optics, atmospheric science, and particle sizing; it is still widely computed today.

Other work

Mie also developed an intermolecular potential (the Mie potential, related to the Lennard-Jones potential), the Mie–Grüneisen equation of state for solids, and an early attempt at a unified nonlinear theory of matter, which influenced later field theorists. The crater Mie on Mars is named in his honour.
