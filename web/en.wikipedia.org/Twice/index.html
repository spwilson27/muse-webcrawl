Twice (Korean: 트와이스; Japanese: トゥワイス; commonly stylized in all caps) is a South Korean girl group formed by JYP Entertainment. The group is composed of nine members: Nayeon, Jeongyeon, Momo, Sana, Jihyo, Mina, Dahyun, Chaeyoung, and Tzuyu. Twice was formed under the television program Sixteen (2015) and debuted on October 20, 2015, with the extended play (EP) The Story Begins.

Twice rose to domestic fame in 2016 with their single "Cheer Up", which charted at number one on the Gaon Digital Chart, became the best-performing single of the year, and won "Song of the Year" at two major music awards shows. Their first studio album, Twicetagram (2017), and its lead single "Likey" both topped the Billboard World Albums and World Digital Songs charts.

History

The group expanded their popularity in Japan with the release of their maxi single "#Twice" in 2017 and became the first Korean girl group to earn a platinum certification from the Recording Industry Association of Japan. Twice subsequently signed with Warner Music Japan. In 2019, they embarked on their first world tour and ventured into the United States market, signing with Republic Records in 2020.

Artistry and impact

Known for their bubblegum pop sound and choreography built around memorable point moves, Twice have frequently been referred to by various Korean news media as the "Nation's Girl Group". Their fanbase is called "Once". The group has sold over 20 million albums cumulatively in South Korea and Japan.
