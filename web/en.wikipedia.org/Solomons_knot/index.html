Solomon's knot

Solomon's knot in an ancient mosaic pavement

Solomon's knot (Latin: sigillum Salomonis, "Solomon's seal") is a decorative motif found throughout the ancient world, consisting of two interlaced ovals or loops, each passing alternately over and under the other. Despite its name it is not a true knot but a link of two closed loops, and it is distinct from the related Solomon's seal (the hexagram).

Occurrence

The motif appears in Roman mosaics across the empire, in early Christian churches, in synagogue pavements, and in Islamic and medieval art. In Italy it is especially common in Romanesque church floors and carvings — as at the baptistery of San Giovanni in Florence and churches in Pavia — and it was adopted into the decorative repertoire of Lombard and Carolingian art. Because of its association with King Solomon, famed in scripture for wisdom, the knot was interpreted as a symbol of wisdom, eternity, and the union of the divine and human.

In knot theory

In mathematical knot theory, Solomon's knot corresponds to the link known as Solomon's link, with linking number two: two components interlaced twice. It should not be confused with the Solomon's seal knot, a single-component pattern used in heraldry and vexillology.
