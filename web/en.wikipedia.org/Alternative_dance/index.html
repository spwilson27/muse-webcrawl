Alternative dance

Alternative dance flourished in the British "Madchester" scene of the late 1980s.

Alternative dance (also called indie dance or underground dance in the United States) is a musical genre that mixes alternative rock with electronic dance music. It typically combines the song structures, guitars and attitude of indie and alternative rock with the beats, samples and production of house, techno and other club styles.

History

The genre emerged in the 1980s from post-punk and new wave acts such as New Order, whose "Blue Monday" (1983) fused post-punk with electronic dance music. In the late 1980s the Madchester scene in Manchester, England — centred on the Haçienda nightclub and bands such as the Stone Roses and Happy Mondays — married psychedelic indie rock to acid house rhythms. Primal Scream's Screamadelica (1991) became a landmark of the style.

Development

In the 1990s acts including The Prodigy, The Chemical Brothers and Underworld brought big-beat and rave influences into rock venues, while in the 2000s bands such as LCD Soundsystem, Franz Ferdinand and The Rapture led a dance-punk and electroclash revival. The related baggy, shoegaze-dance and nu-rave scenes all fall under the broad alternative dance umbrella.

Characteristics

Alternative dance tracks usually keep verse–chorus structures and live instruments but use drum machines, sequencers and remixes aimed at clubs; extended 12-inch mixes were a hallmark of the genre's singles culture.
