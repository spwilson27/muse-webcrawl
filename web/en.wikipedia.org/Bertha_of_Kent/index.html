Bertha (c. 565 – c. 601), sometimes called Saint Bertha, was a Frankish princess who became queen consort of Kent by marriage to King Æthelberht. Her Christian faith played an important role in the introduction of Christianity to Anglo-Saxon England.

Life

Bertha was the daughter of Charibert I, king of Paris, and his queen Ingoberga. She married Æthelberht of Kent on the condition that she be allowed to practise her Christian religion freely, bringing her own bishop, Liudhard, with her to England.

Role in the Gregorian mission

Bertha worshipped at the church of St Martin in Canterbury, which dated from Roman times. Her faith and influence prepared the way for Augustine's mission of 597, sent by Pope Gregory the Great, which Æthelberht received and eventually embraced, becoming the first Anglo-Saxon king to convert to Christianity.

Veneration

Bertha was later canonised and is venerated as a saint, with her feast day on 1 May.
