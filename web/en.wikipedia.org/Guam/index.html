Guam

Tumon Bay is the centre of Guam's tourism industry.

Guam (Chamorro: Guåhan) is an island territory of the United States in the North Pacific Ocean, the largest and southernmost of the Mariana Islands. Its capital is Hagåtña and its most populous village is Dededo; the population is about 170,000. The indigenous Chamorro people settled Guam some 3,500–4,000 years ago, making it one of the earliest island settlements of Remote Oceania.

History

Ferdinand Magellan's expedition reached Guam in 1521, and Spain formally claimed the island in 1565; it became a provisioning stop for the Manila galleons crossing the Pacific. Spain colonised Guam for more than three centuries, drastically reducing the Chamorro population through disease and war. The United States captured the island in 1898 during the Spanish–American War, and it was ceded by treaty. Japan seized Guam in December 1941, days after Pearl Harbor, and occupied it brutally until American forces retook it in the Battle of Guam in July–August 1944.

The Organic Act of 1950 made Guam an organised unincorporated territory, granting U.S. citizenship to its people and establishing civilian self-government. Guamanians elect a governor and legislature and send a non-voting delegate to the U.S. House of Representatives, but cannot vote in presidential elections; the island's political status — statehood, free association, independence or the status quo — remains a long-standing question in local politics.

Geography and economy

The island is 48 km long and up to 19 km wide, of volcanic origin in the south and raised coral limestone in the north. A major U.S. military presence — Andersen Air Force Base, Naval Base Guam and a Marine Corps base — occupies roughly a third of the land area and underpins the economy alongside tourism, which draws well over a million visitors a year, mainly from Japan and South Korea. Guam lies near the Mariana Trench, the deepest oceanic trench on Earth, and observes Chamorro Standard Time (UTC+10), one day ahead of the continental United States — hence its motto "Where America's Day Begins".
