Sainte-Mère-Église

Sainte-Mère-Église is a commune in the Manche department in Normandy in northwestern France. Founded in the eleventh century, the town is best known for its role in the Normandy landings of the Second World War: in the early hours of 6 June 1944, American paratroopers of the 82nd Airborne Division dropped into and around the town, making it one of the first towns liberated on D-Day.

History

The town grew up around its church — dedicated to the Virgin Mary, hence "Holy Mother Church" — and the old Roman road through the Cotentin Peninsula. For most of its history it was a quiet market town serving the surrounding farmland of the Manche.

D-Day, 6 June 1944

Shortly after 1 a.m. on 6 June 1944, paratroopers of the 505th Parachute Infantry Regiment, 82nd Airborne Division, dropped over Sainte-Mère-Église as part of Mission Boston, intended to secure the town and the road network behind Utah Beach. A fire in a house on the town square lit the scene and drew German fire; some paratroopers landed in trees and on rooftops, and several were killed before they could free themselves. By dawn the town was in American hands, though it was counter-attacked and shelled in the following days. Its early capture helped secure the exits from Utah Beach for the seaborne landings.

John Steele

The town's most famous episode concerns Private John M. Steele, whose parachute caught on the church spire as he descended. Hanging limp to avoid being shot — though wounded in the foot — he was cut down and taken prisoner by German soldiers, later escaping to rejoin his unit. The episode was dramatized in the film The Longest Day (1962), in which Steele was played by Red Buttons. An effigy of a paratrooper, with parachute, still hangs from the church tower in his memory.

Commemoration

The Airborne Museum, opened in 1964 beside the church square, preserves the history of the American airborne landings in Normandy. The town remains a centre of D-Day commemoration, with anniversaries drawing veterans, re-enactors, and commemorative parachute drops.
