Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park

Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park
Location: Province of Huesca, Aragon, Spain
Area: 156 km²
Established: 1918
Highest point: Monte Perdido (3,355 m)
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (part of Pyrénées – Mont Perdu)

Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park (Spanish: Parque nacional de Ordesa y Monte Perdido) is a national park in the Spanish Pyrenees, in the province of Huesca, Aragon. Created in 1918 around the Ordesa Valley, it was Spain's second national park and was greatly expanded in 1982. Together with the French Parc national des Pyrénées it forms part of the Pyrénées – Mont Perdu UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1997.

Geography

The park protects a dramatic limestone massif crowned by Monte Perdido (3,355 m), the highest limestone peak in Europe. Deep glacial canyons — Ordesa, Añisclo, Escuaín and Pineta — cut through the massif, with cliffs, waterfalls such as the Cola de Caballo, and hanging valleys. The Añisclo Canyon is one of the great canyon systems of the Pyrenees.

Flora and fauna

Vegetation ranges from beech and fir forests on the valley floors to alpine meadows and scree. The park is a refuge for the Pyrenean chamois, marmots, bearded vultures and golden eagles. The Pyrenean ibex subspecies known as the bucardo was formerly found here; the last individual died in 2000, an extinction that made the park internationally known.
