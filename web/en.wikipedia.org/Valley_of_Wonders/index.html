The Valley of Wonders (French: Vallée des Merveilles) is a high alpine valley in the Mercantour massif of the French Maritime Alps, in the commune of Tende, Alpes-Maritimes. Together with the neighbouring Fontanalba valley on the slopes of Mont Bégo, it contains one of the largest concentrations of prehistoric rock engravings in Europe: tens of thousands of petroglyphs pecked into glacially polished rock surfaces during the Copper Age and Early Bronze Age, roughly 3300–1800 BCE. The valley lies within the core zone of Mercantour National Park and is protected as a French historic monument.

The engravings

More than 40,000 engravings have been catalogued in the Mont Bégo region, about 37,000 of them figurative. The repertoire is dominated by horned figures — most interpreted as oxen or teams of oxen yoked to ploughs — together with daggers and halberds with triangular blades, scorpions, anthropomorphic figures including the famous "Sorcerer" (a dancing figure with raised arms) and the "Christ" figure, and geometric signs such as reticulated patterns and "ziggurat" motifs. The engravings were made by pecking with quartz tools on reddish permian pelite and greenish sandstone slabs smoothed by glaciers. Scholarly interpretation, led by the decades-long documentation project of Henry de Lumley and his team, generally connects the imagery to an early farming society's cult of the bull, storm and fertility powers centred on the sacred mountain Mont Bégo.

History of research and protection

The engravings were noted by travellers from the seventeenth century — one early account called them the work of Hannibal's soldiers or of shepherds — and systematically drawn by the British naturalist Clarence Bicknell from the 1890s, who founded a museum at Bordighera and catalogued thousands of signs. Carlo Conti's surveys in the 1920s–30s produced the first archaeological maps. Since 1967, de Lumley's teams have recorded the corpus stone by stone. Because the sites lie in the national park's protected heart, access to the main engraved zones is restricted: visitors may walk the designated path and must be accompanied by approved guides to enter the protected sectors off-trail.

Setting

The valley, reached on foot from the hamlet of Casterino or from Les Mesces, lies between about 2,000 and 2,700 metres, dotted with glacial lakes such as Lac Long Supérieur and Lac des Merveilles, beneath the 2,872 m summit of Mont Bégo. The area was Italian territory until 1947, when the Treaty of Paris transferred Tende and La Brigue to France. Summer thunderstorms on the isolated massif — among the most violent in the Alps — are often invoked in explaining the mountain's ancient sanctity.
