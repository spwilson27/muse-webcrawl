Administrative centre

An administrative centre (British English; administrative center in American English) is a seat of regional or local government, and the settlement in which the central institutions of a public administration are located. It is the place where the administrative offices of a territory — for example a county, province, district, or municipality — are concentrated, even when it is not the largest or most economically important settlement of that territory.

Examples

In the United States, the equivalent term is county seat; in England, the county town traditionally served as the administrative centre of a county. In many countries the administrative centre is designated by law: for instance, in Japan each prefecture has a designated capital city housing the prefectural government, and in France each department has a prefecture town.

Distinction from capital

An administrative centre need not be a national capital. The term is most often applied below the national level, though some national capitals are described as administrative capitals when governmental functions are deliberately separated from a country's commercial or historic capital, as with Putrajaya in Malaysia or Dodoma in Tanzania.
