The European migrant crisis, also known as the refugee crisis, was a period from 2014 characterised by a sharp increase in the number of people arriving in the European Union, travelling across the Mediterranean Sea or overland through Southeast Europe. In 2015 alone, more than a million people arrived by sea, most fleeing the Syrian civil war, as well as conflicts and persecution in Afghanistan, Iraq, Eritrea and elsewhere.

Course of the crisis

Arrivals rose through 2014 and surged in the summer of 2015. Most crossed from Turkey to Greek islands such as Lesbos, then travelled the Western Balkan route through North Macedonia, Serbia and Hungary toward Austria and Germany. German Chancellor Angela Merkel's decision in late summer 2015 not to return Syrian asylum seekers to their first EU country of entry, summarised by her phrase "Wir schaffen das" ("we can do this"), coincided with over a million asylum applications in Germany across 2015.

The crisis strained the Dublin Regulation, which assigns responsibility to the first country of entry, and produced deep divisions among EU states: several Central European governments rejected compulsory relocation quotas, and Hungary built a border fence. In November 2015, asylum seekers were among the perpetrators' milieu scrutinised after the Paris attacks, hardening public debate.

EU–Turkey statement and aftermath

In March 2016 the EU and Turkey agreed a statement under which irregular migrants crossing to Greece would be returned to Turkey, in exchange for financial aid, resettlement of Syrians from Turkey, and political concessions. Crossings on the Eastern Mediterranean route fell dramatically, although departures on the Central Mediterranean route to Italy continued and deaths at sea remained high — more than 5,000 people drowned in 2016 alone.

Impact

The crisis reshaped European politics, fuelling support for anti-immigration parties, contributing to the context of the 2016 Brexit referendum, and driving reforms of the Common European Asylum System. The drowning of three-year-old Alan Kurdi in September 2015, photographed on a Turkish beach, became an emblem of the crisis's human cost.
