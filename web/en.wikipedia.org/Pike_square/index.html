Pike square

A pike square (or pike block) is a military tactical formation in which a body of infantry armed with long pikes (and often halberds and two-handed swords) forms a dense square or rectangle, presenting a hedge of spear points on all sides. It was a defining infantry formation of late medieval and early modern warfare, especially associated with the Swiss mercenaries and the German Landsknechte.

Development

The formation emerged in the 14th–15th centuries as Swiss infantry perfected large, deep columns of pikemen that could defeat heavy cavalry and other infantry, winning famous victories at Morgarten (1315), Sempach (1386), and against Charles the Bold of Burgundy in the 1470s. Spanish tercios combined pike squares with arquebusiers and swordsmen in a flexible combined-arms system that dominated European battlefields in the 16th century.

Tactics

Pikemen in the front ranks leveled their 4–6 metre pikes while rear ranks held theirs at angles; officers and halberdiers fought in the interior or at the corners. Squares could advance in step to "push of pike" against enemy infantry. Their weaknesses were vulnerability to artillery and concentrated firearms, slow movement, and difficulty maneuvering on broken ground, which led to their gradual replacement in the late 17th century by linear tactics with muskets and bayonets.
