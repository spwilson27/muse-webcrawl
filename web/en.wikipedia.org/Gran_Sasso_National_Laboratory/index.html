Gran Sasso National Laboratory

LocationBeneath Gran Sasso d'Italia, Abruzzo, Italy
Established1985
OperatorINFN (Istituto Nazionale di Fisica Nucleare)
Rock overburdenAbout 1,400 metres
FieldAstroparticle physics, neutrino physics, dark matter searches

The Gran Sasso National Laboratory (Italian: Laboratori Nazionali del Gran Sasso, LNGS) is one of the world's largest underground research laboratories, operated by Italy's National Institute for Nuclear Physics (INFN). It is located beneath the Gran Sasso massif in the Apennines of Abruzzo, alongside the tunnel of the A24 motorway between L'Aquila and Teramo.

Facility

Proposed by physicist Antonino Zichichi in 1979 and built during the 1980s, the laboratory consists of three main experimental halls, each about 100 metres long, with a total volume of roughly 180,000 cubic metres. About 1,400 metres of rock overburden shields experiments from cosmic rays, reducing the muon flux by about a factor of a million compared with the surface—essential for detecting rare events such as neutrino interactions and hypothetical dark matter collisions.

Experiments

Major experiments at LNGS have included OPERA, which observed neutrino oscillations in a beam sent 730 km from CERN and briefly made headlines in 2011 with an apparent faster-than-light neutrino measurement later traced to equipment faults; Borexino, which measured solar neutrinos with unprecedented purity and completed operation in 2021; the dark matter searches XENON and DarkSide; the CUORE search for neutrinoless double beta decay; and LUNA, an accelerator studying nuclear reactions of astrophysical importance at stellar energies.

Access and context

The laboratory hosts around a thousand scientists from dozens of countries. Surface facilities are located near Assergi, close to L'Aquila, within the Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park.
