Roger Bruce Myerson (born March 29, 1951) is an American economist and professor at the University of Chicago. He holds the David L. Pearson Distinguished Service Chair in Global Conflict Studies at the Harris School of Public Policy, the Griffin Department of Economics, and the College. He has made seminal contributions to mechanism design in economics and to the application of game theory to political science.

Education and career

Myerson studied at Harvard University, receiving a bachelor's degree summa cum laude in applied mathematics in 1973 and a Ph.D. in applied mathematics in 1976, with a dissertation on cooperative game theory supervised by Kenneth Arrow. From 1976 to 2001 he was a professor of economics at Northwestern University's Kellogg School of Management. In 2001 he moved to the University of Chicago.

Contributions

Myerson's most celebrated work is his formalization of the revelation principle and the theory of optimal auctions. His 1981 paper "Optimal Auction Design" showed how a seller should structure an auction to maximize expected revenue when bidders have private information, and introduced techniques that became foundational to mechanism design. He also developed the concept of "proper equilibrium" as a refinement of Nash equilibrium, and applied game-theoretic models to the study of political institutions and federalism.

Nobel Prize

In 2007, Myerson shared the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Leonid Hurwicz and Eric Maskin "for having laid the foundations of mechanism design theory". Mechanism design — sometimes called reverse game theory — studies how institutions can be designed to achieve desired outcomes when participants hold private information, with applications ranging from spectrum auctions to electoral systems.
