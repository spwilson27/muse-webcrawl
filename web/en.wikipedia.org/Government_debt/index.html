Government debt

Government debt (also called public debt or national debt) is the total financial liabilities of a government sector, accumulated over time as a result of borrowing to finance budget deficits. It is usually measured as a gross stock of obligations such as treasury bonds, bills, and loans, and is commonly expressed both in absolute terms and as a percentage of gross domestic product (GDP).

How government debt accumulates

When a government's expenditure exceeds its revenue in a given year, it runs a budget deficit and typically covers the difference by issuing securities. Investors—including pension funds, banks, foreign governments, and central banks—buy these securities in exchange for interest payments. Debt is usually issued in the government's own currency, though some states borrow in foreign currency, which exposes them to exchange-rate risk.

Measurement

Economists distinguish between gross debt and net debt (gross debt minus financial assets held by the government). Debt held by the public excludes obligations the government owes to itself, such as bonds held by social security trust funds. The sustainability of a debt burden depends on the relationship between the interest rate on the debt, the growth rate of the economy, and the government's primary balance—the budget balance excluding interest payments.

Economic effects

Moderate levels of government debt can provide safe assets for financial markets and allow governments to smooth taxes over time, finance infrastructure, and respond to recessions or emergencies. Very high debt, by contrast, can raise borrowing costs, crowd out private investment, limit fiscal flexibility, and—if investors lose confidence—trigger a sovereign debt crisis in which the state must default, restructure, or inflate away its obligations. Historical episodes include defaults by numerous emerging economies and the euro area crisis of the early 2010s.

Debt by country

Japan has long carried one of the highest public debt ratios among advanced economies, exceeding 200 percent of GDP, largely held by domestic investors and the Bank of Japan. The United States federal debt has grown substantially since the 2008 financial crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic. Many European countries operate under fiscal rules, such as the Maastricht criteria, that nominally limit deficits and debt ratios.
