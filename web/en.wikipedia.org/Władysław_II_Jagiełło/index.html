Władysław II Jagiełło

Władysław II Jagiełło
Born: c. 1352/1362, Vilnius
Died: 1 June 1434, Gródek Jagielloński
Reign: 1386–1434 (Poland); Grand Duke of Lithuania 1377–1401
Spouse: Jadwiga of Poland; Anna of Cilli; Elisabeth of Pilica; Sophia of Halshany
House: Jagiellonian dynasty
Religion: Roman Catholic (baptised 1386)

Władysław II Jagiełło (Lithuanian: Jogaila; c. 1352/1362 – 1 June 1434), originally Jogaila, Grand Duke of Lithuania, became King of Poland in 1386 after his conversion to Christianity, baptism as Władysław, and marriage to Queen Jadwiga. He founded the Jagiellonian dynasty, which ruled Poland and Lithuania for nearly two centuries.

Early life and Lithuania

Jogaila was a son of Algirdas, Grand Duke of Lithuania. After his father's death in 1377 he shared and contested power with his uncle Kęstutis and cousin Vytautas. Lithuania was then the last pagan state in Europe, pressed by the Teutonic Order in the west and expanding Muscovy in the east.

Union with Poland

By the Union of Krewo (1385) Jogaila promised to convert to Catholicism, marry the young Queen Jadwiga, release Polish prisoners, and attach Lithuania to the Polish crown. He was baptised in Kraków on 15 February 1386, married Jadwiga, and crowned king on 4 March 1386.

Reign

Jagiełło oversaw the Christianisation of Lithuania (1387), the founding and refounding of the University of Kraków, and a long rivalry and reconciliation with Vytautas, recognised as Grand Duke of Lithuania under the Union of Vilnius and Radom (1401). The climax of his reign was the Polish–Lithuanian victory over the Teutonic Order at the Battle of Grunwald (15 July 1410), one of the largest battles of medieval Europe, followed by the Peace of Thorn (1411).

Later years and legacy

After Jadwiga's death in 1399, Jagiełło remained king by right of election. His later marriages produced heirs Władysław III and Casimir IV through Sophia of Halshany. He died in 1434 and was buried in Wawel Cathedral, Kraków. He is remembered in both Poland and Lithuania as a state-builder who forged a durable Polish–Lithuanian union.
