Situationist International

Détournement — the rerouting of pre-existing cultural material — was a signature situationist technique

The Situationist International (SI) was an international organization of social revolutionaries made up of avant-garde artists, intellectuals and political theorists. It was formed in 1957 in Cosio d'Arroscia, Italy, through the merger of several smaller groups, including the Lettrist International and the International Movement for an Imaginist Bauhaus, and it dissolved in 1972.

Ideas

Rooted in Marxism and the European avant-gardes of Dada and Surrealism, the SI sought to critique advanced capitalism and its culture of spectacle. Its best-known figure, Guy Debord, published The Society of the Spectacle in 1967, arguing that modern social life is replaced by its representation. The group developed concepts such as détournement, the dérive (drifting through urban environments), psychogeography and unitary urbanism, and advocated the construction of "situations" — moments of life deliberately created to break with passive consumption.

Influence

Situationist slogans and tactics strongly influenced the Paris uprising of May 1968, and the group's graphics, writings and provocations later fed into punk culture and subsequent movements in art, architecture and politics. The journal Internationale Situationniste published twelve issues between 1958 and 1969.
