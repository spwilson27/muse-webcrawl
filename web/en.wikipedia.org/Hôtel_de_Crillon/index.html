The Hôtel de Crillon is a historic luxury hotel in Paris, occupying one of the two palaces on the Place de la Concorde at the foot of the Champs-Élysées. One of the world's most famous grand hotels, it has been designated a "palace" hotel by the French ministry of tourism and is operated by Rosewood Hotels & Resorts.

History

The building was commissioned in 1758 by King Louis XV and designed by the architect Ange-Jacques Gabriel as one of the twin palaces framing the then Place Louis XV. It was later acquired by the Duke of Crillon, whose family retained it (with interruptions) until 1907. Converted into a hotel in 1909, the Crillon became a headquarters and residence for diplomats: in 1919 it hosted the American delegation during the Paris Peace Conference, and President Woodrow Wilson stayed there during the drafting of the Covenant of the League of Nations. During the Second World War the building was requisitioned by the German military command.

Renovation and present day

The hotel closed in 2013 for a major restoration led by architect Aline Asmar d'Amman, with suites designed in part by Karl Lagerfeld, and reopened on 5 July 2017 as the Hôtel de Crillon, A Rosewood Hotel. It contains 124 rooms and suites, the restaurant L'Écrin, the bar Les Ambassadeurs, and two courtyards, while preserving its 18th-century façade and listed interiors, including the Salon Marie-Antoinette, where the queen is said to have taken piano lessons.
