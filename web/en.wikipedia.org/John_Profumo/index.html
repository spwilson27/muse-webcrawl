John Profumo

Born: John Dennis Profumo, 30 January 1915, London, England
Died: 9 March 2006 (aged 91), London, England
Office: Secretary of State for War (1960–1963)
Known for: The Profumo affair (1963)

John Dennis Profumo (30 January 1915 – 9 March 2006) was a British politician whose career ended in 1963 after a sexual relationship with the 19-year-old model Christine Keeler in 1961, and lying about it in a personal statement to the House of Commons. The scandal, known as the Profumo affair, severely damaged the credibility of Harold Macmillan's Conservative government.

Early life and career

Profumo was born in Kensington, London, into a wealthy family of Italian descent; his father was a barrister and insurance magnate. Educated at Harrow and Brasenose College, Oxford, he was elected to Parliament in 1940 while serving in the Royal Armoured Corps, becoming then the youngest member of the House of Commons. He landed in Normandy on D-Day, reached the rank of brigadier, and was awarded the OBE. After losing his first seat in 1945 he returned to Parliament for Stratford-on-Avon in 1950 and rose through ministerial office to become Secretary of State for War in 1960. He was married to the actress Valerie Hobson.

The Profumo affair

In 1961 Profumo had a brief affair with Christine Keeler, a model he met at a party at Cliveden. Keeler was also involved with Yevgeny Ivanov, a Soviet naval attaché, raising Cold War security fears. When the affair became public in 1963, Profumo denied impropriety in a personal statement to the Commons on 22 March; ten weeks later, on 5 June 1963, he admitted he had lied and resigned from the government and from Parliament. The ensuing scandal, involving Stephen Ward (who took his own life during his trial), contributed to Macmillan's resignation that autumn and the Conservatives' defeat in the 1964 general election.

Later life

Profumo withdrew from public life and devoted himself to charity work at Toynbee Hall in London's East End, initially washing dishes and later becoming its chief fundraiser and president. His decades of quiet charitable service were widely seen as rehabilitation; he was appointed CBE in 1975 and later appeared publicly alongside the Queen Mother and at Margaret Thatcher's 80th birthday celebration. He died in London in 2006.
