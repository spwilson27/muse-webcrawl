The Holocene is the current geological epoch, which began approximately 11,650 calendar years before present (c. 9700 BCE), after the Last Glacial Period that concluded with the Holocene glacial retreat. The Holocene and the preceding Pleistocene together form the Quaternary Period. Its name comes from the Ancient Greek words holos ("whole") and kainos ("new"), meaning "entirely recent".

Climate

The Holocene has been a period of relatively warm and stable climate — an interglacial — though it includes events such as the Holocene climatic optimum, the 8.2-kiloyear event caused by meltwater discharge, and the cooling trend of the later Neoglacial. This stability coincided with the development of human civilization.

Human history

During the Holocene, agriculture arose independently in several regions, beginning in the Fertile Crescent, and settled societies, cities and writing followed. The epoch also saw the extinction of much of the remaining Pleistocene megafauna and, in recent centuries, accelerating human impact on the Earth system.

Subdivisions and the Anthropocene

In 2018 the Holocene was formally divided into three ages: the Greenlandian, Northgrippian and the Meghalayan, which began 4,200 years ago with a worldwide drought. The proposed Anthropocene epoch, marking humanity's geological influence, was considered as a successor unit but was not ratified as a formal epoch in 2024.
