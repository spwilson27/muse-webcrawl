Cain

Cain is a biblical figure in the Book of Genesis within Abrahamic religions. He is the elder brother of Abel, and the firstborn son of Adam and Eve, the first couple in the Bible. He was a farmer who gave an offering of his crops to God. However, God was not pleased and favored Abel's offering over Cain's. Out of jealousy, Cain murdered his brother, becoming the first murderer in the biblical narrative.

The murder of Abel

Genesis 4 recounts that God warned Cain that sin crouched at his door. Cain nonetheless lured Abel into the field and killed him. When God asked where Abel was, Cain replied, "Am I my brother's keeper?"

Curse and mark

God cursed Cain to be a fugitive and wanderer, with the ground no longer yielding to him. Cain protested the punishment was too great and he would be killed; God therefore placed a mark on Cain to warn would-be killers that vengeance would come sevenfold, and exiled him to the land of Nod, east of Eden.

Descendants

Cain built a city named for his son Enoch. His descendants in Genesis include Lamech, Jabal, Jubal, and Tubal-cain, associated with livestock, music, and metalworking.

In culture

The story has inspired countless works, from medieval drama to Lord Byron's mystery play Cain and Steinbeck's East of Eden.
