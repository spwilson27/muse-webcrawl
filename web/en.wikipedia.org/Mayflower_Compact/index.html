Mayflower Compact

The Mayflower Compact, originally titled the "Agreement Between the Settlers of New Plymouth," was the first governing document of Plymouth Colony. It was written and signed by the male passengers of the Mayflower on 11 November 1620 (Old Style) while the ship lay at anchor in Cape Cod harbour.

Background

The Mayflower had been driven north of its chartered destination in Virginia, landing the colonists outside the jurisdiction of the Virginia Company patent under which they had sailed. Some of the non-Separatist passengers — the "Strangers" — argued that since the patent did not apply, no authority bound them. To forestall disorder, the colony's leaders drafted a covenant combining the settlers into a "civil body politic" that would enact "just and equal laws" for the general good, to which all promised submission and obedience.

Signers and text

Forty-one men signed, including John Carver (chosen as the colony's first governor), William Bradford, Edward Winslow, William Brewster, and Myles Standish. The brief text professes loyalty to King James I while establishing self-government by consent — a combination later celebrated as a foundational moment in American democracy, though its immediate purpose was practical and its franchise limited.

Legacy

The original document is lost; the earliest surviving texts appear in Mourt's Relation (1622) and Bradford's history Of Plymouth Plantation. The Compact remained Plymouth's effective constitution until the colony was absorbed into Massachusetts in 1691.
