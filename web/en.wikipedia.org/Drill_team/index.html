Drill team

A drill team can refer to two distinct kinds of performance groups. In the United States, particularly in Texas, a drill team is a dance team of high-school or college students that performs choreographed routines at football games and competitions. In a military context, a drill team is a unit that performs precision marching and ceremonial drill, sometimes including intricate rifle-handling routines known as exhibition drill.

Dance drill teams

The modern dance drill team originated in Texas, where Gussie Nell Davis founded the Kilgore College Rangerettes in 1940, the first such collegiate team. High-school drill teams perform at halftime in matching uniforms, often incorporating high kicks, splits, and pom or prop routines, and compete in categories such as kick, pom, jazz, and military. Famous professional-adjacent examples include the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders, who grew out of this tradition.

Military drill teams

Military drill teams perform synchronized movements of arms and formation marching. Exhibition drill teams, such as the United States Air Force Honor Guard Drill Team and the Marine Corps Silent Drill Platoon, execute complex sequences including spinning and tossing rifles with fixed bayonets, all in silence and without verbal commands during the performance. JROTC and ROTC units also field drill teams that compete in regulation and exhibition categories.
