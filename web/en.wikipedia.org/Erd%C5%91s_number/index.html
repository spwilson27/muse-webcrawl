Erdős number

Paul Erdős (1913–1996), the prolific Hungarian mathematician whose co-authorship network defines the Erdős number.

The Erdős number (Hungarian: Erdős-szám) describes the "collaborative distance" between the mathematician Paul Erdős and another person, as measured by authorship of mathematical papers. Erdős himself has an Erdős number of zero. A person who co-authored a paper with Erdős has Erdős number 1; someone who co-authored a paper with one of those people (but not with Erdős himself) has Erdős number 2, and so on.

Background

Erdős was one of the most prolific mathematicians of the 20th century, publishing around 1,500 papers with more than 500 collaborators. The number was devised by his colleagues as a tribute and an informal game; Casper Goffman's 1969 paper "And What Is Your Erdős Number?" helped popularise it. The Erdős Number Project, maintained by Jerrold Grossman at Oakland University, tracks the collaboration graph using Mathematical Reviews data: about 268,000 mathematicians have finite Erdős numbers, with a median around 5 and a maximum recorded value of 13 (excluding infinity).

Distribution and variants

Because Erdős collaborated so widely, most publishing mathematicians are within a few steps of him. Analogues have been defined in other fields, such as the Bacon number for film actors (co-starring distance from Kevin Bacon) and the combined Erdős–Bacon number for people active in both mathematics and film. The concept is an example of the structure studied in social network analysis and the "small world" phenomenon; the collaboration graph's distances can also be computed through MathSciNet's collaboration distance tool.
