Air filter

An air filter is a device composed of fibrous, or porous materials which removes solid particulates such as dust, pollen, mold, and bacteria from the air. Filters containing an adsorbent or catalyst such as charcoal (carbon) may also remove odors and gaseous pollutants such as volatile organic compounds or ozone. Air filters are used in applications where air quality is important, notably in building ventilation systems and in engines.

Automotive filters

Internal combustion engines use an air filter to prevent abrasive particles from entering the cylinders, where they would cause mechanical wear and oil contamination. Most modern fuel-injected vehicles use a panel-shaped pleated paper filter in a plastic housing, while older carbureted engines often used cylindrical filters. Cabin air filters clean air entering the passenger compartment.

Building filtration

Heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) systems use filters rated by systems such as MERV in North America. High-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filters remove at least 99.97% of particles 0.3 micrometres in diameter and are used in hospitals, cleanrooms and air purifiers.
