Thrombosis

Thrombosis is the formation of a blood clot, known as a thrombus, within a blood vessel, obstructing the flow of blood through the circulatory system. When a blood vessel is injured, the body uses platelets and fibrin to form a clot to prevent blood loss; when this clotting occurs inappropriately, or when a clot breaks free and travels (becoming an embolus), serious or fatal outcomes can result, including stroke, myocardial infarction (heart attack), and pulmonary embolism.

Causes — Virchow's triad

The causes of thrombosis are classically summarised by Virchow's triad, described in the 19th century:

  • Stasis — abnormal blood flow, such as prolonged immobility, long-haul flights, or paralysis.
  • Vessel wall injury — damage to the endothelium from trauma, surgery, infection, or atherosclerosis.
  • Hypercoagulability — an increased tendency of blood to clot, from genetic disorders (such as Factor V Leiden or prothrombin G20210A), cancer, pregnancy, hormonal contraception, or antiphospholipid syndrome.

Types

Venous thrombosis most commonly occurs in the deep veins of the leg (deep vein thrombosis, DVT), presenting with pain, swelling, and redness; a clot that travels to the lungs causes pulmonary embolism, which can be fatal. Venous thromboembolism is the third most common cardiovascular condition worldwide. Arterial thrombosis usually results from rupture of an atherosclerotic plaque and underlies most heart attacks and many strokes. Clots may also form in the heart (for example, in atrial fibrillation) and embolise to the brain or limbs.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention involves mobilisation after surgery, compression stockings, and anticoagulant prophylaxis in high-risk patients. Treatment of established thrombosis uses anticoagulants such as heparin, warfarin, or direct oral anticoagulants (e.g. rivaroxaban, apixaban, dabigatran). In severe cases, thrombolytic drugs, catheter-directed therapy, or surgical thrombectomy may be used, and an inferior vena cava filter may be placed when anticoagulation is impossible.
