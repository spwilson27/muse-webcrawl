The Great Gatsby

The Great Gatsby is a 1925 novel by the American writer F. Scott Fitzgerald. Set in the Jazz Age on Long Island, the novel depicts narrator Nick Carraway's interactions with the mysterious millionaire Jay Gatsby and Gatsby's obsession to reunite with his former lover, Daisy Buchanan. The novel is widely considered a literary classic and a contender for the title of the "Great American Novel".

Plot

Nick Carraway, a Midwesterner who has moved to the village of West Egg, Long Island, rents a small house next to the mansion of Jay Gatsby, a flamboyant host of lavish parties who grew rich, it is rumoured, by bootlegging. Nick is drawn into Gatsby's quest to win back Daisy Buchanan — now married to the brutish Tom Buchanan — whom Gatsby had loved before the war. After a tense reunion and confrontation, Daisy, driving Gatsby's car, strikes and kills Tom's lover Myrtle Wilson; Gatsby takes the blame and is shot dead by Myrtle's husband. Nick arranges a sparsely attended funeral and returns West, disillusioned with the carelessness of the wealthy.

Themes and reception

Explored themes include decadence, idealism, the corruption of the American Dream, class, and the impossibility of recapturing the past — crystallised in the novel's famous closing meditation on the "green light" at the end of Daisy's dock. Sales during Fitzgerald's lifetime were disappointing; the novel's stature rose after the Second World War, aided by the Armed Services Editions distributed to soldiers, and it is now a fixture of school curricula and repeatedly tops lists of the greatest English-language novels.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times for stage and screen, most notably the 1974 film with Robert Redford and the 2013 Baz Luhrmann film with Leonardo DiCaprio. It entered the public domain in the United States in 2021, prompting prequels, retellings and other derivative works.
