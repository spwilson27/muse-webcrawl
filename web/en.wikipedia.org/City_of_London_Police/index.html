City of London Police

Formed1839
JurisdictionCity of London ("Square Mile")
HeadquartersWood Street, London
OfficersRoughly 800–1,000

The City of London Police is the territorial police force responsible for law enforcement within the City of London, including the Middle and Inner Temples. It is distinct from the much larger Metropolitan Police Service, which polices the rest of Greater London.

History

Policing in the City has ancient origins, and the modern force was formally established under the City of London Police Act 1839, a decade after the Metropolitan Police, after the City resisted being absorbed into the new force. The force faced the Jack the Ripper murders partly within its jurisdiction (Mitre Square, 1888) and, later, Irish republican and jihadist terrorist attacks on the financial district.

Role

Despite the City's tiny resident population, the force polices a huge daytime working population. The City of London Police is the national lead force for fraud and economic crime in England and Wales, hosting Action Fraud, the national fraud and cybercrime reporting centre, and specialist units such as the Police Intellectual Property Crime Unit (PIPCU).

Organisation

The force is overseen by the City of London Corporation's Police Authority Board, with a Commissioner as chief officer. Its officers wear distinctive red-and-white chequered sleeve and cap bands, unique among British police.
