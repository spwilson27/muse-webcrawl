Muawiyah I

Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan (Arabic: معاوية بن أبي سفيان, romanized: Muʿāwiya ibn Abī Sufyān; c. 597, 603 or 605 – April 680), commonly known as Muawiyah I, was the founder and first caliph of the Umayyad Caliphate. He ruled from 661 until his death in 680 and established Damascus as the caliphate's capital.

Governor of Syria

A scribe of Muhammad and son of the Meccan leader Abu Sufyan, Mu'awiya was appointed governor of Syria under Caliph Umar and consolidated his hold over the province under Uthman. After Uthman's assassination in 656, he refused to pledge allegiance to Ali until the murderers were punished, leading to confrontation in the First Fitna, including the Battle of Siffin in 657, which ended with inconclusive arbitration.

Caliphate

After Ali's assassination in 661 and the abdication of Hasan ibn Ali, Mu'awiya was recognized as caliph. He reigned for nearly two decades, moving the capital to Damascus, building the first Muslim navy, campaigning repeatedly against the Byzantine Empire (including sieges of Constantinople), and creating administrative institutions modeled partly on Byzantine practice. By securing the succession of his son Yazid I, he transformed the caliphate into a hereditary monarchy, a move that contributed to the Second Fitna after his death.
