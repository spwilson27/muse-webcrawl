Steven Moffat

Steven Moffat (born 1961), Scottish television writer and producer.

Steven William Moffat OBE (born 18 November 1961) is a Scottish television writer, television producer and screenwriter. He is best known as the showrunner of the BBC science-fiction series Doctor Who (2010–2017) and as co-creator, with Mark Gatiss, of the drama Sherlock (2010–2017).

Early career

Moffat, a former teacher from Paisley, broke into television with the children's drama Press Gang (1989–1993), which won a BAFTA. He went on to write the sitcoms Joking Apart and Coupling, the latter loosely based on his own courtship of producer Sue Vertue, whom he married.

Doctor Who and Sherlock

A lifelong fan, Moffat contributed acclaimed episodes to the revived Doctor Who under Russell T Davies — including "The Empty Child", "The Girl in the Fireplace" and "Blink", which introduced the Weeping Angels — before succeeding Davies as head writer and executive producer with the Eleventh Doctor, Matt Smith, in 2010, and continuing through Peter Capaldi's Twelfth Doctor until 2017. Sherlock, his modern-day reimagining of Arthur Conan Doyle's detective with Gatiss, starred Benedict Cumberbatch and Martin Freeman and became an international success, winning Emmy and BAFTA awards.

Other work

Moffat co-wrote the screenplay for Steven Spielberg's The Adventures of Tintin (2011), co-created the BBC/Netflix series Dracula (2020) and the thriller Inside Man (2022), and returned to Doctor Who as a guest writer in 2024. He was appointed OBE in 2015 for services to drama.
