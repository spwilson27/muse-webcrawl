Airline alliance

An airline alliance is an arrangement between two or more airlines to cooperate on marketing, scheduling, and operations. Members typically codeshare flights, align schedules for connections, share airport lounges, and allow passengers to earn and redeem frequent-flyer miles across the alliance. The three largest global alliances are Star Alliance, SkyTeam, and Oneworld.

History

Early partnerships date to the 1930s, but the modern global alliance emerged in the 1990s, when regulatory barriers prevented cross-border mergers. Star Alliance, founded in 1997 by five airlines including United and Lufthansa, was the first; Oneworld followed in 1999 and SkyTeam in 2000. Together the three alliances carry a majority of the world's scheduled airline passengers.

Benefits and criticism

Alliances extend networks without new routes, feed traffic into members' hubs, and reduce costs through joint purchasing and shared facilities. Critics argue that immunized joint ventures on major routes can reduce competition and raise fares, and alliance membership can shift as airlines merge, go bankrupt, or change strategy.
