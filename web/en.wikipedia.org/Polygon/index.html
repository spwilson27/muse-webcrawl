Polygon

In geometry, a polygon is a plane figure made up of line segments connected end to end to form a closed path. The segments are called edges or sides, and their meeting points are vertices or corners. A polygon with n sides is an n-gon; familiar examples include the triangle (3), quadrilateral (4), pentagon (5), and hexagon (6). The word comes from the Greek polygōnon, "many angles".

Various polygons: simple, concave, and regular.

Classification

A polygon is simple if its boundary does not intersect itself; otherwise it is complex (self-intersecting), like a star polygon. A simple polygon is convex if all its interior angles are less than 180°, and concave otherwise. A regular polygon has all sides equal and all interior angles equal; every regular polygon can be inscribed in a circle. Polygons are also classified as equilateral, equiangular, or cyclic depending on which properties hold.

Properties

The interior angles of a simple n-gon sum to (n − 2) × 180°. A simple polygon has exactly n vertices and n sides, and n(n − 3)/2 diagonals. The area can be computed from vertex coordinates with the shoelace formula, and for a regular n-gon with side length s, the area is ns² / (4 tan(π/n)).

Applications

Polygons are fundamental in computer graphics, where three-dimensional models are built from polygonal meshes (usually triangles), in cartography and geographic information systems, and in architecture. The generalization of a polygon to three dimensions is a polyhedron.
