Idi Amin Dada Oumee (c. 1928 – 16 August 2003) was a Ugandan military officer and politician who served as the third president of Uganda from 1971 to 1979. He seized power in a military coup against President Milton Obote and ruled as a dictator.

Rule

Amin's regime was characterised by severe human rights abuses, political repression, ethnic persecution, extrajudicial killings, nepotism, corruption, and gross economic mismanagement. Estimates of the death toll under his rule range from 100,000 to 500,000. In 1972 he expelled Uganda's Asian population, precipitating economic collapse.

Foreign policy

Amin broke relations with Israel, aligned with Libya's Muammar Gaddafi and the Soviet bloc, and supported the hijackers of Air France Flight 139, whose hostages were rescued by Israeli commandos at Entebbe in 1976.

Overthrow and exile

After Uganda invaded Tanzania in 1978, Tanzanian forces and Ugandan exiles counter-invaded and captured Kampala in April 1979. Amin fled, eventually settling in Saudi Arabia, where he died in 2003.
