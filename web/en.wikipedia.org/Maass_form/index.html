Maass form

In mathematics, a Maass form (also Maass wave form) is a function on the upper half-plane that transforms like a modular form under the action of the modular group but, instead of being holomorphic, is an eigenfunction of the hyperbolic Laplacian. Maass forms were introduced by Hans Maass in 1949 and form a central part of the spectral theory of automorphic forms.

Definition

A Maass form of weight k for the modular group SL(2, ℤ) is a smooth function f on the upper half-plane satisfying: (i) the modular transformation law with the usual automorphy factor; (ii) Δk f = λ f, where Δk is the weight-k hyperbolic Laplacian; and (iii) moderate growth at the cusp. If in addition the constant term of the Fourier expansion vanishes, the form is called a Maass cusp form.

Fourier expansion

Maass forms possess Fourier expansions whose non-constant terms involve modified Bessel functions of the second kind (K-Bessel functions) rather than the exponentials appearing for holomorphic modular forms, reflecting their non-holomorphic character. The eigenvalues λ = 1/4 + r² are conjectured (Selberg eigenvalue conjecture) to satisfy λ ≥ 1/4 for congruence subgroups.

Significance

Maass cusp forms correspond, under the Langlands program, to certain automorphic representations of GL(2). Their L-functions satisfy functional equations analogous to those of holomorphic modular forms. Numerical computation of Maass forms is notoriously difficult because no algebraic construction is known in general; they are found by solving the eigenvalue problem numerically.
