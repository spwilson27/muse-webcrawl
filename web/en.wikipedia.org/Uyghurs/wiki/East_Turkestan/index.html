East Turkestan

East Turkestan (also East Turkistan) is a historical and political term for the eastern part of Turkestan in Central Asia, roughly corresponding to present-day Xinjiang in western China, particularly the Tarim Basin. It is used especially by Uyghur independence advocates.

East Turkestan
Region: Central Asia
Corresponds to: Xinjiang, esp. Tarim Basin
Languages: Uyghur, other Turkic languages

History

The term distinguished the Turkic, largely Muslim east from Russian / West Turkestan. In the 20th century two short-lived East Turkestan Republics were proclaimed: the First (1933–34) around Kashgar and the Second (1944–49) in the Ili region, before incorporation into the People's Republic of China in 1949.

Modern use

Exile groups such as the East Turkistan Government-in-Exile and the World Uyghur Congress use the name in advocacy; Chinese authorities reject it as associated with separatism. See Xinjiang.
