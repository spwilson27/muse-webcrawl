Uyghur language

Uyghur (ئۇيغۇر تىلى, Uyghur tili) is a Turkic language written in a Perso-Arabic script, spoken by the Uyghur people mainly in Xinjiang, China, and by diaspora communities in Central Asia and elsewhere. It has roughly 10–15 million speakers.

Uyghur
Family: Turkic, Karluk branch
Script: Perso-Arabic (official), Latin/Cyrillic in diaspora
Speakers: ~10–15 million
Region: Xinjiang, Central Asia

History

Modern Uyghur descends from the Karluk languages related to Chagatai, the literary language of Central Asia. Old Uyghur, written in its own script, was a language of the Uyghur Khaganate and Qocho kingdom and influenced Mongolian script.

Features

Uyghur is agglutinative, with vowel harmony reduced compared with other Turkic languages, subject-object-verb word order, and extensive vocabulary from Persian, Arabic, Russian and Mandarin Chinese. Dialects include Central, Southern (Hotan) and Eastern (Lopnor). See Xinjiang and East Turkestan.
