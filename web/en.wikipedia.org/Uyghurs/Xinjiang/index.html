Xinjiang

Xinjiang, officially the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, is an autonomous region of China in the northwest of the country. It is China's largest province-level division, bordering Central and South Asia, and is home to the Uyghur people and many other ethnic groups.

Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region
Capital: Ürümqi
Area: ~1.66 million km²
Population: ~25.9 million
Major groups: Uyghur, Han, Kazakh, Hui

Geography

The Tian Shan divides Xinjiang into the Junggar Basin in the north and the Tarim Basin in the south, the latter containing the Taklamakan Desert and oasis cities such as Kashgar, Hotan and Turpan.

History

A crossroads of the Silk Road, the region was ruled by Turkic khaganates, Tibetan and Mongol empires, and was incorporated into Qing China in the 18th century, becoming a province in 1884 and an autonomous region in 1955. Some Uyghurs refer to it as East Turkestan.

Contemporary issues

Since the 2010s international attention has focused on reports of mass detention, surveillance and cultural restrictions affecting Uyghurs — described by China as vocational training and counter-terrorism — drawing sanctions and genocide allegations disputed by Beijing.
