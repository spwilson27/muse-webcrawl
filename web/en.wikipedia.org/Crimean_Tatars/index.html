Crimean Tatars

Crimean Tatars (Crimean Tatar: qırımtatarlar)
Regions: Crimea, Ukraine, Turkey, Romania, Uzbekistan
Population: ~500,000–2,000,000 (estimates vary)
Languages: Crimean Tatar, Russian, Ukrainian, Turkish
Religion: predominantly Sunni Islam

Crimean Tatars (Crimean Tatar: qırımtatarlar, singular: qırımtatar) are an Eastern European Turkic ethnic group and nation that originated in Crimea during the 13th to 17th centuries, formed from a mixture of the Kipchak and Oghuz Turkic peoples who had settled the peninsula since the 8th century and earlier inhabitants including Greeks, Goths and Genoese.

History

Crimean Tatars were the founders and predominant population of the Crimean Khanate (1441–1783). Following the Russian annexation of Crimea in 1783, waves of emigration to the Ottoman Empire began, and the Tatar share of the Crimean population steadily fell.

Deportation of 1944

On 18 May 1944, the Soviet government under Joseph Stalin forcibly deported the entire Crimean Tatar population—roughly 190,000 people—to Central Asia, chiefly Uzbekistan, accusing them of collaboration with Nazi Germany. A large proportion died from starvation and disease during deportation and exile; Crimean Tatar activists estimate losses at up to 46% of the population. The deportation is recognised as a genocide by Ukraine and several other states.

Return and recent history

Crimean Tatars were permitted to return to Crimea only in the late 1980s under perestroika, rebuilding their community around the representative body known as the Qurultay and its executive, the Mejlis, long chaired by Mustafa Dzhemilev. After Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, which Crimean Tatar leaders largely opposed, the Mejlis was banned by Russian authorities in 2016 and many Tatar activists faced prosecution and pressure.
