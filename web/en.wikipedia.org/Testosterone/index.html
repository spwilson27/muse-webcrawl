Testosterone is the principal male sex hormone and an anabolic steroid. In humans it is produced mainly by the Leydig cells of the testes in males and in smaller amounts by the ovaries and adrenal glands in females. It plays a key role in the development of male reproductive tissues and secondary sexual characteristics, and influences muscle mass, bone density, body hair, libido and mood in both sexes.

Physiology

Testosterone secretion is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis: luteinizing hormone from the pituitary stimulates production, and testosterone feeds back to suppress further release. In tissues it may act directly, be converted by 5α-reductase to the more potent dihydrotestosterone (DHT), or be aromatised to estradiol. Most circulating testosterone is bound to sex hormone-binding globulin and albumin; only a small fraction is free. Male levels rise sharply at puberty and decline gradually with age.

Medical use and misuse

Synthetic testosterone and its esters, first produced in 1935, are used to treat male hypogonadism and delayed puberty, and in gender-affirming hormone therapy. Supraphysiological doses are misused for performance enhancement in sport, where testosterone is prohibited by anti-doping authorities; long-term misuse is associated with cardiovascular, hepatic and psychiatric risks.
