Hoard

A hoard or hoarding is a deposit of valuable objects or artifacts, sometimes buried in the ground, often deliberately hidden by their owner with the intention of recovering them later — an intention that, for archaeologists, went unfulfilled. Hoards can include coin hoards, jewellers' hoards, metalworkers' hoards, and votive deposits. Because they represent a snapshot of the valuables available at a particular place and time, hoards provide important evidence for dating, trade, and economic history.

Types and reasons for burial

People buried hoards for safekeeping in times of invasion or unrest, as savings, or as ritual offerings. Coin hoards are particularly common from the Roman period and the Viking Age. Famous examples include the Hoxne Hoard (late Roman Britain, discovered 1992), the Staffordshire Hoard of Anglo-Saxon gold and silver (discovered 2009, the largest of its kind), and the Cuerdale Hoard of Viking silver.

Legal status

In the United Kingdom, hoards meeting the definition of treasure fall under the Treasure Act 1996 and must be reported; museums may acquire them with a reward to the finder. Laws differ widely elsewhere, and illicit metal detecting and looting of hoards remain problems for archaeology.
