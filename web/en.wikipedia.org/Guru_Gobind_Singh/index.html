Guru Gobind Singh

Guru Gobind Singh
Born: 22 December 1666, Patna, India
Died: 7 October 1708, Nanded, India
Predecessor: Guru Tegh Bahadur
Known for: Founding the Khalsa

Guru Gobind Singh (22 December 1666 – 7 October 1708), born Gobind Rai, was the tenth and last human Sikh Guru. A spiritual leader, warrior, poet and philosopher, he succeeded his father, Guru Tegh Bahadur, at the age of nine after the elder Guru's execution. He is best known for founding the Khalsa, the collective body of initiated Sikhs, in 1699.

The Khalsa

On Vaisakhi in 1699, at Anandpur Sahib, Guru Gobind Singh initiated the first five Khalsa Sikhs, the Panj Pyare ("five beloved ones"), and established a code of discipline including the Five Ks: uncut hair (kesh), a wooden comb (kangha), a steel bracelet (kara), cotton undergarments (kachera) and a ceremonial sword (kirpan). He also gave initiated Sikhs the names Singh ("lion") for men and Kaur ("princess") for women.

Later life

The Guru fought a series of battles against Mughal forces and hostile hill rajas, and all four of his sons were killed in the conflicts of the era. In 1708 he declared the Guru Granth Sahib, the Sikh scripture, to be the eternal Guru of the Sikhs. He died at Nanded in October 1708 after being stabbed by an assassin.
