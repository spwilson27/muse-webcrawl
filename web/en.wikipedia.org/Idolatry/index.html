Idolatry

Idolatry
[Image: Worship of the golden calf, a classic biblical depiction of idolatry]
DefinitionWorship of an idol or cult image as though it were God
Condemned inJudaism, Christianity, Islam

Idolatry is the worship of a physical object — an idol or cult image — as a god, or more broadly the devotion to something other than God that takes God's place. In the Abrahamic religions idolatry is a major sin: the first of the Ten Commandments forbids the worship of other gods and, in the usual interpretation, the making of graven images for worship. The Hebrew Bible repeatedly denounces the idolatry of Israel's neighbours, most famously in the episode of the golden calf in Exodus.

Views by religion

In Judaism, idolatry (avodah zarah, "foreign worship") is one of the three cardinal sins that must be resisted even at the cost of one's life. Islam regards idolatry (shirk, associating anything with Allah) as the gravest sin; the Prophet Muhammad's clearing of the idols from the Kaaba is a foundational episode. In Christianity, attitudes have varied: the early church condemned pagan cult images; the Byzantine iconoclasm controversy turned on whether venerating icons amounted to idolatry, and the Second Council of Nicaea (787) distinguished veneration of images from worship due to God alone. Protestant reformers levelled charges of idolatry against Catholic image-veneration, while Catholic and Orthodox theology maintains the distinction between latria (worship) and dulia (veneration).

Other traditions and metaphorical use

Religions such as Hinduism use sacred images (murti) as focal points of devotion without identifying the image with the deity itself, and practitioners often reject the label "idolatry" as a misunderstanding of their practice. In modern usage the word is also used metaphorically for excessive devotion to money, celebrity, or ideology.
