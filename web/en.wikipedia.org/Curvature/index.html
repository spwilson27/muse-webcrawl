Curvature

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, curvature is any of several strongly related concepts in geometry that intuitively measure the amount by which a curve deviates from being a straight line, or a surface deviates from being a plane.

Curvature of a plane curve

For a plane curve given parametrically, the (signed) curvature at a point is the reciprocal of the radius of the osculating circle — the circle that best approximates the curve at that point:

κ = 1/R

Equivalently, curvature is the rate of change of the tangent angle with respect to arc length. A straight line has curvature zero; a circle of radius R has constant curvature 1/R.

Curvature of surfaces

For surfaces in three-dimensional space, curvature at a point is described by the two principal curvatures, the maximum and minimum curvatures of normal sections through the point. From these are derived:

  • Gaussian curvature — the product of the principal curvatures; an intrinsic property of the surface (Gauss's Theorema Egregium).
  • Mean curvature — their average, related to minimal surfaces and surface tension.

Higher dimensions

In Riemannian geometry, curvature of a manifold is encoded in the Riemann curvature tensor, with derived quantities including sectional curvature, Ricci curvature, and scalar curvature. In general relativity, spacetime curvature is related to the distribution of mass and energy by the Einstein field equations.
