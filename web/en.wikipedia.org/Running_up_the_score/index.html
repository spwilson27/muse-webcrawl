Running up the score

Running up the score (or "piling on") is a sports term that refers to a team continuing to score points or goals after a comfortable, insurmountable lead has been established, rather than conserving effort or substituting reserve players. It is generally considered unsportsmanlike at amateur and youth levels, though its acceptability in professional sport is debated.

Debate

Critics argue that continuing to attack against a beaten opponent humiliates them and risks injury, while defenders counter that professional athletes are paid to compete for the entire game, that substitutes benefit from real playing time, and that slowing down can be equally disrespectful. In some sports, margin of victory matters for rankings or tiebreakers, giving teams a legitimate incentive to keep scoring.

Notable attitudes

In North American football, blowout victories — such as Georgia Tech's 222–0 defeat of Cumberland in 1916 — are frequently cited in discussions of the practice. Many youth leagues counteract it with mercy rules that end a contest once a threshold margin is reached. In soccer, heavily one-sided international results, such as Australia's 31–0 win over American Samoa in 2001, prompted changes to World Cup qualification formats.

Related concepts

The opposite behaviour, deliberately easing off, is sometimes called "calling off the dogs" or "taking the foot off the gas". Related practices include running out the clock and kneeling to end an American football game.
