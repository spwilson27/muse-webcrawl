Prominence2,372 m
LocationLom, Innlandet, Norway

Galdhøpiggen is the highest mountain in Norway, Scandinavia, and Northern Europe, rising to 2,469 metres (8,100 ft) above sea level in the Jotunheimen range, within Jotunheimen National Park in the municipality of Lom. Its name derives from gald (a steep road up a mountainside) and piggen ("the peak").

Geography

The summit is a rocky peak partly covered by snow and ice; a small cabin, built by the Norwegian Trekking Association tradition of summit shelters, stands at the top. For much of the twentieth century the nearby glacier-capped Glittertinden was thought possibly higher, since its ice cap's thickness varied; measurements eventually settled the question in Galdhøpiggen's favour, as Glittertinden's ice has thinned.

Ascent

The mountain is a popular hike rather than a technical climb. The standard routes start from the lodges at Spiterstulen (a long walk entirely on land) and Juvasshytta, the highest road-accessible lodge in Norway, from which guided groups cross the Styggebreen glacier roped together. In clear summer weather thousands of walkers reach the summit each season, with views across much of southern Norway.

History

The first recorded ascent was made in 1850 by the schoolteacher Steinar Sulheim with companions. Galdhøpiggen's status as the roof of Northern Europe, together with the surrounding concentration of Norway's highest peaks, has made Jotunheimen the heartland of Norwegian mountain tourism since the nineteenth century.
