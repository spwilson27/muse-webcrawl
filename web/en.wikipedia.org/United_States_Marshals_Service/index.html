The United States Marshals Service (USMS) is a U.S. federal law enforcement agency within the Department of Justice. Operating as the enforcement arm of the federal judiciary, it is the oldest federal law enforcement agency in the United States, created by the Judiciary Act of 1789. Its duties include protecting the federal judiciary, apprehending federal fugitives, managing seized assets, housing and transporting federal prisoners, and operating the Witness Security Program.

History

The Judiciary Act of 1789, signed by President George Washington on 24 September 1789, created the office of United States Marshal for each of the thirteen federal judicial districts. Early marshals executed court orders, served process, conducted the federal census (until 1870), and enforced federal law on the frontier — marshals such as Wyatt Earp and Wild Bill Hickok's contemporaries in the territories became fixtures of American folklore, though Earp himself served as a deputy marshal.

Marshals played notable roles in American history: enforcing the Fugitive Slave Acts, desegregating Southern schools in the 1960s (including escorting Ruby Bridges and protecting James Meredith at the University of Mississippi in 1962), and responding to the 1973 Wounded Knee occupation. The Marshals Service as a unified agency was formally established in 1969.

Duties

  • Judicial security — protecting federal judges, courthouses, and judicial proceedings
  • Fugitive operations — the USMS is the federal government's primary agency for fugitive investigations, arresting tens of thousands of fugitives annually, often through regional fugitive task forces with state and local partners
  • Prisoner operations — housing and transporting federal prisoners via the Justice Prisoner and Alien Transportation System (JPATS), nicknamed "Con Air"
  • Witness security — the WITSEC programme, created in 1970, has protected roughly 19,000 witnesses and their families
  • Asset forfeiture — managing and disposing of assets seized by federal law enforcement

Organization

The Service is headed by a Director appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate. There are 94 judicial districts, each headed by a presidentially appointed U.S. Marshal, while day-to-day operations are staffed by career Deputy U.S. Marshals. The Special Operations Group (SOG) is the service's tactical unit.
