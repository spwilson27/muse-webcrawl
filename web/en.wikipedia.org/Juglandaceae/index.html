Juglandaceae

The Juglandaceae are a family of flowering plants in the order Fagales, commonly known as the walnut family. It comprises about 60 species of trees and shrubs in roughly nine genera, distributed mainly in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Members are typically large, aromatic, deciduous trees with pinnately compound leaves. The flowers are usually unisexual and wind-pollinated (the plants are monoecious), the male flowers borne in catkins. The fruit is a nut or nut-like drupe.

Genera and economic importance

Important genera include Juglans (walnuts), Carya (hickories and pecans), Pterocarya (wingnuts), Engelhardia, Platycarya and Cyclocarya. The family is economically significant for edible nuts — walnuts and pecans — and for valuable timber, especially black walnut (Juglans nigra) and hickory wood.
