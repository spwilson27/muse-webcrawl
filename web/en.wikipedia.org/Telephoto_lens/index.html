Telephoto lens

A telephoto lens is a long-focus camera lens whose physical length is shorter than its focal length, achieved by a telephoto group — a positive front element group combined with a negative rear group. In common usage, "telephoto" refers to any lens with a long focal length and narrow angle of view, used to magnify distant subjects.

Characteristics

Telephoto lenses compress apparent perspective (making distant objects appear closer together), have shallow depth of field at wide apertures, and are subject to noticeable camera shake, so fast shutter speeds or image stabilisation are often needed. Typical focal lengths for 35 mm format range from about 85 mm (short or "portrait" telephoto) to super-telephoto lenses of 400–800 mm used in sports and wildlife photography.

Uses

Telephotos are standard equipment for sports, wildlife, news and portrait photography, and for surveillance and astronomy. Catadioptric (mirror) telephoto designs fold the optical path with mirrors to achieve very long focal lengths in compact bodies, at the cost of fixed aperture and distinctive doughnut-shaped out-of-focus highlights.
