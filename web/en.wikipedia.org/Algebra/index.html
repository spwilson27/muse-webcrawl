Algebra

Algebra is a branch of mathematics that studies mathematical structures and the rules for manipulating them. In its elementary form, algebra generalises arithmetic by using letters (variables) to represent numbers and by studying equations and how to solve them. In its modern abstract form, it investigates structures such as groups, rings, fields, and vector spaces.

History

The word "algebra" derives from the Arabic al-jabr, part of the title of the 9th-century treatise by Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi, The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing, which systematically solved linear and quadratic equations. Diophantus of Alexandria had earlier treated similar problems in his Arithmetica. In the 16th century, European mathematicians such as Cardano and Tartaglia solved cubic and quartic equations, and Viète introduced systematic symbolic notation. The 19th century saw the birth of abstract algebra with Galois's group-theoretic analysis of polynomial solvability and, later, Emmy Noether's structural approach.

Branches

Elementary algebra deals with solving polynomial equations and manipulating expressions. Linear algebra studies vector spaces and linear maps, with applications throughout science and computing. Abstract (or modern) algebra studies algebraic structures in themselves; algebraic geometry and algebraic number theory connect algebra with geometry and arithmetic.
