Sergei Winogradsky

Sergei Nikolaevich Winogradsky (Russian: Сергей Николаевич Виноградский; 1 September 1856 – 25 February 1953) was a Ukrainian and Russian microbiologist, ecologist and soil scientist who pioneered the modern understanding of the nitrogen cycle and discovered chemosynthesis.

Research

Working in Strasbourg and Zurich in the late 1880s, Winogradsky showed that sulfur bacteria such as Beggiatoa obtain energy by oxidising hydrogen sulfide — the first demonstration of chemoautotrophy. In 1890–91 he isolated the nitrifying bacteria responsible for converting ammonia to nitrite and nitrite to nitrate, establishing that these organisms fix carbon dioxide using chemical rather than light energy.

In 1893 he isolated Clostridium pasteurianum, the first known free-living nitrogen-fixing bacterium. He also developed the enrichment culture technique and the Winogradsky column, still used to study microbial communities.

Later life

Winogradsky directed the Institute of Experimental Medicine in St Petersburg before emigrating to France in 1922, where he headed the agricultural microbiology division of the Pasteur Institute at Brie-Comte-Robert until his death at the age of 96.
