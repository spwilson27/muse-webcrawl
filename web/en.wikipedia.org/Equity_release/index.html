Equity release

Main types
Lifetime mortgage; home reversion
Typical market
Homeowners aged 55+ (UK)
Regulator (UK)
Financial Conduct Authority

Equity release is a means of retaining use of a house or other property while also obtaining a lump sum or steady income, using the property as security. The term is most commonly used in the United Kingdom for products aimed at older homeowners, allowing them to release part of the value tied up in their home without moving out.

Types

The two main forms are the lifetime mortgage, a loan secured on the home on which interest rolls up (or is optionally paid) and which is repaid from the sale of the property when the borrower dies or enters long-term care, and home reversion, in which part or all of the home is sold to a reversion company in exchange for a lump sum and a lifetime right to live there rent-free.

Risks and regulation

Because interest compounds, roll-up loans can erode the remaining equity quickly, reducing inheritances and means-tested benefits. Following mis-selling scandals in the late 1980s, the sector adopted safeguards such as the no-negative-equity guarantee promoted by the Equity Release Council, and products are now regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority with mandatory advice.
