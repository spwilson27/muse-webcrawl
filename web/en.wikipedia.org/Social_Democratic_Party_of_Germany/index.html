The Social Democratic Party of Germany (German: Sozialdemokratische Partei Deutschlands, SPD) is a social democratic political party in Germany and one of the country's oldest and largest parties. Founded in the nineteenth-century workers' movement, it has shaped German politics through the Empire, the Weimar Republic, exile and suppression under Nazism, and the Federal Republic.

History

The SPD traces its origins to Ferdinand Lassalle's General German Workers' Association (1863) and the Social Democratic Workers' Party of August Bebel and Wilhelm Liebknecht (1869), which merged at Gotha in 1875. Banned under Bismarck's Anti-Socialist Laws (1878–1890), it nonetheless grew into the largest party in the Reichstag by 1912. In November 1918, SPD leader Friedrich Ebert became head of government and the party was central to founding the Weimar Republic; Ebert served as its first president.

The SPD was the only party to vote against the Enabling Act of 1933 in the Reichstag—Otto Wels's defiant speech is celebrated in German democratic memory—and was then banned by the Nazi regime. Refounded after 1945, the party under Kurt Schumacher opposed Konrad Adenauer's Christian Democrats. At Bad Godesberg in 1959 the SPD formally abandoned Marxist economics and embraced the social market economy, paving the way to power under Willy Brandt (chancellor 1969–1974), whose Ostpolitik won him the Nobel Peace Prize, and Helmut Schmidt (1974–1982).

Recent decades

Gerhard Schröder led a red–green coalition from 1998 to 2005, enacting the Agenda 2010 labour-market reforms that proved deeply divisive within the party. The SPD subsequently served as junior partner in grand coalitions under Angela Merkel for much of 2005–2021. In the 2021 federal election the party, led by Olaf Scholz, finished first and formed a "traffic light" coalition with the Greens and Free Democrats; Scholz became chancellor in December 2021.

Organisation and policies

The SPD is a membership party with strong links to trade unions, though formal ties have loosened. Its programme combines support for a welfare state, workers' rights, European integration, and a social-ecological transformation of the economy.
