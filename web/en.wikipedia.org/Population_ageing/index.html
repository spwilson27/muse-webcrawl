Population ageing

Population ageing is an increasing median age in a population due to declining fertility rates and rising life expectancy. Most countries have rising life expectancy and an ageing population, trends that emerged first in more economically developed countries but are now seen in virtually all parts of the world. The United Nations estimates that the number of people aged 65 or over worldwide will double by 2050.

Causes

The two main drivers are declining fertility, which shrinks the proportion of children, and increasing longevity, which expands the proportion of older adults. International migration can moderate or accentuate ageing in particular countries.

Effects

Ageing populations increase the old-age dependency ratio, putting pressure on pension systems, healthcare and long-term care, while shrinking workforces in many countries. At the same time, older adults contribute through work, caregiving and consumption, and policy responses include raising retirement ages, encouraging later-life employment, and investing in preventive health.
