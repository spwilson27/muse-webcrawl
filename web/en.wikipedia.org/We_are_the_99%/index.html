We are the 99%

TypePolitical slogan
OriginOccupy movement, 2011

"We are the 99%" is a political slogan widely used and coined during the Occupy movement of 2011, beginning with Occupy Wall Street in New York City. It refers to the concentration of income and wealth among the top 1% of earners compared with the rest of the population — the 99% — and became a rallying cry against economic inequality, corporate influence in politics and the effects of the 2008 financial crisis.

Origins

The slogan spread through a Tumblr blog, "We Are the 99 Percent," launched in August 2011, on which people posted photographs of themselves holding handwritten signs describing their economic hardships: student debt, medical bills, unemployment and foreclosure. The framing drew on statistics about the growth of top incomes in the United States, popularised by economists such as Joseph Stiglitz, whose 2011 article "Of the 1%, by the 1%, for the 1%" highlighted the widening gap.

Meaning and use

The slogan contrasts the wealthiest one percent — said to control a disproportionate share of wealth and political influence — with everyone else. It was chanted at Occupy encampments in hundreds of cities worldwide and was adopted by later campaigns for higher minimum wages, student-debt relief and progressive taxation. Critics questioned the precise statistics and the slogan's breadth, but its framing of inequality as "1% versus 99%" entered mainstream political vocabulary and has been credited with shifting public debate toward income distribution.
