The Hereford is a British breed of beef cattle that originated in the county of Herefordshire, in the West Midlands of England. It is one of the most widespread beef breeds in the world, exported since the early nineteenth century to many temperate grazing regions, and is instantly recognizable by its deep red body and contrasting white face.

History

The breed was developed from local red cattle of the Welsh Marches, with systematic improvement beginning in the eighteenth century; Benjamin Tomkins of Hereford is often credited as a founder breeder. Herefords were first exported to the United States in 1817 by Henry Clay, and the breed's hardiness, grazing ability, and early maturity made it a mainstay of the American, Canadian, Australian, Argentine, and Uruguayan beef industries. The Hereford Herd Book Society was founded in 1878.

Characteristics

Herefords are docile, fertile, and efficient foragers, noted for longevity and the ability to finish on grass. Mature bulls commonly weigh 800–1,100 kg and cows 550–750 kg. A naturally hornless strain, the Polled Hereford, was developed in the United States from the early 1900s and is now often registered together with the horned breed.

Crossbreeding

The Hereford is widely used in crossbreeding; its first-cross progeny inherit the white face. Famous crosses include the Black Baldy (Hereford × Angus) and the Braford (Hereford × Brahman), valued for hybrid vigor and maternal qualities.
