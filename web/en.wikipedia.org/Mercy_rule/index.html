Mercy rule

A mercy rule, also known as a slaughter rule or run rule, is a rule in sporting events that ends a contest prematurely when one competitor has built an insurmountable lead over the other. Mercy rules are most common in youth and scholastic sports, where they are intended to spare losing teams further humiliation and reduce the risk of injury.

Baseball and softball

In softball, international rules commonly end a game after a set number of innings if one team leads by a specified margin (for example, 20 runs after three innings, or 10 runs after five). Little League baseball uses a 10-run rule after four innings. In NCAA baseball, a 10-run rule may apply in conference play after seven innings.

American football

Many U.S. high school associations employ a mercy rule in which a running clock is used once a team's lead reaches a threshold (often 35 points) in the second half.

Other sports

Similar provisions exist in ice hockey tournaments, boxing (referee stops contest), and some video game competitions. Professional leagues such as Major League Baseball generally do not use mercy rules, though trailing teams sometimes use position players to pitch late in blowout games.
