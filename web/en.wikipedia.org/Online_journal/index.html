Online journal

An online journal is a diary or periodical maintained on the World Wide Web. The term covers several distinct kinds of publication that emerged as the web developed: personal journals, an early form of blogging; academic and scientific journals published electronically; and literary or professional periodicals issued online.

Personal online journals

In the mid-1990s, before the word "blog" was coined, early web diarists published dated personal entries on hand-built pages. Services such as Open Diary (1998) and LiveJournal (1999) made the practice widespread, providing accounts, friends lists and privacy controls, and building communities of diarists. The form declined as blogging platforms and later social media absorbed personal writing, though newsletter and journalling services have revived aspects of it.

Scholarly journals online

Academic publishing moved online from the 1990s onward: most scholarly journals now appear in electronic editions, often exclusively online, and preprint servers and open-access journals publish only on the web. Electronic journals are typically accessed through library subscriptions or aggregators and identified by ISSN and DOI.

Other senses

Newspapers and magazines also publish "online journals" as web editions, blogs or opinion sections — for example, the web journals and opinion blogs maintained by financial papers. In computing, the term can also refer to logs or journaling files maintained by software, though this is more often called an electronic log.
