Music Has the Right to Children is the debut studio album by Scottish electronic music duo Boards of Canada, released on 20 April 1998 by Warp Records and Skam Records. It is widely regarded as a landmark of intelligent dance music (IDM) and downtempo, noted for its warm, degraded analogue textures, childlike melodies and nostalgic atmosphere.

Background and sound

Brothers Michael Sandison and Marcus Eoin produced the album using vintage synthesisers, samples and tape manipulation, layering short interludes between melodic tracks such as "Roygbiv", "An Eagle in Your Mind", "Turquoise Hexagon Sun" and "Aquarius" — the latter built around a counting sample from Sesame Street. The music evokes half-remembered childhood and 1970s educational films, an aesthetic the duo developed in their earlier, scarcer EPs.

Reception and legacy

The album was acclaimed on release and has repeatedly appeared on lists of the greatest electronic albums; Pitchfork placed it among the best albums of the 1990s. Its influence spread widely through downtempo and ambient music, and it preceded Boards of Canada's subsequent albums Geogaddi (2002) and The Campfire Headphase (2005).
