Welsh cuisine

Welsh cuisine encompasses the cooking styles, traditions and recipes associated with Wales. It developed from the island's agricultural economy of sheep farming, dairy production, oats, barley, and coastal fishing, and was shaped by the mining communities of the industrial south.

Traditional dishes

Well-known dishes include cawl, a broth or stew of meat and root vegetables; Welsh rarebit, a savoury sauce of melted cheese, ale, and mustard served over toast; bara brith, a spiced fruit loaf; Welsh cakes, griddled tea-time cakes with currants; and laverbread, cooked seaweed (laver) traditionally eaten with cockles and bacon for breakfast in the south. Glamorgan sausages are vegetarian sausages made from cheese, leek, and breadcrumbs. Lamb is the emblematic meat, and cheeses such as Caerphilly are long established; leeks are a national symbol and feature widely.

Modern cuisine

Since devolution, Welsh food has enjoyed a revival, with protected-status products including Welsh lamb and beef (PGI), Halen Môn sea salt, and wines from a growing number of vineyards. Food festivals such as Abergavenny's promote local producers, and modern Welsh restaurants reinterpret traditional ingredients.
