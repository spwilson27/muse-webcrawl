Andean condor

The Andean condor (Vultur gryphus) is a South American bird of prey in the New World vulture family Cathartidae, found in the Andes mountains and adjacent Pacific coasts. It is one of the largest flying birds in the world, with a wingspan of up to about 3.3 metres (10 ft 10 in) and a weight of up to 15 kg.

Description

Adults are mostly black with white patches on the wings; males have a large comb on the head and a white ruff around the neck. The bare skin of the head changes colour with the bird's emotional state. Condors soar for hours on mountain updrafts, searching for carrion such as deer, cattle, and guanaco carcasses.

Reproduction and lifespan

Andean condors mature slowly, not breeding until about six years of age, and lay a single egg every other year on inaccessible cliff ledges. They are among the longest-lived birds, sometimes exceeding 70 years in captivity.

Conservation and culture

The species is listed as Vulnerable, threatened by habitat loss and secondary poisoning from carcasses of animals shot with lead ammunition. Captive-breeding and reintroduction programmes operate in several countries. The condor is a national symbol of Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru, and figures prominently in Andean mythology and folklore.
