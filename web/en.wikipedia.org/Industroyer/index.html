Industroyer, also known as Crashoverride, is a malware framework designed to attack electrical substations and other industrial control systems. It was identified by the Slovak security firm ESET in June 2017 and is believed to have been used in the cyberattack of 17 December 2016 that cut power to parts of Kyiv, Ukraine, for about an hour.

Capabilities

Unlike Stuxnet, which targeted a specific configuration, Industroyer is modular and directly implements industrial communication protocols used in power systems worldwide, including IEC 60870-5-101, IEC 60870-5-104, IEC 61850, and OLE for Process Control Data Access (OPC DA). Its main backdoor can communicate with command-and-control servers, install payload components, and issue commands to open circuit breakers, while additional modules include a port scanner, a denial-of-service tool against Siemens SIPROTEC protective relays, and a wiper component intended to render systems inoperable.

The 2016 Kyiv attack

The malware was deployed against a transmission-level substation serving Kyiv. Analysts attributed the operation to a state-sponsored Russian group (often identified as Sandworm). The attack was shorter in effect than the December 2015 Ukrainian power outage caused by BlackEnergy, but Industroyer was considered more dangerous because it could automate large-scale attacks on grid equipment without operator interaction.

Industroyer2

In April 2022, ESET and CERT-UA reported detecting a variant dubbed Industroyer2 targeting a Ukrainian energy provider during the Russian invasion of Ukraine; the attempt was thwarted before it caused an outage.
