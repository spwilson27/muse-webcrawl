Cognition

Cognition refers to the mental processes involved in gaining knowledge and comprehension, including thinking, knowing, remembering, judging and problem-solving. These are higher-level functions of the brain and encompass processes such as attention, perception, memory, language, learning and reasoning.

Study

Cognition is studied principally by cognitive psychology and by the interdisciplinary field of cognitive science, which also draws on neuroscience, linguistics, philosophy and artificial intelligence. Cognitive psychologists investigate how people acquire, process and store information, using experiments on memory, attention and problem solving; pioneers included Ulric Neisser, Jean Piaget, and the information-processing theorists of the mid-20th century "cognitive revolution".

Processes

Major cognitive processes include perception (organising sensory information), attention (selecting information for processing), memory (encoding, storing and retrieving information), language, learning, and executive functions such as planning, decision-making and cognitive control. Disorders of cognition — including amnesia, agnosia and the dementias — are studied in neuropsychology.
