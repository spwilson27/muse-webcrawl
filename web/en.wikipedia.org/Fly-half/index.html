Fly-half

The fly-half is a position in rugby union and rugby league. In rugby union the fly-half wears the number 10 jersey and is the team's principal decision-maker and playmaker, standing outside the scrum-half (number 9) and receiving the ball first from most set-piece and breakdown plays. The position is also known as the outside-half, stand-off (in rugby league and in some regions), outhalf, or first five-eighth in New Zealand.

Role in rugby union

The fly-half directs the team's attack, deciding whether to pass the ball along the backline, kick for territory or goal, or run at the defence. Along with the scrum-half, the fly-half forms the "half-back" pairing and typically takes most tactical kicks — clearance kicks, cross-field kicks, up-and-unders and box-kick alternatives — and often also serves as the team's goal-kicker for penalties and conversions. In defence the fly-half guards the channel inside the centres and must be a reliable tackler. Great fly-halves are prized for game management, distribution skills and composure under pressure.

Famous fly-halves

Regarded among the greatest number 10s are Dan Carter of New Zealand, the all-time leading points scorer in Test rugby; Jonny Wilkinson of England, whose drop goal won the 2003 Rugby World Cup final; Johnny Sexton of Ireland; Hugo Porta of Argentina; Barry John and Jonathan Davies of Wales; Naas Botha and Handré Pollard of South Africa; and Beauden Barrett of New Zealand.

Rugby league

In rugby league, the equivalent position is the stand-off half (number 6 in the standard numbering), who partners the scrum-half (number 7) in the halves, organizing attacking plays and kicking, though in the modern game the role is often split between playmakers on each side of the field.
