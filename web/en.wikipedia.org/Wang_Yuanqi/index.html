Wang Yuanqi (Chinese: 王原祁; 1642–1715) was a Chinese painter of the Qing dynasty, the youngest of the "Four Wangs", and the grandson of the painter Wang Shimin. One of the most influential artists and theorists of the orthodox school of landscape painting, he served the Kangxi Emperor as a court official, connoisseur, and editor of imperial painting catalogues.

Career

Taught painting by his grandfather from childhood, Wang Yuanqi passed the jinshi examination in 1670 and rose in official service while becoming the leading arbiter of painting at court. He helped compile the Peiwenzhai Shuhua Pu, an encyclopedic compendium on calligraphy and painting, and authenticated works in the imperial collection. The emperor valued his judgment highly, and his Loudong school (named for his home region) dominated orthodox painting in the early 18th century.

Style and theory

Where Wang Hui excelled in integrating colour and scope, Wang Yuanqi pursued strongly abstract, architectonic landscape structures built from dry-brush ink textures modelled especially on Huang Gongwang. He theorized landscape in terms of the "dragon veins" (longmai) of opening, closing, rising, and falling movement through a composition, and considered creative reinterpretation of old masters the proper path of painting — ideas recorded in his writings, including the Yuchuang manbi (Occasional notes from the rain window).
