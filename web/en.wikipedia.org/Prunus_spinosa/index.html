Prunus spinosa

Kingdom: Plantae
Family: Rosaceae
Genus: Prunus
Species: P. spinosa
Common names: blackthorn, sloe

Prunus spinosa, called blackthorn or sloe, is a species of flowering plant in the rose family, Rosaceae. It is native to Europe, western Asia, and locally in northwest Africa. It is a deciduous large shrub or small tree growing to about 5 metres tall, with blackish bark and dense, stiff, spiny branches.

Description

The leaves are small and oval with a toothed margin. The hermaphrodite flowers, about 1.5 cm across with five creamy-white petals, appear in early spring before the leaves and are pollinated by insects. The fruit, called a sloe, is a drupe about 10–12 mm in diameter, black with a purple-blue waxy bloom, ripening in autumn. Sloes are very sour and astringent, becoming somewhat more palatable after frost.

Uses

Sloes are used to flavour sloe gin, a liqueur made by steeping the fruit in gin with sugar, and similar drinks elsewhere in Europe, such as pacharán in Spain. The dense, hard wood has traditionally been used for walking sticks, including the Irish shillelagh, and for tool handles. Blackthorn is also planted as a stock-proof hedge and provides valuable cover and early nectar for wildlife.

Ecology

Blackthorn is a food plant for the larvae of several Lepidoptera, including the black hairstreak butterfly. Its thickets provide nesting habitat for birds such as the nightingale.
