Year of the Four Emperors

The Year of the Four Emperors, AD 69, was the first civil war of the Roman Empire, during which four men successively claimed the imperial throne in a single year: Galba, Otho, Vitellius, and Vespasian. The chaos followed the suicide of Emperor Nero in 68 and exposed what Tacitus called the "secret of empire" — that an emperor could be made somewhere other than Rome.

VespasianJuly 69 – 79 (proclaimed by the eastern legions; founded the Flavian dynasty)

Background

Nero's misrule and suicide ended the Julio-Claudian dynasty. Galba, governor of Hispania Tarraconensis, marched on Rome and was recognised emperor, but his stinginess and severity alienated the army and the Praetorian Guard. The legions of Germania refused to swear allegiance to him and proclaimed their commander Vitellius in January 69.

The civil war

On 15 January 69 the Praetorians murdered Galba and proclaimed Marcus Salvius Otho emperor. Vitellius's armies invaded Italy and defeated Otho at the First Battle of Bedriacum in April; Otho took his own life. Vitellius's brief reign in Rome was marked by extravagance and cruelty. Meanwhile the eastern legions proclaimed Vespasian, commander in the Jewish War, emperor on 1 July 69. His forces under Antonius Primus crushed the Vitellians at the Second Battle of Bedriacum (Cremona) in October, stormed Rome in December, and Vitellius was lynched. Vespasian arrived in 70 and founded the stable Flavian dynasty.

Significance

The year revealed the military foundations of the Principate: provincial armies, not the Senate, now made emperors. Our chief narrative source is Tacitus's Histories, supplemented by Suetonius and Cassius Dio.
