Gun barrel

A rifled barrel's helical grooves impart spin to the projectile.

A gun barrel is a metal tube through which a controlled explosion or rapid expansion of gases is released to propel a projectile at high velocity. Barrels are a critical component of firearms and artillery, withstanding chamber pressures of tens of thousands of psi in modern rifles. The interior of the barrel is called the bore; its diameter is the calibre.

Construction

Modern barrels are drilled and reamed from solid steel bar stock, then rifled — cut or cold-hammer-forged with helical grooves that spin the bullet for gyroscopic stability — or left smooth in the case of most shotguns. Historically, barrels were built up by welding strips of iron around a mandrel (the "Damascus" barrel). Barrel length strongly affects muzzle velocity, sight radius and handling; many jurisdictions set legal minimums.

Components and behaviour

The barrel comprises the chamber at the breech, the bore, and the muzzle, which may carry devices such as muzzle brakes, flash suppressors or suppressor mounts. Firing heats and erodes the barrel, gradually degrading accuracy; barrels are therefore considered consumable components in precision and automatic weapons.
