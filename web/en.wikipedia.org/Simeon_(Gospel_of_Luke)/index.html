Simeon (Gospel of Luke)

Simeon is a righteous and devout man of Jerusalem who appears in the second chapter of the Gospel of Luke. According to the narrative, the Holy Spirit had promised him that he would not die until he had seen the Messiah; recognising the infant Jesus at the Presentation in the Temple, he uttered the prayer known as the Nunc Dimittis.

The Nunc Dimittis

Taking the child in his arms, Simeon praises God: "Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word; for mine eyes have seen thy salvation." The canticle became a fixed part of the Christian evening liturgy, sung at Compline and Evensong. Simeon also prophesied to Mary that the child would be a sign spoken against and that a sword would pierce her own soul.

Veneration

Simeon is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and Oriental Orthodox churches. Tradition holds that his relics were translated to Constantinople and later to Zadar, Croatia, where an ornate reliquary chest is still displayed.
