SHA-3

SHA-3 (Secure Hash Algorithm 3) is the latest member of the Secure Hash Algorithm family of cryptographic hash functions, standardized by NIST in FIPS 202 in August 2015. Unlike SHA-1 and SHA-2, SHA-3 is based on the Keccak algorithm and uses a sponge construction rather than the Merkle–Damgård structure, giving it a completely different internal design.

SHA-3
Designers: Guido Bertoni, Joan Daemen, Michaël Peeters, Gilles Van Assche
First published: 2015 (FIPS 202)
Digest sizes: 224, 256, 384, 512 bits
Structure: Sponge construction (Keccak-f[1600])

History

NIST announced a public competition in 2007 to develop a new hash standard to complement SHA-2 after attacks on MD5 and SHA-1 raised concern. From 64 entries, the Keccak submission was announced as the winner on 2 October 2012. The standard also defines the SHAKE128 and SHAKE256 extendable-output functions (XOFs), which can produce hashes of arbitrary length.

Design

SHA-3 operates on a 1600-bit state arranged as a 5×5 array of 64-bit lanes, transformed by the Keccak-f permutation over 24 rounds using the step mappings θ, ρ, π, χ, and ι. In the sponge construction, input is absorbed into the state in blocks determined by the rate, and output is squeezed out; security depends on the hidden capacity portion of the state. Because of the sponge structure, SHA-3 is naturally resistant to length-extension attacks that affect SHA-2.

Instances

Standardized instances are SHA3-224, SHA3-256, SHA3-384, and SHA3-512, plus SHAKE128 and SHAKE256. NIST positions SHA-3 as a complementary alternative to SHA-2 rather than a replacement, since SHA-2 remains secure.
