Mount Washington Cog Railway

The Mount Washington Cog Railway is a rack railway climbing Mount Washington in New Hampshire's White Mountains, the first mountain-climbing cog railway in the world. Opened to the summit in 1869, it carries passengers about 3 miles (4.8 km) from the Marshfield base station, at roughly 2,700 feet, to the 6,288-foot summit on an average grade of 25 percent, reaching a maximum of 37.4 percent on the Jacob's Ladder trestle — long the steepest railway section in the world.

History

The line was the invention of Sylvester Marsh, who, after getting lost in bad weather on the mountain in 1857, resolved to build a railway to the top. Widely dismissed as impossible — legislators joked about granting a charter to the Moon — Marsh patented his rack-and-pinion system, in which a cog wheel beneath the locomotive engages a ladder-like rack of rungs between the rails. The first locomotive, Old Peppersass, climbed in 1866; trains reached the summit on 3 July 1869.

Operation

For most of its life the railway used coal-fired steam locomotives, each burning about a ton of coal per round trip, with the locomotive pushing a single tilted carriage; since 2008 most services have been worked by biodiesel locomotives, with steam retained for early-morning and special trains. The line operates from late spring into the autumn; the summit, famous for some of the worst weather in the world, is served by the Sherman Adams building of the Mount Washington Observatory.
