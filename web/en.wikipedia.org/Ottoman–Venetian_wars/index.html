Ottoman–Venetian wars

The Ottoman–Venetian wars were a series of conflicts between the Ottoman Empire and the Republic of Venice fought between the 15th and 18th centuries for control of trade and territory in the eastern Mediterranean, Greece and the Adriatic.

Course

Early wars in the 1460s–1470s cost Venice Negroponte and other Aegean possessions. The war of 1537–1540 and the Cyprus War of 1570–1573 saw Venice lose Cyprus despite the Christian victory at Lepanto in 1571. The long Cretan War (1645–1669) ended with the fall of Candia and Venetian loss of Crete.

Venice briefly regained ground in the Morean War (1684–1699) as part of the Holy League, taking the Peloponnese, but lost it again in 1714–1718, after which the Treaty of Passarowitz confirmed Ottoman recovery.

Significance

The wars marked Venice's gradual decline as a Mediterranean great power and the consolidation of Ottoman dominance in the eastern Mediterranean.
