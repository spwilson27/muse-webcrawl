Spanish Golden Age theatre
Period: c. 1590 – c. 1681
Key figures: Lope de Vega, Calderón de la Barca, Tirso de Molina
Principal venues: Corrales de comedias

Spanish Golden Age theatre refers to the theatre of Spain during the Siglo de Oro, roughly from the 1590s to the death of Pedro Calderón de la Barca in 1681. It was one of the most productive dramatic eras in world literature: thousands of plays were written and performed for a paying public audience, and dramatists such as Lope de Vega, Tirso de Molina and Calderón reshaped European drama.

Development

Early Spanish drama drew on medieval religious theatre, Italian commedia dell'arte, and humanist models. Juan del Encina and Bartolomé de Torres Naharro were important precursors. The decisive figure was Lope de Vega (1562–1635), whose Arte nuevo de hacer comedias (1609) defended a popular, mixed form: three acts, mingled tragedy and comedy, varied verse forms, and a disregard for the classical unities. Lope was credited with some 1,800 plays, of which around 400 survive.

Forms

The standard commercial play was the comedia, a three-act drama in polymetric verse, often treating themes of love and honour. Other forms included the auto sacramental, a one-act allegorical play on the Eucharist performed for Corpus Christi — Calderón was its supreme master — as well as the entremés (comic interlude), the loa (prologue) and the baile.

Playwrights

Major dramatists included Lope de Vega (Fuenteovejuna, The Dog in the Manger), Tirso de Molina (creator of Don Juan in The Trickster of Seville), Calderón de la Barca (Life Is a Dream, The Mayor of Zalamea), Juan Ruiz de Alarcón, Guillén de Castro, and Ana Caro, one of the few known women playwrights of the period.

Theatres

Plays were performed in corrales de comedias, courtyard playhouses such as Madrid's Corral del Príncipe and Corral de la Cruz, administered by charitable confraternities. Audiences ranged from standing mosqueteros in the patio to nobles in balconies. The literature of the period is inseparable from this vibrant commercial stage.
