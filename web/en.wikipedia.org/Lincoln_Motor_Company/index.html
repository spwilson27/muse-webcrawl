Lincoln Motor Company

The Lincoln Motor Company, commonly called Lincoln, is the luxury vehicle division of American automobile manufacturer Ford Motor Company. Founded in 1917 by Henry M. Leland and his son Wilfred, it was named after Abraham Lincoln, the first president for whom Leland had voted.

History

The company first built Liberty aircraft engines during the First World War, then produced luxury automobiles. Facing financial trouble after the transition to car production, Lincoln was acquired by Ford in 1922, where Edsel Ford shaped it into a design-led luxury marque. The Lincoln Continental, introduced in 1939 as Edsel Ford's personal car, became one of its most famous nameplates, and Lincoln supplied presidential state cars for the White House for much of the 20th century.

Modern range

In recent decades Lincoln has shifted from sedans to luxury SUVs and crossovers, selling the Corsair, Nautilus, Aviator, and Navigator, and has pursued sales in China as well as North America. The brand markets itself around "Quiet Flight" design and comfort.
