Gewürztraminer

Gewürztraminer (German: [ɡəˈvʏʁtstʁaˈmiːnɐ]) is an aromatic wine grape variety used in white wines, best known for the intensely perfumed wines of Alsace in France. The name literally means "spice Traminer", the variety being a mutation of the Savagnin/Traminer family originating near Tramin (Termeno) in South Tyrol, Italy. Its berries have a distinctive pink-to-red skin, although the wines made from it are white.

Gewürztraminer wines are noted for powerful aromas of lychee, rose petals, Turkish delight, ginger and tropical fruit, with high alcohol, low acidity and a rich, sometimes oily texture. Styles range from dry to very sweet late-harvest wines (vendanges tardives and sélection de grains nobles).

History

The Traminer grape has been documented around Tramin since the Middle Ages. The aromatic, pink-skinned mutation known as Gewürztraminer spread through Alsace and Germany, where the prefix Gewürz (spice) was added. DNA profiling shows Gewürztraminer is a mutation of Savagnin (Traminer), closely related to Savagnin blanc.

Viticulture and winemaking

The variety is vigorous but shy-bearing, prone to coulure, and performs best in cool climates on clay soils, where slow ripening preserves its aromatics. It ripens with high sugar levels, so winemakers must balance richness against its naturally low acidity. In Alsace it is one of the four noble grape varieties permitted in grand cru wines.

Wine regions

Alsace produces the classic expression, from dry, spicy wines to luscious dessert wines. Plantings also exist in Germany, Austria, Italy's Alto Adige, Eastern Europe, New Zealand, Australia, Canada and the United States.
