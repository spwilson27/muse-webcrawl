Stevenson screen

A Stevenson screen (or instrument shelter) is a white, louvred wooden or plastic enclosure that shields meteorological instruments — typically thermometers and hygrometers — from direct sunlight and precipitation while allowing air to circulate freely, so that they measure true air temperature.

Design

Designed by Scottish civil engineer Thomas Stevenson in 1864, the screen has double-louvred walls, a double roof, and is painted white to reflect solar radiation. It is mounted with its door facing poleward (away from the sun) at a height of about 1.25–1.5 metres over grass. Standardized designs are specified by the World Meteorological Organization.

Importance

Uniform use of Stevenson screens worldwide makes temperature records comparable across stations and over time, which is essential for climate studies. Modern versions may be plastic, and automated sensors are increasingly housed in smaller aspirated shields.
