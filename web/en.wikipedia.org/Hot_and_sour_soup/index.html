Hot and sour soup

Hot and sour soup is a soup from Asian culinary traditions — versions exist in both Chinese and Thai cuisines (such as tom yum). The Chinese version (suānlàtāng, 酸辣湯) is commonly associated with the cuisines of Sichuan and northern China, and is flavoured to be both spicy (from white or black pepper and chilli) and sour (from vinegar). In Chinese-American restaurants it is a standard menu item.

Ingredients

Typical ingredients include day lily buds, wood ear fungus, bamboo shoots, tofu, and sometimes pork or chicken, in a broth thickened with cornstarch and enriched with beaten egg swirled in at the end. The sourness comes from rice vinegar (often black vinegar) and the heat traditionally from ground white pepper rather than chilli oil. Regional variants include the Henan style hulatang and versions with coagulated blood.

Variations

In Thailand, tom yum achieves a hot-and-sour profile with lemongrass, galangal, lime juice, fish sauce, and chillies, usually with prawns or chicken. Vietnamese canh chua is a sour soup flavoured with tamarind. In India and Pakistan, "hot and sour" soups appear on Indo-Chinese menus, adapted with local spices and soy sauce.
