PJ Harvey

Polly Jean Harvey MBE (born 9 October 1969), known as PJ Harvey, is an English singer-songwriter, musician and poet from Dorset. Emerging in the early 1990s with a raw trio format, she became one of the most acclaimed British artists of her generation and is the only artist to have won the Mercury Prize twice.

Career

Harvey's first albums, Dry (1992) and Rid of Me (1993, produced by Steve Albini), established her fierce, blues-inflected style. To Bring You My Love (1995) broadened her sound; Stories from the City, Stories from the Sea (2000), featuring Thom Yorke, won the Mercury Prize. Uh Huh Her (2004) and the piano-led White Chalk (2007) followed, then Let England Shake (2011) — songs about war and England that won her second Mercury Prize. The Hope Six Demolition Project (2016) drew on reportage from Kosovo, Afghanistan and Washington, D.C., and I Inside the Old Year Dying (2023) set poems from her verse novel Orlam, written in Dorset dialect.

Recognition

Beyond music she has composed for theatre and film and published poetry collections. She was appointed MBE in 2013 for services to music.
