Bloomsbury Group

The Bloomsbury Group was a group of associated English writers, intellectuals, philosophers and artists who, in the first half of the 20th century, lived, worked and studied together near Bloomsbury, London. Its best-known members included Virginia Woolf, John Maynard Keynes, E. M. Forster, Lytton Strachey, Leonard Woolf, Vanessa Bell, Clive Bell, Duncan Grant and Roger Fry.

History

The group originated around 1905 among Cambridge graduates and siblings Vanessa and Virginia Stephen, who began holding "Thursday evening" gatherings at 46 Gordon Square. They were united by a belief in the importance of personal relationships and the pursuit of individual pleasure, influenced by G. E. Moore's philosophy as set out in Principia Ethica (1903).

Influence

Although loosely organised and never issuing a manifesto, the group had a wide-ranging influence on literature, aesthetics, criticism and economics, as well as on modern attitudes toward feminism, pacifism and sexuality. The Hogarth Press, founded by Leonard and Virginia Woolf in 1917, published many of the group's works.
