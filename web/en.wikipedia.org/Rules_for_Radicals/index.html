Rules for Radicals: A Pragmatic Primer for Realistic Radicals is a 1971 book by American community organizer and writer Saul D. Alinsky, published shortly before his death in 1972. Written for the "have-nots", it sets out Alinsky's philosophy and methods for grassroots organizing and became a standard text for community organizers, activists and political campaigners across the spectrum.

Content

The book opens with chapters on pragmatism, means and ends, and the qualities of a successful organizer, and closes with thirteen tactical rules. Among the best-known rules are "Power is not only what you have but what the enemy thinks you have"; "Never go outside the expertise of your people"; "Ridicule is man's most potent weapon"; "Make the enemy live up to its own book of rules"; and "Pick the target, freeze it, personalize it, and polarize it".

Reception and influence

Rules for Radicals has been cited by figures in labour unions, the civil rights movement and both left- and right-wing political movements, including activists associated with the Tea Party. Critics have described some of its tactics as manipulative or polarising, while supporters regard it as a realistic manual for democratic participation by the powerless. The book remains in print and continues to be assigned in courses on social work, politics and organizing.

Background

Alinsky founded the Industrial Areas Foundation in 1940 and spent decades organizing communities in Chicago and elsewhere, work described in his earlier book Reveille for Radicals (1946). Rules for Radicals distilled that experience into a short, deliberately provocative handbook addressed to a younger generation of activists.
