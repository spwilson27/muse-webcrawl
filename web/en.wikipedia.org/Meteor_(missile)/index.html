The Meteor is a European active radar guided beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile (BVRAAM) developed and manufactured by MBDA. It is powered by a throttleable ducted rocket — a type of ramjet — which sustains high speed throughout its flight and gives it one of the largest no-escape zones of any air-to-air missile.

Development

The programme was launched by the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, and Sweden to replace or supplement the American AIM-120 AMRAAM on European combat aircraft. Meteor entered service with the Swedish Air Force's Gripen fighters in 2016, the first operational ramjet-powered air-to-air missile. It has since been integrated on the Eurofighter Typhoon and Dassault Rafale, and is being integrated on the F-35 Lightning II.

Design

Unlike conventional rocket-powered missiles, which burn out early and coast, Meteor's boron-fuelled ramjet can be throttled between minimum and maximum thrust, preserving endgame energy and manoeuvrability. It uses inertial guidance with mid-course updates via a two-way datalink, and terminal homing by an active radar seeker derived from the Aster and Mica families. Its range is officially described as in excess of 100 km, with published estimates considerably higher.
