Enumerative combinatorics

Enumerative combinatorics is an area of combinatorics that deals with the number of ways certain patterns can be formed. Two types of problems arise: counting a set of objects directly, and counting with prescribed properties. Typically one seeks an exact formula, a recurrence relation, a generating function, or an asymptotic estimate for the number of configurations of a given size.

Basic counting

The subject begins with permutations and combinations, the binomial coefficients, and the "twelvefold way" — a unified framework for counting functions between finite sets according to whether the functions are required to be injective or surjective and whether the sets' elements are considered distinguishable. Classical sequences such as the Catalan numbers, the Stirling numbers and the partition numbers arise naturally and recur across mathematics.

Generating functions

Generating functions are the central tool of the field: a combinatorial class is encoded by a power series whose coefficients count its objects. Operations on classes — disjoint union, product, sequence, composition — correspond to algebraic operations on series, turning counting problems into calculations. Richard Stanley's two-volume Enumerative Combinatorics is the standard reference.

Applications

Enumeration underlies the analysis of algorithms, probability theory (counting equally likely outcomes), statistical mechanics and the study of algebraic structures such as symmetric functions and Young tableaux.
