Diocese of Moray

The Diocese of Moray was one of the most important of the medieval dioceses of Scotland. Its bishops were based at a succession of churches—Birnie, Kinneddar, and Spynie—before Bishop Bricius de Douglas fixed the seat at Elgin Cathedral in the early thirteenth century, where it remained until the Scottish Reformation.

Elgin Cathedral, seat of the medieval bishops of Moray.

History

The diocese probably emerged in the eleventh and early twelfth centuries during the Gaelic kingdom's expansion, with the first known bishop, Gregoir, appearing in the reign of David I. The great cathedral at Elgin, founded in 1224, was burned by Alexander Stewart, the "Wolf of Badenoch", in 1390 and again damaged in later centuries. After the Reformation of 1560, the diocese continued for a time within the Episcopal church, but bishops were abolished in the Church of Scotland in 1689. The title survives in the Roman Catholic Diocese of Aberdeen's region and in the Scottish Episcopal Diocese of Moray, Ross and Caithness, founded in the nineteenth century.
