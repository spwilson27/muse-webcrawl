Mahout

A mahout is an elephant rider, trainer, or keeper. The word derives from Hindi mahāvat, from Sanskrit mahāmātra. Traditionally, mahoutship was a hereditary profession in South and Southeast Asian cultures, with a boy assigned to an elephant early in his life and remaining with the same animal for decades of working life.

Practice

The mahout rides on the elephant's neck and guides it with verbal commands and tools such as the ankus (bullhook) and a wooden stick. Mahouts and their elephants have historically been used in logging, warfare, ceremony, and transport in India, Thailand, Myanmar, Cambodia, and Sri Lanka. Today many work in conservation camps, temples, and tourism.

Culture

The relationship between mahout and elephant is a recurring theme in Asian literature and religion; the Kui people of Thailand and Surin province are particularly noted elephant keepers, and annual elephant festivals such as Surin's celebrate the tradition.
