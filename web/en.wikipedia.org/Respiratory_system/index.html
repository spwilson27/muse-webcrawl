Respiratory system

The respiratory system (also respiratory apparatus or ventilatory system) is a biological system consisting of specific organs and structures used for gas exchange in animals and plants. In land animals, including humans, it supplies the body with oxygen and removes carbon dioxide, while also playing roles in vocalisation, smell, and regulation of blood pH.

Anatomy

In humans the respiratory system is divided into the upper respiratory tract — the nose and nasal cavity, pharynx, and larynx — and the lower respiratory tract, comprising the trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, and the lungs. Within the lungs, the bronchioles end in alveoli, tiny air sacs surrounded by capillaries where gas exchange occurs by diffusion across the alveolar–capillary membrane.

Breathing

Ventilation is driven mainly by the diaphragm and the intercostal muscles. Contraction of the diaphragm expands the thoracic cavity, drawing air in (inhalation); relaxation allows elastic recoil to expel air (exhalation). Breathing is controlled automatically by respiratory centres in the brainstem, which respond principally to carbon dioxide levels and blood pH, and in an average adult produce a resting rate of about 12–20 breaths per minute.

Functions

Beyond gas exchange, the respiratory system filters, warms, and humidifies inspired air; provides the airflow for speech and singing; hosts the olfactory receptors; and helps maintain acid–base balance. Diseases of the system include infections such as pneumonia, obstructive diseases such as asthma and COPD, and lung cancer.
