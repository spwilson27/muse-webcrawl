New Caledonia (French: Nouvelle-Calédonie) is a sui generis collectivity of overseas France in the southwest Pacific Ocean, south of Vanuatu and about 1,210 km east of Australia. The archipelago includes the main island of Grande Terre, the Loyalty Islands, the Chesterfield Islands, the Belep archipelago, the Isle of Pines and a number of remote islets. Its land area is about 18,575 square kilometres, and its capital and largest city is Nouméa.

History

The islands were first settled around 1600 BC by Lapita peoples, ancestors of the indigenous Kanak people. British explorer James Cook sighted the islands in 1774 and named them New Caledonia after Caledonia, the Latin name for Scotland. France annexed the territory in 1853, initially using it partly as a penal colony. Kanak resistance culminated in the revolt of 1878. In the 1980s, conflict between pro-independence Kanaks and loyalists (the period known as Les Événements) led to the Matignon Accords of 1988 and the Nouméa Accord of 1998, which set a path toward referendums on independence. In referendums in 2018, 2020 and 2021, voters chose to remain part of France.

Geography and biodiversity

Grande Terre, the main island, is about 400 km long and is divided by a central mountain range. New Caledonia separated from the ancient supercontinent Gondwana tens of millions of years ago, and its flora and fauna evolved in long isolation; the archipelago is recognised as a biodiversity hotspot, with many endemic species, including the flightless kagu bird and extensive stands of ancient conifers such as Amborella. The barrier reef surrounding the main island forms some of the world's largest lagoon systems, listed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site in 2008.

Economy

New Caledonia holds a large share of the world's known nickel reserves — often estimated at up to a quarter of the global total — and mining and metallurgy dominate exports. Tourism and transfers from France also contribute substantially to the economy.

Demographics

The population of roughly 270,000 is composed mainly of Kanaks (the indigenous Melanesian people, about 41%), people of European descent (about 24%), and smaller communities of Wallisians, Futunans, Tahitians, Ni-Vanuatu and various Asian origins. French is the official language, alongside some 28 recognised Kanak languages.
