Health at Every Size

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Health at Every Size (HAES) is a weight-neutral approach to health and a social movement that advocates for behaviours associated with health — such as eating for wellbeing and enjoyable physical activity — rather than pursuing weight loss. It emerged from the fat-acceptance movement and was popularised by Linda Bacon's 2008 book Health at Every Size. HAES is a registered trademark of the Association for Size Diversity and Health (ASDAH), which codifies its principles.

Principles

The HAES framework, as set out by ASDAH, includes weight inclusivity (accepting that body size is not a reliable proxy for health), health enhancement, respectful care (challenging weight stigma and bias in medicine), eating for wellbeing, and life-enhancing movement. Proponents argue that dieting usually fails in the long term, harms self-esteem, and that health markers can improve with behaviour change even without weight change; they cite studies of HAES interventions showing improvements in blood pressure, lipids, and eating behaviours.

Criticism

Critics in mainstream medicine argue that HAES understates the well-established association between obesity and risks such as type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and some cancers, and that it may discourage effective weight-management treatment. The movement's evidence base — mostly small, short trials — is debated, and HAES remains a minority position relative to conventional weight-focused public health guidance.
