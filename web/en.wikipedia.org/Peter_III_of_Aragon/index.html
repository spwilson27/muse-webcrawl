Peter III, "the Great," king of Aragon and Sicily.

Peter III of Aragon (c. 1239 – 11 November 1285), known as Peter the Great (Pere el Gran in Catalan), was King of Aragon, King of Valencia, and Count of Barcelona from 1276 to 1285, and King of Sicily from 1282. The eldest son of James I the Conqueror, he expanded Aragonese power into the Mediterranean and came to dominate Sicily after the Sicilian Vespers, defeating the Angevins and a French crusade sent against him.

Early life and accession

Peter was the son of James I and his second wife, Violant of Hungary. In 1262 he married Constance, daughter and heiress of Manfred of Sicily, the Hohenstaufen king killed at Benevento in 1266 — a marriage that gave Peter his claim to Sicily. As infante he governed Catalonia, campaigned against Muslim revolts in Valencia and Murcia, and suppressed noble unrest. He succeeded his father in July 1276, inheriting Aragon, Valencia, and Catalonia (his brother James received Majorca and the Balearics).

The Sicilian Vespers and the conquest of Sicily

Peter spent his first years preparing a great fleet, officially for a crusade against Tunis. When the Sicilians rose against the Angevin Charles I at the Sicilian Vespers on Easter Monday 1282, they offered Peter the crown by right of his wife. He landed at Trapani on 30 August 1282 and was crowned in Palermo. Angevin forces were driven from the island, and Peter's admiral, Roger of Lauria, won a series of crushing naval victories over Angevin and French fleets. Pope Martin IV excommunicated Peter, declared him deposed, and granted Aragon to Charles of Valois, son of Philip III of France.

The Aragonese Crusade

In 1285 Philip III invaded Catalonia with a huge army in the so-called Aragonese Crusade. The invasion foundered on disease, supply failure, and Peter's scorched-earth resistance; Roger of Lauria destroyed the French fleet at Les Formigues, and the French army disintegrated in retreat through the Pyrenees, Philip dying at Perpignan. At home, however, Peter had been forced by war taxation to concede the Privilegio de la Unión (1283) to the Aragonese nobility, strengthening the fueros and the Cortes. He died at Vilafranca del Penedès on 11 November 1285, weeks after his victory, and was buried at the monastery of Santes Creus. Dante placed him in the Purgatorio, praising his worth among contemporary rulers.
