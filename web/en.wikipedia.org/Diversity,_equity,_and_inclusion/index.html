Diversity, equity, and inclusion

Diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) is a term used to describe organizational frameworks that seek to promote the fair treatment and full participation of all people, particularly groups who have historically been underrepresented or subject to discrimination. The three concepts together are sometimes treated as a single program under a chief diversity officer, and are related to broader practices of equal employment opportunity, accessibility and belonging.

Concepts

  • Diversity refers to the presence of differences within a setting — among them race, ethnicity, gender, age, religion, disability, sexual orientation, socioeconomic status and viewpoint.
  • Equity refers to fair and just practices and policies that ensure all individuals can thrive; it is often distinguished from equality, which provides the same resources to everyone regardless of differing needs or starting positions.
  • Inclusion refers to outcomes in which diverse individuals are welcomed, feel respected and valued, and can participate fully in decision-making.

History

Workplace diversity efforts in the United States grew out of the civil rights movement and the equal employment opportunity legislation of the 1960s and 1970s. Diversity training became common in corporations from the 1980s, and by the 2000s and 2010s many universities, companies, government agencies and nonprofits had established DEI offices and programmes. Following protests in 2020, many organizations expanded their DEI commitments, funding and staffing.

Practices

Common DEI practices include diversity training, mentorship and sponsorship programmes, employee resource groups, supplier-diversity requirements, inclusive-hiring practices, accessibility accommodations, collection of demographic data, and the creation of dedicated leadership roles. Programmes are sometimes evaluated through representation statistics and employee surveys.

Criticism and debate

DEI has been the subject of substantial public and scholarly debate. Supporters argue it remedies discrimination and improves organizational performance and legitimacy. Critics argue that some programmes are ineffective, ideological, or in tension with meritocratic selection and equal-treatment law. Studies of diversity training find mixed and often modest effects. From 2023 onward, a number of U.S. states restricted DEI offices and programmes at public universities and agencies, several corporations scaled back their initiatives, and legal challenges were brought against race-conscious policies following the Supreme Court's 2023 decisions on affirmative action in university admissions.
