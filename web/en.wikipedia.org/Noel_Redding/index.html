David Noel Redding (25 December 1945 – 11 May 2003) was an English rock and roll guitarist and bassist, best known as the bass player for the Jimi Hendrix Experience. A guitarist by trade, he was recruited in 1966 — legend has it partly on the strength of his afro hairstyle — when he auditioned for what he believed was a guitarist's job with the New Animals, and instead joined Hendrix's new band on bass.

The Jimi Hendrix Experience

With drummer Mitch Mitchell, Redding was a member of the Experience for its three studio albums: Are You Experienced (1967), Axis: Bold as Love (1967) and Electric Ladyland (1968). His songwriting and singing were featured on "Little Miss Strange" and "She's So Fine". He also played guitar in his side band Fat Mattress, which sometimes opened for the Experience. Frustrated by his limited creative role and by the chaos surrounding Hendrix, he left the group in 1969, though he briefly returned for some 1970 sessions.

Later life

Redding subsequently played with the bands Road and the Noel Redding Band, and settled in County Cork, Ireland. He spent many years in dispute over royalties and his share of the Hendrix catalogue, having signed away rights for a lump sum in the 1970s; his memoir Are You Experienced? (1990), co-written with Carol Appleby, recounts his career and grievances. He was found dead at his home in Clonakilty in May 2003. In 2022 a settlement was reported between his estate and the Hendrix estate regarding rights to the Experience recordings.
