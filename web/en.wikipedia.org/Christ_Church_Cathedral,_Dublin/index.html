Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin

Christ Church Cathedral (or, more formally, the Cathedral of the Holy Trinity) is the elder of Dublin's two medieval cathedrals, the other being St Patrick's Cathedral. It is the cathedral of the Church of Ireland Diocese of Dublin and Glendalough and is located in the heart of the city's medieval district, near the River Liffey.

Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin, with its distinctive bridge to the former Synod Hall

History

The first cathedral on the site was founded around 1028 by Sitric Silkenbeard, the Norse king of Dublin, as a wooden church. After the Anglo-Norman invasion, the stone cathedral was rebuilt beginning in the 1170s under Archbishop Lorcán Ua Tuathail (St Laurence O'Toole) and the patronage of Strongbow (Richard de Clare), whose tomb — or a later replacement monument — stands in the nave. The cathedral was extensively restored by the architect George Edmund Street in the 1870s, funded by the distiller Henry Roe, when much of its present Gothic Revival appearance was created.

Features

Notable features include the extensive 12th-century crypt — one of the largest in Britain and Ireland — the tomb of Strongbow, the heart of St Laurence O'Toole (stolen in 2012 and recovered in 2018), and the famous mummified cat and rat found in an organ pipe. A covered bridge links the cathedral to the former Synod Hall, now home to the Dublinia exhibition.
