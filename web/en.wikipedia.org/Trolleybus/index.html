A trolleybus is an electric bus that draws power from dual overhead wires (generally suspended from roadside posts) using spring-loaded trolley poles. Two wires, and two poles, are required to complete the electrical circuit, because unlike a tram the vehicle runs on rubber tyres and cannot return current through rails.

History

The first experimental trolleybus was demonstrated by Werner von Siemens in 1882. Commercial systems spread rapidly in the early 20th century; more than 800 systems have existed, though many were replaced by diesel buses after the 1950s. Roughly 300 systems remain in operation, concentrated in Europe, China, Russia and North and South America.

Advantages and disadvantages

Trolleybuses are quiet, produce no exhaust at street level and climb hills well. Their disadvantages include the cost and visual impact of overhead wiring and limited ability to deviate from wired routes, though modern vehicles carry batteries for off-wire operation.
