Capital city

A capital city (or simply capital) is the municipality holding primary status in a country, state, province, department, or other administrative region, usually as its seat of government. A capital is typically a city that physically encompasses the offices of government, including the legislature, executive residence or offices, and often the highest courts and foreign embassies. The word derives from the Latin caput, "head".

Characteristics

In many countries the capital is also the largest city and the economic and cultural centre — a so-called "primate city" such as London, Paris, or Tokyo. In others, political and economic centres are separated: Washington, D.C. versus New York City, Canberra versus Sydney, Brasília versus São Paulo, and Ottawa versus Toronto. Some states have multiple or divided capitals: South Africa splits functions among Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative), and Bloemfontein (judicial), while the Netherlands has Amsterdam as constitutional capital but The Hague as the seat of government.

Planned and relocated capitals

Governments sometimes build new capitals from scratch or relocate existing ones for strategic, political, or developmental reasons. Notable purpose-built capitals include Washington, D.C., Canberra, Brasília, Islamabad, Abuja, and Naypyidaw. Indonesia has legislated to move its capital from Jakarta to the new city of Nusantara in Borneo, and Egypt has been constructing a New Administrative Capital east of Cairo.

Historical capitals

Historically, a ruler's court was sometimes itinerant, moving between residences, and capitals have shifted with dynasties and empires — for example, China's capital moved among Xi'an, Luoyang, Kaifeng, Nanjing, and Beijing over the centuries.
