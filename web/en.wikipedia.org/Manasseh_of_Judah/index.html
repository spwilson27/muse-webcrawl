Manasseh of Judah

Reignc. 697–643 BCE
PredecessorHezekiah
SuccessorAmon

Manasseh (Hebrew: מְנַשֶּׁה) was the fourteenth king of the Kingdom of Judah, son and successor of Hezekiah. He is portrayed in the Hebrew Bible as the longest-reigning king of Judah (about 55 years) and as an idolatrous ruler who reversed his father's religious reforms.

Biblical account

According to 2 Kings 21 and 2 Chronicles 33, Manasseh rebuilt pagan altars, practised divination, and shed much innocent blood. The Book of Chronicles adds that he was taken captive to Babylon by the Assyrians, repented, and was restored — an episode associated with the Prayer of Manasseh in the Apocrypha. Neo-Assyrian records list "Menasseh, king of Judah" among tributary kings of Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal.
