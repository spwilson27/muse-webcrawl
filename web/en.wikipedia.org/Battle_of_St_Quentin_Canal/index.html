Battle of St Quentin Canal

Battle of St Quentin Canal
Date29 September – 10 October 1918
LocationNear Bellicourt and Bellenglise, France
ResultAllied victory; Hindenburg Line breached
BelligerentsBritish Empire, United States, France; German Empire
CommandersHenry Rawlinson, John Monash; German Second Army command

The Battle of St Quentin Canal was fought from 29 September 1918 during the Hundred Days Offensive of the First World War. British Fourth Army troops, including the Australian Corps and the American II Corps, assaulted the strongest sector of the German Hindenburg Line, where the defences ran along the deep St Quentin Canal.

At the northern end, British troops crossed the canal by surprise using lifebelts, ladders and boats after a heavy bombardment; in the famous attack at the Riqueval Bridge, Captain Charles Hudson's men seized the bridge intact before German demolition charges could be fired. To the south, in the battle's best-known episode, troops stormed through the canal tunnel sector at Bellicourt. Within days the supposedly impregnable Siegfried Position was broken through.

Significance

The breach of the Hindenburg Line convinced the German high command that the war could no longer be won; Ludendorff pressed for an armistice request in the battle's immediate aftermath. The iconic photograph of British troops cheering on the canal bank at Riqueval became one of the defining images of the war's final phase.
