Grand Slam (rugby union)

CompetitionSix Nations Championship (and predecessors)
AchievementWinning every match in a single championship season
Most slamsEngland (13)
Related honoursTriple Crown (Home Nations); Championship title

In rugby union, a Grand Slam is achieved when a team wins all of its matches in the Six Nations Championship (or its predecessors, the Home Championship and Five Nations) in a single season. The term, borrowed from bridge and golf, was popularised by journalists in the mid-20th century and carries no separate trophy, though it is regarded as the championship's supreme achievement.

History

England completed the first grand slam in 1913, winning the Five Nations with France included, and repeated it in 1914 and 1921. Wales dominated the early Five Nations era, while Scotland's celebrated 1925 side won a slam at the newly opened Murrayfield. France, admitted in 1910 and expelled in the 1930s, won its first slam in 1968. The championship became the Six Nations in 2000 with the admission of Italy, who have yet to win one.

Records

As of the mid-2020s, England lead with 13 grand slams, followed by Wales with 12, France with 10, Ireland with 4 and Scotland with 3. Ireland's slams came in 1948, 2009, 2018 and 2023; the 2009 achievement, their first in 61 years, was sealed by a famous 17–15 win in Cardiff decided by Ronan O'Gara's late drop goal. Back-to-back slams are rare: England (1991–92), Wales (1908–09) and France (1997–98) are among the few to manage it, and no team has won three in a row.

Touring Grand Slams

The term is also used for a touring side that defeats all four Home Nations (England, Ireland, Scotland and Wales) on a single tour. South Africa achieved this four times (1912–13, 1931–32, 1951–52, 1960–61), New Zealand four times (1978, 2005, 2008, 2010) and Australia once (1984, the celebrated side captained by David Campese's generation and coached by Alan Jones).
