Harold Stephen Black

Harold Stephen Black (April 14, 1898 – December 11, 1983) was an American electrical engineer who invented the negative feedback amplifier in 1927, one of the foundational ideas of modern electronics and control theory.

Harold Stephen Black
Born: April 14, 1898, Leominster, Massachusetts
Died: December 11, 1983
Employer: Bell Telephone Laboratories
Known for: Negative feedback amplifier

Invention of negative feedback

Black conceived negative feedback while commuting on the Lackawanna Ferry across the Hudson River on August 2, 1927. Working at Bell Laboratories on the problem of distortion in repeater amplifiers for long-distance telephone lines, he realised that feeding a portion of the output signal back to the input with opposite phase could greatly reduce distortion, at the cost of gain — a trade that could be repaid with additional amplification stages. His patent application, filed in 1928, was not granted until 1937 because the concept seemed so counterintuitive.

Later work

Black also worked on pulse-code modulation and wrote the influential book Modulation Theory (1953). He received numerous honours, including induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame.
