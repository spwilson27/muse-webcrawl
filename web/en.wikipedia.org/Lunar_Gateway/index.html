The Lunar Gateway, or simply Gateway, is a planned space station in lunar orbit intended to serve as a solar-powered communication hub, science laboratory, and short-term habitation module for government astronauts, as well as a holding area for rovers and other robots. It is a multinational collaborative project involving the space agencies of the United States (NASA), Europe (ESA), Japan (JAXA), and Canada (CSA), with the Mohammed Bin Rashid Space Centre (MBRSC) of the United Arab Emirates providing an airlock.

Artist's concept of the Gateway with its initial elements in lunar orbit.

Planned elements

The station is to be assembled from modules launched separately. The first two elements—the Power and Propulsion Element (PPE), built by Maxar, and the Habitation and Logistics Outpost (HALO), built by Northrop Grumman—are planned to launch together on a Falcon Heavy rocket and spend about a year travelling to lunar orbit. Later elements include the European System Providing Refuelling, Infrastructure and Telecommunications (ESPRIT), the International Habitation Module (I-HAB), the Canadarm3 robotic arm, and the Crew and Science Airlock provided by the UAE.

Orbit

Gateway will occupy a near-rectilinear halo orbit (NRHO) with a period of about seven days, bringing the station within roughly 3,000 km of the lunar north pole at closest approach and out to about 70,000 km over the south pole. The orbit offers near-continuous communication with Earth and access to lunar polar regions of interest for the Artemis programme.

Role in Artemis

Gateway is designed to support crewed missions to the lunar surface under NASA's Artemis programme, serving as a rendezvous point between the Orion spacecraft and human landing systems, and later as a staging point for missions deeper into the Solar System.
