Acetaldehyde (IUPAC systematic name ethanal) is an organic chemical compound with the formula CH3CHO. It is one of the most important aldehydes, occurring widely in nature and produced on a large scale in industry. It is a colourless liquid with a pungent, suffocating odour at high concentration and a fruity smell when dilute.

Occurrence and production

Acetaldehyde occurs naturally in coffee, bread and ripe fruit, and is produced by plants and by yeast during alcoholic fermentation. Industrially it has been made by hydration of acetylene, by oxidation of ethanol, and — most importantly since the 1960s — by the Wacker process, the palladium(II)-catalysed oxidation of ethylene. It is mainly a chemical intermediate: major uses include the production of acetic acid, pyridine bases, pentaerythritol and perfumery chemicals.

Biochemistry and health

In the human body, ethanol is first metabolised to acetaldehyde by alcohol dehydrogenase; acetaldehyde is then converted to acetate by aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH). Acetaldehyde is more toxic than ethanol itself and is responsible for many hangover symptoms and for alcohol flush reaction in people with deficient ALDH2, common in East Asian populations. Acetaldehyde in alcoholic beverages and formed from drinking alcohol is classified by the IARC as a Group 1 carcinogen; acetaldehyde itself is Group 2B.
