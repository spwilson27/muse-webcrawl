Bluenose was a fishing and racing schooner built in 1921 in Lunenburg, Nova Scotia, Canada. A celebrated racing ship and Canadian icon, she came to symbolize the province of Nova Scotia and later appeared on the Canadian dime. Under Captain Angus Walters, Bluenose was undefeated in the International Fisherman's Trophy races against American schooners, winning the trophy in 1921, 1922, 1923, 1931, and 1938.

Designed by William James Roué, Bluenose was built to combine speed with the working requirements of a Grand Banks fishing schooner. She was 161 feet long overall and carried over 10,000 square feet of sail.

Later years and legacy

After her racing career, Bluenose was sold as a freighter in the Caribbean and was wrecked on a reef near Haiti in 1946. Her image was placed on the Canadian ten-cent piece in 1937 and remains there today; "Bluenose" also survives as a nickname for Nova Scotians. A replica, Bluenose II, was launched in 1963 and serves as a sailing ambassador for Nova Scotia.
