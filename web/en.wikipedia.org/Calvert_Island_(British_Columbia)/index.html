Calvert Island is an island on the Central Coast of British Columbia, Canada, located in the Queen Charlotte Sound region between Vancouver Island and the mainland. It lies within the traditional territories of the Heiltsuk and Wuikinuxv Nations.

Geography

The island is roughly 32 kilometres long and up to 15 kilometres wide. Fitz Hugh Sound separates it from the mainland to the east, while Hakai Passage separates it from Hunter Island to the north. Its terrain is rugged and largely covered by temperate rainforest, with Mount Buxton its highest peak.

History and research

Archaeological evidence shows human occupation of the Calvert Island area stretching back more than 13,000 years; ancient footprints preserved in clay on the island were reported by researchers in the 2010s. The island is home to the Hakai Institute's Calvert Island Ecological Observatory, a research station supporting studies of coastal ecosystems, salmon and climate change. Much of the island and surrounding waters are protected within the Hakai Luxvbalis Conservancy, one of the largest provincial conservancies in British Columbia.
