Politburo of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union

Members of the Politburo reviewing a parade from atop the Lenin Mausoleum.

The Politburo (Russian: Политбюро, short for "Political Bureau") of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union (CPSU) was the highest policy-making authority within the party and, in practice, the supreme governing body of the Soviet Union. Formally elected by the Central Committee, it directed the work of the party between Central Committee plenums.

History

The first Politburo was established in October 1917, on the eve of the October Revolution, with seven members including Vladimir Lenin, Leon Trotsky, and Joseph Stalin. It became a permanent institution in 1919. Under Stalin, who as General Secretary controlled appointments, the Politburo's collective role was subordinated to his personal dictatorship, and many members perished in the Great Purge.

From 1952 to 1966 the body was renamed the Presidium of the Central Committee, reverting to Politburo under Leonid Brezhnev. In its late form it comprised roughly a dozen full (voting) members and several candidate (non-voting) members, drawn from the top party and state leadership: the General Secretary, the head of government, the foreign and defence ministers, the KGB chairman, and key regional party bosses.

Role and end

The Politburo set policy across every sphere — economy, foreign affairs, defence, and ideology — and its decisions were implemented by the state apparatus. Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms after 1985 gradually shifted power to new state institutions; the Politburo was abolished along with the CPSU in 1991, in the final months of the USSR.
