Tikhonov regularization

Tikhonov regularization, named after the Soviet mathematician Andrey Tikhonov, is a method of stabilizing the solutions of ill-posed inverse problems by adding a penalty term that expresses a preference for solutions with desirable properties. It is one of the most widely used regularization techniques in applied mathematics, statistics and machine learning.

Formulation

For a linear problem Ax = b whose solution is unstable — small errors in b producing huge changes in x — Tikhonov regularization replaces the least-squares problem of minimising ‖Ax − b‖² with the minimisation of

‖Ax − b‖² + λ²‖Γx‖²

where λ > 0 is the regularization parameter balancing fidelity to the data against the penalty, and Γ (often the identity, or a derivative operator) encodes the preferred structure. The solution satisfies the modified normal equations (AᵀA + λ²ΓᵀΓ)x = Aᵀb. When Γ is the identity matrix this is exactly ridge regression in statistics.

Choosing the parameter

The success of the method depends on the choice of λ: too small leaves the solution noisy and unstable; too large oversmooths and biases it. Standard selection methods include the discrepancy principle (choose λ so the residual matches the known noise level), generalised cross-validation, and the L-curve criterion, which picks the corner of a log–log plot of solution norm against residual norm. Tikhonov regularization also has a Bayesian interpretation: the penalty corresponds to a Gaussian prior on the solution.

Applications

The method is used in image deblurring and computed tomography, geophysical inversion, numerical differentiation of noisy data, ill-conditioned least squares, and as a weight-decay penalty (L2 regularization) in statistical learning and neural networks.
