Brighton & Hove Albion Football Club, commonly referred to simply as Brighton, is a professional football club based in the city of Brighton and Hove, East Sussex, England. The club competes in the Premier League, the top tier of English football. Nicknamed "the Seagulls", the club's traditional colours are blue and white stripes.

History

Founded in 1901, Brighton played in the Southern League before being elected to the Football League in 1920. The club enjoyed its first sustained period in the top flight between 1979 and 1983, and in 1983 reached the FA Cup Final, drawing 2–2 with Manchester United before losing the replay 4–0; the final is remembered for Gordon Smith's late chance — "and Smith must score" — in the first match.

The 1990s brought near-disaster: financial problems led to the sale of the Goldstone Ground in 1997, and the club came within one match of relegation from the Football League the same year. Brighton ground-shared at Gillingham and then played at the Withdean Stadium before moving in 2011 to the new Falmer Stadium, known as the Amex, built under chairman Tony Bloom, who had taken over in 2009.

Brighton won promotion to the Premier League in 2017 under Chris Hughton. Under Graham Potter and then Roberto De Zerbi the club established itself in the top half, finishing sixth in 2022–23 — its highest-ever league position — and qualifying for European competition (the UEFA Europa League) for the first time in its history.

Stadium

The club plays at Falmer Stadium, sponsored as the American Express Stadium ("the Amex"), a roughly 31,000-capacity all-seater stadium in Falmer, north-east of Brighton, opened in 2011.
