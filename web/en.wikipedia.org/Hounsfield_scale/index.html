Hounsfield scale

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hounsfield scale, named after Sir Godfrey Hounsfield, is a quantitative scale for describing radiodensity in computed tomography (CT). It is expressed in Hounsfield units (HU), a linear transformation of the measured X-ray attenuation coefficient relative to distilled water at standard pressure and temperature, which is defined as 0 HU, while the radiodensity of air is defined as −1000 HU.

[Image: CT scan with regions of interest showing Hounsfield unit values]

Definition

The Hounsfield unit is calculated as HU = 1000 × (μ − μwater) / (μwater − μair), where μ is the linear attenuation coefficient of the material in question. A change of one Hounsfield unit represents a 0.1 per cent change in attenuation relative to water.

Clinical use

Hounsfield measurements are routinely used to characterise tissues and lesions: for example, distinguishing cysts from solid masses, detecting fat within adrenal adenomas, assessing bone density, and identifying acute haemorrhage, which typically measures 60–80 HU.
