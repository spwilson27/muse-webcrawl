Standard hydrogen electrode

The standard hydrogen electrode (abbreviated SHE), is a redox electrode which forms the basis of the thermodynamic scale of oxidation-reduction potentials. Its absolute electrode potential is estimated to be 4.44 ± 0.02 V at 25 °C, but for the purpose of providing a basis for comparison with all other electrochemical reactions, hydrogen's standard electrode potential (E°) is declared to be zero volts at any temperature.

Construction

The SHE consists of a platinum electrode immersed in a solution with a hydrogen ion activity of 1, with hydrogen gas at a pressure of 1 bar bubbled over the platinum surface. The platinum is platinized (coated with platinum black) to increase its surface area and catalyze the reversible reaction 2H+(aq) + 2e− ⇌ H2(g). Because a true SHE is awkward to use in the laboratory, practical measurements are made with secondary reference electrodes such as the silver/silver chloride or saturated calomel electrode, whose potentials relative to the SHE are well established.

Relation to standard potentials

All standard electrode potentials are reported on the SHE scale. The standard cell potential of a reaction is computed from the difference between the electrode potentials of the half-cells combined with a SHE. The SHE should not be confused with the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE), which adjusts for the pH of the solution in use, or the normal hydrogen electrode (NHE), an older term defined with a 1 N acid solution.
