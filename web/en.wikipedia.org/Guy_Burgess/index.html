Guy Francis de Moncy Burgess (16 April 1911 – 30 August 1963) was a British diplomat and Soviet double agent, and a member of the Cambridge Five spy ring that fed British and American secrets to the Soviet Union during and after World War II.

Recruitment and career

Educated at Eton and Trinity College, Cambridge, Burgess embraced communism as an undergraduate and was recruited by Soviet intelligence in the mid-1930s. He worked for the BBC, briefly for MI6's Section D, and then the Foreign Office, using his charm and connections to pass large quantities of classified documents to his handlers, at one point while serving in Washington, D.C.

Defection

In May 1951, warned that fellow spy Donald Maclean was about to be arrested, Burgess fled with him to the Soviet Union, an escape arranged by the ring's members and facilitated by Kim Philby's tip-off. The defection caused a major scandal in Britain and badly damaged Anglo-American intelligence relations. Burgess surfaced with Maclean at a Moscow press conference in 1956.

Later life

Burgess never adapted to life in the USSR; heavy drinking ruined his health, and he died of acute liver failure in Moscow in 1963, aged 52. His ashes were returned to England. He has been portrayed in numerous books and dramas, including Alan Bennett's An Englishman Abroad, based on actress Coral Browne's encounter with him in Moscow.
