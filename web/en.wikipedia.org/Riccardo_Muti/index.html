Riccardo Muti

Riccardo Muti (born 28 July 1941) is an Italian conductor. He is the music director emeritus of the Chicago Symphony Orchestra and has held leading posts with the Philharmonia Orchestra, the Philadelphia Orchestra, La Scala in Milan, and the Orchestra Giovanile Luigi Cherubini, which he founded.

Career

Muti was born in Naples and studied piano at the Conservatory of San Pietro a Majella before turning to composition and conducting in Milan. He came to attention in 1967 by winning the Guido Cantelli Conducting Prize. He became principal conductor of the Maggio Musicale Fiorentino and then of London's Philharmonia Orchestra (1973–1982). From 1980 to 1992 he succeeded Eugene Ormandy as music director of the Philadelphia Orchestra.

In 1986 he became music director of La Scala, Milan, a post he held until his resignation in 2005 amid disputes with the theatre's management and staff. From 2010 to 2023 he was music director of the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, thereafter becoming its music director emeritus for life. He has conducted the Vienna Philharmonic's New Year's Concert a record number of times and is closely associated with the Salzburg Festival and with Verdi's operas.

Style and honours

Muti is known for fidelity to composers' scores and for championing restored, uncut versions of Verdi operas. He has received numerous honours, including the Birgit Nilsson Prize and appointment as honorary Knight Commander of the Order of the British Empire.
