Quality assurance

Quality inspection on a production line

Quality assurance (QA) is the term used in both manufacturing and service industries to describe the systematic efforts taken to assure that the product delivered to the customer meets the agreed requirements. A quality assurance system is said to increase customer confidence and a company's credibility, improve work processes and efficiency, and enable a company to compete more effectively.

QA versus quality control

Quality assurance is process-oriented and preventive: it aims to build quality into the way work is done so that defects do not occur. Quality control (QC), by contrast, is product-oriented and detective: it inspects outputs to identify and reject defects. The concept developed from wartime industrial inspection and statistical process control, through the work of figures such as Walter Shewhart, W. Edwards Deming and Joseph Juran, into broader frameworks of total quality management.

Methods and standards

Modern QA practice includes process documentation and audits under the ISO 9000 family of standards, failure testing and statistical sampling, capability maturity models in software, and continuous-improvement cycles such as PDCA (plan–do–check–act). In software development, QA encompasses code review, automated testing, and release management; in regulated industries such as pharmaceuticals, QA operates within formal good-manufacturing-practice (GMP) rules.
