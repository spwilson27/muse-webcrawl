Lake Conway is a 6,700-acre (27 km²) lake in Faulkner County, Arkansas, United States, and the largest reservoir ever made by a state wildlife commission. It was created by the Arkansas Game and Fish Commission in 1948 by damming Palarm Creek near Conway, and was formally opened in 1951.

Fishing

The lake is famous for sport fishing, particularly for largemouth bass, crappie, bream, and catfish. It is one of the most popular fishing lakes in central Arkansas and is known for its cypress trees and stumps, which provide fish habitat but require careful navigation.

2023 oil spill

In March 2023, a pipeline rupture near the lake spilled diesel and crude oil into a cove and wetlands, prompting a cleanup response and temporary closures; state and federal agencies monitored effects on wildlife and fish.

Renovation

The Arkansas Game and Fish Commission undertook a major renovation of the lake beginning in 2023, including draining the lake to repair the dam and improve fish habitat, with refilling and restocking planned afterwards.
