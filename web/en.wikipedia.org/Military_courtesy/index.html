Military courtesy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Military courtesy is the body of customs, gestures, and forms of address by which members of the armed forces show respect to one another, to superiors, and to symbols of national authority. Though often grouped with military discipline, courtesy is traditionally distinguished from mere obedience: it is a voluntary expression of mutual respect intended to build cohesion and esprit de corps.

Forms

The best-known act of military courtesy is the salute, rendered by bringing the hand to the headgear or brow when meeting an officer outdoors or on formal occasions. Other courtesies include standing at attention when a senior officer enters, addressing superiors as "sir" or "ma'am", using rank titles, observing precedence in seating and movement, and rendering honours to the national flag and anthem, to colours and standards, and to passing funeral processions. Naval services maintain additional customs such as piping officers aboard ship and saluting the quarterdeck.

Purpose and teaching

Courtesies are taught from the first days of recruit training. Military manuals describe them not as servility but as the outward sign of an inward discipline: a junior who renders a smart salute acknowledges the authority of rank, while the officer who returns it acknowledges the dignity of the subordinate. Courtesy also extends between allies, and protocols govern exchanges between services of different nations.
