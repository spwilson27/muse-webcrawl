The University of Michigan (U-M, UMich, or Michigan) is a public research university in Ann Arbor, Michigan, United States. Founded in 1817 in Detroit as the Catholepistemiad and moved to Ann Arbor in 1837, it is one of the oldest universities in the United States and is consistently ranked among the top public universities in the world. It is a founding member of the Association of American Universities.

History

The university was established by an act of the Michigan Territory in 1817, championed by Judge Augustus Woodward, Father Gabriel Richard and the Reverend John Monteith. It moved to Ann Arbor in 1837 onto 40 acres of land granted by the Treaty of Fort Meigs parties. The university admitted women from 1870, among the earliest major American universities to do so. In the twentieth century Michigan grew into a research powerhouse, and its affirmative action policies were the subject of the landmark U.S. Supreme Court cases Grutter v. Bollinger and Gratz v. Bollinger (2003), and later Students for Fair Admissions litigation reshaped admissions nationally in 2023.

Academics

Michigan comprises 19 schools and colleges, including the College of Literature, Science, and the Arts, the College of Engineering, the Ross School of Business, the Law School, and the Medical School. It awards more than 250 undergraduate degree programmes and spends over $1.7 billion annually on research, one of the largest research budgets of any American university. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity."

Campus and student life

The Ann Arbor campus spans Central, North, Medical and South campuses, connected by bus. Landmarks include the Law Quadrangle, the Burton Memorial Tower, Hill Auditorium and Michigan Stadium — "the Big House" — the largest stadium in the United States, with an official capacity of 107,601. The Michigan Wolverines compete in the NCAA Division I Big Ten Conference; the football team's rivalry with Ohio State is one of the most famous in American sport. The university's fight song is "The Victors."

Notable alumni

Alumni include U.S. President Gerald Ford, Google co-founder Larry Page, playwright Arthur Miller, astronaut David Scott, and Madonna (who attended but did not graduate). Faculty and alumni include numerous Nobel laureates, Pulitzer Prize winners, and a Fields Medalist.
