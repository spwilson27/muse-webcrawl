Hybrid Theory is the debut studio album by the American rock band Linkin Park, released on 24 October 2000 by Warner Bros. Records. Recorded at NRG Recordings in North Hollywood, California, and produced by Don Gilmore, the album's lyrical themes deal with the personal problems experienced by vocalist Chester Bennington during his adolescence, including drug abuse and the constant fighting and divorce of his parents.

Background and recording

Linkin Park formed in 1996 in Agoura Hills, California, originally under the name Xero. After recruiting vocalist Chester Bennington in 1999 and changing their name first to Hybrid Theory and then to Linkin Park, the band signed with Warner Bros. Records. The album title was drawn from the band's former name and reflects the mixture of styles — metal, hip hop, electronic, and pop — that characterises the record.

Singles and reception

Four singles were released: "One Step Closer", "Crawling", "Papercut", and "In the End". "In the End" reached number two on the Billboard Hot 100 and became one of the most recognisable songs of the decade. "Crawling" won the Grammy Award for Best Hard Rock Performance in 2002. The album received generally positive reviews, with critics praising its blend of rap, metal, and electronic elements, although some criticised it as formulaic.

Commercial performance and legacy

Hybrid Theory peaked at number two on the Billboard 200 and was the best-selling album of 2001 in the United States. It has sold more than 30 million copies worldwide, making it one of the best-selling debut albums of all time, and it was certified Diamond (12× platinum) in the United States. The album is widely credited with popularising nu metal and is frequently ranked among the greatest albums of the 2000s. A 20th anniversary edition was released in 2020.
