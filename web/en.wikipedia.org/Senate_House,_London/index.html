Senate House, London

Senate House
Location: Malet Street, Bloomsbury, London
Architect: Charles Holden
Built: 1932–1937
Height: 64 m (210 ft), 19 storeys
Listed: Grade II*

Senate House is the administrative centre of the University of London, in Bloomsbury, central London. Designed by Charles Holden and built between 1932 and 1937 in a monumental stripped-classical style faced with Portland stone, its 19-storey tower was for years one of London's tallest secular buildings. It houses the Senate House Library and the university's central administration.

History and cultural references

The building was funded in part by a bequest from Henry Thomas Wills. During the Second World War it housed the Ministry of Information, an experience that helped inspire George Orwell's depiction of the Ministry of Truth in the novel Nineteen Eighty-Four; Orwell's wife worked in the building. Its imposing architecture has made it a frequent film and television location.

The Library

Senate House Library is one of the largest academic libraries in the United Kingdom, with particular strengths in the arts, humanities and social sciences and extensive special collections and archives.
