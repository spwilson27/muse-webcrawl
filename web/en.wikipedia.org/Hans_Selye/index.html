Hans Selye

Hans Selye (26 January 1907 – 16 October 1982) was a Hungarian-Canadian endocrinologist who pioneered research on the biological concept of stress and formulated the general adaptation syndrome.

Research

Working at McGill University and the Université de Montréal, Selye found in the 1930s that diverse harmful agents produced a consistent pattern of physiological responses in laboratory animals: enlargement of the adrenal cortex, shrinkage of lymphatic organs, and gastrointestinal ulcers.

General adaptation syndrome

He described three stages — alarm, resistance, and exhaustion — and popularised the term stress in its biological sense in works such as The Stress of Life (1956). His work profoundly influenced medicine and psychology. See also Stress.
