Theodor W. Adorno

Theodor W. Adorno (11 September 1903 – 6 August 1969), born Theodor Ludwig Wiesengrund, was a German philosopher, sociologist, psychologist, musicologist, and composer, a leading member of the Frankfurt School of critical theory.

Work

With Max Horkheimer he wrote Dialectic of Enlightenment (1947), arguing that Enlightenment reason had turned into a new mythology and domination, and coining the critique of the "culture industry". His other major works include Minima Moralia (1951), Negative Dialectics (1966), and the posthumous Aesthetic Theory.

Life

Adorno fled Nazi Germany, working in Oxford, New York, and Los Angeles, where he contributed to The Authoritarian Personality (1950). He returned to Frankfurt in 1949, directing the Institute for Social Research until his death in 1969, shortly after student unrest in which he became a controversial figure.
