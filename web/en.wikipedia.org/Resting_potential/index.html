Resting potential

The resting potential is the electrical potential difference across the plasma membrane of an unstimulated cell. In a typical neuron it is approximately −70 millivolts, meaning that the inside of the cell is negative relative to the outside. It provides the baseline from which action potentials and other electrical signals develop.

Ionic basis

Intracellular fluid contains a high concentration of potassium ions and negatively charged proteins, while extracellular fluid contains more sodium and chloride. The sodium–potassium pump transports three sodium ions out of the cell and two potassium ions in for each ATP hydrolysed. This maintains concentration gradients and contributes a small direct electrogenic effect.

Potassium conductance

At rest, the membrane is much more permeable to potassium than to sodium because many potassium leak channels are open. Potassium tends to diffuse out down its concentration gradient, leaving unbalanced negative charge inside. The resulting electrical gradient pulls potassium back in. Equilibrium would occur near potassium's equilibrium potential, about −90 mV, but limited sodium influx and other ionic currents make the actual resting potential less negative.

The Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz equation combines the concentrations and relative permeabilities of potassium, sodium, and chloride.

Function

The resting potential stores energy in ionic gradients and allows excitable cells to respond rapidly when voltage-gated channels open. Changes in extracellular potassium, channel function, or pump activity can alter excitability. All living cells have membrane potentials, though the value and physiological role differ among neurons, muscle fibres, plant cells, and other cell types.

Measurement

Resting potential can be measured with a microelectrode inserted into a cell or estimated using patch-clamp methods and voltage-sensitive dyes. The value is not completely static: synaptic input, metabolic state, and ion-channel modulation continuously perturb it around a resting range.
