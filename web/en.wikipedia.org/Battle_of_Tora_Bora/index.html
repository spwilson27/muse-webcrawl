The Battle of Tora Bora was a military engagement that took place in the cave complex of Tora Bora, eastern Afghanistan, from 6 to 17 December 2001, during the opening stages of the United States invasion of Afghanistan. The U.S. and its Afghan allies sought to capture or kill al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden, who was believed to be hiding in the mountains near the Pakistani border.

Background

After the fall of Kabul and Kandahar, intelligence indicated bin Laden had retreated to the fortified cave positions of Tora Bora, developed during the Soviet–Afghan War. The U.S. relied principally on massive airpower — including B-52 strikes and BLU-82 "daisy cutter" bombs — and on Afghan militia ground forces, with only a small number of U.S. special operations troops on the ground.

The battle

Afghan militias advanced slowly against entrenched al-Qaeda fighters, and a negotiated local ceasefire on 11–12 December gave many fighters time to slip away. By the time the caves were overrun on 17 December, bin Laden had escaped, most likely across the border into Pakistan's tribal areas.

Aftermath

The failure to capture bin Laden at Tora Bora became one of the most debated episodes of the war; a 2009 U.S. Senate report concluded that the decision not to commit sufficient U.S. ground troops allowed bin Laden to escape and prolong the conflict. He remained at large until killed by U.S. forces in Abbottabad, Pakistan, in May 2011.
