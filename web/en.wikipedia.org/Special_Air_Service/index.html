Special Air Service

The Special Air Service (SAS) is a special forces unit of the British Army. It was founded as a regiment in 1941 by David Stirling, and in 1950 it was reconstituted as a corps. The unit specialises in a number of roles including counter-terrorism, hostage rescue, direct action and special reconnaissance. Much of the information about the SAS is highly classified, and the unit is not commented on by either the British government or the Ministry of Defence due to the secrecy and sensitivity of its operations.

History

The Special Air Service was founded in North Africa in July 1941 by David Stirling, then a lieutenant serving with No. 8 Commando. Stirling conceived of a small unit that could operate behind enemy lines, attacking airfields and supply depots. The first successful raids destroyed dozens of Axis aircraft on the ground. After the Second World War the regiment was disbanded, but it was re-formed during the Malayan Emergency and later served in Oman, Borneo, Aden and Northern Ireland.

Iranian Embassy siege

The SAS came to public prominence during the Iranian Embassy siege in London in 1980, when members of the regiment's Counter Revolutionary Warfare wing abseiled from the roof of the embassy and stormed the building live on television, rescuing all but one of the hostages.

Organisation

The regular regiment, 22 SAS, is based at Stirling Lines in Credenhill, Herefordshire. Two reserve regiments, 21 SAS and 23 SAS, form part of United Kingdom Special Forces. Selection for the SAS is considered among the most demanding military training programmes in the world, with a famously low pass rate.
