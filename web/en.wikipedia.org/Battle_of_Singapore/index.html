The Battle of Singapore, also known as the Fall of Singapore, was fought in the South-East Asian theatre of the Second World War from 8 to 15 February 1942. Japanese forces under General Tomoyuki Yamashita captured the British stronghold of Singapore; the surrender of some 80,000 British, Indian and Australian troops was the largest capitulation of British-led forces in history. Prime Minister Winston Churchill called it the "worst disaster" in British military history.

Background

Japanese forces had invaded Malaya in December 1941 and advanced rapidly down the peninsula, using bicycles, light tanks and air superiority, while the sinking of the battleship Prince of Wales and battlecruiser Repulse removed British naval power. By late January 1942 the defenders had withdrawn across the Johore Strait onto Singapore Island.

The battle

On the night of 8–9 February, Japanese troops crossed the strait and landed on Singapore's northwest coast, where defences were thin. Despite local counterattacks, the invaders seized the reservoirs and high ground; with water supplies failing and the city crowded with refugees, Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival surrendered on 15 February.

Aftermath

Singapore was renamed Syonan-to and occupied until 1945. Prisoners were sent to Changi Prison and many were later worked to death on projects such as the Burma Railway. Massacres of ethnic Chinese civilians (the Sook Ching) followed the occupation. The defeat shattered the prestige of the British Empire in Asia.
