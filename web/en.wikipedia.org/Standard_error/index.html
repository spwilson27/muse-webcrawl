Standard error

DefinitionStandard deviation of a statistic's sampling distribution
For the meanSE = σ / √n
UsesConfidence intervals, hypothesis tests, error bars

The standard error (SE) of a statistic is the standard deviation of its sampling distribution — the distribution of values the statistic would take over repeated samples from the same population. It quantifies the precision with which the statistic estimates the corresponding population parameter.

Standard error of the mean

For a sample mean based on n independent observations from a population with standard deviation σ, the standard error is σ/√n. Because σ is usually unknown, it is estimated using the sample standard deviation s, giving s/√n. The key implication is that quadrupling the sample size halves the standard error.

Interpretation

The standard error underlies confidence intervals (estimate ± a multiple of the SE) and test statistics in hypothesis testing. On graphs, error bars frequently represent the standard error of the mean, although the standard deviation and the confidence interval are alternatives that must not be confused with it: the standard deviation describes the spread of the data, while the standard error describes the precision of the mean.

Other statistics

Analogous standard errors exist for proportions (√[p(1−p)/n]), regression coefficients, medians and other estimators, derived analytically or by resampling methods such as the bootstrap. In finite populations a correction factor may be applied.
