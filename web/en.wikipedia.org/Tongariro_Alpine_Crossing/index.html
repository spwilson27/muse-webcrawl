Tongariro Alpine Crossing

Length19.4 km (one way)
LocationTongariro National Park, New Zealand
Highest point1,886 m (Red Crater)

The Tongariro Alpine Crossing is a tramping track in Tongariro National Park on New Zealand's North Island, widely regarded as one of the finest one-day walks in the world. The 19.4-kilometre traverse passes between the volcanoes Mount Tongariro and Mount Ngauruhoe through a stark volcanic landscape.

Route

Starting at Mangatepopo, the track climbs past Soda Springs and up to the South Crater, then to the summit of Red Crater (1,886 m), the route's highest point. Walkers descend past the vividly coloured Emerald Lakes and the Blue Lake before zigzagging down through tussock and native forest to Ketetahi. Side trips include the summit of Mount Ngauruhoe, though Ngāti Hikairo asks walkers not to climb it out of respect for its sacred status.

Popularity and management

The crossing draws tens of thousands of walkers each summer; a booking system now manages numbers. Alpine weather changes rapidly, and the Department of Conservation advises that the track is a serious undertaking in poor conditions.
