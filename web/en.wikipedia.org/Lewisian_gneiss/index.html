Lewisian gneiss

The Lewisian gneiss, or Lewisian complex, consists of Precambrian metamorphic rocks that outcrop in the northwestern part of Scotland, forming part of the Hebridean terrane and the North Atlantic Craton. These rocks are of Archaean and Paleoproterozoic age, ranging from about 3 billion to 1.7 billion years old, and are among the oldest rocks in Europe. The Lewisian is named after the Isle of Lewis in the Outer Hebrides, where exposures are extensive. It forms the basement beneath the younger Torridonian sandstones and Cambrian sediments of the Northwest Highlands and underlies much of the Outer Hebrides and the far northwest mainland coast.

Distribution

On the mainland, the Lewisian forms the foreland west of the Moine Thrust Belt, from Cape Wrath and Sutherland down through Wester Ross (Gairloch, Scourie, Lochinver) to the Applecross peninsula. It also outcrops in the Outer Hebrides from Lewis to Barra, and in inliers further east within the Moine rocks. The complex is divided regionally into a central granulite-facies block around Scourie flanked by amphibolite-facies regions.

Geological history

The history of the complex is conventionally divided into events: the Scourian (c. 3.0–2.5 Ga), when the original igneous protoliths were emplaced and subjected to granulite-facies metamorphism in the deep crust; the Inverian, a period of shearing and retrogression; intrusion of the Scourie dykes (c. 2.4–2.0 Ga), a major swarm of basic dykes used as time markers; and the Laxfordian (c. 1.9–1.7 Ga), a Paleoproterozoic orogenic reworking with folding, amphibolite-facies metamorphism and granitic intrusion, especially intense near Laxford. Later still, the Lewisian provided the floor on which the Mesoproterozoic Stoer Group and Neoproterozoic Torridon Group (Torridonian) sandstones were deposited unconformably.

Significance

The banded, swirling grey-and-pink gneisses give the northwest Scottish landscape much of its character, forming low, ice-scoured "knock and lochan" terrain. Scientifically, the Lewisian is a classic natural laboratory for the deep-crustal processes of early Earth and for unravelling polyphase deformation, and its radiometric dating was foundational to Precambrian geochronology in Britain.
