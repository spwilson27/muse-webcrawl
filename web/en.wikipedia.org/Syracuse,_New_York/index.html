Syracuse, New York

Syracuse is a city in and the county seat of Onondaga County, in the US state of New York. With a population of 148,620 at the 2020 census, it is the fifth-most populous city in the state and the economic and educational hub of Central New York, a region of over a million people.

History

The area was the homeland of the Onondaga Nation, whose capital remains nearby. French missionaries and traders arrived in the 1650s. After the American Revolution, settlers exploited the brine springs around Onondaga Lake, and Syracuse — named in 1820 after Syracuse, Sicily, another salt-producing city on a harbour — became a major salt producer, earning the nickname “The Salt City”. The Erie Canal (1825) and later railroads made it a crossroads; today Interstates 81 and 90 meet in the city. Industry shifted over time from salt to chemicals, typewriters, and air conditioning (Carrier Corporation was founded there), followed by long post-industrial decline and population loss.

Education and culture

Syracuse University, founded in 1870, is the city's largest institution, known for the Maxwell School, the Newhouse School of communications, and its Orange sports teams, whose home is one of the largest domed stadiums on a US campus. Other institutions include SUNY Upstate Medical University and Le Moyne College. The city hosts the Great New York State Fair, the oldest and one of the largest state fairs in the country, and is notorious for its snowy winters, averaging over 120 inches of lake-effect snow a year.
