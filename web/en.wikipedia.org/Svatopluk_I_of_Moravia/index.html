Svatopluk I of Moravia

Svatopluk I or Svätopluk I, also known as Svatopluk the Great (Latin: Zentibald or Sventopolk; c. 840 – 894), was a ruler of Great Moravia, which attained its greatest territorial extent during his reign, from 870–871 and again from 871 until his death in 894. He is remembered in Slovak tradition as a national hero and is sometimes styled "King of the Slovaks" in later legend.

Rise to power

Svatopluk was a nephew of Prince Rastislav, under whom he governed the Principality of Nitra as a vassal. In 870 he handed Rastislav over to the Franks of Louis the German, and Rastislav was blinded and imprisoned. Svatopluk himself was soon accused of treason and imprisoned by the Franks, but a Moravian revolt led by the priest Slavomir forced his release, and he turned on the Frankish garrisons and expelled them.

Reign

In 874 Svatopluk concluded the Peace of Forchheim with Louis the German, accepting a loose Frankish overlordship in return for peace. Thereafter he greatly expanded Moravian power, bringing Bohemia, Silesia, parts of Pannonia and other Slavic territories under his authority. He supported the Slavonic liturgy of the disciples of Cyril and Methodius for much of his reign, though after Methodius's death in 885 he allowed the Latin clergy to expel Methodius's followers.

Svatopluk's wars with Arnulf of Carinthia in his later years weakened the realm, and in 894 he died — according to legend, after being thrown from his horse while hunting. His sons Mojmir II and Svatopluk II quarrelled over the succession, hastening Great Moravia's collapse before the Hungarian invasions of the following decade.

Legacy

Svatopluk features prominently in Slovak national mythology, including the legend of the three rods — a parable of unity he is said to have taught his sons on his deathbed. A monumental equestrian statue of Svatopluk stands at Bratislava Castle.
