Movement disorder

Movement disorder
SpecialtyNeurology
TypesHypokinetic, hyperkinetic

Movement disorders are clinical syndromes with either an excess of movement (hyperkinesia) or a paucity of voluntary and automatic movements (hypokinesia), unrelated to weakness or spasticity. Movement disorders are conventionally divided into two major categories — hyperkinetic and hypokinetic — and most often arise from dysfunction of the basal ganglia or related motor circuits.

Hypokinetic disorders are typified by Parkinson's disease, in which bradykinesia, rigidity and rest tremor result from loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra. Hyperkinetic disorders include tremor, dystonia, chorea (as in Huntington's disease), myoclonus, tics (as in Tourette syndrome), ballism, athetosis and stereotypies. Ataxia, usually of cerebellar origin, is sometimes classified among movement disorders as well.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is primarily clinical, based on phenomenology — the character, distribution and triggers of the abnormal movement — supplemented by neuroimaging, laboratory tests, and genetic testing, since many movement disorders are inherited (e.g. Huntington's disease, Wilson disease, spinocerebellar ataxias). The history of drug exposure is vital, because dopamine-blocking agents can cause tardive dyskinesia and drug-induced parkinsonism.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the disorder: levodopa and dopaminergic drugs for Parkinson's disease, botulinum toxin injections for focal dystonia, and deep brain stimulation for selected cases of Parkinson's disease, essential tremor and dystonia. Many hyperkinetic disorders respond to dopamine-depleting agents such as tetrabenazine.
