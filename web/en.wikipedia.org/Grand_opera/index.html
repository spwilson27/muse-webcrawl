Grand opera

Grand opera (French: grand opéra) is a genre of 19th-century opera characterised by large-scale casts, orchestras, choruses and ballet, spectacular stage effects, and plots usually drawn from serious, often historical, subjects. It developed in Paris in the 1820s–1850s and is quintessentially associated with the Paris Opéra (Salle Le Peletier).

Canonical grand operas are in five acts and include Rossini's Guillaume Tell (1829), Auber's La muette de Portici (1828), Meyerbeer's Les Huguenots (1836) and Le prophète (1849), and Halévy's La Juive (1835). Verdi's Don Carlos and Aida, and Wagner's early Rienzi, also adopted elements of the genre. Grand opera's demands for spectacle influenced opera production worldwide.
