Obafemi Awolowo

Chief Obafemi Jeremiah Oyeniyi Awolowo (6 March 1909 – 9 May 1987) was a Nigerian nationalist, statesman, and federalist who played a central role in Nigeria's struggle for independence. He was the first Premier of the Western Region (1954–1960) and later leader of the opposition in the federal parliament, finance minister during the civil war era, and a perennial presidential candidate.

Early life and politics

Born in Ikenne, in present-day Ogun State, Awolowo worked as a clerk, trader, and journalist while studying commerce and law; he was called to the Bar at Inner Temple, London, in 1946. In 1945 he co-founded the Yoruba cultural-political organisation Egbe Omo Oduduwa, and in 1951 the Action Group party, which won the Western Region.

Premier of the Western Region

As premier, Awolowo implemented pioneering policies: free universal primary education (1955), free health services for children, agricultural development through produce boards, and Nigeria's first television station, WNTV (1959). His administration is widely remembered in the southwest as a golden age of development planning. His writings, including Path to Nigerian Freedom (1947), advocated federalism as the solution to Nigeria's diversity.

Later career

After moving to federal politics he was imprisoned in 1963 on treasonable-felony charges, released by the military government in 1966, and served as federal commissioner for finance under Yakubu Gowon during the Nigerian Civil War. He led the Unity Party of Nigeria in the Second Republic, losing the 1979 and 1983 presidential elections. Often called "the best president Nigeria never had", he died in Ikenne in 1987; his portrait appears on the 100-naira note.
