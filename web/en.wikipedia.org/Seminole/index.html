The Seminole are a Native American people who developed in Florida in the 18th century, composed mainly of Muscogee (Creek) migrants from Georgia and Alabama, together with earlier Florida peoples and escaped African slaves. Their name derives from the Spanish cimarrón ("runaway, wild one"), rendered in Muscogee as simanó-li. Today the Seminole are organised principally in two federally recognised tribes in Florida — the Seminole Tribe of Florida and the Miccosukee Tribe of Indians — and the Seminole Nation of Oklahoma.

History

After Spain ceded Florida to the United States in 1821, American settlers pressed for Seminole removal. Resistance produced the Seminole Wars: the First (1817–18), the Second (1835–42) — the costliest Indian war in U.S. history, led on the Seminole side by figures such as Osceola — and the Third (1855–58). Most Seminole were forcibly removed to Indian Territory (Oklahoma) along the "Trail of Tears," but a few hundred, protected by the Everglades, never surrendered, giving rise to the Florida Seminole claim of being "Unconquered People."

Culture and modern life

Traditional Seminole culture is matrilineal and organised in clans; distinctive arts include colourful patchwork clothing, palmetto-fibre dolls, and wood carving. The Green Corn Dance remains the central ceremonial event. In the 20th century the Florida Seminole built a substantial economy on tourism, cattle, and gaming: in 1979 the tribe opened a high-stakes bingo hall on its reservation, a venture whose legal battles — notably Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Butterworth — helped launch the modern tribal gaming industry in the United States. In 2006 the Seminole Tribe of Florida purchased the Hard Rock International restaurant and casino brand for $965 million.
