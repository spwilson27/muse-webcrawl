Laniakea Supercluster

The Laniakea Supercluster (Laniakea, Hawaiian for "immense heaven"; also called the Local Supercluster or Local SCl) is the galaxy supercluster that is home to the Milky Way and approximately 100,000 other nearby galaxies. It was defined in September 2014 by a team led by R. Brent Tully, using a new way of defining superclusters according to the relative velocities of galaxies.

Laniakea Supercluster
Major clusters: Virgo Cluster, Hydra–Centaurus, Pavo–Indus
Number of galaxies: about 100,000
Diameter: about 520 million light-years
Mass: about 1017 solar masses

Definition

Previous definitions relied on overdensities of galaxies in space. Tully's team instead mapped flows of galaxies under gravity — using peculiar velocity data from the Cosmicflows-2 catalogue — and defined a supercluster as the volume within which flows converge toward a common gravitational attractor. Laniakea's basin is centred near the Great Attractor.

Components

Laniakea incorporates the former Virgo Supercluster (including the Local Group) as one filament, along with the Hydra–Centaurus Supercluster and the Pavo–Indus Supercluster. Neighbouring basins include the Shapley, Hercules, Coma and Perseus–Pisces concentrations.

Name

The name, honouring Polynesian navigators who used knowledge of the heavens to voyage across the Pacific, was suggested by Hawaiian-language professor and astronomer Nawaʻa Napoleon.
