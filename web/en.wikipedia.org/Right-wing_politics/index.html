Spectrum: Left–right political spectrum
Origin of term: French Revolution seating arrangements

Right-wing politics

Right-wing politics are political positions and ideologies that generally regard some forms of social hierarchy and inequality as natural, inevitable, or desirable, and typically support tradition, established institutions, private property, and market-based economies. The terms "right" and "left" originated in the French National Assembly after 1789, where supporters of the king and the old order sat on the president's right.

Strands

Right-wing movements encompass conservatism, which emphasises continuity and gradual change; classical liberal and libertarian currents favouring limited government and free markets; nationalism; and, at the extremes, reactionary and far-right movements. Positions vary by country and era: economic questions such as taxation and regulation, and cultural questions such as immigration, religion, and national identity, divide the right internally.

History

From nineteenth-century conservative defenders of monarchy and church, through twentieth-century anti-communism and the free-market turns associated with figures such as Margaret Thatcher and Ronald Reagan, to contemporary nationalist movements, the right has repeatedly redefined itself while retaining themes of order, property, and national community.
