Gendarmerie

A gendarmerie is a military force with law enforcement duties among the civilian population. The term derives from the French gens d'armes ("men-at-arms"). Gendarmeries combine the discipline and organization of an army with police functions such as patrolling rural areas, riot control, criminal investigation, and border security.

Characteristics

Members of a gendarmerie are soldiers subject to military law, but their day-to-day mission is policing civilians rather than combat against an external enemy. The model spread from Napoleonic France across Europe, Latin America, Africa, and the Middle East. Examples include France's National Gendarmerie, Italy's Carabinieri, Spain's Guardia Civil, the Royal Netherlands Marechaussee, and Turkey's Gendarmerie General Command.

History

The French Gendarmerie traces its lineage to the Maréchaussée of the Ancien Régime, reorganized as the Gendarmerie Nationale in 1791. Napoleon exported the institution to conquered territories, and many successor states retained it after independence.

Role in modern states

Gendarmeries often fill the gap between civilian police and the army: they are used for public order, protection of government buildings, military police duties, and peacekeeping missions abroad under organizations such as the European Gendarmerie Force.
