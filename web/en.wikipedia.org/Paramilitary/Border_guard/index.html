Border guard

A border guard is a national security agency or member of such an agency responsible for border control: the security and protection of a country's borders, the prevention of illegal border crossing, smuggling, and trafficking, and sometimes customs and immigration enforcement.

Roles and functions

Border guards patrol land frontiers and coastlines, staff checkpoints at ports of entry, inspect documents and cargo, and interdict unauthorized crossings. In many countries the force is a uniformed paramilitary service under the interior ministry; in others it is a branch of the armed forces or a civilian law-enforcement agency.

Examples

Notable border guard organizations include the United States Border Patrol, India's Border Security Force (among the largest border forces in the world), the Bangladesh Border Guard Bangladesh, Ukraine's State Border Guard Service, and the European Border and Coast Guard Agency (Frontex), which coordinates the external borders of the European Union.

History

Formal frontier guards date to antiquity, but modern border guards emerged with the nation-state in the 19th and 20th centuries, expanding greatly after the World Wars as passport controls and customs regimes became universal.
