Irregular military

Irregular military refers to armed forces and fighters that fall outside a state's regular army organization. Irregulars include militias, guerrilla fighters, partisans, paramilitaries, mercenaries, insurgents, and armed civilians who fight outside the standard military hierarchy and often outside conventional rules of organization.

Characteristics

Irregular forces typically lack the standardized training, uniforms, equipment, and command structure of regular troops. They often rely on local knowledge, mobility, and asymmetric tactics, and may be raised for a particular campaign or cause. Because they may not be clearly distinguishable from civilians, their status under the laws of armed conflict can be contested; the Geneva Conventions grant combatant status only to organized groups meeting conditions such as carrying arms openly and following a chain of command.

Historical use

States have employed irregulars throughout history, from tribal auxiliaries attached to Roman legions, to Cossack hosts, partisan bands in occupied Europe during the Second World War, and proxy forces in Cold War conflicts. Governments may also struggle to demobilize irregular allies after a war ends.
