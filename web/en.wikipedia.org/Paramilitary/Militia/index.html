Militia

A militia is a military force raised from the civilian population to supplement a regular army in an emergency. Historically the term described the body of able-bodied citizens eligible for call-up in defense of their community, distinct from professional soldiers; in modern usage it may also refer to non-state armed groups, reserve forces, or private paramilitary organizations.

History

Militia systems were the mainstay of defense in medieval and early modern Europe, in colonial America, and in the early United States, where the Militia Act of 1792 required able-bodied men to enroll and provide their own arms. The Second Amendment to the U.S. Constitution refers to a "well regulated Militia". Over the 19th and 20th centuries most states replaced citizen levies with professional armies and organized reserves such as national guards.

Types

Militias may be organized (state-controlled bodies such as the U.S. National Guard or state defense forces), unorganized (the statutory pool of citizens liable for service), or private groups formed without state sanction. In some countries, notably Switzerland, a militia system remains the basis of national defense, with citizens keeping service weapons at home.
