TRPV1 (transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily V member 1) is a non-selective cation channel best known as the receptor for capsaicin, the pungent compound in chilli peppers. It is expressed mainly in nociceptive sensory neurons, where it detects noxious heat and contributes to pain sensation and neurogenic inflammation.

Function

TRPV1 opens in response to temperatures above about 43 °C, acidic conditions, and vanilloid compounds such as capsaicin and resiniferatoxin, allowing calcium and sodium influx that depolarises the neuron and signals pain and burning. Endogenous activators (endovanilloids) include anandamide and certain lipoxygenase products. Repeated stimulation desensitises the channel — a property exploited by high-dose capsaicin patches used to treat neuropathic pain.

Discovery and significance

The channel was cloned in 1997 by Michael Caterina in David Julius's laboratory. Work on TRPV1 and the cold receptor TRPM8 revealed how temperature is sensed at the molecular level and was recognised by the 2021 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, awarded to David Julius and Ardem Patapoutian. TRPV1 antagonists were pursued as analgesics, though many caused hyperthermia, which limited clinical development.
