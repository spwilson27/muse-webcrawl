Reginald Fessenden

Reginald Aubrey Fessenden (October 6, 1866 – July 22, 1932) was a Canadian-born inventor and engineer who conducted pioneering experiments in radio, including the first transmission of speech by radio (1900) and what is regarded as the first radio broadcast of entertainment and music (1906). He developed the heterodyne principle, the fathometer, and early sonar devices.

Career

Fessenden worked with Thomas Edison and later for Westinghouse before holding academic posts and founding his own companies. Working for the United States Weather Bureau on Cobb Island, Maryland, he transmitted intelligible speech over a distance of about one mile in December 1900. On Christmas Eve 1906, from Brant Rock, Massachusetts, he broadcast a programme of music and speech heard by ships' radio operators in the Atlantic. His disputes with corporate backers limited his commercial success, but his roughly 500 patents established him as one of the founders of radio.
