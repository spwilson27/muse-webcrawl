Gorzów Wielkopolski


The cathedral of St Mary in Gorzów Wielkopolski.

Gorzów Wielkopolski (Polish pronunciation: [ˈɡɔʐuf vjɛlkɔˈpɔlskʲi]; German: Landsberg an der Warthe) is a city in western Poland, on the Warta River. With about 120,000 inhabitants it is one of the two capitals of Lubusz Voivodeship (alongside Zielona Góra, seat of the regional assembly; Gorzów hosts the voivode). The city is an industrial and cultural centre known for machinery, chemicals and its speedway tradition.

History

The town was founded in the mid-13th century as Landsberg by the margraves of Brandenburg and developed as a fortified trading post on the Warta. It remained a German city in the Neumark region of Brandenburg and later Prussia for nearly seven centuries. Devastated by fires and sieges in the Thirty Years' War and the Seven Years' War, it industrialized rapidly in the 19th century after railway connections arrived. In 1945, under the Potsdam Agreement, the city passed to Poland; its German population was expelled and replaced largely by Poles displaced from Poland's former eastern territories. The city's name gained the qualifier Wielkopolski ("Greater Polish") in 1946.

Landmarks and sport

The Gothic cathedral of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary dates to the late 13th century. The city is celebrated for motorcycle speedway: Stal Gorzów has won multiple Polish team championships, and three-time world champion Bartosz Zmarzlik began his career there. The Edward Jancarz Stadium is among Poland's best-known speedway venues.
