Red River of the North

LengthAbout 885 km (550 mi)
SourceConfluence of the Bois de Sioux and Otter Tail rivers
MouthLake Winnipeg

The Red River of the North is a river in the north-central United States and central Canada. Formed by the confluence of the Bois de Sioux and Otter Tail rivers at Wahpeton, North Dakota, and Breckenridge, Minnesota, it flows northward through the Red River Valley, forming the boundary between North Dakota and Minnesota, then crosses Manitoba to empty into Lake Winnipeg.

Flooding

Because it flows north across a very flat former lake bed (glacial Lake Agassiz), meltwater in the south can be blocked by ice further north, causing severe spring floods. Major floods include those of 1826, 1950, and 1997, the latter devastating Grand Forks and East Grand Forks, and flooding in Winnipeg led to construction of the Red River Floodway, nicknamed "Duff's Ditch".

History

The valley was home to Indigenous peoples including the Ojibwe, Dakota, and Métis, and was a centre of the fur trade. The Selkirk settlers established the Red River Colony in what is now Manitoba in the early 19th century.
