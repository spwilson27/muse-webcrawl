Hosios Loukas (Greek: Ὅσιος Λουκᾶς, "Blessed Luke") is a historic walled Byzantine monastery near Distomo in Boeotia, Greece. Founded in the early 10th century by the hermit Saint Luke of Steiris (896–953), it is one of the most important monuments of middle Byzantine architecture and art, and has been listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1990, together with Daphni and Nea Moni of Chios.

History

Luke settled on the slopes of Mount Helicon around 946; the community grew around his cell and his reputation as a healer and prophet. After his death a large church was built over his tomb. The monastery prospered under imperial patronage, was plundered during the Frankish period, and played a role in the Greek War of Independence.

The churches

The complex contains two joined churches: the smaller Church of the Theotokos (10th century) and the larger Katholikon (early 11th century), among the earliest examples of the cross-in-square type on a large scale. The Katholikon is famed for its mosaics on a gold ground — including a Pantocrator in the dome — considered masterpieces of the Macedonian Renaissance, as well as frescoes, marble revetment and opus sectile floors.
