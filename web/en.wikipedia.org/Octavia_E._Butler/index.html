Octavia E. Butler

Born: June 22, 1947, Pasadena, California
Died: February 24, 2006, Seattle, Washington
Occupation: Novelist
Genre: Science fiction
Notable works: Kindred, Parable of the Sower, Dawn

Octavia Estelle Butler (June 22, 1947 – February 24, 2006) was an American science fiction author and a multiple recipient of the Hugo and Nebula awards. In 1995, she became the first science-fiction writer to receive a MacArthur Fellowship.

Life

Butler was born in Pasadena, California, the daughter of a housemaid and a shoeshine man who died when she was a baby. Shy and dyslexic, she found refuge in the public library, where she began writing stories as a child. She attended the Clarion Science Fiction Writers' Workshop in 1970, where she was encouraged by Harlan Ellison. For years she supported herself with menial jobs — dishwasher, telemarketer, potato chip inspector — while writing in the early morning hours.

Works

Her breakthrough novel was Kindred (1979), in which a modern Black woman is repeatedly transported to a slaveholding Maryland plantation. The Patternist series, the Xenogenesis trilogy (published together as Lilith's Brood), and the Parable series — Parable of the Sower (1993) and Parable of the Talents (1998), the latter winning the Nebula Award — explored hierarchy, power, race, gender, and human-alien symbiosis. Her short story "Bloodchild" won both Hugo and Nebula awards.

Legacy

Butler's reputation has grown steadily since her death in 2006. She was inducted into the Science Fiction Hall of Fame in 2010, her Parable novels became bestsellers decades after publication, and in 2018 Los Angeles County declared her home region honours; NASA named the Mars 2020 Perseverance rover's landing site the "Octavia E. Butler Landing" in 2021.
