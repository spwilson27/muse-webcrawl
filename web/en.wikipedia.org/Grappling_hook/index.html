Grappling hook

Type: Climbing and boarding tool

Components: Metal hook with multiple prongs, attached to a rope

A grappling hook or grapnel is a device that typically has multiple hooks (known as claws or flukes) attached to a rope or cable; it is thrown, dropped, sunk, projected by hand, or mounted directly onto the target to hold or grip it. Generally, grappling hooks are used to temporarily secure one end of a rope, or to retrieve objects at a distance.

History and uses

Grappling hooks have been used since antiquity in naval warfare, where they were thrown onto the rigging or deck of an enemy vessel so that boarding parties could pull the ships together. They were used in siege warfare to scale walls and to pull down defences. Roman forces used grapnels in naval battles, and the tool appears in many maritime traditions.

Modern uses include climbing, mountaineering, search and rescue, explosive ordnance disposal (dragging suspected devices or clearing tripwires), and recovering sunken objects from water. Special forces and police tactical units use compact grappling hooks with pneumatic or rocket launchers to gain access to buildings and ships. Lightweight folding grapnels are also carried by kayakers and maintenance workers for retrieving items from water.

Design

A typical grappling hook is made from forged or cast metal with three or four prongs curved so they can catch on ledges, branches or rails. Folding designs allow the prongs to collapse for compact carriage. The rope is usually tied through an eye at the base of the hook, and the whole device must be strong enough to bear the load of a climber plus equipment.

In popular culture

Grappling hooks feature prominently in fiction, comics and video games, where characters such as ninjas, Batman and action heroes use them to scale buildings rapidly. Real-world physics imposes more modest performance, but the device remains an iconic image of climbing and infiltration.
