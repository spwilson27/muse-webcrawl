Kālidāsa (Devanagari: कालिदास, "Servant of Kali") was a Classical Sanskrit author who is often considered ancient India's greatest playwright and dramatist. His plays and poetry are primarily based on the Vedas, the Rāmāyaṇa, the Mahābhārata and the Purāṇas.

Life

Almost nothing is known with certainty about his life. Tradition places him at the court of a king named Vikramāditya, commonly identified with the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II (r. c. 375–415 CE). Legends depict him as a foolish shepherd transformed by the grace of the goddess Kali into a brilliant poet.

Works

His drama Abhijñānaśākuntalam (The Recognition of Śakuntalā) is regarded as the masterpiece of Sanskrit literature; its translation by Sir William Jones (1789) and later by Goethe's enthusiastic praise introduced Kālidāsa to Europe. The lyric poem Meghadūta (The Cloud Messenger), in which an exiled yakṣa begs a cloud to carry a message to his beloved, set the model for the messenger-poem genre. His epics Raghuvaṃśa and Kumārasambhava and the plays Mālavikāgnimitram and Vikramōrvaśīyam complete the canon of works attributed to him.
