Energy–momentum relation

In physics, the energy–momentum relation, or relativistic dispersion relation, is the relativistic equation relating a particle's total energy E to its rest mass m0 and momentum p. It generalises the famous mass–energy equivalence E = mc2 to moving bodies:

E2 = (pc)2 + (m0c2)2

Special cases

For a particle at rest, p = 0 and the relation reduces to Einstein's rest-energy equation E0 = m0c2. For a massless particle such as a photon, m0 = 0 and the relation becomes E = pc, consistent with the electromagnetic relation E = hc/λ. At non-relativistic speeds the relation approaches the classical kinetic energy p2/2m plus the rest energy.

Four-momentum formulation

In the language of special relativity, energy and momentum form a four-vector, the four-momentum (E/c, px, py, pz). The energy–momentum relation is then the statement that the squared Minkowski norm of this four-vector is invariant and equals (m0c)2, the same in every inertial frame.

Applications

The relation is fundamental in particle physics, where it governs the kinematics of collisions and decays, and in condensed matter physics, where analogous dispersion relations describe quasiparticles in solids.
