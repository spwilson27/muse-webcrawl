Flat white

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A flat white is a coffee drink consisting of espresso and steamed milk with a thin layer of microfoam. It originated in Australia or New Zealand in the 1980s — both countries claim its invention — and has since spread worldwide as a staple of specialty coffee shops. Compared with a latte, a flat white is typically smaller, has a higher proportion of coffee to milk, and a thinner, velvety layer of foam that allows the espresso flavour to dominate.

Origins

One account attributes the drink to Alan Preston, who says he coined the term at his Moors Espresso Bar in Sydney in 1985, asking for his cappuccino "flat" — without the froth. A rival account credits Derek Townsend of Auckland, who says he developed it while working at a café in Wellington after failing to make a cappuccino with the low-fat milk available. The dispute is a minor but celebrated piece of trans-Tasman rivalry.

Preparation

A flat white is made by pouring microfoam — steamed milk with minute, uniform bubbles giving a glossy, paint-like texture — over a single or double shot of espresso, usually in a smaller ceramic cup of about 150–160 ml. The goal is a harmonious integration of milk and coffee rather than distinct layers.

Spread

The drink arrived in the United Kingdom around 2005, became a marker of independent coffee culture, and was adopted by major chains: Starbucks added it to menus in the United States in 2015. Google celebrated the flat white with a Doodle in March 2024, and the Oxford English Dictionary added the term in 2011.
