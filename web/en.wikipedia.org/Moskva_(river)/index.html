Moskva (river)

Moskva River (Russian: Москва-река)

Length: 473 km (294 mi)
Basin size: approx. 17,600 km²
Source: Smolensk Upland / Moscow Upland region, Smolensk Oblast
Mouth: Oka River at Kolomna
Cities: Moscow, Mozhaysk, Zvenigorod, Zhukovsky, Kolomna

The Moskva River (Russian: река Москва, Москва-река) is a river in western Russia that flows through Smolensk Oblast and Moscow Oblast, passing through the city of Moscow, to which it gives its name. It is a tributary of the Oka, which it joins at Kolomna; the Oka in turn flows into the Volga.

Course

The Moskva rises in marshy uplands near the Smolensk–Moscow height watershed and flows generally eastward for about 473 km. It meanders through Moscow in a series of wide bends past landmarks including the Kremlin, St. Basil's Cathedral, Gorky Park, and the Luzhniki Stadium, and is crossed by dozens of bridges within the city. Major tributaries include the Ruza, Istra, Yauza, Pakhra, and Severka.

Water supply and navigation

The river is a key source of Moscow's water, supplemented by the Moscow Canal (completed 1937), which connects the Moskva with the Volga at Ivankovo Reservoir. A system of dams and reservoirs on the upper Moskva regulates flow. The river is navigable through Moscow with the help of locks, and pleasure cruises are a popular feature of the city's embankments.

Name

The origin of the name "Moskva" is uncertain; it is probably pre-Slavic, possibly of Baltic or Finno-Ugric origin, with folk explanations linking it to words for wetness or boggy ground.
