The Wahine disaster occurred on 10 April 1968, when the New Zealand inter-island ferry TEV Wahine struck Barrett Reef at the entrance to Wellington Harbour during Cyclone Giselle — one of the worst storms in New Zealand's recorded history — and capsized near Steeple Rock. Of the 734 people aboard, 53 died. It remains New Zealand's worst modern maritime disaster.

The wreck

The Wahine, a turbine-electric roll-on/roll-off ferry launched in 1965, was approaching Wellington from Lyttelton in winds gusting to over 270 km/h when a huge wave drove her onto Barrett Reef around 6:40 a.m., shearing off a rudder and holing the hull. Anchored off Seatoun while flooding slowly worsened, the ship listed progressively until, early in the afternoon, Captain H. G. Robertson ordered abandon ship. The ferry rolled over shortly afterward. Lifeboats that reached the rocky Pencarrow shore were smashed in the surf; most survivors were picked up on the Seatoun side or by rescue vessels battling the storm.

Aftermath

A court of inquiry found no one culpable, attributing the loss to the exceptional violence of the storm, though it criticised some aspects of the abandonment. The wreck was later cut up in place after salvage attempts failed. The disaster led to improvements in Wellington harbour navigation, weather warnings, and ferry safety. Wahine Memorial Park in Seatoun and a memorial at Frank Kitts Park commemorate the dead; the ship's mast stands as a memorial in the city.
