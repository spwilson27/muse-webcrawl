Bethlehem

Bethlehem (Arabic: Beit Lahm; Hebrew: Beit Lehem) is a Palestinian city in the West Bank, about 10 kilometres south of Jerusalem, with a population of roughly 28,000. In Christian tradition it is the birthplace of Jesus, and the Church of the Nativity, built over the grotto traditionally identified as the site of the Nativity, is one of the oldest continuously operating churches in the world and a major pilgrimage destination.

History

Bethlehem is first mentioned in the Amarna letters of the 14th century BC. In the Hebrew Bible it is the hometown of David, who was anointed king there, and in the New Testament Gospels of Matthew and Luke it is the birthplace of Jesus. The Emperor Constantine's mother Helena commissioned the Church of the Nativity in the 4th century AD; the present church largely dates to the reign of Justinian in the 6th century. The city passed through Byzantine, Muslim, Crusader, Mamluk, and Ottoman rule, then British administration under the Mandate, Jordanian control after 1948, and Israeli occupation after 1967, before coming under the Palestinian Authority in 1995.

The city today

Bethlehem's economy depends heavily on tourism and pilgrimage, supplemented by stone cutting, olive-wood carving, mother-of-pearl crafts, and textiles. It is adjacent to the refugee camps of Dheisheh, Aida, and Beit Jibrin and to the towns of Beit Jala and Beit Sahour. The Israeli West Bank barrier, built around the city during the Second Intifada, has significantly affected movement and the local economy. Bethlehem University, founded in 1973, is the first university established in the West Bank.
