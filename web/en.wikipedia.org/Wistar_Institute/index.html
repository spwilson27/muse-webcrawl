Wistar Institute

The Wistar Institute is an independent, nonprofit biomedical research institution located in the University City section of Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Founded in 1892, it is the oldest independent biomedical research institute in the United States and is internationally known for research in cancer biology, immunology and vaccine development.

History

The institute was established through a bequest associated with the family of Caspar Wistar, a prominent Philadelphia physician and anatomist, and was developed under the leadership of his great-nephew Isaac Jones Wistar, a Civil War general and businessman. It was initially affiliated with the University of Pennsylvania and housed anatomical and biological collections.

In the early twentieth century, under director Milton Greenman, Wistar became famous for developing the Wistar rat, the first standardized laboratory rat strain, which was distributed to laboratories around the world and remains a staple of biomedical research.

Research achievements

Wistar scientists have made major contributions to virology and vaccinology. Leonard Hayflick developed the WI-38 human cell strain there, used in the production of numerous vaccines, and formulated the concept of the Hayflick limit. Hilary Koprowski directed the institute from 1957 to 1991 and developed an oral polio vaccine tested in the Congo. Stanley Plotkin developed the rubella vaccine RA 27/3 at Wistar, and the rotavirus vaccine work of H. Fred Clark and Paul Offit also grew out of the institute's vaccine tradition.

Today Wistar is a designated National Cancer Institute Cancer Center, with programs in tumor microenvironment, metastasis, cancer genetics and immunotherapy, alongside work on HIV and other infectious diseases.
