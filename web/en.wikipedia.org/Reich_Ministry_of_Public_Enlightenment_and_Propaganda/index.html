Reich Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda

Reichsministerium für Volksaufklärung und Propaganda
Formed: 13 March 1933
Dissolved: 1945
Minister: Joseph Goebbels
Headquarters: Berlin

The Reich Ministry of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda (Reichsministerium für Volksaufklärung und Propaganda) was a government ministry of Nazi Germany, established on 13 March 1933 shortly after Adolf Hitler came to power. It was headed throughout its existence by Joseph Goebbels.

Role

The ministry controlled the content of the press, literature, visual arts, film, theatre, music, and radio broadcasting in Germany, using them to promote Nazi ideology and suppress dissent. Through bodies such as the Reich Chamber of Culture, it licensed cultural workers and excluded Jews and political opponents from cultural life.

Structure

Departments of the ministry covered administration and law, propaganda, radio, film, the press, and foreign publicity. During the Second World War it produced newsreels, posters, and broadcasts aimed both at the German public and at enemy countries.
