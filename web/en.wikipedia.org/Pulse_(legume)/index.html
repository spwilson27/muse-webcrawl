Pulse (legume)

Pulses are the edible seeds of plants in the legume family (Fabaceae) that are harvested solely for the dry seed. The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations recognizes 11 primary types of pulses, including dry beans, dry peas, chickpeas, lentils, cowpeas, pigeon peas, lupins, Bambara beans, and vetches. Pulses do not include crops harvested green (such as green peas or green beans, which are classed as vegetables), crops used mainly for oil extraction (such as soybeans and peanuts), or legumes used for sowing.

History

Pulses are among the earliest domesticated crops. Lentils were cultivated in the Fertile Crescent some 10,000 years ago, and archaeological evidence shows peas were grown in the Near East and Mediterranean from Neolithic times. Pulses formed a dietary staple across the ancient world and were a cornerstone of medieval European agriculture, where their ability to fix nitrogen through symbiotic bacteria in root nodules made them valuable in crop rotation.

Nutrition

Pulses are high in protein (typically 20–25 percent), dietary fiber, and complex carbohydrates, and are good sources of iron, potassium, folate, and B vitamins, while being low in fat. Their protein is relatively low in the amino acid methionine, so diets combining pulses with cereals — whose proteins complement them — provide a complete amino-acid profile.

Production

Global production of pulses exceeds 90 million tonnes annually. India is the largest producer and consumer, followed by Myanmar, Canada, Australia, and Turkey. Canada is the world's largest exporter of lentils and dry peas. The United Nations General Assembly declared 2016 the International Year of Pulses to raise awareness of their nutritional benefits and their low environmental footprint compared with animal protein.

Environmental role

Because legumes fix atmospheric nitrogen, pulses grown in rotation reduce the need for synthetic nitrogen fertilizer and improve soil fertility. They generally require less water than many other protein sources, making them central to sustainable agriculture and plant-based diets.
