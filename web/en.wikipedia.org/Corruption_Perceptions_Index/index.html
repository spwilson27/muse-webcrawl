The Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI) is an annual index published by the non-governmental organisation Transparency International since 1995. It ranks countries and territories by how corrupt their public sector is perceived to be, as assessed by experts and businesspeople.

Methodology

The CPI is a composite index combining data from a range of surveys and assessments of corruption collected by independent institutions. Since a 2012 overhaul of the methodology, each country is scored on a scale from 0 (highly corrupt) to 100 (very clean), and scores are in principle comparable over time. A country must be covered by at least three data sources to be included; recent editions rank 180 countries and territories.

Findings

Nordic countries — Denmark in particular — have consistently occupied the top of the index, while states affected by conflict and weak institutions, such as Somalia, Syria, and South Sudan, have ranked at the bottom. Transparency International emphasises that more than two-thirds of countries score below the midpoint of 50, indicating what it describes as a global failure to control corruption.

Criticism

Because the CPI measures perceptions rather than corruption itself, critics note that scores may reflect media coverage, reputation lags, or the biases of expert respondents, and may not track actual changes in corrupt behaviour. Transparency International itself cautions that the index does not capture matters such as money laundering or corruption in the private sector, and has revised its methodology and presentation in response to criticism.
