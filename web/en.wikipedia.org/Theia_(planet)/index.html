Theia (planet)

Theia is a hypothesized ancient planet in the early Solar System that, according to the giant-impact hypothesis, collided with the early Earth about 4.5 billion years ago. Debris ejected by the impact is thought to have coalesced to form the Moon.

The hypothesis

Theia is proposed to have been roughly the size of Mars. It may have formed at one of Earth's Lagrangian points (L4 or L5) before gravitational perturbations sent it onto a collision course. The impact would have largely vaporised and mixed the two bodies, explaining why Earth and Moon rocks share strikingly similar isotope ratios.

Evidence

Apollo samples show lunar rocks closely match Earth's mantle in oxygen, titanium, and tungsten isotopes. Computer simulations reproduce a Moon of the right mass and iron-poor composition. In 2023, researchers proposed that dense remnants of Theia's mantle may survive as the large low-shear-velocity provinces deep inside the Earth.

Name

Theia is named for the Greek Titaness, mother of Selene, goddess of the Moon.
