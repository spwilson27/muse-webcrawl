Giengen (full name: Giengen an der Brenz) is a town in the district of Heidenheim in Baden-Württemberg, Germany. It is situated on the river Brenz, in the eastern foothills of the Swabian Jura (Schwäbische Alb), about 30 kilometres northeast of Ulm. The town consists of the core town and the districts of Burgberg, Hohenmemmingen, Hürben and Sachsenhausen.

History

Giengen was first mentioned in a document in 1078. In the 13th century it received town rights and in 1395 became a Free Imperial City of the Holy Roman Empire, a status it retained until 1803, when it was mediatised to Württemberg. The town suffered heavy damage in the great fire of 1634 during the Thirty Years' War. A famous daughter of Giengen was Margarete Steiff (1847–1909), founder of the Steiff teddy bear company, whose factory and museum remain major attractions in the town.

Sights

Landmarks include the Protestant Stadtkirche (town church) with its distinctive twin towers, one Romanesque and one Gothic, the market square with the town hall, and the Steiff Museum. Nearby in the Hürben district lies the Charlottenhöhle, one of the longest show caves in southern Germany, and the Vogelherd Cave area in the Lonetal, part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the Caves and Ice Age Art in the Swabian Jura.

Economy

Local employers include Steiff, manufacturers of plush toys, as well as engineering firms and suppliers to the regional automotive industry. Giengen lies on the Brenz railway between Aalen and Ulm and near the A7 motorway.
