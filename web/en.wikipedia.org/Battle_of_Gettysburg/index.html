The Battle of Gettysburg was a three-day battle of the American Civil War, fought from 1 to 3 July 1863 in and around the town of Gettysburg, Pennsylvania. The Union Army of the Potomac under Major General George Meade defeated attacks by General Robert E. Lee's Confederate Army of Northern Virginia, ending Lee's second invasion of the North. It is often described as the war's turning point and was its bloodiest battle, with roughly 51,000 casualties combined.

First day

Confederate infantry advancing on Gettysburg collided with Union cavalry under John Buford on 1 July. Fighting escalated as both armies converged; the Confederates drove Union troops through the town, but the Union army secured the high ground of Cemetery Hill and Cemetery Ridge south of Gettysburg.

Second day

On 2 July Lee attacked both Union flanks. Fierce fighting raged at Little Round Top, the Wheatfield, the Peach Orchard, and Devil's Den. The Union line bent but held, with Colonel Joshua Chamberlain's 20th Maine famously defending the extreme left at Little Round Top.

Third day and aftermath

On 3 July, after a massive artillery bombardment, some 12,500 Confederate infantry made the frontal assault known as Pickett's Charge against the Union centre on Cemetery Ridge. It was repulsed with heavy losses. Lee retreated to Virginia, and the Army of Northern Virginia never again mounted a major invasion. That November, President Abraham Lincoln dedicated the soldiers' national cemetery at Gettysburg with his Gettysburg Address.
