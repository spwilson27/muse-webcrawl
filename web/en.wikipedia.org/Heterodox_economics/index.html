Heterodox economics

Heterodox economics is an umbrella term for schools of economic thought outside the mainstream (or "orthodox") neoclassical tradition. Heterodox approaches typically question core mainstream assumptions such as perfect rationality, market equilibrium as a natural state, and the reducibility of the economy to individual optimization, emphasizing instead institutions, history, uncertainty, power, and social structure.

Schools of thought

Major heterodox traditions include Post-Keynesian, Marxian, institutional, feminist, ecological, Austrian (in some classifications), and social economics, along with strands of behavioral and complexity economics. Despite their differences, these schools often share methodological pluralism and an emphasis on real-world relevance over formal elegance.

Institutions and debates

Heterodox economists publish in journals such as the Cambridge Journal of Economics and the Journal of Post Keynesian Economics, and are organized in associations distinct from mainstream bodies. Debates include the Cambridge capital controversy and criticisms sharpened after the 2008 financial crisis. See also mainstream economics and political economy.
