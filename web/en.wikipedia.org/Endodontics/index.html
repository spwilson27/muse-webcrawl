Endodontics
Field: Dentistry
Focus: Dental pulp and tissues surrounding the roots of teeth
Common procedures: Root canal therapy, endodontic surgery, treatment of dental trauma

Endodontics (from the Greek roots endo- "inside" and odont- "tooth") is the dental specialty concerned with the study and treatment of the dental pulp. Endodontists perform a variety of procedures including endodontic therapy (commonly known as root canal therapy), endodontic retreatment, surgery, and treating cracked teeth and dental trauma.

Root canal therapy

Root canal therapy is one of the most common procedures. If the dental pulp (containing nerves, arterioles, venules, lymphatic tissue, and fibrous tissue) becomes diseased or injured, endodontic treatment is required to save the tooth. The pulp chamber and root canals are cleaned, disinfected, shaped, and filled with an inert material such as gutta-percha.

Training

Endodontists are dentists who complete two to three additional years of specialized postgraduate training after dental school. The specialty is recognized by dental associations in many countries, including the American Association of Endodontists.
