Marcus Hutchins

Marcus Hutchins (born 1994), also known by his online handle MalwareTech, is a British computer security researcher known for stopping the WannaCry ransomware attack in May 2017. He worked for the cybersecurity firm Kryptos Logic and became widely known in the malware analysis community through his blog and social media posts analysing botnets and other malware.

WannaCry

On 12 May 2017, the WannaCry ransomware worm began spreading rapidly around the world, infecting hundreds of thousands of computers and disrupting organisations including the UK's National Health Service. While analysing the malware, Hutchins discovered that it queried a long, unregistered domain name. He registered the domain for about $10.69, intending to track the spread of the malware; doing so acted as a "kill switch" that stopped the worm's propagation for computers able to reach the domain. Hutchins was widely hailed as an "accidental hero" in the press.

Arrest and Kronos case

In August 2017, Hutchins was arrested by the FBI in Las Vegas after attending the DEF CON hacking conference. He was indicted on charges relating to the creation and distribution of the Kronos banking trojan in 2014 and 2015. Hutchins initially pleaded not guilty, but in April 2019 he pleaded guilty to two counts connected with writing and selling malware as a teenager. In July 2019 he was sentenced to time served and one year of supervised release, with the judge acknowledging his later work in security research.

Later career

After the case, Hutchins continued working in cybersecurity and spoke publicly about his path from teenage malware writing to professional security research, including the ethical questions raised by his case.
