Lerma River

CountryMexico
Length~750 km (466 mi)
SourceAlmoloya del Río, State of Mexico
MouthLake Chapala

The Lerma River (Spanish: Río Lerma) is Mexico's longest river wholly within the country, flowing roughly 750 kilometres from the Toluca Valley highlands westward through the states of México, Querétaro, Guanajuato, Michoacán and Jalisco to empty into Lake Chapala, Mexico's largest freshwater lake. From Chapala, its waters continue toward the Pacific as the Río Grande de Santiago, making the Lerma–Chapala–Santiago system one of Mexico's most important hydrological basins.

Course and use

The river rises at Almoloya del Río southeast of Toluca at over 2,500 metres elevation, passes near Salamanca, La Piedad and other Bajío cities, and forms stretches of state boundaries before reaching Chapala. Its basin supports dense population and intensive agriculture and industry, including the Salamanca refinery and petrochemical complex.

Environmental issues

The Lerma is among Mexico's most polluted rivers, burdened by untreated municipal sewage, industrial discharges, and agricultural runoff, while heavy abstraction for irrigation and for supplying Guadalajara and (via inter-basin transfer from its headwaters system) Mexico City has sharply reduced flows reaching Lake Chapala. Government clean-up and basin-management programmes have been pursued since the late twentieth century with mixed results.
