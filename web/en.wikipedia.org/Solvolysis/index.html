Solvolysis

Solvolysis is a chemical reaction in which the solvent acts as the nucleophile, cleaving a bond in the reactant. When the solvent is water the reaction is hydrolysis; with alcohol it is alcoholysis (e.g. methanolysis), and with ammonia it is ammonolysis.

Mechanism

Solvolysis commonly proceeds by an SN1 mechanism: the leaving group departs to form a carbocation, which the solvent then attacks. Rates therefore increase in polar, ionising solvents and with substrates that form stable carbocations (tertiary and benzylic halides). Because the planar carbocation can be attacked from either side, solvolysis of a chiral substrate typically gives a racemic product.

Solvent effects

The Winstein–Grunwald equation relates solvolysis rates to the ionising power of the solvent, and solvolysis rates of standard substrates were historically used to define solvent ionising-power scales.
