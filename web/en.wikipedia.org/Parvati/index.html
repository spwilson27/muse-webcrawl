Parvati

Parvati (Sanskrit: पार्वती, Pārvatī) is a Hindu goddess of power, energy, nourishment, harmony, love, beauty, and motherhood. She is the consort of the god Shiva and the mother of Ganesha and Kartikeya. A gentle aspect of the supreme goddess Mahadevi, she is regarded as a reincarnation of Shiva's first wife, Sati.

Mythology

Parvati's name means "daughter of the mountains": she is the daughter of Himavan, king of the Himalayas. In the Puranic narratives, after Sati immolated herself, Shiva withdrew into ascetic isolation. Parvati undertook severe penance (tapas) to win him back, and their marriage restored balance to the cosmos. Their wedding is celebrated in texts such as Kālidāsa's Kumārasambhava.

Forms

Parvati has many forms and names — Uma, Gauri, Durga, and in fierce aspects Kali and Chandi. As Annapurna she is the goddess of food and nourishment. Together with Lakshmi and Saraswati she forms the Tridevi. She is worshipped across India, notably in the Shakti Peetha temples. See also Hinduism and Devi.
