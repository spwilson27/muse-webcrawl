Efraín Ríos Montt

José Efraín Ríos Montt (16 June 1926 – 1 April 2018) was a Guatemalan military officer and politician who served as de facto president of Guatemala from 1982 to 1983 after leading a military coup. His brief regime was one of the bloodiest periods of the Guatemalan Civil War, and in 2013 he became the first former head of state to be convicted of genocide by a court in his own country, although the verdict was later annulled on procedural grounds.

Early life and career

Born in Huehuetenango, Ríos Montt pursued a military career and studied in the United States. After electoral fraud denied him the presidency in the 1974 election — in which he had run as the candidate of a Christian Democrat-led coalition — he left the army and politics for a time, converting to evangelical Christianity and becoming a preacher in the California-based Church of the Word.

Rule

On 23 March 1982 Ríos Montt came to power in a coup by junior officers against the corrupt Lucas García regime, initially ruling as head of a three-man junta and then as sole president. His government pursued a counterinsurgency strategy known as "beans and bullets" (frijoles y fusiles): food and work for peasants who submitted, and military action against communities deemed to support the guerrillas. The accompanying scorched-earth campaign in the Ixil region and elsewhere destroyed hundreds of villages and killed tens of thousands of mostly Maya civilians. The UN-backed Historical Clarification Commission later concluded that state forces had committed acts of genocide against Maya groups during this period.

Ríos Montt was overthrown on 8 August 1983 by his defence minister, Óscar Humberto Mejía Víctores.

Later career and trial

He returned to politics as founder of the Guatemalan Republican Front, serving as president of Congress in the 1990s and running for president in 2003. In January 2012 he was indicted for genocide and crimes against humanity in connection with the massacre at Dos Erres and the campaign against the Ixil Maya. On 10 May 2013 a Guatemalan court convicted him of genocide and crimes against humanity and sentenced him to 80 years in prison; ten days later the Constitutional Court annulled the verdict on procedural grounds. A retrial began in 2016 behind closed doors after he was diagnosed with dementia, and he died in April 2018 while proceedings were ongoing.
