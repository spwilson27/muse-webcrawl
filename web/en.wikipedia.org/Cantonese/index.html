Cantonese is a language within the Yue branch of the Sinitic languages, originating from the city of Guangzhou (historically known as Canton) and its surrounding area in southeastern China. It is the traditional prestige variety of Yue Chinese and is the official spoken language in Hong Kong and Macau (alongside English and Portuguese respectively in official contexts), and the lingua franca of the Pearl River Delta region.

Phonology

Cantonese preserves many features of Middle Chinese lost in Mandarin, including final consonants -p, -t, -k, and -m. It is tonal, with six tones in the Hong Kong variety (nine if checked tones are counted separately). The standard romanization systems are Jyutping, developed by the Linguistic Society of Hong Kong, and the older Yale system.

Written Cantonese

While formal writing in China uses Standard Written Chinese based on Mandarin grammar and vocabulary, Cantonese has a written form employing characters specific to Cantonese words, used widely in Hong Kong in informal media, advertising, and social messaging. Cantonese-language cinema, television, and Cantopop have spread the language's influence throughout the Chinese diaspora, where Cantonese historically dominated Chinatowns in North America and Southeast Asia.
