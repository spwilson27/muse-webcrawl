Ruhollah Khomeini

Ruhollah Musavi Khomeini (17 May 1900 – 3 June 1989) was an Iranian Islamic revolutionary, politician and religious leader who served as the first Supreme Leader of Iran from 1979 until his death. A Twelver Shia ayatollah, he led the 1979 Iranian Revolution that overthrew the Pahlavi monarchy and founded the Islamic Republic of Iran, ruling it under the doctrine of Velayat-e Faqih (Guardianship of the Islamic Jurist), which he had set out in lectures published as Islamic Government (1970).

Died3 June 1989 (aged 89), Tehran, Iran

Rise and exile

Born in Khomeyn and educated in Arak and Qom, Khomeini became a leading scholar of Shia jurisprudence and philosophy. He emerged as an opponent of Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi in 1963, denouncing the White Revolution and capitulations to the United States; his arrest provoked the June 1963 uprising, and in 1964 he was exiled — first to Turkey, then to Najaf in Iraq for thirteen years, and finally to France in 1978, from where cassette recordings of his sermons mobilised mass opposition inside Iran.

Rule

Khomeini returned to Tehran on 1 February 1979 as the Shah fled; a referendum established the Islamic Republic, and a new constitution made him Supreme Leader. His decade in power encompassed the Iran hostage crisis (1979–81), the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), which he prolonged in hopes of victory before accepting a ceasefire in 1988, cultural purges and mass executions of opponents (notably in 1988), and the 1989 fatwa calling for the death of author Salman Rushdie over The Satanic Verses, which strained Iran's relations with the West. He died in June 1989 and was succeeded by Ali Khamenei; his mausoleum in Tehran remains a major shrine. Time named him Man of the Year for 1979, and he remains a polarising figure: revered in Iran as the founder of the Islamic Republic, condemned by critics for authoritarianism and human rights abuses.
