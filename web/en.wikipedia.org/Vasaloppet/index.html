Vasaloppet

Vasaloppet
Sport: Cross-country skiing
Distance: 90 km (56 mi)
Route: Sälen to Mora, Sweden
First held: 1922
Participants: ~15,000 (main race)

Vasaloppet is an annual long-distance cross-country ski race held in Dalarna, Sweden, over a 90 km course from Sälen to Mora. First run in 1922, it is the oldest and one of the largest cross-country ski races in the world, attracting around 15,000 skiers in the main event and tens of thousands more across the Vasaloppet Winter Week.

History

The race commemorates the journey of Gustav Vasa, who in 1521 fled from Danish troops and later returned to lead the Swedish War of Liberation from Danish rule; according to legend, skiers from Mora caught up with him at Sälen and persuaded him to return. Journalist Anders Pers proposed the race in a newspaper article, and the first Vasaloppet was held on 19 March 1922 with 136 participants, won by Ernst Alm.

The race

The race is held on the first Sunday in March using classic technique, passing checkpoints including Smågan, Mångsbodarna, Oxberg and Hökberg. Winning times have fallen from over seven hours in the early years to under four hours with modern equipment. Women's participation was officially recognised from 1997, though women had skied the course earlier. Since 2013 the race has been part of the Ski Classics series.
