The Pittsburgh Pirates are an American professional baseball team based in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. They compete in Major League Baseball as a member of the National League Central Division. The club was founded in 1882 as the Pittsburgh Alleghenys and adopted the "Pirates" name in 1891, allegedly after being accused of "pirating" a player from another team.

History

The Pirates played in the first modern World Series in 1903, losing to Boston, and won their first championship in 1909 behind shortstop Honus Wagner. Their most famous victory came in 1960, when Bill Mazeroski's walk-off home run in Game 7 beat the New York Yankees — still the only Game 7 walk-off homer in World Series history. Roberto Clemente led the 1971 champions, and the "We Are Family" Pirates of Willie Stargell won in 1979. Since 2001 the team has played at PNC Park, widely praised for its views of the Pittsburgh skyline.

Identity

The team's colours are black, gold and white, matching the other major Pittsburgh sports teams. The Pirates are sometimes nicknamed the "Bucs", short for buccaneers.
