Eli, high priest at Shiloh and mentor of Samuel

Eli (Hebrew: עֵלִי, ʿĒlī) was, according to the Books of Samuel, a high priest and judge of Israel at the sanctuary of Shiloh in the period before the monarchy. He is best known as the guardian and mentor of the young Samuel and for the downfall of his own priestly house.

Narrative

In 1 Samuel 1, Eli initially mistakes the silently praying Hannah for a drunk woman, then blesses her; she leaves her son Samuel in Eli's care at Shiloh. Eli's own sons, Hophni and Phinehas, are corrupt priests who abuse the sacrifices and the people; Eli rebukes them only weakly, and a man of God prophesies the destruction of his house. The child Samuel then receives his famous night-time call from God (1 Samuel 3), whose message confirms judgment on Eli's family.

In 1 Samuel 4, Israel is defeated by the Philistines at Aphek; Hophni and Phinehas are killed and the Ark of the Covenant is captured. Eli, aged ninety-eight and nearly blind, falls backwards from his seat on hearing the news and dies of a broken neck. His daughter-in-law, dying in childbirth, names her son Ichabod — "the glory has departed from Israel."

Interpretation

Eli is traditionally portrayed as pious but indulgent and failing in authority — a contrast to Samuel, whose rise the narrative traces as Eli's house falls. Later tradition counts Eli among the judges of Israel, judging Israel for forty years (1 Samuel 4:18).
