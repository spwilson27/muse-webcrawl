Legal Tender Cases

The Legal Tender Cases were a series of United States Supreme Court cases in the early 1870s concerning the constitutionality of the Legal Tender Acts of 1862 and 1863, by which Congress had made United States Notes ("greenbacks") legal tender for the payment of debts.


The United States Supreme Court

Hepburn v. Griswold

In Hepburn v. Griswold (1870), the Court held 4–3 that the acts were unconstitutional as applied to contracts made before their passage. Chief Justice Salmon P. Chase — who, as Secretary of the Treasury, had overseen the issuance of the greenbacks — wrote the majority opinion.

Reversal

On the same day the decision was announced, President Ulysses S. Grant nominated two new justices, William Strong and Joseph P. Bradley. In Knox v. Lee and Parker v. Davis (1871), the enlarged Court reversed Hepburn 5–4 and upheld the acts in full. The episode has frequently been cited in debates about "court packing".

Later confirmation

Juilliard v. Greenman (1884) upheld Congress's power to issue legal tender paper money in peacetime as well as war, by an 8–1 majority.
