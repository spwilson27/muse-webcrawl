Odoacer

Odoacer (c. 433 – 15 March 493), also spelled Odovacer, was a barbarian soldier who became the first King of Italy (476–493). His deposition of the last western Roman emperor, Romulus Augustus, on 4 September 476 is conventionally used to mark the end of the Western Roman Empire and the beginning of the Middle Ages in Western Europe.

Rise to power

Odoacer, probably the son of Edeco — a Hun chieftain in the entourage of Attila — led Germanic foederati (Heruli, Rugii and Sciri) in Italy. In 476 the foederati demanded one-third of the land of Italy; when the patrician Orestes refused, they proclaimed Odoacer king on 23 August 476. Odoacer captured Pavia, killed Orestes, and deposed Orestes' son, the young emperor Romulus Augustus, in Ravenna. Romulus was pensioned off, and the imperial insignia were sent to Constantinople — an act interpreted as the formal end of the western empire.

King of Italy

Nominally ruling as the viceroy of the eastern emperor Zeno, and later styled king, Odoacer governed Italy capably: he preserved the Roman Senate, administration, laws and coinage, maintained religious tolerance between his Arian followers and Catholic Romans, and extended his authority over Sicily, Dalmatia and Noricum. He settled his Germanic followers on confiscated land, granting the one-third allotment that had triggered the revolt.

Fall

Zeno, eager to remove the Ostrogothic king Theodoric from the Balkans, encouraged him to invade Italy. After a hard-fought war (489–493) including the battles of the Isonzo and Verona and a long siege of Ravenna, Odoacer surrendered on terms in February 493. Days later, at a banquet, Theodoric killed Odoacer — reportedly cleaving him in two with his own sword — and massacred his followers, becoming king of Italy in his place.
