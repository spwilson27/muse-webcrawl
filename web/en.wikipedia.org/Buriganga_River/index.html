Buriganga River

The Buriganga River (Bengali: বুড়িগঙ্গা, "Old Ganges") is a river in Bangladesh that flows past the southwest outskirts of Dhaka, the capital city. It is one of the most important rivers of the country commercially and historically: Dhaka itself grew up on its northern bank, and the river remains a principal transport artery, with the busy Sadarghat launch terminal handling thousands of passengers daily travelling to southern Bangladesh.

Course

The Buriganga is a distributary channel of the Dhaleshwari River. It flows through the western part of Dhaka district and eventually joins the Dhaleshwari again near Fatullah. The river is tidal and connected to the vast Ganges–Brahmaputra delta system.

Pollution

The Buriganga is among the most polluted rivers in the world. Untreated industrial effluent — especially from the tanneries long concentrated at Hazaribagh, textile dyeing works, and shipyards — together with sewage and solid waste from the megacity, has rendered stretches of the river biologically dead, with dissolved oxygen near zero in the dry season. Relocation of the tannery industry to a dedicated estate at Savar and various government clean-up drives have been undertaken since the 2010s, but enforcement remains a challenge.

Importance

Despite the pollution, the river supports the livelihoods of boatmen, traders, and the many people who rely on river transport. It is also a source of water, fish, and sand for construction, and its banks host ship-breaking and repair yards that service Bangladesh's large inland fleet.
