Display aspect ratio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The display aspect ratio (DAR) is the proportional relationship between the width and height of a display or image, usually expressed as two numbers separated by a colon, as in 16:9. It describes the shape of the displayed picture regardless of its absolute size or resolution. The aspect ratio depends only on the ratio of the dimensions, so a 1920×1080 display and a 1280×720 display share the same 16:9 ratio.

Common ratios

Early television and the Academy film standard used 4:3 (1.33:1). Widescreen cinema formats popularised wider ratios such as 1.85:1 and 2.39:1. High-definition television standardised on 16:9 (1.78:1), which became the dominant ratio for monitors and smartphones, although many phones now use taller ratios such as 19.5:9, and ultrawide monitors use 21:9 or 32:9. Older computer displays commonly used 5:4, 4:3 or 16:10.

Related concepts

The display aspect ratio is related to the pixel aspect ratio (PAR) and the storage aspect ratio (SAR): DAR = SAR × PAR. When image content does not match the display's ratio, techniques such as letterboxing, pillarboxing or cropping are used to reconcile them.
