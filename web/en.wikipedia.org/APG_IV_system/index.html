The APG IV system of flowering plant classification is the fourth version of the modern, predominantly molecular-phylogenetic system published by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG). Released in 2016, it supersedes the APG III system of 2009 and remains the prevailing reference classification for angiosperms used by herbaria, databases, and floras worldwide.

Changes from APG III

APG IV recognizes 64 orders and 416 families, against 59 orders and 415 families in APG III. New orders established include the Boraginales, Dilleniales, Icacinales, Metteniusales, and Vahliales, reflecting a decade of accumulated molecular evidence. Several families were recircumscribed — for example within the lamiids and the former Icacinaceae — and the system clarified placements of previously uncertain genera. As in earlier versions, some families are left unplaced in an order, and bracketed alternative circumscriptions were largely abandoned in favour of single, broader families.

Approach

The APG systems classify only into monophyletic groups (clades), using evidence primarily from DNA sequences of plastid, mitochondrial, and nuclear genes, summarized in consensus phylogenetic trees. Formal ranks above the family are treated as named clades — such as magnoliids, monocots, eudicots, rosids, and asterids — rather than traditional subclasses, and the authors explicitly present APG as a working system to be revised as phylogenetic knowledge improves.
