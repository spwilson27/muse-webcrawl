Serbian epic poetry

Serbian epic poetry is a form of epic poetry created by Serbs originating in today's Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia, North Macedonia and Montenegro. The poems were composed and recited by itinerant bards called guslari, who accompanied themselves on the single-stringed gusle. The corpus was largely recorded and published in the 19th century by the language reformer Vuk Stefanović Karadžić.

Cycles

The poems are traditionally organised in cycles: a mythological or non-historical cycle, the cycle of Marko Kraljević (Prince Marko), cycles concerning the Battle of Kosovo (1389) and the heroes of the Serbian Empire, the hajduk (outlaw) and uskok cycles, and poems about the liberation struggles of the 19th century.

Theory and influence

In the 1930s the classicists Milman Parry and Albert Lord studied South Slavic epic singing as a living laboratory for the oral-formulaic theory of how the Homeric epics were composed. Serbian epic poetry profoundly influenced Serbian national identity and European Romanticism; the poems were praised by Goethe and translated by Jacob Grimm.
