Highway

A highway is any public or private road or other public way on land. The term has different meanings in different jurisdictions: in British law it historically means any route over which the public has a right of passage; in North America and many other countries it refers to a major road, especially one connecting cities and designed for relatively fast traffic, ranging from two-lane rural highways to multi-lane controlled-access freeways.

History

Highways descend from ancient routes such as the Persian Royal Road and Roman roads, and in England were maintained under the Highways Acts by parishes and later turnpike trusts. The 20th century brought purpose-built motor roads: Germany's Autobahn network from the 1930s, Italy's autostrade, and the United States Interstate Highway System, authorised in 1956 and now some 79,000 km (49,000 mi) long. Numbered highway systems exist in most countries, and routes such as the Pan-American Highway and the Asian Highway Network link whole continents.

Design and effects

Highways are engineered with controlled geometry, medians, grade-separated interchanges on higher classes, and standards for lane width, sight distance and safety barriers. They reduce travel times and freight costs but are criticised for land consumption, community severance, induced demand, noise and air pollution, and for having been routed through disadvantaged neighbourhoods in some cities.
