Bicycle touring

Bicycle touring is the taking of self-contained trips for pleasure, adventure or autonomy rather than sport, commuting or exercise. Touring can range from single-day trips to multi-year journeys around the world. Trips are often organised by touring companies, clubs, or by the tourists themselves.

History

Bicycle touring began in the late 19th century with the advent of the safety bicycle. Early pioneers undertook remarkable journeys: Thomas Stevens rode around the world on a penny-farthing in 1884–1886, and Annie Londonderry became the first woman to bicycle around the world in 1894–95. In Britain the Cyclists' Touring Club, founded in 1878, promoted touring and road rights. The modern era saw long-distance riders such as Heinz Stücke, who spent decades cycling worldwide, and Dervla Murphy, whose book Full Tilt described her 1963 ride from Ireland to India.

Types of touring

Loaded touring involves carrying camping and cooking equipment in panniers on the bicycle. Credit-card touring uses minimal luggage and paid accommodation. Supported touring uses a vehicle to carry gear. Bikepacking adapts lightweight gear for off-road travel. Organised rides and charity events draw thousands of participants, and long-distance routes include the EuroVelo network in Europe and Adventure Cycling routes in the United States.

Equipment

Touring bicycles have sturdy frames, low gearing, mounts for racks and mudguards, and durable wheels. Riders typically carry tools, spares, clothing, and camping gear distributed between front and rear panniers.
