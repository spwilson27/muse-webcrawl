Stress
Field: Psychology, medicine, biology
Key concepts: Stressor, coping, allostasis

Stress, in psychology and biology, is an organism's response to a stressor — a change in the environment, a demand, or a threat — that strains physical or mental resources. Hans Selye popularised the term in the 1930s and described the general adaptation syndrome of alarm, resistance and exhaustion.

Physiology

The stress response activates the sympathetic nervous system and the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis, releasing adrenaline and cortisol. Acutely this mobilises energy and attention; chronically it is associated with cardiovascular disease, immune suppression, anxiety and depression.

Psychological models

Richard Lazarus's transactional model treats stress as arising when a person appraises demands as exceeding their coping resources. Coping strategies are divided into problem-focused and emotion-focused approaches.

Management

Exercise, sleep, social support, relaxation techniques and psychotherapy can reduce harmful stress. In engineering and physics, stress also denotes force per unit area within materials — a separate sense of the word.
