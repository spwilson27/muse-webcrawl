Fallow

Fallow is a farming technique in which arable land is left without sowing for one or more vegetative cycles. The goal of leaving land fallow is to allow the land to recover and store organic matter while retaining moisture and disrupting pest and disease life cycles.

Practices

Fallow periods were a central feature of crop rotation systems such as the three-field system of medieval Europe, in which one field in three lay fallow each year. Fallow land may be left bare, or planted with cover crops ("green fallow") that fix nitrogen and suppress weeds. In dryland farming, summer fallow stores soil moisture for the following crop.

Modern use

The use of synthetic fertilisers and pesticides has reduced the need for fallowing in intensive agriculture, but fallow periods remain important in arid regions and in organic and conservation agriculture, and are used in some government set-aside programmes to reduce production and protect soil.
