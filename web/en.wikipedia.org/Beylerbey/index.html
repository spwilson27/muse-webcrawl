Beylerbey

Beylerbey (from Turkish beylerbeyi, literally "bey of beys", i.e. commander of commanders) was a high-ranking title in the Ottoman Empire and Safavid Persia denoting the governor of a large province, the eyalet or beylerbeylik. Holders of the rank governed multiple subordinate districts administered by sanjakbeys and ranked immediately below the viziers in the imperial hierarchy.

Ottoman usage

The title was established in the 14th century as the empire's highest military command. Initially there were two great beylerbeyliks — Rumelia, covering the European provinces, and Anatolia in Asia — and the Beylerbey of Rumelia took precedence over all other provincial governors. As the empire expanded, the number of eyalets grew to more than thirty by the early 17th century, including Egypt, Baghdad, Buda, and Algiers.

The beylerbey combined civil and military authority in his province: he maintained order, collected revenues assigned to him through the timar system, and led his provincial forces on campaign. In the 19th-century Tanzimat reforms, the eyalets were reorganised as vilayets and the title fell out of official use.

Safavid usage

In Safavid Persia the equivalent title, beglarbegi, was given to the governors of the largest and most important provinces, such as Azerbaijan, Iraq, and Fars; it ranked below the chief royal officers but above ordinary begs.
