Evangelista Torricelli (15 October 1608 – 25 October 1647) was an Italian physicist and mathematician, best known for his invention of the barometer. A pupil and successor of Galileo Galilei, he made fundamental contributions to the study of atmospheric pressure, vacuums and hydrodynamics.

The barometer experiment

In 1643–44, working with Vincenzo Viviani, Torricelli filled a glass tube with mercury and inverted it into a mercury basin. The column fell to about 76 cm, leaving an empty space — the Torricellian vacuum — above it. Torricelli concluded that the mercury was held up by the pressure of the air, famously writing that "we live submerged at the bottom of an ocean of air." The experiment produced the first sustained vacuum in a laboratory and the first barometer. The unit of pressure the torr is named after him.

Mathematics and fluid dynamics

Torricelli gave the first description of Gabriel's horn (Torricelli's trumpet), a solid with finite volume but infinite surface area. He also derived Torricelli's law, which states that the speed of fluid flowing out of an opening is proportional to the square root of the height of the fluid above the opening, and worked on projectile motion and the method of indivisibles.

Legacy

Torricelli died in Florence at the age of 39, shortly after Galileo, whose chair he had inherited. His collected works on geometry influenced the development of integral calculus.
