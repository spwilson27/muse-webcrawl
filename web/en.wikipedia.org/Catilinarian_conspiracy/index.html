Catilinarian conspiracy

The Catilinarian conspiracy was a plot by the Roman senator Lucius Sergius Catilina (Catiline) to overthrow the Roman Republic in 63 BC. After twice failing to win the consulship, Catiline gathered discontented aristocrats, indebted veterans, and others in a plan to seize power by force.

Exposure and suppression

The consul Cicero, alerted by informants, denounced Catiline in a series of speeches in the Senate — the famous Catilinarian Orations, beginning with "Quo usque tandem abutere, Catilina, patientia nostra?" ("How long, Catiline, will you abuse our patience?"). Catiline fled Rome to join his army in Etruria. Five conspirators remaining in the city were arrested after incriminating letters were intercepted, and, following a celebrated Senate debate in which Julius Caesar argued for imprisonment and Cato for death, they were executed without trial on Cicero's authority.

Aftermath

Catiline died fighting at the Battle of Pistoria in January 62 BC. The execution of citizens without trial later became a charge against Cicero, contributing to his exile in 58 BC. The main sources are Cicero's speeches and Sallust's monograph Bellum Catilinae.
