A paraganglioma is a rare neuroendocrine neoplasm that may develop at various body sites, most commonly in the head, neck, thorax, and abdomen. These tumors originate from chromaffin cells in clusters of neurons known as paraganglia. Tumors that arise in the adrenal medulla are classified separately as pheochromocytomas, while those arising outside the adrenal glands are called paragangliomas.

Signs and symptoms

Paragangliomas may be functional, secreting catecholamines such as norepinephrine and producing episodic headaches, palpitations, sweating, and hypertension, or nonfunctional, presenting as a slow-growing mass. Head and neck paragangliomas — including carotid body tumors and glomus jugulare tumors — are usually nonfunctional and may cause pulsatile tinnitus, hearing loss, or cranial nerve deficits.

Genetics and treatment

A large proportion of paragangliomas are hereditary, associated with mutations in the succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) subunit genes — SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD, and SDHAF2 — and also with von Hippel–Lindau disease, multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2, and neurofibromatosis type 1. Surgical resection is the mainstay of treatment when feasible; radiotherapy and radionuclide therapy are used for unresectable or metastatic disease. Unlike most benign-appearing tumors, malignant behavior is defined by metastasis rather than by histologic appearance.
