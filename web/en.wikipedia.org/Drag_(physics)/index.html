Drag (physics)

In fluid dynamics, drag (sometimes called air resistance or fluid resistance) is a force acting opposite to the relative motion of any object moving with respect to a surrounding fluid. This can exist between two fluid layers or between a fluid and a solid surface. Drag forces are a consequence of the viscosity of the fluid and of pressure differences around the moving body.

Types of drag

Drag is commonly divided into:

  • Parasitic drag — drag on a non-lifting body, comprising form drag (due to the shape of the body and flow separation) and skin friction drag (due to viscosity at the surface).
  • Lift-induced drag — drag that arises as a by-product of generating lift, associated with wingtip vortices.
  • Wave drag — drag that appears at transonic and supersonic speeds as shock waves form.

Drag equation

For a body moving at speed v through a fluid of density ρ, the drag force is approximately

FD = ½ ρ v² CD A

where CD is the drag coefficient and A is the reference area. At low speeds, drag is approximately proportional to velocity (Stokes' law); at higher speeds it is approximately proportional to the square of velocity.

Applications

Reducing drag is central to the design of aircraft, cars, ships, and bicycles. Streamlining, smooth surfaces, and careful shaping of trailing edges all reduce drag. Conversely, drag is deliberately increased by parachutes, air brakes, and speed brakes.
