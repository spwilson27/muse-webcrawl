Linn LM-1

The Linn LM-1 Drum Computer, manufactured by Linn Electronics and introduced in 1980, was the first drum machine to use digital samples of acoustic drums. Designed by Roger Linn, it became a defining sound of 1980s popular music, used extensively by artists including Prince, Michael Jackson, Madonna, and the Human League. The LM-1 sold for about $4,995, a price that limited it to professional studios; around 525 units were built before it was superseded by the cheaper LinnDrum in 1982.

Manufacturer: Linn Electronics
Dates: 1980–1983
Designer: Roger Linn
Price: US$4,995

Features

The LM-1 offered 12 drum sounds sampled at limited fidelity (8-bit companded samples), each with individual volume, tuning and stereo output controls. It provided swing (shuffle) quantisation and the ability to correct timing errors in real time, features that made it feel more human than earlier preset rhythm boxes. Only hi-hat, snare and kick samples were derived from real drums played by session drummer Art Woodhead; other sounds were sourced from ordinary objects and instruments around Linn's studio.

Influence

Prince's use of the LM-1 — pitched down and processed with effects — produced the distinctive drum sound of albums such as 1999 and Purple Rain. The machine's approach of sample playback with programmable patterns established the template for all subsequent drum machines and sampler-based production.
