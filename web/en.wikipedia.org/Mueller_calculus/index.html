Mueller calculus

Mueller calculus is a matrix method for manipulating Stokes vectors, which represent the polarization of light. It was developed in 1943 by Hans Mueller, then a professor of physics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. In this technique, optical elements are characterized by 4×4 Mueller matrices, and the passage of light through a system is computed by matrix multiplication of the Stokes vector of the incident beam by the matrices of the successive elements.

Polarization transformation of light

Comparison with Jones calculus

Unlike Jones calculus, which treats only fully polarized, coherent light using 2×2 complex matrices, Mueller calculus handles polarized, partially polarized, and unpolarized light, and can describe depolarizing elements. Mueller matrices for polarizers, retarders, rotators and mirrors are standard tools in polarimetry, remote sensing and biomedical imaging.
