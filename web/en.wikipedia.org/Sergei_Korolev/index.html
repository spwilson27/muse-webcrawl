Sergei Korolev

Sergei Pavlovich Korolev (12 January 1907 – 14 January 1966) was the lead Soviet rocket engineer and spacecraft designer during the Space Race between the United States and the Soviet Union in the 1950s and 1960s. He is regarded by many as the father of practical astronautics. He designed the R-7 Semyorka, the world's first intercontinental ballistic missile and the launcher of Sputnik 1 and of Yuri Gagarin's Vostok 1 flight. His identity was a state secret during his lifetime; he was known publicly only as the "Chief Designer".

Early life and imprisonment

Korolev was born in Zhytomyr, in present-day Ukraine, and trained as an aircraft designer in Kyiv and Moscow, working with the GIRD rocket research group. In 1938, during the Great Purge, he was arrested and spent several years in the Gulag, including the Kolyma gold mines, before being transferred to a sharashka, a prison design bureau, where he worked on rocket-assisted aircraft. He was released in 1944.

Chief Designer

After the war Korolev led the Soviet long-range missile program, developing the R-1 through R-7 rockets. The R-7 launched the first artificial satellite, Sputnik 1, on 4 October 1957, and the first human spaceflight on 12 April 1961. Under his direction the Soviet program achieved the first spacewalk, the first probe impacts and photographs of the Moon, and the Voskhod missions. He was simultaneously designing the N1 rocket intended to carry cosmonauts to the Moon.

Death and legacy

Korolev died in January 1966 during surgery, weakened by his years in the camps. His identity was revealed only after his death. The N1 program faltered without him and was cancelled after four failed launches. The city of Kaliningrad near Moscow was renamed Korolyov in his honour, and his R-7's descendant, the Soyuz rocket, remains in service more than six decades later.
