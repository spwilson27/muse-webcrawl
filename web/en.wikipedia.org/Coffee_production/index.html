Coffee production

Coffee production is the industrial process of converting the raw fruit (coffee cherries) of the coffee plant into the finished coffee beans shipped around the world. Coffee is one of the most traded agricultural commodities, grown in more than 50 countries in the equatorial "Bean Belt" of Latin America, Africa, and Asia. The leading producers are Brazil, Vietnam, Colombia, Indonesia, and Ethiopia.

Cultivation

Coffee plants grow best in tropical highlands with rich soil, mild temperatures, frequent rain, and shaded sun. Arabica is typically cultivated at 600–2000 m, while robusta tolerates lower, hotter conditions. Trees take three to five years to bear fruit; cherries ripen unevenly, so quality production relies on repeated selective hand-picking, though large estates in flat terrain use mechanical stripping.

Processing

After harvest, the fruit must be processed promptly. In the washed method, cherries are pulped, fermented to remove mucilage, washed, and dried. In the natural method, whole cherries are dried on beds or patios before hulling. Honey processing removes the skin but leaves some mucilage during drying. Dried beans are milled to remove parchment and husk, graded by size and density, and bagged as green coffee for export.

Economics and sustainability

Some 25 million smallholder farmers depend on coffee for income, and prices are set on commodity exchanges in New York (arabica) and London (robusta). Certification schemes such as Fairtrade and Rainforest Alliance address volatile prices, labour conditions, deforestation, and the growing threat of climate change, which is shrinking suitable arabica-growing land.
