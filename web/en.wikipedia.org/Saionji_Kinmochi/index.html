Prince Saionji Kinmochi (7 December 1849 – 24 November 1940) was a Japanese statesman who served as Prime Minister of Japan in 1906–1908 and again in 1911–1912. A court noble by birth who participated in the Boshin War and the Meiji Restoration, he became one of the genrō, the informal oligarchy of elder statesmen who guided imperial Japan, and was the last surviving genrō.

Early life and career

Born into the Saionji family of kuge (court nobility) in Kyoto, Kinmochi supported the imperial cause as a young man and fought for the imperial army in the Boshin War. After the Restoration he spent a decade studying law in France, where he absorbed liberal ideas, and on his return helped found a newspaper advocating popular rights. He later served as ambassador to several European countries, as education minister, and as president of the Privy Council.

Premier and party politics

Saionji succeeded Itō Hirobumi as leader of the Rikken Seiyūkai party and formed two cabinets. A liberal by inclination, he sought to restrain military spending and to work with party government, which brought him into conflict with the army and the Yamagata faction. He headed the Japanese delegation to the Paris Peace Conference in 1919.

The last genrō

From the 1920s Saionji's principal role was advising the Emperor on the selection of prime ministers, a function he used to favour moderate, party-based governments and international cooperation. As militarism advanced in the 1930s his influence waned, and he survived an assassination attempt during the February 26 Incident of 1936. He died in 1940, months before Japan entered the Second World War in the Pacific. See also Meiji Restoration and Genrō.
