Ocellaris clownfish

Scientific name: Amphiprion ocellaris
Family: Pomacentridae
Range: Eastern Indian Ocean and western Pacific

The ocellaris clownfish (Amphiprion ocellaris), also known as the common clownfish, is a marine fish belonging to the family Pomacentridae, which includes clownfishes and damselfishes. It is bright orange with three distinctive white bars outlined in black and can grow to about 11 cm in length. The species is widely kept in saltwater aquariums and was popularised by the film Finding Nemo.

Habitat and symbiosis

The ocellaris clownfish is native to warm waters of the eastern Indian Ocean and the western Pacific, including the Great Barrier Reef. It forms a symbiotic mutualism with sea anemones: the fish is protected from predators by the anemone's stinging tentacles, to which its mucus coat makes it immune, while the anemone receives nutrients from the fish's waste and protection from polyp-eating fish.

Social structure and reproduction

Groups live in a strict size-based dominance hierarchy within a single anemone. The largest individual is the breeding female and the second largest the breeding male; clownfish are protandrous hermaphrodites, so if the female dies the breeding male changes sex and takes her place. Eggs are laid on a flat surface beside the anemone and guarded by the male.

In the aquarium

The species is hardy and largely captive-bred for the aquarium trade; numerous colour morphs, such as the snowflake and midnight variants, have been selectively bred.
