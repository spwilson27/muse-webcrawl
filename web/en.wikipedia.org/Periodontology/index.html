The periodontium: gingiva, periodontal ligament, cementum and alveolar bone.

Periodontology or periodontics is the specialty of dentistry that studies supporting structures of teeth (the periodontium), as well as diseases and conditions that affect them. A periodontist is a dentist who specialises in the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of periodontal disease, in cosmetic periodontal procedures, and in the placement of dental implants.

Periodontal disease

Periodontal diseases are infections of the structures around the teeth, most commonly caused by bacterial biofilm (dental plaque). Gingivitis, inflammation of the gums, is reversible with good oral hygiene; untreated, it can advance to periodontitis, in which inflammation destroys the periodontal ligament and alveolar bone, forming periodontal pockets and eventually causing tooth loss. Risk factors include smoking, diabetes, genetic susceptibility and poor oral hygiene. Periodontal disease has been associated with cardiovascular disease, diabetes complications and adverse pregnancy outcomes.

Treatment

Treatment ranges from professional cleaning and scaling and root planing (deep cleaning below the gumline), to antimicrobial therapy, regenerative and resective surgery, gum grafting for recession, and implant placement to replace lost teeth.
