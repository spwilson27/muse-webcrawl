Visconti of Milan

The Visconti arms: a biscione (serpent) devouring a man

Country: Duchy of Milan, Italy

Founded: 1075

Founder: Eriprando Visconti

The Visconti of Milan are a noble Italian family who rose to power in Milan during the Middle Ages, ruling the city first as lords and then as dukes from 1277 to 1447. Their emblem, a serpent devouring a human figure (the biscione), remains a symbol of Milan and appears in the badge of Alfa Romeo and the logo of Inter Milan.

Rise to power

The family's dominance began when Ottone Visconti, appointed Archbishop of Milan, defeated the rival Della Torre family at the Battle of Desio in 1277. His great-nephew Matteo I Visconti became imperial vicar and effective lord of Milan. Succeeding generations — including Galeazzo I, Azzone, Luchino, and Giovanni — expanded Milanese territory across Lombardy and beyond.

Dukes of Milan

In 1395 Gian Galeazzo Visconti purchased the title Duke of Milan from Emperor Wenceslaus, becoming the first duke. Under Gian Galeazzo, Milanese dominion reached much of northern and central Italy. After the reigns of Giovanni Maria and Filippo Maria, the male line died out in 1447; the duchy passed, after the brief Ambrosian Republic, to Francesco Sforza, husband of Filippo Maria's illegitimate daughter Bianca Maria Visconti.

Legacy

The Visconti sponsored major works such as Milan Cathedral, the Certosa di Pavia, and the Visconti Castle in Pavia. A junior branch, the Visconti di Modrone, produced the film director Luchino Visconti.
