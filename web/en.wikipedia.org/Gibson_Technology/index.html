Gibson Technology

Gibson Technology is an English automotive and motorsport engineering company based in Repton, Derbyshire. Founded in 1981 by former Lotus engineer Bill Gibson as Zytek Engineering, the company became a leading supplier of racing engines, chassis and electronic systems, particularly in sports car racing. It was rebranded Gibson Technology in 2014 after a management buyout.

Gibson's engines have powered the LMP2 class of the FIA World Endurance Championship and the 24 Hours of Le Mans as the sole-specification engine supplier, its 4.2-litre GK428 V8 being selected for the category from 2017. The company also developed engines for A1 Grand Prix, Formula Renault 3.5 and numerous prototype programmes, and has worked on hybrid and electric vehicle technology for road and racing applications.

History

Zytek built its reputation supplying engine management systems and complete powertrains to motorsport series worldwide. Its 3.4-litre V8 powered A1 Grand Prix in the series' early seasons, and Zytek developed one of the first hybrid racing powertrains raced in the American Le Mans Series. Following the 2014 management buyout led by Bill Gibson, the motorsport business adopted the Gibson Technology name, while the automotive division continued as Zytek Automotive under Continental ownership.

LMP2 programme

In 2015 Gibson was chosen as the single engine supplier for the new global LMP2 regulations. The resulting normally aspirated GK428 4.2-litre V8, producing around 600 hp in class trim, has powered every LMP2 car in the WEC, European and Asian Le Mans Series and IMSA competition since 2017, earning a reputation for reliability over endurance distances.
