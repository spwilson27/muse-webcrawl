The Pacific Islands Forum (PIF) is an inter-governmental organisation that aims to enhance cooperation among countries and territories of Oceania, including by coordinating collective responses to regional political, security, economic, and environmental issues. Founded in 1971 as the South Pacific Forum, it adopted its present name in 1999 in order to encompass the north Pacific members. Its Secretariat is based in Suva, Fiji.

Membership

The Forum has 18 members: Australia, the Cook Islands, the Federated States of Micronesia, Fiji, French Polynesia, Kiribati, Nauru, New Caledonia, New Zealand, Niue, Palau, Papua New Guinea, the Republic of the Marshall Islands, Samoa, Solomon Islands, Tonga, Tuvalu, and Vanuatu. French Polynesia and New Caledonia, previously associate members, became full members in 2016. A number of outside states, including the United States, China, Japan, and the European Union, participate as dialogue partners. In 2021 the five Micronesian members announced their intention to withdraw over a dispute about the rotation of the secretary-generalship; a negotiated compromise (the Suva Agreement) in 2022 averted the split.

Activities

The Forum Leaders Meeting is held annually, rotating among member countries. Landmark agreements include the Rarotonga Treaty (1985), establishing the South Pacific Nuclear Free Zone; the Biketawa Declaration (2000) on regional crisis response, invoked during the Solomon Islands intervention (RAMSI); the Boe Declaration (2018), which identified climate change as the region's single greatest security threat; and the 2050 Strategy for the Blue Pacific Continent, adopted in 2022. The Forum also coordinates trade work, including the PACER Plus agreement, and its members commonly act together in the United Nations, where small island states have been influential in climate negotiations.

Issues

Climate change and sea-level rise dominate the Forum's external advocacy, together with fisheries management (the Pacific's tuna stocks are a major collective asset), decolonisation questions concerning New Caledonia and French Polynesia, and the strategic competition between China and the United States and their partners in the Pacific, which has tested members' commitment to acting as a single "Blue Pacific" bloc.
