Non-binary gender

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Non-binary (also spelled nonbinary and sometimes shortened to enby) is an umbrella term for gender identities that are neither exclusively male nor female. Non-binary people may identify with two or more genders, with no gender (agender), with a gender that changes over time (genderfluid), or with a gender outside the male–female binary altogether.

Terminology and definitions

The term gained wide use in the early 2010s, though related concepts such as "genderqueer" date to the 1990s. Many non-binary people use singular they/them pronouns, though some use she/her, he/him, neopronouns, or a mixture. Non-binary identity is distinct from intersex status, which concerns sex characteristics rather than gender identity.

History

Many cultures have historically recognised more than two genders, including hijra communities in South Asia, Two-Spirit people in some Indigenous North American cultures, fa'afafine in Samoa, and sworn virgins in the Balkans. Modern Western non-binary movements grew out of queer and transgender activism in the late twentieth century, influenced by writers such as Kate Bornstein and Leslie Feinberg.

Legal recognition

A growing number of jurisdictions recognise non-binary genders in law. Argentina, Canada, Germany, the Netherlands, Australia, New Zealand, India, Pakistan, Nepal, and several US states permit a third gender marker, often "X", on passports or identity documents.
