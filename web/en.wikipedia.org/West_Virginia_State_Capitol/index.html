The West Virginia State Capitol is the seat of government for the U.S. state of West Virginia, and houses the West Virginia Legislature and the office of the Governor of West Virginia. Located in Charleston on the banks of the Kanawha River, the capitol was designed by architect Cass Gilbert and dedicated on June 20, 1932, West Virginia Day.

The building is topped by a 292-foot (89 m) gold dome, the tallest state capitol dome in the United States. Five feet taller than the dome of the United States Capitol, it is covered in gold leaf and crowned by a lantern. The capitol is part of the West Virginia Capitol Complex, which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

History

West Virginia's capital moved several times between Wheeling and Charleston in the decades after statehood in 1863. After the previous capitol building in Charleston burned in 1921, the state appointed a capitol commission, which selected Cass Gilbert, architect of the Minnesota State Capitol and the Woolworth Building. Construction of the west wing began in 1924; the main unit with its great dome was built between 1930 and 1932.

Architecture

The capitol is built of Indiana limestone and buff marble, with interiors featuring Tennessee marble and gold accents. The rotunda beneath the dome contains a large crystal chandelier. The grounds include statues commemorating Abraham Lincoln, Stonewall Jackson, and West Virginia veterans.

Capitol Complex

The surrounding complex includes the West Virginia Culture Center, the Governor's Mansion, and Building 3, connected by landscaped grounds along the Kanawha River.
