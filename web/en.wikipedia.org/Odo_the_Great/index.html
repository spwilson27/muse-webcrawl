Odo the Great

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Odo the Great (also Eudes, Eudo or Odo; died c. 735) was Duke of Aquitaine and Vasconia from the late seventh century until his death. Ruling a virtually independent principality in southwestern Gaul, he is best remembered for defeating an Umayyad army at the Battle of Toulouse in 721 — the first major check to Muslim expansion into Western Europe — more than a decade before Charles Martel's victory at Tours.

Reign

Odo's origins are obscure; he may have succeeded Duke Lupo I of Aquitaine around 700. He extended his authority over Vasconia (Gascony) and resisted attempts by the Merovingian kings and the rising Carolingian mayors of the palace to subordinate him. In 718 he aided Chilperic II, and he later made peace with Charles Martel, presenting him with the captured spoils of Toulouse.

Toulouse and Tours

On 9 June 721 Odo defeated al-Samh ibn Malik al-Khawlani, governor of al-Andalus, who was besieging Toulouse; al-Samh died of his wounds. Odo later allied with the Berber lord Munuza and married his daughter to him, but in 732 Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi invaded, crushed Odo at the Battle of the River Garonne near Bordeaux, and marched north — forcing Odo to seek Charles Martel's help, which led to the Battle of Tours (732). Odo died around 735 and was succeeded by his son Hunald I. See also Aquitaine, Battle of Tours and Charles Martel.
