Frost line (astrophysics)

In astronomy and planetary science, the frost line, also known as the snow line or ice line, is the particular distance in a protoplanetary disk from the central protostar where it is cold enough for volatile compounds such as water, ammonia and methane to condense into solid ice grains. In the Solar System it lay at roughly 2.7–5 astronomical units from the Sun, in the region of the present asteroid belt.

Formation and importance

Inside the frost line, only refractory materials — silicates and metals — are solid, so planets that form there are small and rocky, like the terrestrial planets. Beyond it, icy solids are several times more abundant, allowing cores to grow massive enough to capture hydrogen and helium and become gas giants and ice giants. Each volatile has its own condensation front, producing a compositional gradient in the disk.

Observations

The ALMA telescope has directly imaged snow lines in disks around young stars, notably the water snow line around V883 Orionis, where an outburst pushed the line outward to about 40 AU.
