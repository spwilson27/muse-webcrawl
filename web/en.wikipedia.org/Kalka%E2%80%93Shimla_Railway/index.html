Kalka–Shimla Railway

Length: 96.6 km (60 mi)

Gauge: 762 mm (2 ft 6 in) narrow gauge

Opened: 9 November 1903

Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (since 2008)

The Kalka–Shimla Railway is a narrow-gauge mountain railway in northern India that climbs from Kalka in Haryana (at 658 metres) to Shimla in Himachal Pradesh (at 2,075 metres), the summer capital of British India. Opened in 1903, the line is celebrated for its dramatic engineering: 102 tunnels, 864 bridges and viaducts, and 919 curves along a route of about 96.6 kilometres.

History

The railway was built by the Delhi-Ambala-Kalka Railway Company under chief engineer H. S. Harington, with the difficult tunnel works supervised in part by Bhalku Ram, a local who features in railway folklore. It was constructed to provide reliable access to Shimla, seat of the Government of India each summer. The Barog tunnel, at 1,143 metres the longest on the line, is associated with a well-known story about the engineer Colonel Barog.

Heritage

In 2008 UNESCO added the Kalka–Shimla Railway to the World Heritage List as part of the "Mountain Railways of India," together with the Darjeeling Himalayan and Nilgiri Mountain railways. Diesel-hauled passenger trains, including the deluxe Shivalik Express and glass-roofed Vistadome services, still run the full route, a journey of about five hours.
