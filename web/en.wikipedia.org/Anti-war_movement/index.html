Anti-war movement

An anti-war movement is a social movement, usually in opposition to a particular nation's decision to start or carry on an armed conflict. The term anti-war can also refer to pacifism, which is the opposition to all use of military force during conflicts, or to anti-war books, paintings and other works of art.

History

Organized opposition to war grew in the 19th century with peace societies and the Hague Conventions movement. It became a mass phenomenon during the First World War, when conscientious objectors and socialist movements opposed conscription, and again during the Vietnam War, when huge demonstrations in the United States and worldwide helped shift public opinion and policy. On 15 February 2003, coordinated protests against the impending invasion of Iraq drew millions of people in cities around the world, in what was described as the largest protest event in history.

Tactics and participants

Anti-war movements use marches, vigils, civil disobedience, draft resistance, teach-ins and cultural production, and typically draw together students, religious groups, labour unions, veterans and political parties. Governments have sometimes responded with surveillance and prosecution of activists.
