Geometric algebra

A bivector represents an oriented plane segment, a fundamental object of geometric algebra.

In mathematics, a geometric algebra (also known as a Clifford algebra in its general algebraic treatment) is an algebra that extends ordinary vector algebra by a product — the geometric product — that unifies the dot product and the exterior (wedge) product. Elements of a geometric algebra, called multivectors, can represent points, lines, planes, volumes, and transformations such as rotations and reflections in a single framework.

History

The foundations were laid by Hermann Grassmann, whose exterior algebra appeared in 1844, and by William Kingdon Clifford, who combined Grassmann's work with Hamilton's quaternions in 1878. The subject was revived in the twentieth century, notably by David Hestenes, who promoted geometric algebra as a unified language for physics and engineering.

The geometric product

For vectors a and b, the geometric product is written ab = a·b + a∧b, combining the scalar inner product and the bivector-valued outer product. The product of a vector with itself gives the squared length, a² = |a|², which makes many vectors invertible and greatly simplifies the treatment of rotations: a rotation can be expressed by a rotor, in close analogy with unit quaternions.

Applications

Geometric algebra is used in classical and relativistic mechanics, electromagnetism, computer graphics, robotics, and computer vision, where it offers compact, coordinate-free formulations of geometric operations.
