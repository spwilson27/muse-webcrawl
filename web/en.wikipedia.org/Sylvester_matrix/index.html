Sylvester matrix

In mathematics, the Sylvester matrix of two polynomials is a square matrix constructed from their coefficients. Its determinant equals the resultant of the two polynomials, so the matrix is singular precisely when the polynomials share a common root (over an algebraically closed field) or, equivalently, a common factor of positive degree. It is named after the English mathematician James Joseph Sylvester.

Definition

Let p and q be univariate polynomials of degrees m and n respectively:

p(x) = p0 + p1x + … + pmxm,   q(x) = q0 + q1x + … + qnxn.

The Sylvester matrix is the (m + n) × (m + n) matrix built from n rows holding the coefficients of p, each shifted by one more position, followed by m rows holding the coefficients of q, similarly shifted. The determinant of this matrix is the resultant Res(p, q).

Applications

Sylvester matrices are a basic tool of elimination theory: solving the linear system defined by the matrix corresponds to finding polynomial combinations that eliminate the variable. They appear in Bézout-type identities, in the computation of greatest common divisors of polynomials, and in control theory, where related block matrices are used to study the Sylvester equation AX + XB = C. A closely related construction is the Bézout matrix, which is smaller but carries similar information.
