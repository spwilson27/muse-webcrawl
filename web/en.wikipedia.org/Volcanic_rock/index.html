Volcanic rock

Volcanic rock is a rock formed from lava erupted from a volcano that cools and solidifies at or near the Earth's surface. Because cooling is rapid, volcanic rocks are typically fine-grained, glassy, or vesicular, in contrast to the coarse crystals of intrusive igneous rocks that cool slowly underground.

Common types

  • Basalt — a dark, mafic lava rock rich in iron and magnesium; the most abundant volcanic rock, forming most of the ocean floor and great flood-basalt provinces.
  • Andesite — intermediate in composition, typical of subduction-zone volcanoes such as the Andes.
  • Rhyolite — the felsic, silica-rich equivalent of granite, often erupted explosively.
  • Obsidian — natural volcanic glass formed when lava cools too quickly for crystals to grow.
  • Pumice and scoria — highly vesicular rocks frothing with gas bubbles; pumice is light enough to float on water.
  • Tuff — rock consolidated from volcanic ash and pyroclastic deposits.

Classification

Volcanic rocks are classified by chemical composition (especially silica content, from ultramafic to felsic) and by texture. The TAS classification (total alkali versus silica) is the standard chemical scheme used by geologists.
