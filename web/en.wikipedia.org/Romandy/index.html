Romandy (French: la Romandie) is the French-speaking part of western Switzerland. The term, modelled on the name of the German-speaking region's unofficial designation, refers to the area in which the population speaks Swiss French and is generally considered to comprise the cantons of Geneva, Vaud, Neuchâtel, and Jura, together with the French-speaking parts of the cantons of Bern, Valais, and Fribourg.

Geography

Romandy occupies the western portion of Switzerland, bordering France along the Jura mountains and the Lake Geneva basin. Its principal cities are Geneva, Lausanne (the capital of Vaud and seat of the International Olympic Committee), Neuchâtel, Fribourg, Sion, and La Chaux-de-Fonds. The region includes much of the Swiss wine country along Lake Geneva's northern shore, whose terraced Lavaux vineyards are a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Language

Swiss French differs from standard French mainly in vocabulary and in a handful of numerical forms — septante (70) and nonante (90) replace soixante-dix and quatre-vingt-dix, with huitante used for 80 in some cantons. Historically, the indigenous language of most of Romandy was Franco-Provençal (locally called patois), which has largely been displaced by French and survives in pockets, notably in Valais and Fribourg. The linguistic boundary with German-speaking Switzerland is often called the Röstigraben ("rösti ditch"), a jocular term for the cultural and political divide between the two language communities.

Culture and politics

Romandy is home to major international institutions in Geneva — the European headquarters of the United Nations, the Red Cross, and CERN (which straddles the Franco-Swiss border). In federal referendums, French-speaking cantons have often voted differently from the German-speaking majority, notably in favouring closer European integration. The region has its own public broadcaster, RTS (Radio Télévision Suisse), and newspapers such as Le Temps and 24 heures.
