Ammonium nitrate is a chemical compound with the formula NH₄NO₃. It is a white crystalline salt consisting of ions of ammonium and nitrate. It is highly soluble in water and hygroscopic as a solid, although it does not form hydrates. It is predominantly used in agriculture as a high-nitrogen fertilizer, and is also a major component of many explosive mixtures used in mining and construction, such as ANFO (ammonium nitrate / fuel oil).

Production

The industrial production of ammonium nitrate entails the acid–base reaction of ammonia with nitric acid:

HNO₃ + NH₃ → NH₄NO₃

The concentrated melt is prilled or granulated for sale. Global production runs to tens of millions of tonnes annually, most of it for fertilizer.

Applications

Fertilizer-grade ammonium nitrate (about 33.5% nitrogen) is one of the most important nitrogen fertilizers worldwide. Mixed with about 6% fuel oil it forms ANFO, the most widely used bulk industrial explosive. Dissolving ammonium nitrate in water is strongly endothermic, so it is used in instant cold packs. It has also been used as an oxidiser in rocket propellants and in solid-fuel rocket motors.

Safety and disasters

Ammonium nitrate is an oxidiser and can decompose explosively when heated, contaminated with combustible material, or confined — hazards that have caused catastrophic accidents, including the Oppau explosion in Germany (1921), the Texas City disaster (1947), the West Fertilizer Company explosion in Texas (2013), the Tianjin explosions (2015), and the Beirut port explosion of 2020, in which some 2,750 tonnes of stored ammonium nitrate devastated much of Beirut. Because it can be misused in improvised explosives, its sale and storage are regulated in many jurisdictions.
