Astana is the capital city of Kazakhstan, located on the banks of the Ishim River in the north of the country, within the Akmola Region. It is the second-largest city in Kazakhstan after Almaty, with a population of about 1.4 million. The city became the capital in 1997, replacing Almaty by decision of President Nursultan Nazarbayev.

History

Founded in 1830 as the Cossack fortress of Akmolinsk, the city was renamed Tselinograd in 1961 as the centre of the Soviet Virgin Lands campaign, and Akmola after independence. Renamed Astana ("capital" in Kazakh) in 1998, it was transformed by a vast construction programme designed by architects including Kisho Kurokawa and Norman Foster. Landmarks include the Bayterek observation tower, the Khan Shatyr shopping centre, the Palace of Peace and Reconciliation pyramid, and the Hazrat Sultan Mosque. In March 2019 the city was renamed Nur-Sultan after the departing president; in September 2022 the name reverted to Astana.

Climate and events

Astana has an extreme continental climate with very cold winters, frequently cited as among the coldest capital cities in the world. It hosted Expo 2017 ("Future Energy") and the Astana peace talks on Syria.
