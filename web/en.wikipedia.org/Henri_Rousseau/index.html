Henri Julien Félix Rousseau (21 May 1844 – 2 September 1910) was a French Post-Impressionist painter in the naïve or primitive manner. Known as Le Douanier ("the customs officer") after his day job as a Paris toll and tax collector, he was self-taught, began painting seriously only in his forties, and was ridiculed by critics during his lifetime — yet his dreamlike jungle scenes and meticulous, seemingly childlike style came to be celebrated by the avant-garde and he is now recognised as a master of modern art.

Life and career

Rousseau was born in Laval in northwestern France, the son of a tinsmith. After military service he worked for the Paris municipal toll service (the octroi) from 1871, collecting duties on goods entering the city — the post that gave him his nickname, though he was never a full customs officer. He obtained permission to copy works in the Louvre and began exhibiting at the Salon des Indépendants in 1886. He retired from his job in 1893 to paint full-time, living in considerable poverty.

Major works

His early success was The Sleeping Gypsy (1897), in which a lion pauses over a sleeping musician in a moonlit desert. Although Rousseau never left France, he painted lush jungle fantasies — informed by visits to the Paris botanical gardens and zoos, illustrated books and colonial exhibitions — including The Snake Charmer (1907), The Hungry Lion Throws Itself on the Antelope (1905) and his final large canvas, The Dream (1910), showing a nude woman reclining on a sofa set within a jungle. He also painted portraits, Parisian landscapes and allegories such as War (1894).

Recognition and legacy

Younger artists, including Pablo Picasso, Robert Delaunay, Wassily Kandinsky and the poet Guillaume Apollinaire, championed Rousseau; Picasso famously held a semi-ironic banquet in his honour in 1908. Rousseau died of a leg infection in 1910, just as his reputation was turning. His work influenced Surrealism and later figurative painting, and his major canvases hang in the Museum of Modern Art in New York, the Musée d'Orsay, the National Gallery of Art in Washington and the Pushkin Museum.
