Suo jure

Suo jure is a Latin phrase, used in English to mean "in his own right" or "in her own right". In the context of nobility and royalty, it refers to a title held by a person independently rather than through a spouse. A woman who holds a peerage or throne suo jure possesses the title in her own person, as opposed to a consort who holds a corresponding style jure uxoris (in right of his wife) or by courtesy.

Usage

Examples include queens regnant, who reign suo jure rather than as queen consorts, and peeresses in their own right under systems that allow women to inherit titles. The phrase is also used of rulers of territories or of holders of offices whose authority derives from their own inheritance or election rather than from marriage or appointment by another. The masculine and feminine forms share the same expression; context makes clear whether the holder is a man ("in his own right") or a woman ("in her own right").
