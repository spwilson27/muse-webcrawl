Anvil

An anvil is a metalworking tool consisting of a large block of metal (usually forged or cast steel), with a flattened top surface, upon which another object is struck (or "worked"). In most cases the anvil is used as a forging tool. Before the advent of modern welding technology, it was the primary tool of metal workers.

Structure

The typical modern blacksmith's anvil has a flat face for general work, a projecting conical horn used for bending and shaping curves, a hardy hole that holds specialized tools, and a pritchel hole for punching. The mass and rigidity of the anvil cause energy from the hammer to be efficiently transferred to the workpiece.

History

Anvils evolved from stone tools through bronze and iron versions. The London pattern anvil became standard in the 19th century. Although machine tools have replaced much hand forging, anvils remain in use by blacksmiths, farriers, and hobbyists.

Other uses

Anvils also refer to the incus, one of the small bones (ossicles) of the middle ear, so named for its shape. In meteorology, the flat top of a cumulonimbus cloud is called an anvil cloud.
