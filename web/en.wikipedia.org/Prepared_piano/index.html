Prepared piano

A prepared piano is a piano that has had the sounds of individual keys temporarily altered by placing objects — bolts, screws, rubber erasers, coins, pieces of wood and other materials — on or between the strings. The technique transforms the instrument's timbre, often producing percussive, bell-like or muted tones reminiscent of a gamelan ensemble.

John Cage

John Cage developed the prepared piano around 1938–1940, initially for the dance accompaniment Bacchanale (1940), when space constraints prevented a percussion ensemble. His Sonatas and Interludes (1946–48), a cycle of sixteen sonatas and four interludes requiring elaborate preparations specified in a detailed chart, is the landmark work for the instrument. Cage credited Henry Cowell, who pioneered playing directly on piano strings, as a precursor.

Later use

Composers including Lou Harrison, Christian Wolff and George Crumb adopted or adapted the technique, and it has appeared in popular music — for example in songs by the Velvet Underground, Tori Amos and Aphex Twin, and in Hauschka's prepared-piano albums. Some pianists and technicians caution that careless preparation can damage strings and soundboard, leading to standardised preparation guides.
