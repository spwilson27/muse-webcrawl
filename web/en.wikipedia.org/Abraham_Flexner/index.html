Abraham Flexner

Born13 November 1866, Louisville, Kentucky
Died21 September 1959, Falls Church, Virginia
OccupationEducator, reformer
Known forFlexner Report (1910); founding the Institute for Advanced Study

Abraham Flexner (1866–1959) was an American educator whose 1910 report on medical education transformed the training of physicians in North America. He was also the founding director of the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey.

The Flexner Report

Commissioned by the Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching, Flexner visited all 155 medical schools in the United States and Canada. His report, Medical Education in the United States and Canada (1910), exposed inadequate facilities, poorly trained faculty and commercial "diploma mill" schools. It recommended university-based medical education, rigorous entrance requirements, laboratory science and full-time clinical teaching, on the model of Johns Hopkins. In the years that followed, roughly half of North American medical schools closed or merged, and standards rose dramatically — though the closures also reduced the schools training Black doctors, a consequence historians continue to debate.

Later work

Flexner later worked for the Rockefeller-funded General Education Board, helping to channel philanthropy into medical schools. In 1930 he became the first director of the Institute for Advanced Study, recruiting scholars such as Albert Einstein and creating a model institution for advanced research free of teaching duties. His 1939 essay "The Usefulness of Useless Knowledge" remains a classic defence of curiosity-driven research.
