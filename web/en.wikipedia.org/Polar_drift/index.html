Polar drift

Polar drift is the long-term secular movement of Earth's rotation axis relative to the planet's crust, as distinct from the periodic wobbles of polar motion and the much slower plate-tectonic phenomenon of true polar wander over geological time.

Observations

Since systematic observations began in the late 19th century, the mean pole has drifted generally toward North America along roughly the 70°W meridian, at a rate of several centimetres per year. Satellite gravity missions such as GRACE have allowed the drift to be tracked with high precision.

Causes

The drift is driven mainly by glacial isostatic adjustment — the continuing rebound of the crust after the melting of the Pleistocene ice sheets — together with present-day redistribution of mass from melting ice in Greenland and Antarctica and changes in terrestrial water storage. Studies in the 2010s found the direction of drift changed noticeably around the year 2000, reflecting accelerated ice loss.
