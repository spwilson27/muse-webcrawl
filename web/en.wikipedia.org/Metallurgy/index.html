Metallurgy

Metallurgy is a domain of materials science and engineering that studies the physical and chemical behavior of metallic elements, their inter-metallic compounds, and their mixtures, which are known as alloys. It encompasses both the technology of extracting metals from their ores and refining them (process or extractive metallurgy) and the science of the structure, properties and performance of metals in use (physical metallurgy).

History

Native copper and gold were worked in prehistory; smelting of copper ores began by the 6th millennium BCE. Alloying copper with tin produced bronze, giving the Bronze Age, and the harder technology of iron smelting ushered in the Iron Age from about 1200 BCE. Landmark later advances include crucible steel, the blast furnace, the Bessemer process (1856), the open-hearth and basic oxygen steelmaking processes, and electrolytic aluminium refining (Hall–Héroult, 1886).

Extractive metallurgy

Extractive metallurgy separates valuable metals from ore and refines them. Its subfields are mineral processing (crushing, grinding and concentrating ore), pyrometallurgy (high-temperature smelting and refining), hydrometallurgy (leaching and solution purification, such as cyanidation of gold ores like those of the Merensky Reef district) and electrometallurgy (electrowinning and electrorefining).

Physical metallurgy

Physical metallurgy studies how composition, crystal structure, deformation and heat treatment determine properties such as strength, ductility, hardness and corrosion resistance. Core techniques include microscopy, X-ray diffraction, mechanical testing and phase-diagram analysis; core processes include casting, forging, rolling, welding, powder metallurgy and heat treatments such as annealing, quenching and tempering.
