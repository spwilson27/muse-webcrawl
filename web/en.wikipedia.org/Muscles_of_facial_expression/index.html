Muscles of facial expression

The muscles of facial expression are a group of striated skeletal muscles supplied by the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII) that, among other things, control facial expression. These muscles are also called mimetic muscles. Unusually for skeletal muscles, they generally originate on bone or fascia and insert into the skin, allowing them to move the skin of the face.

Structure

The muscles of facial expression include the frontalis (which raises the eyebrows and wrinkles the forehead), orbicularis oculi (which closes the eyelids), zygomaticus major and minor (which draw the mouth upward in smiling), orbicularis oris (which puckers the lips), buccinator (which compresses the cheek), platysma, corrugator supercilii, nasalis, depressor anguli oris and the mentalis. They develop from the second pharyngeal arch, which accounts for their common innervation by the facial nerve.

Function

These muscles produce the movements underlying expressions such as smiling, frowning, winking and pouting, and they also serve practical functions: the orbicularis oculi protects the eye, the orbicularis oris is essential for speech and eating, and the buccinator keeps food between the teeth during chewing. Damage to the facial nerve, as in Bell's palsy, causes weakness or paralysis of the muscles on the affected side of the face.
