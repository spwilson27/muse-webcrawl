The Tribune Tower is a 36-story, 462-foot-tall (141 m) neo-Gothic skyscraper located at 435 North Michigan Avenue in Chicago, Illinois. It was the home of the Chicago Tribune and the Tribune Company for most of the twentieth century and is one of the city's best-known landmarks.

The 1922 competition

The design was chosen through a famous international architectural competition held in 1922, which offered a $50,000 prize and drew 263 entries from 23 countries. The winning entry by New York architects John Mead Howells and Raymond Hood drew on French Gothic models, notably the tower of Rouen Cathedral, with ornate flying buttresses at the crown. Eliel Saarinen's second-place design, though unbuilt, proved hugely influential on later skyscraper design.

Features

Embedded in the exterior walls at street level are stones and fragments collected by Tribune correspondents from famous sites around the world, including the Taj Mahal, the Great Pyramid, Westminster Abbey, the Great Wall of China and the Berlin Wall; a piece of moon rock displayed in a lobby window is on loan from NASA. Carved ornament near the entrance illustrates Aesop's fables, and the lobby is inscribed with quotations on freedom of the press.

Later history

The Tribune vacated the building in 2018. The tower has since been converted into luxury condominiums while its exterior landmark status was preserved.
