World's Columbian Exposition

The World's Columbian Exposition, also known as the Chicago World's Fair, was a world's fair held in Chicago from May 1 to October 30, 1893, to celebrate the 400th anniversary of Christopher Columbus's arrival in the New World in 1492. It drew more than 27 million visitors and covered 690 acres (280 hectares), principally in Jackson Park.

The White City

The fair's grand Beaux-Arts buildings, designed under the direction of Daniel Burnham and Frederick Law Olmsted, were coated in white stucco and lit by electric lights, earning the grounds the nickname "White City". The Court of Honor centred on a large basin with the Statue of the Republic. The original Ferris Wheel, built by George Washington Gale Ferris Jr., debuted on the Midway Plaisance.

Legacy

The exposition greatly influenced American architecture and urban planning, inspiring the City Beautiful movement. Most of the fair buildings were temporary; the Palace of Fine Arts, rebuilt in stone, survives as the Museum of Science and Industry.
