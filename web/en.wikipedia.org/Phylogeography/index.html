Phylogeography

Phylogeography is the study of the historical processes that may be responsible for the past to present geographic distributions of genealogical lineages. This is accomplished by considering the geographic distribution of individuals that possess a gene genealogy tracing to a common ancestor.

Methods

The field, named by John Avise and colleagues in the late 1980s, grew out of work on mitochondrial DNA within species. Researchers map the spatial distribution of haplotypes onto a phylogenetic tree or haplotype network, then interpret patterns in terms of vicariance, dispersal, refugia and range expansion — for example, how species survived in glacial refugia during the Pleistocene and recolonised higher latitudes as ice retreated.

Applications

Phylogeography informs conservation biology (identifying evolutionarily significant units), human history (tracing migrations out of Africa), and epidemiology. Modern approaches use genome-wide data and coalescent-based statistical models.
