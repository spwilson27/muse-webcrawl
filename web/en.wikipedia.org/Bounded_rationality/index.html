Bounded rationality

Decision-making under cognitive limits

Bounded rationality is the idea that rationality is limited when individuals make decisions: by the tractability of the decision problem, by available information, and by the cognitive limitations of the mind. The concept was introduced by Herbert A. Simon, who proposed it as an alternative basis for mathematical models of decision-making, as used in economics, political science, and related disciplines.

Satisficing

Simon argued that decision-makers do not optimise — searching for the single best option — but instead satisfice: they search until they find an option that meets an aspiration level, an acceptability threshold. Human agents use heuristics, mental shortcuts that simplify decisions but can introduce systematic biases, as later documented by Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman.

Influence

Bounded rationality underpins the field of behavioural economics and influenced theories of the firm, organisational behaviour, and artificial intelligence, where resource-bounded agents must act under limits of time and computation.
