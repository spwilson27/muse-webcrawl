Bhopal

Bhopal is the capital city of the Indian state of Madhya Pradesh and a major urban centre of central India, with a population of around two million. Known as the "City of Lakes" for its numerous natural and artificial lakes, it was the capital of the princely state of Bhopal, notable for being ruled for much of the 19th and early 20th centuries by a succession of women rulers, the Nawab Begums.

History

The modern city was founded in the early 18th century by Dost Mohammad Khan, an Afghan soldier of fortune; tradition also links the site to the 11th-century Raja Bhoja, who is said to have built the great lakes. Bhopal state merged with India in 1949 and Bhopal became the capital of Madhya Pradesh in 1956.

The 1984 disaster

On the night of 2–3 December 1984, a leak of methyl isocyanate gas from the Union Carbide India Limited pesticide plant killed thousands of residents and injured hundreds of thousands more — the world's worst industrial disaster. The event led to major changes in Indian environmental law and remains the subject of continuing litigation and activism over compensation and site contamination.

City today

Bhopal combines an old city of mosques — including the Taj-ul-Masajid, one of India's largest — bazaars and lakes (the Upper and Lower Lakes) with a planned new city containing the state secretariat, universities and cultural institutions such as the Bharat Bhavan arts centre and the Tribal Museum. The Buddhist monuments of Sanchi lie about 46 kilometres away.
