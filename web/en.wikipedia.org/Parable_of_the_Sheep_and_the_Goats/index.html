Parable of the Sheep and the Goats

Parable of the Sheep and the Goats
SourceMatthew 25:31–46
SettingOlivet Discourse; final judgment
CriterionWorks of mercy: food, drink, welcome, clothing, care, visiting prisoners

The Parable of the Sheep and the Goats concludes the Olivet Discourse in the Gospel of Matthew. It describes the Son of Man coming in glory and separating the nations as a shepherd separates sheep from goats — the sheep, placed at his right hand, inherit the kingdom; the goats, at his left, depart into punishment.

Content

The judgment turns on concrete acts of mercy: "For I was hungry and you gave me food, I was thirsty and you gave me drink, I was a stranger and you welcomed me, I was naked and you clothed me, I was sick and you visited me, I was in prison and you came to me." Both groups are surprised, and the king answers: "As you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me" — and, to the goats, "as you did not do it to one of the least of these, you did not do it to me."

Interpretation

The passage is one of the principal New Testament texts on the Last Judgment and on the identification of Christ with the poor and needy. Debates concern whether "the least of these my brothers" means all people in need or specifically Jesus's disciples and missionaries. Its sheep imagery connects with the shepherd language of the Good Shepherd discourses and the other parables of Jesus.
