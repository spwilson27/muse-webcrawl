IERS Reference Meridian

The IERS Reference Meridian (IRM) is the prime meridian (0° longitude) maintained by the International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS). It is the reference meridian used by GPS, the WGS 84 coordinate system, and modern timekeeping.

Location

The IRM passes through Greenwich, London, but lies about 102 metres east of the historic Airy Transit Circle at the Royal Observatory, which marked the former Greenwich prime meridian. The offset results from the difference between astronomical longitude, measured relative to the local direction of gravity, and geodetic coordinates in a modern Earth-centred reference frame.

Definition

The IRM is realized by a network of observing stations whose coordinates are fixed in the International Terrestrial Reference Frame; it is the meridian plane containing the IERS reference pole and the frame's origin orientation. The difference between the IRM and the Airy meridian amounts to about 5.3 seconds of arc.
