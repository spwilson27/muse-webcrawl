Treaty of Wedmore
Date878
LocationWedmore, Somerset, England
PartiesAlfred the Great of Wessex; Guthrum of the Danish Great Heathen Army
OutcomePeace between Wessex and the Danes; division of England; baptism of Guthrum

The Treaty of Wedmore is a name sometimes given to the agreement made in 878 between Alfred the Great, king of Wessex, and the Danish Viking leader Guthrum, following Alfred's victory over the Danes at the Battle of Edington (Ethandun) in May 878. The terms of the agreement are not preserved in a single treaty document; they are inferred from the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle and from the later, formal Treaty of Alfred and Guthrum.

Terms

After Edington, Guthrum's army withdrew to Chippenham and, starved into submission after a fourteen-day siege, sued for peace. Guthrum agreed to leave Wessex and to be baptised a Christian. Alfred stood as sponsor at Guthrum's baptism at Aller, near Athelney, where Guthrum took the baptismal name Æthelstan, and the ceremonial unbinding of the chrism (the crismlising) took place eight days later at Wedmore — hence the treaty's name.

Consequences

The peace allowed Guthrum to withdraw to East Anglia, which he ruled as a Christian king until his death in 890. A few years later the boundary between English and Danish territory was formalised in the Treaty of Alfred and Guthrum, running up the Thames, along the Lea, to Bedford and along the Ouse to Watling Street — the agreement that defined the Danelaw. The conversion of Guthrum and his leading men was a significant step in the Christianisation and integration of the Danish settlers in England.
