Bicycle parking

Common formsInverted-U racks, Sheffield stands, two-tier racks, lockers, automated underground parking
Largest facilitiesUtrecht Centraal, Netherlands (over 12,500 spaces)
PurposeSecure, weather-protected storage of bicycles

Bicycle parking is the infrastructure and equipment that support the secure storage of bicycles when not in use. Adequate parking — ranging from a simple rack outside a shop to vast guarded parking garages at railway stations — is considered essential to encouraging utility cycling, since fear of theft is a leading deterrent to bicycle use.

Equipment

The standard short-stay device is the inverted-U rack (Sheffield stand in the UK): a sturdy tube to which the frame and a wheel can be locked with a U-lock or chain. Guidelines stress that racks should support the bicycle upright without a kickstand, allow locking of the frame, and be placed close to destinations on firm surfaces. Long-stay parking adds weather cover, lighting, access control, and surveillance; bicycle lockers and rented cages serve individual users, while two-tier and vertical racks increase capacity in dense facilities.

Large-scale facilities

The Netherlands operates guarded bicycle parking at most railway stations; the facility at Utrecht Centraal, opened in stages from 2017, holds more than 12,500 bicycles and is among the world's largest. Japan has built automated underground systems such as the ECO Cycle, which store thousands of bicycles robotically below street level. Cities elsewhere increasingly require bicycle parking in building codes and zoning, analogous to car-parking minimums.

Theft and security

Bicycle theft is among the most common property crimes in cycling cities. Secure parking design — visibility, anchoring hardware to concrete, controlled access — together with registration and marking schemes aims to reduce losses, though determined thieves with power tools remain a challenge that shapes facility design.
