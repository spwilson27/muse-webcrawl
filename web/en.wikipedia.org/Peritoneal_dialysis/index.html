Peritoneal dialysis

Peritoneal dialysis (PD) is a treatment for kidney failure that uses the peritoneum — the membrane lining the abdomen — as a natural filter to remove waste products and excess fluid from the blood. It is one of the two main forms of dialysis, alongside haemodialysis.

Procedure

A permanent catheter is placed in the abdomen. Sterile dialysis solution (dialysate) is infused into the peritoneal cavity and left to dwell for several hours; solutes diffuse across the peritoneal membrane from the blood while glucose in the fluid draws out water by osmosis (ultrafiltration). The fluid is then drained and replaced. In continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) exchanges are done by hand several times a day; in automated peritoneal dialysis (APD) a cycler machine performs exchanges overnight.

Advantages and complications

PD can be performed at home, preserves residual kidney function longer, and avoids the rapid fluid shifts of haemodialysis. Its chief complication is peritonitis, an infection of the peritoneal cavity, along with catheter infections, hernias and long-term membrane changes that may reduce effectiveness.
