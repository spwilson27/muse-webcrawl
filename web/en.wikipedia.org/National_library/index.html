National library

Type: Public library maintained by a nation

Function: Legal deposit, national bibliography, preservation

A national library is a library established by a government as a country's preeminent repository of information and holder of legal deposit. Unlike public lending libraries, national libraries rarely allow citizens to borrow books; they collect and preserve the nation's publications and heritage for research.

Legal deposit

Under legal deposit law, publishers must deposit a copy of every publication with the national library. This guarantees the preservation of the country's published output, including books, periodicals, maps, music and, increasingly, websites and digital publications.

Examples

Famous examples include the British Library in London, the Library of Congress in Washington, D.C., the Bibliothèque nationale de France in Paris, the Russian State Library in Moscow, the National Library of China, and the National Diet Library of Japan. Many originated from royal or presidential collections in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Functions

National libraries compile the national bibliography, assign ISBNs in some countries, preserve manuscripts and archives, provide inter-library services, host exhibitions, and lead digitisation programmes. The International Federation of Library Associations (IFLA) coordinates cooperation between them.

History

The idea grew from early modern royal libraries opened to scholars, the revolutionary confiscation of collections in France, and 19th-century nation-building, when a comprehensive national collection became a symbol of cultural sovereignty.
