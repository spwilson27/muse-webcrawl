Kibbutz volunteer

A kibbutz volunteer is a person, usually a young adult from abroad, who works for a period of weeks or months on a kibbutz — a collective agricultural community in Israel — in exchange for accommodation, food, and a small allowance, rather than wages.

History

The volunteer phenomenon began in earnest after the Six-Day War of 1967, when a labour shortage in Israel drew tens of thousands of idealistic young people, many of them non-Jewish Europeans and North Americans attracted by the socialist-Zionist experiment and the adventure of communal living. At the movement's peak in the 1970s and 1980s, around 10,000–12,000 volunteers were present in Israel at any given time, spread across hundreds of kibbutzim.

Work and life

Volunteers typically worked in agriculture — picking citrus fruit, dates, avocados, or cotton — or in kitchens, laundries, dining halls, and factories. Days began early, often at dawn, and after work volunteers socialised in the volunteers' quarters and the kibbutz pub. Programmes were coordinated by the Kibbutz Program Center in Tel Aviv, which assigned volunteers to communities and handled visas.

Decline

Numbers fell sharply from the 1990s onward, as the kibbutz movement itself privatised and hired wage labour, and security situations deterred visitors. Smaller programmes continue today, including post-army and heritage-oriented volunteer schemes, but on a much reduced scale compared with the heyday of the 1970s.

Notable volunteers

Famous former kibbutz volunteers include comedian Jerry Seinfeld, who worked at Kibbutz Sa'ar as a teenager; actress Sigourney Weaver; actor Sacha Baron Cohen; and musician Bob Dylan, who spent time at Kibbutz Givat Haim. British prime minister Boris Johnson volunteered at Kibbutz Kfar HaNassi in the 1980s.
