The spotted hyena (Crocuta crocuta), also known as the laughing hyena, is a hyena species, currently classed as the sole member of the genus Crocuta, native to sub-Saharan Africa. It is the largest living member of the Hyaenidae and, unusually for a mammal, females are larger than and socially dominant over males.

Description

Spotted hyenas have powerful forequarters and a sloping back, massive jaws and bone-crushing premolars that let them consume entire carcasses, including bones and hides. Adults weigh roughly 40–86 kg. Their repertoire of vocalisations includes the famous "laugh" (a high-pitched giggling heard during social excitement or submission), whoops used as long-distance contact calls, and groans and growls.

Behaviour and ecology

Spotted hyenas live in large, fission–fusion groups called clans, which can number up to 80–100 individuals, with complex social hierarchies comparable to those of primates; rank is largely inherited through maternal lines, and even the lowest-ranking female outranks all adult males. Contrary to their scavenger reputation, spotted hyenas are highly efficient hunters that kill much of what they eat, and lions steal from them at least as often as the reverse. They range widely, communicate with scent marking ("pasting"), and den communally, though mothers nurse only their own cubs.

Reproduction

Females have uniquely masculinised external genitalia (a pseudo-penis), making mating and birth anatomically difficult; cubs are born in dens after a gestation of about 110 days, usually as twins, and sibling aggression among newborns is common. Females' high androgen exposure is thought to underlie their size, aggression and dominance.

Relationship with humans

Spotted hyenas feature ambivalently in African folklore and have long had a negative image in Western culture. They occasionally prey on livestock and, rarely, people, and populations are declining outside protected areas because of persecution, snaring and habitat loss, though the species as a whole is listed as Least Concern.
