Post-punk revival

The post-punk revival (also known as indie rock revival in some contexts) is a genre and movement of indie and alternative rock that emerged in the late 1990s and flourished in the 2000s, drawing inspiration from the sound and aesthetic of the original post-punk and new wave bands of the late 1970s and early 1980s.

Characteristics

Bands of the revival favoured angular guitar lines, danceable bass-driven rhythms, minimalist arrangements and, frequently, a retro fashion sense of skinny ties, blazers and vintage clothing. Influences commonly cited include Joy Division, Gang of Four, Wire, Talking Heads and The Fall.

History

The movement coalesced around New York's The Strokes, whose 2001 debut Is This It was a critical and commercial breakthrough, and Interpol's Turn On the Bright Lights (2002). In Britain, Franz Ferdinand, Bloc Party, Editors and Arctic Monkeys led a parallel wave; other key acts include The White Stripes, The Killers, Yeah Yeah Yeahs, LCD Soundsystem and The Rapture, who helped drive the related dance-punk scene. By the late 2000s the movement's commercial peak had passed, though its influence persisted in indie rock and a further wave of post-punk-influenced bands emerged in the late 2010s.
