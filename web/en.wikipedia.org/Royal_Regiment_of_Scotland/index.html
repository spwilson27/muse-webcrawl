Royal Regiment of Scotland

Soldiers of the Royal Regiment of Scotland in ceremonial dress.

The Royal Regiment of Scotland (SCOTS) is the senior and only current Scottish line infantry regiment of the British Army Infantry. It was formed on 28 March 2006 by the amalgamation of Scotland's six remaining regular infantry regiments, following the 2004 defence review Delivering Security in a Changing World.

Formation

The regiment brought together the Royal Scots, the King's Own Scottish Borderers, the Royal Highland Fusiliers, the Black Watch, the Highlanders (Seaforth, Gordons and Camerons), and the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders. The amalgamation was controversial in Scotland, with public campaigns opposing the loss of the historic regimental names; the battalions retained those names as subtitles.

Structure

The regiment initially consisted of five regular battalions and two Territorial Army (now Army Reserve) battalions. Following the Army 2020 restructuring, the regiment was reduced to four regular battalions, with the 5th Battalion, the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders, reduced to a public duties incremental company (Balaklava Company). The battalions are numbered 1 SCOTS through 4 SCOTS, with the Royal Scots Borderers as 1 SCOTS and the Black Watch as 3 SCOTS.

Traditions

The regiment's cap badge combines the Saltire of St Andrew with the Lion Rampant of the Royal Arms of Scotland. Its motto is Nemo Me Impune Lacessit ("No one assails me with impunity"). Battalions wear variations of the Government tartan (Black Watch) and kilts or trews according to their antecedent regiments. The regiment's headquarters is at Edinburgh Castle.

Deployments

Battalions of the regiment have served on operations in Iraq and Afghanistan, as well as on peacekeeping and training missions worldwide, including deployments to Cyprus, Kenya, and on Operation Shader.
