Piston

A piston is a component of reciprocating engines, pumps and compressors: a cylindrical element that moves back and forth inside a cylinder, transferring force between a fluid and a crankshaft via a connecting rod.

Function

In an internal combustion engine, expanding gases from ignited fuel push the piston down the cylinder, converting chemical energy into mechanical work. In a compressor or pump the motion is reversed: the piston is driven to compress or move fluid. Piston rings seal the gap between piston and cylinder wall, preventing gas leakage and controlling oil.

Materials

Modern engine pistons are usually cast or forged aluminium alloys, chosen for low mass and good heat conduction; older and heavy-duty designs use cast iron or steel.
