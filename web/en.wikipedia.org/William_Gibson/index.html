William Gibson

William Ford Gibson (born March 17, 1948) is an American-Canadian speculative fiction writer and essayist widely credited with pioneering the cyberpunk subgenre. His first novel, Neuromancer (1984), popularized the term "cyberspace" — which he had coined in his 1982 story "Burning Chrome" — and envisioned a globally networked information space years before the World Wide Web.

Works

Neuromancer won the Hugo, Nebula, and Philip K. Dick awards and opened the Sprawl trilogy, completed by Count Zero (1986) and Mona Lisa Overdrive (1988). With Bruce Sterling he co-authored the steampunk landmark The Difference Engine (1990). The Bridge trilogy (Virtual Light, Idoru, All Tomorrow's Parties) followed, and the Blue Ant trilogy (Pattern Recognition, 2003; Spook Country; Zero History) moved into a near-present milieu. His later novels The Peripheral (2014) and Agency (2020) explore alternate timelines and time communication.

Life

Born in Conway, South Carolina, Gibson moved to Canada in 1968 during the Vietnam War era and settled in Vancouver, where he has lived since. His cool, dense, brand-saturated prose style and his dictum that "the street finds its own uses for things" have made him one of the most influential science fiction writers of the late 20th century.
