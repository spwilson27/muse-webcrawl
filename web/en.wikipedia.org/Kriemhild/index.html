Kriemhild

Kriemhild in a medieval manuscript of the Nibelungenlied.

Kriemhild is a central character in the Nibelungenlied, the Middle High German epic poem of around 1200. She is the sister of the Burgundian kings Gunther, Gernot and Giselher, the wife of the hero Siegfried, and, after his murder, the wife of Etzel (Attila), king of the Huns. Her unrelenting quest for vengeance drives the tragic second half of the poem.

Role in the Nibelungenlied

Kriemhild begins the poem as a sheltered princess who, having dreamed of a falcon torn apart by two eagles, vows never to love. She nevertheless marries Siegfried, the slayer of the dragon Fafnir. A public quarrel with her sister-in-law Brünhild over precedence sets in motion the plot by which Hagen murders Siegfried. Widowed and robbed of the Nibelung treasure, Kriemhild marries Etzel and, after years of waiting, invites her brothers and Hagen to Hungary, where she engineers a catastrophic battle in which nearly all the principals die — including Kriemhild herself, cut down by Hildebrand after she kills Hagen.

Origins and parallels

Kriemhild corresponds to the Norse figure Gudrun (Guðrún Gjúkadóttir) in the Poetic Edda and Völsunga saga, though the Scandinavian Gudrun avenges her brothers rather than her husband. The character is often compared to the historical Ildico, the Germanic bride of Attila.
