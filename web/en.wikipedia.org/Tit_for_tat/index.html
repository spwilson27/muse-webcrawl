Tit for tat

Tit for tat is a strategy in game theory for the iterated prisoner's dilemma. It was introduced by Anatol Rapoport and won Robert Axelrod's influential computer tournaments of the early 1980s, described in his book The Evolution of Cooperation (1984).

The strategy

An agent using tit for tat cooperates on the first move, and thereafter copies the opponent's previous move: it cooperates if the opponent cooperated, and defects if the opponent defected. The strategy is therefore nice (never the first to defect), retaliatory (it punishes defection immediately), forgiving (it returns to cooperation as soon as the opponent does), and clear (easy for opponents to understand).

Significance

Axelrod's tournaments showed that this simple reciprocal strategy could outperform far more complex ones, providing a influential account of how cooperation can evolve among self-interested agents through reciprocal altruism. Variants such as "tit for two tats" and generous or contrite versions have been studied to handle noise and mistaken defections.
