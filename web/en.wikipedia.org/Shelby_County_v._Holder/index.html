Shelby County v. Holder

Shelby County v. Holder, 570 U.S. 529 (2013), is a landmark decision of the United States Supreme Court holding that Section 4(b) of the Voting Rights Act of 1965 — the coverage formula determining which states and localities were required to obtain federal preclearance before changing voting laws under Section 5 — was unconstitutional.

Decision

In a 5–4 decision written by Chief Justice John Roberts, the Court reasoned that the formula, based on decades-old data, imposed current burdens without justification and violated principles of federalism and the equal sovereignty of the states. Because the formula was struck down, Section 5 preclearance became inoperative unless Congress enacted a new formula. Justice Ginsburg's dissent, joined by three others, argued that Congress's extensive record on reauthorisation in 2006 justified retaining the formula.

Effects

After the ruling, several previously covered jurisdictions implemented voting changes — including voter ID laws and redistricting — that would previously have required preclearance, prompting extensive debate and further litigation over voting rights in the United States.
