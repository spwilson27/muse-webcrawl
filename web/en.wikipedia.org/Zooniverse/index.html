Zooniverse

TypeCitizen science portal
LaunchedDecember 2009
PredecessorGalaxy Zoo (2007)
OperatorCitizen Science Alliance

The Zooniverse is a citizen science web portal owned and operated by the Citizen Science Alliance. It hosts dozens of projects that allow volunteers to participate in research across the sciences and humanities, including astronomy, ecology, climate science, biology and history. The organisation grew from the original Galaxy Zoo project and launched the Zooniverse portal in December 2009.

Galaxy Zoo, launched in July 2007 by astronomers including Kevin Schawinski and Chris Lintott, asked volunteers to classify the shapes of roughly a million galaxies imaged by the Sloan Digital Sky Survey — a task completed with a speed and scale far beyond what the small research team could manage. Its success demonstrated that large numbers of volunteers could produce scientifically reliable classifications when multiple independent judgements were combined. The Zooniverse generalised this model, providing a shared platform, interface design and data infrastructure for projects such as Old Weather (transcribing historical ship's logs for climate data), Whale FM, Snapshot Serengeti (classifying camera-trap images), and Planet Hunters (searching exoplanet transit data).

Discoveries

Volunteers have been credited with notable finds, including Hanny's Voorwerp, a quasar ionisation echo discovered by Dutch volunteer Hanny van Arkel in Galaxy Zoo data, and the "Green Pea" galaxies, a compact class of star-forming galaxies identified by forum participants. Zooniverse projects register millions of volunteers; classifications by untrained participants, aggregated across many users, have been shown to match expert accuracy on many tasks.
