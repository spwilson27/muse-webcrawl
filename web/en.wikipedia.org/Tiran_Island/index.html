Tiran Island (Arabic: جزيرة تيران) is an island at the entrance of the Straits of Tiran, which separate the Red Sea from the Gulf of Aqaba. Its strategic position has made it a flashpoint in the Arab–Israeli conflict: together with the neighbouring island of Sanafir it guards the only shipping channel to the ports of Eilat (Israel) and Aqaba (Jordan).

History

Egyptian forces occupied Tiran and Sanafir in 1950 with Saudi consent. Egypt's blockade of the straits to Israeli shipping was among the causes of the Suez Crisis of 1956 and, again, of the Six-Day War in June 1967, when Israel occupied the island. Under the 1979 Egypt–Israel peace treaty the island was returned to Egypt, demilitarised and patrolled by the Multinational Force and Observers; the treaty guarantees freedom of navigation through the straits. In 2016 Egypt agreed to transfer sovereignty over Tiran and Sanafir to Saudi Arabia as part of a maritime border demarcation; after litigation in Egyptian courts, the Egyptian parliament ratified the agreement in June 2017.

Geography

The island is barren and mountainous, rising to about 290 metres, and is surrounded by coral reefs — including the Jackson, Woodhouse, Thomas and Gordon reefs — that are famous diving sites near Sharm el-Sheikh. The straits contain dangerous shoals; several ships have wrecked on them, including the German vessel Loullia on Gordon Reef in 1981.
