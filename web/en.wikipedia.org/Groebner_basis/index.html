Groebner basis

A Groebner basis (also written Gröbner basis) is a particular kind of generating set of an ideal in a polynomial ring over a field. Introduced by Bruno Buchberger in his 1965 doctoral thesis, named after his advisor Wolfgang Gröbner, Gröbner bases make many problems about polynomial ideals computationally tractable, generalising such classical tools as Gaussian elimination and the Euclidean algorithm.

Definition

Given a monomial ordering on the polynomial ring, every nonzero polynomial has a leading term. For an ideal I, consider the ideal generated by the leading terms of all its elements. A finite subset G of I is a Gröbner basis if the leading terms of the elements of G generate that leading-term ideal.

Buchberger's algorithm

Buchberger's algorithm computes a Gröbner basis from any generating set of an ideal by repeatedly forming S-polynomials of pairs of basis elements and reducing them; any nonzero remainder is added to the basis. The algorithm terminates by Dickson's lemma. Reduced Gröbner bases are unique for a given ideal and monomial order.

Applications

Gröbner bases are used to solve systems of polynomial equations, to test ideal membership, to compute elimination ideals, and in applications ranging from robotics and coding theory to automated theorem proving. They are implemented in computer algebra systems such as Singular, Macaulay2, Magma and CoCoA.
