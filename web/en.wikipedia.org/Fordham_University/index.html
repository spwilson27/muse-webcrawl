Fordham University is a private Jesuit research university in New York City. Founded in 1841 as St. John's College by Bishop John Hughes, it was entrusted to the Society of Jesus in 1846 and renamed Fordham University in 1907, after the Fordham neighbourhood of the Bronx where its main campus, Rose Hill, is located.

Campuses

The university operates three main campuses: Rose Hill in the Bronx, home to the undergraduate colleges and known for its collegiate Gothic architecture; the Lincoln Center campus in Manhattan, home to the School of Law and other programmes; and a campus in West Harrison, Westchester County. Fordham enrolls roughly 17,000 students across ten schools, including Fordham College, the Gabelli School of Business, and schools of law, education and social service.

History and notable associations

Fordham's football programme was famous in the 1930s for its "Seven Blocks of Granite" offensive line, which included Vince Lombardi. The university's alumni include politicians, judges, writers and business leaders, and its WFUV public radio station broadcasts from Rose Hill.
