Samsara (Sanskrit and Pali: saṃsāra, literally "wandering" or "world") in Buddhism is the beginningless cycle of repeated birth, mundane existence and death that all beings pass through. It is characterized by dukkha (suffering, dissatisfaction), impermanence (anicca), and the doctrine of no-self (anatta). Samsara is perpetuated by ignorance (avidya) and by karma, the volitional actions of body, speech and mind.

The cycle of rebirth

According to Buddhist cosmology, beings are reborn among six realms of existence: the gods (deva), demigods (asura), humans, animals, hungry ghosts (preta), and hell beings. The realm into which a being is born is determined by its karma. None of these states is permanent; when the karma that produced a particular rebirth is exhausted, the being dies and is reborn elsewhere, in an endless round driven by craving (tanha) and clinging.

The mechanism of the cycle is described by the doctrine of dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), traditionally presented as twelve links (nidānas) beginning with ignorance and ending with aging and death. The wheel of life (bhavacakra), a diagram said to have been designed by the Buddha himself, depicts these realms and links, held in the clutches of Yama, the lord of death.

Liberation

The goal of Buddhist practice is liberation from samsara: the attainment of nirvana, the cessation of suffering and of rebirth. Following the Noble Eightfold Path — right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration — the practitioner uproots ignorance and craving. An awakened being, an arhat or a buddha, is no longer subject to rebirth after death, a state called parinirvana.

In other traditions

The concept of samsara is shared, with differing interpretations, by Hinduism, Jainism and Sikhism. In Hindu thought samsara is the transmigration of the eternal self (atman); Buddhism denies such a permanent self while affirming continuity of the causal stream of consciousness. Jainism regards samsara as the bondage of the soul (jiva) by karmic matter.
