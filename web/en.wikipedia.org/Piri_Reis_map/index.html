Piri Reis map

The Piri Reis map is a world map compiled in 1513 by the Ottoman admiral and cartographer Piri Reis. Drawn on gazelle skin parchment, the surviving fragment — roughly the western third of the original — depicts the Atlantic Ocean, the Americas, West Africa and Europe, and is the oldest surviving Turkish map of the New World.

Sources and discovery

Piri Reis stated that he compiled the map from about twenty source charts, including a map drawn from the voyages of Christopher Columbus, Portuguese charts, and older Islamic and classical sources. The fragment was rediscovered in 1929 in the Topkapı Palace in Istanbul, where it is still held.

Significance

The map is notable for its comparatively accurate depiction of the South American coast so soon after European contact. Fringe claims about it depicting Antarctica have been rejected by historians of cartography, who interpret the southern landmass as the conjectured Terra Australis or a distorted South American coastline.
