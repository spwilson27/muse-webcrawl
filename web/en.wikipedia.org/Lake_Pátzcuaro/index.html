Lake Pátzcuaro

Lake Pátzcuaro (Spanish: Lago de Pátzcuaro) is a lake in the municipality of Pátzcuaro in the state of Michoacán, Mexico. It is one of Mexico's best-known lakes, famous for the butterfly fishermen who use wing-like nets, for the island of Janitzio, and for Day of the Dead celebrations in lakeside Purépecha villages.

Geography

The lake lies in an endorheic basin at over 2,000 metres elevation. It has no natural outlet. Islands include Janitzio, topped by a large statue of José María Morelos, La Pacanda, Yunuén, Tecuena, and the Urandén islands.

Settlements

The principal town is Pátzcuaro on the southern shore. Erongarícuaro, Quiroga and Tzintzuntzán — a former capital of the Purépecha Empire — also stand on or near the lake.

Ecology and fisheries

Native fish include the Pátzcuaro chub and the pescado blanco (Chirostoma estor), a silverside prized in local cuisine and now threatened. Introduced species, water pollution, siltation and falling water levels have harmed the fishery. Traditional butterfly-net fishing is an iconic image of the lake.

Culture

Lakeside villages are renowned for Night of the Dead (Noche de Muertos) observances on 1–2 November, when families hold vigils in cemeteries, notably on Janitzio and in Tzurumútaro and Tzintzuntzán.
