June Carter Cash

Valerie June Carter Cash (June 23, 1929 – May 15, 2003) was an American country music singer, songwriter, and actress. A member of the legendary Carter Family and a five-time Grammy Award winner, she is best known for her work with her husband, Johnny Cash.

Career

Born in Maces Spring, Virginia, into the "first family of country music", June performed from childhood with her mother Maybelle Carter and her sisters as Mother Maybelle and the Carter Sisters. A versatile performer — she sang, played autoharp, guitar, banjo, and harmonica, and did comedy routines — she toured with Elvis Presley and other stars in the 1950s. With Merle Kilgore she co-wrote "Ring of Fire" (1963), a hit for Johnny Cash.

Marriage and later life

June married Cash in 1968, having helped him through his struggles with addiction; their duets, including "Jackson" and "If I Were a Carpenter", won Grammy Awards. She continued recording as a solo artist, winning a Grammy for her 1999 album Press On. She died in Nashville in May 2003, four months before Cash; her final album, Wildwood Flower, was released posthumously and also won a Grammy. She was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 2025 as a member of the Carter Family's legacy generation, and was portrayed by Reese Witherspoon in the film Walk the Line (2005).
