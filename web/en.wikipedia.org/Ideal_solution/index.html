Ideal solution

Total and partial vapor pressures of an ideal solution follow Raoult's law linearly.

An ideal solution or ideal mixture is a solution that exhibits thermodynamic properties analogous to those of a mixture of ideal gases. The enthalpy of mixing is zero, as is the volume change on mixing, so the interactions between unlike molecules are, on average, the same as those between like molecules. Ideal solutions are defined to obey Raoult's law over the entire range of compositions: the partial vapor pressure of each component is proportional to its mole fraction.

Characteristics

For an ideal solution, the chemical potential of component i takes the form μi = μi* + RT ln xi, where μi* is the chemical potential of the pure component. The entropy of mixing is the same as for an ideal gas mixture, and the Gibbs energy of mixing is purely entropic. Mixtures of chemically very similar liquids, such as benzene and toluene, or hexane and heptane, approach ideal behavior closely.

Non-ideality

Most real solutions deviate from ideality. Positive deviations from Raoult's law occur when unlike interactions are weaker than like interactions, and negative deviations when they are stronger; mixtures such as ethanol and water form azeotropes as a result. For dilute solutions, the solute instead follows Henry's law, and departures from ideality are treated quantitatively with activity coefficients in the framework of regular and non-ideal solution models.
