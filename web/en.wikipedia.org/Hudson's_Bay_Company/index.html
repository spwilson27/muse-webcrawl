Hudson’s Bay Company

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hudson’s Bay Company (HBC; French: Compagnie de la Baie d’Hudson) is a Canadian retail business group and historically a fur-trading company. Incorporated by English royal charter on 2 May 1670, it functioned as the de facto government of Rupert’s Land, a vast part of British North America, for nearly two centuries and is the oldest incorporated joint-stock company in Canada and one of the oldest in the world.

Founded: 2 May 1670, London, England
Founders: Pierre-Esprit Radisson; Médard des Groseilliers (explorers); Prince Rupert (first governor)
Headquarters: Toronto, Ontario, Canada
Industry: Retail (Hudson’s Bay department stores)

Fur trade era

The charter granted HBC a monopoly of trade in the entire Hudson Bay drainage basin, named Rupert’s Land after the company’s first governor, Prince Rupert of the Rhine. From posts (“factories”) on the bay, the company traded European goods for beaver pelts with Indigenous peoples. After fierce competition with the Montreal-based North West Company, the two merged in 1821, and HBC administered much of what is now western and northern Canada until it surrendered Rupert’s Land to the new Dominion of Canada in 1870.

Retail era

HBC’s trading posts evolved into department stores, and its striped point blankets became an enduring Canadian icon. In the 20th and 21st centuries the company expanded and retrenched repeatedly; it was acquired by the American firm NRDC Equity Partners in 2008, and its flagship chain traded as Hudson’s Bay. In 2025 the company entered creditor protection and liquidated its Canadian stores.
