Yucatán is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located on the northern part of the Yucatán Peninsula, bordering the Gulf of Mexico to the north and west, the state of Campeche to the southwest, and Quintana Roo to the east and southeast. Its capital and largest city is Mérida.

History

The region was a heartland of the Maya civilisation, whose cities — Chichén Itzá, Uxmal, Ek' Balam, Mayapán — flourished for centuries before and after the collapse of the Classic-period southern lowlands. The Spanish conquest of Yucatán, led by Francisco de Montejo and his son in the 1520s–1540s, was prolonged by fierce Maya resistance; Mérida was founded in 1542 on the Maya city of T'ho. During the colonial era the region was relatively isolated. In the 19th century Yucatán twice declared independence from Mexico, and from 1847 the Maya uprising known as the Caste War convulsed the peninsula for decades. A henequen (sisal) boom around 1900 made Mérida one of Mexico's wealthiest cities.

Geography

Yucatán is a flat limestone platform with almost no surface rivers; rainwater filters into underground systems of caves and sinkholes called cenotes, which were sacred to the Maya and remain vital water sources and tourist attractions. The Chicxulub crater, formed by the asteroid impact that ended the age of dinosaurs 66 million years ago, is centred near the state's northern coast.

Culture

Yucatecan culture blends Maya and Spanish influences in its language (Yucatec Maya is widely spoken), cuisine (cochinita pibil, sopa de lima), traditional dress, and music. The state is also known for its numerous haciendas, many restored as hotels and museums.
