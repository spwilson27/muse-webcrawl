The Red Ensign or "Red Duster" is the civil ensign of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. It is one of three British ensigns in use, the others being the Blue Ensign and the White Ensign. The flag consists of a red field with the Union Jack in the canton.

The Red Ensign is flown by British merchant ships and by private vessels registered in the United Kingdom. Several British colonies and former colonies adopted red ensigns defaced with the local coat of arms as the basis for their national flags, most famously Canada, whose national flag debate was preceded by decades of use of the Canadian Red Ensign.

History

The Red Ensign originated in the early 17th century as an English naval flag. In 1674, a Royal Proclamation of Charles II confirmed that the Red Ensign was the appropriate flag for English merchantmen. The design evolved with the Acts of Union: the canton changed in 1707 with the union of England and Scotland, and again in 1801 when the cross of Saint Patrick was added to the Union Jack following the union with Ireland.

Under the Merchant Shipping Act 1895, the plain Red Ensign without defacement was confirmed as the national colours for British merchant vessels. The flag's popular nickname, the "Red Duster", reflects its constant exposure to the grime of working ships.

Variants

A number of British territories fly defaced Red Ensigns as their civil or territorial flags, including Bermuda, whose red ensign is unusual among British overseas territories (most others use the Blue Ensign). The Isle of Man and several Canadian provinces historically used defaced red ensigns. Red Ensigns with badges were also the basis of the flags of many former colonies in Africa, Asia and the Pacific.
