HMS Victory is a 104-gun first-rate ship of the line of the Royal Navy. She was ordered in 1758, laid down in 1759, and launched in 1765. She is best known for her role as Lord Nelson's flagship at the Battle of Trafalgar on 21 October 1805.

Service

Victory served as flagship to several admirals during the American Revolutionary War, including at the Battle of Ushant (1778) under Admiral Keppel, and under Sir John Jervis at the Battle of Cape St Vincent (1797). In 1803 she became Nelson's flagship in the Mediterranean. At Trafalgar, Victory led the weather column that broke the Franco-Spanish line; Nelson was mortally wounded on her quarterdeck by a French sharpshooter, and the ship suffered heavy damage and casualties before the battle ended in British victory.

Design

The ship carries three gun decks armed with smoothbore cannon up to 42-pounders on the lower deck, is about 69 metres (227 ft) long overall, and displaced roughly 3,500 tons. Her complement at Trafalgar was about 850 officers and men.

Preservation

After decades as a harbour ship at Portsmouth, Victory was moved into No. 2 Dry Dock in 1922 and restored to her Trafalgar configuration. She remains in commission as the flagship of the First Sea Lord, the world's oldest naval vessel still in commission, and a major museum ship visited by hundreds of thousands of people each year; a long-term conservation programme addresses the condition of her timbers and rigging.
