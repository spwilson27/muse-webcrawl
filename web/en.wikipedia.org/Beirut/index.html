Beirut

Beirut is the capital and largest city of Lebanon. As of 2014, Greater Beirut has a population of 2.2 million, which makes it the third-largest city in the Levant region. The city is situated on a peninsula at the midpoint of Lebanon's Mediterranean coast. Beirut has been inhabited for more than 5,000 years, making it one of the oldest cities in the world.

History

Beirut was a Phoenician port known as Berytus, later a Roman colony famous for its law school. It grew under Ottoman rule and flourished after Lebanon's independence as a regional banking and cultural centre, earning the nickname "Paris of the Middle East". The Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990) divided the city along the Green Line and devastated its centre, which was rebuilt in the 1990s and 2000s. On 4 August 2020 a massive explosion at the city's port killed more than 200 people and caused widespread destruction.

Geography and culture

The city lies on a triangular peninsula jutting into the Mediterranean Sea, backed by the Lebanon mountains. It is home to the American University of Beirut, founded in 1866, and is known for its publishing industry, cuisine and nightlife.
