Pepperoni

Pepperoni is an American variety of spicy salami made from cured pork and beef seasoned with paprika or other chili pepper. It is characterised by its bright red colour, soft texture and tendency, when baked, to curl up at the edges into crisp "cups". Pepperoni is the most popular pizza topping in the United States.

History and etymology

The name derives from peperoni, the plural of the Italian peperone, meaning bell pepper; in Italy the sausage itself is not called pepperoni, and ordering a "pepperoni pizza" there yields a pizza with peppers. The product was developed by Italian-American butchers in the United States in the early 20th century, adapting southern Italian dry sausages such as salsiccia and the spicy salami of Calabria to American tastes and ingredients. It gained wide popularity with the growth of pizzerias after the Second World War.

Production and use

Pepperoni is typically made from ground pork and beef mixed with salt, curing agents (historically sodium nitrite), paprika, cayenne or other peppers, garlic and fennel or mustard seed, then stuffed into casings and dried or smoked. A typical serving is relatively high in fat, sodium and calories. As a pizza topping it is usually sliced thin; "cup and char" styles, sliced thicker with natural casings, curl and crisp at the rims when baked. Pepperoni is also eaten in sandwiches, on antipasto platters, and as a snack, and is used in dishes such as pepperoni rolls, a regional speciality of West Virginia.
