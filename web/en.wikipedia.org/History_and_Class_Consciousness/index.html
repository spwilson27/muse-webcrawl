AuthorGyörgy Lukács
Published1923
SubjectMarxist philosophy, sociology
Key conceptsReification, class consciousness, totality

History and Class Consciousness: Studies in Marxist Dialectics (German: Geschichte und Klassenbewußtsein) is a 1923 collection of essays by the Hungarian philosopher György Lukács. It is regarded as a founding text of Western Marxism and one of the most influential works of 20th-century Marxist thought.

Contents and arguments

The central essay, "Reification and the Consciousness of the Proletariat", argues that under capitalism social relations take on the character of things — a process of reification (Verdinglichung) that fragments consciousness. The proletariat, by becoming conscious of itself as both subject and object of the historical process, can grasp society as a totality and overcome reification. Other essays treat orthodox Marxism, Rosa Luxemburg, and the methodology of historical materialism.

Reception

The book was condemned by the Comintern, and Lukács later repudiated parts of it, yet it profoundly influenced the Frankfurt School, existential Marxism, and later theorists of ideology and culture. An English translation appeared in 1971.
