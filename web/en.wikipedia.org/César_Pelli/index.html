César Pelli (October 12, 1926 – July 19, 2019) was an Argentine-American architect who designed some of the world's tallest buildings and major urban landmarks. His most famous work is the Petronas Towers in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, which were the tallest buildings in the world from 1998 to 2004. In 1991, the American Institute of Architects named him one of the ten most influential living American architects.

Early life and education

Pelli was born in San Miguel de Tucumán, Argentina. He studied architecture at the Universidad Nacional de Tucumán, graduating in 1949, and emigrated to the United States in 1952 to study at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, where he earned a master's degree. He became a naturalised US citizen in 1964.

Career

Pelli worked for a decade in the office of Eero Saarinen, contributing to projects such as the TWA Flight Center and Yale's Morse and Stiles Colleges. He later worked in Los Angeles, where projects like the Pacific Design Center (1975) drew attention, before becoming dean of the Yale School of Architecture (1977–1984) and founding his own practice, César Pelli & Associates (later Pelli Clarke Pelli Architects), in New Haven in 1977.

Major works include the expansion of the Museum of Modern Art in New York (1984), the World Financial Center in New York (1985–1988), One Canada Square at Canary Wharf in London (1991), the Petronas Towers (1998), the Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport terminal, the Salesforce Tower in San Francisco (2018), and the Torre Costanera in Santiago, the tallest building in South America.

Awards

Pelli received the AIA Gold Medal in 1995 and numerous other honours, including the Aga Khan Award for Architecture for the Petronas Towers.
