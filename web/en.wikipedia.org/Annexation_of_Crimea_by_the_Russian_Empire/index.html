Annexation of Crimea by the Russian Empire

The annexation of Crimea by the Russian Empire in 1783 was the absorption of the Crimean Khanate into the Russian Empire under Catherine the Great. It ended more than three centuries of the khanate's existence — formerly an Ottoman vassal state — and gave Russia a firm hold on the northern Black Sea coast.

Background

The Crimean Khanate, ruled by the Giray dynasty, had been an Ottoman protectorate since 1475. Russo-Turkish wars progressively weakened Ottoman influence. After the Russo-Turkish War of 1768–1774, the Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca (1774) declared the khanate independent of the Ottomans — a status that left it open to Russian interference. Russia installed the pliable Şahin Giray as khan, whose reforms and pro-Russian stance provoked revolts.

Annexation

Amid civil unrest, Catherine II's minister Grigory Potemkin advised annexation. On 19 April 1783 (New Style), Catherine issued a manifesto incorporating Crimea, the Taman Peninsula, and the Kuban into the Russian Empire. Russian troops occupied the peninsula largely without bloodshed; Şahin Giray abdicated. The Ottoman Empire, unprepared for war, acquiesced, formally acknowledging the annexation by the Treaty of Jassy in 1792 after a further war.

Consequences

Crimea became the Taurida Governorate. Sevastopol was founded in 1783 as the base of the new Black Sea Fleet. Many Crimean Tatars emigrated to Ottoman lands in successive waves, while Russian, Ukrainian, Greek, Bulgarian, and German settlers colonised the steppe. Catherine's celebrated tour of the region in 1787 displayed Russia's new southern possessions to Europe.
