Polish Corridor

The corridor gave Poland access to the Baltic Sea but separated East Prussia from Germany proper.

The Polish Corridor (German: Polnischer Korridor; Polish: Pomorze Gdańskie) was a strip of territory in interwar Poland that gave the Second Polish Republic access to the Baltic Sea. Created by the Treaty of Versailles in 1919 from former German territory in West Prussia and Pomerelia, it separated the German exclave of East Prussia from the rest of Germany.

Background

The corridor corresponded broadly to Poland's pre-partition coastline and had a mixed Polish, Kashubian, and German population. The port city of Danzig (Gdańsk) was not included in Poland but made a Free City under League of Nations protection, with Poland controlling its foreign representation and customs. Poland built the new port of Gdynia in the corridor, which by the 1930s rivalled Danzig in traffic.

Dispute and Second World War

The corridor and the status of Danzig were persistent sources of German–Polish tension. Nazi Germany demanded the return of Danzig and an extraterritorial route across the corridor; Poland's refusal, backed by British and French guarantees, formed the immediate background to Germany's invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939, which began the Second World War in Europe.

After 1945 the entire area was placed within Poland's new borders, and its German population was expelled westward.
