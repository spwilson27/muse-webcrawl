Stationery

Stationery refers to commercially manufactured writing materials, including cut paper, envelopes, writing implements, and other office supplies. The word derives from "stationer", a bookseller or publisher in the Middle Ages who sold goods from a fixed station, as opposed to itinerant peddlers.

History

Stationers were organised in London in the Stationers' Company, chartered in 1557, which regulated printing and publishing. With the rise of the modern office in the 19th and 20th centuries, stationery expanded to include paper clips, staplers, folders, and pre-printed business forms.

Categories

  • Writing paper: letterheads, notepads, notebooks, index cards
  • Instruments: pens, pencils, markers, fountain pens
  • Desk supplies: staplers, hole punches, adhesive tape, correction fluid
  • Filing: binders, folders, labels
  • Occasion stationery: wedding invitations, greeting cards, thank-you notes

Personal and luxury stationery

Fine stationery — heavyweight cotton paper, engraved or letterpress-printed — remains associated with formal correspondence and calligraphy. Japanese stationery culture, centred on notebooks, washi tape, and precision pens, has a large international following.

Digital era

Email and messaging reduced routine business correspondence, but stationery sales have persisted through journaling, bullet-journal planning, and collectable writing instruments.
