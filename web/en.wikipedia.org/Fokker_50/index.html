Fokker 50

The Fokker 50 is a turboprop-powered regional airliner designed and built by the Dutch aircraft manufacturer Fokker. A successor to the successful Fokker F27 Friendship, it was developed in the 1980s with new engines, modern avionics, and improved aerodynamics. First flown in 1985 and entering service in 1987, the Fokker 50 typically seats 50 passengers.

Development

Although derived from the F27, the Fokker 50 incorporated Pratt & Whitney Canada PW125B turboprops driving six-bladed Dowty propellers, an electronic flight instrument system, redesigned wings with winglets, and extensive use of composite materials. A stretched, more powerful derivative, the high-performance Fokker 60 utility transport, was built in small numbers for the Royal Netherlands Air Force.

Operational history

The type served with regional airlines worldwide, including KLM Cityhopper, SAS Commuter, and carriers in Southeast Asia, Australia, and Africa. Production ended when Fokker went bankrupt in 1996–97, but many aircraft remained in passenger and freighter service into the 2020s, valued for rugged short-field performance.
