Burma Road

The Burma Road was a road linking Burma (now Myanmar) with southwest China, built during the Second Sino-Japanese War to carry supplies to the Republic of China. Running roughly 1,150 kilometres (717 miles) from Lashio in Burma to Kunming in Yunnan province, it crossed mountainous terrain via winding switchbacks and the Salween and Mekong river gorges.

History

The road was constructed in 1937–38 by some 200,000 Chinese labourers working largely by hand. After Japan occupied China's seaports, the Burma Road became China's lifeline for war materiel landed at Rangoon and railed to Lashio. Under British pressure, Britain temporarily closed the road in 1940, but reopened it months later. When Japanese forces conquered Burma in 1942, the road was cut, prompting the Allies to supply China by air over "the Hump" of the Himalayas and to build the Ledo Road (later called the Stilwell Road) from India, which rejoined the Burma Road route in 1945.

Legacy

The road remains part of the highway network of Myanmar and Yunnan, and its wartime role is commemorated in both countries. Its construction is regarded as a remarkable feat of engineering under primitive conditions.
