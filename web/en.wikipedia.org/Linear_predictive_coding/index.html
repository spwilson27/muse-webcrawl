Linear predictive coding

Linear predictive coding (LPC) is a method used in audio signal processing and speech processing to represent the spectral envelope of a digital speech signal in compressed form, using the information of a linear predictive model. A speech sample is approximated as a linear combination of previous samples, s[n] ≈ Σ ak s[n−k], and the prediction coefficients are computed by minimising the squared prediction error, typically via the autocorrelation method and the Levinson–Durbin algorithm.

Principle

LPC is based on a source–filter model of speech production: the vocal tract acts as a resonant filter whose spectral shape is captured by an all-pole model, while the excitation is either periodic (voiced speech) or noise-like (unvoiced speech). Because the coefficients change slowly, only a small number of parameters per frame need be transmitted or stored, enabling very low bit-rate speech coding.

Applications

LPC underlies classic speech codecs such as the LPC-10 (FS-1015) vocoder and the GSM full-rate codec, and its descendants — including code-excited linear prediction (CELP) — are used in modern mobile telephony. LPC analysis is also used in formant estimation, speech recognition front-ends, and audio effects such as cross-synthesis and "talk box" processing.
