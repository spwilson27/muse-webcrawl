Jangarh Singh Shyam

Jangarh Singh Shyam (1962 – 2001) was a pioneering contemporary Indian artist from the Gond tribe of central India. He was the founder of a new school of Indian tribal art, sometimes called Jangarh Kalem ("the Jangarh style"), which adapted the dot-and-line traditions of Gond wall painting to paper and canvas.

Born: 1962, Patangarh, Madhya Pradesh, India

Died: 2001, Tokamachi, Japan

Known for: Gond art, painting

Movement: Jangarh Kalem

Life and career

Born into a Pardhan Gond family in the village of Patangarh in Madhya Pradesh, Shyam worked as a cowherd before his talent was discovered in 1981 by artists and scouts of Bharat Bhavan, the multi-arts complex in Bhopal, under the guidance of Jagdish Swaminathan. His large mural for the Bharat Bhavan building brought Gond imagery — animals, trees, deities and scenes of village life rendered in dense fields of coloured dots — to national attention.

Shyam exhibited internationally, including at the Pompidou Centre's Magiciens de la terre exhibition in Paris (1989), and undertook residencies in Japan. He mentored many younger Gond artists, transforming the economic prospects of tribal art in the region. He died in Japan in 2001 while on a residency at the Mithila Museum in Tokamachi. His wife Nankusia Shyam and children Mayank and Japani Shyam continued the tradition.
