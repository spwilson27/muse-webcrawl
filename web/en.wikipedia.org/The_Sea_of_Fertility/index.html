The Sea of Fertility

The Sea of Fertility (Japanese: 豊饒の海, Hōjō no Umi) is a tetralogy of novels written by the Japanese author Yukio Mishima. The four novels are Spring Snow (1969), Runaway Horses (1969), The Temple of Dawn (1970) and The Decay of the Angel (1971).

Mishima delivered the final manuscript on the morning of 25 November 1970, the day he committed seppuku, and considered the tetralogy his masterpiece and farewell work.

Structure and themes

The novels follow the lawyer Shigekuni Honda from 1912 to the 1970s as he believes he successively encounters reincarnations of his school friend Kiyoaki Matsugae — as the nationalist Isao, the Thai princess Ying Chan, and the orphan Tōru. Themes include reincarnation, the decline of Japan, beauty, and the emptiness at the cycle's end.

The novels

  • Spring Snow — a doomed aristocratic romance in Taishō-era Tokyo.
  • Runaway Horses — a young kendo enthusiast plots a right-wing uprising in 1932.
  • The Temple of Dawn — Honda in Thailand and India amid wartime and postwar change.
  • The Decay of the Angel — Honda, old and cynical, adopts Tōru; the abbess Satoko's final revelation closes the cycle.

Reception

The tetralogy is widely regarded as one of the great works of 20th-century Japanese literature.
