Oaxaca

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Church of Santo Domingo de Guzmán, Oaxaca City

Oaxaca, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Oaxaca, is one of the 32 states of Mexico. Located in the country's southwest, it is bordered by Guerrero, Puebla, Veracruz, and Chiapas, with the Pacific Ocean to the south. Its capital and largest city is Oaxaca de Juárez (Oaxaca City). The state has a population of about 4.1 million and is divided into 570 municipalities — more than any other Mexican state.

History and culture

Oaxaca is one of the most ethnically diverse states in Mexico, home to large Zapotec and Mixtec populations among at least sixteen officially recognised indigenous groups, many preserving their own languages. The Zapotec civilisation built the great hilltop city of Monte Albán, founded around 500 BC and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, as is Oaxaca City's historic centre. President Benito Juárez, a Zapotec from Guelatao, was born in the state.

Cuisine and crafts

Oaxacan cuisine is celebrated across Mexico, famous for its seven moles, tlayudas, chapulines (grasshoppers), quesillo cheese, and mezcal, made from agave in the state's central valleys. The state is also known for alebrijes, black pottery from San Bartolo Coyotepec, and woven textiles.

Geography

The terrain is mostly mountainous, formed by the Sierra Madre del Sur and Sierra Madre de Oaxaca; the Isthmus of Tehuantepec crosses the state's east. The Guelaguetza festival each July celebrates the region's indigenous cultures.
