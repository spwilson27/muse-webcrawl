Æthelstan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Æthelstan or Athelstan (c. 894 – 27 October 939) was King of the Anglo-Saxons from 924 to 927 and King of the English from 927 to his death in 939. The grandson of Alfred the Great and son of Edward the Elder, he is regarded as the first king of England, having conquered the last surviving Viking kingdom, York, in 927 and gained overlordship over the kings of Scotland and Wales.

Reign

Æthelstan's victory at the Battle of Brunanburh in 937, against a coalition of Scots, Vikings, and Strathclyde Britons, secured his rule and was celebrated in a famous Old English poem preserved in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle. His court was a center of learning and reform with wide continental connections; he collected relics and books, patronized scholars, and issued extensive law codes. His coinage and charters styled him "king of the whole of Britain" (rex totius Britanniae).

Legacy

Though later overshadowed by his grandfather Alfred, Æthelstan is increasingly recognized by historians as one of the most effective early English kings. He never married and was succeeded by his half-brother Edmund I. He is buried at Malmesbury Abbey in Wiltshire.
