Solfatara (volcano)

LocationPozzuoli, Campania, Italy
Volcanic complexPhlegraean Fields
Last eruption1198
ElevationAbout 93 m

Solfatara (Italian: Solfatara di Pozzuoli) is a shallow volcanic crater at Pozzuoli, near Naples, Italy. It is part of the Phlegraean Fields volcanic area. It is a dormant volcano, which still emits streams of steam with sulfurous fumes. Its name comes from the Latin Sulpha terra, "land of sulfur".

Geology and activity

The crater formed around 3,900 years ago and last erupted in 1198, probably with a small phreatic (steam-driven) explosion. The crater floor is a popular tourist attraction, with numerous fumaroles and mud pools, and bright yellow sulfur deposits. The most vigorous vent, the Bocca Grande, emits superheated steam at around 160 °C. Because the area is subject to bradyseism — slow uplift and subsidence — the crater is closely monitored; the surrounding Phlegraean Fields caldera is considered a supervolcano and showed significant unrest in the early 1980s and again in the 2020s.

History

The site was known in antiquity and associated with the god Vulcan and with entrances to the underworld; Strabo described it as the Forum Vulcani. In the Middle Ages its sulfur and alum were mined, and its minerals were used medicinally. In 2017 the area was closed to unsupervised public access after a fatal accident in which three members of a family died in a restricted zone.

Terminology

The name has become generic: a "solfatara" is any natural volcanic fumarole vent emitting sulfurous gases, as at Solfatara-like fields worldwide.
