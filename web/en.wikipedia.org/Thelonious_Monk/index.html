Thelonious Monk

Thelonious Sphere Monk (October 10, 1917 – February 17, 1982) was an American jazz pianist and composer. One of the founders of bebop, he had a highly distinctive improvisational style marked by dissonant harmonies, angular melodies, percussive attack and dramatic use of silence.

BornOctober 10, 1917, Rocky Mount, North Carolina
DiedFebruary 17, 1982, Englewood, New Jersey
GenresJazz, bebop
InstrumentsPiano

Career

Monk was the house pianist at Minton's Playhouse in Harlem, where bebop was forged in the early 1940s. His compositions — including "'Round Midnight", "Blue Monk", "Straight, No Chaser" and "Well, You Needn't" — are among the most recorded jazz standards. His 1957 residency at the Five Spot with John Coltrane marked his breakthrough to wider acclaim.

Legacy

Monk is the second-most-recorded jazz composer after Duke Ellington. The Thelonious Monk Institute of Jazz (now the Herbie Hancock Institute) was founded in his honour.
