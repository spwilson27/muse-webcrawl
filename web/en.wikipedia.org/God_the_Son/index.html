God the Son

God the Son is the second person of the Trinity in Christian theology. The doctrine of the Trinity holds that God is one being who exists eternally as three distinct persons — the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit — who are each fully God. God the Son is held to be distinct from but consubstantial with the Father and the Spirit: begotten, not made, and eternally proceeding from the Father.

In mainstream Christian belief, God the Son became incarnate as Jesus of Nazareth — fully God and fully human — born of the Virgin Mary. Through the Incarnation, the crucifixion, and the resurrection, the Son is held to accomplish the salvation of humanity. He is also identified with the Logos, the divine Word described in the opening of the Gospel of John: "the Word was with God, and the Word was God."

Theological development

The New Testament applies divine titles and worship to Jesus and speaks of the Son's pre-existence and role in creation. In the early centuries of the Church, disputes over how the Son could be both divine and distinct from the Father led to the ecumenical councils. The First Council of Nicaea (325) declared the Son to be "of one substance" (homoousios) with the Father, rejecting Arianism, which taught that the Son was a created being. The Council of Chalcedon (451) further defined Christ as one person in two natures, fully divine and fully human.

Distinctions

"God the Son" is distinguished from the title "Son of God", which is also applied in scripture to figures such as Israel, angels, and Adam; the term God the Son specifically asserts the Son's full deity. Christian traditions that reject Trinitarian doctrine — including Unitarian, Oneness Pentecostal, and some restorationist movements — interpret the Son's divinity differently.
