Pokémon

Pikachu, the mascot of the Pokémon franchise.

Pokémon (Japanese: ポケットモンスター, "Pocket Monsters") is a Japanese media franchise managed by The Pokémon Company, a joint venture between Nintendo, Game Freak, and Creatures. Created by Satoshi Tajiri in 1996, it is centred on fictional creatures called Pokémon, which humans, known as Pokémon Trainers, catch and train to battle each other for sport.

History

The franchise began as a pair of video games, Pokémon Red and Green, developed by Game Freak and published by Nintendo for the Game Boy in Japan in 1996. Tajiri conceived the idea from his childhood hobby of insect collecting. The games were released internationally in 1998 as Red and Blue.

Media and merchandise

Pokémon expanded into an animated television series (which follows the trainer Ash Ketchum for its first 25 seasons), films, a trading card game, manga, toys, and licensed merchandise of every kind. The mobile game Pokémon Go, released in 2016, became a global phenomenon and one of the most successful mobile games of all time.

With revenue estimated in excess of US$100 billion across all media and merchandise, Pokémon is the highest-grossing media franchise of all time. The video game series alone has sold more than 480 million copies, and over a thousand species of Pokémon have been introduced across nine generations of core games.

Gameplay

In the core role-playing games, players explore a fictional region, capture wild Pokémon using devices called Poké Balls, train them by battling, and compete against other trainers, ultimately challenging an elite group of trainers and a champion. The franchise's slogan is "Gotta Catch 'Em All!".
