An olympiad (Greek: Ὀλυμπιάς) is a period of four years, particularly the interval between consecutive Olympic Games. In ancient Greece, Olympiads provided a standard system for dating events across the Greek world, counting from the first recorded games in 776 BC.

Ancient usage

Events were dated by the year within the Olympiad — for example, "the third year of the 97th Olympiad". The system was used by historians such as Timaeus, Polybius, and Diodorus Siculus, allowing events from different city-states to be synchronised.

Modern usage

In the modern era, "Olympiad" formally denotes the four-year cycle of the Olympic Games, numbered from the first modern Games of 1896; the Games of the XXXII Olympiad were held in Tokyo in 2021. The term is also used for periodic competitions in other fields, such as the International Mathematical Olympiad and other science olympiads for students.
