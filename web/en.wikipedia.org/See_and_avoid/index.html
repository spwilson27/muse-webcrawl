See and avoid

See and avoid is a fundamental principle of aviation safety under which pilots operating in visual meteorological conditions are responsible for visually detecting other aircraft and manoeuvring to avoid a collision. It is the primary method of traffic separation in uncontrolled airspace and under visual flight rules (VFR).

Principle

Under see and avoid, the pilot in command must maintain vigilance to see and avoid other aircraft, terrain, and obstacles. Air traffic control separation services supplement, but do not replace, this responsibility in visual conditions. Regulations in many jurisdictions state that when weather conditions permit, it is the pilot's duty to avoid mid-air collisions regardless of whether the flight is operated under VFR or instrument flight rules (IFR).

Limitations

The human eye has significant limitations as a collision-avoidance sensor. Empty-field myopia, the difficulty of detecting stationary-appearing objects on a collision course, high closure rates, cockpit blind spots, and pilot workload all reduce effectiveness. Studies of mid-air collisions show that many occur in good visibility and daylight, and that an unalerted pilot may have only a few seconds to react once another aircraft becomes visible.

Technology

Because of these limitations, see and avoid is increasingly supplemented by technology: traffic collision avoidance systems (TCAS) in larger aircraft, ADS-B traffic displays, and, for unmanned aircraft, "detect and avoid" (DAA) systems designed to replicate the function for remotely piloted and autonomous aircraft.
