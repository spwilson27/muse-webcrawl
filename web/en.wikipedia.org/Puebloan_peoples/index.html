The Puebloans, or Pueblo peoples, are Native Americans in the Southwestern United States who share common agricultural, material, and religious practices. The word pueblo is the Spanish word both for "town" and for "people"; it refers to their distinctive multi-storey adobe and stone dwellings. When the Spanish arrived in the mid-16th century, they found these multi-story towns, which had been built and occupied for centuries by peoples descended from the Ancestral Puebloans (formerly called Anasazi) and related cultures such as the Mogollon and Hohokam.

Culture

Pueblo communities are traditionally centred on terraced apartment-like buildings of adobe or sandstone around plazas, with ceremonial rooms called kivas. Their agriculture, adapted to an arid land, centres on the "three sisters" of maize, beans, and squash, supplemented by cotton and turkeys. Religious life involves katsina (kachina) ceremonies, clan and society organisations, and calendars tied to the agricultural year; pottery, weaving, jewellery (notably turquoise and silver work), and basketry are renowned crafts. The Pueblo peoples speak languages from four unrelated families—Keresan, Tanoan, Zuni (an isolate), and Hopi—reflecting distinct origins unified by shared lifeways.

History

Ancestral Puebloans built the great houses of Chaco Canyon and the cliff dwellings of Mesa Verde between about 900 and 1300 CE before migrating to the Rio Grande valley and other areas. Spanish colonisation from 1598 brought missions, tribute demands, and repression of native religion, provoking the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 under Popé of Ohkay Owingeh, which expelled the Spanish for twelve years—the most successful Indigenous uprising in North American colonial history. Reconquest followed in 1692–1696, after which a more accommodated colonial order emerged.

Under the United States, Pueblo land grants were partly confirmed, and the Pueblo Lands Act of 1924 addressed encroachment. The 20th century saw both cultural preservation and landmark assertions of rights, including the return of Blue Lake to Taos Pueblo in 1970. Today there are nineteen federally recognised Pueblos in New Mexico, the Hopi Tribe in Arizona, and Ysleta del Sur Pueblo in Texas, each a sovereign community.
