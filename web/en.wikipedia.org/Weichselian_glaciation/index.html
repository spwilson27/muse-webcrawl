Weichselian glaciation

Periodc. 115,000 – 11,700 years BP
RegionNorthern Europe, Scandinavia, northern Germany and Poland
Named afterWeichsel (Vistula) river
EquivalentWisconsin glaciation (North America), Würm glaciation (Alps)

The Weichselian glaciation was the last glacial period affecting northern Europe, named after the German name of the Vistula River (Weichsel) in Poland. It lasted from about 115,000 years ago until about 11,700 years ago, when the Holocene began.

Extent

At its maximum, around 20,000–24,000 years ago, the Fennoscandian Ice Sheet covered Scandinavia, the Baltic region, and parts of northern Germany, Poland and northwestern Russia, reaching thicknesses of up to roughly 3 km over the Gulf of Bothnia area. Sea levels fell by around 120 metres worldwide.

Stages

The glaciation is divided into the Early, Middle (Pleniglacial) and Late Weichselian. The coldest phase, the Last Glacial Maximum, was followed by rapid deglaciation interrupted by cold reversals such as the Younger Dryas. Meltwater shaped landscapes including tunnel valleys, outwash plains and the Baltic Ice Lake.

Legacy

Weichselian ice and meltwater created much of the modern landscape of northern Europe: moraines, eskers, drumlins and the lake districts of Finland and Sweden. The weight of the ice depressed the crust, and post-glacial rebound continues today, especially around the Gulf of Bothnia.
