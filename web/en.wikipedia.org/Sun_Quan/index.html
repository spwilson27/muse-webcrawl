Sun Quan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sun Quan (182 – 21 May 252), courtesy name Zhongmou, was the founder of the Eastern Wu dynasty, one of the Three Kingdoms of China. He inherited control of the warlord regime established in the Jiangdong region by his elder brother, Sun Ce, and his father, Sun Jian, and proclaimed himself emperor of Wu in 229.

Sun Quan was born in 182, while his father Sun Jian was serving as an official of the Han dynasty. After Sun Ce's assassination in 200, the eighteen-year-old Sun Quan took over the territories in the lower Yangtze region and, advised by capable officials such as Zhang Zhao and Zhou Yu, consolidated his rule.

Reign

In 208, Sun Quan allied with Liu Bei against the northern warlord Cao Cao; their combined forces won the decisive Battle of Red Cliffs, preserving the independence of the south. Over the following decades Wu fought both Cao Wei and Shu Han, capturing Jing Province from Liu Bei in 219 and defeating Shu at the Battle of Xiaoting in 222.

Sun Quan formally declared himself emperor in 229, taking the era name Huanglong, and moved his capital to Jianye (modern Nanjing). His reign saw Wu become a maritime power, sending expeditions as far as Taiwan and Southeast Asia and receiving envoys from distant states.

Final years

Sun Quan's later years were marred by a prolonged succession struggle between his sons Sun He and Sun Ba, which weakened the court and led to the purge or exile of many officials. He died in 252 at the age of 70 and was succeeded by his youngest son, Sun Liang. He was posthumously honoured as Emperor Da of Wu ("Great Emperor of Wu").
