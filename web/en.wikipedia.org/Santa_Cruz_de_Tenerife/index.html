Santa Cruz de Tenerife, commonly known as Santa Cruz, is a city and port on the island of Tenerife in the Canary Islands, Spain. It is the capital of the island and of the province of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, and it shares the status of capital of the autonomous community of the Canary Islands with Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. Its port is among the most important in Spain, and its carnival is one of the largest in the world.

History

The area was inhabited by the Guanche people before the Castilian conquest of Tenerife. Alonso Fernández de Lugo founded Santa Cruz in 1494 during the final campaign for the island. In July 1797 the city famously repelled a British attack led by Rear-Admiral Horatio Nelson, who lost his right arm in the failed assault on the port — an event commemorated annually in the Gesta re-enactment. Santa Cruz grew with Atlantic trade, coaling and later oil refining and tourism; during the Spanish Civil War it was where General Franco was stationed when the military uprising of July 1936 began.

Landmarks and culture

The city's skyline is defined by the sweeping white Auditorio de Tenerife, designed by Santiago Calatrava and opened in 2003, and by the twin Torres de Santa Cruz, among the tallest residential towers in Spain. Landmarks include the Plaza de España, rebuilt around the Monumento a los Caídos with an artificial lake; the Iglesia de la Concepción; the TEA (Tenerife Espacio de las Artes); and César Manrique's Parque Marítimo seafront pools. The Carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, held each February, rivals Rio de Janeiro's in scale and was declared a Festival of International Tourist Interest.

Economy

The Port of Santa Cruz handles cargo, ferries, cruise ships and offshore oil-rig servicing for West Africa, and the city's refinery was long a pillar of the island economy. Santa Cruz is the commercial and administrative heart of Tenerife, while the neighbouring city of San Cristóbal de La Laguna hosts the university.
