Orchid

Orchids are plants of the family Orchidaceae, one of the two largest families of flowering plants, with roughly 28,000 accepted species in about 760 genera. They are found on every continent except Antarctica, in habitats from tropical rainforest to tundra, and are famed for their intricate, often highly specialized flowers and their relationships with pollinators and fungi.

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Asparagales
Family:Orchidaceae Juss.

Characteristics

Orchid flowers are bilaterally symmetric, with three sepals and three petals, one petal modified into a labellum that serves as a landing platform for pollinators. Pollen is packaged into masses called pollinia. Many orchids are epiphytes growing on trees in the tropics, while temperate species are terrestrial; nearly all depend on mycorrhizal fungi to germinate their dust-like seeds, which lack endosperm. Charles Darwin's 1862 book on orchid fertilization by insects remains a classic study of coevolution.

Pollination

Orchids display extraordinary pollination strategies, including food and sexual deception: some species mimic female insects so precisely that males attempt to mate with the flowers. Others, such as the Madagascan star orchid Angraecum sesquipedale, evolved nectar spurs matching the tongues of specific hawkmoths.

Human uses

Orchids are among the most popular ornamental plants, with genera such as Phalaenopsis, Cattleya, and Dendrobium bred into tens of thousands of hybrids. The family also gives us vanilla, from the pods of Vanilla planifolia, one of the world's most valuable spices. All orchids are listed under CITES to protect wild populations from over-collection.
