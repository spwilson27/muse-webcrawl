Stem cell therapy

Field: Regenerative medicine
Established use: Haematopoietic stem cell transplantation
Cell sources: Bone marrow, adipose tissue, umbilical cord blood, iPSCs

Stem cell therapy is the use of stem cells to treat or prevent a disease or condition. Stem cells are cells that can differentiate into specialised cell types and can self-renew to produce more stem cells, offering the possibility of repairing damaged tissues.

Established treatments

The longest-established and most widely used stem cell therapy is haematopoietic stem cell transplantation — bone marrow transplant — used since the 1960s to treat leukaemias, lymphomas and certain immune and genetic blood disorders. Stem cell–based skin grafts are also used for severe burns, and limbal stem cell therapy can restore damaged corneas.

Research and controversy

Researchers are investigating stem cells for conditions including Parkinson's disease, spinal cord injury, heart disease and type 1 diabetes, using adult stem cells, embryonic stem cells, and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). Most such applications remain experimental. Regulators have warned against unproven commercial "stem cell clinics" marketing treatments without evidence, which have caused serious harm to some patients. Ethical debate has surrounded the use of embryonic stem cells, though iPSC technology avoids many of those concerns.
