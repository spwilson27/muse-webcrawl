Cultivar

A cultivar (from "cultivated variety") is a plant that people have selected for desired traits and which retains those traits when propagated. Most cultivars have arisen in cultivation, but a few are special selections from the wild. Popular ornamental plants such as roses, camellias, tulips, and rhododendrons are cultivars produced by careful breeding or selection of sport mutations.

Nomenclature

Cultivar names are governed by the International Code of Nomenclature for Cultivated Plants (ICNCP). A cultivar name consists of a botanical name followed by a cultivar epithet in single quotes, written in roman type, for example Malus domestica 'Granny Smith'. Cultivars must be distinct, uniform, and stable in their characteristics.

Propagation

Many cultivars, especially fruit trees and ornamental shrubs, are propagated vegetatively — by grafting, cuttings, or tissue culture — because they do not come true from seed. Others, such as many vegetable varieties, are maintained as true-breeding seed lines. Commercial cultivars may be protected by plant breeders' rights.
