Superachromat

Diagram of a superachromatic lens bringing four wavelengths to a common focus.

A superachromat or superachromatic lens is a lens system that brings at least four wavelengths of light to a common focus. Ordinary achromatic doublets correct chromatic aberration for two wavelengths (typically red and blue), and apochromatic lenses correct for three; the superachromat extends the correction to four wavelengths, substantially reducing the residual secondary and tertiary spectrum across a broad band of wavelengths.

Design

Superachromatic correction requires a careful choice of optical glasses whose partial dispersions deviate from the "normal line" in a complementary way. Designers combine glasses with anomalous partial dispersion — often including fluorite or fluorophosphate ED glasses — in three or more elements so that the focal positions for four selected wavelengths coincide. The residual focus shift between those wavelengths (the tertiary spectrum) is then very small across the visible spectrum, and good correction can also extend into the near infrared or near ultraviolet.

Applications

Superachromatic lenses are used where extremely low chromatic aberration is needed over a wide spectral range, for example in microscopy, high-quality telescope objectives, aerial and reconnaissance photography, and precision measuring instruments. The term is also applied to some photographic telephoto lenses marketed in the mid twentieth century whose designs incorporated fluorite elements for improved correction.
