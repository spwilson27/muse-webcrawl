Vâlcea County (Romanian pronunciation: [ˈvɨltʃe̯a]) is a county (județ) of Romania, in the historical regions of Oltenia and Wallachia. Its capital and largest city is Râmnicu Vâlcea, on the Olt River.

Geography

The county spans the Southern Carpathians in the north — including parts of the Făgăraș and Cozia mountains and the Cozia National Park — and the hills and plains along the Olt valley in the south. It borders Argeș, Sibiu, Alba, Hunedoara, Gorj, Dolj and Olt counties.

Attractions

Vâlcea is known for its monasteries, notably the UNESCO-listed Monastery of Horezu, founded by Prince Constantin Brâncoveanu, and for spa resorts such as Călimănești-Căciulata and Băile Olănești. The Transalpina road passes through the county's northwest.
