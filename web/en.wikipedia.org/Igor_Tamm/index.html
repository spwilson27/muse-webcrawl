Igor Tamm

Born: 8 July 1895, Vladivostok, Russian Empire
Died: 12 April 1971, Moscow, Soviet Union
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (1958)

Igor Yevgenyevich Tamm (8 July 1895 – 12 April 1971) was a Soviet physicist who received the 1958 Nobel Prize in Physics, jointly with Pavel Cherenkov and Ilya Frank, for their explanation of Cherenkov radiation. He also proposed, together with Andrei Sakharov, the tokamak concept for controlled thermonuclear fusion.

Life and work

Tamm was born in Vladivostok and studied physics at Moscow State University, where he later taught. In 1934, working with Ilya Frank, he developed the theoretical explanation of the blue glow emitted when charged particles pass through a medium faster than the speed of light in that medium — the Cherenkov effect — work that was recognised by the Nobel Prize a quarter-century later.

Fusion research

Around 1950, Tamm and Sakharov proposed confining hot plasma in a toroidal magnetic field — the tokamak — which became the leading design in magnetic confinement fusion research worldwide. Tamm also headed a group in the Soviet hydrogen bomb project, though he later distanced himself from weapons work. He was a mentor to many leading Soviet physicists, including Sakharov and Vitaly Ginzburg.
