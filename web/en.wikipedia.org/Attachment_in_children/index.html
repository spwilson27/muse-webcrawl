Attachment in children

Attachment in children refers to the emotional bond that develops between an infant or young child and their primary caregivers. Attachment theory, developed by John Bowlby and elaborated by Mary Ainsworth, holds that this bond is a fundamental human need that shapes social, emotional and cognitive development.

Attachment styles

Ainsworth's Strange Situation procedure identified patterns including secure attachment, anxious-avoidant attachment and anxious-ambivalent attachment. Mary Main later added a fourth category, disorganized attachment, often associated with frightening or inconsistent caregiving.

Development

Attachment behaviours such as proximity seeking, separation protest and use of the caregiver as a secure base emerge over the first two years of life. Sensitive, responsive caregiving is associated with secure attachment, which predicts later peer competence and emotional regulation, though outcomes are probabilistic rather than deterministic.

Disorders

Severely disrupted care can lead to reactive attachment disorder and disinhibited social engagement disorder, both described in the DSM-5 and associated with institutional deprivation or repeated changes of caregiver.
