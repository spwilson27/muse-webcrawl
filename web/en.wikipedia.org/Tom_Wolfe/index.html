Thomas Kennerly Wolfe Jr. (2 March 1930 – 14 May 2018) was an American author and journalist widely known for his association with New Journalism, a style of news writing and journalism developed in the 1960s and 1970s that incorporated literary techniques. Famous for his exuberant prose — laden with exclamation marks, italics, and onomatopoeia — he was almost equally recognisable for his trademark white suit.

Nonfiction

Wolfe's early collections The Kandy-Kolored Tangerine-Flake Streamline Baby (1965) and The Pump House Gang (1968) chronicled American popular culture. The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test (1968) followed Ken Kesey and the Merry Pranksters, and The Right Stuff (1979), about the Mercury astronauts and test pilots such as Chuck Yeager, became his best-known work and a successful film. Radical Chic & Mau-Mauing the Flak Catchers (1970) coined "radical chic" and popularised the "Me Decade" label for the 1970s.

Novels

His first novel, The Bonfire of the Vanities (1987), a sweeping portrait of ambition, race, and Wall Street in 1980s New York, was a bestseller. Later novels include A Man in Full (1998), I Am Charlotte Simmons (2004), and Back to Blood (2012).
