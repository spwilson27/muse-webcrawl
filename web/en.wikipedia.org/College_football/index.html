College football

College football is gridiron football played by teams of student athletes fielded by American universities, colleges, and military academies. It is one of the most popular spectator sports in the United States. The first game, between Rutgers and Princeton in 1869, is considered the birth of American football. The sport is governed chiefly by the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA), with the NAIA and junior college leagues covering smaller programs.

Organization

Programs are grouped into conferences and divisions. The top tier, the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS), culminates in the College Football Playoff, expanded to 12 teams in 2024, plus dozens of bowl games. The Football Championship Subdivision (FCS), Division II, and Division III hold their own playoffs. Recruiting, scholarships, and transfer rules regulate the sport, and since 2021 athletes may profit from their name, image, and likeness (NIL).

Culture and economics

College football is central to university identity in much of the South and Midwest; stadiums such as Michigan Stadium and Neyland Stadium hold over 100,000 spectators, and marching bands, fight songs, and fierce rivalries (for example, Michigan–Ohio State and Alabama–Auburn in the Iron Bowl) define the culture. Top programs generate hundreds of millions of dollars in media rights, and the sport is the main talent pipeline for the National Football League.

History

Formative figures include Walter Camp, who devised downs, the line of scrimmage, and standardized scoring in the 1880s, and Knute Rockne, whose Notre Dame teams popularized the forward pass. The Heisman Trophy, awarded since 1935, honours the most outstanding player each season.
