Long barrow

A long barrow is a rectangular or trapezoidal tumulus traditionally interpreted as a collective burial place, built during the Neolithic period, roughly 4000–2500 BCE, across western Europe.

Construction

Earthen long barrows were mounds flanked by quarry ditches, sometimes with timber or stone mortuary structures at one end. Chambered long barrows, such as West Kennet in Wiltshire, contain stone galleries and side chambers holding human remains.

Distribution

Thousands survive in Britain, France, Germany, Poland and Scandinavia. Non-megalithic and megalithic traditions differ regionally, and forms include long cairns in Scotland and hunebeds in the Netherlands.

Interpretation

Barrows likely served as territorial markers and ritual monuments as well as tombs; excavations reveal disarticulated remains of dozens of individuals, grave goods and evidence of repeated use.
