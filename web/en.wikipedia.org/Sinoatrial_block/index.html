Sinoatrial block

A sinoatrial block (also called an SA exit block) is a disorder in the normal rhythm of the heart, in which the electrical impulse generated in the sinoatrial (SA) node is blocked before it can enter the atrial tissues and depolarise the atria. It is a form of heart block distinguished from atrioventricular block, in which the impulse is delayed or blocked at the AV node.

Classification

SA blocks are classified in a similar manner to AV blocks:

  • First degree: delayed conduction from the SA node to the atria; it cannot be detected on a surface ECG because the SA node discharge itself is not visible.
  • Second degree, type I (Wenckebach): progressive shortening of the P-P interval until a P wave fails to appear, producing a pause shorter than twice the preceding cycle.
  • Second degree, type II: intermittent failure of impulse exit without preceding prolongation; the pause is approximately twice the normal P-P interval.
  • Third degree (complete): no impulses reach the atria; indistinguishable on ECG from sinus arrest, with an escape rhythm taking over.

Causes and treatment

Causes include intrinsic sinus node disease, increased vagal tone, ischaemia, fibrosis, and drugs such as beta blockers, calcium channel blockers and digoxin. Asymptomatic blocks often require no treatment beyond removing offending drugs; symptomatic cases causing syncope may require a pacemaker.
