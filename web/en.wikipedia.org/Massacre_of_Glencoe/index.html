Massacre of Glencoe

Date13 February 1692
LocationGlencoe, Scotland
PerpetratorsGovernment troops under Captain Robert Campbell

The Massacre of Glencoe took place in Glen Coe in the Scottish Highlands on 13 February 1692. Soldiers of the Earl of Argyll's Regiment of Foot, who had been billeted with the MacDonalds of Glencoe for twelve days, turned on their hosts in the early morning, killing 38 men, women, and children; many more died of exposure fleeing into the hills.

Background

The massacre followed the Glorious Revolution: clan chiefs were required to swear allegiance to William III by 1 January 1692. MacIain, chief of the Glencoe MacDonalds, arrived late through bad weather and official delays, a technicality used to justify making an example of his clan.

Aftermath

A parliamentary inquiry in 1695 declared the killings murder, but no one was punished. The event became an enduring symbol of betrayal in Scottish memory, remembered as murder under trust.
