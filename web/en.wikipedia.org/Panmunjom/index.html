Panmunjom, also known as Panmunjeom, is a former village just north of the de facto border between North and South Korea in the Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ). It was the site of the negotiations that ended the Korean War and is now famous for the Joint Security Area (JSA), where North and South Korean forces stand face to face.

History

The original village of Panmunjom was a small settlement of farmers. During the Korean War it became the venue for armistice talks beginning in 1951; the Korean Armistice Agreement was signed there on 27 July 1953. The village itself lay within what became the DMZ, and its inhabitants were not permitted to return. The nearby JSA, established under the armistice, contains a series of buildings — including the blue-roofed conference row bisected by the Military Demarcation Line — used for meetings between the two Koreas and the United Nations Command.

Incidents and diplomacy

Panmunjom has been the scene of dramatic episodes, including the 1976 axe murder incident in the JSA, defections by soldiers across the line, and telephone hotline communications between Seoul and Pyongyang. On 27 April 2018, it hosted the inter-Korean summit between South Korean president Moon Jae-in and North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, who symbolically crossed the demarcation line in both directions; the Panmunjom Declaration for peace was signed there. In June 2019, U.S. president Donald Trump met Kim at the site as well.

Tourism

The JSA is one of the most visited sites in the DMZ by foreign tourists, who attend briefings and briefly step across the border line inside the conference rooms under guard. Tours have periodically been suspended during periods of tension or public-health restrictions.
