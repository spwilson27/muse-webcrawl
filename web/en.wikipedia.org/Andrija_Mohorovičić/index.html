Andrija Mohorovičić

Andrija Mohorovičić (23 January 1857 – 18 December 1936) was a Croatian geophysicist, meteorologist, and seismologist. He is best known for discovering the Mohorovičić discontinuity — the boundary between the Earth's crust and mantle, usually called the Moho — one of the foundational discoveries of modern geophysics.

Life

Mohorovičić was born in Volosko, near Opatija, and studied mathematics and physics at Charles University in Prague, where one of his teachers was Ernst Mach. He taught at the nautical school in Bakar and then at the University of Zagreb, where he directed the meteorological observatory and founded Croatia's seismological service and time service.

Discovery of the Moho

Studying seismograms of the 8 October 1909 earthquake in the Kupa Valley (near Zagreb), Mohorovičić noticed that seismic waves arrived at distant stations in distinctive sets, implying that some waves were refracted along a deeper, faster layer. He concluded that the Earth's crust rests on a distinct mantle, separated by a sharp boundary at a depth he estimated at about 54 km (modern estimates vary from about 5–10 km beneath oceans to 30–70 km beneath continents). The discontinuity bearing his name, presented in his 1910 paper, was the first strong seismological evidence for the layered structure of the Earth's interior.

Other work

Mohorovičić also worked on meteorology, including tornadoes and wind patterns, improved seismograph instrumentation in Zagreb, and proposed seismic methods for exploring the subsurface that anticipated later techniques. A crater on the far side of the Moon and the asteroid 8422 Mohorovičić are named after him.
