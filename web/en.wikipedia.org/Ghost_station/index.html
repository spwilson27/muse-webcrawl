Ghost station


Illustration: Ghost station

A ghost station is a disused metro or railway station through which trains pass without stopping, often leaving the station area abandoned or dimly lit. The term is especially associated with stations of the Berlin U-Bahn and S-Bahn that were closed during the division of the city.

Berlin Geisterbahnhöfe

After the construction of the Berlin Wall in 1961, several West Berlin transit lines passed beneath East Berlin territory. Stations on East Berlin soil were closed and guarded, becoming "Geisterbahnhöfe" — ghost stations — where trains slowed but never stopped. Nordbahnhof and Potsdamer Platz were among them. They reopened after reunification in 1990. The lines were operated by the Berliner Verkehrsbetriebe (BVG).

Other examples

Examples elsewhere include abandoned stations on the London Underground such as Aldwych, the never-opened stations of the Cincinnati Subway, and New York City's City Hall station.
