Molecule

A molecule is a group of two or more atoms held together by attractive forces known as chemical bonds. Depending on context, the term may or may not include ions. In quantum physics, organic chemistry, and biochemistry, the distinction from ions is dropped and molecule is often used when referring to polyatomic ions.

Overview

A molecule may be homonuclear, composed of atoms of a single chemical element — as with two atoms in the oxygen molecule (O2) — or heteronuclear, a chemical compound composed of more than one element, as with water (two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom, H2O). Molecular science is called molecular physics when it focuses on physical properties and molecular chemistry when it focuses on chemical structure and reactivity.

Properties

Molecules have characteristic sizes, shapes, and bond geometries. Most molecules are far too small to be seen with the naked eye, although exceptions include many polymers and macromolecules such as DNA. The smallest molecule is the diatomic hydrogen (H2), with a bond length of 0.74 Å. Substances whose particles are molecules are held together in bulk by relatively weak intermolecular forces, which explains their comparatively low melting and boiling points.

Molecular formulas

The composition of a molecule is expressed by its chemical formula, such as C6H12O6 for glucose. Structural formulas and three-dimensional models convey additional information about connectivity and geometry.
