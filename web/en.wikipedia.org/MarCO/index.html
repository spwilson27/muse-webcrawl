MarCO

Full name: Mars Cube One
Operator: NASA / Jet Propulsion Laboratory
Launched: 5 May 2018 (with InSight)
Spacecraft: Two 6U CubeSats (MarCO-A and MarCO-B)

Mars Cube One (MarCO) was a NASA mission of two small CubeSat spacecraft that flew by Mars in 2018, becoming the first interplanetary CubeSats. The twin craft, MarCO-A and MarCO-B — nicknamed "Eve" and "Wall-E" after the film characters — demonstrated that inexpensive miniaturised spacecraft could survive deep space and relay data.

Mission

MarCO launched on 5 May 2018 aboard the same Atlas V rocket as the InSight Mars lander, then separated and flew to Mars on their own trajectories. During InSight's entry, descent and landing on 26 November 2018, the CubeSats relayed the lander's telemetry to Earth in near real time, a task that would otherwise have waited hours for an orbiting spacecraft. MarCO-B also returned a farewell image of Mars as it flew past.

Design

Each spacecraft was a 6-unit CubeSat, roughly briefcase-sized, with deployable solar panels, a flat high-gain antenna panel for X-band communications, cold-gas thrusters (which inspired the Wall-E nickname) and miniaturised radios and propulsion. Contact was lost in early 2019 as the pair receded from the Sun, and the mission was declared complete, having exceeded its objectives and influenced later small deep-space missions.
