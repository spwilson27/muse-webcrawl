Geosteering

Geosteering is the real-time adjustment of the direction of a borehole during drilling, based on geological information obtained while drilling, in order to place the wellbore in the most productive part of a reservoir. It is most commonly used in the oil and gas industry for drilling long horizontal wells through thin hydrocarbon-bearing layers.

Overview

In directional drilling, a well is steered according to a pre-drilling plan based on seismic surveys and offset-well data. Because the subsurface inevitably differs in detail from the model, geosteering updates that plan as the well is drilled: measurements taken near the bit are transmitted to the surface, interpreted against the geological model, and steering commands sent back downhole to keep the well within the target zone ("geosteering in the zone") or to approach a planned target ("geosteering to the target").

Measurements

Geosteering relies on logging while drilling (LWD) and measurement while drilling (MWD) tools. Key measurements include gamma ray, resistivity, density and neutron porosity readings that identify rock type and fluid content. Azimuthal and deep-reading resistivity tools can detect bed boundaries several metres from the wellbore, allowing the driller to anticipate approaching the top or base of the reservoir and to correct the trajectory before exiting the pay zone. Downhole data are typically transmitted by mud-pulse telemetry.

Steering technology

Modern wells are steered with rotary steerable systems or steerable mud motors that can change inclination and azimuth while drilling. Survey measurements define the well's position, and geosteering software compares the incoming logs with type logs from the model in real time, often with geosteering specialists working from remote operations centres around the clock.

Applications

Geosteering is standard practice in unconventional shale plays, offshore extended-reach wells, and thin-bedded reservoirs where a conventional planned trajectory would spend much of its length outside the productive interval. By maximising reservoir contact, geosteering can significantly increase production and recovery from a single wellbore.
