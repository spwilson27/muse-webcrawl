The Hunchback of Notre-Dame (French: Notre-Dame de Paris, "Our Lady of Paris") is a French Gothic novel by Victor Hugo, published in 1831. Set in Paris in 1482, during the reign of Louis XI, it revolves around the Cathedral of Notre-Dame and intertwines the fates of the beautiful Romani street dancer Esmeralda; Quasimodo, the cathedral's deaf, deformed bell-ringer; Claude Frollo, the obsessive archdeacon who raised him; and the vain captain Phoebus de Châteaupers.

Plot summary

During the Festival of Fools, Quasimodo is crowned King of Fools for his ugliness, while the poet Pierre Gringoire stumbles on the Court of Miracles, the underworld of Parisian beggars, and is "married" to Esmeralda after she saves his life. Frollo, consumed by desire for Esmeralda, orders Quasimodo to abduct her; the attempt is foiled by Phoebus, with whom Esmeralda falls in love. Frollo later stabs Phoebus during a rendezvous and Esmeralda is tried for the murder and for witchcraft, tortured, and condemned. Quasimodo rescues her from the gallows into sanctuary in the cathedral — crying "Sanctuary!" — but after the truands storm the cathedral and troops seize her, she is hanged. Quasimodo, watching with Frollo, pushes the archdeacon from the tower, then disappears; years later his skeleton is found embracing Esmeralda's in the charnel house of Montfaucon.

Themes

Hugo wrote the novel partly as a plea for the preservation of Gothic architecture, then widely neglected and "restored" destructively; its famous chapter "This Will Kill That" argues that the printing press would supersede architecture as humanity's great means of expression. The book's success transformed public attitudes to Notre-Dame and helped spur the French historic-monuments movement, leading to the cathedral's great 19th-century restoration by Viollet-le-Duc.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times, notably the 1923 silent film with Lon Chaney, the 1939 film with Charles Laughton, the 1956 French film with Anthony Quinn and Gina Lollobrigida, Disney's animated The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1996), and the French stage musical Notre-Dame de Paris (1998).
