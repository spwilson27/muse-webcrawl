Kīlauea is an active shield volcano on the island of Hawaiʻi, within Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park. One of the world's most active volcanoes, it is the youngest and southeasternmost of the volcanoes forming the island and is traditionally considered the home of the Hawaiian goddess Pele.

Eruptive history

Kīlauea's recorded history includes the explosive eruption of 1790, among the deadliest in United States history. From 1983 to 2018 the volcano erupted nearly continuously at Puʻu ʻŌʻō and other vents on its East Rift Zone, building new land and destroying communities including Kalapana and Kapoho. In 2018 a major lower East Rift Zone eruption and summit caldera collapse ended that long episode. Summit eruptions within Halemaʻumaʻu crater resumed in December 2020 and have recurred in episodes since, largely confined to the national park.

Monitoring

The volcano is monitored by the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory (founded 1912), which tracks earthquakes, ground deformation, and gas emissions to warn of hazards such as lava flows, volcanic smog (vog), and explosive ash events.
