Out-of-home advertising

Also calledOOH advertising, outdoor advertising
FormatsBillboards, transit ads, street furniture, digital screens

Out-of-home advertising (OOH) is advertising that reaches consumers while they are outside their homes. It includes billboards along highways, posters and shelters at bus stops, advertisements on buses and trains, airport displays, and large digital screens in public spaces.

History

Outdoor advertising is among the oldest forms of marketing, from ancient notices and inn signs to the lithographed posters and painted "bull" boards of the nineteenth century. The standardised billboard industry emerged in the United States in the early twentieth century, following the growth of automobile travel.

Formats and measurement

Formats range from static billboards to digital out-of-home (DOOH) panels that rotate messages and can be bought programmatically. Audience measurement relies on traffic counts, mobility data, and visibility studies to estimate impressions. Because audiences cannot easily skip outdoor ads, the medium is valued for reach and frequency, though it is also criticised for visual clutter and is restricted in some jurisdictions — the Brazilian city of São Paulo famously banned most outdoor advertising in 2007.
