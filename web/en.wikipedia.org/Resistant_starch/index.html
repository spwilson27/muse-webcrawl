Resistant starch

Resistant starch is starch and starch degradation products that escape digestion in the small intestine of healthy individuals and pass to the large intestine, where they are fermented by the gut microbiota. It behaves physiologically much like dietary fibre.

Types

Four main types are recognised: RS1, physically inaccessible starch in seeds and grains; RS2, granular starches such as raw potato and green banana; RS3, retrograded starch formed when cooked starch cools, as in cooked-and-cooled rice or potatoes; and RS4, chemically modified starches.

Health effects

Fermentation of resistant starch produces short-chain fatty acids, especially butyrate, the preferred fuel of colonocytes. Studies associate resistant starch intake with improved insulin sensitivity, reduced postprandial glucose, and beneficial changes in gut microbiota, though evidence for some claimed benefits remains preliminary.
