Gambino crime family

The Gambino crime family is one of the "Five Families" that dominate organized crime activities in New York City under the American Mafia. Founded in the early 20th century, it takes its name from Carlo Gambino, its boss from 1957 to 1976, who expanded it into one of the most powerful crime syndicates in the United States, with operations extending across the country.

History

The family's roots trace to the D'Aquila gang of the 1910s; successive bosses included Vincent Mangano and Albert Anastasia, head of Murder, Inc., whose 1957 assassination in a barber's chair cleared Gambino's path to power. Gambino ruled quietly and profitably, avoiding the spotlight, and died of natural causes in 1976. His successor, Paul Castellano, was murdered in 1985 outside Sparks Steak House in Manhattan on the orders of John Gotti, who seized control and became the flamboyant "Teflon Don."

Decline

Gotti's conviction in 1992 — secured after underboss Sammy "The Bull" Gravano turned government witness — sent him to prison for life and marked the start of the family's decline under sustained RICO prosecutions. Leadership passed through the Gotti family and later bosses, and federal raids, including the large 2011 "Mafia takedown day" arrests, further weakened the organization, though it remains active in construction, extortion, gambling, and other rackets.
