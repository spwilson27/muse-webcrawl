Ovule

In seed plants, the ovule is the structure that gives rise to and contains the female reproductive cells. After fertilisation it develops into a seed. It consists of the nucellus, one or two protective integuments, and the funiculus attaching it to the placenta inside the ovary.

Structure

The micropyle is a small opening in the integuments through which the pollen tube enters. Inside, meiosis produces a megaspore that develops into the embryo sac (female gametophyte), containing the egg cell.

Types

Ovules are classified by orientation — orthotropous (upright), anatropous (inverted, the commonest form), campylotropous and others. In gymnosperms ovules are borne exposed on cone scales; in angiosperms they are enclosed in the ovary.
