Benito Amilcare Andrea Mussolini (29 July 1883 – 28 April 1945) was an Italian politician and journalist who founded Fascism and served as Prime Minister of Italy from the March on Rome in 1922 until his deposition in 1943. Styled Il Duce ("the Leader"), he built a one-party dictatorship, allied Italy with Nazi Germany in the Axis, and led Italy into World War II. Deposed and rescued by Germany in 1943, he headed the puppet Italian Social Republic until he was captured and executed by Italian partisans in April 1945.

Early life and rise to power

Mussolini was born in Predappio in the Romagna, the son of a blacksmith and socialist activist. He became a schoolteacher, then a socialist journalist, editing the party newspaper Avanti! from 1912. Breaking with the Socialists over his support for Italian intervention in World War I, he founded the newspaper Il Popolo d'Italia and, in 1919, the Fasci di Combattimento — the nucleus of Fascism, combining nationalism, anti-socialism, and the grievances of veterans. His paramilitary Blackshirts used violence against socialists and trade unions with the tolerance of landowners and the authorities.

The Fascist regime

After the March on Rome (27–28 October 1922), King Victor Emmanuel III appointed Mussolini Prime Minister at 39, the youngest in Italian history. Following the murder of the socialist deputy Giacomo Matteotti in 1924 and a speech of 3 January 1925 accepting responsibility, Mussolini dismantled liberal institutions and established a dictatorship (1925–26). His regime pursued corporatist economic organisation, public works (including the draining of the Pontine Marshes and the battle for grain), population growth, and imperial expansion. The Lateran Treaty of 1929 settled the Roman Question with the Vatican. In 1935–36 Italy conquered Ethiopia using, among other means, poison gas, prompting League of Nations sanctions and driving Mussolini toward Hitler.

Axis and World War II

Mussolini proclaimed the Rome–Berlin "Axis" in 1936, intervened in the Spanish Civil War on Franco's side, signed the Pact of Steel with Germany (1939), and enacted antisemitic racial laws in 1938. Italy declared war on France and Britain on 10 June 1940. Italian offensives in Greece and North Africa failed and required German rescue; by 1943 Italy had lost its empire, suffered invasion of Sicily, and seen Rome bombed.

Downfall and death

On 25 July 1943 the Fascist Grand Council voted no confidence and the King had Mussolini arrested. German commandos freed him in the Gran Sasso raid (12 September 1943), and he headed the Italian Social Republic at Salò on Lake Garda while Italy split into civil war. On 27 April 1945, fleeing toward Switzerland, he was captured by partisans near Lake Como and shot the next day at Giulino di Mezzegra, together with his mistress Clara Petacci. Their bodies were displayed in Milan's Piazzale Loreto.

Legacy

Mussolini created the template of the fascist leader cult later emulated by Hitler and others. Assessments in Italy remain fiercely contested: he is condemned for dictatorship, political murder, colonial crimes, racial laws, and a catastrophic war, while a revisionist current credits his regime with modernisation — a debate sharpened by the survival of his tomb at Predappio as a site of neo-fascist pilgrimage.
