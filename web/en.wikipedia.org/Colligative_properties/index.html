Colligative properties

Colligative properties are properties of solutions that depend on the ratio of the number of solute particles to the number of solvent particles in a solution, and not on the nature of the chemical species present. The number ratio can be related to the various units for concentration of a solution, for example molarity, molality, or normality.

History

The word colligative is derived from the Latin colligatus meaning bound together. French chemist François-Marie Raoult performed extensive experiments on solutions in the 1880s that led to Raoult's law, establishing that the vapour pressure of a solvent is lowered proportionally to the mole fraction of solute.

Types

The four main colligative properties are relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point (ebullioscopy), depression of freezing point (cryoscopy), and osmotic pressure. For a given solute-solvent mass ratio, all colligative properties are inversely proportional to the molar mass of the solute.

Applications

Freezing point depression is used in de-icing roads and aircraft, in antifreeze, and in cryoscopy to determine molar masses. Osmotic pressure underlies reverse osmosis desalination and medical dialysis. Boiling point elevation is used in cooking and industrial processes.
