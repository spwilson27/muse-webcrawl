Second-language acquisition

Second-language acquisition (SLA), sometimes called second-language learning, is the scientific discipline devoted to studying the process by which people learn a language in addition to their native language(s). The term "second language" refers to any language learned after the first, whether it is the second, third, or fourth.

Key ideas

Researchers have found that learners develop an "interlanguage" — a systematic, evolving linguistic system between the native and target languages. Stephen Krashen's Monitor Model proposed that acquisition is driven by comprehensible input slightly above the learner's current level. Other influential frameworks emphasise interaction, output, and noticing as drivers of development.

Factors affecting acquisition

Age is widely debated: the critical period hypothesis holds that children acquire native-like pronunciation and grammar more readily than adults. Motivation, aptitude, first-language distance from the target language, and exposure also shape outcomes. Learner errors often reflect rule-governed development rather than carelessness, and some errors "fossilise" permanently.

Instruction

Approaches to teaching include communicative language teaching, task-based instruction, and focus on form. Meta-analyses generally find that explicit instruction combined with meaningful practice benefits learners.
