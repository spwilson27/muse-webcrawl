Avian influenza

Avian influenza, commonly known as bird flu, is a type of influenza caused by viruses adapted to birds. The disease is caused by influenza A viruses that occur naturally among wild aquatic birds worldwide and can infect domestic poultry and other bird and animal species.

Classification

Avian influenza viruses are divided into subtypes based on two surface proteins: hemagglutinin (H) and neuraminidase (N). Subtypes such as H5N1 and H7N9 have caused serious disease in humans. Viruses are further classified as low pathogenic (LPAI) or highly pathogenic (HPAI) based on their severity in poultry.

Transmission

Wild waterfowl are considered the natural reservoir of influenza A viruses. Infection spreads through contact with virus shed in saliva, nasal secretions, and feces, as well as through contaminated surfaces and water. Migratory birds can carry the virus over long distances.

Human infections

Human infections are rare and usually result from direct, prolonged contact with infected birds or contaminated environments. Symptoms can range from mild conjunctivitis and flu-like illness to severe pneumonia. Sustained human-to-human transmission of avian influenza viruses has not been observed, though health authorities monitor the viruses closely because of their pandemic potential.

Outbreaks and control

Outbreaks in poultry are controlled through surveillance, culling of infected flocks, movement restrictions, and in some countries vaccination. Since 2020, the H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b has caused unprecedented mortality in wild birds and spread to numerous mammal species.
