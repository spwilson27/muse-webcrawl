Battle of Alcatraz

Date2–4 May 1946
LocationAlcatraz Federal Penitentiary, San Francisco Bay, California
ResultEscape attempt defeated
Deaths2 correctional officers, 3 inmates; 14 officers and 1 inmate wounded

The Battle of Alcatraz was a failed escape attempt at Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary, lasting from 2 to 4 May 1946, in which two guards and three inmates were killed and many others injured. It was the most violent escape attempt in the island prison's history.

The escape attempt

The attempt was planned by Bernard Coy, a bank robber, who had spent months studying guard routines. Working as a cellhouse orderly, Coy used a makeshift bar spreader to widen the bars of the gun gallery, climbed in barefoot, and overpowered the guard, obtaining rifles, pistols, and keys. He freed accomplices including Joseph Cretzer, Marvin Hubbard, Sam Shockley, Miran Thompson, and Clarence Carnes. The prisoners seized control of much of the cellhouse and took nine guards hostage in two cells.

The plan failed because inmate Ed Miller, released from segregation, had jammed the lock of the recreation-yard door with the wrong key, trapping the escapees inside. When guards discovered the situation, alarms sounded; the prisoners shot the hostages in the cells, killing officers William Miller and Harold Stites, though most hostages survived by feigning death or being shielded by colleagues.

Suppression

Warden James A. Johnston requested assistance; United States Marines, led in part by Brigadier General Joseph Vogel and advised by famed Marine officer "Chesty" Puller's staff, joined prison and federal officers. Marines fired rifle grenades and dropped explosives down ventilation shafts into the utility corridor where the ringleaders hid. Coy, Cretzer, and Hubbard were found dead in the corridor on 4 May. Shockley, Thompson, and Carnes were tried for the murders; Shockley and Thompson were executed in the San Quentin gas chamber in December 1948, while Carnes, aged 19, received a second life sentence.

Legacy

The battle remains the defining event of Alcatraz's federal prison era and has been dramatized in books and film. The prison itself closed in 1963, citing high operating costs.
