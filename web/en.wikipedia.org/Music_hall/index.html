Music hall

Music hall is a type of British theatrical entertainment popular from the Victorian era to the mid-20th century. It involved a mixture of popular songs, comedy, speciality acts and variety entertainment, performed in purpose-built venues where audiences drank, ate and socialised during the show.

Origins

Music hall grew out of entertainment in London pubs and supper rooms in the 1830s–1850s; the Canterbury Music Hall (1852) was among the first purpose-built halls. By the 1860s–70s large halls such as the London Palladium's predecessors operated across Britain, run by major syndicates.

Performers and songs

Stars included Marie Lloyd, Dan Leno, Harry Lauder, George Formby and Charlie Chaplin, who began his career in music hall. Famous songs such as "Daisy Bell" and "My Old Man (Said Follow the Van)" emerged from the halls. The form declined with cinema, radio and television, though its style fed into variety theatre and modern stand-up comedy.
