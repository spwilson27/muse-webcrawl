Boleslaus II, Duke of Bohemia

Boleslaus II the Pious (Czech: Boleslav II. Pobožný; c. 920 – 7 February 999) was a member of the Přemyslid dynasty and Duke of Bohemia from 972 until his death, succeeding his father Boleslaus I, "the Cruel".

Reign

Boleslaus II consolidated Přemyslid rule and supported the Christianization of Bohemia. In 973 he secured the establishment of the Bishopric of Prague, attached to the Archbishopric of Mainz, ending Bohemia's ecclesiastical dependence on Regensburg; its first bishop was Thietmar, followed by the celebrated Adalbert (Vojtěch), later canonized as a martyr saint. The duke founded monasteries, including the Benedictine abbey at Břevnov (993) and Ostrov.

His reign also saw violent dynastic conflict with the rival Slavník family. In 995 forces loyal to the Přemyslids massacred the Slavníks at Libice nad Cidlinou; Bishop Adalbert, a member of that family, had by then left Bohemia, and he was martyred in Prussia in 997.

Family and succession

Boleslaus had several sons, among them Boleslaus III, Jaromír, and Oldřich, whose rivalries after his death plunged the duchy into a succession crisis and invited Polish intervention by Bolesław I of Poland. Despite these troubles, later tradition remembered him favorably under the epithet "the Pious" for his church foundations.
