The politics of Andorra take place within a parliamentary representative democracy and co-principality, in which the Government of Andorra exercises executive power and legislative power is shared between the government and the General Council. Andorra is unique in having two co-princes as heads of state: the Bishop of Urgell in Spain and the President of the French Republic, a diarchy dating to the paréages of 1278 and 1288.

Constitution

The Constitution of Andorra, approved by referendum on 14 March 1993, established Andorra as a sovereign parliamentary co-principality, introduced a modern separation of powers, and for the first time gave Andorrans full political rights under a written constitution. The co-princes, whose role is largely ceremonial and exercised through personal representatives, jointly promulgate laws and ratify treaties.

Government and legislature

The General Council (Consell General) is a unicameral legislature of 28 members elected for four-year terms, half from the seven parishes and half from a national constituency. It elects the head of government (Cap de Govern), who appoints the cabinet. Recent politics has been dominated by Democrats for Andorra and its predecessors; Xavier Espot Zamora became head of government in 2019.

Parties and elections

Political parties were legalised only in 1993. Major parties include Democrats for Andorra, the Social Democratic Party, and Citizens Committed. Voting is restricted to Andorran citizens, who form a minority of residents, and proposals to extend nationality and voting rights are perennially debated.
