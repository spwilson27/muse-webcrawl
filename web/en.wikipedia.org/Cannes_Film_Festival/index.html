The Cannes Film Festival (French: Festival de Cannes), founded in 1946, is an annual film festival held in Cannes, France, which previews new films of all genres, including documentaries, from around the world. It is one of the "Big Three" major European film festivals, alongside Venice and Berlin, and one of the "Big Five" worldwide.

Founded as a French response to the politicised Venice Film Festival of the fascist era, the first edition in September 1939 was halted after a single screening by the outbreak of war; the festival was relaunched in 1946. Its most prestigious award, the Palme d'Or, was introduced in 1955.

Awards

The competition jury awards the Palme d'Or for best film, the Grand Prix, Best Director, Best Screenplay, Best Actor, Best Actress, the Jury Prize, and the Short Film Palme d'Or. Separate juries honour Un Certain Regard (for innovative and daring works) and the Caméra d'Or for best first feature. Independent sidebars — Directors' Fortnight (since 1969) and Critics' Week — run alongside the official selection.

The market and the spectacle

The Marché du Film, founded in 1959, is the world's largest film market, drawing over 12,000 industry professionals. The festival is equally famous for its red-carpet photocalls on the Croisette, yacht parties, and occasional scandals and protests, which together guarantee global media coverage for the films selected.

Notable Palme d'Or winners

Winners include The Third Man (1949, as Grand Prix), La Dolce Vita (1960), Viridiana (1961), The Leopard (1963), Taxi Driver (1976), Apocalypse Now (1979, tied), Paris, Texas (1984), Pulp Fiction (1994), The Pianist (2002), Amour (2012), Shoplifters (2018), Parasite (2019), and Anora (2024).
