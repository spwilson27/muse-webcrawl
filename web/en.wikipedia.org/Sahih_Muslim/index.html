Sahih Muslim

Sahih Muslim (Arabic: صحيح مسلم) is a collection of hadith compiled by the Persian scholar Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (c. 815–875). It is one of the six major hadith collections in Sunni Islam (the Kutub al-Sittah) and is generally regarded, together with Sahih al-Bukhari, as the most authentic (sahih) of them.

Compilation

Muslim travelled widely through Arabia, Iraq, Syria and Egypt collecting traditions, selecting his material — according to tradition, from some 300,000 narrations — over a period of about fifteen years. Unlike al-Bukhari, Muslim organised his collection strictly by subject matter (books on faith, purification, prayer, almsgiving, pilgrimage, marriage, and so on) and usually grouped variant chains of transmission (isnad) of the same report together to facilitate comparison of the texts.

Structure and contents

The collection opens with a famous introduction (Muqaddima) setting out Muslim's methodology for evaluating narrators and chains. The body comprises more than fifty books covering doctrine, worship, law, ethics, history and eschatology. Commentaries on the work include the celebrated Sharh Sahih Muslim of Imam al-Nawawi.

Significance

Within Sunni Islam, Sahih Muslim is second in authority only to the Quran and, among hadith collections, is usually placed alongside or just after Sahih al-Bukhari. Its reports are foundational sources for Islamic jurisprudence and theology.
