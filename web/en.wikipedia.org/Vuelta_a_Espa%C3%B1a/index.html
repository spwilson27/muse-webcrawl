The Vuelta a España (Spanish for "Tour of Spain") is an annual multi-stage bicycle race primarily held in Spain, while also occasionally making passes through nearby countries. Along with the Tour de France and the Giro d'Italia, the Vuelta is one of cycling's prestigious, three-week-long Grand Tours.

First held in 1935, the race was interrupted by the Spanish Civil War and the Second World War, and became an annual event from 1955. It was traditionally held in spring before moving to late August and September in 1995 to avoid direct competition with the Giro. The leader of the general classification wears a red jersey, known as la roja.

History

Inspired by the success of the Tour de France and Giro d'Italia, the newspaper Informaciones organised the first Vuelta in 1935, won by the Belgian Gustaaf Deloor. Roberto Heras holds the record with four overall victories, while riders such as Tony Rominger and Primož Roglič have won three consecutive editions.
