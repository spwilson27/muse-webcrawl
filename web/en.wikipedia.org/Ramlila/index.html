Ramlila

OriginNorthern India
Based onRamayana and Ramcharitmanas
SeasonPerformed during autumn, around Dussehra
RecognitionUNESCO Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity (2005)

Ramlila (Hindi: रामलीला, literally "Rama's play") is a traditional performance of the Hindu epic Ramayana in a series of scenes that include song, narration, recital and dialogue. It is performed across northern India during the autumn festival season, culminating in Dussehra, when giant effigies of the demon king Ravana are burned to celebrate Rama's victory.

Performances

The staging lasts between ten and thirty-one nights, sometimes longer, following the text of Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas. Most Ramlilas recount the entire life of Rama, from his birth to his battle with Ravana. Actors are often young boys from the community playing the principal roles, and performances draw large crowds who treat the enactment as both theatre and devotional practice (darshan).

Notable traditions

The Ramlila of Ramnagar, across the Ganges from Varanasi, is among the most famous: staged for a month each year under the patronage of the Kashi Naresh, it uses permanent structures and locations spread across the town as its sets. UNESCO proclaimed Ramlila a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity in 2005, and it was inscribed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008.
