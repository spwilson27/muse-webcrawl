San Francisco, officially the City and County of San Francisco, is a commercial, financial and cultural centre in Northern California. It sits at the tip of a peninsula between the Pacific Ocean and San Francisco Bay, and is the second-most densely populated large city in the United States after New York City.

History

Spanish colonists founded a presidio and the Mission San Francisco de Asís in 1776. The small settlement, known as Yerba Buena, was renamed San Francisco in 1847 and exploded in size during the California Gold Rush of 1849, becoming the largest city on the West Coast. The 1906 earthquake and subsequent fires destroyed much of the city, which was rapidly rebuilt. In the 20th century it became a centre of liberal activism — the Beat Generation, the Summer of Love of 1967, and the gay rights movement — and later a hub of the technology industry centred on nearby Silicon Valley.

Landmarks

The Golden Gate Bridge, completed in 1937, is the city's most famous symbol. Other landmarks include Alcatraz Island, Fisherman's Wharf, Chinatown (the oldest in North America), the cable cars, the "Painted Ladies" Victorian houses, Coit Tower on Telegraph Hill, and the Transamerica Pyramid.

Climate

San Francisco has a warm-summer Mediterranean climate characteristically modified by coastal fog, locally nicknamed "Karl the Fog". Summers are cool and foggy, winters mild and wet, and temperature variation through the year is small.
