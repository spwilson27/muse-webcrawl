Crown of Castile
Established: 1230 (union of Castile and León)
Capital: Toledo, later Madrid
Languages: Castilian, Galician, Basque, others

The Crown of Castile was a medieval and early modern polity in the Iberian Peninsula, formed in 1230 by the definitive union of the kingdoms of Castile and León under Ferdinand III. It grew through the Reconquista to encompass most of central and southern Iberia.

History

Successive monarchs conquered Córdoba, Seville and, in 1492, Granada, completing the Christian conquest of al-Andalus. In the same year Columbus's voyage, sponsored by Isabella I, opened the Americas to Castilian expansion, and the Crown administered the vast Spanish Empire in the Americas through the Council of the Indies.

Institutions

The Crown comprised multiple kingdoms, each with its own laws and cortes, united in the person of the monarch. Castilian became the dominant administrative language. The Nueva Planta decrees of the early 18th century effectively ended the Crown of Castile as a distinct legal entity within a centralising Spanish monarchy.
