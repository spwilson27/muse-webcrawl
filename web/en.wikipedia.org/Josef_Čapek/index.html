Josef Čapek

BornMarch 23, 1887, Hronov, Bohemia, Austria-Hungary
DiedApril 1945, Bergen-Belsen concentration camp, Germany
OccupationsPainter, writer, graphic artist, critic
RelativesKarel Čapek (brother)

Josef Čapek (March 23, 1887 – April 1945) was a Czech painter, writer and graphic artist, and the elder brother of writer Karel Čapek. Working first in a Cubist idiom and later in a more personal, childlike style, he was also a noted book illustrator, stage designer and children's author.

Life and work

Čapek studied in Prague and Paris and was a member of the Skupina výtvarných umělců group of Cubist artists. With Karel he co-wrote plays including The Insect Play (1921) and Adam the Creator. It was Josef who suggested the word "robot" — from the Czech robota, forced labour — for Karel's 1920 play R.U.R., the work that introduced the term to the world. His own books include the beloved children's tale Povídání o pejskovi a kočičce ("Tales of Doggie and Pussycat", 1929), which he wrote and illustrated.

Death and legacy

An outspoken anti-fascist, Čapek was arrested by the Gestapo in 1939 and spent the war in concentration camps, including Dachau and Sachsenhausen. He died at Bergen-Belsen in April 1945, shortly before liberation; poems written in the camps were published posthumously. His paintings and illustrations remain central to Czech modernism.
