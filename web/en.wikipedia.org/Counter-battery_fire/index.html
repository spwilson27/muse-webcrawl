Counter-battery fire

Counter-battery fire
TypeArtillery tactic
PurposeLocate and destroy enemy artillery
Key equipmentCounter-battery radar, sound ranging, observers

Counter-battery fire is artillery fire directed against the enemy's artillery. It is a central task of artillery in modern warfare, aimed at destroying, neutralising, or suppressing hostile guns, mortars, and rocket launchers before they can inflict serious damage.

Locating hostile batteries

Counter-battery operations begin with target acquisition. Historically this relied on aerial observation, flash spotting, and sound ranging — techniques refined in the First World War. Today, counter-battery radars track incoming shells and rockets in flight and compute the point of origin within seconds; drones and satellite imagery supplement them.

Engagement

Once located, hostile batteries are engaged with artillery, multiple rocket launchers, or air power. Speed is essential: modern self-propelled guns use "shoot-and-scoot" tactics, displacing immediately after firing to escape counter-battery fire, which has turned the contest into a duel of sensors, communications, and reaction times.

History

Counter-battery work became a specialised science in the First World War, when artillery dominated the battlefield. It remained important in the Second World War and Korea, and has featured prominently in recent high-intensity conflicts, where radar-directed counter-battery fire is a leading cause of artillery losses.
