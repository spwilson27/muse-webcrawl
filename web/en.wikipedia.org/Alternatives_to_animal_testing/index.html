Alternatives to animal testing

Alternatives to animal testing are the development and implementation of test methods that avoid the use of live animals. There is widespread agreement that animal studies, used together with complementary in vitro and computational approaches, should be minimized; the guiding framework is the “three Rs” proposed by W. M. S. Russell and R. L. Burch in 1959: replacement, reduction, and refinement of animal use in experiments.

Approaches

  • In vitro methods — cell and tissue cultures, reconstructed human skin and corneal models, and organ-on-a-chip systems
  • In silico methods — computer modelling, quantitative structure–activity relationships (QSAR), and read-across between similar chemicals
  • Other organisms — using invertebrates, embryos, or microorganisms where appropriate
  • Human-based methods — microdosing studies, imaging, and epidemiological data

Regulation and adoption

Validation bodies such as EURL ECVAM in Europe and ICCVAM in the United States evaluate alternative methods for regulatory acceptance. The European Union banned animal testing of cosmetics in stages, completing the ban in 2013, and directives require alternatives to be used wherever scientifically satisfactory. Calls for alternatives arise from ethical concerns, cost and speed, and the limited predictivity of some animal models for human biology.
