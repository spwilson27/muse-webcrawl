Yohanan ben Zakkai (Hebrew: יוֹחָנָן בֶּן זַכַּאי; c. 1st century CE) was a Jewish sage and a leading figure in the formative period of Rabbinic Judaism. A disciple of Hillel the Elder, he is credited with preserving and reshaping Jewish religious life after the Roman destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE.

Escape from Jerusalem and Yavneh

According to rabbinic tradition, during the Roman siege of Jerusalem ben Zakkai opposed the Zealots' revolt and, convinced the city would fall, had his disciples smuggle him out in a coffin. Brought before the Roman commander Vespasian, he prophesied that Vespasian would become emperor; when the prophecy came true, he was granted his request: "Give me Yavneh and its sages." At Yavneh (Jamnia) he established an academy and a reconstituted Sanhedrin that became the centre of Jewish learning.

Enactments and legacy

Ben Zakkai issued a series of ordinances (takkanot) adapting Temple rituals to life without the Temple — for example, permitting the shofar to be blown at Yavneh when Rosh Hashanah fell on Shabbat, and transferring the lulav ceremony to all seven days of Sukkot. His work laid the foundations for the Mishnah and the survival of Judaism as a Torah-centred religion. His famous saying holds that acts of loving-kindness atone for Israel as sacrifices once did.
