Indiegogo

Indiegogo is an American crowdfunding website founded in 2008 by Danae Ringelmann, Slava Rubin, and Eric Schell. Headquartered in San Francisco, California, it was one of the first sites to offer crowd-funded fundraising, allowing people to solicit funds for an idea, charity, or start-up business. Indiegogo charges a 5% fee on money raised. The site runs on a rewards-based system, meaning donors, investors, or customers contribute in exchange for products or perks.

History

The founders conceived the platform after experiencing difficulty raising money for their own projects: Ringelmann for a theatrical production, Rubin for research into multiple myeloma after losing his father to cancer, and Schell for a Chicago theatre company. Indiegogo launched at the Sundance Film Festival in January 2008 with a focus on film funding. It later broadened to all categories of creative, entrepreneurial, and charitable projects.

Model

Indiegogo is known for its flexible funding option, which allows campaigners to keep the money they raise even if they do not meet their goal, in contrast to the all-or-nothing model used by rivals such as Kickstarter (fixed funding is also offered). Its InDemand program lets successful campaigns continue raising funds after their official end date, effectively serving as a pre-order storefront. High-profile campaigns have included the Flow Hive beekeeping system, the Solar Roadways project, and numerous consumer electronics products.

Reception and criticism

Because flexible funding does not require projects to reach a goal or deliver rewards, the platform has faced criticism over campaigns that raised large sums but failed to ship products. Indiegogo has responded with trust and verification measures, and in 2017 it launched an equity crowdfunding service allowing investments in start-ups under the JOBS Act.
