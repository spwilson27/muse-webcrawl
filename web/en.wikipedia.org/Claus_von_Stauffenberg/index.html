Claus Philipp Maria Schenk Graf von Stauffenberg (15 November 1907 – 21 July 1944) was a German army officer who is best known for his failed attempt on 20 July 1944 to assassinate Adolf Hitler at the Wolf's Lair, and for his central role in the accompanying coup attempt, Operation Valkyrie.

Early life and career

Stauffenberg was born at Jettingen in Württemberg into one of southern Germany's oldest Catholic aristocratic families. He joined the Reichswehr in 1926 and rose rapidly through the General Staff, distinguishing himself as a brilliant staff officer. He served in the invasions of Poland and France and on the Eastern Front. In April 1943, while serving with the 10th Panzer Division in Tunisia, he was gravely wounded by a strafing Allied fighter, losing his left eye, his right hand, and two fingers of his left hand.

Resistance

Disillusioned by Nazi crimes and Germany's deteriorating military position, Stauffenberg joined the military resistance centred on Ludwig Beck, Carl Goerdeler and Henning von Tresckow. As chief of staff of the Reserve Army from July 1944, he had direct access to Hitler's briefings and became the plot's indispensable executor: he would plant the bomb and then fly to Berlin to direct the seizure of the capital under the Valkyrie emergency plan.

The 20 July plot and death

On 20 July 1944 Stauffenberg carried a bomb in a briefcase into the conference room at the Wolf's Lair and placed it near Hitler before leaving on a pretext. The heavy oak table leg shielded Hitler, who survived with minor injuries. Stauffenberg flew to Berlin believing Hitler dead and pressed ahead with the coup, but it faltered; by evening loyal forces had regained control. Stauffenberg was arrested and shot by firing squad in the courtyard of the Bendlerblock shortly after midnight, reportedly crying "Long live sacred Germany!"

Legacy

Long treated ambivalently in postwar Germany, Stauffenberg is now widely honoured as a hero of the resistance; the Bendlerblock houses the German Resistance Memorial Center, and his anniversary is marked by Bundeswehr ceremonies.
