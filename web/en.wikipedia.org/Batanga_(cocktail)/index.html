Batanga (cocktail)

Batanga
TypeCocktail
Base spiritTequila
IngredientsTequila, cola, lime juice, salt
OriginTequila, Jalisco, Mexico

The Batanga is a Mexican cocktail made with tequila, cola and lime juice, served over ice in a highball glass with a salted rim. It is closely related to the Cuba libre, with tequila replacing rum.

History

The drink is credited to Don Javier Delgado Corona, owner of La Capilla bar in the town of Tequila, Jalisco, who is said to have created it in 1961. Don Javier famously stirred the drink with a long knife — the same knife used to cut limes and other bar ingredients — a ritual still associated with the Batanga. La Capilla became a pilgrimage site for bartenders and tequila enthusiasts.

Preparation

A highball glass is rimmed with coarse salt, filled with ice, and charged with a generous pour of blanco or reposado tequila, the juice of half a lime, and cola (traditionally Mexican Coca-Cola made with cane sugar) to fill. It is stirred — purportedly ideally with a knife — and garnished with the spent lime shell.
