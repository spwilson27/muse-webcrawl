Territories of the United States

The territories of the United States are sub-national administrative divisions overseen by the federal government of the United States. Unlike U.S. states, territories are not sovereign entities, and their relationship with the federal government is governed by the Territory Clause of the U.S. Constitution.

The United States also administers a number of minor outlying islands, including Wake Island, Midway Atoll, Johnston Atoll, and Navassa Island. The five permanently inhabited territories elect non-voting delegates to the U.S. House of Representatives; their residents generally cannot vote in U.S. presidential elections.

Constitutional status

The Supreme Court's Insular Cases (1901–1922) established the doctrine of incorporated and unincorporated territories, holding that the full Constitution does not automatically apply in unincorporated territories. People born in Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands and the Northern Mariana Islands are U.S. citizens at birth; those born in American Samoa are U.S. nationals unless they naturalize.
