The Paralympic Games or Paralympics is a periodic series of international multisport events involving athletes with a range of disabilities. There are Winter and Summer Paralympic Games, which since the 1988 Summer Olympics in Seoul, South Korea, are held almost immediately following the corresponding Olympic Games. All Paralympic Games are governed by the International Paralympic Committee (IPC).

The Paralympics has grown from a small gathering of British World War II veterans in 1948 to become one of the largest international sporting events by the early 21st century. Paralympians strive for equal treatment with non-disabled Olympic athletes, but there is a large funding gap between Olympic and Paralympic athletes.

History

The movement began with Dr. Ludwig Guttmann of Stoke Mandeville Hospital in England, who in 1948 organized a sports competition for Second World War veterans with spinal cord injuries, deliberately timed to coincide with the London Olympics. The first international Stoke Mandeville Games were held in 1952. In 1960, the competition moved to Rome and is considered the first Paralympic Games, featuring 400 athletes from 23 countries. The first Winter Paralympics took place in Örnsköldsvik, Sweden, in 1976. The term "Paralympic" was officially adopted in the 1980s; it derives from the Greek preposition para ("beside" or "alongside") and "Olympic", signifying a parallel movement.

Classification

Paralympic athletes are grouped into sport classes according to how their impairment affects performance in a given sport, covering physical impairments (such as limb deficiency, impaired muscle power, and vision impairment), and intellectual impairment. Classification systems are sport-specific and aim to ensure that winning is determined by skill, fitness, and tactics rather than by degree of impairment. Prominent para sports include para athletics, para swimming, wheelchair racing, wheelchair basketball, sitting volleyball, goalball, para alpine skiing, and para ice hockey.

Relationship with the Olympics

Since 2001, an agreement between the IPC and the International Olympic Committee requires host cities to stage both the Olympic and Paralympic Games. The Paralympic symbol features three Agitos — curved figures in red, blue, and green — encircling a central point, representing the motto "Spirit in Motion".
