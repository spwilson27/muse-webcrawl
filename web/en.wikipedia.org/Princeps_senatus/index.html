Princeps senatus

The princeps senatus (plural principes senatus), in English the Leader of the Senate, was the first member by precedence of the Roman Senate. Although officially not having any executive powers, the princeps senatus held enormous prestige.

Role

The princeps was chosen by the censors every five years from among senators of consular rank, usually the most senior ex-censor. He was listed first on the senatorial roll and was the first to be asked his opinion by the presiding magistrate, an honour that often set the tone of debate.

Under the Empire

From Augustus onward, the emperors held the title themselves; Augustus used princeps ("first citizen") as his preferred style, giving rise to the term "Principate" for the early Empire.
