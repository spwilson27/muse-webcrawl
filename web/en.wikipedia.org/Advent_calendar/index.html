Advent calendar

An Advent calendar is a special calendar used to count the days of Advent in anticipation of Christmas. Since the date of the First Sunday of Advent varies, falling between November 27 and December 3 inclusive, many Advent calendars, especially reusable ones, often begin on December 1, although those that are produced for a specific year often include the last few days of November as part of the calendar.

Advent calendar
Season: Advent
Origin: 19th-century Germany
Features: 24 (or 25) doors/windows

History

The tradition dates to German Lutherans in the 19th century, who marked the days to Christmas with chalk lines or candles. Printed calendars appeared in the early 20th century; Gerhard Lang is credited with producing the first printed Advent calendar with small doors around 1908–1920.

Designs

Most calendars take the form of a large rectangular card with "windows", one for each day of December leading up to and including Christmas Eve or Christmas Day. Behind each window is an image, a poem, a portion of the Christmas story, or a small gift such as a chocolate. Modern variants include calendars containing toys, cosmetics, beverages, and other themed gifts.
