Lower Egypt

Lower Egypt (Arabic: مصر السفلى)
Region: Northern Egypt — the Nile Delta and the valley north of Cairo to the Mediterranean Sea
Symbol: Red Crown (Deshret), papyrus
Principal cities (ancient): Memphis, Sais, Bubastis, Tanis, Alexandria
[Image placeholder: The green farmland of the Nile Delta in Lower Egypt]

Lower Egypt is the northernmost region of Egypt, consisting of the fertile Nile Delta between modern-day Cairo and the Mediterranean Sea. It is called "lower" because it lies downstream — the Nile flows from south to north — in contrast to Upper Egypt in the south.

History

In predynastic times Lower Egypt was a distinct kingdom, symbolised by the Red Crown (Deshret) and the papyrus plant. Around 3100 BCE it was united with Upper Egypt, traditionally by the pharaoh Narmer (Menes), forming the Old Kingdom state. Pharaonic kings thereafter wore the Double Crown and styled themselves "King of Upper and Lower Egypt". The region was administered in districts called nomes — eventually twenty in Lower Egypt — and its great cities included Memphis, near the apex of the Delta, and later Alexandria on the coast.

Geography

The Delta fans out from near Cairo into a triangle of rich alluvial farmland watered by the Rosetta and Damietta branches of the Nile. The climate is milder and wetter than in Upper Egypt.
