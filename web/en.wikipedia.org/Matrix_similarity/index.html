Matrix similarity

In linear algebra, two n-by-n matrices A and B are called similar if there exists an invertible n-by-n matrix P such that B = P−1AP. Similar matrices represent the same linear map under two different bases, with P being the change of basis matrix.

Properties

Similarity is an equivalence relation on the space of square matrices. Similar matrices share many properties, including their rank, determinant, trace, eigenvalues (with the same algebraic multiplicities), characteristic polynomial and minimal polynomial. They also have the same Jordan normal form up to permutation of the Jordan blocks.

Conversely, two matrices with the same Jordan normal form are similar. However, sharing the determinant, trace and eigenvalues alone does not imply similarity: for example, a matrix and a nontrivial Jordan block with the same eigenvalue may share all of those invariants yet not be similar.

Canonical forms

The question of whether two matrices are similar can be answered by computing canonical forms such as the Jordan normal form (over an algebraically closed field) or the rational canonical form (over the ground field). Similarity over the real numbers can be more subtle because the Jordan form may require complex entries.

Applications

Similarity transformations are used to simplify matrix computations, notably diagonalization: if A is similar to a diagonal matrix, powers and exponentials of A become easy to compute. In numerical analysis, orthogonal similarity transformations underlie the QR algorithm for computing eigenvalues.
