Charles IV, Holy Roman Emperor

Charles IV
Born: 14 May 1316, Prague
Died: 29 November 1378, Prague
Reign: King of Bohemia from 1346; crowned Holy Roman Emperor 1355
House: Luxembourg

Charles IV (Czech: Karel IV.; 1316–1378), a member of the House of Luxembourg, was King of Bohemia from 1346 and Holy Roman Emperor from 1355 until his death. Raised at the French court, he became one of the most powerful and cultured rulers of late medieval Europe, and his reign is regarded as a golden age of Bohemian history.

Reign

The son of John of Luxembourg ("John the Blind"), who fell at the Battle of Crécy in 1346, Charles secured his election as King of the Romans against the Wittelsbach emperor Louis IV and was crowned emperor in Rome in 1355. His Golden Bull of 1356 fixed the constitution of the Empire's electoral college — the seven prince-electors — and remained fundamental law until 1806.

Bohemia and Prague

Charles made Prague an imperial capital. He founded Charles University in 1348, the first university in Central Europe; commissioned the Charles Bridge (begun 1357) and St Vitus Cathedral; founded the New Town of Prague and Karlštejn Castle; and issued the (never-enacted) Maiestas Carolina law code for Bohemia. He expanded Bohemian crown lands into Silesia, Lusatia, and Brandenburg. His autobiography, the Vita Caroli, is a rare medieval royal memoir.

Legacy

Charles was succeeded by his son Wenceslaus IV. Voted the greatest Czech figure in a 2005 television poll, he remains a central figure of Czech national memory, his name attached to Prague's university, bridge, and square.
