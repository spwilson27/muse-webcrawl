Epiphany (from the Greek epiphaneia, "manifestation") is a Christian feast day celebrated on 6 January, or on a nearby Sunday in some countries, marking the conclusion of the twelve days of Christmas. In Western Christianity it primarily commemorates the visit of the Magi (the Three Wise Men) to the infant Jesus, and thus Christ's manifestation to the Gentiles. In Eastern Christianity the feast, often called Theophany, centres on the baptism of Jesus in the Jordan River, where the Trinity was manifested.

History

The feast is among the oldest in the Christian calendar, originating in the East by the third or fourth century, where it celebrated several manifestations of Christ, including his birth, baptism and first miracle at Cana. As Christmas on 25 December spread from the West, Epiphany in the West came to focus on the Magi. In the Roman calendar reforms of 1955 and 1969 the feast's date and octave were adjusted; many countries transfer its observance to the nearest Sunday.

Customs

Celebrations vary widely. In Spain and much of Latin America, Three Kings' Day is the principal gift-giving day for children, with parades (cabalgatas) on the eve. In France and francophone regions a galette des rois or king cake with a hidden charm is shared. Orthodox churches celebrate the Great Blessing of Waters, sometimes throwing a cross into the sea, a river or a lake for swimmers to retrieve. In parts of central and northern Europe, carol singers known as Sternsinger ("star singers") bless houses, chalking the year and the initials C+M+B above the door. In some traditions, Christmas decorations are taken down on Twelfth Night, the eve of Epiphany.
