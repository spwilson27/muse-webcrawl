AGM-88 HARM

The AGM-88 HARM (High-speed Anti-Radiation Missile) is an American tactical air-to-surface anti-radiation missile designed to home in on enemy radar emissions. It is the principal weapon used in the suppression of enemy air defences (SEAD), carried by aircraft such as the F-16CJ, EA-18G Growler, and Tornado ECR.

Design

The HARM's seeker detects and locates radar transmissions, and the missile homes on the emitting antenna even at long range — over 150 km in some profiles. It carries a 66 kg blast-fragmentation warhead. Successive versions improved seeker sensitivity and the ability to engage radars that shut down mid-attack; the AGM-88E AARGM adds a millimetre-wave radar and GPS guidance to strike relocated or silent emitters, and the AARGM-ER extends the range further in a redesigned airframe.

Operational history

The HARM entered service in the mid-1980s and saw its first combat use against Libya in 1986. It was used extensively in the Gulf War, the Balkans, Iraq, and Libya in 2011. In 2022, HARMs supplied to Ukraine were adapted for launch from its Soviet-era aircraft.
