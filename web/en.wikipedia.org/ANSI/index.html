American National Standards Institute

ANSI
Formation: October 19, 1918
Type: Nonprofit organization
Headquarters: Washington, D.C., United States
Purpose: Voluntary consensus standards

The American National Standards Institute (ANSI) is a private nonprofit organization that oversees the development of voluntary consensus standards for products, services, processes, systems, and personnel in the United States. ANSI also coordinates U.S. standards with international standards so that American products can be used worldwide.

History

ANSI was founded in 1918 as the American Engineering Standards Committee by five engineering societies and three government agencies. It was reorganized as the American Standards Association in 1928, became the United States of America Standards Institute in 1966, and adopted its current name in 1969.

Role

ANSI does not itself write most standards. Instead, it accredits standards-developing organizations — such as ASME, IEEE, and ASTM International — and designates their output as American National Standards when they follow ANSI's consensus procedures. Its membership comprises more than 1,000 companies, organizations, government agencies, and institutions. ANSI is the official U.S. representative to the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and, through the U.S. National Committee, to the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC).

Notable standards

Well-known ANSI-related standards include the ANSI character encoding derived from ASCII, ANSI C (the standardized form of the C programming language), safety standards such as Z87.1 for eye protection, and the ANSI escape codes used by computer terminals. The distinctive ANSI art scene of the BBS era took its name from those escape sequences.
