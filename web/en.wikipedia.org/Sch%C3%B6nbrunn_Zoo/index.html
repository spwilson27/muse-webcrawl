Schönbrunn Zoo (German: Tiergarten Schönbrunn) is a zoo in the gardens of Schönbrunn Palace in Vienna, Austria. Founded in 1752 as an imperial menagerie, it is the oldest continuously operating zoo in the world. Together with the palace, it forms part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The menagerie was founded by the Habsburg monarch Franz I. Stephan at the instigation of his wife, Maria Theresa. Architect Jean Nicolas Jadot de Ville-Iselin designed thirteen animal enclosures arranged like slices of a pie around an octagonal central pavilion, where the imperial couple could observe the animals while breakfasting. The zoo opened to selected visitors in 1752 and to the general public in 1779.

Animals and exhibits

Today the zoo houses more than 700 species on 17 hectares. Notable exhibits include a rainforest house, an aquarium, a polar bear enclosure and a giant panda enclosure; Schönbrunn achieved early successes breeding giant pandas by natural mating. The zoo is active in international conservation and breeding programmes.

Recognition

Schönbrunn Zoo has repeatedly been ranked among Europe's best zoos, topping the European zoo ranking compiled by Anthony Sheridan several times.
