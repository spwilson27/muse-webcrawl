Poor Laws

The Poor Laws were a system of poor relief in England and Wales that developed out of late-medieval and Tudor legislation. They provided relief through a system of taxes administered at the parish level, lasting from the late 16th century until the rise of the welfare state after the Second World War.

History

The Elizabethan Poor Law of 1601 (the "Old Poor Law") made each parish responsible for its own poor, funded by a local rate, with relief given as outdoor payments or in almshouses. The Poor Law Amendment Act 1834 (the "New Poor Law") centralized administration, grouped parishes into unions, and made relief conditional on entering workhouses designed to be deliberately harsh under the principle of "less eligibility".

Legacy

The workhouse system was progressively dismantled in the early 20th century; the boards of guardians were abolished in 1929, and the Poor Laws were formally repealed by the National Assistance Act 1948.
