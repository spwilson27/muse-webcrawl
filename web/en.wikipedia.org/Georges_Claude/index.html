Georges Claude

Born24 September 1870, Paris, France
Died23 May 1960 (aged 89), Saint-Cloud, France
NationalityFrench
Known forNeon lighting; industrial liquefaction of gases

Georges Claude (24 September 1870 – 23 May 1960) was a French engineer and inventor, best known as the inventor of the neon lamp and as a pioneer of the industrial liquefaction of air and gases. He founded the company Air Liquide and was one of the most prominent French industrial scientists of the first half of the twentieth century.

Early life and education

Claude was born in Paris and studied at the École supérieure de physique et de chimie industrielles de la ville de Paris (ESPCI), the school where Pierre and Marie Curie had worked. He began his career in electricity generation before turning to the problems of gas liquefaction.

Air Liquide and gas liquefaction

In 1902 Claude developed a practical process for liquefying air by expansion through an engine, improving on the earlier Linde process. With businessman Paul Delorme he founded Air Liquide, which became one of the world's largest industrial gas companies. His process allowed the economical production of oxygen, nitrogen and rare gases on an industrial scale.

Neon lighting

Working with the discharge of electricity through rarefied gases, Claude found that neon gas in a sealed tube produced a brilliant orange-red light. He patented the neon tube in 1910 and demonstrated it publicly at the Paris Motor Show in December of that year. In 1912 the first commercial neon sign was installed at a Paris barber shop, and in 1923 Claude's company introduced neon signs to the United States, where they transformed advertising and urban nightlife.

Other work

Claude pursued many other projects, including early experiments with ocean thermal energy conversion (OTEC): in 1930 he built a plant in Cuba, and in 1934 off Brazil, attempting to generate electricity from the temperature difference between surface and deep ocean water. During the Second World War he collaborated with the Vichy regime and was imprisoned after the Liberation, and expelled from the French Academy of Sciences, to which he had been elected in 1924.

Death and legacy

Claude died at Saint-Cloud in 1960. His name remains associated with neon lighting and with the founding of Air Liquide; the "Claude process" for gas liquefaction is still referenced in cryogenic engineering.
