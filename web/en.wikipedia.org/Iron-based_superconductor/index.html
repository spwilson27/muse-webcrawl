Iron-based superconductor

Iron-based superconductors (FeSCs) are a family of unconventional superconductors whose parent compounds are iron compounds, usually with an iron-pnictide or iron-chalcogenide layer. They were discovered in 2006–2008, when Hideo Hosono's group in Tokyo reported superconductivity in LaFePO and then, in 2008, critical temperatures up to about 55 K in fluorine-doped LaFeAsO — the highest then known outside the cuprates.

Families

The main structural families are the "1111" pnictides (LaFeAsO type), the "122" compounds (BaFe2As2 type), the "111" type (LiFeAs), and the "11" chalcogenides (FeSe). FeSe also forms the basis of intercalated and monolayer systems in which Tc can be much enhanced.

Properties

Like the cuprates, iron-based superconductors arise from doping magnetically ordered parent materials, display high upper critical fields, and are widely believed to have an s± pairing state in which the superconducting gap changes sign between electron and hole Fermi pockets. Their multiband electronic structure makes them a major test-bed for theories of high-temperature superconductivity.
