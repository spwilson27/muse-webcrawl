Battle of Guadalajara

Date: 8–23 March 1937
Location: Guadalajara, Spain
Result: Spanish Republican victory

The Battle of Guadalajara (8–23 March 1937) was fought during the Spanish Civil War around the city of Guadalajara, northeast of Madrid. Nationalist forces, spearheaded by the Italian Corps of Volunteer Troops (CTV) sent by Mussolini, attempted to encircle Madrid from the northeast. The attack was repelled by the Spanish Republican Army, assisted by the International Brigades, including Italian anti-fascist volunteers of the Garibaldi Battalion.

Background

After earlier Nationalist attempts to cut the Madrid–Valencia road at the Jarama failed, Franco authorised an Italian-led offensive from Sigüenza toward Guadalajara and Alcalá de Henares, intended to complete the encirclement of the capital.

The battle

The Italian advance began on 8 March and initially broke through Republican positions. However, snow, rain, and mud slowed the motorised columns, airfields became unusable while Republican aircraft operated from better-prepared bases, and a counterattack from 18 March drove the Italians back. The CTV suffered heavy losses in men and equipment and abandoned large quantities of matériel.

Aftermath

Guadalajara was the first major Republican victory of the war and a humiliation for Mussolini's Italy, celebrated in Republican propaganda as a triumph of anti-fascism. It also strained relations between Franco and his Italian allies and persuaded Hitler and Mussolini to limit further direct offensives around Madrid.
