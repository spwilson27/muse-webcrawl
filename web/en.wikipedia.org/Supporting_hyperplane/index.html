Supporting hyperplane

A convex set and one of its supporting hyperplanes at a boundary point.

In geometry, a supporting hyperplane of a set S in Euclidean space Rn is a hyperplane that has both of the following two properties:

  • S is entirely contained in one of the two closed half-spaces bounded by the hyperplane.
  • The hyperplane contains at least one boundary point of S.

In two dimensions the supporting hyperplane is a supporting line; in three dimensions it is a supporting plane.

Supporting hyperplane theorem

The supporting hyperplane theorem states that if S is a nonempty convex set in Rn and x0 is a point on the boundary of S, then there exists a supporting hyperplane containing x0. Formally, there exists a nonzero vector c such that c·x ≤ c·x0 for all x in S. The theorem is closely related to the Hahn–Banach separation theorems of functional analysis.

If the boundary of S is smooth at x0, the supporting hyperplane there is unique and equals the tangent hyperplane. If S is not convex, a supporting hyperplane need not exist at every boundary point.

Applications

Supporting hyperplanes underlie the geometry of convex analysis and optimization. In economics, a supporting hyperplane at a point of a production set or preference set can be interpreted as a price vector that supports an optimal allocation, an idea central to the fundamental theorems of welfare economics. In machine learning, the maximum-margin hyperplane of a support-vector machine is related to supporting hyperplanes of the convex hulls of the training classes.
