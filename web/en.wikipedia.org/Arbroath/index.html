Arbroath (Scottish Gaelic: Obar Bhrothaig) is a former royal burgh and the largest town in the council area of Angus, Scotland, with a population of about 23,500. It lies on the North Sea coast, some 16 miles (26 km) north-east of Dundee.

History

The town grew around Arbroath Abbey, founded in 1178 by William the Lion and dedicated to Thomas Becket. The abbey is famous as the place where, in 1320, the Declaration of Arbroath—a letter from the nobles of Scotland to Pope John XXII asserting Scottish independence—was drawn up. Arbroath later developed as a fishing port and textile town, known for sailcloth and jute.

Culture and cuisine

Arbroath is the home of the Arbroath smokie: haddock that has been salted and hot-smoked over hardwood in a traditional barrel, accorded protected geographical status. The town is also known for its signal tower, built in 1813 in connection with the Bell Rock Lighthouse offshore, and for Arbroath F.C., whose 36–0 victory over Bon Accord in 1885 remains a record score in senior football.
