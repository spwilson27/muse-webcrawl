John Tenniel

Died25 February 1914 (aged 93), London, England
OccupationIllustrator, cartoonist

Sir John Tenniel (28 February 1820 – 25 February 1914) was an English illustrator, graphic humourist and political cartoonist whose work was prominent during the second half of the 19th century. He illustrated Lewis Carroll's Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (1865) and Through the Looking-Glass (1871), creating the definitive images of Alice, the Mad Hatter and the Cheshire Cat.

Punch career

Tenniel joined the satirical magazine Punch in 1850 and became its principal political cartoonist, producing some 2,300 cartoons over fifty years, including the famous "Dropping the Pilot" (1890) on Bismarck's dismissal.

Alice illustrations

Recruited by Lewis Carroll in 1864, Tenniel drew 42 illustrations for Alice's Adventures in Wonderland and 50 for Through the Looking-Glass. Though the two men clashed over detail, Tenniel's grotesque yet precise engravings fixed the visual identity of Carroll's world; Alice herself was not modelled on Alice Liddell but on Tenniel's own idealised drawings.

Later life

Knighted in 1893 — the first illustrator so honoured — Tenniel retired from Punch in 1901 and died in 1914, three days short of his 94th birthday.
