The Rugby Championship is an international rugby union competition contested annually by Argentina, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa. These are traditionally the four highest-ranked national teams in the Southern Hemisphere; the Six Nations is the Northern Hemisphere counterpart. Prior to the 2012 tournament, when Argentina joined, it was known as the Tri Nations. The competition is administered by SANZAAR, a consortium of the four participating unions.

Format

The tournament is normally played from July to October on a home-and-away basis, with each team playing the others twice, though Rugby World Cup years have used shortened formats. Points are awarded on the standard rugby system: four for a win, two for a draw, and bonus points for scoring four or more tries or losing by seven points or fewer. Within the tournament, the Bledisloe Cup (Australia–New Zealand), Mandela Challenge Plate (Australia–South Africa), and Freedom Cup (New Zealand–South Africa) are also contested.

History

The Tri Nations began in 1996 following the advent of professionalism and the SANZAR broadcasting deal. New Zealand's All Blacks have dominated the competition's history, winning the majority of titles; South Africa and Australia have won several each, while Argentina, despite famous victories over all three opponents, has yet to win the title.

Results

New Zealand won the inaugural 1996 Tri Nations and the first Rugby Championship in 2012. South Africa won in 1998, 2004, 2009, and 2019; Australia won in 2000, 2001, 2011, and 2015.
