The Western Union Company is an American multinational financial services company headquartered in Denver, Colorado. Founded in 1851 as a telegraph company, it built and operated the first transcontinental telegraph line in 1861 and grew into the dominant American telegraph operator. Today it is best known for consumer-to-consumer money transfers and remittances through a global network of agent locations.

History

The company was founded in Rochester, New York, and renamed the Western Union Telegraph Company in 1856 after merging with rival lines. It completed the first transcontinental telegraph in October 1861, five years before the transatlantic cable. Western Union introduced the first widely used stock ticker, pioneered money transfer by telegraph in 1871, and later offered services such as singing telegrams. It also launched the first American communications satellites (the Westar series, 1974).

Modern business

Western Union ended its telegram service in 2006. Its modern operations centre on cross-border remittances: customers send money through hundreds of thousands of agent locations in more than 200 countries and territories, or digitally via its website and app, with recipients able to collect cash or receive funds in bank accounts and mobile wallets.
