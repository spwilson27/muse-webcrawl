Western Armenian (Western Armenian: Արեւմտահայերէն, Arevmdahayeren) is one of the two standardized forms of modern Armenian, the other being Eastern Armenian. It is based on the Armenian dialect of Istanbul and developed during the 19th century among Armenians of the Ottoman Empire. It is the principal language of the Armenian diaspora communities in the Middle East, Europe and the Americas.

Western Armenian traditionally uses the classical (Mashtotsian) orthography, in contrast to the reformed spelling used for Eastern Armenian in the Republic of Armenia. The two standards differ in pronunciation — notably in the voicing of certain consonants — as well as in some vocabulary and grammar.

History

Before the Armenian genocide of 1915, Western Armenian was spoken across eastern Anatolia, in towns such as Van, Erzurum, Sivas, Diyarbakır and Harput, each with its own local dialect. The genocide destroyed these speech communities; survivors carried the Istanbul-based standard into exile, where schools, churches, newspapers and publishing houses in cities such as Beirut, Aleppo, Paris and Los Angeles maintained it.

Current status

UNESCO lists Western Armenian as "definitely endangered," since it is no longer the language of a territorial homeland and many diaspora children grow up speaking the host country's language. Efforts to sustain it include diaspora day schools, the Armenian studies programmes of universities, and organizations such as the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation supporting educational initiatives.
