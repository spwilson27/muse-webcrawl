Frost line

Frost line
DefinitionMaximum depth to which soil freezes in winter
Also calledFrost depth, freezing depth
RelevanceBuilding foundations, buried pipes

The frost line, or frost depth, is the maximum depth to which groundwater in soil is expected to freeze in winter in a given climate. It depends on latitude, altitude, soil type, snow cover, and the severity of the cold season, ranging from negligible in warm climates to several metres in subarctic regions.

Construction significance

When water in soil freezes it expands, and repeated freeze–thaw cycles can heave and crack structures founded above the frost line. Building codes therefore require footings and foundations to extend below the local frost depth, and water pipes to be buried below it or insulated. Frost heave is also a major cause of damage to roads and pavements in cold climates.

Astrophysics usage

The term "frost line" is also used in astrophysics for the snow line of a protoplanetary disk — the distance from the young star beyond which volatile compounds such as water condense into ice. That concept is treated separately from the geotechnical sense used here.
