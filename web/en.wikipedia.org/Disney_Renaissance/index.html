Disney Renaissance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Disney Renaissance is the period from 1989 to 1999 during which Walt Disney Feature Animation returned to making commercially and critically successful animated films based predominantly on well-known stories, having suffered a decline after the death of Walt Disney and the departure of the studio's original animators. The ten films of the era are The Little Mermaid (1989), The Rescuers Down Under (1990), Beauty and the Beast (1991), Aladdin (1992), The Lion King (1994), Pocahontas (1995), The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1996), Hercules (1997), Mulan (1998), and Tarzan (1999).

Background

Following Walt Disney's death in 1966, the studio's animated output had declined in popularity and profitability through the 1970s and early 1980s. New management under Michael Eisner, Frank Wells and Jeffrey Katzenberg, together with Roy E. Disney, reinvigorated the animation division, investing in talent and adopting the structure of Broadway musicals for the films' storytelling.

Characteristics and success

The Renaissance films share distinctive features: songs integrated into the narrative, often written by Alan Menken and Howard Ashman; strong female protagonists; comic sidekicks; and story structures modelled on stage musicals. Beauty and the Beast became the first animated film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture, and The Lion King was for a time the highest-grossing animated film ever released. The era also saw the founding of Pixar's partnership with Disney and intensified competition from studios such as DreamWorks Animation.

Decline

By the late 1990s the films' returns had begun to level off amid rising production costs and market saturation with imitative animated features, and the rise of computer animation after Toy Story (1995) shifted the industry's direction, bringing the Renaissance period to a close.
