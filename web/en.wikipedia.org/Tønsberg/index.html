Tønsberg

Tønsberg is a city and municipality in Vestfold county, Norway, on the western shore of the Oslofjord. It is often described as Norway's oldest city, traditionally founded in 871, before the Battle of Hafrsfjord. Tønsberg is the administrative centre of Vestfold county and a regional centre for commerce, shipping, and industry.

History

According to Snorri Sturluson, Tønsberg was founded before the Battle of Hafrsfjord (traditionally dated 872), making it one of Scandinavia's oldest towns. The nearby Oseberg ship burial of 834, excavated in 1904, testifies to the area's Viking Age importance. In the Middle Ages Tønsberg was a royal residence and Hanseatic trading post; the fortress on Slottsfjellet (Castle Mountain) was one of Norway's largest medieval strongholds. The Bagler civil-war faction took its name from the town. Whaling and shipping dominated the 19th-century economy, and the city was a centre of the sealing and whaling fleets.

Geography

The municipality, enlarged in 2020 by a merger with Re, stretches along the Oslofjord and includes the northern tip of the island of Nøtterøy across a bridge. The tidal-current channel and marina make it a popular summer boating destination.

Landmarks

Slottsfjell Museum and the Slottsfjellet tower (built 1888) overlook the town. The Oseberg and Gokstad Viking ships, excavated nearby, are now displayed in Oslo. Tønsberg Cathedral dates from 1858.
