Denton True "Cy" Young (March 29, 1867 – November 4, 1955) was an American Major League Baseball pitcher. During his 22-season career (1890–1911), he pitched for five teams, most notably the Cleveland Spiders and the Boston Americans/Red Sox. Young established numerous pitching records, some of which have stood for over a century.

Career

Young's 511 career wins are the most in major league history and remain widely considered unbreakable; he also leads in games started, complete games (749), and innings pitched (7,356). In 1901 he won the American League pitching Triple Crown, and in 1903 he helped Boston win the first modern World Series. He pitched three no-hitters, including a perfect game in 1904.

Legacy

Young was elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1937. A year after his death, the Cy Young Award was created to honour the best pitcher in each league; since 1967 it has been given to one pitcher in each league annually.
