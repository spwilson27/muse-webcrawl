Shrimp and grits

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: shrimp and grits with bacon and scallions]

Shrimp and grits is a dish of the cuisine of the Southern United States, particularly associated with the Lowcountry of South Carolina and Georgia. It consists of shrimp cooked — often in a sauce of bacon fat, garlic, and stock, sometimes with tomatoes — and served over a bed of grits, a porridge of stone-ground corn.

The dish began as a humble breakfast of coastal fishermen, who sautéed freshly caught shrimp in bacon grease and spooned them over plain grits seasoned with salt. For generations it was little known outside the Charleston area, where it was sometimes called "breakfast shrimp".

Rise to prominence

Shrimp and grits became nationally famous in the 1980s after chef Bill Neal of Crook's Corner in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, served an enriched version with cheese grits, mushrooms, bacon, and lemon; Craig Claiborne praised the dish in The New York Times in 1985. It has since become a signature of New Southern cooking, appearing on menus across the United States, with variations including cheese grits, Tasso ham gravy, and Cajun-style preparations.
