Albedo
Reflection of solar radiation from surfaces of differing albedo
Symbolα
QuantityReflectance (dimensionless)
Range0 (black body absorber) – 1 (perfect reflector)

Albedo (from Latin albedo, "whiteness") is the fraction of sunlight that a surface or body reflects back into space. It is a dimensionless quantity measured on a scale from 0, corresponding to a perfectly absorbing black body, to 1, corresponding to a perfectly reflecting surface. Albedo is a key concept in climatology, astronomy and environmental science.

Earth's average planetary albedo is about 0.30, largely because of clouds and polar ice. The Moon's albedo is only about 0.12, despite appearing bright in the night sky, while Venus, completely covered by reflective sulphuric-acid clouds, has an albedo near 0.75 — the highest of any planet in the Solar System. The Saturnian moon Enceladus, coated in fresh ice, approaches 0.99 in geometric albedo.

Climate effects

Albedo strongly influences surface temperature. The ice–albedo feedback is a positive feedback in climate change: warming melts bright ice and snow, exposing darker land or ocean that absorbs more sunlight and warms further. Conversely, large volcanic eruptions and aerosols can raise planetary albedo and cause temporary cooling. Human land-use changes — deforestation, urbanisation and agriculture — also modify regional albedo.

Astronomical albedo

Astronomers distinguish the Bond albedo (the fraction of total incident electromagnetic energy reflected in all directions) from the geometric albedo (the brightness at zero phase angle compared with a perfectly diffusing disc). Albedo measurements help determine the surface composition of asteroids and are used to estimate the sizes of distant Kuiper belt objects.
