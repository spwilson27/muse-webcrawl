The Cleveland Orchestra is an American orchestra based in Cleveland, Ohio. Founded in 1918, it is traditionally considered one of the "Big Five" orchestras of the United States and is widely praised for its precision, transparency of sound and chamber-music-like discipline.

History

The orchestra was founded by pianist and impresario Adella Prentiss Hughes, with Nikolai Sokoloff as its first music director. Artur Rodziński followed, and in 1946 George Szell began a celebrated 24-year tenure, drilling the orchestra into an instrument of legendary exactness and building an extensive, highly regarded discography for Columbia Records. Lorin Maazel and Christoph von Dohnányi succeeded him.

Home and activities

Since 1931 the orchestra's home has been Severance Hall (now Severance Music Center), noted for its art deco and neoclassical design and fine acoustics. Its summer festival is held at the Blossom Music Center in Cuyahoga Falls. Franz Welser-Möst became music director in 2002; the orchestra maintains residencies in Miami and Vienna and a large education and community programme in Northeast Ohio.

Reputation

Gramophone magazine ranked Cleveland among the world's finest orchestras, and critics regularly cite its recordings under Szell — of Mozart, Haydn, Beethoven, Brahms, Dvořák and Prokofiev — as reference versions.
