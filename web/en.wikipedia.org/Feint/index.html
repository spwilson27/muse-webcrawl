Feint

Feint, a French term that entered English via the discipline of swordsmanship, is a maneuver designed to distract or mislead, whether in warfare, in combat sports, or in other fields. A feint is a deceptive action, attack, or movement intended to draw an opponent's attention and resources toward one point while the real effort is directed elsewhere.

In warfare

In military usage, a feint is a mock attack or demonstration intended to deceive the enemy about the location or timing of the main attack. Feints may involve limited contact with the enemy — enough to appear credible — while the main effort falls elsewhere. Historically notable examples include feint operations preceding amphibious landings, such as the deception measures surrounding the Second World War's Operation Overlord, in which phantom formations and false attacks suggested landings at the Pas-de-Calais rather than Normandy.

In combat sports

In fencing, boxing, and martial arts, a feint is a deceptive movement — a false thrust, punch, or step — used to provoke a reaction from the opponent, opening a line of attack. In fencing the feint is one of the standard compound attacks: an apparent attack into one line that draws a parry, followed by a disengage and a real attack into another line. A "feint in time" similarly deceives the opponent about when an action will occur rather than where.

Other uses

The term is also used in team sports (for example, a feinted pass in football) and in general usage for any sham or pretended action. In printing, "feint" (or "faint") ruled paper refers to paper bearing very light lines.
