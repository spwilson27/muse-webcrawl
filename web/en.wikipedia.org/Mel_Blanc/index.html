Mel Blanc

Melvin Jerome Blanc (born Blank; May 30, 1908 – July 10, 1989) was an American voice actor and radio personality whose career spanned over 60 years. Known as "The Man of a Thousand Voices", he was the original voice of Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, Porky Pig and most of the other characters from the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies theatrical cartoons produced by Warner Bros. during the golden age of American animation.

DiedJuly 10, 1989 (aged 81), Los Angeles, California, U.S.

Early career

Born in San Francisco and raised in Portland, Oregon, Blanc began his career in radio in 1927, performing voices and sound impressions on local stations. He moved to Los Angeles and became a regular on network radio comedy, most famously on The Jack Benny Program, where he played characters including Benny's Maxwell automobile (voiced entirely by Blanc's sputtering noises), Professor LeBlanc and Sy the Mexican.

Warner Bros.

Blanc joined Leon Schlesinger's cartoon studio in 1936. He took over Porky Pig in 1937 and soon created the voices of Daffy Duck, Bugs Bunny (from 1940), Tweety, Sylvester, Yosemite Sam, Foghorn Leghorn, Speedy Gonzales, Marvin the Martian and hundreds of others. He negotiated a contract giving him screen credit — rare for voice actors of the era — with the billing "Voice characterizations by Mel Blanc".

Later work

For Hanna-Barbera he voiced Barney Rubble in The Flintstones and characters in many other series. In 1961 he survived a near-fatal car accident, recording lines from a hospital bed and later at home in a full-body cast. He continued performing his classic characters in new productions into the 1980s. Blanc died in 1989; his gravestone reads "That's All Folks", the closing line of the Warner cartoons.
