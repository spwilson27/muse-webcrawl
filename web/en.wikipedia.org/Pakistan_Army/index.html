The Pakistan Army is the land service branch and largest component of the Pakistan Armed Forces. It traces its origins to the British Indian Army regiments allocated to Pakistan at independence in 1947. The President of Pakistan is its ceremonial commander-in-chief, while professional command rests with the Chief of Army Staff, headquartered at the General Headquarters (GHQ) in Rawalpindi.

Organization

The army is organized into corps headquartered around the country — including I Corps at Mangla, II Corps at Multan, IV Corps at Lahore, V Corps at Karachi, X Corps at Rawalpindi, and XI Corps at Peshawar — together with the strategic Army Strategic Forces Command, which controls land-based nuclear delivery systems. Its fighting arms include infantry regiments such as the Punjab, Baloch, Frontier Force, Sindh, and Azad Kashmir regiments, the Armoured Corps, artillery, air defence, engineers, signals, and the Army Aviation Corps. Officer training takes place at the Pakistan Military Academy at Kakul, Abbottabad.

History

The army has fought in the Indo-Pakistani wars of 1947, 1965, and 1971, the Kargil conflict of 1999, and the Siachen conflict with India since 1984. It has also conducted extensive counter-insurgency operations in the former Federally Administered Tribal Areas and Swat Valley, and it has repeatedly intervened in politics, imposing martial law in 1958, 1969, 1977, and 1999. Pakistan Army contingents are among the largest contributors to UN peacekeeping deployments.
