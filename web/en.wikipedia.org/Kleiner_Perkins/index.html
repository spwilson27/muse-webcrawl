Kleiner Perkins

Kleiner Perkins' headquarters on Sand Hill Road, Menlo Park.

Kleiner Perkins, formerly Kleiner Perkins Caufield & Byers (KPCB), is an American venture capital firm headquartered in Menlo Park, California, on Sand Hill Road. Founded in 1972 by Eugene Kleiner and Tom Perkins, it is one of the oldest and most influential firms in Silicon Valley, an early backer of companies including Genentech, Sun Microsystems, Amazon, Google, Electronic Arts, Intuit, Netscape and Twitter.

History

Kleiner, one of the "traitorous eight" who founded Fairchild Semiconductor, and Perkins, a Hewlett-Packard executive, raised an initial $8 million fund in 1972; Frank Caufield and Brook Byers joined later in the decade. The firm pioneered the practice of partners taking active roles in portfolio companies — Perkins chaired Tandem Computers and Genentech — and its 1976 investment in Genentech helped launch the biotechnology industry. John Doerr, who joined in 1980, led investments in Sun, Netscape, Amazon and Google and became one of the industry's most prominent figures.

Recent decades

In the 2000s the firm made a high-profile push into green technology and later returned to its roots in early-stage technology investing, backing companies such as Uber, Spotify, Slack, Robinhood and DoorDash. In 2015 the firm won a widely publicised gender-discrimination suit brought by former partner Ellen Pao, a case that intensified debate about diversity in venture capital. The firm shortened its name to Kleiner Perkins in 2019.
