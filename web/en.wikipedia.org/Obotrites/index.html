Obotrites

The Obotrites (German: Abodriten) were a confederation of medieval West Slavic tribes situated in what is now Mecklenburg and Holstein in northern Germany, along the Baltic coast between the Elbe and the Oder. They were among the Polabian Slavs and frequently appear in Frankish and Saxon chronicles from the 8th to the 12th centuries.

History

First mentioned around 789, the Obotrites initially allied with Charlemagne against the Saxons and Danes, receiving land in Holstein in return. Their princes, such as Thrasco and later the Nakonids, ruled from strongholds like Mecklenburg castle. The Obotrite prince Gottschalk (c. 1043–1066) built a Christian Slavic state with bishoprics at Oldenburg, Mecklenburg, and Ratzeburg, but he was killed in a pagan uprising. His son Henry later re-established the principality with Danish and Saxon support.

In the 12th century the Obotrite lands were conquered during the Wendish Crusade (1147) and by Henry the Lion of Saxony; the last Obotrite prince, Pribislav, accepted baptism and Saxon overlordship and founded the House of Mecklenburg, which ruled until 1918. Over subsequent centuries the Slavic-speaking population was gradually Germanised, though Slavic place names remain pervasive in the region.

Society and religion

The Obotrites were organised into sub-tribes, including the Wagri, Polabi, and Warnabi, governed by princes and assemblies. They practised Slavic paganism with cult centres such as Rethra (associated with the wider region) and worshipped deities including Radegast and Prove, resisting Christian mission for centuries.
