Human semen

Summary

Components: Spermatozoa and seminal plasma

Produced by: Testes, seminal vesicles, prostate, bulbourethral glands

Typical volume: 1.5–5 mL per ejaculate

pH: 7.2–8.0

Human semen, also known as seminal fluid, is an organic fluid created to contain spermatozoa. It is secreted by the male gonads (sexual glands) and other sexual organs and is ejaculated during the male orgasm. Its primary biological function is to transport sperm cells to the female reproductive tract to enable fertilisation of the ovum.

Composition

Semen is composed of spermatozoa, produced in the testes, suspended in seminal plasma. The seminal vesicles contribute the largest share of the fluid volume, including fructose, which provides energy for sperm. The prostate gland contributes enzymes such as prostate-specific antigen (PSA), zinc, and citric acid. The bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands secrete a clear mucus that lubricates the urethra. Although sperm make up only a small fraction of the volume, a typical ejaculate may contain tens to hundreds of millions of sperm cells.

Characteristics

Fresh semen is a viscous, whitish, opalescent fluid with a mildly alkaline pH, which helps neutralise the acidity of the vaginal environment. It typically coagulates briefly after ejaculation and then liquefies within about 15 to 30 minutes through the action of prostate enzymes.

Health and analysis

A semen analysis is a standard laboratory test in the evaluation of male fertility, measuring volume, sperm concentration, motility, and morphology. The World Health Organization publishes reference values for normal semen parameters. Semen can also transmit sexually transmitted infections, including HIV, which is why barrier contraception is recommended for disease prevention.
