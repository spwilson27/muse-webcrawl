Michael Laban Walzer (born March 3, 1935) is an American political theorist and public intellectual, professor emeritus at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey, and long-time co-editor of the democratic socialist journal Dissent. He is one of the leading contemporary writers on the ethics of war and distributive justice.

Work

Just and Unjust Wars (1977) revived just war theory in Anglophone philosophy in the wake of the Vietnam War, articulating the distinction between jus ad bellum (justice of going to war) and jus in bello (just conduct in war), the principle of non-combatant immunity, and the doctrine of "supreme emergency". The book became required reading in military academies and remains a standard reference on the morality of war.

In Spheres of Justice (1983), Walzer argued against a single distributive principle: different social goods — money, political power, education, healthcare, membership — belong to distinct "spheres", each with its own internal distributive logic derived from shared social meanings. Justice, on his account, is "complex equality": preventing advantage in one sphere (such as wealth) from translating into dominance in others (such as political power). His communitarian approach and method of "connected" social criticism — the critic as insider interpreting a community's own values — influenced debates with liberals such as John Rawls.

Other works include Interpretation and Social Criticism (1987), On Toleration (1997), Exodus and Revolution (1985), and The Jewish Political Tradition, which he co-edited.
