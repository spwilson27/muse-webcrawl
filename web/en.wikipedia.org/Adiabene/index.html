Adiabene

Adiabene was an ancient kingdom in northern Mesopotamia, roughly corresponding to part of modern Iraqi Kurdistan, between the Great Zab and Little Zab rivers. Its capital was Arbela (modern Erbil).

History

Adiabene was at various times a vassal of the Parthian Empire and later contested between Rome and Persia. In the 1st century CE its royal house — notably Queen Helena and King Izates II — famously converted to Judaism, and members of the dynasty supported Jerusalem, where Helena built palaces and was buried.

Christianity

The region became an early centre of Syriac Christianity; the Church of the East maintained a metropolitan province of Adiabene centred on Erbil.
