Photolyase

Enzyme classDNA repair enzyme
ReactionReversal of pyrimidine dimers using light energy

Photolyases are DNA repair enzymes that repair damage caused by exposure to ultraviolet light. They use energy from visible light in a mechanism known as photoreactivation.

Function

Ultraviolet radiation causes adjacent pyrimidine bases in DNA to fuse into cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers, distorting the helix and blocking replication. Photolyase binds these lesions and, upon absorbing blue or near-UV light, transfers an electron that splits the dimer and restores the original bases.

Distribution

Photolyases occur in bacteria, fungi, plants and many animals, but placental mammals (including humans) lack them and rely instead on nucleotide excision repair. Cryptochromes, proteins involved in circadian rhythms, evolved from photolyases.
