Agulhas National Park

Agulhas National Park is a South African national park situated in the Agulhas Plain in the Western Cape, around Cape Agulhas — the southernmost point of the African continent, where the Atlantic and Indian Oceans meet. Proclaimed in 1999, the park protects an area of exceptional botanical diversity within the Cape Floristic Region.

Geography and nature

The park covers roughly 21,000 hectares of coastal lowland fynbos, wetlands, and limestone hills. The Agulhas Plain hosts thousands of plant species, many endemic, together with wetlands of international importance for migratory birds. Offshore, the waters mark the meeting of the warm Agulhas Current and the cold Benguela system.

Landmarks

The Cape Agulhas Lighthouse, built in 1848 and modelled on the Pharos of Alexandria, is a prominent landmark. The region's coast, known as part of the "Graveyard of Ships," has claimed hundreds of vessels, including the Japanese trawler Meisho Maru No. 38, whose wreck remains visible near the cape.

Conservation

Management focuses on clearing invasive alien plants, restoring wetlands, and expanding the park through land purchases to connect fragmented fynbos habitats.
