Mogoșoaia Palace

Mogoșoaia Palace (Palatul Mogoșoaia)

Location: Mogoșoaia, Ilfov County, Romania

Built: 1698–1702

Architectural style: Romanian Renaissance (Brâncovenesc)

Built for: Constantin Brâncoveanu

Mogoșoaia Palace (Romanian: Palatul Mogoșoaia) is situated about 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) from Bucharest, Romania. It was built between 1698 and 1702 by Constantin Brâncoveanu, Prince of Wallachia, in what is termed the Romanian Renaissance style, also known as the Brâncovenesc style — a combination of Venetian and Ottoman elements, characterised by carved stone loggias, external staircases, and rich ornamentation.

History

The palace was built on land near Lake Mogoșoaia, which Brâncoveanu had acquired from the widow Mogoș, after whom the estate is named. After Brâncoveanu and his family were executed by the Ottomans in 1714, the palace was seized and converted into an inn, and it suffered damage during later wars. In the 1830s it passed to the Bibescu family. Prince George Bibescu renovated it, and his descendant, Princess Martha Bibescu, undertook a celebrated restoration in the early 20th century, making Mogoșoaia a centre of cultural and political salon life.

The complex

The ensemble includes the palace itself, the watchtower, the cuhnia (kitchen building), guest houses, the church of Saint George (built 1688) near the entrance, and extensive gardens and parkland descending to the lake. After the Second World War the complex was nationalised; it now functions as a museum and cultural centre, housing the Brâncovenesc art museum and hosting exhibitions, concerts, and events.
