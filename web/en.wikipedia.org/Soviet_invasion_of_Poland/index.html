Soviet invasion of Poland

Red Army soldiers in Poland, 1939

The Soviet invasion of Poland was a military operation by the Soviet Union without a formal declaration of war. On 17 September 1939, the Soviet Union's Red Army invaded Eastern Poland. The territory was then split according to the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact with Nazi Germany, which had invaded Poland from the west on 1 September 1939, beginning the Second World War.

Background

The secret protocol of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 23 August 1939 divided Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of influence. After the German invasion, the Soviet government claimed that the Polish state had ceased to exist and that it was intervening to protect the Ukrainian and Belarusian populations of Poland's eastern territories (the Kresy).

The invasion

Soviet forces, numbering more than 450,000 troops in the initial assault echelon, faced a Polish defence already collapsing under the German onslaught; Polish commander-in-chief Edward Rydz-Śmigły ordered border units not to engage the Soviets except in self-defence, although fighting nevertheless occurred at several points, including Wilno, Grodno and Lwów. By early October, Poland had been completely divided between Germany and the Soviet Union.

Aftermath

The Soviet Union annexed some 201,000 square kilometres of Polish territory with a population of about 13.5 million. The occupation was accompanied by mass arrests, deportations of Polish citizens to Siberia and Kazakhstan, and the murder of some 22,000 Polish military officers and intelligentsia in the Katyn massacre of 1940. The annexed territories were incorporated into the Ukrainian and Byelorussian Soviet republics, a settlement substantially confirmed after the war.
