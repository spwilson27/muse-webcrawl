UNRWA

The United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) is a UN agency created in 1949 to provide relief, education, health care and social services to Palestine refugees in the Gaza Strip, the West Bank (including East Jerusalem), Jordan, Lebanon and Syria. It is the only UN agency dedicated to a specific group of refugees.

Definition and mandate

UNRWA defines Palestine refugees as persons whose normal place of residence was Palestine between 1 June 1946 and 15 May 1948 and who lost both home and means of livelihood as a result of the 1948 conflict, together with their descendants. The agency's mandate, originally temporary, has been repeatedly renewed by the UN General Assembly pending a political settlement of the refugee question.

Services

Education is UNRWA's largest programme, operating around 700 elementary and preparatory schools attended by more than half a million pupils, plus vocational training centres. The agency also runs about 140 primary health clinics, provides food and cash assistance, microfinance, and infrastructure and camp improvement works in the 58 recognised refugee camps. In emergencies, notably in Gaza and Syria, UNRWA provides shelter, food aid and humanitarian relief on a large scale.

Funding and controversies

UNRWA is funded almost entirely by voluntary contributions, chiefly from governments such as the United States, Germany and EU institutions, and has faced chronic funding shortfalls. Its role is politically contested: critics, including successive Israeli governments, accuse it of perpetuating refugee status and of failures over staff neutrality, while supporters argue it provides essential services and regional stability. In 2024 several donor states temporarily suspended funding following allegations about staff involvement in the 7 October 2023 attacks, most later resuming contributions.
