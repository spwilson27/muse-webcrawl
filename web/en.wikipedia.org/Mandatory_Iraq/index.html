The Mandatory Iraq, formally the Kingdom of Iraq under British mandate, was a League of Nations Class A mandate administered by the United Kingdom from 1920 until Iraq's admission to the League as an independent state in 1932.

History

Following the Ottoman defeat in the First World War, Britain combined the former Ottoman provinces of Basra, Baghdad, and Mosul into a single territory. A major revolt in 1920 led Britain to install Faisal, formerly king of Syria, as king in 1921 under a system of indirect rule. The Anglo-Iraqi Treaty of 1930 prepared the way for nominal independence while preserving British military and oil interests.

Legacy

The mandate period shaped Iraq's borders, its Hashemite monarchy, and its oil industry, and it set the pattern for British influence that lasted until the revolution of 1958.
