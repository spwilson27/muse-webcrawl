Free kick (association football)

A free kick is a method of restarting play in association football, awarded to a team when an opponent commits a foul or other infringement. Free kicks are governed by Law 13 of the Laws of the Game and come in two kinds: direct free kicks, from which a goal may be scored directly, and indirect free kicks, from which a goal counts only if the ball touches another player first — the referee signals an indirect kick by raising one arm.

Procedure

The kick is taken from where the offence occurred (or, for offences in the goal area or penalty area, under special placement rules). Opponents must remain at least 9.15 m (10 yards) from the ball until it is in play; defending teams commonly form a "wall" of players to protect the goal. A free kick awarded for a direct-kick offence inside the offender's own penalty area is instead given as a penalty kick. The ball must be stationary, and the kicker may not touch it again until another player has.

Offences

Direct free kicks are awarded for careless, reckless, or excessively forceful challenges, holding, handball (with exceptions), and similar offences; indirect free kicks cover offences such as dangerous play, impeding an opponent without contact, goalkeeper handling violations, and offside restarts. Noted free-kick specialists — players such as David Beckham, Juninho Pernambucano, and Lionel Messi — have built reputations on scoring directly from these set pieces.
