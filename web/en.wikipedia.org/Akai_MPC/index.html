Akai MPC

The Akai MPC (originally MIDI Production Center, now Music Production Center) is a series of music workstations produced by Akai from 1988 onwards that combine sampling and sequencing functions. Designed in collaboration with drum machine designer Roger Linn, the MPC became one of the most influential instruments in hip hop and electronic music production.

History

The first model, the MPC60 (1988), combined a 12-bit sampler with a sequencer and velocity-sensitive pads. The MPC3000 (1994) refined the concept, and the MPC2000 and MPC2000XL made the line more affordable and ubiquitous in studios. Later models added hard-disk recording, colour screens and computer integration; modern MPCs such as the MPC Live and MPC X are standalone production systems with large touchscreens.

Impact

The MPC's pad-based workflow and its characteristic swing quantization shaped the sound of golden-age hip hop and producers such as J Dilla, DJ Premier and Kanye West. Its sample-chopping workflow remains a standard paradigm in beat-making.
