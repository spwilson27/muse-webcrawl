Marlborough House is a mansion in St James's, London, on Pall Mall just east of St James's Palace. It was built in 1711 for Sarah Churchill, Duchess of Marlborough, to designs by Christopher Wren. The house later became a royal residence and is today the headquarters of the Commonwealth Secretariat.

History

Sarah Churchill, the formidable wife of the 1st Duke of Marlborough, obtained the site from Queen Anne before their famous quarrel, and Wren's red-brick mansion was completed in 1711. In 1817 the house was bought for Princess Charlotte, and after her death it passed through several royal occupants, including Queen Adelaide and later Edward, Prince of Wales (the future Edward VII), for whom it became a major social centre.

Commonwealth Secretariat

In 1959 Queen Elizabeth II made the house available to the Commonwealth, and since 1965 it has served as the headquarters of the Commonwealth Secretariat and the seat of the Commonwealth Secretary-General.
