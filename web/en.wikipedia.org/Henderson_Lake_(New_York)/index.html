Henderson Lake is a lake in the Adirondack Mountains of Essex County, New York, in the town of Newcomb. It is historically significant as the lake near whose outlet the Hudson River begins: the outflow of Henderson Lake flows southward and is conventionally regarded, along with nearby Lake Tear of the Clouds, as part of the headwaters system of the Hudson River, which flows some 315 miles south to New York City.

History

The lake is named for David Henderson, a co-owner of the Adirondack Iron Works at the nearby settlement of Tahawus (the "Upper Works"). In 1826 Henderson and his partners followed an Native American guide to rich iron ore deposits near the lake, and the iron works were established at the outlet. In 1845 Henderson died in an accidental shooting near the lake; a stone monument known as the Henderson Monument, and the nearby Calamity Pond, commemorate the event ("Calamity" being the name given to the pond in memory of the accident).

In September 1901, Vice President Theodore Roosevelt was vacationing at the Camp Santanoni area and climbing Mount Marcy when he received the news that President William McKinley, shot days earlier, was dying. His famous midnight wagon ride out of the mountains to the railhead at North Creek began from the Tahawus area near Henderson Lake.

Recreation

The lake and its surroundings, within the Adirondack Forest Preserve's High Peaks Wilderness area, are used for paddling, fishing, and hiking. A lean-to and campsites are maintained near the shore, and the lake offers views toward the MacIntyre Range and other High Peaks.
