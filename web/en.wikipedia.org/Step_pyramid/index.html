Step pyramid

The Step Pyramid of Djoser at Saqqara, Egypt — the earliest colossal stone building in Egypt.

A step pyramid is an architectural structure built in stepped layers, resembling a series of stacked platforms of decreasing size. Step pyramids were constructed by several ancient civilisations, most famously in Egypt and Mesopotamia (where they are called ziggurats), and in Mesoamerica.

Egypt

The earliest Egyptian step pyramid is the Pyramid of Djoser at Saqqara, built during the Third Dynasty (around 2670 BCE) for the pharaoh Djoser by his architect Imhotep. It began as a square mastaba and was enlarged in stages into a six-stepped pyramid about 62 metres high, surrounded by a vast funerary complex of courtyards, chapels and dummy buildings in stone. It marks the transition from mudbrick mastaba tombs to the smooth-sided pyramids of Giza; the later Meidum pyramid began as a step pyramid whose steps were subsequently filled in.

Mesopotamia

Mesopotamian ziggurats, such as the Ziggurat of Ur and the Etemenanki of Babylon, were stepped temple towers of mudbrick with a shrine at the summit, serving as a platform between the human and divine realms.

Mesoamerica and elsewhere

Pre-Columbian cultures built stepped temple pyramids — including the Pyramid of the Sun at Teotihuacan, El Castillo at Chichen Itza, and Aztec and Maya temples generally — typically with a stairway to a summit shrine. Stepped pyramid forms also occur in Southeast Asia, for example at the temple-mountain of Angkor Wat.
