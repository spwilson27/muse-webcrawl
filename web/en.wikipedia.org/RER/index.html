RER

Réseau Express Régional (RER)
Location: Paris and Île-de-France, France
Opened: 1977
Lines: 5 (A–E)
Stations: 257
Length: 602 km

The RER (Réseau Express Régional, "Regional Express Network") is a hybrid commuter rail and rapid transit system serving Paris and its surrounding Île-de-France region. It combines underground stations in central Paris with surface suburban lines, carrying passengers from distant suburbs through the city centre without changing trains — a model later imitated in many cities.

History

The RER grew out of plans dating to the 1930s and the regional master plan of 1965, which proposed east–west and north–south regional express lines. The first section of what became Line A, between Nation and Boissy-Saint-Léger, opened in 1969; the network was formally inaugurated in 1977 when the east–west line crossed the city. Lines are operated jointly by the RATP, which runs the Paris Métro, and the national railway SNCF.

Network

The system has five lines, A through E. Line A, running east–west via Châtelet–Les Halles and La Défense, is one of the busiest urban rail lines in the world. Double-decker trains, deep central stations and through-running distinguish the RER from the Métro, which it complements within the city. The network is being extended westward with the prolongation of Line E (the EOLE project).
