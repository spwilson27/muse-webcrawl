Leafcutter ant

Leafcutter ants are ants of the genera Atta and Acromyrmex (tribe Attini), found in tropical and subtropical Central and South America. They are named for cutting fragments of leaves, which they carry back to underground nests — not to eat directly, but to feed a symbiotic fungus (Leucoagaricus gongylophorus) that the ants cultivate in fungus gardens and consume.

Leafcutter colonies are among the largest and most complex insect societies, with up to several million workers divided into size-based castes, from tiny gardeners to large soldiers with powerful mandibles capable of cutting leaves and defending the colony. Their nests can extend many metres underground and their harvesting significantly affects forest ecosystems; in some regions they are major agricultural pests.

The fungus-growing system, maintained with antibiotics produced by bacteria on the ants' bodies, evolved tens of millions of years ago and is a classic example of mutualism.
