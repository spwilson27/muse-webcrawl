Pere Ubu

Pere Ubu is an American rock band formed in Cleveland, Ohio, in 1975. Named after Père Ubu, the protagonist of Alfred Jarry's play Ubu Roi (1896), the band is regarded as a pioneer of underground and alternative rock, describing its own music as "avant-garage". Fronted throughout by singer David Thomas (1953–2025), Pere Ubu fused rock and roll with electronic noise, musique concrète influences and art-rock experimentation.

History

Pere Ubu grew out of the Cleveland proto-punk band Rocket from the Tombs. After early singles such as "30 Seconds Over Tokyo" (1975), the band released the landmark albums The Modern Dance and Dub Housing in 1978, featuring Allen Ravenstine's synthesizer textures and David Thomas's distinctive vocals. The group disbanded in 1982 and reformed in 1987, pursuing a more pop-oriented direction on albums such as The Tenement Year (1988) before returning to more experimental work.

Legacy

Though never commercially successful on a large scale, Pere Ubu is frequently cited as one of the most influential American underground bands, an inspiration to post-punk, indie rock and noise artists. David Thomas remained the band's constant member until his death in April 2025.
