Crisis communication

Crisis communication is a sub-field of public relations concerned with protecting an organization's reputation when it faces a public challenge, emergency, or scandal. It involves preparing for crises in advance and communicating with stakeholders and the media during and after the event.

Practice

Crisis communication typically includes a pre-drafted crisis plan, designated spokespersons, rapid acknowledgment of the problem, accurate and consistent messaging, and expressions of concern for those affected. Practitioners study response strategies ranging from denial and evasion of responsibility to corrective action and full apology, frameworks formalized in theories such as W. Timothy Coombs's Situational Crisis Communication Theory.

Famous cases

Johnson & Johnson's handling of the 1982 Tylenol tampering murders — swift product recall and transparent communication — is frequently cited as exemplary, while slow or defensive responses in cases such as the Exxon Valdez spill are cited as failures.
