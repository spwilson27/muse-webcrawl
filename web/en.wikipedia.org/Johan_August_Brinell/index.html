Johan August Brinell

Johan August Brinell (21 June 1849 – 17 November 1925) was a Swedish metallurgist best known for inventing the Brinell hardness test, a method for measuring the hardness of metals.

Born21 June 1849, Bringetofta, Sweden
Died17 November 1925, Stockholm
NationalitySwedish
Known forBrinell hardness test

Life and career

Brinell worked at ironworks in Lesjöfors and Fagersta, becoming chief engineer at Fagersta Ironworks. He studied transformations in steel during heating and cooling.

Brinell hardness test

Proposed in 1900, the test presses a hardened steel or tungsten carbide ball into a material under load and measures the diameter of the indentation. The Brinell hardness number (HB) became a standard in engineering and metallurgy, related to hardness and steel.

Legacy

Brinell was elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences and the Royal Swedish Academy of Engineering Sciences.
