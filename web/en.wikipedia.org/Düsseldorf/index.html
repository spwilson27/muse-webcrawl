Düsseldorf

Düsseldorf is the capital city of the German state of North Rhine-Westphalia and the seventh-largest city in Germany, with a population of over 600,000. It lies on the banks of the Rhine, at the confluence with the small Düssel river from which it takes its name.

History

The settlement was first mentioned in 1135 and received town privileges in 1288 following the Battle of Worringen. It grew as the capital of the Duchy of Berg and later of the United Duchies of Jülich-Cleves-Berg. Heavily rebuilt after the Second World War, it developed into a centre of commerce and fashion.

Economy and culture

Düsseldorf is a major financial, telecommunications and trade-fair centre, noted for the Königsallee shopping boulevard, its fashion industry, and the Königsallee and Media Harbour (MedienHafen) architecture. The city is famous for its Altbier beer, its carnival, and its large Japanese community, one of the largest in Europe. It also has a friendly rivalry with neighbouring Cologne.

The city is home to the art academy where Joseph Beuys taught, the electronic music pioneers Kraftwerk, and the Kunstsammlung Nordrhein-Westfalen museums.
