Wawel Cathedral

Location: Wawel Hill, Kraków, Poland
Consecrated: 1364 (present Gothic church)
Denomination: Roman Catholic

Wawel Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Basilica of Saints Stanislaus and Wenceslaus, is a Roman Catholic church on Wawel Hill in Kraków, Poland. For centuries it was the coronation church of Polish monarchs and their principal burial place, making it one of the most historically important churches in the country and a national sanctuary.

History

The present Gothic cathedral, consecrated in 1364, is the third church on the site; two earlier Romanesque cathedrals were destroyed by fire and invasion. Between the 14th and 18th centuries nearly all Polish kings were crowned here, and most are buried in its crypts, together with national heroes such as Tadeusz Kościuszko and Józef Piłsudski, and the poet Adam Mickiewicz.

Architecture and treasures

The cathedral is surrounded by a ring of chapels from different eras, the most famous being the Renaissance Sigismund Chapel (1519–1533), with its gilded dome, considered a masterpiece of Renaissance art north of the Alps. The Sigismund Tower houses the Sigismund Bell, cast in 1520 and rung only on major occasions. The cathedral treasury and museum hold royal regalia, including the Szczerbiec coronation sword.
