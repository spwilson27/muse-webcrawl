Bernd Schneider (born 20 July 1964) is a German racing driver, widely known as "Mister DTM" for his record five championships in the Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters with Mercedes-Benz. Though his Formula One career in the late 1980s was unproductive, he became one of the most successful touring car and GT drivers of his generation, also winning the 1997 FIA GT Championship and the 2013 24 Hours of Nürburgring and Dubai and Spa endurance races with Mercedes-AMG customer teams.

Early career and Formula One

Schneider dominated German karting and won the 1987 German Formula Three Championship, attracting the attention of Erich Zakowski's Zakspeed Formula One team, with which he made his Grand Prix debut in 1988. The underfunded Zakspeed and a switch to Arrows in 1990 yielded nine starts and no points, largely because of pre-qualifying failures, and Schneider turned to sports cars.

Sportscars and ITC/DTM

With Joest Porsche and then Mercedes, Schneider flourished: he won races in the Interserie and World Sportscar Championship, and in 1995 won both the DTM and the International Touring Car Championship (ITC) titles for Mercedes. In 1997 he won the FIA GT Championship with the Mercedes-Benz CLK GTR, and raced the CLK-LM at Le Mans in 1998.

DTM record

When the DTM was relaunched in 2000, Schneider won the title at the first attempt, and added championships in 2001, 2003, and 2006 — five DTM crowns in total, a record — along with 43 victories, also a series benchmark for many years. He retired from full-time DTM racing at the end of 2008 but continued to race successfully in GT3 endurance events with Mercedes-AMG into his fifties, collecting outright wins at the Nürburgring 24 Hours (2013, 2016), Spa 24 Hours (2013), and Dubai 24 Hour (2013, 2015). He has also served as a brand ambassador and driver coach for Mercedes-AMG.
