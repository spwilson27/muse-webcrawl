Mecca, officially Makkah al-Mukarramah, is the capital of Mecca Province in the Hejaz region of western Saudi Arabia and the holiest city in Islam. It lies in a desert valley roughly 70 km inland from Jeddah on the Red Sea. As the birthplace of the prophet Muhammad and the site of the Kaaba — the cube-shaped shrine within the Great Mosque (Masjid al-Haram) toward which Muslims pray — Mecca is the destination of the Hajj pilgrimage, one of the Five Pillars of Islam, which draws around two to three million pilgrims each year. Non-Muslims are prohibited from entering the city.

Religious significance

Islamic tradition associates Mecca with Abraham (Ibrahim), who is believed to have built the Kaaba with his son Ishmael, and with Hagar, whose search for water for the infant Ishmael is commemorated in the rite of sa'i between the hills of Safa and Marwah; the well of Zamzam is likewise held sacred. Muslims worldwide face the qibla, the direction of the Kaaba, in their daily prayers. Besides the Hajj, millions perform the lesser pilgrimage, Umrah, throughout the year.

History

Mecca was an ancient Arabian trading and religious centre, controlled in Muhammad's time by the tribe of Quraysh. Muhammad was born there around 570 CE and received his first revelations on nearby Mount Hira in 610. Persecution drove him and his followers to emigrate to Medina in 622 (the Hijrah, year 1 of the Islamic calendar); he returned to take the city peacefully in 630 and rededicated the Kaaba to the worship of God alone. Mecca later passed under the Umayyad, Abbasid, and Ottoman caliphates, was ruled by the Hashemite sharifs from the 10th century until 1924–25, and was then conquered by Ibn Saud, founder of Saudi Arabia.

Modern city

Under Saudi rule, oil wealth has transformed Mecca: the Great Mosque has been repeatedly and massively expanded, and the skyline is dominated by the Abraj Al Bait complex, whose Makkah Royal Clock Tower (601 m) is among the world's tallest buildings. Managing the annual Hajj — its logistics, safety, and religious services — is the city's defining task and a major source of income alongside year-round religious tourism.
