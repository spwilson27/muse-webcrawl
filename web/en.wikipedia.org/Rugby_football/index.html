Rugby football

Rugby football is the collective name for the team sports of rugby union and rugby league. The games share a common origin in the football played at Rugby School in England in the nineteenth century, in which players may run with the ball in hand. Both codes are played with an oval ball on a rectangular field with H-shaped goalposts; points are scored mainly by grounding the ball beyond the opponents' goal line for a try, and by kicking goals.

Origins

According to legend, the game began in 1823 when Rugby School pupil William Webb Ellis picked up the ball during a football match and ran with it. Though the story is apocryphal, handling and running were established features of the Rugby School game, whose first written rules were drawn up by pupils in 1845. The Rugby Football Union was founded in 1871, and the first international match, between England and Scotland, took place the same year.

The split of 1895

In 1895, disagreement over compensating working-class players for time missed from work ("broken-time payments") led 22 northern English clubs to break away and form the Northern Rugby Football Union, later the Rugby Football League. The two codes diverged in laws and structure: league reduced teams to 13 players and abolished contested scrums and lineouts in their union form, while union retained 15 players and its set-piece contests. Both games spread through the British Empire, becoming especially entrenched in Wales, New Zealand, South Africa, France, Australia and the Pacific Islands.

Variants

Shortened variants include rugby sevens — an Olympic sport since 2016 — and tens, as well as touch rugby and tag rugby with reduced contact. Modern professionalism, adopted by union in 1995, brought the two codes closer in athleticism while their laws and cultures remain distinct.
