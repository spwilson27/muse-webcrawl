The Small Isles (Scottish Gaelic: Na h-Eileanan Tarsainn) are a small archipelago in the Inner Hebrides of Scotland, lying south of Skye and north of Ardnamurchan in the Sea of the Hebrides. The four main islands are Rùm, Eigg, Muck and Canna, together with the tidal island of Sanday (linked to Canna) and several skerries. They form a parish in the Highland council area and include a National Scenic Area.

The islands

Rùm, the largest, is a nature reserve managed by NatureScot, famous for its red deer research, the Edwardian Kinloch Castle, and mountains formed from the eroded centre of a Palaeogene volcano. Eigg was bought by a community trust in 1997 in a celebrated land reform purchase and runs largely on community-owned renewable energy. Muck, the smallest inhabited island, is low-lying and fertile. Canna and Sanday belong to the National Trust for Scotland and are noted for seabird colonies, including puffins and Manx shearwaters.

History

The islands were settled from prehistory, passed through Norse and then Scottish lordship, and suffered during the Highland Clearances, particularly on Rùm, whose population was evicted in the 1820s–30s before the island became a sporting estate. The islands have no road links to the mainland and depend on the ferry service from Mallaig; none of them has a resident doctor, and Eigg's pioneering off-grid electricity system, launched in 2008, attracts international study.
