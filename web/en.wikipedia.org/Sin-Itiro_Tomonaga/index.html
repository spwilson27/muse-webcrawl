Sin-Itiro Tomonaga

BornMarch 31, 1906, Tokyo, Japan
DiedJuly 8, 1979 (aged 73), Tokyo, Japan
Known forQuantum electrodynamics; Tomonaga–Schwinger equation
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (1965)

Sin-Itiro Tomonaga (March 31, 1906 – July 8, 1979) was a Japanese physicist influential in the development of quantum electrodynamics, work for which he shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1965 with Richard Feynman and Julian Schwinger.

Life and career

Tomonaga was born in Tokyo and studied physics at Kyoto Imperial University, graduating in 1929. He worked with Yoshio Nishina at RIKEN and spent time in Leipzig with Werner Heisenberg's group. During the Second World War he worked on microwave systems and developed his relativistically invariant formulation of quantum field theory in wartime isolation.

Scientific work

In 1943 Tomonaga completed a relativistic generalization of the Dirac equation formalism, now known through the Tomonaga–Schwinger equation, providing a covariant framework for renormalization in quantum electrodynamics. His group's calculations of the Lamb shift and the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron agreed with experiment. He also contributed to the theory of collective motion in nuclei and to the one-dimensional many-body problem, where the Tomonaga–Luttinger liquid bears his name.
