Bolivarian Revolution

The Bolivarian Revolution is a political process in Venezuela led by President Hugo Chávez, founder of the Fifth Republic Movement and later the United Socialist Party of Venezuela (PSUV). Named after Simón Bolívar, the 19th-century independence leader, it sought to implement socialist reforms, a new constitution, and resistance to what its supporters call US imperialism.

History

Chávez, a former army officer who had led a failed coup in 1992, was elected president in 1998. A Constituent Assembly drafted the 1999 Constitution of the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela. His government survived a brief coup in 2002 and an oil strike in 2002–03, and survived a recall referendum in 2004.

Policies

The "Bolivarian missions" were social programmes in health, education, food and housing funded largely by oil revenues from the state company PDVSA. Land reform, nationalisations of key industries, and price and currency controls were also carried out. Supporters credited the government with reducing poverty and illiteracy; critics blamed its policies for economic mismanagement, corruption and erosion of democratic institutions.

After Chávez

Chávez died in office in March 2013 and was succeeded by Nicolás Maduro. Venezuela subsequently underwent a severe economic crisis marked by hyperinflation, shortages and the emigration of millions of Venezuelans.
