Pontianak is the capital of the Indonesian province of West Kalimantan, on the island of Borneo. The city straddles the equator — marked by the Equator Monument (Tugu Khatulistiwa) just north of the centre — and sits at the confluence of the Kapuas River, Indonesia's longest river, and the Landak River, close to the Natuna Sea.

History

Pontianak was founded in 1771 by Syarif Abdurrahman Alkadrie, who established the Pontianak Sultanate and built its palace and mosque at the river junction. The name is said to derive from the pontianak (kuntilanak), a vampiric female ghost of Malay folklore, said to have haunted the site. The sultanate later came under Dutch colonial influence, and the city became an important trading port for rubber, pepper and forest products.

Geography and people

Built on low, swampy delta land, much of Pontianak lies near sea level and is threaded by canals and wooden boardwalks. Its population is multi-ethnic: Malay, Dayak, Chinese Indonesian, Javanese, Bugis and Madurese communities, with the Teochew and Hakka Chinese long prominent in trade. Major landmarks include the Kadriyah Palace, the Jami Mosque, and the West Kalimantan Museum.
