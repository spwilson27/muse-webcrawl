The Gunpowder Plot of 1605 was a failed attempt by a group of English Catholic conspirators to assassinate King James I by blowing up the House of Lords during the State Opening of Parliament on 5 November 1605. Led by Robert Catesby, the plotters hoped to end the persecution of English Catholics and spark a popular revolt that would install the king's young daughter, Princess Elizabeth, as a Catholic monarch.

The conspiracy

The conspirators — including Thomas Wintour, John Wright, Thomas Percy and Guy Fawkes, a soldier with experience in the Spanish Netherlands — rented an undercroft beneath the House of Lords and filled it with 36 barrels of gunpowder. An anonymous warning letter sent to Lord Monteagle led to a search of the cellars on the night of 4–5 November, during which Fawkes was discovered guarding the explosives and arrested.

Aftermath

Fawkes was tortured and named his accomplices. Catesby and several others were killed resisting arrest at Holbeche House in Staffordshire; the surviving conspirators were tried and executed for high treason in January 1606. The plot intensified anti-Catholic legislation and sentiment for generations.

Legacy

The failure of the plot is commemorated in Britain every 5 November as Guy Fawkes Night (Bonfire Night), with fireworks and the burning of effigies. The rhyme "Remember, remember, the fifth of November" preserves the event in popular memory, and the ceremonial search of the Palace of Westminster cellars by the Yeomen of the Guard still precedes each State Opening.
