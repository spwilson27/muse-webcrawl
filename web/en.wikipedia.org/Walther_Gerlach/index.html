Walther Gerlach

Walther Gerlach (1 August 1889 – 10 August 1979) was a German physicist who co-discovered, through laboratory experiment, spin quantization in a magnetic field, the Stern–Gerlach effect. The effect was conclusively proven in February 1922, demonstrating that the spatial orientation of angular momentum is quantized.

Education and career

Gerlach was born in Biebrich, Rhineland, and studied at the University of Tübingen, receiving his doctorate in 1912 under Friedrich Paschen. He served as a soldier in World War I, conducting research on wireless telegraphy. After the war he worked at the University of Frankfurt am Main, where with Otto Stern he performed the famous experiment in which a beam of silver atoms was split by an inhomogeneous magnetic field.

Later work

Gerlach held professorships at Tübingen, Göttingen, and Munich. During World War II he became plenipotentiary for nuclear physics in 1943, heading the German nuclear research effort's physics section. He was detained at Farm Hall in England after the war as part of Operation Epsilon, with nine other German scientists. After returning to Germany, he became a professor at the University of Bonn and later at Munich, and played a role in rebuilding German science, signing the Göttinger Manifesto against nuclear armament of the Bundeswehr in 1957.
