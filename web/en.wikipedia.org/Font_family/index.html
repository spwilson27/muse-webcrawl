Font family

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In typography, a font family (also called a typeface family) is a set of fonts that share common design features but differ in particular attributes such as weight, slope, width, optical size, and ornamentation. For example, the family Times includes roman, italic, bold, and condensed variants that are recognisably related because they are built on the same underlying letterforms and proportions.

Terminology

Historically, a font referred to a particular size, weight, and style of a typeface — a complete set of metal type in one size. In digital typography the terms have blurred, but "font family" generally denotes the whole collection of related styles, while "font" often denotes a single member (for example, "Helvetica Bold 12 pt").

Attributes

Members of a font family are typically distinguished along several axes:

  • Weight — from thin or light through regular to bold and black.
  • Slope — roman (upright), italic (a cursive-derived sloped form), or oblique (a mechanically slanted roman).
  • Width — condensed, normal, or extended letterforms.
  • Optical size — designs adjusted for display use at large sizes or text use at small sizes.
  • Decorative variants — small caps, swash capitals, outline and shadowed forms.

Web typography

In CSS, the font-family property specifies a prioritised list of font families for an element, ending in a generic family such as serif, sans-serif, monospace, cursive, or fantasy. The browser uses the first available family in the list.
