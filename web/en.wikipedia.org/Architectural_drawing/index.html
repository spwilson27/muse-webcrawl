Architectural drawing

An architectural drawing or architect's drawing is a technical drawing of a building (or building project) that falls within the definition of architecture. Architectural drawings are used by architects and others to develop a design idea into a coherent proposal, to communicate ideas and concepts, to convince clients, to record completed work, and to enable a building contractor to construct the design.

Types of drawings

Standard orthographic views include floor plans, which cut horizontally through the building; elevations, which show exterior faces; sections, which cut vertically through the structure; and details at larger scales. Axonometric and perspective drawings communicate three-dimensional form, while site plans situate the building on its plot.

History

Surviving architectural drawings date to antiquity, including Egyptian plans on papyrus and the full-scale incised templates on the walls and floors of the Greek temple at Didyma. Medieval master masons used tracing floors and parchment plans such as the Plan of Saint Gall. Renaissance architects, following Leon Battista Alberti, established scaled orthographic drawing as the architect's primary design medium.

Conventions and media

Drawings follow conventions for scale, line weight, dimensioning, symbols, and title blocks. Historically produced with ink on linen and later by diazo and blueprint reproduction, architectural drawing is now dominated by computer-aided design (CAD) and building information modeling (BIM), which generate drawings from a coordinated digital model.

Drawing sets

A construction document set typically organizes drawings by discipline: architectural, structural, mechanical, electrical, and civil sheets, each numbered and cross-referenced with schedules and specifications.
