Electrolyte

An electrolyte is a substance that produces an electrically conducting solution when dissolved in a polar solvent such as water, or that conducts in the molten state. Most salts, acids, and bases are electrolytes: they dissociate into cations and anions that carry current.

In physiology

In the body, the principal electrolytes are sodium, potassium, chloride, calcium, magnesium, bicarbonate, and phosphate. They regulate fluid balance, blood pressure, pH, nerve impulses, and muscle contraction. Disturbances — such as hyponatremia or hyperkalemia — can be life-threatening and are treated with oral or intravenous replacement, including oral rehydration therapy for dehydration.

Applications

Electrolytes are essential in batteries, fuel cells, electroplating, and electrolysis, where ionic conduction completes the circuit between electrodes.
