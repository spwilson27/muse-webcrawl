Edward Theodore Gein (August 27, 1906 – July 26, 1984), also known as the Butcher of Plainfield and the Plainfield Ghoul, was an American murderer, suspected serial killer, and body snatcher. His crimes, committed around his hometown of Plainfield, Wisconsin, gathered widespread notoriety in 1957 after authorities discovered that he had exhumed corpses from local graveyards and fashioned keepsakes from their bones and skin.

Life and crimes

Gein was raised by a domineering, religiously fanatical mother, Augusta, whose death in 1945 left him alone on the family farmhouse. After hardware-store owner Bernice Worden disappeared in November 1957, police searched Gein's property and found Worden's body and an array of artifacts made from human remains, including bowls made from skulls, a belt of nipples, lampshades and chair coverings made of skin, and masks fashioned from faces.

Gein confessed to killing Worden and tavern owner Mary Hogan (who had disappeared in 1954) and to robbing graves. He was found mentally incompetent at first, then tried in 1968 and found guilty of Worden's murder but legally insane; he spent the rest of his life in mental institutions.

In popular culture

Gein's case inspired iconic fictional killers: Norman Bates in Psycho, Leatherface in The Texas Chain Saw Massacre, and Buffalo Bill in The Silence of the Lambs.
