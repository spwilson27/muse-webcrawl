The Six-Day War (also known as the June War or the 1967 Arab–Israeli War) was fought from 5 to 10 June 1967 between Israel and a coalition of Arab states led by Egypt, Syria and Jordan. In six days Israel achieved a decisive victory and captured territory more than tripling the area under its control — land whose status remains at the heart of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

Origins

Tensions had risen through the spring of 1967: Egypt's President Gamal Abdel Nasser ordered UN peacekeepers out of Sinai, massed troops there, and closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping — an act Israel had declared a cause for war. Egypt, Syria and Jordan signed mutual defence pacts and Arab rhetoric promised Israel's destruction.

Course of the war

On the morning of 5 June, Israel launched Operation Focus, a surprise air strike that destroyed most of the Egyptian air force on the ground within hours; the Syrian and Jordanian air forces were likewise wrecked. Israeli armour then overran the Sinai Peninsula, reaching the Suez Canal, and captured the Gaza Strip. On the Jordanian front, Israel took East Jerusalem — including the Old City — and the West Bank. In the final days, Israeli forces stormed Syria's Golan Heights. A UN-brokered ceasefire took effect on 10 June.

Consequences

Roughly 15,000–25,000 Arab soldiers and about 1,000 Israelis were killed. Israel's occupation of the West Bank, Gaza, Sinai and Golan began; hundreds of thousands of Palestinians fled or were displaced. In November 1967 the UN Security Council passed Resolution 242, calling for Israeli withdrawal from occupied territories in exchange for peace — the "land for peace" formula. Israel returned Sinai to Egypt under the 1979 peace treaty, but the war's territorial legacy — Jerusalem's status, West Bank settlements, the Golan — is still unresolved.
