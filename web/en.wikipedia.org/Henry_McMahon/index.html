Lieutenant-Colonel Sir Arthur Henry McMahon (28 November 1862 – 29 December 1949) was a British Indian Army officer and diplomat who served as High Commissioner in Egypt and as a boundary commissioner in the North-West Frontier and Tibet negotiations. His name is attached to two of the most debated legacies of British imperial diplomacy: the McMahon–Hussein Correspondence of 1915–16 and the McMahon Line between India and Tibet.

Career

McMahon came from a family long associated with the East India Company and the Indian political service. Commissioned into the Indian Army, he specialised in frontier administration, serving in the Durand Line boundary commission with Afghanistan in the 1890s and in the Seistan boundary arbitration between Persia and Afghanistan. He became Foreign Secretary to the Government of India and, in 1914, led the British side in the Simla Convention negotiations with Tibet and China, from which the McMahon Line — the boundary drawn along the Himalayan crest in the eastern sector — takes its name.

Egypt and the Arab correspondence

In January 1915 McMahon succeeded Lord Kitchener as High Commissioner in Egypt. From Cairo he conducted the celebrated correspondence with Hussein bin Ali, Sharif of Mecca (July 1915 – March 1916), in which Britain appeared to promise support for an independent Arab state in return for an Arab revolt against the Ottoman Empire. The exact extent of the promises — particularly whether Palestine was included or excluded from the promised area — remained controversial for decades and sat uneasily beside the Sykes–Picot Agreement and the Balfour Declaration. McMahon left Egypt in 1917 and retired soon after. He died in London in 1949.
