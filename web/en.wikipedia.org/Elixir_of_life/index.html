Elixir of life

The elixir of life, also known as the elixir of immortality and sometimes equated with the name of the philosopher's stone, is a potion that supposedly grants the drinker eternal life and youth. It is a legendary substance sought by alchemists in Europe, China and India, and closely related to ideas such as the Indian amrita and the waters of life found in many myths.

Chinese alchemy

In Chinese external alchemy (waidan), adepts compounded elixirs from cinnabar, gold, jade and other minerals, believing that ingesting incorruptible substances would confer incorruptibility. Several Chinese emperors are recorded to have died from elixir poisoning, often from mercury compounds. Ge Hong's Baopuzi (4th century CE) is a major source on these practices, which later gave way to internal alchemy (neidan), focused on meditation and breath control.

Indian traditions

In Hindu mythology, amrita is the nectar of immortality produced by the churning of the ocean of milk by gods and demons. Ayurvedic practice includes rasayana, a branch concerned with rejuvenation preparations, some containing processed metals and minerals.

European alchemy

European alchemists sought the elixir alongside the philosopher's stone, which would transmute base metals into gold and cure disease. Figures such as Nicolas Flamel acquired legendary reputations for having achieved it. In the modern era, "elixir of life" survived mainly as a phrase in folklore, fiction and marketing.
