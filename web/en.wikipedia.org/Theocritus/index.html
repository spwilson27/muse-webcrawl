Theocritus

Theocritus (c. 300 – c. 260 BC) was a Greek poet of Syracuse and Alexandria, traditionally credited with creating bucolic (pastoral) poetry. His surviving works, known as the Idylls, are short poems in hexameters, many featuring singing contests and dialogues between shepherds in idealised Sicilian landscapes.

Bornc. 300 BC, Syracuse
Diedc. 260 BC
OccupationPoet
Notable worksIdylls

Works

About thirty poems and epigrams survive under his name, though some are spurious. Idyll 15, "The Syracusan Women", is a lively urban mime, while Idyll 17 is an encomium to Ptolemy II Philadelphus.

Influence

Theocritus was imitated by Virgil in the Eclogues and founded the pastoral tradition that runs through Renaissance and English poetry, including Milton's Lycidas.
