The Mark Twain National Forest is a U.S. National Forest located in the southern half of Missouri, within the Ozark Highlands. Named after the Missouri-born author Mark Twain, it covers roughly 1.5 million acres spread across 29 counties, making it the largest public land holding in the state.

Geography

The forest lies in the rugged, oak-hickory covered hills of the Ozarks, a region known for its clear spring-fed streams, limestone bluffs and sinkhole ponds. It is administered in several non-contiguous ranger districts, including areas around Rolla, Salem, Poplar Bluff and Doniphan.

History

The forest was established in 1939 on cut-over and eroded lands acquired during the Great Depression. Civilian Conservation Corps crews planted trees, built roads and developed recreation sites across the forest during the 1930s.

Recreation

Popular activities include hiking, camping, fishing and floating on rivers such as the Eleven Point River, part of which is designated a National Wild and Scenic River. The forest also contains designated wilderness areas, including the Irish Wilderness and Bell Mountain Wilderness.
