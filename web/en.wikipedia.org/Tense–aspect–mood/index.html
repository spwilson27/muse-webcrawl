Tense–aspect–mood

Tense–aspect–mood (TAM) is a group of grammatical categories which are important to understanding spoken or written content in linguistics. The three categories are: tense, the positioning of an event in time (past, present, future); aspect, the temporal structure of an event as viewed by the speaker (completed, ongoing, habitual); and mood (or modality), which indicates the speaker's attitude toward the proposition, such as whether it is factual, desired, commanded or hypothetical.

Interaction

The three categories are often fused in verb morphology: a single inflection may simultaneously mark past tense, perfective aspect and indicative mood. Languages differ greatly in which distinctions are obligatory — English primarily marks tense and some aspect (progressive, perfect), while many languages grammaticalize evidentiality or realis/irrealis mood instead.

Terminology

Because the categories overlap and are hard to separate in many languages, linguists often treat TAM as a single system, and use compound terms such as "mood–aspect" or describe verb forms with combined labels like "past perfective indicative".
