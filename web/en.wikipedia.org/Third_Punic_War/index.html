The Third Punic War (149–146 BC) was the third and last of the Punic Wars fought between Carthage and Rome. The war was a much smaller engagement than the two preceding it and consisted primarily of the siege of Carthage, ending with the city's complete destruction and the annexation of its territory as the Roman province of Africa.

Background

In the fifty years after the Second Punic War, Carthage, though disarmed and bound by a treaty forbidding it to make war without Roman consent, recovered economically and repeatedly clashed with its neighbor Numidia. Carthage's armed response to Numidian incursions in 151 BC gave Rome a pretext for war; in the Senate, Cato the Elder had long ended his speeches with the demand that "Carthage must be destroyed" (Carthago delenda est).

The war

When Carthage submitted to extreme Roman demands — surrendering hostages, arms, and finally being ordered to abandon and demolish the city itself and relocate inland — the Carthaginians refused and prepared a desperate defense. Roman assaults failed for two years until Scipio Aemilianus took command in 147 BC, tightened the blockade, and in the spring of 146 BC stormed the city. After six days of house-to-house fighting, the survivors on the Byrsa citadel surrendered; some 50,000 Carthaginians were enslaved, and the city was systematically burned and demolished. The popular story that the Romans salted the earth is a later invention.
