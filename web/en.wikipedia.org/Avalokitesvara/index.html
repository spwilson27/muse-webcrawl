Avalokitesvara

Avalokitesvara, the bodhisattva who embodies the compassion of all Buddhas.

Avalokitesvara is the spelling sometimes used for the bodhisattva more commonly transliterated Avalokiteśvara (Sanskrit: अवलोकितेश्वर, "the lord who looks down [in compassion]"), one of the most widely venerated figures in Mahayana Buddhism. Avalokiteśvara embodies the compassion of all Buddhas and is said to hear the cries of beings in suffering throughout the world.

Scriptures and forms

The bodhisattva is celebrated in the Lotus Sutra, whose 25th chapter describes how Avalokiteśvara takes whatever form is needed — monk, nun, layperson, deity — to rescue those who call on the bodhisattva's name, and in the Heart Sutra, whose framing teaching is attributed to Avalokiteśvara. In the Karandavyuha Sutra the six-syllable mantra oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ is proclaimed as the essence of the bodhisattva's compassion. Esoteric traditions developed many-armed forms, most famously the eleven-headed, thousand-armed Sahasrabhuja, whose many eyes and hands symbolise perceiving and aiding all beings.

Across Asia

In China the bodhisattva became the goddess-like Guanyin (Guanshiyin, "Perceiver of the World's Sounds"), predominantly female from the Song dynasty onward, and is likewise Kannon in Japan, Gwanse-eum in Korea, and Quán Thế Âm in Vietnam. In Tibet the bodhisattva is Chenrezig, regarded as the patron deity of the country; the Dalai Lamas are considered his emanations. In Theravada countries such as Sri Lanka and Myanmar he appears as Natha-deva and Lokanat.

Names and etymology

The earliest form of the name, found in some manuscripts, is Avalokitasvara ("one who looks down at sound"), matching the Chinese translation Guanshiyin. The later form Avalokiteśvara inserts īśvara ("lord"). Both spellings — with diacritics (Avalokiteśvara) and without (Avalokitesvara) — refer to the same bodhisattva.
