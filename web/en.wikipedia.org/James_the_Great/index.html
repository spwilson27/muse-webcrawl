James the Great (died AD 44) was one of the Twelve Apostles of Jesus, the elder brother of John the Apostle and a son of Zebedee. He is called "the Great" to distinguish him from James the Less. Together with Peter and John, James formed an inner circle among the disciples, present at the Transfiguration and at the Agony in the Garden of Gethsemane.

Life in the Gospels

James and John were fishermen on the Sea of Galilee whom Jesus called while they were mending nets with their father. Jesus nicknamed the brothers Boanerges, "sons of thunder," perhaps for their fiery temperament; they once asked to call down fire on a Samaritan village and, through their mother, requested the places of honour in Jesus's kingdom. According to the Acts of the Apostles, King Herod Agrippa I had James executed by the sword around AD 44, making him the first apostle to be martyred.

The Spanish tradition

Medieval tradition held that James had preached in Spain and that his relics were miraculously transported there and rediscovered in the 9th century at what became Santiago de Compostela in Galicia. The shrine grew into one of Christendom's greatest pilgrimage destinations, the destination of the Way of St James (Camino de Santiago). As Santiago Matamoros ("St James the Moor-slayer") he became the patron saint of Spain.
