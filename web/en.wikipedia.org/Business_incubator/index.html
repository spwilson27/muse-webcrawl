Business incubator

A business incubator is an organisation that supports the development of new and startup companies by providing services such as office space, management training, mentoring, administrative support, and help in obtaining financing. Incubators are commonly sponsored by universities, economic development agencies, or private investors.

History

The concept is often traced to the Batavia Industrial Center in New York, opened in 1959, which rented space and shared services to young businesses. The model spread widely in the 1980s as governments sought to promote entrepreneurship and regional development, and thousands of incubators now operate worldwide.

Related models

Business incubators differ from startup accelerators such as Y Combinator, which run short, fixed-term programmes for cohorts in exchange for equity, and from science parks, which primarily provide property. Incubators typically admit companies on a rolling basis for longer stays, sometimes specialising in sectors such as biotechnology, clean energy, or creative industries. Studies of their effectiveness are mixed, though well-run incubators are associated with improved survival rates for tenant firms.
