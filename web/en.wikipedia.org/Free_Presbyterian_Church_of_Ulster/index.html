Free Presbyterian Church of Ulster

Founded1951, Crossgar, County Down, Northern Ireland
FounderIan Paisley
ClassificationProtestant, fundamentalist, Presbyterian
HeadquartersBelfast, Northern Ireland

The Free Presbyterian Church of Ulster is a Presbyterian denomination founded in 1951 by Ian Paisley in Northern Ireland. Theologically fundamentalist and separatist, it emphasises biblical inerrancy, personal conversion, and separation from churches regarded as apostate, and it maintains a strongly unionist ethos among its overwhelmingly Northern Irish membership.

Origins

The church arose from a dispute in the Presbyterian Church in Ireland over a planned evangelistic mission in Ballymena: when the presbytery prevented Paisley and others from preaching in a local church hall, the Crossgar congregation withdrew and, on 17 March 1951 — St Patrick's Day — the new denomination was constituted. Paisley served as its moderator for nearly 57 years, an unusually long tenure, before stepping down in 2007.

Beliefs and reach

The church subscribes to the Westminster Confession of Faith, practices believer's and infant baptism debates internal to its Calvinist doctrine, and takes conservative positions on moral questions; historically it campaigned vigorously against ecumenism and Roman Catholicism. Congregations exist across Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland, and affiliated works under the Free Presbyterian name have been established in Great Britain, North America (as the Free Presbyterian Church of North America), Australia, and elsewhere, with a theological seminary, the Whitefield College of the Bible, in Northern Ireland.
