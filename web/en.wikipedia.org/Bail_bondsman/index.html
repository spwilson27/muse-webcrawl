Bail bondsman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A bail bondsman, bail bond agent, or bond dealer is a person, agency, or corporation that will act as a surety and pledge money or property as bail for the appearance of a defendant in court. Bail bond agents are almost exclusively found in the United States, one of the few countries that permit commercial bail bonding. The industry charges a non-refundable premium — commonly around 10% of the bail amount — in exchange for posting the full bond.

How it works

When a court sets cash bail that a defendant cannot post, a bondsman agrees to be responsible for the full amount if the defendant fails to appear. The defendant or family pays the premium and often pledges collateral such as real estate or vehicles. The bond itself is usually underwritten by an insurance company for which the bondsman acts as agent. If the defendant appears as required, the bond is released and the collateral returned, but the premium is retained as the agent's fee.

Fugitive recovery

If a defendant skips bail, the bondsman may hire a bounty hunter (fugitive recovery agent) to locate, apprehend, and return the defendant, thereby avoiding forfeiture. Under the U.S. Supreme Court's decision in Taylor v. Taintor (1872), a surety and its agents historically enjoyed broad powers to pursue and arrest a principal, including across state lines and by entering the principal's home, though most states now license and regulate recovery agents and restrict these practices.

Regulation and criticism

Bail bond agents are licensed under state law, and commercial bail is prohibited in a handful of states including Illinois, Kentucky, Oregon, and Wisconsin, as well as in the District of Columbia's court system in practice. Critics argue the money-bail system penalizes poverty and that commercial bonding profits from pretrial detention; reform efforts in several jurisdictions have sharply curtailed or eliminated cash bail. The Philippines is the only other country with a comparable commercial bail industry, a legacy of the American colonial period.
