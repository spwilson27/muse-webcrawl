Kähler manifold

In mathematics and especially differential geometry, a Kähler manifold is a manifold with three mutually compatible structures: a complex structure, a Riemannian structure, and a symplectic structure. On a Kähler manifold, the metric is Hermitian, and its imaginary part, the Kähler form ω, is a closed differential 2-form: dω = 0.

Locally, a Kähler metric can always be written as arising from a single real-valued function, the Kähler potential: in local holomorphic coordinates, the metric components are the mixed second partial derivatives of the potential. Kähler manifolds are named after the German mathematician Erich Kähler, who introduced the concept in the 1930s.

Equivalent definitions

A Kähler manifold may equivalently be defined as a Hermitian manifold whose associated (1,1)-form is closed; as a Riemannian manifold with holonomy group contained in the unitary group U(n); or as a symplectic manifold with a compatible integrable almost complex structure. The equivalence of these definitions is a consequence of the Newlander–Nirenberg theorem.

Examples

  • Complex Euclidean space ℂn with the flat metric.
  • Complex projective space ℂPn with the Fubini–Study metric.
  • Every Riemann surface (complex dimension one) is trivially Kähler.
  • Every smooth complex projective variety, and more generally every compact complex submanifold of a Kähler manifold.
  • Calabi–Yau manifolds, which are Kähler with vanishing first Chern class, central to string theory compactifications.

Properties

Kähler geometry is rigid in useful ways: the Hodge decomposition of cohomology holds, giving b2k+1 even-type constraints on Betti numbers; holomorphic forms are harmonic; and compact Kähler manifolds satisfy the ∂∂̄-lemma. Compact Kähler manifolds need not be projective, but the Kodaira embedding theorem characterizes those that are.
