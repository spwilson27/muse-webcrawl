Wreck of the Titanic

Sank15 April 1912
Discovered1 September 1985
Depth≈3,800 m (12,500 ft)
LocationNorth Atlantic Ocean, ≈370 miles southeast of Newfoundland

The wreck of the RMS Titanic lies at a depth of about 3,800 metres (12,500 ft) on the seabed of the North Atlantic Ocean. The ocean liner sank on 15 April 1912 after striking an iceberg during her maiden voyage, with the loss of more than 1,500 lives. The wreck lies in two main pieces about 600 metres apart, surrounded by a large debris field.

Discovery

The wreck was located on 1 September 1985 by a joint French-American expedition led by Jean-Louis Michel and Robert Ballard, using the research vessels Le Suroît and Knorr and the deep-towed sonar system Argo. Ballard returned in 1986 aboard Atlantis II and explored the wreck in the submersible Alvin.

Condition and deterioration

The wreck is being consumed by iron-eating bacteria, including the species Halomonas titanicae, and is deteriorating rapidly; the crow's nest and parts of the officers' quarters have collapsed. Expeditions have recovered thousands of artifacts for conservation and exhibition. The wreck is protected under international agreements and the UNESCO Convention on the Protection of the Underwater Cultural Heritage.

Expeditions

Director James Cameron visited the wreck repeatedly while making the 1997 film Titanic. In June 2023 the submersible Titan, operated by OceanGate, imploded while descending to the wreck, killing all five people on board.
