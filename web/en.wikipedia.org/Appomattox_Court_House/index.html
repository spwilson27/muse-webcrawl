Appomattox Court House is a historic village in Appomattox County, Virginia, best known as the site of the surrender of Confederate General Robert E. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia to Union Lieutenant General Ulysses S. Grant on April 9, 1865, an event that effectively ended the American Civil War. Despite its name, the surrender meeting took place not in a courthouse but in the parlor of the private home of Wilmer McLean.

Background

In the spring of 1865, after the fall of Petersburg and Richmond, Lee's army retreated westward, pursued closely by Grant's forces. Union cavalry under Philip Sheridan blocked the Confederate line of retreat and seized supply trains at Appomattox Station. Surrounded and outnumbered, Lee agreed to meet Grant to discuss surrender terms.

The surrender

On the afternoon of April 9, 1865 — Palm Sunday — Lee and Grant met in the McLean House. Grant's terms were generous: Confederate officers and men were to be paroled and allowed to return home, officers could keep their sidearms and private horses, and the soldiers were permitted to keep horses for spring planting. Lee accepted, and the agreement was signed the same day. In the following days, roughly 28,000 Confederate soldiers stacked their arms and were formally paroled in ceremonies at the village.

Historical park

The original courthouse building burned in 1892; the county seat subsequently moved to the modern town of Appomattox. The McLean House was dismantled in the 1890s amid plans, never completed, to move it to Washington, D.C., and was later reconstructed on its original site. The village is now preserved as the Appomattox Court House National Historical Park, administered by the National Park Service, with many restored or reconstructed period buildings.
