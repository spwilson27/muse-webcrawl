Compact Cassette

Compact Cassette
Developer: Philips
Introduced: 1963
Medium: magnetic tape, 3.81 mm wide
Typical lengths: C60, C90, C120

The Compact Cassette, commonly called the cassette tape, is a magnetic tape sound recording format developed by Philips and released in 1963. Its small plastic shell contains two spools and a loop of tape providing two stereo pairs of tracks, one for each side.

Development and rise

Philips licensed the format royalty-free, helping it defeat rival cartridges. Improvements in tape formulations (chromium dioxide and metal) and Dolby noise reduction made cassettes a serious hi-fi medium by the 1970s. The Sony Walkman (1979) made them the dominant portable music format.

Culture

Cassettes enabled home taping and the mixtape, spread music in regions where vinyl and later CDs were scarce, and became vehicles for punk, hip-hop and underground distribution. Pre-recorded cassettes outsold LPs in many markets during the 1980s.

Decline and revival

The compact disc displaced the cassette in the 1990s and major labels ended mass production in the 2000s. A niche revival in the 2010s saw rising cassette sales as a novelty and independent-artist format.
