Margaret Tudor (28 November 1489 – 18 October 1541) was Queen of Scots from 1503 to 1513 as the wife of King James IV of Scotland. Eldest daughter of Henry VII of England and Elizabeth of York, and elder sister of Henry VIII, she served as regent of Scotland for her son James V following her husband's death at the Battle of Flodden. Her marriage, arranged by the Treaty of Perpetual Peace, ultimately united the English and Scottish crowns: her great-grandson James VI of Scotland inherited the English throne in 1603.

Early life and marriage

Margaret was born at Westminster Palace and baptised at St Margaret's, Westminster, after which she was named. As part of the peace settlement between England and Scotland, she was betrothed to James IV, and the marriage was celebrated by proxy in London in January 1503; the poet William Dunbar celebrated the match in The Thrissil and the Rois. Margaret travelled north in the summer of 1503, met James at Dalkeith, and the couple were married in person at Holyrood Abbey on 8 August 1503. Of their six children, only the future James V survived infancy.

Regency and second marriage

James IV was killed at Flodden on 9 September 1513, and the seventeen-month-old James V was crowned. By her husband's will, Margaret was regent so long as she remained a widow. In August 1514, however, she secretly married Archibald Douglas, 6th Earl of Angus, forfeiting the regency to John Stewart, Duke of Albany, and alienating much of the Scottish nobility. Margaret's struggle for custody of her sons — besieged in Stirling Castle, she surrendered them to Albany in 1515 — and her flight to England, where she gave birth to Lady Margaret Douglas at Harbottle Castle, form one of the dramatic episodes of the period. She returned to Scotland in 1517.

Later life

Margaret spent the following years manoeuvring between pro-English and pro-French factions, seeking an annulment of her marriage to Angus (granted in 1527) and marrying Henry Stewart, later Lord Methven. She was reconciled with James V when he assumed power in 1528, but her relations with her son were often strained, and she continued to intrigue with her brother Henry VIII — at times proposing a meeting between the two kings. She died of a palsy at Methven Castle in October 1541 and was buried at the Charterhouse in Perth.

Legacy

Through her son James V and granddaughter Mary, Queen of Scots, and through her daughter Margaret Douglas (whose son was Henry Stuart, Lord Darnley), Margaret Tudor is an ancestress of the later Stuart monarchs and of the present British royal line. Her marriage to James IV is traditionally symbolised as the union of the thistle and the rose.
