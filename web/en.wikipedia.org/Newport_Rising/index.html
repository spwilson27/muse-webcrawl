Newport Rising

The Newport Rising was the last large-scale armed rebellion against authority in Great Britain, led by Chartists in Newport, Monmouthshire, on 4 November 1839. Several thousand working-class men, many of them miners, marched on Newport intending to free imprisoned Chartists including Henry Vincent and to spark a wider uprising.

Events

Three columns converged on Newport, the largest led by John Frost, a former mayor of the town. Soldiers of the 45th Regiment of Foot were stationed inside the Westgate Hotel, where the mayor and magistrates had gathered. When the Chartists attacked the hotel, the soldiers opened fire, killing at least 22 marchers and wounding around 50. The rising collapsed within the day.

Aftermath

Frost, Zephaniah Williams, and William Jones were convicted of high treason and sentenced to death; the sentences were commuted to transportation to Van Diemen's Land after public outcry. Frost was eventually pardoned and returned to Britain in 1856. The bullet marks on the Westgate Hotel pillars are still visible, and the rising is commemorated in Newport as a foundational event in Welsh democratic history.
