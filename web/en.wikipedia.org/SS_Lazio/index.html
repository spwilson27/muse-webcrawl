Società Sportiva Lazio (BIT: LAZI), commonly referred to as Lazio, is an Italian professional sports club based in Rome, most known for its football activity. The society, founded in 1900, plays in Serie A and have spent most of their history in the top tier of Italian football. Lazio have been Italian champions twice (1974 and 2000), and have won the Coppa Italia seven times, the Supercoppa Italiana five times, and on the European stage the UEFA Cup Winners' Cup and UEFA Super Cup in 1999.

Lazio share Rome's Stadio Olimpico with rivals AS Roma.

History

The club was founded in 1900 in the Prati district of Rome by a group of young athletes led by Luigi Bigiarelli, choosing the name Lazio for the wider region and sky blue and white colours in honour of Greece, the home of the Olympic Games. Lazio was a founding member of Serie A in 1929 and famously resisted the Fascist regime's attempt to merge Rome's clubs into one team in 1927, remaining independent while Roma was formed from the others. Its first golden era came in 1973–74 under coach Tommaso Maestrelli, when the team of Giorgio Chinaglia won the scudetto; a second came around 2000 under Sven-Göran Eriksson, with a star-studded squad funded by president Sergio Cragnotti winning the league, two Coppe Italia and the last Cup Winners' Cup.

Rivalries and supporters

Lazio's fiercest rivalry is with cross-town AS Roma, contested in the Derby della Capitale, one of the most heated derbies in world football. The club's eagle mascot, Olimpia, is flown around the Stadio Olimpico before home matches, in keeping with the club's imperial Roman symbolism.
