Fungal infection

A fungal infection, also called a mycosis, is a disease caused by fungi. Fungal infections range from superficial skin conditions such as athlete's foot and ringworm to life-threatening systemic infections in immunocompromised people. Common pathogens include Candida, Aspergillus, dermatophytes, and Cryptococcus.

Classification

Mycoses are classified by the tissue level colonised: superficial (outer skin layers), cutaneous (skin, hair, nails), subcutaneous, and systemic or deep mycoses affecting internal organs. Opportunistic infections occur mainly in people with weakened immunity, including those with HIV/AIDS or on immunosuppressive therapy.

Treatment

Treatment uses antifungal medications such as azoles, polyenes (e.g., amphotericin B), and echinocandins, applied topically or systemically depending on severity. Rising antifungal resistance — notably in Candida auris — is an emerging public-health concern, and the WHO published its first fungal priority pathogens list in 2022.
