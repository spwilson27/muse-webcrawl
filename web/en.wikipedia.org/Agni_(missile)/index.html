Agni (missile)

TypeBallistic missile family (MRBM–ICBM)
OriginIndia
DesignerDefence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO)
VariantsAgni-I through Agni-VI, Agni-P

The Agni missile (Sanskrit: अग्नि, "fire") is a family of medium- to intercontinental-range ballistic missiles developed by India. Named after one of the five elements of nature, the Agni missiles are long-range, surface-to-surface, nuclear weapons-capable ballistic missiles that form the backbone of India's land-based nuclear deterrent under its Strategic Forces Command.

Development

The programme began under the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme in the 1980s, led by A. P. J. Abdul Kalam. The first Agni technology demonstrator flew in 1989. Unlike India's earlier liquid-fuelled Prithvi missile, the Agni series uses solid propellant in all stages, improving readiness, mobility, and storage life; later variants are canister-launched from road and rail mobile launchers.

Variants

Agni-I is a medium-range missile (700–1,200 km) intended mainly against Pakistan. Agni-II extends range to 2,000–3,500 km, followed by Agni-III (3,000–3,500 km) and Agni-IV (around 4,000 km, with ring laser gyro guidance). Agni-V, first tested in 2012, is an intercontinental ballistic missile with a range exceeding 5,000 km and, from 2024, tested MIRV capability (Mission Divyastra). Agni-P (Prime) is a newer, lighter canisterised missile of 1,000–2,000 km range, and an Agni-VI with still greater range has been reported in development.

Strategic role

The Agni series underpins India's declared policy of credible minimum deterrence and no first use. Together with the Prithvi and the sea-based K-series missiles, it forms India's nuclear triad delivery systems.
