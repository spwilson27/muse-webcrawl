Mouse double minute 2 homolog (MDM2), known in humans as HDM2, is a protein that in humans is encoded by the MDM2 gene. MDM2 is an important negative regulator of the p53 tumour suppressor protein: it binds p53, blocks its transcriptional activity, and targets it for degradation by the proteasome through its E3 ubiquitin ligase activity.

MDM2 binds the N-terminal transactivation domain of p53, forming the core of a negative feedback loop.

Discovery

The mdm2 gene was originally identified as one of three genes amplified on double minute chromosomes in a spontaneously transformed mouse cell line. Its human homolog was later found to bind and inactivate p53, and amplification or overexpression of MDM2 was observed in a variety of human cancers, particularly sarcomas, where it provides an alternative to p53 mutation as a means of disabling the p53 pathway.

Regulation of p53

p53 transcriptionally activates the MDM2 gene, so the two proteins form an autoregulatory feedback loop: rising p53 levels induce MDM2, which then promotes p53 degradation and keeps p53 activity in check in unstressed cells. Cellular stresses such as DNA damage disrupt the MDM2–p53 interaction through phosphorylation, stabilising p53. The tumour suppressor p14ARF also inhibits MDM2, linking oncogene activation to p53 stabilisation.

Clinical significance

Because many tumours retain wild-type p53 but overexpress MDM2, small-molecule inhibitors of the MDM2–p53 interaction — such as the nutlins, the first such compounds described — have been developed as anticancer agents intended to reactivate p53 in those tumours.
