The Kronstadt rebellion (Russian: Кронштадтское восстание) was a 1921 insurrection of Soviet sailors and soldiers, together with their civilian supporters, against the Bolshevik government of the Russian Socialist Federative Soviet Republic. It was the last major revolt against Bolshevik rule on Russian territory during the Civil War era.

Background

The sailors of the Baltic Fleet base at Kronstadt — once praised by Trotsky as the "pride and glory of the Revolution" for their role in 1917 — were disillusioned by War Communism, forced grain requisitioning and the suppression of strikes in Petrograd. On 1 March 1921 a mass meeting adopted the Petropavlovsk Resolution, demanding new elections to the soviets by secret ballot, freedom of speech and the press for workers and left socialist parties, freedom of assembly, and equal rations — summarised in the slogan "All power to the soviets and not the parties".

Suppression

After negotiations failed, the Red Army under Mikhail Tukhachevsky assaulted the fortress across the ice of the Gulf of Finland. Following heavy fighting, Kronstadt fell on 18 March 1921; thousands of rebels were executed or sent to camps, while about 8,000 fled across the ice to Finland. The rebellion coincided with Lenin's introduction of the New Economic Policy at the Tenth Party Congress, which replaced grain requisitioning with a tax in kind.
