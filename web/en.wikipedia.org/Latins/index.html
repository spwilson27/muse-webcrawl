Latins

The Latins were an Italic tribe of ancient Italy who inhabited Latium, the plain south of the Tiber, from the early Iron Age. Speaking Latin, they founded cities including Alba Longa, Lavinium and Rome, and were united in the Latin League for mutual defence and shared cult at the Alban Mount.

History

Rome gradually subordinated its Latin neighbours; after the Latin War (340–338 BC) the League was dissolved and the Latins received gradations of Roman citizenship—the 'Latin right' (ius Latii) later extended widely in the empire. Latin language and culture spread across western Europe and underpin the Romance languages.

Modern usage

'Latin' later denoted adherents of the Latin Church, speakers of Romance languages, and peoples of Latin America and Latin Europe.
