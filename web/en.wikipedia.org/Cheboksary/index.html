Overview

Cheboksary (Russian: Чебоксары; Chuvash: Шупашкар, Shupashkar) is the capital city of the Chuvash Republic, Russia, and a major port on the Volga River. It lies about 630 kilometres (390 miles) east of Moscow, on the right bank of the Volga, near the Cheboksary Reservoir formed by the Cheboksary Hydroelectric Station downstream at Novocheboksarsk.

History

The settlement was first mentioned in chronicles in 1469, when Russian troops stopped there on campaign against the Khanate of Kazan. A fortress was built in 1555 after the conquest of Kazan, and the town developed as a trading post for grain, timber and furs. It received city status in 1781. In the Soviet era Cheboksary industrialised rapidly, gaining major plants for electrical equipment, tractors and textiles, and population grew several-fold after the Second World War.

Culture and sights

The city is a centre of Chuvash culture, home to the Chuvash National Museum, the Chuvash State Opera and Ballet Theatre, and a monument to Mother the Patroness (Mat'-Pokrovitel'nitsa), a 46-metre statue overlooking the Cheboksary Bay that has become the symbol of the city. Institutions include Chuvash State University. The reconstructed bay area, churches such as the Vvedensky Cathedral (1657) and the Church of Archangel Michael are notable landmarks.
