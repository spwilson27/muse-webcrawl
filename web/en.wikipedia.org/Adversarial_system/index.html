Adversarial system

The adversarial system or adversary system is a legal system used in common law countries where two advocates represent their parties' case or position before an impartial person or group of people, usually a judge or jury, who attempt to determine the truth and pass judgment accordingly.

Adversarial system
Used in: Common law jurisdictions
Contrast: Inquisitorial system
Decision-maker: Judge or jury

Features

Each side gathers and presents its own evidence, calls and cross-examines witnesses, and makes legal arguments. The judge acts largely as a neutral referee ensuring rules of evidence and procedure are followed, though judges may also decide cases in bench trials.

History

The system evolved in England from medieval trial practices, including trial by jury, and spread throughout the British Empire to countries including the United States, Canada, Australia, and India.

Criticism

Critics argue the system can favor wealthy parties who can afford better representation, and that the contest structure may obscure the truth. Defenders respond that party-driven investigation protects against state bias and motivates thorough presentation of evidence.
