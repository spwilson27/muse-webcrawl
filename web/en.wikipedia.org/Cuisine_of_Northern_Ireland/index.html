Cuisine of Northern Ireland

The cuisine of Northern Ireland shares much with the wider Irish culinary tradition, built on potatoes, breads, pork, dairy and seafood, with distinctive regional specialities of its own. Farming and fishing remain central to the food culture, and local produce such as Comber potatoes, Lough Neagh eels and Dulse seaweed are celebrated regional foods.

The Ulster fry

The best-known dish is the Ulster fry, a cooked breakfast of bacon, sausages, black and white pudding, eggs, mushrooms and tomatoes, distinguished by its inclusion of fried soda bread and potato bread (potato farls), often with wheaten bread on the side. It is served throughout the day in cafés and homes.

Breads and baked goods

Northern Ireland is noted for its griddle breads: soda farls, potato farls, wheaten (brown soda) bread and barmbrack, a fruited tea loaf. Traybakes — uncooked or simply baked sweet slices such as fifteens, named for their fifteen biscuits, marshmallows and cherries — are a hallmark of home baking.

Regional specialities

Other specialities include champ (mashed potato with scallions), boxty pancakes, Belfast baps, pastie suppers from fish and chip shops, and seafood from the coasts of County Down and County Antrim, including Strangford Lough oysters and Portavogie prawns.
