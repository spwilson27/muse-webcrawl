Richard Schoen

Richard Melvin Schoen (born 23 October 1950) is an American mathematician known for fundamental work in differential geometry, geometric analysis, and mathematical general relativity.

Career

Schoen received his PhD from Stanford University in 1977. He has held positions at the Courant Institute, UC Berkeley, UC San Diego, Stanford University, and UC Irvine. With Shing-Tung Yau he proved the positive mass theorem (1979), a landmark result confirming that the total energy of an isolated gravitational system is non-negative.

Contributions

Schoen's work includes the resolution of the Yamabe problem (1984), the differentiable sphere theorem proved with Simon Brendle (2007), and deep contributions to minimal surface theory, harmonic maps, and the geometry of manifolds with positive curvature. His analytic techniques shaped the modern field of geometric analysis.

Awards

He has received the Bôcher Memorial Prize, a MacArthur Fellowship, the Wolf Prize in Mathematics (2017, shared with Charles Fefferman), and the Rolf Schock Prize, and is a member of the National Academy of Sciences.
