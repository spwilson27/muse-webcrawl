Seascape

A seascape is a work of art, such as a painting, photograph, or other artistic media, that depicts the sea. In painting, the term was first identified in the early nineteenth century and describes a genre with a long history, particularly as a tradition within European art associated with marine art and landscape painting.

Seascape
MediumPainting, photography, print
SubjectThe sea, ships, coastlines
Related genresMarine art, landscape painting

History

Seascapes featured in Dutch Golden Age painting, where artists such as Jan van Goyen and Simon de Vlieger captured the busy shipping and changeable skies of the Netherlands. In Britain, J. M. W. Turner made the seascape a vehicle for dramatic light and atmosphere, while in France the Impressionists, notably Claude Monet, painted the coast of Normandy repeatedly.

Characteristics

Seascapes typically give prominence to the sea and sky, with attention to weather, light on water, waves, and vessels. Some works focus on naval battles or working harbours ("marine" subjects), while others depict empty shores and open ocean for contemplative effect.
