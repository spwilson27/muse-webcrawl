Carlos Ghosn

Carlos Ghosn (born 9 March 1954) is a Brazilian-born businessman of Lebanese descent who served as chairman and chief executive of the French carmaker Renault and the Japanese carmaker Nissan, and as chairman of Mitsubishi Motors. He is best known for leading the turnaround of Nissan in the early 2000s and for building the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance into one of the world's largest automotive groups. In November 2018 he was arrested in Tokyo on allegations of financial misconduct, and in December 2019 he fled Japan for Lebanon while awaiting trial.

Early life and career at Michelin and Renault

Ghosn was born in Porto Velho, Brazil, to Lebanese parents and was educated in Beirut and Paris, graduating in engineering from the École Polytechnique and the École des Mines de Paris. He joined the tyre maker Michelin in 1978, eventually running its South American and North American operations and earning a reputation as a turnaround specialist nicknamed "le cost killer". In 1996 he moved to Renault as executive vice-president, where his restructuring programme returned the company to profitability.

Nissan revival

After Renault took a controlling stake in the heavily indebted Nissan in 1999, Ghosn became Nissan's chief operating officer and then president and CEO. His "Nissan Revival Plan" closed plants, cut 21,000 jobs, reduced purchasing costs and broke with aspects of the keiretsu supplier system, returning the company to profitability within a year. Ghosn became a celebrity in Japan, where his life was depicted in manga, and was widely credited with one of the most dramatic turnarounds in automotive history.

From 2005 he simultaneously led Renault and Nissan — an unusual dual role — and in 2016 became chairman of Mitsubishi Motors after Nissan took a controlling stake. Under his leadership the Alliance became a leader in electric vehicles with models such as the Nissan Leaf.

Arrest and escape

On 19 November 2018, Ghosn was arrested at Haneda Airport in Tokyo on charges of under-reporting his compensation and misusing company assets; Nissan and Mitsubishi removed him as chairman, and Renault later accepted his resignation. After periods in detention and house arrest in Japan, he fled the country on 29 December 2019, reportedly concealed in a large audio-equipment case aboard a private jet to Turkey, and resurfaced in Beirut. Ghosn maintains that he is innocent and describes himself as a victim of Japan's justice system and of a boardroom conspiracy; Japanese prosecutors and Nissan deny this. Interpol issued a red notice for him, but Lebanon does not extradite its citizens, and he has remained in Beirut, giving interviews and publishing accounts of his career and flight.
