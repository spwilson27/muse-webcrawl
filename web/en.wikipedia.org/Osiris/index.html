Osiris

Osiris is the god of fertility, agriculture, the afterlife, the dead, resurrection, life, and vegetation in ancient Egyptian religion. He was classically depicted as a green-skinned deity with a pharaoh's beard, partially mummy-wrapped, wearing a distinctive crown with two large ostrich feathers.

Mythology

In the Osiris myth, the god was the eldest son of Geb and Nut and husband of his sister Isis. His brother Set murdered and dismembered him; Isis gathered the pieces and temporarily resurrected him, conceiving their son Horus. Osiris then became ruler of the Duat, the Egyptian underworld, where he presided over the judgment of the dead.

Worship

Osiris was worshipped from at least the Fifth Dynasty (25th century BCE) until the Roman era; his cult centre was Abydos. Annual festivals re-enacted his death and resurrection, symbolising the renewal of life and the flooding of the Nile. Believers hoped for an afterlife in which they would be identified with Osiris.

Judgment of the dead

In the afterlife judgment, the heart of the deceased was weighed against the feather of Ma'at before Osiris and a tribunal of gods; the righteous passed to the Field of Reeds.
