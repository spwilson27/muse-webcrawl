Freeze response

The freeze response is a defensive reaction to a perceived threat in which an animal or person becomes immobile, vigilant, and physiologically prepared for action. It is commonly grouped with the fight-or-flight response as part of the defence cascade, and is sometimes summarised as "fight, flight, or freeze".

In animals

Many prey animals freeze when a potential predator is detected at a distance. Immobility reduces the chance of detection, since many predators are keyed to movement, and freezing often precedes flight when the predator approaches. A more profound form, tonic immobility or thanatosis ("playing dead"), may occur when escape appears impossible and can discourage predators that prefer live prey.

In humans

In humans the freeze response involves attentive immobility: the body stops moving while heart rate may decelerate and muscles tense, readying the person for fight or flight. Freezing has been documented in responses to traumatic events, including assaults, where involuntary immobility (tonic immobility) is reported by a significant proportion of victims and is associated with later post-traumatic stress symptoms.

Physiology

The response is coordinated by threat-detection circuits including the amygdala and the periaqueductal gray of the midbrain, with autonomic changes combining sympathetic arousal and parasympathetic braking, producing heightened attention with suppressed movement.
