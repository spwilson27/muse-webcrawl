Brane cosmology

Brane cosmology refers to several theories in particle physics and cosmology motivated by superstring theory, M-theory and their descendants, in which our observable universe is confined to a (3+1)-dimensional membrane, or brane, embedded in a higher-dimensional space called the bulk.

In these models, the fundamental forces other than gravity may be confined to the brane, while gravity can propagate through the bulk. This provides a possible explanation for why gravity is so much weaker than the other fundamental forces, and offers an alternative framework for addressing the hierarchy problem.

Motivation and models

The idea emerged in the late 1990s with the ADD model of Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos and Dvali, according to which the extra dimensions could be relatively large, and the Randall–Sundrum models, which feature a warped extra dimension. Brane-world scenarios suggest that the Big Bang may have resulted from a collision between branes, as in the ekpyrotic and cyclic models proposed by Steinhardt, Turok and collaborators.

D-branes

In string theory, D-branes are hypersurfaces on which open strings can end. The gauge interactions of the Standard Model can be realised by open strings attached to a stack of D-branes, while closed strings — which include the graviton — move through the full higher-dimensional bulk.
