Prince Hermann Ludwig Heinrich von Pückler-Muskau (30 October 1785 – 4 February 1871) was a German nobleman, landscape gardener, and writer, best known for creating the landscaped park at Muskau (now a UNESCO World Heritage Site shared by Germany and Poland) and the park at Branitz, and for his witty travel writings published under the persona of a deceased traveller ("Semilasso").

Life

Born at Schloss Muskau in Upper Lusatia, Pückler inherited the great Muskau estate in 1811. After military service he travelled in England in 1812 and again in the 1820s, studying the landscape gardens of Humphry Repton and others. In 1817 he married Lucie von Hardenberg; the couple later divorced amicably so that he could seek a wealthy bride to finance his park-making — a quest chronicled, with Lucie's cooperation, in his bestselling Letters of a Deceased Man (Briefe eines Verstorbenen, 1830–31), an account of travels in England, Wales, and Ireland.

Landscape gardening

From 1815 Pückler laid out Muskau Park, one of the largest English-style landscape parks in Europe, straddling the Neisse river. His treatise Hints on Landscape Gardening (Andeutungen über Landschaftsgärtnerei, 1834) is a classic of garden theory, advocating naturalistic planting, carefully composed views, and zoned progression from formal pleasure grounds near the house to wilder parkland. Forced by debts to sell Muskau in 1845, he created a second masterpiece at Branitz near Cottbus, with its celebrated earth pyramids — including the lake pyramid in which he was buried.

Travels and writings

In the 1830s Pückler travelled extensively in North Africa and the Near East — Egypt, Nubia, Syria, and Greece — publishing lively, sometimes controversial accounts under the name "Semilasso". His memoirs and letters remain valuable social documents of Biedermeier Europe. A famous bon vivant, he is also credited in Germany with popularising a layered ice cream that still bears his name, Fürst-Pückler-Eis (a Neapolitan-style sliced ice cream).

Legacy

Muskau Park (Park Mużakowski) was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2004 as an exemplary work of European landscape art. Branitz Park is preserved as a museum of his life and work.
