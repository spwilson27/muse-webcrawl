Abscess

An abscess is a collection of pus that has built up within the tissue of the body. Signs and symptoms of abscesses include redness, pain, warmth, and swelling. The swelling may feel fluid-filled when pressed. The area of redness often extends beyond the swelling. Carbuncles and boils are types of abscess that often involve hair follicles, with carbuncles being larger.

Abscesses are usually caused by a bacterial infection. Often many different types of bacteria can be involved in a single infection. In the United States and many other areas of the world, the most common bacteria present is methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA). Rarely, parasites can cause abscesses.

Causes

An abscess is a defensive reaction of the tissue to prevent the spread of infectious materials to other parts of the body. Organisms or foreign materials destroy the local cells, triggering the release of cytokines. The cytokines trigger an inflammatory response, which draws large numbers of white blood cells to the area and increases the regional blood flow. The final structure of the abscess is an abscess wall, or capsule, that is formed by the adjacent healthy cells in an attempt to keep the pus from infecting neighboring structures.

Diagnosis

Skin abscesses are typically diagnosed based on their appearance and confirmed by opening them and discovering pus inside. In areas of the body where an abscess is deeper, diagnosis may require ultrasound or computed tomography (CT) imaging.

Treatment

The standard treatment for most skin or soft tissue abscesses is incision and drainage: cutting the abscess open and allowing the pus to drain. There is some evidence that using antibiotics in addition to drainage may speed healing for uncomplicated abscesses. Simply using antibiotics alone, without drainage, is not usually effective because antibiotics often cannot reach the inside of the abscess.

Abscesses can occur in any kind of tissue but most frequently within the skin surface, in the brain, lungs, teeth (dental abscess), and abdominal organs.
