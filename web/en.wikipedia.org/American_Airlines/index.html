American Airlines

American Airlines, Inc. is a major United States airline headquartered in Fort Worth, Texas. Tracing its origins to a 1926 conglomeration of small airmail carriers, it is by fleet size, passengers carried and revenue passenger-miles among the largest airlines in the world, operating an extensive domestic and international network.

History

American grew out of the Aviation Corporation's consolidation of dozens of airmail companies and was reorganised as American Airlines in 1934 under C. R. Smith, who pioneered the modern hub-and-spoke system and introduced the DC-3 to commercial service. After deregulation in 1978 it expanded aggressively, acquired Trans World Airlines (TWA) in 2001, and — despite parent AMR Corporation's 2011 Chapter 11 bankruptcy — merged with US Airways in 2013 to reclaim the title of world's largest airline. The merged company kept the American name and joined the Oneworld alliance, which American co-founded in 1999.

Operations

American's largest hub is Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, with other major hubs at Charlotte, Chicago O'Hare, Miami, Phoenix, Philadelphia, Los Angeles, New York and Washington National. Its mainline and American Eagle regional fleets together number roughly 1,500 aircraft, and its frequent-flyer programme, AAdvantage — launched in 1981 — was the first of its kind and remains a major profit centre.
