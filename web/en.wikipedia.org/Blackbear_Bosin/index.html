Blackbear Bosin (May 5, 1921 – August 9, 1980) was a Kiowa-Comanche artist from Oklahoma, best known as the designer and sculptor of the Keeper of the Plains statue in Wichita, Kansas, one of the most recognizable works of public art in the American Midwest.

Bosin was born in Cyril, Oklahoma, and was largely self-taught as an artist. He worked as a commercial artist and illustrator, including for Boeing in Wichita, while developing a painting practice rooted in Kiowa and Comanche oral traditions, ledger art influences, and the stories told to him by his grandparents.

Keeper of the Plains

In 1974, Wichita commissioned Bosin to create a sculpture for the Bicentennial celebrations. The resulting Keeper of the Plains is a 44-foot-tall steel sculpture of a Native American man with arms raised toward the sky, standing at the confluence of the Arkansas and Little Arkansas rivers, a site sacred to indigenous peoples of the region. The statue was completed in 1974 and later surrounded by the "Ring of Fire," a circle of fire drums lit nightly.

Legacy

Bosin's paintings are held in numerous museum collections, and he is regarded as one of the most significant Native American artists of the 20th century. The Mid-America All-Indian Museum in Wichita preserves much of his work and story.
