Pomace brandy

Pomace brandy is a liquor distilled from pomace, the solid remains of grapes, olives, or other fruit after pressing for juice or oil. It is traditionally a byproduct spirit of winemaking regions.

Varieties

Well-known pomace brandies include Italian grappa, French marc, Spanish orujo, Portuguese aguardente bagaceira, Greek tsipouro and tsikoudia, Georgian chacha, and Balkan rakia in some of its forms. Most are bottled at 40–45% alcohol by volume, though some are stronger.

Production

After pressing, the fermented grape pomace—skins, pulp, seeds, and stems—is distilled, historically in pot stills and today often in column or steam stills. Some pomace brandies are aged in wooden barrels, acquiring colour and softness; most are bottled clear and unaged.

History

Pomace distillation arose as a peasant economy practice to extract value from winery waste, and was long produced informally. In the twentieth century, regions such as Grappa del Piemonte and Grappa del Trentino obtained protected designations for their products.
