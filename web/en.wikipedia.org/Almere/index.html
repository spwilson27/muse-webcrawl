Almere

Almere (Dutch pronunciation: [ɑlˈmeːrə]) is a planned city and municipality in the province of Flevoland, Netherlands, built on land reclaimed from the IJsselmeer. With a population of over 200,000, it is one of the largest cities in the country despite being among its youngest: its first houses were completed only in 1976.

History

Almere was built on the Southern Flevoland polder, drained in 1968 as part of the Zuiderzee Works, to relieve housing pressure in Amsterdam, some 25 kilometres to the west. Unusually for a new town, it was designed as a polynuclear city of separate districts — Almere Stad, Almere Haven, Almere Buiten, Almere Hout, Almere Poort, and Almere Pampus — separated by green corridors, with dedicated bus lanes and one of the world's largest networks of segregated cycle paths.

Architecture and districts

The city centre, Almere Stad, was comprehensively redeveloped in the 2000s to a master plan by Rem Koolhaas's OMA, with buildings by internationally known architects. Almere Haven, the oldest district, retains a small harbour and village character, while Almere Hout includes the experimental self-build district of Oosterwold, where residents design their own homes under loose planning rules.

Transport

Almere is connected to Amsterdam by the Flevolijn railway, with six stations in the municipality, and by motorway across the Hollandse Brug. The city hosted the Floriade horticultural exposition in 2022 on a site being converted into the Hortus residential district.
