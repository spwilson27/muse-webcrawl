ASEAN Charter

Signed20 November 2007, Singapore
Effective15 December 2008
Signatories10 ASEAN member states
SubjectConstitutive charter of ASEAN

The ASEAN Charter is the constitutive treaty of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN). Adopted and signed at the 13th ASEAN Summit in Singapore on 20 November 2007, it entered into force on 15 December 2008 after ratification by all ten member states. For the first time in the bloc's forty-year history, the Charter gave ASEAN a legal personality as an intergovernmental organisation and a codified institutional and legal framework.

Contents

The Charter restates ASEAN's purposes and principles, including respect for the independence, sovereignty and territorial integrity of member states, non-interference in internal affairs, and the peaceful settlement of disputes. At the same time it commits members to democracy, good governance, the rule of law and the protection of human rights. It formalised the structure of ASEAN's organs — the Summit, the Coordinating Council, the Community Councils for the Political-Security, Economic and Socio-Cultural communities, and the Secretariat in Jakarta — and established a Committee of Permanent Representatives. It also provided for the creation of an ASEAN human rights body, realised in 2009 as the ASEAN Intergovernmental Commission on Human Rights (AICHR).

Drafting and reception

The Charter was drafted by a High Level Task Force drawing on the report of an Eminent Persons Group, which had proposed bolder reforms, some of which — such as sanctions for non-compliance — were dropped. Ratification was delayed in several states, notably the Philippines, where legislators linked approval to the human rights situation in Myanmar. Critics have argued that the Charter preserves consensus decision-making and non-interference, limiting enforcement, while supporters regard it as a major step towards a rules-based community, supporting the launch of the ASEAN Community in 2015.
