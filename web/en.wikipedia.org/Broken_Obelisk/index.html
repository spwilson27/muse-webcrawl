Broken Obelisk

Broken Obelisk is a monumental sculpture by the American artist Barnett Newman, designed between 1963 and 1967 and fabricated in Cor-Ten steel. Standing about 7.7 metres (25 feet) tall, it consists of a triangular pyramid surmounted by an obelisk that appears broken in two, the upper segment balanced point-upward on the pyramid's apex, touching it only at a single point. It is the largest and most famous of Newman's six sculptures and an emblem of postwar American public art.

Casts and locations

Three casts were produced. One stands outside the Institute of Religion and Human Development (the Rothko Chapel) in Houston, Texas, where it was dedicated in 1971 to the memory of Martin Luther King Jr.; it sits in a reflecting pool designed by Philip Johnson. Another is installed at the University of Washington in Seattle, and the third is at the Museum of Modern Art in New York, whose version has also been displayed at the Corcoran Gallery of Art in Washington, D.C.

Interpretation

Newman combined two archetypal forms of ancient Egypt — the pyramid, associated with death, and the obelisk, associated with life and rebirth — in a precarious, inverted relation. He called the work a tribute "to man's ambition," and critics have read its fracture and balance as a meditation on heroism, mortality, and broken monuments, consonant with the artist's "zip" paintings and his writings on the sublime.
