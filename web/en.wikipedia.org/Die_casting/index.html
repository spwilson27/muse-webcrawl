Die casting

Die casting is a metal casting process characterised by forcing molten metal under high pressure into a mould cavity (the die). The mould is made from machined tool steel, and the process produces dimensionally accurate, smooth parts in high volumes.

Process

The two main machine types are hot-chamber machines, used for low-melting-point alloys such as zinc, and cold-chamber machines, used for aluminium and magnesium alloys. Molten metal is injected into the die, held under pressure while it solidifies, and then ejected; cycle times can be as short as a few seconds.

Materials and applications

Die casting is mainly used with non-ferrous metals, especially zinc, aluminium, magnesium, and copper alloys. Applications include automotive components, housings for electronics, toys, and hardware. The process is economical for large production runs because the expensive tooling cost is spread over many parts.
