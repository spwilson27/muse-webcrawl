Energy–maneuverability theory

Energy–maneuverability theory (E-M theory) is a model of aircraft performance developed by United States Air Force Colonel John Boyd and mathematician Thomas P. Christie at Eglin Air Force Base in the 1960s. It relates an aircraft's energy state — altitude, airspeed, acceleration, and turning performance — to its ability to maneuver in aerial combat.

Specific excess power

The theory's central quantity is specific excess power, PS = (T − D)V/W, where T is thrust, D is drag, V is velocity, and W is weight. Positive excess power lets an aircraft climb or accelerate; comparing the PS envelopes of opposing fighters allows designers and pilots to predict which aircraft holds the advantage in a dogfight.

Influence

E-M analysis strongly influenced the design of the F-15 Eagle and F-16 Fighting Falcon, contributing to the "fighter mafia" philosophy of lightweight, highly maneuverable fighters optimized for energy maneuverability.
