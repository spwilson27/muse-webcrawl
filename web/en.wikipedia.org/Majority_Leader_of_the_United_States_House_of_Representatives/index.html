Majority Leader of the United States House of Representatives

The Majority Leader of the United States House of Representatives is the second-in-command of the majority party in the House of Representatives, ranking below the Speaker of the House. The Majority Leader is elected by the members of the majority party caucus or conference at the start of each Congress.

Role

The Majority Leader acts as the chief floor strategist of the majority party: scheduling legislation for floor consideration, planning the party's daily and weekly legislative agenda, consulting with committee chairs, and directing floor debate on major bills. Working with the Majority Whip, who counts votes and persuades members, the Leader is responsible for assembling the majorities needed to pass the party's programme. The office emerged in its modern form in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; the first person formally designated majority leader was Sereno Payne in 1899.

Counterpart and succession

The minority party's counterpart is the Minority Leader, with whom the Majority Leader's office coordinates on scheduling. Unlike the Senate majority leader, the House Majority Leader is not the party's most powerful figure — that role belongs to the Speaker — but the post is a common stepping stone to the speakership; several Speakers, including Sam Rayburn and Nancy Pelosi's contemporaries in leadership, served as leader first.
