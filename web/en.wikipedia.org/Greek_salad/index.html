Greek salad

A traditional Greek salad topped with a single slab of feta.

Greek salad or horiatiki salad (Greek: χωριάτικη σαλάτα, "village salad") is a popular salad in Greek cuisine, generally made with tomatoes, cucumbers, onion, and green bell pepper, dressed with salt, Greek oregano, and olive oil, and topped with Kalamata olives and a slab of feta cheese. It is a quintessential summer dish in Greece, made with freshly harvested vegetables, and contains no lettuce.

Preparation

The ingredients are coarsely cut rather than finely chopped. The feta is traditionally served as a single slice on top of the salad rather than crumbled, and the salad is seasoned with dried oregano and dressed generously with extra-virgin olive oil; lemon juice or red wine vinegar is sometimes added. Common additions in Greece include capers and caper leaves, particularly in the Cyclades, and occasionally rusks in the Cretan variant known as dakos. The dish is typically served with bread used to soak up the seasoned oil and juices.

Outside Greece

Versions served abroad frequently depart from the traditional recipe, adding lettuce, crumbled feta, pepperoncini, beets, radishes, or bottled dressings; in North America a lemon-oregano vinaigrette is common. In Cyprus, a similar village salad typically includes finely chopped vegetables, cabbage, and parsley.
