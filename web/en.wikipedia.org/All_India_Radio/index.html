All India Radio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Akashvani Bhavan, New Delhi

All India Radio (AIR), officially known since 1957 as Akashvani (from Sanskrit: "Voice from the Sky"), is the national public radio broadcaster of India and is a division of Prasar Bharati. It was established in 1936. It is the sister service to Prasar Bharati's Doordarshan, an Indian television broadcaster.

Headquartered at the Akashvani Bhavan in New Delhi, it houses the Drama Section, the FM Section, the National Service, and is also home to the Indian television station Doordarshan Kendra (Delhi).

History

Broadcasting began in India with the formation of the Indian Broadcasting Company in 1927, with stations in Bombay and Calcutta. The service passed through liquidation and government takeover, becoming the Indian State Broadcasting Service, which was renamed All India Radio on 8 June 1936.

After independence in 1947, AIR expanded rapidly. In 1957 the name Akashvani was adopted for the Hindi service and later for the broadcaster generally. The popular film music channel Vividh Bharati was launched in 1957, and FM services began in the 1970s and expanded in the 1990s and 2000s.

Services

All India Radio is one of the largest broadcasting organisations in the world in terms of the number of languages broadcast and the spectrum of socioeconomic and cultural diversity it serves. AIR's home service comprises hundreds of stations located across India, reaching nearly the whole population of the country and broadcasting in 23 languages and 179 dialects.

Its External Services Division broadcasts in numerous foreign languages to listeners abroad, and its news service, the News Services Division, produces bulletins around the clock. Commercial services include Vividh Bharati, and FM networks AIR FM Gold and AIR FM Rainbow.
