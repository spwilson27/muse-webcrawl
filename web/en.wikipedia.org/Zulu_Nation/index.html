The Universal Zulu Nation is an international hip hop awareness group formed by hip hop pioneer Afrika Bambaataa in the Bronx, New York City, in 1973. Taking its name from the 1964 film Zulu and the Zulu people of southern Africa, the organisation sought to channel the energy of Bronx street gangs into music, dance and art, and it spread the foundational elements of hip hop culture — DJing, MCing, breakdancing (b-boying) and graffiti art — around the world.

History

After witnessing the film Zulu and travelling to Africa, Bambaataa organised the Zulu Nation around 1973–1974, drawing members from the Black Spades gang and local youth. The group promoted the motto "peace, unity, love and having fun". Chapters spread across North America, Europe, Japan, Africa and Australia as hip hop globalised in the 1980s and 1990s. The organisation's later years were overshadowed by sexual-abuse allegations against Bambaataa that surfaced in 2016, which he denied; the Zulu Nation apologised to victims and announced organisational changes, and Bambaataa was removed from his position.
