Knowledge management

Knowledge management (KM) is the collection of methods relating to creating, sharing, using and managing the knowledge and information of an organisation. It emerged as a discipline in the early 1990s, drawing on organisational theory, management consulting and information technology.

Concepts

KM distinguishes explicit knowledge, which can be documented, from tacit knowledge, held in people's experience. Nonaka and Takeuchi's SECI model describes conversion between the two through socialisation, externalisation, combination and internalisation. Common practices include lessons-learned databases, communities of practice, mentoring, intranets, wikis and after-action reviews.

Goals and criticism

The goals are to improve performance, innovation, retention of expertise and decision-making, and to avoid repeating mistakes when staff leave. Critics note that knowledge is context-dependent and not easily codified, and that many KM programmes became overly focused on technology repositories rather than people and culture.
