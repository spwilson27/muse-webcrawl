Juilliard School

The Juilliard School (/ˈdʒuːliɑːrd/) is a private performing arts conservatory in New York City. Founded in 1905, the school offers undergraduate and graduate degrees in music, dance, and drama. It is widely regarded as one of the world's leading music schools.

History

Juilliard began as the Institute of Musical Art, founded by Frank Damrosch with funds from Augustus Juilliard, a textile merchant whose bequest also established the Juilliard Graduate School in 1924; the two merged in 1926 as the Juilliard School of Music. In 1969 the school moved to Lincoln Center and added its drama division (1968) — whose first class included actors such as Kevin Kline and Patti LuPone — and dance became a full division. The school was renamed The Juilliard School in 1951.

Programs and admissions

Juilliard enrolls about 800 dance, drama, and music students, admitted through highly competitive live auditions; acceptance rates are among the lowest of any American conservatory. Its faculty and alumni include numerous celebrated performers — among them Itzhak Perlman, Yo-Yo Ma, Renée Fleming, and Wynton Marsalis. The school's home at Lincoln Center includes the Peter Jay Sharp Theater and Alice Tully Hall.
