Georges Jean Franz Köhler (17 April 1946 – 1 March 1995) was a German biologist who shared the 1984 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Niels Kaj Jerne and César Milstein.

Work

Working as a postdoctoral researcher in Milstein's laboratory at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge, Köhler co-developed the hybridoma technique, published in Nature in 1975. By fusing antibody-producing B lymphocytes with immortal myeloma cells, the method produced hybrid cells that could secrete unlimited quantities of a single, specific antibody — a monoclonal antibody. The technique transformed immunology, diagnostics and, eventually, antibody-based therapeutics.

Later career

From 1984 Köhler was director of the Max Planck Institute of Immunobiology in Freiburg. He died of a heart attack in 1995 at the age of 48.
