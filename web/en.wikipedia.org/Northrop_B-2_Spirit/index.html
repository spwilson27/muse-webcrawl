The Northrop B-2 Spirit, also known as the Stealth Bomber, is an American heavy strategic bomber featuring low-observable stealth technology designed to penetrate dense anti-aircraft defences. A subsonic flying wing with a crew of two, the B-2 was developed by Northrop (later Northrop Grumman) from the secret Advanced Technology Bomber (ATB) programme of the 1980s.

Design

The B-2's pure flying-wing planform, radar-absorbent materials and structure, buried engines and treated exhausts give it a very small radar cross-section for its size, allowing it to operate at altitudes up to 50,000 feet with a range of about 6,900 miles (11,000 km) unrefuelled. It can carry conventional and thermonuclear weapons, including up to eighty 500-pound-class bombs or sixteen B83 nuclear bombs.

History and service

Revealed publicly in November 1988 and first flown on 17 July 1989, the B-2 entered service with the U.S. Air Force in 1997. Cost growth reduced the planned fleet of 132 to just 21 aircraft, making it among the most expensive aircraft ever built (around US$2.1 billion per aircraft when development costs are included). Based at Whiteman Air Force Base, Missouri, B-2s saw combat over Kosovo (1999), Afghanistan, Iraq and Libya, flying intercontinental round-trip missions. One aircraft was lost in a crash on takeoff from Guam in 2008 and another was retired after a 2022 accident. The B-2 is to be succeeded by the B-21 Raider.
