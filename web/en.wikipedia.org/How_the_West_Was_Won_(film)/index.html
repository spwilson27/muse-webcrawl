How the West Was Won (film)

How the West Was Won is a 1962 American epic Western film directed by Henry Hathaway, John Ford, and George Marshall. It was one of the last films photographed in the three-panel Cinerama widescreen process. The story, narrated by Spencer Tracy, follows several generations of the fictional Prescott family as they move westward across America between 1839 and 1889.

Plot

The film is divided into five segments: "The Rivers" (1839), "The Plains" (1851), "The Civil War" (1861–1865), "The Railroad" (1868), and "The Outlaws" (1889). Across them the Prescotts and their descendants travel by river raft, wagon train, and railroad, encountering mountain men, the Civil War, buffalo stampedes, and train robbers.

Reception and legacy

The film was a major commercial success and won three Academy Awards, including Best Original Screenplay, out of eight nominations. In 1997 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant". Its wide multi-panel format made restoration and home-video presentation challenging; modern editions digitally blend the three panels.
