David J

David J (born David John Haskins, 24 April 1957, in Northampton, England) is an English musician, best known as the bassist of the influential post-punk band Bauhaus and later of Love and Rockets. His melodic, dub-influenced bass playing helped define the sound of gothic rock.

Bauhaus

David J co-founded Bauhaus in Northampton in 1978 with his brother Kevin Haskins, Peter Murphy, and Daniel Ash. The band's debut single, "Bela Lugosi's Dead" (1979), became an anthem of the emerging goth scene. Bauhaus released four studio albums before splitting in 1983, and have reunited for tours and new recordings several times since, including the 2008 album Go Away White.

Love and Rockets and solo work

After Bauhaus, David J formed Love and Rockets with Ash and Kevin Haskins, achieving international success with the 1989 hit "So Alive." He has also maintained a solo career, beginning with Etiquette of Violence (1983), and has collaborated widely as a producer, playwright, and author. His memoir Who Killed Mister Moonlight? Bauhaus, Black Magick and Benediction was published in 2014.
