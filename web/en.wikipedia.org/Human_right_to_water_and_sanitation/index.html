Human right to water and sanitation

Right to water and sanitation
RecognisedUN General Assembly Resolution 64/292 (2010)
BasisRight to an adequate standard of living; right to health
SDG linkSustainable Development Goal 6

The human right to water and sanitation (HRWS) is a principle stating that clean drinking water and sanitation are a universal human right because of their essential importance to sustaining every person's life and health. On 28 July 2010 the United Nations General Assembly adopted Resolution 64/292 explicitly recognising the right to safe and clean drinking water and sanitation as a human right essential for the full enjoyment of life and all human rights; the Human Rights Council affirmed it later that year.

Legal basis and content

The right is derived from the right to an adequate standard of living and the right to health in the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (1966), elaborated in General Comment No. 15 (2002) of the Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights. It entitles everyone to water that is sufficient, safe, acceptable, physically accessible and affordable — the availability, quality, accessibility and affordability criteria — with sanitation recognised as a distinct right by the General Assembly in 2015.

Implementation

States must respect, protect and fulfil the right progressively within available resources, without discrimination. The UN appointed a Special Rapporteur on the human rights to safe drinking water and sanitation (Catarina de Albuquerque from 2008), and the right is embedded in Sustainable Development Goal 6 (clean water and sanitation for all by 2030). Several constitutions — including South Africa's — guarantee a right to water, and litigation such as the Mazibuko case in Johannesburg has tested its content in court. Despite recognition, billions of people still lack safely managed drinking water or sanitation services.
