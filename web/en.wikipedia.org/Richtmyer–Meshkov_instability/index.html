Richtmyer–Meshkov instability

The Richtmyer–Meshkov instability occurs when the interface between two fluids of different densities is impulsively accelerated, for example by the passage of a shock wave. It was predicted theoretically by Robert D. Richtmyer in 1960 and confirmed experimentally by Evgeny E. Meshkov in 1969.

Mechanism

When a shock crosses a perturbed interface between a heavy and a light fluid, misaligned pressure and density gradients deposit vorticity along the interface (baroclinic torque). Small ripples then grow: spikes of heavy fluid penetrate the light fluid and bubbles of light fluid rise into the heavy one. Unlike the Rayleigh–Taylor instability, which needs sustained acceleration, Richtmyer–Meshkov growth follows a single impulsive acceleration and grows roughly linearly in time at first, later becoming turbulent mixing.

Applications

The instability is important in inertial confinement fusion, where it can spoil capsule implosions; in supernova explosions, where it helps mix stellar layers; and in supersonic combustion and shock-driven mixing generally.
