Energy in the United Kingdom

Energy in the United Kingdom covers production, consumption, and import and export of energy in the UK. Historically supplied by coal, the country developed major North Sea oil and gas fields from the 1970s, and in the twenty-first century has shifted rapidly toward natural gas, wind, and other renewables.

Electricity generation

Coal's share of electricity generation fell from around two-thirds in 1990 to zero in 2024, when the last coal plant closed. Wind power is now the largest source, followed by gas and nuclear. The grid is operated by the National Energy System Operator and connected to neighbouring countries by subsea interconnectors.

Policy

Under the Climate Change Act 2008, the UK committed to net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050, with interim carbon budgets. Policy debates centre on energy security, prices, offshore wind expansion, and new nuclear construction such as Hinkley Point C.
