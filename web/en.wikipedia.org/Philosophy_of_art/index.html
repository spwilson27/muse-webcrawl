Branch of: Aesthetics
Key questions: Definition of art, interpretation, artistic value

Philosophy of art

The philosophy of art is the branch of aesthetics that studies the nature of art, including concepts such as interpretation, representation, and expression, and the criteria used to distinguish works of art from other objects. Closely related to aesthetics, it focuses specifically on artworks rather than on beauty and taste in general.

Definition of art

A central problem is the definition of art: what conditions make something a work of art? Traditional theories appealed to representation (mimesis), expression of emotion, or significant form. In the twentieth century, ready-mades and conceptual art challenged essentialist definitions, prompting institutional theories, which hold that an object is art when the artworld confers that status on it, and historical or procedural definitions.

Interpretation and value

Philosophers of art also examine how artworks should be interpreted, whether interpretation should aim at the artist's intention, and what makes art valuable — whether its value is intrinsic, cognitive, moral, or some combination.

Related issues

Other topics include forgery and authenticity, the ontology of works in reproducible media such as music and film, the relationship between art and morality, and the paradox of fiction — how audiences can feel genuine emotions toward characters they know do not exist.
