Slayer

Slayer was an American thrash metal band formed in Huntington Park, California, in 1981 by guitarists Kerry King and Jeff Hanneman, drummer Dave Lombardo and bassist/vocalist Tom Araya. One of the "Big Four" of thrash metal alongside Metallica, Megadeth and Anthrax, Slayer became famous for fast, aggressive music and controversial lyrics dealing with war, violence, religion and serial killers.

Slayer

Origin: Huntington Park, California, US
Years active: 1981–2019
Genre: Thrash metal
Members (final): Tom Araya, Kerry King, Gary Holt, Paul Bostaph

Career

The band's third album, Reign in Blood (1986), produced by Rick Rubin, is considered a landmark of extreme metal. Subsequent albums such as South of Heaven (1988) and Seasons in the Abyss (1990) broadened their sound without softening it. Hanneman died in 2013 and Lombardo departed the same year; the band released its final album, Repentless, in 2015 and concluded a farewell world tour in November 2019.

Legacy

Slayer's downtuned riffs, atonal solos and double-bass drumming were foundational for death metal and black metal. The band won two Grammy Awards for Best Metal Performance.
