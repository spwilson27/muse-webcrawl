Firmament

In biblical cosmology, the firmament is a solid dome created by God in the Genesis creation narrative to divide the "waters above" from the "waters below", and to hold the sun, moon and stars. The word derives from the Latin firmamentum, used in the Vulgate to translate the Greek stereoma and the Hebrew raqia, terms connoting something beaten or hammered out like a sheet of metal.

Ancient cosmologies

Similar conceptions appear across the ancient Near East: in Babylonian cosmology the heavens were formed from the body of Tiamat, and in Egyptian thought the sky goddess Nut arched over the earth. Greek astronomy replaced the solid dome with nested celestial spheres, though the outermost sphere of the fixed stars retained some of the firmament's character. The idea persisted through the Middle Ages and was only gradually displaced by the Copernican model and the recognition that the stars are remote suns.

Modern usage

Today "firmament" survives mainly in poetic and figurative use as a synonym for the sky or heavens, and in discussions of ancient Hebrew cosmology and the history of astronomy.
