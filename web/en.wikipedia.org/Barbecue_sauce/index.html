Barbecue sauce

Barbecue sauce (also abbreviated BBQ sauce) is a sauce used as a marinade, basting sauce, condiment, or topping for meat — especially pork and beef ribs and chicken — cooked in the barbecue style. It is a ubiquitous accompaniment to barbecue in the United States and is also used on other foods.

Regional styles

American barbecue sauces vary strongly by region. Kansas City style is thick, sweet, and tomato- and molasses-based, and is the most common commercial style. South Carolina traditions include vinegar-and-pepper sauces, mustard-based "Carolina gold", and tomato variants. North Carolina styles split between the vinegar-based sauce of the east and the tomato-tinged "Lexington dip" of the west. Texas sauces are often thinner and less sweet, sometimes flavoured with chili peppers and cumin, while Alabama is known for a white, mayonnaise-based sauce served with smoked chicken. Memphis sauces are typically thinner tomato-vinegar blends.

Ingredients

Common ingredients include tomato paste or ketchup, vinegar, sweeteners such as brown sugar, molasses or corn syrup, mustard, Worcestershire sauce, liquid smoke, and spices such as paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, black pepper, and chili. Commercial production began in the early 20th century; brands such as Kraft, Sweet Baby Ray's, and KC Masterpiece became supermarket staples.
