Parkinson's disease

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a long-term neurodegenerative disorder of the central nervous system that mainly affects the motor system. It is characterised by the death of dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra, a region of the midbrain, whose loss produces the cardinal motor symptoms: tremor, rigidity, slowness of movement (bradykinesia), and postural instability. Non-motor symptoms — including depression, sleep disturbance, loss of smell, and eventually cognitive impairment and dementia — are also common. The disease is named after the English physician James Parkinson, who published the first detailed description, An Essay on the Shaking Palsy, in 1817.

Signs and symptoms

The motor symptoms are collectively called parkinsonism. A resting tremor, classically a "pill-rolling" tremor of the hand, is often the first noticed sign. Movements become slow and small: handwriting shrinks (micrographia), the face becomes expressionless, speech softens, and gait shortens into shuffling steps with a tendency to freeze. As the disease advances, balance problems and falls become prominent. Non-motor features may precede the motor signs by years and often dominate late disease.

Cause and pathology

The cause is unknown in most cases. A small proportion are hereditary, with mutations in genes such as SNCA, LRRK2, and GBA implicated; environmental exposures, notably certain pesticides and the contaminant MPTP, increase risk. Pathologically, affected neurons contain Lewy bodies — clumps of the protein alpha-synuclein — and the progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra disrupts the basal ganglia circuits that control movement.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is clinical, based on the history and neurological examination, and supported by a good response to dopaminergic medication; brain imaging is mainly used to exclude other conditions. There is no cure and no treatment proven to slow progression. Symptoms are managed chiefly with levodopa combined with carbidopa, dopamine agonists, and MAO-B inhibitors; deep brain stimulation of the subthalamic nucleus or globus pallidus helps selected patients with motor complications. Exercise and multidisciplinary rehabilitation also play an important role.

Epidemiology

Parkinson's disease affects roughly 1% of people over 60 and becomes more common with age; men are affected somewhat more often than women. It is the second most common neurodegenerative disorder after Alzheimer's disease, with an estimated 10 million people living with the condition worldwide.
