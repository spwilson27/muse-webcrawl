Anaphora (linguistics)

In linguistics, anaphora is the use of an expression whose interpretation depends upon another expression in context, called its antecedent. The dependent expression is an anaphor. Most commonly, an anaphor is a pronoun referring back to an earlier noun phrase, as in "Sally arrived, but nobody saw her", where her refers to Sally. Anaphora is a central topic in the study of syntax, semantics, discourse and natural language processing.

Types

Anaphora takes many forms: pronominal anaphora (pronouns), noun-phrase anaphora ("the woman"), verb-phrase ellipsis ("Sam left, and Ben did too"), and one-anaphora ("a green one"). When the dependent expression precedes its antecedent, as in "When he arrived, John sat down", the relation is called cataphora. Reflexive and reciprocal pronouns are governed in English by binding principles described in Chomsky's binding theory.

Resolution

Understanding anaphora requires resolving which antecedent an anaphor picks out, drawing on grammatical constraints, agreement in gender and number, discourse salience and world knowledge. Computational linguists study anaphora resolution (coreference resolution) as a key task for machine translation, summarisation and question answering.
