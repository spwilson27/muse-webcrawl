Rideau Hall

Rideau Hall is the official residence in Ottawa of the Canadian monarch and of the Governor General of Canada. Located at 1 Sussex Drive in the New Edinburgh neighbourhood, it has been the home of every governor general since 1867.

History

The original villa was built in 1838 for Thomas McKay, a wealthy stonemason involved in building the Rideau Canal, from whose nearby falls it takes its name. The Crown leased the house in 1864 as a temporary home for the governor general, Viscount Monck, and purchased it in 1868. Successive additions, including the Mappin Wing, the Tent Room and a new front facade in 1913–14, transformed the villa into a palace of 175 rooms set in 36 hectares (88 acres) of grounds.

Use

Rideau Hall hosts state ceremonies, investitures of the Order of Canada, swearing-in of ministers, and visits by foreign heads of state. The monarch stays there on royal tours. The grounds and parts of the residence are open to the public, and the residence's art collection includes notable Canadian works.

Architecture

The building is an amalgam of Regency, Edwardian and later styles arranged around the original villa. It is protected as a classified federal heritage building.
