Étienne Tshisekedi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 14 December 1932, Kananga, Belgian Congo
Died: 1 February 2017, Brussels, Belgium
Known for: Leader of the UDPS; long-time opposition figure in Zaire/DR Congo

Étienne Tshisekedi wa Mulumba (14 December 1932 – 1 February 2017) was a Congolese politician and the leader of the Union for Democracy and Social Progress (UDPS), the main opposition party in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. For decades he was the leading opposition figure under Mobutu Sese Seko and his successors, and he served briefly as prime minister of Zaire three times during the early 1990s transition period.

Early life and career

Tshisekedi was born in Kananga (then Luluabourg) and was among the first Congolese to earn a law degree, graduating in 1961. He held ministerial posts in the 1960s, including Minister of the Interior, before becoming disillusioned with Mobutu's one-party state.

Opposition

In 1982 he co-founded the UDPS, defying the ban on opposition parties; he was repeatedly imprisoned, placed under house arrest and exiled internally. During the Sovereign National Conference era he was elected prime minister in 1991 and again in 1992–93, but was repeatedly blocked from governing by Mobutu. He boycotted the 2006 elections, contested the 2011 presidential election, and remained the central figure of the opposition until his death in Brussels in 2017. His son, Félix Tshisekedi, later became President of the DR Congo.
