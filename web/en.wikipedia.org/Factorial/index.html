Factorial

In mathematics, the factorial of a non-negative integer n, denoted by n!, is the product of all positive integers less than or equal to n:

n! = n · (n − 1) · (n − 2) · … · 3 · 2 · 1

The value of 0! is defined as 1, according to the convention for an empty product. Factorials count permutations: n! is the number of distinct orderings of n different objects.

History

Products of consecutive integers appear in Indian mathematics by the 12th century (Bhāskara II) and in the counting of permutations in Jewish mysticism (Sefer Yetzirah). The notation n! was introduced by Christian Kramp in 1808. Factorials occur throughout combinatorics, algebra, and mathematical analysis — in binomial coefficients, Taylor series (for example, ex = Σ xn/n!), and probability distributions.

Properties

Factorials grow faster than exponential functions. Stirling's approximation gives n! ≈ √(2πn) (n/e)n. Legendre's formula counts the prime factorisation of n!, from which it follows that the number of trailing zeros in n! equals the number of factors of 5. Wilson's theorem states that (n − 1)! ≡ −1 (mod n) if and only if n is prime.

Generalizations

The gamma function, Γ(n) = (n − 1)!, extends the factorial to all complex numbers except the non-positive integers. Other generalisations include the double factorial, the falling and rising factorials (Pochhammer symbols), and hyperfactorials and superfactorials.
