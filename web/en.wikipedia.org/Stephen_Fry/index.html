Stephen Fry

Stephen Fry (born 1957), English actor, comedian, writer and broadcaster.

Stephen John Fry (born 24 August 1957) is an English actor, comedian, writer, presenter and activist. He first came to prominence in the 1980s in sketch comedy with Hugh Laurie (A Bit of Fry and Laurie), and went on to star in Blackadder, Jeeves and Wooster and numerous films, and to host the BBC quiz show QI from 2003 to 2016.

Early life and education

Fry was born in Hampstead, London, and grew up in Norfolk. After a troubled school career — including a spell in prison for credit-card fraud as a teenager — he won a scholarship to Queens' College, Cambridge, where he joined the Footlights and met Hugh Laurie and Emma Thompson.

Career

With Laurie, Fry wrote and performed in The Cellar Tapes and A Bit of Fry and Laurie. He played General Melchett in Blackadder Goes Forth, Jeeves opposite Laurie's Wooster, Oscar Wilde in the film Wilde (1997), and appeared in Gosford Park, V for Vendetta and The Hobbit. His stage work includes the hit play Latin! and a successful run in Twelfth Night; he adapted Me and My Girl for the stage.

Writing and broadcasting

Fry is the author of novels including The Liar and Making History, the poetry guide The Ode Less Travelled, the Greek-myth retellings Mythos, Heroes and Troy, and several volumes of autobiography beginning with Moab Is My Washpot. He narrated the British audiobook editions of the Harry Potter novels and made acclaimed documentaries on subjects from bipolar disorder (The Secret Life of the Manic Depressive) to language and travel in America.

Personal life

Fry is openly gay and has spoken publicly about his bipolar disorder, becoming a prominent mental-health campaigner; he served as president of the charity Mind from 2011 to 2024. He married Elliott Spencer in 2015.
