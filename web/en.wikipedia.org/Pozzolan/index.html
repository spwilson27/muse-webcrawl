Pozzolan

Pozzolans are a broad class of siliceous and aluminous materials which, in themselves, possess little or no cementitious value but which, in finely divided form and in the presence of water, will react chemically with calcium hydroxide at ordinary temperature to form compounds having cementitious properties. The quantification of the capacity of a pozzolan to react with calcium hydroxide and water is given by measuring the pozzolanic activity.

Origin and history

The name derives from Pozzuoli (ancient Puteoli), a town near Naples, Italy, in the shadow of Mount Vesuvius, where a volcanic ash with these properties was mined. The Romans used the ash, mixed with lime, to produce a hydraulic concrete (opus caementicium) that could set underwater and enabled structures such as harbours, bridges, and the dome of the Pantheon, the world's largest unreinforced concrete dome. Vitruvius described the material in De architectura.

Types and uses

Natural pozzolans include volcanic ashes, pumice, diatomaceous earth, and opaline shales. Artificial or processed pozzolans include fly ash from coal combustion, silica fume from silicon alloy production, ground granulated blast-furnace slag (though often classed separately as a latent hydraulic material), metakaolin, and rice husk ash. Pozzolans are widely used as supplementary cementitious materials in modern Portland cement concrete, where they improve long-term strength, reduce permeability, increase resistance to sulphate attack and alkali-silica reaction, and lower the carbon footprint of cement production. ASTM C618 classifies fly ash and natural pozzolans into classes N, F, and C.

Chemistry

The pozzolanic reaction consumes calcium hydroxide (portlandite) released by hydrating cement and forms additional calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H), the principal binding phase in concrete. Because the reaction is slower than cement hydration, pozzolanic concretes characteristically gain strength over longer timescales and exhibit lower early heat of hydration, which is advantageous in mass concrete such as dams.
