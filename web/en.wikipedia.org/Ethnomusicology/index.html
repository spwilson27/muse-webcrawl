Ethnomusicology

Ethnomusicology is the study of music in its social and cultural contexts, examining music-making, instruments, dance and ritual across all societies, not only Western art music. Ethnomusicologists combine anthropological fieldwork with musical analysis, typically learning to perform the traditions they study.

History

The field grew from comparative musicology in late-19th-century Berlin, where scholars such as Carl Stumpf and Erich von Hornbostel compared recordings of world music. After the Second World War, figures including Jaap Kunst (who coined 'ethnomusicology'), Alan Merriam, Mantle Hood and Bruno Nettl shifted emphasis from comparison toward cultural understanding, founding institutions such as the Society for Ethnomusicology (1955).

Methods and topics

Ethnographic fieldwork, participant-observation, organology, transcription and oral history are central methods. Topics include music and identity, migration and diaspora, ritual and religion, popular music, music and politics, sound studies and the ethics of recording and archiving.

Debates

The discipline has grappled with its colonial origins, the politics of representation, and applied work in heritage preservation. See also Musicology, Anthropology and World music.
