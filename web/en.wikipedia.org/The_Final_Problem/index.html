"The Final Problem" is a short story by Arthur Conan Doyle featuring his detective character Sherlock Holmes. First published in The Strand Magazine in December 1893, it was intended by Doyle to be the last Holmes story.

Plot

Holmes, having unmasked the criminal mastermind Professor James Moriarty — "the Napoleon of crime" — flees with Dr. Watson across Europe to escape Moriarty's gang. Their journey ends at the Reichenbach Falls in Switzerland, where Holmes and Moriarty grapple on a cliff path and apparently plunge together to their deaths. Watson finds only Holmes's farewell note.

Aftermath

Public demand, and generous offers, eventually forced Doyle to resurrect his detective: Holmes returned in The Hound of the Baskervilles (1901, set before the events at Reichenbach) and was formally restored to life in "The Adventure of the Empty House" (1903). The Reichenbach confrontation has been adapted countless times and remains the most famous episode in the Holmes canon.
