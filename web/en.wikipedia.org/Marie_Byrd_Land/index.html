Marie Byrd Land is the portion of West Antarctica lying east of the Ross Ice Shelf and south of the Amundsen Sea, extending roughly between 103°W and 158°W. It is one of the most remote and least visited regions of the continent.

Exploration

The region was explored during the expeditions of Rear Admiral Richard E. Byrd, beginning with flights in 1929, and was named by him for his wife, Marie. Its coasts include the Walgreen, Bakutis, Hobbs, Ruppert and Saunders coasts, and its interior is dominated by the Executive Committee Range and other volcanic mountain groups.

Territorial status

Marie Byrd Land is notable as the largest remaining piece of unclaimed land on Earth: no nation has asserted sovereignty over it, apart from tiny Peter I Island considerations nearby. Research activity includes the United States' Byrd Station and the WAIS Divide ice-core project.
