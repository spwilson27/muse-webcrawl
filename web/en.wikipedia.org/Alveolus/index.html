Alveolus

An alveolus (plural alveoli; from Latin alveolus, "little cavity") is, in anatomy, a small cavity or pit. The term is used most prominently for the pulmonary alveoli, the microscopic air sacs at the ends of the respiratory tree in the lungs, and also for the tooth sockets (dental alveoli) and the milk-producing sacs of the mammary glands.

Pulmonary alveoli

The human lungs contain several hundred million alveoli, each about 0.2–0.3 mm in diameter, clustered around alveolar ducts like grapes. Their walls consist of a single layer of epithelial cells — flattened type I pneumocytes across which gas exchange occurs, and cuboidal type II pneumocytes that secrete pulmonary surfactant, which reduces surface tension and prevents collapse. A dense network of capillaries surrounds each alveolus; oxygen diffuses into the blood while carbon dioxide diffuses out. Alveolar macrophages patrol the surfaces, removing dust and debris.

Dental alveoli

The dental alveoli are the sockets in the maxilla and mandible into which the roots of teeth are set, anchored by the periodontal ligament. See tooth.
