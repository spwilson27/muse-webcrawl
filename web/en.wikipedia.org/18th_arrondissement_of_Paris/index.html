The 18th arrondissement of Paris (XVIIIe arrondissement) is one of the 20 arrondissements of the capital city of France. Known as the arrondissement des Buttes-Montmartre, it is located on the Right Bank of the River Seine and is best known for the hill of Montmartre, crowned by the white-domed Basilica of the Sacré-Cœur.

Geography and quarters

The arrondissement is divided into four quarters: Grandes-Carrières, Clignancourt, Goutte-d'Or, and Chapelle. It includes the village-like streets of Montmartre, the vineyard of Clos Montmartre, the Moulin Rouge cabaret at Place Blanche, and the multicultural Barbès and Goutte d'Or districts.

Culture

Montmartre was a centre of bohemian and artistic life in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, home to artists such as Picasso, Renoir, and Toulouse-Lautrec, and to cabarets and studios including the Bateau-Lavoir. The neighbourhood remains a major tourist destination.
