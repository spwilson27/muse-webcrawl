Leading-tone

In music theory, a leading-tone (also leading-note) is a note or scale degree that leads strongly toward a resolution, most commonly the seventh degree of a major scale (the subtonic raised by a semitone in natural minor), which lies a semitone below the tonic and tends to resolve upward to it.

For example, in C major the leading-tone is B, which resolves to C; in the harmonic minor scale, the raised seventh serves the same function. The leading-tone is the third of the dominant chord and a component of the diminished triad built on the seventh degree, and its strong pull underpins dominant-to-tonic cadences in tonal music.

The term is sometimes distinguished from the subtonic, the lowered seventh degree found in natural minor and modal music, which lies a whole tone below the tonic and has a weaker tendency to resolve.
