Electromyography

Electromyography (EMG) is a technique for evaluating and recording the electrical activity produced by skeletal muscles. EMG is performed using an instrument called an electromyograph to produce a record called an electromyogram.

Procedure

An electromyographer inserts a needle electrode through the skin into the muscle tissue and records activity at rest and during voluntary contraction. Surface EMG instead uses electrodes placed on the skin, and is common in biomechanics and rehabilitation research.

What it measures

The signals represent motor unit action potentials. Abnormal spontaneous activity, such as fibrillations and positive sharp waves, suggests denervation, while characteristic motor unit changes distinguish muscle disease (myopathy) from nerve disease (neuropathy).

Clinical use

Together with nerve conduction studies, EMG helps diagnose disorders such as carpal tunnel syndrome, radiculopathy, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and myasthenia gravis.
