Dummy pronoun

"It is raining": it fills the subject position without referring to anything

A dummy pronoun, also called an expletive pronoun, is a pronoun that fulfils a syntactic requirement of a sentence without contributing meaning or having a referent. English normally requires an overt subject, so dummy pronouns appear where the meaning of the sentence supplies no real subject.

Weather "it"

The best-known example is it in expressions of weather and time: "It is raining", "It is five o'clock", "It seems that…". Here it refers to nothing in particular; it simply occupies the subject position required by English grammar. Similar constructions exist in many languages, such as French il pleut.

Other examples

The pronoun there in existential sentences ("There are three problems") is sometimes analysed as a dummy subject, and it appears in extraposition ("It is obvious that she left") and cleft sentences ("It was John who called"). In some analyses, you and they used generically ("You can't buy happiness") are also treated as dummy-like pronouns.

In other languages

Languages such as Spanish and Italian are "pro-drop" languages that permit null subjects, so weather verbs simply have no subject ("Llueve", "Piove"). Comparing languages with and without dummy subjects is a classic topic in generative grammar, related to the Extended Projection Principle, which requires clauses to have subjects.
