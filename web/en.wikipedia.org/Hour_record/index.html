Hour record

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The hour record is the record for the longest distance cycled in one hour on a bicycle, one of the most prestigious marks in cycling. Attempts take place on a velodrome, with the rider alone on the track, and the record is governed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI).

History

The first commonly recognised record was set in 1876. Through the twentieth century the mark was raised by champions including Fausto Coppi, Jacques Anquetil, and Eddy Merckx, whose 1972 ride in the thin air of Mexico City — 49.431 km — stood for years as the benchmark "athlete's hour." Chris Boardman's 56.375 km in 1996, achieved in an extreme "superman" position, prompted the UCI to split the record into categories and restrict equipment and riding positions.

Unified record

In 2014 the UCI unified the rules, permitting modern pursuit bikes and positions. A rapid succession of attempts followed: Jens Voigt, Rohan Dennis, Alex Dowsett, and Bradley Wiggins (54.526 km, London, 2015) all held the record. Victor Campenaerts raised it to 55.089 km in 2019 at altitude in Aguascalientes, Mexico. Dan Bigham set 55.548 km in August 2022, and Filippo Ganna — an Olympic and world pursuit champion — set 56.792 km in October 2022 in Grenchen, Switzerland.

Women's record

The women's hour record dates to the 1950s. Notable holders include Jeannie Longo, Leontien van Moorsel, and Evelyn Stevens. Under the unified rules, Vittoria Bussi, Joss Lowden, and Ellen van Dijk successively raised the mark, with van Dijk reaching 49.254 km in May 2022; Bussi reclaimed the record in 2023 and improved it further in 2025, approaching 50.5 km.

Factors

Performance depends on aerodynamic drag, altitude (thinner air reduces drag but also oxygen availability), track surface, pacing strategy, and equipment. Modern attempts use wind-tunnel-optimised positions, skinsuits, and disc wheels, and are planned with extensive physiological testing.
