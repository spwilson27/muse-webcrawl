Richard Ryan (psychologist)

Richard M. Ryan is an American psychologist and professor, best known as the co-founder, with Edward L. Deci, of self-determination theory (SDT), one of the most influential frameworks in the study of human motivation and well-being.

Self-determination theory

Developed at the University of Rochester from the 1970s onward, SDT proposes that people have basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness, and that satisfying these needs supports intrinsic motivation, healthy development, and well-being. Ryan and Deci's 1985 book Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior and their 2017 synthesis Self-Determination Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development, and Wellness are standard references.

Research and influence

Ryan's work spans education, health care, work organisations, sport, and video games, examining how social contexts support or thwart motivation. He has been among the most highly cited psychologists worldwide and co-founded the Center for Self-Determination Theory. He later took a position at the Institute for Positive Psychology and Education at Australian Catholic University.
