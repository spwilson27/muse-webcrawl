Tactical voting

Tactical voting (or strategic voting) occurs in voting systems when a voter supports a candidate other than their sincere preference in order to prevent an undesirable outcome. For example, in a plurality election a voter may vote for their second choice if they believe their preferred candidate cannot win, hoping to defeat a third, less-preferred candidate. Tactical voting is closely related to the phenomenon described by Duverger's law, under which plurality elections tend toward two-party competition.

Tactical voting: supporters of a trailing candidate switch to block a common opponent

Mechanisms

The most familiar form is the "lesser of two evils" or "wasted vote" logic under first-past-the-post: voters desert smaller parties for viable alternatives. Other forms include voting to keep a party below a threshold in proportional systems, "loaning" votes to an allied party so it clears an electoral threshold, and voting against a front-runner in primaries. The Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem shows that every non-dictatorial voting system with more than two possible outcomes is susceptible to some form of strategic voting.

Examples

Tactical voting is prominent in United Kingdom general elections, where campaigns encourage supporters of third parties in marginal constituencies to back whichever candidate can defeat the incumbent's party; anti-Conservative tactical voting was widely credited in the 1997 and 2024 elections. In Canada, "strategic voting" campaigns similarly urge voters to unite behind the candidate best placed to defeat a disliked party. Two-round elections in France often feature a "republican front" in which voters of different parties combine in the second round to block the far right.

Mitigation

Alternative electoral systems—such as ranked-choice (instant-runoff) voting and certain proportional systems—aim to reduce incentives for tactical voting by allowing voters to express sincere preferences without "wasting" their vote, though no system eliminates strategic incentives entirely.
