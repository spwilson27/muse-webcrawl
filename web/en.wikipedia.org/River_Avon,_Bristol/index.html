River Avon

Length120 km (75 mi)
SourceNear Acton Turville, Gloucestershire
MouthSevern Estuary at Avonmouth

The River Avon (also called the Bristol Avon) is a river in the south west of England, flowing 120 kilometres (75 miles) from near Acton Turville in South Gloucestershire, through Wiltshire and Bath, and then through Bristol to join the Severn Estuary at Avonmouth. Its name derives from the Welsh word afon, simply meaning "river" — a naming pattern shared by several English Rivers Avon.

Course

The river passes Chippenham, Melksham, Bradford-on-Avon and Bath, where it is joined by the Kennet and Avon Canal, before entering Bristol through the Avon Gorge, spanned by Isambard Kingdom Brunel's Clifton Suspension Bridge (completed 1864). In central Bristol the historic harbour was created by diverting the river into the "New Cut" (1804–1809) and impounding the old course as the Floating Harbour.

Navigation and ecology

The Avon is navigable from the Severn to Bath as part of the Kennet and Avon Canal route, reopened in 1990. The tidal reach of the gorge has one of the largest tidal ranges in the world. The river supports otters, kingfishers and a migratory fish fauna, and the gorge is noted for rare whitebeam trees and other limestone flora.
