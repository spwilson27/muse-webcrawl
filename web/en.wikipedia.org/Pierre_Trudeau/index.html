Pierre Trudeau

Joseph Philippe Pierre Yves Elliott Trudeau (October 18, 1919 – September 28, 2000) was a Canadian politician and statesman who served as the 15th prime minister of Canada from 1968 to 1979 and again from 1980 to 1984. A charismatic and controversial figure, he reshaped Canadian federalism, championed bilingualism and multiculturalism, and patriated the Canadian constitution.

Political career

A Montreal intellectual and law professor, Trudeau entered federal politics in 1965 and succeeded Lester B. Pearson as Liberal leader and prime minister in 1968 amid the fervour dubbed "Trudeaumania." As justice minister he had decriminalized homosexuality, remarking that "there's no place for the state in the bedrooms of the nation." His government passed the Official Languages Act (1969) and adopted multiculturalism as official policy (1971).

Crises and constitutional reform

During the October Crisis of 1970, Trudeau invoked the War Measures Act after a Quebec separatist cell, the FLQ, kidnapped a British diplomat and a provincial minister — the latter was murdered. Reelected in 1980 after a brief defeat, he defeated the Quebec sovereignty referendum and then patriated the Constitution with the Constitution Act, 1982, including the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, over Quebec's objections. He retired in 1984. His son Justin Trudeau became prime minister in 2015.
