Menno Simons

Menno Simons (1496 – 31 January 1561) was a Roman Catholic priest from Friesland in the Low Countries who became an influential Anabaptist religious leader. His followers, reorganised under his leadership after the catastrophe of the Münster rebellion, became known as Mennonites — a name derived from his — and his writings and organisational work shaped one of the historic peace churches of the Radical Reformation.

Life

Menno was born in Witmarsum, Friesland, and ordained a priest in 1524. Doubts about transubstantiation, prompted by reading the New Testament and works of Luther and other reformers, grew through the 1520s. The execution of the Anabaptist Sicke Snijder in 1531 and the violent apocalyptic kingdom established by Anabaptists at Münster (1534–35) — whose followers included his own brother Pieter — forced a crisis. Renouncing his priesthood in January 1536, Menno was baptised on his confession of faith and in 1537 ordained an elder by Obbe Philips. For the remaining 25 years of his life he travelled the Low Countries and northern Germany organising congregations, preaching and writing, despite an imperial price on his head; he died at Wüstenfelde near Bad Oldesloe in Holstein, sheltered by the nobleman Bartholomeus von Ahlefeldt.

Teachings

Menno emphasised a literal, obedient discipleship to Christ (Nachfolge Christi): believers' baptism; a pure, disciplined church practising the ban (avoidance) of unrepentant members; refusal of oaths, military service and civil office; and "the new birth" as the foundation of the Christian life. His motto text was 1 Corinthians 3:11 — "For no one can lay any foundation other than the one already laid, which is Jesus Christ" — the title of his principal work, the Foundation Book (1539). Though he upheld nonresistance as absolute, later divisions — such as the Flemish–Frisian split of 1566–67 over the strictness of the ban — fragmented the movement he had unified.

Legacy

Mennonite churches in the Netherlands, Germany, Russia, and North and South America trace their identity to Menno's tradition of peace, simplicity and congregational discipline. His complete works were collected and published in Dutch in 1681 and remain in print in English translation.
