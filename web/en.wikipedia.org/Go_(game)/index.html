Go (game)

Go is played on a 19×19 grid with black and white stones.

Go is an abstract strategy board game for two players in which the aim is to control more territory than the opponent. The game was invented in China more than 2,500 years ago and is believed to be the oldest board game continuously played to the present day. It is known as wéqí in Chinese, igo or go in Japanese, and baduk in Korean.

Rules

Two players alternately place black and white stones on the intersections of a 19×19 grid (beginners often use 9×9 or 13×13 boards). A stone or a connected group of stones must have at least one empty adjacent point, a "liberty", to remain on the board; groups that lose their last liberty are captured and removed. Simple rules — including the ko rule, which forbids immediately recreating the previous board position — generate enormous strategic depth: the number of legal positions vastly exceeds the number of atoms in the observable universe.

History and culture

Go originated in ancient China, where it was counted among the four essential arts of the scholar-gentleman, and spread to Korea and Japan, where professional systems, schools, and title tournaments flourished for centuries. In Japan the Edo-period "four houses" of Go shaped the classical game. Today the game is most popular in East Asia, with millions of players and professional leagues in China, Japan, and South Korea, alongside a growing international community.

Computer Go

Go's vast search space long resisted computer programs, and it was considered a grand challenge of artificial intelligence. In 2016, DeepMind's AlphaGo defeated top professional Lee Sedol 4–1 in a widely publicized match, and its successor systems surpassed the strongest humans entirely, revolutionizing professional play and study of the game.
