Point Nemo

Point Nemo lies in the South Pacific Ocean, more than 2,600 km from the nearest land.

Point Nemo, located at approximately 48°52.6′S 123°23.6′W, is the oceanic pole of inaccessibility: the place in the ocean that is farthest from land. It lies in the South Pacific Ocean, roughly 2,688 kilometres (about 1,670 miles) from the nearest lands — Ducie Island (part of the Pitcairn Islands) to the north, Motu Nui near Easter Island to the northeast, and Maher Island off Antarctica to the south.

Name and discovery

The point was calculated in 1992 by the Croatian-Canadian survey engineer Hrvoje Lukatela using geospatial software. It is named after Captain Nemo, the fictional submarine commander of Jules Verne's novel Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas; "nemo" is also Latin for "no one" or "nobody".

Significance

Because of its remoteness, Point Nemo is used as a "spacecraft cemetery": space agencies direct defunct satellites, cargo spacecraft, and space stations to re-enter the atmosphere there, minimizing the risk of debris falling on inhabited areas. More than 260 spacecraft have been disposed of in the region, including Russia's Mir station and numerous Progress cargo craft; the International Space Station is also planned to be deorbited there.

The nearest humans to Point Nemo are often the astronauts aboard the International Space Station, which passes overhead at an altitude of about 400 km — far closer than any person on Earth's surface. The area is also biologically quiet, lying within the South Pacific Gyre, far from nutrient-rich waters.
