Gustavo Gutiérrez

Gutiérrez, founder of liberation theology, in his native Peru.

Gustavo Gutiérrez Merino (8 June 1928 – 22 October 2024) was a Peruvian philosopher, Catholic theologian and Dominican priest, widely regarded as the founder of liberation theology. His book A Theology of Liberation (1971; Spanish: Teología de la liberación) argued that Christian faith requires a "preferential option for the poor" and the transformation of unjust social structures.

Life and thought

Born in Lima of mixed Quechua and Spanish descent, Gutiérrez suffered osteomyelitis as a youth and was often bedridden. He studied medicine and philosophy in Peru and theology in Lyon and Rome, reading Marx, Freud and the nouvelle théologie. Ordained in 1959, he served poor parishes in Lima and taught at the Pontifical Catholic University of Peru. At the 1968 Medellín conference of Latin American bishops he helped articulate the Church's turn toward the poor.

Legacy

Liberation theology spread across Latin America and influenced theologies of struggle worldwide; it was criticised by the Vatican's Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith in the 1980s for its use of Marxist analysis, though Gutiérrez himself was never condemned and was later honoured by Pope Francis. He received numerous honours, including the Princess of Asturias Award (2003, with no — shared recognition) and membership of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
