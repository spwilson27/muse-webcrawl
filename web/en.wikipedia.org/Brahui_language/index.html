Brahui (براہوئی) is a Dravidian language spoken mainly in the central parts of Balochistan, Pakistan, with smaller communities in Afghanistan and Iran. It is remarkable as a northern outlier of the Dravidian family, separated by more than 1,500 km from the Dravidian languages of South India.

Origins

The presence of a Dravidian language in the northwest has been used as evidence for a formerly wider distribution of Dravidian languages, possibly connected with the Indus Valley civilisation, although some scholars have proposed a later migration of Brahui speakers from central India. Brahui has been heavily influenced by neighbouring Iranian languages, especially Balochi, from which it has borrowed much vocabulary; many Brahui speakers are bilingual in Balochi.

Linguistic features

Brahui shares core Dravidian features—agglutinative morphology, inclusive/exclusive pronouns, and a decimal numeral base—but its numerals above three are borrowed from Balochi. It has lost the Dravidian retroflex series contrasts in some dialects. The language is traditionally oral; it is written in an extended Perso-Arabic script, and a Latin-based orthography (Brolikva) was developed in the 2000s.

Status

Brahui is considered vulnerable, as many younger speakers shift to Balochi or Urdu. Literary activity, newspapers, and radio broadcasts in Brahui are centred on Quetta, where the University of Balochistan hosts a Brahui department.
