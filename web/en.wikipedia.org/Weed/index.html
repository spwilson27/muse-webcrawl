Weed

A weed is a plant considered undesirable in a particular situation, growing where it conflicts with human preferences, needs, or goals. Weeds are commonly unwanted in gardens, lawns, agricultural fields and other human-controlled settings. The term is applied to any plant that grows or reproduces aggressively, or is invasive outside its native environment. There is no botanical category of weeds: a plant that is a weed in one context may be a valued crop or wildflower in another.

Characteristics

Weeds typically produce abundant seed that persists in the soil seed bank, germinate rapidly after disturbance, grow quickly, and reproduce both sexually and vegetatively through rhizomes and stolons. Familiar examples include the dandelion, crabgrass, chickweed, plantain, kudzu and Japanese knotweed.

Control

Weed control methods include hand-pulling, hoeing, mulching, mowing, crop rotation, cover crops and the application of herbicides. Some weeds have become resistant to multiple herbicides, a growing problem in agriculture. Many weeds are also beneficial: they protect bare soil from erosion, provide pollen and nectar for insects, and some, such as purslane and lamb's quarters, are edible.

Invasive species

When introduced outside their native ranges, some weeds become invasive species that displace native plants and alter ecosystems, causing major economic and environmental damage.
