Chugach Mountains

Location: South-central Alaska, United States

Highest point: Mount Marcus Baker, 4,016 m

Length: About 500 km

The Chugach Mountains form a rugged arc in south-central Alaska, extending from the Kenai Peninsula and Turnagain Arm eastward toward the Saint Elias Mountains. They separate Anchorage and the Matanuska-Susitna lowlands from Prince William Sound and the Copper River region.

Geography and climate

Heavy Pacific storms bring deep snow and sustain extensive glaciers, including the Columbia, Matanuska, and Knik glaciers. Steep fjords, alpine valleys, and coastal rainforest characterize the range. Much of it lies within Chugach National Forest and Chugach State Park.

Recreation and ecology

The mountains support salmon streams, moose, bears, Dall sheep, and mountain goats, and are popular for hiking, skiing, climbing, and mountaineering close to Anchorage.
