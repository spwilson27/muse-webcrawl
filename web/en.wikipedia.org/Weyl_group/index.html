Weyl group

Reflections generating the Weyl group of a root system

In mathematics, in particular the theory of Lie algebras, the Weyl group of a root system Φ is a subgroup of the isometry group of that root system. Specifically, it is the subgroup generated by reflections through the hyperplanes orthogonal to the roots, and as such is a finite reflection group. In fact it turns out that most finite reflection groups are Weyl groups. Abstractly, Weyl groups are finite Coxeter groups and are important examples thereof.

Definition and examples

The Weyl group of a semisimple Lie group, a semisimple Lie algebra, or a linear algebraic group is the Weyl group of the group's root system. For the special linear group SLn, the Weyl group is the symmetric group Sn, permuting the diagonal entries. For the root system of type A2, the Weyl group is the dihedral group of order 6 — the symmetry group of an equilateral triangle.

Structure

The Weyl group is generated by the reflections sα associated with the simple roots, subject to Coxeter relations determined by the Cartan matrix; it is therefore a finite Coxeter group with a presentation depending only on the Dynkin diagram. Elements of the Weyl group have a well-defined length, and there is a unique longest element. The group can equivalently be defined as the quotient of the normaliser of a maximal torus by its centraliser, N(T)/Z(T).

Applications

Weyl groups play a central role in representation theory: the Weyl character formula expresses the characters of irreducible representations of a semisimple Lie algebra in terms of the group, and the Weyl dimension formula gives their dimensions. They also appear in the theory of algebraic groups, Hecke algebras, and the geometry of flag varieties, where the Bruhat decomposition is indexed by Weyl group elements. The groups are named after Hermann Weyl.
