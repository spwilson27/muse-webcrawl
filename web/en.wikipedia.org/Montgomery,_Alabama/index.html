Montgomery is the capital city of the US state of Alabama and the seat of Montgomery County. Standing on the Alabama River, it played a central role both in the founding of the Confederacy and, a century later, in the American civil rights movement.

The Alabama State Capitol in Montgomery.

History

Montgomery was incorporated in 1819, named for Revolutionary War general Richard Montgomery, and became Alabama's capital in 1846. In February 1861 delegates from the seceding Southern states met there and formed the Confederate States of America; Jefferson Davis was inaugurated on the steps of the state capitol, making Montgomery the first capital of the Confederacy before the seat moved to Richmond, Virginia.

Civil rights

In 1955–56, Montgomery became the focus of the Montgomery bus boycott, sparked by Rosa Parks's arrest for refusing to give up her bus seat; the young Martin Luther King Jr., pastor at the Dexter Avenue Baptist Church, emerged as the boycott's leader, and the protest ended with a Supreme Court ruling desegregating public transport. In 1965 the Selma to Montgomery marches culminated at the capitol. The National Memorial for Peace and Justice, opened in 2018, commemorates victims of lynching.

Today

State government, Maxwell Air Force Base and automobile manufacturing, including a large Hyundai plant, are major employers. Sites include the Rosa Parks Museum, the Alabama State Capitol and the First White House of the Confederacy.
