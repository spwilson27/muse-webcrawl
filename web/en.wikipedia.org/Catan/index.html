Catan, originally titled The Settlers of Catan, is a multiplayer board game designed by Klaus Teuber and first published in Germany in 1995 by Franckh-Kosmos Verlag. Players assume the roles of settlers on the fictional island of Catan, building settlements, cities, and roads while acquiring and trading resources. The game is credited with launching the modern "Eurogame" boom and popularising designer board games outside Germany.

Gameplay

The island is built from hexagonal terrain tiles producing five resources: lumber, brick, wool, grain, and ore. At the start of each turn a player rolls two dice; terrain hexes bearing the rolled number produce resources for adjacent settlements and cities. Players spend resources to build roads, settlements, and cities and to buy development cards; they may trade resources with each other or with the bank. A roll of seven activates the robber, which blocks a hex's production and lets a player steal a resource. Victory points are awarded for settlements (1), cities (2), the Longest Road and Largest Army bonuses, and certain development cards; the first player to reach ten points wins.

Expansions and versions

Major expansions include Seafarers (1997), adding ships and island chains; Cities & Knights (1998), adding barbarian invasions and city improvements; Traders & Barbarians (2007); and Explorers & Pirates (2013). The game has also spawned card games, dice games, digital editions, and themed versions.

Reception and impact

Catan won the Spiel des Jahres in 1995 and has sold tens of millions of copies worldwide in dozens of languages. Its combination of modular board, resource production driven by probability, and negotiation-based trading has influenced countless later games, and it is frequently cited as a "gateway game" that introduced board gaming to a mass audience.
