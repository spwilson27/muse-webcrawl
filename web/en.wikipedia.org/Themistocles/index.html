Themistocles

Themistocles (c. 524 – c. 459 BC) was an Athenian politician and general, and one of the most important figures of the Greco-Persian Wars. A populist leader who rose from non-aristocratic origins, he persuaded Athens to build a powerful navy, which proved decisive in the defence of Greece against the Persian Empire of Xerxes I.

Career

As archon and leading statesman in the 480s BC, Themistocles used silver revenues from the mines at Laurium to construct a fleet of triremes, ostensibly for war against Aegina but in reality against Persia. In 480 BC he engineered the evacuation of Athens and lured the Persian fleet into the narrow straits of Salamis, where the Greek navy won a decisive victory at the Battle of Salamis.

Exile and death

After the war, Themistocles rebuilt Athens' walls despite Spartan objections, but his power waned and he was ostracized around 471 BC. Accused of treason, he fled Greece and eventually entered the service of the Persian king Artaxerxes I, who made him governor of Magnesia in Asia Minor, where he died.
