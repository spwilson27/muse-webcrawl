Alchon Huns

Active: c. 400 – 670 CE
Regions: Bactria, Gandhara, northern India
Notable rulers: Khingila, Toramana, Mihirakula

The Alchon Huns, also known as the Alkhan, Alchono, or Walxon, were a nomadic people who established states in Central Asia and South Asia during the 4th and 6th centuries CE. They were one of the main groups of the so-called "Iranian Huns", alongside the Kidarites, Hephthalites, and Nezak. The name Alchon derives from the Bactrian legend alxanno on their coinage.

History

The Alchons first appeared south of the Hindu Kush around 400 CE, displacing the Kidarites in Gandhara. Under King Khingila (c. 430–490), they consolidated power in the region. In the early 6th century, Alchon rulers Toramana and his son Mihirakula invaded northern India, penetrating as far as Gwalior and Malwa and contributing to the decline of the Gupta Empire. Mihirakula was defeated around 528 CE by a coalition associated with the Aulikara king Yashodharman and the Gupta emperor Narasimhagupta, after which Alchon power receded to Kashmir and Gandhara.

Coinage and culture

Alchon coinage imitated Sasanian and Gupta types, marked by a distinctive tamga (symbol) and portraits that often display artificially elongated skulls — a practice of cranial deformation shared with other Hunnic groups. Their coins later influenced the coinage of Kashmir and early medieval northern India.

Legacy

After the collapse of their Indian empire, Alchon groups lingered in the northwest for another century. They are sometimes linked to later peoples of the region, and their invasions mark a turning point between ancient and early medieval Indian history.
