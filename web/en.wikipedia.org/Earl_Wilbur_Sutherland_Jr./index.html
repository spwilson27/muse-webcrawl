Earl W. Sutherland Jr.

Earl Wilbur Sutherland Jr.
Born: 19 November 1915, Burlingame, Kansas, U.S.
Died: 9 March 1974, Miami, Florida, U.S.
Known for: Discovery of cyclic AMP and the second messenger concept
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (1971)

Earl Wilbur Sutherland Jr. (November 19, 1915 – March 9, 1974) was an American pharmacologist and biochemist. He won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1971 "for his discoveries concerning the mechanisms of the action of hormones", centred on his discovery of cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cyclic AMP) and its role as a "second messenger" in cells.

Sutherland discovered cyclic AMP, the archetypal second messenger.

Research

Working at Case Western Reserve University in the 1950s, Sutherland studied how the hormones adrenaline (epinephrine) and glucagon stimulate the breakdown of glycogen in the liver. He showed that the hormones do not enter the cell but instead trigger the formation inside the cell of a heat-stable compound, cyclic AMP, which then carries the signal onward — the origin of the "second messenger" concept, in which the hormone is the first messenger. His collaborator Theodore Rall helped isolate and characterise cyclic AMP.

Career

Sutherland received his MD from Washington University in St. Louis in 1942. After wartime service, he held posts at Washington University and Case Western Reserve, moving in 1963 to Vanderbilt University, where he remained until 1973, when he joined the University of Miami Medical School. In addition to the Nobel Prize, he received the Albert Lasker Award for Basic Medical Research (1970) and the National Medal of Science (1973).
