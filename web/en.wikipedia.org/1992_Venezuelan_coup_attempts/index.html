1992 Venezuelan coup attempts

The 1992 Venezuelan coup attempts were two failed coups d'état against the government of President Carlos Andrés Pérez in 1992. The first, on 4 February, was led by members of the Movimiento Bolivariano Revolucionario-200 (MBR-200), including Lt. Col. Hugo Chávez. A second attempt followed on 27 November, led by a coalition of military officers and civilians. Both failed, but the February coup made Chávez a nationally known figure and laid the groundwork for his election as president in 1998.

Background

Pérez's second presidency (from 1989) was marked by an IMF-backed austerity programme ("El Gran Viraje") whose fare increases triggered the Caracazo riots of February–March 1989, in which hundreds were killed. Corruption, declining oil revenues, and public anger at the political establishment eroded the legitimacy of the two-party system. Within the army, Chávez and fellow officers had founded the clandestine MBR-200 in the early 1980s, dedicated to refounding the republic on Bolivarian principles.

February coup (4 February 1992)

Rebel units seized positions in Caracas, Maracaibo, Valencia, and Maracay, and Chávez's paratroopers attempted to capture Pérez, who escaped to a television studio and rallied loyal forces. Fighting left at least 143 dead by official figures, possibly several hundred. Allowed to address the nation on television to order other rebels to surrender, Chávez assumed responsibility and declared that his movement had failed "por ahora" ("for now") — a phrase that made him a folk hero. He and his fellow officers were imprisoned.

November coup (27 November 1992)

A second group of officers, joined by civilian organisations, seized the state television channel and air bases; rebel aircraft bombed government buildings in Caracas before loyalist forces prevailed. Around 170 people were killed. Hundreds of participants fled to Peru, where President Alberto Fujimori granted them asylum.

Aftermath

The coups accelerated the collapse of Venezuela's old party system. Pérez was impeached and removed in 1993 over a corruption scandal. President Rafael Caldera pardoned Chávez and the other February rebels in 1994; Chávez founded the Fifth Republic Movement and won the December 1998 presidential election, beginning a transformation of Venezuelan politics that continued with the 1999 constitution.
