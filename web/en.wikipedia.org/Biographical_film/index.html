Biographical film

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A biographical film, or biopic, is a film that dramatizes the life of a real person or people. Such films show the life of a historical person and the person's real name is used. They differ from historical drama films in that they focus on a single person's story rather than an era or event, although the two genres often overlap.

Characteristics

Biopics typically compress a life into a conventional narrative arc — often the "rise and fall" of a public figure — and select episodes that define the subject's public significance. Casting is crucial: actors are frequently praised for physical resemblance and impersonation, and performances in biopics have won many Academy Awards for acting, including portrayals of figures such as Abraham Lincoln, Mahatma Gandhi, Ray Charles, Margaret Thatcher and Freddie Mercury.

Accuracy and controversy

Because they dramatise, biopics routinely alter chronology, merge characters or invent dialogue. Historians and subjects' families sometimes object to inaccuracies or unflattering portrayals, while filmmakers defend such changes as necessary licence. The degree of fidelity varies widely, from authorised, estate-approved portraits to deliberately speculative works.

Notable examples

Classic and modern biopics include The Story of Louis Pasteur (1936), Lawrence of Arabia (1962), Gandhi (1982), Amadeus (1984), Schindler's List (1993), A Beautiful Mind (2001), The Social Network (2010), 12 Years a Slave (2013), Bohemian Rhapsody (2018) and Oppenheimer (2023). The genre remains one of the most commercially and critically prominent forms of prestige filmmaking.
