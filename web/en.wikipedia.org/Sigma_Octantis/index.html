Sigma Octantis

Sigma Octantis is a faint star in the southern constellation of Octans. It is the southern pole star, lying roughly one degree from the south celestial pole, and serves as the southern counterpart to Polaris. With an apparent magnitude of about 5.4, it is only just visible to the naked eye under dark skies.

Characteristics

Sigma Octantis is a yellow-white giant star of spectral type F0 III, located about 294 light-years from Earth. It has roughly twice the mass of the Sun and is modestly variable, classified as a Delta Scuti variable with a small amplitude and a period of a few hours.

As the pole star

Because of its faintness, Sigma Octantis is of limited use for navigation; navigators in the southern hemisphere more often locate the south celestial pole using the Southern Cross and the pointer stars Alpha and Beta Centauri. The star appears on the flag of Brazil, where it represents the Federal District.
