Whipsnade Zoo, formerly known as ZSL Whipsnade Zoo, is a zoo and safari park located at Whipsnade, near Dunstable in Bedfordshire, England. Covering about 600 acres on the Chiltern Hills escarpment, it is the United Kingdom's largest zoo by area and one of the largest wildlife conservation parks in Europe.

History

The zoo was established by the Zoological Society of London under its secretary Peter Chalmers Mitchell, who envisioned a park where animals could be kept in spacious paddocks rather than the cages of the society's London Zoo at Regent's Park. The site at Hall Farm, Whipsnade, was purchased cheaply during the agricultural depression, and the zoo opened to the public on 23 May 1931. During the Second World War it received animals evacuated from London Zoo.

Animals and attractions

Whipsnade is known for large free-ranging herds — including white rhinoceros, Asian elephants, giraffes, zebras, and deer in the drive-through Passage Through Asia — as well as lions, tigers, Amur leopards, brown bears, cheetahs, penguins, red pandas, and a butterfly house. The Great Whipsnade Railway, a narrow-gauge railway, carries visitors around the park; the zoo's chalk lion, cut into the hillside in 1933, is a local landmark visible for miles. The zoo plays a significant role in ZSL's international breeding and conservation programmes, including the conservation breeding of species extinct in the wild such as the scimitar-horned oryx (housed at the zoo historically) and Père David's deer.
