Land Rover is a British brand of predominantly four-wheel drive, off-road capable vehicles, owned by Jaguar Land Rover, a subsidiary of India's Tata Motors. The name originated with the Land Rover utility vehicle launched by the Rover Company in 1948, inspired by the wartime Jeep and designed for agricultural and light industrial use with an aluminium alloy body and a simple steel chassis.

History

The original Land Rover, designed by Maurice Wilks, was unveiled at the Amsterdam Motor Show in 1948. Intended as a stopgap to generate exports and use surplus aluminium after the Second World War, it proved so successful that it outlasted the Rover car range. Successive Series I, II, and III models became ubiquitous in agriculture, the military, and expeditions worldwide. In 1970 the upmarket Range Rover introduced coil-spring comfort to the formula, founding the luxury SUV segment. The Discovery followed in 1989 and the Freelander in 1997.

Ownership

Land Rover passed through British Leyland, became a standalone company, and was acquired by BMW in 1994, then by Ford in 2000, which combined it with Jaguar. Tata Motors bought Jaguar Land Rover from Ford in 2008. Under the company's later "House of Brands" strategy, Range Rover, Defender, and Discovery were promoted as distinct brands within the Land Rover family.

Models and legacy

The long-running Defender, descended directly from the 1948 original, ended production in 2016 after 68 years and was succeeded by a completely redesigned model in 2020. Land Rovers have served with armed forces around the world and are strongly associated with exploration and safari use; the marque is regularly cited as an icon of British industrial design.
