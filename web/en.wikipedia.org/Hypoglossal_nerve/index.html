Hypoglossal nerve

The hypoglossal nerve, also known as the twelfth cranial nerve, cranial nerve XII, or simply CN XII, is a cranial nerve that innervates all the extrinsic and intrinsic muscles of the tongue except for the palatoglossus, which is innervated by the vagus nerve. It is a purely motor nerve. The name comes from the Greek hypo (under) and glossa (tongue).

Course

The nerve arises from the hypoglossal nucleus in the medulla oblongata, emerges between the pyramid and the olive, and exits the skull through the hypoglossal canal in the occipital bone. It descends in the neck, loops around the occipital artery, passes deep to the digastric muscle, and runs forward on the surface of the hyoglossus to reach the tongue muscles: the styloglossus, hyoglossus, genioglossus and the intrinsic tongue musculature.

Clinical significance

Damage to the nerve causes weakness and wasting of the tongue on the same side. A classic clinical test is to ask the patient to protrude the tongue: with a lower motor neuron lesion it deviates toward the side of the lesion, because the intact genioglossus pushes it across ("the tongue licks its wounds"). Causes include tumours, stroke (medial medullary syndrome), carotid surgery complications and motor neuron disease.
