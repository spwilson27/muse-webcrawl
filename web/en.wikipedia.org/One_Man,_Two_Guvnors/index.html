One Man, Two Guvnors

Written byRichard Bean
Based onThe Servant of Two Masters by Carlo Goldoni (1743)
Premiere2011, National Theatre, London
SettingBrighton, 1963

One Man, Two Guvnors is a comedy play by Richard Bean, an English adaptation of Carlo Goldoni's 1743 commedia dell'arte classic The Servant of Two Masters. It relocates the action to Brighton in 1963, amid small-time gangsters, skiffle music and the world just before the Beatles. The play premiered at the National Theatre in London in May 2011, directed by Nicholas Hytner, and became an international hit.

Plot

Francis Henshall, a perpetually hungry failed skiffle musician, takes two jobs at once: minding Roscoe Crabbe — in fact Roscoe's twin sister Rachel in disguise — and working for toff Stanley Stubbers, who has killed the real Roscoe and is Rachel's lover. Francis must keep each "guvnor" ignorant of the other while engineering meals for himself, producing escalating farce: a chaotic dinner service in which he is assisted by the ancient waiter Alfie, beatings, mistaken identities and direct audience participation, including the famous (seemingly improvised) business with an audience member's sandwich.

Productions and reception

James Corden's performance as Francis won him the Tony Award for Best Actor when the production transferred to Broadway in 2012; the play also transferred to the West End with other casts, toured widely, and was licensed for hundreds of productions. In April 2020 a recording of the original production streamed free as the first National Theatre at Home release during the COVID-19 lockdowns, reaching millions of viewers and reviving interest in the show worldwide.
