The Australia men's national rugby union team, nicknamed the Wallabies, represents Australia in men's international rugby union. The team is one of the sport's traditional powers and has won the Rugby World Cup twice, in 1991 and 1999 — the first nation to win the tournament twice.

History

Australia played its first Test match in 1899 against the touring British Lions in Sydney, winning 13–3. The team toured Britain in 1908–09, where the nickname "Wallabies" was coined. Australia competes annually in The Rugby Championship against New Zealand, South Africa and Argentina, and contests the Bledisloe Cup with New Zealand.

World Cup record

The Wallabies won the 1991 World Cup in England, defeating England in the final at Twickenham, and the 1999 tournament in Wales, beating France in the final. They were runners-up in 2003, losing to England in extra time in Sydney, and again in 2015.

Colours

Australia plays in gold jerseys with green trim, with the wallaby as its emblem.
