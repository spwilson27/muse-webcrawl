Polarity item

FieldLinguistics, formal semantics
TypesNegative polarity items (NPIs), positive polarity items (PPIs)
Examplesany, ever, yet, lift a finger (NPI)

In linguistics, a polarity item is a lexical item that is sensitive to the polarity of its environment, occurring only in certain contexts. Negative polarity items (NPIs) such as any, ever, and at all appear in negative and related environments ("I didn't see anyone") but not in simple affirmative sentences ("*I saw anyone"). Positive polarity items show the opposite distribution.

Licensing

The environments that license NPIs include negation, questions, conditionals, comparatives, and other downward-entailing contexts. The theory of licensing, notably developed by Gilles Fauconnier and William Ladusaw, links NPI distribution to the semantic property of downward entailment: the reasoning patterns valid in those contexts mirror the behaviour of negative sentences.

Examples across languages

Many languages have rich polarity systems: Dutch hoeven, German jemals, Greek emphatic NPIs, and Japanese indeterminate pronouns with particles. Idioms such as English "lift a finger" and "budge an inch" act as minimizers and are classic NPIs.
