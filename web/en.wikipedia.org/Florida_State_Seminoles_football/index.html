Florida State Seminoles football

The Florida State Seminoles football team represents Florida State University (FSU) in the sport of American football. The Seminoles compete in the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) and the Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC). They play their home games at Doak Campbell Stadium in Tallahassee, Florida.

History

Florida State fielded early teams in 1902–1904 before the school became a women's college. Football returned in 1947 when the institution again admitted men. The program rose to national prominence under head coach Bobby Bowden, who coached from 1976 to 2009. Bowden's teams finished in the top five of the AP poll for a record 14 consecutive seasons (1987–2000) and won national championships in 1993 and 1999.

Championships

Jimbo Fisher, Bowden's successor, led the Seminoles to a third national title in the 2013 season, defeating Auburn in the BCS National Championship Game behind Heisman Trophy-winning quarterback Jameis Winston. Florida State has also produced Heisman Trophy winners Charlie Ward (1993) and Chris Weinke (2000).

Traditions

The team's symbols include Chief Osceola and his horse Renegade, who plant a flaming spear at midfield before home games — a tradition conducted with the blessing of the Seminole Tribe of Florida, which has officially supported the university's use of the Seminoles name. The "Tomahawk Chop" chant is a hallmark of the fan base.
