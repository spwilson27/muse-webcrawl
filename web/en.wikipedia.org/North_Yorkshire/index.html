North Yorkshire is a ceremonial county in Northern England. It is the largest county in England by area, covering much of the historic North Riding of Yorkshire. The county includes two national parks — the Yorkshire Dales and the North York Moors — and cities and towns including York, Harrogate, Scarborough, and Middlesbrough.

Geography

The county stretches from the North Sea coast in the east to the Pennine hills in the west. The Vale of York forms a fertile lowland between the limestone and gritstone uplands of the Dales and the heather moorland of the North York Moors. Major rivers include the Swale, Ure, Nidd, Wharfe, and Tees. Coastal resorts such as Whitby and Scarborough line the eastern shore.

History

North Yorkshire was created as a non-metropolitan county in 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, largely from the North Riding with parts of the East and West Ridings. In 1996 York became a separate unitary authority, and in 2023 the county's districts were merged into a single unitary North Yorkshire Council, while the ceremonial county also embraces York, Middlesbrough, and parts of Stockton-on-Tees and Redcar and Cleveland.

Economy and culture

Tourism, agriculture, and food production are major employers, alongside public services and, around York and Harrogate, finance, science, and conference industries. Attractions include York Minster, Fountains Abbey, Castle Howard, the North Yorkshire Moors Railway, and the Wensleydale cheese creamery. The Tour de France's Grand Départ passed through the county in 2014.
