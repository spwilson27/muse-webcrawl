Jerusalem is a city in the Southern Levant, situated on a plateau in the Judaean Mountains between the Mediterranean and the Dead Sea. One of the oldest cities in the world, it is considered holy to the three major Abrahamic religions — Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Its Old City contains the Western Wall and Temple Mount, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, and the Dome of the Rock and Al-Aqsa Mosque.

History

Jerusalem was first settled in the 4th millennium BCE. According to the Hebrew Bible, King David conquered the city around 1000 BCE and his son Solomon built the First Temple; the Babylonians destroyed it in 586 BCE, and a Second Temple stood until the Romans destroyed it in 70 CE. The city was subsequently ruled by Byzantine, early Islamic, Crusader, Ayyubid, Mamluk and Ottoman powers. After Ottoman rule ended in 1917, Jerusalem came under the British Mandate.

Under the 1947 UN Partition Plan the city was to be internationalized, but the 1948 war divided it: Israel held the west, Jordan the east including the Old City. Israel captured East Jerusalem in the Six-Day War of 1967 and later annexed it — a move not recognised by most of the international community. Israel regards the whole city as its capital, while Palestinians claim East Jerusalem as the capital of a future Palestinian state; the city's final status remains one of the central issues of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

Religious significance

For Jews, Jerusalem is the site of the Temples and the direction of prayer; the Western Wall is a surviving retaining wall of the Temple Mount plaza. For Christians, it is where Jesus was crucified and resurrected; the Church of the Holy Sepulchre enshrines those sites. For Muslims, the Al-Aqsa compound is the third-holiest site in Islam, associated with Muhammad's Night Journey, and the Dome of the Rock (built 691–692 CE) is its landmark shrine.

Geography and demography

The city lies about 60 km east of Tel Aviv and the Mediterranean. Its population of roughly one million is approximately 60% Jewish and 38% Palestinian Arab, divided among distinct neighbourhoods; the walled Old City, covering under a square kilometre, is divided into Muslim, Christian, Armenian and Jewish quarters.
