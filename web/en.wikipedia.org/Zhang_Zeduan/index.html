Zhang Zeduan (Chinese: 張擇端; 1085–1145), courtesy name Zhengdao, was a Chinese painter of the transition from the Northern Song to the Southern Song dynasty. He is best known as the creator of the masterpiece Along the River During the Qingming Festival, one of the most celebrated works in Chinese art.

Life

Little is recorded of Zhang's life. He was born in Dongwu in modern Shandong, studied in the capital Kaifeng, and is believed to have served as a court painter under Emperor Huizong, specialising in jiehua — the precise, ruler-drawn depiction of architecture — and in lively scenes of boats, bridges and city life.

Along the River During the Qingming Festival

His famous handscroll, over five metres long, depicts in panoramic detail the daily life of people from all classes in the Northern Song capital Bianjing (Kaifeng) during the Qingming Festival — from the countryside and river traffic to crowded markets, restaurants, and the great Rainbow Bridge. The original scroll is now held by the Palace Museum in Beijing and is rarely displayed because of its fragility and immense value. Numerous later copies and reinterpretations were made, including a famous Qing dynasty version in Taipei.
