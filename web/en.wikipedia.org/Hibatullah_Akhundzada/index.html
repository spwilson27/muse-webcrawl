Hibatullah Akhundzada

Hibatullah Akhundzada (born c. 1961) is an Afghan Islamic scholar and cleric who has served as the supreme leader of the Taliban since 2016 and as head of state of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan since the Taliban's return to power in August 2021. Known by the title Amir al-Mu'minin ("Commander of the Faithful"), he rules largely from Kandahar and rarely appears in public.

Background and rise

Akhundzada, an ethnic Pashtun from Panjwayi District in Kandahar Province, served as a judge in the Taliban's sharia courts during their 1996–2001 regime and later headed the movement's judicial system. He was appointed leader in May 2016 after his predecessor, Akhtar Mansour, was killed in a US drone strike.

Rule

As supreme leader he has issued decrees enforcing the Taliban's interpretation of Islamic law, including bans on girls' secondary education and most women's employment, which have drawn international condemnation and kept Afghanistan's government unrecognized by most states. See also Afghanistan.
