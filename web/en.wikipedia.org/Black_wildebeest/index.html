The black wildebeest or white-tailed gnu (Connochaetes gnou) is one of two wildebeest species, native to the southern African plains. It is a large antelope of the family Bovidae, distinguished by its dark brown to black coat, white "paintbrush" tail, forward-curving horns, and an erect mane.

Description

Adults stand about 111–121 cm at the shoulder and weigh 110–180 kg. Both sexes bear horns that curve forward and then upward, reaching up to 78 cm in males. The species is a grazer of short grasses on the highveld and Karoo plains.

Conservation history

Once abundant across South Africa's central plateau, the black wildebeest was hunted almost to extinction in the 19th century, surviving only on a few private farms. Careful protection and reintroduction have restored the population to well over 15,000 animals, and it is now classified as Least Concern, though nearly all individuals live on private or protected land close to the areas where the species historically occurred.
