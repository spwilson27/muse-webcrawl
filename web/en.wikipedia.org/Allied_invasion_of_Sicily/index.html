Allied invasion of Sicily

The Allied invasion of Sicily, codenamed Operation Husky, was a major campaign of the Second World War in which British and American forces invaded and captured the Italian island of Sicily from Axis forces. It began on the night of 9–10 July 1943 and lasted until 17 August 1943, opening the Italian campaign and contributing to the fall of Benito Mussolini.

The operation

The invasion force comprised the US Seventh Army under George S. Patton and the British Eighth Army under Bernard Montgomery, supported by the largest amphibious and airborne operation of the war up to that point, with some 150,000 troops landed on the first day. A celebrated deception, Operation Mincemeat — in which a corpse carrying false documents was floated ashore in Spain — helped convince the Axis that the invasion would strike Sardinia or Greece instead.

Outcome

Allied forces advanced across the island in a race between Patton's westward-and-north drive through Palermo and Montgomery's advance up the east coast toward Messina. Although the Allies took Sicily in 38 days, much of the German and Italian garrison escaped across the Strait of Messina to mainland Italy. The campaign led to Mussolini's removal on 25 July 1943 and Italy's armistice in September, though Germany occupied much of the country and fighting continued until 1945.
