Psyche (mythology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Psyche (/ˈsaɪkiː/; Greek: Ψυχή, romanized: Psykhḗ, meaning "soul" or "breath of life") is the Greek goddess of the soul and often depicted as a beautiful woman with butterfly wings. Born a mortal princess whose extraordinary beauty provoked the jealousy of Aphrodite, Psyche is best known from the story of Cupid and Psyche, told in the Metamorphoses (The Golden Ass) of the 2nd-century CE Roman writer Apuleius — the only source for the full tale, although the pairing of Eros (Cupid) and Psyche appears in Greek art centuries earlier.

The myth of Cupid and Psyche

Aphrodite, enraged that mortals worshipped Psyche's beauty instead of hers, ordered her son Cupid to make Psyche fall in love with a monster; instead, Cupid fell in love with her himself. By oracle, Psyche was carried by the West Wind to a magnificent palace where an unseen husband visited her only at night, forbidding her to look at him. Provoked by her jealous sisters, she lit a lamp while he slept, discovered the god, and woke him with a drop of burning oil — whereupon he fled.

Searching for her lost husband, Psyche submitted to Aphrodite, who imposed four impossible tasks: sorting a huge heap of mixed grains (done with the help of ants), gathering golden wool from violent rams, filling a vial with water from the Styx (aided by an eagle), and descending to the underworld to bring back a box of Persephone's beauty. Psyche completed the tasks with supernatural help, though opening the box on her return plunged her into a deathlike sleep. Cupid revived her, appealed to Zeus, and the gods granted Psyche immortality; she and Cupid were married on Olympus, and their daughter was Pleasure (Voluptas).

Interpretation and legacy

The tale is often read as an allegory: Psyche ("soul") must pass through trials and suffering to be united with Eros ("love") and attain immortality. It is a classic example of the folktale type of the search for the lost husband. The goddess Psyche's name gave rise to the word "psyche" for the human soul and mind, and hence to psychology and related terms. In art, Psyche with butterfly wings — the butterfly being a symbol of the soul — became a widespread motif, and the story inspired works by Raphael, Canova, Thorvaldsen, and many later artists, as well as retellings such as C. S. Lewis's novel Till We Have Faces.
