Transport in Singapore

Transport in Singapore is dominated by a comprehensive public transport system complemented by extensive road infrastructure and one of the world's busiest ports and airports. Land transport policy, overseen by the Land Transport Authority, strongly manages car ownership through the Certificate of Entitlement quota system and electronic road pricing.

Public transport

The Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) metro network and Light Rail Transit (LRT) feeders form the rail backbone, operated by SMRT and SBS Transit. An extensive bus network and a large taxi and private-hire fleet complete the system, unified by the EZ-Link and SimplyGo fare platforms.

Roads

Expressways ring and cross the island; the two causeway and bridge links to Malaysia (the Johor–Singapore Causeway and the Tuas Second Link) carry heavy cross-border traffic. Singapore pioneered congestion pricing with the Area Licensing Scheme in 1975, succeeded by Electronic Road Pricing gantries.

Air and sea

Changi Airport is repeatedly rated among the world's best airports and is the hub of Singapore Airlines. The Port of Singapore is one of the world's busiest transshipment container ports, anchoring Singapore's role as a global maritime hub.
