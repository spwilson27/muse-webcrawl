Ballets Russes

The Ballets Russes was an itinerant ballet company founded in 1909 by the Russian impresario Sergei Diaghilev, based in Paris. It revolutionized 20th-century dance by uniting leading choreographers, composers, dancers, and visual artists in collaborative productions.

Productions

Landmark works include Stravinsky's The Firebird (1910), Petrushka (1911), and The Rite of Spring (1913), whose premiere provoked a famous scandal. Other productions featured music by Debussy, Ravel, Prokofiev, and Satie, with designs by Bakst, Picasso, and Matisse.

Key figures

Choreographers included Michel Fokine, Vaslav Nijinsky, Léonide Massine, Bronislava Nijinska, and George Balanchine; star dancers included Anna Pavlova, Tamara Karsavina, and Nijinsky himself. The company dissolved after Diaghilev's death in 1929, but its influence shaped ballet companies worldwide.
