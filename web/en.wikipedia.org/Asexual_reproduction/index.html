Asexual reproduction

Type: Reproduction

Parents required: One

Offspring: Genetically (nearly) identical clones

Asexual reproduction is a type of reproduction that does not involve the fusion of gametes or change in the number of chromosomes. The offspring that arise by asexual reproduction from either unicellular or multicellular organisms inherit the full set of genes of their single parent and are therefore genetically identical to the parent, except for any mutations that have accumulated.

Types

  • Binary fission — the organism duplicates its genetic material and divides into two equal parts; typical of bacteria and many protists.
  • Budding — a new organism grows from an outgrowth of the parent, as in yeast and hydra.
  • Fragmentation — the body breaks into pieces, each of which can regenerate a complete organism, as in many sea stars and annelid worms.
  • Vegetative propagation — new plants grow from stems, roots, or leaves, as in strawberry runners and potato tubers.
  • Parthenogenesis — an embryo develops from an unfertilised egg, found in some invertebrates and a few vertebrates.
  • Sporogenesis — reproduction by spores, common in fungi, algae, and non-flowering plants.

Advantages and disadvantages

Asexual reproduction allows rapid population growth when conditions are favourable and requires no mate. Its chief disadvantage is a lack of genetic variation, which leaves a population vulnerable to disease and environmental change. Many organisms, such as aphids and numerous plants, alternate between asexual and sexual reproduction depending on conditions.
