Citizen Kane is a 1941 American drama film directed by, produced by, and starring Orson Welles, in his feature directorial debut. The quasi-biographical film examines the life and legacy of Charles Foster Kane, a publishing tycoon partly based on William Randolph Hearst, through the investigation of a reporter seeking the meaning of Kane's dying word, "Rosebud."

Plot and structure

The film pioneered a fractured, non-linear narrative: after a newsreel obituary, reporter Jerry Thompson interviews Kane's associates, whose flashbacks present conflicting portraits of the man — his rise from poverty to immense wealth and power, his failed political career, his two marriages, and his lonely death in his vast estate, Xanadu.

Production

Welles, aged 25, was given extraordinary creative control by RKO. Cinematographer Gregg Toland's deep-focus photography, Bernard Herrmann's score, innovative sound design, and dramatic low-angle compositions made the film a landmark of film technique.

Reception and legacy

Hearst's newspapers campaigned against the film, and it was a modest commercial disappointment on release, though it received nine Academy Award nominations and won for Best Original Screenplay. From the 1950s it was reappraised as a masterpiece; it topped the British Film Institute's Sight and Sound critics' poll five consecutive times (1962–2002) and is routinely cited as one of the greatest films ever made.
