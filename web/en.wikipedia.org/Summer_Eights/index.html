Summer Eights

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Crews racing in Summer Eights on the Isis in Oxford.

Summer Eights is a bumps race in rowing held annually in Oxford, England, in the week after Trinity term (usually late May), on the stretch of the River Thames known as the Isis. It is the principal inter-collegiate rowing competition of the University of Oxford in the summer, the counterpart to Torpids in Hilary term.

Format

College crews race in divisions over four consecutive days. Boats start lined up along the bank at intervals and chase the boat ahead, aiming to "bump" — physically touch or overlap — it before being bumped themselves; crews that bump move up the start order for the next day. A crew that bumps on all four days is awarded "blades"; a crew bumped every day receives the ironic "spoons". Overbumping — catching the boat two places ahead after the intervening boats have bumped out — is also possible.

Headship

The leading crew in the first division holds the Headship of the river. The competition, organised under Oxford University Rowing Clubs, dates to the early nineteenth century, with eights racing in Oxford recorded from 1815 and the bumps format codified in the decades that followed.

Culture

Eights Week is a major social occasion in the Oxford calendar, drawing crowds to the boathouses along the towpath, and concludes with celebrations by successful crews.
