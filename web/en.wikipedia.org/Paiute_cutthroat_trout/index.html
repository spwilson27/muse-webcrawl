Paiute cutthroat trout
Scientific nameOncorhynchus clarkii seleniris
Status (ESA)Threatened
Native rangeSilver King Creek, California

The Paiute cutthroat trout (Oncorhynchus clarkii seleniris) is one of the fourteen recognized subspecies of cutthroat trout. It is native only to Silver King Creek, a tributary of the Carson River in the Sierra Nevada of Alpine County, California, and is named for the Paiute people of the region.

Description

Paiute cutthroat are distinguished from other cutthroat trout by the near absence of spots on the body — most individuals have few or none — together with a purplish, iridescent sheen and the characteristic red-orange slash marks under the jaw that give cutthroat trout their name. Adults in the small mountain streams they inhabit rarely exceed 25 centimetres.

Conservation

The subspecies was nearly wiped out by hybridization with introduced rainbow trout and competition from stocked brook and brown trout, and was listed as endangered under early U.S. conservation law before being downlisted to threatened in 1975. Conservation efforts have involved chemically removing non-native fish above barriers such as Llewellyn Falls and reintroducing pure Paiute cutthroat from refuge populations that had been transplanted to fishless creeks. In 2023 the fish was restored to its entire historic habitat in Silver King Creek, allowing limited catch-and-release angling for the first time in over a century.
