Columbidae is the bird family comprising the pigeons and doves. The names "pigeon" and "dove" are used interchangeably, with "dove" often applied to smaller, slimmer species, but there is no scientific distinction. The family includes about 350 species found nearly worldwide, from the ubiquitous feral pigeon of cities to the crowned pigeons of New Guinea, the largest living members. The extinct dodo and Rodrigues solitaire, flightless birds of the Mascarene Islands, were also columbids, as was the passenger pigeon, hunted to extinction in 1914.

Characteristics

Columbids are stout-bodied birds with short necks, small heads and short, slender bills with a fleshy cere. Most feed on seeds, fruit and plants. Unusually among birds, both parents produce "crop milk" — a nutrient-rich secretion from the crop lining — to feed their nestlings ("squabs"). They also drink by suction, immersing the bill and drinking continuously without tilting the head back. Their flight is fast and direct, powered by large flight muscles, and many species are highly vocal with cooing calls.

Relationship with humans

The rock dove was domesticated thousands of years ago, giving rise to fancy breeds, racing pigeons and the homing pigeons used for messaging into the twentieth century. Doves are widespread symbols of peace and love. Several island species are threatened by habitat loss and introduced predators.
