Solanum

KingdomPlantae
OrderSolanales
FamilySolanaceae
GenusSolanum L.
SpeciesAbout 1,200–1,700

Solanum is a large and diverse genus of flowering plants, which include three food crops of high economic importance: the potato, the tomato and the eggplant (aubergine, brinjal). It is also the genus of the nightshades and of the bush tomatoes of Australia, and contains many species grown as ornamentals or used in traditional medicine. The genus was among those described by Carl Linnaeus in Species Plantarum (1753).

Description

Species of Solanum range from annual herbs to shrubs and small trees. The flowers are typically pentamerous with fused petals forming a star or bell shape and prominent yellow anthers that release pollen through apical pores (buzz pollination). Fruits are usually berries. Many species bear prickles and accumulate steroidal glycoalkaloids such as solanine, which render unripe fruit and foliage toxic.

Important species

Solanum tuberosum, the potato, is one of the world's most important food crops, domesticated in the Andes. Solanum lycopersicum, the tomato, originated in western South America and is now grown worldwide. Solanum melongena, the eggplant, was domesticated in Asia. Other notable species include S. nigrum (black nightshade), S. dulcamara (bittersweet), S. quitoense (naranjilla) and S. betaceum (tamarillo).

Taxonomy

With roughly 1,200 to 1,700 accepted and proposed species, Solanum is one of the largest genera of flowering plants. Molecular phylogenetic studies have led to the inclusion of formerly separate genera such as Lycopersicon (the tomatoes) and Cyphomandra within Solanum.
