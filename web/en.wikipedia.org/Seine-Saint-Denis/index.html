Seine-Saint-Denis

The Stade de France in Saint-Denis, the largest stadium in France, hosted events of the 2024 Paris Olympics.

Seine-Saint-Denis (French pronunciation: [sɛn sɛ̃ d(ə)ni]) is a department of France in the Île-de-France region, located immediately northeast of Paris. It is numbered 93 and colloquially known as "le neuf-trois". Its prefecture is Bobigny, and its largest communes include Saint-Denis, Montreuil and Aulnay-sous-Bois. Despite being one of the smallest departments by area (236 km²), it is densely populated, with about 1.7 million inhabitants.

History

The department was created in 1968, when the former Seine and Seine-et-Oise departments were broken up. It takes its name from the river Seine and from Saint Denis, the patron saint of France, whose basilica in Saint-Denis contains the necropolis of the French kings. The area industrialised heavily in the 19th and 20th centuries along the Canal Saint-Denis and the rail corridors, and received successive waves of immigration, making it one of the most ethnically diverse parts of France.

Economy and society

Seine-Saint-Denis is the poorest department in metropolitan France, with high unemployment and pockets of severe deprivation in some housing estates, yet it also hosts major economic poles: the Stade de France and the Saint-Denis business district, studios and media companies around La Plaine Saint-Denis, and logistics zones near Charles de Gaulle Airport. The department was a major site of the 2024 Summer Olympics, hosting the Olympic Village, aquatics centre and athletics at the Stade de France, and it is at the centre of the Grand Paris Express metro expansion, which is transforming its transport links.

Geography

The department is entirely urbanised as part of the Paris agglomeration. It is bordered by Paris to the southwest, Hauts-de-Seine, Val-de-Marne, Seine-et-Marne and Val-d'Oise. The Seine and the Marne touch its edges, and the Canal de l'Ourcq crosses it.
