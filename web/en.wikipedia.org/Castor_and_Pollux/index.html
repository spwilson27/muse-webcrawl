Castor and Pollux

Castor and Pollux (or Polydeuces) are twin half-brothers in Greek and Roman mythology, known together as the Dioscuri or Gemini. They were the brothers of Helen of Troy and Clytemnestra, and half-brothers to each other: according to the most familiar version, Zeus, in the form of a swan, fathered Pollux, while the Spartan king Tyndareus fathered Castor — so Pollux was divine and immortal, Castor mortal.

Myths

The twins were celebrated as horsemen, athletes and protectors of sailors. They sailed with Jason among the Argonauts, took part in the Calydonian boar hunt, and rescued their sister Helen after Theseus abducted her. When Castor was killed — in a quarrel with Idas and Lynceus over cattle or brides — Pollux begged Zeus not to be separated from him. Zeus allowed them to share Pollux's immortality, each spending alternate days on Olympus and in the underworld. Together they became the constellation Gemini.

Cult

The Dioscuri were widely worshipped across the Greek and Roman worlds as helpers of humans, especially sailors, to whom they appeared as St. Elmo's fire. At Rome their temple stood in the Forum, and they were said to have fought beside the Romans at the Battle of Lake Regillus. They remain patrons of sailors, soldiers and travellers, and their image — two stars, two horsemen — persists in art and heraldry.
