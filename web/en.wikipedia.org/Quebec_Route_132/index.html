Route 132 is the longest provincial highway in Quebec, Canada. It follows the south shore of the St. Lawrence River from the U.S. border southwest of Montreal, through the Montérégie, Centre-du-Québec, Chaudière-Appalaches and Bas-Saint-Laurent regions, to the Gaspé Peninsula, which it circles almost completely before ending in a loop at Sainte-Flore near Grand-Métis.

Route description

West of Montreal's suburbs the route passes Kahnawake and runs parallel to Autoroute 20; east of Quebec City it is the principal road of the Bas-Saint-Laurent, passing through Rivière-du-Loup and Rimouski. At Sainte-Flore the highway divides into two branches forming the Tour de la Gaspésie: one follows the St. Lawrence coast north-east to Gaspé and Percé, the other crosses the peninsula interior to the Baie des Chaleurs coast, the two reuniting at the east end. Renowned as one of Canada's great scenic drives, the route passes lighthouses, fishing villages and Forillon National Park.

History

Much of the road was formerly Route 2 and Route 6 in the pre-1970s numbering; the present designation dates from the 1970s renumbering of Quebec highways.
