Lipizzan

The Lipizzan or Lipizzaner (Croatian: Lipicanac, Slovene: Lipicanec) is a European breed of riding horse developed in the Habsburg Empire in the early modern era. It is closely associated with the Spanish Riding School of Vienna, Austria, where the horses demonstrate the haute école or "high school" movements of classical dressage, including the highly controlled, stylized jumps and other movements known as the "airs above the ground".

Characteristics

Lipizzans measure usually between 14.2 and 15.2 hands (58 to 62 inches) and are most commonly grey: foals are born dark, usually bay or black, and lighten with age until the coat is fully white at between six and ten years. A few remain dark into adulthood, and tradition holds that a dark horse in the Spanish Riding School's quadrille is good luck. They have a convex or straight profile, a strong, arched neck, and a compact, muscular body.

History

The breed was founded at the court stud at Lipica (Lipizza) in present-day Slovenia in 1580, using Spanish, Italian, and Arabian bloodlines; eight foundation sire lines are still recognized. The Lipizzan studbooks of Slovenia, Austria, Croatia, and other countries were inscribed in 2022 on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
