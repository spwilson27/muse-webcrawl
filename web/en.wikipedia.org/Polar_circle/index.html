Polar circle

NorthernArctic Circle, about 66°33′ N
SouthernAntarctic Circle, about 66°33′ S
PhenomenaMidnight sun, polar night

A polar circle is one of the two parallels of latitude marking the region within which the Sun remains above or below the horizon for at least 24 hours at the solstices. The northern polar circle is the Arctic Circle and the southern is the Antarctic Circle.

Position

The circles lie at latitudes equal to 90° minus the Earth's axial tilt, currently about 66°33′. Because the axial tilt oscillates slowly over tens of thousands of years, the polar circles drift: the Arctic Circle is currently moving northwards by roughly 15 metres per year, and the Antarctic Circle correspondingly southwards.

Climate and life

Within the polar circles lie the frigid zones of polar climate, characterised by long dark winters, continuous summer daylight, permafrost, and tundra or ice cap landscapes. The Arctic Circle passes through eight countries, including Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia, the United States (Alaska), Canada, Greenland, and Iceland.
