Saudade

Saudade (Portuguese: [sɐwˈðaðɨ]) is an emotional state of melancholic or profoundly nostalgic longing for a beloved yet absent someone or something. It is often associated with a repressed knowledge that the object of longing might never be had again. It is a recollection of feelings, experiences, places, or events, often elusive, that cause a sense of separation from the exciting, pleasant, or joyous sensations and cause a surfacing of melancholic, bittersweet feelings of missingness and longing.

Saudade derives from the Latin word for solitude. It is often considered a characteristically Portuguese emotion and a defining feature of fado music, though it is also felt in Galician (morriña is a related but distinct term) and Brazilian culture. Historically, the sentiment is linked to Portugal's maritime age, when families waited—indefinitely, often in vain—for sailors to return from voyages of the Discoveries.

Etymology and history

The word is attested in Portuguese texts of the 13th and 14th centuries, written as soidade, from Latin solitatem ("solitude"). In the 15th century it took on its modern form and meaning. The concept deepened during Portugal's Age of Discovery and later waves of emigration, which made separation from home a central experience of Portuguese life. The related philosophy of saudosismo, championed by poet Teixeira de Pascoaes in the early 20th century, elevated saudade into a national-spiritual doctrine.

In music and literature

Saudade is the animating theme of fado, the Portuguese urban song tradition sung most famously by Amália Rodrigues, and of much Brazilian music, including bossa nova; the 1958 song "Chega de Saudade" ("No More Blues"), by Antônio Carlos Jobim and Vinícius de Moraes, helped launch the bossa nova movement. Writers from Fernando Pessoa to Eduardo Lourenço have treated saudade as a key to the Portuguese imagination.

Because it blends absence, memory, hope, and a subtle pleasure in longing itself, saudade is frequently cited as an example of an "untranslatable" word, comparable to German Sehnsucht or Welsh hiraeth.
