Static margin

In aircraft stability and control, the static margin is the distance between the aircraft's centre of gravity (CG) and its neutral point, expressed as a fraction (or percentage) of the mean aerodynamic chord. It is the standard measure of an aircraft's inherent longitudinal static stability.

Definition

A positive static margin means the neutral point lies behind the centre of gravity, so that a disturbance in angle of attack produces a restoring (nose-down pitching) moment. Generally, a larger static margin implies greater stability but reduced manoeuvrability and higher trim drag. Typical values for conventional aircraft range from about 5 to 15 percent of the mean aerodynamic chord.

Relation to stability

Static margin is directly related to the slope of the pitching-moment curve with lift coefficient: a stable aircraft has a negative slope, and the magnitude of that slope is proportional to the static margin. Moving the CG aft shrinks the static margin; if the CG reaches the neutral point the aircraft is neutrally stable, and beyond it the aircraft becomes unstable—a condition deliberately accepted in some modern fighter aircraft flown with fly-by-wire controls.

Measurement

The static margin can be inferred in flight test by measuring the elevator deflection needed to trim at different speeds and CG positions, and it is central to the certification of aircraft loading envelopes.
