Cachaça

TypeDistilled spirit
Country of originBrazil
Alcohol by volume38–48%
Main ingredientFresh sugarcane juice

Cachaça is a distilled spirit made from fermented sugarcane juice. Often called pinga, caninha or aguardente, it is the most popular distilled alcoholic beverage in Brazil and is the base spirit of the caipirinha, the country's national cocktail.

Unlike most rum, which is made from molasses, cachaça is distilled from fresh-pressed sugarcane juice, giving it a grassier, more vegetal character. Production dates to the 16th century on Portuguese sugar plantations. Today there are thousands of producers: industrial brands distilled in column stills, and artisanal cachaça de alambique from copper pot stills, often aged in barrels of native Brazilian woods such as amburana, balsam and jequitibá, as well as oak. Unaged white cachaça is rested briefly in stainless steel or neutral tanks; aged styles are classified by their time in wood.

Regulation

Cachaça is a protected Brazilian designation; in 2013 the United States recognized it as a distinctive product of Brazil, ending its labelling there as "Brazilian rum."
