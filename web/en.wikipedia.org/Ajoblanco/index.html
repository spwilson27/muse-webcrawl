Ajoblanco

Ajoblanco (sometimes written ajo blanco, meaning "white garlic" in Spanish) is a popular Spanish cold soup typical from Granada and Málaga (Andalusia). This dish is related to gazpacho and is made of bread, crushed almonds, garlic, water, olive oil and salt, and sometimes vinegar and sherry. It is usually served with grapes or slices of melon.

History

The dish is thought to have originated in Roman or Greek cuisine and to have been refined in al-Andalus, where almonds and garlic were staples. Ajoblanco predates gazpacho in its tomato form, since tomatoes arrived from the Americas only in the sixteenth century. In Almáchar, Málaga, a festival dedicated to ajoblanco is held each September.

Preparation

Stale bread is soaked in water or milk, then blended with ground almonds, garlic, olive oil and salt into a smooth, creamy white soup, chilled before serving and garnished with green grapes or melon.
