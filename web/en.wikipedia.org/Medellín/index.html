Medellín

Medellín (Spanish pronunciation: [meðeˈʝin]), officially the Special District of Science, Technology and Innovation of Medellín, is the second-largest city in Colombia after Bogotá, and the capital of the department of Antioquia. It is located in the Aburrá Valley, a central region of the Andes Mountains in South America. As of 2018, Medellín had a population of roughly 2.5 million, with over 4 million in the metropolitan area.

Medellín, in the Aburrá Valley, is known as the "City of Eternal Spring".

History

The Aburrá Valley was first settled by Spanish colonists in 1616, and the town was formally founded in 1675 as Villa de Nuestra Señora de la Candelaria de Medellín. It became the capital of Antioquia in 1826. In the 20th century the city grew into an industrial centre, especially for textiles. In the 1980s and early 1990s, Medellín became notorious as the base of Pablo Escobar and the Medellín Cartel, and suffered extreme violence. After Escobar's death in 1993, homicide rates fell dramatically over the following decades.

Urban transformation

In the 2000s the city pursued widely praised "social urbanism": the Metro system (opened 1995, the only one in Colombia) was extended with Metrocable gondola lines and outdoor escalators into the poor hillside districts (comunas), alongside library parks and schools. In 2013 the Urban Land Institute named Medellín the most innovative city in the world.

Climate

Medellín's elevation of about 1,500 metres gives it a mild, spring-like climate year-round, with average temperatures around 22 °C (72 °F) — hence its nickname, "City of Eternal Spring". The annual Flower Fair (Feria de las Flores) is the city's best-known festival.
