Baptism of Jesus

The baptism of Jesus is an event described in the canonical Gospels of Matthew, Mark, and Luke, in which Jesus was baptised in the Jordan River by John the Baptist. It is considered the beginning of Jesus's public ministry and is regarded by most modern theologians as a historical event to which a high degree of certainty can be assigned, along with the crucifixion of Jesus.

Biblical accounts

In the Gospel of Mark, Jesus comes from Nazareth in Galilee and is baptised by John in the Jordan. As Jesus emerges from the water, he sees the heavens torn open and the Spirit descending on him like a dove, and a voice from heaven declares, "You are my Son, the Beloved; with you I am well pleased." Matthew's account adds a brief dialogue in which John protests his unworthiness, and Luke's account emphasises Jesus praying after the baptism. The Gospel of John does not directly narrate the baptism but records John the Baptist's testimony that he saw the Spirit descend and remain on Jesus.

Location

The baptism is traditionally located at Bethany beyond the Jordan, identified with the site of Al-Maghtas on the east bank of the Jordan River in modern Jordan, and at Qasr el Yahud on the west bank. Both sites are major pilgrimage destinations.

Significance

Most Christian denominations view the baptism of Jesus as a foundational event: it marks the start of his ministry, is interpreted as an anointing by the Holy Spirit, and serves as a model for the Christian rite of baptism. In the Eastern Orthodox Church the event is celebrated as Theophany, and in the Roman Catholic Church as a feast within the Christmas season.
