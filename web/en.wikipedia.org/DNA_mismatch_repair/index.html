DNA mismatch repair

DNA mismatch repair (MMR) is a system of repair proteins that recognises and corrects errors arising during DNA replication, such as incorrectly paired bases and small insertion–deletion loops. By fixing mistakes that escape the proofreading of DNA polymerase, MMR improves the fidelity of replication roughly a hundred- to a thousand-fold.

Mechanism

In bacteria such as E. coli, the MutS protein detects a mismatch, MutL coordinates the response, and MutH nicks the newly synthesised strand — identified by its lack of methylation — so that the error-containing segment can be removed and resynthesised. Eukaryotes use homologues of MutS (MSH proteins) and MutL (MLH and PMS proteins), with strand discrimination thought to involve nicks and the sliding clamp PCNA.

Defects and disease

Inherited defects in human MMR genes cause Lynch syndrome, a hereditary predisposition to colorectal and other cancers, because unrepaired replication errors accumulate as mutations, especially in repetitive microsatellite sequences — a phenomenon called microsatellite instability. Acquired loss of MMR, for instance by silencing of the MLH1 gene, is also found in a significant minority of sporadic colorectal tumours, which tend to respond differently to chemotherapy and immunotherapy.
