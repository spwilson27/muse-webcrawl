Hawker Siddeley Trident

The Hawker Siddeley HS-121 Trident is a British airliner produced by Hawker Siddeley. In 1957, the state-owned British European Airways (BEA) requested that De Havilland produce a short-haul airliner with a capacity of 100 passengers; the Trident was a trijet with an airframe adapted to that requirement, similar in concept to the later Boeing 727. The Trident made its maiden flight on 9 January 1962 and entered service with BEA in 1964. Only 117 Tridents were made.

Development and design

HS-121 Trident

Role: Narrow-body trijet airliner

Manufacturer: Hawker Siddeley (orig. de Havilland)

First flight: 9 January 1962

Introduction: 1 April 1964 (BEA)

Produced: 1962–1978

Number built: 117

The Trident was designed around the Rolls-Royce Spey turbofan, with all three engines mounted at the rear — two in pods on either side of the rear fuselage and the third in the base of the tail fin, fed by an S-duct. Its specification was tailored closely to BEA's European routes, which limited its appeal to other airlines; the competing Boeing 727, sized for the larger American market, sold in far greater numbers (1,832). The aircraft was nevertheless technologically pioneering: it was the first airliner equipped to perform a fully automatic blind landing (autoland) in fog, demonstrated in service in the 1960s, and it carried an early flight data display system. A nose gear offset to the port side was a distinctive feature.

Variants and operators

The Trident 1C was built for BEA; the longer-range Trident 2E was sold to airlines including Cyprus Airways, Iraqi Airways and CAAC Airlines of China; the stretched Trident 3B, with a small additional booster engine in the tail for hot-and-high performance, served BEA and British Airways. China became the largest operator outside Britain after acquiring ex-Pakistani and new aircraft, and Chinese Tridents remained in service into the 1990s. The type's accident history included the 1972 Staines air disaster (British European Airways Flight 548), in which 118 people died shortly after takeoff from Heathrow, then Britain's worst air disaster, and the 1976 Zagreb mid-air collision.

The Trident was retired from British service in the mid-1980s. A few examples survive in museums and as stored airframes in China.
