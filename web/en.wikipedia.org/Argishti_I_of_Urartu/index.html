Argishti I of Urartu

Argishti I (Armenian: Արգիշտի) was the sixth known king of Urartu, reigning from 786 BC to 764 BC. He was the son and successor of Menua and founded the city of Erebuni, whose site is the origin of the modern Armenian capital, Yerevan.

Reign

Argishti continued the expansion of Urartu begun by his father. His annals, inscribed in cuneiform on stone stelae, record campaigns in Diauehi, against the Assyrians, and into the Ararat plain and the region around Lake Sevan. During his reign Urartu reached its greatest territorial extent and was the strongest power of the Near East, rivalling Assyria under Shalmaneser IV and his successors.

Erebuni and Argishtihinili

In 782 BC Argishti founded the fortress of Erebuni on Arin Berd hill in the Ararat plain; the foundation inscription notes that 6,600 prisoners of war were settled there. Erebuni is regarded as the founding site of Yerevan, which celebrated its 2,750th anniversary in 1968 on that basis. Argishti also founded Argishtihinili in the plain, a major administrative centre of the kingdom.

Argishti was succeeded by his son Sarduri II.
