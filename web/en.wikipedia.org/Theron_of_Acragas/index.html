Theron of Acragas (died 473/472 BC) was a tyrant of the Greek city of Acragas (modern Agrigento) in Sicily from 488 BC until his death. A member of the Emmenid family, he seized power in Acragas and expanded its influence, ruling alongside his son-in-law Gelon, the tyrant of Syracuse. Together they decisively defeated the Carthaginians at the Battle of Himera in 480 BC, traditionally said to have occurred on the same day as the Greek victory at Salamis.

Rule

Theron took control of Acragas in 488 BC. He allied himself with Gelon of Syracuse by marrying his daughter Damarete to Gelon, while Gelon's brother Hieron married Theron's niece. When Terillus, the tyrant of Himera, was expelled by Theron and appealed to Carthage, the resulting Carthaginian expedition under Hamilcar was crushed at Himera in 480 BC. The victory brought Theron great wealth in spoils and prisoners, which he used for an ambitious building programme in Acragas, including the enormous Temple of Olympian Zeus, one of the largest Doric temples ever attempted, and an extensive water-supply system attributed to the engineer Phaeax.

The poet Pindar celebrated Theron's victories in chariot racing at the Olympic and Pythian Games in his Olympian Odes 2 and 3, composed for Theron's Olympic chariot victory of 476 BC. Theron was remembered as a comparatively mild and just ruler. On his death in 473/472 BC he was succeeded by his son Thrasydaeus, whose harsh rule provoked a revolt; he was defeated by Hieron of Syracuse and expelled, after which Acragas became a democracy.
