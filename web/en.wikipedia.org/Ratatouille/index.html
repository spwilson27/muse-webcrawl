Ratatouille

Ratatouille (French: [ʁatatuj]) is a French Provençal dish of stewed vegetables that originated in Nice and is sometimes referred to as ratatouille niçoise. Recipes and cooking times differ widely, but common ingredients include tomato, garlic, onion, courgette (zucchini), aubergine (eggplant), bell pepper, and leafy green herbs common to the region, such as thyme, rosemary, and basil, often combined as herbes de Provence.

History

The word ratatouille derives from the Occitan ratatolha and is related to the French verb touiller, meaning "to stir up". From the late 18th century, in French, it merely indicated a coarse stew. The modern ratatouille — containing tomatoes, garlic, onion, courgette, aubergine, peppers, and herbs — appeared in print in the early 20th century and is associated with the peasant cooking of Provence, where farmers used abundant summer vegetables.

Preparation

There are two main schools of preparation. In the traditional method, all the vegetables are cooked together in a single pot until tender. In the second method, favoured by many chefs, each vegetable is cooked separately before being combined and baked, which preserves the distinct flavour and texture of each ingredient. A refined layered presentation, in which thin slices of the vegetables are arranged in overlapping rows and baked, is sometimes called confit byaldi — the version popularised by the 2007 Pixar animated film Ratatouille.

Similar dishes

Similar stewed vegetable dishes exist across the Mediterranean, including Catalan samfaina, Mallorcan tumbet, Spanish pisto, Italian caponata and peperonata, Greek briam, and Turkish şakşuka and imam bayıldı.
