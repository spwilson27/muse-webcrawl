The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) is an international organisation working in the field of nature conservation and sustainable use of natural resources. Founded in 1948 at Fontainebleau, France, originally as the International Union for the Protection of Nature, it is the world's oldest and largest global environmental network, bringing together more than 1,400 member organisations and the input of some 16,000 experts.

Red List

The IUCN's best-known product is the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species, established in 1964, the world's most comprehensive inventory of the global conservation status of biological species. Species are assessed against quantitative criteria and assigned to categories ranging from Least Concern through Near Threatened, Vulnerable, Endangered and Critically Endangered to Extinct in the Wild and Extinct. The Red List is widely used by governments and conservation organisations to guide policy and funding.

Structure and work

The IUCN is a membership union composed of both government and civil society organisations. Its work is organised through six expert commissions, including the Species Survival Commission and the World Commission on Protected Areas, and a secretariat headquartered in Gland, Switzerland. The World Conservation Congress, held every four years, brings members together to set the union's policies and programme. The IUCN also classifies protected areas into management categories (Ia–VI) and publishes the Green List of well-managed protected and conserved areas.

History

The IUCN helped establish the World Wildlife Fund (now WWF) in 1961 and played a central role in drafting international conventions including CITES (1973), the Ramsar Convention on Wetlands (1971), and in advising UNESCO on natural World Heritage sites. Its current name dates from 1956.
