Flamsteed designation

A Flamsteed designation is a combination of a number and constellation name that uniquely identifies most naked-eye stars in the modern constellations visible from southern England. Examples are 51 Pegasi and 61 Cygni. The designations are named after John Flamsteed, the first Astronomer Royal, whose star catalogue Historia Coelestis Britannica (published posthumously in 1725) listed some 3,000 stars.

Origin

Flamsteed himself did not number the stars in his catalogue; the numbers were assigned by the French astronomer Joseph Jérôme de Lalande in a 1783 French edition of Flamsteed's catalogue, and they came into general use during the 18th and 19th centuries. Within each constellation, stars were numbered in order of increasing right ascension. Because of precession and the re-drawing of constellation boundaries (formalised by the International Astronomical Union in 1928), a few stars now lie in a different constellation from the one in their designation, and some numbers in a sequence are no longer used.

Use

Flamsteed designations remain in common professional and amateur use for stars that lack a Bayer designation (a Greek letter assigned by Johann Bayer in 1603), or instead of one when the number is better known. About 2,554 stars carry Flamsteed numbers. Stars visible only from the southern hemisphere were not covered by Flamsteed; many later received numbers from the catalogues of Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille and, for Gould designations, Benjamin Gould. Bright stars are usually referred to by Bayer letters or proper names, while fainter stars are identified by catalogue numbers such as those of the Henry Draper Catalogue.
