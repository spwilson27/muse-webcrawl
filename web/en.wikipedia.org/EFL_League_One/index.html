EFL League One

EFL League One, commonly known as League One, is the third tier of the English football league system. Introduced for the 2004–05 season as a rebrand of the old Football League Second Division, it is contested by 24 professional clubs.

Competition format

Each club plays the others twice, home and away, over a 46-match season. The champions and runners-up are automatically promoted to the EFL Championship, and a third promotion place is decided by play-offs contested by the clubs finishing third to sixth, culminating in a final at Wembley Stadium. The bottom four clubs are relegated to League Two.

History

League One's forerunners include the Third Division (1920–1992) and the Second Division (1992–2004). Notable recent champions include Leicester City (2008–09), Sheffield United (2016–17) and Luton Town (2018–19), each of whom climbed further up the pyramid soon afterwards. Wigan Athletic and Blackburn Rovers are among the clubs to have won the division while also having top-flight pedigree.

Finances

League One clubs operate under squad cost management rules linked to turnover, intended to control wage spending. Despite its third-tier status, the division regularly records attendances rivalling second-tier leagues elsewhere in Europe, reflecting the depth of English professional football.
