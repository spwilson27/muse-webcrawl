Hot dog variations

Many regions of the United States and the world have local variations on the hot dog, distinguished by the type of sausage, the bun, and especially the combination of toppings and condiments. These regional styles are often a point of local pride and identity.

United States

The Chicago-style hot dog is an all-beef frankfurter on a poppy seed bun, "dragged through the garden" with yellow mustard, chopped onions, bright green sweet pickle relish, a dill pickle spear, tomato slices, sport peppers, and celery salt — ketchup is famously omitted. The New York–style dog is typically topped with mustard and sauerkraut or onion sauce. The Coney dog of Michigan and the Midwest is covered with a meat chilli sauce, mustard, and onions. The Sonoran hot dog of Arizona is wrapped in bacon and topped with beans, onions, tomatoes, mayonnaise, and jalapeños. Southern variations include the slaw dog, topped with coleslaw.

International

In Iceland, hot dogs (pylsur) are served with raw and crispy fried onions, ketchup, sweet mustard, and remoulade. The Chilean completo is loaded with chopped tomatoes, avocado, and mayonnaise. In Sweden, the tunnbrödsrulle wraps a hot dog with mashed potatoes in flatbread. Japan and Korea have their own adaptations, including corn dogs and dogs topped with teriyaki sauce and seaweed.
