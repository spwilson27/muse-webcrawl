Gibson Les Paul

The Gibson Les Paul is a solid-body electric guitar first sold by the Gibson Guitar Corporation in 1952. Developed with the guitarist and inventor Les Paul, it is one of the most famous and imitated guitar designs in the world, closely associated with rock, blues, and heavy metal.

History

Gibson introduced the Les Paul in response to the success of Fender's solid-body guitars, signing an endorsement deal with Les Paul, then one of America's most popular guitarists. The first model, the Goldtop, had a gold finish, a mahogany body with a carved maple top, and two P-90 single-coil pickups. The design evolved through the 1950s: the Tune-o-matic bridge appeared in 1955, and humbucking pickups — designed by Seth Lover — were fitted from 1957.

The sunburst-finished Standards of 1958–1960, played by guitarists such as Eric Clapton, Jimmy Page, and Duane Allman, became the most coveted electric guitars ever made; original examples now sell for hundreds of thousands of dollars. Sales of the original single-cutaway design declined in the early 1960s and it was discontinued in 1961 (its replacement shape later became the SG), but demand from rock musicians led Gibson to reissue the Les Paul in 1968. It has remained in continuous production since.

Design

The Les Paul is characterised by a heavy single-cutaway mahogany body with a maple cap, a glued-in (set) mahogany neck, and two humbucking pickups with individual volume and tone controls. Its construction gives it a thick tone and long sustain prized in rock music. Numerous variants have been produced, including the Custom, Standard, Junior, Special, and Deluxe, as well as models under Gibson's budget Epiphone brand.
