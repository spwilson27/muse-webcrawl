Jean-Dominique Bauby

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jean-Dominique Bauby (23 April 1952 – 9 March 1997) was a French journalist, author, and editor of the French fashion magazine Elle. He was the author of the memoir The Diving Bell and the Butterfly (Le Scaphandre et le Papillon), which he dictated after a massive stroke left him with locked-in syndrome.

Stroke and locked-in syndrome

On 8 December 1995, at the age of 43, Bauby suffered a massive stroke of the brain stem while driving. When he woke up in the hospital twenty days later, he found that his body was almost completely paralysed: he could control only his left eyelid. The condition, locked-in syndrome, left his mind fully intact while his body was immobile.

The Diving Bell and the Butterfly

With the help of a transcriber, Claude Mendibil, Bauby dictated his memoir letter by letter: she would recite a frequency-ordered alphabet (E, S, A, R, I, N, T, U, L, O, M, P, C, F, B, V, H, G, J, Q, Z, Y, X, K, W) and he would blink when she reached the desired letter. The process, roughly 200,000 blinks in total, produced a short book in which the "diving bell" describes the oppressive weight of his paralysed body and the "butterfly" the freedom of his imagination and memory. The book was published in France on 7 March 1997 to acclaim.

Death and legacy

Bauby died of pneumonia two days after the book's publication, on 9 March 1997, in Garches, France. His memoir was adapted into the 2007 film The Diving Bell and the Butterfly, directed by Julian Schnabel, which won awards at Cannes and elsewhere and brought his story to a worldwide audience.
