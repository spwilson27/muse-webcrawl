Point bar

A point bar deposited on the inner bank of a meander

A point bar is a depositional feature made of alluvium that accumulates on the inside bend of streams and rivers below the slip-off slope. Point bars are found in abundance in mature and meandering streams, where the flow is slowest and shallowest along the inner bank of a bend, allowing sediment — sand, gravel and silt — to settle out of the water column.

Formation

As water flows around a bend, centrifugal effects and the helical secondary flow of the channel direct the fastest current toward the outer bank, where it erodes the bank (forming a steep cut bank, sometimes an undercut cliff), and sweep bed load toward the inner bank. The velocity and turbulence over the inner bank are insufficient to keep coarser particles in motion, so they accumulate there as a gently sloping bar. As the outer bank erodes and the inner bar grows, the meander migrates laterally across its floodplain, and the point bar surface records this migration in ridge-and-swale topography and inclined strata known as epsilon cross-bedding. Point-bar deposits characteristically fine upward, from gravel and coarse sand at the base to fine sand and mud at the top.

Associated features

Point bars are part of the meandering-river system that includes the cut bank opposite them, natural levees along channel margins, and oxbow lakes formed when a meander is cut off. The bar itself is typically unvegetated or sparsely vegetated while active, since it is flooded each year; older, higher parts stabilise and are colonised by pioneer species such as willows. In gravel-bed rivers, equivalent features are sometimes called point bars interchangeably with other mid-channel and bank-attached bars, though fluvial geomorphologists distinguish them by position and formation process.

Significance

Point-bar deposits are important in geology because their internal architecture — inclined, fining-upward layers — allows ancient meandering rivers to be recognised in the rock record, and because such deposits can form reservoirs for groundwater and hydrocarbons. Their sands and gravels are also widely exploited as aggregate, and point bars provide seasonally exposed habitat used by nesting shorebirds and spawning fish.
