African literature

African literature is literature from Africa, in oral and written forms and in African and European languages. Oral traditions—epics such as the Sundiata epic of the Mali Empire, praise poetry, proverbs, and folktales—predate written literature by centuries.

Written traditions

Written literatures are ancient in Ge'ez and Arabic; modern African literature in European languages expanded in the twentieth century with writers such as Chinua Achebe, whose Things Fall Apart (1958) became the most widely read African novel, and Wole Soyinka, the first African Nobel laureate in Literature (1986). Naguib Mahfouz won the Nobel Prize in 1988, followed by Nadine Gordimer, J. M. Coetzee, Doris Lessing, and Abdulrazak Gurnah.

Themes

Common themes include colonialism and its aftermath, identity, political corruption, and the tension between tradition and modernity. Contemporary writers such as Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie and Tsitsi Dangarembga address gender, migration, and diaspora.
