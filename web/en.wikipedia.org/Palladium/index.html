Palladium is a chemical element with the symbol Pd and atomic number 46. It is a rare and lustrous silvery-white metal discovered in 1802 by the English chemist William Hyde Wollaston. He named it after the asteroid Pallas, which was itself named after the Greek goddess Athena (Pallas Athena). Palladium, platinum, rhodium, ruthenium, iridium and osmium form the platinum group metals.

Characteristics

Palladium is the least dense and has the lowest melting point of the platinum group metals. It is soft and ductile when annealed and is remarkable for absorbing up to 900 times its own volume of hydrogen at room temperature, forming palladium hydride. It does not tarnish in air at standard temperatures but is attacked by concentrated nitric and hot sulfuric acids.

Occurrence and production

Palladium is mined mainly in Russia (the Norilsk–Talnakh deposits), South Africa (the Bushveld Complex), Canada and the United States (the Stillwater Complex, Montana), usually as a by-product of nickel and platinum mining. Supply has frequently run in deficit, driving prices above those of gold for periods in 2019–2022.

Applications

The largest use of palladium is in catalytic converters for petrol-engine vehicles, which convert toxic exhaust pollutants into less harmful substances. Palladium catalysts (such as palladium on carbon) are also fundamental in organic chemistry — several palladium-catalysed cross-coupling reactions were recognised by the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Other uses include electronics (multilayer ceramic capacitors), dentistry, hydrogen purification, jewellery (as a white-gold alloy) and coinage.
