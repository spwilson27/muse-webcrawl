Thyssen

Thyssen is a German family name associated with one of Germany's great industrial dynasties. The family's steel business was founded by August Thyssen (1842–1926), who built the Thyssen group from a rolling mill in Mülheim an der Ruhr into one of the largest mining and steel combines in Europe.

The company

August's son Fritz Thyssen (1873–1951) inherited and expanded the firm; he was an early financial supporter of the Nazi Party but later broke with the regime and was imprisoned. After the Second World War the group was reorganized, and in 1999 Thyssen AG merged with Krupp to form ThyssenKrupp, one of the world's largest steel and industrial engineering companies, headquartered in Essen.

Other members and legacy

The family is also known for the art collection assembled by Heinrich Thyssen (1875–1947) and his son Hans Heinrich Thyssen-Bornemisza (1921–2002), much of which is now displayed in the Museo Nacional Thyssen-Bornemisza in Madrid, one of the city's "Golden Triangle" of art museums.
