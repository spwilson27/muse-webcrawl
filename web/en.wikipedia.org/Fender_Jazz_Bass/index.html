Fender Jazz Bass

The Fender Jazz Bass, introduced in 1960, is one of the most influential electric bass guitars.

The Fender Jazz Bass (often shortened to J-Bass) is the second model of electric bass guitar created by Leo Fender, introduced in 1960 as a higher-end companion to the Precision Bass. The Jazz Bass is known for its bright, rich tone with more emphasis on treble and midrange, its slim neck profile, and its offset-waist body design modelled on the Jazzmaster guitar.

The original design featured two single-coil pickups, each with its own volume control, blended through a master tone control — later versions used volume/volume/tone in various configurations. Stacked concentric knobs appeared on the earliest models. The two pickups allow a wide range of sounds, from a deep, mellow neck-pickup tone to the aggressive bridge-pickup "growl" associated with players such as Jaco Pastorius.

Notable players

The Jazz Bass has been used by countless bassists across genres, including Jaco Pastorius, Marcus Miller, Geddy Lee of Rush, John Paul Jones of Led Zeppelin, Flea of the Red Hot Chili Peppers (in modified form), and Larry Graham, pioneer of slap bass. It remains one of the two foundational designs of the electric bass, alongside the Precision Bass.
