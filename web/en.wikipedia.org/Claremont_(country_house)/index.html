Claremont (country house)

Claremont
Location: Esher, Surrey, England
Architects: Henry Holland and Capability Brown (house); Capability Brown (landscape)
Built: 1768–1774 (present house)
Notable owners: Robert Clive; Princess Charlotte and Prince Leopold; Queen Victoria

Claremont is an 18th-century Palladian country house and landscaped estate near Esher in Surrey, England. The present house was built for Robert Clive, 1st Baron Clive ("Clive of India"), who purchased the estate in 1768 and had the earlier house by Sir John Vanbrugh demolished and replaced. The new house was designed by Henry Holland with the assistance of Lancelot "Capability" Brown, who also redesigned the gardens.

History

The first house at Claremont was built in 1708–1711 for Thomas Pelham-Holles, later Duke of Newcastle, to designs by Vanbrugh, who coined the name "Claremont" for its clear prospect. Clive bought the estate on his return from India and spent a fortune remodelling it. After Clive's death the estate passed through several owners before being bought by the Crown in 1816 as a wedding present for Princess Charlotte, the only child of the Prince Regent, and her husband Prince Leopold of Saxe-Coburg. Charlotte died at Claremont in 1817 after childbirth, a national tragedy. Queen Victoria frequently stayed at Claremont as a young princess and later granted it to her youngest son, Prince Leopold, Duke of Albany. From 1930 to 2016 the grounds housed Claremont Fan Court School; the house is Grade I listed and the landscape is noted for its early English landscape garden features, including a turf amphitheatre and a lake with an island pavilion.

Landscape

The Claremont landscape garden is one of the earliest surviving English landscape gardens, worked on successively by Charles Bridgeman, William Kent, and Capability Brown.
