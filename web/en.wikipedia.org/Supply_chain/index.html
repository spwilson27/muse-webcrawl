Supply chain

A supply chain is the network of organisations, people, activities, information and resources involved in supplying a product or service to a consumer. It encompasses the sourcing of raw materials, manufacturing, transportation, warehousing, distribution and retail, together with reverse flows such as returns and recycling. The discipline of coordinating these activities is supply chain management.

Structure

Supply chains are commonly described in tiers: a manufacturer buys components from tier-one suppliers, who in turn depend on tier-two and tier-three suppliers of parts and raw materials. Alongside the flow of goods run flows of information (orders, forecasts, tracking) and finance (payments and credit). "Upstream" activities concern sourcing and production; "downstream" activities concern distribution and sale.

Management and strategies

Supply chain management seeks to balance cost, speed, reliability and flexibility. Approaches include lean and just-in-time production, pioneered by Toyota, which minimise inventory; vertical integration, in which a firm owns successive stages; and global sourcing, which lowers cost but lengthens and complicates chains. Small forecast errors can amplify as they move upstream — the bullwhip effect — causing damaging swings in inventory and production.

Disruption and resilience

Events such as the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake, the COVID-19 pandemic and the 2021 blockage of the Suez Canal exposed the fragility of highly optimised global chains, prompting firms and governments to pursue diversification, "friend-shoring", larger buffer stocks and greater visibility into lower supplier tiers. Sustainability and labour standards throughout the chain have also become major concerns.
