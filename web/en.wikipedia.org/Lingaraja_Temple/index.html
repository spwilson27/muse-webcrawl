Lingaraja Temple

LocationBhubaneswar, Odisha, India
DeityShiva (as Lingaraja / Harihara)
ArchitectureKalinga architecture
Built11th century CE (main structure)

The Lingaraja Temple is a Hindu temple dedicated to Shiva, located in the old town of Bhubaneswar, the capital of Odisha, India. It is the largest and one of the oldest temples in the city, a major pilgrimage destination, and a masterpiece of Kalinga architecture. The deity, Lingaraja ("King of Lingams"), is worshipped in a form combining Shiva and Vishnu (Harihara).

Architecture

The temple complex covers about 26,000 m² within a high laterite wall and contains more than 50 shrines. The main tower (vimana/deul) rises about 55 metres, built in the rekha deul style with sculpted bands and a ribbed amalaka. The present temple is generally attributed to the Somavamsi king Jajati Keshari in the 11th century, with later additions under the Ganga dynasty.

Worship

Daily rituals are performed by hereditary servitors, and the temple celebrates festivals such as Shivaratri and the annual Rukuna Rath Yatra chariot festival. Entry to the inner temple is traditionally restricted to Hindus; a viewing platform outside allows others to see the complex — famously used by Lord Curzon during the British period.
