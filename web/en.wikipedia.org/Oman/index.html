Oman, officially the Sultanate of Oman, is an Arab country on the southeastern coast of the Arabian Peninsula in Western Asia. It borders the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia and Yemen, and has a coastline on the Arabian Sea and the Gulf of Oman, including the Musandam exclave overlooking the Strait of Hormuz. Its capital and largest city is Muscat.

History

Oman is one of the oldest independent states in the Arab world. Inhabited since the Stone Age, the region — known in antiquity as Magan — traded copper and frankincense with Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley. Islam arrived in the 7th century, and Oman adopted the Ibadi school, which remains influential. In the 17th–19th centuries the Omani Empire became a major maritime power in the western Indian Ocean, ruling territories on the East African coast including Zanzibar, until the empire was split in 1856. Sultan Qaboos bin Said took power in 1970 and modernised the country with its oil revenues; he was succeeded in 2020 by Haitham bin Tariq.

Geography

Most of Oman is gravel desert, with the Al Hajar Mountains rising in the north (Jebel Shams, about 3,000 m, is the highest point), a fertile coastal plain around Muscat and Batinah, and the monsoon-greened hills of Dhofar in the south around Salalah. The country is famous for its aflaj irrigation systems, several of which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Economy

Petroleum and natural gas dominate exports and government revenue, though production is modest by Gulf standards; the government is pursuing diversification (Oman Vision 2040) into tourism, logistics, fisheries and manufacturing. Oman is known for a traditionally neutral foreign policy, often acting as a mediator in regional disputes.
