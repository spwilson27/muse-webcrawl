Varaha

Affiliation: Avatar of Vishnu
Weapon: Sudarshana Chakra, Kaumodaki
Consort: Bhudevi (Varahi)

Varaha (Sanskrit: वराह, "boar") is an avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu, in the form of a wild boar. Varaha is generally listed as the third of the ten principal avatars of Vishnu, the Dashavatara. He is best known for rescuing the earth goddess Bhudevi from the depths of the cosmic ocean, where the demon Hiranyaksha had dragged her.

Legend

According to the Puranas, the demon Hiranyaksha stole the earth and hid her in the primordial waters. Vishnu took the form of a boar, dived into the ocean, and after a battle lasting a thousand years slew the demon, raising the earth back to the surface balanced on his tusks. Varaha is also associated with the earlier Vedic boar Emusha and with the cosmogonic myth of the earth being drawn out of the waters.

Iconography and temples

Varaha is depicted either fully as a boar or as a man with a boar's head, standing with one foot on the serpent Shesha and holding Bhudevi on his tusks or elbow. Famous early images include the monumental 5th-century relief at the Udayagiri Caves of the Gupta period. The Varaha Temple at Khajuraho and the Varaha temple at Eran are notable shrines. The Varaha Purana is named after him.
