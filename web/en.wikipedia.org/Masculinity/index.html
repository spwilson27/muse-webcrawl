Masculinity

Masculinity is a set of attributes, behaviors, and roles associated with men and boys. Scholars generally treat masculinity as socially constructed and varying across cultures and historical periods, though it is often linked to traits such as strength, independence, assertiveness, and the role of provider or protector.

Study and concepts

Men's studies emerged within sociology and psychology in the late 20th century. Raewyn Connell's influential framework distinguished hegemonic masculinity — the culturally dominant ideal — from complicit, marginalized, and subordinated masculinities. Later debate introduced concepts such as "toxic masculinity," used for norms (emotional suppression, aggression) considered harmful to men and others, a term that remains contested.

Culture and change

Ideals of masculinity are shaped by media, work, military service, religion, and family structures, and have shifted with industrialization, feminism, and changing labor markets. Different societies hold markedly different expectations, from Mediterranean honor cultures to East Asian scholarly ideals, illustrating that there is no single universal masculinity.
