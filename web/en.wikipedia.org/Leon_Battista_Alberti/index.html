Leon Battista Alberti

Leon Battista Alberti (Italian: [leˈom batˈtista alˈbɛrti]; 14 February 1404 – 25 April 1472) was an Italian Renaissance humanist author, artist, architect, poet, priest, linguist, philosopher, and cryptographer; he epitomised the nature of those identified now as polymaths. He is considered the founder of Western cryptography, having described a polyalphabetic cipher, frequency analysis, and the first mechanical encryption device. Alberti is considered one of the modern era's most versatile geniuses, and among the great Italian architects who yet did not have their buildings quite finished for them in time to prevent their mangling by subsequent, less-skilled, architects.

Life

Alberti was born in Genoa, the illegitimate son of Lorenzo di Benedetto Alberti, a member of a wealthy Florentine family exiled from Florence, and Bianca Fieschi. He was educated in Padua and studied law at Bologna. After his father's death he was left in financial difficulty, and he entered the papal chancery in Rome in 1431 as an abbreviator. Living mainly in Florence and Rome, he moved in the circle of the Medici and the papal court, and pursued an extraordinary range of intellectual and artistic work.

Works on art and architecture

His treatise De pictura ("On Painting", 1435, in Latin; 1436 in Italian as Della pittura) gave the first systematic account of linear perspective and of the theory of painting, dedicating the Italian version to Filippo Brunelleschi. His De re aedificatoria ("On the Art of Building", written from about 1443, printed 1485) was the first architectural treatise of the Renaissance, modelled on Vitruvius and centred on the ideal of harmonious proportion. Buildings designed by Alberti include the Palazzo Rucellai and the façade of Santa Maria Novella in Florence, the Tempio Malatestiano in Rimini, and the churches of San Sebastiano and Sant'Andrea in Mantua.

Other writings and interests

Alberti wrote the dialogue Della famiglia on household life and education, the autobiographical Vita, comedies, fables, and works on sculpture (De statua) and mathematics. His treatise De componendis cifris (c. 1467) described the polyalphabetic cipher and a cipher disk, making him a founding figure of Western cryptology.

Legacy

Alberti's synthesis of theory and practice, and his embodiment of the "universal man" (uomo universale), made him an archetype of the Renaissance humanist, anticipating Leonardo da Vinci. Jacob Burckhardt praised his versatility in The Civilization of the Renaissance in Italy.
