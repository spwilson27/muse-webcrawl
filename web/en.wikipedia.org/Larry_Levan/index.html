Larry Levan

Larry Levan (born Lawrence Philpot; July 20, 1954 – November 8, 1992) was an American DJ best known for his decade-long residency at the New York City nightclub Paradise Garage, which has been described as the prototype of the modern dance club. His eclectic sets and pioneering remixes helped define disco, post-disco, and the house music subgenre known as garage house, named after the club.

Career

Levan began DJing alongside Frankie Knuckles at the Continental Baths before becoming resident at the Paradise Garage, which opened in 1977 at 84 King Street in Manhattan. The Garage's members-only, no-alcohol atmosphere focused entirely on dancing and on Richard Long's state-of-the-art sound system, over which Levan exercised total control, famously adjusting the system to the room and holding marathon sets on Saturday nights into Sunday afternoon—parties devotees called "Saturday Mass".

Music and legacy

Levan's programming mixed disco, funk, soul, early hip hop, new wave, and dub-influenced productions; his remixes for artists such as Gwen Guthrie ("Padlock"), Taana Gardner, and his own group the Peech Boys ("Don't Make Me Wait") became classics of the era. After the Garage closed in 1987, Levan struggled with drug addiction and died of heart failure in 1992, aged 38. He is remembered as one of the most influential DJs in history; a section of King Street was renamed Larry Levan Way in 2014.
