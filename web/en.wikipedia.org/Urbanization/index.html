Urbanization (or urbanisation) is the population shift from rural to urban areas, the corresponding decrease in the proportion of people living in rural areas, and the ways in which societies adapt to this change. It is predominantly the process by which towns and cities are formed and grow as more people begin living and working in central areas.

History

Cities arose with the agricultural surplus of the Neolithic era, in Mesopotamia, the Indus Valley, Egypt, and China, but remained small for millennia; in 1800 only about 3 percent of the world's population lived in cities. The Industrial Revolution in Britain triggered rapid urban growth as factory work drew people from the countryside, a pattern repeated across Europe, North America, and later the developing world. In 2007 the world's urban population exceeded its rural population for the first time. The United Nations projects that about 68 percent of humanity will live in urban areas by 2050, with most growth in Africa and Asia.

Causes

Rural-to-urban migration is driven by "push" factors such as land scarcity, disasters, and limited rural services, and "pull" factors including jobs, education, healthcare, and entertainment. Natural population increase within cities also contributes. Urbanization accompanies — and helps drive — economic development, as agglomeration raises productivity and specialization.

Effects

Urban living brings better access to services and infrastructure alongside challenges: housing shortages and informal settlements, congestion, air and water pollution, urban heat islands, and strain on sanitation. Public health, planning, and governance responses range from zoning and mass transit to slum upgrading. Urbanization also transforms social life, culture, and the environment on regional and global scales.
