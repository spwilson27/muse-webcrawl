Nelumbo nucifera

Nelumbo nucifera, also known as the sacred lotus, Indian lotus, or simply lotus, is one of two extant species of aquatic plant in the family Nelumbonaceae. It is sometimes colloquially called a water lily, though this more often refers to members of the family Nymphaeaceae. The plant is native to East Asia and northern Australia and is widely cultivated across Asia. Under favorable circumstances, its seeds remain viable for many years, with the oldest recorded lotus germination being from seeds about 1,300 years old recovered from a dry lakebed in northeastern China.

Botany

The lotus grows in shallow ponds and lakes, rooted in mud, with large peltate leaves that rise above the water on long stalks and repel water due to microscopic wax structures — the famous "lotus effect" of superhydrophobicity. Its pink or white flowers, borne in summer, can regulate their temperature to attract pollinating beetles. The distinctive conical seed pod resembles a shower head.

Cultural significance

The lotus is sacred in Hinduism, closely associated with the goddess Lakshmi and the gods Vishnu and Brahma, and in Buddhism, where it symbolises purity, enlightenment, and rebirth because its flowers rise unstained from muddy water. It is the national flower of India and Vietnam. In China it symbolises purity and harmony.

Uses

Nearly all parts of the plant are used in Asian cuisine: the rhizomes (lotus root) are sliced and cooked, the seeds are eaten fresh, dried, or in pastes, the leaves serve as food wrappers, and the stamens are used in herbal teas. Compounds from the plant are also studied in traditional and modern medicine.
