IgA nephropathy

Mesangial IgA deposition shown by immunofluorescence in IgA nephropathy.

IgA nephropathy (IgAN), also known as Berger's disease, is a kidney disease that occurs when the antibody immunoglobulin A (IgA) accumulates in the kidneys, causing inflammation and progressive damage to the glomeruli. It is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide. The disease was first described by the French physician Jean Berger in 1968.

Signs and symptoms

The classic presentation is episodic visible blood in the urine (macroscopic haematuria) occurring within a day or two of an upper respiratory tract infection, in contrast to the longer delay seen in post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis. Many patients instead have only microscopic haematuria with mild proteinuria found on routine testing. Over years or decades, a substantial fraction of patients progress to chronic kidney disease and kidney failure. The underlying mechanism involves production of galactose-deficient IgA1, formation of autoantibodies against it, and deposition of the resulting immune complexes in the mesangium.

Diagnosis and treatment

Definitive diagnosis requires a kidney biopsy showing dominant or codominant mesangial IgA deposits on immunofluorescence. There is no universally curative therapy; management focuses on blood pressure control and reduction of proteinuria with ACE inhibitors or ARBs, salt restriction, and cardiovascular risk reduction. In recent years, targeted-release budesonide, sparsentan, and other agents have been approved in some jurisdictions for patients at high risk of progression.
