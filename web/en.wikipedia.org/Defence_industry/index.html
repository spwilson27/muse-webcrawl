Defence industry

The defence industry, also known as the military industry, comprises government and commercial organisations involved in the research, development, production and servicing of military equipment, facilities and matériel. It includes arms manufacturers, aerospace and shipbuilding firms, and suppliers of electronics, vehicles, ammunition and support services.

Scale

Global military expenditure exceeds two trillion US dollars annually, and the largest defence contractors — firms such as Lockheed Martin, RTX, Northrop Grumman, Boeing and BAE Systems — each record tens of billions of dollars in revenue. The United States accounts for the largest share of both spending and exports, followed by countries including Russia, France, China and Germany.

Relationship with government

Unlike most industries, defence has essentially one customer per country: the state. Contracts are shaped by procurement policy, secrecy and export controls such as the US ITAR regime and multilateral arrangements like the Wassenaar Arrangement. Critics speak of a "military–industrial complex", a phrase popularised by President Dwight D. Eisenhower in 1961, warning of undue industry influence over policy; supporters emphasise deterrence, jobs and technological spillovers.
