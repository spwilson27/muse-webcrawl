Secured transaction

In a secured transaction the debtor grants the creditor a security interest in specific collateral.

A secured transaction is a loan or credit transaction in which the lender acquires a security interest in collateral owned by the borrower, allowing the lender to repossess and sell the collateral if the borrower defaults. Secured transactions lower the lender's risk and therefore usually carry lower interest rates than unsecured credit. In the United States they are governed principally by Article 9 of the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), which covers security interests in personal property and fixtures.

Attachment and perfection

A security interest becomes enforceable against the debtor when it attaches: value has been given, the debtor has rights in the collateral, and the debtor has authenticated a security agreement describing the collateral (or the creditor has possession or control of it). To be effective against third parties, the interest must usually be perfected, most commonly by filing a financing statement (Form UCC-1) in a public registry, by taking possession of the collateral, or automatically for certain purchase-money security interests in consumer goods.

Priority

When several creditors claim the same collateral, priority rules decide who is paid first from its sale. The general rule is "first to file or perfect". A purchase-money security interest (PMSI), granted to a lender whose loan enables the debtor to buy the collateral, enjoys special priority if perfected within the required time. Unperfected interests are subordinate to perfected ones and may lose to a bankruptcy trustee.

Default and enforcement

On default, a secured party may take possession of the collateral without breaching the peace, and may sell it in a commercially reasonable manner after giving reasonable notice to the debtor. Proceeds are applied to the debt; any surplus goes to the debtor, and the debtor usually remains liable for a deficiency. Special rules protect consumers, and strict foreclosure — keeping the collateral in full satisfaction of the debt — is restricted.
