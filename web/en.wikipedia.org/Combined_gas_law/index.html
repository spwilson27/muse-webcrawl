Combined gas law

The combined gas law is a gas law that combines Charles's law, Boyle's law and Gay-Lussac's law into a single expression relating the pressure, volume and absolute temperature of a fixed amount of gas:

pV / T = k

where p is the pressure, V is the volume, T is the temperature in kelvin, and k is a constant for a given amount of gas. Equivalently, for a gas changing between two states, p1V1/T1 = p2V2/T2.

Component laws

Boyle's law states that pressure and volume are inversely proportional at constant temperature; Charles's law states that volume is proportional to absolute temperature at constant pressure; and Gay-Lussac's law states that pressure is proportional to absolute temperature at constant volume. Each is a special case of the combined law with one variable held fixed.

Relation to the ideal gas law

The combined gas law can be derived from the ideal gas law, pV = nRT, when the amount of gas n is constant. It is widely used in chemistry and physics problems involving gases undergoing simultaneous changes of pressure, volume and temperature.
