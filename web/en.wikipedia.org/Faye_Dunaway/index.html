Dorothy Faye Dunaway (born 14 January 1941) is an American actress. One of the leading stars of the New Hollywood era, she received an Academy Award, three Golden Globe Awards and a BAFTA, and was one of the defining screen performers of the 1970s.

Career

Dunaway began on the stage before breaking through in film as the outlaw Bonnie Parker opposite Warren Beatty in Bonnie and Clyde (1967), a landmark of American cinema that earned her an Academy Award nomination. She followed it with The Thomas Crown Affair (1968) and went on to star in Roman Polanski's Chinatown (1974) as Evelyn Mulwray, and in Sidney Lumet's satire Network (1976), for which she won the Oscar for Best Actress as the ruthless television executive Diana Christensen.

Later work

Her portrayal of Joan Crawford in Mommie Dearest (1981) became notorious and initially damaged her career, though it later gained a cult following. Dunaway continued working in film, television and theatre in the following decades, including roles in Barfly (1987) and Arizona Dream (1993), and returned to the stage in the play Tea at Five.
