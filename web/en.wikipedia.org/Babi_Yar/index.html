Babi Yar

Babi Yar
Location: Ravine near Kyiv, Ukraine
Dates: 1941–1943
Principal massacre: 29–30 September 1941
Victims: 33,771 Jews in the first massacre; over 100,000 total estimated

Babi Yar (Russian: Бабий Яр) or Babyn Yar (Ukrainian: Бабин Яр) is a ravine in the Ukrainian capital Kyiv and a site of massacres carried out by Nazi forces during World War II. In the most infamous of these, on 29–30 September 1941, 33,771 Jews were murdered — one of the largest single mass killings of the Holocaust.

The massacre

Following the German occupation of Kyiv in September 1941, Jews were ordered to assemble near the ravine, ostensibly for resettlement. Over two days, Sonderkommando 4a of Einsatzgruppe C, assisted by German police battalions and Ukrainian auxiliary police, marched groups of victims to the edge of the ravine and shot them; their bodies fell into the ravine below. Killings at the site continued through 1943, with victims including Roma, Soviet prisoners of war, communists, and psychiatric patients; total deaths at Babi Yar are estimated at 100,000 to 150,000.

Memory

After the war, Soviet authorities long suppressed specific commemoration of the Jewish victims. Yevgeny Yevtushenko's 1961 poem "Babi Yar" and Dmitri Shostakovich's Thirteenth Symphony, which set it, drew international attention to the site. A memorial to the Jewish victims was erected in 1991, and the Babyn Yar Holocaust Memorial Center was established to document the site. In March 2022 the area was struck during the Russian invasion of Ukraine, drawing worldwide condemnation.
