Foot

Foot
DetailsTerminal part of the leg in humans and many animals; bears weight and enables locomotion
Bones26 in the human foot: tarsals, metatarsals and phalanges
SystemMusculoskeletal system

The foot (plural: feet) is an anatomical structure found in many vertebrates. It is the terminal portion of a limb which bears weight and allows locomotion. In many animals with feet, the foot is a separate organ at the terminal part of the leg made up of one or more segments or bones, generally including claws or nails.

Human foot

The human foot is a complex mechanical structure containing 26 bones, 33 joints, and more than a hundred muscles, tendons and ligaments. It is divided into the hindfoot (talus and calcaneus), the midfoot (navicular, cuboid, and three cuneiform bones) and the forefoot (metatarsals and toes). The arches of the foot — medial and lateral longitudinal arches and a transverse arch — act as springs, absorbing shock and storing energy during walking and running.

Function

Feet provide support, balance and propulsion. Human bipedalism places unusual demands on the foot, and its structure — including the short toes and large heel bone — distinguishes humans from other primates, whose feet retain grasping ability.

Conditions

Common foot problems include plantar fasciitis, bunions, flat feet, athlete's foot and fractures. The medical specialty concerned with the foot is podiatry.
