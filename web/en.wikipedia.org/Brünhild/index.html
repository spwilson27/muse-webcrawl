Brünhild

Brünhild (Brynhildr) in Norse and Germanic legend. (Image placeholder)

Brünhild, also spelled Brunhild, Brunhilda or Brynhildr (Old Norse: Brynhildr, "armour-battle"), is a female figure from Germanic heroic legend who appears in the Norse Völsunga saga and Poetic Edda, and in the Middle High German Nibelungenlied. She is a shieldmaiden and, in the Norse tradition, a valkyrie.

In Norse legend

In the Völsunga saga, Brynhildr is a valkyrie punished by Odin for disobedience and condemned to sleep within a ring of fire until a hero brave enough to cross the flames awakens her. The hero Sigurd (Siegfried) rides through the fire and they pledge themselves to each other. Later, through trickery — Sigurd takes the shape of the Burgundian king Gunnar to win her for him — Brynhildr marries Gunnar. When she learns of the deception, her wounded pride drives the plot toward Sigurd's murder, after which she kills herself and is burned on his funeral pyre.

In the Nibelungenlied

In the German epic, Brünhild is the immensely strong queen of Iceland who will marry only a man who can defeat her in three contests of strength. King Gunther, aided by Siegfried wearing a cloak of invisibility, wins her; the deception and a later quarrel between Brünhild and Kriemhild over precedence set in motion the Burgundians' destruction.

Legacy

Brünhild is one of the principal characters of Richard Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen (as Brünnhilde), where she is Wotan's daughter and the heroine of Die Walküre and Götterdämmerung. A possible historical origin has been proposed in Brunhilda of Austrasia, the 6th–7th-century Frankish queen.
