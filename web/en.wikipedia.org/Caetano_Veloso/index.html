Caetano Veloso
Born: 7 August 1942, Santo Amaro, Bahia, Brazil
Occupations: Singer, songwriter, guitarist, writer
Genres: MPB, tropicália, bossa nova, rock

Caetano Emanuel Viana Teles Veloso (born 7 August 1942) is a Brazilian composer, singer, guitarist, writer, and political activist. Veloso first became known for his participation in the Brazilian musical movement Tropicalismo, or Tropicália, which encompassed theatre, poetry, and music in the 1960s, at the beginning of the Brazilian military dictatorship. He has remained a constant creative influence and best-selling performing artist and composer ever since.

Life and career

Veloso was born in Santo Amaro, Bahia. His sister is the singer Maria Bethânia. In 1967–68, with Gilberto Gil, Gal Costa, Tom Zé and Os Mutantes, he launched the Tropicália movement, blending Brazilian popular music with rock, psychedelia and avant-garde art. The military regime arrested and jailed Veloso and Gil in 1969, and they spent several years in exile in London before returning to Brazil in 1972.

Work and influence

Veloso's albums, including Caetano Veloso (1968), Transa (1972), Bicho (1977) and Livro (1997), are landmarks of Brazilian music, and his songwriting is celebrated for its poetic lyricism and melodic inventiveness. He has won multiple Grammy and Latin Grammy awards and is regarded internationally as one of the great songwriters of the 20th century.
