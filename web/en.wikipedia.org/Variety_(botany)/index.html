Variety (botany)

In botanical nomenclature, a variety (Latin: varietas, abbreviated var.) is a taxonomic rank below that of species and subspecies, but above that of form. It is used for populations of plants that differ in minor but stable characteristics from the typical form of the species and usually occupy a distinct geographic range or habitat.

Nomenclature

Varietal names are governed by the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants. A variety name consists of the genus, species epithet and a varietal epithet, as in Brassica oleracea var. capitata (cabbage). When a variety is named, the remaining typical form automatically receives the same epithet (an autonym), e.g. var. oleracea.

Distinctions

A variety differs from a subspecies in degree: subspecies usually show stronger geographic or morphological separation. It differs from a cultivar, which is a plant grouping produced by selective breeding and governed by the Cultivated Plant Code, and from a form (forma), a lower rank used for sporadic minor variants such as flower colour sports occurring within populations.
