William Shakespeare (baptised 26 April 1564 – 23 April 1616) was an English playwright, poet and actor, widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language and the world's pre-eminent dramatist. He is often called England's national poet and the "Bard of Avon". His extant works, including collaborations, consist of some 39 plays, 154 sonnets, three long narrative poems and a few other verses.

Life

Shakespeare was born and raised in Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, the son of John Shakespeare, a glove-maker and alderman, and Mary Arden. At 18 he married Anne Hathaway, with whom he had three children: Susanna and twins Hamnet and Judith. Between 1585 and 1592 he began a successful career in London as an actor, writer and part-owner of the playing company the Lord Chamberlain's Men, later known as the King's Men.

Plays

Shakespeare's plays are conventionally divided into comedies, histories and tragedies. His early work included comedies such as A Midsummer Night's Dream and histories such as Richard III and Henry V. Around 1600 he produced the great tragedies: Hamlet, Othello, King Lear and Macbeth. His late plays, including The Tempest and The Winter's Tale, mix tragedy and romance.

Poems

Shakespeare's narrative poems Venus and Adonis (1593) and The Rape of Lucrece (1594) were published during plague closures of the theatres. His 154 sonnets, published in 1609, explore love, beauty, mortality and the passage of time.

Legacy

Shakespeare's works have been translated into every major language and are performed more often than those of any other playwright. His plays were collected in the First Folio of 1623, published by his fellow actors John Heminges and Henry Condell. He retired to Stratford around 1613 and died there in 1616; he is buried in the chancel of Holy Trinity Church.
