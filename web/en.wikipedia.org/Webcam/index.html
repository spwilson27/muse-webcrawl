Webcam

A webcam is a video camera that feeds or streams its image in real time to or through a computer network, such as the Internet. Webcams are typically small cameras that sit on a desk, attach to a monitor, or are built into laptops and other hardware.

History

The first webcam, the Trojan Room coffee pot camera at the University of Cambridge (1991–1993), let researchers check whether a coffee pot was full without leaving their desks. Consumer devices spread in the late 1990s, and Connectix's QuickCam (1994) was an early commercial success. Built-in cameras became standard on laptops in the 2000s.

Uses

Webcams are used for video calling and conferencing, live streaming, video recording, security and monitoring, and computer vision applications. Their ubiquity during the remote-work expansion of 2020 made them essential equipment. Webcams also raise privacy concerns, as malware can activate them surreptitiously; many users fit physical covers.
