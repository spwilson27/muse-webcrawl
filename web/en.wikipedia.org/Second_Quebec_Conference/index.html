Second Quebec Conference

The Second Quebec Conference (codenamed "OCTAGON") was a high-level military conference held during the Second World War between the British and American governments. It took place in Quebec City, Canada, from 12 to 16 September 1944, at the Citadel and the Château Frontenac, with Winston Churchill and Franklin D. Roosevelt as the principal participants and Canadian prime minister William Lyon Mackenzie King as host.

President Roosevelt and Prime Minister Churchill at Quebec, September 1944.

Discussions

It was the second conference at Quebec, following the QUADRANT conference of August 1943. The leaders reviewed the progress of the war in Europe after the Normandy landings and in the Pacific, and agreed on plans for the final phase against Japan, including a major role for British forces in the Pacific and the projected invasion of Japan. They also reached agreement on the continued Lend-Lease support for Britain into the post-surrender period ("Phase II").

The Morgenthau Plan

The conference is chiefly remembered for the initialling of the Morgenthau Plan, proposed by U.S. Treasury Secretary Henry Morgenthau Jr., which envisaged the deindustrialization and partition of postwar Germany into largely agrarian states. Roosevelt and Churchill initialled a memorandum accepting the plan's outline, but after it leaked to the press and provoked controversy, Roosevelt retreated from it within weeks, and it was never implemented.
