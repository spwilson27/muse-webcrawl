Port Arthur, Ontario

Port Arthur was a city in Northern Ontario, Canada, on the north shore of Lake Superior at Thunder Bay. Founded in the 1870s as the Lake Superior terminus of transcontinental transport, it amalgamated with its twin city, Fort William, on 1 January 1970 to form the modern city of Thunder Bay; "Port Arthur" survives as the name of the northern district of that city.

Port Arthur's waterfront on Thunder Bay, Lake Superior.

History

Surveyed in 1875 as the eastern terminus of the Dawson Route and named for Prince Arthur, Duke of Connaught, the settlement boomed when the Canadian Pacific Railway arrived in the 1880s, shipping prairie grain, silver (notably from Silver Islet), and timber. It was incorporated as a town in 1884 and a city in 1907. Its long rivalry with Fort William — over railway facilities, grain elevators, and amalgamation itself, debated for decades — ended only with the provincially ordered merger approved by plebiscite in 1969.

Today

The Port Arthur side of Thunder Bay contains the downtown north core, Marina Park on the waterfront, Hillcrest Park with views of the Sleeping Giant, and much of the city's grain elevator row. The former Port Arthur City Hall and other heritage structures recall its independent existence.
