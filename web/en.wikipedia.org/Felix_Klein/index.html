Christian Felix Klein (25 April 1849 – 22 June 1925) was a German mathematician and mathematics educator, known for his work with group theory, complex analysis, non-Euclidean geometry, and the associations between geometry and group theory. His 1872 Erlangen program, classifying geometries by their underlying symmetry groups, was influential in unifying much of the mathematics of the time and remained the standard method of characterising geometries well into the 20th century.

Life and work

Klein studied at Bonn under Rudolf Lipschitz and Julius Plücker, and held chairs at Erlangen (1872), Munich, Leipzig (1880) and finally Göttingen (1886), where he built a world-leading centre of mathematics and recruited David Hilbert. His name is attached to the Klein bottle, the one-sided surface he described in 1882; the Klein four-group; and the Klein quartic. His lectures on the icosahedron linked geometry with Galois theory.

Health problems after 1882 shifted his energy toward organisation and education: he presided over the great Encyklopädie der mathematischen Wissenschaften, founded institutes at Göttingen, and led reforms of mathematics teaching in German schools ("Meraner Reform", 1905), pressing for the early introduction of calculus and functional thinking.
