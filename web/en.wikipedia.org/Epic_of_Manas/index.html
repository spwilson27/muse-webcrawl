The Epic of Manas (Kyrgyz: Манас дастаны) is a cycle of epic poems central to the culture of the Kyrgyz people of Central Asia. It recounts the deeds of the hero Manas, who unites the scattered Kyrgyz tribes, and of his son Semetey and grandson Seytek. The trilogy is one of the longest epic poems in the world: the fullest recorded versions run to more than 500,000 lines, far exceeding the combined length of the Iliad and the Odyssey.

Content

The first part tells how Manas, born to the childless Jakyp, grows into a mighty warrior who unites the forty Kyrgyz tribes, wins back their homeland in the Ala-Too mountains, and battles enemies including the Kalmyks and the forces of China. The second part follows his son Semetey, raised in exile, who returns to claim his inheritance; the third follows his grandson Seytek, who restores the family's fortunes. The poems blend heroic warfare with feasts, marriages, funerals, and counsel, and preserve a portrait of nomadic life on the steppe.

Oral tradition and the manaschi

The epic was transmitted orally by specialist bards called manaschi, who recite it in a rhythmic, chanting delivery that can last for days. Celebrated manaschi of the twentieth century included Sagymbai Orozbakov and Sayakbai Karalaev, whose dictated versions form the basis of most published texts. The epic's roots are debated: some scholars trace its origins to the era of the Kyrgyz Khaganate of the ninth century, while much of its material reflects the fifteenth to eighteenth centuries, when the Kyrgyz fought the Oirat Mongols.

Cultural significance

Manas is the national hero of Kyrgyzstan. His name is borne by the country's main airport, a university, and an order of chivalry; a monumental statue of Manas stands in Bishkek. In 2013 the Kyrgyz epic trilogy of Manas, Semetey, and Seytek was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Elements of the epic have inspired opera, ballet, film, and a large body of scholarship.
