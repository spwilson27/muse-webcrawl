Spitsbergen

Spitsbergen (formerly known as West Spitsbergen; Norwegian: Spitsbergen, Russian: Шпицберген) is the largest and the only permanently populated island of the Svalbard archipelago in northern Norway in the Arctic Ocean.

Geography

The island covers about 37,673 km² (14,546 sq mi), making it the largest island in Norway and the 36th-largest in the world. Its administrative center is Longyearbyen, one of the world's northernmost settlements. More than half the island is covered by glaciers; the terrain is mountainous, as suggested by the name, which means "pointed mountains" in Dutch, given by explorer Willem Barentsz, who sighted the island in 1596.

History and economy

Spitsbergen was a base for whaling in the 17th and 18th centuries and later for coal mining by Norwegian and Russian companies; the Russian settlement of Barentsburg remains active. Under the Svalbard Treaty of 1920, Norway has sovereignty but citizens of signatory states have equal rights of economic access. Today the economy centers on coal mining (declining), research, tourism, and satellite services; the Svalbard Global Seed Vault is built into a mountain near Longyearbyen.

Wildlife

The island hosts polar bears, Svalbard reindeer, Arctic foxes, walruses, and large seabird colonies.
