Fox sisters

The Fox sisters were three sisters from Rochester, New York, who played an important role in the creation of Spiritualism: Leah (1813–1890), Margaretta (also called Maggie) (1833–1893), and Catherine (also called Kate) Fox (1837–1892). The two younger sisters used "rappings" to convince their older sister and others that they were communicating with spirits. Their older sister then took charge of them and managed their careers for some time. They all enjoyed success as mediums for many years.

In 1888, Margaretta confessed that the rappings had been a hoax and publicly demonstrated how the sisters produced the sounds by cracking their toe and knee joints. Margaretta later attempted to retract her confession, and the sisters' reputations were ruined regardless. They died in poverty within five years. Spiritualism, the movement they had helped to launch, continued without them.

The Hydesville events

In March 1848, in a house in Hydesville, New York, the Fox family reported unexplained knocking sounds. Young Kate and Maggie claimed the noises were made by the spirit of a murdered peddler supposedly buried in the cellar, and developed a code of raps to answer questions. Neighbours and investigators flocked to the house. In 1849 the sisters gave public demonstrations in Rochester, launching their careers as mediums under Leah's management.

Careers as mediums

The sisters toured widely and held séances for paying clients, helping to ignite the Spiritualist movement in the United States and Britain. Adherents — eventually numbering in the millions — included figures such as newspaper editor Horace Greeley and the chemist William Crookes, who investigated and endorsed Kate. Sceptics, including a Buffalo medical faculty committee in 1851, attributed the raps to the sisters' joints, but public belief persisted.

Confession and decline

Alcoholism and feuds over money divided the sisters. On 21 October 1888 Margaretta, paid by a newspaper, confessed before a capacity audience at the New York Academy of Music and demonstrated cracking the joints of her toes to produce the raps. She retracted the confession the following year, but the damage was done. Maggie died in 1893 and Kate in 1892, both impoverished. In 1904 schoolchildren playing in the ruined Hydesville house found skeletal remains behind a cellar wall, which believers cited as the peddler's skeleton — though the wall and remains have also been explained naturalistically.

Legacy

Historians credit the Fox sisters with catalysing modern Spiritualism and the professional mediumship industry. Their confession is regarded as one of the most famous exposures of mediumistic fraud, yet organised Spiritualist churches still exist and revere them as founders.
