UCAS

The Universities and Colleges Admissions Service (UCAS) is the United Kingdom's shared admissions service for higher education. Based in Cheltenham, Gloucestershire, UCAS operates the application process for nearly all full-time undergraduate courses at UK universities and colleges, as well as services for postgraduate teacher training (UCAS Teacher Training) and conservatoires.

History

UCAS was formed in 1993 by the merger of the Universities Central Council on Admissions (UCCA, founded 1961) and the Polytechnics and Colleges Admissions Service (PCAS), following the Further and Higher Education Act 1992, which granted university status to polytechnics. Applications moved fully online in the early 2000s.

Application process

Applicants typically apply for up to five courses through a single online application, including a personal statement and an academic reference. The standard deadline for most courses is in late January; applications for medicine, dentistry, veterinary science, and for the University of Oxford and University of Cambridge close in mid-October. Universities make conditional or unconditional offers, and applicants select one firm and one insurance choice. Places are confirmed when examination results (such as A-levels) are published; applicants who miss their offers may enter Clearing, the system matching unplaced students to courses with vacancies.

Tariff

The UCAS Tariff converts qualifications and grades into points used by some institutions in making offers. The tariff was reformed in 2017 so that, for example, an A* at A-level is worth 56 points and an A 48 points.

Other services

UCAS also runs UCAS Media, provides statistics on admissions through its analysis reports, and operates the apprenticeship and further education search tools. Its end-of-cycle reports are a principal source of data on participation in UK higher education.
