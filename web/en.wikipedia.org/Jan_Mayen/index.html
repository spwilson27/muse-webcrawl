Jan Mayen is a Norwegian volcanic island in the Arctic Ocean, situated between the Greenland Sea and the Norwegian Sea, about 600 km north-east of Iceland and 500 km east of Greenland. The island is 55 km long and partially covered by glaciers around Beerenberg, the world's northernmost active volcano above sea level.

History

The island may have been known to Norse sailors, but its documented rediscovery came in 1614, when it was named after Dutch whaling captain Jan Jacobszoon May van Schellinkhout. Dutch whalers used it as a summer blubber-rendering station in the 17th century. Norway assumed administration in 1929–1930, and a meteorological station has operated since 1921.

Geography and nature

Jan Mayen is a nature reserve, protected since 2010. It hosts large seabird colonies and a small number of Arctic foxes and polar bears visiting on drift ice. The only occupants are personnel of the Norwegian Armed Forces and the Norwegian Meteorological Institute at the Olonkinbyen station.

Beerenberg

Beerenberg (2,277 m) erupted in 1970 and 1985, and remains active; its glaciers calve into the sea on the island's northern half.
