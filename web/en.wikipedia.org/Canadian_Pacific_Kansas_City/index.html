Canadian Pacific Kansas City

Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC) is a Class I freight railway operating in Canada, the United States, and Mexico. Headquartered in Calgary, Alberta, it was created on 14 April 2023 by the merger of Canadian Pacific Railway (CP) and Kansas City Southern (KCS), forming the first — and currently only — single-line rail network connecting all three USMCA countries, with roughly 32,000 km (20,000 mi) of track.

Formation

Canadian Pacific announced in March 2021 that it would acquire Kansas City Southern for about US$29 billion, prevailing over a rival bid from Canadian National after KCS terminated the CN agreement in September 2021. The U.S. Surface Transportation Board approved the combination on 15 March 2023, and the two railways merged a month later under CEO Keith Creel, who had led CP since 2017.

Network

The combined network runs from Vancouver and Montreal through Chicago, Kansas City, and Dallas to Mexico City, Veracruz, and the port of Lázaro Cárdenas. It carries grain, energy, automobiles, intermodal containers, and chemicals, and benefits from growing North American nearshoring trade, particularly between Mexico and the United States and Canada.

History of predecessors

Canadian Pacific, founded in 1881, built Canada's first transcontinental railway, completed at Craigellachie, British Columbia, in 1885. Kansas City Southern, founded in 1887, grew into the smallest Class I railway in the United States but a strategic one, with its Mexican concession (TFM, later KCSM) reaching the Pacific port of Lázaro Cárdenas.
