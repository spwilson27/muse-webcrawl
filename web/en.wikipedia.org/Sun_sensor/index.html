Sun sensor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A sun sensor is an optical instrument used by spacecraft and other aerospace vehicles for detecting the position of the Sun. Sun sensors are used in attitude determination and control systems (ADCS) to provide a reference vector toward the Sun, allowing the vehicle to orient solar panels, antennas, or instruments and to maintain a known attitude.

Types

Sun sensors are classified by the precision and format of their output. Coarse sun sensors, often built from photovoltaic cells mounted on several faces of a spacecraft, provide a rough Sun direction and are valued for their simplicity and reliability, including for safe-mode recovery. Fine sun sensors use position-sensitive detectors, coded apertures, or imaging arrays behind a slit or pinhole to measure the Sun's direction to accuracies of arcminutes or better, sometimes outputting a digital signal directly.

Operation

A typical fine sun sensor admits sunlight through a narrow slit onto a linear detector array. The position of the illuminated spot on the array indicates the angle of incidence in one axis; two crossed sensors, or a two-dimensional array, provide both angles. The electronics convert the measurement into a Sun unit vector in the spacecraft body frame, which the attitude controller combines with data from other instruments, such as magnetometers and star trackers.

Applications

Sun sensors have flown on most Earth-orbiting satellites and interplanetary probes since the early space age. Because they are lightweight, consume little power, and have no moving parts, they remain standard equipment even on spacecraft that also carry more precise star trackers. They are also used on high-altitude balloons, solar-tracking ground installations, and some aircraft.
