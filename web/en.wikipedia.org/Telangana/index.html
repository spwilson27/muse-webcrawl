Telangana is a state in south-central India, formed on 2 June 2014 as the 29th state of India when it was separated from the north-western portion of Andhra Pradesh after a decades-long movement for statehood. It is bordered by Maharashtra to the north, Chhattisgarh to the north-east, Karnataka to the west, and the residuary Andhra Pradesh to the south and east. Its capital and largest city is Hyderabad, a major centre of technology and pharmaceuticals.

History

The region was ruled successively by the Satavahanas, the Kakatiyas (whose capital was Warangal), the Delhi Sultanate, the Bahmani Sultanate, and the Qutb Shahi dynasty of Golconda, before the Asaf Jahi Nizams ruled the princely state of Hyderabad from 1724 until its integration into India in 1948. In 1956 Telangana was merged with Andhra State to form a united Telugu-speaking Andhra Pradesh, despite misgivings recorded by the States Reorganisation Commission; grievances over water, jobs, and funds fuelled a separate-state movement that intensified after 2001 under K. Chandrashekar Rao's Telangana Rashtra Samithi, leading to the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act of 2014.

Geography and economy

Telangana lies on the Deccan Plateau, drained by the Godavari and Krishna rivers, with a hot, semi-arid climate. Its economy combines Hyderabad's IT and life-sciences sectors ("Genome Valley") with agriculture — rice, cotton, and maize — supported by large irrigation projects such as the Kaleshwaram Lift Irrigation Project on the Godavari.

Culture

Telugu is the predominant language, with a significant Urdu-speaking minority in Hyderabad. Festivals distinctive to the state include Bathukamma and Bonalu; the state is known for Warangal's Kakatiya heritage, the Ramappa Temple (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), and Hyderabadi cuisine such as biryani.
