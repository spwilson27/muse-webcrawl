The Green Mountain Boys were a militia organization formed in the 1770s in the territory between the British provinces of New York and New Hampshire, known as the New Hampshire Grants and later the state of Vermont. Led by Ethan Allen and members of his extended family, they resisted New York's attempts to control the territory.

American Revolution

On 10 May 1775, the Green Mountain Boys under Ethan Allen, together with Benedict Arnold, captured Fort Ticonderoga from the British in the first offensive American victory of the Revolutionary War; the fort's cannon were later hauled to Boston by Henry Knox. Seth Warner's regiment of Green Mountain Boys fought in the invasion of Canada and at the Battle of Hubbardton and Battle of Bennington in 1777.

Legacy

The Green Mountain Boys were instrumental in securing Vermont's de facto independence, declared in 1777, before it joined the Union in 1791. Their name is carried today by the Vermont National Guard, and their flag—a green field with blue canton and stars—remains a symbol of Vermont.
