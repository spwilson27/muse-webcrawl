Alappuzha district is one of the fourteen districts of Kerala state in south-western India. Formed as Alleppey district on 17 August 1957, it is the smallest district of Kerala by area and one of the most densely populated. Its headquarters is the coastal city of Alappuzha, long celebrated — in a phrase popularised by Lord Curzon — as the "Venice of the East" for its network of canals, lagoons, and backwaters.

Geography

The district is a narrow strip of land between the Arabian Sea and the Vembanad Lake, India's longest lake, laced with rivers and canals. The Kuttanad region, often called the "rice bowl of Kerala", lies below sea level and is one of the few places in the world where farming is carried out well below mean sea level, behind protective dykes. Punnamada Lake at Alappuzha hosts the annual Nehru Trophy Boat Race, in which long snake boats (chundan vallams) compete each August.

Economy

Alappuzha is the historic centre of Kerala's coir industry, processing coconut fibre into mats, matting, and yarn, though the sector has declined with mechanisation and competition. Agriculture (paddy and coconut), fishing, and tourism — particularly houseboat cruises on the backwaters — are mainstays. The district has a high literacy rate and a notable history of labour organisation and communist politics in twentieth-century Kerala.
