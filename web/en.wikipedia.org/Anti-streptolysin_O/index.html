Anti-streptolysin O

Anti-streptolysin O (ASO or ASLO) is an antibody produced by the immune system against streptolysin O, a toxin secreted by group A streptococcus bacteria. Measuring the ASO level in blood — the ASO titre test — provides evidence of a recent streptococcal infection.

Clinical use

Antibodies rise roughly one to three weeks after infection, peak at three to five weeks and decline over months. A high or rising ASO titre supports the diagnosis of post-streptococcal complications such as rheumatic fever, scarlet fever and post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, especially where the original throat or skin infection was not cultured. The test is often paired with anti-DNase B measurement, which is more sensitive after skin infections.

Interpretation

Reference values vary with age and geography; values above about 200 IU/mL in adults (higher in children) are generally considered elevated. A single raised titre shows past exposure rather than active infection, and modest elevations can occur in healthy carriers, so results are interpreted alongside the clinical picture.
