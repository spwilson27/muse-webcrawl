Bernard Bolzano

Bernard Placidus Johann Nepomuk Bolzano (5 October 1781 – 18 December 1848) was a Bohemian mathematician, logician, philosopher, theologian, and Catholic priest of Italian and German descent, known for his contributions to mathematics and philosophy, many of which were recognised only long after his death.

Life

Bolzano was born and spent his life in Prague. He studied philosophy and mathematics at the Charles University in Prague and was ordained a priest in 1805, taking up a specially created chair of philosophy of religion. His liberal sermons and views displeased the Habsburg authorities, and in 1819 he was dismissed from his post and placed under police surveillance; he devoted the rest of his life to scholarship in retirement, supported by friends.

Mathematics

In his Rein analytischer Beweis (1817) Bolzano gave a purely analytic proof of the intermediate value theorem and an early rigorous definition of continuity, freeing analysis from appeals to geometry and intuition. He gave the first purely analytic definition of a continuous function and described a continuous function that is nowhere differentiable — an example later rediscovered by Weierstrass. The Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem on convergent sequences bears his name. His posthumous Paradoxien des Unendlichen (1851) investigated the paradoxes of infinite sets and influenced Cantor's set theory.

Philosophy and logic

His four-volume Wissenschaftslehre ("Theory of Science", 1837) developed a theory of objective propositions (Sätze an sich), ideas, and logical consequence, anticipating themes later central to Husserl and analytic philosophy. Bolzano also wrote on ethics and social reform, advocating a form of utopian socialism in On the Best State.
