Tihar (festival)

Tihar (Nepali: तिहार), also known as Deepawali and Yamapanchak, is a five-day Hindu festival celebrated in Nepal and in parts of India by ethnic Nepali communities. It is the festival of lights, in which homes, courtyards and temples are decorated with oil lamps, candles and strands of electric bulbs, and marigold and globe amaranth garlands are hung across doorways. Tihar honours not only the goddess Lakshmi and the god of death Yama, but also the animals associated with them — the crow, the dog, the cow and the ox.

The five days

  • Day 1 — Kaag Tihar (crow day): Crows, regarded as messengers of Yama, are offered sweets and food on rooftops so that they will carry away sadness and avert misfortune.
  • Day 2 — Kukur Tihar (dog day): Dogs, both pets and strays, are garlanded, marked with a red tika and given special food in honour of their loyalty and their role as guardians of the gates of the afterlife.
  • Day 3 — Gai Tihar and Lakshmi Puja: Cows are worshipped in the morning; in the evening, Lakshmi, goddess of wealth, is welcomed into cleaned and lamp-lit homes. This is the central day of the festival.
  • Day 4 — Goru Tihar (ox day): Oxen are honoured; among the Newar community this day is also Mha Puja, the worship of one's own body and inner self, marking the start of the Nepal Sambat new year.
  • Day 5 — Bhai Tika (brothers' day): Sisters place a distinctive seven-coloured tika on their brothers' foreheads, pray for their long life, and exchange gifts and food. Brothers in turn pledge to protect their sisters.

Customs

Throughout Tihar, groups of girls and boys go from house to house singing traditional songs — bhailo (sung mainly by girls) and deusi (sung mainly by boys) — and are rewarded with money, rice and sweets. Elaborate mandalas of coloured powder are drawn on floors, and in the Kathmandu Valley the festival overlaps with the Newar celebration of Swanti. Fireworks, card games and family feasts fill the evenings, and government offices close for the holiday period.

Significance

Tihar is associated with the story of Yama and his sister Yamuna, whose prayers are said to have saved her brother's life — the origin of the Bhai Tika rite. The festival is also read as a celebration of the bonds between humans, animals and gods, and it falls in the post-harvest season, when Lakshmi is thanked for abundance. It is Nepal's second most important Hindu festival after Dashain.
