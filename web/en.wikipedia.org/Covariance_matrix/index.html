Covariance matrix

In probability theory and statistics, a covariance matrix (also known as an auto-covariance matrix, dispersion matrix, variance matrix, or variance–covariance matrix) is a square matrix giving the covariance between each pair of elements of a given random vector. Intuitively, the covariance matrix generalizes the notion of variance to multiple dimensions: its diagonal entries are the variances of the individual variables, and its off-diagonal entries are the covariances between pairs of variables.

Definition

For a random vector X = (X₁, …, Xₚ) with means μ, the covariance matrix Σ has entries Σᵢⱼ = cov(Xᵢ, Xⱼ) = E[(Xᵢ − μᵢ)(Xⱼ − μⱼ)]. It can be written compactly as Σ = E[(X − E[X])(X − E[X])ᵀ]. Every covariance matrix is symmetric and positive semi-definite; conversely, any symmetric positive semi-definite matrix is the covariance matrix of some random vector.

Estimation

Given a sample of observations, the sample covariance matrix is computed from the deviations of each observation from the sample mean, dividing by n − 1 for an unbiased estimator. Estimation becomes difficult when the number of variables approaches or exceeds the number of observations, motivating shrinkage estimators such as the Ledoit–Wolf estimator.

Applications

Covariance matrices are central to multivariate statistics and machine learning: they define the multivariate normal distribution, underlie principal component analysis (whose components are the matrix's eigenvectors), determine Mahalanobis distance, and appear in Kalman filters, Gaussian processes, portfolio theory, and the propagation of measurement uncertainty.
