Coreference

Coreference
FieldLinguistics; natural language processing
Related conceptsAnaphora, cataphora, binding

In "The trophy doesn't fit in the suitcase because it is too big", resolving "it" requires coreference reasoning.

In linguistics, coreference occurs when two or more expressions in a text refer to the same person, thing, or event in the world. The referring expressions are said to be coreferential. Typical examples involve pronouns and definite noun phrases: in "Mary said she was tired", Mary and she corefer.

Types

When the referring expression follows the expression it refers to, the relation is an anaphoric one; the pronoun is an anaphor and the earlier expression its antecedent. When the order is reversed, the relation is called cataphora, as in "When he arrived, John sat down." Linguists also distinguish bound-variable anaphora, in which a pronoun behaves like a variable bound by a quantifier ("Every farmer who owns a donkey beats it"), from strict coreference with a referential antecedent.

Theoretical treatment

Generative grammar has traditionally analysed coreference through binding theory, which constrains when pronouns, reflexives, and full noun phrases may or must corefer within a sentence. Semantic approaches instead model discourse referents that referring expressions introduce and pick up, as in discourse representation theory.

Computational linguistics

Coreference resolution — automatically identifying all expressions that refer to each entity in a text — is a core task in natural language processing, important for information extraction, question answering, and machine translation. Classic difficulties include Winograd schemas, sentence pairs whose correct resolution depends on world knowledge. Modern systems use machine learning and neural language models, evaluated on shared corpora with metrics such as MUC, B³, and CEAF.
