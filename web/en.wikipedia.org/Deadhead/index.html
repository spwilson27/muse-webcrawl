Deadhead

Deadhead or Dead Head is the name given to fans of the American rock band the Grateful Dead. The term emerged in the early 1970s and became widely recognized when notices on the band's albums—beginning with a 1971 album insert urging "Dead Heads unite"—encouraged fans to write in for tour news.

Culture

Deadheads were known for following the band on tour, attending dozens or hundreds of shows, collecting and trading live tape recordings (with the band's blessing, tapers were given a dedicated section), and developing a distinctive parking-lot marketplace culture of tie-dyed clothing, crafts, and vegetarian food. Because the Grateful Dead rarely repeated setlists, fans could attend consecutive shows and hear different songs each night.

After 1995

Following Jerry Garcia's death in 1995, the community persisted through successor acts such as Dead & Company, Phil Lesh and Friends, and Furthur, as well as through the band's extensive official live archive releases. Scholars have studied Deadheads as an example of participatory fan culture and gift economy.

Other uses

"Deadheading" also refers to the airline and transport practice of crew traveling as passengers to reposition for duty, and in horticulture to removing spent flowers to encourage further blooming.
