Frederic Augustus Thesiger, 2nd Baron Chelmsford (31 May 1827 – 9 April 1905) was a British Army general, commander of British forces during the Anglo-Zulu War of 1879. His name is chiefly associated with the disaster at the Battle of Isandlwana and the subsequent British victory at Ulundi.

Early career

Thesiger was the son of Frederic Thesiger, 1st Baron Chelmsford, a former Lord Chancellor. Educated at Eton, he took a commission in the Rifle Brigade and then the Grenadier Guards, serving in the Crimean War, the Indian Mutiny of 1857, and the expedition to Abyssinia in 1868, where he was deputy adjutant-general. In the Ninth Cape Frontier War (1877–78) he commanded British forces in South Africa, gaining promotion and a reputation for methodical, if cautious, generalship.

Anglo-Zulu War

When war with the Zulu kingdom of Cetshwayo broke out in January 1879, Chelmsford invaded Zululand in three columns. On 22 January 1879, while Chelmsford was absent from his central column's camp with part of the force, the Zulu army overwhelmed and annihilated the camp at Isandlwana — one of the worst defeats ever suffered by a British army against an indigenous force. The same day, a small garrison held out at Rorke's Drift. Chelmsford was heavily criticised at home and superseded in overall command by Sir Garnet Wolseley, but before Wolseley could take over he defeated the Zulus decisively at the Battle of Ulundi on 4 July 1879, burning the royal kraal and effectively ending the war.

Later life

Despite Ulundi, Chelmsford never held another major field command. He served as Lieutenant of the Tower of London and was promoted general, dying in 1905. His eldest son, Frederic John Napier Thesiger, became the 1st Viscount Chelmsford and Viceroy of India.
