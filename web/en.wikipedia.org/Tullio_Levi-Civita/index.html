Tullio Levi-Civita

Born29 March 1873, Padua, Italy
Died29 December 1941, Rome, Italy
Known forTensor calculus, Levi-Civita connection, Levi-Civita symbol

Tullio Levi-Civita (1873–1941) was an Italian mathematician, most famous for his work on absolute differential calculus (tensor calculus) and its applications to the theory of relativity.

Work

With his teacher Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro, Levi-Civita developed the tensor calculus presented in their 1900 memoir, which became the mathematical language of Einstein's general theory of relativity. He introduced the concept of parallel transport of vectors on curved manifolds, defining what is now called the Levi-Civita connection, and the antisymmetric Levi-Civita symbol bears his name.

Later life

Levi-Civita taught at Padua and Rome. In 1938 he was expelled from his university chair under the Fascist racial laws because he was Jewish, and he died in Rome in 1941.
