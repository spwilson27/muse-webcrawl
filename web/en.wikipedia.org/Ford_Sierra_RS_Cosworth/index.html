The Ford Sierra RS Cosworth is a high-performance version of the Ford Sierra developed by Ford Europe's Special Vehicle Engineering (SVE) division with engine builder Cosworth, launched in 1986. Built as a homologation special for Group A touring car racing, it became one of the most successful racing saloons of its era and an iconic performance car, famous for its large rear "whale tail" spoiler.

Development and specifications

The car used a 1,993 cc Cosworth YBD/YBB engine — a twin-overhead-camshaft, 16-valve unit developed from Ford's Pinto block and fitted with a Garrett turbocharger — producing 204 hp (152 kW) in road trim, enough for a top speed around 149 mph (240 km/h) and 0–60 mph in about 6.5 seconds. The three-door body gained a body kit and the signature biplane rear wing, which generated genuine downforce. Ford was required to build 5,000 examples for homologation, and demand exceeded supply.

RS500

In 1987 Ford released the Sierra RS500 Cosworth, an evolution run of 500 cars built by Aston Martin Tickford, with a larger turbocharger, uprated intercooler, and engine changes enabling far greater racing power — up to around 550 hp in competition trim. The RS500 dominated touring car racing, winning the 1987 World Touring Car Championship manufacturers' title for Ford and countless national series, including the British Touring Car Championship and Australia's Bathurst 1000 (1988 and 1989 with Dick Johnson Racing).

Later versions

From 1988 the Cosworth became a four-door Sapphire saloon, and in 1990 a four-wheel-drive version, the Sierra RS Cosworth 4×4, was introduced partly to support Ford's rallying programme, in which the rear-wheel-drive Sierra had struggled for traction against 4WD rivals. Production ended in 1992, when the Escort RS Cosworth succeeded it as Ford's performance flagship.
