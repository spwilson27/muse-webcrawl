Pennsylvania Station, commonly known as Penn Station, is the main intercity railroad station in New York City and the busiest transportation facility in the Western Hemisphere, serving more than 600,000 passengers per weekday. Located in Midtown Manhattan beneath Madison Square Garden, it is served by Amtrak, NJ Transit, and the Long Island Rail Road.

The original station

The original Pennsylvania Station, opened in 1910 by the Pennsylvania Railroad, was a Beaux-Arts masterpiece designed by McKim, Mead & White, with a vast travertine waiting room modelled on the Baths of Caracalla and a steel-and-glass train shed. It enabled direct rail access to Manhattan through tunnels under the Hudson and East Rivers.

Demolition and replacement

Facing declining rail revenues, the railroad demolished the head house between 1963 and 1966 to make way for Madison Square Garden and an office tower, leaving the tracks and platforms in a cramped underground station. The destruction of the celebrated building galvanized the historic preservation movement and led to New York's Landmarks Law (1965) and the saving of Grand Central Terminal.

Recent developments

Moynihan Train Hall, an expansion into the adjacent James A. Farley Building, opened on January 1, 2021, providing a new Amtrak concourse recalling the grandeur of the original. Further redevelopment of the station and the surrounding Penn District remains a major planning project.
