Microclimate

Microclimate
ScaleFrom centimetres to a few kilometres
InfluencesTopography, soil, vegetation, water, buildings
FieldClimatology, ecology, agriculture

A microclimate is a local atmospheric zone whose climate differs from that of the surrounding area. Microclimates can occur over a few square metres — beneath a tree canopy or beside a sun-warmed wall — or across entire city districts. The term may also refer to the climate within a confined space such as a greenhouse or a building.

Factors

Slope and aspect determine how much solar radiation a surface receives; south-facing slopes in the northern hemisphere are warmer and drier. Soil colour and moisture, vegetation cover, proximity to water, and wind shelter all modify temperature and humidity. Cold air drainage can create frost hollows in valleys, while urban areas form urban heat islands, with temperatures several degrees higher than surrounding countryside.

Ecology and agriculture

Microclimates are vital refuges for species at the edges of their ranges and influence where organisms can survive as the wider climate changes. Gardeners and farmers exploit microclimates by planting tender crops against warm walls, in sheltered courtyards or on favourable slopes; viticulture in particular depends on the microclimates of individual vineyard plots, an aspect of terroir.

Urban microclimates

Urban design increasingly manages microclimate through shade trees, green roofs, reflective surfaces and ventilation corridors to reduce heat stress and energy demand.
