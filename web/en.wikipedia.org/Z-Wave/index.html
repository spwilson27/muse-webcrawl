Z-Wave

The Z-Wave logo

Z-Wave is a wireless communications protocol used primarily for residential and commercial home automation. It is a mesh network using low-energy radio waves to communicate from device to device, allowing for wireless control of smart home devices such as smart lights, security systems, thermostats, sensors, door locks, and garage door openers. The Z-Wave standard was developed by Zensys, a Danish company, and later acquired by Sigma Designs and then Silicon Labs. The Z-Wave Alliance oversees certification and interoperability of devices bearing the Z-Wave logo.

Z-Wave operates in the sub-gigahertz frequency bands (around 800–900 MHz, varying by region), which avoids interference with the crowded 2.4 GHz band used by Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and Zigbee, and allows signals to penetrate walls more effectively. A Z-Wave network can support up to 232 devices and route messages through up to four hops between mains-powered nodes.

Technical specifications

Devices communicate at data rates of 9.6, 40, and 100 kbit/s. Z-Wave uses a source-routed mesh network topology in which a central controller establishes routes between nodes. The protocol supports AES-128 encryption; the S2 security framework, introduced in 2017, is mandatory for certification and provides improved security and efficiency over the earlier S0 framework.

Z-Wave Long Range

Announced in 2020, Z-Wave Long Range (Z-Wave LR) extends the topology to a star network with a range of up to a mile (1.6 km) line-of-sight and support for up to 4,000 nodes on a single network, targeting larger homes, multi-dwelling units, and commercial installations.

Interoperability

A central selling point of Z-Wave is certified interoperability: all certified devices must work with any certified controller, regardless of manufacturer or product generation. Thousands of certified products are on the market from hundreds of manufacturers.
