Aktion T4

Duration: 1939 – 1941 (officially); killings continued to 1945

Administered from: Tiergartenstraße 4, Berlin

Victims: approx. 70,000 in the official phase; up to 300,000 overall

Aktion T4 was the Nazi programme of involuntary euthanasia — the systematic murder of people with physical and mental disabilities in Nazi Germany. The name derives from the address of its Berlin headquarters at Tiergartenstraße 4. The programme was authorised by Adolf Hitler in 1939 and run under Philipp Bouhler and Karl Brandt.

The programme

Beginning with the "child euthanasia" programme, the killing expanded to adults in psychiatric institutions. Questionnaires were circulated to asylums, and panels of doctors marked patients for death without examining them. Victims were transported to six killing centres — including Hadamar, Hartheim, Grafeneck, and Brandenburg — where they were murdered in gas chambers using carbon monoxide, cremated, and their families sent falsified death certificates and urns of ashes.

Opposition and aftermath

Public knowledge of the killings grew, and protests — most famously the sermon of Bishop Clemens von Galen in August 1941 — contributed to Hitler's official stop order of 24 August 1941, after 70,273 people had been recorded as killed. Decentralised killing by starvation and lethal medication ("wild euthanasia") continued until 1945, bringing the total number of victims to between 250,000 and 300,000. Personnel and gassing expertise from T4 were later transferred to the Operation Reinhard extermination camps of the Holocaust. Several perpetrators were tried after the war, including in the Hadamar trial and the Doctors' Trial at Nuremberg.
