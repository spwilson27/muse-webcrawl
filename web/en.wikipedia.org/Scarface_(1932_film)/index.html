Scarface, also known as Scarface: The Shame of the Nation, is a 1932 American pre-Code gangster film directed by Howard Hawks and produced by Howard Hughes. Loosely based on Armitage Trail's 1929 novel of the same name, the film stars Paul Muni as Italian immigrant gangster Antonio "Tony" Camonte, a character inspired by Al Capone.

Plot

Tony Camonte, a bodyguard and enforcer for Chicago crime boss Johnny Lovo, murders rival boss "Big" Louis Costillo and rises through the underworld during Prohibition. As he seizes control of the city's bootlegging trade, his brutality and ambition bring him into conflict with the police, rival gangs and his own boss, while his possessive protectiveness toward his sister Cesca leads to tragedy and his eventual downfall.

Production and censorship

The screenplay was written principally by Ben Hecht. The film's graphic violence sparked a prolonged battle with censors, delaying release for about two years and requiring alternate scenes and the addition of the subtitle "The Shame of the Nation" along with a moralising preface. Even so, some states banned the film outright.

Legacy

Scarface is regarded as one of the defining gangster films of its era, alongside Little Caesar and The Public Enemy. In 1994 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry. Brian De Palma's 1983 film Scarface is a loose remake that updates the story to the Miami cocaine trade.
