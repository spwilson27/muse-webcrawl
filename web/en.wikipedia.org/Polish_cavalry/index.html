Polish cavalry

CountryPoland / Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth
Notable unitsWinged hussars, ulans
Famous engagementsKircholm (1605), Vienna (1683), Somosierra (1808)

The Polish cavalry was for centuries the most celebrated arm of the Polish military, dominating the battlefields of Eastern Europe from the sixteenth to the eighteenth century and retaining a storied role into the twentieth. Its elite formation, the winged hussars, is often called the finest heavy cavalry of its age.

Winged hussars

The husaria developed in the late sixteenth century into heavily armoured shock cavalry, famous for the wooden frames of feathers attached to their backs and for their long lances. They won crushing victories at Kircholm (1605) and Klushino (1610) and led the great charge that relieved Vienna in 1683 under King John III Sobieski.

Nineteenth century

Polish light cavalry — the ulans — served throughout Europe during the Napoleonic Wars; the Polish lancers of the Imperial Guard made the celebrated charge at Somosierra in 1808. The uhlan became a standard cavalry type in Prussian, Austrian, and Russian service.

Twentieth century

Polish cavalry fought in the Polish–Soviet War, including the 1920 Battle of Komarów, one of the last great cavalry battles. In 1939 Polish brigades fought mostly as mobile infantry, using horses for transport; the famous charge at Krojanty against German infantry was later distorted by propaganda into a myth of lances against tanks. The last Polish cavalry actions came in 1945.
