Basel Program

The Basel Program was the first political platform of the Zionist movement, adopted at the First Zionist Congress held in Basel, Switzerland, on 29–31 August 1897. The congress, convened and presided over by Theodor Herzl, adopted the program drafted by a committee under Max Nordau.

The First Zionist Congress, Basel, 1897.

Text and aims

The program declared: "Zionism seeks to establish a home for the Jewish people in Palestine secured under public law." To this end it proposed the promotion of Jewish agricultural, artisanal, and commercial settlement in Palestine; the organisation and uniting of Jewry through local and international bodies; the strengthening of Jewish national sentiment; and preparatory steps to obtain the consent of governments. The deliberately chosen wording — a "home" (Heimstätte) rather than a "state" — was intended to avoid alarming the Ottoman Empire, which then ruled Palestine.

The Basel Program remained the foundational statement of Zionist aims until it was superseded by the Jerusalem Program of 1951, adopted after the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948.
