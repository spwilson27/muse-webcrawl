Transfer of merit

The transfer of merit is a standard part of Buddhist spiritual practice, in which the merit (puñña) gained from good deeds — giving, meditation, recitation — is shared with or dedicated to others, including deceased relatives, all beings, or the goal of enlightenment itself.

Practice

In Theravada countries the transfer is often accompanied by pouring water from a vessel into a bowl while verses are recited, symbolising the gift of merit. In Mahayana Buddhism, dedicating merit is central to the bodhisattva ideal, and the Diamond Sutra and other texts praise the merit generated by teaching the Dharma as surpassing material gifts.

Interpretations

Scholars debate how merit can be "transferred" without being diminished; common explanations treat rejoicing in another's good deeds as itself generating merit for the rejoicer, so merit is shared rather than divided.
