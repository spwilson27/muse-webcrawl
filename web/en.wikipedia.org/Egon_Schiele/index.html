Egon Schiele

Born: 12 June 1890, Tulln an der Donau, Austria-Hungary

Died: 31 October 1918, Vienna, Austria-Hungary

Movement: Expressionism

Known for: Painting, drawing

Egon Leo Adolf Ludwig Schiele (12 June 1890 – 31 October 1918) was an Austrian Expressionist painter. A protégé of Gustav Klimt, Schiele was one of the leading figures of early modernism in Vienna, noted for the intensity and raw sexuality of his work and for his many self-portraits, including nudes.

Life and work

Schiele was born in Tulln, Lower Austria, where his father was a station master for the Austrian Imperial State Railways. He entered the Vienna Academy of Fine Arts in 1906 but, frustrated by its conservatism, left three years later and founded the Neukunstgruppe with other dissatisfied students. Klimt mentored the young artist, bought his drawings and introduced him to patrons and models.

Schiele's early work shows Klimt's decorative influence, but by 1910 he had developed a distinctive style of angular, emaciated figures rendered with wiry line and unsettling colour. His subjects — self-portraits, portraits of his partner Walburga "Wally" Neuzil, landscapes and allegories such as Death and the Maiden — explored psychology, eroticism and mortality.

Controversy and later years

In 1912 Schiele was arrested in Neulengbach on charges related to his use of young models; he spent 24 days in prison, an experience recorded in a series of prison drawings. He married Edith Harms in 1915 and was drafted into the Austrian army during the First World War, though he continued to paint. In 1918 he was invited to exhibit at the Vienna Secession's 49th exhibition, which proved a triumph. That autumn both Schiele and his pregnant wife died in the Spanish flu pandemic, three days apart, aged 28.

Legacy

Schiele's oeuvre comprises some 300 paintings and thousands of drawings. The Leopold Museum in Vienna holds the world's largest collection of his work. His paintings have achieved record prices at auction, and he is ranked with Klimt and Oskar Kokoschka as a founder of Viennese modernism.
