Science fiction fandom

Science fiction fandom is the community of fans of the science fiction genre and its media. It is generally considered the oldest organised fandom, originating in the letter columns of Hugo Gernsback's pulp magazines in the late 1920s, where readers began corresponding with one another and forming clubs.

History

Early fan activity centred on fanzines — amateur publications such as The Comet (1930), usually cited as the first — and on local clubs such as the Los Angeles Science Fantasy Society. In 1939 fans organised the first World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) in New York. Fans invented much of the infrastructure now common across fandoms: conventions, fan awards (the Hugo Awards, voted by Worldcon members since 1953), filk music, costuming, fan fiction, and a rich jargon collected in the Fancyclopedia.

Culture and influence

Science fiction fandom has been a training ground for many professional authors and editors, including Ray Bradbury, Isaac Asimov, and Forrest J Ackerman, who coined the term "sci-fi." Though the growth of media fandoms and the internet has diversified fan culture, SF fandom's traditions of conventions, amateur press associations, and awards continue to shape fan communities worldwide.
