Herzog & de Meuron

Founded: 1978, Basel, Switzerland
Founders: Jacques Herzog, Pierre de Meuron
Awards: Pritzker Prize (2001), RIBA Royal Gold Medal (2007)

Herzog & de Meuron Basel Ltd. is a Swiss architecture firm founded in Basel in 1978 by Jacques Herzog (born 1950) and Pierre de Meuron (born 1950), who studied together at ETH Zurich and were influenced by the artist Joseph Beuys and architect Aldo Rossi. The practice is known for its inventive use of materials and surfaces combined with rigorous, often minimalist forms.

Notable works

The firm achieved international prominence with the conversion of London's Bankside Power Station into Tate Modern (2000), later extended by the Switch House (2016). Their stadium designs include the St. Jakob-Park in Basel, the Allianz Arena in Munich (2005) with its colour-changing inflated ETFE panels, and the Beijing National Stadium — the "Bird's Nest" — built for the 2008 Olympics with artist Ai Weiwei. Other major projects include the Ricola storage building, the Dominus Winery in Napa Valley (gabion stone walls), the Prada Aoyama store in Tokyo, the Elbphilharmonie concert hall in Hamburg (2016), the M+ museum in Hong Kong, and 1111 Lincoln Road in Miami Beach.

Recognition

Herzog and de Meuron received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2001, the first Swiss winners, and the RIBA Royal Gold Medal in 2007. The firm employs several hundred people with offices in Basel, London, New York, Hong Kong, and elsewhere, and its partners include Christine Binswanger, Ascan Mergenthaler, and Stefan Marbach.
