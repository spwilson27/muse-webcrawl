From hand-built curiosities to the assembly line: the automobile within a generation became mass transport.

The history of the automobile begins with steam-powered road vehicles in the 18th century — Nicolas-Joseph Cugnot's steam tractor of 1769 is often called the first automobile — and takes its modern form with the internal combustion engine in the 1880s. Few technologies have so thoroughly reshaped cities, economies and daily life.

Early development

Experimenters in Britain, France, Germany and the United States built steam coaches in the early 19th century, but restrictive legislation and railway competition slowed road steam in Britain. The crucial breakthroughs were the light, high-speed petrol engine of Gottlieb Daimler and Wilhelm Maybach and Karl Benz's Patent-Motorwagen (patented 1886), the first purpose-designed automobile. Bertha Benz's long-distance drive of 1888 proved its practicality. French makers such as Panhard standardised the front-engine, rear-drive layout, and by 1900 France led world production.

Mass motorisation

In the United States, Ransom Olds pioneered production-line methods, and Henry Ford's Model T (1908) — made dramatically cheaper by the moving assembly line (1913) and the $5 day wage (1914) — put the world on wheels: by 1927 over 15 million had been built. General Motors under Alfred Sloan answered with annual model changes and a ladder of brands. Cars drove suburbanisation, road-building, and the oil industry, and provoked regulation on safety and emissions, especially after Ralph Nader's Unsafe at Any Speed (1965).

Post-war and global era

Post-war designs added unibody construction, front-wheel drive, automatic transmissions and fuel injection; Japanese manufacturers (Toyota, Honda) rose on quality and efficiency, reshaping the industry with lean production. Safety (seatbelts, airbags, crash standards) and emissions controls became universal. Since the 2010s the industry has been transforming again toward battery-electric vehicles, software-defined cars and driver-assistance systems, with China becoming the largest car market and producer.
