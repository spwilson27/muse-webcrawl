Flåm Line

The Flåm Line (Norwegian: Flåmsbana) is a 20.2 km (12.6 mi) railway line between Myrdal and Flåm in Vestland county, Norway. A branch of the Bergen Line, it descends from the mountain station of Myrdal, at 867 meters above sea level, to Flåm on the shore of Aurlandsfjord. It is one of the steepest standard-gauge railway lines in the world and one of Norway's most popular tourist attractions.

Construction

Built to connect the Bergen Line with the Sognefjord and provide an ice-free port link, construction began in 1924 and proved arduous: the line has 20 tunnels totaling 5.7 km, many excavated by hand, plus bridges and tight curves through the Flåmsdalen valley. Trains began running in 1940, with steam replaced by electric traction in 1944.

The journey

The single-track line makes one intermediate stop of note at Kjosfossen station, built so passengers can view the Kjosfossen waterfall. Trains use powerful locomotives and dynamic braking to manage the descent, in which 80 percent of the route runs at gradients of 5.5 percent or steeper.

Tourism

Marketed as one of the world's most beautiful train journeys, the line carries close to a million passengers in peak years, many arriving by cruise ship at Flåm.
