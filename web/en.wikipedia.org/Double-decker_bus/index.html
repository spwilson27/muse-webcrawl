Double-decker bus

A red double-decker bus in London

A double-decker bus or double-deck bus is a bus that has two storeys or decks. They are used for mass transit in many cities, most famously London, where the red double-decker has become a national symbol of England, and for sightseeing tours in many parts of the world.

History

Two-level horse-drawn omnibuses circulated in Paris and London in the 19th century. Motorized double-deckers appeared in the early 20th century; the AEC Routemaster, introduced in London in 1956, became the most iconic design, with an open rear platform and half-cab layout. Its successor, the New Routemaster, entered service in 2012.

Design

Passengers reach the upper deck by one or two staircases. Double-deckers carry roughly twice the seated capacity of a single-decker of the same length but are restricted by vehicle height limits, typically around 4.4 metres in the United Kingdom. Open-top versions are common for tourist services.

Use worldwide

Beyond the United Kingdom and Ireland, double-deckers operate in Hong Kong, where they form the backbone of franchised bus services, and in cities across Europe, North America, and Asia, often for tour routes.
