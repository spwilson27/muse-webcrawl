Cilician Gates
Turkish: Gülek Boğazı
Elevation: about 1,050 m
Location: Taurus Mountains, Turkey

The Cilician Gates (Turkish: Gülek Boğazı) are a pass in the Taurus Mountains of southern Turkey, forming the principal route between the lowlands of Cilicia and the Anatolian plateau. The narrow gorge, carved by the Gökoluk River, has been a strategic corridor since antiquity.

History

Armies that traversed the pass include those of the Ten Thousand described by Xenophon, Alexander the Great on his way to the Battle of Issus in 333 BC, the legions of Cicero, who governed Cilicia, and the armies of the First Crusade. The pass was guarded in Byzantine and Armenian times by fortresses, and the Ottoman Empire later controlled it as the main road from Anatolia to Syria. In the 1830s Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt fortified the pass during his invasion of Anatolia.

Modern times

Today the Cilician Gates are crossed by the motorway from Adana to Pozantı and by the railway line toward Konya, keeping the ancient corridor in use.
