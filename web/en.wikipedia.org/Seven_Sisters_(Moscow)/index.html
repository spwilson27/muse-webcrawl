Seven Sisters (Moscow)

Moscow State University main building

The Seven Sisters (Russian: Сталинские высотки, "Stalinist high-rises") are a group of seven skyscrapers in Moscow built in the Stalinist architectural style between 1947 and 1953. They were among the first projects undertaken after the Second World War and were intended to demonstrate Soviet achievement to the world; the style, sometimes called "wedding-cake" architecture, combines Russian Baroque and Gothic elements with stepped, spired silhouettes reminiscent of American skyscrapers of the 1930s.

The buildings

  • Main building of Moscow State University (240 m, completed 1953) — the tallest of the group and, at completion, the tallest building in Europe; it remains the tallest educational building in the world.
  • Kotelnicheskaya Embankment Building (176 m, 1952) — residential apartments overlooking the Moskva River.
  • Kudrinskaya Square Building (156 m, 1954) — apartments near the Moscow Zoo, nicknamed the "House of Aviators."
  • Hotel Ukraina (198 m, 1957) — now the Radisson Royal Hotel, on the bank of the Moskva River.
  • Ministry of Foreign Affairs (172 m, 1953) — the only sister whose spire lacks a red star, added design features aside.
  • Red Gates Administrative Building (138 m, 1953) — incorporates an entrance to the Moscow Metro.
  • Hotel Leningradskaya (136 m, 1954) — now a Hilton hotel; the smallest and most ornately decorated of the seven.

History

The first stones were laid simultaneously on 7 September 1947, the 800th anniversary of Moscow, partly in response to American skyscrapers and the planned Palace of the Soviets, which was never completed. Construction relied heavily on German prisoners of war and Gulag labour. An eighth sister, the Zaryadye Administrative Building, was begun but cancelled after Stalin's death; its foundations were later used for the Rossiya Hotel. The style influenced monumental buildings across the Eastern Bloc, including the Palace of Culture and Science in Warsaw and the House of the Free Press in Bucharest.
