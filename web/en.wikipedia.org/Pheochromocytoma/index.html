Pheochromocytoma

SpecialtyEndocrinology, oncology
SiteAdrenal medulla (chromaffin cells)
SymptomsHeadache, palpitations, sweating, hypertension
TreatmentSurgical resection after alpha blockade

Pheochromocytoma (or phaeochromocytoma in British English) is a rare tumour of the chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla that secretes excessive amounts of the catecholamines adrenaline (epinephrine) and noradrenaline (norepinephrine). Similar tumours arising outside the adrenal gland are called paragangliomas. Together they are a rare but important cause of secondary hypertension.

A pheochromocytoma of the adrenal gland

The classic presentation is of paroxysmal episodes ("spells") of severe headache, palpitations, profuse sweating and pallor, accompanied by markedly raised blood pressure; sustained hypertension is also common. Patients may additionally have tremor, anxiety, hyperglycaemia and weight loss. Attacks can be provoked by exercise, pressure on the tumour, or certain drugs, and may cause dangerous arrhythmias or hypertensive crisis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on measuring plasma free metanephrines or urinary fractionated metanephrines — the metabolites of catecholamines — followed by adrenal imaging with CT or MRI, and functional imaging such as MIBG scanning or gallium-68 DOTATATE PET in selected cases. A substantial minority of cases are hereditary, associated with MEN2 (RET), von Hippel–Lindau disease (VHL) and succinate dehydrogenase (SDHx) mutations, so genetic testing is widely recommended. Definitive treatment is surgical removal, but only after careful preparation with alpha-adrenergic blockade (typically phenoxybenzamine or doxazosin), generous hydration and salt loading to prevent catastrophic swings in blood pressure during operation.
