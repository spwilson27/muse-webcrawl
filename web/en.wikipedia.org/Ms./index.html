Ms.

Ms. (pronounced "miz")
Type: courtesy title for women
Introduced in modern form: 1901 (proposed); popularised 1970s
Counterparts: Mr., Mrs., Miss, Mx.

Ms. is an English-language courtesy title used with the surname of a woman, regardless of her marital status. Unlike Mrs. (traditionally for married women) and Miss (for unmarried women), Ms. reveals nothing about whether the woman is married — exactly as Mr. reveals nothing about a man. It is the default title for women in business correspondence and official forms in much of the English-speaking world.

History

The title in its modern sense was proposed as early as 1901 in a Massachusetts newspaper, as a tactful form for addressing a woman whose marital status was unknown, but it attracted little attention until the women's movement of the 1960s and 1970s promoted it. The feminist activist Sheila Michaels championed the term from 1961, and in 1971 Gloria Steinem and others made it the name of Ms. magazine, whose first standalone issue appeared in 1972. The New York Times resisted the title until 1986, when it announced it would accept "Ms." in its pages.

Pronunciation and usage

Ms. is pronounced "miz" (/mɪz/), with a voiced final consonant distinguishing it from Miss. Style guides generally recommend using the title a woman herself prefers, and defaulting to Ms. when the preference is unknown.
