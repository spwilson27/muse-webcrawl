Emil Artin (March 3, 1898 – December 20, 1962) was an Austrian mathematician of Armenian descent. Artin was one of the leading mathematicians of the twentieth century. He is best known for his work on algebraic number theory, contributing greatly to class field theory and a new construction of L-functions. He also worked on ring theory, group theory, and field theory.

Life

Artin was born in Vienna to an opera singer mother and an art dealer father of Armenian descent. He studied at the University of Vienna and later in Leipzig and Göttingen, receiving his doctorate in 1921. He took a position at the University of Hamburg, where he became a full professor. In 1937, because his wife was of Jewish ancestry, he emigrated to the United States, teaching at Notre Dame, Indiana University and Princeton. He returned to Hamburg in 1958.

Work

Artin's contributions include the Artin reciprocity law in class field theory, Artinian rings and the Artin–Wedderburn theorem, Artin L-functions, and Artin's conjecture on primitive roots. His elegant lecture-based writing, and his textbook on Galois theory, shaped generations of algebraists. His students included many eminent mathematicians, among them John Tate.

Legacy

Along with Emmy Noether, Artin is considered a founder of modern abstract algebra. The asteroid 15378 Artin is named in his honour.
