Jantar Mantar

The Jantar Mantar is a collection of five astronomical observatories built in northern India in the early 18th century by Maharaja Jai Singh II of Jaipur. Located in New Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Mathura and Varanasi, they consist of large masonry instruments for measuring time, predicting eclipses and tracking the positions of celestial bodies with the naked eye. The observatory at Jaipur, the largest and best preserved, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Built: 1724–1735

Builder: Maharaja Jai Singh II

Locations: New Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Mathura, Varanasi

Purpose: Astronomical observation

Instruments

The Jaipur observatory contains some twenty fixed instruments, including the Samrat Yantra, a giant triangular sundial whose hypotenuse stands about 27 metres high and whose shadow moves visibly, allowing time to be read to an accuracy of about two seconds. Other instruments include the Jai Prakash Yantra, used to measure the positions of stars, and the Rama Yantra, for measuring altitude and azimuth. Jai Singh, an accomplished astronomer, consulted Hindu, Muslim and European sources, including the tables of Philippe de La Hire, in designing the observatories.

Name

The name derives from yantra, "instrument", and mantra, here in the sense of "calculation" — hence "instrument for calculation".
