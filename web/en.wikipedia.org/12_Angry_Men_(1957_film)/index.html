12 Angry Men (1957 film)

12 Angry Men
Directed by: Sidney Lumet
Written by: Reginald Rose
Starring: Henry Fonda, Lee J. Cobb, Martin Balsam
Release date: 10 April 1957
Running time: 96 minutes

12 Angry Men is a 1957 American legal drama film directed by Sidney Lumet in his feature directorial debut, adapted by Reginald Rose from his own 1954 teleplay. It tells the story of a jury of twelve men deliberating the conviction or acquittal of a teenager charged with murder, on the basis of reasonable doubt.

Plot

In a New York courthouse, a jury retires to decide the fate of an 18-year-old accused of stabbing his father. An initial vote is 11–1 for guilty; the lone holdout, Juror 8 (Henry Fonda), argues the evidence deserves discussion. Over a tense afternoon in the jury room, he and others expose inconsistencies in the testimony, gradually persuading every juror until the verdict is unanimously not guilty.

Reception and legacy

Though a modest box-office performer on release, the film was acclaimed by critics, nominated for three Academy Awards, and is now regarded as one of the greatest films ever made. It was selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry in 2007.
