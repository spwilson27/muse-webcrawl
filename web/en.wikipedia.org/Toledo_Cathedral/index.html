The Primatial Cathedral of Saint Mary of Toledo (Spanish: Catedral Primada Santa María de Toledo), otherwise known as Toledo Cathedral, is a Roman Catholic church in Toledo, Spain. It is the seat of the Metropolitan Archdiocese of Toledo, whose archbishop holds the title Primate of Spain. The cathedral is considered one of the greatest works of Spanish High Gothic architecture and one of the most emblematic cathedrals in Spain.

History

The cathedral stands on the site of a Visigothic church and, after the Muslim conquest, the city's great mosque. Following Alfonso VI's reconquest of Toledo in 1085, the mosque-cathedral initially remained, but in 1226–1227, under Archbishop Rodrigo Jiménez de Rada and King Ferdinand III, construction began on the present Gothic cathedral, modelled on Bourges Cathedral in France. The main structure was completed by the end of the 15th century, with the vaults of the main nave finished in 1493.

Architecture and art

The cathedral has five naves, numerous chapels, and a single tower (the second was never completed as a spire and carries a bell pavilion). Highlights include the great main altarpiece (retablo mayor), the carved choir stalls depicting the conquest of Granada, the Chapter House with its Mudéjar ceiling and portraits of the archbishops, and the Sacristy, which functions as a gallery of paintings by El Greco, Velázquez, Titian, Rubens, and Goya.

The Transparente, a Baroque masterpiece by Narciso Tomé (1721–1732), opens the ambulatory wall and ceiling to light, illuminating the tabernacle with a flood of daylight in dramatic Churrigueresque style. The cathedral's treasury preserves the enormous monstrance of Enrique de Arfe (1517–1524), carried through the streets in Toledo's famous Corpus Christi procession.

Music

Toledo Cathedral has a distinguished musical tradition; composers associated with it include Cristóbal de Morales, one of the leading Spanish Renaissance polyphonists, who served as chapelmaster in the 1540s.
