Emiliano Zapata

Emiliano Zapata Salazar (8 August 1879 – 10 April 1919) was a Mexican revolutionary and the leader of the Liberation Army of the South during the Mexican Revolution. Born into a peasant family in the village of Anenecuilco in Morelos, Zapata became the foremost champion of agrarian reform, demanding the return of lands seized from villages by the great sugar haciendas. His rallying cry was "Tierra y Libertad" (Land and Freedom).

Origins

Zapata grew up in a modestly prosperous peasant family that had long defended the communal lands of Anenecuilco against encroaching haciendas. In 1909 he was elected head of the village defence board, and he soon took up arms to occupy land the village claimed, making him a wanted man even before the Revolution began.

The Revolution

When Francisco I. Madero called for revolution against Porfirio Díaz in 1910, Zapata joined the movement in the south. Disappointed by Madero's failure to carry out immediate land reform after taking power, Zapata repudiated him in the Plan of Ayala (November 1911), which demanded the restitution of stolen village lands and the expropriation of one-third of hacienda estates. Zapata's forces fought successively against Madero, the Huerta regime, and the Constitutionalists of Venustiano Carranza, controlling much of Morelos and introducing agrarian reform in the areas they held.

Death

On 10 April 1919 Zapata was lured to the hacienda of Chinameca by Colonel Jesús Guajardo, who pretended to wish to defect, and was shot by an honour guard as he entered. His body was displayed in Cuautla to prove his death.

Legacy

Zapata became a national hero of Mexico and an enduring symbol of peasant resistance. The agrarian provisions of Article 27 of the 1917 Constitution owed much to his movement. In 1994 the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN) in Chiapas took his name. His famous saying — "It is better to die on your feet than to live on your knees" — is attributed to his cause.
