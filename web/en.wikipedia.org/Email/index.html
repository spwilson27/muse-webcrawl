Email

Electronic mail (email or e-mail) is a method of transmitting and receiving messages using electronic devices. It was conceived in the late 20th century as the digital version of, or counterpart to, mail (hence e- + mail). Email is a ubiquitous and very widely used communication medium; in current use, an email address is often treated as a basic and necessary part of many processes in business, commerce, government, education, entertainment, and other spheres of daily life in most countries.

Operation

Email operates across computer networks, primarily the Internet. Today's email systems are based on a store-and-forward model. Servers accept, forward, deliver, and store messages. Messages are exchanged between servers using the Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP); users retrieve mail using protocols such as POP3 or IMAP, typically through an email client or webmail interface. An email message consists of headers (such as From, To, Subject and Date), a body, and optionally attachments encoded using MIME.

History

Computer-based messaging between users of the same system dates to the early 1960s, and network email on the ARPANET emerged in the early 1970s, when Ray Tomlinson adopted the "@" sign to separate the user name from the host name. Email grew rapidly through the 1980s and 1990s, becoming one of the principal uses of the Internet. Challenges including spam and phishing have shaped its development and the filtering technologies around it.
