The Western States Endurance Run, known commonly as the Western States 100, is a 100.2-mile (161 km) ultramarathon held annually on trails in California's Sierra Nevada on the last full weekend of June. It is regarded as the world's oldest 100-mile trail race and one of the most prestigious ultramarathons, forming part of the Grand Slam of Ultrarunning.

Course

The race begins at Olympic Valley (formerly Squaw Valley), near Lake Tahoe, climbs over Emigrant Pass, and follows the Western States Trail through the Granite Chief Wilderness and the canyons of the Middle Fork of the American River before finishing on the track at Placer High School in Auburn. Runners face extreme heat in the canyons, river crossings including the waist-deep ford at Rucky Chucky, and finish within a 30-hour limit; those finishing under 24 hours earn the coveted silver belt buckle, and finishers under 30 hours receive a bronze buckle.

History

The course follows the route of the Tevis Cup horse ride. In 1974, Gordy Ainsleigh, having no fit horse, ran the trail on foot and finished within the ride's 24-hour limit, proving the feat possible. The run became a formal race in 1977 with 14 starters. Entry is by a competitive lottery held each December, as demand vastly exceeds the roughly 369 places allowed under wilderness permits.

Notable champions include Scott Jurek, who won seven consecutive times (1999–2005), Ann Trason, who won 14 times, Kilian Jornet, Jim Walmsley, and Courtney Dauwalter.
