Rogue (video game)

DesignersMichael Toy, Glenn Wichman, Ken Arnold
Release1980
GenreDungeon crawler, role-playing
ModeSingle-player

Rogue is a dungeon-crawling video game created by Michael Toy and Glenn Wichman, with later contributions by Ken Arnold, first released in 1980 for Unix-based mainframe systems. The player descends through procedurally generated dungeon levels in search of the Amulet of Yendor, represented on screen by ASCII characters.

Gameplay and legacy

Each playthrough generates a new random layout of rooms, monsters, potions and magical items whose effects must be identified anew; death is permanent, forcing the player to start over. Rogue became so influential that the whole genre of similar games is called "roguelikes", and its core design ideas — procedural generation and permadeath — remain central to the modern roguelike and roguelite genres.
