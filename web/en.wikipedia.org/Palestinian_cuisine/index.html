Palestinian cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Palestinian cuisine comprises the food traditions of Palestine and the Palestinian diaspora. It is a regional expression of Levantine cuisine, built on olive oil, wheat, rice, lentils, chickpeas, yogurt, summer vegetables, and herbs and spices such as sumac, za'atar, allspice, and cumin, with distinct sub-traditions in the Galilee and the north, the West Bank hills, the coastal cities, and the Gaza Strip.

Characteristic dishes

Musakhan — roasted chicken with sumac-seasoned onions, pine nuts, and olive oil served over taboon flatbread — is widely considered the national dish. Other emblematic dishes include maqluba (rice, vegetables, and meat "upside-down" from the pot), musakhan rolls, qidreh (spiced rice and meat cooked in a brass pot, associated with Hebron), maftoul (large hand-rolled couscous-like pearls), and Gaza's sumaghiyyeh (a sumac and tahini stew) and spicy seafood dishes. Shared Levantine dishes such as hummus, falafel, fattoush, tabbouleh, shish taouk, and stuffed vine leaves are everyday staples.

Sweets, breads and drinks

Palestine is famous for knafeh, the cheese-and-syrup pastry associated above all with Nablus, made with white Nabulsi cheese. Other sweets include baklava, qatayef (especially in Ramadan), and halva. Taboon bread baked in a clay oven accompanies most meals. Drinks include Arabic coffee, mint or sage tea, tamarind and carob juices, and in Gaza a distinctive spiced coffee.

Cultural role

Food carries strong cultural and political resonance for Palestinians: olive trees and olive oil symbolise attachment to the land, and dishes such as musakhan mark the olive harvest season. Communal eating, generous hospitality, and seasonal preservation techniques (drying, pickling, jams) structure much of the cuisine's social life, and Palestinian restaurants and cookbooks have carried it widely through the diaspora.
