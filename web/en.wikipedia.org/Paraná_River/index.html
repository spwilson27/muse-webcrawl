Paraná River

The Paraná River is a river in south-central South America, running through Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina for some 4,880 kilometres. It is second in length only to the Amazon among South American rivers, and merges with the Paraguay River and then the Uruguay River to form the Río de la Plata estuary.

Course

The Paraná proper begins at the confluence of the Paranaíba and Rio Grande rivers in southern Brazil. It flows generally southwest, forming the border between Brazil and Paraguay and then between Paraguay and Argentina, before turning south and east across Argentina past Rosario to the Río de la Plata near Buenos Aires.

Economy and dams

The river is a vital transport artery and source of hydroelectric power. The Itaipu Dam on the Brazil–Paraguay border is one of the largest power stations in the world; the Yacyretá Dam lies further downstream. The Paraná Delta near its mouth is a rich wetland ecosystem.

Ecology

The basin supports abundant fisheries and wetlands of international importance, including the Iberá Wetlands fed by the Paraná system in Argentina.
