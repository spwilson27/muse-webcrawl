Carpool

Carpooling is the sharing of car journeys so that more than one person travels in a car, usually arranged among colleagues, neighbours, or parents driving children to school. By having more people using one vehicle, carpooling reduces each person's travel costs, fuel consumption, traffic congestion, and carbon emissions.

History

Carpooling became prominent in the United States during the Second World War under fuel rationing, and again during the 1970s oil crises, when governments promoted ridesharing and some transport planners introduced high-occupancy vehicle (HOV) lanes rewarding cars carrying multiple occupants. Modern carpooling is often arranged through workplace programmes, dedicated park-and-ride lots, and mobile apps.

Forms

Arrangements range from informal, where a driver takes turns or passengers contribute to fuel costs, to formal corporate schemes with guaranteed ride home provisions. Casual carpooling, known as "slugging" in the Washington, D.C. area, involves strangers forming ad hoc pools at designated spots so drivers can use HOV lanes.
