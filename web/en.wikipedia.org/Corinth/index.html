Corinth

Corinth is a city in Greece, successor to the famous ancient city of the same name, located on the Isthmus of Corinth, the narrow land bridge connecting the Peloponnese with the rest of mainland Greece. The modern city lies about 80 kilometres west of Athens and near the ruins of the ancient polis.

Ancient Corinth

Ancient Corinth was a wealthy and powerful city-state, controlling trade between northern Greece and the Peloponnese and operating two ports, Lechaeum on the Gulf of Corinth and Cenchreae on the Saronic Gulf. It founded colonies including Syracuse and Corcyra and was a leading member of the Peloponnesian League. Rome destroyed the city in 146 BC; Julius Caesar refounded it as a Roman colony a century later (44 BC). In the New Testament, the city is the recipient of Paul's First and Second Epistles to the Corinthians.

Modern Corinth

After an earthquake destroyed the old town in 1858, New Corinth was rebuilt on the coast nearby. The Corinth Canal, completed in 1893, cuts through the isthmus but is too narrow for most modern ships.
