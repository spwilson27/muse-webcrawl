Torngat Mountains

LocationLabrador Peninsula, Canada
Highest peakMount Caubvick, 1,652 m
Rock ageUp to ~3.9 billion years (among Earth's oldest)

The Torngat Mountains are a mountain range on the Labrador Peninsula at the northern tip of Quebec and Labrador, Canada. They stretch some 300 kilometres from Cape Chidley southward along the Labrador Sea coast and contain the highest peaks in mainland Canada east of the Rockies.

Geography

The highest summit is Mount Caubvick (also known as Mont d'Iberville on the Quebec side) at 1,652 metres. Deep fjords — including Nachvak and Saglek — cut the coast, and small glaciers survive in sheltered cirques. The range's rocks are part of the ancient Canadian Shield, among the oldest on Earth.

Culture and conservation

The name derives from the Inuktitut Tongait, "place of spirits", and the mountains are sacred to the Inuit of Nunatsiavut and Nunavik. Much of the range is protected within Torngat Mountains National Park, established in 2005 and managed cooperatively with Inuit, known for polar bears, caribou and dramatic Arctic scenery.
