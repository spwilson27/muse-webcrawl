Antonio da Ponte (c. 1512 – 20 March 1595) was a Venetian architect and engineer, best known as the designer and builder of the Rialto Bridge in Venice, the oldest of the four bridges spanning the Grand Canal. He worked for the Republic of Venice on numerous public works, often in collaboration with his nephew Antonio Contin.

Career

Da Ponte rose through the Venetian building administration to become proto (chief architect) of the Salt Office, responsible for public works. His projects included work on the Doge's Palace — where he helped rebuild the structure after fires, including supervising construction connected with the Bridge of Sighs built by his nephew — repairs to fortifications, bridges and other infrastructure in Venice and its territories, and involvement in the rebuilding of the church of San Salvatore in a design associated with him.

The Rialto Bridge

The wooden bridge at the Rialto, the commercial heart of Venice, had collapsed several times, and the Republic held competitions for a permanent stone replacement. Designs were submitted by celebrated architects including Michelangelo, Palladio and Sansovino. Remarkably, the contract was awarded in 1588 to Da Ponte, then in his seventies. His design — a single bold stone arch of about 28 metres spanning the canal, with two inclined ramps lined with shops and a central portico — was considered daring, and sceptics predicted its collapse. Construction, supervised by Da Ponte and his nephew Antonio Contin, was completed in 1591. The bridge has stood ever since and remains one of the most famous landmarks of Venice.

Other works and legacy

Among other duties, Da Ponte worked on Venetian defences and public buildings and was consulted on hydraulic questions affecting the lagoon. He died in Venice in 1595. His nephew Antonio Contin went on to design the Bridge of Sighs (1600–1603). Da Ponte's Rialto Bridge, an audacious piece of engineering clothed in Istrian stone, is his enduring monument.
