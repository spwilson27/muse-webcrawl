Mario Puzo

Mario Francis Puzo (15 October 1920 – 2 July 1999) was an American author and screenwriter. He is best known for his crime novels about the Italian-American Mafia, most notably The Godfather (1969), which he co-adapted into the Academy Award-winning film trilogy directed by Francis Ford Coppola.

Life and early work

Puzo was born in New York City to Italian immigrants from Sicily and grew up in the Hell's Kitchen neighbourhood. After serving in the US Army Air Forces in the Second World War, he studied at the New School and Columbia University and worked as a journalist and pulp magazine editor. His first novels, The Dark Arena (1955) and The Fortunate Pilgrim (1965), won critical respect but little money.

The Godfather and screenwriting

Written partly to pay off gambling debts, The Godfather became a bestseller, spending 67 weeks on the New York Times list. Puzo shared the Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay for The Godfather (1972) and The Godfather Part II (1974). He also wrote the original screenplays for Superman (1978), Superman II and the disaster film Earthquake (1974), and the novel Fools Die (1978). Later Mafia novels include The Sicilian (1984) and, published posthumously, Omertà (2000). Puzo died of heart failure at his home on Long Island in 1999.
