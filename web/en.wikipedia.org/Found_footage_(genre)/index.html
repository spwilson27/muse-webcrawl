Found footage (genre)

Found footage is a horror film subgenre in which all or a significant part of the work is presented as if it were film or video recordings discovered after the events depicted. The footage is often shot by the characters themselves with handheld cameras, creating an impression of realism and immediacy.

Characteristics

Found footage films typically feature shaky handheld camerawork, naturalistic acting and improvisation, minimal or no musical score, and a framing device explaining how the footage was recovered. Marketing often blurs fiction and reality.

History

The technique has roots in Cannibal Holocaust (1980). The Blair Witch Project (1999) — made for tens of thousands of dollars and grossing nearly $250 million — launched the modern wave, followed by franchises such as Paranormal Activity, REC, Cloverfield, and V/H/S.

Notable films

Cannibal Holocaust (1980), The Blair Witch Project (1999), Paranormal Activity (2007), REC (2007), Cloverfield (2008), Trollhunter (2010).
