The Oligocene is a geologic epoch of the Paleogene Period, lasting from about 33.9 to 23.03 million years ago. It follows the Eocene and precedes the Miocene Epoch, the first epoch of the Neogene Period. The name, coined by Heinrich Ernst Beyrich in 1854, comes from the Greek oligos ("few") and kainos ("new"), referring to the sparsity of additional modern forms of molluscs among its fossils compared with other epochs.

Climate

The Oligocene began with the Eocene–Oligocene transition, a major cooling event associated with the formation of the Antarctic ice sheet and the opening of ocean passages around Antarctica, which established the Antarctic Circumpolar Current. The warm, forested world of the Eocene gave way to cooler, drier and more seasonal climates; grasslands expanded in many regions, and in Eurasia the faunal turnover known as the Grande Coupure replaced many endemic European species with immigrants from Asia.

Life

Forests retreated and open habitats spread, favouring grazing and running animals. Mammals diversified into forms closer to modern groups: early horses such as Mesohippus, entelodonts, oreodonts, the giant hornless rhinoceros Paraceratherium (among the largest land mammals ever), early elephants, and the first apes and Old World monkeys. In the oceans, whales continued to evolve, and kelp forests appeared. Snakes and lizards diversified as climates cooled at the poles and warmed at mid-latitudes.
