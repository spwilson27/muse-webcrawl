Triglav (pronounced [ˈtɾiːɡlau̯]; German: Terglau; Italian: Monte Tricorno) is, at 2,864 metres (9,396 ft) above sea level, the highest mountain in Slovenia and the highest peak of the Julian Alps. The mountain is the pre-eminent symbol of the Slovene nation. It is the centrepiece of Triglav National Park, Slovenia's only national park, and it is featured on the Slovenian flag and coat of arms.

Name

The name Triglav literally means "three-headed" in Slovene, describing the mountain's appearance from the Bohinj side, where its three peaks are visible. The name may also refer to the Slavic god Triglav, an early Slavic deity with three heads, although there is no direct evidence linking the deity's cult to the mountain.

History

The first recorded ascent of Triglav was made on 26 August 1778 by Luka Korošec, Matija Kos, Štefan Rožič, and Lovrenc Willomitzer, at the initiative of the industrialist and patron Sigmund Zois. The ascent took place decades before most comparable peaks in the Alps were climbed.

In 1895 the priest and mountaineer Jakob Aljaž purchased the summit area for a nominal sum and in 1896 erected the Aljaž Tower (Aljažev stolp), a small metal cylinder that still serves as a shelter, storm refuge, and triangulation point. The tower is a protected cultural monument and one of Slovenia's best-known landmarks.

Geography

Triglav rises above the glacial valleys of the Sava and Soča (Isonzo) river systems. Its northern face, the Triglav North Wall, is about 3 kilometres wide and over 1,000 metres high — one of the largest walls in the Eastern Alps. The mountain's summit ridge connects to the neighbouring peaks of Mali Triglav ("Little Triglav", 2,725 m) and Rjavina.

A major glacier, the Triglav Glacier, once covered the plateau below the summit but has shrunk dramatically over the past century owing to climate change.

Symbolism

Triglav appears on the coat of arms of Slovenia and consequently on the national flag. The former Slovene 50-tolar coin also bore its image. A popular saying holds that a true Slovene must stand on the summit of Triglav at least once in his life; first-time climbers traditionally receive a playful ritual spanking at the Aljaž Tower.
