Alaska Marine Highway

The Alaska Marine Highway (AMH), also marketed as the Alaska Marine Highway System (AMHS), is a state-operated ferry service in Alaska, run by the Alaska Department of Transportation and Public Facilities. Its roll-on/roll-off vessels link the coastal communities of Southeast Alaska, the Alaska Peninsula, and the Aleutian Chain — many of which have no road access — with each other and with Bellingham, Washington, and Prince Rupert, British Columbia. The route network was designated a National Scenic Byway and an All-American Road.

History and operations

The system began in 1963 with the vessel Malaspina and grew into a lifeline "highway" for isolated towns such as Haines, Skagway, and Sitka, carrying passengers, cars, and freight. Signature mainliners including the Columbia, Kennicott, and Tustumena — the "Trusty Tusty" serving the Aleutians — have operated for decades; newer Alaska-class ferries Tazlina and Hubbard were built in Ketchikan. Budget cuts from 2019 onward severely reduced sailings, sparking political controversy in the state and renewed reform proposals.
