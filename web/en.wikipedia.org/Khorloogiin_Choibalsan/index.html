Khorloogiin Choibalsan

Khorloogiin Choibalsan
Born: 8 February 1895, Achit Beysiin, Mongolia
Died: 26 January 1952, Moscow, Soviet Union
Office: Leader of the Mongolian People's Republic (de facto), 1930s–1952; Premier 1939–1952

Khorloogiin Choibalsan (Mongolian: Хорлоогийн Чойбалсан; 8 February 1895 – 26 January 1952) was a Mongolian revolutionary and statesman who served as the leader of the Mongolian People's Republic from the 1930s until his death in 1952. A close ally of Joseph Stalin, Choibalsan consolidated power through purges modelled on the Soviet Great Terror, in which tens of thousands of Mongolians — including Buddhist monks, intellectuals and alleged counter-revolutionaries — were executed.

Early life

Choibalsan was born near present-day Choibalsan city in Dornod Province, then part of Qing-ruled Outer Mongolia. He studied at a Buddhist monastery school before travelling to Irkutsk in Russia in 1914, where he absorbed revolutionary ideas. After the Russian Revolution he joined the Mongolian revolutionary movement and became a founding member of the Mongolian People's Party in 1920.

Rise to power

Following the Mongolian Revolution of 1921, Choibalsan rose through the party and military ranks, becoming commander-in-chief of the Mongolian People's Army. During the 1930s he eliminated rivals within the leadership, and from 1937 to 1939 directed the Great Repression, during which most of Mongolia's Buddhist monasteries were destroyed and an estimated 17,000–25,000 people were killed. He became Premier (Chairman of the Council of People's Commissars) in 1939.

Second World War and later years

Mongolian forces under Choibalsan fought alongside the Red Army against Japan at the Battles of Khalkhin Gol in 1939 and in the Soviet invasion of Manchuria in 1945. After the war he pursued closer integration with the USSR and ambitions of unifying Inner and Outer Mongolia, which Moscow discouraged. Choibalsan died of kidney cancer in Moscow in 1952. The city of Bayan Tümen was renamed Choibalsan in his honour in 1941.
