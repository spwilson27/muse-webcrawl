Rapeseed oil

Rapeseed oil is a vegetable oil obtained from the seeds of rapeseed (Brassica napus and related species). It is one of the world's most widely produced vegetable oils, after palm and soybean oil. Traditional rapeseed oil contains high levels of erucic acid, which limited its use in food; in the 1970s Canadian breeders developed low-erucic, low-glucosinolate varieties sold as canola (from "Canadian oil, low acid"), which became a major cooking oil.

Production

Rapeseed is grown extensively in Canada, China, India, Germany, France and Poland. Seeds are cleaned, flaked, cooked and pressed; remaining oil is extracted with solvent, then refined, bleached and deodorised. The residual meal is used as livestock feed.

Uses

Food-grade canola oil is valued for its mild flavour, high smoke point and favourable fatty-acid profile — low in saturated fat and relatively rich in monounsaturated fat and alpha-linolenic acid (an omega-3). Historically, high-erucic rapeseed oil served as a lubricant, including for steam engines and marine applications, because it clings to metal surfaces washed with water or steam. Rapeseed oil is also processed into biodiesel, especially in Europe, and used in soaps, paints and bioplastics.

Health considerations

High intakes of erucic acid caused heart lesions in animal studies, prompting regulatory limits in food oils. Modern canola oil contains less than 2% erucic acid and is generally recognised as safe by food authorities.
