Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khalji

Ikhtiyar al-Din Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khalji, also known as Bakhtiyar Khalji, was a Turko-Afghan military general of the Khalji tribe who led the Muslim conquest of Bengal and Bihar around 1203–1204, establishing the first Muslim rule in Bengal.

Serving the Ghurid commander Qutb al-Din Aibak, he carved out a principality in eastern India and ruled from Lakhnauti (Gaur) until his death in 1206.

Conquests

With a small body of cavalry, Bakhtiyar Khalji overran Bihar, destroying great Buddhist monastic centres; the sack of the monastery at Nalanda (or, some argue, Odantapuri) around 1197–1203 is traditionally attributed to his forces. In about 1204 he advanced into Bengal and took Nadia, the capital of the Sena king Lakshmana Sena, who fled to eastern Bengal; Khalji then occupied Lakhnauti and organised the province, issuing coins and having the khutba read in his suzerain's name.

Tibet expedition and death

In 1206 Khalji led a disastrous expedition toward Tibet through the Brahmaputra valley; defeated by disease, terrain and local resistance, he retreated with heavy losses. While ill at Devkot after the failure, he was assassinated by his subordinate Ali Mardan Khalji in 1206. His tomb stands at Pirpal in present-day West Bengal.
