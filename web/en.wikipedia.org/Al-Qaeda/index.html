Al-Qaeda (Arabic: القاعدة, al-Qāʿidah, "the base" or "the foundation") is a pan-Islamist militant organisation founded in 1988 by Osama bin Laden, Abdullah Azzam and other Arab volunteers who had fought against the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan. It seeks the removal of foreign influence from Muslim countries and the establishment of governments ruled by its interpretation of Islamic law. It has been designated a terrorist organisation by the United Nations Security Council, the United States, the European Union and many other states.

History

Al-Qaeda emerged from the Maktab al-Khidamat network that channelled Arab fighters to Afghanistan. After the Soviet withdrawal, bin Laden turned the network against the United States and its regional allies. Based first in Sudan (1991–1996) and then in Afghanistan under Taliban protection, the group carried out the 1998 US embassy bombings in Kenya and Tanzania and the 2000 attack on USS Cole.

September 11 attacks

On 11 September 2001, nineteen al-Qaeda hijackers crashed four airliners into the World Trade Center, the Pentagon and a field in Pennsylvania, killing nearly 3,000 people. The United States responded by invading Afghanistan and launching a global "war on terror". Bin Laden evaded capture for a decade before being killed by US special forces in Abbottabad, Pakistan, in May 2011. His successor, Ayman al-Zawahiri, was killed by a US drone strike in Kabul in July 2022.

Network

After 2001, al-Qaeda evolved into a decentralised network of regional affiliates, including al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP), al-Shabaab in Somalia, al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb and its Sahel successor JNIM, and al-Qaeda in the Indian Subcontinent. It also inspired "lone actor" attacks. The group has rivalled the Islamic State (IS), which broke away from its Iraqi affiliate in 2014.
