Coronal plane

The coronal plane (also known as the frontal plane) is an anatomical plane that divides the body into dorsal (back) and ventral (front) portions. It is one of the three principal planes of the body, alongside the sagittal plane and the transverse (axial) plane, and is oriented vertically, perpendicular to both.

Terminology

The term "coronal" derives from the Latin corona (crown or garland), because the plane roughly coincides with the line of the coronal suture of the skull, which runs side to side across the top of the cranium and was imagined to follow the line of a crown. Movements that occur in the coronal plane include abduction and adduction at joints — for example, raising the arm sideways away from the body — as well as lateral flexion of the trunk.

In medical imaging

Coronal sections are standard views in computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), particularly for the brain, sinuses, chest and abdomen, and in obstetric ultrasound, where the coronal view of the fetal face is routinely assessed. In dentistry and in histology the term is also used for planes through the crown of a tooth or through sections of organs.
