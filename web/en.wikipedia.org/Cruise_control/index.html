Cruise control

Cruise control (also called speed control or autocruise) is a system that automatically controls the speed of a motor vehicle. The driver sets a desired speed and the system takes over the throttle to maintain it, reducing fatigue on long journeys.

History

Speed-regulating governors were used on early automobiles; the modern system was invented by blind engineer Ralph Teetor and marketed by Chrysler from 1958 as the Auto-Pilot. Cruise control became widespread in the 1970s, aided by fuel-economy concerns.

Adaptive cruise control

Adaptive cruise control (ACC) uses radar, lidar or cameras to detect vehicles ahead and automatically adjust speed to maintain a set following distance, braking and accelerating with traffic. Combined with lane-centring, ACC forms the basis of many driver-assistance systems.
