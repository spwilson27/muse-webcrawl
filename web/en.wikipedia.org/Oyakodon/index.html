Oyakodon

CourseMain dish
Place of originJapan
Main ingredientsChicken, egg, scallion, rice

Oyakodon (親子丼), literally "parent-and-child donburi", is a Japanese rice bowl dish in which chicken and egg — the "parent and child" — are simmered together in a sweet-savoury stock of dashi, soy sauce, and mirin, then served over a bowl of steamed rice.

Preparation

Sliced chicken thigh and onions are simmered in the seasoned broth until cooked; beaten eggs are then poured over and gently cooked until just set. The dish is typically garnished with scallions or mitsuba and sometimes with sanshō pepper or shichimi spice.

History and variations

Oyakodon is said to have originated in the late 19th century in Tokyo, at the restaurant Tamahide in Ningyōchō. Variations substitute other "parent and child" combinations, such as salmon and salmon roe (sake oyakodon in some regions uses the term differently), or beef with egg, known as tanindon — "stranger bowl".
