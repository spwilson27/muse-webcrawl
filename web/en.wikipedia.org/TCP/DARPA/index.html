DARPA

The Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) is a research and development agency of the United States Department of Defense responsible for the development of emerging technologies for use by the military. Originally established in 1958 as the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) in response to the Soviet launch of Sputnik 1, DARPA has funded work that produced foundational technologies including the ARPANET, the precursor of the Internet.

Role in the creation of TCP/IP

In the 1970s DARPA funded the development of the packet-switching protocols that became TCP/IP. Building on the ARPANET's original Network Control Program, Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn designed the Transmission Control Program to interconnect separate networks — including satellite and radio packet networks — into a single "internet". DARPA sponsored implementations on multiple operating systems and required TCP/IP on the ARPANET from 1 January 1983, a transition known as "flag day".

Organisation and mission

DARPA employs roughly a hundred programme managers on fixed terms who fund research performed by universities, industry and government laboratories. Its stated mission is "to make pivotal investments in breakthrough technologies for national security".

Notable projects

Beyond networking, DARPA programmes have contributed to GPS miniaturisation, stealth aircraft technology, unmanned aerial vehicles, and the Grand Challenge competitions that accelerated self-driving car research. It also funded early work on computer graphics, artificial intelligence and the computer mouse through its Information Processing Techniques Office.
