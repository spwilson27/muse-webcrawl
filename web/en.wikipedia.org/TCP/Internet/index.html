Internet

The Internet is the global system of interconnected computer networks that uses the Internet protocol suite (TCP/IP) to communicate between networks and devices. It is a network of networks consisting of private, public, academic, business, and government networks of local to global scope, linked by a broad array of electronic, wireless, and optical networking technologies. The Internet carries many information resources and services, such as the World Wide Web, electronic mail, telephony, and file sharing.

History

The Internet originated in the ARPANET, a packet-switching network funded by the US DARPA and first demonstrated in 1969. To interconnect heterogeneous networks, Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn developed the Transmission Control Protocol and Internet Protocol during the 1970s. The ARPANET adopted TCP/IP on 1 January 1983, and in the 1980s networks such as NSFNET linked universities and research centres. Commercial restrictions were lifted in the early 1990s, and the Web, invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989, drove mass adoption.

Architecture

Internet traffic is exchanged between autonomous systems — networks operated by internet service providers, companies and institutions — using the Border Gateway Protocol. The Domain Name System translates human-readable names into the numeric IP addresses used for routing. Data is transported in packets that may traverse many networks before reaching their destination.

Governance and society

No single body owns or controls the Internet; standards are developed openly by the IETF, and names and numbers are coordinated by ICANN. With more than five billion users, the Internet has transformed commerce, media, education and social interaction, while raising debates about privacy, security, censorship and the digital divide.
