IPv6

Internet Protocol version 6 (IPv6) is the most recent version of the Internet Protocol (IP), the communications protocol that provides an identification and location system for computers on networks and routes traffic across the Internet. IPv6 was developed by the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) to deal with the long-anticipated problem of IPv4 address exhaustion. It was first specified in RFC 1883 in 1995 and standardised in RFC 8200 in 2017.

Addressing

IPv6 uses 128-bit addresses, theoretically allowing 2128, or approximately 3.4×1038, unique addresses. Addresses are written as eight groups of four hexadecimal digits separated by colons, for example 2001:0db8:85a3:0000:0000:8a2e:0370:7334, and leading zeros and runs of zero groups may be compressed. The huge address space permits hierarchical allocation and largely eliminates the need for network address translation.

Packet format

The IPv6 header is fixed at 40 bytes and is simpler than IPv4's: fields for header length and checksum were removed, fragmentation is handled only by the sending host, and optional information is carried in extension headers. A flow label field supports quality-of-service handling.

Deployment

IPv6 deployment began in the 2000s and accelerated after World IPv6 Launch in June 2012. Major content providers and mobile networks now deliver a large share of traffic over IPv6, though IPv4 remains in widespread use alongside it through dual-stack operation and transition mechanisms such as NAT64.
