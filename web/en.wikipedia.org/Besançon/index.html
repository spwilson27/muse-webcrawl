Besançon is a city in eastern France, the capital of the department of Doubs and the historical and cultural capital of Franche-Comté. It lies in a loop of the river Doubs, overlooked by the Citadel of Besançon, a masterpiece of the military architect Vauban and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The city grew from the Gallo-Roman settlement of Vesontio, mentioned by Julius Caesar. It later became a free imperial city and was incorporated into France in 1678. From the late eighteenth century Besançon became the capital of the French watchmaking industry.

Culture

Besançon is the birthplace of Victor Hugo and the Lumière brothers. Its historic centre contains the astronomical clock of Besançon Cathedral, the Musée du Temps, and one of France's oldest public museums.
