Infection control

Infection control is the discipline concerned with preventing healthcare-associated infection; it is a practical, sub-discipline of epidemiology and an essential part of the infrastructure of health care. Infection control addresses factors related to the spread of infections within a healthcare setting, whether patient-to-patient, from patients to staff or from staff to patients, and encompasses prevention (hand hygiene, barrier precautions, sterilisation, disinfection, vaccination), monitoring and surveillance of infections, investigation of outbreaks, and management of infection-control programmes.

Key measures

  • Hand hygiene — the single most effective measure; promoted through the WHO "Five Moments" and alcohol-based hand rubs, following the tradition of Semmelweis and Lister.
  • Standard and transmission-based precautions — gloves, gowns, masks and eye protection; contact, droplet and airborne precautions (including isolation rooms and respirators).
  • Sterilisation and disinfection — of instruments and environments; aseptic technique for invasive procedures.
  • Surveillance and stewardship — tracking device-associated infections (catheter, ventilator, surgical-site) and antimicrobial stewardship to combat resistance.

Organisations

Guidelines are issued by bodies such as the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and its Healthcare Infection Control Practices Advisory Committee, the World Health Organization, and professional associations including APIC and the Society for Healthcare Epidemiology of America.
