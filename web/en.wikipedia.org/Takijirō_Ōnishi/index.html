Takijirō Ōnishi

Takijirō Ōnishi (大西 瀧治郎, Ōnishi Takijirō, 2 June 1891 – 16 August 1945) was an admiral in the Imperial Japanese Navy during the Second World War. He is best known as the founder of the kamikaze suicide attack tactic, which he organised in the Philippines in October 1944 as commander of the First Air Fleet.

Early career

Ōnishi graduated from the Imperial Japanese Naval Academy in 1912 and became one of the pioneers of Japanese naval aviation. He served in Britain and France during the First World War to study combat aircraft development, and afterwards held a series of aviation-related staff and command posts, promoting the development of carrier air power alongside figures such as Isoroku Yamamoto.

Second World War

Ōnishi helped plan Japanese naval air operations in China and was involved in the early stages of planning connected with the attack on Pearl Harbor. In October 1944, as the American invasion of the Philippines began, he took command of the First Air Fleet in Luzon. Concluding that conventional attacks could no longer inflict meaningful damage with the few aircraft available, he formed the first "special attack" (tokkōtai) units, whose pilots deliberately crashed their aircraft into American warships. The first attacks took place during the Battle of Leyte Gulf.

Death

Ōnishi was recalled to Tokyo in May 1945 as Vice-Chief of the Naval General Staff. After Japan's surrender was announced, he committed ritual suicide (seppuku) on 16 August 1945, leaving a note apologising to the spirits of the special attack pilots and urging young civilians to work for Japan's reconstruction.

Legacy

Ōnishi remains a controversial figure: remembered by some as a devoted officer who shared his men's fate, and by others as the architect of a tactic that cost thousands of young pilots their lives.
