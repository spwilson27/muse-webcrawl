Carlo Ancelotti Cavaliere OMRI (born 10 June 1959) is an Italian professional football manager and former player who is the head coach of the Brazil national team. Nicknamed "Carletto" in Italy and "Don Carlo" in Spain, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest managers of all time. Ancelotti is the most decorated manager in UEFA Champions League history, having won the trophy a record five times as a coach (twice with AC Milan and three times with Real Madrid). He is also the only manager to have won league titles in all of Europe's top five leagues.

Playing career

Ancelotti began his career in 1974 with Parma, helping the club to promotion to Serie B in 1979. He moved to Roma the following season, where he won one Serie A title and four Coppa Italia titles, and captained the side. In 1987 he transferred to AC Milan, where he won two Serie A titles, two European Cups, two European Super Cups and two Intercontinental Cups as part of Arrigo Sacchi's famous side. A creative and industrious midfielder, his career was curtailed by persistent knee injuries, and he retired in 1992 at the age of 33. He earned 26 caps for Italy, scoring once, and was part of the squad that finished third at the 1990 FIFA World Cup on home soil.

Managerial career

Early years and Juventus

After studying at Coverciano and serving as an assistant to Sacchi with the Italy national team at the 1994 World Cup, Ancelotti began his managerial career with Reggiana in 1995, winning promotion to Serie A at the first attempt. He then managed Parma, guiding them to second place in Serie A in 1997, before taking over at Juventus in 1999, where he finished as league runner-up twice.

AC Milan

Ancelotti joined Milan in 2001 and led the club to a golden era: the UEFA Champions League in 2003 and 2007, the Serie A title in 2004, the Coppa Italia in 2003, two UEFA Super Cups, and the FIFA Club World Cup in 2007. His Milan side, built around Andrea Pirlo, Kaká, Clarence Seedorf and Paolo Maldini, was renowned for its elegant midfield "Christmas tree" formation.

Chelsea, Paris Saint-Germain and Real Madrid

In 2009 Ancelotti moved to Chelsea, winning the Premier League and FA Cup double in his first season. He then led Paris Saint-Germain to the Ligue 1 title in 2013 before joining Real Madrid, where he delivered "La Décima" — the club's long-awaited tenth European Cup — in 2014, along with the Copa del Rey.

Bayern Munich, Napoli and Everton

Ancelotti won the Bundesliga with Bayern Munich in 2017, then had spells with Napoli and Everton before returning to Madrid in 2021.

Return to Real Madrid and Brazil

In his second spell at Real Madrid, Ancelotti won two more Champions League titles (2022 and 2024), two La Liga titles, and completed a full set of major trophies with the club. In 2025 he left Madrid to become head coach of the Brazil national team, the first foreigner to hold the position on a permanent basis in a century.

Managerial style

Ancelotti is noted for his tactical flexibility, calm man-management and ability to build harmonious dressing rooms. Rather than imposing a rigid system, he adapts formations to the strengths of his players, a philosophy set out in his autobiography Quiet Leadership.

Honours

  • As player: Serie A (Roma 1983; Milan 1988, 1992), European Cup (Milan 1989, 1990).
  • As manager: UEFA Champions League ×5 (Milan 2003, 2007; Real Madrid 2014, 2022, 2024); league titles in Italy, England, France, Germany and Spain — the only manager to win all five.
