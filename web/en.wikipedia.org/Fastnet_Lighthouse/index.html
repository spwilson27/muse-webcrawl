Fastnet Lighthouse is a 54-metre-tall lighthouse situated on the remote Fastnet Rock in the Atlantic Ocean, off the southern coast of Ireland. It is the most southerly point of Ireland and lies 6.5 kilometres southwest of Cape Clear Island and 13 kilometres from the Irish mainland. Because many emigrant ships passed it on their way to America, the rock was nicknamed "Ireland's Teardrop".

History

The first lighthouse, a cast-iron tower designed by George Halpin, was lit in 1854, replacing an earlier light on Cape Clear. It proved too weak for the Atlantic storms — vibrations shook the tower and waves smashed over it. A new tower of interlocking Cornish granite blocks, designed by William Douglass, was built around the base of the old one and first lit in 1904; it remains one of the most famous lighthouses in the world. The lighthouse was automated in 1989.

Fastnet Race

The rock is the turning mark of the Fastnet Race, the biennial offshore yacht race from Cowes around Fastnet Rock and back to Plymouth (now finishing at Cherbourg). The 1979 race was hit by a severe storm in which 15 sailors died.
