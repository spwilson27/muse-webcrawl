Ichthyology

Ichthyology is the branch of zoology devoted to the study of fish, including bony fish (Osteichthyes), cartilaginous fish (Chondrichthyes), and jawless fish (Agnatha). The word derives from Greek ikhthús (fish) and logos (study). The field encompasses fish biology, anatomy, physiology, taxonomy, ecology, behaviour and evolution; practitioners are ichthyologists. More than 34,000 fish species have been described — more than all other vertebrate groups combined — and hundreds of new species are described each year.

History

Aristotle provided the first systematic account of fishes in the 4th century BCE. Renaissance works by Pierre Belon, Guillaume Rondelet and Conrad Gessner laid the foundations of scientific description. Peter Artedi, a contemporary of Linnaeus, is often called the father of ichthyology for his classification of fishes into orders, genera and species. The 19th and 20th centuries saw great catalogues by Albert Günther, George Albert Boulenger and David Starr Jordan, and the founding of professional societies such as the American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists (1913) and the journal Copeia.

Modern practice

Modern ichthyologists work in museums, universities, fisheries agencies and aquariums. Methods include field collecting, morphological measurement and meristics (counting fin rays and scales), anatomical study, DNA barcoding and phylogenomics, otolith (ear-stone) ageing, tagging and telemetry, and acoustic surveys. Reference works such as Fishes of the World (Nelson et al.), the Catalog of Fishes maintained by the California Academy of Sciences, and the FishBase database anchor the discipline. Research also underpins fisheries management, aquaculture, and conservation of threatened species — from freshwater fishes (the most imperilled vertebrates) to commercially vital stocks like those behind Icelandic cuisine.

Notable ichthyologists

Notable figures include Marcus Elieser Bloch, Georges Cuvier, Louis Agassiz, Theodore Gill, Eugenia Clark (the "Shark Lady"), and Ethelwynn Trewavas. The largest fish collections, such as those of the Smithsonian Institution and the Natural History Museum, London, hold millions of specimens.
