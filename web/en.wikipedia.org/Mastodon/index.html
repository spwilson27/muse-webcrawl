Mastodon

FamilyMammutidae
EpochMiocene to Pleistocene
ExtinctionAbout 10,000 years ago

Mastodons are extinct proboscideans of the genus Mammut (family Mammutidae) that inhabited North and Central America from the late Miocene until their extinction at the end of the Pleistocene, roughly 10,000 to 11,000 years ago. They resembled elephants and mammoths but were browsers with distinctive conical teeth adapted to chewing leaves and twigs.

Description

The American mastodon (Mammut americanum) stood about 2.3 to 3.3 metres at the shoulder and was covered in coarse hair. Unlike the flat grinding teeth of mammoths, mastodon molars had pointed cusps suited to forest vegetation.

Extinction

Mastodons disappeared during the Quaternary extinction event, likely through a combination of climate change at the end of the last Ice Age and hunting by early humans.
