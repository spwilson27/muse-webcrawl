Frenet–Serret formulas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In differential geometry, the Frenet–Serret formulas describe the kinematic properties of a particle moving along a continuous, differentiable curve in three-dimensional Euclidean space, or equivalently the geometric properties of the curve itself. More specifically, the formulas describe the derivatives of the so-called tangent, normal, and binormal unit vectors of the curve in terms of each other. They were discovered independently by Jean Frédéric Frenet (in his 1847 thesis) and Joseph Alfred Serret (in 1851).

The Frenet frame

Let r(t) be a curve in Euclidean space parametrised by arc length s. The unit tangent vector is T = dr/ds. Where the curvature κ is nonzero, the unit normal vector N points in the direction of dT/ds, and the binormal vector is B = T × N. The three vectors T, N, B form an orthonormal basis called the Frenet frame, which moves along the curve.

The formulas

The Frenet–Serret formulas state:

dT/ds = κ N dN/ds = −κ T + τ B dB/ds = −τ N

where κ is the curvature and τ is the torsion of the curve. Curvature measures how sharply the curve bends away from a straight line, while torsion measures how sharply it twists out of its osculating plane.

Interpretation and applications

Written in matrix form, the formulas show the frame rotating with angular velocity given by the Darboux vector τT + κB. They are fundamental to the theory of space curves: a curve is determined up to rigid motion by its curvature and torsion as functions of arc length. The formulas are used in physics (for the motion of particles along curved paths), computer graphics, and the kinematics of robotics.

The plane-curve case reduces to the single relation dT/ds = κN. Generalisations to higher dimensions replace the frame with a full moving frame and multiple curvatures.
