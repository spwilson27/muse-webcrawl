Halloween, or Hallowe'en (a contraction of "All Hallows' evening"), also known as All Hallows' Eve or All Saints' Eve, is a celebration observed in many countries on 31 October, the eve of the Western Christian feast of All Hallows' Day (All Saints' Day). It begins the season of Allhallowtide, dedicated to remembering the dead, including saints (hallows), martyrs and the faithful departed.

History

Halloween is widely thought to have roots in the ancient Celtic harvest festival of Samhain, when people lit bonfires and wore costumes to ward off ghosts, and the boundary between the worlds of the living and the dead was believed to blur. In the eighth century, Pope Gregory III designated 1 November as a time to honour all saints, and the evening before became All Hallows' Eve. Over the centuries the festival absorbed customs such as "souling" and "guising" in Britain and Ireland, and was carried to North America by immigrants, where it developed into a major secular and commercial celebration, especially after the Second World War.

Traditions

Trick-or-treating

Children in costume go door to door asking for treats with the phrase "trick or treat" — a playful suggestion that a mischievous trick may be played if no treat is given. The custom descends from souling, mumming and guising practices and became widespread in the United States and Canada in the mid-20th century, later spreading to many other countries.

Costumes and parties

Dressing in costume — traditionally as ghosts, witches, vampires and other supernatural beings, and in modern times as characters from popular culture — is central to Halloween for both children and adults. Costume parties, haunted houses and haunted attractions, horror-film marathons and visits to supposedly haunted locations are common.

Jack-o'-lanterns

Faces carved into pumpkins (originally turnips in Ireland and Scotland) and lit from within by candles are displayed as jack-o'-lanterns, associated in folklore with the wandering soul of "Stingy Jack" and with the warding off of evil spirits.

Food

Seasonal foods include candy and toffee apples, barmbrack in Ireland, soul cakes, and pumpkin-based dishes; Halloween is one of the largest confectionery-selling occasions of the year.

Religious observance and responses

Christians have historically marked the vigil of All Saints with church services and prayers for the dead, and some continue to do so; others object to Halloween's imagery or pagan associations, and some schools and communities substitute harvest or "light" parties. The festival's relationship to the Mexican Day of the Dead (1–2 November) is often noted, though the two are distinct traditions.

Economic impact

In the United States, Halloween is among the most commercially significant holidays, with annual spending on costumes, decorations, candy and greeting cards exceeding ten billion dollars in recent years.
