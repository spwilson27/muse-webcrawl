Wichita State University (WSU) is a public research university in Wichita, Kansas, United States. It is part of the Kansas Board of Regents system and is classified among "R2: Doctoral Universities – High research activity." The university offers more than 60 undergraduate degree programs in more than 200 areas of study in its colleges of business, education, engineering, fine arts, health professions, and liberal arts and sciences.

History

Wichita State began in 1886 as a preparatory school operated by the Congregational Church, and in 1895 became Fairmount College, named for Fairmount Avenue in Wichita. In 1926 the city of Wichita acquired the college and renamed it the Municipal University of Wichita. In 1964 it entered the Kansas state university system and took its present name.

Campus and innovation

The main campus occupies 330 acres. The university is noted for its National Institute for Aviation Research (NIAR) and its Innovation Campus, which hosts industry partners including Airbus and Dassault Systèmes. WSU is also home to the original Pizza Hut building, preserved as a museum; the restaurant chain was founded in Wichita in 1958 by brothers Dan and Frank Carney, who were students at the university.

Athletics

The Wichita State Shockers compete in NCAA Division I as members of the American Athletic Conference. The men's basketball team reached the Final Four in 1965 and 2013, the latter capping an undefeated regular season run that reached 35–0. The nickname "Shockers" derives from students who harvested wheat ("shocked" wheat) to earn money during harvest season.
