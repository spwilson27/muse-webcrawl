Teotihuacan

Teotihuacan is an ancient Mesoamerican city located in the Basin of Mexico, about 40 km northeast of modern Mexico City. It was one of the largest cities in the Americas, reaching a population of over 125,000 at its zenith around 450 CE, and is known for its vast pyramids, apartment compounds and grid plan oriented along the Avenue of the Dead.

History

The city began to grow around 200 BCE and became the dominant power in highland Mexico during the Classic period. Its main monuments — the Pyramid of the Sun, the Pyramid of the Moon and the Temple of the Feathered Serpent — were built in the first centuries CE. Teotihuacan exerted influence across Mesoamerica, including at Maya cities such as Tikal. The city declined and its centre was burned around 550 CE.

Name and people

The name Teotihuacan was given by the Aztecs centuries after the city's fall; it means "place where the gods were created" in Nahuatl. The ethnicity and language of its founders remain debated, as is the identity of its rulers — no royal tombs or portraits of kings have been securely identified, suggesting a possibly collective form of government.
