Old master print
Periodc. 1400 – 1800
TechniquesWoodcut, engraving, etching, mezzotint, aquatint
Key figuresDürer, Rembrandt, Schongauer, Marcantonio Raimondi, Goya

An old master print is a work of art produced by a printing process within the Western tradition, produced during the period extending from the invention of printmaking in about 1400 to about 1800. The term is used by art dealers, collectors, and museums to distinguish the fine-art prints of this era from later reproductive and commercial printmaking.

History

Printmaking emerged in Europe in the early 15th century, first as woodcuts of religious images and playing cards. Martin Schongauer raised engraving to a fine art in the late 15th century, and his influence spread across Europe. Albrecht Dürer revolutionized both woodcut and engraving, creating prints of unprecedented technical virtuosity that were traded across the continent and made him famous throughout Europe in his lifetime.

In Italy, Marcantonio Raimondi built a career engraving the designs of Raphael, effectively inventing the reproductive print trade. In the 17th century, Rembrandt van Rijn transformed etching into an expressive medium, producing around 300 etchings. In the 18th century, Giovanni Battista Piranesi's etchings of Rome and Francisco Goya's series such as Los Caprichos extended the medium's range; Goya exploited the new technique of aquatint for tonal effect.

Techniques

The principal techniques of the period were relief printing (woodcut), intaglio processes (engraving, etching, drypoint, mezzotint, and aquatint), and, at the very end of the period, lithography, invented in 1796. Prints were typically sold loose, pasted into albums, or bound into books; print rooms and collectors' cabinets became standard features of courts and wealthy houses.

Collecting and connoisseurship

Old master prints are catalogued in standard reference works (Bartsch, Hollstein) that assign each print a number and record its "states" — successive versions as the artist reworked the plate. Impressions printed in the artist's lifetime, especially early, rich impressions, command the highest prices. Major public collections are held by the British Museum, the Albertina in Vienna, and the Metropolitan Museum of Art.
