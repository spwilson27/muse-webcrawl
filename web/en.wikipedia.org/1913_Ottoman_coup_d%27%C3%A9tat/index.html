The 1913 Ottoman coup d'état, also known as the Raid on the Sublime Porte (Turkish: Bâb-ı Âlî Baskını), was a coup by the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP) that took place on 23 January 1913, during the First Balkan War. A group of CUP officers led by Enver Bey (later Enver Pasha) stormed the Sublime Porte, the seat of government, and forced Grand Vizier Kâmil Pasha to resign at gunpoint; the Minister of War, Nazım Pasha, was shot dead during the raid.

Background

The coup was a response to the Liberal Union government's willingness to accept peace terms surrendering Edirne (Adrianople) to Bulgaria amid catastrophic defeats in the Balkan War. The CUP had lost power in 1912 to a rival military-backed government and regained it through the raid.

Consequences

After the coup, the CUP established an increasingly authoritarian regime dominated by the "Three Pashas" — Enver, Talaat, and Djemal — who would lead the Ottoman Empire into the First World War on the side of the Central Powers.
