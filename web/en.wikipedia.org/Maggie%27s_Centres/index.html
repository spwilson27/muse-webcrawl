Maggie's, formally the Maggie Keswick Jencks Cancer Caring Trust and known for its Maggie's Centres, is a British charity that provides free practical, emotional, and social support to people with cancer and their family and friends. Its centres are purposely built, architecturally distinguished buildings sited in the grounds of major NHS cancer hospitals, offering a drop-in, non-clinical environment that contrasts deliberately with hospital wards.

Origins

The charity was the idea of writer, gardener, and designer Maggie Keswick Jencks, who, during her own treatment for recurrent cancer, concluded that patients needed a place of their own near the hospital for information, psychological support, and quiet reflection. With her husband, the architectural critic Charles Jencks, she wrote the blueprint for the first centre before her death in 1995. The first Maggie's Centre opened in Edinburgh in 1996, in a converted stable block designed by Richard Murphy.

Architecture

Maggie's is noted for commissioning leading architects to design small but striking buildings. Centres have been designed by Frank Gehry (Dundee), Zaha Hadid (Fife), Rem Koolhaas/OMA (Glasgow's centre was by OMA), Richard Rogers (London, Hammersmith), Norman Foster (Manchester), Snøhetta (Aberdeen), and Steven Holl (St Bartholomew's, London). Charles Jencks argued that good architecture itself could contribute to wellbeing, and each centre is domestic in scale, centred on a kitchen table, with no reception desk or signage of a clinical kind.

Services

Centres are staffed by cancer support specialists, psychologists, and benefits advisers. They offer psychological support, courses on relaxation and nutrition, assistance with money worries, and simply a calm place to sit; no appointment is needed. All support is free, and the charity is funded entirely by voluntary donations.
