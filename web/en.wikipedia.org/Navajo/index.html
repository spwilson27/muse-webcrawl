The Navajo (spelled Navaho in older usage; Navajo: Diné or Naabeehó Bináhásdzo) are a Native American people of the Southwestern United States. The Navajo Nation, covering about 17.5 million acres (roughly 27,000 square miles) across northeastern Arizona, southeastern Utah and northwestern New Mexico, is the largest reservation in the United States, and the Navajo Nation has the largest enrolled population of any federally recognised tribe.

History

The Navajo are a Southern Athabaskan people whose ancestors migrated south from the subarctic regions of present-day Canada and Alaska, reaching the Southwest by around the 15th century. They adopted farming, weaving and other practices partly through contact with Pueblo peoples. Spanish colonisation brought conflict as well as sheep, horses and silversmithing traditions.

In 1863–1864, US forces under Kit Carson conducted a scorched-earth campaign against the Navajo, forcing some 8,000–10,000 people on the "Long Walk" to imprisonment at Bosque Redondo (Fort Sumner) in New Mexico. The Treaty of 1868 allowed the Navajo to return to a portion of their homeland and established the reservation. During the Second World War, Navajo "code talkers" used their language as an unbreakable code for US Marine communications in the Pacific.

Culture

Navajo culture is traditionally matrilineal, with clan identity passed through the mother. The hogan is the traditional dwelling and ceremonial structure. Navajo weaving — rugs and blankets of striking geometric pattern — and silver and turquoise jewellery are internationally celebrated arts. Ceremonial life centres on "sings" such as the Blessingway, aimed at restoring hózhó (harmony, beauty and balance).

Language

The Navajo language (Diné bizaad) is a Southern Athabaskan language of the Na-Dené family, noted for its tonal contrasts and complex verb morphology. With roughly 170,000 speakers, it is the most widely spoken Indigenous language north of Mexico, though it is considered endangered and is the focus of revitalisation efforts, immersion schools and a Navajo-language Wikipedia and media.

Government

The Navajo Nation is governed from Window Rock, Arizona, by an elected president, vice-president and the Navajo Nation Council. Its economy includes coal, oil, gas and uranium resources — uranium mining left a major legacy of contamination — alongside agriculture, tourism and tribal enterprises.
