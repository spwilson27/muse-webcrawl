President pro tempore of the United States Senate

The president pro tempore of the United States Senate (often shortened to president pro tem) is the second-highest-ranking official of the U.S. Senate and its most senior member of the majority party by custom. The office is established by Article One of the U.S. Constitution, which provides for a president pro tempore to preside in the absence of the vice president.

Role

The president pro tempore presides over the Senate when the vice president is absent, though in practice junior senators usually rotate in the chair. The officeholder is third in the line of succession to the presidency, after the vice president and the Speaker of the House, under the Presidential Succession Act of 1947.

History

By tradition since the late 19th century, the position goes to the longest-serving senator of the majority party. Notable holders include Strom Thurmond, Robert Byrd, and Patrick Leahy. The president pro tempore emeritus title is sometimes given to a former holder from the minority party.
