Võ Nguyên Giáp (25 August 1911 – 4 October 2013) was a Vietnamese general and statesman, regarded as one of the greatest military strategists of the 20th century. He commanded the Viet Minh and the People's Army of Vietnam under Ho Chi Minh.

Early life

Born in Quảng Bình province, Giáp was educated in Huế and Hanoi, worked as a teacher and journalist, and joined the Indochinese Communist Party. He fled to China in 1940, where his wife was imprisoned and died, and returned to build armed propaganda units in the northern mountains.

Wars

In the First Indochina War Giáp's forces defeated France at the Battle of Dien Bien Phu in 1954, ending French colonial rule. During the Vietnam War he was commander-in-chief against South Vietnam and the United States, overseeing the strategy behind the Tet Offensive of 1968 and the 1975 Ho Chi Minh Campaign that reunified Vietnam, working alongside the Viet Cong in the south.

Later life

Giáp served as defence minister until 1980 and deputy prime minister until 1991. He lived to 102, was honoured as a national hero, and is remembered for his doctrine of protracted people's war — gradually wearing down a stronger enemy politically and militarily.
