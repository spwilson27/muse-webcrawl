Epiphyte

An epiphyte is a plant that grows on the surface of another plant (a phorophyte) and derives its moisture and nutrients from the air, rain, water, or accumulated debris. Epiphytes are not parasites: they use the host plant only for physical support. Familiar examples include many orchids, bromeliads such as Spanish moss, ferns, and mosses.

Epiphytes colonizing a tree branch in a tropical forest

Adaptations

Because they are disconnected from the soil, epiphytes face chronic shortages of water and nutrients. Adaptations include thick, water-storing leaves; absorptive scales (trichomes) in bromeliads; velamen-covered aerial roots in orchids that soak up rainwater; and crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM) photosynthesis, which reduces water loss. Tank bromeliads collect water in rosettes of leaves, creating miniature ecosystems used by frogs and insects.

Ecology

Epiphytes are most diverse in tropical rainforests, where they can constitute a large fraction of plant species and biomass in the canopy. They provide food and habitat for canopy animals and contribute significantly to nutrient cycling in forests.
