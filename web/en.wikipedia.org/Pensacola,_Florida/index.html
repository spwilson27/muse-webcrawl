Pensacola, Florida

Pensacola is the county seat of Escambia County, Florida, and the westernmost city in the Florida Panhandle. Situated on Pensacola Bay, an inlet of the Gulf of Mexico, it had a population of about 54,000 at the 2020 census. Pensacola is nicknamed "The City of Five Flags" for the five governments that have flown flags over it: Spain, France, Great Britain, the Confederacy and the United States.

History

The area was first settled by Europeans in 1559 when Tristán de Luna y Arellano established a colony, making Pensacola one of the earliest European settlements in what is now the United States; a hurricane destroyed it two years later. A permanent Spanish presidio was founded in 1698. Pensacola passed to Britain in 1763 after the Seven Years' War, serving as capital of British West Florida, and was recaptured by Spanish forces under Bernardo de Gálvez at the Siege of Pensacola in 1781. Spain ceded Florida to the United States in 1821.

Economy and culture

The military is a major employer: Naval Air Station Pensacola, established in 1914, is the primary training base for U.S. naval aviation and home of the Blue Angels flight demonstration squadron. Tourism centres on the white-sand beaches of Pensacola Beach on Santa Rosa Island. The city hosts the National Naval Aviation Museum, one of the world's largest aviation museums. Pensacola is also known for its historic downtown, the Saenger Theatre, and seasonal festivals. Hurricane Ivan caused severe damage in 2004.
