Igor Stravinsky

Born 17 June 1882, Oranienbaum, Russia
Died 6 April 1971, New York City
Notable works The Firebird, Petrushka, The Rite of Spring

Igor Fyodorovich Stravinsky (17 June 1882 – 6 April 1971) was a Russian composer and conductor, later a citizen of France and the United States. He is widely considered one of the most important and influential composers of the 20th century.

Early career and the Ballets Russes

A pupil of Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov, Stravinsky achieved fame with three ballets written for Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes in Paris: The Firebird (1910), Petrushka (1911), and The Rite of Spring (1913), whose premiere provoked a famous near-riot and whose rhythmic innovations transformed modern music.

Later styles

After leaving Russia, Stravinsky adopted a neoclassical style in works such as Pulcinella and the opera The Rake's Progress, and in the 1950s he turned to serial techniques. He settled in the United States in 1939 and died in New York in 1971.
