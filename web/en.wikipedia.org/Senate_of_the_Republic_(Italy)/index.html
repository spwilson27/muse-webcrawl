Senate of the Republic (Italy)

The Senate of the Republic (Italian: Senato della Repubblica) is the upper house of the Italian Parliament, the lower house being the Chamber of Deputies. Together the two houses form a system of perfect bicameralism, in which both chambers have essentially equal legislative powers. The Senate meets at Palazzo Madama in Rome.

Composition

Since the constitutional reform approved in 2020, the Senate has 200 elected members: 196 elected in Italy and 4 elected by Italians abroad. Senators are elected by citizens aged 18 and over; candidates must be at least 40 years old. In addition, former Presidents of the Republic are senators for life by right, and the President may appoint up to five citizens who have honoured the nation through exceptional merits as senators for life. Senators serve five-year terms, as does the Chamber of Deputies.

History

The present Senate is the successor of the Senate of the Kingdom of Sardinia (1848), extended to the whole Kingdom of Italy after unification, whose members were appointed for life by the king. The Constituent Assembly of 1946–47 transformed it into an elected body, and it first convened on 8 May 1948 under the new republican Constitution. A 2016 constitutional referendum that would have sharply reduced the Senate's powers and size was rejected by voters; a later reform (2020) instead cut the number of parliamentarians, reducing elected senators from 315 to 200.

Powers

Legislation must be approved in identical form by both houses. The government must win a vote of confidence in the Senate as well as in the Chamber. The President of the Senate — second in the order of precedence after the President of the Republic — acts as deputy of the head of state and presides over joint sittings in some circumstances.
