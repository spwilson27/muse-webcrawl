Doritos

Nacho Cheese Doritos, the best-known flavour.

Doritos (/dəˈriːtoʊs/) is an American brand of flavoured tortilla chips produced by Frito-Lay, a subsidiary of PepsiCo. The chips, made from ground corn, oil, and seasoning, were first created in 1964 and launched nationally in the United States in 1966. The name derives from the Spanish doradito, roughly "little golden thing". Doritos has become one of the most popular snack brands in the world.

History

The product originated at the Casa de Fritos restaurant at Disneyland in Anaheim, California, where excess tortillas were fried and seasoned rather than discarded. Frito-Lay executive Arch West championed the idea, and Doritos were launched as the first tortilla chip sold nationally in the United States. Flavours expanded from the original toasted corn to Taco in 1967 and Nacho Cheese in 1972, followed by Cool Ranch (Cool Original outside North America) in 1986.

Marketing

Doritos is known for heavy advertising aimed at young consumers, including long-running Super Bowl commercials and the "Crash the Super Bowl" contest that aired fan-made advertisements. Logos have periodically removed the brand name in campaigns claiming the chips are recognisable without it.

Flavours and variants

Dozens of flavours and formats have appeared, including Spicy Sweet Chili, Flamin' Hot, Doritos 3D, Doritos Locos Tacos (produced with Taco Bell), and limited "mystery" editions. The recipe has occasionally been reformulated; in 2006 trans fats were removed from the US product.
