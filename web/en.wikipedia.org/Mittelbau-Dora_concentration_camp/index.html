Mittelbau-Dora (also Dora-Mittelbau and Nordhausen-Dora) was a Nazi concentration camp established in August 1943 near Nordhausen in Thuringia, Germany, to supply forced labour for the production of V-2 ballistic missiles and other weapons in the underground Mittelwerk factory, built inside tunnels of the Kohnstein mountain. It began as a subcamp of Buchenwald and became an independent camp in October 1944, eventually administering a network of some 40 subcamps.

History

After the RAF bombing of Peenemünde in August 1943, the Nazi leadership decided to move rocket production underground. The first prisoners arrived from Buchenwald on 28 August 1943 and were forced to enlarge existing gypsum-mine tunnels into a factory. During the first months, prisoners slept inside the tunnels themselves, in four-tier bunks, without daylight, ventilation, or sanitation; thousands died of exhaustion, disease, and malnutrition. Roughly 2,900 V-2 rockets and large numbers of V-1 flying bombs were assembled at Mittelwerk by the end of the war. More prisoners died manufacturing the V-2 than were killed by its use as a weapon.

Evacuation and liberation

In early April 1945 the SS evacuated most prisoners on death marches and transports toward Bergen-Belsen and other camps; many thousands died. American troops of the 3rd Armored and 104th Infantry Divisions liberated the largely emptied camp on 11 April 1945, finding the survivors and the underground factory largely intact.

Aftermath

Evidence from Dora featured in the Dachau trials, where 15 former SS staff and kapos were tried in 1947. Wernher von Braun and other rocket engineers connected with Mittelwerk were taken to the United States under Operation Paperclip. Today the site is a memorial (KZ-Gedenkstätte Mittelbau-Dora), with a museum and access to part of the tunnel system.
