The goalkeeper (GK) is the most defensive position in association football. The goalkeeper's primary role is to prevent the opposing team from scoring by stopping shots on goal, and they are the only player permitted to handle the ball — and then only within their own penalty area.

Laws of the game

Under the Laws of the Game, a match cannot proceed without a goalkeeper. Handling privileges come with restrictions: since the 1992 back-pass rule, a goalkeeper may not handle a ball deliberately kicked to them by a team-mate, nor one received directly from a team-mate's throw-in. A goalkeeper who handles outside the area, or denies an obvious goalscoring opportunity, can be sent off like any other player.

Role and skills

Goalkeepers must combine shot-stopping reflexes, command of the penalty area on crosses, and distribution — by throwing or kicking — to launch attacks. Since the back-pass rule, foot skills have become essential, and "sweeper-keepers" such as Manuel Neuer play far off their line behind a high defensive line. Goalkeepers wear a jersey of a colour distinct from both teams and the officials, and almost all wear gloves.

Notable goalkeepers

Lev Yashin of the Soviet Union remains the only goalkeeper to win the Ballon d'Or (1963). Other widely acclaimed goalkeepers include Gordon Banks, Dino Zoff, Gianluigi Buffon, Iker Casillas and Manuel Neuer.
