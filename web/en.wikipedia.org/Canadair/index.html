Canadair

Canadair Ltd. was a Canadian civil and military aircraft manufacturer that operated from 1944 to 1986. Based in Montreal, it built a wide range of aircraft, including licence-built American designs and original types such as the CL-415 water bomber. Although operations folded into Bombardier Aerospace, Canadair lives on as the brand behind Bombardier's Challenger business jets and the CRJ regional jet family.

History

Canadair was created as a separate entity by Vickers in 1944 at the former Canadian Vickers plant at Cartierville Airport in Saint-Laurent, Montreal. It was first operated by the Canadian government as a wartime enterprise, was sold to the Electric Boat Company in 1947, and became part of General Dynamics in 1952. The federal government bought the company back in 1976 after financial difficulties, and in 1986 sold it to Bombardier Inc.

Aircraft

Major products included the North Star (a development of the Douglas DC-4), licence-built versions of the Lockheed T-33 Shooting Star and the North American F-86 Sabre (the Canadair Sabre), the CF-104 Starfighter, the CL-215 and CL-415 amphibious water bombers, the CL-41 Tutor trainer, the CL-84 Dynavert tilt-wing experimental aircraft, and the Challenger 600 series of business jets. The Canadair Regional Jet (CRJ) programme, launched just before the Bombardier takeover, grew out of the Challenger design.
