Sun Wukong

Sun Wukong (孫悟空), also known as the Monkey King, is a literary and religious figure best known as one of the main characters in the 16th-century Chinese novel Journey to the West. In this novel, he is a monkey born from a stone who acquires supernatural powers through Taoist practices. After rebelling against heaven, he is imprisoned under a mountain by the Buddha. Five hundred years later, he accompanies the monk Tang Sanzang on a journey to retrieve Buddhist sutras from India.

Abilities

Sun Wukong possesses immense strength, can travel 108,000 li (54,000 km) in a single somersault on a cloud, and masters the 72 Earthly Transformations, allowing him to shapeshift into animals and objects. He wields the Ruyi Jingu Bang, a magical staff that can change size at will and weighs 13,500 jin. Each of his hairs can be transformed into clones, tools, or weapons. He also knows spells to command wind and water, freeze people in place, and become invisible.

Story

Born from a magic stone on the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit, Sun Wukong becomes king of the monkeys, studies under the Taoist master Subhuti, and grows so disruptive that the Jade Emperor attempts to pacify him with heavenly titles — famously the lowly post of stable keeper, and later the self-proclaimed title "Great Sage, Equal to Heaven." His rebellion ends when the Buddha imprisons him under Five Elements Mountain. Released to serve Tang Sanzang, and controlled by a golden headband that tightens when the monk recites a spell, he protects the pilgrimage against demons, ultimately attaining Buddhahood as the "Victorious Fighting Buddha."

Influence

Sun Wukong is one of the most enduring figures in Chinese literature and popular culture, worshipped by some as a god, and has inspired countless adaptations in opera, film, television, comics, and video games across East Asia and beyond.
