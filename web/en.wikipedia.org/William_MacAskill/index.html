William David MacAskill (born March 24, 1987) is a Scottish philosopher and a founder of the effective altruism movement. He is a professor of philosophy at the University of Oxford and a senior research fellow at the Global Priorities Institute.

Effective altruism

MacAskill co-founded Giving What We Can in 2009, an organisation whose members pledge to donate at least 10% of their income to effective charities, and 80,000 Hours in 2011, which advises people on high-impact careers. His book Doing Good Better (2015) introduced effective altruism — using evidence and reasoning to maximise the good one's resources do — to a wide audience.

Longtermism

In What We Owe the Future (2022), MacAskill argues for longtermism: the view that positively influencing the long-term future is a key moral priority of our time, given the vast number of people who could exist in the future and the risks that could cut human civilisation short. The book drew wide attention and criticism, and MacAskill's associations with the movement came under further scrutiny following the collapse of the FTX cryptocurrency exchange, whose founder had been a prominent donor to effective-altruism causes.
