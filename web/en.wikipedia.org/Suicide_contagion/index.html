Suicide contagion

Suicide contagion is the exposure to suicide or suicidal behaviours within one's family, peer group, or through media reports, which can result in an increase in suicide and suicidal behaviours. The phenomenon is often called the Werther effect, after the wave of imitative suicides said to have followed the 1774 publication of Goethe's novel The Sorrows of Young Werther.

Evidence

Research, much of it associated with sociologist David Phillips, who coined the term "Werther effect" in 1974, has found measurable increases in suicide rates following heavily publicised suicides, particularly celebrity deaths, with the magnitude related to the amount and prominence of coverage. Suicide clusters among adolescents are a recognised public-health concern.

Media guidelines

Health organisations including the World Health Organization publish guidelines for responsible reporting: avoid sensational headlines, do not describe methods in detail, and include information about support services. The protective counterpart — stories of people coping with crises — is termed the Papageno effect.

Prevention

Postvention programmes in schools and communities aim to identify at-risk individuals after a suicide and reduce the risk of further deaths.
