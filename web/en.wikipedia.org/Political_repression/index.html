Political repression

Political repression is the act of a state entity controlling a citizenry by force for political reasons, particularly for the purpose of restricting or preventing the citizenry's ability to take part in the political life of a society, thereby reducing their standing among their fellow citizens. It is in stark violation of human rights and is often manifested through policies such as censorship, surveillance, imprisonment of dissidents, police brutality, and the suppression of civil liberties.

Political repression can be employed by both authoritarian and nominally democratic governments, and ranges from subtle discrimination against targeted groups to systematic persecution campaigns involving secret police, show trials, forced disappearances and mass incarceration in labour camps.

Methods

Common instruments of repression include restrictions on freedom of speech, the press and assembly; the banning of opposition parties and trade unions; electoral manipulation; and the use of security services to monitor, intimidate, arrest or kill political opponents. Governments may also use deprivation of citizenship, exile, property confiscation and employment blacklists to punish dissent without overt violence.

Motivations

Repression is typically used by a regime to maintain control and defend the ruling system, to eliminate challenges to the authority of the state, or to enforce ideological conformity. Scholars distinguish between repression aimed at preventing collective action before it occurs and reactive repression responding to protests, strikes or insurgencies.

Examples

Well-documented cases include the Great Purge in the Soviet Union under Joseph Stalin, the persecution of dissidents in Nazi Germany, McCarthyism in the United States during the early Cold War, the Chilean and Argentine military dictatorships of the 1970s, and the suppression of the pro-democracy movement in China in 1989. In each case, repression was justified by the state in the name of national security or public order.

Opposition and monitoring

International organisations such as Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch document political repression worldwide. The United Nations Human Rights Committee monitors compliance with the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which guarantees freedoms of expression, assembly and association.
