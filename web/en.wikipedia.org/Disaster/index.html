Disaster

Damage caused by a severe natural disaster

A disaster is a serious problem occurring over a period of time that causes widespread loss of life, property damage, or environmental destruction, which overwhelms the affected community's ability to cope using its own resources. Disasters may be natural, such as earthquakes, floods, hurricanes, and volcanic eruptions, or human-made, such as industrial accidents, transport crashes, and armed conflict.

Classification

Emergency management commonly distinguishes natural disasters (geophysical, hydrological, meteorological, climatological, and biological) from technological and human-instigated disasters. The severity of a disaster is measured in terms of deaths, injuries, economic losses, and the scale of response required.

Response and management

Disaster management follows a cycle of mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery. Governments, humanitarian organizations such as the Red Cross and Red Crescent, and United Nations agencies coordinate relief efforts, providing shelter, food, medical care, and reconstruction support. Long-term risk reduction includes building codes, land-use planning, and early-warning systems.

Effects

Beyond immediate casualties, disasters can displace populations, disrupt economies and infrastructure, spread disease, and cause lasting psychological harm. Developing countries and impoverished communities are often disproportionately affected because of weaker infrastructure and fewer resources for recovery.
