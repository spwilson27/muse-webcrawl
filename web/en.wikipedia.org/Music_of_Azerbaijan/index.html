Music of Azerbaijan

The music of Azerbaijan encompasses folk traditions, the classical art of mugham, and modern composed and popular music. Mugham — a highly developed modal improvisational art combining sung poetry with instrumental ensembles of tar, kamancha and daf — was proclaimed by UNESCO a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.

Traditions

Folk music includes the bardic art of the ashigs, who accompany epic songs on the saz, as well as dance tunes and lyrical songs. Instruments include the tar (long-necked lute), kamancha (spike fiddle), balaban (double-reed pipe), zurna and nagara drums. In the early 20th century Uzeyir Hajibeyov fused mugham with Western forms, composing the first opera in the Muslim East, Leyli and Majnun (1908), and founding Azerbaijan's composed music tradition.

Modern music

Azerbaijan developed a distinctive jazz scene (notably Vagif Mustafazadeh's jazz-mugham fusion), a national symphonic school led by composers such as Fikret Amirov and Gara Garayev, and a popular music industry prominent since independence. Azerbaijan debuted at the Eurovision Song Contest in 2008 and won in 2011.
