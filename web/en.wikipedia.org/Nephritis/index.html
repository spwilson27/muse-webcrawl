Nephritis

Nephritis is inflammation of the kidneys and may involve the glomeruli, tubules, or interstitial tissue surrounding the glomeruli and tubules. It is a major category of kidney disease and can be acute or chronic, potentially progressing to kidney failure if untreated.

Types

Glomerulonephritis is inflammation of the glomeruli, the tiny filtering units of the kidney. Interstitial nephritis (or tubulo-interstitial nephritis) affects the interstitium and tubules and is often caused by allergic reactions to medications or by infections. Pyelonephritis is a type of urinary tract infection that reaches the renal pelvis of the kidney. Lupus nephritis is an inflammation of the kidneys caused by systemic lupus erythematosus.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms vary with cause and severity and may include blood in the urine (haematuria), proteinuria, reduced urine output, swelling (oedema) of the face and limbs, high blood pressure, and fatigue. Diagnosis typically involves urinalysis, blood tests of kidney function, imaging, and sometimes kidney biopsy.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause and may include corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants for immune-mediated forms, antibiotics for infection, and management of blood pressure and fluid balance. Severe cases may require dialysis.
