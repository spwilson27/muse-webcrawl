Baguette

Baguette
Type: Bread
Place of origin: France
Main ingredients: Flour, water, yeast, salt

A baguette is a long, thin type of bread of French origin, distinguished by its length and crisp crust. It is made from a basic lean dough of flour, water, yeast and salt. A standard baguette is about 65 cm long and 5–6 cm in diameter, though lengths of one metre or more are made.

History

Long loaves became common in France in the 18th century, and the development of deck ovens with steam injection in the 19th century gave the baguette its characteristic crust. By law, French "traditional" baguettes may contain only flour, water, yeast and salt, with no additives or freezing. In 2022, the artisanal know-how and culture of baguette-making were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Characteristics

The crust is formed by baking in a heavily steamed oven, producing a crackling, golden exterior and an open, airy crumb. Diagonal slashes (grigne) on the surface allow the loaf to expand in the oven. Baguettes stale quickly and are traditionally bought fresh daily.

Consumption

Baguettes are eaten at breakfast with butter and jam, used for sandwiches such as the jambon-beurre, and served sliced alongside meals. Variants include the flûte, ficelle and bâtard.
