Anne Dacier (born Anne Le Fevre; 5 August 1645 – 17 August 1720), often known as Madame Dacier, was a French scholar and translator of Greek and Latin classics. Educated by her father, the philologist Tanneguy Le Fevre, she became one of the most celebrated classical scholars of her age and the first woman to produce complete French prose translations of the Iliad and the Odyssey.

Life and work

Anne Le Fevre grew up in Saumur, where her father taught at the Protestant academy. She published an edition of Florus at age 25 and went on to produce editions of Callimachus, Dictys Cretensis, Eutropius and Aurelius Victor, several of them for the prestigious "Delphin" series of Latin classics. After her marriage to her father's former student Andre Dacier, the couple converted to Catholicism following the revocation of the Edict of Nantes in 1685 and settled in Paris.

The Homer translations and the Quarrel

Her prose translations of the Iliad (1699) and Odyssey (1708) were accompanied by influential critical prefaces defending Homer against modernist critics. When Antoine Houdar de la Motte published a shortened, versified and "corrected" Iliad in 1714, Dacier responded with Des causes de la corruption du gout (1714), a central document of the Quarrel of the Ancients and the Moderns, in which she defended fidelity to ancient texts and taste.
