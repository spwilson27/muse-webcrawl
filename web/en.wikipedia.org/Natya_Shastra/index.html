Natya Shastra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Natya Shastra (Sanskrit: Nāṭyaśāstra) is a Sanskrit treatise on the performing arts, traditionally attributed to the sage Bharata Muni. Its first complete version is variously dated between 200 BCE and 200 CE. The text, comprising some 6,000 poetic verses in 36 or 37 chapters, is the foundational work of Indian aesthetics for theatre, dance and music.

Contents

The treatise covers stage design, music, dance, makeup, the roles of actor and director, and the organization of theatre companies. Its most influential contribution is the theory of rasa ("essence", "taste"), the aesthetic flavour evoked in the audience by the performer's portrayal of emotions (bhava). The eight (later nine) rasas include love, humour, sorrow, anger, heroism, terror, disgust, wonder and peace.

Structure and performance theory

The Natya Shastra describes dramatic structure, genres of drama, body movements (angika abhinaya), hand gestures (mudras), music theory including scales and rhythm, and audience etiquette. It frames drama as a "fifth Veda" accessible to all classes, created by Brahma at the request of the gods.

Influence

The text underlies the classical dance forms of India—Bharatanatyam, Kathakali, Kathak, Odissi, Kuchipudi and others—and its commentaries, notably Abhinavagupta's Abhinavabharati (c. 1000 CE), shaped Indian aesthetic philosophy for a millennium.
