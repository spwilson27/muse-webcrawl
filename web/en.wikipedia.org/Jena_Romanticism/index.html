Jena Romanticism

Jena Romanticism (German: Jenaer Romantik), also known as early Romanticism (Frühromantik), was the first phase of the Romantic movement in German literature, centred in the university town of Jena from roughly 1798 to 1804. It is regarded as one of the most intense intellectual collaborations in German cultural history.

The circle

The group formed around the brothers August Wilhelm Schlegel and Friedrich Schlegel, their wives Caroline Schlegel and Dorothea Veit, the poets Novalis (Friedrich von Hardenberg) and Ludwig Tieck, and the philosopher F. W. J. Schelling. Johann Gottlieb Fichte's philosophy, taught at Jena until 1799, provided a formative stimulus, as did the nearby court of Weimar, where Goethe and Schiller presided over Weimar Classicism.

Ideas

Through their journal Athenaeum (1798–1800), the Jena Romantics developed the fragment as a literary form, the concept of Romantic irony, and the ideal of "progressive universal poetry" that would unite literature, philosophy, nature and life. Their revaluation of the Middle Ages, of myth and of the infinite striving of the self shaped Romantic movements across Europe.
