Bent's Old Fort National Historic Site

Bent's Old Fort National Historic Site
LocationOtero County, Colorado, USA
Established3 June 1960
Administered byNational Park Service

Bent's Old Fort National Historic Site, near La Junta in southeastern Colorado, preserves a reconstructed 1840s adobe fur-trading post on the Arkansas River along the Santa Fe Trail. The original fort, built in 1833 by brothers William and Charles Bent with Ceran St. Vrain, was the centre of the Bent, St. Vrain and Company trade empire on the southern Great Plains.

History

The fort traded manufactured goods for buffalo robes and horses with the Cheyenne, Arapaho, and other Plains peoples, and served travellers, trappers, and later soldiers on the Santa Fe Trail. After Charles Bent was killed in Taos in 1847 and trade declined, William Bent abandoned and partially destroyed the fort in 1849. The site became a National Historic Site in 1960, and the fort was reconstructed in 1975–76 from archaeological evidence and period sketches.

Today

The reconstructed fort is furnished as it would have appeared in 1845–46, with living-history demonstrations of the fur trade era.
