William Wilson Morgan (1906–1994) was an American astronomer best known for his work on the classification of stars and galaxies and for providing the first convincing evidence of the spiral arms of the Milky Way.

Career

Morgan spent most of his career at the Yerkes Observatory of the University of Chicago, serving as its director from 1960 to 1966. With Philip C. Keenan and Edith Kellman he produced the Atlas of Stellar Spectra (1943), which established the two-dimensional MKK (Morgan–Keenan–Kellman) system of stellar classification, refining the Harvard scheme by adding luminosity classes.

Spiral structure

In 1951 Morgan, together with Stewart Sharpless and Donald Osterbrock, used the distribution of H II regions around hot young stars to trace out segments of the Milky Way's spiral arms, demonstrating that the Galaxy is a spiral galaxy like Andromeda.

Galaxy classification

Morgan also devised a classification of galaxies based on their central concentration of light, and identified the class of giant elliptical cD galaxies found at the centres of rich clusters. Asteroid 3180 Morgan is named in his honour.
