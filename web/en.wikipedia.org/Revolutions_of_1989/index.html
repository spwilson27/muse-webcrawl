Revolutions of 1989

The revolutions of 1989, also called the Fall of Communism or the Autumn of Nations, were a wave of largely nonviolent revolutions that overthrew the communist states of Central and Eastern Europe in late 1989, ending the Cold War division of Europe and leading within two years to the dissolution of the Soviet Union.

Sequence of events

The wave began in Poland, where the Solidarity movement's semi-free election victory in June 1989 produced the Eastern Bloc's first non-communist-led government. Hungary opened its border with Austria, prompting mass East German emigration; protest movements forced the resignation of Erich Honecker, and on 9 November 1989 the Berlin Wall fell. Czechoslovakia's Velvet Revolution, Bulgaria's palace change, and Romania's violent revolution — ending with the execution of Nicolae Ceaușescu — followed within weeks.

Aftermath

Germany reunified in 1990, the Warsaw Pact dissolved in 1991, and former satellite states began transitions to democracy and market economies, later joining NATO and the European Union. Mikhail Gorbachev's policies of glasnost and perestroika, and his refusal to use Soviet force to prop up allied regimes, were decisive enabling conditions.
