Russo-Turkish War (1787–1792)

Date19 August 1787 – 9 January 1792
LocationEastern Europe, Black Sea, Caucasus
BelligerentsRussian Empire and Habsburg Austria vs. Ottoman Empire
ResultRussian victory; Treaty of Jassy

The Russo-Turkish War of 1787–1792 was fought between the Russian Empire (joined in 1788 by Habsburg Austria) and the Ottoman Empire, which sought to reverse the losses of the previous war and Russia's annexation of the Crimea in 1783. The war ended in Russian victory, confirming Russian dominance on the northern Black Sea coast.

Alexander Suvorov's storming of Izmail, December 1790, was the war's most famous siege.

Course of the war

The Ottomans declared war in August 1787 and attacked the Russian fortress of Kinburn, where Alexander Suvorov won a notable defensive victory. In 1788 Russian forces under Prince Grigory Potemkin besieged and captured the great fortress of Ochakov after a costly six-month siege, while Austria — Russia's ally under Joseph II — campaigned in Serbia and captured Belgrade. At sea, Fyodor Ushakov's Black Sea Fleet defeated the Ottoman navy at Fidonisi (1788), Tendra (1790), and Cape Kaliakra (1791). On land, Suvorov won brilliant victories at Focșani and Rymnik (1789, with Austrian allies) and stormed the fortress of Izmail in December 1790, an assault notorious for heavy Ottoman casualties. Austria left the war under the Treaty of Sistova (1791), pressed by Prussia and the Brabant Revolution.

Treaty of Jassy

The Treaty of Jassy, signed on 9 January 1792, confirmed the Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca (1774), formally ceded Crimea to Russia, and awarded Russia the Ottoman lands between the Southern Bug and the Dniester, establishing the Dniester as the frontier. The city of Odessa was founded a few years later on the newly acquired coast.
