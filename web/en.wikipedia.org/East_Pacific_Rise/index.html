East Pacific Rise

The East Pacific Rise (EPR) is a mid-ocean rise (termed a ridge in the North Pacific) at a divergent tectonic plate boundary, located along the floor of the Pacific Ocean. It separates the Pacific Plate to the west from (north to south) the North American, Rivera, Cocos and Nazca plates.

Geology

The EPR is the fastest-spreading ridge system on Earth, opening at rates from about 60 mm/yr in the north to over 150 mm/yr in the far south. Because spreading is fast, the ridge crest lacks the deep rift valley typical of slow-spreading ridges like the Mid-Atlantic Ridge and instead rises as a smooth, broad swell with a shallow axial trough.

Hydrothermal vents

In 1979 scientists aboard the submersible Alvin discovered "black smokers" at 21°N on the EPR — chimneys venting superheated, mineral-rich water above 350 °C. These vents support chemosynthetic ecosystems of tube worms, clams and shrimp independent of sunlight.
