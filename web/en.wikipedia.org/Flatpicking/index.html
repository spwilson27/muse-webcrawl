Flatpicking

Flatpicking is a technique for playing the guitar in which the strings are struck with a plectrum (pick) held between the thumb and forefinger. In bluegrass and American roots music it refers particularly to a virtuosic lead style on the acoustic guitar, adapted from fiddle tunes, characterised by fast single-note runs and crosspicking patterns.

History

Although the guitar was traditionally a rhythm instrument in string bands, players in the mid-20th century began using the flatpick to play melody. Pioneers included George Shuffler, who developed crosspicking with the Stanley Brothers, and Doc Watson, whose clean, rapid renditions of fiddle tunes set a new standard. Clarence White and Tony Rice later expanded the style's harmonic and rhythmic vocabulary.

Technique

Flatpickers typically use a heavy-gauge pick and medium or heavy strings, and anchor or float the picking hand to generate volume enough to compete with banjos and fiddles. The style emphasises alternate picking, economy of motion, and rhythmic drive.
