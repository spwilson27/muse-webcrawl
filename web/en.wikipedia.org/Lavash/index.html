Lavash

Fresh lavash, a staple flatbread of the Caucasus and Middle East
Lavash
TypeFlatbread
RegionArmenia, Azerbaijan, Iran, Turkey, the wider Caucasus and Middle East
Main ingredientsWheat flour, water, salt (sometimes a piece of old dough as starter)
CookingBaked on the wall of a clay oven (tonir/tandoor) or on a convex griddle (saj)
UNESCO"Lavash, the preparation, meaning and appearance of traditional bread" inscribed for Armenia (2014); multinational flatbread-making culture including lavash inscribed 2016

Lavash is a thin, soft flatbread traditionally baked in a clay oven and eaten across Armenia, Azerbaijan, Iran, Turkey and the wider region of Western Asia and the Caucasus. Made from a simple dough of flour, water and salt, the dough is rolled out paper-thin — often a metre or more across — stretched over a special cushion and slapped onto the hot wall of a tonir, where it bakes in well under a minute, blistering and browning in spots.

Preparation and use

Fresh lavash is soft and pliable and is used as a wrap for cheese, herbs and grilled meats; the Armenian khoravats wrap and the Turkish dürüm both depend on it. Sheets left to dry become crisp and can be stored for months, then revived by sprinkling with water and resting under a cloth — a property that made lavash a travelling and winter bread across the Armenian highlands. In Armenian tradition the bread carries ceremonial weight: a sheet is draped over the shoulders of newlyweds for prosperity, and bread-making is a communal, usually female, activity accompanied by song.

Recognition

In 2014 UNESCO inscribed "Lavash, the preparation, meaning and appearance of traditional bread as an expression of culture in Armenia" on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. A broader multinational nomination in 2016 covered the flatbread-making culture — including lavash and its regional counterparts such as katyrma, jupka and yufka — for Azerbaijan, Iran, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Turkey.
