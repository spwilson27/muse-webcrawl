Diu

Diu is a coastal town and island in the union territory of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, India, located at the southern tip of the Kathiawar peninsula in Gujarat, on the Arabian Sea.

History

Diu was a strategic port on trade routes between India, Arabia and East Africa. In 1535 the Portuguese, under an alliance with the Sultan of Gujarat against the Mughals, gained control and built the formidable Diu Fortress. The Ottoman–Portuguese struggle for the Indian Ocean saw a famous naval battle off Diu in 1509 and sieges in 1538 and 1546, after which Portugal held the enclave.

Along with Goa and Daman, Diu remained Portuguese until December 1961, when it was annexed by India in Operation Vijay.

Geography and tourism

The island, about 11 km long, is connected to the mainland by bridges. Its beaches (such as Nagoa), the Portuguese-era fort, St Paul's Church and the nearby fishing village of Vanakbara make it a popular tourist destination.
