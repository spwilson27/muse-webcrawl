Grand Prix motorcycle racing is the highest class of motorcycle road racing events held on road circuits sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM). Independent motorcycle racing events have been held since the start of the twentieth century and large national events were often given the title Grand Prix. The foundation of the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme as the international governing body for motorcycle sport in 1949 provided the opportunity to coordinate rules and regulations in order that selected events could count towards official World Championships. It is the oldest established motorsport world championship.

Grand Prix motorcycles are purpose-built racing machines that are unavailable for purchase by the general public and unable to be ridden legally on public roads. This contrasts with the various production-based categories of racing, such as the Superbike World Championship. The championship is currently divided into four classes: the historic MotoE, Moto2, Moto3 and the pinnacle MotoGP. The most successful rider in Grand Prix history is Giacomo Agostini with 15 titles and 122 race wins. Valentino Rossi, with nine titles, is the most successful premier-class rider of the modern era, while Marc Márquez dominated the 2010s with six premier-class championships for Honda before joining Ducati.

History

The FIM Road Racing World Championship Grand Prix was first organized in 1949. The premier class was originally the 500cc formula, contested by two-stroke machines by the 1970s. In 2002 the class was renamed MotoGP and opened to 990cc four-stroke prototypes; engine capacity was reduced to 800cc in 2007 for safety, then raised to 1000cc in 2012, where it remains. Sprint races were added to each weekend's schedule in 2023.

Regulations

MotoGP bikes are prototypes with engines of up to 1000cc, using a spec electronics package and unified software. Riders compete over a season of around twenty Grands Prix held worldwide, scoring points on a 25-18-15 system for the top fifteen finishers, with additional points for Saturday sprint races.

Support classes

Moto2 uses a spec 765cc Triumph three-cylinder engine, while Moto3 features 250cc single-cylinder machines and serves as the entry point for young riders. The all-electric MotoE World Cup ran from 2019 until its suspension after 2025.
