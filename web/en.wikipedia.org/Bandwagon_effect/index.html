Bandwagon effect

The bandwagon effect is a psychological phenomenon in which people adopt beliefs, ideas or behaviours because others have already done so — the probability of adoption increases with the proportion of people who have already adopted. The phrase comes from the bandwagons used in 19th-century American political campaigns, onto which supporters would climb.

Applications

The effect is studied in politics, where voters may shift toward a candidate perceived to be winning; in consumer behaviour, where popularity signals drive fads and bubbles; and in social media, where visible metrics such as likes and follower counts reinforce adoption. It is related to, but distinct from, herd behaviour and groupthink, and is one basis of the argumentum ad populum fallacy.

Counter-effects

The opposite tendency is sometimes called the underdog effect or snob effect, in which people reject an option because it has become too popular.
