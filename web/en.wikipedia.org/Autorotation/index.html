Autorotation

Autorotation is a state of flight in which the main rotor of a helicopter or autogyro turns by the action of air moving up through it, rather than being driven by the engine, in the same way a windmill or a falling maple seed spins.

In helicopters

If a helicopter's engine fails, the pilot lowers the collective pitch so that the descent drives the rotor, storing energy in its rotation. Just before touchdown the pilot flares and raises collective, using that stored energy to cushion the landing. Successful autorotative landings depend on altitude and airspeed combinations described by the height-velocity diagram; flight within the "avoid" region of that diagram leaves too little margin for a safe landing.

In autogyros and seeds

Autogyros (gyroplanes) fly in continuous autorotation, their unpowered rotor driven by forward motion from a separate propeller. Botanical autorotation in winged seeds such as maple samaras slows their descent and disperses them away from the parent tree.
