Deprescribing

Deprescribing is the planned and supervised process of reducing the dose of, or stopping, medication that may be causing harm or is no longer of benefit. It is a core element of good prescribing practice, particularly for older patients taking many medicines (polypharmacy) and for drug classes with high risk of adverse effects, such as benzodiazepines, anticholinergics and proton-pump inhibitors.

Rationale

As people age, the balance of benefit and harm from medicines shifts: frailty, renal impairment and drug interactions increase the risk of falls, cognitive impairment and hospitalisation. Deprescribing aims to improve quality of life and reduce adverse drug events while maintaining therapeutic goals agreed with the patient.

Process

The process typically involves a full medication review, identifying drugs that are potentially inappropriate, prioritising those to stop, tapering where withdrawal effects are possible, and monitoring after discontinuation. Barriers include time pressure, reluctance of patients and prescribers, and lack of evidence about stopping long-term therapies.
