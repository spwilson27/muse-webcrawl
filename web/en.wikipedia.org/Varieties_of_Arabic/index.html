Varieties of Arabic

The varieties of Arabic (sometimes called dialects or vernaculars) are the linguistic variants of the Arabic language spoken across the Arab world. They range from regional colloquial forms used in everyday life to Modern Standard Arabic, the standardised literary language used in writing, education and formal speech — a situation known as diglossia.

Classification

Colloquial varieties are broadly grouped as Maghrebi (North African, including Moroccan, Algerian, Tunisian and Libyan), Egyptian, Levantine (Syrian, Lebanese, Palestinian and Jordanian), Mesopotamian (Iraqi), Peninsular (including Gulf, Saudi and Yemeni) and Sudanese. Mutual intelligibility decreases with geographic distance; speakers often accommodate by using a regional standard or Modern Standard Arabic.

Standard and Classical Arabic

Classical Arabic is the language of the Quran and classical literature. Modern Standard Arabic derives from it with updated vocabulary and is understood across the Arab world, though it is no one's native spoken language. Colloquial varieties differ from the standard in phonology, vocabulary and grammar, having dropped case endings and developed their own features.

Media and education

Pan-Arab media, especially satellite television and film (historically dominated by Egyptian cinema), have spread familiarity with major dialects. In recent decades, colloquial Arabic has been increasingly written, particularly in social media and texting, sometimes in Latin script ("Arabizi").
