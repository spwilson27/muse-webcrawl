Eastman Wind Ensemble

Founded: 1952
Founder: Frederick Fennell
Affiliation: Eastman School of Music, Rochester, New York

The Eastman Wind Ensemble is an American concert band founded by Frederick Fennell at the Eastman School of Music in 1952. Regarded as one of the world's premier wind ensembles, it pioneered the concept of the wind ensemble — a flexible group of roughly fifty players, typically one player per part — as distinct from the larger concert band.

History

Fennell founded the ensemble to perform serious original music for winds at the highest artistic standard, and its landmark Mercury Living Presence recordings of the 1950s helped establish the modern wind repertoire. Successors as conductor have included Donald Hunsberger, who led the ensemble for decades, and Mark Scatterday. The ensemble has premiered numerous works and inspired the formation of wind ensembles at universities throughout the United States and abroad.

Repertoire and recordings

The Eastman Wind Ensemble has released dozens of recordings spanning standard works, new commissions, and collaborations with guest artists, and it tours nationally and internationally, including historic tours of Japan and Southeast Asia.
