Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)
Date24 April 1877 – 3 March 1878
LocationBalkans and Caucasus
ResultRussian victory; Treaties of San Stefano and Berlin
BelligerentsRussian Empire and allies; Ottoman Empire

The Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878 was a conflict between the Ottoman Empire and a coalition led by the Russian Empire, including Romania, Serbia, Montenegro and Bulgarian volunteers. It arose from Balkan nationalism, the Ottoman suppression of the Bulgarian April Uprising of 1876, and Russia's aim to gain influence in the Balkans and access toward the Mediterranean.

Campaigns

Russian forces crossed the Danube, fought the costly Siege of Plevna against Osman Pasha, crossed the Balkan Mountains in winter, and advanced toward Constantinople, while a second army campaigned in the Caucasus, capturing Kars.

Outcome

The Treaty of San Stefano (March 1878) created a large autonomous Bulgaria, but the other Great Powers, fearing Russian dominance, revised it at the Congress of Berlin (July 1878). The settlement recognised the full independence of Romania, Serbia and Montenegro, granted Bulgaria autonomy in reduced borders, and awarded Russia territory in the Caucasus and southern Bessarabia.
