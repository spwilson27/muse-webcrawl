Instant messaging


A typical mobile instant-messaging conversation.

Instant messaging (IM) is a form of online communication that offers real-time text transmission between two or more people over the Internet or another computer network. Unlike email, instant messages are exchanged within a continuous conversation session, generally with presence information showing whether contacts are online and available. Modern IM services extend plain text with voice and video calls, file transfer, images, stickers, group chats and end-to-end encryption.

History

Precursors of IM appeared on multi-user systems of the 1960s and on bulletin board systems and early online services. Internet Relay Chat (IRC), created in 1988, popularised channel-based chat. Dedicated IM clients took off in the late 1990s with ICQ (1996), AIM, Yahoo! Messenger and MSN Messenger, organised around "buddy lists". With smartphones, mobile-first messengers such as WhatsApp (founded 2009), WeChat, Telegram and Signal came to dominate personal communication, while Slack and Microsoft Teams reshaped workplace messaging. Open protocols such as XMPP once promised interoperability between services, but proprietary, closed networks prevailed.

Features and issues

Contemporary clients support message reactions, read receipts, disappearing messages, channels and bots. End-to-end encryption, popularised by the Signal protocol, protects message content from the provider itself. Instant messaging has raised questions about misinformation, online harassment, business-record retention and the blurring of work and private life, and has largely displaced SMS texting in many countries.
