The Chiang Mai Initiative (CMI) is a currency swap arrangement launched by the ASEAN Plus Three countries (the ten ASEAN members plus China, Japan and South Korea) in Chiang Mai, Thailand, on 6 May 2000. It was created in response to the 1997 Asian financial crisis to provide regional liquidity support and reduce dependence on the International Monetary Fund.

Structure

The original initiative was a web of bilateral swap agreements between central banks, allowing a member facing a balance-of-payments crisis to borrow foreign currency against its own. It also established regional surveillance through the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office (AMRO). Borrowings beyond a small first tranche were linked to an IMF programme.

Multilateralisation

In March 2010 the CMI was replaced by the Chiang Mai Initiative Multilateralisation (CMIM), a single contractual pool initially of US$120 billion, doubled to US$240 billion in 2012, with the IMF-delinked portion progressively raised (to 40% in 2021). The reserve pool has never been drawn upon, leading analysts to describe it as a confidence-building and integration instrument as much as a crisis fund.
