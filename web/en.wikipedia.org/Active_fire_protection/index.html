Active fire protection

Active fire protection (AFP) is an integral part of fire protection in buildings. Unlike passive fire protection, which relies on static features such as fire-resistant walls, floors and doors to contain a fire, active fire protection consists of systems that require some form of action or motion to work effectively — for example detecting a fire, sounding an alarm, and suppressing or extinguishing the flames.

Categories

Fire detection

Detection systems include smoke detectors, heat detectors, flame detectors, fire alarm control panels and manual call points. Their purpose is to discover a fire early and alert occupants and emergency services.

Fire suppression

Suppression systems act directly on the fire. They include water-based fire sprinkler systems, foam systems, and gaseous clean-agent systems used in spaces such as data centres where water damage must be avoided. Portable fire extinguishers, selected according to the class of fire, are the simplest form of active suppression.

Smoke control and ventilation

Mechanical smoke exhaust and pressurization systems keep escape routes tenable by controlling the movement of smoke and heat, supporting safe evacuation and firefighting access.

Maintenance and regulation

Because AFP systems must operate reliably after long periods of dormancy, building codes such as those of the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) require regular inspection, testing and maintenance. In the United States, sprinkler systems are commonly designed to NFPA 13, and alarm systems to NFPA 72.
