1620 Geographos, provisional designation 1951 RA, is a highly elongated, stony asteroid and near-Earth object of the Apollo group, with a mean diameter of approximately 2.5 kilometres. It was discovered on 14 September 1951 by astronomers Albert George Wilson and Rudolph Minkowski at the Palomar Observatory in California. The name means "geographer" in Greek, honouring the National Geographic Society.

Orbit and shape

Geographos is a potentially hazardous asteroid whose orbit crosses that of Earth. Radar imaging during its 1994 approach — the closest approach to Earth until 2024 — revealed it to be one of the most elongated objects in the Solar System, roughly 5 × 2 × 1.5 kilometres, resembling a cigar or peanut shape. It rotates once every 5.22 hours.

Exploration

The asteroid was the intended target of NASA's Clementine mission, which was to fly by it after mapping the Moon in 1994; a malfunction in one of the spacecraft's thrusters caused the encounter to be cancelled.
