The Diving Bell and the Butterfly

The Diving Bell and the Butterfly (French: Le Scaphandre et le Papillon) is a memoir by French journalist Jean-Dominique Bauby, published in March 1997. It describes his life after a massive stroke left him with locked-in syndrome.

Background

Bauby (1952–1997) was the editor-in-chief of the French fashion magazine Elle. On 8 December 1995 he suffered a stroke that left him in a coma for twenty days; he awoke paralysed except for some movement of his head and his left eye. The "diving bell" of the title is his inert body; the "butterfly" is his imagination and memory, which remained free.

Composition

Bauby composed the book letter by letter: a transcriber, Claude Mendibil, recited a frequency-ordered alphabet and Bauby blinked his left eyelid to select each letter — some 200,000 blinks in all, at sessions of several hours a day over the summer of 1996. In short, impressionistic chapters he recalled his family, his career, hospital routines, fantasies of travel and food, and the visit of his children. He died of pneumonia two days after the book's publication.

Film

Julian Schnabel's 2007 film adaptation, from Ronald Harwood's screenplay, won the Best Director prize at Cannes and four Academy Award nominations, with Mathieu Amalric as Bauby.
