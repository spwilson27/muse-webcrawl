Edward V of England

Reign: 9 April – 25 June 1483

Born: 2 November 1470, Westminster, London

Died: c. 1483 (aged about 12), Tower of London (presumed)

Predecessor: Edward IV

Successor: Richard III

House: York

Father: Edward IV

Mother: Elizabeth Woodville

Edward V (2 November 1470 – c. 1483) was King of England and Lord of Ireland from 9 April to 25 June 1483. He succeeded his father, Edward IV, upon the latter's death. Edward V was never crowned, and his brief reign was ended by his uncle and Lord Protector, Richard, Duke of Gloucester, who deposed him and succeeded as Richard III.

Edward V and his younger brother Richard of Shrewsbury, Duke of York, became known as the Princes in the Tower. They disappeared after being lodged in the Tower of London, and their fate remains one of the most famous mysteries in English history; Richard III is commonly suspected of having had them murdered, though the evidence is circumstantial and disputed.

Early life

Edward was born on 2 November 1470 at Westminster while his father was in exile in the Low Countries during the Wars of the Roses. He was created Prince of Wales in June 1471 following Edward IV's restoration to the throne, and was given a household at Ludlow Castle in the Welsh Marches, where the Council of Wales and the Marches administered the principality on his behalf.

Reign

Edward IV died unexpectedly on 9 April 1483. The twelve-year-old Edward was proclaimed king, and he travelled from Ludlow toward London for his coronation. On the way, his uncle Richard, Duke of Gloucester, intercepted the royal party at Stony Stratford and arrested the king's half-brother Richard Grey and his chamberlain Thomas Vaughan. Edward was lodged in the Tower of London, then a royal residence, to await his coronation, and was soon joined by his younger brother.

Deposition and disappearance

In June 1483 a sermon preached by Ralph Shaa declared Edward IV's marriage to Elizabeth Woodville invalid on grounds of a prior contract, making Edward and his siblings illegitimate. Parliament accepted this claim in the act known as Titulus Regius, and Richard took the throne as Richard III on 26 June 1483. The two princes were seen less and less frequently in the Tower, and by the autumn of 1483 they had vanished entirely. In 1674 workmen at the Tower discovered a wooden box containing two small skeletons, which were reburied in Westminster Abbey as those of the princes.

Legacy

The mystery of the Princes in the Tower has inspired centuries of debate, literature, and investigation. Edward V is one of only a handful of English monarchs never to have been crowned, and his reign of 78 days is among the shortest in English history.
