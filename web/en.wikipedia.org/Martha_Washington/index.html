Martha Washington

Martha Dandridge Custis Washington (June 2, 1731 – May 22, 1802) was the wife of George Washington, the first president of the United States. Although the title was not coined until much later, she is regarded as the first First Lady of the United States. Known as "Lady Washington", she set many of the precedents for the presidential spouse and was a beloved public figure of the early republic.

Martha Washington, the first First Lady of the United States.

Early life and first marriage

Martha Dandridge was born on her family's plantation, Chestnut Grove, in New Kent County, Virginia. In 1750 she married Daniel Parke Custis, a wealthy planter, with whom she had four children, two of whom survived infancy. When Custis died in 1757, Martha was one of the richest widows in Virginia, managing some 17,500 acres. On January 6, 1759, she married George Washington, a marriage that greatly increased Washington's landholdings and social standing.

Revolutionary War

During the American Revolutionary War, Martha Washington joined her husband at his winter camps, including Valley Forge, nursing soldiers, boosting morale, and organizing other officers' wives to make clothing for the troops. She also managed Mount Vernon and the family's estates throughout the war.

First Lady

As the president's wife in New York and Philadelphia (1789–1797), Martha Washington hosted weekly receptions and dinners that shaped the social protocol of the new republic. She privately found the public role confining, writing that she felt "more like a state prisoner," but performed it with grace.

Later life and legacy

After George Washington's death in 1799, Martha burned their private correspondence, of which only a few letters survive. She freed Washington's slaves a year ahead of the schedule in his will. She died at Mount Vernon in 1802 and was buried beside her husband. She was the first American woman depicted on U.S. postage and appeared on currency including the $1 silver certificate of 1886.
