Monday Night Football

ESPN Monday Night Football

Genre: Sports broadcast
First aired: September 21, 1970
Networks: ABC (1970–2005), ESPN (2006–present)
Country: United States

Monday Night Football (abbreviated as MNF and also known as ESPN Monday Night Football on ESPN) is an American live television broadcast of weekly National Football League (NFL) games during the regular season. It is one of the longest-running and most-watched programs in American television history.

History

The program premiered on ABC on September 21, 1970, with a game between the Cleveland Browns and the New York Jets. Conceived by NFL commissioner Pete Rozelle and produced by Roone Arledge, it transformed sports broadcasting with innovations such as multiple cameras, instant replays, and an entertainment-oriented presentation. The original broadcast team featured Keith Jackson, Howard Cosell, and Don Meredith; Frank Gifford replaced Jackson in 1971, forming the iconic trio of Gifford, Cosell, and Meredith.

Monday Night Football aired on ABC for 36 seasons before moving to ESPN in 2006, with NBC taking over the Sunday night package. The move made it a flagship property of cable sports television. Since 2020, select games have also been simulcast on ABC.

Broadcast features

The broadcast is famous for its theme music, including Hank Williams Jr.'s "All My Rowdy Friends Are Coming Over Tonight" adaptation used from 1989, and the "Heavy Action" instrumental theme. Notable broadcasters have included Al Michaels, John Madden, Mike Tirico, Jon Gruden, and, since 2022, Joe Buck and Troy Aikman. An alternate broadcast, the "ManningCast," featuring Peyton and Eli Manning, debuted on ESPN2 in 2021.

Cultural impact

MNF became a cultural institution in the United States, regularly drawing large audiences and influencing the scheduling of the NFL week. Memorable moments include the "Miracle at the Meadowlands" aftermath coverage and numerous record-setting performances under the Monday night spotlight.
