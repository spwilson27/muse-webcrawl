Berlin U-Bahn

The Berlin U-Bahn (short for Untergrundbahn, "underground railway") is a rapid transit system in Berlin, the capital of Germany, and a major part of the city's public transport network. Opened in 1902, it is among the oldest metro systems in the world. Together with the S-Bahn, trams, and buses it forms the backbone of Berlin's transport, operated by the Berliner Verkehrsbetriebe (BVG).

History

The first line opened in 1902, largely elevated, running from Warschauer Brücke through the city center. The network expanded rapidly before the First World War, built in the narrower "small profile" (Kleinprofil) standard. Later lines, beginning in the 1920s, used the wider "large profile" (Großprofil) loading gauge.

Division and reunification

During Berlin's division, the U-Bahn network was split: lines passing through East Berlin were truncated or closed, producing famous "ghost stations" (Geisterbahnhöfe) where West Berlin trains passed through shuttered East stations without stopping. After reunification in 1990 the network was reintegrated, extensions such as the U5's completion to Hauptbahnhof in 2020 linked the former halves.

Network

The system has nine lines: the small-profile U1, U2, U3, and U4, and the large-profile U5, U6, U7, U8, and U9. Most of the network is underground, though sections of the U1 and U2 run on elevated viaducts. Trains are painted in the BVG's distinctive yellow livery.

Tickets and operations

The U-Bahn operates within the Verkehrsverbund Berlin-Brandenburg (VBB) fare system on a proof-of-payment basis, with no turnstiles. Services run from about 4 a.m. until about 1 a.m., with all-night service on weekends.
