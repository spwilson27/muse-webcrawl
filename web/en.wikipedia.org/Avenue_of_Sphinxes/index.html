Avenue of Sphinxes

The Avenue of Sphinxes is an ancient Egyptian ceremonial avenue in Luxor, Egypt, lined with sphinx statues, which connects the Karnak Temple complex with the Luxor Temple. The avenue stretches for roughly 2.7 kilometres (1.7 miles) and was used in antiquity for religious processions, most famously during the annual Opet Festival.

History

Construction of the avenue began during the New Kingdom, around the reign of Hatshepsut in the 15th century BCE, and continued under later pharaohs. The most famous section, near Luxor Temple, is lined with human-headed sphinxes erected during the reign of Nectanebo I in the 4th century BCE. Other sections feature ram-headed sphinxes (criosphinxes) associated with the god Amun.

Description

The avenue was originally lined with more than a thousand sphinx statues. Many were buried beneath later construction, including houses, mosques, and churches built over the centuries. Excavations over several decades uncovered hundreds of the statues and restored long sections of the route.

Restoration and reopening

A major restoration project, involving the demolition of buildings constructed over the ancient route, culminated in the avenue's reopening to the public on 25 November 2021 with a televised ceremony modelled on the ancient Opet Festival procession.
