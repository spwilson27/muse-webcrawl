Wiikwemkoong (formerly spelled Wikwemikong) is an unceded First Nations territory in the Canadian province of Ontario, located on the eastern peninsula of Manitoulin Island in Lake Huron. It is one of the largest First Nations communities in Canada by population, home to members of the Odawa, Ojibwe and Potawatomi nations, who together form part of the Three Fires Confederacy.

History

Wiikwemkoong is notable as unceded territory: unlike most First Nations land in Ontario, it was never surrendered to the Crown by treaty. In 1836, Sir Francis Bond Head proposed that Indigenous peoples relocate to Manitoulin Island; the resulting agreements did not cede the land. The 1862 Manitoulin Treaty divided and ceded other parts of the island, but the Wiikwemkoong community refused to sign, preserving its unceded status — a position upheld in subsequent negotiations with the Canadian government.

The community was a centre of Jesuit mission activity in the nineteenth century, and its Holy Cross Mission church, built of stone in the 1840s and 1850s, remains a landmark. Ruins of the mission complex stand beside the present church.

Culture

Wiikwemkoong hosts the Wiikwemkoong Cultural Festival, held each August since 1961 and regarded as one of the earliest and largest Indigenous cultural festivals and powwows in Canada. The community is also known for Debajehmujig Storytellers, a professional Indigenous theatre and arts company founded in 1984. Anishinaabemowin language revitalisation and traditional teachings are central to community life.

Government

The territory is governed by the Wiikwemkoong Unceded Territory band council, led by an ogimaa (chief) and elected councillors. The community maintains its own police service, health centre, and schools, including Wasse-Abin Pontiac School.
