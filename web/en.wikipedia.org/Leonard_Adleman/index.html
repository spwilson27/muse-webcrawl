Leonard Adleman

Leonard Max Adleman (born December 31, 1945) is an American computer scientist. He is one of the creators of the RSA encryption algorithm with Ron Rivest and Adi Shamir, and is widely known as the father of DNA computing.

Born: December 31, 1945, California, US
Alma mater: UC Berkeley
Known for: RSA, DNA computing
Awards: Turing Award (2002)

Life and career

Adleman was born in California and grew up in San Francisco. He received his bachelor's degree in mathematics from the University of California, Berkeley in 1968 and his PhD in computer science in 1976. He has been a professor at the University of Southern California and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.

RSA

In 1977, with Rivest and Shamir at MIT, Adleman helped develop RSA, the first practical public-key cryptosystem; the letters RSA stand for their surnames. The system, based on the difficulty of factoring large integers, remains foundational to secure internet communication. The three received the 2002 ACM Turing Award for the work.

DNA computing

In 1994 Adleman published a landmark paper describing how he used DNA molecules to solve a seven-node instance of the Hamiltonian path problem, launching the field of DNA computing. He also coined the term "computer virus" in work with his student Fred Cohen.
