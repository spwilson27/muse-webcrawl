Langmuir adsorption model

The Langmuir adsorption model, proposed by Irving Langmuir in 1916, describes the adsorption of molecules onto a solid surface as a monolayer. It assumes a fixed number of identical adsorption sites, no interaction between adsorbed molecules, and dynamic equilibrium between adsorption and desorption.

Equation

The fractional surface coverage θ is given by θ = KP/(1+KP) for a gas at pressure P, where K is the equilibrium constant. A linear form is widely used to extract monolayer capacity from data.

Applications

The model is applied to catalysis, gas storage, chromatography and surface chemistry, though real surfaces often require extensions such as the BET model for multilayer adsorption.
