Field of fractions

In abstract algebra, the field of fractions of an integral domain is the smallest field in which the domain can be embedded. The construction generalises the way the rational numbers ℚ are formed from the integers ℤ: every non-zero element of the domain gains a multiplicative inverse.

Construction

Given an integral domain R, consider pairs (a, b) with a, b ∈ R and b ≠ 0, intended to represent fractions a/b. Two pairs are declared equivalent when (a, b) ~ (c, d) if and only if ad = bc — a definition that works precisely because R has no zero divisors. Addition and multiplication are defined as for ordinary fractions, and the resulting set of equivalence classes forms a field, in which R embeds via a ↦ a/1.

Universal property

The field of fractions Frac(R) has the universal property that any injective ring homomorphism from R into a field factors uniquely through Frac(R). Examples include the rational numbers ℚ = Frac(ℤ), the field of rational functions k(x) as the field of fractions of the polynomial ring k[x], and Laurent series fields.

Generalisation

The construction is a special case of localisation of a ring at a multiplicative set — here, the set of all non-zero elements. Localisation at prime ideals is a fundamental tool of commutative algebra and algebraic geometry.
