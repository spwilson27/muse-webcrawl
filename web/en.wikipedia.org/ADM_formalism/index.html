ADM formalism

The ADM formalism, named after its developers Richard Arnowitt, Stanley Deser, and Charles W. Misner, is a Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity that originated in work from 1959 to 1962. It plays a central role in attempts to quantize gravity and in numerical relativity.

Overview

The formalism foliates spacetime into a family of spatial hypersurfaces, labelled by a time coordinate. The four-dimensional metric is decomposed into the three-dimensional metric of each slice, together with the lapse function, which measures proper time elapsed between slices, and the shift vector, which describes how spatial coordinates are relabelled between slices. The dynamics are governed by the Hamiltonian constraint and the momentum (diffeomorphism) constraint.

Applications

The ADM decomposition underlies the initial-value formulation of Einstein's equations used in numerical simulations of black hole mergers and neutron star collisions. Its Hamiltonian structure, in which the total energy of an asymptotically flat spacetime can be defined as a surface integral (the ADM energy), also provided an influential notion of mass in general relativity.
