Kurt Student (1890–1978) was a German general in the Luftwaffe during the Second World War. A First World War fighter pilot, he became the architect of Germany's airborne forces, the Fallschirmjäger, and commanded the first major airborne invasion in history.

Career

Student joined the Imperial German Army in 1910, transferred to the air service, and scored six aerial victories in the First World War. In the interwar years he worked on gliders and clandestine air development, and from 1938 he built the 7th Air Division into the world's first large-scale parachute force.

Second World War

His paratroops spearheaded the invasions of Norway, the Netherlands and Belgium in 1940 (including the glider assault on Fort Eben-Emael); Student himself was accidentally shot and wounded by German troops in Rotterdam. In May 1941 he planned Operation Mercury, the airborne conquest of Crete — a costly victory that persuaded Hitler to forbid further large airborne operations. Later he commanded paratroop formations in Italy, France and the Netherlands, organized the defence against Operation Market Garden in 1944, and briefly led Army Group Vistula in 1945.

Postwar

A British military court convicted Student of war crimes connected with Crete in 1947, but he was released in 1948 after the conviction was overturned on appeal-related proceedings; he lived quietly in West Germany until his death in 1978.
