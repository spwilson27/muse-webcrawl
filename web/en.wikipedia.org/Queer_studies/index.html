Queer studies

Queer studies is an interdisciplinary academic field devoted to the study of sexual orientation, gender identity, and the cultures and politics of sexual and gender minorities (LGBTQ people), as well as the social norms — heteronormativity and binary gender — that structure them. It emerged in the early 1990s from gay and lesbian studies, women's studies, and critical theory.

Intellectual foundations

Key founding texts include Judith Butler's Gender Trouble (1990), which theorized gender as performative, and Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick's Epistemology of the Closet (1990). Teresa de Lauretis coined the term "queer theory" at a 1990 conference. The field draws on Michel Foucault's History of Sexuality (1976), which argued that sexual identities are historically constructed.

Scope

Queer studies examines queer history, literature, and community life, and intersects with transgender studies, disability studies, and critical race theory. Critics within and outside the field debate its abstraction, its Western institutional focus, and the relationship between academic theory and activism.
