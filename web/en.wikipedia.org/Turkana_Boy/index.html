Turkana Boy

Catalog no.KNM-WT 15000
SpeciesHomo ergaster / Homo erectus
Age~1.5–1.6 million years
Discovered1984, Lake Turkana, Kenya

Turkana Boy, catalogued as KNM-WT 15000, is a nearly complete skeleton of a juvenile early human, attributed to Homo ergaster (or African Homo erectus), who lived about 1.5–1.6 million years ago near Lake Turkana in Kenya.

Discovery

The skeleton was discovered in 1984 at Nariokotome by Kamoya Kimeu, a member of Richard Leakey's team. It remains one of the most complete early hominin skeletons ever found, with roughly 90% of the bones represented.

Description

The individual was an adolescent — estimates of age at death range from about 7 to 12 years depending on the growth standard used — and was tall and slender, with a body adapted to hot, dry conditions. Adults of his kind may have reached heights comparable to modern humans.

Significance

Turkana Boy provides key evidence about body proportions, growth, and brain size (~900 cm³ projected for an adult) in early Homo, and about the origins of human-like life history.
