Bourbon Restoration (Spain)

The Bourbon Restoration in Spain (Spanish: Restauración borbónica) is the period of Spanish history from the proclamation of Alfonso XII as king on 29 December 1874 until the proclamation of the Second Spanish Republic on 14 April 1931. It followed the turbulent Sexenio Democrático (1868–1874) and restored the Bourbon dynasty after the First Spanish Republic.

The Restoration system

The architect of the system was Antonio Cánovas del Castillo, who designed a constitutional monarchy under the Constitution of 1876. Power alternated peacefully between two dynastic parties — Cánovas's Conservatives and Práxedes Mateo Sagasta's Liberals — in the turno pacífico, an arrangement sustained by managed elections and local bosses (caciques). The reign of Alfonso XII (1874–1885) was followed by the regency of Maria Christina of Austria and the reign of Alfonso XIII (1902–1931).

Crisis and collapse

The system weathered the loss of Cuba, Puerto Rico, and the Philippines in the Spanish–American War of 1898 but was progressively undermined by Catalan and Basque nationalism, labour unrest, the Moroccan wars (including the disaster of Annual in 1921), and assassinations, including that of Cánovas in 1897. In 1923 General Miguel Primo de Rivera seized power in a coup tolerated by the king. When his dictatorship fell in 1930, the discredited monarchy could not recover; municipal elections in April 1931 were treated as a plebiscite against Alfonso XIII, who left the country as the Republic was proclaimed.
