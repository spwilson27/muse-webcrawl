Artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the intelligence of machines or software, as opposed to the intelligence of living beings, primarily of humans. It is a field of study in computer science that develops and studies intelligent machines. Such machines may be called AIs.

Artificial intelligence aims to create machines capable of tasks that typically require human intelligence.

AI technology is widely used throughout industry, government and science. Some high-profile applications include advanced web search engines (e.g., Google Search), recommendation systems (used by YouTube, Amazon, and Netflix), interacting via human speech (e.g., Google Assistant, Siri, and Alexa), self-driving cars (e.g., Waymo), generative and creative tools (e.g., ChatGPT and AI art), and superhuman performance in strategy games (e.g., chess and Go).

History

The field of AI research was founded at a workshop held at Dartmouth College in 1956, organised by John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester and Claude Shannon. McCarthy coined the term "artificial intelligence". Early optimism gave way to periods of reduced funding known as "AI winters", before statistical machine learning methods and later deep learning drove rapid progress in the 2010s and 2020s.

Goals and approaches

The traditional goals of AI research include reasoning, knowledge representation, planning, learning, natural language processing, perception, and support for robotics. Approaches include symbolic reasoning, search and optimisation, statistical methods such as neural networks, and integrated agent architectures. General intelligence (AGI) — the ability to perform any intellectual task a human can — remains a long-term goal.

Risks and ethics

The development of AI has raised concerns about algorithmic bias, unemployment, disinformation, autonomous weapons, and existential risk from hypothetical superintelligent systems. Governments and researchers have proposed various forms of regulation and AI safety research in response.
