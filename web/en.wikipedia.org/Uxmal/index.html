Uxmal

Uxmal
Location: Yucatán, Mexico
Culture: Maya
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1996)

Uxmal (Yucatec Maya: Óoxmáal) is an ancient Maya city of the classical period located in present-day Yucatán state, Mexico, about 62 kilometres south of Mérida. It is considered one of the most important archaeological sites of Maya culture and a leading example of the Puuc architectural style. UNESCO designated it a World Heritage Site in 1996.

Architecture

Major buildings include the Pyramid of the Magician (Pirámide del Adivino), an unusual elliptical-based stepped pyramid; the Nunnery Quadrangle (Cuadrángulo de las Monjas), with elaborate carved facades of latticework and Chaac masks; the Governor's Palace (Palacio del Gobernador), noted for its mosaic frieze; and the Great Pyramid, ballcourt, and the House of the Doves.

History

Uxmal flourished in the Terminal Classic period, roughly AD 850–925, when it may have housed some 15,000–25,000 people and dominated the Puuc region, possibly in alliance with Chichen Itza. The name Uxmal may mean "thrice-built". In a region without surface rivers, the city depended on chultuns (cisterns) and rain, reflected in the prominence of the rain god Chaac in its art.
