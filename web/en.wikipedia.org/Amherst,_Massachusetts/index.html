Amherst is a town in Hampshire County, Massachusetts, United States, in the Connecticut River Valley. With a population of 39,263 at the 2020 census, it is home to Amherst College, Hampshire College, and the University of Massachusetts Amherst, the flagship campus of the University of Massachusetts system.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Norwottuck people. English colonists settled the land in the early 1700s, and the town was incorporated in 1759, named after Jeffery Amherst, a British commander in the French and Indian War. The town developed as an agricultural community before its colleges transformed it in the 19th century.

Education

Amherst is part of the Five College Consortium along with nearby Northampton and South Hadley institutions. The University of Massachusetts Amherst, founded in 1863, is the largest employer in the region.

Notable people

The poet Emily Dickinson was born in Amherst in 1830 and lived there most of her life; her home is now the Emily Dickinson Museum. The town was also home to Noah Webster, who compiled his dictionary while living there.
