Bartleby, the Scrivener

"Bartleby, the Scrivener: A Story of Wall Street" is a short story by American author Herman Melville, first published in Putnam's Magazine in 1853. It follows a Wall Street lawyer and his clerk Bartleby, who answers requests with the phrase "I would prefer not to."

Plot

The narrator hires Bartleby as a copyist. Bartleby initially works prodigiously, then progressively refuses tasks, stops working altogether, and refuses to leave the office, eventually being removed to prison, where he dies.

Themes

The story is read as an exploration of alienation, passive resistance, depression, and the limits of charity and comprehension.

Reception

Neglected in Melville's lifetime, the story is now among the most famous American short stories and a precursor to existentialist and absurdist literature.
