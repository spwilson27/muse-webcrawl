Kyiv Pechersk Lavra

Kyiv Pechersk Lavra (Ukrainian: Києво-Печерська лавра), also known as the Kyiv Monastery of the Caves, is a historic Eastern Orthodox Christian monastery complex in Kyiv, Ukraine. Founded in 1051 by the monks Anthony and Theodosius, it grew into a foremost centre of Orthodox Christianity and learning in Eastern Europe.

History and site

The lavra takes its name from the caves (pechery) dug out by its early monks, which still contain mummified saints and serve as pilgrimage sites, divided into the Near Caves and Far Caves. Above ground stand numerous churches and structures, including the Dormition Cathedral (rebuilt after its destruction in 1941 and reconsecrated in 2000) and the Great Bell Tower, a landmark of the Kyiv skyline.

Status

Together with Saint Sophia Cathedral, the lavra was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1990. It also houses museum collections. Since 2022–2023, amid Russia's invasion of Ukraine, control of parts of the complex has shifted from the Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate) toward the state and the Orthodox Church of Ukraine.
