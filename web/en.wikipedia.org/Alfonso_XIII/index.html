Alfonso XIII

Alfonso XIII (17 May 1886 – 28 February 1941), nicknamed "the African", was King of Spain from his birth until the proclamation of the Second Spanish Republic in 1931. He was a posthumous child, born after the death of his father Alfonso XII, and his mother Maria Christina acted as regent until 1902.

Reign

Alfonso's reign was marked by the collapse of the Restoration political system, colonial war in Morocco (including the disaster of Annual in 1921), and growing social conflict. In 1923 he acquiesced in the military dictatorship of Miguel Primo de Rivera, fatally damaging the monarchy's standing.

Exile and death

Following municipal elections in April 1931 widely read as a plebiscite against the monarchy, Alfonso left Spain and the Second Republic was proclaimed, though he never formally abdicated until shortly before his death. He died in Rome in 1941. His grandson Juan Carlos I became king in 1975 after the death of Franco.

Interests

Alfonso was an enthusiastic sportsman and promoter of Spanish football and tourism; several clubs bearing the title "Real" (royal), including Real Madrid, received their royal patronage during his reign.
