Seed saving

Seed saving is the practice of saving seeds or other reproductive material (such as tubers) from vegetables, grains, herbs and flowers for use from year to year. It was the traditional way farms and gardens were maintained for most of human history.

Practice

Seed saving works best with open-pollinated varieties, which grow true to type; hybrid (F1) varieties do not reliably reproduce their parents' traits. Savers must manage pollination to prevent unwanted cross-pollination, select plants with desirable characteristics, harvest seed at maturity, and clean, dry and store it at low temperature and humidity to preserve viability.

Importance

The practice preserves heirloom varieties and locally adapted landraces, maintains genetic diversity lost when agriculture relies on a narrow set of commercial varieties, and supports seed sovereignty movements. Organised efforts include seed libraries, seed exchanges and seed banks such as the Svalbard Global Seed Vault.
