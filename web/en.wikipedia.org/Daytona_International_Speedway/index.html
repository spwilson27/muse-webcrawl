Daytona International Speedway

Daytona International Speedway is a race track in Daytona Beach, Florida, United States. Since opening in 1959, it has been the home of the Daytona 500, the most prestigious race in NASCAR. The venue also hosts the Rolex 24 at Daytona sports car endurance race, the Coke Zero Sugar 400, and Bike Week events, and has a seating capacity of about 101,500.

The track

The main superspeedway is a 2.5-mile (4.0 km) tri-oval with 31-degree banking in the turns, 18 degrees in the frontstretch tri-oval, and 3 degrees on the backstretch. An infield road course of 3.56 miles incorporates most of the oval with technical infield sections and is used for the Rolex 24 and other sports car events. NASCAR founder Bill France Sr. conceived the speedway to replace racing on the beach-road course, and the track was built on land leased from the city near Daytona Beach International Airport.

Daytona Rising

Between 2013 and 2016 the speedway underwent a $400 million redevelopment, "Daytona Rising," which rebuilt the grandstands with wider seats, escalators, elevators, and five expanded fan entrances, transforming the venue into a modern stadium-style facility. The infield contains Lake Lloyd, a 29-acre lake used for boat racing and jet ski events.
