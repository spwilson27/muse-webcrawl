Library catalog

A library catalog (or library catalogue) is a register of all bibliographic items found in a library or group of libraries, such as a network of libraries at several locations. It records each item's title, author, publication details, subject, and location so that users can find material.

History

Early catalogs were handwritten lists. In the 19th century the printed book catalog and then the card catalog became standard, with Charles Ammi Cutter's Rules for a Dictionary Catalog (1876) setting foundational principles. The Library of Congress distributed printed cards to libraries worldwide from 1901, standardizing cataloging practice.

Online catalogs

From the 1960s, machine-readable cataloging (MARC) records enabled computerized catalogs. Online public access catalogs (OPACs) spread in the 1980s and 1990s, allowing users to search by keyword, title, author, or subject. Modern "discovery layers" add faceted search, relevance ranking, cover images, and links to electronic resources. Union catalogs such as WorldCat combine the holdings of thousands of libraries.

Purpose

Cutter's objectives for a catalog remain classic: to enable a person to find a book of which the author, title, or subject is known; to show what the library has by a given author, on a given subject, or in a given kind of literature; and to assist in the choice of a book.
