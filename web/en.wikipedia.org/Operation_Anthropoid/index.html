Date27 May 1942
LocationPrague, Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia
TargetReinhard Heydrich

Operation Anthropoid was the code name for the assassination of Reinhard Heydrich, the high-ranking Nazi official who headed the Reich Security Main Office and served as acting Reich Protector of Bohemia and Moravia. It was carried out in Prague on 27 May 1942 by two Czechoslovak soldiers in exile, Jozef Gabčík and Jan Kubiš, who had been trained by Britain's Special Operations Executive and parachuted into the occupied Protectorate. Heydrich died of his wounds on 4 June 1942. He was the highest-ranking Nazi assassinated during the Second World War.

Background

Heydrich, one of the architects of the Holocaust and chairman of the Wannsee Conference, had ruled the Protectorate through a mixture of terror and incentives. The Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London, led by Edvard Beneš, authorised the operation partly to demonstrate Czechoslovak resistance and to strengthen its diplomatic position regarding the Munich Agreement.

The attack

Gabčík and Kubiš waited at a hairpin bend on Heydrich's daily route to Prague Castle, where his open-top Mercedes had to slow. As the car approached, Gabčík stepped forward with a Sten submachine gun, which jammed. Kubiš then threw a modified anti-tank grenade, which exploded against the rear wheel, driving fragments and material from the car's upholstery into Heydrich's body. Heydrich initially appeared to recover but died a week later, most likely of septicaemia from his wounds.

Reprisals and manhunt

The Nazis responded with savage reprisals: the village of Lidice was razed and its male inhabitants shot, its women and children deported; the village of Ležáky was similarly destroyed. Thousands of Czechs were arrested and executed. Betrayed by a fellow parachutist, Karel Čurda, the assassins and five other resistance fighters were cornered in the Saints Cyril and Methodius Cathedral in Prague on 18 June 1942. After a long gun battle, those in the crypt took their own lives rather than be captured.

Legacy

Gabčík and Kubiš are remembered as national heroes in the Czech Republic and Slovakia. The operation has been dramatised in several films, including Operation Daybreak (1975), The Man with the Iron Heart (2017), and Anthropoid (2016).
