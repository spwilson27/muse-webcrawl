Jharkhand ("land of forests") is a state in eastern India, carved out of the southern part of Bihar on 15 November 2000 after a decades-long movement for a separate tribal homeland. It occupies much of the Chota Nagpur Plateau and borders Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, and Uttar Pradesh. Its capital is Ranchi and its largest city the steel town of Jamshedpur.

Geography and people

The state is a rugged, forested upland cut by rivers including the Damodar, Subarnarekha, and North Koel, and noted for waterfalls such as Hundru and Dassam. About a quarter of the population belongs to Scheduled Tribes, including the Santhal, Munda, Oraon, and Ho peoples, whose languages, festivals, and traditions give the state a distinctive culture. The tribal freedom fighter Birsa Munda, who led a rebellion against British rule in the 1890s, is a revered figure; the state was formed on his birth anniversary.

Economy

Jharkhand holds a large share of India's mineral wealth—coal, iron ore, copper, uranium, and mica—and hosts major industrial centres such as Jamshedpur (home of Tata Steel), Bokaro, and Dhanbad. Despite this resource wealth, much of the largely rural population remains poor, and disputes over land, mining, and tribal rights are central political issues.
