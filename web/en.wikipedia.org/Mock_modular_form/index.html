Mock modular form


Image placeholder: coefficients of a mock theta function

In mathematics, a mock modular form is the holomorphic part of a harmonic weak Maass form, and a mock theta function is essentially a mock modular form of weight 1/2. Mock theta functions were first described by Srinivasa Ramanujan in his last letter to G. H. Hardy, written in January 1920, shortly before his death, in which he listed 17 examples of functions he called "mock theta functions" because they imitate the behaviour of classical theta functions near roots of unity without being theta functions themselves.

History

Ramanujan gave no precise definition, and for over 80 years mock theta functions remained a tantalising mystery: they had striking q-series identities and connections to partition theory but no underlying framework explaining them. Freeman Dyson speculated about a hidden structure unifying them. The breakthrough came in the thesis of Sander Zwegers (2002), who showed that Ramanujan's examples can be completed into harmonic Maass forms by adding a specific non-holomorphic correction term; the obstruction to modularity is governed by a classical modular form called the "shadow" of the mock modular form.

Definition

A mock modular form is a function f on the upper half-plane for which there exists a modular form g (the shadow) such that f, augmented by a period integral of g, transforms like a modular form. Equivalently, f is the holomorphic part of a harmonic weak Maass form whose image under the ξ-operator is a cusp form (up to a factor) equal to the shadow.

Applications

Mock modular forms appear in the enumerative theory of partitions, in the representation theory of vertex algebras, in Donaldson–Thomas theory, and in string theory, notably in the counting of black hole microstates and in "umbral moonshine," a phenomenon connecting mock modular forms with the Mathieu group M24 and other sporadic simple groups.
