Head Start (program)

Launched1965
AgencyU.S. Department of Health and Human Services
ServesChildren from birth to age five in low-income families
Part ofWar on Poverty

Head Start is a United States federal program that provides comprehensive early childhood education, health, nutrition and parent involvement services to children from low-income families. Launched in 1965 as part of President Lyndon B. Johnson's War on Poverty, it was designed to break the cycle of poverty by preparing disadvantaged children for school.

Services

Head Start programs serve children from age three to five; Early Head Start, added in 1994, serves infants, toddlers and pregnant women. Services go beyond classroom teaching to include medical and dental screening, meals, mental-health support and help for parents in finding jobs and housing. Programs are run by local grantees — school districts, non-profits and community agencies — under federal standards.

Impact and debate

Research finds positive short-term gains in school readiness; debates continue about how long the advantages persist ("fade-out") versus long-term benefits in health and attainment found in some follow-up studies. The program serves roughly a million children a year and remains one of the largest US early-childhood initiatives.
