Stanley Kubrick (26 July 1928 – 7 March 1999) was an American film director, producer and screenwriter widely considered one of the greatest and most influential filmmakers in cinematic history. His films, almost all adaptations of novels or short stories, are noted for their technical innovation, dark humour and exploration of human nature.

Career

Kubrick began as a photographer for Look magazine before making documentaries and the noir features Killer's Kiss (1955), The Killing (1956) and Paths of Glory (1957). After Spartacus (1960), he moved to England, where he made Lolita (1962), the Cold War satire Dr. Strangelove (1964) and 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), a landmark of science-fiction cinema whose special effects won him his only competitive Academy Award.

Later films

His later output, made with famously long productions and numerous takes, includes A Clockwork Orange (1971), the period epic Barry Lyndon (1975), the horror film The Shining (1980), the Vietnam film Full Metal Jacket (1987), and his final work, Eyes Wide Shut (1999), completed days before his death.

Style and reputation

Kubrick's trademarks include one-point perspective cinematography, Steadicam tracking shots, meticulous production design, classical music scores and detached, ironic tone. A perfectionist who controlled every aspect of production and distribution, he is consistently ranked among the finest directors of all time, and many of his films are preserved in the U.S. National Film Registry.
