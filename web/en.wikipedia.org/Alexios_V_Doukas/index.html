Alexios V Doukas (Greek: Ἀλέξιος Δούκας; died December 1204), nicknamed Mourtzouphlos for his heavy, overhanging eyebrows, was Byzantine emperor from 5 February to 13 April 1204, during the final phase of the Fourth Crusade and the sack of Constantinople.

Rise to power

A member of the Doukas family and a court official under the Angeloi emperors, Alexios emerged as a leader of the anti-Latin party in Constantinople, which resented the restored emperor Alexios IV's dependence on the Crusaders encamped outside the city. In late January 1204 a popular uprising deposed Alexios IV; Doukas had him strangled in prison, and on 5 February was crowned emperor as Alexios V. Isaac II died, reportedly of shock, days later.

Defence of Constantinople

Alexios V broke off negotiations with the Crusaders, strengthened the city's walls, closed the gates and attempted a night attack on the Crusader fleet with fireships, which failed. He fought bravely during the assaults of 9 and 12 April, but when the Crusaders breached the sea walls on 13 April 1204, he fled the city by night with his wife. Constantinople was then subjected to a three-day sack of legendary brutality.

Death

Alexios sought refuge with the former emperor Alexios III in Thrace, marrying his daughter Eudokia — but Alexios III had him blinded and abandoned. Captured by the Crusaders, Alexios V was brought back to Constantinople and executed in December 1204 by being thrown from the top of the Column of Theodosius, a punishment reserved as a spectacular deterrent.
