Learning theory (education)

Learning theory in education describes how students receive, process and retain knowledge during learning. The major theoretical frameworks include behaviourism, which focuses on observable behaviour shaped by conditioning and reinforcement (Pavlov, Skinner, Thorndike); cognitivism, which treats learning as internal mental processing, including memory and problem-solving (Piaget, information-processing theory); and constructivism, which holds that learners actively construct knowledge from experience (Vygotsky, Bruner, Dewey).

Later approaches include humanism, emphasising motivation and self-actualisation; social learning theory (Bandura), emphasising modelling and self-efficacy; and connectivism, proposed for learning in digital networks. Instructional design draws on these theories — together with concepts such as Bloom's taxonomy, scaffolding, and the zone of proximal development — to structure teaching.

No single theory explains all learning; teachers commonly combine approaches depending on subject, learners and goals.
