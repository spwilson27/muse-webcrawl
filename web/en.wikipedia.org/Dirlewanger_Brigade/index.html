The Dirlewanger Brigade, also known as the SS-Sturmbrigade Dirlewanger, was a Waffen-SS penal and anti-partisan unit of Nazi Germany commanded by Oskar Dirlewanger. It was originally formed in 1940 from convicted poachers, and later recruited heavily from criminals, concentration camp prisoners, and military delinquents. The unit became infamous for extraordinary brutality and war crimes against civilians, chiefly in occupied Poland and Belarus.

Formation and operations

The unit began as the Oranienburg Poacher's Unit (Wilddiebkommando Oranienburg), formed on Heinrich Himmler's initiative from poachers supposedly skilled in tracking. Deployed in the Lublin district of occupied Poland and then in Belarus, it conducted so-called anti-partisan operations (Bandenbekämpfung) that in practice involved mass killings of civilians, burning of villages, and other atrocities. Dirlewanger's men also participated in suppressing the Warsaw Uprising of 1944, during which the unit was implicated in massacres of civilians, including patients and staff of hospitals. In early 1945 the formation was expanded on paper into the 36th Waffen Grenadier Division of the SS before being largely destroyed fighting the Red Army; Dirlewanger himself died in Allied custody in June 1945 under disputed circumstances.

Composition and reputation

Recruitment broadened over time from poachers to violent criminals, foreign volunteers, Soviet prisoners, and eventually political prisoners and concentration-camp inmates promised commutation of sentences; discipline was maintained by terror and execution. Historians regard the brigade as one of the most criminal formations of the war, and its record is a central example of the Waffen-SS's involvement in genocide and crimes against humanity.
