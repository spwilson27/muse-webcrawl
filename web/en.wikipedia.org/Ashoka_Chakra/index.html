Ashoka Chakra

The Ashoka Chakra is a depiction of the Buddhist Dharmachakra ("wheel of dharma") with 24 spokes. It is named after the Mauryan emperor Ashoka, whose pillars bore representations of the wheel. The Ashoka Chakra is most widely known as the centrepiece of the national flag of India, adopted on 24 July 1947, where it appears in navy blue on a white background, replacing the spinning wheel (charkha) of earlier flag designs.

Symbolism

The wheel represents the Buddhist concept of dharma — the universal law of righteousness and cosmic order. Its 24 equally spaced spokes are often interpreted as signifying that there is life in movement and death in stagnation, and that the nation should move forward in peaceful change. The navy blue colour is said to represent the universal sky and the depth of the ocean, symbolising universal truth.

Use

Besides the flag, the Ashoka Chakra appears in the State Emblem of India and gives its name to India's highest peacetime military decoration for gallantry, the Ashoka Chakra Award, instituted in 1952. It is also depicted on Indian currency and official seals.

History

The wheel motif derives from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath, erected around 250 BCE at the site of the Buddha's first sermon, where the Buddha "set in motion the wheel of dharma". The Constituent Assembly of India selected the Ashoka Chakra for the national flag shortly before independence in 1947.
