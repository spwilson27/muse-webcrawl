Marathon des Sables

Location: Sahara Desert, Morocco
First held: 1986
Distance: ~250 km (156 mi) over 6 days
Type: Multi-stage ultramarathon, self-sufficiency race

The Marathon des Sables (MdS), or Sahara Marathon, is a multi-day ultramarathon held annually in the Sahara Desert of southern Morocco. Often described as the toughest footrace on Earth, it covers roughly 250 kilometres (156 miles) in six stages over seven days — the equivalent of about five and a half regular marathons.

Format

Competitors must be self-sufficient: they carry all their food and equipment for the week in a backpack, while organisers supply rationed water and a shared open-sided tent each night. The longest stage is typically around 80–90 km, run through dunes, rocky hamada plains and dried lake beds in daytime temperatures that can approach 50 °C.

History

The race was founded by French concert promoter Patrick Bauer, who in 1984 crossed part of the Sahara alone on foot and conceived the event; the first edition ran in 1986 with 23 participants. It now attracts over a thousand runners from dozens of countries, plus charity entrants. Moroccan runner Rachid El Morabity has been its most dominant champion, and the event has inspired sister stage races in other deserts and regions.

Notable incidents

In 1994 Italian participant Mauro Prosperi became lost in a sandstorm and survived nine days in the desert before being found in Algeria, an ordeal that became one of endurance sport's best-known survival stories.
