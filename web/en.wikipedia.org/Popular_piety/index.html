Popular piety

Popular piety (also called popular religiosity or folk religion in some contexts) refers to the expressions of faith and devotion practiced by ordinary believers, often outside or alongside official liturgy. In the Roman Catholic tradition the term covers practices such as processions, pilgrimages, novenas, the rosary, devotion to saints, and the veneration of relics and sacred images.

Description

Popular piety arises from the religious culture of a people and expresses faith through local customs, festivals, music, and art. The Second Vatican Council and later documents, including the 2001 Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy, encouraged that such devotions be harmonized with the liturgy while respecting their cultural value.

Examples

  • Pilgrimages to shrines such as Lourdes, Fatima, and Guadalupe
  • Fiestas in honor of patron saints
  • Eucharistic adoration and Corpus Christi processions
  • The Stations of the Cross and May devotions to the Virgin Mary
