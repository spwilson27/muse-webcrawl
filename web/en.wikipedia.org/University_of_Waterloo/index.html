The University of Waterloo (UWaterloo or UW) is a public research university in Waterloo, Ontario, Canada. Founded in 1957 with engineering and co-operative education at its core, it operates the largest post-secondary co-operative education program in the world, in which students alternate study terms with paid work placements.

Academics

Waterloo enrolls more than 40,000 students in six faculties: Mathematics (the largest concentration of mathematical and computer science talent in the world, home to the David R. Cheriton School of Computer Science), Engineering, Science, Arts, Environment, and Health. It co-founded the Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics, and its Institute for Quantum Computing is a leading centre in quantum information science. The university's inventor-owned intellectual property policy has fostered a dense startup ecosystem in the Waterloo Region, sometimes called Canada's "Silicon Valley North"; alumni include BlackBerry co-founder Mike Lazaridis and Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin.

Co-operative education

More than 25,000 students take part in co-op, working for over 7,000 employers worldwide. The Tatham Centre is among the largest co-op facilities of its kind.

Campus

The main campus covers about 400 hectares in Waterloo, including an environmental reserve. Satellite campuses include the School of Architecture in Cambridge and a health sciences campus in Kitchener.
