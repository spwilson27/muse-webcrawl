Outer ear

The outer ear, or external ear, is the external portion of the ear, consisting of the auricle (pinna) and the ear canal (external acoustic meatus). It collects sound waves and channels them to the tympanic membrane (eardrum), which separates the outer ear from the middle ear.

Structure

The auricle is made of elastic cartilage covered by skin, shaped to funnel sound and aid localisation. The ear canal is about 2.5 cm long in adults, lined with skin containing ceruminous glands that produce earwax (cerumen), which protects the canal.

Clinical significance

Common conditions include otitis externa ("swimmer's ear"), impacted cerumen, and perichondritis. Cauliflower ear results from repeated trauma, typically in contact sports.
