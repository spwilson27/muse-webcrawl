Aquila is a constellation on the celestial equator. Its name is Latin for "eagle" and it represents the bird that carried Zeus/Jupiter's thunderbolts in Greek and Roman mythology. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, and remains one of the 88 modern constellations recognised by the International Astronomical Union.

Notable features

The constellation's brightest star is Altair (Alpha Aquilae), a white main-sequence star about 17 light-years from Earth and one of the nearest stars visible to the naked eye. Altair forms one corner of the Summer Triangle asterism, together with Vega in Lyra and Deneb in Cygnus. Altair is flanked by the stars Beta Aquilae (Alshain) and Gamma Aquilae (Tarazed). Aquila lies within the Milky Way and contains rich star fields, dark nebulae such as Barnard's E-shaped nebula, and several planetary nebulae and open clusters.

Mythology

In Greek mythology, Aquila was the eagle that carried the thunderbolts of Zeus, and in some versions was the bird that abducted the Trojan youth Ganymede to Olympus to serve as cupbearer to the gods. In Hindu tradition, the pattern of Altair and its flanking stars is associated with Vishnu.
