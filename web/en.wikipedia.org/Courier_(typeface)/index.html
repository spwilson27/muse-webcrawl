Courier (typeface)

Designer: Howard "Bud" Kettler

Foundry: IBM

Date released: 1955

Classification: Monospaced slab-serif

Courier is a monospaced slab-serif typeface designed by Howard "Bud" Kettler in 1955 for IBM's typewriters. Because IBM did not trademark or exclusively license the design, it became a standard typeface on typewriters from many manufacturers and later on computers. Courier New, a refined version by Adrian Frutiger for IBM, is bundled with most operating systems.

Characteristics

As a monospaced font, every character occupies the same horizontal width (historically 10 or 12 characters per inch, "elite" and "pica"). Its slab serifs, low contrast, and generous letter spacing mimic typewritten text, which made it a longstanding standard for screenplays (where one page in 12-point Courier approximates one minute of screen time), legal documents, and computer programming.

Variants

Besides Courier New, common variants include IBM Courier, Courier Prime (designed for screenwriting), Courier Screenplay, and the free Courier Prime and Nimbus Mono designs. The U.S. State Department used Courier New as its standard typeface until 2023.
