Big Four (banking)

The term Big Four is used in many countries to describe the dominant quartet of banks in that market, though the phrase has other uses in finance. In the United States and globally, it often refers to the largest banks by assets and market capitalisation, while in countries such as the United Kingdom, Australia, China, and South Africa it names specific long-standing groups of major retail banks.

By country

In the United Kingdom, the Big Four are Lloyds Banking Group, NatWest Group, Barclays, and HSBC. In Australia, the "four pillars" are the Commonwealth Bank, Westpac, ANZ, and National Australia Bank. China's Big Four state-owned commercial banks are the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Agricultural Bank of China, and Bank of China — among the largest banks in the world by assets.

United States

In the US, the four largest banks by assets are JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup, and Wells Fargo. Together they hold a very large share of domestic deposits and were central to debates about "too big to fail" after the 2008 financial crisis and the regulatory reforms that followed.

Significance

Concentration among a few large banks raises questions about competition, systemic risk, and political influence. Global systemically important banks are subject to additional capital requirements under the Basel framework, and regulators maintain lists of institutions whose failure could threaten the financial system.
