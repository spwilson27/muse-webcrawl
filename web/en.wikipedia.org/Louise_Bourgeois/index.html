Louise Bourgeois

Louise Joséphine Bourgeois (25 December 1911 – 31 May 2010) was a French-American artist. Although she worked in painting and printmaking, she is best known for her large-scale sculpture and installation art, including her monumental spider sculptures titled Maman.

Work

Born in Paris, Bourgeois emigrated to New York in 1938. Her art, informed by psychoanalysis, childhood memory and themes of the body, sexuality, family and repair, gained wide recognition late in life; a 1982 retrospective at the Museum of Modern Art was the first given there to a woman artist. Her steel and marble spiders, the largest over nine metres tall, have been exhibited worldwide.

Legacy

Bourgeois worked until her death at 98 and is regarded as a foundational figure for feminist and contemporary art.
