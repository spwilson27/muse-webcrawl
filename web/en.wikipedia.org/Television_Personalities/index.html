The Television Personalities are an English post-punk band formed in Chelsea, London, in 1977, centred on singer-songwriter Dan Treacy. Their witty, lo-fi songs about 1960s pop culture, London life, and Treacy's literary heroes made them one of the most influential cult bands of the indie era.

History

Treacy formed the band with school friends including Ed Ball. Their early singles — "14th Floor" and the celebrated "Part Time Punks" (1978) — were released on their own Whaam! Records and championed by John Peel. Debut album ...And Don't the Kids Just Love It (1981), whose sleeve paid homage to Twiggy, John Barry, and Patrick Macnee, mixed mod, punk, and psychedelic influences. Subsequent albums included Mummy Your Not Watching Me (1982), The Painted Word (1984), and Privilege (1989).

Influence and later years

The band's DIY aesthetic and Treacy's confessional songwriting strongly influenced indie pop and the C86 generation, as well as later acts such as Kurt Cobain (a fan) and MGMT, who recorded the tribute "Song for Dan Treacy". Treacy's struggles with mental health, addiction, and a period of imprisonment interrupted the band's career, but he returned with albums including My Dark Places (2006) and A Memory Is Better Than Nothing (2010), and the group has continued to perform sporadically.
