Beinn Eighe

Beinn Eighe is a mountain massif in the Torridon area of Wester Ross in the Northwest Highlands of Scotland. Lying south of Loch Maree, it forms a long ridge with several peaks, the highest being Ruadh-stac Mòr at 1,010 metres (3,314 ft), which holds Munro status. The name means "file mountain" in Scottish Gaelic.

Nature reserve

Beinn Eighe was designated in 1951 as Britain's first National Nature Reserve. The reserve covers the mountain and surrounding land, including remnants of ancient Caledonian pinewood at Coille na Glas-Leitir on the slopes above Loch Maree. In 2016 the reserve became part of the Wester Ross Biosphere Reserve under UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere programme.

Climbing

The mountain offers extensive walking and climbing. The Triple Buttress of Coire Mhic Fhearchair, reached by a long approach, is one of the most celebrated mountain corries in Scotland.
