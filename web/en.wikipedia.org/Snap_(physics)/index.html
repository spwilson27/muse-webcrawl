Snap (physics)

Position and its first four time derivatives

In physics, snap or jounce is the fourth derivative of the position vector with respect to time; equivalently, it is the rate of change of jerk. The sequence runs: position, velocity (first derivative), acceleration (second), jerk (third) and snap (fourth). The fifth and sixth derivatives are sometimes jokingly called crackle and pop, after the Rice Krispies mascots Snap, Crackle and Pop.

Definition and units

If r(t) is position, snap s = d⁴r/dt⁴ = dj/dt, where j is jerk. Its SI unit is metres per second to the fourth power, m/s⁴ (equivalently m·s⁻⁴). Unlike velocity and acceleration, snap rarely appears as a fundamental quantity in physical law; there is no equivalent of Newton's second law for it, and no commonly used instrument measures it directly.

Applications

Snap matters in engineering contexts where smooth motion profiles are designed. In road and railway design, and in the programming of CNC machines, lifts and camera rigs, limiting jerk — and hence controlling snap — reduces vibration, wear and passenger discomfort. In particle physics and cosmology, higher derivatives of position and of the cosmic scale factor occur in some analyses; the derivatives of the scale factor have their own named quantities in the cosmographic expansion.
