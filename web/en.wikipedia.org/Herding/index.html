Herding

Herding is the act of bringing individual animals together into a group (a herd), maintaining the group, and moving it from place to place — or any combination of these. It is an essential practice in animal husbandry and pastoralism wherever livestock such as sheep, cattle, goats, reindeer, horses or yaks are raised and must be moved between pastures, gathered for shearing, milking, veterinary care or market, and protected from predators.

Methods

Human herders — shepherds, cowherds, goatherds, drovers and stockmen — manage animals on foot, on horseback, or with vehicles, using voice, whistles, staffs and knowledge of animal behaviour. Herding exploits the natural flocking and flight-zone behaviour of prey animals: pressure applied at the edge of an animal's flight zone moves it in a predictable direction, while releasing pressure halts movement. Low-stress stockmanship emphasises calm, consistent handling to keep animals manageable and healthy.

Herding dogs

Herding dogs are among the oldest working dogs, bred for centuries to gather, drive and control livestock under a handler's direction. Breeds such as the Border Collie, Australian Shepherd, Australian Cattle Dog and Old English Sheepdog display modified predatory behaviours — eyeing, stalking and chasing — without the final kill bite. Handlers direct dogs with spoken commands, whistles or hand signals. Sheepdog trials, in which dogs work a small flock through a set course, test and celebrate these skills and are popular spectator events in many countries.

Historical and cultural significance

Herding arose with the domestication of animals in the Neolithic and underlies nomadic and transhumant pastoral cultures across Eurasia, Africa and the Americas, from Sami reindeer herders and Mongolian nomads to the cowboys of the American West and gauchos of South America. Seasonal movements between lowland and mountain pastures (transhumance) shaped landscapes, economies and traditions, and drove long-distance droving routes to urban markets. Modern ranching still depends on herding, though helicopters, quad bikes and fencing have replaced some traditional techniques.
