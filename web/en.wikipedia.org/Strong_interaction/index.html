The strong interaction (also called the strong force or colour force) is one of the four fundamental interactions of nature, alongside electromagnetism, the weak interaction and gravitation. It binds quarks together into protons, neutrons and other hadrons, and binds protons and neutrons into atomic nuclei.

Quantum chromodynamics

The strong interaction is described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD), a gauge theory with symmetry group SU(3). Quarks carry one of three colour charges — red, green or blue — and exchange gluons, which themselves carry colour and anticolour. Because gluons interact with each other, the force does not diminish with distance as electromagnetism does.

Key properties

  • Colour confinement — quarks and gluons are never observed in isolation; the energy stored in the colour field grows with separation until it becomes favourable to create new quark–antiquark pairs.
  • Asymptotic freedom — at very short distances (high energies) quarks behave almost as free particles; discovered by Gross, Wilczek and Politzer, who received the 2004 Nobel Prize in Physics.
  • Residual strong force — the nuclear force that binds nucleons in nuclei is a weaker, van der Waals-like remnant of the colour force between colour-neutral hadrons.

Strength

At distances of about a femtometre, the strong coupling is roughly 137 times stronger than electromagnetism, about a million times stronger than the weak interaction, and some 1038 times stronger than gravity — yet its confinement to nuclear scales means it has no direct influence on everyday macroscopic phenomena.
