Contemporary RnB

Contemporary RnB is an alternate rendering of contemporary R&B, the popular music genre that combines rhythm and blues with pop, soul, funk, hip hop and electronic music. The abbreviation “RnB” (without ampersands) is common in British and industry usage, music databases and streaming metadata, while “R&B” remains the standard form in most publications.

Terminology

The term rhythm and blues was coined in 1949 by Jerry Wexler of Billboard magazine as a marketing label for Black popular music. By the 1980s, “contemporary R&B” distinguished the polished, synthesiser-driven successor of classic soul and funk from earlier styles. Reference works variously index the genre under R&B, RnB, or R'n'B.

Overview of the genre

Contemporary RnB features drum machines, layered synthesisers, smooth vocal production and melisma. Landmark figures include Michael Jackson, Whitney Houston, Mariah Carey, Boyz II Men, TLC, Usher, Alicia Keys and Beyoncé; the 2010s alternative R&B movement — Frank Ocean, The Weeknd, SZA — broadened the sound with atmospheric textures and introspective songwriting.

Charts and industry

In the United States, Billboard tracks the genre through its Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs chart, while the Grammy Awards honour it in categories such as Best R&B Album and Best Progressive R&B Album.
