Rawinsonde

A rawinsonde is a weather balloon instrument system that measures the vertical profile of the atmosphere — pressure, temperature, and humidity — while being tracked to determine wind speed and direction at successive heights. The name is a contraction of radar wind sonde: early systems tracked the balloon by radar, while most modern systems use GPS signals.

Operation

The radiosonde package is carried aloft by a helium- or hydrogen-filled balloon rising at about 5 m/s, transmitting measurements by radio telemetry. As the balloon ascends to altitudes of 20–35 km it eventually bursts, and the instrument descends by parachute. Tracking the balloon's position over time yields the wind profile, which distinguishes a rawinsonde from a simple radiosonde.

Use

Rawinsonde observations, conventionally launched twice daily at 00 and 12 UTC from hundreds of stations worldwide, are a fundamental input to numerical weather prediction and are used to plot Skew-T log-P diagrams for analysing atmospheric stability, cloud layers, and severe weather potential.
