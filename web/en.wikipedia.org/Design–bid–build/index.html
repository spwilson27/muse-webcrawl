Design–bid–build

Design–bid–build (DBB), also known as the traditional or hard-bid method, is a project delivery system used in the construction industry in which an owner contracts separately with a designer (architect or engineer) and a builder (general contractor), in sequential phases.

Process

The process unfolds in three distinct stages:

  1. Design — The owner engages an architect or engineer, who completes full design documents: drawings and specifications.
  2. Bid — The finished documents are put out to competitive tender; contractors submit bids, and the work is typically awarded to the lowest responsible bidder.
  3. Build — The selected contractor constructs the project, while the designer often administers the contract and observes construction on the owner's behalf.

Advantages and disadvantages

Design–bid–build is widely understood, offers the owner firm price competition based on complete documents, and keeps the designer as the owner's independent advocate. Its drawbacks include a longer overall schedule, since construction cannot begin until design and bidding finish, and limited contractor input during design, which can lead to cost overruns or disputes when documents prove incomplete.

Alternatives

Alternative delivery methods include design–build, in which one entity handles both design and construction; construction management at-risk; and integrated project delivery.
