Softball

Softball is a team sport similar to baseball, played with a larger ball (11 to 16 inches in circumference) on a smaller field. It was invented in Chicago in 1887 as an indoor game, and by 1933 had taken the name "softball". There are two main types: fastpitch, in which the pitcher delivers the ball with an underhand windmill motion at high speed, and slowpitch, in which the ball is pitched with an arc and teams of ten fielders are used.

Field and equipment

The infield has four bases in a diamond; in fastpitch the bases are 60 feet apart and the pitching distance is 43 feet for women and 46 feet for men. Bats are typically aluminium or composite. Despite the name, the ball is not soft relative to a baseball; it is larger and slightly less dense.

Rules compared with baseball

Games last seven innings. Base stealing is restricted in slowpitch; bunting is common in fastpitch. The windmill delivery can propel the ball at over 70 mph (113 km/h), which, from the shorter pitching distance, gives batters reaction times comparable to elite baseball pitching.

International competition

Softball is governed internationally by the World Baseball Softball Confederation. Women's fastpitch softball was an Olympic sport from 1996 to 2008 and returned at the Tokyo 2020 Games. NCAA women's college softball is hugely popular in the United States, highlighted by the Women's College World Series.
