Politics of Mexico

The politics of Mexico take place in the framework of a federal presidential representative democratic republic, in which the President of Mexico is both head of state and head of government, and of a multi-party system. The federal government represents the United Mexican States. It is divided into three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial, as established by the Political Constitution of the United Mexican States, published in 1917.

Institutions

The executive is headed by the president, elected by popular vote for a single six-year term (sexenio) with no re-election. The legislature, the Congress of the Union, is bicameral: the Chamber of Deputies (500 members) and the Senate (128 members), elected by a mixture of plurality and proportional representation. The judiciary is headed by the Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation. Mexico is a federation of 31 states and Mexico City, each with its own constitution and elected governor.

Political history

For most of the 20th century Mexico was governed by the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) and its predecessors, which held the presidency continuously from 1929 to 2000 — a system once called "the perfect dictatorship." The opposition National Action Party (PAN) won the presidency in 2000 under Vicente Fox, inaugurating alternation in power; the PRI returned under Enrique Peña Nieto in 2012, and the left-wing Morena party, founded by Andrés Manuel López Obrador, won in 2018. In 2024 Claudia Sheinbaum was elected as Mexico's first woman president.

Elections and issues

Elections are organized by the autonomous National Electoral Institute (INE). Major political issues include public security and the drug war, corruption, economic inequality, energy policy, migration, and relations with the United States.
