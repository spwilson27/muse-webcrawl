Oirats

The Oirats (Mongolian: Ойрад, Oirad) are the westernmost group of the Mongols, historically inhabiting the Altai region and Dzungaria. The name derives from the confederation of the "Four Oirats" (Dörben Oirad) — traditionally the Choros, Torghut, Khoshut, and Dörbet tribes. Their descendants today include the Kalmyks of Russia's Caspian steppe, and Oirat populations in western Mongolia and China's Xinjiang region.

LanguagesOirat, Mongolian, Russian

Oirat groups preserve a distinct dialect and the Clear script devised by Zaya Pandita.

History

The Oirats first appear as forest peoples west of Lake Baikal, submitting to Genghis Khan in 1207. After the fall of the Yuan dynasty they rivalled the eastern (Khalkha) Mongols for supremacy; under Esen Taishi they briefly dominated Mongolia and famously captured the Ming emperor at the Tumu Crisis of 1449. In the 17th century, branches migrated widely: the Torghuts and Dörbets moved to the Volga steppe, becoming the Kalmyks; the Khoshuts established a khanate in Tibet and Kokonor; and the Choros-led Dzungar Khanate became the last great nomadic empire of Central Asia before its destruction by the Qing dynasty in the 1750s.

Culture

In 1648 the Oirat monk Zaya Pandita created the Clear script (Todo bichig), adapted from the Mongolian script to represent Oirat phonology precisely. Tibetan Buddhism, adopted from the late 16th century, remains central to Oirat identity.
