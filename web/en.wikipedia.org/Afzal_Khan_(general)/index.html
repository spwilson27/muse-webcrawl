Afzal Khan (died 10 November 1659) was a general of the Adil Shahi dynasty of the Bijapur Sultanate in the Deccan. He is best known for his fatal confrontation with the Maratha leader Shivaji at the Battle of Pratapgad in 1659, an episode that became one of the most celebrated stories of Maratha history.

Career

Afzal Khan was an experienced commander who had distinguished himself in Bijapur's campaigns in Karnataka, including the capture of the nayakas' strongholds. When Shivaji's rising power in the Pune region threatened Bijapur's authority, the dowager regent Badi Sahiba entrusted Afzal Khan with a large army to capture or kill him. Khan advanced, desecrating temples at Tuljapur and Pandharpur to provoke Shivaji into open battle, and camped below the fortress of Pratapgad.

Meeting and death

After negotiations, both leaders agreed to a personal meeting on 10 November 1659, each to be accompanied by only a few attendants. Accounts — Maratha and Persian sources differ in detail — agree that the meeting ended in violence: Shivaji, wearing concealed armor and iron claws (the wagh nakh, "tiger claws"), killed Afzal Khan, while Khan's attack (in Maratha accounts, a treacherous dagger blow) failed. A hidden Maratha army then routed the leaderless Bijapur force in the Battle of Pratapgad, capturing its artillery, treasure, and supplies and greatly enhancing Shivaji's prestige.

Legacy

The episode is commemorated in Maratha ballads (powadas) and in later plays, films, and textbooks. Afzal Khan's tomb stands at Pratapgad, maintained to this day.
