The Andijan massacre occurred on 13 May 2005 in Andijan, a city in eastern Uzbekistan's Fergana Valley, when Uzbek government troops fired on a large crowd of protesters in the city's central Babur Square. The Uzbek government of President Islam Karimov acknowledged 187 deaths, while human rights organisations and witnesses estimated that several hundred, possibly up to 1,500, unarmed civilians were killed.

Background

Protests had grown around the trial of 23 local businessmen accused by the authorities of membership in an extremist Islamic movement, charges their supporters called fabricated. On the night of 12–13 May, armed men stormed a prison and freed the defendants along with hundreds of other inmates, seized government buildings, and took hostages. Thousands of residents gathered in Babur Square to air grievances about poverty, corruption and repression.

The massacre

In the late afternoon, after negotiations failed, security forces surrounded the square and opened fire on the crowd. Witnesses described armoured vehicles and snipers firing indiscriminately as people tried to flee along Cholpon Avenue. Hundreds of survivors fled across the border into Kyrgyzstan. The government blamed Islamic militants, maintained that only "terrorists" were killed, and refused calls for an international investigation.

Aftermath

The events sharply strained Uzbekistan's relations with the West: the United States and European Union criticised the crackdown, the EU imposed sanctions, and Uzbekistan expelled US forces from the Karshi-Khanabad air base. Tashkent moved closer to Russia and China, which supported its account. Journalists and human rights workers were expelled or imprisoned, and the true death toll has never been independently established.
