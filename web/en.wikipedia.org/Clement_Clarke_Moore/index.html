Clement Clarke Moore (15 July 1779 – 10 July 1863) was an American scholar and professor of Oriental and Greek literature at Columbia College. He is best known as the credited author of the Christmas poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas", better known today as "'Twas the Night Before Christmas", first published anonymously in 1823.

Life and career

Moore was the son of Benjamin Moore, Episcopal Bishop of New York and president of Columbia College. He graduated from Columbia in 1798 and inherited the family estate of Chelsea in Manhattan, parts of which he later developed and donated for the establishment of the General Theological Seminary. A serious scholar, he compiled a two-volume Hebrew and English lexicon, among the first works of its kind in America, and served as professor of biblical learning at the seminary.

"A Visit from St. Nicholas"

The poem was first printed in the Troy Sentinel on 23 December 1823 without attribution. It was widely reprinted, and Moore eventually claimed authorship, including it in his 1844 volume of poems. The poem popularised many features of the modern image of Santa Claus, including his sleigh drawn by eight named reindeer, and helped shift American Christmas celebrations toward a domestic, child-centred holiday. Some scholars have argued that the poem was actually written by Henry Livingston Jr., a claim that remains disputed.

Death and legacy

Moore died at his summer home in Newport, Rhode Island, in 1863, and was buried in Trinity Church Cemetery in New York. His name remains inseparable from the poem that begins "'Twas the night before Christmas".
