1999 Formula One World Championship

The 1999 FIA Formula One World Championship was the 53rd season of FIA Formula One motor racing, contested over 16 Grands Prix from 7 March to 31 October 1999. Mika Häkkinen of McLaren-Mercedes retained the Drivers' Championship, while Ferrari won the Constructors' Championship — its first since 1983 — after a season dramatically reshaped by Michael Schumacher's leg-breaking accident at Silverstone.

Mika Häkkinen's McLaren MP4/14, the car that carried him to a second title.

Season summary

Häkkinen and Schumacher traded wins in the early races. At the British Grand Prix on 11 July, Schumacher crashed at Stowe corner after a brake failure and broke his right leg, missing six races. His place at Ferrari was taken by Mika Salo, while Eddie Irvine unexpectedly became Ferrari's title challenger, winning in Austria, Germany, and Malaysia. Häkkinen's season included spins while leading in San Marino and Italy — the latter moving him to tears — which kept the championship open to the final round.

At the penultimate race in Malaysia, Schumacher returned and let Irvine win, but both Ferraris were disqualified for illegal bargeboards; Ferrari successfully appealed, and the results were reinstated. In the final race at Suzuka, Häkkinen won and Irvine finished third, giving the Finn the championship by two points.

Constructors' Championship

Ferrari scored 128 points to McLaren's 124, ending a sixteen-year drought in the constructors' standings and beginning the team's dominant era. Heinz-Harald Frentzen's two wins for Jordan lifted the team to third, its best-ever result. The season was also notable as the debut year of the British American Racing team and the final season for the Stewart Grand Prix team before it became Jaguar Racing.

Legacy

The 1999 season is remembered for its unpredictability — six different winners and points finishes for backmarker teams in chaotic wet races — and as the last championship before the Schumacher-Ferrari five-year dominance of the early 2000s.
