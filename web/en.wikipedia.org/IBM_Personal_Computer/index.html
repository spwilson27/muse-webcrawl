IBM Personal Computer

The IBM Personal Computer (model 5150, commonly known as the IBM PC) is the first microcomputer released in the IBM PC model line and the basis for the IBM PC compatible de facto standard. Released on August 12, 1981, it was created by a team of engineers and designers at IBM's Boca Raton, Florida facility, led by Don Estridge.

Design

The PC used an Intel 8088 processor at 4.77 MHz, came with 16 to 256 kilobytes of RAM, and ran PC DOS, provided by Microsoft. Unusually for IBM, it was built largely from off-the-shelf components with an open architecture and published technical references, encouraging a large third-party market for expansion cards and software.

Legacy

The IBM PC transformed the microcomputer industry: within a few years, "IBM compatible" clones from manufacturers such as Compaq came to dominate the market, and the architecture's descendants — the PC/XT, PC/AT and PS/2 lines — shaped the standard platform on which DOS and later Windows computing was built. "Personal computer" became synonymous with the IBM standard.
