The Toronto Maple Leafs are a professional ice hockey team based in Toronto, Ontario. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Atlantic Division of the Eastern Conference. Founded in 1917, the club is one of the "Original Six" teams and one of the league's founding franchises.

History

The franchise began as the Toronto Arenas in 1917, became the Toronto St. Patricks in 1919, and was renamed the Maple Leafs in 1927 after being purchased by Conn Smythe. Under Smythe, the Leafs became a dynasty, winning multiple Stanley Cups in the 1940s under coach Hap Day, with stars such as Ted Kennedy and Syl Apps.

The Leafs won their most recent Stanley Cup in 1967, defeating the Montreal Canadiens in the final year of the Original Six era. The subsequent championship drought — among the longest in North American professional sports — has become a defining feature of the franchise's modern identity, despite consistently strong attendance and the team's status as one of the most valuable franchises in hockey.

Home arenas

The team played at Maple Leaf Gardens from 1931 to 1999 before moving to the Air Canada Centre (now Scotiabank Arena), shared with the Toronto Raptors of the NBA.

Rivalries

The Maple Leafs' historic rivalry with the Montreal Canadiens is one of the oldest and most storied in hockey. Regional rivalries with the Ottawa Senators (the "Battle of Ontario") and the Buffalo Sabres are also prominent.
