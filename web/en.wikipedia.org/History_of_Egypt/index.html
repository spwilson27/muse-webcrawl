The history of Egypt stretches back to farming settlements along the Nile by about 6000 BCE and the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt around 3100 BCE under the first pharaohs. Pharaonic civilisation lasted three millennia, organised into the Old, Middle and New Kingdoms separated by intermediate periods.

Pharaonic Egypt

The Old Kingdom built the great pyramids of Giza; the Middle Kingdom reunified the country and expanded into Nubia; the New Kingdom (c. 1550–1070 BCE) created an empire under rulers such as Hatshepsut, Akhenaten, Seti I and Ramesses II. Egypt later fell under Kushite, Assyrian and Persian rule before Alexander the Great's conquest in 332 BCE founded the Ptolemaic dynasty, ending with Cleopatra VII and Roman annexation in 30 BCE.

Islamic and Ottoman periods

Arab armies conquered Egypt in 639–642 CE; Cairo was founded in 969 under the Fatimids, and Al-Azhar University became a centre of Islamic learning. Saladin founded the Ayyubid dynasty, succeeded by the Mamluk sultanate (1250–1517) and then Ottoman rule. Napoleon invaded in 1798; Muhammad Ali's dynasty modernised the country in the 19th century, and the Suez Canal opened in 1869. Britain occupied Egypt from 1882, nominally recognising independence in 1922.

Modern Egypt

The 1952 revolution ended the monarchy; Gamal Abdel Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal in 1956, fought the Six-Day War (1967), and was succeeded by Anwar Sadat, who made peace with Israel in 1979. Hosni Mubarak ruled from 1981 until the 2011 revolution; after electoral and military upheavals, Abdel Fattah el-Sisi became president in 2014.
