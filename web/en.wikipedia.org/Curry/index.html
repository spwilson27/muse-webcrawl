Curry

Curry is a dish based on a sauce seasoned with a mixture of spices, originating in the Indian subcontinent and now found in cuisines worldwide. The word derives from the Tamil kaṟi, meaning a spiced sauce or relish served with rice. Curry dishes typically combine spices such as turmeric, cumin, coriander, ginger, and chili, though recipes vary enormously by region.

History

Spiced stews have been cooked in the Indus Valley region for millennia. The arrival of chili peppers from the Americas via Portuguese traders in the 16th century transformed South Asian cooking. Under British rule, "curry" became a catch-all term, curry powder was commercialized in Britain, and dishes were adapted to imperial tastes; soldiers and administrators carried the concept around the empire.

Regional varieties

In India, regional curries range from Goan vindaloo and Punjabi butter chicken to Bengali fish curries and South Indian sambar. Thai curries use curry pastes of fresh herbs, lemongrass, and coconut milk (red, green, massaman). Japanese curry, introduced via the Royal Navy from Britain, is thick and mild, served as karē raisu. Caribbean curries, including Jamaican curry goat, reflect Indian indentured labourers' influence, while South African bunny chow and Cape Malay curries show further adaptation.

Curry powder and pastes

Curry powder is a British-era commercial blend; cooks in South Asia more often use freshly ground masalas. Thai and Southeast Asian cuisines rely on wet pastes pounded from fresh ingredients.
