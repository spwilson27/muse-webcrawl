William Saroyan

William Saroyan
Born: August 31, 1908, Fresno, California
Died: May 18, 1981, Fresno, California
Occupation: Novelist, playwright, short-story writer

William Saroyan (August 31, 1908 – May 18, 1981) was an Armenian-American novelist, playwright, and short story writer. He gained fame during the Great Depression with exuberant, warmly humane stories celebrating the lives of Armenian immigrants and the poor. He won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama in 1940 for The Time of Your Life — and refused it — and won an Academy Award for the film adaptation of his novel The Human Comedy.

Life

Saroyan was born in Fresno, California, to Armenian immigrants from Bitlis in the Ottoman Empire. His father died when he was three, and he and his siblings spent several years in an orphanage in Oakland — an experience that left a deep mark on his writing. He left school at fifteen and educated himself while working jobs including telegraph messenger.

Career

His breakthrough came in 1934 with the short story "The Daring Young Man on the Flying Trapeze," published in Story magazine, followed by the collection of the same name. The Time of Your Life (1939) set its gentle action in a San Francisco bar. My Name Is Aram (1940) collected stories of an Armenian boyhood in Fresno. The Human Comedy (1943), written first as a film for MGM, tells of a telegraph messenger in wartime California.

Saroyan's later years were marked by financial and personal difficulties, gambling losses, and declining popularity, though he continued publishing memoirs and plays prolifically. He died in Fresno in 1981; half his ashes were buried in Armenia. His last public statement, released shortly before his death, read in part: "Although it is my dying day, I do believe that somewhere it is the morning of a new day."
