Hyperbolic group

Cayley graphs of hyperbolic groups are δ-hyperbolic spaces.

In group theory, a hyperbolic group, also called a word hyperbolic group or Gromov hyperbolic group, is a finitely generated group equipped with a word metric satisfying certain properties abstracted from classical hyperbolic geometry. The notion was introduced by Mikhail Gromov in his influential 1987 essay "Hyperbolic groups", which inaugurated the field of geometric group theory.

Definition

A geodesic metric space is called δ-hyperbolic if every geodesic triangle is δ-slim: each side lies in the δ-neighbourhood of the union of the other two. A finitely generated group is hyperbolic if its Cayley graph (with the word metric from some finite generating set) is δ-hyperbolic for some δ ≥ 0. Hyperbolicity is independent of the chosen generating set, since different Cayley graphs are quasi-isometric.

Examples

Free groups are hyperbolic (their Cayley graphs are trees, which are 0-hyperbolic). Fundamental groups of closed surfaces of genus at least 2 are hyperbolic, as are most fundamental groups of finite-volume hyperbolic manifolds, and groups acting properly and cocompactly on hyperbolic spaces. By contrast, a hyperbolic group cannot contain a copy of Z2; hence abelian groups of rank 2 or more, and groups containing them, are never hyperbolic.

Properties

Hyperbolic groups have solvable word and conjugacy problems: indeed they admit finite Dehn presentations, and Dehn's algorithm solves the word problem in linear time. Their growth is exponential unless the group is virtually cyclic. A "random" finitely presented group is hyperbolic with overwhelming probability in several models of random groups, a phenomenon behind the slogan that hyperbolic groups are generic.
