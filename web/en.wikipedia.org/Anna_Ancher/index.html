Anna Ancher (born Anna Kirstine Brøndum; 18 August 1859 – 15 April 1935) was a Danish artist associated with the Skagen Painters, the community of Scandinavian artists who gathered each summer in the village of Skagen in the far north of Jutland. She is regarded as one of Denmark's greatest visual artists, celebrated especially for her luminous interiors and her masterly rendering of light and colour.

Life

Anna Brøndum was born in Skagen, the daughter of Ane Hedvig Møller and Erik Andersen Brøndum, keeper of the town's general store and the Brøndums Hotel, where the artists' colony gathered. She was the only member of the Skagen Painters actually born in Skagen. She studied drawing for three years at the Vilhelm Kyhn College of Painting in Copenhagen, where she developed her own style. In 1880 she married the painter Michael Ancher, whom she had met in Skagen; their daughter Helga Ancher was born in 1883.

Although the culture of the time discouraged married women from pursuing careers, Anna Ancher continued painting with her husband's encouragement. She travelled to Vienna in 1882 and to Paris in 1889, where she studied under Marie Triepcke and exhibited at the Exposition Universelle.

Work

Ancher's art is noted for its interplay of light and colour, often depicting sunlit interiors in which rays stream through windows, and scenes from the lives of Skagen's fisherfolk, women and children. Well-known works include The Girl in the Kitchen (c. 1886), Sunlight in the Blue Room (1891), A Funeral (1891) and Evening Prayer (1888). She exhibited widely, received the Ingenio et Arti medal in 1913 and the Tagea Brandt travel grant in 1924.

Legacy

The Anchers' home in Skagen, purchased in 1884, is now the Michael and Anna Ancher House museum (Anchers Hus). Anna and Michael Ancher were depicted on the Danish 1,000-kroner banknote issued in 1998. A major retrospective of her work was organised in the 2020s, affirming her reputation as a central figure in Danish painting.
