Photo opportunity

A photo opportunity (often abbreviated photo op or photo-op) is an arranged opportunity for journalists to photograph a politician, a celebrity, or for the media to record an event, usually arranged by a public relations practitioner, with the purpose of generating favourable media coverage. The term gained currency in the administration of United States President Richard Nixon, whose press secretary, Ron Ziegler, is credited with coining it.

Among political spin terms it is perhaps the most familiar, though the phrase has spread to non-political contexts as well, such as celebrity appearances and promotional events.

Usage

Photo opportunities are typically carefully staged events, designed to project a particular image of the subject. Examples include politicians visiting factories while wearing protective gear, signing ceremonies for legislation, or leaders photographed at summits. Participants are generally not expected to take probing questions at such events, distinguishing a photo op from a press conference.

A prominent example is the 2003 photograph of President George W. Bush in front of a "Mission Accomplished" banner aboard the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln, which was widely distributed but later became controversial as the Iraq War continued.
