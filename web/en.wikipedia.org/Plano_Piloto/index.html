Plano Piloto

Plano Piloto (Pilot Plan) is the central area of Brasília, the capital of Brazil, corresponding roughly to the Federal District's Administrative Region I. The name comes from Lúcio Costa's winning 1957 urban plan for the new capital, whose layout — two crossing axes resembling an aeroplane or bird — organised the city around a monumental axis and a residential axis.

Layout

Along the Monumental Axis stand the ministries, the National Congress, the Cathedral of Brasília and other works by architect Oscar Niemeyer. The residential axis is lined with superquadras — superblocks of apartment buildings on pilotis surrounded by greenery — embodying modernist planning principles.

Heritage

The Plano Piloto was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987, recognised as a landmark of modernist urban planning and architecture. It remains the administrative and symbolic heart of Brazil.
