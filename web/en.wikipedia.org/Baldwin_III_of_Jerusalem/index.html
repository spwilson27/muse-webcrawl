Baldwin III (1130 – 10 February 1163) was King of Jerusalem from 1143 to 1163. He was the eldest son of Queen Melisende and King Fulk of Jerusalem. He became king while still a minor and at first reigned under his mother's regency; a struggle for power between mother and son ended in 1152 with Baldwin ruling alone.

Early life and co-rule

Baldwin was thirteen when his father died in a hunting accident in 1143. He was crowned co-ruler with his mother on Christmas Day 1143. The early years of the reign saw the fall of Edessa in 1144 to Zengi, atabeg of Mosul, an event that prompted the Second Crusade. When the crusader armies of Louis VII of France and Conrad III of Germany arrived in 1148, the ensuing campaign against Damascus ended in failure.

Struggle with Melisende

As Baldwin came of age, tensions with Melisende grew into open conflict in 1152. After a brief civil war, the kingdom was partitioned, but Baldwin quickly reunited it, and Melisende retired to Nablus, remaining an influential figure until her death in 1161. The historian William of Tyre, who knew Baldwin personally, praised the young king's intelligence, piety, and physical courage.

Reign as sole ruler

Baldwin's greatest military success was the capture of Ascalon in August 1153, after a siege of several months, which removed the last Fatimid stronghold on the Palestinian coast and secured the kingdom's southern frontier. He intervened repeatedly in the affairs of the northern crusader states, the County of Tripoli and the Principality of Antioch, and dealt with the growing power of Nur al-Din in Syria.

In 1158 Baldwin married Theodora Komnene, niece of the Byzantine emperor Manuel I Komnenos, cementing an alliance with Byzantium. He died childless at Beirut on 10 February 1163, reportedly of dysentery, and was succeeded by his brother Amalric I.
