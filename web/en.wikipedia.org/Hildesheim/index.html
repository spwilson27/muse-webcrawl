Hildesheim (German pronunciation: [ˈhɪldəsˌhaɪm]; Low German: Hilmessen) is a city in Lower Saxony, in north-central Germany, with a population of approximately 102,000. It is the district capital of the District of Hildesheim, located about 30 km (19 mi) southeast of Hanover on the Innerste River. Founded in 815 as the seat of a bishopric established by Louis the Pious, Hildesheim grew into an important religious and trading centre and was a member of the Hanseatic League.

Sights and history

The city is famous for its Romanesque churches: St Mary's Cathedral and St Michael's Church, both UNESCO World Heritage Sites since 1985, preserve outstanding Ottonian art including the Bernward Doors and the bronze Christ Column (c. 1020), and the cathedral's legendary thousand-year-old rosebush. The historic market square, destroyed in an air raid on 22 March 1945 that devastated much of the old town, was rebuilt in the 1980s in its original form, including the Knochenhaueramtshaus (1589), often called one of the most beautiful half-timbered houses in Germany. The Roemer- und Pelizaeus-Museum holds one of Europe's finest ancient Egyptian collections.
