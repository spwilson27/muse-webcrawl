U-Bahn

The blue "U" sign marks U-Bahn stations across Germany.

U-Bahn (German: [ˈuːbaːn]; short for Untergrundbahn, "underground railway") is the common name for rapid transit metro systems in German-speaking countries, including Germany and Austria. In Germany, four cities operate full U-Bahn networks: Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, and Nuremberg, while many other cities have Stadtbahn (light rail) systems that share some characteristics and are partly branded as U-Bahn. Vienna's U-Bahn is Austria's principal metro.

Systems

The Berlin U-Bahn, opened in 1902, is the largest network in Germany and one of the oldest in the world. Hamburg's system also dates to 1912, while Munich and Nuremberg opened theirs in 1971 and 1972, the latter being Germany's only fully automated driverless rubber-tyred metro. Vienna's U-Bahn opened in 1978, evolving partly from earlier Stadtbahn lines.

Characteristics

German U-Bahn systems are distinguished from the S-Bahn (suburban rail) in that they are operated by municipal transit companies rather than the national railway, use dedicated rights-of-way at frequent intervals, and are typically integrated into regional fare associations (Verkehrsverbünde).
