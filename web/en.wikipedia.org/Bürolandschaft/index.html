Bürolandschaft

Bürolandschaft (German for "office landscape") is an early form of open-plan office design developed in Germany in the late 1950s by the Quickborner Team, a management consultancy founded by the brothers Wolfgang and Eberhard Schnelle. It replaced rigid rows of desks with organically arranged workstations intended to encourage communication and reflect the flow of work.

Design principles

Rather than cellular offices or grid layouts, Bürolandschaft used irregular groupings of desks separated by plants, low screens and storage units. Seating arrangements were derived from studies of paperwork flow and interpersonal communication, and all staff — including managers — shared the same open space.

Influence

The concept spread through northern Europe and influenced office planning in Britain and North America during the 1960s and 1970s. Although the fully landscaped office declined amid complaints about noise and lack of privacy, it is regarded as the origin of the modern open-plan office.
