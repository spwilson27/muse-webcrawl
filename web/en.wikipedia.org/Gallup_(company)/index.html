Gallup, Inc. is an American analytics and advisory company based in Washington, D.C., founded by George Gallup in 1935. The company became famous for its public opinion polls conducted in the United States and, through the Gallup World Poll, in more than 140 countries. Since the late 1980s, under the Clifton family, Gallup has increasingly emphasised management consulting, employee engagement research and strengths-based development alongside polling.

History

George Gallup, a journalism professor and advertising researcher, founded the American Institute of Public Opinion in Princeton in 1935. His scientific sampling methods won fame when the Gallup Poll correctly called the 1936 US presidential election for Franklin D. Roosevelt, while the much larger Literary Digest straw poll wrongly predicted Alf Landon. A notable failure came in 1948, when Gallup and rival pollsters predicted Thomas Dewey over Harry Truman, leading to revisions in quota sampling.

In 1958 the firm became the Gallup Organization. Donald O. Clifton's company Selection Research Inc. acquired Gallup in 1988; Clifton's son Jim Clifton became chairman and CEO, shifting the business toward workplace consulting. Gallup's employee engagement Q12 survey and the CliftonStrengths (formerly StrengthsFinder) assessment became major products, popularised in books such as Now, Discover Your Strengths (2001) and StrengthsFinder 2.0 (2007).

Polling and research

Gallup's US polling includes presidential job approval — a series maintained since the Truman administration — and daily tracking of wellbeing and economic confidence. The Gallup World Poll, launched in 2005, surveys residents of countries representing over 99% of the world's adult population on law and order, food and shelter, institutions, jobs and life evaluations; its data feed the World Happiness Report's Cantril ladder rankings. After errors in the 2012 US election polling, Gallup reviewed its methods and in 2015 announced it would no longer conduct horse-race polling for presidential primaries and general elections.
