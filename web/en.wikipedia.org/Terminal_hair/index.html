Terminal hair

Terminal hair is the thick, long, and pigmented hair that grows on the scalp, and, after puberty, on the face, chest, armpits, and pubic region. It develops from fine, short, lightly pigmented vellus hair under the influence of androgens, which enlarge the hair follicle and lengthen the growth (anagen) phase.

Development

Humans are born with follicles that produce vellus hair over most of the body. At puberty, rising androgen levels convert vellus follicles in androgen-sensitive areas into terminal follicles. Paradoxically, in genetically predisposed individuals, dihydrotestosterone miniaturizes scalp follicles in androgenetic alopecia, reversing the process so terminal hairs revert to vellus-like hairs.

Characteristics

Terminal hairs have a larger diameter, a medulla, deeper roots extending into the dermis or subcutaneous fat, and associated sebaceous glands. Their density and distribution differ by sex, ancestry, and hormonal status; conditions such as hirsutism and hypertrichosis involve abnormal terminal hair growth.
