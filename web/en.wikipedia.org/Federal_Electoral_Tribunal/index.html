Federal Electoral Tribunal

Electoral Tribunal of the Federal Judiciary
Spanish: Tribunal Electoral del Poder Judicial de la Federación (TEPJF)
Established: 1996
Location: Mexico City, Mexico
Composition: Superior Chamber of 7 magistrates; 5 regional chambers
The Electoral Tribunal is the highest Mexican court specialising in electoral disputes.

The Federal Electoral Tribunal, formally the Electoral Tribunal of the Federal Judiciary (Spanish: Tribunal Electoral del Poder Judicial de la Federación, TEPJF), is the specialised court of Mexico's federal judiciary responsible for resolving disputes arising from federal elections and for safeguarding the political-electoral rights of citizens. Its rulings on federal electoral matters are final and unappealable, including the validation of presidential elections.

History

The tribunal originated in electoral reforms of the late twentieth century as Mexico moved from a dominant-party system — in which Congress effectively certified its own elections — toward independent electoral adjudication. A Federal Electoral Tribunal was created in 1990 as an autonomous body; the constitutional reform of 1996 incorporated it into the federal judiciary as its specialised electoral chamber, strengthening its independence and making it the final authority in electoral litigation.

Structure and jurisdiction

The tribunal consists of a Superior Chamber (Sala Superior) of seven magistrates elected by the Senate from shortlists proposed by the Supreme Court, and five regional chambers, each with three magistrates, seated in the country's electoral districts. It hears challenges to the acts of the National Electoral Institute (INE), disputes over election results, cases concerning the registration and financing of political parties, and actions for the protection of citizens' political-electoral rights, such as the rights to vote, to be elected, and to join a party.

Notable cases

The tribunal's decisions have been pivotal in Mexico's democratic development. It validated the historic 2000 presidential election that ended seventy years of PRI rule, and after the closely contested 2006 presidential election it ordered a partial recount of ballot boxes before declaring Felipe Calderón the winner, rejecting claims that irregularities warranted annulment. It has also ruled on campaign finance violations, broadcast advertising rules, and the electoral rights of independent candidates and indigenous communities.
