Mountaineering Scotland

Mountaineering Scotland is the national representative body for hill walkers, climbers, mountaineers and ski tourers in Scotland. Founded in 1970 as the Mountaineering Council of Scotland and rebranded in 2016, it represents tens of thousands of individual members and affiliated clubs, campaigns for access and conservation in the Scottish hills, and is recognised by sportscotland as the governing body for mountaineering in Scotland.

Work

The organisation played a central role in securing the statutory right of responsible access embodied in the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 and the Scottish Outdoor Access Code. Its practical work includes mountain safety promotion — notably the Avalanche Information Service partnership and weather-information services — skills training, support for climbing competitions and facilities, and conservation campaigns over issues such as windfarm development in wild land and path erosion. It publishes the magazine The Scottish Mountaineer and maintains guidance on Munro-bagging culture, bothies and winter skills.

History

The Council was formed in 1970 from the Association of Scottish Climbing Clubs, in response to the growth of hill recreation after the war. It is a member of the International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation (UIAA) and works alongside the British Mountaineering Council, which covers England and Wales.
