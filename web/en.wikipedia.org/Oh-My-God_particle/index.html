Oh-My-God particle

The Oh-My-God particle was an ultra-high-energy cosmic ray detected on 15 October 1991 by the Fly's Eye cosmic ray detector at Dugway Proving Ground, Utah, United States. Its observed energy was approximately 3.2 × 1020 electron volts (about 51 joules), making it the highest-energy cosmic ray observed at the time and roughly 40 million times more energetic than the highest-energy particles produced in terrestrial particle accelerators of the era.

Significance

The particle's energy far exceeded the Greisen–Zatsepin–Kuzmin (GZK) limit, the theoretical threshold above which cosmic rays from distant sources should lose energy through interactions with the cosmic microwave background. Its existence posed a puzzle about its origin: few known astrophysical accelerators could impart such energy, and its arrival direction did not coincide with an obvious nearby source.

Comparisons

A single subatomic particle carrying ~51 joules has kinetic energy comparable to a baseball travelling at about 100 km/h. Later detectors, including the Pierre Auger Observatory and the Telescope Array, have observed other ultra-high-energy events; in 2023 the Telescope Array reported the "Amaterasu particle" with an estimated energy of about 2.4 × 1020 eV, among the most energetic ever recorded.
