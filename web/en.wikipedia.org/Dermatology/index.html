Dermatology

Dermatology is the branch of medicine dealing with the skin. It is a speciality with both medical and surgical aspects. A dermatologist is a specialist medical doctor who manages diseases related to skin, hair, nails, and some cosmetic problems.

Scope

Dermatologists diagnose and treat more than 3,000 conditions, including skin cancers such as melanoma and basal-cell carcinoma, inflammatory diseases such as eczema and psoriasis, infections, acne, and disorders of the hair and nails. Subspecialties include dermatopathology, Mohs surgery, pediatric dermatology and cosmetic dermatology.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis relies on visual inspection, aided by dermoscopy, and on skin biopsy for histopathological examination. Patch testing is used to identify contact allergens, and Wood's lamp examination can assist in detecting certain infections and pigment disorders.

History

Descriptions of skin diseases date to ancient Egyptian and Greek medicine. The field became a distinct specialty in the 19th century with the work of figures such as Ferdinand von Hebra in Vienna and the development of systematic classification of skin diseases by their appearance.
