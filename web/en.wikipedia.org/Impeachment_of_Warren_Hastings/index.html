Impeachment of Warren Hastings

The impeachment of Warren Hastings, the first Governor-General of India, was one of the most famous political trials in British history. It began in 1788 in Westminster Hall and lasted until 1795, when Hastings was acquitted on all charges. The prosecution was led by Edmund Burke and was driven by allegations of corruption, cruelty, and misgovernment during Hastings's administration of British India from 1773 to 1785.

Background

Warren Hastings had served in India since 1750 and became Governor of Bengal in 1772 and Governor-General under the Regulating Act of 1773. His tenure coincided with wars against Mysore and the Marathas, administrative reform, and bitter feuds within his council, notably with Philip Francis. In Britain, growing scrutiny of the East India Company's affairs — through the select committees and Pitt's India Act of 1784 — set the stage for charges against him.

The charges

Burke and his allies, including Charles James Fox and Richard Brinsley Sheridan, charged Hastings with high crimes and misdemeanors: accepting bribes and presents, the harsh treatment of the Rohillas in 1774, the execution of the revenue official Nand Kumar, the extortionate settlement imposed on Chait Singh, Raja of Benares, and the confiscation of the treasures of the Begums of Oudh.

The trial

The trial opened with great ceremony on 13 February 1788, with Burke delivering a four-day speech. Proceedings dragged on for seven years, sitting only intermittently, as public attention faded with the onset of the French Revolutionary Wars. Hastings defended himself ably and at length. On 23 April 1795 the House of Lords acquitted him on all charges by wide margins. The East India Company later granted him a pension and compensation for his legal expenses.

Significance

Although it failed to convict, the trial established norms of accountability for imperial officials and gave Burke a platform for his argument that all power, including colonial power, is a trust to be exercised for the benefit of the governed. It remains a landmark in the history of impeachment and of British India.
