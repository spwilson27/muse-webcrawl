The Rajya Sabha (Hindi: राज्य सभा, "Council of States") is the upper house of the bicameral Parliament of India. It has a maximum membership of 250: 238 members representing the states and union territories, and up to 12 members nominated by the President of India for contributions to art, literature, science, and social services. The current strength is 245 members. As the council of states, it represents the federating units of the Union in the national legislature.

Composition and elections

Members are elected by the elected members of state legislative assemblies through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote; the allocation of seats among states is based roughly on population, with Uttar Pradesh returning the most members (31). The Rajya Sabha is a permanent body not subject to dissolution: members serve six-year terms, with one-third retiring every two years — a design intended to provide continuity and deliberation insulated from immediate electoral swings. The Vice President of India is its ex officio Chairman.

Powers

The Rajya Sabha shares legislative power with the Lok Sabha (the lower house), except in financial matters: money bills can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha may only recommend amendments to them. Constitutional amendments require the special majority of both houses. The Rajya Sabha has special powers to authorize Parliament to legislate on state subjects in the national interest (Article 249) and to create new All-India Services (Article 312). It can also initiate the removal of the Vice President.

History

The Rajya Sabha first sat in 1952 under the Constitution of India, succeeding the Council of State of the colonial legislature in function. Its first Chairman was Dr. S. Radhakrishnan. Debates in the house have played a role in major legislation, and its nominated seats have been occupied by figures such as Rukmini Devi Arundale, Sachin Tendulkar, and Lata Mangeshkar.
