HO scale

Ratio1:87

HO or H0 scale is a scale for model railways in which 3.5 millimetres represents one foot, a ratio of 1:87. The name derives from "Half O", being roughly half the size of O scale. Standard-gauge track is modelled at a gauge of 16.5 mm. HO is the most popular scale worldwide for model trains, dominant in North America, continental Europe and Australia, though in Britain the near-identical 00 gauge (4 mm scale on the same 16.5 mm track) prevails.

History

HO emerged in the 1920s–30s as manufacturers sought models smaller and cheaper than O gauge; in Germany, Märklin and Trix promoted "Halb Null" (half zero) systems, and after the Second World War ready-to-run HO sets spread widely in the United States through makers such as Athearn, Bachmann and Atlas. Improvements in injection moulding, and later Digital Command Control (DCC), brought high levels of detail and realistic operation.

Standards and variants

Standards are maintained by the NMRA in North America and by MOROP (NEM standards) in Europe. Narrow-gauge prototypes are represented by variants such as HOn3 (3 ft gauge), HOn30/HOe (2 ft and 750 mm gauges) and HOm (metre gauge). Because of its popularity, HO offers the widest range of locomotives, rolling stock, buildings and scenic materials of any scale.
