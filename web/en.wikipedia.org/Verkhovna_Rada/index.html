Verkhovna Rada

The Verkhovna Rada of Ukraine (Ukrainian: Верховна Рада України, lit. "Supreme Council of Ukraine") is the unicameral parliament of Ukraine. Under the Constitution of Ukraine it is the sole body of legislative power in the country. It consists of 450 People's Deputies elected for five-year terms.

History

The name derives from the Ukrainian word rada ("council"), recalling the Cossack councils and the Central Rada of 1917–1918. The Supreme Soviet of the Ukrainian SSR became the Verkhovna Rada of independent Ukraine in 1991. The constitution adopted in 1996 defines its powers; amendments adopted in 2004 (effective 2006, later contested and reinstated) shifted Ukraine towards a parliamentary-presidential system in which the Rada's majority forms the government through a coalition.

Powers and elections

The Rada adopts laws and the state budget, approves the Prime Minister and cabinet programme, appoints and dismisses key officials, ratifies treaties, and can impeach the President. Since 2012 elections have used a mixed system: half the deputies elected by proportional representation on closed party lists with a 5% threshold, and half in single-member constituencies — although electoral reform toward open-list proportional representation was legislated in 2019. The parliament sits in a domed neoclassical building on Hrushevsky Street in Kyiv, completed in 1939.
