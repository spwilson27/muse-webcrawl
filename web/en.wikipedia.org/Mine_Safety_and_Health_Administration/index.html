Mine Safety and Health Administration

AcronymMSHA
Formed1978
Parent agencyU.S. Department of Labor
HeadquartersArlington, Virginia

The Mine Safety and Health Administration (MSHA) is a large agency of the United States Department of Labor that administers the provisions of the Federal Mine Safety and Health Act of 1977 (Mine Act) to enforce compliance with mandatory safety and health standards as a means to eliminate fatal accidents, reduce the frequency and severity of nonfatal accidents, minimise health hazards, and promote improved safety and health conditions in the nation's mines.

History

Federal mine oversight began with the creation of the U.S. Bureau of Mines in 1910, initially with only advisory powers. Disasters such as the Farmington Mine disaster of 1968 prompted the Federal Coal Mine Health and Safety Act of 1969, and the 1977 Mine Act extended protections to all mining, transferred enforcement to the Department of Labor, and created MSHA, which began operating in 1978. The MINER Act of 2006, passed after the Sago Mine disaster, strengthened emergency-response, communication and rescue requirements.

Operations

MSHA inspectors are required to inspect each underground mine at least four times a year and each surface mine at least twice, and can issue citations, closure orders and civil penalties. The agency also runs the National Mine Health and Safety Academy in West Virginia, investigates accidents, and sets exposure limits for hazards such as coal dust — the cause of black lung disease — and respirable silica.
