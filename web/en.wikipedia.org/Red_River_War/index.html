Red River War

The Red River War was a military campaign launched by the United States Army in 1874 to remove the Comanche, Kiowa, Southern Cheyenne and Arapaho Native American tribes from the Southern Plains and forcibly relocate them to reservations in Indian Territory (present-day Oklahoma).

Five converging army columns, including forces under Nelson A. Miles and Ranald S. Mackenzie, campaigned through the Texas Panhandle. Key engagements included the Battle of Palo Duro Canyon on 28 September 1874, where Mackenzie's troops destroyed the tribes' horse herd and winter supplies. Deprived of food and mounts, most groups surrendered by June 1875, when Quanah Parker led the last band of Comanches into Fort Sill.

The war effectively ended free Plains Indian resistance in Texas. See also Quanah Parker and American Indian Wars.
