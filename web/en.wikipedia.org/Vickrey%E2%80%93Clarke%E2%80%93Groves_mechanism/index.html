Vickrey–Clarke–Groves mechanism

The Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) mechanism is a family of incentive-compatible mechanisms for allocating goods or choosing outcomes when participants have private valuations. It generalizes the Vickrey (second-price sealed-bid) auction. In a VCG mechanism, each participant reports a valuation; the mechanism selects the outcome maximizing the total reported welfare, and charges each participant the externality their presence imposes on the others — the difference between the welfare others would have achieved without them and the welfare others actually achieve with them.

Properties

Truthful reporting is a dominant strategy in a VCG mechanism, and the chosen outcome is socially efficient, assuming quasi-linear preferences. The mechanism is named for William Vickrey, Edward H. Clarke and Theodore Groves, who developed its components in the 1960s and 1970s. VCG mechanisms are used in spectrum allocation analysis, online advertising auctions (in modified form), and multi-agent systems, though they can suffer from budget imbalance, vulnerability to collusion, and computational difficulty in finding the welfare-maximizing outcome.
