Regicide

Regicide is the deliberate killing of a monarch, or the person who carries out such an act. Regicides have punctuated political history, from the assassinations of Roman emperors to the judicial execution of Charles I of England in 1649.

Notable examples

The trial and execution of Charles I by the English Parliament was the first time a king was publicly tried and executed by his own subjects, and those who signed his death warrant were themselves known as "the regicides". Louis XVI of France was executed in 1793 during the French Revolution. Other famous victims include Julius Caesar (as dictator rather than king), Tsar Nicholas II in 1918, and numerous Byzantine and Ottoman rulers.

Legal and cultural treatment

Regicide was traditionally treated as both murder and treason, punished with exceptional severity. In literature and political thought, debate over tyrannicide — whether killing a tyrannical ruler is justified — has a long history stretching from classical antiquity through early modern resistance theory.
