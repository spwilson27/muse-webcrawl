Soviet atomic bomb project

The Soviet atomic bomb project was the research and development programme authorised by Joseph Stalin in the Soviet Union to develop nuclear weapons during and after World War II. The programme culminated in the first Soviet nuclear test, RDS-1, on 29 August 1949 at the Semipalatinsk Test Site in Kazakhstan, making the Soviet Union the second nation to detonate a nuclear device.

Origins

Soviet physicists, including Igor Kurchatov, Yulii Khariton, Georgy Flyorov and Pyotr Kapitsa, had conducted significant nuclear research in the 1930s. In 1940 Flyorov and Konstantin Petrzhak discovered spontaneous fission. After the German invasion in 1941 most research was suspended, but reports of Allied nuclear work — reinforced by intelligence — led Stalin to authorise a modest programme in 1942–43 under Kurchatov's scientific direction.

Acceleration after 1945

Following the American atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945, the project was given unlimited priority and placed under the political administration of Lavrentiy Beria. Intelligence from the American Manhattan Project, supplied by spies including Klaus Fuchs, provided the design of the American plutonium implosion weapon, which the Soviet leadership ordered replicated for the first device. Enormous industrial complexes were built in closed cities such as Arzamas-16 (Sarov) and Chelyabinsk-40, using in part Gulag labour, to produce plutonium and enriched uranium.

RDS-1 and aftermath

RDS-1 (known in the West as "Joe-1") yielded about 22 kilotons and was closely modelled on the American Fat Man design. Its detonation ended the American nuclear monopoly and accelerated the arms race of the Cold War. The Soviet Union went on to test RDS-2, the first Soviet production bomb, in 1951, an indigenous boosted and thermonuclear designs followed, including the RDS-6s "sloika" device in 1953 and the two-stage RDS-37 in 1955.
