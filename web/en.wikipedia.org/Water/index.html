Water is essential to all known forms of life.

Water is an inorganic compound with the chemical formula H2O. It is a transparent, tasteless, odourless liquid, the main constituent of Earth's hydrosphere and the fluids of all known living organisms, in which it acts as a solvent. It is vital for all known forms of life, despite not providing food energy or organic micronutrients. Water covers about 71% of Earth's surface, mostly in oceans and other large bodies, with about 1.7% in groundwater, 1.7% in glaciers and ice caps, and a small fraction in vapour, clouds and precipitation.

Properties

A water molecule consists of two hydrogen atoms covalently bonded to one oxygen atom in a bent geometry, making it polar. Hydrogen bonding between molecules gives water unusually high melting and boiling points for its molar mass, a high specific heat capacity and heat of vaporisation, and high surface tension. Unusually, water expands on freezing: ice is less dense than liquid water, so it floats — a property of great ecological importance. Water reaches its maximum density at about 3.98 °C. It is amphoteric and a near-universal solvent for polar and ionic substances, though it does not dissolve non-polar substances such as oils.

Cycle and distribution

Water continually moves through the water cycle of evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation and runoff. The total volume on Earth is about 1.386 billion cubic kilometres; about 97.5% is saline ocean water and only about 2.5% is fresh water, most of that locked in ice and groundwater, leaving a small fraction in accessible lakes, rivers and soil moisture.

Life and human use

All known life requires liquid water; the human body is roughly 60% water by mass. Water is indispensable for drinking, sanitation, agriculture (the largest use, about 70% of withdrawals), industry and ecosystems. Access to safe drinking water and sanitation is recognised by the United Nations as a human right, yet billions of people lack safely managed services. Water scarcity, pollution and the effects of climate change on the hydrological cycle are major global concerns.
