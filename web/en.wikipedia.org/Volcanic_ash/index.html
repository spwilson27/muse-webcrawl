Volcanic ash

Volcanic ash consists of fragments of rock, mineral crystals and volcanic glass, less than 2 mm in diameter, created during explosive volcanic eruptions. Ash is formed when magma shatters as dissolved gases expand, and when the walls of the vent are pulverized. Unlike the soft ash of burned material, volcanic ash is hard, abrasive and does not dissolve in water.

Hazards

Ashfall can collapse roofs under its weight, damage crops and machinery, contaminate water supplies and cause respiratory problems. Airborne ash clouds are a severe hazard to aviation: ash melted inside jet engines can re-solidify as glass and cause engine failure, as happened to British Airways Flight 9 over Indonesia in 1982, and the 2010 eruption of Eyjafjallajökull in Iceland closed much of European airspace for days. Deposits of ash (tephra) are used by geologists to date and correlate rock layers, and weathered ash can produce fertile soils.
