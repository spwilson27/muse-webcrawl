LocationSeville, Andalusia, Spain
StyleMudéjar, Gothic, Renaissance

The Royal Alcázar of Seville (Spanish: Real Alcázar de Sevilla) is a royal palace in Seville, Spain, originally developed by Moorish Muslim rulers and widely regarded as one of the finest examples of Mudéjar architecture. It is the oldest royal palace still in use in Europe; the Spanish royal family occupies its upper storeys when in Seville.

History

The palace stands on the site of a tenth-century Muslim fortress. Under the Almohads and later the Christian kings of Castile it was repeatedly rebuilt. Pedro I of Castile, in the fourteenth century, employed Muslim craftsmen to create the palace's most celebrated sections, including the Patio de las Doncellas and the Patio de las Muñecas.

Architecture and gardens

The complex blends Islamic tilework, carved stucco, and horseshoe arches with Gothic and Renaissance additions. Its extensive gardens combine mazes, fountains, and pavilions, including the Mercury Pond. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987, together with Seville Cathedral and the Archivo de Indias.
