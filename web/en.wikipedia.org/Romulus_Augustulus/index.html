Romulus Augustus (c. 460s – after 476, possibly alive until 507), nicknamed Romulus Augustulus ("little Augustus"), was a Roman emperor who reigned in the West from 31 October 475 until 4 September 476. He is traditionally regarded as the last Western Roman emperor, and his deposition by the Germanic leader Odoacer is conventionally used to mark the end of the Western Roman Empire and the beginning of the Middle Ages.

Reign

Romulus was the son of Orestes, a Roman official who had once served as a secretary to Attila the Hun. After deposing the emperor Julius Nepos in 475, Orestes placed his young son on the throne. The boy was a figurehead; real power lay with his father. His name combined Romulus, the legendary founder of Rome, with Augustus, its first emperor — a coincidence often remarked on by historians.

Deposition

When Orestes refused to grant land in Italy to the Germanic foederati, their leader Odoacer revolted, defeated and killed Orestes at Pavia in August 476, and on 4 September 476 forced Romulus to abdicate at Ravenna. Odoacer sent the imperial insignia to the Eastern emperor Zeno in Constantinople, and the Western imperial office was abolished. Remarkably, Romulus's life was spared — reportedly because of his youth and beauty — and he was granted an annual pension of 6,000 solidi and retired to the Castel dell'Ovo in Naples, where he may have lived for decades.
