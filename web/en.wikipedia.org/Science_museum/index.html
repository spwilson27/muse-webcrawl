Science museum

A science museum is a museum devoted primarily to science and the history of science and technology. Early science museums tended to concentrate on static displays of objects related to natural history, paleontology, industry, and medicine. The trend in modern science museums is toward interactive, hands-on exhibits that let visitors experiment with scientific principles, a model pioneered by institutions such as the Exploratorium in San Francisco and the Ontario Science Centre, both opened in 1969.

History

The origins of science museums lie in Renaissance cabinets of curiosities and in collections of scientific instruments held by universities and academies. The Museum of the History of Science in Oxford, the Science Museum in London (developed from the South Kensington collections in the 19th century), and the Deutsches Museum in Munich (founded 1903) established the model of the large national museum of science and technology.

Science centers

From the late 1960s, a new kind of institution — the science center — emphasized interactive exhibits over collections. Frank Oppenheimer's Exploratorium (1969) in San Francisco and the Ontario Science Centre in Toronto were influential pioneers; hundreds of science centers now operate worldwide, many coordinated through associations such as ASTC and Ecsite. Planetariums and natural history museums often overlap with science museums in scope.

Notable science museums

Major science museums include the Science Museum, London; the Deutsches Museum, Munich; the Cité des Sciences et de l'Industrie, Paris; the National Air and Space Museum and the Smithsonian Institution museums in Washington, D.C.; the Museum of Science and Industry, Chicago; and the China Science and Technology Museum in Beijing.
