Ileostomy

An ileostomy is a surgical opening (stoma) created by bringing the end or a loop of the small intestine, the ileum, out onto the surface of the abdomen. Intestinal waste passes out of the ileostomy and is collected in an external ostomy appliance (pouch) adhered to the skin.

Indications

Ileostomies are performed when the colon is diseased or has been removed, for example in ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease, familial adenomatous polyposis, bowel obstruction, or colorectal cancer. They may be temporary — protecting a downstream anastomosis while it heals — or permanent.

Types

An end ileostomy follows removal or disconnection of the colon; a loop ileostomy brings a loop of ileum to the surface with two openings and is usually temporary and reversible.

Care

Output from an ileostomy is liquid and continuous, so patients manage hydration and electrolytes carefully and learn pouch changing and peristomal skin care, often with support from stoma care nurses and ostomy associations.
