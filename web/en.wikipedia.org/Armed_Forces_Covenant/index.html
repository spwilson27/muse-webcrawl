Armed Forces Covenant

The Armed Forces Covenant is a promise by the United Kingdom that those who serve in the British Armed Forces, whether Regular or Reserve, those who have served in the past, and their families, should face no disadvantage compared to other citizens in the provision of public and commercial services. Special consideration is appropriate in some cases, especially for those who have given most, such as the injured and bereaved.

History

The concept of a military covenant in Britain entered public debate in the early 2000s, amid concern about the treatment of service personnel deployed in Iraq and Afghanistan. The phrase "Military Covenant" was used by the British Army's doctrine publication. In 2011 the government published The Armed Forces Covenant document, and the principles of the Covenant were written into law through the Armed Forces Act 2011, which requires the Secretary of State to report annually to Parliament on the effects of membership of the armed forces community on healthcare, education and housing.

Principles

  • Members of the armed forces community should not face disadvantage compared to other citizens in the provision of public and commercial services.
  • Special treatment may be appropriate in some cases, especially for the injured or bereaved.

Community Covenant

Local authorities across the United Kingdom have signed Community Covenant pledges committing them to support the armed forces community in their areas. Many businesses and charities have also signed the Covenant, undertaking commitments such as guaranteeing job interviews to veterans or offering discounts.

Criticism

Campaigners, including the Royal British Legion, have periodically argued that delivery of the Covenant falls short in areas such as housing, compensation and access to services, and have pressed for stronger legal duties. The Armed Forces Act 2021 introduced a duty on specified public bodies to have due regard to the principles of the Covenant in certain functions.
