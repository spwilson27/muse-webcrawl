Wang Anshi (December 8, 1021 – May 21, 1086) was a Chinese economist, statesman, chancellor, and poet of the Song dynasty who attempted major and controversial socioeconomic reforms known as the New Policies. His reforms are considered by some historians to be an early experiment in state intervention in the economy, and they split the Song court into reformist and conservative factions for decades.

Rise to power

Born in Linchuan (in present-day Jiangxi), Wang passed the imperial examinations in 1042 and served in a series of provincial posts, gaining a reputation for competent local administration and principled refusal of prestigious capital appointments. His 1058 "Memorial of Ten Thousand Words" to Emperor Renzong outlined a comprehensive program of reform that was ignored at the time.

The New Policies

Summoned by Emperor Shenzong in 1069 and made chancellor the following year, Wang enacted the New Policies: the Green Sprouts loan program providing state agricultural credit, a revamped baojia militia system, state trading and price-stabilization measures, land survey and tax equalization, commutation of corvée labor into a cash tax, and reforms of the examination system and schools. The measures aimed to strengthen state finances and the military against the Liao and Western Xia.

Opposition and legacy

The reforms met fierce resistance from conservative officials including Sima Guang and Su Shi, who criticized their implementation and effects on the peasantry. Wang resigned in 1076 and retired to Jiangning, where he devoted himself to scholarship and poetry; he is counted among the Eight Great Prose Masters of the Tang and Song. After Shenzong's death in 1085 the New Policies were largely repealed. Wang's legacy has been repeatedly re-evaluated, praised in the twentieth century by admirers of state-led reform and blamed by traditional historians for the factional strife that weakened the dynasty.
