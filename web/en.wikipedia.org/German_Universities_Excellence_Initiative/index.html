The German Universities Excellence Initiative (Exzellenzinitiative) is a funding programme of the German federal and state governments designed to strengthen top-level university research and raise the international visibility of German higher education. It introduced competitive, project-based funding on a large scale into a university system traditionally funded on egalitarian lines.

Funding lines

The initiative funded three categories: graduate schools to train early-career researchers; clusters of excellence for large interdisciplinary research collaborations; and institutional strategies, under which a small number of universities received additional funding to develop their overall research profile — informally, these winners were dubbed "elite universities".

Rounds and winners

Selections were made in rounds in 2006, 2007 and 2012. Universities that held institutional-strategy funding at various times included LMU Munich, the Technical University of Munich, Heidelberg University, the University of Freiburg, the University of Göttingen, RWTH Aachen, the Free University and Humboldt University of Berlin, the University of Konstanz, Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, the University of Cologne, TU Dresden, the University of Tübingen and the University of Bremen.

Excellence Strategy

From 2019 the programme continues as the Excellence Strategy, jointly funded at roughly €533 million per year, with lines for clusters of excellence and "universities of excellence". Critics have debated whether the competition concentrates resources excessively, while supporters credit it with improving German universities' performance in international rankings.
