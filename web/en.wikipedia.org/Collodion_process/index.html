Collodion process

The collodion process is an early photographic process, invented in 1851 by Frederick Scott Archer. The plate had to be coated, sensitised, exposed and developed within about fifteen minutes, while still wet — hence "wet plate".

Technique

A glass plate was coated with collodion containing potassium iodide, dipped in silver nitrate, exposed in the camera, and developed with pyrogallic acid or ferrous sulfate. The process produced negatives of exceptional sharpness and inexpensive positives as ambrotypes and tintypes.

History

Dominant from the 1850s to the 1880s, wet plate documented the American Civil War in the work of Mathew Brady's operators. It was superseded by dry gelatin plates in the 1880s but is revived today by fine-art photographers.
