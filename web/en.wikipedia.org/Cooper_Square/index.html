Cooper Square

Cooper Square is a small public square and street junction in Manhattan, New York City, at the meeting point of the Bowery, Third Avenue and Fourth Avenue around East 6th and 7th Streets, between the East Village and NoHo. It is named for the industrialist and philanthropist Peter Cooper, whose Cooper Union for the Advancement of Science and Art, founded in 1859, anchors the square with its landmark Foundation Building.

History

The open space originated in the 1850s when the junction was formalised as a public place; it was named Cooper Park (later Cooper Square) in 1850 in honour of Peter Cooper, then still living. A statue of Cooper by Augustus Saint-Gaudens, unveiled in 1897, stands at the square's southern end. The surrounding blocks developed with institutions, printing houses and theatres along the Bowery, and in the twentieth century with offices, hotels and university buildings.

Geography and redevelopment

The Bowery crosses the square diagonally, splitting it into smaller segments. A redesign completed in the 2010s as part of the city's streetscape programme rationalised traffic, restored the statue's setting and enlarged pedestrian areas. Nearby landmarks include the Cooper Union's modern academic building at 41 Cooper Square, completed in 2009 to designs by Thom Mayne of Morphosis, and McSorley's Old Ale House a block east.
