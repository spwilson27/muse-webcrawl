German cruiser Blücher

Blücher was the second of five Admiral Hipper-class heavy cruisers built for Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine in the mid-1930s. Named for Field Marshal Gebhard Leberecht von Blücher, the ship was sunk on its first combat operation, during the invasion of Norway in April 1940.

Loss

On 9 April 1940, leading the naval force bound for Oslo, Blücher entered the narrow Oscarsborg narrows of the Oslofjord. The Norwegian fortress of Oscarsborg opened fire at close range with guns and torpedoes, sinking the cruiser with heavy loss of life — including much of the Gestapo and administrative staff embarked to seize the Norwegian capital. The delay allowed the Norwegian king and government to escape Oslo.
