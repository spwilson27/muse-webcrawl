The State Hermitage Museum (Russian: Государственный Эрмитаж) is a museum of art and culture in Saint Petersburg, Russia. It is one of the largest and oldest museums in the world, founded in 1764 by Catherine the Great and open to the public since 1852. Its collections comprise over three million items, displayed across a complex of historic buildings on the Palace Embankment of the Neva River, including the Winter Palace, former residence of the Russian emperors.

History

Catherine II began the collection in 1764 by purchasing 225 paintings from the Berlin merchant Johann Ernst Gotzkowsky, housing them in a private retreat adjoining the Winter Palace — the "hermitage" from which the museum takes its name. Successive emperors enlarged the holdings through purchases of entire European collections. Nicholas I opened the New Hermitage, Russia's first purpose-built public museum building, in 1852. After the October Revolution of 1917 the imperial collections were nationalized and merged with confiscated private holdings, greatly expanding the museum. During the Siege of Leningrad the bulk of the collection was evacuated to Sverdlovsk (Yekaterinburg).

Collections and buildings

The Hermitage holds the largest collection of paintings in the world, with masterpieces by Leonardo da Vinci (the Benois Madonna and Madonna Litta), Raphael, Titian, Rembrandt (34 paintings, including The Return of the Prodigal Son), Rubens, and one of the finest groups of French Impressionist and Post-Impressionist works, including Matisse and Picasso. It also holds prehistoric art (including Scythian gold), Egyptian, classical and Oriental antiquities, arms and armour in the Knights' Hall, jewellery, and applied arts. The main complex comprises the Winter Palace, Small Hermitage, Old and New Hermitages and the Hermitage Theatre; the General Staff Building across Palace Square now houses the modern art collections, and the Menshikov Palace and the Museum of the Imperial Porcelain Factory are branches. Famous residents include the Hermitage cats, kept to control rodents since the 18th century.
