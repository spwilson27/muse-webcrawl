Exotica

Exotica records evoked an imagined, romanticised tropics through lush orchestration and imitated jungle sounds.

Cultural origins1950s, United States
Typical instrumentsVibraphone, marimba, piano, congas, bird calls
Key figuresMartin Denny, Les Baxter, Arthur Lyman

Exotica is a musical genre that was popular in the 1950s and early 1960s in the United States, named after the 1957 Martin Denny album of the same name. It combined lush orchestral arrangements with instruments and sounds evoking the South Pacific and other faraway places — vibraphones, marimbas, conga drums, and imitated bird calls and jungle noises — offering suburban listeners an armchair fantasy of the tropics, closely associated with tiki culture.

Principal artists

Composer Les Baxter laid the groundwork with his 1950 album Music Out of the Moon and 1951's Ritual of the Savage. Martin Denny's combo, playing at the Shell Bar in Honolulu, scored a hit in 1959 with "Quiet Village", and his vibraphonist Arthur Lyman became a successful solo act within the genre. Other notable performers include Yma Sumac, Korla Pandit, Juan García Esquivel and Robert Drasnin.

Revival

Interest in exotica revived in the 1990s alongside the lounge and cocktail-nation revival, with reissues on CD and new acts drawing on the style.
