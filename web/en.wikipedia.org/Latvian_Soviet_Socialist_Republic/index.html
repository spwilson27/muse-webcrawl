Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic

The Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic (Latvian: Latvijas Padomju Sociālistiskā Republika), also known as Soviet Latvia, was one of the constituent republics of the Soviet Union from 1940 to 1991, apart from the period of German occupation during the Second World War (1941–1944/45). Its capital was Riga. The republic was established after the Soviet occupation of the independent Republic of Latvia in June 1940 and was formally admitted to the USSR on 5 August 1940.

History

Under the secret protocols of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, Latvia fell within the Soviet sphere of influence. After an ultimatum, the Red Army occupied the country in June 1940; staged elections produced a "People's Saeima" that requested incorporation into the USSR. In June 1941 more than 15,000 residents were deported to Siberia. Nazi Germany occupied Latvia from 1941 until the Red Army returned in 1944–45; the Latvian Waffen-SS legions and the Holocaust in Latvia were features of the German period. A second mass deportation, Operation Priboi, removed some 42,000 people in March 1949 as collectivisation was imposed.

Economy

The Latvian SSR was one of the most industrialised Soviet republics, producing railway rolling stock (RVR), minibuses (RAF), radios and electronics (VEF, Radiotehnika), pharmaceuticals and processed food. Its ports at Riga, Ventspils and Liepāja handled a large share of Soviet foreign trade, and Ventspils became a major oil export terminal.

Demographics

Soviet-era industrialisation drew large numbers of Russian-speaking migrants. The ethnic Latvian share of the population fell from about 77% in 1935 to roughly 52% by 1989, a demographic shift that became a central grievance of the national movement.

End of the republic

During glasnost, the Latvian Popular Front campaigned for independence. On 4 May 1990 the Supreme Council adopted a declaration restoring the Republic of Latvia, and full independence was re-established on 21 August 1991, during the failed Moscow coup. The Soviet Union recognised Latvian independence on 6 September 1991.
