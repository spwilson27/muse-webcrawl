Cephalochordata

Cephalochordata
Common name: Lancelets
Phylum: Chordata
Species: About 30

Cephalochordata is a subphylum of chordates comprising the lancelets, small fish-like marine animals such as Branchiostoma (amphioxus). They retain all key chordate features — notochord, dorsal nerve cord, pharyngeal slits and post-anal tail — throughout adult life.

Anatomy and lifestyle

Lancelets, typically 5–8 cm long, burrow in sand in shallow seas and filter-feed on plankton using mucus and cilia. They lack a true brain, heart and paired fins, but have segmented muscles and a simple circulatory system.

Evolutionary significance

Because their body plan resembles the inferred ancestral chordate, lancelets are key model organisms for studying vertebrate origins and development. See Chordate and Lancelet.
