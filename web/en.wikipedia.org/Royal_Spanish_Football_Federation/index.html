The Royal Spanish Football Federation (Spanish: Real Federación Española de Fútbol; RFEF) is the governing body of football in Spain. It is based in La Ciudad del Fútbol of Las Rozas, a municipality near Madrid. It organises the national teams, the Copa del Rey, the Supercopa de España and, jointly with regional federations, the lower divisions of Spanish football.

History

The federation was founded in 1913 after earlier attempts at a national body had collapsed amid disputes between clubs. Spain played its first international match in 1920, at the Antwerp Olympics, and the national team went on to win the FIFA World Cup in 2010 and multiple European Championships.

Competitions

The RFEF organises the Copa del Rey, the Supercopa, the Primera Federación and the amateur and youth game. The top two professional leagues, La Liga and the Segunda División, are run by LaLiga under licence, with the federation retaining responsibility for referees and disciplinary matters in coordination with it.

National teams

The federation administers the Spain national football team and the Spain women's national football team — World Cup winners in 2010 and 2023 respectively — as well as futsal and beach soccer national teams and youth sides.
