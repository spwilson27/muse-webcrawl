Approximation theory
FieldMathematics
Key resultsWeierstrass approximation theorem, Chebyshev polynomials, minimax approximation

Approximation theory is a branch of mathematics concerned with how functions can best be approximated with simpler functions, and with quantitatively characterising the errors introduced thereby. It studies questions such as how well continuous functions can be approximated by polynomials, rational functions, splines, or trigonometric sums, and it underlies much of numerical analysis and computer-aided geometric design.

Polynomial approximation

The Weierstrass approximation theorem guarantees that any continuous function on a closed interval can be uniformly approximated by polynomials. The quality of approximation is governed by the smoothness of the function, as described by Jackson's theorems, with converse results due to Bernstein. For a fixed degree, the best uniform approximation problem is characterised by Chebyshev's equioscillation theorem, and Chebyshev polynomials minimise the maximum error among monic polynomials.

Other topics

The theory also covers rational approximation and Padé approximants, spline approximation, least-squares approximation, Fourier series and wavelet approximation, and approximation in normed spaces generally. Algorithms such as the Remez exchange algorithm compute near-optimal polynomial approximants in practice.
