Chandrayaan-2

OperatorISRO
Launch22 July 2019, Satish Dhawan Space Centre
Launch vehicleLVM3 (GSLV Mk III)
ComponentsOrbiter, Vikram lander, Pragyan rover
Landing attempt6 September 2019 (lander lost)

Chandrayaan-2 is the second lunar exploration mission developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), after Chandrayaan-1. It consisted of a lunar orbiter, the Vikram lander, and the Pragyan rover, all designed to study the Moon's south polar region and demonstrate India's soft-landing capability.

Mission profile

The spacecraft launched aboard an LVM3 rocket on 22 July 2019 and entered lunar orbit on 20 August. The Vikram lander separated from the orbiter on 2 September and began its powered descent on 6 September, aiming for a high plain between the craters Manzinus C and Simpelius N, at about 70 degrees south latitude — further south than any previous landing attempt. During the final descent phase, at an altitude of roughly 2 kilometres, communications with the lander were lost; it had deviated from its intended trajectory and impacted the surface.

Orbiter science

The orbiter, designed for a one-year mission that was later expected to extend to about seven years owing to ample fuel reserves, carries eight scientific instruments, including a high-resolution camera, dual-frequency synthetic aperture radar, and spectrometers. It has mapped the lunar surface, studied the distribution of water ice and hydroxyl in polar regions, and measured the Moon's tenuous exosphere. In December 2019, NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter team located the Vikram impact site with the help of a tip from an Indian amateur observer.

Legacy

Although the landing failed, ISRO assessed the mission as highly successful in its orbiter and technological objectives. Lessons from the failure informed Chandrayaan-3, which achieved a successful soft landing near the lunar south pole in August 2023.
