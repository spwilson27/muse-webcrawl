Martin of Tours

Saint Martin of Tours
Bornc. 316, Savaria, Pannonia
Died8 November 397, Candes, Gaul
Feast day11 November (Martinmas)
PatronageSoldiers, tailors, the poor; France

Martin of Tours (Latin: Sanctus Martinus Turonensis; c. 316 – 397) was the third bishop of Tours in Gaul and one of the most famous saints of the Western Church. A former Roman soldier who became a monk and bishop, he was a pioneer of monasticism in Gaul.

Life

Born in Pannonia to a military family, Martin was conscripted into the Roman cavalry. The most famous episode of his life, recorded by his biographer Sulpicius Severus, occurred at Amiens: Martin cut his military cloak in half with his sword to share it with a freezing beggar, and that night dreamed of Christ wearing the half-cloak. Leaving the army, he became a disciple of Hilary of Poitiers, founded the monastery of Ligugé — the first in Gaul — and in 371 was acclaimed bishop of Tours, where he campaigned against paganism and heresy while living an austere monastic life.

Veneration

Martin's tomb at Tours became a major pilgrimage centre, and his cult spread across Europe. His feast on 11 November, Martinmas, traditionally marks the beginning of winter in many countries. The word "chapel" itself derives from the shrine of his cloak (cappa).
