Inter-State River Water Disputes Act

The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 (also known as the ISRWD Act) is an Act of the Parliament of India enacted under Article 262 of the Constitution of India. It empowers the central government to set up ad hoc tribunals to adjudicate disputes between states over the waters of inter-state rivers and river valleys when negotiations fail.

Mechanism

When a state government complains that its interests in an inter-state river are being prejudiced, the central government first attempts settlement by negotiation; if that fails, it constitutes a Water Disputes Tribunal. The tribunal's award, once published, is binding on the parties and equivalent to a decree of the Supreme Court. Well-known tribunals include those for the Krishna, Godavari, Narmada, Ravi-Beas, and Cauvery disputes — the Cauvery dispute between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu being among the longest running and most politically charged.

Amendments

A 2002 amendment imposed time limits on adjudication and required the central government to set up a scheme for implementing awards. A further amendment bill proposing a single permanent tribunal with benches has been introduced in Parliament to speed up resolution.
