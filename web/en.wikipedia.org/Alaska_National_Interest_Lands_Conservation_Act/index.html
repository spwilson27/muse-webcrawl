The Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act (ANILCA) is a United States federal law signed by President Jimmy Carter on December 2, 1980. It provided varying degrees of special protection to more than 100 million acres of federal land in Alaska, more than doubling the size of the National Park System.

Provisions

ANILCA created or expanded numerous national parks, wildlife refuges, national monuments, and wild and scenic rivers, including Gates of the Arctic and Wrangell–St. Elias National Parks. It designated over 56 million acres as wilderness and protected the subsistence way of life of rural Alaskans by giving rural residents a priority for subsistence uses of fish and wildlife on federal lands.

Background

The act fulfilled a requirement of the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act of 1971, which had directed Congress to set aside up to 80 million acres for conservation. President Carter's use of the Antiquities Act in 1978 to proclaim national monuments increased pressure for a legislative settlement.
