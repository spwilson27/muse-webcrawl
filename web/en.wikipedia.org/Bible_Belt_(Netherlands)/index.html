Bible Belt (Netherlands)

The Dutch Bible Belt (Dutch: Bijbelgordel) is a strip of land in the Netherlands with the highest concentration of conservative orthodox Calvinist Protestants in the country. Although the term is of recent origin, the religious culture of the region has existed for centuries.

The Bible Belt stretches from Zeeland in the southwest, through the West-Betuwe and Veluwe in the centre of the country, to the northwestern parts of the province Overijssel. Municipalities in this area are noted for their large number of conservative Protestant churches. People in the Biblebelt often live in traditional families with a relatively large number of children, and there is a large presence of the orthodox Reformed political party SGP, which has typically won a plurality of votes in several municipalities.

History

When Flanders and the province of North Brabant were reconquered by Spain in the Eighty Years' War, their Protestant inhabitants fled to the north. The south remained predominantly Catholic, while orthodox Calvinism took firm root in many rural communities of the north and centre. The region's distinctive religious character was reinforced by later secessions from the Dutch Reformed Church, such as the Secession of 1834 and the Doleantie of 1886, which produced numerous small, strict denominations.

Characteristics

The Bible Belt is known for strict observance of Sunday rest, traditional views on the roles of men and women, low rates of vaccination in some communities, and opposition to practices such as abortion and euthanasia. Outbreaks of diseases such as measles and polio have periodically been reported in areas with low vaccination coverage. At the same time, residents often report strong social cohesion and mutual support within their church communities.
