Overtime

Overtime is the time worked by an employee beyond normal working hours, typically compensated at a higher rate ("time and a half" or "double time") where labour law or contracts so require.

Regulation

In the United States, the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 requires non-exempt employees to be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek; many salaried "exempt" employees fall outside the rule. The European Union's Working Time Directive generally caps the average working week at 48 hours including overtime. Japan has historically struggled with excessive overtime and karoshi (death from overwork), prompting statutory caps since 2019. Some jurisdictions instead grant time off in lieu (compensatory time).

Economics and health

Employers use overtime to meet temporary peaks in demand without hiring. Critics note that chronic overtime is associated with fatigue, accidents, cardiovascular disease and reduced hourly productivity, and that unpaid overtime is widespread among professionals whose hours are nominally flexible.
