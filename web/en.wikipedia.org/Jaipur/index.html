Jaipur is the capital and largest city of the Indian state of Rajasthan. Founded in 1727 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II, ruler of Amer, it was one of the earliest planned cities of modern India, laid out on a grid according to principles of Vastu Shastra. Jaipur forms part of the popular "Golden Triangle" tourist circuit with Delhi and Agra, and is known worldwide as the Pink City for the terracotta-pink colour of its old walled quarter.

History

Jai Singh II, an astronomer-king, moved his capital from the hill fortress of Amer to the new city of Jaipur on the plain below, planning it with the Bengali architect Vidyadhar Bhattacharya. In 1876, for the visit of the Prince of Wales (later Edward VII), the city was painted pink — the traditional colour of hospitality — a scheme maintained ever since. Jaipur was the capital of the princely state of Jaipur under British paramountcy and became the capital of Rajasthan after Indian independence and the integration of the Rajputana states.

Sights

Landmarks include the Hawa Mahal (Palace of Winds, 1799), a five-storey pink sandstone façade with 953 latticed windows; the City Palace complex; the Jantar Mantar astronomical observatory built by Jai Singh II, a UNESCO World Heritage Site; the Amber Fort in the Aravalli hills; and the Jal Mahal set in Man Sagar Lake. The walled city of Jaipur itself was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019.

Economy and culture

Jaipur is a major centre for gemstone cutting and polishing (especially emeralds), jewellery, textiles, block printing, blue pottery, and handicrafts, and has grown rapidly as an IT and services hub. It hosts the annual Jaipur Literature Festival, among the world's largest literary gatherings, and the spectacle of Holi and the Teej and Gangaur festivals is prominent in its calendar.
