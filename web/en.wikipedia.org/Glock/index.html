Glock

Glock GmbH is an Austrian manufacturer of weapons, field equipment and plastic products based in Deutsch-Wagram, Austria. The company is best known for its line of polymer-framed, short recoil-operated, striker-fired semi-automatic pistols, which are widely used by military and law enforcement agencies around the world, as well as by civilian shooters.

History

The company was founded in 1963 by engineer Gaston Glock, initially producing curtain rods and later polymer and steel products including knives and entrenching tools for the Austrian Army. In the early 1980s the Austrian military issued a requirement for a new service pistol. Gaston Glock, who had no prior experience designing firearms, assembled a team of small-arms experts and produced a prototype that won the trials. The pistol entered Austrian service in 1982 as the Pistole 80, commercially the Glock 17 — so named because it was the company's 17th patent.

Design

Glock pistols introduced several features that became influential across the industry: a frame made from a proprietary high-strength nylon-based polymer, a striker-fired action rather than an external hammer, and the "Safe Action" system of three passive, independently operating safety mechanisms — a trigger safety, a firing pin safety and a drop safety — that disengage sequentially as the trigger is pulled and re-engage automatically when it is released. The pistols are known for their simplicity, with significantly fewer parts than many competitors, high magazine capacity, low weight and resistance to corrosion thanks to a ferritic nitrocarburizing surface treatment.

Models and adoption

The Glock line has expanded into many calibres, including 9x19mm Parabellum, .40 S&W, .45 ACP, .357 SIG, 10mm Auto and .380 ACP, and into full-size, compact, subcompact, competition and single-stack "Slimline" variants. Numbered models run from the Glock 17 through the Glock 40s. Glock pistols are in service with armed forces and police in dozens of countries; the Glock 17 was adopted by Norway in 1984 and later by Sweden, and in 2013 the British Armed Forces adopted the Glock 17 Gen4 to replace the Browning Hi-Power. In the United States, Glocks became the most common police sidearm, carried by a large share of law enforcement officers.
