Juan Perón

Juan Domingo Perón (8 October 1895 – 1 July 1974) was an Argentine military officer and statesman who served as President of Argentina from 1946 to 1955 and again from 1973 to 1974. He founded the political movement known as Peronism, which remains a dominant force in Argentine politics.

Rise to power

Perón rose through the army and participated in the 1943 coup. As Secretary of Labour he built a mass following among workers — the descamisados ("shirtless ones") — through wage increases, social welfare, and alliances with trade unions. Arrested by rivals in October 1945, he was freed after mass demonstrations on 17 October, a foundational date for Peronism. In 1946 he won the presidency, with his charismatic wife Eva Perón ("Evita") championing women's suffrage (achieved in 1947) and social aid through her foundation.

Presidency, exile, and return

His governments pursued economic nationalism, industrialization, and social justice ("justicialismo") alongside restrictions on opposition press and courts. Re-elected in 1951, he was overthrown in 1955 and exiled for 18 years, mostly in Spain, while Peronism was banned at home. He returned in 1973 and won a third term with his wife Isabel as vice president; he died in office in 1974, and Isabel succeeded him until her own overthrow in 1976.
