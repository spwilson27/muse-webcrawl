Boudhanath

Boudhanath is a stupa and surrounding neighborhood in Kathmandu, Nepal. One of the largest spherical stupas in the world, it is a central pilgrimage site for Tibetan Buddhism and a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Kathmandu Valley listing.

The stupa

The massive white mandala-shaped dome is surmounted by a gilded tower painted with the all-seeing eyes of the Buddha and a thirteen-tiered spire. The stupa is said to entomb relics of Kassapa Buddha. Its origins are traced to the Licchavi period, around the 6th century CE, though tradition ascribes its founding to earlier legends. It stands on the ancient trade route from Tibet, and Tibetan merchants have rested and prayed here for centuries.

Community

Since the 1959 Tibetan diaspora, the area around Boudhanath has become the center of Nepal's Tibetan community, with dozens of monasteries (gompas) built around the stupa. Pilgrims and visitors circle the stupa clockwise — performing kora — spinning prayer wheels and counting rosaries, especially at dawn and dusk and during festivals such as Losar. The stupa was damaged in the April 2015 earthquake and was rebuilt and reconsecrated in 2016.
