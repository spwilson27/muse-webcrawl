U-Roy

Born1942, Kingston, Jamaica (as Hugh Roy Beckford)
Died2021
GenreReggae, dancehall

U-Roy (1942–2021), born Hugh Roy Beckford, was a Jamaican vocalist and pioneer of toasting, the rhythmic talking-over of records that made him one of the founding fathers of the deejay style in Jamaican music and an important influence on hip hop.

Career

U-Roy began working on sound systems in the early 1960s and rose to fame in 1970 with a string of hits for producer Duke Reid, including "Wear You to the Ball" and "Wake the Town", recorded over existing rhythm tracks. His albums for Virgin Records in the 1970s, such as Dread in a Babylon, brought his style to an international audience.

Legacy

U-Roy's success opened the door for generations of Jamaican deejays and is widely credited as a precursor of rap; he operated his own Stur-Gav sound system into his later years.
