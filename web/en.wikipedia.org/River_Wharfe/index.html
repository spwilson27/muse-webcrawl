River Wharfe

Length105 km (65 mi)
SourceConfluence of Oughtershaw Beck and Green Field Beck, near Beckermonds, North Yorkshire
MouthRiver Ouse at Cawood

The River Wharfe is a river in Yorkshire, England, about 105 kilometres (65 miles) long. Its valley, Wharfedale, is one of the Yorkshire Dales. The river rises in Langstrothdale in the Yorkshire Dales National Park and flows south-east past Grassington, Bolton Abbey, Ilkley, Otley, Wetherby and Tadcaster before joining the River Ouse near Cawood. Its name is thought to derive from a Brittonic word meaning "winding" or "twisting".

The Strid

Near Bolton Abbey the river is forced through the Strid, a narrow cleft in gritstone only a few feet wide but very deep, where the whole flow of the Wharfe passes through a gap overhung by rocks — a notorious hazard that has claimed the lives of visitors who attempted to jump across and slipped.

Other features

The Wharfe is tidal from Tadcaster downstream. Ilkley's stretch of the river became England's first designated river bathing water in 2020, prompting scrutiny of sewage discharges. The river supports salmon, trout, chub and barbel, and its valley carries the Dales Way long-distance footpath for much of its length.
