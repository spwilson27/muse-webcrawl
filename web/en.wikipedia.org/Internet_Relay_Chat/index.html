Internet Relay Chat

Internet Relay Chat (IRC) is a text-based chat system for instant messaging. IRC is designed for group communication in discussion forums, called channels, but also allows one-on-one communication via private messages as well as chat and data transfer, including file sharing.

Internet Relay Chat is implemented as an application layer protocol to facilitate communication in the form of text. The chat process works on a client–server networking model. Users connect, using a client—which may be a web app, a standalone desktop program, or embedded into part of a larger program—to an IRC server, which may be part of a larger IRC network.

History

IRC was created by Jarkko Oikarinen in August 1988 to replace a program called MUT (MultiUser Talk) on a BBS called OuluBox at the University of Oulu in Finland, where he was working at the Department of Information Processing Science. Oikarinen first proposed the idea to friends on Usenet, and the first server ran at tolsun.oulu.fi.

IRC gained prominence during the 1991 Persian Gulf War, when users logged reports of events in real time, and again during the attempted Soviet coup d'état of August 1991. At its peak in the mid-2000s, networks such as QuakeNet and EFnet hosted hundreds of thousands of users, before usage declined with the rise of web-based social platforms.

Technical information

IRC is an open protocol that uses TCP and, optionally, TLS. An IRC server can connect to other IRC servers to expand the IRC network. Users access IRC networks by connecting a client to a server. Channel names are prefixed with a hash character (#). IRC operators manage servers, while channel operators moderate individual channels.

Networks

Major networks have included EFnet (the oldest), IRCnet, Undernet, DALnet, QuakeNet, and freenode (now Libera Chat). In May 2021, freenode underwent a controversial takeover and most projects migrated to the new Libera Chat network.
