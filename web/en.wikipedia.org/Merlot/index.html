Merlot

Merlot is a dark blue–colored wine grape variety used both for varietal wines and as a blending component. Its name is said to derive from merle, the French word for blackbird, a reference to the grape's dark color.

Characteristics

Merlot typically produces medium-bodied, soft wines with flavors of plum, cherry, and chocolate. It ripens earlier than Cabernet Sauvignon and has thinner skins and looser bunches, making it somewhat more susceptible to rot but easier to grow in cooler climates.

Regions

Merlot is the most widely planted grape in Bordeaux, France, dominating blends on the Right Bank in Saint-Émilion and Pomerol, where estates such as Château Pétrus produce some of the world's most expensive wines almost entirely from Merlot. Significant plantings also exist in Italy, California, Washington State, Chile, and Australia.

Popularity

Merlot surged in popularity in the United States during the 1990s. Demand dipped after the 2004 film Sideways, in which the protagonist disparages the variety, though plantings and quality production have since recovered.
