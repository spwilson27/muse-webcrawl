National Film Award for Best Actress

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The National Film Award for Best Actress (officially known since 2021 as the National Film Award for Best Actress in a Leading Role) is an honour presented annually at the National Film Awards of India. Instituted in 1968, it is one of the most prestigious acting awards in Indian cinema, recognising the best performance by an actress in a leading role across all Indian-language films of the year. The award includes a Silver Lotus (Rajat Kamal) and a cash prize.

History

The National Film Awards were established in 1954, but acting categories for actresses were introduced later; the first Best Actress award went to Nargis for her performance in Raat Aur Din (1967), presented at the 15th National Film Awards in 1968.

Notable winners

Shabana Azmi holds the record with five Best Actress awards, for films including Ankur, Arth, Khandhar, Paar, and Godmother. Kangana Ranaut has won four, and several actresses have won three times, including Sharada, Archana, Shobana, Tabu, Priyanka Chopra (including a supporting award), and Vidya Balan. Winners span India's many film industries and languages — Hindi, Bengali, Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and others — reflecting the award's pan-Indian scope. Sridevi received the award posthumously for Mom (2017).
