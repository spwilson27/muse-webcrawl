Muslims (Arabic: مُسْلِمُون) are people who adhere to Islam, a monotheistic religion founded on the teachings of the Quran, regarded by Muslims as the word of God (Allah) revealed to the prophet Muhammad. The word "Muslim" means "one who submits (to God)". With roughly two billion adherents, Muslims make up about a quarter of the world's population, forming the second-largest religious group after Christians.

Beliefs and practice

Core Muslim practice is summarised in the Five Pillars: the declaration of faith (shahada), daily prayer (salah), almsgiving (zakat), fasting during Ramadan (sawm), and the pilgrimage to Mecca (hajj) at least once in a lifetime if able. Muslims believe in one God, his angels, revealed books, prophets (including Abraham, Moses and Jesus), the Day of Judgment, and divine decree.

Branches

The two largest branches are Sunni Islam (roughly 87–90% of Muslims) and Shia Islam (roughly 10–13%), which differ historically over the rightful succession to leadership after Muhammad's death. Smaller groups include the Ibadi and various Sufi orders that cut across branches.

Demographics

The country with the largest Muslim population is Indonesia; large populations also live in Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nigeria and Egypt. Muslims form majorities in 49 countries, concentrated in North Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia and Southeast Asia, with significant minorities elsewhere.
