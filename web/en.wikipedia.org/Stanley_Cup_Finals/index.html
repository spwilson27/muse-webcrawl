Stanley Cup Finals

The Stanley Cup Finals in ice hockey is the National Hockey League's (NHL) championship series to determine the league's champion and the winner of the Stanley Cup. The surviving champion and runner-up of each of the league's two conferences, after three rounds of playoff competition, advance to the finals, which is a best-of-seven series.

History

The Stanley Cup was donated in 1893 by Lord Stanley of Preston, the Governor General of Canada, as a challenge cup for Canada's amateur hockey champion. Professional teams became eligible in the 1900s, and from 1915 the champions of the eastern NHA/NHL and the western PCHA (later WCHL/WHL) met for the Cup. Since 1926 the Cup has been competed for exclusively by NHL teams, and since 1982 the Finals have matched the playoff champions of the Eastern and Western Conferences.

Moments from the Finals—such as Bobby Orr's flying goal in 1970, the New York Rangers ending a 54-year drought in 1994, and numerous Game 7 finishes—rank among the most famous in hockey history. The Montreal Canadiens have won the Cup a record 24 times.

Format

The Finals are played in a 2–2–1–1–1 home-ice format, with the team with the better regular-season record hosting games 1, 2, 5 and 7. The series follows the last of the conference finals in late May and June. The Conn Smythe Trophy is awarded to the most valuable player of the entire playoffs, and is presented at the conclusion of the Finals along with the Stanley Cup itself.
