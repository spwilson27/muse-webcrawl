The Adventures of Tom Sawyer is an 1876 novel by American author Mark Twain about a boy, Tom Sawyer, growing up along the Mississippi River. It is set in the 1840s in the fictional town of St. Petersburg, inspired by Hannibal, Missouri, where Twain lived as a boy. In the novel, Sawyer has several adventures, often with his friend Huckleberry Finn. Originally a commercial failure, the book ended up being the best-selling of Twain's works during his lifetime.

Plot

Tom Sawyer, an orphan raised by his Aunt Polly, is a mischievous boy whose adventures include tricking other children into whitewashing a fence for him, attending his own funeral after running away to Jackson's Island with Huck Finn and Joe Harper, and winning a Bible at Sunday school by dubious means. With Huck, he witnesses a murder in a graveyard committed by Injun Joe, who frames the drunk Muff Potter; tormented by guilt, Tom eventually testifies and exonerates Potter. Later, Tom and his sweetheart Becky Thatcher get lost in a cave, where Tom encounters Injun Joe; after their rescue the cave is sealed, Injun Joe starves inside, and Tom and Huck recover his hidden gold, becoming wealthy local heroes.

Sequels and legacy

The novel's sequel, Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (1884), is widely regarded as the Great American Novel. Twain wrote two further, less successful sequels, Tom Sawyer Abroad and Tom Sawyer, Detective. Tom Sawyer has been adapted many times for film, television, stage, and comics, and its fence-whitewashing episode has entered the language as an emblem of ingenious persuasion.
