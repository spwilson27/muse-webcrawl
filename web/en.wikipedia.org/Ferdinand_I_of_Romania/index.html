Ferdinand I of Romania

Ferdinand I (24 August 1865 – 20 July 1927), nicknamed the Unifier (Întregitorul), was King of Romania from 1914 until his death in 1927. A prince of the German House of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen, he led Romania through the First World War on the side of the Allies against his own native country, and presided over the creation of Greater Romania.

Early life and accession

Ferdinand was born in Sigmaringen, Germany, the second son of Leopold, Prince of Hohenzollern. When his elder brother William renounced his succession rights, Ferdinand became heir to his uncle, Carol I of Romania, and moved to Bucharest, becoming crown prince in 1889. In 1893 he married Princess Marie of Edinburgh, a granddaughter of Queen Victoria, whose energy and popularity complemented his own shy, reserved character. He succeeded Carol I on the king's death in October 1914.

First World War

Romania remained neutral for two years before joining the Allies in August 1916, declaring war on Austria-Hungary in hopes of gaining Transylvania. The campaign began badly: Bucharest fell to the Central Powers in December 1916, and the government withdrew to Iași. Romanian forces, reorganised with French assistance, won defensive victories at Mărăști, Mărășești, and Oituz in 1917. Forced to sign the Treaty of Bucharest in May 1918, Ferdinand refused to ratify it, and Romania re-entered the war on 10 November 1918, the day before the general armistice.

Greater Romania

During 1918, Bessarabia, Bukovina, and Transylvania united with the kingdom, roughly doubling its territory and population. Ferdinand and Marie were crowned as sovereigns of Greater Romania at Alba Iulia on 15 October 1922. His reign saw land reform, the adoption of universal male suffrage, and the 1923 constitution. He died at Sinaia in 1927 and was succeeded, after his son Carol renounced the throne, by his young grandson Michael I under a regency.
