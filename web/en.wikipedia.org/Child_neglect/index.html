Child neglect

Child neglect is a form of child abuse in which a caregiver fails to provide for a child's basic needs, including adequate food, shelter, clothing, supervision, medical care, and emotional support. It is the most commonly reported form of child maltreatment in many countries.

Forms and effects

Neglect may be physical, medical, educational, or emotional. Long-term consequences can include impaired cognitive and social development, attachment difficulties, health problems, and increased risk of mental illness. Poverty is strongly associated with reported neglect, and distinguishing neglect caused by material deprivation from wilful failure to care is a central challenge for child protection systems.

Response

Most jurisdictions impose mandatory reporting duties on professionals and empower child protective services to investigate and intervene, with responses ranging from family support services to removal of the child in severe cases.
