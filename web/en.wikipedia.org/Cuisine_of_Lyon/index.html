The cuisine of Lyon is the traditional cooking of the French city of Lyon, long regarded as the capital of French gastronomy. Sitting between the cattle and dairy country of Bresse and Charolais, the wine regions of Beaujolais and the Rhône, and the produce of the Saône plain, the city developed a rich, generous bourgeois and working-class cuisine centred on pork, offal, poultry, freshwater fish and cream.

Bouchons and the Mères

Its emblematic restaurants are the bouchons — small, convivial bistros serving dishes such as quenelles de brochet (pike dumplings in Nantua sauce), salade lyonnaise with lardons and a poached egg, andouillette sausage, tablier de sapeur (breaded tripe), cervelle de canut (a herbed fresh-cheese spread named for the silk workers) and praline tarts. In the 19th and early 20th centuries the city's kitchens were famously run by women, the Mères lyonnaises ("Lyon mothers"), of whom Mère Brazier — the first chef to hold six Michelin stars across two restaurants — trained the young Paul Bocuse, whose nearby restaurant anchored modern French haute cuisine.

Recognition

The food writer Curnonsky named Lyon the world capital of gastronomy in 1935, and the city remains dense with starred restaurants and the great food hall Les Halles de Lyon Paul Bocuse.
