Siege of Sevastopol (1854–1855)

The siege of Sevastopol (at the time called the siege of Sebastopol) lasted from October 1854 until September 1855, during the Crimean War. The allied armies of Britain, France, the Ottoman Empire and Sardinia encircled the Russian naval base of Sevastopol in the Crimea.

Course of the siege

After landing in the Crimea and winning the Battle of the Alma, the allies marched around Sevastopol and invested it from the south. The Russians, led by engineers under Eduard Totleben, built formidable earthworks. Major engagements included Balaclava, Inkerman and the Chernaya. Repeated bombardments culminated in the French capture of the Malakoff redoubt on 8 September 1855.

Fall of the city

With the Malakoff lost, the Russians evacuated the southern side of the city overnight, scuttling their remaining ships and blowing up the forts. The allied occupation of Sevastopol effectively ended major operations in the war.

Significance

The siege pioneered trench warfare and the use of railways and the electric telegraph in war reporting; it is also remembered for the work of Florence Nightingale in the military hospitals at Scutari.
