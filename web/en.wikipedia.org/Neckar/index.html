Neckar

The Neckar is a 362-kilometre-long river in southwestern Germany, flowing almost entirely through the state of Baden-Württemberg. It rises near Villingen-Schwenningen in the Black Forest region and flows northeast past Rottweil, Tübingen, Stuttgart, Heilbronn and Heidelberg before joining the Rhine at Mannheim.

Course

From its source on the Baar plateau at about 705 metres above sea level, the Neckar cuts through the Swabian Alb foothills and the fertile Gäu landscapes. At Plochingen it turns northwest through the Stuttgart basin, where the valley is lined with vineyards and industry. Between Heilbronn and Heidelberg it winds through the Odenwald in a deeply incised, scenic valley dotted with castles, before crossing the Upper Rhine Plain to its confluence at Mannheim.

Navigation and economy

The lower 200 kilometres of the river are navigable, regulated by a chain of locks and weirs built mainly between the 1920s and 1960s. The river connects the Stuttgart region to the Rhine and thus to the North Sea port of Rotterdam. Its banks host much of Baden-Württemberg's industry, including the automotive plants of Stuttgart and the port of Heilbronn.
