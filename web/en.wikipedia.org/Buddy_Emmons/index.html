Buddy Gene Emmons (January 27, 1937 – July 21, 2015) was an American musician widely regarded as the world's foremost pedal steel guitarist. Nicknamed the "Big E", he profoundly influenced the sound and technique of the instrument in country music and beyond, and his innovations in tuning, pedals, and guitar design shaped the modern pedal steel.

Career

Born in Mishawaka, Indiana, Emmons began playing lap steel as a child and went professional in his teens. He worked with Little Jimmy Dickens, Ernest Tubb's Texas Troubadours, and Ray Price's Cherokee Cowboys, becoming a first-call Nashville session player. He recorded with artists across country and popular music, toured with the Everly Brothers, and later played with Danny Gatton and in western swing and jazz settings.

Innovations

Emmons helped pioneer the split-pedal concept that allowed independent raising and lowering of strings, expanded the standard E9 tuning, and pushed for additional strings and pedal changes. In 1963 he co-founded the Sho-Bud-influenced Emmons Guitar Company, whose push-pull pedal steel guitars became prized instruments. His playing combined bebop-influenced harmony, speed, and expressive touch; landmark recordings include his work with Ray Price and his own albums, including Steel Guitar Jazz (1963).

Legacy

Emmons was inducted into the Steel Guitar Hall of Fame in 1981. Generations of steel guitarists cite him as their primary influence, and he remains the benchmark against whom pedal steel players are measured.
