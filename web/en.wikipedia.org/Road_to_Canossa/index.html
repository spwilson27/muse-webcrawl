Road to Canossa

Canossa Castle, site of Henry IV's penance
DateJanuary 1077
LocationCanossa, Emilia-Romagna, Italy
ParticipantsHenry IV, Holy Roman Emperor; Pope Gregory VII; Matilda of Tuscany
OutcomeGregory VII absolves Henry IV and lifts his excommunication

The Road to Canossa (German: Gang nach Canossa; Italian: Umiliazione di Canossa, "Humiliation of Canossa") refers to the journey of the Holy Roman Emperor Henry IV to Canossa Castle in northern Italy in January 1077, where he stood as a penitent before Pope Gregory VII during the Investiture Controversy.

Background

The dispute between Henry IV and Gregory VII centred on the right to appoint (invest) bishops. In 1076 Gregory excommunicated Henry, releasing his subjects from their oaths of allegiance, and German princes threatened to elect a new king unless Henry was absolved within a year. Henry therefore crossed the Alps in mid-winter to intercept the Pope, who was travelling north, at the castle of Matilda of Tuscany.

The penance

According to contemporary accounts, Henry stood for three days — from 25 to 27 January 1077 — outside the castle gate, dressed in a hair shirt, fasting and barefoot in the snow, begging forgiveness. On 28 January Gregory finally admitted him, celebrated Mass with him, and lifted the excommunication.

Legacy

The episode became the classic symbol of the submission of secular power to the Church, although its long-term effect was limited: Henry soon renewed the conflict and eventually installed an antipope. The phrase "going to Canossa" (nach Canossa gehen) remains an idiom in German and other languages for a humiliating act of submission or penance. Chancellor Otto von Bismarck famously declared during the Kulturkampf in 1872: "We will not go to Canossa" (Nach Canossa gehen wir nicht).
