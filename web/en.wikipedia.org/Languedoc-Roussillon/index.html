Languedoc-Roussillon

Languedoc-Roussillon (Occitan: Lengadòc-Rosselhon; Catalan: Llenguadoc-Rosselló) is a former administrative region of France. On 1 January 2016 it merged with Midi-Pyrénées to form the new region of Occitanie. It comprised five departments: Aude, Gard, Hérault, Lozère, and Pyrénées-Orientales, and its capital and largest city was Montpellier.

Geography

The region stretched along the Mediterranean coast from the Rhône delta to the Spanish border, backed by the Cévennes mountains and the Pyrenees. It is famous for its extensive vineyards—one of the largest wine-producing areas in the world—its beaches and resorts along the Golfe du Lion, and historic cities such as Nîmes, Narbonne, Carcassonne, and Perpignan.

History and culture

The region's name derives from the langue d'oc, the Occitan language traditionally spoken in the Languedoc, and from Roussillon, the Catalan-speaking area around Perpignan that was ceded by Spain to France by the Treaty of the Pyrenees in 1659. The medieval Cathar castles of the Aude and the Pont du Gard Roman aqueduct are among its best-known landmarks. Rugby league is especially popular in the region, earning it the nickname Languedoc-Roussillon rugby country alongside rugby union.
