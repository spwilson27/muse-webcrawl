Photoplotter

A photoplotter is a precision machine that produces high-resolution images on photographic film by exposing it with a controlled light source, following vector data from a computer file. Photoplotters were essential in the manufacture of printed circuit boards (PCBs), producing the film artwork ("photools") from which circuit patterns are transferred to copper-clad board.

Operation

Traditional flatbed photoplotters move a light head over stationary film, drawing lines and flashing apertures of defined shapes. Later laser photoplotters raster-scan a laser beam across the film, using data in Gerber (RS-274X) format, the industry standard originating with the Gerber Scientific company, which built the first photoplotters in the 1960s.

Uses

Beyond PCB fabrication, photoplotters produce photomasks and artwork for printing, chemical milling, and screen manufacture. Although direct imaging technologies have displaced film in many shops, photoplotters long set the standard for accuracy in large-format, fine-line artwork.
