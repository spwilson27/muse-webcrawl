George Graham (7 July 1673 – 20 November 1751) was an English clockmaker, inventor, and geophysicist, and a Fellow of the Royal Society. He was a partner with the clockmaker Thomas Tompion and, after Tompion's death, succeeded him as Britain's leading maker of clocks and scientific instruments.

Career

Graham was apprenticed to the clock trade in London and came to work for Tompion in 1695, marrying Tompion's niece. He invented the deadbeat escapement for clocks around 1715 and the mercury-compensated pendulum in 1726, both of which greatly improved the accuracy of regulator clocks used in astronomy.

Instruments and science

Graham made precision astronomical instruments, including the great mural quadrant at Greenwich Observatory, and supplied instruments to observatories across Europe. He discovered the aberration of light independently and studied the daily variation of the magnetic needle. John Harrison received an interest-free loan from Graham that helped fund his marine timekeepers.

Death

Graham died in 1751 and was buried in Westminster Abbey in the same grave as Tompion, an honour reflecting his eminence in British horology.
