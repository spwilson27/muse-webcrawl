LocationPalace of Westminster, London

FrequencyAnnually (with each new parliamentary session)

The State Opening of Parliament is a formal ceremony marking the beginning of a session of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It is held in the House of Lords chamber at the Palace of Westminster and is the only regular occasion when the three constituent parts of Parliament — the Sovereign, the House of Lords, and the House of Commons — meet together.

Ceremony

The monarch travels from Buckingham Palace to Westminster in a state procession, escorted by the Household Cavalry, and arrives wearing the Imperial State Crown. Beforehand, in a ritual dating to the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, the Yeomen of the Guard search the cellars of the Palace. An MP is also held "hostage" at Buckingham Palace to ensure the sovereign's safe return.

Once enthroned in the Lords, the monarch instructs Black Rod to summon the Commons. In a display of parliamentary independence dating from the Civil War era, the door of the Commons is slammed in Black Rod's face and reopened only after knocking three times. Members then process to the Lords, led by the prime minister and the leader of the opposition.

The King's Speech

The sovereign reads the King's Speech (Queen's Speech under a female monarch), a text written by the government setting out its legislative programme for the coming session. The speech is then debated by both Houses over several days, beginning with a motion on an "humble Address" thanking the monarch — the first real test of the government's majority.

History

State openings date to the 16th century; the modern ceremonial form was largely established under Queen Victoria and continued by her successors. Queen Elizabeth II opened Parliament almost every year of her reign; Charles III has continued the tradition.
