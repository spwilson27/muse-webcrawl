Ilterish Qaghan (Old Turkic: 𐰃𐰠𐱅𐰼𐰾 𐰴𐰍𐰣; personal name Ashina Qutlugh, also rendered Kutlug Iltirish Qaghan) was the founder of the Second Turkic Khaganate, which restored Turkic power on the Mongolian steppe at the end of the 7th century. His regnal name, Ilterish — "he who gathers the realm" — proclaimed his achievement of reuniting the Turkic tribes after half a century of subjection to Tang China.

Rise

Qutlugh was a descendant of the Ashina clan that had ruled the First Turkic Khaganate until its collapse and submission to the Tang dynasty in 630. In 682 he raised a revolt in the Ötüken region, beginning, according to the Orkhon inscriptions, with only seventeen followers. Through audacious raids he gathered the scattered Turkic clans, defeated rival claimants and, with the statesman Tonyukuk as his adviser and commander, established an independent khaganate.

Reign

From his base in the sacred Ötüken forest, Ilterish waged dozens of campaigns — the inscriptions credit him with forty-seven expeditions — against the Tang, the Toquz Oghuz, the Kyrgyz and other steppe peoples, restoring a realm stretching across much of Mongolia. He organised the revived state on the old model, with the khagan at Ötüken ruling through subordinate khans and tribal chiefs of the left and right wings.

Ilterish died in 691 and was succeeded by his younger brother Qapaghan Qaghan, under whom the khaganate reached its greatest extent. His sons, Bilge Khagan and Kul Tigin, later led the state, and the great runic stelae erected in the Orkhon valley in the 730s recount Ilterish's restoration as the founding deed of the Turkic nation.

Legacy

The revival under Ilterish ended China's half-century domination of the eastern steppe and created the polity whose Orkhon inscriptions provide the oldest substantial texts in any Turkic language. He is remembered in Turkish and Mongolian historiography as a national refounder, and his name — Kutlug, "blessed/fortunate" — is sometimes used in modern Turkish naming.
