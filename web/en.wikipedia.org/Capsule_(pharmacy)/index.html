Capsule (pharmacy)
RouteOral (mainly)
Shell materialsGelatin, HPMC, starch
TypesHard two-piece, softgel
Patented1834 (Mothes and DuBlanc)

In pharmacy, a capsule is a dosage form consisting of a shell, usually made of gelatin or a plant-based polymer, enclosing a drug in powder, granule, liquid, or semi-solid form, intended to be swallowed. Capsules mask unpleasant tastes and odours, protect ingredients from light and moisture, and allow precise dosing.

Types

Hard capsules have two telescoping halves filled with dry material and are widely used for powders. Soft capsules (softgels), invented commercially in the 1930s, are one-piece hermetically sealed shells containing liquids or suspensions, common for oils and fat-soluble vitamins. Capsules dissolve in the stomach, releasing their contents for absorption.

Manufacture

Modern production uses automated dipping, drying, and filling machinery producing standardised sizes, from size 000 (largest, for veterinary use) to size 5 (smallest).
