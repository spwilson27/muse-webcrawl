Sima Yan (236 – 17 May 290), courtesy name Anshi, was the founder of the Jin dynasty of China and reigned as Emperor Wu of Jin. The grandson of Sima Yi and son of Sima Zhao, he forced the last emperor of Cao Wei, Cao Huan, to abdicate in 266 and proclaimed the Jin dynasty. In 280 his forces conquered Eastern Wu, ending the Three Kingdoms period and reunifying China for the first time since the fall of the Han dynasty.

Early life and accession

Sima Yan was the eldest son of Sima Zhao, the regent who effectively controlled the Wei state, and a grandson of Sima Yi, who had seized power in the coup of 249. When Sima Zhao died in 265, Sima Yan inherited his position and the title King of Jin. Within months he compelled Cao Huan to abdicate in a ritual modelled on earlier dynastic transitions, ascending the throne in early 266.

Reunification of China

After consolidating his rule, Emperor Wu turned to the conquest of Eastern Wu, the last of the Three Kingdoms, ruled by the tyrannical Sun Hao. In the winter of 279–280 Jin armies attacked along several routes, with Wang Jun's fleet descending the Yangtze from Sichuan. Wu resistance collapsed, Sun Hao surrendered at Jianye (Nanjing), and China was reunified in 280, beginning the era sometimes called the Taikang prosperity.

Reign and succession

Emperor Wu enfeoffed many of his relatives as princes with substantial military commands, hoping to secure the dynasty, though the arrangement later fuelled civil war. He is also remembered for extravagance in later years, maintaining a vast harem. Despite advice to the contrary he named his intellectually disabled son Sima Zhong as heir; after Emperor Wu's death in 290, the succession triggered the War of the Eight Princes, which devastated the dynasty he had founded.

Family

His first empress, Yang Yan, and her successor Yang Zhi were both from the Hongnong Yang clan. His son Sima Zhong succeeded him as Emperor Hui; another son, Sima Wei, became one of the Eight Princes.
