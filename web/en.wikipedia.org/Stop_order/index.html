Stop order

A stop order, also referred to as a stop-loss order, is an order to buy or sell a stock once the price reaches a specified value, known as the stop price. When the stop price is reached, a stop order becomes a market order and is executed at the next available price.

Stop-loss and stop-entry

A sell stop order is typically placed below the current price to limit a loss or protect a profit on a long position; this is the classic stop-loss. A buy stop order is placed above the current price, used to limit a loss on a short position or to enter a trade on a breakout. Because the order converts to a market order, the final execution price may differ from the stop price, especially in fast markets or when a stock gaps at the open.

Variants

  • Stop-limit order: becomes a limit order (not a market order) at the stop price, adding price protection at the risk of non-execution.
  • Trailing stop: the stop price is set at a fixed amount or percentage away from the market price and moves ("trails") as the price moves favorably.
  • Guaranteed stop: offered by some brokers, guarantees execution at the stop price for a premium.
