St Edward's Crown

St Edward's Crown
Made: 1661, for Charles II
Material: Gold, set with semi-precious stones
Weight: 4 lb 12 oz (2.15 kg)
Kept at: Jewel House, Tower of London

St Edward's Crown is the centrepiece of the Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom. Named after Saint Edward the Confessor, it is the crown traditionally used to crown English and British monarchs at their coronations.

History

The medieval crown believed to have belonged to Edward the Confessor was destroyed after the English Civil War. The present crown was made in 1661 for the coronation of Charles II by the royal goldsmith Robert Vyner. It was not used for over 200 years after 1689, when William III and subsequent monarchs chose lighter crowns, but the tradition was revived by George V in 1911.

Description and use

The crown is made of a solid gold frame decorated with tourmalines, white and rose diamonds, rubies, amethysts, sapphires, garnets, topazes and citrines, surmounted by a monde and cross pattée. Because of its weight of nearly 5 pounds, Elizabeth II joked that it could break the wearer's neck. Charles III was crowned with St Edward's Crown at Westminster Abbey on 6 May 2023.
