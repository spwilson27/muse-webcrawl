CSX Transportation

CSX Transportation (reporting mark CSXT) is a Class I freight railroad operating in the eastern United States and the Canadian provinces of Ontario and Quebec. It is the principal subsidiary of CSX Corporation, headquartered in Jacksonville, Florida, and operates approximately 20,000 route miles (32,000 km) of track.

History

CSX Transportation was formed through the consolidation of the Chessie System and Seaboard System railroads under CSX Corporation, itself created by the 1980 merger agreement between Chessie System, Inc. and Seaboard Coast Line Industries. In 1986 the Seaboard System was renamed CSX Transportation, and the Chessie System lines were merged into it in 1987.

In 1997, CSX and Norfolk Southern Railway agreed to divide Conrail, the government-created freight railroad in the Northeast; CSX acquired about 42 percent of Conrail's assets, including much of the former New York Central system, beginning operations over them in 1999.

Operations

The railroad moves freight including coal, chemicals, agricultural products, automobiles, and intermodal containers across 23 states east of the Mississippi River, the District of Columbia, and parts of Canada. Major hubs include Jacksonville, Atlanta, Nashville, Indianapolis, Chicago, and Baltimore. Locomotives and rolling stock carry the CSXT reporting mark and are typically painted in CSX's blue, grey, and yellow livery.
