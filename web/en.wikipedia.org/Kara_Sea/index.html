The Kara Sea (Russian: Ка́рское мо́ре, Karskoye more) is a marginal sea of the Arctic Ocean lying off the coast of Siberia, between the Barents Sea to the west and the Laptev Sea to the east. It is separated from the Barents Sea by the Kara Strait and the archipelago of Novaya Zemlya, and from the Laptev Sea by Severnaya Zemlya.

Geography

The sea receives the great Siberian rivers Ob, Yenisei, Pyasina and Taimyr, whose fresh water lowers its salinity and brings in large sediment loads; the Ob and Yenisei enter through long estuaries in the south. The Kara Sea is shallow — on average about 110 metres — and is covered by ice for much of the year, typically breaking up only in late summer. Major islands include Dikson, the Nordenskiöld Archipelago, Bely Island and Vize Island.

Navigation and resources

The sea forms part of the Northern Sea Route along Russia's Arctic coast, historically navigable only in a short summer window with icebreaker escort. It overlies extensions of the West Siberian petroleum basin; large offshore oil and gas discoveries, such as in the South Kara basin, have attracted exploration, though development is hampered by ice and remoteness.

Environmental concerns

During the Soviet era, the Kara Sea and its bays were used for dumping nuclear waste, including several submarine reactors, raising long-running concerns about radioactive contamination that Russian and international expeditions continue to monitor.
