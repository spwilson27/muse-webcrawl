Krishna (Sanskrit: कृष्ण, Kṛṣṇa) is a major deity in Hinduism. He is worshipped as the eighth avatar of the god Vishnu and also as the supreme God in his own right. Krishna is the god of protection, compassion, tenderness and love, and is one of the most popular and widely revered of all Indian divinities.

Life in tradition

According to the Mahabharata, the Harivamsa, the Bhagavata Purana and other texts, Krishna was born in Mathura to Devaki and Vasudeva, and was smuggled across the Yamuna River to Gokul to escape the tyrant king Kamsa, who sought to kill him. Raised by the cowherd couple Nanda and Yashoda in Vrindavan, the child Krishna is celebrated for his mischievous pranks — stealing butter, teasing the gopis (milkmaids) — and for miracles such as lifting Mount Govardhan to shelter the villagers from the storm god Indra. His youthful love for Radha became the central theme of later devotional poetry.

As an adult, Krishna slew Kamsa, founded the city of Dwarka, and played a decisive role in the Mahabharata war without taking up arms himself: he served as charioteer, friend and counsellor to the Pandava prince Arjuna. On the battlefield of Kurukshetra, in the dialogue known as the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna instructed Arjuna on duty (dharma), selfless action, devotion and the nature of the self — one of the most influential texts in Hindu philosophy.

Worship and festivals

Krishna devotion (bhakti) has inspired many traditions, including Gaudiya Vaishnavism, whose modern expression is the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON, "Hare Krishnas"). His birthday is celebrated as Krishna Janmashtami, marked by fasting, singing, and the Dahi Handi ritual of breaking suspended pots of curd; his play with colours is recalled at Holi. Major pilgrimage centres include Vrindavan, Mathura, Dwarka and the Jagannath temple at Puri.

Iconography

Krishna is typically depicted with dark blue or black skin, wearing a yellow dhoti and a peacock-feather crown, playing the bamboo flute (bansuri), often accompanied by cows or by Radha. As a child he is shown crawling or dancing on the serpent Kaliya; as the charioteer he holds the reins of Arjuna's horses.
