St Mel's Cathedral is a Roman Catholic cathedral church in Longford, Ireland. It is the cathedral of the Diocese of Ardagh and Clonmacnoise and is dedicated to Saint Mel, the fifth-century nephew and disciple of Saint Patrick who founded the diocese of Ardagh.

History

The cathedral was designed in a neoclassical style by John Bourke of Dublin. The foundation stone was laid in 1840 and the building was substantially complete by the 1850s, though work continued for decades; the tower and portico, designed by George Ashlin, were added later, and the cathedral was consecrated in 1893. Built of grey limestone, its interior is noted for its tall Corinthian columns and coffered ceiling, and it has been described as a "cathedral of the midlands".

Fire and restoration

In the early hours of Christmas Day 2009, a fire broke out in the cathedral and caused devastating damage: the roof collapsed, the interior was gutted, and many treasures were destroyed, although the historic St Mel's Crozier, a ninth-century relic housed in the cathedral museum, was recovered from the ruins. A major restoration project followed, led by architects and craftspeople from across Ireland. The restored cathedral reopened in December 2014, retaining the original columns and much of the nineteenth-century fabric while adding new stained glass by Kim En Joong and new liturgical furnishings. The restoration won several architectural awards.

St Mel's College

The cathedral stands beside St Mel's College, a diocesan secondary school founded in 1865, whose grounds include the cathedral precinct.
