I-beam

An I-beam, also known as an H-beam, universal beam, or rolled steel joist, is a beam with an I- or H-shaped cross-section. The horizontal elements of the cross-section are called flanges, and the vertical element is the web. I-beams are usually made of structural steel and serve as critical support trusses in construction and civil engineering.

Design

The I-shaped cross-section is highly efficient for carrying bending and shear loads in the plane of the web: most of the material is concentrated in the flanges, far from the neutral axis, which maximizes the second moment of area while the web resists shear. A disadvantage is poor resistance to torsion compared with closed sections.

Types

Common variants include the wide-flange W-beam used in North America, the European IPE and HE profiles, and timber or composite I-joists used in residential floor construction. Sizes are standardized and specified by depth and weight per unit length.
