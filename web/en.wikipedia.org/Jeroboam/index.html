Jeroboam

Reignc. 931 – c. 910 BCE (biblical chronology)
PredecessorSolomon (as king of Israel)
SuccessorNadab
HouseHouse of Jeroboam

Jeroboam I (Hebrew: יָרָבְעָם‎) was, according to the Hebrew Bible, the first king of the northern Kingdom of Israel following the division of the United Monarchy. The Books of Kings describe how the ten northern tribes, rebelling against the heavy taxation of Solomon's son Rehoboam, made Jeroboam—the son of Nebat, an Ephraimite who had earlier been an official of Solomon—king over Israel, while Rehoboam retained Judah in the south.

Golden calves

Fearing that pilgrimages to the Temple in Jerusalem would draw his subjects' loyalty back to the southern kingdom, Jeroboam is said to have established alternative shrines at Bethel and Dan, setting up golden calves at each and instituting his own priesthood and festival calendar. In the Deuteronomistic history this act—"the sin of Jeroboam"—became the archetype of Israelite apostasy, invoked against every subsequent northern king.

Reign and legacy

The biblical account describes conflict with Judah, the curse of a man of God at Bethel, and the death of Jeroboam's son Abijah. Jeroboam reigned 22 years and was succeeded by his son Nadab, whose short reign ended in assassination, extinguishing Jeroboam's dynasty. Historicity of details is debated by scholars, though the existence of a northern kingdom separating from Judah in the late 10th century BCE is broadly consistent with the archaeological picture.
