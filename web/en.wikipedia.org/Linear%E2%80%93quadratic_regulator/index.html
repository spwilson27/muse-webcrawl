Linear–quadratic regulator

The linear–quadratic regulator (LQR) is a foundational method of optimal control theory for designing feedback controllers for linear dynamical systems. Given a linear system ẋ = Ax + Bu and a quadratic cost functional penalising state deviations and control effort, J = ∫(xᵀQx + uᵀRu)dt with Q positive semidefinite and R positive definite, the optimal control law is a linear state feedback u = −Kx.

Solution

The feedback gain is K = R−1BᵀP, where P is the unique positive semidefinite solution of the algebraic Riccati equation AᵀP + PA − PBR−1BᵀP + Q = 0. For the finite-horizon problem, P instead solves a differential Riccati equation integrated backwards from a terminal condition. Discrete-time versions exist for sampled-data systems.

Properties and use

Under standard stabilisability and detectability assumptions, the resulting closed-loop system is asymptotically stable, and the classical continuous-time LQR has guaranteed robustness margins (gain margin from ½ to infinity, and phase margin of at least 60° at the plant input). LQR is used widely in aerospace, robotics, and process control, and combined with a Kalman filter it yields the linear–quadratic–Gaussian (LQG) controller.
