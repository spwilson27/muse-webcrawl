Sobol sequence

Sobol sequences (also called LPτ sequences or (t, s)-sequences in base 2) are an example of quasi-random low-discrepancy sequences. They were first introduced by the Russian mathematician Ilya M. Sobol in 1967. The sequences generate points in the unit hypercube that fill space more uniformly than independent random points, improving the convergence of numerical integration.

The first points of a 2-D Sobol sequence fill the unit square evenly

Construction

Sobol sequences are built dimension by dimension using primitive polynomials over the field GF(2) and sets of "direction numbers" derived from them. Points are computed with bitwise exclusive-or (Gray code) operations, which make generation fast. Each dimension uses a different primitive polynomial; good choices of initial direction numbers — for example tables published for thousands of dimensions — are essential for uniformity in high dimensions.

Applications

Low-discrepancy sequences underpin the quasi-Monte Carlo method, which attains an integration error shrinking roughly as O((log N)s/N) rather than the O(1/√N) of ordinary Monte Carlo. Sobol sequences are widely used in financial engineering (option pricing, risk analysis), computer graphics global illumination, and uncertainty quantification.
