Ottawa ankle rules


Purpose: Decide whether an injured ankle or foot needs an X-ray
Developer: Ian Stiell and colleagues, Ottawa
Introduced: 1992

The Ottawa ankle rules are a set of clinical decision rules developed by emergency physician Ian Stiell and colleagues in Ottawa, Canada, to determine whether a patient with ankle or midfoot pain after an injury needs radiography to exclude a fracture. They are among the most thoroughly validated decision rules in medicine, with sensitivity approaching 100% for clinically significant fractures in adults.

The rules

An ankle X-ray series is required only if there is pain in the malleolar zone plus any of: bone tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of the lateral malleolus, bone tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of the medial malleolus, or inability to bear weight both immediately after the injury and in the emergency department (four steps). A foot X-ray is required only if there is midfoot pain plus tenderness at the base of the fifth metatarsal, tenderness at the navicular bone, or inability to bear weight. The rules do not apply to children under 18 (though a modified use is studied), intoxicated patients, or those with diminished sensation.

Impact

By safely ruling out fractures at the bedside, the rules reduce unnecessary X-rays by roughly 30–40%, cutting costs, radiation exposure and waiting times; they have been implemented in emergency departments worldwide.
