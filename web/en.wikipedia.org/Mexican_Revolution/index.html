Mexican Revolution

Date: 20 November 1910 – 1920 (conventionally)
Location: Mexico
Result: Constitution of 1917; end of the Porfiriato; revolutionary governments consolidated

The Mexican Revolution was a major armed struggle beginning in 1910 against the long dictatorship of Porfirio Díaz. It evolved into a multi-sided civil war that transformed Mexican society, produced the Constitution of 1917 — among the most progressive of its era, with land reform, labor rights, and restrictions on the Church — and cost an estimated 1–2 million lives.

Course of the conflict

Francisco I. Madero's call to arms (the Plan of San Luis Potosí) forced Díaz's resignation in 1911, but Madero was overthrown and murdered in Victoriano Huerta's 1913 coup. Venustiano Carranza's Constitutionalist Army, with generals Álvaro Obregón and Pancho Villa, defeated Huerta; Emiliano Zapata's Liberation Army of the South pressed agrarian demands under the Plan de Ayala. The victorious factions then fought each other: Obregón crushed Villa at Celaya (1915), Zapata was assassinated in 1919, and Carranza was killed in 1920 as Obregón took power.

Legacy

The revolution institutionalized itself in the party that became the PRI, which governed Mexico for most of the 20th century. Land redistribution (ejidos), secular education, and muralist art (Rivera, Orozco, Siqueiros) spread its ideals, and it remains foundational to Mexican national identity.
