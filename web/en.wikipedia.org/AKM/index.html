AKM

The AKM is a Soviet assault rifle designed by Mikhail Kalashnikov and adopted in 1959 as the standard service rifle of the Soviet Union. It is a modernised version of the AK-47, chambered for the 7.62×39mm cartridge, and is the most widely produced variant of the Kalashnikov family.

Improvements over the AK-47

The AKM's receiver is made from stamped and riveted sheet metal rather than a heavy milled forging, making the rifle lighter (about 3.1 kg unloaded) and cheaper to manufacture in large numbers. It added a rate-reducer in the trigger mechanism, a slanted muzzle compensator, an improved dust cover and rear sight, and provisions for a bayonet and night sights.

Service

The AKM replaced the AK-47 throughout the Warsaw Pact during the 1960s and was exported and licence-produced worldwide, seeing service in the Vietnam War and countless other conflicts. Tens of millions were made, and the type remains in use in many countries, gradually superseded in Russian service by the 5.45mm AK-74 and its successors.
