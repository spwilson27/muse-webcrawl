Nevada Semipalatinsk movement

The Nevada Semipalatinsk movement was an anti-nuclear protest movement in Kazakhstan, founded in 1989 to campaign for the closure of the Semipalatinsk Test Site, where the Soviet Union had conducted 456 nuclear tests between 1949 and 1989.

History

The movement was founded on 28 February 1989, days after a radioactive gas leak from an underground test, and was led by the writer Olzhas Suleimenov. It took its name from the twin test sites of Semipalatinsk (Kazakh SSR) and Nevada (USA), calling for an end to testing at both. Mass rallies drew hundreds of thousands of people. Under public pressure, testing at Semipalatinsk was suspended and, on 29 August 1991, Kazakh president Nursultan Nazarbayev formally closed the site — one of the first major victories of a popular anti-nuclear movement, and a model for international test-ban campaigning.
