Averroes

Averroes (Ibn Rushd)
Born: 14 April 1126, Córdoba, Al-Andalus
Died: 11 December 1198, Marrakesh
Fields: Philosophy, medicine, law, astronomy, physics

Ibn Rushd (Arabic: ابن رشد; 14 April 1126 – 11 December 1198), often Latinized as Averroes, was an Andalusian polymath and philosopher. A master of Aristotelian philosophy, Islamic philosophy, Islamic theology, Maliki law and jurisprudence, logic, psychology, politics, Arabic music theory, and the sciences of medicine, astronomy, geography, mathematics, and physics, he wrote more than 100 books and treatises during his lifetime.

Commentaries on Aristotle

Averroes is best known in the West for his extensive commentaries on the works of Aristotle, earning him the title "The Commentator" in Latin scholastic tradition. His commentaries were translated into Latin and Hebrew and profoundly influenced European philosophy, particularly at the University of Paris and in the movement known as Latin Averroism.

Philosophy

Averroes sought to reconcile Aristotelian philosophy with Islamic theology. In The Incoherence of the Incoherence (Tahafut al-Tahafut), he defended philosophy against the attacks of al-Ghazali's The Incoherence of the Philosophers. His doctrine of the unity of the intellect — that all human beings share a single active intellect — was controversial and was among the propositions condemned at Paris in 1270 and 1277.

Medicine

His medical encyclopedia, Kitab al-Kulliyat fi al-Tibb (known in Latin as the Colliget), was used in European medical education for centuries. He also served as court physician and chief judge (qadi) in Córdoba under the Almohad caliphate.
