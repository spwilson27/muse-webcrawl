Paedomorphosis

Paedomorphosis
FieldEvolutionary developmental biology
MechanismsNeoteny, progenesis, postdisplacement
OppositePeramorphosis

Paedomorphosis, also spelled pedomorphosis, is the retention by an adult organism of traits previously seen only in juveniles of its ancestors. It is a form of heterochrony — a change in the timing or rate of development — and is the opposite of peramorphosis.

Mechanisms

Paedomorphosis can arise through three principal mechanisms. In neoteny, somatic development is slowed while sexual maturation proceeds at the normal rate. In progenesis, development is truncated because sexual maturation is accelerated, ending growth early. In postdisplacement, the onset of a developmental trait is delayed relative to the ancestor.

Examples

The classic example is the axolotl, a Mexican salamander that remains aquatic and keeps its external gills throughout life, though it can be induced to metamorphose with thyroid hormones. Many other salamanders, including the mudpuppy and the olm, are paedomorphic. Paedomorphosis has also been proposed as important in the evolution of insects (where some female larviform beetles remain larva-like), in domesticated animals such as dogs, and — controversially, following Louis Bolk and Stephen Jay Gould — in human evolution, where adult humans retain juvenile ape features such as a flat face and large head relative to body size.
