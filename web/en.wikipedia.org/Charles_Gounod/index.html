Charles-François Gounod (17 June 1818 – 18 October 1893) was a French composer. He wrote twelve operas, of which the most popular has always been Faust (1859), along with his setting of the "Ave Maria" based on a piece by Bach.

Life

Gounod was born in Paris into an artistic and musical family. He studied at the Paris Conservatoire, where his teachers included Fromental Halévy and Pierre Zimmerman, and won the prestigious Prix de Rome in 1839. While in Rome he became deeply interested in sacred music, especially the polyphony of Palestrina; his lifelong tension between religious devotion and the theatre shaped his career. He briefly considered the priesthood before turning to opera.

Works

His early operas, including Sapho (1851) and La nonne sanglante (1854), had mixed success. Faust, based on Goethe's play, premiered in 1859 and became one of the most frequently performed operas worldwide. Roméo et Juliette (1867) also entered the standard repertoire. Gounod wrote much sacred music, including masses and oratorios such as La rédemption and Mors et vita, and many songs. His short "Funeral March of a Marionette" (1872) later became famous as the theme to the television series Alfred Hitchcock Presents.

Style and influence

Gounod's melodic gift and refined orchestration influenced a generation of French composers including Bizet, Saint-Saëns and Massenet. His Méditation sur le Premier Prélude de Piano de S. Bach — the melody superimposed on Bach's C major prelude and later fitted with the "Ave Maria" text — remains one of the best-known sacred pieces in the repertoire.
