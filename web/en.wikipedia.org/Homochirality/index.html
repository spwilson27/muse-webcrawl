Homochirality

Homochirality is a uniform handedness, or chirality, of molecular units. In biology, the amino acids that make up proteins are almost exclusively left-handed (L-amino acids), while the sugars in DNA, RNA, and metabolism are right-handed (D-sugars). This uniformity is a distinctive feature of life on Earth, since abiotic chemistry typically produces equal mixtures (racemates) of both forms.

Origin

How life became homochiral is an unsolved problem in the origin of life. Proposed mechanisms include amplification of a small initial imbalance through autocatalysis (as in the Soai reaction), chiral influences such as circularly polarized light in star-forming regions, the weak nuclear force's parity violation, and delivery of enantioenriched organic molecules by meteorites — the Murchison meteorite contains amino acids with slight L-excesses. Experiments such as "chiral induced spin selectivity" and crystallization processes (Viedma ripening) show routes by which a near-racemic mixture can evolve to a single handedness.

Significance

Homochirality is functionally important: enzymes and receptors are stereospecific, so a mixed-handedness biochemistry would not work coherently. It is also considered a potential biosignature in the search for extraterrestrial life.
