Scientific American, informally abbreviated SciAm, is an American popular science magazine. Founded by inventor and publisher Rufus Porter in 1845 as a four-page weekly newspaper, it is the oldest continuously published monthly magazine in the United States, and has brought articles by notable scientists — including many Nobel laureates — to a general audience.

History

Porter sold the magazine in 1846 to Alfred Ely Beach and Orson Desaix Munn, whose Munn & Co. ran it for over a century and used it to promote inventions and operate a patent agency. In the 20th century the magazine shifted from inventions and industry toward authoritative articles written by scientists themselves, a policy consolidated under editor Dennis Flanagan from 1947. It is now published by Springer Nature, with international editions in many languages.

Content and columns

Long-running features have included Martin Gardner's "Mathematical Games" column (1957–1981), which popularised recreational mathematics, and the "Amateur Scientist" column. Scientific American articles frequently present new research in physics, biology, medicine, and technology in depth for educated lay readers, and the magazine has won numerous journalism awards.
