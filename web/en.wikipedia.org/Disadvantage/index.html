Disadvantage

This article is about the general concept of an unfavourable circumstance. For social disadvantage, see social exclusion.

A disadvantage is an unfavourable circumstance or condition that reduces the chances of success or effectiveness of a person, group, or course of action. It is the opposite of an advantage. The concept appears across many fields, from strategy and sport to social policy and law.

In social policy

In public policy, "disadvantage" commonly refers to the comparatively unfavourable circumstances experienced by groups facing poverty, poor housing, low educational attainment, disability, or discrimination. Policy concepts such as social disadvantage and multiple deprivation describe how such factors cluster and reinforce one another, and governments use area-based and individual measures to target support.

In games and sport

In competitive settings a disadvantage may arise from position, resources, or rules; handicapping systems are sometimes used to offset disadvantages between competitors of unequal ability. In chess, the disadvantage can refer to having the worse position, and specific opening gambits accept a material disadvantage in return for positional compensation.

In law and economics

Legal usage includes detriment or prejudice suffered by a party, and competition law addresses firms placed at a competitive disadvantage by unfair practices.
