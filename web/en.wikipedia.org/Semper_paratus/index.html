Semper paratus

Semper Paratus (Latin for "Always Ready") is the motto of the United States Coast Guard. "Semper Paratus" is also the name of the official Coast Guard song (march), composed in 1922 by Captain Francis Saltus Van Boskerck and given music in 1927 by Alfred Henry Miles and Charles A. Zimmerman.

As the Coast Guard motto

The exact origin of the phrase as the Coast Guard's motto is obscure. The earliest known use traces to the Revenue Cutter Service in the 1890s: a 1890s issue of a Revenue Cutter Service publication used "Semper Paratus" as a heading, and the phrase appeared on the ensign of the Revenue Cutter Service. It was officially adopted when the Coast Guard was formed in 1915 from the Revenue Cutter Service and the Life-Saving Service. The motto reflects the service's layered missions—search and rescue, law enforcement, environmental protection, and military readiness.

Other uses

The phrase is used as a motto by several other organizations, including:

  • The 16th Infantry Regiment of the United States Army, whose regimental song is derived from it.
  • Various fire departments, police units and schools in the United States and elsewhere.
  • It is also the title of a 1940 Hardy Boys novel and appears in heraldry of some Canadian and British units.

The march

The first verse of Van Boskerck's song begins "From Aztec Shore to Arctic Zone, / To Europe and Far East..." and the chorus proclaims "We're always ready for the call." The song is performed at Coast Guard ceremonies and graduations at the United States Coast Guard Academy.
