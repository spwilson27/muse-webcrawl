Georges Prosper Remi (22 May 1907 – 3 March 1983), known by the pen name Hergé (from the French pronunciation of his reversed initials, R.G.), was a Belgian cartoonist. He is best known for creating The Adventures of Tintin, the series of bande dessinée albums which remains one of the most popular European comics of the twentieth century.

Tintin

Hergé introduced the boy reporter Tintin and his dog Snowy in Tintin in the Land of the Soviets (1929), serialized in the children's supplement of the newspaper Le Vingtième Siècle. Over the following five decades he produced 24 albums, including The Blue Lotus, The Secret of the Unicorn, Destination Moon, and Tintin in Tibet, working with the Studios Hergé, founded in 1950. The series has been translated into more than 70 languages and sold over 200 million copies.

Style

Hergé pioneered the ligne claire ("clear line") drawing style, characterized by clean, uniform outlines, flat colors, and meticulously researched backgrounds combined with simply drawn, iconic characters. His later albums were extensively researched, and he revised several early works for republication in color.

Legacy

The Hergé Museum opened in Louvain-la-Neuve in 2009. Although aspects of his early work — notably its colonial-era depictions and his publication in German-occupied Belgium during the Second World War — remain subjects of debate, Hergé is widely regarded as one of the founders of the modern European comic album.
