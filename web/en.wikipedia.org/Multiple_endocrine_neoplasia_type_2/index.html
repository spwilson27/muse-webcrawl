Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2

Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN2) is an autosomal dominant hereditary cancer syndrome caused by gain-of-function mutations in the RET proto-oncogene. It is characterised by the near-universal development of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), together with a substantial risk of pheochromocytoma and, in one subtype, primary hyperparathyroidism.

Subtypes

MEN2A (the most common subtype) combines medullary thyroid carcinoma, pheochromocytoma in roughly half of cases, and parathyroid hyperplasia or adenoma in a minority. MEN2B features earlier and more aggressive MTC, pheochromocytoma, mucosal neuromas of the lips and tongue, intestinal ganglioneuromas and a marfanoid body habitus, but not hyperparathyroidism. Familial medullary thyroid carcinoma is regarded as a variant of MEN2A in which MTC occurs alone. MEN2B is most often caused by the RET M918T mutation.

Diagnosis and management

Genetic testing for RET mutations is recommended for all patients with medullary thyroid carcinoma and for at-risk relatives. Because MTC arises from calcitonin-producing C cells and can metastasise early, mutation carriers are offered prophylactic thyroidectomy in childhood, timed according to the risk level of the specific mutation, plus lifelong screening for pheochromocytoma and hyperparathyroidism.
