DNA nanotechnology

DNA nanotechnology is the design and manufacture of artificial nucleic acid structures for technological uses, exploiting the predictable base-pairing of DNA as an engineering material rather than as genetic information.

History

The field was founded by Nadrian Seeman in the early 1980s, who proposed building three-dimensional lattices from branched DNA junctions. In 2006 Paul Rothemund introduced DNA origami, folding a long scaffold strand with short staple strands into arbitrary shapes.

Approaches

Structural DNA nanotechnology builds static assemblies such as tiles, polyhedra, and origami. Dynamic DNA nanotechnology uses strand displacement to make walkers, logic circuits, and molecular machines.

Applications

Proposed uses include drug delivery vehicles, biosensors, templates for nanoelectronics, and programmable materials.
