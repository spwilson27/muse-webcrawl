Psychohistory (fictional)

Psychohistory is a fictional science in Isaac Asimov's Foundation series, combining history, sociology and mathematical statistics to make general predictions about the future behaviour of very large groups of people. It was first introduced in the short stories that became the novel Foundation (1951).

Premise

Within the series, the mathematician Hari Seldon develops psychohistory during the decline of the Galactic Empire. Because the behaviour of a sufficiently large population is statistically predictable — much as the behaviour of gas molecules is predictable in aggregate — Seldon forecasts the fall of the Empire and devises a plan to shorten the ensuing dark age from 30,000 years to 1,000 by establishing the Foundation.

Axioms and limitations

Psychohistory requires that the population studied be large enough for statistical treatment and that the population remain unaware of the predictions, so as not to alter its behaviour. It cannot predict the actions of individuals. The appearance of the Mule, a mutant with emotion-controlling powers, breaks Seldon's predictions and becomes a central plot point of Foundation and Empire.

Influence

Asimov said he modelled Seldon's science partly on the real discipline of psychohistory and on the fall of the Roman Empire as described by Edward Gibbon. The concept has been cited in discussions of cliometrics, big data and predictive analytics.
