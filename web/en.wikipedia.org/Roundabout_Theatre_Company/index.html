Roundabout Theatre Company

The Roundabout Theatre Company is a non-profit theatre company based in New York City. Founded in 1965 by Gene Feist and Elizabeth Owens, it has grown from a small off-Broadway troupe performing in a supermarket basement in Chelsea into one of the largest theatrical institutions in the United States, operating several Broadway and off-Broadway venues and presenting revivals of classic plays and musicals alongside new works.

History

Roundabout began in 1965 in the basement of a Chelsea supermarket. It moved through several homes, including a space on 26th Street, before becoming a Broadway producer. Under artistic director Todd Haimes, who led the company from the 1980s until his death in 2023, Roundabout rescued and renovated historic theatres, including the former Selwyn Theatre, reopened in 2000 as the American Airlines Theatre (later renamed the Todd Haimes Theatre), and Studio 54, which it acquired and operates as a Broadway house.

Venues and productions

The company operates the Todd Haimes Theatre and Studio 54 on Broadway, the Stephen Sondheim Theatre (managed for productions), and off-Broadway's Laura Pels Theatre in the Harold and Miriam Steinberg Center for Theatre, which also houses the Black Box Theatre. Notable productions include revivals of Cabaret, A Man for All Seasons, The Pajama Game, Anything Goes, She Loves Me, and the premiere of The Humans (transferred from off-Broadway). Roundabout has won numerous Tony Awards, and runs extensive education and artist-development programmes, including training for early-career technicians from underrepresented communities.
