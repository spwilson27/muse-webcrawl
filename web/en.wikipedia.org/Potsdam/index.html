Potsdam is the capital and largest city of the German state of Brandenburg. It lies on the Havel River immediately southwest of Berlin and is famous for its palaces and parks, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site as the largest such ensemble in Germany.

History

Founded as a Slavic settlement, Potsdam became a residence of the electors of Brandenburg and then of the Prussian kings. Under Frederick the Great it was transformed into a baroque residence city, crowned by the rococo palace of Sanssouci (1747) and the vast Neues Palais (1769). The Glienicke Bridge gained Cold War fame as a site of spy exchanges between East and West.

In July–August 1945 the Cecilienhof Palace in Potsdam hosted the Potsdam Conference, at which the leaders of the Soviet Union, the United States and the United Kingdom settled the administration of defeated Germany and postwar borders. During the division of Germany, Potsdam lay in the GDR, directly against the Berlin Wall; since reunification in 1990 it has again been a state capital and a centre for science, with three universities and many research institutes, and for film at the historic Babelsberg Studios, among the oldest large-scale film studios in the world.
