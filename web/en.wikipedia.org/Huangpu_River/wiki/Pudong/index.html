Pudong

Pudong, officially the Pudong New Area, is a district of Shanghai located east of the Huangpu River, across from the historic city center of Puxi. Once largely farmland, Pudong was transformed after 1990 into one of China's principal financial and commercial hubs, home to the Lujiazui skyline of supertall skyscrapers.

Pudong New Area
Country: China
Municipality: Shanghai
Area: about 1,210 km²
Population: over 5 million
Established as New Area: 1993 (announced 1990)

History

Until the late 20th century Pudong ("east of the Huangpu") was relatively undeveloped compared with Puxi on the west bank, consisting of farmland, warehouses, and wharves. In April 1990 the Chinese government announced the development and opening of Pudong, making it the flagship of China's reform and opening-up policies for the decade. Special economic policies attracted massive domestic and foreign investment.

Lujiazui and landmarks

The Lujiazui Finance and Trade Zone, directly across the river from the Bund, contains the Oriental Pearl Radio & Television Tower (1994), the Jin Mao Tower (1999), the Shanghai World Financial Center (2008), and the Shanghai Tower (2015), at 632 metres the tallest building in China. Pudong also hosts the Shanghai Stock Exchange, the Shanghai International Airport at Pudong (PVG), the Shanghai Disneyland Resort (opened 2016), and the Lingang and Zhangjiang high-technology and free-trade zones.

Administration

Pudong is a sub-provincial-level district of Shanghai with expanded economic management powers; in 2009 it absorbed the former Nanhui District, greatly enlarging its territory to include the coastline and the Lingang New City development.
