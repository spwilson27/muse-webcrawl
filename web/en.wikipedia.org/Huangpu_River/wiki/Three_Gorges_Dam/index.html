Three Gorges Dam

The Three Gorges Dam is a hydroelectric gravity dam spanning the Yangtze River near Sandouping in Yichang, Hubei province, China. Completed in 2012 (with the ship lift finished in 2015), it is the world's largest power station in terms of installed capacity, at 22,500 megawatts, and one of the largest construction projects ever undertaken.

Design and construction

The dam body is about 2,335 metres long and 185 metres high, creating a reservoir stretching some 600 kilometres upstream toward Chongqing. Construction began in 1994; the reservoir's filling displaced more than a million people and submerged numerous towns, archaeological sites, and farmland. The project includes twin five-stage ship locks and a ship lift capable of raising vessels of up to 3,000 tonnes.

Purposes and controversies

Besides power generation, the dam was built to improve navigation on the Yangtze and to control the devastating floods that historically plagued the river's middle reaches. It has been controversial for its environmental effects — including habitat loss for species such as the baiji, erosion, and landslide risk — and for sedimentation that may reduce its effectiveness over time.
