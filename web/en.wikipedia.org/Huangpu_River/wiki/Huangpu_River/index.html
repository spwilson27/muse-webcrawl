Huangpu River

Huangpu River
Chinese: 黄浦江
Length: about 113 km (70 mi)
Location: Shanghai, China
Mouth: Yangtze River estuary

The Huangpu River is a river flowing through the centre of Shanghai, China. About 113 kilometres (70 miles) long, it is one of the last significant tributaries of the Yangtze River near its mouth at the East China Sea. The river divides Shanghai into two broad parts: Pudong to the east and Puxi to the west. Its name, literally "Yellow Bank River," is traditionally connected with Chunshen Jun (Huang Xie), a statesman of the Chu state in the Warring States period, for whom the river is said to have been named.

Course

The Huangpu is fed by waters from the Tai Lake basin, reaching Shanghai through Dianshan Lake and a network of waterways in the city's western districts. From there it flows northward through the urban core, bending eastward past the historic Bund waterfront and the Lujiazui financial district in Pudong, before continuing to Wusongkou, where it joins the Yangtze estuary. Its major tributary is the Suzhou Creek, which meets the Huangpu just north of the Bund.

History and significance

For centuries the river has been Shanghai's principal waterway, supporting shipping, trade, and the growth of one of the world's largest ports. During the treaty-port era, the western bank became lined with the foreign concessions' grand commercial buildings along the Bund, while shipyards, wharves, and warehouses spread along both shores. In the late twentieth and twenty-first centuries, redevelopment transformed much of the waterfront: the Lujiazui towers — including the Oriental Pearl Tower, Jin Mao Tower, Shanghai World Financial Center, and Shanghai Tower — rose opposite the Bund across a bend of the river.

Crossings and transport

The Huangpu is crossed by numerous bridges, including the Nanpu, Lupu, Yangpu, and Xupu bridges, and by a series of road tunnels and Shanghai Metro lines. Ferries still carry passengers and cyclists across the river, and sightseeing boats cruise the stretch between the Bund and Lujiazui, especially at night when both skylines are illuminated. The river remains a working channel for cargo vessels as well as a source of much of the city's raw water supply.
