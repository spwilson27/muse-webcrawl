Suzhou Creek

Suzhou Creek (Chinese: 苏州河), also called the Wusong River (吴淞江) in its upper course, is a river in China that passes through the centre of Shanghai. About 125 kilometres long, it rises at Lake Tai near Suzhou in Jiangsu province and joins the Huangpu River at the northern end of the Bund, just before the Huangpu itself flows into the Yangtze.

History

The Wusong was once the principal channel by which Lake Tai drained to the sea and carried Shanghai's trade with the interior; the Huangpu was originally its tributary until Ming-dynasty dredging works in the early 15th century reversed their relative importance. In the treaty-port era the creek's name in English shifted from the Wusong to Suzhou Creek, reflecting its role as the route to Suzhou. Its banks were lined with mills, warehouses, and factories, and it formed part of the boundary between the foreign concessions.

Modern condition

By the late 20th century Suzhou Creek was heavily polluted, notorious for its dark colour and smell. A major rehabilitation programme begun in 1998 cleaned the water substantially, and the creek's banks have since been redeveloped with promenades, housing, and arts districts in converted warehouses, notably around Moganshan Road.
