Shanghai

Shanghai (Chinese: 上海) is a direct-administered municipality and the most populous urban area in China, with a population of roughly 29 million in the municipality as of the early 2020s. It lies on the southern estuary of the Yangtze River, where the Huangpu River flows through its centre and divides the historic Puxi districts from the modern financial district of Pudong.

Geography and the Huangpu

The city sits on the Yangtze River Delta, mostly on flat alluvial plain only a few metres above sea level. The Huangpu River, a 113-kilometre tributary of the Yangtze, winds through the city past the Bund and Lujiazui, and is joined by Suzhou Creek near the northern end of the Bund. Shanghai's port, spread along the Huangpu, the Yangtze estuary, and the offshore Yangshan islands, is the busiest container port in the world.

History

Shanghai grew from a fishing village into a county town in 1292 and a walled city in the Ming dynasty. After the Treaty of Nanking (1842) it became one of the first treaty ports open to foreign trade, hosting British, French, and American concessions that shaped the architecture of the Bund and the French Concession. In the 20th century it was a centre of industry, finance, and revolutionary politics — the Chinese Communist Party was founded there in 1921. Following reform and opening, and especially the 1990 decision to develop Pudong, Shanghai re-emerged as a global financial and commercial centre.

Economy and culture

Shanghai is China's centre for finance, trade, shipping, and innovation, hosting the Shanghai Stock Exchange and the headquarters of many state and private enterprises. It is known for Shanghainese cuisine, Shikumen lane houses, and landmarks such as Yu Garden, the Oriental Pearl Tower, and the Shanghai Tower.
