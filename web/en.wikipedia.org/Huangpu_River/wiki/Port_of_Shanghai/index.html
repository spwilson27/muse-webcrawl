Port of Shanghai

The Port of Shanghai is the world's busiest container port, a title it has held since 2010 when it surpassed the Port of Singapore. It is situated in and around the municipality of Shanghai, China, at the mouth of the Yangtze River, and includes facilities along the Huangpu River, the Yangtze estuary, and the deep-water Yangshan Port offshore.

Facilities

The port is operated by Shanghai International Port Group (SIPG). Its principal terminals include the Waigaoqiao terminals on the southern bank of the Yangtze estuary, the older terminals along the Huangpu River, and the Yangshan Deep Water Port, built on islands in Hangzhou Bay and connected to the mainland by the 32.5-kilometre Donghai Bridge, which opened in 2005 to allow the largest container ships to berth in deep water. Yangshan's Phase IV, opened in 2017, is one of the largest automated container terminals in the world.

Throughput

The port has handled more than 40 million TEUs (twenty-foot equivalent units) of containers annually in recent years, reaching around 49 million TEUs in 2023, and total cargo throughput of several hundred million tonnes. Its growth mirrors Shanghai's rise as a global trading and manufacturing hub following China's economic reforms.

History

Shanghai grew as a treaty port after 1842, and its wharves along the Bund and the Huangpu long handled a large share of China's foreign trade. Modern expansion began in the 1990s with the development of Waigaoqiao and accelerated with the Yangshan project in the 2000s.
