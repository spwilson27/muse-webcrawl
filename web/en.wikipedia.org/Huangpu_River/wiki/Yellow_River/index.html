Yellow River

The Yellow River (Chinese: Huáng Hé) is the second-longest river in China and the sixth-longest in the world, at an estimated length of 5,464 kilometres. It originates in the Bayan Har Mountains of Qinghai province on the Tibetan Plateau and flows eastward through nine provinces before emptying into the Bohai Sea near Dongying, Shandong.

Name and loess

The river takes its name from the vast quantities of yellow loess sediment it carries from the Loess Plateau, which give its waters their distinctive colour and enrich (and burden) its lower course. So much sediment settles in the slow-flowing lower reaches that the riverbed has risen above the surrounding plain, making the river a "hanging river" confined by levees.

History

The Yellow River basin is often called the cradle of Chinese civilisation, home to early dynastic capitals and cultures such as Yangshao. Yet the river is also known as "China's Sorrow" for its catastrophic floods, among the deadliest natural disasters in history, including those of 1887 and 1931, and for dramatic course changes that shifted its mouth by hundreds of kilometres between the Bohai Sea and the Yellow Sea.
