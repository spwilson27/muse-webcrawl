Lujiazui

Lujiazui (Chinese: 陆家嘴) is a locality in Pudong, Shanghai, on the eastern bank of the Huangpu River, directly across from the Bund. Since the early 1990s it has been developed as Shanghai's principal financial district and is home to one of the most recognisable skylines in the world.

Development

In 1990 the Chinese government announced the development and opening of Pudong, and Lujiazui was designated a finance and trade zone — the only one in China named for finance. Land that had held warehouses, factories, and housing was transformed within two decades into a dense cluster of office towers. The district is organised around a circular pedestrian bridge and wide avenues beneath the elevated ring road.

Landmarks

Lujiazui contains three of China's best-known supertall skyscrapers: the Oriental Pearl Radio & Television Tower (468 m, completed 1994), the Jin Mao Tower (421 m, 1999), the Shanghai World Financial Center (492 m, 2008), and the Shanghai Tower (632 m, 2015), the tallest building in China. The Shanghai Stock Exchange, the China Financial Information Center, and the headquarters of many banks and insurers are located in the district.

Transport

The district is served by Lujiazui station on Shanghai Metro Line 2, by road tunnels beneath the Huangpu, and by the Bund Sightseeing Tunnel linking it to the Bund.
