The Bund

The Bund (Chinese: 外滩, Wàitān) is a waterfront area in central Shanghai, running along the western bank of the Huangpu River in Huangpu District. Facing the modern skyscrapers of Lujiazui in Pudong across the river, it is one of the most famous tourist destinations in Shanghai and is protected as a historic district.

History

The name "bund" derives from an Anglo-Indian term for an embanked waterfront. After Shanghai became a treaty port in 1842, the area developed as the centre of the British and later International Settlement: banks, trading houses, consulates, clubs, and hotels lined the embankment in grand Western styles ranging from neoclassical to art deco. Notable buildings include the Custom House (1927) with its clock tower, the former HSBC Building (1923), the Bank of China Building, and the Peace Hotel.

Present day

The Bund's flood walls and elevated promenade, rebuilt in the 1990s and again for Expo 2010, give views across the Huangpu to the Oriental Pearl Tower and the towers of Lujiazui. At its northern end, near the confluence of Suzhou Creek with the Huangpu, stands the Monument to the People's Heroes and the Waibaidu (Garden) Bridge, Shanghai's first steel truss bridge.
