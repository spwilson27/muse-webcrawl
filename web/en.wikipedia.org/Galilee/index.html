Galilee

Galilee (Hebrew: הַגָּלִיל, HaGalil; Arabic: الجليل, al-Jalil) is a region in northern Israel and southern Lebanon, traditionally divided into Upper Galilee, Lower Galilee, and Western Galilee. It is bounded by the Mediterranean Sea to the west, the Jezreel Valley to the south, the Jordan Valley and Sea of Galilee to the east, and the Litani River to the north.

Geography

Upper Galilee is mountainous, rising to Mount Meron at 1,208 metres, the highest point in Israel within its internationally recognized territory. Lower Galilee consists of lower ranges separated by broad valleys. The region's principal lake is the Sea of Galilee (Lake Kinneret), Israel's largest freshwater reservoir. Major cities include Nazareth, Tiberias, Safed, Acre, and Haifa (on its southern edge).

History

Galilee was inhabited from prehistoric times and appears in ancient Egyptian records. In the biblical period it was allotted to the tribes of Naphtali, Asher, Zebulun, and Issachar. It is best known as the setting for much of the life and ministry of Jesus of Nazareth, who grew up in Nazareth and preached around the Sea of Galilee. After the Jewish revolts against Rome, Galilee became a center of Jewish scholarship; the Mishnah and the Jerusalem Talmud were compiled there, and Safed later became a center of Kabbalah. The region passed through Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk, and Ottoman rule before becoming part of British Mandatory Palestine and, in 1948, the State of Israel.

Demographics

Today Galilee has a mixed population of Jews, Muslim and Christian Arabs, and Druze, and is known for agriculture, tourism, and religious pilgrimage.
