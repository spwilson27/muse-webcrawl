Pattern formation

Pattern formation is the process by which initially equivalent cells or materials acquire complex spatial organisation. In developmental biology it explains how a fertilised egg generates the organised structures of a body; in physics and chemistry it covers stripes, spots, spirals, and waves arising from spontaneous symmetry breaking.

Mechanisms

Alan Turing's 1952 paper "The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis" proposed that two interacting morphogens — an activator and an inhibitor diffusing at different rates — can produce stationary patterns such as animal coat markings. Other mechanisms include gradients of morphogen concentration (positional information), cell adhesion differences, and mechanical forces.

Examples

  • Vertebrate limb digits and feather/hair follicle spacing
  • Zebra stripes and leopard spots
  • Reaction–diffusion in the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction
  • Snowflake growth and other crystal patterns
  • Vegetation stripes in arid landscapes
