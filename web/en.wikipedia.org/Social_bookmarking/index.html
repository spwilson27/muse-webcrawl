Social bookmarking

Social bookmarking is an online service which allows users to add, annotate, edit, and share bookmarks of web documents. Unlike file sharing, social bookmarking does not save the resources themselves, only bookmarks that reference them. Descriptions may be added in the form of metadata, comments, and user-assigned keywords called tags, whose collective organisation is known as a folksonomy.

A social bookmarking feed with user-supplied tags

History

Early services such as itList and Backflip appeared in the late 1990s. The concept was popularised by Delicious (founded 2003 as del.icio.us), which pioneered tagging and tag clouds. Later services include Diigo, Pinboard, and the social-news offshoots Digg and Reddit, in which bookmarked links are ranked by community voting. Pinterest adapted the idea to image-based "pinning".

Advantages and criticism

Social bookmarking lets users organise links accessibly from any device, discover resources through other users' collections, and create emergent classification through tags. Criticisms include the inconsistency and ambiguity of free-form tags, susceptibility to spam links, and the impermanence of services — the shutdown or degradation of Delicious stranded many collections and prompted migration to paid alternatives such as Pinboard.
