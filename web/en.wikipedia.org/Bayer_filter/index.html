Bayer filter

Bayer filter
TypeColour filter array
InventorBryce Bayer (Eastman Kodak)
Patented1976

A Bayer filter mosaic is a colour filter array for arranging RGB colour filters on a square grid of photosensors. Its particular arrangement of colour filters is used in most single-chip digital image sensors in cameras, camcorders and scanners to create a colour image. The filter pattern has 50% green, 25% red and 25% blue, hence it is also called BGGR, RGBG, GRBG or RGGB, depending on the exact layout.

History and principle

The array is named after its inventor, Bryce Bayer of Eastman Kodak (US patent 3,971,065, filed 1975). Because the human eye is most sensitive to green light and luminance detail, the pattern samples green at twice the rate of red and blue. Each photosite records only one colour; the full-resolution colour image is reconstructed by an interpolation process called demosaicing.

Demosaicing and artefacts

Demosaicing algorithms range from simple bilinear interpolation to sophisticated edge-aware methods. The subsampling of colours can produce artefacts such as false colour and moiré on fine patterns, which some cameras counter with optical low-pass (anti-aliasing) filters, at some cost in sharpness.

Alternatives

Alternative designs include the X-Trans pattern used by Fujifilm, RGBW arrays, the Foveon sensor (which stacks colour layers), and three-sensor beam-splitter cameras. The Bayer pattern nevertheless remains by far the most widely used.
