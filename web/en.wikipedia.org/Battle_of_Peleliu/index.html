The Battle of Peleliu, codenamed Operation Stalemate II by the United States, took place in the Pacific Theater of World War II between September and November 1944. U.S. Marines of the 1st Marine Division, and later Army soldiers of the 81st Infantry Division, fought to capture the airfield on the small coral island of Peleliu in the Palau group.

Background

Peleliu was assaulted to protect the flank of the planned invasion of the Philippines and to seize its airfield. Major General William Rupertus predicted the island would fall in four days. The Japanese commander, Colonel Kunio Nakagawa, had abandoned beach defence in favour of an elaborate in-depth system of caves and pillboxes in the coral ridges of the Umurbrogol high ground in the island's north — a model for the later defences of Iwo Jima and Okinawa.

The battle

The Marines landed on 15 September 1944 and seized the airfield within days, enduring a fierce armoured counterattack across it. The battle then degenerated into a grinding struggle for the Umurbrogol Pocket — the "Bloody Nose Ridge" — where interlocking caves were reduced one by one with flamethrowers, demolitions and point-blank artillery. Temperatures above 46 °C (115 °F), water shortages and casualties approaching 40 percent in some regiments exhausted the 1st Marine Division, which was relieved by the Army's 81st Division. Organised resistance ended on 27 November; Nakagawa burned his regimental colours and took his own life.

Aftermath

The United States suffered roughly 10,000 casualties, including about 2,300 killed; nearly all of the approximately 10,900 Japanese defenders died, and only a handful chose capture — a small group of holdouts did not surrender until 1947. Because the Philippines landings succeeded without needing Peleliu's airfield, the battle's strategic value has been debated ever since, while its ferocity made it one of the most bitterly contested small-island fights of the Pacific War.
