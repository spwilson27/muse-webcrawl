Great Canadian flag debate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Canadian flag debate (or Great Flag Debate) was a national controversy in 1964 over the design of a new official flag for Canada. Prime Minister Lester B. Pearson had pledged to give Canada a distinctive national flag in time for the 1967 centennial, replacing the Canadian Red Ensign, which had served as the de facto national flag but retained British symbols.

The debate

The proposal split the country. Supporters of the Red Ensign, led by Conservative leader John Diefenbaker and veterans' groups, saw the change as severing ties with Britain and dishonouring war dead who had fought under the old flag. Pearson's government first favoured a design by Alan Beddoe — three red maple leaves between blue bars, the "Pearson Pennant" — but it was widely mocked. Parliament debated bitterly for weeks; the government finally imposed closure and referred the choice to an all-party committee, which, on the suggestion of MP John Matheson, selected the single-maple-leaf design drawn by George Stanley, inspired by the flag of the Royal Military College of Canada.

Outcome

The House of Commons adopted the new flag on 15 December 1964, and the Senate two days later. Queen Elizabeth II proclaimed it on 28 January 1965, and the red-and-white Maple Leaf flag was raised on Parliament Hill on 15 February 1965 — now commemorated as National Flag of Canada Day. The debate is remembered as a defining episode in the formation of a post-colonial Canadian identity.
