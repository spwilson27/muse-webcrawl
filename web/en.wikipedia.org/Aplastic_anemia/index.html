Aplastic anemia

SpecialtyHematology
SymptomsFatigue, pallor, infections, bleeding
CausesOften autoimmune; drugs, chemicals, viruses, radiation; inherited syndromes

Aplastic anemia (also spelled aplastic anaemia) is a rare, serious blood disorder in which the bone marrow fails to produce sufficient new blood cells, resulting in pancytopenia — shortages of red cells, white cells, and platelets. It may develop suddenly or slowly and can be life-threatening.

Causes

Most acquired cases are idiopathic and are thought to result from an autoimmune attack on hematopoietic stem cells. Triggers include certain drugs (such as chloramphenicol), benzene exposure, radiation, and viral infections. Inherited forms occur in disorders such as Fanconi anemia.

Symptoms

Anemia causes fatigue and pallor; low white-cell counts lead to recurrent infections; low platelets cause easy bruising, bleeding gums, and petechiae.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis requires blood counts showing pancytopenia and a bone marrow biopsy demonstrating a markedly hypocellular marrow. Treatment includes immunosuppressive therapy (anti-thymocyte globulin with ciclosporin), the thrombopoietin agonist eltrombopag, transfusions, and — curatively for younger patients with a suitable donor — hematopoietic stem cell transplantation.
