Conquest of the Azores

The conquest of the Azores in 1583 was a Spanish amphibious campaign during the War of the Portuguese Succession, in which forces loyal to Philip II of Spain defeated the supporters of António, Prior of Crato, and their French allies, completing Spanish control of Portugal and its Atlantic empire.

Background

After Philip II was proclaimed king of Portugal in 1580, the Azores remained loyal to the rival claimant António. A French expedition supporting António was defeated at the Battle of Ponta Delgada in 1582 by a Spanish fleet under Álvaro de Bazán, Marquis of Santa Cruz, but the island of Terceira continued to resist.

The campaign

In the summer of 1583 Santa Cruz returned with a large fleet and an army of some 15,000 men. After a feint toward another island, Spanish troops landed near Angra on Terceira on 26 July and defeated the Portuguese and French defenders. The remaining islands submitted, and the conquest ended organised resistance to Philip's rule in Portugal.

Consequences

The victory secured the mid-Atlantic staging point used by the Spanish treasure fleets and confirmed the Iberian Union. The campaign is often cited as one of the earliest successful large-scale amphibious operations conducted at long range by a European power.
