Marie-Pierre Kœnig

Marie-Pierre Kœnig
Born: 10 October 1898, Caen, France
Died: 2 September 1970 (aged 71), Neuilly-sur-Seine
Allegiance: France, Free France
Rank: Général d'armée; Marshal of France (posthumous, 1984)

Marie-Pierre Kœnig (10 October 1898 – 2 September 1970) was a French general who commanded Free French forces in North Africa during the Second World War. He is best remembered for the defence of Bir Hakeim in 1942.

Career

Kœnig volunteered in the First World War and was decorated and wounded. Between the wars he served in Morocco and with the French forces in Norway in 1940. Refusing the armistice, he joined de Gaulle in London and fought in the Free French campaigns in Syria, then commanded the 1st Free French Brigade in Libya. At Bir Hakeim (May–June 1942) his roughly 3,700 men held off repeated Axis attacks for over two weeks, covering the British Eighth Army's retreat, before breaking out at night — an episode that made his name a symbol of French resistance.

Later service

He later commanded the French Forces of the Interior (FFI) in 1944, became military governor of Paris, and after the war commanded French occupation forces in Germany. Entering politics, he served as Minister of Defence in the 1950s in the governments of Pierre Mendès France and Edgar Faure. In 1984 he was posthumously raised to the dignity of Marshal of France.
