Elwood Haynes (14 October 1857 – 13 April 1925) was an American inventor, metallurgist, automobile pioneer, entrepreneur and industrialist. A leading figure of Indiana's natural-gas boom, he was one of the earliest successful builders of automobiles in the United States, and his metallurgical research produced the high-temperature alloys Stellite and Hastelloy, which remain commercially important in jet engines, chemical processing and medical implants.

Automobile pioneer

Haynes designed and drove one of the first American automobiles on 4 July 1894 at Kokomo, Indiana — a one-cylinder, two-horsepower carriage built from his own drawings. With partners Edgar and Elmer Apperson he formed the Haynes-Apperson company in 1896, generally regarded as one of the first successful automobile manufacturers in the United States; the partnership dissolved in 1902 and Haynes continued as the Haynes Automobile Company until its receivership in 1924.

Metallurgy

In his own laboratory Haynes developed Stellite, a family of corrosion-resistant, high-temperature cobalt-chromium alloys prized for cutting tools, and Hastelloy, a nickel-based alloy family resistant to extreme corrosion and heat, used in chemical plants and aerospace. His work on chrome-nickel steels contributed materially to the development of stainless steel. The companies he founded, Haynes International and the former Stellite divisions, long outlived him.

Public life

A prominent progressive and philanthropist, Haynes served as a member of the Kokomo city council, founded the Haynes Foundation, and endowed the YMCA and other institutions. The Elwood Haynes Museum in Kokomo preserves his home and laboratory.
