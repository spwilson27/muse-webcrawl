Chinon


Château de Chinon

Chinon is a commune in the Indre-et-Loire department in central France, situated on the banks of the river Vienne, about 45 km southwest of Tours. It is well known for the Château de Chinon, a major medieval fortress, and for its red wines made from Cabernet Franc, produced under the Chinon AOC. The town has a population of roughly 8,000.

History

Chinon developed around a strategic promontory fortified since antiquity. The castle was a residence of Henry II of England, who died there in 1189, and of his son Richard the Lionheart. Under the Plantagenets, Chinon was a key stronghold in Anjou. In 1429, during the Hundred Years' War, the fourteen-year-old (or seventeen-year-old) Joan of Arc arrived at Chinon and famously recognised the disguised Dauphin, the future Charles VII, among his courtiers; after their meeting she was sent to Poitiers for examination and then to relieve the siege of Orléans. The writer François Rabelais was born near Chinon at Seuilly, and the town celebrates its association with him; in French slang a "chinon" has occasionally lent its name to gastronomic and literary traditions of the region.

Wine

Chinon wines, produced from vineyards in the surrounding communes along the Vienne and Loire, are overwhelmingly red or rosé wines made from Cabernet Franc (locally called Breton), ranging from light, early-drinking styles grown on gravel terraces to structured wines from tuffeau limestone slopes capable of long ageing. A small amount of white Chinon is made from Chenin Blanc.

Sights

The fortress, substantially restored in the 21st century, includes the royal apartments where Joan of Arc's meeting with Charles VII is commemorated, towers associated with the Knights Templar (Jacques de Molay was imprisoned there), and extensive views over the Vienne valley. Below it lies the well-preserved medieval old town.
