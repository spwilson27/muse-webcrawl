State of Mexico

The Cosmovitral in Toluca, the state capital.

The State of Mexico (Spanish: Estado de México), often abbreviated Edomex, is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico. It surrounds Mexico City on three sides and, with a population of roughly 17 million, is the country's most populous state. Its capital is Toluca de Lerdo, and its largest city is Ecatepec de Morelos, part of the Greater Mexico City metropolitan area.

Geography and history

The state occupies part of the Valley of Mexico and the Toluca Valley, bordered by mountains including the Sierra Nevada with the volcanoes Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl to the east. The territory was home to the Aztec (Mexica) civilization, whose capital Tenochtitlan lay within what is now Mexico City, and the ancient city of Teotihuacan is located in the state's northeast. The state was created in 1824, and its present boundaries were established after surrounding territories — including Mexico City itself — were separated from it during the 19th century.

Economy and municipalities

The State of Mexico is a major industrial and commercial centre, divided into 125 municipalities. Industrial corridors around Toluca and the Mexico City suburbs host manufacturing in automobiles, chemicals, food processing, and pharmaceuticals, while tourism centres on archaeological sites, colonial towns such as Valle de Bravo and Malinalco, and the Monarch Butterfly Biosphere Reserve.
