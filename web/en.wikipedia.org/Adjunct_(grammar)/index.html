Adjunct (grammar)

In linguistics, an adjunct is an optional part of a sentence, clause or phrase that, if removed, does not affect the remainder in a grammatically essential way. Adjuncts contrast with complements, which are required by a verb or other head.

Examples

In "She met him in a park", the phrase "in a park" is an adjunct giving location; the sentence remains complete without it. Adverbial phrases of time, manner, place and reason commonly function as adjuncts.

Tests

Adjuncts are typically iterable, can often be moved, and are not selected by the head. Complements fail these tests more often than adjuncts do.
