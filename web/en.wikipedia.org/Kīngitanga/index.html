Kīngitanga

The Kīngitanga, or Māori King Movement, is a movement arising among the Māori of New Zealand that established a role similar in status to that of the monarch of the colonising power. Founded in 1858, it aimed to unify Māori tribes, halt the sale of land to settlers, and uphold Māori authority under the Treaty of Waitangi.

History

The first king, the Waikato chief Pōtatau Te Wherowhero, was crowned in 1858 at Ngāruawāhia, which remains the movement's seat at Tūrangawaewae Marae. The Kīngitanga was a central party in the New Zealand Wars; after the invasion of the Waikato in 1863–64 its lands were confiscated, and the second king, Tāwhiao, withdrew into the King Country for two decades.

Succession

The position is hereditary in practice within Te Wherowhero's line, though formally elective. Holders include Tāwhiao, Mahuta, Korokī, Dame Te Atairangikaahu (reigned 1966–2006, the longest-serving), Tūheitia (2006–2024), and Ngawai Hono i te Pō, who succeeded in 2024.
