White River (Washington)

LocationWashington, United States
SourceEmmons Glacier, Mount Rainier
MouthPuyallup River
Length68 mi (109 km)

The White River is a river in the U.S. state of Washington. It flows about 68 miles (109 km) from its source, the Emmons Glacier on Mount Rainier, to join the Puyallup River near Sumner. Its name derives from the milky colour given to its water by glacial flour.

Course

From Mount Rainier National Park the river flows north and west through the Cascade foothills, passing the Mud Mountain Dam, which was built for flood control after damaging floods in the early 20th century. It forms part of the boundary between King and Pierce counties along stretches of its lower course.

History

In 1906, during a flood, the White River partially avulsed into the Stuck River channel, changing its historical course, which had run north to the Green and Duwamish rivers. Engineering works later made the southern route permanent, a change that figured in boundary disputes between King and Pierce counties. The river valley was also the site of fighting during the Puget Sound War of 1855–56.

Recreation

The upper river is popular for whitewater rafting and fishing, and the river supports runs of salmon and steelhead, with restoration efforts aimed at recovering Chinook salmon habitat.
