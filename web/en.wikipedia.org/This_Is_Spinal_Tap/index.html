This Is Spinal Tap (stylised as This Is Spın̈al Tap, with a metal umlaut over the n) is a 1984 American mockumentary comedy film co-written and directed by Rob Reiner in his directorial debut. It stars Christopher Guest, Michael McKean and Harry Shearer as members of the fictional English heavy metal band Spinal Tap, chronicled by documentary filmmaker Marty Di Bergi (Reiner) on a disastrous American comeback tour.

Plot

The film follows the band — lead guitarist Nigel Tufnel (Guest), lead singer/guitarist David St. Hubbins (McKean) and bassist Derek Smalls (Shearer) — through cancelled gigs, tiny audiences, an 18-inch Stonehenge stage prop, drummers who keep dying in bizarre circumstances, and an amplifier that "goes to eleven". Much of the dialogue was improvised from an outline, lending the satire of rock-star pretension an uncanny realism; many musicians initially believed Spinal Tap was a real band.

Reception and legacy

Modestly successful on release, the film grew into a cult classic and is routinely ranked among the greatest comedies ever made. In 2002 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, and aesthetically significant. The cast later made other improvised ensemble comedies including Waiting for Guffman and Best in Show, and performed as Spinal Tap in real concerts and albums. A sequel, Spinal Tap II: The End Continues, was released in 2025.
