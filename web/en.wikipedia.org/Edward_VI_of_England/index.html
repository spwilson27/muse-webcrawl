Edward VI of England

Edward VI (12 October 1537 – 6 July 1553) was King of England and Ireland from 28 January 1547 until his death at the age of 15. The son of Henry VIII and Jane Seymour, he was England's first monarch raised as a Protestant, and his short reign saw the Church of England move decisively toward Protestantism.

Edward VI

Accession and regency

Edward was crowned at Westminster Abbey at the age of nine. Because of his youth, England was governed by a regency council, dominated first by his uncle Edward Seymour, Duke of Somerset, as Lord Protector, and after Somerset's fall in 1549 by John Dudley, Duke of Northumberland. The young king, educated by leading humanists, kept a detailed chronicle of his reign and took an increasing personal interest in government.

Religious reform

Under Edward, Archbishop Thomas Cranmer introduced the Book of Common Prayer (1549, revised in a more Protestant form in 1552), the Forty-two Articles of religion, communion in both kinds, and the removal of images and altars from churches — changes that defined the Anglican settlement later moderated under Elizabeth I. Reform provoked the Prayer Book Rebellion in the West Country in 1549, while Kett's Rebellion in Norfolk protested enclosure.

Death and succession crisis

Edward fell ill in early 1553, probably with tuberculosis, and died on 6 July 1553. Fearing a return to Catholicism under his half-sister Mary, Edward and Northumberland drew up a "Devise for the Succession" excluding Mary and Elizabeth and naming his Protestant cousin Lady Jane Grey as heir. Jane was proclaimed queen but was overthrown by Mary within nine days. Edward is buried in Westminster Abbey.
