Abbreviation: PRPP
Type: Pentose phosphate intermediate

Phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate

Phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate (PRPP) is a pentose phosphate and a biochemical intermediate in the formation of purine and pyrimidine nucleotides. It consists of a ribose sugar bearing a pyrophosphate group at the 5′ carbon and a phosphate at the 1′ carbon, providing an activated ribose unit for nucleotide synthesis.

Synthesis and role

PRPP is formed from ribose 5-phosphate and ATP by the enzyme ribose-phosphate diphosphokinase (PRPP synthetase), transferring the pyrophosphate group of ATP. PRPP is a substrate in de novo purine synthesis — where PRPP amidotransferase catalyses the committed step — and in purine salvage pathways via HGPRT and APRT, pyrimidine synthesis, and the synthesis of NAD and histidine and tryptophan in some organisms.

Clinical significance

Overactivity of PRPP synthetase increases purine production and uric acid, causing gout. Deficiency of HGPRT, which consumes PRPP in salvage, leads to Lesch–Nyhan syndrome, with severe neurological symptoms and hyperuricemia.
