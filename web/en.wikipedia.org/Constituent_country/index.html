Constituent country

A constituent country is a country that forms part of a larger sovereign state or political union. The term is most commonly used for the four countries of the United Kingdom — England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland — each of which is called a country in its own right while together constituting the sovereign state of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. Unlike federal states, the United Kingdom is a unitary state in which power is devolved to Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland by the Parliament at Westminster, which retains sovereignty; England has no separate devolved government.

Other examples

The Kingdom of the Netherlands likewise consists of four constituent countries (in Dutch, landen) — the Netherlands, Aruba, Curaçao, and Sint Maarten — each an equal partner under the Charter for the Kingdom since the dissolution of the Netherlands Antilles in 2010. The Kingdom of Denmark comprises Denmark together with the self-governing territories of Greenland and the Faroe Islands, sometimes described as constituent countries (the "Unity of the Realm").

The term is also applied historically and comparatively to the republics of the Soviet Union and Yugoslavia, and to the states of federations, though "constituent state" or "federated state" is more usual for federal systems such as those of the United States, Germany, and Australia. Constituent countries typically field their own national symbols and, in the British case, separate sports teams, while defence, foreign affairs, and citizenship are handled by the sovereign state as a whole.
