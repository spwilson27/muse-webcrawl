Les Halles

Les Halles is a district in the 1st arrondissement of Paris, France, named after the city's historic central fresh-food market, which stood on the site for roughly eight centuries. Today the area is occupied by the Forum des Halles shopping and leisure complex and one of Europe's busiest transport interchanges.

History

A market was established at Les Halles in the 12th century under King Louis VI. In the 1850s the architect Victor Baltard built a celebrated complex of iron-and-glass pavilions, described by Émile Zola in his novel Le Ventre de Paris ("The Belly of Paris"). Congestion led the wholesale market to move to Rungis, south of Paris, in 1971, and the Baltard pavilions were demolished amid public controversy.

Modern development

The Forum des Halles opened in 1979 above the Châtelet–Les Halles station, a hub for RER and Métro lines used by hundreds of thousands of passengers daily. A major redevelopment completed in 2016 added the sweeping glass-and-steel Canopée roof over the forum and a cultural centre.
