Richborough

Richborough is a settlement north of Sandwich on the east coast of Kent, England. It was the site of the Roman fort and port of Rutupiae, one of the main entry points to Roman Britain and later one of the Saxon Shore forts.

Roman history

Richborough is widely regarded as the landing place of the Roman invasion force of 43 AD under Claudius. A supply base and port developed, and a monumental four-way arch (quadrifrons), among the largest in the Roman Empire, marked the ceremonial gateway to the province, at the start of Watling Street. Around AD 273 the site was fortified as a Saxon Shore fort, whose massive stone walls survive to a considerable height.

Later history

After the Roman period the site declined as the Wantsum Channel silted up. A small chapel dedicated to St Augustine stood within the fort, near the traditional landing place of Augustine in 597. During the First World War a secret military port was constructed at Richborough to supply the Western Front.

Today

The Roman remains, managed by English Heritage, include the fort walls, ditches of successive phases, and the foundations of the triumphal arch.
