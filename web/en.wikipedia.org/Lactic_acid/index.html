Lactic acid

IUPAC name: 2-hydroxypropanoic acid
Formula: C3H6O3
Molar mass: 90.08 g/mol
Appearance: colourless liquid (white solid when pure)
pKa: 3.86

Lactic acid is an organic acid with the molecular formula C3H6O3. It is white in the solid state and miscible with water. In solution it can lose a proton from the carboxyl group, producing the lactate ion.

Lactic acid is chiral, consisting of two optical isomers: L-lactic acid and D-lactic acid, plus a racemic mixture. It is primarily found in sour milk products, such as laban, yoghurt, buttermilk, and sourdough.

History

Lactic acid was first isolated in 1780 by the Swedish chemist Carl Wilhelm Scheele from sour milk. Its structure was established by Johannes Wislicenus in 1873. The name reflects its initial discovery in milk (Latin: lac).

Production

Industrial production is carried out chiefly by bacterial fermentation of carbohydrates such as corn starch or molasses, using species of Lactobacillus. In the body, lactate is produced from pyruvate during anaerobic glycolysis when oxygen supply is limited, and is recycled to glucose in the liver via the Cori cycle.

Applications

Lactic acid is used as a food additive (E270), a flavouring and preservative, in pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, and in the production of the biodegradable plastic polylactic acid (PLA).
