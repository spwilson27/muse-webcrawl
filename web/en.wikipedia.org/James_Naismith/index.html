James Naismith (6 November 1861 – 28 November 1939) was a Canadian-American physical educator, physician, and sports coach who invented the game of basketball in December 1891. The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in Springfield, Massachusetts, is named in his honour.

Early life

Naismith was born in Almonte, Ontario, to Scottish immigrant parents and was orphaned young. A talented all-round athlete at McGill University in Montreal, where he studied physical education and divinity, he moved to the United States to teach at the International YMCA Training School in Springfield, Massachusetts.

Invention of basketball

In the harsh New England winter of 1891, Naismith's superior Luther Gulick gave him fourteen days to devise an indoor game that would keep unruly athletes occupied. Drawing on childhood games such as "duck on a rock" and aiming to minimise rough contact, Naismith wrote thirteen rules, had two peach baskets nailed to the lower rail of the gymnasium balcony — 10 feet (3.05 m) above the floor, a height that stuck — and on 21 December 1891 refereed the first game, played nine-a-side with a soccer ball. The first public game followed in March 1892, and the sport spread rapidly through the YMCA network worldwide.

Later career

Naismith became the first basketball coach at the University of Kansas in 1898, founding a programme whose later coaches (including his own player Phog Allen) built it into a power; ironically he remains the only Kansas coach with a losing record. He served as a chaplain and medical officer in the First World War, earned a medical degree, and in 1936 saw basketball debut as an Olympic sport in Berlin, where he presented the medals. His original rules manuscript later sold at auction for $4.3 million and is displayed at the University of Kansas.
