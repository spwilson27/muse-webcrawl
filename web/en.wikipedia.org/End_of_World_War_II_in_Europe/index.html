End of World War II in Europe

The end of World War II in Europe occurred in May 1945, when Nazi Germany surrendered unconditionally to the Allied forces. Adolf Hitler had died by suicide on 30 April 1945 during the Battle of Berlin, and the German Instrument of Surrender was signed on 7 May at Reims and ratified on 8 May in Berlin. Victory in Europe Day (VE Day) was celebrated on 8 May 1945 in most Allied countries, and on 9 May in the Soviet Union.

Final offensives

By early 1945 Germany was being crushed from both east and west. The Red Army's Vistula–Oder Offensive carried Soviet forces to within striking distance of Berlin, while Western Allied armies crossed the Rhine in March. American and Soviet troops met at the Elbe near Torgau on 25 April 1945. The Battle of Berlin (16 April – 2 May) ended with the city's garrison surrendering after intense fighting; Hitler killed himself in the Führerbunker on 30 April.

Hitler's succession and the Flensburg government

In his political testament Hitler named Grand Admiral Karl Dönitz as his successor as head of state. Dönitz established a government at Flensburg near the Danish border and sought to negotiate partial surrenders in the west while continuing to fight the Soviets, in part to allow German soldiers and civilians to flee westward.

Surrenders

German forces in Italy surrendered on 2 May (effective). On 4 May, German forces in north-west Germany, Denmark, and the Netherlands surrendered to Field Marshal Montgomery at Lüneburg Heath. The general unconditional surrender was signed by General Alfred Jodl at SHAEF headquarters in Reims in the early hours of 7 May, to take effect at 23:01 on 8 May. At Soviet insistence a second, ratified act was signed by Field Marshal Wilhelm Keitel in Berlin-Karlshorst late on 8 May, which was already 9 May in Moscow — hence Victory Day is marked on 9 May in Russia and some other states.

Aftermath

Germany was divided into four occupation zones administered by the United States, the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, and France; Berlin was similarly divided. The Dönitz government was arrested on 23 May. Millions of German prisoners of war were held, and large populations of ethnic Germans were expelled from Eastern Europe. The war in the Pacific continued until Japan's surrender in August–September 1945.
