Interstellar object

An interstellar object is an astronomical object (such as an asteroid, comet, or rogue planet) located in interstellar space that is not gravitationally bound to a star. Such objects pass through the Solar System on hyperbolic trajectories that betray their extrasolar origin.

Known examples

The first confirmed interstellar object, 1I/ʻOumuamua, was discovered in October 2017 by the Pan-STARRS telescope; its extreme elongation and non-gravitational acceleration sparked extensive study and speculation. The second, 2I/Borisov, found in 2019 by amateur astronomer Gennadiy Borisov, resembled a Solar System comet. A third, 3I/ATLAS, was discovered in 2025. Vast numbers of such bodies are thought to drift through the galaxy, ejected from their home systems during planet formation.
