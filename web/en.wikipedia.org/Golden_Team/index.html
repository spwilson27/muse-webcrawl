Golden Team


Hungary's Golden Team of the 1950s.

The Golden Team (Hungarian: Aranycsapat), also called the "Magical Magyars" or "Mighty Magyars", was the Hungary national football team of the 1950s, widely regarded as one of the greatest teams in football history.

The team

Coached by Gusztáv Sebes and built around Ferenc Puskás, Sándor Kocsis, Nándor Hidegkuti, Zoltán Czibor and József Bozsik, Hungary revolutionised tactics with fluid formations, a deep-lying centre-forward (Hidegkuti) and heavy emphasis on fitness and teamwork. The team went unbeaten in 50 matches between 1950 and 1956 (excluding one loss), won gold at the 1952 Helsinki Olympics, and humiliated England 6–3 at Wembley in 1953 — the "Match of the Century" — and 7–1 in Budapest in 1954.

1954 World Cup and aftermath

As overwhelming favourites at the 1954 World Cup in Switzerland, Hungary reached the final but lost 3–2 to West Germany in the "Miracle of Bern", despite leading 2–0 within eight minutes. The team dispersed after the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, when many stars, including Puskás, Kocsis and Czibor, defected to the West while touring abroad with Budapest Honvéd. Their innovations profoundly influenced later football, from the Dutch Total Football to modern pressing games.
