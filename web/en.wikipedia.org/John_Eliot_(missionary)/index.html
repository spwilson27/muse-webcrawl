John Eliot (missionary)

Born1604, Widford, Hertfordshire, England
Died21 May 1690 (aged 85), Roxbury, Massachusetts Bay Colony
OccupationPuritan missionary, minister
Known forTranslating the Bible into the Massachusett language

John Eliot (1604 – 21 May 1690) was a Puritan missionary to the American Indians who has been called the "apostle to the Indians" and also the founder of Roxbury Latin School in the province of Massachusetts Bay. He is best known for translating the Bible into the Massachusett language, producing the first Bible printed in America.

Early life and ministry

Eliot was baptised at Widford, Hertfordshire, in 1604 and educated at Jesus College, Cambridge. Finding Puritan worship suppressed in England, he emigrated to Massachusetts Bay in 1631 and became teacher of the church at Roxbury, where he served for nearly sixty years.

Mission to the Native Americans

Eliot learned the Massachusett language with the help of a young Native interpreter, studied it systematically, and began preaching to Native audiences in 1646. He helped establish a series of "praying towns" — self-governing Christian Native communities, the first at Natick in 1651. Fourteen such towns were founded in total.

The Indian Bible

Eliot's translation of the New Testament appeared in 1661 and the complete Bible, Mamusse Wunneetupanatamwe Up-Biblum God, in 1663 — the first Bible printed in the Americas, in any language. He also translated catechisms and works such as The Indian Grammar Begun (1666), which helped preserve the Massachusett language.

Later life and legacy

The praying towns were largely destroyed during King Philip's War (1675–76), but Eliot continued his mission work until his death at Roxbury in 1690. His Bible translation has been used in modern efforts to revive the Wampanoag language.
