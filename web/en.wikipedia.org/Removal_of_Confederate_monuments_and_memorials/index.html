Removal of Confederate monuments and memorials

There are more than 1,500 public symbols of the Confederate States of America in the United States, including monuments, memorials, and namesakes of schools, roads, and military bases. Many were erected during the Jim Crow era, decades after the American Civil War, and particularly in response to the civil rights movement. Since the 2010s, and especially following the 2015 Charleston church shooting, the 2017 Unite the Right rally in Charlottesville, and the 2020 murder of George Floyd, an increasing number of these symbols have been removed or renamed by governments, institutions, and protesters.

Background

The Southern Poverty Law Center's "Whose Heritage?" report has catalogued Confederate symbols across the United States. Critics of the monuments argue that many were erected to promote white supremacy and the Lost Cause myth rather than to memorialize the dead, pointing to the peaks of monument-building in the 1900s–1920s and 1950s–1960s. Defenders argue that the monuments preserve history and heritage and that removing them erases the past. Several states, including Alabama, Georgia, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, and Virginia, enacted preservation laws restricting local governments' ability to remove monuments, though Virginia repealed its law in 2020.

Notable removals

After the Charleston shooting in 2015, the Confederate battle flag was removed from the South Carolina State House grounds. In 2017, New Orleans removed four monuments, and Baltimore removed its Confederate statues overnight. Charlottesville's statue of Robert E. Lee, whose planned removal prompted the Unite the Right rally, was finally taken down in 2021. In Richmond, Virginia, the former Confederate capital, the monumental statue of Lee on Monument Avenue was removed in September 2021 following a ruling by the Supreme Court of Virginia. In 2020, the United States Department of Defense began renaming Army bases named for Confederate generals, a process completed in 2023 when nine bases received new names.
