Past progressive

The past progressive, also called the past continuous, is a grammatical tense in English that combines the past tense with the progressive aspect. It is formed with the past tense of the verb be (was or were) plus the present participle of the main verb, as in "I was walking". It is used to describe actions that were in progress at a particular moment in the past, or that formed a background to another, usually shorter, past event.

The past progressive marks an action ongoing at a past reference point.

Uses

The past progressive is used for an action in progress at a specific past time ("At midnight we were still driving"), for an ongoing action interrupted by a completed one expressed in the simple past ("I was having a bath when the phone rang"), for parallel simultaneous actions ("While I was cooking, he was washing up"), and for repeated or irritating behaviour, often with adverbs such as always and constantly. In narration it provides scene-setting background, contrasting with the simple past, which advances the story line.

Formation in other languages

Many languages express similar meanings without an exact equivalent of the English form: Spanish uses the imperfect with a gerund (estaba caminando), French uses the imparfait, and German typically uses the simple past with adverbials. English learners frequently overuse or underuse the form because of these differences.
