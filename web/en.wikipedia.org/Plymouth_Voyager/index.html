Plymouth Voyager

The Plymouth Voyager is a minivan produced by the Plymouth division of Chrysler from the 1984 to 2000 model years. Alongside its twin, the Dodge Caravan, the Voyager was one of the first modern minivans and is credited with creating an entirely new vehicle segment. A longer-wheelbase Grand Voyager was added in 1987. Following the discontinuation of the Plymouth brand, the model continued as the Chrysler Voyager.

History

Introduced in November 1983 for the 1984 model year, the Voyager was derived from Chrysler's front-wheel-drive K-car platform, giving it car-like handling, a low floor, and seating for up to seven with sliding side doors. Its formula—smaller and more economical than a full-size van, yet far roomier than a station wagon—proved enormously popular with families and effectively defined the minivan market for decades.

Generations

Three generations were produced under the Plymouth name: the first (1984–1990), the second (1991–1995), which added integrated child seats and available all-wheel drive, and the third (1996–2000), which added a driver's-side sliding door. Engines ranged from four-cylinder units sourced from Chrysler and Mitsubishi to V6 engines of up to 3.8 litres. After Plymouth's demise, the Voyager name was briefly used on a Chrysler-badged model and was revived in 2020 as the entry-level version of the Chrysler Pacifica minivan.
