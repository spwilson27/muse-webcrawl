Casablanca (Arabic: الدار البيضاء, ad-Dār al-Bayḍāʾ, "the White House") is the largest city in Morocco and the country's economic and business centre. Located on the Atlantic coast of the Chaouia plain, it is home to the Port of Casablanca, one of the largest artificial ports in Africa, and serves as the chief naval base of the Royal Moroccan Navy. Originally the Berber settlement of Anfa, the city was destroyed by the Portuguese in 1468, rebuilt as Casa Branca, and took its present Spanish-derived name after an earthquake in 1755. Under the French protectorate (1912–1956) it was transformed into a modern, planned city.

History

The Phoenicians and Romans used Anfa as a port. In the Middle Ages it was a base for pirates and was repeatedly attacked; the Portuguese destroyed it in 1468 and built a fortress town in 1515. After the 1755 Lisbon earthquake destroyed the settlement, Sultan Mohammed III rebuilt it, and trade with Europe grew rapidly in the 19th century. In January 1943 Casablanca hosted the Casablanca Conference between Franklin D. Roosevelt and Winston Churchill, at which the Allies adopted the policy of "unconditional surrender." The city was also the setting and title of the celebrated 1942 film Casablanca.

Geography and landmarks

Casablanca has an Atlantic Mediterranean climate with mild, wet winters and warm, dry summers. Its landmarks include the Hassan II Mosque, completed in 1993 — one of the largest mosques in the world, with a 210-metre minaret, the tallest on Earth, built partly over the ocean — the Habous quarter, Mohammed V Square, and extensive Art Deco architecture from the colonial period in the city centre. The old medina survives near the port.

Economy

The city accounts for a major share of Morocco's industrial output and hosts the Casablanca Stock Exchange, the headquarters of many national firms, and the phosphate export trade. Mohammed V International Airport, south of the city, is Morocco's busiest airport.

Demographics

Greater Casablanca's population approaches five million, making it the largest metropolitan area in the Maghreb. The population is overwhelmingly Muslim, with historic Jewish and Christian communities; the city was for centuries home to one of Morocco's largest Jewish populations.
