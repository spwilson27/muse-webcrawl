Apokatastasis

Apokatastasis (Greek: ἀποκατάστασις, "restoration" or "return") is a theological doctrine of the eventual restoration of all things, and in Christian theology specifically the belief that all rational creatures — including the damned and even the Devil — will ultimately be reconciled to God. It is a form of universal salvation.

Origins

The term appears in Acts 3:21, speaking of the "restoration of all things". The doctrine was developed especially by the Alexandrian theologians Clement and Origen in the 2nd–3rd centuries, and was defended by Gregory of Nyssa in the 4th century. In Stoic philosophy, apokatastasis referred to the cyclical return of the cosmos to its original state after the great conflagration (ekpyrosis).

Controversy

Origenist teachings on universal restoration were condemned in the 6th century (associated with the anathemas of 543 and the Second Council of Constantinople, 553), though the precise scope of those condemnations remains debated. The idea recurred in later mystical and universalist traditions, including some Anabaptist and Pietist thought.
