Milling cutter

Used withMilling machine
Common typesEnd mill, face mill, ball nose, slab mill
MaterialsHigh-speed steel, carbide

Milling cutters are cutting tools used in milling machines to perform milling operations. They are rotary tools with one or more cutting teeth, each of which intermittently removes material as the cutter rotates and is fed into or across the workpiece. Milling cutters are typically made of high-speed steel or cemented carbide, often with coatings such as titanium nitride to extend tool life.

Common varieties include the end mill, which cuts with both its end and its fluted sides; the face mill, a large-diameter cutter with indexable inserts for surfacing flat areas; the ball nose cutter, used for contouring dies and molds; the slab mill for heavy cuts on flat surfaces; and form cutters such as gear cutters and T-slot cutters shaped to produce specific profiles.

Geometry and use

Key features of a milling cutter include its flutes (helical grooves that evacuate chips), helix angle, rake angle, and relief. The number of flutes affects chip space and finish: fewer flutes clear chips better in soft materials such as aluminum, while more flutes give smoother finishes in steel. Choosing feeds, speeds, and coolant appropriate to the cutter and material is central to the machining process.
