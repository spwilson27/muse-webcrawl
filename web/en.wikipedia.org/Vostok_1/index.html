Vostok 1 was the first spaceflight of the Vostok programme and the first human orbital spaceflight in history. The Vostok 3KA space capsule was launched on 12 April 1961 from Baikonur Cosmodrome with Soviet cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin aboard, making him the first human to journey into outer space.

Background

The flight took place at the height of the Space Race between the Soviet Union and the United States. The Vostok spacecraft, designed under the leadership of chief designer Sergei Korolev, evolved from the Zenit reconnaissance satellite and had already flown in several uncrewed test missions, some carrying dogs, before being certified for a human passenger. Gagarin, a 27-year-old senior lieutenant in the Soviet Air Forces, was selected in part for his small stature (1.57 m), suited to the cramped capsule.

The flight

Vostok 1 was launched at 09:07 Moscow time on 12 April 1961. As the rocket lifted off, Gagarin exclaimed "Poyekhali!" ("Let's go!"). The capsule reached an orbit with a perigee of about 169 km and an apogee of about 327 km, completing a single orbit of the Earth in 108 minutes. Because doctors were unsure how a human would react to weightlessness, the spacecraft's manual controls were locked; an override code was sealed in an envelope in case Gagarin needed to take control.

During the flight Gagarin reported that he could see the Earth's curvature and remarked on its beauty. He ate and drank—the first person to do so in weightlessness—to test whether it was possible. Re-entry was not without incident: the equipment module failed to separate cleanly from the spherical descent module for about ten minutes, causing the craft to tumble before the connecting straps burned through. Gagarin ejected from the capsule at about 7 km altitude, as planned for Vostok flights, and landed separately by parachute near the village of Engels in the Saratov region.

Aftermath and legacy

Gagarin became an international celebrity and was awarded the title Hero of the Soviet Union. The Soviet Union initially concealed that he had ejected and landed separately from the capsule, since aviation record rules then required a pilot to land with his craft for the flight to count as an official record; the FAI nonetheless recognized the achievement. The date, 12 April, is now celebrated annually as Yuri's Night and as the International Day of Human Space Flight. The flight came less than a month before Alan Shepard's suborbital Mercury flight and intensified American efforts that culminated in the Apollo Moon landings.
