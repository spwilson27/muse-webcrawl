Residual (entertainment industry)

In the entertainment industry, residuals are payments made to actors, writers, directors, and other creative contributors when a film or television programme is reused — rerun, syndicated, released on home media, or distributed on streaming platforms — beyond its initial exhibition. In the United States they are administered under collective bargaining agreements of unions such as SAG-AFTRA, the Writers Guild of America, and the Directors Guild of America.

History

Residuals were first won in the 1950s and 1960s as television reruns became economically significant; the 1960 Writers Guild strike established key precedents. Each new distribution technology — cable, videocassette, DVD, and streaming — has required renegotiation of residual formulas, and streaming residuals were a central issue in the 2023 Hollywood strikes.

Calculation

Formulas vary by medium and contract: some pay a percentage of distributor's gross, others a fixed fee per rerun, often declining over successive runs. Residuals are a major income source for working performers between jobs.
