New Villages

New Villages (Kampung Baru) were settlements created during the Malayan Emergency (1948–1960) under the Briggs Plan, in which roughly half a million rural inhabitants, mostly ethnic Chinese squatters on the jungle fringe, were resettled into guarded, fenced villages.

Purpose

The aim was to cut off food, supplies and intelligence flowing from the squatters to the guerrillas of the Malayan National Liberation Army, by concentrating the population where it could be policed and provided with services.

Legacy

More than 450 New Villages were created. Conditions improved over time with schools, clinics and utilities, and most villages endured after independence, becoming established Malaysian towns and villages whose communities remain predominantly Chinese.
