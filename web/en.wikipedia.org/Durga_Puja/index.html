Durga Puja is an annual Hindu festival originating in the Indian subcontinent which reveres and pays homage to the goddess Durga and is also celebrated because of Durga's victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura. It is particularly celebrated in the Indian state of West Bengal and in Bangladesh, Assam, Odisha, Tripura and Bihar.

The festival is observed in the month of Ashvin, typically September or October of the Gregorian calendar, and is a ten-day festival of which the last five are of the most significance. In 2021, UNESCO inscribed "Durga Puja in Kolkata" on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Mythology

According to the Devi Mahatmyam, the gods, unable to defeat the buffalo demon Mahishasura, combined their energies to create the goddess Durga, who after a fierce battle slew the demon. The festival thus celebrates the victory of good over evil and the power of the divine feminine (shakti).

Celebrations

Celebrations centre on elaborately decorated temporary stages called pandals housing clay idols of Durga with her children — Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartikeya — astride her lion, spearing Mahishasura. Festivities include scripture recitation, cultural performances, feasting and gift-giving. On the final day, Vijayadashami, idols are immersed in rivers or the sea (visarjan).

Pandals and themes

In Kolkata, thousands of community ("barowari") pujas compete in artistry; themed pandals replicate famous monuments or address social themes, drawing millions of visitors. Artisans of Kumartuli craft the idols from clay of the Hooghly riverbank.

Rituals

Key rituals include Bodhon (awakening of the goddess), Nabapatrika (consecration of nine plants), Sandhi Puja at the junction of Ashtami and Navami, and Sindoor Khela, in which married women smear vermilion on the goddess and each other on the final day.
