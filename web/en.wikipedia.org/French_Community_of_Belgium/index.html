French Community of Belgium

The French Community of Belgium (French: Communauté française de Belgique), also known since 2011 by the promoted name Wallonia-Brussels Federation (French: Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles), is one of the three linguistic communities of Belgium, alongside the Flemish Community and the German-speaking Community. It is a federal entity responsible for French-speaking culture, education, and related matters for the roughly 4.5 million French speakers of Wallonia and Brussels.

Institutions

The Community has its own parliament — the Parliament of the French Community, with 94 members drawn from the Walloon Parliament and the French-speaking members of the Brussels Parliament — and its own government, headed by a minister-president. Its capital is Brussels, and its public holiday is 27 September, commemorating the September Days of the Belgian Revolution of 1830. Its emblem is the Walloon rooster.

Competences

Under Belgium's federal structure, the communities are responsible for person- and language-related matters: education (the Community's largest budget item), culture and audiovisual media (including the broadcaster RTBF), youth and sports policy, and parts of health and social welfare. The French Community exercises most of its powers in the Walloon Region (except the German-speaking area) and, for French-language institutions, in the bilingual Brussels-Capital Region.

Name

The designation "Wallonia-Brussels Federation" was adopted for communication purposes in 2011 to emphasise the link between Wallonia and Brussels, though the constitutional name remains the French Community; the change was not recognised in the Belgian Constitution and drew objections from Flemish politicians.
