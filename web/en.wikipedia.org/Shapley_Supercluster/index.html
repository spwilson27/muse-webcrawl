Shapley Supercluster


Constellation: Centaurus
Distance: ~650 million light-years
Mass: ~1016 solar masses
Contents: thousands of galaxies in dozens of clusters

The Shapley Supercluster or Shapley Concentration is the largest known concentration of galaxies in the nearby universe, forming a gravitationally interacting unit that pulls toward itself rather than expanding with the universe. It lies in the direction of the constellation Centaurus, roughly 650 million light-years away.

Discovery

The concentration was first noticed by Harlow Shapley in the 1930s during his surveys of galaxies, who described a vast cloud of galaxies in Centaurus. Systematic redshift surveys in the 1980s revealed the full extent of the structure, which contains dozens of rich galaxy clusters, including Abell 3558 at its core, and thousands of individual galaxies.

Significance

The Shapley Supercluster is one of the most massive known structures in the local universe, with a total mass on the order of 1016 solar masses. Its gravitational pull contributes substantially to the peculiar motion of the Local Group of galaxies, complementing the influence of the Great Attractor region closer to home.
