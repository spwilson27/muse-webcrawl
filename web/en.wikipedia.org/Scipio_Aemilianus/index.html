Scipio Aemilianus

Scipio Aemilianus (185–129 BC), destroyer of Carthage and Numantia.

Publius Cornelius Scipio Aemilianus (185–129 BC), also known as Scipio Africanus the Younger, was a Roman general and statesman. He is best known for commanding the final siege and destruction of Carthage in 146 BC, ending the Third Punic War, and for capturing Numantia in Spain in 133 BC. By birth the son of Lucius Aemilius Paullus, he was adopted into the Cornelii Scipiones and was the grandson by adoption of Scipio Africanus.

Career

Scipio distinguished himself as a military tribune in Spain and at the Battle of Pydna campaign's aftermath in Macedonia. Elected consul for 147 BC despite being below the legal age, he took command in Africa and destroyed Carthage the following year, receiving the agnomen Africanus. As censor in 142 BC he attempted to curb luxury and corruption. Consul again in 134 BC, he reduced Numantia after a methodical siege.

The Scipionic Circle

Scipio was a patron of Greek culture in Rome, gathering around him the historian Polybius, the Stoic philosopher Panaetius, and the playwright Terence—a group known as the Scipionic Circle. Cicero set his dialogue De re publica, including the famous Dream of Scipio, in Scipio's company.

Death

Scipio opposed the agrarian reforms of his brother-in-law Tiberius Gracchus and the Gracchan land commission. In 129 BC he was found dead on the morning of a day he was to speak on the issue; contemporaries suspected murder, though no culprit was proven.
