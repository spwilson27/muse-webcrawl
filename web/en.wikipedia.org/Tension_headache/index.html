Tension headache

Tension headaches typically cause a dull, band-like pain around the head

A tension headache, also called a tension-type headache, is the most common type of primary headache. It is characterized by mild to moderate, non-pulsating pain that is often described as a tight band or pressure around the head, lasting from 30 minutes to several days.

Signs and symptoms

Tension headaches are usually bilateral and of pressing or tightening quality. Unlike migraine, they are not typically accompanied by nausea, vomiting, or sensitivity severe enough to halt activity, though mild sensitivity to light or sound may occur. Episodes are classified as episodic (fewer than 15 days per month) or chronic (15 or more days per month for more than three months).

Causes

The exact mechanism is incompletely understood. Contributing factors include stress, poor posture, eye strain, fatigue, dehydration, and increased tenderness of the pericranial muscles. Central pain-processing changes are thought to play a role in the chronic form.

Treatment

Simple analgesics such as paracetamol (acetaminophen), ibuprofen, and aspirin are the standard acute treatment; frequent use must be avoided because it can cause medication-overuse headache. Preventive options for chronic cases include the tricyclic antidepressant amitriptyline, stress management, biofeedback, and physical therapy.
