Citizens UK

A Citizens UK assembly bringing together member institutions.

Citizens UK is a community organising alliance in the United Kingdom that brings together local institutions — including faith congregations, schools, universities, trade unions and community groups — to campaign on social justice issues. Founded in 1989 as the Citizens Organising Foundation, it is best known for launching the Living Wage campaign in 2001 and for its work on refugee resettlement, low pay, housing and neighbourhood safety. Citizens UK is affiliated with the tradition of broad-based community organising that traces back to the Industrial Areas Foundation founded by Saul Alinsky in the United States.

History

The organisation grew out of the East London Citizens' Organisation (TELCO), founded in 1996, and similar alliances in other cities. In 2001, London Citizens launched the Living Wage campaign at an assembly in East London, initially targeting cleaning contractors at hospitals and banks in Canary Wharf. The campaign persuaded employers to pay wages above the statutory minimum, calculated according to the real cost of living, and led to the creation of the Living Wage Foundation in 2011 to accredit employers. By the 2020s thousands of employers had been accredited.

Campaigns

Beyond the Living Wage, Citizens UK has campaigned to end the detention of children for immigration purposes, contributing to a government commitment in 2010 to end the practice; organised "CitySafe" campaigns to make streets safer for young people; pressed for action on housing affordability and rogue landlords; and, through the Sponsor Refugees programme, promoted community sponsorship of refugee families, including in response to the Syrian civil war and the war in Ukraine.

Structure and method

Citizens UK is a federation of local chapters — including London Citizens, Citizens Cymru, Leeds Citizens, Greater Manchester Citizens and others — each composed of dues-paying member institutions. Professional community organisers train volunteer leaders, hold large accountability assemblies at which public officials are asked to commit to specific asks, and emphasise relational one-to-one meetings as the foundation of civic power.
