The Foreign Intelligence Service of the Russian Federation (Russian: Служба внешней разведки Российской Федерации, Sluzhba vneshney razvedki, abbreviated SVR) is Russia's principal civilian foreign intelligence agency. It was created in December 1991 from the First Chief Directorate of the Soviet KGB after the dissolution of the USSR, inheriting the Soviet service's foreign stations, archives, and "illegals" programmes. The SVR is responsible for intelligence collection outside Russia; military intelligence is the remit of the GRU, and domestic security of the FSB.

Organization and duties

Under the 1996 Law on Foreign Intelligence, the SVR supplies political, economic, scientific-technical, and military-strategic intelligence to the President of Russia, who appoints its director. Its headquarters is at Yasenevo in south-west Moscow. The service runs residencies (stations) in Russian embassies abroad, conducts signals and human intelligence operations, and maintains a programme of "illegal" officers operating under deep non-official cover — a network of such officers in the United States was arrested by the FBI in 2010 and exchanged in a spy swap.

History

The SVR traces its institutional lineage to the Cheka's Foreign Department founded in December 1920, and celebrates that date as its founding. Its first post-Soviet director, Yevgeny Primakov (1991–1996), later foreign minister and prime minister, reorganized the service and published public intelligence reports. Subsequent directors included Vyacheslav Trubnikov and Mikhail Fradkov; Sergey Naryshkin, formerly speaker of the State Duma, became director in 2016.

Notable operations and controversies

The SVR has been accused by Western governments of involvement in cyber operations: in 2021 the United States formally attributed the SolarWinds supply-chain compromise to the SVR's hacking group (known as APT29 or Cozy Bear). The service is also linked historically to "active measures" — disinformation and influence operations — a KGB speciality that analysts say continues in modern form. Its officers and agents have featured in major espionage cases, including Aldrich Ames, the CIA officer who spied for Moscow, handled initially by the KGB and later the SVR.
