Qutb Shahi dynasty

The Qutb Shahi dynasty ruled the Sultanate of Golkonda in southern India from 1518 to 1687. A Shia Islamic dynasty of Turkmen origin, it was founded by Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk, a member of the Qara Qoyunlu confederation who had served the Bahmani Sultanate before declaring independence as the Bahmani kingdom fragmented into the Deccan sultanates.

History

The eight Qutb Shahi sultans were notable patrons of Persian and Telugu culture. Ibrahim Qutb Shah (r. 1550–1580) patronised Telugu poets and participated in the Deccan alliance that destroyed Vijayanagara at the Battle of Talikota (1565). Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah (r. 1580–1612) founded the city of Hyderabad in 1591 and built the Charminar; he is also credited with early poetry in Deccani Urdu. The kingdom grew rich on the diamond trade — the Golconda mines produced stones including the Koh-i-Noor and the Hope Diamond — and on the port of Masulipatnam's textile trade.

The sultanate became a Mughal tributary under Shah Jahan, and in 1687 the emperor Aurangzeb besieged Golconda for eight months and annexed the kingdom, imprisoning the last sultan, Abul Hasan Qutb Shah ("Tana Shah"), in Daulatabad fort until his death.

Culture and architecture

The dynasty's architectural legacy includes the elaborated tombs of the Qutb Shahi sultans near Golconda fort, the Charminar and Mecca Masjid in Hyderabad, and fortress and irrigation works. Qutb Shahi culture blended Persianate court forms with the Telugu language and local traditions of the Deccan.
