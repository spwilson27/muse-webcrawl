Seto Inland Sea

Islands of the Seto Inland Sea

The Seto Inland Sea (Japanese: 瀬戸内海, Seto Naikai), sometimes shortened to the Inland Sea, is the body of water separating Honshū, Shikoku, and Kyūshū, three of the four main islands of Japan. It serves as a waterway connecting the Pacific Ocean to the Sea of Japan, and it connects to Osaka Bay via the Kii Channel and to the Pacific through the Bungo Channel.

Geography

The Inland Sea stretches roughly 450 kilometres (280 mi) from east to west. Its average depth is 38 metres, and its surface area is about 23,203 square kilometres, making it one of the largest inter-island seas in the world. The sea is dotted with some 3,000 islands, including Awaji Island, Shōdoshima, the Iyo Islands, and the Shiwaku Islands. Many of these islands are part of the Setonaikai National Park, designated in 1934 as one of Japan's first national parks.

The region around the sea, known as the Setouchi region, enjoys a mild climate with relatively low rainfall, and is often called "the land of fair weather" (hare no kuni). Major cities on its shores include Hiroshima, Iwakuni, Matsuyama, Takamatsu, Tokushima, Kobe, and Kitakyushu.

History

During the Edo period, the Inland Sea was a vital transport route between the Kansai region and Kyushu, carrying rice, salt, and other goods. The famous naval battles of the Genpei War, including the final Battle of Dan-no-ura (1185) in the Shimonoseki Strait, were fought at its western entrance. In the Meiji era and afterwards, the sea became a centre of heavy industry, and its shores are now lined with steel mills, shipyards, and petrochemical complexes.

Transport

Three great bridge systems — the Seto-Ōhashi Bridge, the Akashi Kaikyō Bridge (the world's longest suspension bridge span at its opening), and the Nishiseto Expressway (Shimanami Kaidō) — now connect Honshū and Shikoku across the sea. Ferries remain important for reaching the smaller islands, while the sea's principal ports include Kobe, Osaka (via Osaka Bay), Hiroshima, and Takamatsu.

Ecology

The Inland Sea is known for its red tides, caused by algal blooms, and for fisheries producing oysters, sea bream, and fugu. Environmental laws enacted after severe industrial pollution in the 1970s have gradually improved water quality.
