Keratinocyte

A keratinocyte is the predominant cell type in the epidermis, the outermost layer of the skin, constituting about 90% of epidermal cells. Keratinocytes produce keratin and form a protective barrier against environmental damage, pathogens and water loss.

LocationEpidermis
FunctionBarrier, keratin production
ProducesKeratin

Development

Keratinocytes originate in the stratum basale and move outward through the stratum spinosum, stratum granulosum and stratum corneum, flattening, losing nuclei and becoming corneocytes over roughly a month. See skin and epidermis.

Function

Beyond barrier formation, keratinocytes participate in immune signalling, cytokine production, wound healing and vitamin D synthesis pathways.

Clinical significance

Dysregulation is involved in psoriasis, eczema, skin cancers such as squamous cell carcinoma, and inherited disorders like epidermolysis bullosa.
