Bioaerosol

Bioaerosols are airborne particles of biological origin, including bacteria, fungal spores, pollen, viruses, and fragments of organisms and their by-products. They are released from soil, water, plants, animals and human activities and can travel long distances in the atmosphere.

Sources and composition

Natural sources include windblown soil, sea spray, vegetation and wildlife. Indoor and occupational sources include people (breathing, coughing, sneezing), farming, composting, wastewater treatment and healthcare settings. Particle sizes range from nanometres (viruses) to over 100 micrometres (pollen).

Health effects

Bioaerosols transmit infectious diseases such as influenza, tuberculosis, Legionnaires' disease and COVID-19, and trigger allergies and asthma from pollen and mould. Endotoxins and mycotoxins can cause inflammatory responses. Risk depends on viability, dose and host susceptibility.

Environmental roles

Some bioaerosols act as ice nuclei and cloud condensation nuclei, influencing weather and climate, and disperse microorganisms and plant propagules between ecosystems.

Sampling and control

Methods include impaction, filtration, liquid impingement and molecular assays such as PCR. Ventilation, filtration (e.g. HEPA), respirators and source control reduce exposure.
