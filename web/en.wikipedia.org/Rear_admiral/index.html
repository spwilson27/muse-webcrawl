Rear admiral is a senior naval flag rank, above commodore (or captain) and below vice admiral, used in many of the world's navies. The title originates in the historical organisation of sailing fleets into squadrons: the admiral commanded the van, the vice admiral the centre, and the rear admiral — the most junior of the three — the rear squadron, where a capable deputy was needed to direct the ships most likely to be cut off in battle.

By country

In the United States Navy and Coast Guard the rank is divided into rear admiral (lower half), a one-star rank (O-7), and rear admiral (upper half), a two-star rank (O-8). In the British Royal Navy, rear admiral is a two-star rank (NATO OF-7) above commodore. Equivalent titles in other navies include Konteradmiral (Germany) and contre-amiral (France).
