Mariology

Mariology is the theological study of Mary, the mother of Jesus. It seeks to relate the doctrine of Mary to other doctrines of the faith, such as those concerning Jesus, the Church and salvation.

Catholic Mariology

Roman Catholic Mariology rests on four dogmas: Mary's divine motherhood (declared at the Council of Ephesus in 431, where she was proclaimed Theotokos, "God-bearer"), her perpetual virginity, the Immaculate Conception (defined 1854) and the Assumption into heaven (defined 1950). Catholic teaching presents Mary as an intercessor and model of faith, while insisting that devotion to her is subordinate to the worship of God.

Other traditions

Eastern Orthodox theology, or Theotokology, shares much of the Catholic doctrine of Mary while framing the Immaculate Conception differently. Most Protestant traditions honour Mary as the mother of Jesus but reject dogmas lacking, in their view, explicit scriptural basis, as well as invocation of saints. Islam also reveres Mary (Maryam), the only woman named in the Qur'an.

History

Mariology developed through the Church Fathers, medieval scholasticism, and papal teaching; the Second Vatican Council treated Mary within its document on the Church, Lumen gentium, and modern popes such as John Paul II issued major Marian encyclicals like Redemptoris Mater (1987).
