Nationalization

Nationalization (or nationalisation) is the process of transforming private assets into public assets by bringing them under the ownership or control of a national government or state. It is the opposite of privatization. Nationalization may occur with or without compensation to the former owners, and is usually justified on grounds of public interest, natural monopoly, national security, or control of natural resources.

History

Waves of nationalization followed the Second World War: in Britain the Labour government nationalized the Bank of England, coal, railways, steel, electricity and gas between 1946 and 1951; France nationalized banks, insurance and Renault. Decolonizing states took control of foreign-owned assets, most famously Egypt's nationalization of the Suez Canal Company in 1956, which triggered the Suez Crisis, and oil nationalizations in the Middle East, Mexico (1938) and Venezuela. The late twentieth century saw widespread privatization, though partial renationalizations followed the 2008 financial crisis — including bank rescues in the UK and US — and "resource nationalism" in the 2000s in Russia, Bolivia and elsewhere.

Compensation and law

International law generally requires "prompt, adequate and effective" compensation for expropriated foreign property, though the standard is disputed; many constitutions require compensation for takings. State-owned enterprises created by nationalization may be run as government departments, public corporations or commercial companies wholly owned by the state.
