Kurchatov, Kazakhstan

Country: Kazakhstan
Region: Abai Region
Founded: 1947
Named for: Igor Kurchatov

Kurchatov (Kazakh: Курчатов) is a town in the Abai Region of eastern Kazakhstan, named after Soviet physicist Igor Kurchatov. It served as the administrative and scientific centre of the Semipalatinsk nuclear test site.

History

The town was founded in 1947 to support the test site, where the Soviet Union conducted its first atomic bomb test in 1949. During the Soviet era it was a closed city, appearing on maps only under code names such as Semipalatinsk-21 and "Moscow-400." Scientists and military personnel staffed its institutes and laboratories.

Present day

After Kazakhstan closed the test site in 1991, the town became home to the National Nuclear Center of Kazakhstan and its Institute of Atomic Energy. Its population is roughly 12,000. Research reactors, including the tokamak KTM, operate there today.
