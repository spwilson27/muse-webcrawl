Rinzai school

The Rinzai school (Japanese: 臨済宗, Rinzai-shū) is one of three main schools of Zen Buddhism in Japan, together with the Sōtō and Ōbaku schools. It is the Japanese line of the Chinese Linji school (from which its name derives), founded by the Tang-dynasty master Linji Yixuan (died 866).

Ryōan-ji in Kyoto, a Rinzai temple famed for its Zen rock garden.

History

Rinzai Zen was transmitted to Japan by Myōan Eisai, who travelled to China and returned in 1191. It found favour with the samurai class and the imperial court, and its great monasteries—the Gozan ("Five Mountain") temples of Kyoto and Kamakura—became major centres of art and Chinese learning. The 18th-century master Hakuin Ekaku revitalised Rinzai practice and systematised its curriculum, shaping all modern Rinzai training.

Practice

Rinzai is characterised by its emphasis on kenshō (seeing one's true nature) and the use of kōan—paradoxical questions or exchanges, such as Hakuin's "What is the sound of one hand?"—studied under a teacher in intensive zazen and sanzen interviews. Training is often described as vigorous and confrontational, contrasting with the Sōtō emphasis on shikantaza ("just sitting"). Rinzai temples also became centres of tea ceremony, calligraphy, ink painting and garden design, leaving a deep imprint on Japanese culture.
