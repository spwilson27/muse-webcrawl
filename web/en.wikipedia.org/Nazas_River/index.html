The Nazas River is a river of north-central Mexico, flowing through the states of Durango and Coahuila. It rises in the Sierra Madre Occidental and runs eastward into the arid Bolsón de Mapimí, where it historically fed the Laguna de Mayrán near Torreón, though its waters are now almost entirely impounded and diverted for irrigation.

Course and use

The river is regulated by dams, notably the Lázaro Cárdenas Dam (El Palmito). Its waters sustain the La Laguna region, an important agricultural area producing cotton, forage and dairy. Because of heavy diversion, the lower river rarely reaches its natural terminus.

Name

The name derives from the nazas, fish traps used by early Spanish explorers and settlers along its banks.
