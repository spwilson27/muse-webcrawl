Pythagoreion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Pythagoreion is the archaeological site of the ancient town of Samos (modern Pythagoreio), on the island of Samos in the eastern Aegean Sea, Greece. Together with the nearby Heraion of Samos, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1992. The modern town took its name from the mathematician Pythagoras, who was born on Samos around 570 BC.

Remains

The site preserves substantial remains of one of the most powerful Greek maritime cities of the Archaic period, much of it dating to the reign of the tyrant Polycrates in the sixth century BC, whose engineering works were praised by Herodotus. Principal remains include:

  • The Tunnel of Eupalinos, a 1,036-metre aqueduct tunnel dug through Mount Kastro from both ends simultaneously — a masterpiece of ancient engineering, designed by Eupalinos of Megara.
  • The ancient harbour mole, among the earliest known artificial harbour works in the Mediterranean, whose foundations still underlie the modern breakwater.
  • Sections of the city walls, the Roman baths, the agora, and sanctuaries including the great Temple of Hera at the Heraion.

History

Ancient Samos flourished as a trading and naval power in the sixth century BC, colonising and trading across the Mediterranean. The city continued to be inhabited through Hellenistic, Roman and Byzantine times; its ruins were excavated principally by German and Greek archaeologists from the nineteenth century onward.
