Harmonices Mundi

Author: Johannes Kepler

Published: 1619, Linz

Language: Latin

Subject: Astronomy, geometry, music theory

Harmonices Mundi (The Harmony of the World), published by Johannes Kepler in 1619, is a work of astronomy and natural philosophy in five books. Extending the Pythagorean idea of the "music of the spheres", Kepler sought the geometrical and musical harmonies he believed God had used in creating the cosmos — and in the process announced his third law of planetary motion.

Contents

The first two books treat regular polygons and the congruence of harmonic figures, laying geometrical foundations. Book III develops a theory of musical harmony and applies it to astrology and the soul. Book IV continues the metaphysical and psychological themes, while Book V turns to the motions of the planets, assigning each planet a musical scale or melody generated by its varying orbital speed.

Third law

Buried within Book V is Kepler's third (harmonic) law: the square of a planet's orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit (T² ∝ a³). Together with his first two laws, published in Astronomia Nova (1609), it completed the description of planetary motion later explained by Newton's gravitation.
