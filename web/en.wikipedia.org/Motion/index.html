Motion

In physics, motion is the phenomenon in which an object changes its position over time with respect to a reference point. Motion is described mathematically in terms of displacement, distance, velocity, acceleration, speed, and time.

Description

An object is said to be in motion if its position relative to a chosen frame of reference changes with time; there is no absolute rest — motion is always relative to the observer's frame. The branch of physics that describes motion without regard to its causes is kinematics, while dynamics studies the forces that produce it.

Laws of motion

Classical mechanics summarizes motion in Newton's laws of motion: an object maintains its velocity unless acted on by a net force; the net force equals mass times acceleration; and every action produces an equal and opposite reaction. These laws govern everyday and celestial motion to high accuracy, but they are superseded in extreme regimes: at speeds near that of light by special relativity, and at atomic scales by quantum mechanics, in which particles lack definite trajectories.

Types of motion

Common classifications distinguish translational and rotational motion, periodic motion (such as oscillations and waves), projectile motion, and random Brownian motion. All observable motion in the universe, from subatomic particles to galaxies, is studied within the frameworks of classical mechanics, relativity, and quantum theory.
