Watling Street

Watling Street is a historic route in England that crosses the country from the Channel coast at Dover (and Richborough) through London to Wroxeter in Shropshire, and in extended form toward north Wales. Originally an ancient trackway used by the Britons, it was paved and developed by the Romans after their invasion of Britain in AD 43.

History

The road was one of the principal routes of Roman Britain. It was the scene of Boudica's final defeat at the Battle of Watling Street in AD 60 or 61, at an uncertain location along the road. After the Anglo-Saxon settlement, the road formed part of the boundary between the English and the Danelaw under the Treaty of Alfred and Guthrum in the late 9th century.

Name and modern route

The name derives from the Old English Wæcelinga stræt, "the paved road of the Wæcelingas", a group associated with St Albans. Much of the route is followed today by the A2 road from Dover to London and the A5 road from London toward Shrewsbury and Holyhead. Sections remain as local streets named Watling Street in towns such as Dartford, Rochester, Canterbury, and St Albans.
