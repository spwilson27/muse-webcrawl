Chinese language

Chinese is a group of languages forming the Sinitic branch of the Sino-Tibetan language family, spoken by more than 1.3 billion people, principally in China. The varieties are often mutually unintelligible in speech but share a common writing system based on Chinese characters.

Varieties

Major groups include Mandarin (the largest, basis of Standard Chinese), Wu, Yue (including Cantonese), Min, Xiang, Hakka and Gan. Standard Chinese, based on the Beijing dialect of Mandarin, is the official language of the People's Republic of China and Taiwan and one of the official languages of Singapore and the United Nations.

Writing and phonology

Chinese is tonal and largely monosyllabic and analytic. Characters are logographic, each generally representing a syllable with meaning. Simplified characters are used in mainland China, traditional characters in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau. Pinyin romanises Mandarin pronunciation.
