Thyristor

Thyristor
TypeSemiconductor device
StructureFour layers, three p–n junctions
First produced1956 (Bell Labs / General Electric)

A thyristor is a solid-state semiconductor device with four layers of alternating P- and N-type materials and three terminals: an anode, a cathode, and a gate. It acts as a bistable switch, conducting when its gate receives a current pulse and continuing to conduct as long as current flows through it above a holding threshold.

Operation and types

The best-known thyristor is the silicon controlled rectifier (SCR). Other members of the family include the TRIAC, which conducts in both directions and is widely used in lamp dimmers, and the gate turn-off thyristor (GTO), which can be switched off from the gate. Because thyristors can control very large voltages and currents, they are central to power electronics.

Applications

Thyristors are used in high-voltage direct current (HVDC) power transmission, motor speed controllers, light dimmers, pressure control systems, and surge protection (crowbar circuits).
