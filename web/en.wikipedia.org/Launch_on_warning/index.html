Launch on warning

Launch on warning (LOW) is a nuclear strategy in which a retaliatory strike is launched after early-warning systems detect incoming enemy missiles but before those missiles arrive and detonate. It is distinguished from launch under attack, which waits for confirmed nuclear detonations.

Rationale and risks

The posture aims to preserve land-based missile forces that might be destroyed in their silos, deterring a first strike. Critics note it compresses decision time to a few minutes and risks catastrophic error from false alarms — several Cold War incidents, such as the 1983 Serpukhov-15 false alarm reported by Stanislav Petrov, illustrate the danger. Whether any state has formally adopted LOW is often ambiguous in official doctrine.
