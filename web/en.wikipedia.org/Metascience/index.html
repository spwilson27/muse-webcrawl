Metascience

Metascience (also known as meta-research) is the use of scientific methodology to study science itself. It examines how research is conducted, reported, rewarded and replicated, with the aim of improving the efficiency and reliability of scientific inquiry.

Areas of study

Metascience investigates, among other topics, publication bias, questionable research practices such as p-hacking, the design and incentives of peer review, funding allocation, and the reproducibility of published findings. Large-scale replication projects in psychology, economics and cancer biology during the 2010s brought the field prominence by showing that many published results could not be reproduced.

Reforms

Metascientific evidence motivates reforms including study pre-registration, registered reports, open data and code sharing, statistical reforms, and changes to academic incentives. Prominent researchers include John Ioannidis, author of the influential 2005 paper "Why Most Published Research Findings Are False".

Relation to other fields

Metascience draws on and overlaps with scientometrics, the history, philosophy and sociology of science, and research-on-research in medicine, but is distinguished by its explicitly reform-oriented, experimental approach.
