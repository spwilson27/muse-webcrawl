Reference class forecasting

Reference class forecasting (RCF) is a method of predicting the future by examining similar past situations and their outcomes. Developed by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky and later applied to major projects by Bent Flyvbjerg, it is designed to counter the optimism bias that afflicts conventional forecasts.

Method

The forecaster (1) identifies a reference class of comparable past projects, (2) establishes the distribution of outcomes for that class, and (3) positions the specific project within that distribution, adjusting the conventional estimate accordingly. Kahneman called this the "outside view", as opposed to the "inside view" that focuses on the particulars of the case at hand.

Applications

RCF has been endorsed in guidance for transport infrastructure appraisal in the United Kingdom, Denmark, and elsewhere, where data show large systematic cost overruns on projects such as rail schemes. It is also used in business and policy forecasting wherever reliable reference-class data exist.
