Charlotte of Belgium (7 June 1840 – 19 January 1927), known in Mexico as Carlota, was a Belgian princess who became Empress of Mexico as the wife of Emperor Maximilian I during the Second Mexican Empire (1864–1867). The only daughter of King Leopold I of Belgium, she was a granddaughter of Louis Philippe I of France and first cousin of Queen Victoria.

Marriage and Mexico

In 1857 Charlotte married Archduke Maximilian of Austria, serving with him in Lombardy–Venetia and at Miramare Castle near Trieste. When Mexican conservatives and Napoleon III offered Maximilian the Mexican crown, Charlotte enthusiastically supported acceptance. Crowned in Mexico City in 1864, she acted as regent during Maximilian's absences, promoted roads, charities, and liberal reforms, and administered the empire with more decisiveness than her husband. But the empire, sustained only by French troops, was crumbling against the republican forces of Benito Juárez and growing United States pressure after the American Civil War.

Madness and later life

In 1866 Charlotte sailed to Europe to plead for continued support from Napoleon III and Pope Pius IX. The mission failed, and in Rome she suffered a severe mental breakdown, manifesting paranoid delusions. Maximilian, refusing to abdicate, was captured and executed at Querétaro in June 1867; Charlotte never learned to fully accept his death. Declared insane, she lived the remaining sixty years of her life in seclusion — first at Miramare, then at the Château de Tervuren and finally at Bouchout Castle in Meise, Belgium — cared for by her brother Leopold II's household. She died in 1927; her final years saw flashes of lucidity reported by attendants, but she never recovered her sanity. Her tragic life has inspired novels, plays, and films, including Juarez (1939) and the Mexican telenovela tradition.
