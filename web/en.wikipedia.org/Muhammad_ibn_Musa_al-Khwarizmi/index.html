Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi

Bornc. 780, Khwarazm (probably)
Diedc. 850, Baghdad
Known forAlgebra; Hindu–Arabic numerals; astronomy and geography
EraIslamic Golden Age

Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi (c. 780 – c. 850) was a Persian mathematician, astronomer, and geographer who worked at the House of Wisdom in Baghdad under the Abbasid caliphs al-Ma'mun and his successors. His name indicates origin in Khwarazm, the region south of the Aral Sea. He is regarded as the founder of algebra as an independent discipline, and his works introduced Hindu–Arabic numerals and systematic algebra to the Latin West.

Algebra

His treatise The Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing (Arabic: Kitāb al-Jabr wa'l-Muqābala, c. 820) gave algebra its name, from al-jabr ("completion", the restoring of subtracted terms). Written in prose without symbols, the book classified linear and quadratic equations into six standard types and solved them by geometric arguments, applying the methods to inheritance law, trade, and land measurement. Latin translations from the 12th century onward — one by Robert of Chester — made "algebra" a European discipline.

Arithmetic and numerals

His book on Hindu reckoning, surviving in Latin as Algoritmi de numero Indorum, described the decimal positional system with nine figures and zero. The Latinization of his name, Algoritmi, is the origin of the word "algorithm". Through these translations, Hindu–Arabic numerals gradually displaced Roman numerals in European commerce and science.

Astronomy and geography

Al-Khwarizmi compiled astronomical tables (zij), works on the astrolabe and sundials, and a Book of the Description of the Earth (c. 833) revising Ptolemy's geography with coordinates for more than two thousand localities and an improved map of the known world. He also participated in al-Ma'mun's project to measure the length of a degree of the Earth's meridian.

Legacy

His name survives in "algebra" and "algorithm"; the crater Al-Khwarizmi on the Moon and a monument in Khiva commemorate him. Historians rank him among the most influential mathematicians of the medieval world.
