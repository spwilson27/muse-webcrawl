Iguazu River

The Iguazu River (Spanish: Río Iguazú; Portuguese: Rio Iguaçu) is a river in Brazil and Argentina and a tributary of the Paraná River. It rises in the Serra do Mar mountains near Curitiba, Brazil, flows generally westward for about 1,320 kilometres (820 mi), and joins the Paraná at the "Triple Frontier" where Brazil, Argentina, and Paraguay meet, near the cities of Foz do Iguaçu and Puerto Iguazú.

Iguazu Falls

About 23 kilometres before its confluence with the Paraná, the river widens and plunges over the edge of the Paraná Plateau, forming the Iguazu Falls — a system of about 275 separate waterfalls extending for 2.7 kilometres, with heights of up to 82 metres. The falls, set in subtropical rainforest protected by national parks in both countries, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New7Wonders of Nature.

Course and ecology

The upper Iguazu flows through the Brazilian state of Paraná, where it is dammed for hydroelectric power (including the Salto Santiago and Salto Osório plants). Its lower course forms part of the Brazil–Argentina border. The basin supports rich Atlantic Forest biodiversity, much of it protected in Iguaçu and Iguazú National Parks.
