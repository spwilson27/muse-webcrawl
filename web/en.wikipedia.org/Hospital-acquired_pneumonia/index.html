Hospital-acquired pneumonia

SpecialtyPulmonology, infectious disease
Onset48 hours or more after hospital admission
Risk factorsMechanical ventilation, prolonged hospitalisation, immunosuppression

Hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP), or nosocomial pneumonia, is a type of pneumonia that occurs 48 hours or more after admission to a hospital and was not incubating at the time of admission. It is among the most common hospital-acquired infections and a significant cause of morbidity and mortality in hospitalised patients.

Ventilator-associated pneumonia

Pneumonia that arises more than 48 hours after endotracheal intubation is termed ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP). Intubated patients are at particular risk because the endotracheal tube bypasses normal airway defences and provides a surface for bacterial colonisation and aspiration of oropharyngeal secretions.

Causes

Common pathogens include Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA), and Enterobacteriaceae such as Klebsiella pneumoniae and Escherichia coli. Hospital pathogens are frequently resistant to multiple antibiotics, which complicates treatment.

Prevention and treatment

Prevention strategies include elevating the head of the bed, oral hygiene with chlorhexidine, hand hygiene, early mobilisation, and minimising the duration of mechanical ventilation. Treatment requires prompt empirical antibiotic therapy chosen according to local resistance patterns and risk factors for multidrug-resistant organisms, later refined by culture results.
