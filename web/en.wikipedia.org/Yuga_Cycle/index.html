The Yuga Cycle is the cyclical conception of time in Hindu cosmology, in which the world passes through four ages (yugas) of declining virtue and lifespan. Together the four yugas form a mahāyuga (great age) of 4,320,000 years, and the cycle repeats indefinitely.

The four yugas

In the Satya Yuga (1,728,000 years), dharma is complete and humanity is virtuous. The Treta Yuga (1,296,000 years) sees righteousness diminish by a quarter, followed by the Dvapara Yuga (864,000 years), in which virtue halves. Finally the Kali Yuga (432,000 years), the present age, is characterised by conflict and decline, after which the cycle renews.

Cosmological scale

A thousand mahāyugas make a kalpa, a single day of the god Brahma, followed by a night of equal length. Traditional calculations date the beginning of the current Kali Yuga to 3102 BCE, associated with the aftermath of the Mahābhārata war.
