Heaven

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Heaven, or the heavens, is a common religious cosmological or transcendent supernatural place where beings such as deities, angels, souls, saints, or venerated ancestors are said to originate, be enthroned, or reside. According to the beliefs of some religions, heavenly beings can descend to Earth or incarnate and earthly beings can ascend to Heaven in the afterlife or, in exceptional cases, enter Heaven without dying.

Heaven is often described as a "best place", a paradise, in contrast to an underworld or Hell. In some religions, the heavens are also identified with the sky and with the dwelling of a creator god.

Historical development

Ideas of a blissful realm above parallel the development of the religions of the ancient Near East. In Mesopotamian and Egyptian thought the sky was the gods' domain; Egyptian religion also described the Field of Reeds, a paradisiacal continuation of earthly life. In Zoroastrianism, the soul of the righteous crosses the Chinvat Bridge to the House of Song. Ancient Greek religion placed the gods on Mount Olympus and the virtuous dead in Elysium, within or beyond the realm of Hades.

In Abrahamic religions

In Judaism, shamayim denotes the sky and the divine abode; later tradition developed Gan Eden (paradise) and the World to Come (Olam Ha-Ba). In Christianity, heaven is the destiny of the saved: the state or place of eternal life and union with God, involving in Catholic teaching the beatific vision, preceded possibly by purgatory, and culminating in resurrection and a new heaven and new earth in many eschatologies. In Islam, Jannah (Paradise) is described in the Qur'an as gardens beneath which rivers flow, the reward for the righteous, within a seven-layered heaven (samāwāt) traversed by Muhammad on the Night Journey (Mi'raj).

In Asian religions

Hindu cosmology describes multiple heavens (Svarga, the abode of Indra, among others) that are impermanent stations within samsara; liberation (moksha) lies beyond them. Buddhism likewise recognises heavenly realms (Devaloka) inhabited by long-lived devas, yet these too are impermanent and not the goal — nirvana is. Jain and Sikh traditions have their own conceptions of exalted states and realms of the divine.

Concepts and depictions

Theologians debate whether heaven is a physical place, a state of being, or both; near-death experiences and mystical literature (such as Dante's Paradiso) have shaped popular imagery of light, music, reunions with loved ones and the absence of suffering. The singular "Heaven" in English generally denotes the religious concept, while "the heavens" may refer to the sky or outer space.
