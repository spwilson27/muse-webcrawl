Grant Vernon Hart (March 18, 1961 – September 13, 2017) was an American musician, best known as the drummer and co-singer/songwriter for the alternative rock and hardcore punk band Hüsker Dü. Formed in Saint Paul, Minnesota, in 1979 with Bob Mould and Greg Norton, Hüsker Dü became one of the most influential American underground bands of the 1980s.

Hüsker Dü

Hart shared singing and songwriting duties with guitarist Bob Mould. While Mould's songs tended toward the aggressive and introspective, Hart's contributions — including "It's Not Funny Anymore", "Books About UFOs", "Diane" and "Turn on the News" — often displayed a poppier, more melodic sensibility. The band signed to Warner Bros. Records in 1986 and released the albums Candy Apple Grey and Warehouse: Songs and Stories before internal tensions, compounded by drug use and the suicide of manager David Savoy, led to their breakup in 1988.

Later career

Hart subsequently formed the band Nova Mob, which released two albums in the early 1990s, and recorded as a solo artist. His solo albums include Intolerance (1989), Good News for Modern Man (1999), Hot Wax (2009) and the double concept album The Argument (2013), based on John Milton's Paradise Lost. Hart was also a visual artist, producing collage work exhibited in galleries.

Death and legacy

Hart died of liver cancer on September 13, 2017, at the age of 56. He is remembered for helping pioneer the melodic hardcore and alternative rock sounds that influenced bands such as the Pixies and Nirvana.
