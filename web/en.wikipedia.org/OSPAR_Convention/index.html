OSPAR Convention

Signed22 September 1992, Paris
Effective25 March 1998
Parties15 governments and the European Union
SubjectProtection of the marine environment of the North-East Atlantic

The OSPAR Convention is the current legal instrument guiding international cooperation on the protection of the marine environment of the North-East Atlantic. It was adopted at the ministerial meeting of the Oslo and Paris Commissions in Paris on 22 September 1992 and entered into force in 1998. Work under the convention is managed by the OSPAR Commission, made up of representatives of the 15 signatory governments and the European Commission.

Background

The convention combined and updated the 1972 Oslo Convention, which addressed dumping at sea, and the 1974 Paris Convention, which addressed land-based sources of marine pollution. Hence the name "OSPAR," from Oslo and Paris.

Scope

OSPAR's work covers the prevention and elimination of pollution from land-based, offshore and dumping sources; the protection and conservation of ecosystems and biological diversity; hazardous and radioactive substances; and the monitoring and assessment of the quality status of the North-East Atlantic, reported in its periodic Quality Status Reports.
