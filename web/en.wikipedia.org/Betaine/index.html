Betaine

Betaine, in chemistry, is any neutral chemical compound with a positively charged cationic functional group (such as a quaternary ammonium) that bears no hydrogen atom, and a negatively charged group such as a carboxylate. In biology, the term usually refers specifically to trimethylglycine, a naturally occurring compound first isolated from sugar beets (Beta vulgaris), from which it takes its name.

Biological role

Trimethylglycine functions as an osmolyte, helping cells maintain volume under osmotic stress, and as a methyl donor in the remethylation of homocysteine to methionine, a reaction catalysed by the enzyme betaine–homocysteine methyltransferase. Betaine is obtained from foods such as beets, spinach, quinoa and wheat, and can also be synthesised in the body from choline.

Uses

Anhydrous betaine is used as a dietary supplement and, in pharmaceutical form, to treat homocystinuria, a genetic disorder of methionine metabolism. Betaine hydrochloride has been marketed as a digestive aid. In industry, betaines such as cocamidopropyl betaine are widely used as amphoteric surfactants in shampoos and cosmetics.
