Jacob's Ladder

Jacob's Ladder (Hebrew: סֻלָּם יַעֲקֹב, Sullām Yaʿăqōḇ) is a ladder or staircase leading to heaven that the biblical patriarch Jacob dreamed about during his flight from his brother Esau, as described in the Book of Genesis (Genesis 28:10–19).

The biblical account

Fleeing from Beersheba toward Haran, Jacob stopped for the night and slept with a stone for a pillow. He dreamed of a stairway resting on the earth with its top reaching to heaven, on which the angels of God were ascending and descending. God stood above it and renewed to Jacob the covenant made with Abraham: the gift of the land, innumerable descendants, and divine protection. Waking, Jacob declared:

Surely the Lord is in this place — and I did not know it! … How awesome is this place! This is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven.

He set up the stone as a pillar, poured oil on it, and named the place Bethel ("house of God").

Interpretation

Jewish commentators have read the ladder variously as a symbol of prayer, of the link between heaven and earth, of exile and return, or of Mount Sinai. In Christian interpretation, the ladder is frequently read as a type of Christ, following John 1:51, in which Jesus tells Nathanael that he will see "angels of God ascending and descending on the Son of Man". The image is also important in Islam, where Jacob (Yaʿqūb) is a prophet, and the ladder has been compared with Muhammad's ascension (Mi'raj).

In culture

The image of Jacob's ladder has inspired centuries of art, hymns and literature — from medieval manuscripts to the African-American spiritual "We Are Climbing Jacob's Ladder". The phrase has been applied to many other things named after the biblical image, including a high-voltage electrical arc device, the flowering plant Polemonium, films, novels and a 1990 psychological horror film, Jacob's Ladder.
