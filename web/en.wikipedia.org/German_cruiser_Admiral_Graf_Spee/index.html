Admiral Graf Spee was a Deutschland-class Panzerschiff ("armoured ship"), known in Britain as a "pocket battleship", built for the German Reichsmarine and serving in the Kriegsmarine. Named after Admiral Maximilian von Spee, commander of the East Asia Squadron lost at the Battle of the Falkland Islands in 1914, she combined cruiser speed with six 28 cm (11 in) guns — powerful enough to outgun any faster ship and fast enough to escape any stronger one — while nominally respecting the Treaty of Versailles' 10,000-ton limit, which her true displacement in fact exceeded.

Pre-war service

Launched at Wilhelmshaven in 1934 and commissioned in 1936, Graf Spee made several non-intervention patrols off Spain during the Spanish Civil War and represented Germany at the Coronation Naval Review of 1937. In May 1939 she cruised the Atlantic in exercises that foreshadowed her wartime raiding role.

Commerce raiding, 1939

Graf Spee sailed before the outbreak of war and, under Captain Hans Langsdorff, began attacking Allied merchant shipping in the South Atlantic and Indian Ocean in late September 1939. Supplied by the tanker Altmark, she sank nine merchant ships totalling about 50,000 tons; following explicit orders, Langsdorff took crews prisoner and no lives were lost on the ships he sank.

Battle of the River Plate and scuttling

On 13 December 1939 Graf Spee engaged a British hunting group — the heavy cruiser HMS Exeter and light cruisers HMS Ajax and HMNZS Achilles — in the Battle of the River Plate, the first major naval battle of the war. She badly damaged Exeter but was herself hit and withdrew into the neutral port of Montevideo, Uruguay. Deceived by British disinformation into believing overwhelming forces awaited him outside, and refused more than 72 hours to repair, Langsdorff sailed out on 17 December and scuttled his ship in the River Plate estuary before thousands of spectators. He shot himself in Buenos Aires three days later. The wreck was partially salvaged; artefacts including her rangefinder and bronze eagle are displayed in Montevideo, and the wreck was formally surveyed in later decades.
