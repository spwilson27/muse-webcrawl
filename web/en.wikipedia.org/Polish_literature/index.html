Polish literature

Polish literature is the literary tradition of Poland, written mostly in Polish. Its beginnings date to the Middle Ages, with Latin chronicles by Gallus Anonymus and later vernacular works in the Renaissance by Jan Kochanowski, considered the father of Polish poetry.

The Romantic period produced the Three Bards — Adam Mickiewicz, Juliusz Słowacki and Zygmunt Krasiński — whose works sustained national consciousness during the partitions. Mickiewicz's epic Pan Tadeusz (1834) is regarded as the national epic. In the late 19th century Henryk Sienkiewicz won the Nobel Prize in 1905.

Modern literature

Twentieth-century Nobel laureates include Władysław Reymont (1924), Czesław Miłosz (1980) and Wisława Szymborska (1996). Olga Tokarczuk received the Nobel Prize in 2018. Notable authors also include Stanisław Lem, Bruno Schulz, Witold Gombrowicz and Ryszard Kapuściński.
