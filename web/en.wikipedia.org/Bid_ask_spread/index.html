Bid–ask spread


Illustration: Bid–ask spread

The bid–ask spread (also bid–offer or bid/ask) is the difference between the prices quoted for an immediate sale (bid) and an immediate purchase (ask) of an asset. In other words, it is the difference between the highest price that a buyer is willing to pay and the lowest price that a seller is willing to accept.

Components

The spread compensates market makers for inventory risk, order-processing costs and adverse selection — the risk of trading with better-informed counterparties. Liquid markets with heavy trading volume, such as major currency pairs, typically have very tight spreads, while illiquid securities have wide spreads.

Example

If a stock is quoted at a bid of $10.00 and an ask of $10.05, the spread is $0.05. A trader buying at the ask and immediately selling at the bid loses the spread, which is why it is a key measure of transaction costs. Related topics include day trading.
