Silk Road was an online black market and the first modern darknet market. It operated as a Tor hidden service, so that users could browse it anonymously and securely without potential traffic monitoring. Launched in February 2011, it is best known as a platform for selling illegal drugs, though it also offered other illicit goods and services; the site's operators prohibited items whose purpose was to "harm or defraud," such as stolen credit cards, assassinations, and weapons of mass destruction. All transactions were conducted in Bitcoin, an early large-scale use of the cryptocurrency, with an escrow system and eBay-style seller reputation ratings.

Founder and shutdown

The site was run by the pseudonymous "Dread Pirate Roberts," identified by the FBI as Ross William Ulbricht, a former physics graduate and libertarian who had built the site to create an online market free from government interference. Publicity came with a June 2011 Gawker article, followed by calls from U.S. Senator Charles Schumer for a shutdown. After a lengthy investigation — including an undercover agent joining the site's staff and the tracing of early forum posts — Ulbricht was arrested on 1 October 2013 in the Glen Park branch of the San Francisco Public Library, and the FBI seized the site the next day. A successor, Silk Road 2.0, launched within weeks and was itself shut down in November 2014 in Operation Onymous.

Trial and aftermath

Ulbricht was convicted in federal court in Manhattan in February 2015 on seven counts, including engaging in a continuing criminal enterprise, narcotics trafficking, and money laundering, and was sentenced to life imprisonment without parole in May 2015. (He also faced murder-for-hire allegations in a separate indictment, which were never tried.) Two federal agents on the investigation, Carl Mark Force IV and Shaun Bridges, were themselves convicted of stealing bitcoin from the site. Ulbricht's case became a cause célèbre among libertarians and crypto advocates; in January 2025, U.S. President Donald Trump granted him a full and unconditional pardon. Researchers have credited Silk Road with pioneering darknet markets and with a harm-reduction argument — that buying drugs online reduced violence associated with street dealing — while critics stressed the harms of the trade it enabled.
