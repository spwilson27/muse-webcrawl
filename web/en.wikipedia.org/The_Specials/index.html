The Specials
Origin: Coventry, England
Genres: 2 Tone, ska, new wave
Years active: 1977–1984, 1993–present (various line-ups)
Labels: 2 Tone, Chrysalis
Members / founders: Jerry Dammers, Terry Hall, Neville Staple, Horace Panter, Roddy Radiation, John Bradbury, Lynval Golding

The Specials, also known as the Special AKA, are an English 2 Tone and ska revival band formed in 1977 in Coventry. They pioneered the 2 Tone movement, combining Jamaican ska and rocksteady with punk energy and politically charged lyrics about racial unity and social decay.

History

Founded by keyboardist and songwriter Jerry Dammers, the band launched the 2 Tone Records label. Their debut single "Gangsters" (1979) and albums The Specials (1979) and More Specials (1980) produced hits including "A Message to You, Rudy", "Too Much Too Young" and the UK number one "Ghost Town" (1981), which captured Britain's urban unrest.

Split and reunions

Hall, Staple and Golding left in 1981 to form Fun Boy Three; Dammers continued as the Special AKA, releasing "Free Nelson Mandela" (1984). Various reunions followed from the 1990s, with a successful reformation in 2008–2009 and the album Encore (2019), their first UK number one album. Terry Hall died in December 2022.

Legacy

Their black-and-white imagery, multiracial line-up and fusion sound defined 2 Tone and influenced ska punk and Britpop.
