Sapta Puri

The Sapta Puri (Sanskrit: सप्तपुरी, "seven cities") are seven holy pilgrimage centres in Hinduism, revered as places where a devotee may attain moksha (liberation from the cycle of rebirth). The seven cities are Ayodhya, Mathura, Haridwar, Varanasi, Kanchipuram, Ujjain and Dwarka. They are among the most ancient continuously celebrated sacred places in India, and together they form one of the classic pilgrimage circuits of the Hindu tradition.

The seven cities

  • Ayodhya — on the Sarayu river in Uttar Pradesh, birthplace of Rama, avatar of Vishnu.
  • Mathura — on the Yamuna in Uttar Pradesh, birthplace of Krishna.
  • Haridwar (Maya) — in Uttarakhand, where the Ganges descends from the Himalayas to the plains; one of the four sites of the Kumbh Mela.
  • Varanasi (Kashi) — on the Ganges in Uttar Pradesh, the city of Shiva, famed for its ghats and the Kashi Vishwanath Temple.
  • Kanchipuram — in Tamil Nadu, a city of a thousand temples sacred to both Shiva and Vishnu.
  • Ujjain (Avantika) — on the Shipra in Madhya Pradesh, home of the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga and another Kumbh Mela site.
  • Dwarka — on the coast of Gujarat, the legendary capital of Krishna's kingdom.

Significance

A verse in the Garuda Purana lists the seven as givers of liberation: "Ayodhya, Mathura, Maya (Haridwar), Kashi, Kanchi, Avantika (Ujjain) and Dvaravati (Dwarka) — these seven cities bestow moksha." Several of the Sapta Puri are also among the Char Dham or host Jyotirlinga shrines, multiplying their importance in Hindu sacred geography. Pilgrimage (tirtha yatra) to these cities is believed to cleanse sins and, particularly in the case of dying or being cremated at Varanasi, to secure release from samsara.
