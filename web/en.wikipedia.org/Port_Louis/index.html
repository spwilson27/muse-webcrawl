Port Louis is the capital and largest city of Mauritius, in the Indian Ocean. Founded by the French in 1735 and named after King Louis XV, it is the country's principal port, financial centre and seat of government, set in a natural harbour on the northwest coast beneath the Moka mountain range.

History

The harbour had been used by Dutch sailors from 1606; under Governor Bertrand-François Mahé de La Bourdonnais from 1735, Port Louis became a fortified capital and supply base for French shipping to India. Britain captured the island in 1810 and kept the city as capital. Indentured labourers from India arrived through the Aapravasi Ghat immigration depot — now a UNESCO World Heritage Site — from 1834. Mauritius became independent in 1968 with Port Louis as its capital.

Landmarks and economy

Sights include the Citadel (Fort Adelaide), the Champ de Mars racecourse (1812, among the oldest in the southern hemisphere), the Central Market, Chinatown, and the Blue Penny Museum, home to the famous 1847 "Post Office" stamps. The port handles most of the island's trade, and the city anchors Mauritius's financial-services and business districts.
