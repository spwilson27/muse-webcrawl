Rock (geology)

Rock
CompositionOne or more minerals or mineraloids
Main classesIgneous, sedimentary, metamorphic
StudyPetrology

In geology, a rock is a naturally occurring solid mass or aggregate of minerals or mineraloid matter. Rocks form the Earth's outer solid layer, the crust, and much of its interior. They are classified by how they were formed into three great groups — igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic — and are continually recycled through the rock cycle.

Igneous rocks

Igneous rocks crystallise from molten material: magma underground, or lava at the surface. Slow cooling deep below ground produces coarse-grained intrusive rocks such as granite; rapid cooling of lava produces fine-grained or glassy extrusive rocks such as basalt and obsidian. Composition ranges from silica-rich (felsic) to iron- and magnesium-rich (mafic).

Sedimentary rocks

Sedimentary rocks form by the deposition, burial and lithification of sediments — fragments of older rock (sandstone, shale), chemical precipitates (limestone, rock salt) or accumulations of organic matter (coal). They cover about three-quarters of the land surface as a thin veneer and preserve nearly all fossils, making them the primary archive of Earth's surface history. Mountain belts such as the Rocky Mountains expose thick folded sequences of them.

Metamorphic rocks

Metamorphic rocks arise when existing rocks are transformed by heat, pressure or chemically active fluids without melting — for example shale baking into slate and then schist and gneiss with increasing grade, or limestone recrystallising into marble.

Importance

Rocks are humanity's principal source of building stone, aggregate, metals, fossil fuels and groundwater (from aquifers). Their scientific study, petrology, underpins geology, mining and civil engineering.
