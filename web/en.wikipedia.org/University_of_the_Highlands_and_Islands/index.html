The University of the Highlands and Islands (UHI; Scottish Gaelic: Oilthigh na Gàidhealtachd agus nan Eilean) is a public, federated university serving the Highlands and Islands of Scotland. It is unusual in combining further and higher education across a vast rural region — covering roughly half of Scotland's landmass — through a partnership of colleges and research institutes rather than a single campus.

History

The institution grew from the UHI Millennium Institute, formed in 2001 as a partnership of local further education colleges in Inverness, Perth, Moray, Orkney, Shetland, Argyll, the Outer Hebrides, and elsewhere, together with specialist bodies such as the Scottish Association for Marine Science and Sabhal Mòr Ostaig, the Gaelic college on Skye. Degree-awarding powers followed, and full university status was granted by the Privy Council in February 2011.

Structure and teaching

Students study at local campuses and learning centres across the region, connected by one of the UK's most extensive video-conferencing and virtual learning networks — a model designed to let people gain degrees without leaving remote communities. Courses reflect the region's character, including Gaelic language and culture, marine science, renewable energy, adventure tourism, and archaeology.
