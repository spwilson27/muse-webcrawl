Mozzarella (Italian: [mottsaˈrɛlla]; Neapolitan: muzzarella) is a semi-soft non-aged cheese prepared by the pasta filata ('spun paste') method with origins from southern Italy. It is prepared with cow's milk or buffalo milk, taking the following names: "Mozzarella fior di latte" or "mozzarella" for the cow's milk type, and "Mozzarella di Bufala Campana" for the buffalo milk type, which has protected designation of origin (PDO) status under EU law. Fresh mozzarella is generally white but may vary from off-white to slightly yellow depending on the animal's diet. It is a stretched-curd cheese: fresh curd is heated in hot water and stretched, kneaded, and formed into balls until smooth and elastic.

Production

Milk is curdled with rennet and starter bacteria (typically Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus species), and the curd is cut, drained, and allowed to acidify. The acidified curd is then immersed in hot water (around 80–90 °C) and stretched and folded by hand or machine until it forms long, glossy strands — the pasta filata process it shares with provolone, caciocavallo, and Oaxaca cheese. The hot paste is cut ("mozzata", whence the name) and shaped into balls, braids (treccia), or small spheres (bocconcini, ciliegine), then cooled in brine and sold in whey or brine.

Varieties

Beyond fresh buffalo and cow's-milk mozzarella, the category includes burrata (a mozzarella pouch filled with stracciatella and cream), fior di latte, smoked mozzarella (mozzarella affumicata), and the firm, low-moisture mozzarella developed in the United States for pizza — sold in blocks or pre-shredded, it melts evenly and browns well. Industrial mozzarella production in the US, driven by the pizza industry, exceeds Italian output by a wide margin.

Uses

Fresh mozzarella is eaten as an antipasto, classically in the Caprese salad with tomatoes, basil, and olive oil, where its colours echo the Italian flag. Melted, it is the defining cheese of Neapolitan pizza (where mozzarella di bufala campana or fior di latte is specified by the AVPN rules), lasagne, parmigiana, calzones, and numerous baked pasta dishes. Its mild, milky flavour and clean melt make it one of the world's most consumed cheeses.
