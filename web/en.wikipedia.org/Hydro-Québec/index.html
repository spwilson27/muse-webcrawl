Hydro-Québec is a Canadian Crown corporation, owned by the Government of Quebec, that manages the generation, transmission, and distribution of electricity in the province of Quebec. Founded in 1944, it is one of the largest producers of hydroelectric power in the world, operating more than 60 hydroelectric generating stations with a total installed capacity of roughly 38,000 megawatts. Its vast reservoir system, concentrated in the James Bay region of northern Quebec, gives it one of the largest energy storage capacities of any utility.

History

Hydro-Québec was created by the government of Adélard Godbout in 1944 through the nationalisation of Montreal Light, Heat and Power. The corporation was greatly expanded during the Quiet Revolution under Premier Jean Lesage and his minister René Lévesque: in 1962–1963 the government bought out the remaining private electricity companies, making Hydro-Québec the dominant provincial utility, in line with the campaign slogan Maîtres chez nous ("masters in our own house").

The 1970s saw the launch of the James Bay Project, one of the largest hydroelectric developments ever undertaken, built under the James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement with the Cree and Inuit. The project remains controversial for its effects on Indigenous lands and ecosystems. Hydro-Québec's engineers also pioneered 735-kV transmission, among the highest voltages used anywhere when introduced.

Operations

Because more than 94% of its generation is hydroelectric, Hydro-Québec supplies some of the lowest-carbon and lowest-priced electricity in North America, exporting surplus power to Ontario, New Brunswick, and the northeastern United States, including under a long-term agreement to supply New York City via the Champlain Hudson Power Express line. Residential electricity rates in Quebec are among the lowest in North America, and large industrial users are attracted by inexpensive power.
