Seasoning (cookware)

Seasoning is the process of treating the surface of cookware, typically cast iron or carbon steel, with heated oil or fat so that it polymerises and bonds to the metal, forming a hard, protective, relatively non-stick coating. A well-seasoned pan resists rust and releases food more easily than bare metal.

Seasoning (cookware)
Applies toCast iron, carbon steel, woks
MaterialDrying or cooking oil/fat
PurposeNon-stick surface; rust protection

Process

To season a pan, a very thin layer of oil is applied to clean, dry cookware and heated, usually in an oven above the oil's smoke point, for about an hour. The oil polymerises into a hard layer; several coats build a durable finish. Flaxseed oil and other drying oils are popular choices, though any cooking oil can be used.

Maintenance

Seasoning improves with regular cooking, especially frying. It can be damaged by prolonged soaking, harsh detergents, and acidic foods simmered for long periods, but it can be restored by repeating the seasoning process. Contrary to older advice, washing a seasoned pan with mild dish soap does not normally remove the polymerised layer.

Wok seasoning

Carbon steel woks are seasoned ("wok hei" preparation) by heating the empty wok until it darkens, then swirling in oil and aromatics. The resulting coating, renewed through stir-frying, is prized in Chinese cooking.
