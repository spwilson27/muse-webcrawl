The Theme Building is an iconic structure at Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) in Los Angeles, California. Completed in 1961, its distinctive white form — a flying-saucer-like disc resting on crossed parabolic arches — makes it a landmark example of the Googie and mid-century modern architecture that flourished in postwar Southern California.

Design and construction

The building was designed by a team led by William Pereira and Charles Luckman, with Paul R. Williams and consulting architect Welton Becket, for the airport's jet-age expansion. The structure rises 135 feet, with the restaurant level suspended in a circular "pod" above two intersecting arches of stucco-covered steel. Originally the disc was to rotate slowly, but it was fixed after initial operation. The Theme Building was dedicated by Vice President Lyndon B. Johnson in 1961 and designated a Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monument in 1993.

Uses

For many years the pod housed a restaurant with panoramic views of the airport; its space-age interior was redesigned in 1997 with a retro-futuristic decor. Public access and restaurant operations have changed over time, including closure to casual visitors following security changes after 2001, but the exterior remains one of the most recognizable symbols of Los Angeles and of the Jet Age.
