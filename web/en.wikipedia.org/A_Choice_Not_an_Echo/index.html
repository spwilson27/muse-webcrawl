A Choice Not an Echo: The Inside Story of How American Political Parties Go Broke is a 1964 book by American conservative activist Phyllis Schlafly. Published in the year of Barry Goldwater's presidential campaign, it argued that the Republican Party had for decades been controlled by a small group of wealthy "kingmakers" in the eastern establishment who repeatedly selected moderate, losing "me-too" candidates instead of offering voters a genuinely conservative alternative.

Content

Schlafly traced Republican presidential nominations from 1936 to 1960, contending that kingmakers centred on Wall Street and New York had engineered the nominations of Wendell Willkie, Thomas E. Dewey, Dwight D. Eisenhower and Richard Nixon over more conservative rivals. The title phrase captured her central claim: voters deserved "a choice, not an echo" of Democratic policies. The book endorsed Senator Barry Goldwater of Arizona as the authentic conservative candidate.

Distribution and impact

The book was self-published and sold for 75 cents. Schlafly's network distributed it through conservative bookstores, study groups and door-to-door sales; it reportedly sold more than three million copies, making it one of the best-selling political books of its era. Historians frequently cite it as a foundational text of the modern American conservative movement and a key piece of the grassroots mobilisation that delivered the 1964 Republican nomination to Goldwater, even though Goldwater went on to lose the general election heavily to Lyndon B. Johnson.

Legacy

Schlafly updated the book for later election cycles, and a 50th-anniversary edition appeared in 2014. The phrase "a choice, not an echo"—which Goldwater himself used in his 1964 acceptance speech—remains a touchstone slogan in Republican intraparty debates between conservatives and moderates.
