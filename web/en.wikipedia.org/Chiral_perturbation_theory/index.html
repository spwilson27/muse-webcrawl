Chiral perturbation theory

Chiral perturbation theory (ChPT) is an effective field theory constructed with a Lagrangian based on quantum chromodynamics (QCD), used to describe low-energy interactions of hadrons, in particular the pseudo-Goldstone bosons (pions, kaons, and the eta) that arise from the spontaneous breaking of QCD's approximate chiral symmetry.

Motivation

At low energies QCD becomes strongly coupled, so ordinary perturbation theory in the quark–gluon coupling fails. However, for massless up and down quarks the QCD Lagrangian possesses an approximate SU(2)L × SU(2)R chiral symmetry that is spontaneously broken to the vector subgroup, producing the light pions as Goldstone bosons. Chiral perturbation theory, developed by Steven Weinberg and systematized by Jürg Gasser and Heiri Leutwyler in the 1980s, organizes calculations as an expansion in small momenta and quark masses.

Structure

The effective Lagrangian is written as an infinite series of terms ordered by the number of derivatives and quark-mass insertions. Loop diagrams are suppressed by powers of the expansion parameter p²/(4πfπ)², where fπ is the pion decay constant. Unknown coefficients, the low-energy constants, encode short-distance physics and are fixed from experiment or lattice QCD.

Applications

ChPT has been applied to pion–pion scattering, the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon, semileptonic kaon decays, and the extrapolation of lattice QCD results computed at unphysical quark masses to the physical point. Baryon chiral perturbation theory extends the method to systems containing a single nucleon.
