Salvinorin A


Image placeholder: Salvinorin A

Salvinorin A is the main active psychotropic molecule in Salvia divinorum, a plant traditionally used by Mazatec shamans in Oaxaca, Mexico. It is a neoclerodane diterpene with the chemical formula C23H28O8. Unlike most psychedelics, salvinorin A is not an alkaloid and does not act on serotonin receptors; instead it is a highly potent and selective kappa-opioid receptor agonist, the first known non-nitrogenous ligand of that receptor.

Pharmacology and effects

Salvinorin A is active at doses as low as 200 micrograms when vaporized and inhaled, making it one of the most potent naturally occurring hallucinogens by weight. Effects come on within seconds of inhalation and typically last only a few minutes, and include intense alterations of perception, loss of body awareness, and dysphoria; when the plant is chewed as a quid, effects are milder and longer-lasting. It is rapidly metabolized to the largely inactive salvinorin B.

Research and legal status

Because of its unique pharmacology, salvinorin A has attracted research interest as a tool for studying the kappa-opioid system and as a scaffold for potential treatments for pain, addiction and depression without the abuse liability of mu-opioid drugs. The compound and the plant are controlled or restricted in a number of countries and in many U.S. states, though they are not scheduled under U.S. federal law.
