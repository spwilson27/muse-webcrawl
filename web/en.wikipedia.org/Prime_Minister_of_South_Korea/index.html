Prime Minister of South Korea

The Prime Minister of the Republic of Korea is the deputy head of government and the second highest political office in South Korea. The prime minister is appointed by the President with the approval of the National Assembly. Unlike prime ministers in parliamentary systems, the Korean prime minister is not required to be a member of the legislature and serves as the president's principal assistant rather than as an independent head of government.

Duties

Under the Constitution, the prime minister assists the president and, by presidential order, directs the executive ministries. The prime minister supervises the administration, recommends the appointment and dismissal of ministers, and serves as acting president in the event of the president's incapacity or vacancy. The prime minister also chairs the State Council in the president's absence and heads the Office for Government Policy Coordination.

History

The office was created in 1948 with the founding of the Republic of Korea; Lee Beom-seok was the first holder. The 1960 constitution of the Second Republic made the prime minister the effective head of government under Chang Myon, but the parliamentary system ended with the 1961 military coup. Since democratization in 1987, the presidency has remained dominant, and prime ministers have often been technocratic administrators. Goh Kun and Han Duck-soo both served as acting president during presidential impeachment crises (2004, and in Han's case 2024, following the short-lived declaration of martial law and the impeachment of Yoon Suk Yeol).
