Theoretical computer science

Theoretical computer science (TCS) is the branch of computer science and mathematics concerned with abstract and mathematical aspects of computation, including the theory of computation, algorithms, data structures, computational complexity, automata theory, formal languages, information theory and cryptography.

History

The field's foundations were laid in the 1930s by the work of Alan Turing, Alonzo Church and Kurt Gödel on computability and the limits of formal systems. The Church–Turing thesis identifies the effectively calculable functions with those computable by a Turing machine.

Areas

Major areas include complexity classes such as P and NP and the P versus NP problem, randomised and approximation algorithms, computational learning theory, quantum computing theory, and semantics of programming languages.
