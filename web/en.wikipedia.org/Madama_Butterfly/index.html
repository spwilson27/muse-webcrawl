Madama Butterfly

Madama Butterfly is an opera in three acts (originally two) by Giacomo Puccini, with a libretto by Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa. It is based on the short story "Madame Butterfly" (1898) by John Luther Long, itself drawn from Pierre Loti's novel Madame Chrysanthème, and on David Belasco's one-act play derived from Long's story. The opera premiered at La Scala, Milan, on 17 February 1904.

Plot

In Nagasaki, the American naval officer Pinkerton marries the young geisha Cio-Cio-san ("Butterfly") in a ceremony he treats as a convenience, while she converts to Christianity and is disowned by her family. Pinkerton returns to America; three years later Butterfly, still waiting with their son, learns he has remarried. She agrees to give up the child and takes her own life as Pinkerton returns, filled with remorse, too late.

Reception and revisions

The premiere was a notorious fiasco, but Puccini revised the opera — most importantly dividing the long second act — and the new version, staged at Brescia in May 1904, was a triumph. Madama Butterfly is now among the most performed operas in the world, noted for arias such as "Un bel dì vedremo". Modern productions and scholarship also examine its portrayal of Japan and its themes of colonialism and cultural conflict.
