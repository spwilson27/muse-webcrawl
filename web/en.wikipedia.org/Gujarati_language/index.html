Gujarati language

Gujarati is an Indo-Aryan language native to the Indian state of Gujarat, spoken by about 55 million people worldwide. It is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India and the official language of Gujarat.

History

Gujarati developed from Old Gujarati (c. 1100–1500), the ancestor also of Rajasthani, through Middle Gujarati into the modern language by about 1800. Its literature includes the devotional poetry of Narsinh Mehta in the fifteenth century. Mahatma Gandhi, a native speaker, wrote his autobiography in Gujarati and encouraged the development of its prose and lexicography.

Script and features

Gujarati is written in the Gujarati script, derived from Devanagari but without the characteristic horizontal headline stroke. The language has three genders, a distinction between inclusive and exclusive "we" in some dialects, and retroflex consonants typical of Indo-Aryan languages. Large Gujarati-speaking diaspora communities exist in East Africa, the United Kingdom, the United States and Canada.
