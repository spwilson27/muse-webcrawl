Lena (river)

The Lena (Russian: Лена) is the easternmost of the three great Siberian rivers that flow into the Arctic Ocean, the others being the Ob and the Yenisey. At 4,472 kilometres (2,779 mi) long, it is the longest river in Russia and one of the longest in the world, draining a basin of about 2,490,000 km² across eastern Siberia.

Course

The Lena rises in the Baikal Mountains west of Lake Baikal and flows generally north-east and then north through the Sakha Republic (Yakutia). It passes the city of Yakutsk, receives major tributaries including the Vilyuy, Kirenga, Vitim, Olyokma and Aldan, and empties into the Laptev Sea through the vast Lena Delta, one of the largest river deltas in the world.

Features and history

Near Yakutsk the river is flanked by the Lena Pillars, dramatic limestone rock formations inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2012. The river is frozen for most of the year — roughly from October to June in its lower reaches — and historically served as a principal route for Russian expansion across Siberia in the 17th century. The name "Lena" is believed to derive from the Evenk name Elyu-Ene, meaning "the great river". Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov is traditionally said to have taken his pseudonym "Lenin" from the river, though this is uncertain.
