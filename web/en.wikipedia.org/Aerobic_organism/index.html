Aerobic organism

An aerobic organism or aerobe is an organism that can survive and grow in an oxygenated environment. Obligate aerobes require oxygen to grow, using it as the terminal electron acceptor in cellular respiration, whereas facultative anaerobes can grow with or without oxygen.

Aerobic organism
Requirement: Oxygen (obligates)
Metabolism: Aerobic respiration
Contrast: Anaerobic organism

Types

Obligate aerobes, including many bacteria such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis, must have oxygen. Facultative anaerobes like Escherichia coli prefer oxygen but can ferment in its absence. Microaerophiles require low oxygen concentrations. Aerotolerant organisms survive oxygen but do not use it.

Energetics

Aerobic respiration yields far more ATP per glucose molecule (roughly 30–32) than fermentation (2), because oxygen's high reduction potential allows complete oxidation of glucose to carbon dioxide and water.
