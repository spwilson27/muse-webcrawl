Gas tungsten arc welding
Other namesTIG welding; GTAW
ElectrodeNon-consumable tungsten
Shielding gasArgon, helium or mixtures
Typical usesAerospace, piping, thin stainless steel and aluminium

Gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW), also called tungsten inert gas or TIG welding, is an arc welding process that uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode to produce the weld. The weld area is protected from atmospheric contamination by an inert shielding gas, usually argon or helium.

Process

An electric arc between the electrode and workpiece melts the base metal. Filler metal may be fed into the puddle by hand, or the joint may be fused without filler. Direct current is common for steels, while alternating current helps clean oxides from aluminium and magnesium.

Advantages and limits

GTAW gives precise control and clean, high-quality welds on thin sections and exotic alloys. It is slower than many processes, sensitive to drafts and contamination, and requires considerable operator skill.

Applications

The process is used for aircraft and bicycle frames, pressure vessels, pipelines, nuclear and food equipment, repair work and artistic metal fabrication.
