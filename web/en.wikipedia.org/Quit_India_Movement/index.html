Quit India Movement

Quit India Movement
Date: August 1942 – 1944
Location: British India
Launched by: Indian National Congress
Slogan: "Do or Die"

The Quit India Movement was a mass civil disobedience movement launched by the Indian National Congress in August 1942, demanding an immediate end to British rule in India. At the Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay on 8 August 1942, Mahatma Gandhi issued his famous call to "Do or Die".

Background

The movement followed the failure of the Cripps Mission, which had offered limited self-government after the Second World War in exchange for Indian support for the war effort. Congress rejected the offer, judging that only complete independence was acceptable, and passed the Quit India Resolution demanding immediate British withdrawal.

Suppression

Within hours of Gandhi's speech, the entire Congress leadership was arrested and the party declared unlawful. Leaderless, the movement turned in places to sabotage of railways, telegraph lines and government offices; parallel governments briefly appeared in districts such as Ballia, Tamluk and Satara. The British responded with mass arrests — over 100,000 people were imprisoned — collective fines and, in some places, firing on crowds.

Outcome

Although suppressed by 1944, the movement demonstrated that British rule could no longer rest on Indian consent. Within three years of the war's end, negotiations led to the independence and partition of India in 1947.
