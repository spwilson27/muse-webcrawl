Busbar

In electric power distribution, a busbar (also bus bar) is a metallic strip or bar, typically housed inside switchgear, panel boards, and busway enclosures, for local high current power distribution. They are also used to connect high voltage equipment at electrical switchyards, and low voltage equipment in battery banks.

Design and materials

Busbars are generally flat strips or hollow tubes made of copper, brass or aluminium, sometimes tinned or silver-plated to improve conductivity and resist corrosion. They are generally uninsulated, though they may be supported on insulators or enclosed in bus ducts. Their size is determined by the current they must carry without overheating. Bolted or clamped connections join sections and connect feeders.

Applications

Busbars are used in substations, distribution boards, data centres (as overhead or underfloor busway), battery rooms, and in electric vehicles and power electronics to interconnect components with low inductance. Unlike cables, their rigid geometry and large surface area aid cooling and reduce skin-effect losses.
