The Malmedy massacre trial (United States of America v. Valentin Bersin et al.) was a war crimes trial held at Dachau in May–July 1946. Seventy-three German Waffen-SS soldiers were tried for the murder of 84 American prisoners of war near Malmedy, Belgium, on 17 December 1944, during the Battle of the Bulge, and for killings of other American POWs and Belgian civilians by members of Kampfgruppe Peiper, part of the 1st SS Panzer Division Leibstandarte.

Trial and verdicts

The chief defendant was SS-Standartenführer Joachim Peiper. The prosecution, led by Lt. Col. Burton Ellis, secured convictions of all 73 defendants; 43 were sentenced to death by hanging, the remainder to prison terms.

Controversy and reviews

Allegations that American interrogators used mock trials, beatings and other coercion to extract confessions prompted reviews by the Simpson Commission and a U.S. Senate subcommittee, which included Senator Joseph McCarthy. All death sentences were commuted, and the last prisoner, Peiper, was released in December 1956.
