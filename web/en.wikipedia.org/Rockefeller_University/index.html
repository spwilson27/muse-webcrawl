Rockefeller University

The Rockefeller University
Founded: 1901 (as the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research)
Location: Upper East Side, Manhattan, New York City
Type: Private graduate university
Focus: Biomedical research

Rockefeller University is a private graduate university in New York City, specialising in the biological and medical sciences. Founded in 1901 by John D. Rockefeller as the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research, it was modelled on European research institutes such as the Pasteur Institute and the Robert Koch Institute. It is among the oldest biomedical research centres in the United States and has been associated with dozens of Nobel laureates.

Organisation

Unlike conventional universities, Rockefeller has no academic departments or tenure in the usual sense. Its faculty run relatively small laboratories organised around research areas rather than disciplines, and the university admits only graduate students (PhD and MD–PhD) and postdoctoral researchers. Its campus occupies several blocks on the Upper East Side of Manhattan along York Avenue, adjacent to other major biomedical institutions.

Research and discoveries

Scientists at the institute made landmark contributions, including Oswald Avery's demonstration (with Colin MacLeod and Maclyn McCarty) that DNA carries genetic information, Peyton Rous's discovery of tumour-inducing viruses, Karl Landsteiner's work on blood groups, and later advances in cell biology, immunology, neuroscience, and structural biology. The institute's hospital, founded in 1910, pioneered clinical research methods. In 1965 the institute became a university and adopted its present name.

Notable people

Nobel Prize winners affiliated with Rockefeller include Alexis Carrel, Karl Landsteiner, Peyton Rous, Joshua Lederberg (who served as president), Günter Blobel, Paul Nurse, Roderick MacKinnon, and Michael W. Young, among many others.
