Havana

Havana (Spanish: La Habana) is the capital and largest city of Cuba. The heart of the La Habana Province, Havana is the country's main port and leading commercial center. The city has a population of about 2.1 million inhabitants, and it spans a total of 781.58 km2 (301.77 sq mi) — making it the largest city by area, the most populous city, and the second-largest metropolitan area in the Caribbean region.

History

Havana was founded by the Spanish in 1519, finally on its present site on a natural harbor on Cuba's north coast, and became a stopping point for treasure-laden Spanish galleons returning from the New World. The city was burned by French corsair Jacques de Sores in 1555, prompting Spain to build major fortifications — including the Castillo de la Real Fuerza, among the oldest stone forts in the Americas, and the Morro Castle guarding the harbor entrance. In 1762, during the Seven Years' War, Britain captured Havana and opened it to trade, transforming its economy before Spain regained it the following year in exchange for Florida.

Havana grew rich on sugar and trade in the 19th century. In 1898, the explosion of the USS Maine in Havana harbor helped trigger the Spanish–American War. In the early 20th century the city was a glamorous destination of hotels, casinos and nightclubs, some tied to American organized crime. After the Cuban Revolution of 1959, Fidel Castro's government made Havana the seat of a one-party socialist state, and the city endured decades of economic hardship, especially during the "Special Period" following the collapse of the Soviet Union. The historic center, Old Havana (La Habana Vieja), was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986's listings (1982).

Geography and cityscape

Havana stretches along the Straits of Florida, fronted by the famous Malecón seawall promenade. Its architecture ranges from Spanish colonial baroque and neoclassical palaces to art deco landmarks such as the Bacardi Building, the Capitolio (modeled on the U.S. Capitol), and mid-century modernist hotels. The city is famous for its fleet of vintage American automobiles, the almendrones. Havana is divided into 15 municipalities, including Playa, Plaza de la Revolución, Centro Habana and Habana Vieja.

Culture

The city is a center of Cuban music and dance — son, rumba, salsa and jazz — and hosts the Havana International Jazz Festival and the Havana Biennial art exhibition. El Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, the Fábrica de Arte Cubano, and the famous Tropicana cabaret are among its cultural institutions.
