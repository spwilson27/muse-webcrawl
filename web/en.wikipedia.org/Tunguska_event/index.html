Tunguska event

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tunguska event was a huge explosion that occurred near the Podkamennaya Tunguska River in Yeniseysk Governorate, Siberia, Russia, on the morning of 30 June 1908. The explosion over the sparsely populated East Siberian taiga flattened an estimated 80 million trees over an area of 2,150 km² (830 sq mi) of forest, and eyewitness reports suggest up to three people may have died.

The explosion

At around 07:17 local time, Evenki natives and Russian settlers observed a column of bluish light, nearly as bright as the Sun, moving across the sky. About ten minutes later there was a flash and sounds similar to artillery fire. The shock wave knocked people off their feet hundreds of kilometres away and was registered on seismographs across Eurasia. Estimates of the explosion's yield range from about 3–5 to 10–15 megatons of TNT, with some estimates higher.

Investigation

The remoteness of the site delayed investigation; the first scientific expedition, led by Leonid Kulik in 1927, found the radially felled "telegraph pole" forest but no impact crater, and no macroscopic meteorite fragments have been conclusively recovered.

Cause

The leading explanation is the air burst of a stony asteroid or comet fragment roughly 50–60 metres across, which disintegrated at an altitude of about 5–10 km. The event remains the largest impact-related explosion on Earth in recorded history and is a key case study for planetary defence against near-Earth objects.
