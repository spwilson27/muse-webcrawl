Asiatic lion

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Animalia
Family:Felidae
Genus:Panthera
Species:P. leo
Subspecies:P. l. persica
Status:Endangered

The Asiatic lion (Panthera leo persica) is a population of the lion subspecies Panthera leo leo surviving today only in India. Its range is restricted to the Gir National Park and surrounding areas in the state of Gujarat.

A male Asiatic lion in Gir Forest, Gujarat.

Once ranging from southeastern Europe and the Middle East across northern India, the Asiatic lion was hunted nearly to extinction by the early 20th century, when perhaps fewer than 20 individuals remained. Conservation efforts have since brought the population to several hundred; a 2020 census estimated 674 lions.

Characteristics

Asiatic lions are slightly smaller than African lions, and males have a comparatively short, sparse mane through which the ears remain visible. A distinctive longitudinal fold of skin runs along the belly. Prides tend to be smaller than African prides, and males associate with females mainly at mating or at large kills.

Conservation

Because the entire population descends from a small number of survivors, genetic diversity is low, and the concentration in a single location leaves the lions vulnerable to disease and habitat events. Proposals to establish a second population, notably at Kuno National Park in Madhya Pradesh, have been debated for decades.
