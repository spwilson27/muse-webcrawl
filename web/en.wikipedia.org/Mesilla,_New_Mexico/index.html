Mesilla, New Mexico

Mesilla
State: New Mexico
County: Doña Ana
Settled: 1848
Population: approx. 1,800

Mesilla is a town in Doña Ana County, New Mexico, United States, adjoining Las Cruces in the Mesilla Valley of the Rio Grande. It is celebrated for its well-preserved adobe architecture and central plaza, shaded by cottonwoods and centred on the Basilica of San Albino (built 1906, on the site of an 1852 church).

History

Mesilla was founded about 1848 by Mexican settlers who moved north of the border drawn after the Mexican–American War, believing they remained in Mexico. The Gadsden Purchase of 1854, ratified with a flag-raising ceremony in the plaza, placed the town in the United States — the purchase was partly intended to secure this valley route. During the Civil War Mesilla briefly served as the capital of the Confederate Territory of Arizona, and it was a lively frontier crossroads on routes between Texas, Chihuahua and California; Billy the Kid was tried and sentenced to hang at its courthouse in 1881, and the Butterfield Overland Mail had a station here.

Today

Now largely bypassed by the railroads — which favoured Las Cruces — Mesilla retains a quiet, historic character, with shops, restaurants serving New Mexican cuisine, wineries, and festivals such as Cinco de Mayo and Diez y Seis de Septiembre around the plaza. Much of the town is a designated historic district.
