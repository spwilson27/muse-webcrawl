The Space Launch System (SLS) is an American super heavy-lift expendable launch vehicle developed by NASA since 2011. Derived from Space Shuttle technology — its RS-25 main engines, solid rocket boosters and external-tank-based core stage all descend from Shuttle hardware — it is the most powerful rocket ever flown successfully, and the launch vehicle for NASA's Artemis programme of crewed lunar exploration.

Design

Block 1 consists of an orange foam-insulated core stage powered by four RS-25 engines, flanked by two five-segment solid rocket boosters providing most of the liftoff thrust, topped by the Interim Cryogenic Propulsion Stage and the Orion spacecraft. Later Blocks substitute the more powerful Exploration Upper Stage (Block 1B) and advanced boosters (Block 2), raising payload to low Earth orbit from 95 to 130 tonnes.

History

SLS was mandated by the NASA Authorization Act of 2010 after the Constellation programme's cancellation. Development was long and costly — total programme costs exceeded $20 billion — and the first launch slipped repeatedly. The uncrewed Artemis I mission finally flew on 16 November 2022, sending Orion around the Moon and back in a 25-day test flight. Artemis II, the first crewed flight, is planned to loop around the Moon, with a crewed lunar landing targeted for later Artemis missions. Critics cite the rocket's high per-launch cost (over $2 billion) and expendable design; supporters stress its unmatched single-launch capability and human-rating.
