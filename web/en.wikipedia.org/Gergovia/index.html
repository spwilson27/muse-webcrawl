Gergovia was a fortified Gallic town (oppidum) of the Arverni tribe, located on a basalt plateau south of present-day Clermont-Ferrand in the Auvergne region of France. It is famous as the site of the Battle of Gergovia in 52 BC, one of the few defeats suffered by Julius Caesar during the Gallic Wars.

The battle of 52 BC

During the great Gallic revolt, the Arvernian leader Vercingetorix occupied Gergovia's strong hilltop position. Caesar besieged the oppidum but, according to his own account in the Commentarii de Bello Gallico, his troops attacked prematurely against orders after an assault on an outlying camp, suffering heavy losses — he reports 46 centurions and about 700 soldiers killed. Caesar withdrew, and Vercingetorix followed him, leading eventually to the decisive Roman victory at Alesia later that year.

Archaeology and identification

The site is identified with the plateau of Merdogne, now called the Plateau de Gergovie, at roughly 744 metres elevation. Excavations, begun in earnest under Napoleon III in the 19th century and continued in modern campaigns, have uncovered ramparts, dwellings and Roman siege works. A monument to Vercingetorix and a museum, the Musée de Gergovie, stand on the plateau.
