I-mode

An NTT DoCoMo handset with the i-mode service menu.

i-mode (Japanese: iモード, ai mōdo) is a mobile internet (web browsing and emailing) service offered by Japan's NTT DoCoMo since 1999. It was one of the world's first commercially successful mobile internet platforms, predating the smartphone era, and for years connected tens of millions of Japanese subscribers to email, news, weather, banking, ticketing, and entertainment from their handsets.

Technology and business model

i-mode used packet-switched data over DoCoMo's PDC network (later FOMA 3G) and delivered pages written in cHTML (compact HTML), a subset of HTML designed for small screens and low bandwidth. Crucially, DoCoMo created a curated ecosystem of official content providers whose services were billed through the phone bill, with DoCoMo retaining a small commission (about 9%)—a revenue-sharing model widely credited with aligning incentives and fostering a rich content market, in contrast to Europe's WAP services, which struggled.

Spread and decline

The service was developed under the leadership of Keiichi Enoki, with Mari Matsunaga and Takeshi Natsuno shaping its content strategy. i-mode was licensed to operators in several countries in Europe, Asia, and Australia in the early 2000s, with mixed results. In Japan it reached over 50 million subscribers at its peak. The rise of full-browser smartphones after the iPhone's 2007 launch gradually displaced i-mode; DoCoMo ended sales of i-mode feature phones in the late 2010s and has announced the service's final shutdown alongside its 3G network.
