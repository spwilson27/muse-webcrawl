Chancellor

A chancellor is a title for various official positions in governments, universities and religious institutions. The word derives from the Latin cancellarius, originally an official who stood at the lattice (cancelli) of a law court or chancery.

Government

In Germany and Austria, the Bundeskanzler (federal chancellor) is the head of government, equivalent to a prime minister. Switzerland's Federal Chancellor is the head of the Federal Chancellery, a staff position rather than head of government. In the United Kingdom, the Chancellor of the Exchequer is the finance minister, while the Lord Chancellor is a senior cabinet office historically responsible for the judiciary.

Academia

In many universities, the chancellor is the ceremonial or executive head of the institution. In the United Kingdom the chancellor is usually a figurehead, with day-to-day administration led by a vice-chancellor; in the United States a chancellor often heads a university or an entire university system.

Church

In the Roman Catholic Church, a chancellor is the chief record-keeper of a diocese; in the Anglican tradition a diocesan chancellor is a legal officer who advises the bishop.
