International Shooting Sport Federation

The International Shooting Sport Federation (ISSF) is the governing body of the Olympic shooting events in rifle, pistol and shotgun disciplines, as well as selected non-Olympic disciplines including running target. It was founded in 1907 as the Union Internationale des Fédérations et Associations Nationales de Tir, and currently comprises around 150 national member federations. Its headquarters are in Munich, Germany.

Overview

The ISSF sets the rules for shooting sport events, trains and licenses judges and referees, and organises the ISSF World Shooting Championships (first held in 1897, before the federation itself existed), the annual World Cup series, and World Cup Finals. Shooting has been part of the Olympic programme since the first modern Games in Athens in 1896, and the Olympic programme under ISSF rules currently includes fifteen events in rifle, pistol and shotgun.

History and leadership

The federation was founded by eight national associations and grew to incorporate shooting bodies worldwide, suspending activity during the two world wars. It adopted the English name International Shooting Sport Federation in 1998, replacing its earlier French-based title. Long-serving president Olegario Vázquez Raña of Mexico led the federation from 1980 to 2018, when he was succeeded by Vladimir Lisin; Luciano Rossi became president in 2022. The federation has faced scrutiny over conflicts of interest and governance, prompting reform efforts and, in the 2020s, competition from the breakaway World Shooting Federation founded by Rossi's rivals — a dispute that also saw shooting sport bodies debate the sport's Olympic future.

Disciplines

Events are divided into rifle events (10 m air rifle, 50 m rifle prone and 50 m rifle three positions), pistol events (10 m air pistol, 25 m rapid fire pistol, 25 m pistol and 50 m pistol), and shotgun events (trap and skeet). Many events were rebalanced for gender equality, with mixed team events introduced at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games.
