Kaieteur Falls

Location: Kaieteur National Park, Potaro-Siparuni, Guyana

Total height: 251 m (823 ft)

Number of drops: 1 main drop (226 m)

Watercourse: Potaro River

Kaieteur Falls is a large waterfall on the Potaro River in Kaieteur National Park in the Amazonian rainforest of Guyana. The water drops freely in a single leap of about 226 metres (741 ft) from a sandstone cliff, with rapids below bringing the total height to roughly 251 metres. Combined with its average flow of about 663 cubic metres per second, it is among the most powerful waterfalls in the world.

Discovery and name

The falls were brought to outside attention in 1870 by the geologist Charles Barrington Brown. According to Patamona legend, the falls are named for Kai, a chief who sacrificed himself by paddling over the falls to save his people, petitioning the great spirit Makonaima.

Wildlife and tourism

The spray-filled gorge shelters unusual species including golden rocket frogs, which live in the giant tank bromeliads, and colonies of white-chinned swifts and macaws. Kaieteur is Guyana's premier tourist attraction, reached most easily by small aircraft from Georgetown landing at the airstrip near the falls; a multi-day overland and river journey is also possible.
