Prime Minister of Italy

Official title: President of the Council of Ministers
Residence: Palazzo Chigi, Rome
Appointer: President of the Republic

The prime minister of Italy, officially the President of the Council of Ministers (Italian: Presidente del Consiglio dei ministri), is the head of government of the Italian Republic. The office is established by Articles 92–96 of the Constitution of Italy. The prime minister is appointed by the President of the Republic, who normally selects the leader able to command a majority in Parliament, and must win a vote of confidence in both houses of Parliament: the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate of the Republic.

Powers

The prime minister directs the general policy of the government, promotes and coordinates the activity of ministers, and countersigns acts of the President of the Republic. Unlike many other parliamentary systems, the Italian prime minister cannot unilaterally dismiss individual ministers, a weakness sometimes cited in debates about governmental instability. The prime minister may ask the President to dissolve Parliament, though the decision rests with the head of state.

History

The office originated in the Kingdom of Sardinia and continued after Italian unification in 1861; the first holder was Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour. Under fascism, Benito Mussolini transformed the premiership into a dictatorship as "Head of the Government". Since the founding of the Republic in 1946, Italy has had many governments — typically lasting little more than a year — though individual prime ministers such as Alcide De Gasperi, Giulio Andreotti and Silvio Berlusconi (the longest-serving post-war holder) returned repeatedly. Frequent turnover partly reflects coalition politics and the equal powers of the two parliamentary chambers, a system known as "perfect bicameralism".

Residence

The official residence and seat of the Council of Ministers is Palazzo Chigi in Rome, across from the Column of Marcus Aurelius; the Villa Doria Pamphilj serves for official receptions.
