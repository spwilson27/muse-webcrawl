The Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) is a military alliance in north and east Syria, formed on 10 October 2015 during the Syrian civil war. Its core is the Kurdish-led People's Protection Units (YPG) and Women's Protection Units (YPJ), joined by Arab, Syriac/Assyrian, Turkmen, and other local militias organised partly through subordinate military councils.

Formation and role

The SDF was announced in Hasakah as a multi-ethnic force intended to fight the Islamic State (ISIL) and to secure a federal, decentralised Syria. Backed by the United States-led coalition with air support, advisors, artillery, and equipment, the SDF became the coalition's principal ground partner in Syria. Its commander-in-chief, Mazloum Abdi (Mazloum Kobani), emerged as its leading public figure.

Campaigns against ISIL

After consolidating control east of the Euphrates, SDF forces captured Manbij in 2016 and, following a months-long battle in 2017, took Raqqa, the declared capital of the ISIL "caliphate". In March 2019 the SDF announced the territorial defeat of ISIL after the battle of Baghouz, the group's last enclave. The SDF subsequently administered detention camps and prisons holding thousands of ISIL fighters and displaced families, notably at al-Hol.

Conflicts with Turkey and later developments

Turkey regards the YPG as an extension of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK) and has repeatedly intervened in northern Syria, capturing Afrin in 2018 and a border strip between Tell Abyad and Ras al-Ayn in 2019; fighting between the SDF and Turkish-backed Syrian factions continued thereafter. Following the fall of the Assad government in December 2024, the SDF entered negotiations with the new authorities in Damascus, and in March 2025 signed an agreement envisaging the integration of its military and civil institutions into the Syrian state, though implementation remained contested amid continuing clashes in the north and east.
