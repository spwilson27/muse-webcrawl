Lynn Nolan Ryan Jr. (born January 31, 1947), nicknamed "the Ryan Express", is an American former professional baseball pitcher and sports executive. Over a record 27-year Major League Baseball career (1966–1993), he became famous for his exceptional fastball velocity and durability.

Career

Ryan debuted with the New York Mets and was part of the 1969 "Miracle Mets" World Series champions. Traded to the California Angels, he threw four no-hitters and set the single-season strikeout record of 383 in 1973. With the Houston Astros he became the first player to sign a million-dollar-per-year contract, and with the Texas Rangers he recorded his 5,000th strikeout and his sixth and seventh no-hitters, the last at age 44.

Records and legacy

Ryan holds the all-time records for strikeouts (5,714) and no-hitters (seven), marks widely considered unbreakable. He was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1999. After retirement he served as CEO and president of the Texas Rangers and later as an executive adviser to the Astros.
