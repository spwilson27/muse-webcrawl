Whitney Museum of American Art

Whitney Museum
Founded: 1930
Founder: Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney
Location: 99 Gansevoort Street, New York City
Collection: over 25,000 works

The Whitney Museum of American Art, known informally as "The Whitney", is a modern and contemporary art museum in the Meatpacking District of Manhattan, New York City. It was founded in 1930 by the artist and patron Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney to champion American art and living artists, after the Metropolitan Museum of Art declined her collection.

History and building

The museum occupied several Manhattan homes, most famously the Marcel Breuer-designed building on Madison Avenue (1966–2014), now used by the Metropolitan Museum as the Met Breuer and later by the Frick Collection. In 2015 the Whitney moved to a new building at 99 Gansevoort Street designed by Renzo Piano, with large column-free galleries and outdoor terraces overlooking the Hudson River and the High Line.

Collection and Biennial

The collection holds more than 25,000 paintings, sculptures, drawings, prints, photographs and new media by over 3,500 artists, including Edward Hopper, Alexander Calder, Georgia O'Keeffe and Jasper Johns. The Whitney Biennial, held since 1932, is the longest-running survey of contemporary American art and remains a widely debated barometer of new trends.
