Game mechanics

Game mechanics are the rules and procedures that guide the player and the game's responses at the level of interaction — the constructs that determine how a game plays. In video games and tabletop games alike, mechanics define what actions are possible, what outcomes result, and how players progress toward goals or victory conditions. They are the core of game design: theme, story, and technology sit on top of the mechanical skeleton.

Common mechanics

Frequently encountered mechanics include turn-taking and action points; resource collection and management; dice rolling and card drafting; tile and area placement; auctions and bidding; set collection; player elimination; and, in video games, jumping and platforming, shooting, character progression through experience points and skill trees, crafting, and physics-based manipulation. A "core mechanic" is the single repeated activity around which a game is built — jumping in a platformer, shooting in a shooter.

Analysis

Frameworks such as the MDA framework (Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics) analyze how basic mechanics generate emergent dynamics in play and, from them, the emotional experience of the player. Designers distinguish mechanics from rules in that mechanics are the actions available to agents, while rules constrain and evaluate the whole system. Well-chosen mechanics can produce depth from simplicity, as in chess or Tetris, and many classic games are defined by one novel mechanic later adopted across the medium.
