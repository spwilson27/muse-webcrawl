Ursus (genus)

Ursus
Family: Ursidae
Species: 4 extant
Range: Northern Hemisphere

Ursus is a genus in the family Ursidae (bears) that includes the widely distributed brown bear, the American black bear, the polar bear, and the Asian black bear. The name derives from the Latin ursus, meaning "bear". Members of the genus are large-bodied bears found across North America, Europe, and Asia.

Species

The four living species are: the brown bear (Ursus arctos), with numerous subspecies including grizzly and Kodiak bears; the American black bear (Ursus americanus); the Asian black bear (Ursus thibetanus); and the polar bear (Ursus maritimus). Several extinct species are known from the fossil record, including the cave bear (Ursus spelaeus) and Ursus deningeri. Polar bears and brown bears can interbreed and produce fertile hybrids.

Characteristics

Ursus species are large carnivorans with shaggy hair, plantigrade paws with five nonretractile claws, and short tails. Despite being classified as carnivorans, most are omnivorous, with diets ranging from berries and nuts to fish and mammals; the polar bear is largely carnivorous and the brown bear populations vary widely in diet.
