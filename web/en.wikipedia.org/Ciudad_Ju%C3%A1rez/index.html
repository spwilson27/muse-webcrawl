Country: Mexico
State: Chihuahua

Ciudad Juárez, commonly referred to as Juárez, is the most populous city in the Mexican state of Chihuahua. It lies on the Rio Grande (Río Bravo del Norte), directly across the border from El Paso, Texas, in the United States, with which it forms a major binational metropolitan area. Juárez is one of Mexico's principal manufacturing centers, with a large maquiladora industry.

History

The city was founded in 1659 as the mission settlement El Paso del Norte by Franciscan friars. After the Mexican–American War and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), the Rio Grande became the international boundary; the part of the settlement north of the river became El Paso, Texas. In 1888 the Mexican city was renamed in honor of Benito Juárez, who had made it his base during the French intervention in the 1860s. During the Mexican Revolution, Juárez was a strategic prize, captured by Francisco Madero's forces in 1911 and contested by Pancho Villa's troops.

Economy

Since the Border Industrialization Program of 1965, Juárez has hosted hundreds of maquiladora assembly plants, many owned by U.S. companies, producing auto parts, electronics, and medical devices. Several international bridges link it to El Paso.

Violence

Juárez gained international attention during the Mexican drug war: between 2008 and 2011, turf wars between the Juárez and Sinaloa cartels made it one of the most violent cities in the world, with more than 3,000 homicides in 2010 alone. Homicide rates fell markedly after 2012 but remained a concern. The city also became known in the 1990s for a series of femicides of young women, many of them maquiladora workers.
