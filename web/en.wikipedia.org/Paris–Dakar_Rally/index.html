Dakar Rally

The Dakar Rally (formerly the Paris–Dakar Rally) is an annual rally raid organised by the Amaury Sport Organisation. First run in 1978–79, the event originally raced from Paris, France, to Dakar, Senegal, and is widely regarded as the most gruelling off-road motorsport event in the world. Competitors race cars, motorcycles, quads, trucks, and lightweight vehicles across thousands of kilometres of desert terrain, navigating principally by roadbook.

History

The rally was conceived by French motorcyclist Thierry Sabine, who in 1977 became lost in the Libyan desert during the Abidjan–Nice Rally and resolved to create an event sharing that experience of adventure. The first Paris–Dakar ran from 26 December 1978 to 14 January 1979, with 182 vehicles setting out and 74 finishing. Cyril Neveu won the motorcycle category on a Yamaha.

For most of its history the route started in Paris (or elsewhere in Europe) and finished in Dakar, crossing the Sahara through countries including Mali, Mauritania, and Niger. Security threats forced the cancellation of the 2008 event, and from 2009 to 2019 the rally was held in South America, crossing Argentina, Chile, Peru, and Bolivia. Since 2020 the event has been staged entirely in Saudi Arabia.

Format

The rally is run over roughly two weeks and some 8,000–9,000 kilometres, divided into daily stages including timed special sections and untimed liaison sections. Navigation is by roadbook, and stages frequently cross dunes, rocky plateaux, and dry lake beds. A marathon stage, on which competitors may not receive outside mechanical assistance overnight, is a traditional feature.

Vehicles and classes

The principal classes are motorcycles, quads, cars (including the T1 and T2 production-based categories), lightweight vehicles (side-by-side UTVs), and trucks. A "Dakar Classic" regularity class for historic vehicles was added in 2021. Stéphane Peterhansel, known as "Monsieur Dakar", holds the record for overall victories, with fourteen wins across motorcycles and cars.

Danger and criticism

The rally has a long history of fatalities among competitors and spectators, including its founder Thierry Sabine, who died in a helicopter crash during the 1986 event. It has also been criticised for its environmental impact and for the security and human cost of racing through some of the world's poorest regions; supporters counter that the event brings income and attention to the areas it passes through.
