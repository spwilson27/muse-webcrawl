Wireless sensor network

Wireless sensor networks (WSNs) refer to networks of spatially dispersed and dedicated sensors that monitor and record the physical conditions of the environment and forward the collected data to a central location. WSNs can measure environmental conditions such as temperature, sound, pollution levels, humidity and wind.

Components

A sensor node typically contains a sensing unit, a processing unit, a transceiver unit, and a power unit, usually a battery. Nodes communicate over short-range radio standards such as IEEE 802.15.4 (used by Zigbee) and forward data hop-by-hop to a gateway or sink node.

Applications

Applications include environmental and habitat monitoring, precision agriculture, industrial process control, structural health monitoring of bridges and buildings, healthcare monitoring, and military surveillance. The field emerged partly from the DARPA-funded "smart dust" research of the 1990s.
