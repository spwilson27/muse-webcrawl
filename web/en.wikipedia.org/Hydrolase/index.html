Hydrolase

A hydrolase cleaves a chemical bond by adding water

In biochemistry, a hydrolase is a class of enzymes that commonly perform as catalysts for the cleavage of chemical bonds using water (hydrolysis). In the EC classification system, hydrolases form class EC 3 and are subdivided according to the type of bond they act upon, including ester bonds, glycosidic bonds, peptide bonds, carbon–nitrogen bonds other than peptides, acid anhydrides, carbon–carbon bonds, and several others. A commonly encountered division is between hydrolytic enzymes that cleave their substrates with net consumption of water and related enzymes that use other mechanisms.

Examples

Hydrolases are ubiquitous and essential to digestion and metabolism. Proteases (peptidases) such as trypsin, chymotrypsin, and pepsin hydrolyse peptide bonds in proteins. Lipases split ester bonds in triglycerides; amylases and other glycosidases hydrolyse glycosidic bonds in starch, cellulose, and glycogen; nucleases cleave the phosphodiester bonds of DNA and RNA; phosphatases remove phosphate groups, counterbalancing kinases in cell signalling; and ATPases hydrolyse ATP to power cellular processes. The acetylcholinesterase that terminates nerve impulses at synapses is also a hydrolase — and the target of nerve agents and many insecticides, which inhibit it.

Nomenclature

Systematic names for hydrolases are formed as "substrate hydrolase", while common names usually take the form "substrate-ase" — for example, DNA polymerase is not a hydrolase, but nuclease is. Many hydrolases retain historical names not obviously derived from their substrates. Within EC 3, subclasses include EC 3.1 (ester bonds), EC 3.2 (glycosylases), EC 3.4 (peptide bonds), and EC 3.6 (acid anhydrides, including ATPases and GTPases).

Clinical significance

Defects in hydrolases cause many inherited disorders, particularly the lysosomal storage diseases, such as Tay–Sachs disease (deficiency of hexosaminidase A) and Gaucher's disease (deficiency of glucocerebrosidase), in which undegraded substrates accumulate in lysosomes. Hydrolase inhibitors are also widely used as drugs, from ACE inhibitors to HIV protease inhibitors.
