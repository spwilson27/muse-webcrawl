American chestnut

American chestnut
Scientific name: Castanea dentata
Family: Fagaceae
Native range: Eastern North America
Conservation status: Endangered (functionally extinct as a canopy tree)

The American chestnut (Castanea dentata) is a large, fast-growing deciduous hardwood tree of the beech family native to eastern North America. Before the species was devastated by the chestnut blight, it was one of the most important forest trees throughout its range and was considered one of the finest chestnut trees in the world.

Chestnut blight

The blight, caused by the fungus Cryphonectria parasitica accidentally introduced from Asia around 1904, killed an estimated four billion trees within decades, effectively removing the American chestnut as a canopy species. Sprouts continue to grow from old root systems but usually succumb before maturing.

Restoration efforts

Breeding programs cross American chestnuts with blight-resistant Chinese chestnuts and backcross to recover American form, while researchers have also developed transgenic blight-tolerant trees using a wheat oxalate oxidase gene.
