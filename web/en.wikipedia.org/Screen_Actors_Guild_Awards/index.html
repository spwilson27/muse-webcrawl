Screen Actors Guild Awards

The Screen Actors Guild Awards (often called the SAG Awards) are accolades given by the Screen Actors Guild-American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (SAG-AFTRA) to recognise outstanding performances in film and television. First presented in 1995, the awards are notable as one of the few major ceremonies devoted entirely to acting: winners are chosen by fellow actors, from nominations made by committees of union members, with final voting open to the full SAG-AFTRA membership.

The Actor statuette

The award itself is a bronze figure of a naked man holding both a mask of comedy and a mask of tragedy, called "The Actor". The categories include individual honours for lead and supporting performances in film and in drama and comedy television series, stunt ensemble awards, and ensemble prizes — most prominently Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture, regarded as the ceremony's equivalent of a best picture award.

Significance

Because actors form the largest branch of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, the SAG Awards are watched closely as predictors of the Academy Awards for acting, and the ensemble award is a key indicator in the Oscar best picture race. The ceremony also presents a Life Achievement Award for career achievement and humanitarian accomplishment; past recipients include Sidney Poitier, Elizabeth Taylor, James Earl Jones, Carol Burnett and Barbra Streisand.

Broadcast history

The awards were long televised in the United States, airing for many years on TNT and TBS. Following changes in the television landscape, the ceremony moved to streaming: the 2024 ceremony was streamed live globally on Netflix, the first major awards show to be carried live by the platform under a multi-year agreement.
