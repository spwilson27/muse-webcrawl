Lujiazui

Lujiazui is a locality in Pudong, Shanghai, on the eastern bank of the Huangpu River opposite the Bund. Since the 1990s it has been developed as China's premier financial district, forming a skyline of supertall skyscrapers that has become a symbol of modern China.

Landmarks

The Oriental Pearl Tower (468 m, completed 1994), Jin Mao Tower (421 m), Shanghai World Financial Center (492 m), and Shanghai Tower (632 m, China's tallest building) dominate the area. The district hosts the Shanghai Stock Exchange, major banks, and the Lujiazui Circular Pedestrian Bridge.

History

Named for the Lu family of Ming dynasty scholar Lu Shen, the area was once warehouses, docks, and low housing. In 1990 the Chinese government designated Pudong a special economic zone, triggering rapid construction.

Transport

Lujiazui station on Shanghai Metro Lines 2 and 14 serves the district. Ferries cross to the Bund, and the Bund Sightseeing Tunnel connects the two banks.
