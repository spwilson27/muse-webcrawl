Pony

A pony is a type of small horse, typically measuring under 14.2 hands (58 inches, 147 cm) at the withers when fully grown. Ponies often have proportionally shorter legs, thicker manes and tails, and sturdier builds than full-sized horses.

Characteristics

Many pony breeds developed in harsh environments such as the British Isles, where limited forage favoured small, hardy animals. Well-known breeds include the Shetland, Welsh, Connemara, Exmoor, and Dartmoor ponies. Ponies are generally intelligent, long-lived, and strong relative to their size, and were historically used in mines, for driving, and as children's mounts.

Pony versus horse

The distinction is primarily one of size and phenotype rather than genetics; some individuals of horse breeds fall under the height limit, and some pony breeds occasionally exceed it. Equestrian sport rules usually define ponies by height for competition classes.
