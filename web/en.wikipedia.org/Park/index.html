Park

A park is an area of natural, semi-natural, or planted space set aside for human enjoyment and recreation, or for the protection of wildlife or natural habitats. Parks may consist of grassy areas, rocks, soil, and trees, and may include buildings and other artefacts such as monuments, fountains, playgrounds, and sports facilities.

The word originally denoted an enclosed tract of land reserved for keeping game animals, as in a medieval deer park. Public parks for ordinary citizens emerged in the nineteenth century as industrial cities grew: Derby Arboretum (1840) and Birkenhead Park (1847) in England were early examples, and New York's Central Park, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux from 1858, became the model for the great urban park.

Types

Parks range from small neighbourhood "pocket parks" and urban squares to national parks — vast protected landscapes such as Yellowstone (established 1872, the world's first) — and include state and provincial parks, country parks, and regional parks. Specialised parks include dog parks, skate parks, amusement parks, and business parks. Many parks are administered and maintained by municipalities or park services, and they are recognised as important for public health, biodiversity, cooling cities, and community life.
