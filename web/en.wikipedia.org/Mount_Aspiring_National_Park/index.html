Mount Aspiring National Park is in the Southern Alps of the South Island of New Zealand, north of Fiordland National Park. Established in 1964, it forms part of Te Wahipounamu, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography

The park is centred on Mount Aspiring / Tititea, which at 3,033 metres is New Zealand's 23rd-highest peak and the highest outside the Aoraki / Mount Cook region. The park spans the Main Divide and includes the headwaters of the Wānaka, Hawea, Shotover, Dart, Hollyford and other rivers, with extensive beech forests, alpine meadows and glaciers such as the Bonar, Volta and Therma glaciers.

Recreation

Popular tramping routes include the Routeburn Track (shared with Fiordland National Park), the Rees-Dart Track, the Gillespie Pass circuit and tracks in the Matukituki Valley such as the Rob Roy Glacier track. The park is also a base for mountaineering on peaks including Mount Aspiring itself.
