Antonio Cánovas del Castillo (8 February 1828 – 8 August 1897) was a Spanish statesman and historian, the chief architect of the Bourbon Restoration of 1874 and the dominant political figure of late nineteenth-century Spain. He served six terms as Prime Minister and designed the Restoration political system, including the Constitution of 1876 and the turno pacífico, the managed alternation in power between his Conservative Party and the Liberals of Práxedes Mateo Sagasta.

Career

Born in Málaga, Cánovas rose as a deputy and minister during the reign of Isabella II. After the revolution of 1868 and the First Spanish Republic, he worked for the return of the Bourbons in the person of Alfonso XII, favouring a civilian, constitutional restoration over a military pronunciamiento. Following the Sagunto pronunciamiento of December 1874 he headed the ministry-regency and shaped the new regime.

Assassination

As prime minister during the Cuban War of Independence, Cánovas pursued a hard line against the insurgency. On 8 August 1897 he was assassinated at the spa of Santa Águeda in Mondragón by the Italian anarchist Michele Angiolillo, who said he acted to avenge anarchists executed at Montjuïc.
