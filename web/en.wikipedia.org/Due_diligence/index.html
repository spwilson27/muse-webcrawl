Due diligence

Due diligence is the investigation or exercise of care that a reasonable business or person is normally expected to take before entering into an agreement or contract with another party or an act with a certain standard of care. It can be a legal obligation, but the term will more commonly apply to voluntary investigations.

In business transactions

In mergers and acquisitions, the buying party conducts due diligence on the target company: reviewing audited financial statements, tax records, contracts, intellectual property, litigation, regulatory compliance, environmental liabilities and human-resources matters, typically within a secured "data room". Findings may adjust the price, produce warranties and indemnities in the purchase agreement, or cause the deal to be abandoned. Similar processes occur in venture capital investment, real-estate purchases and initial public offerings, where underwriters rely on a due diligence defence against liability for false statements in a prospectus under securities law.

Other uses

The term is also applied to compliance contexts: customer due diligence (CDD) in banking under anti-money-laundering and know-your-customer rules; human-rights and environmental due diligence in supply chains; and the duty of inquiry expected of directors and trustees.
