Tiger reserves of India

The tiger reserves of India are a network of protected areas established under Project Tiger, a wildlife conservation programme launched by the Government of India in 1973 to ensure the survival of the Bengal tiger in its natural habitats. The reserves are administered by the National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA).

History

Project Tiger was launched on 1 April 1973, initially covering nine reserves spread over about 18,278 km². The programme adopted a "core–buffer" strategy: a core zone kept free of human disturbance, surrounded by a buffer zone of compatible land use. By the 2020s the network had grown to more than fifty reserves covering over 75,000 km², roughly 2.3 percent of India's land area.

Management

Each reserve is managed under a Tiger Conservation Plan. The NTCA, created in 2005 following amendments to the Wildlife (Protection) Act, oversees funding, monitoring and the periodic All India Tiger Estimation, a nationwide census conducted roughly every four years using camera traps and genetic analysis. The 2022 estimation recorded a minimum of about 3,167 tigers, the largest wild population of the species in the world.

Notable reserves

Among the best known are Corbett in Uttarakhand (the first reserve, named after hunter-turned-conservationist Jim Corbett), Ranthambore and Sariska in Rajasthan, Bandhavgarh and Kanha in Madhya Pradesh, the Sundarbans in West Bengal (a mangrove reserve shared with Bangladesh), and Nagarahole and Bandipur in Karnataka.
