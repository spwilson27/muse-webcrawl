Laminated veneer lumber

Laminated veneer lumber (LVL) is an engineered wood product that uses multiple layers of thin wood assembled with adhesives. It is one of the structural composite lumber (SCL) products, alongside parallel strand lumber and laminated strand lumber. LVL offers high strength and dimensional stability and can be made in long lengths.

Manufacture

Logs are rotary-peeled into veneers about 3 mm thick. Unlike plywood, in which successive veneers are cross-oriented, in LVL the grain of the veneers runs predominantly in the same (longitudinal) direction. The veneers are dried, graded — often with ultrasonic testing — coated with a waterproof phenol-formaldehyde resin, laid up and hot-pressed into billets, then sawn to size.

Uses

LVL is used for beams, headers, lintels, rim boards, I-joist flanges, scaffold planks and other structural applications in residential and commercial construction. Because its properties are more uniform than sawn timber, it allows longer spans and uses smaller, fast-growing trees efficiently.

Properties

Typical billets are 35–65 mm thick and can be manufactured in lengths far exceeding those of solid timber. LVL is stronger and stiffer per unit weight than conventional lumber but is generally more expensive and depends on energy-intensive adhesives.
