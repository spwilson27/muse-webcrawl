Culhwch and Olwen (Welsh: Culhwch ac Olwen) is a Welsh tale that survives in the White Book of Rhydderch (c. 1350) and the Red Book of Hergest (c. 1400). Composed around 1100, it is the earliest surviving Arthurian prose story and is included in modern editions of the Mabinogion, though it is not one of the original Four Branches.

Plot

Culhwch, son of Cilydd, is cursed by his stepmother to love none but Olwen, daughter of the giant Ysbaddaden Bencawr ("Chief Giant"). Though he has never seen her, Culhwch falls desperately in love and seeks help from his cousin, King Arthur. Arthur lends him six of his finest warriors, including Cai (Kay), Bedwyr (Bedivere), and Gwalchmei (Gawain). When the suitors demand Olwen's hand, Ysbaddaden — who is fated to die when his daughter marries — sets Culhwch a list of some forty seemingly impossible tasks (anoethau), the centrepiece of which is hunting the monstrous boar Twrch Trwyth to obtain the comb, razor, and shears between its ears with which to shave the giant.

Arthur himself joins the great hunt of Twrch Trwyth, which ranges across Ireland, Wales, and Cornwall. With the tasks accomplished, Ysbaddaden is shaved, his head is struck off by Goreu, and Culhwch marries Olwen.

Significance

The tale is notable for its catalogue of Arthur's court — a list of over 200 heroes, the earliest such roster in Arthurian literature — and for figures drawn from Welsh mythology, including Mabon ap Modron, "the oldest animals" consulted in turn (the Blackbird of Cilgwri, the Stag of Rhedynfre, the Owl of Cwm Cowlyd, the Eagle of Gwernabwy, and the Salmon of Llyn Llyw), and the goddess-like Olwen, whose footsteps leave white trefoils springing up behind her.
