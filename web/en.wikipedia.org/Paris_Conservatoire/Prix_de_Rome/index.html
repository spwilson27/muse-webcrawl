Prix de Rome

Prix de Rome
Type: Art scholarship prize
Founded: 1663
Abolished: 1968
Residence: Villa Medici, Rome

The Prix de Rome was a French scholarship for arts students, established in 1663 under Louis XIV, that funded a period of study in Rome at the Villa Medici. Prizes were awarded in painting, sculpture, architecture, engraving, and from 1803 in music composition.

The competition

Candidates competed under strict rules, producing a set work — for composers, a cantata on a prescribed text. Winning the Premier Grand Prix conferred prestige and launched careers, though the system was often criticized as academic and conservative.

Notable winners

Winners in music included Hector Berlioz (1830), Charles Gounod, Georges Bizet, Jules Massenet, Claude Debussy and Lili Boulanger, the first woman to win the music prize (1913). The prize was abolished in 1968 by André Malraux amid the May events in France.
