Conservatoire de Paris
Type: Music and dance conservatory
Founded: 1795
Location: Paris, France

The Conservatoire de Paris (Conservatoire national supérieur de musique et de danse de Paris) is a prestigious college of music and dance founded in 1795 during the French Revolution. It is considered one of the finest and oldest music schools in the world.

History

The Conservatoire was created by decree of the National Convention on 3 August 1795, merging earlier royal schools of singing, music and declamation. It became a model for conservatories across Europe. Directors have included Luigi Cherubini, who from 1822 imposed strict discipline and high standards.

Education

Admission is by competitive examination. Students study performance, composition, musicology and pedagogy. Famous alumni include Hector Berlioz, Claude Debussy, Maurice Ravel, Olivier Messiaen, Pierre Boulez and Nadia Boulanger (as a teacher). The institution moved to the Cité de la Musique in the Parc de la Villette in 1990.
