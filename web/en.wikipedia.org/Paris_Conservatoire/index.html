Paris Conservatoire

The Paris Conservatoire, officially the Conservatoire national supérieur de musique et de danse de Paris (CNSMDP), is a French college of music and dance founded in 1795. It is one of the world's most prestigious institutions for advanced musical training.

History

The Conservatoire was created by the National Convention during the French Revolution, merging the Royal School of Singing and Declamation (founded 1784) with the music school of the National Guard. Early directors included Luigi Cherubini, who led the institution from 1822 to 1842. For much of its history the Conservatoire was housed in the rue du Faubourg-Poissonnière; in 1990 the music conservatory moved to new premises in the Parc de la Villette, designed by Christian de Portzamparc.

Training

Admission is by competitive audition, and tuition is free. Students study performance, composition, conducting, musicology, and (since the inclusion of dance) choreography. Famous alumni include composers Hector Berlioz, Claude Debussy, Maurice Ravel, and Olivier Messiaen, who also taught there, as did Nadia Boulanger's contemporaries on the faculty. The Conservatoire's Prix de Rome and its annual public competitions have long shaped French musical life.
