Hector Berlioz

Hector Berlioz
Born: 11 December 1803, La Côte-Saint-André
Died: 8 March 1869, Paris
Occupation: Composer, conductor, critic

Louis-Hector Berlioz (1803–1869) was a French Romantic composer and conductor. His best-known works include the Symphonie fantastique (1830), the opera Les Troyens, the choral symphony Roméo et Juliette, and the Grande messe des morts (Requiem).

Life

Berlioz was sent to Paris to study medicine but abandoned it for music, entering the Paris Conservatoire. He won the Prix de Rome in 1830 after several attempts. Largely ignored by the French musical establishment, he supported himself as a music critic and earned much of his conducting reputation in Germany, England and Russia.

Music

Berlioz revolutionized orchestration, writing a famous treatise on the subject, and demanded enormous forces for some works. His music, misunderstood in his lifetime, was championed in the 20th century and is now central to the Romantic repertoire.
