The University of Basel (German: Universität Basel) is a public research university in Basel, Switzerland. Founded on 4 April 1460, it is Switzerland's oldest university and among the world's oldest surviving universities. The university is traditionally counted among the leading institutions of higher learning in the country.

History

The university was founded with a papal bull from Pope Pius II in 1459 and opened the following year, modelled on the universities of Paris and Bologna. During the Reformation era, Basel became a centre of humanism: Erasmus of Rotterdam taught and published there, and the physician Paracelsus lectured at the university in 1527. In 1622 the university library was founded, today one of the largest in Switzerland.

Faculties and research

The university comprises seven faculties: theology, law, medicine, humanities and social sciences, science, business and economics, and psychology. Its affiliated institutes include the Swiss Tropical and Public Health Institute and the Friedrich Miescher Institute for Biomedical Research. The university is particularly noted for life sciences and medicine, and counts numerous Nobel laureates among its affiliates and alumni, including Tadeus Reichstein and Werner Arber.

Notable people

Associated figures include the mathematicians Daniel and Johann Bernoulli, Leonhard Euler, the philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche (professor of philology from 1869), the psychiatrist Carl Jung, and the theologian Karl Barth.
