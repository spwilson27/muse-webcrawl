Rotating black hole

A rotating black hole, or Kerr black hole, is a black hole that possesses angular momentum. Its geometry is described by the Kerr metric, found by Roy Kerr in 1963 as an exact solution of Einstein's field equations for a rotating, uncharged mass.

Structure

Unlike a non-rotating Schwarzschild black hole, a Kerr black hole has two event horizons (an inner and an outer), and its singularity takes the form of a ring. Surrounding the outer horizon is the ergosphere, a region within which spacetime itself is dragged around by the rotation (frame dragging) so strongly that nothing can remain stationary relative to a distant observer.

Energy extraction

Because rotational energy is stored outside the horizon in the ergosphere, it can in principle be extracted, for example by the Penrose process. Astrophysical black holes are expected to rotate, since the stars and gas clouds from which they form carry angular momentum; the spin of merged black holes is measured from gravitational-wave signals.
