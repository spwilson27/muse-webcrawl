Hermann Weyl

Born: 9 November 1885, Elmshorn, Germany
Died: 8 December 1955, Zurich, Switzerland
Fields: Mathematics, theoretical physics
Institutions: Göttingen, ETH Zurich, Princeton (IAS)

Hermann Klaus Hugo Weyl (9 November 1885 – 8 December 1955) was a German mathematician, theoretical physicist, and philosopher. One of the most influential mathematicians of the twentieth century, he made fundamental contributions to analysis, geometry, topology, group theory, and mathematical physics, and was a leading figure of the Göttingen school.

Life and career

Weyl studied under David Hilbert at Göttingen, earning his doctorate in 1908. His 1913 book Die Idee der Riemannschen Fläche gave the first rigorous definition of a Riemann surface. He held chairs at ETH Zurich (1913–1930), where he crossed paths with Einstein, then succeeded Hilbert at Göttingen in 1930. Fleeing Nazi Germany in 1933, he took a permanent position at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, where he remained until his retirement in 1951.

Work

In 1918 Weyl proposed the first gauge theory — an attempt to unify gravity and electromagnetism by allowing local scale invariance; the physical idea failed in its original form, but the gauge principle became foundational to modern particle physics. His book Gruppentheorie und Quantenmechanik (1928) introduced group theory into quantum mechanics; he gave his name to the Weyl tensor in general relativity, Weyl spinors and the Weyl equation for massless fermions, Weyl's law on eigenvalue distributions, and the Weyl character formula in representation theory. He also engaged in the foundations debate, siding briefly with Brouwer's intuitionism against Hilbert's formalism in the famous "foundations crisis."
