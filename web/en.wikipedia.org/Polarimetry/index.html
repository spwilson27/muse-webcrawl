Polarimetry

Polarimetry is the measurement and interpretation of the polarization of transverse waves, most notably electromagnetic waves, such as radio or light waves. Typically polarimetry is done on electromagnetic waves that have traveled through or have been reflected, refracted or diffracted by some material in order to characterize that object.

Applications

Polarimetry of thin films and surfaces is commonly used in chemistry, crystallography and materials science. Plane polarized light is used in polarimeters to measure the optical activity of solutions of chiral substances.

In astronomy, polarimetry is used to study magnetic fields, scattering by dust and atmospheres, and the properties of objects such as pulsars and active galactic nuclei. Remote sensing instruments use polarization measurements to gather information about atmospheric aerosols and surface properties.

Measurement

The polarization state of light can be described by the Stokes parameters, which quantify total intensity, the degree and orientation of linear polarization, and the degree of circular polarization. Instruments combine rotating polarizers or waveplates with detectors to recover these parameters, sometimes at high speed using photoelastic modulators.
