RS-25

RS-25 (Aerojet Rocketdyne)
Also known as: Space Shuttle Main Engine (SSME)
Fuel: Liquid hydrogen / liquid oxygen
Thrust (vacuum): about 2,279 kN (512,300 lbf)
Applications: Space Shuttle; Space Launch System

The RS-25, also known as the Space Shuttle Main Engine (SSME), is a liquid-fuel cryogenic rocket engine built by Aerojet Rocketdyne (originally Rocketdyne). Burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen in a staged-combustion cycle, three RS-25s powered each Space Shuttle orbiter during launch.

Design

The RS-25 operates at one of the highest chamber pressures of any operational rocket engine and achieves a specific impulse of about 452 seconds in vacuum. Its reusable design, including advanced turbopumps and a regeneratively cooled thrust chamber, made it one of the most complex rocket engines ever flown.

Space Launch System

After the Shuttle's retirement in 2011, remaining RS-25s were adapted for NASA's Space Launch System (SLS), which uses four engines on its core stage. For SLS the engines are expended with the stage; new production RS-25s with simplified, lower-cost construction have been ordered for later flights. The first SLS launch, Artemis I, flew in November 2022.
