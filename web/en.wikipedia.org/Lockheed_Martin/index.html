Lockheed Martin Corporation is an American aerospace, arms, defence, information security, and technology corporation headquartered in Bethesda, Maryland. Formed in 1995 by the merger of Lockheed Corporation and Martin Marietta, it is the world's largest defence contractor by revenue, deriving the great majority of its income from military sales, principally to the United States government.

History

The company's roots lie in the Lockheed brothers' aircraft ventures of the 1910s–20s and Glenn L. Martin's company founded in 1912. The predecessor firms produced landmark aircraft such as the Lockheed Vega, P-38 Lightning, U-2 and SR-71 reconnaissance planes, the C-130 Hercules and C-5 Galaxy transports, and the F-117 Nighthawk stealth aircraft, as well as Martin-built bombers and the Titan missile family. The 1995 merger—part of the post–Cold War consolidation urged on the industry by the Pentagon—created a company spanning aircraft, missiles, electronics, and space systems. Lockheed Martin subsequently acquired Sikorsky Aircraft in 2015.

Organization and products

Lockheed Martin operates four business areas. Aeronautics builds the F-35 Lightning II—one of the largest defence programs in history—the F-16 and F-22 fighters, C-130 transports, and the secretive Skunk Works advanced development unit. Missiles and Fire Control produces THAAD and PAC-3 air-defence systems, HIMARS-related rocket artillery, and precision munitions. Rotary and Mission Systems covers Sikorsky helicopters such as the Black Hawk, the Aegis combat system, and training and logistics services. Space builds military and civil satellites, strategic missiles including the Trident II D5, and NASA's Orion crew vehicle.

Relationship with government and criticism

Lockheed Martin is consistently the largest single recipient of U.S. federal contract obligations, and its programs are frequent subjects of debate over cost growth and schedule delays—most prominently the F-35 program. The company is also a major exporter through the Foreign Military Sales program, supplying dozens of allied countries, which periodically raises questions about arms exports and regional stability.
