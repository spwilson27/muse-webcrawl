Vehicle dynamics

Vehicle dynamics is the study of vehicle motion — for land vehicles such as cars, trucks and trains — and how it responds to driver inputs and road conditions. The dynamics of a vehicle are described by the forces and moments generated at the tyre contact patches, the distribution of mass, and the geometry and compliance of the suspension and steering systems.

Fundamentals

Motion is conventionally described in three translations (longitudinal, lateral, vertical) and three rotations (roll, pitch, yaw). Key behaviours include understeer and oversteer, load transfer under braking and cornering, ride comfort over bumps, and directional stability at speed. The bicycle model, which lumps each axle's tyres into a single effective tyre, is a widely used simplification for analysing handling.

Tyres

Because all control forces pass through the four contact patches, tyre behaviour dominates vehicle dynamics. The tyre generates lateral force as a function of slip angle and longitudinal force as a function of slip ratio; the combined limits are often illustrated by the friction circle (or ellipse).

Applications

Understanding vehicle dynamics underpins chassis tuning, motorsport setup, and active safety systems such as ABS, electronic stability control and torque vectoring. Simulation tools and test rigs such as the K&C (kinematics and compliance) rig are used in development.
