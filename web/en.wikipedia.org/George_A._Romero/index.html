George A. Romero

George Andrew Romero
Born: 4 February 1940, New York City, U.S.
Died: 16 July 2017, Toronto, Canada
Occupation: Film director, screenwriter, editor
Known for: Night of the Living Dead; the Dead series

George Andrew Romero (February 4, 1940 – July 16, 2017) was an American-Canadian film director, writer, editor, and actor. His Night of the Living Dead series of films about a zombie apocalypse began with the 1968 film of the same name and is often considered a major contributor to the image of the zombie in modern culture. Other notable films in the series include Dawn of the Dead (1978) and Day of the Dead (1985). Romero is frequently described as the "Father of the Modern Zombie Film."

Career

Born in the Bronx and raised in Pittsburgh, Romero graduated from Carnegie Mellon University and began making commercials through his company Image Ten. With a budget of about $114,000, he directed Night of the Living Dead, shot in black and white near Pittsburgh; a copyright error placed it in the public domain, which helped it circulate widely and become one of the most influential independent films ever made. Its flesh-eating "ghouls" established the template for the modern zombie: slow, numerous, contagious, and killable only by destroying the brain.

Through the 1970s and 1980s Romero combined horror with pointed social satire: Dawn of the Dead set its siege in a shopping mall as a satire of consumerism, Day of the Dead turned to science and the military, and he also made the vampire film Martin (1977) and the anthology Creepshow (1982) with Stephen King. He returned to the series with Land of the Dead (2005), Diary of the Dead (2007), and Survival of the Dead (2009), and co-founded the production company responsible for the Tales from the Darkside television series.

Death and legacy

Romero died of lung cancer in Toronto on 16 July 2017, aged 77. His work reshaped horror cinema and popular culture: the modern zombie genre in film, television, games, and literature descends substantially from his inventions, and his low-budget independence became a model for generations of filmmakers.
