Arizona is a state in the Southwestern United States. It is the 6th largest and the 14th most populous of the 50 states. Its capital and largest city is Phoenix. Arizona is part of the Four Corners region with Utah, Colorado, and New Mexico; its other neighbouring states are Nevada, California, and the Mexican states of Sonora and Baja California.

Geography

Arizona is known for its desert landscape in the south (the Sonoran Desert, with saguaro cactus) and its high, forested Colorado Plateau in the north, which includes the Grand Canyon, one of the seven natural wonders of the world, carved by the Colorado River. The state's highest point is Humphreys Peak (3,852 m) near Flagstaff. About a quarter of the state's land comprises federally recognised Native American reservations, including part of the Navajo Nation, the largest reservation in the United States.

History

Indigenous peoples — including the Ancestral Puebloans and Hohokam, builders of extensive irrigation canals — inhabited the region for millennia. Spanish explorers arrived in the 16th century, and the area passed from Spain to Mexico in 1821 and then largely to the United States under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848) and the Gadsden Purchase (1853). Arizona Territory was created in 1863, and Arizona became the 48th and last contiguous state on 14 February 1912. Copper, cattle, cotton, citrus, and climate (the "Five Cs") long defined its economy; rapid growth followed the Second World War with air conditioning and the Phoenix and Tucson metropolitan booms.

Climate

The climate ranges from hot desert in the south (Phoenix averages summer highs above 40 °C) to alpine conditions in the northern highlands, with a monsoon season bringing thunderstorms in late summer. Water supply, especially from the Colorado River, is a central policy concern.
