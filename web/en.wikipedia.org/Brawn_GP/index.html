Brawn GP was a Formula One constructor that competed in the 2009 season, winning both the Drivers' and Constructors' World Championships in its only year of existence. The team arose from a management buyout of the Honda Racing F1 Team after Honda withdrew from the sport in December 2008, and was named after its team principal and owner, Ross Brawn.

The 2009 season

Honda's withdrawal left the team's 2009 car — developed through 2008 with a large budget — without an engine or owner. Brawn bought the team for a nominal £1 in March 2009, weeks before the season, and secured Mercedes engines. The car, the BGP 001, exploited a loophole in the new aerodynamic regulations with a "double diffuser," which rival teams protested but the FIA upheld. Jenson Button won six of the first seven races, building a championship lead he defended through a leaner second half of the season as rivals, especially Red Bull, caught up. Button clinched the Drivers' title at the Brazilian Grand Prix, where Brawn GP also sealed the Constructors' Championship — the only team in Formula One history to win the constructors' title in its sole season. Rubens Barrichello won two further races and finished third.

Sale to Mercedes

In November 2009, Daimler (Mercedes-Benz) bought a controlling stake in the team, renaming it Mercedes GP for 2010, with Button departing for McLaren and Michael Schumacher coming out of retirement to lead the driver line-up. The Brackley-based team went on to become the dominant Mercedes works team of the hybrid era.
