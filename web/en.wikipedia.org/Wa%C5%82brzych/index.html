Wałbrzych

Wałbrzych (German: Waldenburg) is a city in south-western Poland, in the Lower Silesian Voivodeship, situated in the Central Sudetes mountains. With a population of around 100,000, it was historically a major centre of coal mining and textile manufacturing.

History

First mentioned in the 12th–13th centuries, Waldenburg developed around coal extraction and linen weaving, later becoming part of Prussia and then the German Empire. After the Second World War the city was transferred to Poland under the Potsdam Agreement and its German population was expelled and replaced by Polish settlers. The last coal mines closed in the 1990s, causing severe economic hardship.

Landmarks

The city's best-known landmark is Książ Castle (German: Fürstenstein), the largest castle in Silesia, located in the Książ Landscape Park on the city's northern edge. Wałbrzych gained international attention in 2015 over claims of a buried Nazi "gold train" in tunnels near the city, which excavations failed to confirm.
