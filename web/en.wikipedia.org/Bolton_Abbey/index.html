Bolton Abbey

Bolton Abbey is a village and civil parish in North Yorkshire, England, named for the ruined Augustinian priory — properly Bolton Priory — on the banks of the River Wharfe in Wharfedale. The estate, some 30,000 acres, belongs to the Devonshire family and is one of Yorkshire's most popular visitor attractions.

History

The priory was founded in 1154 by Augustinian canons under a charter from Alice de Rumilly, on a site near an earlier foundation at Embsay. It was dissolved under Henry VIII in 1540, but the nave survived as the parish church of St Mary and St Cuthbert, which remains in use. The estate passed through the Clifford and Burlington families to the Dukes of Devonshire.

The estate

The grounds include the Strid, a narrow and notoriously dangerous rocky channel of the Wharfe; stepping stones across the river below the priory; woodlands, moorland and the Barden Tower, a medieval hunting lodge. The Embsay and Bolton Abbey Steam Railway preserves part of the former Midland Railway line.
