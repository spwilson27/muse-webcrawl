Lerwick

Lerwick (Old Norse: Leirvík, "muddy bay") is the main town and port of the Shetland Islands, Scotland. Founded in the 17th century as a centre for the Dutch herring fleet, it is Shetland's only burgh and its administrative capital, with a population of roughly 7,000 — about a third of the islands' total. Lerwick is Britain's northernmost major town, lying at about 60° north, closer to Bergen in Norway than to Edinburgh.

History and economy

The town grew around Bressay Sound, sheltered by the island of Bressay opposite. Fort Charlotte, built in the 17th century and rebuilt in 1781, guards the harbour. The herring boom of the 19th and early 20th centuries made Lerwick a busy fishing port; today its economy rests on fishing, North Sea oil support services (via the nearby Sullom Voe terminal), public services, tourism and ferry links to Aberdeen and Kirkwall. The town hosts the Shetland Museum and Archives and the Mareel arts venue.

Up Helly Aa

Lerwick is famous for Up Helly Aa, the fire festival held on the last Tuesday of January, in which a squad led by the Guizer Jarl, dressed as Vikings, parades through the streets with torches and burns a replica Viking longship.
