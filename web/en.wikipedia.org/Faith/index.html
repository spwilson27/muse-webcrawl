Faith

Faith is confidence or trust in a person, thing or concept. In the context of religion, faith is confidence in a god or in the doctrines of a religion, often characterized as belief not based on proof. The English word derives from Latin fides, and its meaning has been debated by theologians, philosophers and religious studies scholars — variously as belief, trust, loyalty or a distinct cognitive attitude.

In religions

In Christianity, faith in Jesus Christ and in God is central, described in the Epistle to the Hebrews as "the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen"; the Protestant Reformation stressed justification by faith alone (sola fide). In Islam, iman denotes faith in God, his angels, books, prophets, the Day of Judgment and divine decree. Judaism emphasizes emunah, often understood as faithfulness and trust manifest in action. In Buddhism, saddhā is a serene commitment to the practice leading to enlightenment rather than belief in doctrines alone.

Epistemology

Philosophers debate the relationship between faith and reason: fideism holds that faith is independent of, or even opposed to, reason, while other traditions regard faith and reason as compatible or view faith as a form of belief warranted by testimony and experience. Critics such as Bertrand Russell contrasted faith with conclusions held on evidence, while thinkers like William James defended a "will to believe" in certain circumstances.
