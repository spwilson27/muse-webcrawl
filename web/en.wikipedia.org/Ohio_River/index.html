The Ohio River is a 981-mile (1,579 km) river in the United States, the largest tributary by volume of the Mississippi River. It is formed at Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, by the confluence of the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers, and flows generally southwest past Cincinnati and Louisville to join the Mississippi at Cairo, Illinois. Its drainage basin covers about 203,000 square miles across 15 states, and more than 30 million people live in the basin.

Course

For most of its length the Ohio forms state boundaries: its northern bank borders Ohio, Indiana and Illinois, its southern bank West Virginia and Kentucky. Because the colonial-era boundary follows the river's northern low-water mark, most of the river itself lies within Kentucky. Major tributaries include the Kanawha, Kentucky, Green, Wabash, Cumberland and Tennessee rivers; the Tennessee is the largest by discharge.

History

The name derives from the Seneca (Iroquoian) word ohi:yo', "great river" or "good river." The valley was contested in the French and Indian War, then opened to American settlement after the Revolution; flatboats and later steamboats made the Ohio the principal gateway to the West, and cities such as Pittsburgh, Cincinnati and Louisville grew as river ports. Before the Civil War the river separated free states from slave-holding Kentucky, making it a symbolic and practical crossing line on the Underground Railroad. Devastating floods, notably in 1937, prompted a comprehensive system of flood walls, locks and dams.

Navigation and ecology

The modern river is managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers as a staircase of navigable pools maintained by about 20 locks and dams; towboats push barge tows carrying coal, aggregates, chemicals and grain, making the Ohio one of the busiest inland waterways in the world. Water quality, once severely degraded by industrial and municipal pollution, improved markedly after the creation of the Ohio River Valley Water Sanitation Commission (ORSANCO) in 1948 and the Clean Water Act, though combined-sewer overflows, harmful algal blooms and contaminants such as PFAS remain concerns.
