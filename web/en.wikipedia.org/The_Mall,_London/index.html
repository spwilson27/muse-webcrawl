The Mall, London

The Mall is a road in the City of Westminster, London, running from Buckingham Palace at its western end to Admiralty Arch and on to Trafalgar Square at its eastern end. It is approximately 0.8 miles (1.3 km) long and forms the ceremonial route used by the British monarch on state occasions, as well as the finishing straight of the London Marathon.

History

The Mall takes its name from the game of pall-mall, which was played on the site in the 17th century. Laid out as a formal avenue in the early 20th century, it was remodelled under Sir Aston Webb as part of the grand memorial scheme to Queen Victoria, completed in 1911 with the unveiling of the Victoria Memorial at its western end. The surface of the road is coloured red, giving the effect of a giant red carpet leading up to Buckingham Palace.

Landmarks

Along its length stand Admiralty Arch, the Victoria Memorial designed by Sir Thomas Brock, and the tree-lined expanse of St James's Park to the south. The Mall is closed to regular traffic on Sundays and public holidays and during ceremonial events. State visits, trooping the colour, royal weddings and jubilee celebrations have all featured processions along the route, with crowds lining its broad pavements beneath rows of Union Flags.
