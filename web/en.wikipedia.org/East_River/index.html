The East River is a saltwater tidal estuary or strait in New York City, 26 miles (42 km) long (including Hell Gate), connecting Upper New York Bay on its south end to Long Island Sound on its north end. It separates the boroughs of Queens and Brooklyn on Long Island from Manhattan and the Bronx on the mainland. It is not a true river but a tidal strait, whose current reverses with the tides.

Geography

The East River begins at Long Island City, Queens, flows southwest past Astoria and the Bronx shore, then turns south between Manhattan and Brooklyn/Queens. At Hell Gate, a narrow and turbulent section near Astoria Park, it meets the Harlem River and Bronx Kill. Strong tidal currents—up to about 5 knots—flow through the strait. Below Hell Gate, the river passes Roosevelt Island, continues east of Manhattan past the United Nations headquarters, and empties into Upper New York Bay between Lower Manhattan and Brooklyn Heights.

History

The channel was formed some 11,000 years ago by deglaciation. Known to the Dutch as the Hellegat ("Hell Hole"), Hell Gate's dangerous reefs and currents made it treacherous for shipping; in 1885, the US Army Corps of Engineers demolished Flood Rock with a massive explosives blast to clear the channel. The river's East Side docks drove New York's port economy, and its waters supplied the city's oyster beds until pollution closed them in the early 20th century.

Crossings

The Brooklyn Bridge (1883), Williamsburg Bridge (1903), Queensboro Bridge (1909), Manhattan Bridge (1909), Robert F. Kennedy Bridge (1936), Bronx–Whitestone Bridge (1939), and Throgs Neck Bridge (1961) span the river, along with the Robert F. Kennedy/Triborough complex, Hell Gate Bridge (rail), and several subway and vehicle tunnels. Roosevelt Island is also served by the Roosevelt Island Tramway.

Islands

The East River contains several islands, including Randalls Island, Wards Island, Rikers Island (home to New York's main jail complex), North and South Brother Islands, Mill Rock, U Thant Island, and Roosevelt Island. Roosevelt Island houses major residential development and the Cornell Tech campus.
