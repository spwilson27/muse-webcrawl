Percy Spencer

Born: Percy LeBaron Spencer, 9 July 1894, Howland, Maine, US
Died: 8 September 1970 (aged 76)
Known for: Inventing the microwave oven
Awards: Inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame (1990)

Percy LeBaron Spencer (July 9, 1894 – September 8, 1970) was an American physicist and inventor. He is best known as the inventor of the microwave oven while working for the Raytheon company.

Early life

Spencer was born in Howland, Maine. His father died when he was 18 months old, and his mother soon left him in the care of an aunt and uncle. He never graduated from grammar school, but went to work in a spool mill as an apprentice at age 12. At 18 he joined the US Navy as a wireless operator and learned about radio technology; he later taught himself trigonometry, geometry, calculus, physics, chemistry and metallurgy, largely through textbooks.

Career

After the First World War Spencer joined the newly founded Raytheon Company in Cambridge, Massachusetts. During the Second World War he became a leading expert in radar tube design, heading the tube division that developed and mass-produced magnetrons, dramatically increasing wartime production.

In 1945, while working near an active magnetron, Spencer noticed that a peanut cluster bar in his pocket had melted. He experimented with popcorn kernels and an egg, confirming that microwaves could cook food quickly. Raytheon filed a patent on 8 October 1945 for a microwave cooking process, and in 1947 installed the first commercial microwave oven, the "Radarange", in a Boston restaurant. Spencer received a $2 bonus, Raytheon's standard reward for employee inventions, and no royalties. He eventually became a senior vice president and board member of Raytheon and accumulated 150 patents during his career.
