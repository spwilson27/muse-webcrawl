The Monterrey Institute of Technology and Higher Education (Spanish: Instituto Tecnológico y de Estudios Superiores de Monterrey; abbreviated ITESM and widely known as Tec de Monterrey) is a private, non-profit, multi-campus university in Mexico, founded in the city of Monterrey in 1943 by industrialist Eugenio Garza Sada and a group of local businessmen.

The Monterrey campus rectorate, with its mural by Jorge González Camarena.

History

Modelled in part on the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, the institute began with about 350 students in engineering and business. It grew into a system of campuses across Mexico and became known for early adoption of technology in education: it was an early node of the internet in Mexico and pioneered satellite-based distance teaching. Businessman Eugenio Garza Sada was assassinated in a 1973 kidnapping attempt, an event that marked the institution's history.

Academics

The university offers undergraduate and graduate programmes in engineering, business, health sciences, social sciences, humanities and design, and operates high schools (PrepaTec). Its educational model, Tec21, emphasises challenge-based learning. Tec de Monterrey consistently ranks among the top universities in Mexico and Latin America in international rankings, and its EGADE Business School is highly regarded.

Sports and symbols

Its teams are the Borregos (Rams), and its American football programme is among Mexico's most successful. The institution's alumni include prominent business leaders and politicians across Mexico.
