Male impersonator

A male impersonator is a performer, usually female, who impersonates men on stage. Male impersonation was a popular act in British music hall and American vaudeville in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

History

Performers adopted male dress and mannerisms to sing songs — often sentimental or comic songs about male life, sweethearts and soldiering. The most famous was Vesta Tilley, a superstar of the British music hall whose immaculate male attire and popular songs made her one of the highest-paid performers of her era. In the United States, performers such as Ella Wesner were celebrated in vaudeville.

In performance traditions

Related traditions include the pantomime "principal boy" in British theatre, breeches roles in opera, and modern drag king performance, which consciously revives and reworks male impersonation.

Cultural significance

Though performers often presented conventional images of masculinity, male impersonation played with contemporary gender norms and allowed women a stage persona of unusual freedom and authority.
