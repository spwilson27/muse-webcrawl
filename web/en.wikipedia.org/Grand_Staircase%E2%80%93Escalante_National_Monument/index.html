Grand Staircase–Escalante National Monument is a United States national monument protecting 1.88 million acres of rugged canyon country in southern Utah. President Bill Clinton created it in 1996 under the Antiquities Act, naming it for the Grand Staircase — the great sequence of colourful sedimentary cliffs rising from the Grand Canyon toward Bryce Canyon — and the Escalante River canyon system.

Features

The monument spans three distinct regions: the Grand Staircase, with its White, Vermilion, Pink, Gray, and Chocolate Cliffs; the Kaiparowits Plateau, a rich source of Late Cretaceous dinosaur fossils; and the canyons of the Escalante River, carved into Navajo sandstone with slot canyons, natural arches such as Coyote Gulch's, and grottos along the Burr Trail and Hole-in-the-Rock Road.

Boundary disputes

In December 2017 President Donald Trump reduced the monument by nearly half, the largest such reduction in US history, opening excluded lands to potential fossil-fuel development. In October 2021 President Joe Biden restored the original boundaries. The monument remains central to debates over presidential authority under the Antiquities Act, and is administered by the Bureau of Land Management rather than the National Park Service — a first for a monument of its kind.
