Isua Greenstone Belt

The Isua Greenstone Belt is an Archean greenstone belt in southwestern Greenland, near the town of Nuuk. It contains some of the oldest known rocks on Earth, dated to roughly 3.7 to 3.8 billion years old, and is one of the most intensively studied localities for early Earth geology.

Geology

The belt consists of metavolcanic and metasedimentary rocks, including pillow basalts, banded iron formations, and cherts, which were later deformed and metamorphosed. The presence of pillow lavas indicates that some of the rocks formed as oceanic crust erupted under water, providing evidence for liquid water on Earth's surface very early in its history.

Evidence of early life

Carbon isotope ratios in graphite within the belt's metasedimentary rocks have been interpreted as possible evidence of biological activity around 3.7 billion years ago, and in 2016 researchers reported stromatolite-like structures dated to about 3.7 billion years, which—if confirmed—would be among the oldest traces of life on Earth. Both claims remain debated because of the rocks' intense deformation and metamorphism.
