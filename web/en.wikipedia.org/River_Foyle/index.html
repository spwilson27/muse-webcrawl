The River Foyle (Irish: An Feabhal) is a river in west Ulster, in the northwest of Ireland. It is formed by the confluence of the River Finn and the River Mourne at Lifford and Strabane, and flows north past the city of Derry before widening into Lough Foyle and reaching the Atlantic near Greencastle.

Geography

Although only about 32 kilometres long from the confluence of its headstreams, the Foyle drains a catchment of some 2,900 square kilometres through its tributaries, including the Finn, the Mourne, the Strule, and the Deele. The river forms part of the border between County Donegal in the Republic of Ireland and Counties Tyrone and Londonderry in Northern Ireland. It is one of the fastest-flowing rivers in Ireland and is tidal through Derry.

Crossings and ecology

At Derry the river is crossed by the Craigavon Bridge, one of the few double-decker road bridges in Europe, the Foyle Bridge — among the longest bridges in Ireland — and the curved Peace Bridge for pedestrians and cyclists, opened in 2011. The Foyle system is noted for its Atlantic salmon and sea trout fisheries, and the estuary supports internationally important numbers of wintering wildfowl; the river is designated within special areas of conservation.
