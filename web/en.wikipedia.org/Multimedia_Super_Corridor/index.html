Multimedia Super Corridor (MSC Malaysia)
LocationKuala Lumpur region, Malaysia
Established1996
Administered byMalaysia Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC)

The Multimedia Super Corridor (MSC), now branded MSC Malaysia, is a Special Economic Zone and high-technology business district in central-southern Peninsular Malaysia. Envisioned by Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad and launched in 1996, the corridor stretches roughly 15 km × 50 km from the Petronas Towers in Kuala Lumpur southward, encompassing Cyberjaya, Putrajaya, and Kuala Lumpur International Airport.

The MSC was created to leapfrog Malaysia into the information age by attracting world-class technology companies with a package of incentives: tax holidays, unrestricted employment of foreign knowledge workers, no censorship of the Internet (guaranteed under its Bill of Guarantees), and purpose-built infrastructure. Its flagship applications included e-government, smart schools, telemedicine and a national smart card (MyKad).

Development

Putrajaya became the federal administrative capital, while Cyberjaya was built as an "intelligent garden city" tech hub hosting thousands of MSC-status companies and shared-services operations. Oversight originally sat with the Multimedia Development Corporation, now the Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC). In 2022 the government launched Malaysia Digital as the successor framework, expanding the initiative nationwide beyond the original corridor.
