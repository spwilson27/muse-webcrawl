Victim impact statement

A victim impact statement is a written or oral statement presented to the court at the sentencing of a person convicted of a crime. It describes how the criminal act affected the victim and, where the victim died, the surviving family members, and usually asks the court to consider those effects when imposing sentence.

[Image: A victim giving a statement at a sentencing hearing, reading from a prepared letter.]

Content and use

Statements commonly address the physical, emotional, psychological and financial harm suffered, and in capital cases the circumstances of the deceased victim. In the United States, victim impact evidence was upheld by the Supreme Court in Payne v. Tennessee (1991), and the Crime Victims' Rights Act of 2004 guarantees victims a right to be heard at sentencing. Similar procedures exist in Canada, Australia, and parts of Europe.

Criticism

Critics argue that such statements can introduce arbitrary disparities into sentencing, because some victims are more articulate than others, while supporters stress their therapeutic value and democratic legitimacy.
