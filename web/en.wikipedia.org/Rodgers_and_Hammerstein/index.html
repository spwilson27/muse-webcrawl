Rodgers and Hammerstein

Rodgers and Hammerstein was a theater-writing team of composer Richard Rodgers (1902–1979) and lyricist-dramatist Oscar Hammerstein II (1895–1960). They created a string of popular Broadway musicals in the 1940s and 1950s, initiating what is considered the "golden age" of musical theatre.

The partnership began with Oklahoma! (1943), a landmark production that integrated song, dance, and plot. It was followed by Carousel (1945), South Pacific (1949), The King and I (1951), and The Sound of Music (1959), among others. Five of their musicals were made into successful films, and the team collectively won 35 Tony Awards, 15 Academy Awards, two Pulitzer Prizes, and two Grammy Awards.

Before the partnership

Rodgers had previously collaborated with Lorenz Hart on musicals such as Pal Joey and On Your Toes, while Hammerstein had written the books and lyrics for operettas including Show Boat (1927), with music by Jerome Kern. After Hart's declining health ended the Rodgers and Hart partnership, Rodgers approached Hammerstein about adapting the play Green Grow the Lilacs, which became Oklahoma!.

Style and influence

Rodgers and Hammerstein are credited with developing the "integrated musical", in which songs and dances advance the plot and reveal character rather than serving as diversion. Their works often treated serious themes—racism in South Pacific, domestic violence in Carousel—within accessible popular entertainment. Their company, Williamson Music, later the Rodgers & Hammerstein Organization, managed licensing of their catalog.
