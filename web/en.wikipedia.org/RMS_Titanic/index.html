RMS Titanic

RMS Titanic departing Southampton, 10 April 1912

RMS Titanic was a British ocean liner that sank in the North Atlantic Ocean on 15 April 1912 after striking an iceberg during her maiden voyage from Southampton, England, to New York City, United States. Of the estimated 2,224 passengers and crew aboard, about 1,500 died, making the sinking one of the deadliest peacetime maritime disasters in history.

Titanic, operated by the White Star Line, was the largest ship afloat at the time she entered service and the second of three Olympic-class ocean liners. She was built by the Harland and Wolff shipyard in Belfast between 1909 and 1911. Popularly described as "unsinkable" because of her watertight compartments, she carried only enough lifeboats for about half of those aboard, in compliance with outdated regulations.

The sinking

On the night of 14 April 1912, lookouts spotted an iceberg directly ahead; despite evasive action, the ship's starboard side scraped along the berg, buckling hull plates and flooding five of her sixteen watertight compartments—one more than she could survive. The ship sank bow-first over the course of about two hours and forty minutes, breaking in two as she went down. Lifeboats were launched partially filled, and the rescue ship RMS Carpathia arrived hours later to pick up about 710 survivors.

Aftermath and wreck

The disaster shocked the world and led to major reforms in maritime safety, including the first International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) in 1914, requirements for enough lifeboats for everyone aboard, 24-hour radio watches, and the establishment of the International Ice Patrol. The wreck, lying about 3,800 metres (12,500 ft) deep southeast of Newfoundland, was located in 1985 by an expedition led by Robert Ballard and Jean-Louis Michel. It has since been extensively explored and documented, though it is deteriorating rapidly; UNESCO protects it as underwater cultural heritage. The sinking has remained an enduring subject of books, exhibitions, and films, most famously James Cameron's 1997 film Titanic.
