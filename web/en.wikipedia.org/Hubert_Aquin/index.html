Hubert Aquin (24 October 1929 – 15 March 1977) was a French-Canadian novelist, essayist and political activist from Quebec, regarded as one of the most important Quebec writers of the twentieth century. A militant supporter of Quebec independence, he fused avant-garde narrative experiment with the politics of national liberation.

Life

Aquin studied philosophy at the Université de Montréal and at the Institut d'études politiques in Paris. He worked in radio and television production for Radio-Canada and the National Film Board, and edited the journal Liberté. In 1964 he publicly declared himself a member of the underground independence movement and was arrested and detained, spending time in a psychiatric institute, where he wrote his best-known novel.

Works

Prochain épisode (1965; translated as Next Episode) is a hallucinatory first-person narrative by a jailed revolutionary awaiting trial, written in dense, lyrical prose; it became a landmark of Quebec literature. His later novels include Trou de mémoire (1968; Blackout), L'Antiphonaire (1969; The Antiphonary) and Neige noire (1974; Hamlet's Twin). His essay "La fatigue culturelle du Canada-français" (1962) was a key text of the Quiet Revolution era. Aquin took his own life in 1977 in the gardens of Villa Maria convent in Montreal.
