Turkestan, also spelt Turkistan (Persian: ترکستان, "Land of the Turks"), is a historical region of Central Asia corresponding roughly to the lands between Siberia to the north, Persia and Afghanistan to the south, the Caspian Sea to the west and Mongolia and the Gobi Desert to the east. The name was never an official political unit but a geographic and cultural term for the homeland and sphere of the Turkic peoples.

Divisions and history

Turkestan is conventionally divided by the Tian Shan mountains into Western (or Russian) Turkestan and Eastern (or Chinese) Turkestan. The west contains the great Silk Road oasis cities of Samarkand, Bukhara, Khiva and Tashkent, ruled successively by Persian, Arab, Mongol and Turkic dynasties including the Timurids. The east, the Tarim Basin with Kashgar and Turfan, passed between Turkic khanates and Chinese empires. In the 19th century the Russian Empire conquered Western Turkestan—the arena of the "Great Game" with Britain—organising it partly as the Turkestan Governor-Generalship, while Eastern Turkestan became part of Qing and later Chinese Xinjiang.

Modern usage

After the Soviet national delimitation of the 1920s divided Western Turkestan into five republics, and with Xinjiang under Chinese rule, "Turkestan" survives mainly as a historical and cultural designation, and in political movements advocating Turkic unity.
