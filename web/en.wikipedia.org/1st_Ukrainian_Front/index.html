The 1st Ukrainian Front (Russian: Пе́рвый Украи́нский фронт) was a major formation of the Soviet Red Army during the Second World War, equivalent in scale to a Western army group. It was created on 20 October 1943 by renaming the Voronezh Front.

History

Under General Nikolai Vatutin, the front liberated Kyiv in November 1943 and conducted the Korsun–Shevchenkovsky offensive in early 1944. After Vatutin was mortally wounded by Ukrainian nationalist partisans, command passed briefly to Georgy Zhukov and then, in May 1944, to Ivan Konev, who led the front for the rest of the war.

Under Konev the front carried out the Lvov–Sandomierz offensive in the summer of 1944, liberating western Ukraine and south-eastern Poland, and the Vistula–Oder offensive in January 1945, which carried Soviet forces across Poland to the Oder River. In April–May 1945 the front took part in the Battle of Berlin, advancing from the south, and then turned south-west to liberate Prague in the Prague offensive, the last major Soviet operation of the war in Europe.

Composition

At various times the front included the 3rd and 5th Guards Armies, the 13th, 27th, 38th, 40th, 47th, 52nd, 59th and 60th Armies, and the 1st, 3rd and 4th Guards Tank Armies and 2nd Air Army, among other formations.
