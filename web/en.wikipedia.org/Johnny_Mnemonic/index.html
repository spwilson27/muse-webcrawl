"Johnny Mnemonic" is a short story by American-Canadian writer William Gibson, first published in Omni magazine in 1981 and later included in the collection Burning Chrome (1986). Set in Gibson's Sprawl universe, it is an early landmark of the cyberpunk genre and introduced the character Molly Millions, who reappears in Gibson's novel Neuromancer.

Plot

Johnny is a data trafficker who carries sensitive information in a cybernetic implant in his head — data he cannot access himself and which is retrieved only by a password known to the client. When his latest cargo turns out to be stolen Yakuza data of great value, Johnny becomes a target and, with the help of the razor-fingered bodyguard Molly Millions and the Lo Teks, must find a way to extract the data before it kills him or his pursuers do.

Film adaptation

The story was adapted into a 1995 feature film, Johnny Mnemonic, directed by Robert Longo from Gibson's own screenplay and starring Keanu Reeves, Dina Meyer, Ice-T, Takeshi Kitano and Dolph Lundgren. The film significantly expanded the plot, adding the element of a cure for "nerve attenuation syndrome" stored in Johnny's head. It was a commercial disappointment on release but has since attracted cult attention; a black-and-white version, Johnny Mnemonic: In Black and White, was released in 2021 to a warmer reception.
