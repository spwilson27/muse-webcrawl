Côte-Nord

Country: Canada

Province: Quebec

Area: approx. 236,700 km² (land)

Population: approx. 90,000

Regional county municipalities: 6

Côte-Nord ("North Coast") is an administrative region of Quebec, Canada, occupying the northern shore of the St. Lawrence River and the Gulf of St. Lawrence from Tadoussac eastward to the Labrador border, together with Anticosti Island and a vast interior of the Canadian Shield. It is the second-largest administrative region of Quebec by area but one of the least densely populated.

Geography and economy

The region's settlements are strung along Quebec Route 138 on the coast, including the principal cities Baie-Comeau and Sept-Îles, as well as Havre-Saint-Pierre, Forestville and Port-Cartier. The interior is dominated by boreal forest, lakes and some of Quebec's largest hydroelectric installations, including the Manicouagan and Romaine complexes developed by Hydro-Québec. Mining — iron ore shipped through Sept-Îles and Port-Cartier from the Labrador Trough mines — forestry, and aluminium smelting anchor the economy.

People

Côte-Nord is home to several Innu communities, including Essipit, Pessamit, Uashat-Maliotenam and Ekuanitshit, and to the Naskapi Nation of Kawawachikamach. Whale watching near Tadoussac, at the mouth of the Saguenay Fjord, and the Mingan Archipelago National Park Reserve are major visitor attractions.
