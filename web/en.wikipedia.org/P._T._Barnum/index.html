P. T. Barnum

Phineas Taylor Barnum (5 July 1810 – 7 April 1891) was an American showman, businessman and politician remembered for promoting celebrated hoaxes and for founding the circus that became Barnum & Bailey's "Greatest Show on Earth".

Career

Barnum began his entertainment career in the 1830s exhibiting Joice Heth, an elderly enslaved woman falsely billed as George Washington's 161-year-old nurse. In 1841 he acquired the American Museum in New York City, filling it with natural curiosities, theatrical performances and human oddities. He promoted the midget performer General Tom Thumb (Charles Stratton) and the Swedish soprano Jenny Lind, whose 1850 American tour he managed to great acclaim.

The circus

In his sixties Barnum entered the circus business; his travelling show merged with James A. Bailey's in 1881 to form Barnum & Bailey. The show featured Jumbo, the celebrated African elephant whose name entered the language as a synonym for something huge.

Politics and legacy

Barnum served in the Connecticut legislature and as mayor of Bridgeport, and was a benefactor of Tufts University. Though the phrase "There's a sucker born every minute" is often attributed to him, there is no evidence he said it. His autobiography, Struggles and Triumphs, was a bestseller, and his career inspired the 2017 film The Greatest Showman.
