Fender Esquire

The Fender Esquire was the first solid-body electric guitar sold by Fender, introduced in 1950.

The Fender Esquire is a solid-body electric guitar manufactured by Fender, the first solid-body guitar sold by the company. Introduced in 1950, it was the single-pickup precursor to the two-pickup Broadcaster, which was renamed the Telecaster after a trademark dispute with Gretsch.

The Esquire has a single pickup in the bridge position and a distinctive three-way switching circuit that, despite the single pickup, gives three tonal options: a full tone-control setting, a setting that bypasses the tone control for a brighter sound, and a bass-heavy preset with a fixed capacitor network. The body and neck are essentially those of the Telecaster, and the routed neck-pickup cavity is present under the pickguard on many examples, making conversion to Telecaster configuration possible.

History and players

The Esquire remained in production, with interruptions, for decades and has been reissued many times. Notable players include Bruce Springsteen, whose modified 1950s Esquire/Telecaster hybrid adorns the cover of Born to Run; Jeff Beck, who played a 1954 Esquire with the Yardbirds; and Syd Barrett of Pink Floyd. Original 1950s Esquires are highly collectible.
