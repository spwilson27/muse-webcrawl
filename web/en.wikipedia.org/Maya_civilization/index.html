The Maya civilization was a Mesoamerican civilisation that developed in the area that today comprises south-eastern Mexico, all of Guatemala and Belize, and the western portions of Honduras and El Salvador. It is noted for its logosyllabic script — the most sophisticated writing system of the pre-Columbian Americas — as well as for its art, architecture, mathematics, calendar, and astronomical system.

Periods

In the Preclassic period (c. 2000 BCE – 250 CE) Maya society took shape, culminating in huge cities such as El Mirador with some of the largest pyramids ever built in the Americas. The Classic period (c. 250–900 CE) saw dozens of competing city-states ruled by divine kings (k'uhul ajaw), interconnected by trade, dynastic marriage, and warfare, with Tikal and Calakmul as rival superpowers. The southern lowland cities were largely abandoned during the 9th–10th-century Classic Maya collapse, associated with drought, warfare, and political failure, while northern cities such as Chichen Itza and later Mayapan flourished in the Postclassic period.

Culture and knowledge

The Maya wrote histories, king lists, and astronomical tables in hieroglyphs on stelae, lintels, pottery, and bark-paper codices — of which only four survive, the rest destroyed during the Spanish conquest. Their vigesimal mathematics employed an independent concept of zero; their calendar combined the 260-day Tzolk'in, the 365-day Haab', and the Long Count for deep historical time. Maya astronomers tracked Venus and eclipse cycles with great precision. Religion centred on a pantheon of gods, royal bloodletting, the sacred ballgame, and a cosmology of layered heavens and the underworld, Xibalba.

Conquest and the Maya today

Spanish conquest of the Maya began in the 1520s, but the last independent Maya kingdom, the Itza of Nojpetén, held out until 1697. Maya peoples nonetheless preserved their languages and many traditions through the colonial era and the Caste War of Yucatán. Today some seven to eight million Maya people speak around thirty Mayan languages in Mexico and Central America, and Maya culture remains a living presence in the region.
