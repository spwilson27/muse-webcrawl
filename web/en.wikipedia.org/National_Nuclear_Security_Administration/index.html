Formed: 2000

The National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) is a United States federal agency responsible for safeguarding national security through the military application of nuclear science. A semi-autonomous agency within the U.S. Department of Energy, it was established by Congress in 2000 in response to security scandals at the nuclear weapons laboratories.

Missions

The NNSA maintains and enhances the safety, security, and effectiveness of the U.S. nuclear weapons stockpile without explosive testing, through the science-based Stockpile Stewardship Program. It also works to reduce global nuclear dangers — securing vulnerable nuclear materials worldwide and supporting nonproliferation — provides the U.S. Navy with nuclear propulsion for submarines and aircraft carriers, and responds to nuclear and radiological emergencies through its Nuclear Emergency Support Team (NEST).

Sites

The agency oversees the nuclear security enterprise, including three national laboratories — Los Alamos, Lawrence Livermore, and Sandia — and production and testing sites such as the Pantex Plant in Texas, the Y-12 National Security Complex in Tennessee, the Kansas City National Security Campus, the Savannah River Site, and the Nevada National Security Site. Most work is performed by management-and-operating contractors.

Leadership

The NNSA is headed by an Administrator, who also serves as Under Secretary of Energy for Nuclear Security, appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.
