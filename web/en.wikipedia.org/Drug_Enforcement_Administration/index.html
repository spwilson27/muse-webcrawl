Drug Enforcement Administration

The Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) is a United States federal law enforcement agency under the Department of Justice, tasked with combating illicit drug trafficking and distribution within the United States. It is the lead agency for domestic enforcement of the Controlled Substances Act, sharing jurisdiction with the Federal Bureau of Investigation and state and local agencies. The DEA also coordinates drug enforcement abroad, maintaining offices in dozens of countries, and manages the scheduling system that classifies controlled substances in the United States.

DEA headquarters in Arlington, Virginia.

History

The DEA was created by President Richard Nixon through an executive order in July 1973, consolidating several predecessor agencies, including the Bureau of Narcotics and Dangerous Drugs, the Office of Drug Abuse Law Enforcement, and elements of the Customs Service and the CIA's drug intelligence functions. It was a central instrument of the "war on drugs" declared in the early 1970s. Its headquarters are in Arlington, Virginia, across from the Pentagon, with a training academy at Quantico. The agency employs roughly 10,000 people, including about 4,000–5,000 special agents, intelligence analysts, diversion investigators, and forensic scientists, with an annual budget in the region of three billion dollars.

Responsibilities

The DEA investigates drug trafficking organisations, seizes assets and contraband, and regulates the legal manufacture and distribution of controlled substances through its Diversion Control Division, which registers and audits pharmaceutical manufacturers, distributors, pharmacies, and practitioners to prevent diversion of medicines such as opioids into illicit channels. Its forensic laboratories analyse seized drugs, and its intelligence division supports operations. Under the Controlled Substances Act, substances are placed into five schedules according to abuse potential and accepted medical use; the DEA administrator, in consultation with health authorities, manages the scheduling and rescheduling process — a role that has made the agency central to debates over cannabis classification and the response to the opioid epidemic, including the rise of illicit fentanyl.

Criticism and controversy

The DEA has been criticised over aspects of the drug war: the racial and social effects of enforcement, civil asset forfeiture practices, the use of confidential informants, and mass surveillance programmes such as a long-running telephone metadata database revealed in 2013 and discontinued. The agency's resistance to rescheduling cannabis, and its scheduling of emerging synthetic drugs, have drawn scientific and political debate. Supporters credit the DEA with disrupting major cartels and trafficking networks and with regulating the legitimate controlled-substance supply chain. Several former administrators and officials have themselves become critics of aspects of prohibition policy after leaving office.

Organisation

The DEA is headed by an Administrator appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate, reporting through the Attorney General. Domestic operations are organised into field divisions across the United States, supplemented by foreign offices in more than 60 countries. The agency operates its own air wing, special response teams, and the EPIC intelligence centre (El Paso Intelligence Center). Its museum and visitors centre in Arlington documents the history of drug enforcement in the United States.
