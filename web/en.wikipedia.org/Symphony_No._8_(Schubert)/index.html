Symphony No. 8 (Schubert)

The Symphony No. 8 in B minor, D 759, widely known as the Unfinished Symphony, is a symphony by the Austrian composer Franz Schubert, written in 1822. Schubert completed only two movements, although he lived for six more years; a nearly complete sketch of a third movement, a scherzo, also survives. It is one of the most famous works in the orchestral repertoire.

Composition and premiere

Schubert began the symphony in October 1822, apparently as a token of gratitude to the Graz Music Society, which had made him an honorary member. He sent the two finished movements to his friend Anselm Hüttenbrenner in Graz, where the manuscript lay unplayed for decades. It was finally premiered on 17 December 1865 in Vienna, conducted by Johann von Herbeck, with the finale of Schubert's Third Symphony tacked on as a conclusion. Why Schubert abandoned the work remains unknown; theories range from a crisis of confidence after Beethoven's example to his illness following a syphilis diagnosis.

Music

The first movement, Allegro moderato in B minor, opens with a hushed theme in cellos and basses, followed by a famously lyrical second theme in G major played by cellos over syncopated accompaniment. The second movement, Andante con moto in E major, alternates serene lyricism with dramatic outbursts. The surviving scherzo sketch is orchestrated only in its opening measures.

Completions

Many musicians have attempted to complete the symphony, using Schubert's own sketches and entr'acte music from Rosamunde as a finale; in 2019 an artificial-intelligence project sponsored by Huawei produced a widely publicised completion of the missing movements.
