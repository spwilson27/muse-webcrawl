Rogers Pass is a mountain pass in the Selkirk Mountains of British Columbia, Canada, situated in Glacier National Park between Revelstoke and Golden. Discovered in 1881 by Major A. B. Rogers for the Canadian Pacific Railway, it became the route of the transcontinental main line and, since 1962, of the Trans-Canada Highway.

Geography and climate

The pass summit reaches about 1,330 metres. The area receives very heavy snowfall, often exceeding 10 metres per year, and the surrounding slopes generate numerous avalanche paths. A summit interpretive centre explains the railway and avalanche history of the pass. In 1971 Rogers Pass was declared a National Historic Site of Canada in recognition of its role in completing the railway that bound British Columbia to Confederation.
