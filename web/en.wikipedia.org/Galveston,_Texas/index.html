Galveston, Texas

Galveston is a coastal city and port in Texas, United States, located on Galveston Island and Pelican Island in the Gulf of Mexico. The seat of Galveston County, it had a population of about 53,000 at the 2020 census. The city is named for Bernardo de Gálvez, the Spanish colonial governor who aided the American cause during the Revolutionary War.

History

The pirate Jean Lafitte established a settlement on the island in 1817, and the Republic of Texas founded the city in 1839. Through the 19th century Galveston grew into Texas's largest city and one of the busiest ports in the United States, a major center for immigration and the cotton trade, known as the "Wall Street of the Southwest." On September 8, 1900, a catastrophic hurricane struck, killing an estimated 6,000–8,000 people — still the deadliest natural disaster in U.S. history. In response, the city raised its grade by up to 17 feet and built a massive seawall. The port's importance nevertheless shifted to Houston, and Galveston later reinvented itself around tourism, healthcare, and education. Hurricane Ike caused extensive damage in 2008.

Economy and institutions

Galveston hosts the University of Texas Medical Branch, Texas A&M University at Galveston, cruise terminals, beaches, and historic districts such as the Strand, famous for its Victorian iron-front buildings. Juneteenth, commemorating General Order No. 3 announced in Galveston on June 19, 1865, originated in the city.
