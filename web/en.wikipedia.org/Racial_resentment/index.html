Racial resentment


Placeholder image: Racial resentment

Racial resentment is a concept in political science, developed principally by Donald Kinder and Lynn Sanders in their 1996 book Divided by Color. It describes a set of beliefs among some white Americans that African Americans violate traditional American values such as individualism and the work ethic, and receive undeserved advantages.

Measurement

The racial resentment scale uses survey items asking whether respondents agree that Black Americans should "work their way up without special favours", that they have received less than they deserve, and similar statements. Scores strongly predict opposition to race-targeted policies such as affirmative action and welfare spending, and voting behaviour.

Criticism

Critics argue the scale conflates prejudice with principled conservatism or beliefs about government, while defenders respond that the items predict racial policy views beyond ideology. The concept remains influential in studies of American voting and the racialization of politics in the Obama and Trump eras.
