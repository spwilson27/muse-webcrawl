Edward Lear (12 May 1812 – 29 January 1888) was an English artist, illustrator, musician, author and poet, now known largely for his literary nonsense in poetry and prose and especially his limericks, a form he popularised.

Art and travel

Lear began his career as an ornithological draughtsman, producing highly regarded illustrations of parrots for John Gould and others. Plagued by ill health, including epilepsy and chronic bronchitis, he travelled extensively around the Mediterranean — Italy, Greece, Albania, Egypt, India and Ceylon — supporting himself with watercolour landscape drawings and publishing illustrated travel journals. He gave drawing lessons to Queen Victoria in 1846.

Nonsense writings

His A Book of Nonsense (1846), a volume of limericks written for the grandchildren of the Earl of Derby, became a classic. Later works include Nonsense Songs, Stories, Botany, and Alphabets (1871), containing "The Owl and the Pussy-Cat", and Laughable Lyrics (1877). Lear settled in Sanremo, Italy, where he died in 1888, accompanied in his later years famously by his cat Foss.
