Laki or Lakagígar ("Laki craters") is a volcanic fissure system in southern Iceland, part of the Grímsvötn volcanic system within Vatnajökull National Park. It is renowned for one of the largest lava eruptions in recorded history.

1783–84 eruption

On 8 June 1783 a 25-kilometre fissure opened with some 130 craters, erupting in episodes over eight months. The eruption produced roughly 14 cubic kilometres of basalt lava and vast quantities of sulfur dioxide and hydrofluoric acid. In Iceland the resulting famine and livestock losses — known as the Móðuharðindin, the "Mist Hardships" — killed roughly a quarter of the population.

Global effects

The sulfuric haze spread across the Northern Hemisphere, contributing to crop failures, a severe winter in 1783–84, drought as far away as Egypt, and elevated mortality in Europe. The eruption is a benchmark case in the study of volcanism and climate.
