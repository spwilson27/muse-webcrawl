Iridium satellite constellation

The Iridium satellite constellation provides L-band voice and data coverage to satellite phones, pagers, and other transceivers over the entire surface of Earth. Iridium Communications owns and operates the constellation, which consists of 66 active satellites in low Earth orbit — a number chosen in the system's original design, which had envisaged 77 satellites and was named after the element iridium (atomic number 77).

System design

The satellites orbit at an altitude of about 780 km in near-polar orbits arranged in six planes. Unusually for satellite telephony, Iridium satellites relay calls to one another via inter-satellite links, so a signal can travel across the constellation to a ground station without requiring line of sight to a gateway. The system provides true global coverage, including the poles and the open oceans, and is widely used by maritime, aviation, military, and remote-industry users.

History

The first-generation constellation was launched in 1997–1998 by Motorola's Iridium SSC, which entered bankruptcy in 1999 before the system was rescued and relaunched commercially. Between 2017 and 2019, 75 second-generation Iridium NEXT satellites were launched on SpaceX Falcon 9 rockets, replacing the original fleet. Iridium satellites are also famous for "Iridium flares" — bright reflections of sunlight from the original satellites' polished antenna panels.
