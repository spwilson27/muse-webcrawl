Education For All

Education For All (EFA) was a global movement led by UNESCO, launched in 1990 at the World Conference on Education for All in Jomtien, Thailand, and aimed at providing quality basic education for all children, youth, and adults. The movement defined goals that were reaffirmed at the World Education Forum in Dakar, Senegal, in 2000, with a target date of 2015. Its agenda was later absorbed into the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 4 (quality education).

The six Dakar goals

  1. Expand and improve comprehensive early childhood care and education, especially for the most vulnerable children.
  2. Ensure that by 2015 all children, particularly girls and children in difficult circumstances, have access to free and compulsory primary education of good quality.
  3. Ensure that the learning needs of all young people and adults are met through equitable access to appropriate learning and life-skills programmes.
  4. Achieve a 50 percent improvement in levels of adult literacy by 2015, especially for women.
  5. Eliminate gender disparities in primary and secondary education by 2005, and achieve gender equality in education by 2015.
  6. Improve all aspects of the quality of education and ensure excellence so that recognized and measurable learning outcomes are achieved by all.

Monitoring and results

Progress was tracked in the annual EFA Global Monitoring Report, published by UNESCO from 2002. The reports documented major gains — tens of millions more children in school and falling out-of-school numbers — alongside persistent shortfalls in aid and learning quality. By the 2015 deadline, only about a third of countries had achieved all of the measurable goals, and none of the goals was met globally; roughly 58 million children of primary school age remained out of school.

Fast Track Initiative

The Education for All Fast Track Initiative, launched in 2002 to channel aid to countries with credible education plans, became the Global Partnership for Education in 2011 and remains the principal multilateral fund for education in low-income countries.

Successor framework

The Incheon Declaration adopted at the World Education Forum 2015 set the Education 2030 agenda, aligned with Sustainable Development Goal 4: "Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all."
