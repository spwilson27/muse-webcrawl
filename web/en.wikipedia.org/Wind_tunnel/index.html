Wind tunnel

A wind tunnel is a research tool used in aerodynamics to study the effects of air moving past solid objects. A fan or compressor drives air through a ducted test section where a stationary model is mounted; instruments measure forces, pressures and flow patterns, often visualised with smoke or tufts.

History

Francis Wenham built the first wind tunnel in 1871. The Wright brothers used a small tunnel in 1901 to test wing shapes before their first powered flight. Major facilities such as those of NASA Ames and Langley grew through the 20th century, including transonic, supersonic and hypersonic tunnels.

Types and measurements

Tunnels are classed by speed range (subsonic, transonic, supersonic, hypersonic) and by design (open or closed circuit). Similarity requires matching the Reynolds number and, at high speed, the Mach number between model and full scale. Wind tunnels are also used for cars, buildings, bridges, sports equipment and environmental studies, complementing computational fluid dynamics.
