The Tale of the Heike

The Tale of the Heike (Japanese: Heike Monogatari) is an epic account of the struggle between the Taira (Heike) and Minamoto (Genji) clans for control of Japan at the end of the 12th century, culminating in the Genpei War (1180–1185). Compiled in the centuries after the events it describes, it is one of the great works of medieval Japanese literature.

Content

The narrative opens with its famous lines on impermanence: "The sound of the Gion Shōja bells echoes the impermanence of all things." It follows the rise of the Taira under Taira no Kiyomori, their arrogance and fall, and the battles — Ichi-no-Tani, Yashima and the final naval battle of Dan-no-ura, where the child emperor Antoku drowned — that ended Taira rule and led to the establishment of the Kamakura shogunate.

Transmission

The tale was transmitted orally by blind monks, the biwa hōshi, who recited it to the accompaniment of the biwa lute. Many variant texts survive; the best known is the 1371 version associated with Akashi Kakuichi. Its themes of impermanence (mujō) have deeply shaped Japanese aesthetics and culture.
