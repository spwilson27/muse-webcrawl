Alex Steinweiss

Alex Steinweiss (March 24, 1917 – July 17, 2011) was an American graphic designer, widely credited with inventing album cover art. Before his work, phonograph records were sold in plain paper or cardboard sleeves, sometimes bound in book-like albums with generic covers.

Career

In 1939, Columbia Records hired the 22-year-old Steinweiss as its first art director. He persuaded the company to invest in illustrated covers for its releases, arguing that artwork would attract buyers in record shops. One of his first designs, for a collection of Rodgers and Hart songs (1940), depicted a theatre marquee; sales of the record reportedly rose dramatically, and the practice of illustrated album covers quickly spread across the industry.

Steinweiss designed thousands of covers for classical, jazz, and popular recordings, developing a distinctive hand-lettered style; his "Scrawl" lettering became so characteristic it was later issued as a typeface. When Columbia introduced the long-playing record in 1948, he also designed the paperboard LP jacket construction that became the industry standard.

Later life

Steinweiss left the record business in the 1970s as photography displaced illustration and age limits pushed him out of the industry, working afterward in film titles and ceramics before retiring to Sarasota, Florida. Interest in his work revived with retrospectives and books on album art, and in 2011 he died in Sarasota at the age of 94.
