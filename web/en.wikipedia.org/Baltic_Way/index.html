The Baltic Way or Baltic Chain (also known as the Chain of Freedom) was a peaceful political demonstration that occurred on 23 August 1989. Approximately two million people joined hands to form a human chain spanning about 600–675 kilometres (370–420 mi) across the three Baltic states of the Soviet Union — the Estonian SSR, the Latvian SSR, and the Lithuanian SSR. The chain connected the three capitals, Tallinn, Riga, and Vilnius, in a single unbroken line.

Background

The demonstration was held on the fiftieth anniversary of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 1939, whose secret protocols had assigned the Baltic states to the Soviet sphere of influence and preceded their occupation and annexation by the USSR in 1940. Organised by the popular fronts that had emerged during Mikhail Gorbachev's perestroika — Rahvarinne in Estonia, the Popular Front of Latvia, and Sąjūdis in Lithuania — the protest drew the world's attention to the Baltic demand that the pact and its consequences be publicly acknowledged and the annexation recognised as illegal.

The demonstration

At 7 p.m. local time on 23 August 1989, participants linked hands for fifteen minutes along an assigned route of roads and highways. Organisers mapped the chain in advance, assigning specific stretches to towns and organisations, and local radio stations broadcast instructions and signals. The event passed without incident and was widely reported internationally.

Aftermath and legacy

The Baltic Way dramatised the unity of the three nations' independence movements, part of the wider Singing Revolution. Within months the Soviet Congress of People's Deputies declared the secret protocols of the 1939 pact invalid. Lithuania declared the restoration of its independence on 11 March 1990, and Estonia and Latvia followed with effective independence in August 1991. In 2009 UNESCO inscribed the documentary heritage of the Baltic Way into its Memory of the World Register. The demonstration later inspired similar human chains, including the Catalan Way of 2013 and the Hong Kong Way of 2019.
