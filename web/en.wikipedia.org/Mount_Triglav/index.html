Triglav (pronounced [ˈtɾiːɡlau̯]; German: Terglau; Italian: Tricorno), with an elevation of 2,863.65 metres (9,395.2 ft), is the highest mountain in Slovenia and the highest peak of the Julian Alps. The mountain is the pre-eminent symbol of the Slovene nation. Its name means "three-headed", after its three peaks as seen from Bohinj, and it may also honour the Slavic god Triglav.

Triglav dominates the centre of Triglav National Park, Slovenia's only national park. The summit tower, the Aljaž Tower (Aljažev stolp), was erected in 1895 by the priest Jakob Aljaž, who bought the summit area to secure it for Slovene mountaineers. The mountain is depicted on Slovenia's coat of arms and flag, and on the national side of the Slovenian 50 euro cent coin.

History

The first recorded ascent of Triglav was achieved on 26 August 1778 by Luka Korošec, Matija Kos, Štefan Rožič and Lovrenc Willomitzer, on the initiative of the industrialist and polymath Sigmund Zois. During the Second World War the mountain was a symbolic site for Slovene partisans. It is traditionally said that every Slovene should climb Triglav at least once in their life, and first-time climbers are jokingly "baptised" with a rope at the summit.

Climbing

The most popular routes reach the summit from the Krma and Vrata valleys and from Bohinj. The north face, known as "The Wall" (Stena), is about 3 km wide and 1,000 m high and is a major alpine climbing venue. Mountain huts including Triglavski dom on Kredarica, below the summit, support climbers; the final approach follows a secured via ferrata along the narrow summit ridge.
