Simple Lie group

In mathematics, a simple Lie group is a connected non-abelian Lie group G which has no nontrivial connected normal subgroups. Equivalently, its Lie algebra is a simple Lie algebra: a non-abelian Lie algebra whose only ideals are itself and zero. Together with the one-dimensional group of rotations and translations, simple Lie groups form the building blocks from which more general Lie groups are assembled, much as simple groups do in finite group theory.

Classification

The classification of simple Lie algebras, completed by Wilhelm Killing and Élie Cartan, divides them into four infinite families and five exceptional cases. The classical families are the special linear groups, the orthogonal groups (in two series) and the symplectic groups, corresponding to the Dynkin diagrams An, Bn, Cn and Dn. The exceptional simple Lie groups are G2, F4, E6, E7 and E8, the last being the largest and most intricate, with a 248-dimensional adjoint representation.

Compact and noncompact forms

Each complex simple Lie algebra has a unique (up to isomorphism) compact real form; for example, the compact group SU(2) corresponds to the algebra of the rotation group. Noncompact "split" and intermediate real forms also exist, and their classification refines the picture considerably. A finitely covered version of any simple Lie group is determined by its Lie algebra together with its centre and fundamental group.

Role in physics

Simple Lie groups pervade theoretical physics: the gauge groups of the Standard Model, SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1), are built from simple factors; the Lorentz and Poincaré symmetry groups of relativity are closely related to simple groups; and exceptional groups such as E8 appear in string theory and grand unified theories. The classification of simple Lie groups is therefore a bridge between pure mathematics and fundamental physics.
