Orthogonal matrix

An orthogonal matrix is a real square matrix whose columns and rows are orthonormal vectors. Equivalently, a matrix Q is orthogonal if its transpose equals its inverse: QTQ = QQT = I, where I is the identity matrix.

Properties

The determinant of an orthogonal matrix is either +1 or −1. Orthogonal matrices preserve lengths and angles, so they represent rotations and reflections in Euclidean space. The set of all n×n orthogonal matrices forms the orthogonal group O(n); those with determinant +1 form the special orthogonal group SO(n).

Examples

Two-dimensional rotation matrices and permutation matrices are orthogonal. Orthogonal matrices are important in numerical linear algebra, including the QR decomposition, because computations with them are numerically stable.
