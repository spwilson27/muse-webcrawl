SRI International is an American nonprofit scientific research institute headquartered in Menlo Park, California. Founded in 1946 as the Stanford Research Institute, it became independent of Stanford University in 1970 and adopted its present name in 1977. SRI conducts contract research for government agencies and commercial clients in fields including computing, engineering, pharmaceuticals, education and economics.

Notable innovations

SRI is famous for Douglas Engelbart's Augmentation Research Center, whose 1968 "Mother of All Demos" introduced the computer mouse, hypertext and collaborative editing; SRI also hosted one of the first four nodes of the ARPANET in 1969. Other SRI creations include the robotic research that led to Shakey the Robot, the CALO project whose technology fed into Apple's Siri, the da Vinci surgical system (through spin-off Intuitive Surgical), and early work on the computer mouse, inkjet printing and magnetic recording.

Organisation

SRI employs several hundred researchers and has spun off dozens of companies. Its subsidiaries and affiliates have included the contract research organisation SRI Biosciences and the education research division SRI Education. In 2023–2024, SRI combined with Palo Alto Research Center (PARC) under a shared structure, bringing together two of Silicon Valley's historic research laboratories.
