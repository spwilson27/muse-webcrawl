Federal subjects of Russia

Russia is a federation divided into federal subjects (subyekty), its top-level political divisions. According to the constitution, the federation comprises republics, krais (territories), oblasts (provinces), cities of federal importance, an autonomous oblast and autonomous okrugs. Since 2014 Russia has claimed additional subjects in occupied Ukrainian territory, which are not internationally recognised.

Types

Republics are nominally home to a specific ethnic group and have their own constitutions and official languages. Oblasts and krais are more uniformly administered. Moscow, Saint Petersburg and Sevastopol (claimed) are cities of federal importance. Above the subjects sit eight federal districts, each headed by a presidential envoy, created in 2000.

Governance

Each subject has a head (usually a governor) and a regional legislature. Subjects send two representatives each to the Federation Council, the upper house of the Federal Assembly.
