The Liberator (newspaper)

TypeWeekly newspaper
FounderWilliam Lloyd Garrison
FoundedJanuary 1, 1831
CeasedDecember 29, 1865
HeadquartersBoston, Massachusetts

The Liberator was an American abolitionist newspaper founded by William Lloyd Garrison and Isaac Knapp and published weekly in Boston from 1831 to 1865. It advocated the immediate emancipation of all enslaved people in the United States.

History

Garrison announced in the first issue that he would not retreat from his demand for immediate abolition, writing that he would be "as harsh as truth, and as uncompromising as justice". The paper's motto was "Our country is the world — our countrymen are mankind."

Influence

Although its circulation was modest — around 3,000, with many subscribers free Black readers — The Liberator was central to the abolitionist movement. It also supported women's rights; Garrison defended women's participation in the movement. Publication ended after the Thirteenth Amendment abolished slavery in 1865.
