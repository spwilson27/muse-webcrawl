Anuria

SpecialtyNephrology, urology
SymptomsAbsence or near-absence of urine output
CausesAcute kidney injury, urinary obstruction, severe dehydration

Anuria is the lack of urine production, defined clinically as the passage of less than 100 millilitres of urine in a day. It is a serious sign of kidney failure or of a complete obstruction of the urinary tract, and requires urgent medical evaluation.

Causes

Causes of anuria are commonly divided into prerenal (reduced blood flow to the kidneys, as in shock or severe dehydration), renal (intrinsic kidney damage, such as acute tubular necrosis or glomerulonephritis), and postrenal (obstruction of urine outflow, such as from kidney stones, an enlarged prostate, or tumours).

Differential diagnosis

Anuria must be distinguished from oliguria, in which urine output is reduced but not absent (typically 100–400 mL per day), and from urinary retention, in which the kidneys produce urine but the bladder cannot empty.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the underlying cause: restoring blood volume and perfusion in prerenal cases, relieving obstruction with catheterisation or surgery in postrenal cases, and supportive care — sometimes including dialysis — in intrinsic renal failure.
