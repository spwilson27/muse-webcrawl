United Ireland

The island of Ireland, currently divided between the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland.

A united Ireland is a proposed single sovereign state comprising the whole island of Ireland, which is currently politically divided between the Republic of Ireland (a sovereign state) and Northern Ireland (part of the United Kingdom). The aspiration to Irish unity is a central tenet of Irish nationalism, while unionists oppose it.

History

Ireland was partitioned by the Government of Ireland Act 1920; the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 created the Irish Free State in the south while Northern Ireland remained in the UK. The Republic's constitution claimed the whole island until 1998, when, as part of the Good Friday Agreement, the claim was replaced with an aspiration to unity achievable only with the consent of majorities in both jurisdictions (the principle of consent).

Border poll

Under the Good Friday Agreement, the Secretary of State for Northern Ireland must call a referendum (a "border poll") if it appears likely that a majority would vote for a united Ireland. Debate intensified after Brexit, which Northern Ireland voted against, and after Sinn Féin became the largest party in Northern Ireland in 2022.

Models and debate

Proposed models range from a unitary state to a federal Ireland, potentially with continued devolution for Northern Ireland. Debates focus on costs and subvention, healthcare systems, flags and symbols, minority protections, and the form of any new constitution.
