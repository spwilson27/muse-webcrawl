Automaton

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A clockwork automaton

An automaton (plural: automata) is a relatively self-operating machine, or control mechanism designed to automatically follow a sequence of operations, or respond to predetermined instructions. Some automata, such as bellstrikers in mechanical clocks, are designed to give the illusion of being simpler or smaller; others, notably the elaborate mechanical toys and androids of the 18th century, were made to resemble humans or animals.

History

Automata were described in ancient sources: Hero of Alexandria designed programmable temple devices driven by water and weights, and medieval Islamic engineers such as the Banu Musa brothers and al-Jazari built water clocks and programmable automata. In the 18th century, craftsmen like Jacques de Vaucanson (builder of the Digesting Duck, 1739) and Pierre Jaquet-Droz (the Writer, the Musician, the Draughtsman) produced celebrated mechanical figures. Wolfgang von Kempelen's Mechanical Turk (1770) purported to play chess but concealed a human operator.

In computing

The concept underlies automata theory in computer science, where an automaton is an abstract self-propelled computing device — finite automata, pushdown automata, and Turing machines form the Chomsky hierarchy of computational models. Conway's Game of Life popularised cellular automata.
