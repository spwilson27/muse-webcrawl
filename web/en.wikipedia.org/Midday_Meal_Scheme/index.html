Midday Meal Scheme

The Midday Meal Scheme, officially PM-POSHAN (Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman) since 2021, is a school meal programme in India designed to better the nutritional status of school-age children nation-wide. The programme supplies free lunches on working days for children in government primary and upper primary schools, government-aided schools, local body schools, and other eligible institutions.

School meal provision in India has roots in the Madras Presidency in the 1920s and was expanded greatly by the state of Tamil Nadu in the 1960s and 1980s under K. Kamaraj and M. G. Ramachandran. The national programme was launched in 1995, and following a landmark Supreme Court order of 2001, states were directed to provide cooked midday meals in all government-assisted primary schools.

Objectives and impact

The scheme aims to improve child nutrition, increase school enrolment and attendance, reduce classroom hunger, and foster social equity by having children of different castes eat together. It is among the largest school feeding programmes in the world, reaching over 100 million children, and it also provides employment to millions of cooks and helpers, most of them women. The programme has faced challenges including food quality, hygiene incidents, and funding disputes between central and state governments.
