GCHQ

Government Communications Headquarters
Founded: 1 November 1919 (as GC&CS)
Headquarters: Cheltenham, England
Parent: Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office
Motto: Diverted Traffic (unofficial historical)

Government Communications Headquarters, commonly known as GCHQ, is an intelligence and security organisation responsible for providing signals intelligence (SIGINT) and information assurance to the government and armed forces of the United Kingdom. It is part of the UK intelligence community alongside MI5 and MI6.

History

GCHQ descends from the Government Code and Cypher School (GC&CS), formed in 1919, whose wartime home at Bletchley Park became famous for breaking the German Enigma ciphers. After the Second World War the organisation was renamed GCHQ (1946) and moved to Cheltenham in the 1950s. In 2003 staff moved into a distinctive circular headquarters nicknamed "The Doughnut".

Role

GCHQ collects and analyses communications intelligence, protects government communications and information systems, and operates the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), founded in 2016, which handles cyber security incidents and publishes public guidance. The Director of GCHQ reports to the Foreign Secretary.
