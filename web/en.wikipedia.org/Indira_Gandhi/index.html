Died31 October 1984, New Delhi (assassinated)
OfficePrime Minister of India, 1966–1977 and 1980–1984

Indira Priyadarshini Gandhi (1917–1984) was an Indian politician who served as Prime Minister of India from 1966 to 1977 and again from 1980 until her assassination in 1984. The daughter of Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first prime minister, she remains the only woman to have held the office and one of the most powerful and controversial figures in Indian history.

Early life and rise

Gandhi grew up in the freedom movement, studied at Somerville College, Oxford, and married Feroze Gandhi in 1942. She served as her father's unofficial aide, became Congress president in 1959, and joined Lal Bahadur Shastri's cabinet as Minister of Information and Broadcasting. On Shastri's death in 1966 she succeeded him as prime minister.

Prime ministership

Her governments nationalised major banks (1969), abolished princely privy purses, and won a landslide in 1971 on the slogan Garibi Hatao ("Abolish Poverty"). She led India to victory in the 1971 war that created Bangladesh. In 1975, after a court found her guilty of electoral malpractice, she declared a state of Emergency, suspending civil liberties; Congress lost the 1977 election, but she returned to power in 1980.

Death and legacy

In June 1984 she ordered Operation Blue Star against militants in the Golden Temple at Amritsar. On 31 October 1984 she was assassinated by her Sikh bodyguards, triggering anti-Sikh riots. Her son Rajiv Gandhi succeeded her. Her legacy combines centralisation of power and authoritarian episodes with assertive foreign policy and mass electoral appeal.
