Georges Prosper Remi (22 May 1907 – 3 March 1983), known by the pen name Hergé — from the French pronunciation of his reversed initials, R.G. — was a Belgian cartoonist. He is best remembered for creating The Adventures of Tintin, the series of bande dessinée albums that ran from 1929 to 1976 and became one of the most popular and influential European comics of the twentieth century.

Life and career

Hergé was born in Etterbeek, Brussels, and showed an early talent for drawing. After leaving school he joined the Catholic newspaper Le Vingtième Siècle, where in its children's supplement, Le Petit Vingtième, he created the boy reporter Tintin and his fox terrier Snowy, debuting in January 1929 with Tintin in the Land of the Soviets. Early albums were crude and relied on stereotypes that Hergé later regretted; with The Blue Lotus (1936), informed by his friendship with the Chinese art student Zhang Chongren, his work matured into meticulously researched, richly detailed storytelling. Other classic albums include The Secret of the Unicorn, Prisoners of the Sun, Destination Moon, The Calculus Affair and Tintin in Tibet (1960), often regarded as his most personal work.

War and controversy

During the German occupation of Belgium, Hergé continued publishing Tintin in the collaborationist newspaper Le Soir, a decision that led to investigations and lasting criticism after the war, though he was ultimately cleared and resumed work, founding Tintin magazine in 1946 and Studios Hergé in 1950. Elements of the early albums, notably Tintin in the Congo, have been widely criticised for colonialist and racist depictions; Hergé redrew and revised several albums for later editions.

Style and legacy

Hergé pioneered the ligne claire ("clear line") style — strong, even lines, flat colours, highly detailed backgrounds and minimal shading — which influenced generations of European comics artists. He died in Brussels in 1983, leaving the final album, Tintin and Alph-Art, unfinished. The Hergé Museum opened in Louvain-la-Neuve in 2009, and Tintin's adventures, translated into more than 70 languages with sales exceeding 200 million copies, remain a landmark of the medium.
