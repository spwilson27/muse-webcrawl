12 Hours of Sebring

The 12 Hours of Sebring is an annual motorsport endurance race for sports cars held at Sebring International Raceway, on the site of the former Hendricks Army Airfield, in Sebring, Florida. First held in 1952, it is one of the most prestigious endurance races in the world, forming — with the 24 Hours of Le Mans and the 24 Hours of Daytona — the informal "Triple Crown" of endurance racing.

History

The first race, in 1952, was won by Harry Gray and Larry Kulok in a Frazer Nash. The event grew to become a round of the World Sportscar Championship, attracting factory teams from Ferrari, Porsche, Ford and others. American Alec Ulmann founded and promoted the race, building it around the runways and service roads of the wartime airfield.

After a one-year cancellation in 1974 during the fuel crisis, the race became a fixture of the IMSA GT Championship and later the American Le Mans Series and the IMSA SportsCar Championship. Since 2014 it has run under IMSA sanction as part of the WeatherTech SportsCar Championship, traditionally held in mid-March as the second round of the season after Daytona.

The circuit

Sebring is famed for its rough, bumpy surface — a mixture of concrete runways dating from the Second World War and later asphalt — which brutally tests suspension and reliability. The 3.74-mile (6.02 km) circuit has 17 turns. The race runs from mid-morning to late evening, finishing in darkness, and is renowned for its party atmosphere, particularly at the hairpin known as Green Park.

Records

Porsche is the most successful manufacturer at Sebring, and drivers such as Tom Kristensen hold multiple overall wins. Manufacturers including Audi (dominant in the 2000s), Ferrari, Nissan and Cadillac have all claimed overall victories.
