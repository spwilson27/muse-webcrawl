Vande Bharat Express

OperatorIndian Railways
ManufacturerIntegral Coach Factory, Chennai
Entered service15 February 2019
First routeNew Delhi – Varanasi
Design speedup to 180 km/h (operating speeds typically up to 160 km/h)

The Vande Bharat Express is a family of semi-high-speed, self-propelled electric multiple unit (EMU) train services operated by Indian Railways. Designed and built in India at the Integral Coach Factory in Chennai under the Make in India initiative, the trains are the flagship of Indian Railways' modernisation programme and run on dozens of routes connecting major cities.

Development and versions

The first trainset, originally known as Train 18, was launched on the New Delhi–Varanasi route on 15 February 2019. A refined second version (Vande Bharat 2.0) entered service in 2022 with improved seating, and a third version followed with further upgrades. The distributed-traction EMU design — with motors spread along the train rather than a separate locomotive — gives rapid acceleration. Trains offer air-conditioned chair-car and executive-class seating, automatic doors, onboard catering, Wi-Fi and infotainment, and safety systems including the Kavach anti-collision technology on newer sets.

Network and impact

From the initial service, the network expanded rapidly, with scores of Vande Bharat services introduced across Indian states by the mid-2020s, typically covering intercity journeys of a few hundred kilometres in substantially less time than conventional express trains. Shorter regional variants under the "Vande Metro" and sleeper versions for overnight routes have also been developed. The programme is frequently cited as a symbol of domestic manufacturing capability and of the upgrading of India's rail infrastructure.
