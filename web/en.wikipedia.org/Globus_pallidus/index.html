Globus pallidus

The globus pallidus (Latin for "pale globe"), also known as the paleostriatum or dorsal pallidum, is a major component of the basal ganglia in the brain. It consists of two segments, the external segment (GPe) and the internal segment (GPi). Together with the putamen, it forms the lentiform nucleus. The globus pallidus plays a central role in the regulation of voluntary movement.

Structure

The globus pallidus lies medial to the putamen and lateral to the internal capsule. Its two segments are separated by a thin lamina of white matter. The principal neurons of the globus pallidus are large GABAergic cells with extensive dendritic arbors that receive afferents from the striatum and the subthalamic nucleus.

Function

The internal segment, together with the substantia nigra pars reticulata, forms the principal output nucleus of the basal ganglia. Its tonically active inhibitory neurons project to the thalamus and brainstem; phasic decreases in their firing disinhibit thalamic targets and thereby facilitate movement. The external segment is an intrinsic relay within the indirect pathway, reciprocally connected with the subthalamic nucleus. Dysfunction of globus pallidus circuits is implicated in Parkinson's disease, Huntington's disease and dystonia, and the GPi is a common target for deep brain stimulation.
