Hugo Gernsback

Hugo Gernsback
Born: August 16, 1884, Luxembourg City, Luxembourg
Died: August 19, 1967, New York City, United States
Occupation: Inventor, writer, magazine publisher
Known for: Founding Amazing Stories; coining "science fiction"

Hugo Gernsback (born Hugo Gernsbacher, 1884–1967) was a Luxembourgish-American inventor, writer and publisher best known for founding Amazing Stories in 1926, the first magazine devoted entirely to science fiction. He is regarded, together with H. G. Wells and Jules Verne, as one of the "fathers of science fiction", and the annual Hugo Awards presented at Worldcon are named in his honour.

Early life and radio publishing

Gernsback emigrated from Luxembourg to the United States in 1904. He founded the Electro Importing Company, selling radio parts and publishing catalogues, and in 1908 began Modern Electrics, the first magazine about radio and electronics, followed by The Electrical Experimenter (later Science and Invention). His magazines mixed technical articles with futuristic fiction, including his own serialised novel Ralph 124C 41+ (1911–12), which predicted radar, television and other technologies.

Science fiction

In April 1926 Gernsback launched Amazing Stories, filling it initially with reprints of Verne, Wells and Edgar Allan Poe alongside new work. He coined the term "scientifiction", later settling on "science fiction", and insisted stories should be grounded in scientific fact and prophecy. Losing control of the magazine in a 1929 bankruptcy, he founded rival titles including Science Wonder Stories. Gernsback also held dozens of patents and pioneered science broadcasting, but his low pay rates and slow payment made him a controversial figure among writers.

Legacy

Gernsback was Guest of Honour at the 1960 Worldcon and was inducted into the Science Fiction Hall of Fame. The crater Gernsback on the Moon is named after him. While the literary quality of his magazines has been debated, his creation of a dedicated market and fan culture for science fiction was foundational to the genre.
