The Queensferry Crossing is a road bridge in Scotland spanning the Firth of Forth, alongside the Forth Road Bridge and the Forth Bridge. With a length of 2.7 kilometres, it is the longest three-tower, cable-stayed bridge in the world. It carries the M90 motorway across the Forth between Edinburgh and Fife.

Background

The crossing was built to replace the Forth Road Bridge (1964), whose main cables were found to be weakening due to corrosion. Transport Scotland procured the project as the Forth Replacement Crossing, with a budget reduced to about £1.3 billion.

Construction and opening

Construction began in 2011. The bridge features three towers up to 207 metres tall and a central cable-stayed span system with crossing cables that improve stability. It was opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 4 September 2017, having opened to traffic on 30 August 2017 — 53 years to the day after she opened the neighbouring road bridge.

Use

The Queensferry Crossing carries motorway traffic, while the old Forth Road Bridge was repurposed as a public transport corridor for buses, taxis, cyclists, and pedestrians.
