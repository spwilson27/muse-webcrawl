Durrington Walls

Durrington Walls is a large Neolithic henge enclosure in Wiltshire, England, located about 3 kilometres northeast of Stonehenge within the Stonehenge World Heritage Site. With a diameter of roughly 500 metres, it is one of the largest henge monuments in Britain.

History

The site was occupied around 2600–2400 BC, contemporary with the main building phase of Stonehenge. Excavations, particularly those of the Stonehenge Riverside Project in the 2000s, revealed the remains of houses, suggesting that Durrington Walls hosted a large seasonal settlement of the people who built Stonehenge.

Features

The henge consists of a circular ditch and bank enclosing several timber circles, including the Southern Circle, whose post holes were excavated in 1967. A broad avenue led from the site to the River Avon, mirroring the Stonehenge Avenue at the other end of the landscape.

Finds of pig bones and pottery indicate large-scale feasting, particularly around midwinter, supporting the interpretation of the site as a settlement for workers and celebrants.
