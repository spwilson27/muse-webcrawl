Rite of passage

A rite of passage is a ceremony or ritual marking an individual's transition from one stage of life or social status to another. The concept was popularised by the French ethnographer and folklorist Arnold van Gennep, whose 1909 work Les Rites de Passage structured such rituals into three phases: separation, transition (liminality), and incorporation.

Structure

Van Gennep proposed that rites of passage consist of three sub-rites. Separation rites detach the individual from their former status; liminal or transitional rites place them in an ambiguous in-between state; and incorporation rites admit them into their new status. The anthropologist Victor Turner later expanded the study of liminality, emphasising the sense of communitas shared by those undergoing the transition together.

Examples

Common rites of passage include birth and naming ceremonies, puberty initiations, coming-of-age celebrations such as the bar and bat mitzvah, the quinceañera, and the Japanese seijin-shiki; marriage; and funerals. In many societies, initiation into religious orders, military organisations, universities, or professions is also marked by formal rites.
