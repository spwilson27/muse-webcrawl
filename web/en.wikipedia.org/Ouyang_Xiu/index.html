Ouyang Xiu

Ouyang Xiu (1007 – 22 September 1072), courtesy name Yongshu, was a Chinese historian, essayist, poet, and politician of the Song dynasty. He is counted among the Eight Great Prose Masters of the Tang and Song and was a central figure in the classical prose movement.

Life

Born in Mianzhou (in modern Sichuan) and raised in poverty after his father's early death, Ouyang passed the imperial examinations in 1030 and rose through a series of official posts. He supported the reform efforts of Fan Zhongyan and was repeatedly demoted and exiled for his outspoken memorials. As an examination examiner in 1057 he championed younger writers including Su Shi, helping launch their careers.

Works

Ouyang compiled the New Book of Tang (Xin Tang Shu) with Song Qi and single-handedly wrote the New History of the Five Dynasties, notable for its moral commentary. His essays, such as "The Old Drunkard's Pavilion" (Zuiweng Ting Ji), and his ci poetry are classics of Chinese literature. His pen name "Old Drunkard" (Zuiweng) and later "Retired Scholar of Six Ones" reflect his literary persona. He also wrote extensively on epigraphy, collecting and analyzing ancient inscriptions.
