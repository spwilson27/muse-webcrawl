Dynamics

Dynamics is the branch of classical mechanics concerned with the study of forces and their effects on motion. Together with kinematics, which describes motion without regard to its causes, it forms the subject often called dynamics in physics and engineering.

Principles

Classical dynamics is built on Newton's laws of motion, relating the net force on a body to its acceleration. Central concepts include mass, momentum, torque, angular momentum, work and energy. Formulations by Lagrange and Hamilton recast dynamics in powerful generalized forms used throughout modern physics.

Subfields

Important areas include rigid-body dynamics, fluid dynamics, structural dynamics, vehicle dynamics and analytical dynamics. The term "dynamics" is also used more broadly, as in musical dynamics (loudness), system dynamics and stellar dynamics.

The word derives from the Greek dynamis, meaning power, and the modern usage was advanced by Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz.
