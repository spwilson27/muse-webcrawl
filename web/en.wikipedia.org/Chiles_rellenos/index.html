Chiles rellenos

Chiles rellenos (Spanish for "stuffed peppers") is a dish in Mexican cuisine that consists of whole poblano chiles (or, in the United States, sometimes Anaheim, pasilla, or jalapeño peppers) stuffed with cheese, coated in an egg batter, and fried. The dish is most closely associated with the city of Puebla, where it is traditionally said to have been created in the early nineteenth century.

Preparation

The peppers are roasted until their skins blister, then peeled, slit open, and seeded. The traditional filling is a melting cheese such as queso Chihuahua or Oaxaca cheese; a common alternative is picadillo, a spiced mixture of shredded meat, dried fruits, and nuts. The stuffed chiles are dredged in flour, dipped in a beaten-egg batter — the whites are whipped separately to give the coating its characteristic puffiness — and fried in oil until golden. They are usually served in a mild tomato sauce.

History

Stuffed peppers appear in many cuisines, but the Mexican chile relleno is generally traced to Puebla in the colonial period, when convent cooks combined indigenous chiles with European techniques of battering and frying. Puebla's most famous variation, chiles en nogada, is dressed with a walnut cream sauce and pomegranate seeds and is served seasonally around Mexican Independence Day in September.

Variations

  • Chiles en nogada — picadillo-filled poblanos in walnut sauce, garnished with pomegranate and parsley to evoke the Mexican flag.
  • Chile relleno burrito — a flour tortilla wrapped around a chile relleno, popular in the southwestern United States.
  • Jalapeños rellenos — smaller, spicier versions often served as appetizers.
