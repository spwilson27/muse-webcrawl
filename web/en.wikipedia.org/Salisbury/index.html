Salisbury

Salisbury is a cathedral city and civil parish in Wiltshire, England. It lies where the rivers Avon, Nadder and Bourne meet, about 13 km from the prehistoric monument of Stonehenge. The city is best known for Salisbury Cathedral, an early example of English Gothic architecture whose spire, at 123 metres (404 ft), is the tallest church spire in the United Kingdom.

History

The earlier settlement stood at the Iron Age hill fort of Old Sarum, which became a Norman stronghold and cathedral site. The cathedral was moved to the present site in the Avon valley in 1220, and the planned city of New Sarum grew around it on a grid of streets divided by water channels. The cathedral, built between 1220 and 1258, holds one of the four surviving original copies of Magna Carta and the world's oldest working mechanical clock (1386).

Geography and economy

Salisbury sits on the edge of Salisbury Plain, a large chalk plateau used extensively for military training. The city functions as a market and service centre for south Wiltshire, with tourism, retail and the nearby defence establishments at Porton Down and Bulford among its economic mainstays.

2018 poisoning incident

The city gained worldwide attention in March 2018 when former Russian spy Sergei Skripal and his daughter Yulia were poisoned with a Novichok nerve agent, an incident the British government attributed to Russia and which triggered mass diplomatic expulsions.
