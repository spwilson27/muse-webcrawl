List of Egyptian dynasties

The dynasties of ancient Egypt are the ruling families into which Egyptologists divide the roughly three thousand years of pharaonic history. The division derives from the Aegyptiaca, a history of Egypt written in Greek in the 3rd century BCE by the priest Manetho, who grouped Egypt's kings into thirty (later thirty-one) dynasties. Modern scholars further group the dynasties into "kingdoms" (periods of strong central government) and "intermediate periods" of fragmentation.

The kingdoms

The Old Kingdom (3rd–6th dynasties) was the age of the pyramid builders: Djoser's Step Pyramid (3rd dynasty) and the Great Pyramid of Khufu at Giza (4th dynasty) date to this era. After a period of decentralisation, Mentuhotep II of the 11th dynasty reunified Egypt, beginning the Middle Kingdom, whose 12th dynasty (Amenemhat and Senusret kings) is regarded as a classical age of literature and administration. The Second Intermediate Period saw the Hyksos (15th dynasty) rule the north from Avaris. Ahmose I expelled them and founded the 18th dynasty, opening the New Kingdom empire: under Thutmose III, Amenhotep III, and the Ramesside kings of the 19th and 20th dynasties, Egypt ruled an empire stretching from Nubia to the Euphrates. The 18th dynasty also includes Akhenaten's religious revolution and the reigns of Tutankhamun and Hatshepsut.

Foreign rule and the Late Period

The Third Intermediate Period saw Egypt divided between Libyan-descended kings, Tanite and Bubastite dynasties (21st–24th), and the Kushite 25th dynasty from Nubia (Piye, Taharqa), which reunified the country before Assyrian invasions. The Saite 26th dynasty reunified Egypt again in the Late Period, followed by two periods of Persian rule (27th and 31st dynasties, as satrapies of the Achaemenid Empire) interrupted by native 28th–30th dynasties. Alexander the Great conquered Egypt in 332 BCE; after his death the Ptolemaic dynasty—counted separately from Manetho's thirty dynasties—ruled until the death of Cleopatra VII in 30 BCE, when Egypt became a Roman province.
