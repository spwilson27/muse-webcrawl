Pancasila (Indonesian pronunciation: [pantʃaˈsila]) is the official, foundational philosophical theory of Indonesia. The name is formed from two words originally derived from Sanskrit: pañca, meaning "five", and sīla, meaning "principles" or "precepts". It is composed of five principles that are held to be inseparable and interrelated, and it serves as the ideological basis of the Indonesian state, enshrined in the preamble to the Constitution of Indonesia of 1945.

The five principles

The five principles of Pancasila, in the order promulgated in the constitution, are:

  1. Belief in the One and Only God (Ketuhanan Yang Maha Esa)
  2. Just and civilized humanity (Kemanusiaan yang adil dan beradab)
  3. The unity of Indonesia (Persatuan Indonesia)
  4. Democracy guided by the inner wisdom in the unanimity arising out of deliberations among representatives (Kerakyatan yang dipimpin oleh hikmat kebijaksanaan dalam permusyawaratan/perwakilan)
  5. Social justice for all of the people of Indonesia (Keadilan sosial bagi seluruh rakyat Indonesia)

History

The principles were first articulated by Sukarno in a speech delivered on 1 June 1945 to the Investigating Committee for Preparatory Work for Independence (BPUPK), as Indonesian nationalists prepared for independence from Dutch colonial rule and the end of the Japanese occupation. Sukarno presented Pancasila as a synthesis intended to unite the archipelago's diverse religious, ethnic and political groups. A committee of nine subsequently drafted the Jakarta Charter, whose wording formed the basis of the constitutional preamble; a controversial clause obliging Muslims to follow sharia law was removed before independence was proclaimed on 17 August 1945, in the interest of national unity.

Symbolism

Each principle is represented by a symbol on the shield borne by the Garuda Pancasila, Indonesia's national emblem: a star for belief in God, a chain for humanity, a banyan tree for unity, the head of a banteng (wild bull) for democracy, and rice and cotton for social justice. A chain of gold in the background links the symbols, expressing the unity of the five principles.

Role in Indonesian politics

Under President Suharto's New Order regime (1966–1998), Pancasila was heavily promoted through mandatory indoctrination programmes such as the P4 courses, and it was used as an ideological instrument to suppress dissent; all political and mass organisations were required to adopt Pancasila as their sole ideological basis. Since the reform era began in 1998, its role has been debated more openly, but it remains the foundational philosophy of the state and a touchstone of Indonesian national identity. Commemoration of Pancasila Day is held on 1 June.
