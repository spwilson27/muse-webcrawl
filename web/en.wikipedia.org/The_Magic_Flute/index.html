The Magic Flute (German: Die Zauberflöte, pronounced [diː ˈtsaʊbɐˌfløːtə]), K. 620, is an opera in two acts by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart to a German libretto by Emanuel Schikaneder. The work is in the form of a Singspiel, a popular form during the time it was composed that included both singing and spoken dialogue. The work premiered on 30 September 1791 at Schikaneder's theatre, the Freihaus-Theater auf der Wieden in Vienna, just two months before the composer's premature death. It was an outstanding success from its first performances, and remains a staple of the opera repertory.

Roles

The principal roles are Tamino, a prince; Pamina, daughter of the Queen of the Night; Papageno, a bird-catcher; the Queen of the Night; Sarastro, high priest of Isis and Osiris; Monostatos, a Moor in Sarastro's service; and Papagena. The Queen of the Night's famous coloratura aria "Der Hölle Rache kocht in meinem Herzen" ("Hell's vengeance boils in my heart") is among the most demanding in the soprano repertoire, reaching F6.

Synopsis

Prince Tamino, pursued by a serpent, is rescued by three ladies in the service of the Queen of the Night. The bird-catcher Papageno claims credit for the rescue. The Queen commissions Tamino to rescue her daughter Pamina from the supposedly evil Sarastro, giving him a magic flute and Papageno a set of magic bells. Tamino learns that Sarastro is in fact a wise and benevolent priest, and that the Queen is the true villain. After trials of silence, fire, and water, Tamino and Pamina are united, and Papageno finds his Papagena.

Composition and premiere

Mozart conducted the premiere himself; Schikaneder played Papageno. The opera draws on fairy-tale sources, Egyptian and Masonic imagery, and Enlightenment ideals. Many scholars have noted Masonic symbolism in the work — Mozart and Schikaneder were both Freemasons — including the prominence of the number three (three ladies, three boys, three temples).

Legacy

The Magic Flute was the last opera Mozart completed. It has been continuously in the repertory since 1791 and is frequently among the most performed operas worldwide. Ingmar Bergman's 1975 film adaptation Trollflöjten is widely admired.
