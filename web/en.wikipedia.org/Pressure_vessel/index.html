Pressure vessel

A pressure vessel is a container designed to hold gases or liquids at a pressure substantially different from the ambient pressure. Pressure vessels are used throughout industry—in chemical plants, refineries, power stations, and water treatment—and in everyday applications such as diving cylinders, compressed air receivers, and domestic water heaters.

Design and construction

Most pressure vessels are spherical or cylindrical with hemispherical or ellipsoidal end caps (heads), shapes that distribute internal pressure stresses efficiently. They are typically fabricated from carbon or stainless steel by welding, and must be designed with adequate wall thickness to resist the hoop and longitudinal stresses generated by internal pressure. Because failure of a pressure vessel can be catastrophic, their design, fabrication, inspection, and testing are governed by strict codes, most notably the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code in North America and the Pressure Equipment Directive in Europe. Safety features include pressure relief valves, rupture discs, and periodic hydrostatic testing.

Applications

Common examples include steam boilers, autoclaves, reactor vessels in nuclear and chemical plants, LPG storage bullets, hydraulic accumulators, and the composite overwrapped pressure vessels used to store high-pressure gas aboard spacecraft.
