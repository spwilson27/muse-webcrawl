American Realism

American Realism was an early 20th-century movement in art, music, and literature that rejected Romanticism and sought to depict contemporary social realities and the lives of ordinary people. In painting it is associated with the Ashcan School and artists such as Robert Henri, John Sloan, and George Bellows, who portrayed gritty urban scenes in New York City.

In literature

Literary realism in America grew after the Civil War through writers such as William Dean Howells, Mark Twain, and Henry James, who depicted middle-class life and regional speech faithfully, and later naturalists like Stephen Crane, Theodore Dreiser, and Frank Norris, who stressed the forces of heredity and environment.

In painting

Thomas Eakins pioneered realistic depiction in American art, and the Ashcan School's "Eight" exhibited in 1908 with scenes of street life, boxing matches, and tenements. Edward Hopper became the movement's best-known later figure, painting scenes of American loneliness and modern life.
