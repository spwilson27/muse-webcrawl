COVID-19 pandemic in Victoria

The COVID-19 pandemic in Victoria was part of the worldwide pandemic of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). The Australian state of Victoria experienced some of the country's most significant outbreaks and longest lockdowns, particularly in its capital, Melbourne.

First wave

The first confirmed case in Victoria, and in Australia, was reported on 25 January 2020, when a man who had returned from Wuhan, China, tested positive. In March 2020, the state government declared a state of emergency, closed non-essential services, and imposed stay-at-home restrictions as part of the nationwide first-wave response.

Second wave and Melbourne lockdown

From late June 2020, Victoria experienced a severe second wave linked to breaches in the hotel quarantine program for returning travellers. Cases rose sharply in Melbourne, prompting the reimposition of stage three restrictions on metropolitan Melbourne and Mitchell Shire on 8 July 2020. On 2 August, a state of disaster was declared and stage four restrictions were imposed, including a nightly curfew, a five-kilometre travel limit, and closure of most retail. The lockdown lasted 111 days in total before restrictions were eased in late October 2020 as cases reached zero.

Later outbreaks and vaccination

Victoria experienced further lockdowns in 2021 in response to outbreaks of the Alpha and Delta variants, including an extended lockdown in Melbourne from August to October 2021 that ended as vaccination coverage rose. The arrival of the Omicron variant in late 2021 produced the largest case numbers of the pandemic, though with lower rates of severe disease among the vaccinated population.

Impact

The pandemic had major effects on Victoria's economy, education system, and public life. Melbourne became known internationally for the cumulative length of its lockdowns, among the longest in the world. Excess mortality, mental health effects, and the performance of the hotel quarantine system were subjects of public inquiries and debate.
