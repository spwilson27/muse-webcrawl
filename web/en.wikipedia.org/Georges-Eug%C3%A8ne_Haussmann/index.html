Georges-Eugène Haussmann (27 March 1809 – 11 January 1891), commonly known as Baron Haussmann, was a French official who served as prefect of the Seine department under Emperor Napoleon III and directed the great renovation of Paris between 1853 and 1870.

Renovation of Paris

Haussmann demolished much of the medieval city and cut broad, tree-lined boulevards through its dense quarters, created new parks such as the Bois de Boulogne, built modern sewers and water supplies, and imposed the uniform stone facades and heights that still define Parisian architecture. The works improved sanitation and circulation but displaced many poor residents and incurred enormous debts.

Downfall and legacy

Attacked for his extravagant financing, Haussmann was dismissed in 1870, months before the fall of the Second Empire. Despite the controversy, his transformation of Paris became a model of modern urban planning studied around the world, and his name remains synonymous with the city's boulevards.
