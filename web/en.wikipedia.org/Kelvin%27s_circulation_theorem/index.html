Kelvin's circulation theorem

In fluid mechanics, Kelvin's circulation theorem, published by William Thomson, 1st Baron Kelvin in 1869, states that in an inviscid, barotropic fluid subject to conservative body forces, the circulation around a closed curve moving with the fluid remains constant with time. Formally, DΓ/Dt = 0, where Γ is the circulation and D/Dt the material derivative.

Statement and proof

The theorem follows from integrating the Euler equations along the moving contour: the pressure term vanishes for a barotropic fluid and the body-force term vanishes for a conservative force field. It underlies results on vortex motion, including the persistence of vortex lines and the irrotational character of flow started from rest.

Limitations

Viscosity, baroclinicity (as in the sea breeze) and non-conservative forces such as the Coriolis force in a rotating frame can change circulation, as described by Bjerknes's extension of the theorem.
