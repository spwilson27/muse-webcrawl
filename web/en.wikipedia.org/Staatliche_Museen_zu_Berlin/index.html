The Staatliche Museen zu Berlin (Berlin State Museums) are a group of museums in Berlin, Germany, administered by the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation (Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz) and jointly funded by the federal government and the German states. Together their collections span art and cultural history from prehistory to the present and rank among the most important in Europe.

Museum Island

The northern half of an island in the River Spree holds five museums built between 1830 and 1930 and UNESCO-listed as a World Heritage Site: the Altes Museum (Greek and Roman antiquities), the Neues Museum (Egyptian Museum and prehistory — home to the bust of Nefertiti), the Alte Nationalgalerie (19th-century art), the Bode Museum (sculpture, Byzantine art and coins) and the Pergamon Museum (Pergamon Altar, Ishtar Gate, and the Museum of Islamic Art). The James-Simon-Galerie opened in 2019 as a central entrance building.

Other locations

At the Kulturforum near Potsdamer Platz stand the Gemäldegalerie (European old master paintings), the Kunstgewerbemuseum, the Kupferstichkabinett (prints and drawings), the Neue Nationalgalerie (20th-century art, in Mies van der Rohe's glass hall) and the Musikinstrumenten-Museum. Further collections sit in Charlottenburg (including the Berggruen collection) and Dahlem, where the Ethnologisches Museum and Museum für Asiatische Kunst have largely moved their displays to the Humboldt Forum in the reconstructed Berlin Palace.

History

The museums grew from the art collections of the Brandenburg electors and Prussian kings, first made public when the Altes Museum opened in 1830. After the Second World War, during which buildings were badly damaged and many objects evacuated or looted, the collections were divided between East and West Berlin and were gradually reunified after German reunification in 1990 under a long-running masterplan for Museum Island.
