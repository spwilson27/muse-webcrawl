Caffeine

Caffeine is a central nervous system stimulant of the methylxanthine class. It is the world's most widely consumed psychoactive drug. Unlike most other psychoactive substances, caffeine remains largely unregulated and legal in nearly all parts of the world. It occurs naturally in the seeds, fruits, nuts and leaves of a number of plants native to Africa, East Asia and South America, where it protects them against herbivorous insects.

Mechanism of action

Caffeine acts primarily by blocking the action of adenosine, a neuromodulator that promotes sleep and suppresses arousal, at its receptors in the brain. By antagonising adenosine receptors, caffeine increases alertness and reduces fatigue. It is metabolised in the liver, mainly by the enzyme CYP1A2, into paraxanthine, theobromine and theophylline.

Effects

At typical doses caffeine increases wakefulness, concentration and reaction speed, and can enhance endurance performance. Side effects include anxiety, restlessness, insomnia, tremor and raised heart rate and blood pressure. Regular use produces tolerance and a mild dependence; abrupt cessation can cause withdrawal symptoms such as headache and irritability. Very high doses can be dangerous, and pure powdered caffeine has caused fatal overdoses.

Sources and consumption

Caffeine is consumed mainly in coffee, tea, soft drinks, energy drinks and chocolate, and is also used in some analgesic and cold medicines. An average cup of coffee contains roughly 95 mg. Decaffeinated products retain small residual amounts. Regulatory bodies such as the European Food Safety Authority consider daily intakes up to 400 mg safe for most adults, and up to 200 mg for pregnant women.

History

Caffeine-containing plants have been used for millennia; coffee drinking spread from Ethiopia and Yemen, and tea from China. The compound itself was isolated from coffee in 1819 by the German chemist Friedlieb Ferdinand Runge. Caffeine is on the World Health Organization's list of essential medicines in the form of caffeine citrate, used to treat apnoea of prematurity in newborns.
