UNESCO

The United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations aimed at promoting world peace and security through international cooperation in education, science, culture and communication.

UNESCO
Founded: 16 November 1945
Headquarters: Paris, France
Members: 194 member states
Director-General: head elected by General Conference
Known for: World Heritage Sites

Mission

UNESCO's constitution, adopted in London in 1945 and in force from 1946, declares that wars begin in the minds of men (and women), so defences of peace must be constructed there. Its programmes cover literacy, teacher training, science policy, ocean research, press freedom and safeguarding heritage.

World Heritage

The 1972 World Heritage Convention maintains the list of cultural and natural sites of outstanding universal value, now over 1,200 sites, plus lists of intangible cultural heritage and endangered sites.

Organization

Governed by a General Conference and Executive Board, UNESCO has faced periodic funding and political disputes, including US withdrawals and rejoins. See United Kingdom, a founding member.
