Brown Scapular

The Scapular of Our Lady of Mount Carmel, commonly known as the Brown Scapular, is a Roman Catholic devotional scapular associated with the Carmelite Order. It consists of two small pieces of brown wool cloth, joined by cords or ribbons, worn over the shoulders with one panel on the chest and one on the back. According to Carmelite tradition, the scapular was given by the Virgin Mary to Saint Simon Stock, an English Carmelite, in a vision traditionally dated to 16 July 1251 in Cambridge, with the promise that whoever died wearing it would not suffer eternal fire.

History

The scapular began as part of the Carmelite habit — a long work apron worn by monastic orders — and was adapted into a smaller devotional form for laypeople affiliated with the order through its Third Order and confraternities. Historians have long debated the authenticity of the Simon Stock vision account, which is first documented considerably later than the thirteenth century; the Carmelites themselves hold the tradition in varying degrees. The devotion spread widely in early modern Catholicism, and enrolment in the scapular — a short rite performed by a priest — became one of the most popular Catholic devotions.

The Sabbatine Privilege

Attached to the Brown Scapular is the so-called Sabbatine Privilege, based on a bull attributed to Pope John XXII (1322), according to which Mary would free the souls of scapular wearers from purgatory on the first Saturday after their death, provided they observed chastity according to their state, prayed the Little Office of the Blessed Virgin Mary (or an approved substitute), and observed certain fasts. The genuineness of the bull is disputed, but the Church has permitted the Carmelites to preach the privilege, and the devotion remains popular.

Teaching on the scapular

Catholic teaching emphasises that the scapular is a sacramental — a sign of consecration to Mary and of commitment to a Christian life — not a talisman guaranteeing salvation regardless of conduct. Pope Pius XII praised the devotion while urging that wearers imitate Mary's virtues. A medal bearing images of the Sacred Heart and of Our Lady of Mount Carmel may be substituted for the cloth scapular after initial enrolment.
