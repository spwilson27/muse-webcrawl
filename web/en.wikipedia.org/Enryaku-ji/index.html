Enryaku-ji

Enryaku-ji is a Tendai Buddhist monastery on Mount Hiei near Kyoto, Japan. Founded by Saichō in the late eighth and early ninth centuries, it became the head temple of Tendai Buddhism and one of the most powerful religious institutions in Japanese history.

Foundation

Saichō established a small temple on Mount Hiei in 788 and later received imperial support after returning from China with Tendai teachings. The mountain complex grew into a vast network of halls, subtemples, and training centers overlooking Kyoto and Lake Biwa.

Power and conflict

Enryaku-ji's warrior monks, or sōhei, at times intervened in court politics and fought rival institutions. In 1571 Oda Nobunaga, viewing the temple as a military threat, destroyed much of the complex and killed many of its inhabitants. The temple was later rebuilt under Tokugawa patronage.

Heritage

Many founders of later Japanese Buddhist schools trained on Mount Hiei, including figures associated with Pure Land, Zen, and Nichiren traditions. Enryaku-ji is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto. See also Tendai and Mount Hiei.
