Hakuin Ekaku

Hakuin Ekaku (白隠 慧鶴, January 19, 1686 – January 18, 1769) was one of the most influential figures in Japanese Zen Buddhism. He is regarded as the reviver of the Rinzai school from a period of stagnation, refocusing it on rigorous training methods integrating meditation and koan practice.

Life

Hakuin was born in the small village of Hara at the foot of Mount Fuji, in what is now Shizuoka Prefecture. Ordained as a monk at fifteen, he travelled and studied with various teachers, experiencing a series of awakenings but also suffering a severe physical and mental breakdown — the "Zen sickness" he later described — which he cured under the guidance of the hermit Hakuyūshi. He settled as abbot of Shōin-ji in his native Hara in 1718 and remained based there for the rest of his life, drawing large numbers of students, both monastic and lay.

Teaching

Hakuin systematized koan practice into a graded curriculum still used in Rinzai training. He is credited with the famous koan "What is the sound of one hand?" — intended to shatter discursive thought. His writings, including the autobiographical Wild Ivy and the treatise Orategama, were composed in accessible Japanese to reach laypeople. He also produced a large body of Zen painting and calligraphy, now highly prized; all Japanese Rinzai lineages trace through his disciple line.
