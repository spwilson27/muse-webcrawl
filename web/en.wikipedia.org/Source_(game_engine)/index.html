Source (game engine)


Half-Life 2 (2004), which debuted the Source engine

Source is a 3D game engine developed by Valve. It debuted with Counter-Strike: Source and Half-Life 2 in 2004, and remained the company's primary engine for over a decade, powering games including Team Fortress 2, the Left 4 Dead series, the Portal series, Dota 2 (before its move to Source 2), and Counter-Strike: Global Offensive. It was succeeded by Source 2.

Technology

Source was developed from the GoldSrc engine used for the original Half-Life, itself derived from the Quake engine. It introduced advanced face and character animation, lip synchronisation, shader-based rendering, high dynamic range lighting (added in 2005 with Half-Life 2: Lost Coast), and a prominent physics system based on the Havok middleware, which underpinned Half-Life 2's gravity gun gameplay. Levels are built with the Hammer Editor using binary space partitioning geometry.

Games and licensing

Valve licensed Source to outside developers, producing titles such as Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines, Titanfall and Titanfall 2 (heavily modified), Dear Esther, and The Stanley Parable. The Source SDK was released free to owners of Source games, fostering a large modding community that produced Garry's Mod, Black Mesa, and numerous other mods.

Branches

Over its life the engine was repeatedly updated in branches associated with major releases, including the Orange Box branch (2007) and the multiplayer branch used by Counter-Strike: Global Offensive. A majority of the engine code was leaked in 2020 following the leak of Team Fortress 2 code.
