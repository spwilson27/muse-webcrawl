Ford Sierra

The Ford Sierra is a mid-size car built by Ford of Europe from 1982 to 1993. It replaced the Cortina in the United Kingdom and the Taunus in continental markets. The Sierra was notable for its rounded, aerodynamic "jelly mould" styling, which was controversial at launch but became influential, and for retaining rear-wheel drive when many rivals had switched to front-wheel drive.

Range and performance versions

The Sierra was sold as a three- and five-door hatchback, a five-door estate, and later a saloon marketed as the Sierra Sapphire. Engines ranged from a 1.3-litre petrol to a 2.9-litre V6. High-performance variants included the XR4i and the RS Cosworth, introduced in 1986 with a turbocharged Cosworth engine and distinctive rear spoiler; the Sapphire RS Cosworth later added four-wheel drive. Cosworth Sierras achieved major success in touring-car racing worldwide.

Legacy

Early sales were hampered by the radical styling and by fleet buyers' conservatism, but the Sierra recovered to become one of Europe's best-selling cars, and it was also sold in North America in modified form as the Merkur XR4Ti. It was replaced in 1993 by the front-wheel-drive Mondeo.
