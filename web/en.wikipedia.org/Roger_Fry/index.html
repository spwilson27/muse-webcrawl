Roger Eliot Fry (14 December 1866 – 9 September 1934) was an English painter and art critic, and a central figure of the Bloomsbury Group. He is credited with bringing modern French painting to the attention of the British public and with coining the term "Post-Impressionism".

Life and career

Fry was educated at Clifton College and King's College, Cambridge, where he studied natural sciences before turning to art. He first made his reputation as a scholar of the Italian Old Masters, and in 1906 became curator of paintings at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York. Returning to London, he organised the exhibition Manet and the Post-Impressionists at the Grafton Galleries in 1910, introducing British audiences to Cézanne, Van Gogh, Gauguin and Matisse; a second Post-Impressionist exhibition followed in 1912. The shows provoked outrage but transformed British taste.

Omega Workshops and writings

In 1913 Fry founded the Omega Workshops with Vanessa Bell and Duncan Grant, applying modernist design to furniture, textiles and pottery. His criticism, collected in Vision and Design (1920) and Transformations (1926), emphasised formal qualities — line, mass, colour and composition — over narrative content, and his monographs on Cézanne (1927) and other artists remain influential. His final lectures as Slade Professor at Cambridge were published posthumously as Last Lectures. Virginia Woolf wrote his biography, published in 1940.
