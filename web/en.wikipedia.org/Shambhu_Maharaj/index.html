Shambhu Maharaj


Born: 1910, Lucknow, British India
Died: 4 November 1970
Occupation: Kathak dancer, guru
Style: Lucknow gharana

Shambhu Maharaj (1910 – 4 November 1970) was a celebrated Indian classical dancer and guru of the Lucknow gharana of Kathak. He was the youngest son of the legendary Kalka Prasad and the younger brother of Acchan Maharaj, and belonged to the illustrious family of Bindadin Maharaj, the founder figure of the Lucknow style of Kathak.

Early life and training

Shambhu Maharaj received his initial training in Kathak from his uncle Bindadin Maharaj and his father Kalka Prasad. He also studied Hindustani classical music, learning tabla and pakhawaj, which gave his dance a firm grounding in rhythm (laya) and tala. His elder brothers Acchan Maharaj and Lachhu Maharaj were also renowned exponents of the form.

Career

Shambhu Maharaj became known for his expressive abhinaya (facial expression and storytelling) and his mastery of intricate rhythmic footwork. In 1952 he joined the Bharatiya Kala Kendra in Delhi (later the Kathak Kendra), where he headed the Kathak unit and trained a generation of students. Among his most famous disciples was his nephew Birju Maharaj, who became the foremost exponent of the Lucknow gharana. He also taught Kumudini Lakhia and other notable dancers.

Awards

He received the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award and was honoured with the Padma Shri by the Government of India in 1956 for his contribution to Kathak.

Legacy

Shambhu Maharaj is remembered as one of the great architects of modern Kathak pedagogy in Delhi. The revival of Kathak in the capital in the post-independence decades is closely associated with his teaching at the Kathak Kendra.
