Divisor (algebraic geometry)

In algebraic geometry, divisors are a generalization of subvarieties of algebraic varieties; two different generalizations are in common use, Weil divisors and Cartier divisors, which coincide when the variety is smooth. Divisors are one of the main tools for studying the geometry of a variety, in particular through the line bundles associated to them.

Weil divisors

A Weil divisor on a normal variety X is a formal integer linear combination of irreducible closed subsets of codimension one. The group of Weil divisors modulo principal divisors (divisors of rational functions) is the divisor class group, an important invariant of the variety.

Cartier divisors

A Cartier divisor is defined by local equations: it is given by an open cover of the variety together with rational functions on each open set, compatible on overlaps. Every Cartier divisor determines a line bundle, and on smooth varieties the two notions of divisor agree.

Divisors on curves

On a smooth projective curve, a divisor is a formal sum of points with integer coefficients. Its degree is the sum of the coefficients, and the Riemann–Roch theorem relates the dimension of the space of meromorphic functions with prescribed poles to the degree and genus of the curve.
