Alec Issigonis

Born: 18 November 1906, Smyrna, Ottoman Empire
Died: 2 October 1988, Edgbaston, Birmingham
Occupation: Automotive engineer and designer
Known for: The Mini (1959)

Sir Alexander Arnold Constantine Issigonis (18 November 1906 – 2 October 1988) was a British-Greek automotive designer best known as the creator of the Mini, launched by the British Motor Corporation (BMC) in 1959 and voted the second most influential car of the 20th century. His career at Morris Motors and BMC also produced the Morris Minor and the BMC ADO16 (1100/1300) range.

Early life and career

Issigonis was born in Smyrna (now İzmir) to a Greek father and a German mother; the family was evacuated to Malta and then moved to England in 1922 during the Greco-Turkish War. He studied engineering at Battersea Polytechnic, joined Humber and then Morris Motors in 1936, where he designed the Morris Minor (1948), the first British car to sell over a million units.

The Mini

Responding to the 1956 Suez fuel crisis, BMC chairman Leonard Lord tasked Issigonis with designing a small, economical car. His solution — a transverse engine driving the front wheels, with wheels pushed to the corners and an exceptionally space-efficient layout — debuted in 1959 as the Morris Mini-Minor and Austin Seven. The Mini became an icon of 1960s Britain, won the Monte Carlo Rally three times, and remained in production until 2000.

Later work and honours

Issigonis became BMC's technical director and designed the ADO16, Britain's best-selling car of the 1960s, and the Austin Maxi. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1967 and knighted in 1969. He retired in 1971 but continued consulting, experimenting with geared steam and gearless designs until his death in 1988.
