Eusébio da Silva Ferreira (1942–2014), nicknamed the "Black Panther," was a Portuguese footballer widely regarded as one of the greatest strikers of all time. Born in Mozambique, he spent the peak of his career at Benfica, scoring around 733 goals in 745 competitive appearances.

Club career

Signed by Benfica in 1961, Eusébio helped the club retain the European Cup in 1962, scoring twice in the final against Real Madrid, and reached three more finals. He won eleven Primeira Liga titles, was the European Cup's top scorer three times, won the Ballon d'Or in 1965, and was the first winner of the European Golden Shoe (1968, again 1973). Later he played in North America and Mexico, including with the Toronto Metros-Croatia, winning the 1976 NASL title.

International career

At the 1966 World Cup in England, Eusébio finished top scorer with nine goals, including four in the famous 5–3 quarter-final comeback against North Korea, leading Portugal to third place — still their best finish. His sportsmanship after the semi-final loss to England became legendary.

Legacy

Eusébio is Benfica's and Portugal's enduring icon; his statue stands outside the Estádio da Luz. His death in 2014 prompted national mourning in Portugal, and his remains were moved to the National Pantheon in 2015.
