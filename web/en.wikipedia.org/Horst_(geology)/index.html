Horst (geology)

A horst flanked by two grabens

In physical geography and geology, a horst is a raised block of the Earth's crust that has lifted, or has remained stationary, while the land on either side (the graben) has dropped. A horst is bounded on both sides by parallel normal faults formed when the crust is stretched by extensional tectonic forces.

Formation and examples

As the crust extends, blocks fracture along normal faults; some blocks subside to form grabens while the blocks left standing (or actively pushed upward) become horsts. The resulting horst-and-graben topography is characteristic of rift valleys. Examples include the Vosges and Black Forest, which flank the Rhine Graben, and ranges of the Basin and Range Province of the western United States. Horsts can range from a few kilometres to over a hundred kilometres in width.

The word horst is German, meaning "heap" or "mass"; it entered geological terminology from German mining and structural geology usage.
