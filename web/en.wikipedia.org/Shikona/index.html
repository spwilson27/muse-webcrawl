Shikona

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: a tegata handprint with a wrestler's shikona]

A shikona (Japanese: 四股名) is a sumo wrestler's ring name. The tradition of giving a wrestler a shikona dates to the Edo period, when names were often chosen to conceal a wrestler's origin or to honour a sponsor, teacher, or famous predecessor. A wrestler may change his shikona several times during his career, sometimes for luck after injury or for promotion.

Shikona frequently incorporate auspicious or evocative kanji, often referencing mountains, the sea, wind, or animals, and commonly end in characters such as -yama (mountain), -umi or -kai (sea), -fuji, or -nishiki. Foreign wrestlers often take names with references to their homelands; the Hawaiian-born Takamiyama and the Mongolian champions Asashōryū and Hakuhō all fought under shikona chosen by their stablemasters.

Retirement names

When a senior wrestler retires and becomes an elder (toshiyori) of the Japan Sumo Association, he adopts an elder name (toshiyori-kabu), which is distinct from his fighting shikona. Ceremonies marking the cutting of the topknot, called danpatsu-shiki, formally close a career under the shikona.
