Moon landings

A Moon landing is the arrival of a spacecraft on the surface of the Moon. This includes both crewed missions and robotic probes. The first human-made object to reach the Moon was the Soviet Union's Luna 2 in 1959, which impacted the surface. The first soft landing was achieved by Luna 9 in 1966.

Crewed landings

The United States' Apollo program remains the only effort to have landed humans on the Moon. Apollo 11 landed in the Sea of Tranquility on 20 July 1969; Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the surface while Michael Collins remained in orbit. Five further Apollo missions landed successfully — Apollo 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17 — between 1969 and 1972, bringing twelve astronauts to the surface in total. Apollo 13, crippled by an explosion, looped around the Moon without landing. Eugene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt of Apollo 17, in December 1972, are the most recent people to have stood on the Moon.

Robotic landings

The Soviet Luna program achieved sample returns and deployed the Lunokhod rovers in the 1970s. After a long hiatus, China landed Chang'e 3 in 2013 and Chang'e 4 on the lunar far side in 2019 — a first — followed by sample returns with Chang'e 5 (2020) and Chang'e 6 from the far side (2024). India's Chandrayaan-3 landed near the lunar south pole in August 2023, making India the fourth nation to soft-land on the Moon, and Japan's SLIM lander touched down in January 2024. Commercial landers began reaching the surface in the mid-2020s under NASA's CLPS program.

Significance

Moon landings transformed planetary science, returned 382 kilograms of lunar rock and soil from Apollo alone, and remain a benchmark of technological achievement. Denial of the Apollo landings persists as a well-documented conspiracy theory, refuted by extensive independent evidence.
