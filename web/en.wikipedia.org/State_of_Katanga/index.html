The State of Katanga (French: État du Katanga) was a breakaway state that proclaimed its independence from the Republic of the Congo on 11 July 1960, eleven days after Congolese independence from Belgium. Led by Moise Tshombe and his CONAKAT party, the secession was never recognized by any country, though it received substantial support from Belgium and from the mining company Union Minière du Haut Katanga, which dominated the copper- and cobalt-rich province.

Background

Katanga was the wealthiest province of the Belgian Congo, producing a large share of the world's cobalt and industrial diamonds, as well as copper, uranium and other minerals. Its revenues were central to the economy of the newly independent Congo. Tensions between the central government of Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba and provincial leaders, together with Belgian business interests fearful of nationalization, set the stage for secession.

Secession and the Congo Crisis

Following a mutiny of the Congolese army in July 1960, Tshombe declared Katanga independent. Belgian troops intervened, ostensibly to protect Belgian nationals, and Belgian officers and mercenaries helped build the Katangese Gendarmerie. The secession became a central episode of the Congo Crisis, drawing in the United Nations, the United States and the Soviet Union at the height of the Cold War.

The UN deployed a large peacekeeping force, ONUC, and after Lumumba's murder in Katanga in January 1961 international pressure for reintegration mounted. UN Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld died in a plane crash near Ndola in September 1961 while travelling to negotiate a ceasefire with Tshombe.

End of the secession

In December 1962 the UN launched Operation Grandslam, its final offensive. Katangese forces were defeated and Élisabethville was captured. Tshombe fled into exile on 15 January 1963 and formally conceded the secession days later. Katanga was reintegrated into the Congo, though Tshombe returned in 1964 as Prime Minister of the whole country.
