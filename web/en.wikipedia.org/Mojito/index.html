Mojito


Type: Cocktail
Origin: Havana, Cuba
Ingredients: White rum, sugar, lime juice, soda water, mint
Served: On the rocks, in a highball glass

The mojito (Spanish: [moˈxito]) is a traditional Cuban punch. The cocktail typically consists of five ingredients: white rum, sugar (traditionally sugar cane juice), lime juice, soda water, and mint. Its combination of sweetness, citrus, and herbaceous mint flavours complements the rum, and has made the mojito a popular summer drink.

History

The mojito's origins are traced to Havana, Cuba, though its exact history is disputed. One theory connects it to "El Draque", a 16th-century drink named for the English privateer Francis Drake, made with aguardiente, lime, sugar, and mint. The modern mojito emerged in the early 20th century and became associated with Havana bars such as La Bodeguita del Medio, where it was a favourite of Ernest Hemingway, whose handwritten endorsement is displayed there.

Preparation

Fresh mint leaves are gently muddled with sugar and lime juice to release the mint oils without shredding the leaves, white rum is added, the mixture is briefly stirred, and the drink is topped with crushed ice and soda water, garnished with mint sprigs and a lime wedge. Variations substitute fruits such as strawberries or passion fruit, or use dark rum, and non-alcoholic "virgin" versions omit the rum.

Popularity

Surveys have repeatedly ranked the mojito among the most popular cocktails in the world, and it has frequently been the best-selling cocktail in British and French bars during summer months.
