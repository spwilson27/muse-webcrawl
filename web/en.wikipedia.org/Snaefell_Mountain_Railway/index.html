Snaefell Mountain Railway

A mountain railway car approaching the Snaefell summit station

The Snaefell Mountain Railway is an electric mountain railway on the Isle of Man in the Irish Sea. It joins Laxey with the summit of Snaefell, at 2,036 feet (621 m) the island's highest point and the only place from which — on a clear day, according to tradition — the kingdoms of England, Scotland, Wales, Ireland, the Isle of Man and Heaven can all be seen. The line is 5 miles (8 km) long, built to 3 ft 6 in (1,067 mm) gauge, and uses a Fell centre rail for braking on the steep gradients.

History and operation

The railway opened on 21 August 1895, having been built in just seven months. It was designed with the involvement of the Snaefell Mining Company interests and climbs at an average gradient of about 1 in 12. At Laxey it connects with the Manx Electric Railway, allowing through journeys from Douglas and Ramsey. The six wooden-bodied tramcars date from the line's opening and remain in service, making them among the oldest regularly working electric trams in the world; their original equipment has been progressively modernised.

The line crosses the Snaefell Mountain Course at the Bungalow, where during TT races trams pause so spectators can cross between the inside and outside of the circuit. Services run seasonally, roughly from spring to autumn, and are suspended in high winds.
