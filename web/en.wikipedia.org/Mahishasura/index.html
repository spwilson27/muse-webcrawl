Mahishasura (Sanskrit: महिषासुर, Mahiṣāsura) is a buffalo demon in Hindu mythology. He is known for his deception and his shape-shifting ability: mahisha means "buffalo" and asura means "demon". According to the scriptures, Mahishasura was the son of the demon king Rambha and a she-buffalo, and hence possessed both human and buffalo forms. He is ultimately killed by the goddess Durga, who earns the epithet Mahishasuramardini ("Slayer of Mahishasura").

Durga as Mahishasuramardini slays the buffalo demon.

Mythology

After performing severe penance, Mahishasura obtained from Brahma the boon that he could be killed by no man or god. Emboldened, he waged war on the devas, defeated Indra, and occupied heaven. The gods combined their energies to create the goddess Durga, arming her with their weapons. She fought Mahishasura for nine nights as he repeatedly changed form—buffalo, lion, man, elephant—until, pinning the buffalo with her foot, she beheaded him with her trident as he attempted to emerge in human form.

Cultural significance

The victory is celebrated at the festivals of Navaratri and Durga Puja, and on Vijayadashami. The city of Mysuru (Mysore) in Karnataka is traditionally said to derive its name from Mahishasura, and a large statue of the demon stands on Chamundi Hill overlooking the city, while the goddess Chamundeshwari—Durga's local form—is its patron deity.
