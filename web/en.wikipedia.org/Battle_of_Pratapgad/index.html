Battle of Pratapgad

LocationPratapgad, present-day Maharashtra, India
ResultDecisive Maratha victory

The Battle of Pratapgad was fought on 10 November 1659 between the forces of the Maratha leader Shivaji and the army of the Adil Shahi general Afzal Khan, sent by the Sultanate of Bijapur to crush Shivaji's growing power in the Sahyadri hills of western India.

Background

By 1659 Shivaji had carved out a domain of forts and territory that Bijapur could no longer ignore. Afzal Khan, a veteran commander who boasted he would drag Shivaji back in chains, marched with a large army — contemporary accounts suggest some 20,000–35,000 troops — desecrating temples on the way to provoke Shivaji into open battle. Shivaji instead withdrew to the strong hill fort of Pratapgad near Wai and opened negotiations for a personal meeting.

The meeting and the battle

The two leaders met on 10 November in a tent at the foot of the fort, each ostensibly bringing only a small escort. According to the Maratha accounts, Afzal Khan attacked Shivaji with a dagger or attempted to crush him in an embrace, whereupon Shivaji — wearing concealed armour and steel "tiger claws" (wagh nakh) — disembowelled him, and his bodyguard Jiva Mahala cut down Khan's own guard Sayyid Banda. At the signal, Maratha troops hidden in the surrounding jungle, under commanders including Netaji Palkar and Kanhoji Jedhe, fell upon the leaderless Bijapuri army in the narrow forested valley and routed it completely.

Aftermath

The victory was a turning point for the young Maratha polity: Shivaji captured enormous booty in artillery, horses, treasure and elephants, annexed territory down toward Kolhapur and the Konkan, and within weeks took the great fortress of Panhala. The killing of Afzal Khan became one of the most celebrated — and disputed — episodes in Maratha history, central to Shivaji's legend as a master of guerrilla warfare and audacious stratagem.
