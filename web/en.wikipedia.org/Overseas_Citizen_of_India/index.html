Overseas Citizen of India

The Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) is an immigration status permitting a foreign citizen of Indian origin to live and work in India indefinitely. It was introduced by the Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2003, in response to demands for dual citizenship by the Indian diaspora. Despite its name, OCI status is not Indian citizenship, which India does not permit to be held concurrently with another nationality.

Eligibility

A foreign national who was eligible to become a citizen of India on 26 January 1950, or was a citizen of India on or after that date, or belonged to a territory that became part of India after 15 August 1947, may register as an OCI cardholder; their children and grandchildren may also be eligible. Spouses of Indian citizens or OCI cardholders may qualify after a registered marriage subsisting for at least two years.

Rights and restrictions

OCI cardholders enjoy a multiple-entry, lifelong visa for visiting India and parity with non-resident Indians in many economic and educational matters. They do not have voting rights, cannot hold constitutional posts or most government jobs, and generally require special permission to buy agricultural or plantation land.

History

The scheme was launched in 2005. In 2015 the related Person of Indian Origin (PIO) card scheme was merged into OCI, and PIO cards were deemed to be OCI cards.
