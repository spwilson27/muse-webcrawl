Dos Erres massacre

Location of Dos Erres in Petén, northern Guatemala.

The Dos Erres massacre of 6–8 December 1982 took place at Dos Erres, a village in the municipality of La Libertad, Petén, Guatemala, during the Guatemalan Civil War. A commando unit of the Guatemalan Army's elite Kaibiles, searching for weapons stolen by guerrillas, murdered more than 200 villagers — men, women, and children — in one of the most notorious atrocities of the conflict.

Background

Dos Erres had been founded in the 1970s by settlers on government-distributed land; its name refers to the initials of two men involved in the original land grant. In the autumn of 1982, guerrillas of the FAR (Rebel Armed Forces) ambushed an army convoy nearby and took rifles and equipment. The army suspected the village of harbouring the guerrillas and their weapons.

The massacre

Early on 6 December, Kaibil commandos entered the village dressed as guerrillas to avoid alerting the inhabitants. Over the following days they tortured and killed the villagers; many were bludgeoned or shot, and bodies were thrown into the village well. Two young boys were taken away alive by soldiers; one, Óscar Ramírez Ramos, was raised by a soldier's family and learned the truth only as an adult.

Investigations and prosecutions

Exhumations by the Guatemalan Forensic Anthropology Foundation in the 1990s recovered the remains of at least 162 people from the well. After years of impunity and legal delay, several former Kaibiles were arrested in Guatemala, the United States, and Canada beginning in 2010, and in 2011–2012 Guatemalan courts sentenced four of them to more than 6,000 years in prison each. Former head of state Efraín Ríos Montt was charged in connection with the massacre before his death in 2018.
