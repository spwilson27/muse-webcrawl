Wind farm

A wind farm, also called a wind park or wind power plant, is a group of wind turbines installed in the same location to produce electricity, connected to the grid by a single point. Large wind farms may consist of several hundred individual turbines spread over an extensive area, onshore or offshore.

Design and operation

Site selection depends on wind resource, grid access, land use and environmental impact. Turbines are spaced several rotor diameters apart to limit wake losses. Modern turbines start generating at wind speeds around 3 m/s and shut down in very high winds for safety. Offshore wind farms exploit stronger, steadier winds at sea but cost more to build and maintain.

Scale

Among the largest installations are the Gansu Wind Farm in China, with a planned capacity exceeding 20 GW, the Hornsea projects off the Yorkshire coast of England, and the Alta Wind Energy Center in California. Wind power has become one of the cheapest sources of new generating capacity in many regions.
