Classical guitar

Classical guitar
Type: String instrument (plucked)
Strings: 6, nylon
Related: Flamenco guitar, acoustic guitar

The classical guitar is a member of the guitar family used in classical music and other styles. An acoustic wooden string instrument with strings made of gut or nylon, it is a precursor of the modern steel-string acoustic and electric guitars, both of which use metal strings. Classical guitars are typically played with the fingertips or fingernails rather than a pick, and the player sits with the guitar resting on a raised left leg.

Construction

The modern classical guitar took shape in 19th-century Spain, above all in the designs of Antonio de Torres, who standardised the larger body, fan bracing of the soundboard, and 650 mm scale length. The top is usually spruce or cedar, with backs and sides of rosewood or other hardwoods. The neck is wide and flat compared with steel-string guitars, suiting the polyphonic fingerstyle technique.

Repertoire and technique

The repertoire extends from lute and vihuela transcriptions through Sor, Giuliani and Tárrega to 20th-century works written for Andrés Segovia, who established the guitar as a concert instrument. Players use free stroke (tirando) and rest stroke (apoyando), and the instrument is also used in bossa nova, folk and jazz contexts.
