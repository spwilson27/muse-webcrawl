Crown Dependencies

The Crown Dependencies are three island territories in the British Islands that are self-governing possessions of the British Crown: the Isle of Man in the Irish Sea, and the Bailiwick of Jersey and the Bailiwick of Guernsey in the English Channel. They are not part of the United Kingdom, nor are they British Overseas Territories; the monarch is their head of state, represented by a lieutenant governor in each.

Constitutional status

Each dependency has its own parliament, legal system, courts and fiscal policy — the Isle of Man's legislature, the Tynwald, claims to be the world's oldest continuously operating parliament. The UK government is responsible for their defence and international representation, and by convention the UK Parliament does not legislate for them without consent. The islands are in a customs union with the UK and their residents hold British citizenship, but they were never members of the European Union.

Economies

All three are low-tax jurisdictions with large financial services sectors; Jersey and Guernsey are also known for dairy produce and tourism, and the Isle of Man for the TT motorcycle races and its film and e-gaming industries.
