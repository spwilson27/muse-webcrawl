Khadija bint Khuwaylid (Arabic: خديجة بنت خويلد, c. 554 – 619 CE) was the first wife and the first follower of the Islamic prophet Muhammad. A successful merchant of the Quraysh tribe in Mecca, she employed Muhammad to trade on her behalf and later proposed marriage to him. According to Islamic tradition she was the first person to believe in his prophethood and supported him throughout the difficult early years in Mecca.

Life

Khadija, twice widowed, ran a prosperous caravan trade between Mecca and Syria. Muhammad's reputation for honesty led her to entrust him with a trading journey, after which she proposed marriage; he was about 25 and she, by the most common tradition, about 40. They were married for some 25 years, and Muhammad took no other wife during her lifetime. Their children included four daughters who survived to maturity, among them Fatima, wife of Ali.

Role in early Islam

When Muhammad reported his first revelation in the cave of Hira, Khadija reassured him and consulted her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, who affirmed the experience. She is honoured in Islam as the first Muslim and as one of the four most perfect women in tradition. Her death in 619, shortly after the lifting of the boycott against Muhammad's clan, came in what became known as the "Year of Sorrow."
