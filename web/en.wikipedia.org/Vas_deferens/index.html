Vas deferens

The vas deferens (Latin: "duct that carries away"; plural vasa deferentia), also called the ductus deferens, is a muscular tube in the male reproductive system of many vertebrates that transports sperm from the epididymis to the ejaculatory ducts in preparation for ejaculation.

Structure

In humans there are two vasa deferentia, each about 30–45 cm long. From the tail of the epididymis each vas ascends within the spermatic cord, passes through the inguinal canal, and enters the pelvis, where it loops over the ureter and expands into the ampulla before joining the duct of the seminal vesicle to form the ejaculatory duct. Its wall contains a thick layer of smooth muscle arranged in inner and outer longitudinal and middle circular coats, giving the tube a characteristic firm, cord-like feel on palpation.

Function

During ejaculation, peristaltic contractions of the muscular wall propel sperm forward. The vas deferens thus delivers sperm from storage in the epididymis to the urethra, where the sperm mixes with secretions from the seminal vesicles, prostate and bulbourethral glands to form semen.

Clinical significance

The vas deferens is divided and sealed in the sterilisation procedure known as a vasectomy. Congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens (CBAVD) is associated with mutations in the CFTR gene and is a cause of male infertility. The artery to the ductus deferens is an important landmark in pelvic surgery.
