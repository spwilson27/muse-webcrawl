Wilhelmina Charlotte Caroline of Brandenburg-Ansbach (1 March 1683 – 20 November 1737) was Queen of Great Britain and Ireland and Electress of Hanover as the wife of King George II. She was one of the most politically influential queens consort in British history, acting as a close adviser to her husband and to the prime minister Robert Walpole.

Early life

Caroline was the daughter of John Frederick, Margrave of Brandenburg-Ansbach, and Princess Eleonore Erdmuthe of Saxe-Eisenach. Orphaned young, she was raised in part at the courts of Berlin and Dresden, where she received an unusually thorough education and moved in intellectual circles that included the philosopher Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz.

Princess of Wales

In 1705 she married George Augustus, Electoral Prince of Hanover, later George II. When her father-in-law became George I of Great Britain in 1714, Caroline became Princess of Wales. During the estrangement between George I and his son, her London residence, Leicester House, became a rival court and a centre of opposition politics.

Queen consort

Caroline became queen on George II's accession in 1727. She wielded considerable influence, supporting Walpole's ministry and acting as regent during her husband's absences in Hanover. A patron of the arts and sciences, she promoted smallpox inoculation in Britain after testing it on condemned prisoners and then on her own children. She died in 1737 following complications from an umbilical hernia; the king, devoted to her memory, never remarried.
