Jackfruit

KingdomPlantae
FamilyMoraceae
GenusArtocarpus
SpeciesA. heterophyllus
OriginSouth and Southeast Asia

The jackfruit (Artocarpus heterophyllus) is a species of tree in the fig, mulberry and breadfruit family (Moraceae), native to the rainforests of the Western Ghats of India and widely cultivated throughout tropical Asia and beyond. It produces the largest fruit of any tree, commonly reaching 10–25 kg and sometimes more than 50 kg, borne directly on the trunk and main branches. Jackfruit is the national fruit of Bangladesh and Sri Lanka and the state fruit of Kerala and Tamil Nadu in India.

Description

The jackfruit tree is an evergreen that grows to 10–20 m tall. Its multiple fruit develops from an entire inflorescence; the oval, spiky green-to-yellow rind encloses hundreds of fleshy, yellow-orange bulbs of sweet flesh, each surrounding a smooth seed. All parts of the plant exude a sticky white latex.

Cultivation and uses

Jackfruit has been cultivated in South Asia for thousands of years and spread through the tropics, including to East Africa, Brazil and the Caribbean. Ripe fruit is eaten fresh or used in desserts, ice cream and chips; the seeds are roasted or boiled. Unripe, green jackfruit has a mild flavour and a fibrous, meat-like texture, and is cooked in curries — and, in recent years, has become popular internationally as a plant-based substitute for pulled pork and other shredded meats. The durable golden timber is valued for furniture and musical instruments.

Nutrition

The fruit is rich in carbohydrates, dietary fibre, vitamin C, potassium and B vitamins. In regions where it is abundant, jackfruit is an important food-security crop, sometimes called the "poor man's food" in South Asia.
