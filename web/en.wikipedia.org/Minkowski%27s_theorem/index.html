Minkowski's theorem

Minkowski's theorem, also called Minkowski's convex body theorem, is a result in the geometry of numbers stating that any convex set in ℝn that is symmetric about the origin and has volume greater than 2n must contain a non-zero lattice point. Hermann Minkowski proved the theorem in the 1890s as the founding result of the geometry of numbers.

Statement

Suppose that Λ is a lattice of determinant d(Λ) in ℝn and S is a measurable subset of ℝn symmetric about the origin (if x is in S, so is −x). If the volume of S exceeds 2nd(Λ), then S contains at least one lattice point other than the origin; if S is also compact and convex, the same conclusion holds when the volume equals 2nd(Λ).

Proof sketch

The proof is a classic application of Blichfeldt's principle: a set of volume greater than d(Λ) cannot map injectively into the quotient space ℝn/Λ, so two points differ by a lattice vector; symmetry and convexity then yield the desired lattice point as half of their difference.

Applications

Classical consequences include simple proofs of Lagrange's four-square theorem, the theorem that every prime p ≡ 1 (mod 4) is a sum of two squares, and bounds in Dirichlet's approximation theorem. The theorem underlies much of modern lattice-based geometry and has extensions such as Minkowski's second theorem and the geometry of successive minima.
