Specialty glass production

Schott AG is a German multinational manufacturer of specialty glass and glass-ceramics headquartered in Mainz. Founded by glass technologist Otto Schott in Jena in 1884, the company is wholly owned by the Carl Zeiss Foundation and is a leading supplier of high-technology glass products.

History

Otto Schott, working with Ernst Abbe and Carl Zeiss, developed new optical and heat-resistant borosilicate glasses in the 1880s, founding the Jenaer Glaswerk Schott & Genossen. After the Second World War the company was divided between East and West Germany; the western works moved to Mainz. The two companies were reunified in 1991.

Products

Schott produces optical glass, borosilicate glass tubing and containers for pharmaceuticals, glass-ceramic cooktop panels (Ceran), fire-resistant glazing, components for astronomy telescopes (Zerodur glass-ceramic, used in major observatory mirrors), and glass for electronics, aviation and solar applications. Its pharmaceutical packaging reaches patients in the form of billions of syringes, vials and cartridges.
