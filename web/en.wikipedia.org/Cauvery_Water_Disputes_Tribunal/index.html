Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal

Constituted2 June 1990
Legal basisInter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956
PartiesKarnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Puducherry
Final award5 February 2007
RiverCauvery (Kaveri)

The Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal was a tribunal constituted by the Government of India in 1990 to adjudicate the long-running dispute over sharing the waters of the Cauvery River among the riparian states of Karnataka and Tamil Nadu and the smaller basin territories of Kerala and Puducherry. The sharing of the river's waters had been governed by agreements of 1892 and 1924 between the Madras Presidency and the princely state of Mysore; after the 1924 agreement lapsed in 1974, disputes intensified as Karnataka expanded irrigation in the basin.


The Cauvery basin, shared by four states and territories

Proceedings and interim order

After decades of failed negotiations, Tamil Nadu pressed for a tribunal under the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, and the tribunal was set up in June 1990. Its interim order of June 1991 directed Karnataka to release 205 thousand million cubic feet (tmcft) of water annually to Tamil Nadu; violent protests followed in Karnataka, and an ordinance by the state government attempting to nullify the order was struck down by the Supreme Court.

Final award (2007)

The tribunal delivered its final award on 5 February 2007, assessing the total utilisable flow at 740 tmcft in a normal year and allocating 419 tmcft to Tamil Nadu, 270 tmcft to Karnataka, 30 tmcft to Kerala, and 7 tmcft to Puducherry, with the remainder reserved for environmental protection and inevitable escapages to the sea. The award was notified in the Gazette in 2013 after Supreme Court direction.

Aftermath

In 2018 the Supreme Court modified the shares slightly — reducing Tamil Nadu's allocation to 404.25 tmcft and raising Karnataka's to 284.75 tmcft, partly to recognise Bengaluru's drinking-water needs and groundwater availability in Tamil Nadu — and directed creation of the Cauvery Water Management Authority to implement the scheme. Implementation, especially releases in deficit monsoon years, remains politically contentious.
