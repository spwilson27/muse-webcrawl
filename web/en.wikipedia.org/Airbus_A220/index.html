Airbus A220

Type: Narrow-body jet airliner

Manufacturer: Airbus Canada Limited Partnership

First flight: 16 September 2013

Introduction: 15 July 2016 with Swiss International Air Lines

Status: In production, in service

The Airbus A220 is a family of five-abreast narrow-body airliners developed by Bombardier Aerospace as the Bombardier CSeries. The family comprises the A220-100 (formerly CS100) seating about 108–130 passengers and the A220-300 (formerly CS300) seating about 130–160.

Development

Bombardier launched the CSeries in 2008, aiming to compete in the 100–150 seat market with a clean-sheet design featuring Pratt & Whitney PW1000G geared turbofan engines, extensive composite and aluminium-lithium construction, and fly-by-wire controls. The first flight took place in September 2013, and Swiss International Air Lines introduced the CS100 into service in July 2016.

After a trade dispute with Boeing and program cost overruns, Bombardier sold a majority stake in the program to Airbus in 2018; the aircraft was rebranded as the A220 in July 2018. Final assembly takes place in Mirabel, Quebec, and in Mobile, Alabama.

Design and operation

The A220 offers fuel burn roughly 20–25% lower per seat than previous-generation aircraft of similar size. Major operators include Delta Air Lines, airBaltic, Swiss, Air France, and JetBlue. The type has a range of about 3,400–3,600 nautical miles depending on variant, enabling transcontinental and thin transatlantic routes.
