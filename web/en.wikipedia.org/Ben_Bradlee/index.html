Benjamin Crowninshield Bradlee (26 August 1921 – 21 October 2014) was an American journalist who served as managing editor, then executive editor, of The Washington Post from 1965 to 1991. Under his leadership the Post rose to national prominence, winning the fight to publish the Pentagon Papers and guiding reporters Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein through the Watergate scandal that led to President Richard Nixon's resignation.

Career

A Harvard graduate and Second World War naval officer, Bradlee worked at the Post after the war, then as a Newsweek correspondent in Paris and bureau chief in Washington, where he became a close friend of Senator and later President John F. Kennedy. Publisher Katharine Graham made him managing editor of the Post in 1965. He expanded its ambitions, launched the Style section, and defended the paper in the 1971 Pentagon Papers case, in which the Supreme Court upheld the right to publish.

Legacy

Bradlee's newsroom tenure transformed the Post into one of the world's leading newspapers, collecting 23 Pulitzer Prizes on his watch. He was portrayed by Jason Robards in All the President's Men (1976) and by Tom Hanks in The Post (2017). His memoir A Good Life appeared in 1995; he received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2013. See also Watergate scandal.
