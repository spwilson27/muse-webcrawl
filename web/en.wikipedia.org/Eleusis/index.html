Eleusis (Greek: Ελευσίνα, Elefsina) is a town and municipality in West Attica, Greece, about 18 kilometres northwest of central Athens. In antiquity it was a deme of Athens and the site of the sanctuary of Demeter and Persephone, where the Eleusinian Mysteries — among the most famous secret religious rites of ancient Greece — were celebrated for nearly two thousand years.

Ancient history

Settlement at Eleusis dates to the Bronze Age; the town is named in tradition for the hero Eleusis, son of Hermes. The Homeric Hymn to Demeter recounts how the goddess, searching for her abducted daughter Persephone, came to Eleusis and founded her rites there. The sanctuary's great hall, the Telesterion, could hold thousands of initiates. The Mysteries comprised the Lesser Mysteries held in spring and the Greater Mysteries in the month of Boedromion (September–October), when a sacred procession travelled the Sacred Way from Athens.

Initiation was open to all Greek-speakers, including women and slaves, and initiates were sworn to secrecy — revealing the rites was punishable by death. The cult survived into Roman times, attracting emperors among its initiates, until the sanctuary was destroyed by Alaric's Goths in 396 AD and suppressed under Christian rule.

Modern Eleusis

Modern Elefsina is an industrial port town with oil refineries, shipyards, cement and steel works; it is also the birthplace of the tragedian Aeschylus (c. 525 BC). The town hosts the Aeschylia Festival each summer and was a European Capital of Culture in 2023. The archaeological site and museum preserve the sanctuary remains.
