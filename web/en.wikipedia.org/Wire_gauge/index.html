Wire gauge

Common systemsAWG, SWG, IEC 60228
MeasuresWire diameter / cross-sectional area

A wire gauge is a measurement of wire diameter, and by extension its cross-sectional area and electrical carrying capacity. Multiple standard systems exist; in most gauge systems a higher number indicates a thinner wire, reflecting the historical process of drawing wire through successively smaller dies — the gauge number roughly counted the drawing steps.

American Wire Gauge

The American Wire Gauge (AWG), also called the Brown & Sharpe gauge, is the standard in North America for electrically conducting wire. Diameters follow a geometric progression between 0.005 inch (36 AWG) and 0.46 inch (0000 AWG, or 4/0). Each decrease of six gauge numbers roughly doubles the diameter; each decrease of three doubles the cross-sectional area and halves the resistance per unit length. Sizes larger than 4/0 for building wiring are commonly specified by circular mils instead.

Other systems

The British Standard Wire Gauge (SWG) was long used in the United Kingdom, while the IEC 60228 standard used internationally specifies nominal cross-sectional areas directly in square millimetres. Jewellery, sheet metal, and hypodermic needles use their own gauge conventions, which are not interchangeable.
