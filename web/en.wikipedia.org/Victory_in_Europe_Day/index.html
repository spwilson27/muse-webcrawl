Victory in Europe Day

Also calledVE Day
Observed8 May (Western Europe); 9 May in Russia and some former Soviet states
CommemoratesFormal acceptance of Nazi Germany's surrender, 1945
RelatedVictory over Japan Day (V-J Day)

Victory in Europe Day (VE Day) is the day celebrating the formal acceptance by the Allies of World War II of Germany's unconditional surrender of its armed forces on Tuesday, 8 May 1945. It marked the official end of World War II in Europe, although fighting continued in some areas for days afterwards, and the war against Japan continued until August–September 1945.

Events of May 1945

Following Adolf Hitler's suicide on 30 April 1945, his successor Karl Dönitz authorised the signing of an instrument of surrender. German forces in northwestern Europe surrendered at Lüneburg Heath on 4 May; the Act of Military Surrender was signed at Reims in the early hours of 7 May, to take effect at 23:01 Central European Time on 8 May. At Soviet insistence a second signing took place in Berlin-Karlshorst late on 8 May — already 9 May in Moscow — which is why Russia and several post-Soviet states celebrate Victory Day on 9 May.

Celebrations

VE Day brought enormous public celebrations: crowds packed Piccadilly Circus in London, where Prime Minister Winston Churchill addressed the people from a Whitehall balcony, and Times Square in New York. In the United Kingdom 8 May 1945 was declared a public holiday, and commemorations — street parties, church services, and moments of remembrance — have marked the anniversary ever since, with major national observances at the 50th (1995), 70th (2015), 75th (2020), and 80th (2025) anniversaries. The day is a public holiday in several European countries, where it is also observed as a day of remembrance for the victims of the war.
