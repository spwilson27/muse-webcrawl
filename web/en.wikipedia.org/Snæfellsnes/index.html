Snæfellsnes is a peninsula situated in western Iceland, largely in the Western Region. It has been nicknamed "Iceland in Miniature" because many of the country's characteristic landscapes and natural features can be found in this one area, including lava fields, black-sand beaches, craters, caves, and a glacier.

Snæfellsjökull

The western tip of the peninsula is dominated by Snæfellsjökull, a 1,446-metre glacier-capped stratovolcano surrounded by Snæfellsjökull National Park, established in 2001. The volcano is famous as the setting for the entrance to the Earth's interior in Jules Verne's 1864 novel Journey to the Center of the Earth, and it figured in Halldór Laxness's novel Under the Glacier.

Settlements

Fishing towns and villages on the peninsula include Stykkishólmur, Ólafsvík, Grundarfjörður (below the distinctive Kirkjufell mountain), Arnarstapi, and Hellnar. The peninsula is also the setting of much of the medieval Eyrbyggja saga and parts of Laxdæla saga.

Geology

Snæfellsnes lies on a divergent plate boundary zone where volcanism has recurred; its rocks range from old basalt of the Tertiary flank zones to young Holocene lava flows erupted after the last ice age.
