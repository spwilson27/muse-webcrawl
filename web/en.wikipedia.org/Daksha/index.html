Daksha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Daksha (Sanskrit: दक्ष, Dakṣa, "able, competent") is a Hindu deity, one of the Prajapatis — the agents of creation and forefathers of humankind. In the Rigveda he is associated with the Adityas; in later tradition he is a son of Brahma, born from his right thumb, and the father of many daughters who were married to gods and sages, making him a grandfather of much of the divine population.

The sacrifice of Daksha

Daksha is best known for the myth of his great sacrifice (yajna), told in the Mahabharata and the Puranas. Daksha's daughter Sati married the god Shiva against her father's wishes. When Daksha held a grand sacrifice and deliberately did not invite Shiva, Sati attended uninvited, was humiliated by her father, and immolated herself in the sacrificial fire. Enraged, Shiva created the fierce Virabhadra, who disrupted the sacrifice and beheaded Daksha. Daksha was later restored to life with the head of a goat, and humbly acknowledged Shiva's greatness. Sati was reborn as Parvati.

Family

With his wife Prasuti (or Asikni in other versions), Daksha fathered numerous daughters. Among the most famous: twenty-seven daughters married to Chandra, the moon god, who became the Nakshatras (lunar mansions); thirteen married to the sage Kashyapa, including Aditi and Diti, mothers of the gods and demons; ten married to Dharma; and others to Angiras, Kratu, and Vasishtha. His daughter Diti's descendants are the Daityas, and Aditi's the Adityas.

In the Vedas and law

In some hymns Daksha appears paradoxically as both father and son of Aditi. The Manusmriti lists Daksha among the ten Prajapatis created by Manu. He also gives his name to Daksha Smriti, a minor Dharmashastra text.
