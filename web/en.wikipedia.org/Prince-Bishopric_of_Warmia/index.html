The Prince-Bishopric of Warmia (Polish: Biskupie Księstwo Warmińskie) was an ecclesiastical principality in Prussia, centred on the diocese of Warmia (Ermland), founded in 1243 during the Prussian Crusade. The bishops held secular authority over roughly a third of the diocesan territory.

History

After the Second Peace of Thorn (1466), Warmia passed from the Teutonic Order to the Crown of the Kingdom of Poland, while retaining considerable autonomy; its bishops were senators of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. The most famous figure associated with the diocese is Nicolaus Copernicus, who served as a canon of the Frombork chapter and administered episcopal estates at Olsztyn. The bishopric was secularized upon the First Partition of Poland in 1772, when it was annexed by the Kingdom of Prussia.
