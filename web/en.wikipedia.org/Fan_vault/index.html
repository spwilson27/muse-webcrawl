Fan vault

A fan vault is a form of vault used in the Gothic style of architecture, in which the ribs are all of the same curve and spaced equidistantly, in a manner resembling a fan. It is an English innovation, characteristic of the Perpendicular Gothic style.

Development

The earliest surviving example is in the cloisters of Gloucester Cathedral (c. 1351–1377), developed from earlier rib vaulting in which the conoids of ribs of equal curvature create a unified, concave surface. Fan vaulting dispenses with the functional distinction between ribs and webs: the whole vault is a carved stone shell patterned with radiating ribs and blind tracery panels.

Notable examples

Famous fan vaults include the chapel of King's College, Cambridge — the largest fan vault in the world — Henry VII's Lady Chapel at Westminster Abbey, St George's Chapel at Windsor Castle, the Divinity School at Oxford, and the retrochoir of Peterborough Cathedral. The style remained largely confined to England, where it was also revived in Gothic Revival buildings.
