C-command

In generative linguistics, c-command (constituent command) is a fundamental structural relation between nodes in a syntactic tree, introduced by Tanya Reinhart in 1976. It underpins theories of binding, scope, and anaphora within government and binding theory and the Minimalist Program.

Definition

In its standard formulation, a node A c-commands a node B if and only if: A does not dominate B, B does not dominate A, and the first (lowest) branching node dominating A also dominates B. Intuitively, a constituent c-commands its siblings and everything its siblings dominate.

Applications

C-command conditions Binding Principles A, B and C: for example, a pronoun must not be c-commanded by a coindexed antecedent in its local domain, while a reflexive must be so bound. Variants include m-command and asymmetric c-command, the latter central to Richard Kayne's antisymmetry theory.
