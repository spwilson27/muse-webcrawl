The Pavilion from the Pavilion Gardens

The Royal Pavilion, also known as the Brighton Pavilion, is a former royal residence in Brighton, England. Built in stages between 1787 and 1823 as a seaside retreat for George, Prince of Wales — later Prince Regent and King George IV — it is famous for its extravagant Indo-Saracenic exterior of domes, minarets and pinnacles designed by John Nash, and for interiors that mix Chinese and Indian decorative schemes in a riot of colour, dragons and bamboo-style staircases.

History

The Prince first lodged in Brighton in 1783, attracted by the fashionable sea-bathing resort and its distance from the court of his father, George III. He leased, then bought, a farmhouse on the Steine and commissioned Henry Holland to enlarge it into a neoclassical Marine Pavilion (1787–95). Between 1801 and 1803 William Porden built the vast stables and riding school in an Indian style whose great dome foreshadowed the palace itself. From 1815 to 1822 Nash transformed the building into the present oriental fantasy, its cast-iron and stucco exterior concealing innovative iron-frame construction.

Inside, designers Frederick Crace and Robert Jones created the extraordinary state rooms: the Banqueting Room, hung with Chinese-style paintings and lit by a huge dragon-held chandelier weighing a ton; the Music Room, with its serpents, dragons and red-and-gold lacquered walls; and the Great Kitchen, fitted with the latest equipment and palm-tree iron columns.

Later use

William IV also used the Pavilion, but Queen Victoria disliked its lack of privacy and sold it to the town of Brighton in 1850 for £53,000. Stripped of its contents for other palaces, the building served as assembly rooms and, during the First World War, as a hospital for Indian soldiers — a use commemorated by the nearby Chattri memorial. A long programme of restoration, aided by the return of many original pieces on loan from the Royal Collection, has recreated the Regency interiors, and the Pavilion is now a museum and the centrepiece of Brighton's cultural quarter, flanked by the Brighton Museum and the Dome concert halls built in the former stables.
