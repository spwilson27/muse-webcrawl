S. H. Raza

Syed Haider Raza (22 February 1922 – 23 July 2016), popularly known as S. H. Raza, was an Indian painter who lived and worked in France for much of his career while maintaining strong ties with India. He was a founding member of the Bombay Progressive Artists' Group and became one of India's most celebrated modern artists.

Early life and career

Raza was born in Babaria, Central Provinces (now Madhya Pradesh). He studied at the Nagpur School of Art and the Sir J. J. School of Art in Bombay. In 1947 he co-founded the Progressive Artists' Group with artists including F. N. Souza and M. F. Husain, seeking to break with the revivalist nationalism of the Bengal school and engage with international modernism.

In 1950 Raza moved to Paris, where he studied at the École nationale supérieure des Beaux-Arts. He won the Prix de la Critique in 1956, the first non-French artist to receive the award. He married the French artist Janine Mongillat in 1959.

The Bindu

From the late 1970s Raza's work increasingly drew on Indian philosophy and cosmology. His signature motif became the Bindu — a point or dot symbolizing the seed of creation and the concentration of divine energy — around which he composed geometric abstractions in vivid color. Works such as Saurashtra (1983) and La Terre (1985) exemplify this mature style.

Honors and legacy

Raza received the Padma Shri (1981), Padma Bhushan (2007), and Padma Vibhushan (2013), among India's highest civilian honors, as well as the French Legion of Honour. His paintings rank among the most expensive by an Indian artist at auction. He returned to India in 2010 and died in New Delhi in 2016.
