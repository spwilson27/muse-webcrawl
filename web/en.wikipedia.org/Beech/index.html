Beech

Beech (Fagus) is a genus of deciduous trees in the family Fagaceae, native to temperate and subtropical regions of Europe, Asia and North America. Beeches are large, long-lived trees with smooth grey bark, oval leaves and small triangular nuts known as beechmast, borne in bristly husks. The European beech (Fagus sylvatica) and American beech (Fagus grandifolia) are the best-known species.

Description and ecology

Beeches can grow 30–40 metres tall, with dense canopies that cast deep shade, often suppressing undergrowth. Their shallow roots and thin bark make them sensitive to drought and sun-scorch. Beechmast is an important food for birds, mammals and insects, produced in large quantities in irregular "mast years". Beech woodland supports distinctive fungi and invertebrate communities. Diseases include beech bark disease, a scale-insect and fungus complex that has devastated North American stands.

Uses

Beech timber is hard, pale and fine-grained, used for furniture, flooring, tool handles, plywood and firewood; it is also the traditional wood for smoking some foods and for the flavouring of Budweiser beer, which is aged on beechwood chips. Copper and weeping beech cultivars are popular ornamental trees, and beech hedging is widely planted. In Germanic tradition, beech bark and tablets were associated with early writing — the word "book" is sometimes linked to the Old English bōc, beech.
