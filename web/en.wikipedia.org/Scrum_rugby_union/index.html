Scrum (rugby)

The scrum is a contested restart of play in both codes of rugby football, rugby union and rugby league. Each team's forwards pack together in formation and push against the opposing pack while competing for possession of the ball, which is fed into the space between the front rows.

In rugby union

In rugby union the scrum is a genuine contest and a major set piece. Eight forwards from each side bind together and drive against each other; the scrum-half of the team in possession puts the ball in, and the hooker strikes for it. Scrums are awarded for knock-ons, forward passes, and certain other minor offences. See the main article Scrum (rugby union).

In rugby league

Rugby league scrums were once contested in a similar way, but since the late twentieth century they have become largely ceremonial: the team awarded the scrum almost always retains possession, and the scrum mainly serves to restart play and temporarily tie up the forwards so the backs have room to attack. League scrums traditionally involved six forwards per side, and rules introduced in recent years have allowed teams more flexibility in scrum formation and positioning.

History

The scrum evolved from the mass scrummages of early football at Rugby School, from which both codes descend. Its form was gradually codified as laws distinguished the set scrum from open play, and it survived the 1895 split between the Northern Union (rugby league) and rugby union, developing differently in each code thereafter.

Safety

The forces generated in a union scrum can exceed several tonnes across the front row, and the phase has been a focus of law changes and coaching standards intended to prevent neck and spinal injuries, particularly in the amateur game.
