Par (score)

In golf, par is the predetermined number of strokes that a scratch (or zero-handicap) golfer should require to complete a hole, a round, or a tournament. It is the standard against which scores are measured and described.

Determining par

Par for a hole is based mainly on its effective playing length: holes up to about 260 yards (men) are typically par 3, mid-length holes par 4, and long holes par 5; par 6 holes exist but are rare. A regulation 18-hole course usually has a total par between 70 and 72. Par assumes two putts on the green plus the strokes needed to reach it in "regulation".

Scoring terms

Scores relative to par have traditional names. One stroke under par on a hole is a birdie, two under an eagle, three under an albatross (double eagle). One over par is a bogey, two over a double bogey. A rare hole-in-one on a par 5 is sometimes called a condor. Overall tournament scores are reported as strokes relative to par, such as "−12" for twelve under.

History

The word derives from the Latin par, "equal". It was first applied to golf in the late 19th century; the term "bogey" originally denoted the expected score, before par was standardised by governing bodies such as the USGA and The R&A.
