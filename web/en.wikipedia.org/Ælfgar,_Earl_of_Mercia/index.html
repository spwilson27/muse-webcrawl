Ælfgar (died c. 1062) was the son of Leofric, Earl of Mercia, and his wife Godgifu (Lady Godiva). He succeeded his father as Earl of Mercia in 1057, having earlier held the earldom of East Anglia.

Career

Ælfgar was twice driven into exile. In 1055 he was outlawed, apparently without cause according to some chroniclers, and allied with Gruffydd ap Llywelyn, king of Gwynedd, and a Norse-Irish fleet to attack Hereford, defeating Earl Ralph's forces before a settlement restored him. Exiled again in 1058, he regained his position with Gruffydd's help.

Family

His sons Edwin and Morcar succeeded in the north and Midlands, while his daughter Ealdgyth married first Gruffydd ap Llywelyn and later Harold Godwinson, the last Anglo-Saxon king of England.
