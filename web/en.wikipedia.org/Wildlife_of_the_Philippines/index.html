Wildlife of the Philippines

The wildlife of the Philippines includes a significant number of endemic plant and animal species, reflecting the archipelago's long isolation and tropical setting. The country is recognised as one of the world's megadiverse countries and one of its biodiversity hotspots, yet its wildlife faces severe pressure from habitat loss, hunting, and the wildlife trade.

Fauna

The Philippines is home to around 200 species of mammals, a high proportion of them found nowhere else. Iconic species include the Philippine tarsier, one of the world's smallest primates; the tamaraw, a dwarf buffalo endemic to Mindoro; the Philippine deer; and the Philippine flying lemur (colugo). Palawan, which has Asian mainland affinities, hosts species such as the Palawan bearcat (binturong) and the Philippine pangolin.

More than 600 bird species have been recorded, with around a third endemic. The Philippine eagle, one of the largest and most powerful forest raptors in the world, is the national bird and is critically endangered. The country also supports rich marine life: its waters lie at the centre of the Coral Triangle, with some of the most diverse coral reefs on Earth, dugongs, whale sharks, and five species of sea turtle.

Flora

Philippine forests historically covered most of the archipelago and include lowland dipterocarp rainforests, montane forests, and mangroves. Thousands of plant species are endemic, including many orchids and the giant rafflesia. Forest cover has fallen dramatically since the mid-20th century, endangering much of this flora.

Conservation

Protected areas such as Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Mount Apo, and the Northern Sierra Madre Natural Park safeguard key habitats. Conservation groups work on species including the Philippine crocodile, one of the world's rarest crocodilians, and the Visayan warty pig.
