Don John of Austria

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Don John of Austria (Spanish: Don Juan de Austria, 24 February 1547 – 1 October 1578) was an illegitimate son of the Holy Roman Emperor Charles V and a Spanish military leader. He is best known for commanding the Holy League fleet that defeated the Ottoman navy at the Battle of Lepanto in 1571.

Early life

Born in Regensburg to Barbara Blomberg, a burgher's daughter, John was raised in Spain under the name Jerónimo, his parentage kept secret. Charles V recognised him in his will, and his half-brother Philip II acknowledged him as a member of the royal family, granting him the style of an infante. Educated at the University of Alcalá alongside the prince Don Carlos, he longed for a military career and in 1568 was given command of a squadron against Barbary corsairs. In 1569–1571 he directed the brutal suppression of the Morisco revolt in the Alpujarras.

Lepanto and later career

Appointed captain-general of the Holy League fleet at the age of 24, John led the combined Spanish, Venetian and papal forces to a crushing victory over the Ottoman fleet at Lepanto on 7 October 1571, one of the most celebrated naval battles of the century. In 1573 he captured Tunis, though it was soon retaken. In 1576 he became governor-general of the Spanish Netherlands, where he struggled against the Dutch Revolt, accepting the Perpetual Edict in 1577 before hostilities resumed. He died of fever (probably typhus) near Namur in 1578, aged 31, and was buried at El Escorial.
