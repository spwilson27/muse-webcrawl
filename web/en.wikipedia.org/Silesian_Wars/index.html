The Silesian Wars (German: Schlesische Kriege) were three wars fought in the mid-18th century between Prussia under Frederick the Great and Austria under Maria Theresa for control of the province of Silesia. The First (1740–1742) and Second (1744–1745) Silesian Wars formed part of the wider War of the Austrian Succession; the Third Silesian War (1756–1763) was part of the Seven Years' War.

Course of the wars

Frederick invaded Silesia in December 1740 on a flimsy dynastic claim, defeating Austria at Mollwitz (1741) and Chotusitz (1742); the Treaties of Breslau and Berlin ceded most of Silesia to Prussia. Fearing Austrian recovery, Frederick struck pre-emptively in 1744, winning at Hohenfriedberg and Soor in 1745; the Treaty of Dresden confirmed his possession. In 1756 he again pre-empted a hostile coalition by invading Saxony, opening the Seven Years' War, in which brilliant victories at Rossbach and Leuthen (1757) alternated with disasters at Kolin (1757) and Kunersdorf (1759).

Outcome

The Treaty of Hubertusburg (1763) restored the status quo ante: Prussia kept Silesia. The wars established Prussia as a European great power, inaugurated the Austro-Prussian rivalry for mastery in Germany, and made Silesia — rich in population, textiles, and later coal — the economic engine of the Prussian state.
