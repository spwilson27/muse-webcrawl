Ivan Aivazovsky

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ivan Konstantinovich Aivazovsky (29 July 1817 – 2 May 1900) was a Russian Romantic painter of Armenian descent, considered one of the greatest masters of marine art. Born in Feodosia in Crimea, he spent most of his life there and painted some 6,000 works, mostly seascapes.

Life and career

Aivazovsky studied at the Imperial Academy of Arts in Saint Petersburg and gained early recognition for his seascapes. He travelled widely in Europe and became court painter to the Russian Navy, accompanying fleets on maneuvers and painting naval battles. His works were celebrated across Europe, and he was a member of several art academies.

Art

His most famous painting, The Ninth Wave (1850), depicts survivors clinging to wreckage under a towering wave lit by dawn. Aivazovsky was known for his ability to render light playing on water and for his dramatic, luminous skies. In later life he also painted Armenian-themed and Orientalist subjects and gave generously to his native Feodosia, founding an art school and museum there.
