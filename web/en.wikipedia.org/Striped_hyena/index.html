Striped hyena

Kingdom: Animalia
Family: Hyaenidae
Genus: Hyaena
Species: H. hyaena
Conservation: Near Threatened

The striped hyena (Hyaena hyaena) is a species of hyena native to North and East Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, and the Indian subcontinent. It is the smallest of the bone-cracking hyenas and the only species of the genus Hyaena with a range extending outside Africa.

Description

The striped hyena has a coarse grey to straw-coloured coat with black vertical stripes on the torso and legs, a bushy tail, and a long mane of hair along the neck and back that can be erected to make the animal appear larger. Shoulder height is about 60–80 cm, and adults weigh roughly 22–55 kg. Its powerful jaws can crush bones, and it digests parts of prey other carnivores leave behind.

Behaviour and diet

Primarily nocturnal and typically solitary or in small family groups, the striped hyena is an opportunistic scavenger and omnivore, feeding on carrion, small mammals, birds, insects, and fruit such as dates and melons. Dens are situated in caves, burrows, or rock crevices.

Conservation

The species is listed as Near Threatened by the IUCN, with a global population estimated at under 10,000 mature individuals and declining, due to habitat loss, persecution, poaching, and declining prey and carrion availability. In folklore across its range the animal figures in beliefs about witchcraft and grave robbery, which have contributed to persecution.
