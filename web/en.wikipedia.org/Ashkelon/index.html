Ashkelon (Hebrew: אַשְׁקְלוֹן; Arabic: عَسْقَلَان, Asqalān) is a coastal city in the Southern District of Israel on the Mediterranean coast, 50 km south of Tel Aviv and 13 km north of the border with the Gaza Strip.

History

Ancient Ashkelon was one of the oldest and largest seaports in Canaan, inhabited from the Neolithic period through the Bronze and Iron Ages. It was one of the five cities of the Philistine pentapolis, mentioned in the Hebrew Bible, and prospered under Egyptian, Persian, Hellenistic and Roman rule. The city was conquered by Muslim armies in the 7th century and, as Ascalon, became a major Crusader battleground: the Battle of Ascalon (1099) followed the First Crusade's capture of Jerusalem, and Richard the Lionheart refortified it in 1192. The Mamluk sultan Baybars destroyed its port in 1270 to prevent further Crusader landings.

The Arab town of al-Majdal stood near the site until 1948. The modern Israeli city was established shortly afterwards, initially as Migdal, and grew with waves of immigration into a mid-sized coastal city with a marina, beaches and the Ashkelon National Park containing the ancient ruins.
