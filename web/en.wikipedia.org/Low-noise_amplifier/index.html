Low-noise amplifier

A low-noise amplifier (LNA) is an electronic amplifier that amplifies a very low-power signal without significantly degrading its signal-to-noise ratio. It is typically the first active stage in a radio receiver, placed as close as possible to the antenna or feed, because the noise figure of the whole receive chain is dominated by the first stage (as described by the Friis formulas for noise).

Design

An LNA is designed for minimum noise figure, adequate gain (often 10–20 dB) and good linearity, with input and output impedance matching — usually to 50 Ω. Noise figures below 1 dB are achievable with modern transistor technologies (GaAs and InP HEMTs, SiGe and CMOS devices) at microwave frequencies; cryogenic cooling lowers noise further for radio astronomy. Trade-offs include power consumption, gain, bandwidth and the ability to withstand strong out-of-band signals without compression (characterised by its P1dB and third-order intercept point, IP3).

Applications

LNAs are used in satellite communications (including the LNB on a dish), GPS receivers, mobile phones and base stations, radio telescopes, radar and wireless infrastructure — anywhere a weak antenna signal must be preserved for later demodulation.
