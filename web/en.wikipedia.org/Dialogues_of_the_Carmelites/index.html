Dialogues of the Carmelites

Dialogues des Carmélites (Dialogues of the Carmelites), FP 159, is an opera in three acts (subdivided into 12 tableaux) by Francis Poulenc, to a libretto by the composer based on the play of the same name by Georges Bernanos. It premiered at La Scala, Milan, on 26 January 1957 (in Italian translation), and in its original French at the Paris Opéra on 21 June 1957.

Background

The story is based on the Martyrs of Compiègne, sixteen Carmelite nuns who were guillotined in Paris in July 1794 during the Reign of Terror of the French Revolution. Bernanos adapted his play from the novella Die Letzte am Schafott (1931) by Gertrud von Le Fort. The opera's protagonist, Blanche de la Force, is a fictional novice who joins the convent seeking refuge from her fear of death.

Synopsis

Set in Paris and Compiègne during the Revolution, the opera follows Blanche as the revolutionary authorities suppress the religious orders. The nuns, led by Madame Lidoine, take a vow of martyrdom; in the final scene they process to the scaffold singing the Salve Regina, their voices falling silent one by one as the guillotine falls, until Blanche — who had fled — steps forward to join her sisters in death.

Music

Poulenc's score is largely diatonic and chant-inflected, built on short motifs and clear, speech-like vocal writing, and is regarded as one of the great operas of the twentieth century.
