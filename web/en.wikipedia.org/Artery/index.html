Artery

An artery is a blood vessel in humans and most animals that takes blood away from the heart to one or more parts of the body (tissues, lungs, brain etc.). Most arteries carry oxygenated blood; the exceptions are the pulmonary and umbilical arteries, which carry deoxygenated blood.

Structure

Arteries have thick, elastic walls composed of three layers: the tunica intima (inner endothelium), the tunica media (smooth muscle and elastic tissue), and the tunica externa (connective tissue). The largest artery is the aorta, which branches into progressively smaller arteries and finally arterioles, which feed capillary beds.

Function

The systemic arteries deliver oxygen-rich blood from the left ventricle to the tissues. The elasticity of arterial walls smooths the pulsatile flow produced by the heartbeat, producing the palpable pulse. Arterial blood pressure is a key clinical measurement.

Clinical significance

Diseases of the arteries include atherosclerosis, in which plaque narrows the lumen and can cause heart attacks and strokes; aneurysm, a localized dilation that may rupture; and arteritis, inflammation of the arterial wall.
