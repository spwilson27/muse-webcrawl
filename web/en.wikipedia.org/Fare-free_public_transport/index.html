Fare-free public transport

Also known asFree public transport, zero-fare public transport
FundingGeneral taxation, employer levies, other public funds
Notable examplesLuxembourg (nationwide, 2020); Tallinn, Estonia; Dunkirk, France

Fare-free public transport, also known as free public transport or zero-fare public transport, is public transport funded in full by means other than collecting fares from passengers. It may be funded by national, regional or local government through taxation, and/or by commercial sponsorship by businesses.

Rationale

Advocates argue that fare-free transport increases ridership, reduces car use and emissions, speeds boarding, removes the cost of fare collection and enforcement, and improves mobility for low-income residents. Critics note that fares can be a significant revenue source, that removing them may strain capacity without adding service, and that subsidies might achieve more if spent on frequency and coverage instead.

Examples

On 1 March 2020, Luxembourg became the first country to offer free public transport nationwide. Tallinn, the capital of Estonia, introduced free transport for residents in 2013, and Dunkirk in France did so in 2018, reporting large increases in ridership. Numerous smaller cities and towns, university systems, and resort communities operate fare-free services, and several cities have trialled free transit during events or air-quality alerts.
