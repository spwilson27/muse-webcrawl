Our Lady of Fátima

Our Lady of Fátima is a Catholic title of Mary, mother of Jesus, based on the Marian apparitions reported in 1917 by three shepherd children at the Cova da Iria in Fátima, Portugal. The three children were Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto. Beginning on 13 May 1917, the children said they saw a lady "more brilliant than the sun" who identified herself as the Lady of the Rosary and asked for prayer, penance, and devotion to her Immaculate Heart.

The apparitions and the Miracle of the Sun

The apparitions recurred on the 13th of each month until October 1917, drawing growing crowds despite the hostility of Portugal's secularist First Republic authorities. On 13 October 1917, a crowd estimated in the tens of thousands witnessed the so-called Miracle of the Sun, in which the sun appeared to dance, change colours, and plunge toward the earth—an event reported even by the anticlerical newspaper O Século. The children also reported three secrets, including a vision of hell, a prediction concerning Russia and a second world war, and a third secret, written down by Lúcia and published by the Vatican only in 2000.

Veneration

The Bishop of Leiria declared the apparitions worthy of belief in 1930. The Sanctuary of Fátima, with its great basilica and the Chapel of the Apparitions, is now one of the world's most visited Catholic pilgrimage sites, drawing millions annually. Francisco and Jacinta, who died in the influenza pandemic of 1918–1920, were canonised in 2017 by Pope Francis during the centenary celebrations; Lúcia became a Carmelite nun and died in 2005.
