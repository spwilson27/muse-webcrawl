Magnetometer

A magnetometer is a device that measures a magnetic field or magnetic dipole moment. Different types measure the direction, strength, or relative change of a field at a given point — from the ambient field of the Earth to the fantastically weak fields of the brain recorded in magnetoencephalography.

Types

Scalar magnetometers (such as proton precession, Overhauser, and optically pumped caesium instruments) measure total field strength, while vector magnetometers (such as fluxgate and induction-coil devices) measure components along particular axes. SQUID magnetometers are the most sensitive of all, able to detect fields of a few femtoteslas. The first magnetometer capable of measuring absolute intensity was invented by Carl Friedrich Gauss in 1832.

Uses

Magnetometers are used in geophysical surveys to find ore bodies and archaeological features, in spacecraft to study planetary fields, in submarines and unexploded-ordnance detection, in smartphones as electronic compasses, and in observatories that monitor the Earth's field — the same global field system whose outer reaches form the magnetosphere.
