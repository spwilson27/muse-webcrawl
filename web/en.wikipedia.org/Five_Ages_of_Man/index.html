Five Ages of Man

The Five Ages of Man is a Greek creation myth, first described in Hesiod's poem Works and Days (c. 750–650 BCE). It recounts five successive ages of humanity—Golden, Silver, Bronze, Heroic, and Iron—each created by the gods on Olympus and progressively worse than the last.

The ages

Golden Age — humans lived under Cronus in peace and plenty, dying peacefully. Silver Age — childish for 100 years, impious, destroyed by Zeus. Bronze Age — violent men of bronze who destroyed themselves. Heroic Age — the demigods and heroes of Thebes and Troy. Iron Age — Hesiod's own age of toil, strife and moral decay.

Parallels

Ovid retold the myth with four ages, and similar decline schemes appear in the Hindu yugas and the statue of Daniel.
