Digital television in the United Kingdom

Digital television in the United Kingdom is delivered through several platforms: terrestrial (Freeview), satellite (Sky and Freesat), cable (Virgin Media) and broadband-delivered services. The UK completed its digital switchover in 2012, ending analogue terrestrial broadcasts.

History

Digital terrestrial television launched in 1998 with ONdigital, later ITV Digital, which collapsed in 2002 and was replaced by Freeview. Digital satellite began with Sky Digital in 1998, and Freesat launched in 2008 as a free-to-air satellite alternative.

Switchover

The switchover programme, run by Digital UK, switched off analogue transmitters region by region between 2007 and 2012, beginning with Whitehaven in Cumbria and ending in Northern Ireland. Freed spectrum was auctioned for 4G mobile services.

Platforms

Freeview provides dozens of free channels via an aerial; subscription services from Sky and Virgin Media offer hundreds of channels, on-demand libraries and high-definition and ultra-high-definition content.
