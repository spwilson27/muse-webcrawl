Roger of Lauria (c. 1245 – 17 January 1305) was a Calabrian admiral in the service of the Crown of Aragon, regarded as one of the greatest naval commanders of the Middle Ages. Raised at the Aragonese court, he led the Aragonese-Sicilian fleet during the War of the Sicilian Vespers and a string of later campaigns, winning every major battle he fought.

Early life

Roger was born in southern Italy, son of a lord of Lauria who had served the Hohenstaufen kings of Sicily. After Charles of Anjou's conquest of the Regno and the execution of Conradin in 1268, his family fled to Aragon, where his mother became a lady-in-waiting to Queen Constance, daughter of Manfred of Sicily. Roger grew up alongside the future Peter III of Aragon and was knighted by him.

War of the Sicilian Vespers

When the Sicilian Vespers revolt of 1282 brought Peter III to Sicily, Roger was appointed admiral of the fleet. In 1283 he destroyed the Angevin fleet at the Battle of Malta. In June 1284, at the Battle of the Bay of Naples, he crushed the Angevin squadron and captured Charles of Salerno, the future Charles II of Naples. In 1285 he annihilated the French fleet at Les Formigues during Philip III's invasion of Catalonia, securing Aragonese control of the western Mediterranean.

Later career

Under James II, Roger's position was complicated by the peace negotiations with the Angevins and by quarrels over his Sicilian estates; he briefly served in opposition but was reconciled. He defeated the Sicilian fleet of Frederick III — his former sovereign — at Cape Orlando in 1299 and at Ponza in 1300, capturing Frederick's brother at the latter. Granted the island of Gerba and estates near Valencia, he retired there and died in 1305. His victories, built on the disciplined Catalan and Sicilian galley crews and Almogavar marines, made Aragon the dominant naval power of the western Mediterranean.
