Loch Lochy

LocationGreat Glen, Highland, Scotland
Length~16 km (10 miles)
Surface elevation~29 m
Part ofCaledonian Canal route

Loch Lochy (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Lochaidh) is a freshwater loch in the Great Glen of the Scottish Highlands, lying southwest of Loch Oich and northeast of Fort William. About 16 kilometres (10 miles) long, it forms the southwesternmost of the three great lochs along the Great Glen Fault, after Loch Ness and Loch Oich.

Geography and canal

The loch is traversed by the Caledonian Canal, which enters from Loch Oich through locks at Gairlochy at its southwestern end, where a short canal section and the River Lochy lead down toward Loch Linnhe at Fort William. The A82 road runs along its northwestern shore, and the small settlements of Gairlochy, Clunes, and Letterfinlay sit on its banks. The loch drains southwestward by the River Lochy.

History and folklore

The Battle of the Shirts (1544), a clan fight between the Frasers and Clanranald MacDonalds, was fought on the loch's northern shore. Like Loch Ness, Loch Lochy has its own reported monster, nicknamed "Lizzie," with sightings recorded since the nineteenth century. During the Second World War the area saw commando training based at Achnacarry Castle nearby, the original home of Commando training — the Commando Memorial at Spean Bridge overlooks the glen not far away.
