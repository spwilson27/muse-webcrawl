Sunan Bonang

Sunan Bonang (born Raden Maulana Makdum Ibrahim, c. 1465 – 1525) was one of the Wali Sanga, the nine revered saints credited with spreading Islam in Java, Indonesia. He was the son of Sunan Ampel and the elder brother of Sunan Drajat.

Biography

Sunan Bonang was born in Tuban, on the north coast of East Java, around 1465. His father, Sunan Ampel (Raden Rahmat), was himself a prominent missionary who had established a major Islamic learning centre at Ampel Denta near Surabaya. Makdum Ibrahim studied first under his father and later travelled to deepen his knowledge of Islamic scholarship, reportedly studying in Malacca and Pasai before returning to Java.

He settled at Bonang, near Lasem in present-day Central Java, from which his popular name derives. From there he conducted his missionary work (dakwah) across the northern coastal region of Java during the final decades of the Majapahit Empire and the rise of the Demak Sultanate.

Method of preaching

Sunan Bonang is remembered for adapting his teaching to local Javanese culture rather than confronting it directly. He is traditionally credited with creating or adapting the bonang, a set of small kettle gongs used in the gamelan orchestra, whose sound was used to draw crowds to hear his sermons. He is also associated with the composition of religious songs and with works of Javanese Islamic mystical literature, including the text known as the Suluk Wujil.

Death and legacy

Sunan Bonang died in 1525 on Pulau Bawean, and his tomb in Tuban remains an important pilgrimage site (ziarah) for Indonesian Muslims. His legacy is commemorated in the naming of mosques, schools, and the state Islamic university UIN Walisongo in Semarang, named collectively after the nine saints.
