Oceanids

In Greek mythology, the Oceanids (Greek: Ὠκεανίδες, Ōkeanídes) are the nymphs who were the three thousand daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. They preside over the sources of fresh water — rivers, lakes, springs, rain, and clouds — each Oceanid being associated with a particular body of water or aspect of the natural world.

The Oceanids, daughters of Oceanus, personified the fresh waters of the world.

In Hesiod

Hesiod's Theogony names dozens of Oceanids, including Styx, the eldest and most honoured, by whom the gods swore their unbreakable oaths. Other notable Oceanids include Metis (first wife of Zeus), Doris (mother of the Nereids), Electra, Eurynome, Callirhoe, Clymene, Perseis, and Tyche. Apollodorus and Hyginus give further lists, which differ in detail.

Roles

Oceanids appear throughout Greek myth: they formed the chorus in Aeschylus's Prometheus Bound, comforting the chained Titan; they attended Persephone when she was abducted by Hades; and several became mothers of heroes and gods by Zeus and other deities. Calypso, in some accounts, was counted among them.
