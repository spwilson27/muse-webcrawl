Distributive justice

Distributive justice

Field: Political philosophy, ethics, economics

Concerned with: Fair allocation of goods, resources, and burdens

Key thinkers: Aristotle, John Rawls, Robert Nozick, Amartya Sen

Distributive justice concerns the principles by which goods, resources, opportunities, and burdens are fairly distributed among the members of a society. It is a central topic in political philosophy, distinct from corrective justice (which rectifies wrongs) and procedural justice (which concerns fair processes).

Principles of distribution

Philosophers have proposed different criteria for a just distribution:

  • Equality — goods should be shared equally, or inequalities require justification.
  • Need — goods should be allocated according to need, a principle associated with Marx's maxim "from each according to his ability, to each according to his needs".
  • Desert and merit — allocation in proportion to contribution or effort.
  • Entitlement — Robert Nozick's libertarian view that holdings are just if acquired and transferred justly, regardless of the resulting pattern.

Rawls

John Rawls's A Theory of Justice (1971) argues that rational parties behind a "veil of ignorance" would choose two principles: equal basic liberties, and the difference principle, which permits social and economic inequalities only if they benefit the least advantaged. Rawls's framework dominated late-20th-century debate on distributive justice.

Other approaches

Utilitarians judge distributions by their effect on aggregate welfare. Amartya Sen and Martha Nussbaum developed the capability approach, which focuses on what people are actually able to do and be. Luck egalitarians hold that inequalities arising from brute bad luck, but not from responsible choice, should be compensated. Communitarians and feminists have critiqued abstract distribution principles for neglecting social context and care.

Applications

Distributive justice informs policy debates over taxation, welfare provision, healthcare rationing, education funding, and climate policy — including how the costs of emissions reductions should be shared between nations and generations.
