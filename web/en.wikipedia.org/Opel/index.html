Opel

Full name: Opel Automobile GmbH

Founded: 21 January 1862, Rüsselsheim am Main, Germany

Founder: Adam Opel

Headquarters: Rüsselsheim am Main

Parent: Stellantis (since 2021)

Products: Automobiles, commercial vehicles

Opel Automobile GmbH, usually shortened to Opel, is a German automobile manufacturer which has been a subsidiary of Stellantis since 16 January 2021. Before that, from 2017 to 2021, Opel and its British sister brand Vauxhall belonged to the French PSA Group, and from 1929 to 2017 they were subsidiaries of the American General Motors. Opel vehicles are sold under the Vauxhall name in the United Kingdom, and have historically been marketed as Buick, Holden, Chevrolet, and Saturn models in other markets.

History

Adam Opel founded the company in 1862 in Rüsselsheim as a manufacturer of sewing machines, and from 1886 it also became a successful bicycle maker — for a time the world's largest. Opel built its first automobile in 1899. After Adam Opel's death, his sons expanded the business; in 1924 Opel opened Germany's first mass-production assembly line, and the affordable Laubfrosch and later the Kadett made it Germany's largest carmaker. General Motors acquired a majority stake in 1929 and full ownership in 1931.

Post-war models such as the Rekord, Kapitän, GT, Manta, Ascona, and Omega established Opel across Europe. The Corsa (1982) and Astra (1991, reviving the Kadett line) became its best-known small and compact cars. GM sold Opel to PSA Group in 2017 for about €2.2 billion; after the merger of PSA with Fiat Chrysler in 2021, Opel became part of Stellantis.

Products and motorsport

Current Opel models include the Corsa, Astra, Crossland, Mokka, Grandland, Insignia, and Vivaro/Combo vans, with a rapid shift toward hybrid and battery-electric variants; Opel announced plans to become an all-electric brand in Europe. The brand's logo is the stylised lightning bolt (the "Blitz", also the name of a famous Opel truck) through a circle. Opel has a long motorsport heritage, including rallying success with the Ascona 400 and Manta 400 — Walter Röhrl won the 1982 World Rally Championship in an Opel — and touring-car racing in the DTM with the Calibra.
