The Trogonidae are the family of birds comprising the trogons and quetzals, the sole members of the order Trogoniformes. The family contains about 46 species in seven genera, distributed through tropical forests of the Americas, Africa and Asia.

Description

Trogons are brilliantly coloured birds with soft, dense plumage, often metallic green, blue, red or yellow. They are unique among birds in having a heterodactyl foot arrangement, with the third and fourth toes pointing forward and the first and second backward. They have short wings, long tails and stout bills.

Behaviour

Trogons feed largely on insects and fruit, perching motionlessly for long periods before sallying out to snatch prey. They nest in cavities in rotten wood or termite nests. The resplendent quetzal (Pharomachrus mocinno) of Central America is the best-known member and the national bird of Guatemala.
