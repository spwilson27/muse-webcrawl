Xenophobia

EtymologyGreek xenos (stranger, guest) + phobos (fear)
DefinitionFear or prejudice against people from other countries or cultures
Related conceptsRacism, nativism, ethnocentrism

Xenophobia is the fear or dislike of people from other countries, or of that which is perceived to be foreign or strange. It typically manifests as prejudice toward immigrants, refugees, or cultural groups regarded as outsiders, and may be expressed through discriminatory attitudes, rhetoric, policies, or violence.

Definitions and usage

Dictionary definitions commonly describe xenophobia as an irrational fear or hatred of foreigners. Scholars distinguish it from racism — hostility based on perceived race or ethnicity — while noting the two often overlap and reinforce one another. In some fields, such as evolutionary psychology, the term is also used for aversion responses in animals toward unfamiliar individuals.

History

Hostility toward outsiders is documented throughout history, from ancient Greek distinctions between Greeks and "barbarians" to modern anti-immigration movements. Xenophobic sentiment often intensifies during economic downturns, epidemics, and wars, when newcomers are scapegoated. Notable episodes include the Chinese Exclusion Act in the United States, periodic xenophobic attacks in South Africa, and discrimination against Asian communities during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Responses

International human rights law, including conventions administered by the United Nations, addresses discrimination based on national origin, and the International Organization for Migration promotes the term as a guide for policy advocacy against anti-migrant prejudice.
