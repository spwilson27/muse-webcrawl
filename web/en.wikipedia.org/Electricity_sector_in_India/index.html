Electricity sector in India
Installed capacity: over 440 GW (mid-2020s)
Largest source: Coal
Grid: National Grid, synchronized since 2013
Access: near-universal household electrification

The electricity sector in India is the world's third largest by generation and consumption, after China and the United States. India has an installed generation capacity exceeding 440 gigawatts, with coal supplying the largest share of electricity, followed by solar, hydroelectric, wind, natural gas, and nuclear power.

History

The first demonstration of electric light in India took place in Calcutta in 1879, and the Darjeeling and Sidrapong hydroelectric stations of the 1890s were among Asia's earliest. After independence in 1947, capacity was only about 1.4 GW; state electricity boards drove expansion under five-year plans. The Electricity Act 2003 restructured the sector, delicensing generation and promoting competition and open access.

Generation mix and renewables

Coal still provides roughly 70 percent of generation, but India has set targets of 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030 and net zero by 2070. Solar capacity has grown rapidly through the National Solar Mission and large solar parks such as Bhadla in Rajasthan; wind is concentrated in Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, and Maharashtra. Major public generators include NTPC, the country's largest power company.

Grid and access

Since 31 December 2013 the regional grids have operated as one synchronized National Grid — "one nation, one grid, one frequency" — managed by the Grid Controller of India. The Saubhagya scheme achieved near-universal household electrification by 2019, though distribution companies (DISCOMs) remain financially strained by losses and subsidized tariffs.

Challenges

Key issues include DISCOM debt (addressed by the UDAY scheme), transmission losses, coal supply, air pollution, and integrating variable renewables while meeting peaks driven by air conditioning and agriculture.
