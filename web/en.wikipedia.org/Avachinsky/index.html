Avachinsky

Avachinsky, one of the "home volcanoes" overlooking Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky.

Avachinsky (Russian: Авачинская сопка, Avachinskaya sopka) is an active stratovolcano on the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East. It rises to 2,741 metres (8,992 ft) and, with its neighbour Koryaksky, dominates the skyline of the regional capital, Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky, some 25 kilometres away.

Geology

Avachinsky sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, where the Pacific Plate subducts beneath the Okhotsk Plate. The modern cone grew inside a large horseshoe-shaped caldera left by a catastrophic eruption about 30,000–40,000 years ago that deposited ignimbrites over the area now occupied by the city. The summit crater, roughly 400 metres wide, contains an active lava dome formed after the 1991 eruption.

Eruptive history

Historical eruptions are recorded from 1737 onwards. The largest recent events occurred in 1827, 1901, 1926–27, 1938 and 1945; in January 1991 a short explosive eruption filled much of the crater with a new lava dome but caused little damage. The 1945 eruption was one of the volcano's largest of the twentieth century. Because of its proximity to a major population centre, Avachinsky was designated a Decade Volcano by the International Association of Volcanology and Chemistry of the Earth's Interior, and it is monitored by the Kamchatka Volcanic Eruption Response Team.

Climbing

The volcano is a popular climb in summer, typically a one-day ascent from a base camp reached by rough track from the city; the summit offers views across Avacha Bay and the neighbouring cones of Koryaksky, Kozelsky and Arik. It forms part of the Nalychevo Nature Park, within the UNESCO World Heritage site "Volcanoes of Kamchatka".
