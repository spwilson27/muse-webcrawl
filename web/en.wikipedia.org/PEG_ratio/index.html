PEG ratio

The price/earnings-to-growth ratio, commonly called the PEG ratio, is a valuation metric for stocks that relates a company's price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio to its expected rate of earnings growth. It is calculated as:

PEG = (Price ÷ Earnings per share) ÷ Annual EPS growth rate

Interpretation

The ratio was popularised by British investor Jim Slater and later by Peter Lynch, who suggested in One Up on Wall Street that a fairly valued company's P/E roughly equals its growth rate, giving a PEG of about 1. A PEG below 1 may indicate a stock is undervalued relative to its growth prospects, while a PEG well above 1 may suggest overvaluation.

Limitations

The PEG ratio depends on forecast growth rates, which are uncertain, and it gives misleading results for companies with very low or negative growth, for mature low-growth firms, and across industries whose typical growth rates differ greatly. It is best used as one of several valuation tools rather than in isolation.
