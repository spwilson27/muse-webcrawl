Sete Cidades

The twin lakes of Sete Cidades on São Miguel Island.

Sete Cidades is a volcanic complex and civil parish at the western end of São Miguel Island in the Azores, Portugal. It is best known for its large caldera, about five kilometres across, which contains two connected lakes — Lagoa Azul and Lagoa Verde — famous for appearing blue and green respectively, and for the village of the same name on the caldera floor.

Geography and geology

The Sete Cidades volcano is a polygenetic stratovolcano capped by a summit caldera formed by collapses in the late Pleistocene and Holocene. Within and around the caldera are younger cones, craters and lakes, including Lagoa do Canário and the Lagoa Empadadas. The differing colours of the twin lakes, which are separated by a narrow strait crossed by a bridge, result from their different depths, algae content and the way each reflects the sky and surrounding vegetation. Viewpoints on the caldera rim, notably Vista do Rei and Boca do Inferno, are among the most visited sights in the Azores.

Legend and settlement

Local legend explains the lakes' colours through a story of a forbidden love between a princess and a shepherd, whose tears formed the blue and green lakes. The parish itself is a small agricultural community within the municipality of Ponta Delgada, with pastureland occupying much of the caldera floor. Sete Cidades is frequently listed among the natural landmarks of Portugal and is a protected landscape within the São Miguel nature park system.
