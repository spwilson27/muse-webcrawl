Coca-Cola, or Coke, is a carbonated soft drink manufactured by the Coca-Cola Company. Originally marketed as a temperance drink and intended as a patent medicine, it was invented in the late 19th century by John Stith Pemberton in Atlanta, Georgia. In 1888, Pemberton sold the ownership rights to Asa Griggs Candler, a businessman, whose marketing tactics led Coca-Cola to its dominance of the global soft-drink market throughout the 20th and 21st centuries.

The drink's name refers to two of its original ingredients: coca leaves and kola nuts (a source of caffeine). The current formula of Coca-Cola remains a closely guarded trade secret; however, a variety of reported recipes and experimental recreations have been published. The secrecy around the formula has been used in marketing, and the company has only produced a handful of advertisements since the 1940s that promote this secrecy.

History

Confederate Colonel John Pemberton, wounded in the American Civil War and addicted to morphine, also had a medical degree and began a quest to find a substitute for the dangerous opiate. In 1885 at Eagles Pharmacy in Columbus, Georgia, he registered Pemberton's French Wine Coca nerve tonic. In 1886, when Atlanta and Fulton County passed prohibition legislation, Pemberton responded by developing Coca-Cola, a nonalcoholic version of the French Wine Coca. It was originally sold as a patent medicine, and Pemberton claimed it was a cure for many diseases, including morphine addiction, indigestion, nerve disorders, headaches, and impotence.

The first sales were at Jacob's Pharmacy in Atlanta, Georgia, on May 8, 1886, initially selling nine servings per day. Pemberton's bookkeeper, Frank Mason Robinson, came up with the name and chose the logo's distinctive cursive script. In 1888, Pemberton sold the rights to the drink to Asa Griggs Candler for $2,300. Candler founded the Coca-Cola Company in 1892 and aggressively promoted the product, distributing coupons for free samples and plastering the Coca-Cola logo on merchandise such as calendars, clocks, and scales.

Formula

The Coca-Cola formula is a trade secret. The original copy of the formula is held in a vault at the World of Coca-Cola museum in Atlanta, where it was moved in 2011 from a SunTrust Bank vault. The company protects the secrecy of its formula by omitting ingredients information from its labels and by limiting knowledge of the full formula to a small number of executives. The drink contains carbonated water, sugar (or high-fructose corn syrup depending on the market), caramel color, phosphoric acid, natural flavorings, and caffeine.

Bottling

Coca-Cola was first bottled in 1894 by Joseph A. Biedenharn in Vicksburg, Mississippi. The distinctive contour bottle, designed by the Root Glass Company of Terre Haute, Indiana, was patented in 1915 and became one of the most recognized icons in the world. Coca-Cola's bottling system, established by Benjamin Thomas and Joseph Whitehead of Chattanooga, Tennessee, allowed the company to scale production through a network of independent bottlers.

Competition and marketing

Coca-Cola's main competitor is Pepsi, produced by PepsiCo; the rivalry between the two is known as the "cola wars." Coca-Cola is credited with popularizing the modern image of Santa Claus through its advertising campaigns beginning in the 1930s, featuring illustrations by Haddon Sundblom. The company's 1971 "I'd Like to Teach the World to Sing" advertisement became one of the most famous in advertising history. In 1985, the company introduced New Coke, a reformulation that provoked a consumer backlash and was withdrawn in favor of the original formula within months.
