Winchester Repeating Arms Company was an American manufacturer of repeating firearms and ammunition, founded in 1866 by Oliver Winchester in New Haven, Connecticut. Its lever-action rifles became icons of the American West.

History

Evolving from the New Haven Arms Company and its Henry rifle, Winchester's Model 1866 and Model 1873 ("The Gun That Won the West") made the company dominant in the repeating-rifle market. The Model 1894, designed by John Browning, became the best-selling sporting rifle of its era, and the bolt-action Model 70 (1936) was famed as "The Rifleman's Rifle." Financial troubles led to sale to employees in 1931 and then to the Olin Corporation; the New Haven plant closed in 2006, and the Winchester name continues under license on firearms made elsewhere.
