Pribilof Islands

Location: Bering Sea, Alaska, United States
Major islands: St. Paul, St. George
Population: about 600

The Pribilof Islands (formerly the Northern Fur Seal Islands) are a group of four volcanic islands in the Bering Sea, lying about 300 miles (480 km) from mainland Alaska. The two inhabited islands are St. Paul (population about 480) and St. George (population about 100); the smaller Otter Island and Walrus Island are uninhabited. The resident population is predominantly Alaska Native, mainly Aleut (Unangan).

History

The islands were sighted in 1786 by the Russian navigator Gavriil Pribylov, who was searching for the fur seal breeding grounds described by earlier explorers; the group was named in his honour. Russian traders forcibly relocated Aleut hunters from the Aleutian Islands to harvest northern fur seals, whose pelts were extremely valuable. After the Alaska Purchase of 1867 the islands passed to the United States, and the commercial sealing industry was regulated under lease arrangements. The North Pacific Fur Seal Convention of 1911, among the first international wildlife conservation treaties, ended pelagic sealing and divided the regulated land harvest among the signatories.

Wildlife

The Pribilofs host the largest breeding colonies of northern fur seals in the world — historically over a million animals, though the population has declined substantially in recent decades. The islands' sea cliffs support enormous seabird colonies, including thick-billed murres, auklets, puffins, kittiwakes and northern fulmars, making the group a renowned birdwatching destination; more than 200 bird species have been recorded, including rare Siberian vagrants. Arctic foxes were introduced for fur farming and remain present.

Economy

Fishing — especially snow crab and halibut processing on St. Paul — and government services dominate the modern economy, supplemented by a regulated subsistence fur seal harvest by island residents and a small amount of wildlife tourism.
