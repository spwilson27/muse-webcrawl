Alternative weekly

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An alternative weekly is a newspaper published, typically weekly, as an alternative to a city's mainstream daily press. Alt-weeklies are usually tabloid-sized, distributed free, and supported by advertising. They are known for long-form and investigative journalism, advocacy and muckraking reporting, and extensive coverage of local arts, music, and nightlife that dailies often neglect.

History

The format grew out of the underground press of the 1960s. The Village Voice, founded in New York in 1955, became the model, followed by papers such as the LA Weekly, Boston Phoenix, and Chicago Reader. The Association of Alternative Newsmedia represents the industry in North America.

Decline

Since the 2000s, the loss of classified and local advertising to the internet has closed or shrunk many alt-weeklies; the Village Voice ended its print edition in 2017.
