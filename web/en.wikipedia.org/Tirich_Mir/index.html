Tirich Mir is the highest mountain of the Hindu Kush range and the highest mountain in the world outside of the Himalaya–Karakoram range. It rises to 7,708 metres (25,289 ft) above sea level in the Chitral District of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, Pakistan, close to the border with Afghanistan's Wakhan Corridor.

Geography

Tirich Mir overlooks the town of Chitral and can be seen from the main bazaar. The mountain massif includes several subsidiary summits, among them Tirich Mir East (7,692 m) and Tirich Mir West. Large glaciers, including the Upper and Lower Tirich glaciers, drain its flanks. In local Khowar tradition the mountain is said to be the abode of spirits and fairies, and its name is sometimes explained as deriving from the Chitrali words for "king" and darkness or shadow.

Climbing history

The first ascent was made on 21 July 1950 by a Norwegian expedition led by Arne Næss. The summit party included Per Kvernberg and Henry Berg; the expedition's account was later described in Næss's book about the climb. The mountain has since been climbed by several routes, though it remains far less frequented than the eight-thousanders of the Himalaya and Karakoram.
