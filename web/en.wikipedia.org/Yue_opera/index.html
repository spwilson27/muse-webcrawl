Yue opera (Chinese: 越剧; pinyin: Yuèjù), also called Yueju opera, is a form of Chinese opera that originated in Shengzhou, Zhejiang province, in the early twentieth century. It is the second most popular opera genre in China and is especially noted for its gentle, lyrical singing, romantic storylines, and troupes in which women traditionally play all roles, including male characters.

History

Yue opera grew out of folk ballad-singing and storytelling in the Shengxian (now Shengzhou) area. Early troupes of male performers developed the form in Shanghai in the 1920s; in the 1930s and 1940s, all-female troupes came to dominate, and performers such as Yuan Xuefen reformed the genre, introducing realistic scenery, modern staging and new plays. After 1949 it was promoted by the People's Republic, and the Yue opera film of The Butterfly Lovers became a classic.

Style and repertoire

The music emphasises soft, flowing melody suited to tales of love and loyalty. Standard repertoire includes The Butterfly Lovers (Liang Shanbo yu Zhu Yingtai), Dream of the Red Chamber, The Peony Pavilion adaptations, and He Wenxiu. Female performers specialise in role types such as the young male scholar (xiaosheng) and the young woman (huadan).
