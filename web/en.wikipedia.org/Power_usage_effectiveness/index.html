Power usage effectiveness

Abbreviation: PUE

Definition: Total facility energy ÷ IT equipment energy

Ideal value: 1.0

Developed by: The Green Grid

Power usage effectiveness (PUE) is a ratio that describes how efficiently a computer data center uses energy; specifically, how much energy is used by the computing equipment in contrast to cooling and other overhead that supports the equipment. It is defined as the total amount of energy used by the facility divided by the energy delivered to computing equipment.

An ideal PUE is 1.0: all energy consumed is used by the IT equipment itself. In practice, cooling systems, power distribution losses, lighting and security consume additional energy; average enterprise data centres have historically reported PUE values around 1.5–2.0, while state-of-the-art hyperscale facilities report values near 1.1.

History and criticism

PUE was developed by the industry consortium The Green Grid and later adopted as an ISO/IEC standard (30134-2). Critics note that PUE says nothing about the efficiency of the IT equipment itself, can be manipulated by measurement choices, and ignores water usage and carbon intensity — concerns addressed by companion metrics such as WUE (water usage effectiveness) and CUE (carbon usage effectiveness). Its reciprocal, DCiE (data center infrastructure efficiency), expresses the same information as a percentage.
