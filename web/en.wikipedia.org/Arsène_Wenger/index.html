Arsène Wenger

Arsène Charles Ernest Wenger (born 22 October 1949) is a French football manager and former player, best known for managing Arsenal from 1996 to 2018 — the longest-serving and most successful manager in the club's history in terms of trophies won. Nicknamed "Le Professeur", he is credited with transforming English football through modern methods of training, diet, sports science and scouting, and with introducing a fluid, passing style of play. Since 2019 he has been FIFA's Chief of Global Football Development.

Career before Arsenal

Wenger had a modest playing career in French football, chiefly as an amateur while studying economics, and began coaching at Nancy before winning the French league title with Monaco in 1988. After a successful spell in Japan with Nagoya Grampus Eight, he was appointed Arsenal manager in October 1996, at the time a little-known figure in England.

Arsenal years

Wenger won the league-and-cup Double in his first full season (1997–98) and again in 2001–02. His 2003–04 team, the "Invincibles", completed the Premier League season unbeaten — a feat unmatched in the modern era — and Arsenal reached the 2006 UEFA Champions League final. Despite tighter budgets following the move to the Emirates Stadium, Wenger kept Arsenal in the Champions League for twenty consecutive seasons and won three further FA Cups in 2014, 2015 and 2017 before stepping down in 2018 after 22 years and 1,235 matches in charge.
