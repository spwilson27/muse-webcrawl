British Phonographic Industry

The British Phonographic Industry (BPI) is the trade association of the British recorded music industry, representing the three major labels — Universal, Sony, and Warner — together with more than 500 independent labels and music businesses. Established in 1973, it lobbies government on copyright and music policy, campaigns against piracy, and promotes British music at home and abroad. Since 2022 it has traded publicly simply as "BPI."

Activities

The BPI organises the Brit Awards and co-owns the Mercury Prize, and it administers the UK's music certification programme, awarding Silver, Gold, Platinum, and multi-Platinum discs for singles and albums. Award thresholds date from 1973 for albums and were extended to singles in 1979; since 2014 streaming has counted toward certifications, with 100 streams equivalent to one download for singles and albums recognised from 2015. The BPI compiles recorded music revenue statistics through its annual yearbook and works with the Official Charts Company, which it co-owns, on the UK's official charts.

Policy work

The association has been a prominent voice on copyright enforcement, pressing for the graduated-response provisions of the Digital Economy Act 2010, site-blocking injunctions against file-sharing services, and, more recently, rules on music streaming economics and generative AI. It also runs the Music Export Growth Scheme to support independent artists' international tours.
