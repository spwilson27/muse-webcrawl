Circumscribed circle (circumcircle)

In geometry, the circumscribed circle or circumcircle of a triangle is a circle that passes through all three vertices of the triangle. Its centre is called the circumcenter and its radius the circumradius. Every triangle has exactly one circumcircle. A polygon that has a circumscribed circle passing through all its vertices is called a cyclic polygon.

Circumcenter

The circumcenter is the point of intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of the triangle's sides. For an acute triangle it lies inside the triangle; for a right triangle it is the midpoint of the hypotenuse (so the hypotenuse is a diameter of the circumcircle, by Thales's theorem); for an obtuse triangle it lies outside. In trilinear coordinates the circumcenter is cos A : cos B : cos C.

Circumradius formulas

If the sides of the triangle are a, b, c and its area is Δ, the circumradius is R = abc / (4Δ). By the law of sines, a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C = 2R. The circumradius and inradius are related in Euler's formula for a triangle, OI² = R(R − 2r), where O is the circumcenter and I the incenter.

Related points

The circumcenter, centroid, and orthocenter of a triangle are collinear on the Euler line, and the circumcircle is closely related to the nine-point circle, whose radius is half the circumradius.
