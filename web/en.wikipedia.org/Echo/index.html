Echo

An echo is a reflection of sound that arrives at the listener with a delay after the direct sound. The delay is proportional to the distance of the reflecting surface from the source and the listener. Typical examples are the echo produced by the bottom of a well, by a building, or by the walls of an enclosed room.

Acoustic phenomenon

For a sound to produce an echo distinct from the original, the reflected sound must arrive at least roughly 50–100 milliseconds later, corresponding to a reflecting surface at least about 17 metres (56 ft) away, given the speed of sound in air of about 343 m/s. True echoes are single reflections; multiple rapid reflections produce reverberation instead. Acoustic echoes are exploited in sonar, echolocation by animals such as bats and dolphins, and medical and industrial ultrasonography.

Mythology and other uses

In Greek mythology, Echo was a mountain nymph who, cursed to repeat only the last words spoken to her, pined for Narcissus. The word is used metaphorically in many fields, from echo chambers in media to the Amazon Echo smart speaker line, and in computing for the echo command and the ECHO protocol.
