Jingkang Incident

The Jingkang Incident (Chinese: 靖康事變; pinyin: Jìngkāng shìbiàn), also known as the humiliation of Jingkang, took place in 1127 when Jin forces captured the Northern Song capital of Kaifeng (Bianjing). The Jin, a Jurchen-led dynasty, seized the Song emperors Huizong and Qinzong, along with thousands of members of the imperial clan, officials, artisans, and treasure, and carried them north into captivity.

Background and events

The Song had allied with the Jin against the Liao dynasty, but the alliance turned to war when the Jin judged the Song weak and duplicitous. Jin armies first besieged Kaifeng in 1126 and withdrew for a huge indemnity; they returned later that year, breached the walls in January 1127, and looted the city. Emperor Qinzong and his father, the retired Emperor Huizong, were deposed and taken north, where both died in captivity.

Aftermath

The fall of Kaifeng ended the Northern Song. Qinzong's half-brother Zhao Gou escaped south and re-established the dynasty as Emperor Gaozong of the Southern Song, with its capital eventually at Lin'an (Hangzhou). The memory of Jingkang remained a powerful symbol of national humiliation in later Chinese historiography and literature, including the story of the general Yue Fei.
