Styx

In Greek mythology, the Styx (Greek: Στύξ, "shuddering" or "hated") is a river that forms the boundary between Earth (Gaia) and the Underworld (Hades). It is one of the five rivers of the Greek underworld, alongside the Acheron, Lethe, Phlegethon and Cocytus. The Styx is also the name of the goddess of the river, a daughter of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys, who personified the waters.

By the waters of the Styx the gods swore their most binding oaths: a god who broke an oath by the Styx lay insensible for a year and was exiled from the councils of Olympus for nine more. In later tradition the souls of the dead were ferried across the Styx (or Acheron) by Charon, the ferryman, for an obol placed in the mouth of the deceased.

Mythology

Styx was the eldest of the Oceanids and, according to Hesiod's Theogony, was honoured by Zeus with the privilege that her waters should be the great oath of the gods, in reward for her support — and that of her children Zelus, Nike, Kratos and Bia — in the Titanomachy. Her dwelling was a lofty house supported by silver pillars, to which Iris flew to fetch the oath-water in a golden jug.

The Styx also appears in the myth of Achilles: in later (post-Homeric) tradition, his mother Thetis dipped the infant Achilles in the river to make him invulnerable, holding him by the heel — the one spot that remained vulnerable, giving rise to the expression "Achilles' heel".

Real-world river

The ancient Arcadian river Mavroneri (the ancient River Styx near Nonacris) was believed to be deadly poisonous, and its waters were said to dissolve every material except a horse's hoof — a legend repeated in stories of the death of Alexander the Great. Dante placed the Styx as the fifth circle of Hell in the Inferno, a marsh in which the wrathful and sullen are punished.
