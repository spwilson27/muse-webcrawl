Lelystad

Lelystad is a municipality and the capital city of the Dutch province of Flevoland. Founded in 1967 on land reclaimed from the IJsselmeer, it is named after Cornelis Lely, the engineer who designed the Afsluitdijk and the Zuiderzee Works that made the reclamation possible. Lelystad lies about five to six metres below sea level and has a population of around 83,000.

History

The Eastern Flevoland polder was drained in 1957, and Lelystad was planned as its central city, designed on modernist lines with separated traffic systems and green districts. The first residents arrived in 1967. Although originally intended to grow into a major regional centre — it was even considered as a site for national institutions — growth was slower than planned because the Markerwaard polder was never built and road links arrived only in the 1980s. Lelystad became capital of the new province of Flevoland in 1986 and gained a railway station in 1988.

Attractions and economy

Lelystad is home to the Bataviawerf shipyard, where a full-scale replica of the Dutch East India Company ship Batavia (1628) was built, the Batavia Stad outlet shopping centre, the Aviodrome aviation museum at Lelystad Airport, and the Nieuw Land heritage centre. Near the city lies the Oostvaardersplassen nature reserve, a noted wetland of rewilded grazers, and Antony Gormley's 26-metre sculpture Exposure stands on the dike. The Houtribdijk separates the IJsselmeer from the Markermeer and carries the road to Enkhuizen.
