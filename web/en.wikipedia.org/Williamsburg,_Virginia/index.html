Williamsburg is an independent city in Virginia, United States. It lies on the Virginia Peninsula between the James and York rivers in the Hampton Roads metropolitan area. The city is best known for Colonial Williamsburg, the restored colonial capital, and for the College of William & Mary, founded in 1693, the second-oldest institution of higher education in the United States.

History

The site began as Middle Plantation, a fortified settlement of 1632 in the Virginia Colony. In 1699, after the state house at Jamestown burned, the capital of the colony was moved there and the town was renamed Williamsburg in honour of King William III. Laid out under Governor Francis Nicholson with a broad main street, Duke of Gloucester Street, anchored by the Capitol and the new college building (the Wren Building), Williamsburg served as the political, social and cultural capital of Britain's largest American colony. Patrick Henry, Thomas Jefferson, George Washington and other revolutionary figures debated and legislated there; in May 1776 the Virginia Convention meeting in Williamsburg instructed its delegates to propose American independence.

When the capital moved to Richmond in 1780, Williamsburg entered a long quiet period as a college and county town. During the Civil War the Battle of Williamsburg was fought nearby in May 1862, and the city was occupied by Union forces.

Colonial Williamsburg

In the 1920s the Reverend W. A. R. Goodwin persuaded philanthropist John D. Rockefeller Jr. to fund the restoration of the colonial town. Over the following decades hundreds of post-colonial buildings were demolished, 88 original buildings were restored, and major structures such as the Capitol and Governor's Palace were reconstructed on their foundations. The resulting 301-acre living-history museum, operated by the Colonial Williamsburg Foundation, employs historical interpreters in period dress demonstrating trades and daily life, and anchors the "Historic Triangle" with Jamestown and Yorktown, connected by the Colonial Parkway.

Economy and education

Tourism dominates the economy, supplemented by the College of William & Mary, whose students make up a large share of the population, and by Busch Gardens Williamsburg theme park and other attractions in surrounding James City and York counties. The city's population was about 15,425 at the 2020 census, a figure that fluctuates with the student body.
