Doha Agreement (2020)

Signed29 February 2020, Doha, Qatar
PartiesUnited States and the Taliban
SignatoriesZalmay Khalilzad; Abdul Ghani Baradar

The Doha Agreement, formally the Agreement for Bringing Peace to Afghanistan, is a peace agreement signed on 29 February 2020 in Doha, Qatar, between the United States and the Taliban. It set the terms for the withdrawal of US and NATO forces from Afghanistan and for Taliban commitments on counterterrorism, paving the way for intra-Afghan negotiations.

Negotiations

Talks between US envoy Zalmay Khalilzad and Taliban negotiators led by Abdul Ghani Baradar took place in Doha over 2018–2020, excluding the Afghan government, which the Taliban refused to recognise. A near-final text collapsed in September 2019 after a Taliban attack killed a US soldier, but negotiations resumed and produced the final agreement.

Terms

The United States committed to withdraw all forces within 14 months, subject to Taliban compliance, and to work toward the release of Taliban prisoners and the lifting of sanctions. The Taliban committed to prevent al-Qaeda and other groups from using Afghan soil to threaten the United States and its allies, and to enter talks with other Afghan parties beginning in March 2020.

Aftermath

Intra-Afghan talks in Doha stalled, violence continued, and the withdrawal proceeded under the Biden administration in 2021. Taliban offensives culminated in the fall of Kabul on 15 August 2021, days before the final US departure, restoring the Taliban's Islamic Emirate. The agreement remains debated: supporters credit it with ending America's longest war; critics argue its terms weakened the Afghan republic and freed Taliban prisoners without securing a political settlement.
