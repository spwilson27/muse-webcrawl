Allan Pinkerton (25 August 1819 – 1 July 1884) was a Scottish-American cooper, abolitionist, detective, and spy, best known for creating the Pinkerton National Detective Agency, the first detective agency in the United States.

Early life

Born in Glasgow, Pinkerton emigrated to the United States in 1842 and settled near Chicago, working as a cooper. His accidental discovery of a gang of counterfeiters led to work as a deputy sheriff and then as Chicago's first police detective. In 1850 he founded his own agency, whose logo — an unblinking eye with the motto "We Never Sleep" — gave rise to the term "private eye".

Civil War and later career

Pinkerton agents foiled the Baltimore Plot to assassinate president-elect Abraham Lincoln in 1861, and Pinkerton served as head of the Union Intelligence Service under General George McClellan, operating under the alias Major E. J. Allen. After the war his agency pursued train robbers and outlaws such as the Reno Gang and the Molly Maguires, and published popular detective memoirs.
