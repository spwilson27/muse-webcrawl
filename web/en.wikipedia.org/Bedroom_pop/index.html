Bedroom pop is a music genre or loose descriptor for artists who record at home rather than in traditional recording spaces. The term was popularised in the late 2010s for music characterised by lo-fi production, intimate and confessional lyrics, hushed vocals, and a do-it-yourself aesthetic made possible by affordable digital audio workstations and online distribution platforms such as SoundCloud, Bandcamp, and YouTube.

Characteristics

Bedroom pop songs are typically recorded with minimal equipment — a laptop, an audio interface, a microphone, and software instruments — and often retain audible room noise, tape hiss, or unquantised performances as part of their charm. Stylistically the genre blends indie pop melodies with dream pop textures, hip hop-influenced beats, and alternative R&B. Lyrically it skews diaristic, addressing adolescence, unrequited love, anxiety, and everyday domestic detail.

History

Home recording has a long lineage, from the lo-fi movement of the 1980s and 1990s (Guided by Voices, Daniel Johnston, early Beck) through 2000s blog-era acts. The genre coalesced as a named phenomenon in the mid-to-late 2010s alongside the rise of streaming and TikTok, whose algorithms rewarded short, emotionally direct songs.

The best-known breakthrough was Clairo, whose homemade video for "Pretty Girl" (2017) went viral while she was a teenager, leading to the EP Diary 001 and the album Immunity (2019). Billie Eilish, who recorded her debut album When We All Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go? (2019) with her brother Finneas O'Connell in a small bedroom studio at their Los Angeles home, became the style's biggest commercial success. Other widely associated acts include Rex Orange County, Girl in Red, Eyedress, Cavetown, Steve Lacy, Men I Trust, Cuco, and Dayglow.

Critical discussion

Critics have debated whether "bedroom pop" describes a sound at all, or simply a mode of production and marketing — noting that many artists so labelled quickly moved into professional studios, and that the tag has been applied disproportionately to young, internet-native, often female or queer artists. The COVID-19 lockdowns of 2020, which forced recording artists of every level into home setups, further blurred the category.
