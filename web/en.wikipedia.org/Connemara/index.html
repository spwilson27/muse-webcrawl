Connemara

Connemara (Irish: Conamara) is a region on the Atlantic coast of western County Galway, in the west of Ireland. It is famed for its rugged mountains, blanket bogs, loughs and coastline, and contains one of the largest Gaeltacht (Irish-speaking) areas in the country.

Geography

Traditionally Connemara is bounded by Galway Bay to the south, Lough Corrib to the east, and Killary Harbour and the Atlantic to the north and west. Its interior is dominated by the Twelve Bens (Na Beanna Beola) and the Maumturk Mountains, with Kylemore Abbey and Connemara National Park among its best-known landmarks. The main towns are Clifden, often called the capital of Connemara, and Roundstone, Letterfrack and Carna.

Culture and economy

The region retains a strong Irish-language culture, with traditional music, sean-nós singing and maritime traditions prominent along the coast. The Connemara pony, one of Ireland's native horse breeds, takes its name from the region. Tourism, farming, fishing and the breeding of ponies underpin the local economy, while the landscape has inspired writers and artists including Oscar Wilde, who holidayed there, and the painter Paul Henry.

History

Connemara suffered severely during the Great Famine of the 1840s, and emigration shaped its modern population. Its remoteness preserved the Irish language more strongly than in most of Ireland, and Raidió na Gaeltachta and TG4, the Irish-language broadcasters, are based in the Galway Gaeltacht nearby.
