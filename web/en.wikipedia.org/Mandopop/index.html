Mandopop

Mandopop is a colloquial abbreviation for "Mandarin popular music". The subgenre is considered to have its roots with the Mandarin-language popular songs produced in the entertainment industry of 1930s Shanghai, and it developed further through the industries of Hong Kong and, especially, Taiwan from the 1970s onward. Today Mandopop is produced principally in Taiwan, mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore and Malaysia, and is consumed throughout the Chinese-speaking world and wider East and Southeast Asia.

Origins

The genre traces to the shidaiqu ("songs of the era") of 1920s–30s Shanghai, which fused Chinese folk melodies with jazz instrumentation; composers and singers such as Li Jinhui and Zhou Xuan were its pioneers. After 1949 the industry split: popular production in the mainland was subordinated to revolutionary music, while in Taiwan and Hong Kong Mandarin pop developed commercially. Teresa Teng, whose gentle ballads were heard across China despite being officially banned in the mainland for a time, became the genre's defining voice in the 1970s and 1980s.

Development

From the 1970s, Taiwan's campus folk song movement and singers such as Teresa Teng, Fong Fei-fei and Lo Ta-yu modernised the genre. The lifting of martial law in Taiwan in 1987 and China's opening up expanded the market enormously. In the 1990s and 2000s stars such as A-mei, Jay Chou, Jolin Tsai, S.H.E, Mayday and JJ Lin set the commercial standard, blending R&B, hip-hop, rock and Chinese styles. Reality television and streaming later reshaped the mainland industry, and Mandopop now coexists and competes with Cantopop and K-pop in Asian markets.

Characteristics

Mandopop emphasises melodic ballads, lyrical themes of love and nostalgia, and — distinctively — the interplay between Mandarin's lexical tones and melody, which songwriters accommodate by matching melodic contour to word tone. Karaoke culture, music videos and large arena tours form central parts of its commercial ecosystem.
