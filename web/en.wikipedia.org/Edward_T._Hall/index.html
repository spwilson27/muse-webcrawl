Edward T. Hall

Edward Twitchell Hall Jr. (May 16, 1914 – July 20, 2009) was an American anthropologist and cross-cultural researcher. He is remembered for developing the concept of proxemics — the study of human use of space — and for his analyses of cultural differences in communication, set out in books including The Silent Language (1959), The Hidden Dimension (1966), Beyond Culture (1976) and The Dance of Life (1983).

Career

Hall earned his doctorate in anthropology from Columbia University in 1942. He worked with the Foreign Service Institute training American diplomats and businesspeople for overseas postings, taught at several universities including Northwestern University, and drew on fieldwork with Navajo and Hopi communities and wartime experience in Europe and the Philippines.

Proxemics and context

Hall divided the space around individuals into intimate, personal, social and public distances, showing that preferred distances vary systematically between cultures. He also distinguished high-context cultures, where communication relies heavily on shared background and nonverbal cues, from low-context cultures, where messages are made explicit in words; and monochronic from polychronic orientations to time. These ideas strongly influenced intercultural communication studies and international business training.
