Verona is a city in the Veneto region of northern Italy, on the Adige River. One of Italy's major tourist destinations, it is famous for its remarkably preserved Roman amphitheatre, the Arena, and as the setting of Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet. The historic centre was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000.

History

Verona became a Roman colony in 89 BC and prospered as a crossroads of northern Italy. In the Middle Ages it was ruled by the Della Scala (Scaliger) family, whose tombs and castles remain landmarks, before passing to Venice in 1405. Austrian rule followed the Napoleonic era until Verona joined the Kingdom of Italy in 1866.

Main sights

The Verona Arena, built around AD 30, hosts a celebrated summer opera festival. Other sights include the Roman theatre, the Ponte Pietra, Castelvecchio and its museum, the Basilica of San Zeno, Piazza delle Erbe, and the balcony associated with Juliet at the Casa di Giulietta, a major draw for visitors despite its literary origins.

Economy

Verona is a hub for logistics, wine production (including Valpolicella and Soave from the surrounding province), marble, and trade fairs such as Vinitaly. It is also a rail and motorway junction between Milan, Venice, and the Brenner route to Austria.
