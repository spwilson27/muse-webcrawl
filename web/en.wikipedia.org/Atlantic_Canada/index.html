Atlantic Canada
Provinces: New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island
Population: approx. 2.4 million (2021)
Largest city: Halifax, Nova Scotia

Atlantic Canada, also called the Atlantic provinces or the Atlantics, is the region of Eastern Canada comprising the provinces located on the Atlantic coast: New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, and Prince Edward Island. The first three of these are also known collectively as the Maritime provinces.

Geography and history

The region is characterised by rugged coastlines, fishing communities, and the Appalachian mountain range running through its interior. It was among the first areas of North America settled by Europeans, with French Acadian settlements established in the early 1600s, and it has deep Indigenous roots among the Mi'kmaq, Wolastoqiyik, Innu, and Inuit peoples. Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, and Prince Edward Island joined Canadian Confederation in 1867 and 1873; Newfoundland, a separate dominion, joined Canada in 1949 as the last province.

Economy

Historic industries include fishing, shipbuilding, forestry, and agriculture. The collapse of the northern cod fishery in the early 1990s caused major economic disruption, especially in Newfoundland and Labrador, where offshore oil production later became a major revenue source. Tourism, seafood, and an increasingly diverse urban economy centred on Halifax are also important. The region has often had higher unemployment and lower incomes than the Canadian average, partly offset by federal equalization payments.
