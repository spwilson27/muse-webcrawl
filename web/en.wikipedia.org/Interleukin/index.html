Interleukins (ILs) are a large group of cytokines — secreted proteins and signal molecules — that were first seen to be expressed by white blood cells (leukocytes). The name "interleukin" derives from inter- ("as a means of communication") and -leukin ("leukocyte"), reflecting the early belief that they served primarily as messengers between leukocytes.

The function of the immune system largely depends on interleukins, and rare deficiencies of a number of them have been described, all featuring autoimmune diseases or immune deficiency. The majority of interleukins are synthesized by CD4+ helper T lymphocytes, as well as by monocytes, macrophages, and endothelial cells. They promote the development and differentiation of T and B lymphocytes and hematopoietic cells.

History

Interleukins were originally identified in the 1970s as soluble factors produced by leukocytes that acted on other leukocytes. The term was coined in 1979 at the Second International Lymphokine Workshop in Ermatigen, Switzerland, to rationalize the growing list of lymphocyte-activating factors. As molecular cloning advanced, it became clear that interleukins are produced by a wide variety of body cells, not just leukocytes, and that they act on many target cell types.

Classification and members

Interleukins are numbered in order of their discovery. Well-studied members include IL-1 (fever and inflammation), IL-2 (T-cell growth factor), IL-4 (B-cell class switching to IgE), IL-6 (acute-phase response), IL-7 (lymphocyte development), IL-10 (anti-inflammatory), IL-12 (Th1 differentiation), IL-17 (recruitment of neutrophils) and IL-23. Families are grouped by shared receptor subunits and sequence homology, such as the IL-1 family, the common gamma-chain family (IL-2, IL-4, IL-7, IL-9, IL-15, IL-21) and the IL-17 family.

Clinical significance

Interleukin signalling is a major drug target. Recombinant IL-2 (aldesleukin) has been used in cancer immunotherapy; blockers of IL-6 receptor (tocilizumab), IL-17 (secukinumab), IL-4/IL-13 receptor (dupilumab) and IL-23 (guselkumab) are used in rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, asthma and inflammatory bowel disease. Dysregulated interleukin production underlies cytokine storm syndromes, including severe cases of sepsis and viral infection.
