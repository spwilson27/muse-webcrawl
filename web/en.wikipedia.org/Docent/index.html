Docent

The term docent is derived from the Latin word docēns, the present active participle of docēre (to teach, to lecture). It has two principal meanings:

  1. An academic title or rank at universities in many European and some other countries
  2. A trained volunteer guide at museums, zoos, historic sites, and similar institutions, especially in the United States

Academic title

In much of Europe — including Sweden, Finland, Norway, Germany (as Dozent or Privatdozent), the Netherlands, Poland, and several other countries — docent is an academic qualification and title denoting scholarship beyond the doctoral level, roughly comparable in research achievement to the tenure requirements of an associate or full professor in the North American system. In Finland and Sweden the title of docent is conferred by a university after an evaluation of the applicant's publications and teaching competence, and historically carried the formal right to teach (venia legendi) and supervise doctoral students. In many Central and Eastern European systems, "docent" is instead a rank — a position between assistant professor and full professor — obtained after the habilitation.

Holders of the title may be expected, beyond their doctoral dissertation, to have produced a substantial further body of research before qualifying.

Museum docents

In the United States, a docent is a trained volunteer who leads tours and educational programs at museums, galleries, zoos, and historic houses. Docent training programs typically run for months and cover the institution's collections, interpretive techniques, and audience engagement. The role is a form of informal education and community volunteering distinct from paid curatorial or education staff.
