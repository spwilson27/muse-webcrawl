Pentagon Memorial

The Pentagon Memorial at dusk, with its cantilevered benches over illuminated pools.

The Pentagon Memorial, formally the National 9/11 Pentagon Memorial, is a permanent outdoor national memorial in Arlington County, Virginia, United States. It honors the 184 people who died as victims in the American Airlines Flight 77 attack on the Pentagon on September 11, 2001, as well as the five hijackers aboard the flight.

The memorial opened to the public on September 11, 2008, seven years after the attack, following a dedication ceremony attended by President George W. Bush. It is located just southwest of the Pentagon, adjacent to the building's western facade near the point of impact.

Design

The memorial was designed by architects Julie Beckman and Keith Kaseman of Kaseman Beckman Advanced Strategies, whose entry won an international design competition that drew more than 1,100 submissions. The 1.93-acre (0.78 ha) site is arranged as a field of 184 cantilevered benches, each dedicated to an individual victim.

Each bench is made of stainless steel inlaid with granite and sits above a shallow, illuminated reflecting pool. The benches are organized by the victims' ages, from the youngest victim, three-year-old Dana Falkenberg, to the oldest, 71-year-old John Yamnicky Sr. Benches are oriented according to the direction of the flight: those honoring the 59 victims aboard Flight 77 face toward the Pentagon's south facade, while those honoring the 125 victims inside the building face the opposite direction, so visitors reading the names on the end of each bench either look toward the sky or toward the Pentagon.

Features

An "Age Wall" rises along the memorial's perimeter from 3 inches to 71 inches in height, echoing the age range of the victims. A grove of paperback maple trees provides shade, and crushed gravel covers the ground. Near the entrance stands a memorial gateway inscribed with the date and time of the attack, 9:37 a.m. EDT on September 11, 2001.
