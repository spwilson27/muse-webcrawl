Istämi (also rendered Istemi, Ishtemi; Middle Chinese: Šɨʔ-tʰiəm-miᴇ; known in Byzantine sources as Dizaboulos and in Sogdian as Štmy) was the yabgu (ruler) of the western part of the First Turkic Khaganate from 552 until his death in 576. He was the younger brother of Bumin Qaghan, the founder of the khaganate, who — after overthrowing the Rouran Khaganate in 552 — assigned the western territories of the new empire to Istämi.

Westward expansion

Governing from the region of the Altai and later the Ili valley, Istämi led the Turks' expansion across the Central Asian steppe. In alliance with the Sasanian Persian king Khosrow I, he crushed the Hephthalite Empire in the 550s–560s, partitioning its lands along the Oxus (Amu Darya) river. Relations with Persia soon soured over control of the Silk Road trade, particularly the lucrative silk commerce dominated by Sogdian merchants who were Istämi's subjects and advisers.

Diplomacy with Byzantium

Seeking to bypass Persia and sell silk directly to the Roman (Byzantine) Empire, Istämi sponsored the celebrated embassy of the Sogdian merchant Maniakh to Constantinople in 568. Maniakh proposed an alliance against Persia and direct trade between the Turks and Byzantium. Emperor Justin II responded by sending the envoy Zemarchus of Cilicia to Istämi's camp in 569–571; Zemarchus's journey, recorded by the historian Menander Protector, is one of the earliest documented diplomatic contacts between a European state and the Turkic nomads of Inner Asia. The resulting alliance led to joint pressure on Persia in the Byzantine–Sasanian war of 572–591.

Legacy

Istämi's western realm, administered through subordinate yabgus, laid the foundations for the later independent Western Turkic Khaganate. His son Tardu succeeded him and eventually became qaghan of the west. Istämi is remembered as one of the architects of the first great trans-Eurasian steppe empire, which at its height stretched from Manchuria to the Black Sea steppe and reshaped the politics of Central Asia, Persia, and Byzantium.
