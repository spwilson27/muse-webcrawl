Liquid biopsy

A liquid biopsy, also known as fluid biopsy or fluid phase biopsy, is the sampling and analysis of non-solid biological tissue, primarily blood. Like a traditional biopsy, it is mainly used as a diagnostic and monitoring tool for diseases such as cancer, with the advantage of being largely non-invasive.

Principle

Tumours release circulating tumour cells (CTCs), cell-free circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA), and extracellular vesicles into the bloodstream. Sequencing and other analyses of these materials can reveal cancer-associated mutations, copy number changes and methylation patterns without the need for surgical tissue sampling.

Clinical use

Liquid biopsies are used to guide targeted therapy (for example, detecting EGFR mutations in non-small-cell lung cancer), monitor treatment response, detect minimal residual disease and emerging resistance, and investigate early cancer detection. Regulatory-approved tests include assays for specific mutations in plasma.

Limitations

Sensitivity can be limited in early-stage disease because ctDNA levels are very low, and clonal haematopoiesis can produce false-positive mutation signals.
