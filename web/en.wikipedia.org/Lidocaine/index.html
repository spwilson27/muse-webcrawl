Lidocaine

Lidocaine, also known as lignocaine, is a local anesthetic of the amino amide type and a class Ib antiarrhythmic drug. It is used to numb tissue in a specific area and to treat ventricular tachycardia.

Medical uses

Lidocaine is used for infiltration, nerve block and surface anesthesia in dentistry, minor surgery and many other procedures. Mixed with a small amount of epinephrine it lasts longer and bleeds less. Intravenously it can treat abnormal heart rhythms. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism

It blocks voltage-gated sodium channels in neuronal membranes, preventing the initiation and conduction of nerve impulses. Onset is rapid, typically within minutes, and effects last from half an hour to three hours depending on formulation.

History

Lidocaine was synthesized by Swedish chemists Nils Löfgren and Bengt Lundqvist in 1943 and marketed from 1949. It largely replaced procaine because of its faster onset and longer action.

Adverse effects

Common effects include dizziness and numbness; serious toxicity, usually from overdose, can cause seizures, arrhythmias and respiratory depression.
