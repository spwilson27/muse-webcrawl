Adverbial clause

An adverbial clause is a dependent clause that functions as an adverb within a sentence; that is, it modifies a verb, adjective, or other adverb, or the sentence as a whole. Adverbial clauses typically express time, place, reason, condition, concession, purpose, result, or manner.

Adverbial clause
Type: Dependent clause
Function: Adverbial modifier
Introduced by: Subordinating conjunction

Types

Clauses of time are introduced by when, while, or before; clauses of reason by because or since; clauses of condition by if or unless; clauses of concession by although; and clauses of purpose by so that. For example, in "I will call you when I arrive", the clause "when I arrive" is an adverbial clause of time.

Reduced clauses

Adverbial clauses can sometimes be reduced to phrases when the subject is understood, as in "While walking home, she saw a deer."
