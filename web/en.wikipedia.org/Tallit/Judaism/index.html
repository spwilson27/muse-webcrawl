Judaism

Judaism — the Abrahamic, monotheistic religion of the Jewish people, originating with the covenant between God and Abraham in the ancient Near East; expressed in the Torah, Talmud, and rabbinic tradition. Roughly 14–15 million adherents worldwide.

Judaism (Hebrew: יַהֲדוּת‎, Yahăduṯ) is an Abrahamic, monotheistic, ethnic religion centred on the covenant between God and the Israelites. It comprises the collective religious, cultural, and legal tradition of the Jewish people. Originating in Bronze Age West Asia, it is among the world's oldest monotheistic religions still practised.

Beliefs

Central tenets include belief in one God, the divine revelation of the Torah (Tanakh, the Hebrew Bible) at Mount Sinai to Moses, the authority of the Oral Torah as codified in the Mishnah and Talmud, and the expectation of a messianic age. The Shema prayer ("Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one") is a core declaration of faith. Maimonides' thirteen principles summarize medieval rabbinic doctrine.

Practices

Religious observance includes prayer three times daily, Sabbath (Shabbat) observance from Friday sunset to Saturday night, dietary laws (kashrut), the cycle of festivals (Passover, Shavuot, Sukkot, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Hanukkah, Purim), and life-cycle rituals such as circumcision, bar/bat mitzvah, marriage, and mourning customs. Ritual garments include the tallit (prayer shawl) with its tzitzit fringes, commanded in Numbers 15 and Deuteronomy 22, and tefillin worn for weekday morning prayer.

Movements

Modern Judaism comprises major streams including Orthodox (and Haredi), Conservative (Masorti), Reform (Progressive), Reconstructionist, and Humanistic Judaism, differing over the binding force of Jewish law (halakha) and adaptation to modernity.
