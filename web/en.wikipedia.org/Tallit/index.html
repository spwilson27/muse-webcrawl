Tallit

A tallit (Hebrew: טַלִּית, plural tallitot) is a fringed garment worn as a Jewish prayer shawl. It is traditionally made of wool or cotton and has specially knotted ritual fringes, called tzitzit, attached to its four corners. The large prayer shawl is called the tallit gadol, while the smaller fringed undergarment worn daily is the tallit katan.

Biblical basis

The commandment to attach fringes to garments derives from the Torah: Numbers 15:38 instructs the Israelites to place fringes (tzitzit) on the corners of their garments, including a thread of blue (tekhelet), so that wearers would remember the commandments. Deuteronomy 22:12 gives a similar instruction.

Customs

The tallit gadol is worn during morning prayers (Shacharit), on Yom Kippur also in the evening, and on certain other occasions. Customs vary by community regarding who wears it and from what age: in many Ashkenazi communities traditionally only married men wore it, while Sephardi boys begin at bar mitzvah; in liberal movements women also wear tallitot. A blessing is recited when donning it, and worshippers often cover their heads with it during prayer.

Design

Tallitot are usually white with stripes — often blue or black — along the edges; the corner reinforcements and the knotting patterns of the tzitzit follow detailed halakhic rules. The collar band (atara) may be embroidered with the blessing. The dye tekhelet, historically obtained from a marine snail, has been revived by some in modern times.
