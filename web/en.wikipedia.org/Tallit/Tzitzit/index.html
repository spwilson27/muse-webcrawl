Tzitzit

Tzitzit (Hebrew: צִיצִית) — specially knotted ritual fringes attached to the four corners of the tallit (prayer shawl) and tallit katan, commanded in the Torah (Numbers 15:38; Deuteronomy 22:12) as a reminder of the commandments.

Tzitzit (Hebrew: צִיצִית‎ Ṣīṣīṯ; plural tzitziyot) are specially knotted ritual fringes, or tassels, worn by observant Jews. They are attached to the four corners of the tallit — the Jewish prayer shawl — and to the tallit katan, a small garment worn under the clothing throughout the day.

Biblical command

Numbers 15:38–40 instructs the Israelites to make fringes on the corners of their garments, with a cord of blue (tekhelet), "so that you shall remember all the commandments of the LORD and do them". Deuteronomy 22:12 similarly commands twisted cords on the four corners of one's covering.

Construction and tying

Each corner has four doubled threads passed through a hole and tied with a series of windings and double knots — commonly five knots separated by winding groups (7, 8, 11, 13 in one tradition), whose total can encode the number 613, the traditional count of commandments. Tradition requires the fringes be tied with intent (lishmah). Whether the blue tekhelet dye came from the Murex trunculus snail — and its modern revival — remains an active debate.

Practice

Customs vary on when boys begin wearing tzitzit, whether garments may be worn visibly, and whether women wear them; the blessing is recited when donning the tallit gadol at morning prayer.
