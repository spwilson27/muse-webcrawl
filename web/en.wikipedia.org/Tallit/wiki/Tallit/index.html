Tallit
TypeJewish prayer shawl
MaterialWool, cotton, or silk
FeaturesTzitzit (fringes) on four corners

A tallit (Hebrew: טַלִּית) is a fringed garment worn as a prayer shawl by religious Jews. The tallit has special twined and knotted fringes known as tzitzit attached to its four corners. Most traditional tallitot are made of wool or cotton, and may bear a decorative band known as an atarah at the neck.

Biblical origin

The commandment to wear fringes derives from Numbers 15:38, which instructs the Israelites to make tassels on the corners of their garments, and Deuteronomy 22:12. Wearing the tallit is associated with morning prayers (Shacharit) and other services.

Varieties

A large tallit worn over outer clothing is called a tallit gadol, while the tallit katan is a smaller four-cornered garment worn under the shirt throughout the day by many observant Jews.
