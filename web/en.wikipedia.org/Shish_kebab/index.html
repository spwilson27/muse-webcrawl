Shish kebab

Shish kebab is a popular dish of skewered and grilled cubes of meat, originating in the Middle East. The term derives from Turkish şiş kebap: şiş means "sword" or "skewer", and kebap means "roast meat". Traditionally made with lamb, shish kebab is now prepared with beef, chicken, pork, goat, or fish, and often interleaved with vegetables such as onions, peppers and tomatoes. It is a staple of Turkish, Arab, Persian, South Asian and Mediterranean cuisines and of kebab houses worldwide.

History

Grilling meat on skewers is ancient: Mycenaean Greeks used firedogs with slots for skewers (souvlaki trays have been found from the 17th century BC), and medieval soldiers in the Near East are said to have grilled meat on their swords. The dish coalesced in the Ottoman world, where kebab culture — from palace kitchens to street stalls — spread across the empire's former territories. In the Caucasus and Russia it became shashlik; in Greece, souvlaki and kalamaki; marinated-meat skewers also developed independently as satay in Southeast Asia and yakitori in Japan.

Preparation

Meat is cut into cubes of roughly 2.5 cm (1 inch), marinated — commonly in oil, lemon juice or vinegar, onion, garlic and spices such as paprika, cumin and oregano — and threaded onto flat metal or soaked wooden skewers, traditionally with pieces of tail fat or vegetables between the cubes. The skewers are grilled over charcoal (a mangal) or under a broiler, turned until browned outside and tender within. Shish kebab is typically served with flatbread (pita or lavash), rice or bulgur pilaf, grilled tomatoes and peppers, sumac onions, and salads or yoghurt sauces.

Variations

Regional variants are legion: Adana kebab uses hand-minced lamb with chilli and tail fat on wide flat skewers; shish taouk is the Levantine marinated chicken version with garlic sauce; Iranian kabab koobideh uses seasoned minced meat moulded onto the skewer, while jujeh kabab is saffron-and-yoghurt chicken. The word "kebab" more broadly also covers dishes such as the döner kebab (vertical rotisserie) and İskender kebab, which are not cooked on skewers at all.
