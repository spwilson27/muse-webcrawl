Theobromine

Theobromine is a bitter alkaloid of the cacao plant, with the chemical formula C7H8N4O2. It is the principal alkaloid in cocoa and chocolate, and is also found in tea and the kola nut. A xanthine compound closely related to caffeine, theobromine is named after the cacao tree genus Theobroma, from the Greek for "food of the gods".

Properties and effects

Theobromine is a mild stimulant and diuretic, weaker than caffeine in its effects on the central nervous system. It relaxes smooth muscle, dilates blood vessels and has been investigated for use as a cough suppressant. Dark chocolate typically contains about 200 mg of theobromine per 30 g serving.

Toxicity in animals

Many animals, notably dogs and cats, metabolize theobromine slowly, so chocolate consumption can cause theobromine poisoning — a well-known veterinary hazard producing vomiting, seizures and, in severe cases, death. Humans tolerate far larger amounts without serious effect. See also Caffeine and Chocolate.
