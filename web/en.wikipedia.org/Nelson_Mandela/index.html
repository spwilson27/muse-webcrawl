Nelson Mandela

Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela (18 July 1918 – 5 December 2013) was a South African anti-apartheid activist and politician who served as the first president of South Africa from 1994 to 1999, the country's first leader elected in a fully representative democratic election.

Early life and activism

Born in Mvezo in the Eastern Cape, Mandela studied law and became involved in anti-colonial politics, co-founding the African National Congress Youth League in 1944. After the National Party introduced apartheid in 1948, he led campaigns of civil disobedience and co-founded Umkhonto we Sizwe, the ANC's armed wing.

Imprisonment

Convicted of sabotage at the Rivonia Trial, Mandela spent 27 years in prison, mostly on Robben Island. Released on 11 February 1990 amid mounting domestic and international pressure, he negotiated with President F. W. de Klerk to end apartheid; both men received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1993.

Presidency and later life

Mandela's government focused on reconciliation, establishing the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, and on reconstruction through housing, education, and health programmes. He retired after one term and remained an elder statesman and campaigner against HIV/AIDS. He died in Johannesburg in 2013 aged 95.
