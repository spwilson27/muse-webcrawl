Kinematics

Kinematics is a subfield of physics and classical mechanics that describes the motion of points, bodies and systems of bodies without considering the forces that cause them to move. It is concerned with quantities such as displacement, velocity, acceleration, jerk and trajectory, and is often described as the "geometry of motion". The study of the causes of motion belongs instead to dynamics (kinetics).

Basic quantities

The position of a particle is given by a vector from a chosen origin; velocity is the rate of change of position with time, and acceleration the rate of change of velocity. For motion with constant acceleration, the kinematic equations relate displacement, initial and final velocity, acceleration and time — for example v = u + at and s = ut + ½at². Kinematic analysis extends to rotational motion, using angular displacement, angular velocity and angular acceleration.

Applications

Kinematics underlies projectile-motion calculations, the design of linkages and gear trains, robotic arm control (forward and inverse kinematics), computer animation and character rigging, and astrophysical descriptions of orbital motion. In special relativity, kinematics is reformulated in terms of spacetime intervals and four-vectors.

History

The term derives from the Greek kinema (motion) and was introduced in its modern sense by André-Marie Ampère in 1834. Systematic treatments of motion began with Galileo's studies of falling bodies and projectile trajectories in the early 17th century.
