Washington, D.C., officially the District of Columbia, is the capital city and federal district of the United States. It lies on the Potomac River, bordering Maryland and Virginia, and was named for George Washington.

History

The Residence Act of 1790 created a federal district on land donated by Maryland and Virginia (the Virginia portion was retroceded in 1846). French-American engineer Pierre Charles L'Enfant designed a grand baroque-style plan of avenues radiating from the Capitol and the President's House. British forces burned public buildings in 1814 during the War of 1812. The District has no voting representation in Congress, a long-standing grievance summed up by the slogan “Taxation Without Representation”; residents gained presidential voting rights by the 23rd Amendment (1961) and an elected mayor and council under Home Rule in 1973.

Government and landmarks

Washington hosts the three branches of the U.S. federal government — Congress at the Capitol, the President at the White House, and the Supreme Court — plus the World Bank, IMF, and many embassies. The National Mall stretches from the Capitol via the Washington Monument to the Lincoln Memorial, lined by Smithsonian museums.

Culture and transport

The city is known for go-go music, a large African American heritage including U Street, universities such as Georgetown and Howard, and the Washington Metro (opened 1976).
