Palestinian right of return

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Palestinian right of return is the political position and principle that Palestinian refugees from the 1948 and 1967 wars — and their descendants — have a right to return to the homes and property they fled or were expelled from in what is now Israel, and to compensation. It is a central and long-standing demand of Palestinian nationalism and a major issue in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict.

Background

During the 1948 war, more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled from their homes in the territory that became Israel, an event Palestinians call the Nakba. Israel's position has been that refugees should be resettled in Arab states or a future Palestinian state, and that a mass return would end Israel's character as a Jewish state; successive Israeli governments have rejected a general right of return while at times offering limited family reunification, compensation funds, or symbolic admissions.

Legal basis claimed

Supporters cite UN General Assembly Resolution 194 (December 1948), which resolved that refugees wishing to return and live at peace should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date, with compensation for those choosing not to return; Article 13 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights; and the Fourth Geneva Convention. Critics of the claim argue that Resolution 194 is a non-binding recommendation, that its wording is conditional, and that no comparable mass-return precedent applies to a population's descendants generations later. Refugee status for descendants is administered uniquely by UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestine refugees, unlike the UNHCR regime for other refugees.

Negotiations

The issue was designated a "final status" question in the Oslo Accords. It was negotiated at Camp David (2000) and Taba (2001) and in the Annapolis process, with proposals involving return mainly to a Palestinian state, compensation, rehabilitation, and limited numbers admitted to Israel — but no agreement has been reached.
