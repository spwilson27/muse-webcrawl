Collectible card game

A collectible card game (CCG), also called a trading card game (TCG) among other names, is a type of card game that mixes strategic deck building elements with features of trading cards. It was introduced with Magic: The Gathering in 1993 by Wizards of the Coast. Cards are sold in randomised booster packs, and players build customised decks from their collections to play against one another.

Gameplay

In a typical CCG, each player constructs a deck before play, drawing on a personal collection rather than using a fixed shared deck. Cards represent creatures, spells, resources and other game elements, usually with rules text that modifies the base game rules. Tournaments, organised play programmes and rarity tiers (common, uncommon, rare) drive collecting and a large secondary market in individual cards.

History

Richard Garfield's Magic: The Gathering (1993) created the genre and was patented as a game system. Its success spawned many imitators, most of them short-lived; enduring franchises include Pokémon (1996), which became one of the best-selling card games of all time, and Yu-Gi-Oh! (1999). Related formats include living card games, in which expansions are sold in fixed sets rather than randomised packs, and digital collectible card games such as Hearthstone (2014), which translated the model to video games.

Market

Rare cards can command very high prices: graded copies of Magic's Black Lotus and of the Pikachu Illustrator Pokémon card have sold for hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars. The genre remains a major segment of the tabletop games industry, with sales surging during the collectibles boom of 2020–2021.
