Donald Griffin

Donald Redfield Griffin (1915–2003) was an American zoologist and professor known for discovering echolocation in bats, and for his later work arguing that animals can think and feel, which he called cognitive ethology.

Echolocation

Working with Robert Galambos in the late 1930s, Griffin demonstrated that bats navigate and hunt in darkness by emitting high-frequency sounds and listening to their echoes — a phenomenon he named "echolocation" in 1944. His experimental work, including careful field studies of bat flight and hearing, settled a long-standing scientific mystery.

Cognitive ethology

After a career at Cornell, Harvard, and Rockefeller University, Griffin turned to the study of animal minds, arguing that cognitive processes should be studied in non-human animals as well as humans. His books include The Question of Animal Awareness (1976) and Animal Minds (1992).
