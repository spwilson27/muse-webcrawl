Annapurna

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Annapurna (Nepali: अन्नपूर्ण) is a massif in the Himalayas of north-central Nepal. Its highest peak, Annapurna I, rises to 8,091 metres (26,545 ft), making it the tenth-highest mountain in the world. The name derives from the Hindu goddess of food and nourishment and means "full of food" or "giver of sustenance".

Climbing history

Annapurna I was the first of the fourteen eight-thousanders to be climbed: on 3 June 1950 Maurice Herzog and Louis Lachenal of a French expedition reached the summit, an achievement that became famous through Herzog's book Annapurna. The pair suffered severe frostbite. The mountain long had the highest fatality rate of any 8,000-metre peak, with avalanches on its flanks claiming many climbers; its south face, climbed in 1970 by a British team including Chris Bonington, remains a major test piece.

The region

The massif is protected by the Annapurna Conservation Area, Nepal's largest protected area. The Annapurna Circuit trek, which circles the range through the Thorong La pass (5,416 m), and the trek to Annapurna Base Camp in the "Sanctuary" are among the world's most popular mountain routes.
