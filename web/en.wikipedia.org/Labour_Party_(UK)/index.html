Labour Party (UK)

The Labour Party, one of the two dominant parties of British politics.

The Labour Party is a political party in the United Kingdom that has been described as centre-left and as an alliance of social democrats, democratic socialists, and trade unionists. Founded in 1900 as the Labour Representation Committee, it grew out of the trade union movement and socialist societies and overtook the Liberal Party in the 1920s to become, with the Conservatives, one of Britain's two main parties. Labour has governed in 1924, 1929–31, 1945–51, 1964–70, 1974–79, 1997–2010, and since 2024.

History

Keir Hardie was among the founders, and Ramsay MacDonald led the first Labour governments, minority administrations in 1924 and 1929–31. Clement Attlee's government of 1945–51 built the modern welfare state: the National Health Service (1948), expanded social security, and nationalization of major industries. Harold Wilson's governments of the 1960s–70s followed, then after eighteen years in opposition Tony Blair's "New Labour" won landslides in 1997, 2001, and 2005, introducing the minimum wage and devolution while also taking Britain into the Iraq War. Defeated in 2010, the party moved left under Jeremy Corbyn (2015–2020), suffered a heavy defeat in 2019, and returned to the centre under Keir Starmer, who won a large majority at the 2024 general election and became Prime Minister.

Organization

Labour's structure links constituency parties, affiliated trade unions, and socialist societies; its annual conference and National Executive Committee set policy, and its leader is elected by members and affiliated supporters. Notable figures include Aneurin Bevan, founder of the NHS, and Barbara Castle.
