Vaisheshika

Vaisheshika (Sanskrit: वैशेषिक) is one of the six orthodox (āstika) schools of Indian philosophy. It is known for its naturalism and its theory of atoms, and it analyses reality into categories called padārthas.

Founder and texts

The school is traditionally founded by the sage Kaṇāda, author of the Vaiśeṣika Sūtra, composed between roughly the 6th and 2nd centuries BCE. The philosopher Praśastapāda's commentary, the Padārthadharmasaṅgraha, systematized the doctrine.

Doctrine

Vaisheshika recognizes categories such as substance (dravya), quality (guṇa), action (karma), generality, particularity, and inherence. Its atomism holds that the physical world is composed of indivisible atoms (aṇu) of earth, water, fire, and air, whose combinations produce perceptible objects. The school later merged closely with Nyāya logic.
