Duchy of Parma

Map of the Duchy of Parma and Piacenza (placeholder image)

The Duchy of Parma (Italian: Ducato di Parma) was a state in northern Italy, created in 1545 from territory of the Duchy of Milan by Pope Paul III for his son Pier Luigi Farnese. Together with Piacenza it formed the Duchy of Parma and Piacenza, ruled first by the Farnese dynasty and later by branches of the Bourbons, with an interlude of Habsburg rule.

Farnese period

Pier Luigi was assassinated in 1547, but his son Ottavio secured the duchy and moved the capital to Parma. The Farnese dukes — including Alessandro Farnese, the great Spanish general and governor of the Netherlands — made Parma a notable court, building the Palazzo della Pilotta and the Farnese Theatre. The male Farnese line died out in 1731.

Bourbon period

After periods of Austrian rule and the tenure of Don Carlos of Spain, the duchy passed in 1748 to Philip, son of Philip V of Spain, founding the House of Bourbon-Parma. Under Ferdinand and his minister Guillaume du Tillot, Parma became known for Enlightenment reforms. Napoleon annexed the duchy to France, and in 1814 the Congress of Vienna assigned it to Napoleon's former wife Marie Louise of Austria, who ruled until 1847.

Unification

The last Bourbon duke, Robert I, was deposed in 1859 during the wars of Italian unification, and in 1860 the duchy voted for annexation to the Kingdom of Sardinia, becoming part of the unified Kingdom of Italy in 1861.
