Panthera

Scientific classification Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Mammalia
Order: Carnivora
Family: Felidae
Subfamily: Pantherinae
Genus: Panthera Oken, 1816

Panthera is a genus within the family Felidae that was named and first described by Lorenz Oken in 1816, who placed all spotted cats in this group. Reginald Innes Pocock revised the classification of this genus in 1916 as comprising the tiger (P. tigris), lion (P. leo), jaguar (P. onca), and leopard (P. pardus) on the basis of common cranial features. Results of genetic analysis indicate that the snow leopard (P. uncia) also belongs to Panthera, a classification that was adopted by IUCN Red List assessors in 2008.

The tiger, lion, leopard, and jaguar are the only cat species with the anatomical structure that enables them to roar. The primary reason for this was formerly assumed to be the incomplete ossification of the hyoid bone. However, new studies show that the ability to roar is due to other morphological features, especially of the larynx.

Species

The five living species are:

  • Tiger (Panthera tigris) — Asia; the largest of the big cats.
  • Lion (Panthera leo) — sub-Saharan Africa and a small population in India's Gir Forest; unique among cats for its pronounced sexual dimorphism and social pride structure.
  • Jaguar (Panthera onca) — the Americas, from Mexico to northern Argentina; the third-largest species and noted for its powerful bite.
  • Leopard (Panthera pardus) — Africa and Asia; the most widespread and adaptable of the big cats.
  • Snow leopard (Panthera uncia) — the mountain ranges of Central and South Asia; unable to roar, and classified historically in its own genus Uncia.

Evolution

The earliest known Panthera species is Panthera blytheae, known from fossils from the late Miocene of the Tibetan Plateau, dated to around 5.9 million years ago, suggesting the genus may have originated in Central Asia. Extinct members include the American lion (P. atrox) and the Eurasian cave lion (P. spelaea). The black panther is not a distinct species but a melanistic colour variant found in leopards and jaguars.

Characteristics

Members of Panthera are large, muscular cats with long bodies, large heads, and powerful limbs. They are apex predators in their respective ecosystems and are important keystone species. All five living species are considered threatened to some degree: the tiger and snow leopard are listed as Endangered and Vulnerable respectively on the IUCN Red List, while the lion, leopard, and jaguar are listed as Vulnerable or Near Threatened.
