The 2016 Democratic Party presidential primaries were a series of electoral contests organised by the Democratic Party in the United States to select the delegates to the 2016 Democratic National Convention, which chose the party's nominees for president and vice president in the 2016 US presidential election.

Candidates and campaign

The race developed into a two-person contest between former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton and US Senator Bernie Sanders of Vermont. Clinton won the popular vote and the majority of pledged delegates, while Sanders ran strongly among younger voters and in caucus states, campaigning on income inequality and campaign-finance reform.

Outcome

Clinton secured enough delegates by early June 2016 and was formally nominated at the convention in Philadelphia, becoming the first woman to be the presidential nominee of a major US political party. Sanders endorsed Clinton before the convention. She selected Senator Tim Kaine of Virginia as her running mate.
