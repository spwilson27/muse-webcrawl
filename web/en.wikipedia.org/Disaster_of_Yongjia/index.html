Disaster of Yongjia

The Disaster of Yongjia (Chinese: 永嘉之亂; pinyin: Yǒngjiā zhī luàn) was an event in 311 CE during the War of the Eight Princes and the uprisings of the Five Barbarians, in which forces of the Xiongnu-led state of Han Zhao sacked Luoyang, the capital of the Western Jin dynasty, massacring much of its population and capturing Emperor Huai of Jin.

Background

The Jin dynasty had been gravely weakened by the civil wars of the Eight Princes (291–306), which exhausted the imperial armies and treasury. Meanwhile, non-Han peoples settled inside the empire—the "Five Barbarians"—rose in rebellion. Liu Yuan, a Xiongnu noble, founded Han Zhao in 304, and his son Liu Cong succeeded him in 310.

The sack

In July 311, Han Zhao generals including Liu Yao, Wang Mi, and Shi Le captured Luoyang. The city was looted and burned, imperial tombs were desecrated, and contemporary accounts describe more than 30,000 deaths. Emperor Huai was taken prisoner to Pingyang and was later executed in 313 after being humiliated at court.

Consequences

The disaster effectively ended Jin rule in the north. Chang'an, the secondary capital, fell in 316 and Emperor Min was captured, formally ending the Western Jin. In 317 a Jin prince re-established the dynasty in the south as the Eastern Jin, while vast numbers of northern aristocrats and refugees fled across the Yangtze—one of the great southward migrations in Chinese history.
