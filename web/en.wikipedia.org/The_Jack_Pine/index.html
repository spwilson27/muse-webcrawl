The Jack Pine

ArtistTom Thomson
Year1916–1917
MediumOil on canvas
Dimensions127.9 cm × 139.8 cm
LocationNational Gallery of Canada, Ottawa

The Jack Pine is a 1916–17 oil painting by the Canadian artist Tom Thomson. Depicting a solitary jack pine silhouetted against a sunset over Grand Lake in Algonquin Provincial Park, Ontario, it is one of the most famous and widely reproduced works of Canadian art.

Creation

Thomson, who worked as a park ranger and guide in Algonquin Park, developed the canvas in his Toronto studio from an oil sketch made on location in the spring of 1916. The lone, windswept tree — an emblem of endurance in a harsh landscape — became a powerful symbol of Canadian identity. Thomson died in Algonquin Park in July 1917, shortly after the painting's completion, in circumstances that remain disputed.

Legacy

The painting is held by the National Gallery of Canada and, together with works such as The West Wind, helped define the landscape style later associated with the Group of Seven, who regarded Thomson as their forerunner.
