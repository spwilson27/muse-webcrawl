4G is the fourth generation of cellular network technology, succeeding 3G and preceding 5G. Unlike its predecessors, 4G is an all-IP network: voice as well as data is carried as packets, enabling mobile broadband for smartphones, mobile streaming, and wireless internet access at speeds far beyond 3G.

Standards

The ITU's IMT-Advanced specification set peak requirements of 1 Gbit/s for low-mobility and 100 Mbit/s for high-mobility use. The two commercial systems marketed as 4G were Mobile WiMAX and LTE (Long Term Evolution), developed by the IEEE and 3GPP respectively. LTE Advanced and WiMAX Release 2 were the first to fully meet IMT-Advanced. TeliaSonera launched the first public LTE service in Stockholm and Oslo in December 2009.

Technology

4G radios use OFDMA in the downlink, MIMO antenna arrays, and adaptive modulation to increase spectral efficiency. The evolved packet core removed circuit switching entirely; voice is provided by VoLTE (Voice over LTE) or falls back to older networks. Later LTE-Advanced features such as carrier aggregation and higher-order MIMO pushed peak rates toward gigabit levels ("Gigabit LTE").

Deployment

By the mid-2010s 4G covered most urban populations worldwide and carried the growth of mobile video and app traffic. Networks began refarming 4G spectrum for 5G in the 2020s, though LTE remains a coverage layer in most 5G deployments.
