Neuropeptide

Neuropeptides are short chains of amino acids that function as signalling molecules in the nervous system. Released by neurons, they act on receptors — typically G protein-coupled receptors — to modulate neural activity, often more slowly and diffusely than classical neurotransmitters.

Synthesis and release

Neuropeptides are synthesised as large precursor proteins (prepropeptides) in the cell body, processed by enzymes, and packaged into dense-core vesicles. They are often co-released with small-molecule neurotransmitters.

Examples

Well-known neuropeptides include the endogenous opioids (endorphins and enkephalins), oxytocin, vasopressin, substance P, neuropeptide Y, and the hypocretins (orexins) involved in sleep regulation. More than 100 neuropeptides are known in mammals, grouped into families by sequence similarity.

Functions

Neuropeptides regulate diverse processes including pain perception, appetite, stress responses, social behaviour, circadian rhythms, and learning.
