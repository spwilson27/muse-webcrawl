Dracula is an 1897 Gothic horror novel by Irish author Bram Stoker. It introduced the character of Count Dracula and established many conventions of subsequent vampire fantasy. The novel tells the story of Dracula's attempt to move from Transylvania to England so that he may find new blood and spread the undead curse, and of the battle between Dracula and a small group of people led by Professor Abraham Van Helsing.

Plot

The novel is an epistolary narrative, told through letters, diary entries, and newspaper clippings. Solicitor Jonathan Harker travels to Castle Dracula in Transylvania to conclude a property sale, and gradually realises he is the prisoner of a vampire. Dracula sails for England aboard the Demeter, whose crew mysteriously perish. In Whitby, Harker's fiancée Mina Murray and her friend Lucy Westenra encounter the Count; Lucy sickens, receives transfusions, and despite the efforts of Dr John Seward and Van Helsing, dies — and rises as a vampire, who must be destroyed.

The group, joined by Arthur Holmwood and the American Quincey Morris, traces Dracula's boxes of earth, destroys his English lairs, and pursues him back to Transylvania. In a final confrontation near the castle, Harker and Morris kill the Count at sunset; Morris dies of his wounds as Dracula crumbles to dust and Mina's scar of the curse vanishes.

Background and influence

Stoker drew on folklore, earlier vampire literature such as Carmilla and Varney the Vampire, and probably on the name and reputation of the 15th-century Wallachian prince Vlad the Impaler, though the degree of direct influence remains debated. A notable source was Emily Gerard's writing on Transylvanian superstitions.

Dracula has never been out of print and has generated hundreds of films, stage plays, and derivative works, from Nosferatu (1922) and the 1931 Universal film with Bela Lugosi to Francis Ford Coppola's Bram Stoker's Dracula (1992). Count Dracula is among the most portrayed literary characters in film history.
