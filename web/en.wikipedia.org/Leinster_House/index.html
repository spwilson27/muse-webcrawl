Leinster House (Irish: Teach Laighean) is the seat of the Oireachtas, the parliament of Ireland, located on Kildare Street in Dublin. Originally built in the 1740s as the townhouse of the Earls of Kildare, later Dukes of Leinster, it became the home of the Irish legislature in 1922 and is one of the most historically significant buildings in the state.

History

The house was commissioned by James FitzGerald, 20th Earl of Kildare (later 1st Duke of Leinster), and designed by the German-born architect Richard Cassels (also spelled Castle). Construction ran from 1745 to 1748. At the time, Kildare Street lay on the unfashionable south side of the River Liffey; the Earl is said to have predicted that fashion would follow him, and the area duly became the centre of Georgian Dublin.

In 1815 the 3rd Duke of Leinster sold the house to the Royal Dublin Society, which used it as its headquarters and developed the surrounding grounds, which later housed the National Library and the National Museum. The Irish Free State government acquired the building in 1922, initially as a temporary home for the new parliament.

Parliamentary use

Leinster House contains the chambers of both houses of the Oireachtas: Dáil Éireann, the lower house, which meets in the former lecture theatre of the Royal Dublin Society, and Seanad Éireann, the upper house, which meets in the former ducal ballroom. Major state events held there have included addresses by United States presidents John F. Kennedy (1963), Ronald Reagan (1984), and Bill Clinton (1995).

The complex has been expanded several times, most notably with the addition of office blocks in the twentieth century. An obelisk-like monument on the grounds commemorates three founders of Irish nationalism: Arthur Griffith, Michael Collins, and Kevin O'Higgins.
