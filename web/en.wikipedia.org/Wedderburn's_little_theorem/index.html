The theorem implies that every finite division ring is a field.

In mathematics, Wedderburn's little theorem states that every finite domain is a field. In other words, for a finite ring, there is no difference between domains, skew-fields and fields. The Artin–Zorn theorem generalises the theorem to alternative rings: every finite alternative division ring is a field.

History

The original proof was given by Joseph Wedderburn in 1905, who also proved it two other ways. Another proof was given by Leonard Eugene Dickson shortly after Wedderburn's, and Dickson acknowledged Wedderburn's priority. The theorem is called the "little theorem" to distinguish it from Wedderburn's theorem on semisimple rings.

Statement and proof sketch

Let D be a finite division ring with centre Z, where Z is a finite field with q elements and D has dimension n over Z, so D has qn elements. The multiplicative group D* acts on itself by conjugation; counting elements in conjugacy classes and using the class equation shows that q − 1 divides each term qd − 1 for proper divisors d of n. A cyclotomic polynomial argument then forces n = 1, so D equals its centre and is commutative — a field.

Relation to the Brauer group

The theorem is equivalent to the statement that the Brauer group of a finite field is trivial: Br(Fq) = 0, since every central simple algebra over a finite field splits.
