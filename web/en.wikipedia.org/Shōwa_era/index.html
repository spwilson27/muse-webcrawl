The Shōwa era (昭和, Shōwa, "enlightened peace") is the period of Japanese history corresponding to the reign of Emperor Hirohito from 25 December 1926 until his death on 7 January 1989. It was the longest reign of any historical Japanese emperor and the longest of any modern monarch until surpassed by Elizabeth II and Rama IX. The Shōwa era was preceded by the Taishō era and followed by the Heisei era.

The era name was drawn from a passage in the Chinese classic Book of Documents ("the people are in harmony, and all nations are enlightened"). During Hirohito's reign, Japan experienced initial democratic developments, militarist expansion and total war, military defeat and Allied occupation, and then a remarkable period of rapid economic growth that made Japan the world's second-largest economy.

Early Shōwa: crisis and militarism

The early Shōwa period saw the financial crisis of 1927, the impact of the Great Depression, and the rise of military influence over politics, marked by assassinations of civilian leaders and incidents such as the Manchurian Incident of 1931, which led to the occupation of Manchuria and the creation of the puppet state of Manchukuo. In 1937 Japan launched a full-scale invasion of China, beginning the Second Sino-Japanese War.

The Pacific War

In December 1941 Japan attacked Pearl Harbor, bringing the United States into the Second World War. Japanese forces rapidly occupied much of Southeast Asia and the western Pacific, but defeats at Midway (1942) and in subsequent island campaigns turned the tide. In August 1945 the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet entry into the war preceded Japan's surrender, announced by the Emperor on 15 August 1945.

Occupation and recovery

From 1945 to 1952 Japan was occupied by the Allied Powers under General Douglas MacArthur. Reforms included a new constitution (effective 1947), in which the Emperor became a symbol of the state, land reform, and the dissolution of the zaibatsu conglomerates. The San Francisco Peace Treaty restored Japanese sovereignty in 1952.

The economic miracle

From the mid-1950s to the early 1970s, Japan achieved sustained double-digit growth, symbolised by the 1964 Tokyo Olympics and the inauguration of the Shinkansen bullet train. Export-led industries in steel, shipbuilding, automobiles, and electronics transformed Japan into a leading industrial power. The era ended amid the asset price bubble of the late 1980s.

Equivalents

In the Japanese imperial year system, the Shōwa year is calculated by adding 1925 to the Gregorian year: for example, 1989 was Shōwa 64, the final year. The era's first day (25 December 1926) and last day (7 January 1989) are observed in connection with the Emperor's life; Hirohito's birthday, 29 April, is now the public holiday Shōwa Day.
