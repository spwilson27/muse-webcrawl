Insulated glazing

Insulated glazing (IG), more commonly known as double glazing or triple glazing, consists of two or three glass panes separated by a space to reduce heat transfer across a building envelope. The sealed unit is called an insulated glass unit (IGU).

Construction

Panes are separated by a spacer, often aluminium or warm-edge material, and sealed with primary and secondary sealants. The cavity is filled with dry air or an inert gas such as argon or krypton, which has lower thermal conductivity than air. Low-emissivity (low-E) coatings may be applied to one surface to reduce radiative heat transfer.

Performance

Insulated glazing is rated by its U-value; lower values indicate better insulation. Double glazing typically achieves U-values around 1.1–2.8 W/(m²·K) depending on gas fill and coatings, while triple glazing can go below 0.8. IGUs also improve acoustic insulation and reduce condensation. Seal failure allows moisture into the cavity, causing fogging between the panes.
