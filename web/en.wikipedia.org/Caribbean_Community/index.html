Caribbean Community

The Caribbean Community (CARICOM) is an intergovernmental organisation of Caribbean states and territories, founded by the Treaty of Chaguaramas in 1973. It has 15 full member states and several associate members, a combined population of about 16 million (over 18 million including Haiti), and its secretariat is headquartered in Georgetown, Guyana. Its pillars are economic integration, foreign policy coordination, human and social development, and security.

History

CARICOM succeeded the short-lived West Indies Federation (1958–1962) and the Caribbean Free Trade Association (CARIFTA, 1965). The founding signatories in 1973 were Barbados, Guyana, Jamaica and Trinidad and Tobago. The Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas (2001) established the CARICOM Single Market and Economy (CSME), intended to allow free movement of goods, services, capital and skilled labour among members, though implementation has been gradual.

Institutions

The Conference of Heads of Government is the supreme organ. Associated institutions include the Caribbean Court of Justice (inaugurated 2005), the University of the West Indies, the Caribbean Development Bank and the Caribbean Examinations Council. CARICOM has coordinated regional responses to hurricanes, the COVID-19 pandemic and climate diplomacy, giving small island states a collective voice in international forums disproportionate to their individual size.
