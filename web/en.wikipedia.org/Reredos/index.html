Reredos

A reredos is a large altarpiece, screen, or decoration placed behind the altar in a church. It often includes religious imagery, carved figures, or paintings, and may be made of stone, wood, or precious materials. The term derives from Anglo-Norman areredos, meaning "behind the back".

Description

Reredoses range from simple carved panels to vast sculptural ensembles, such as the great Spanish retablos of the late medieval and Renaissance periods, which could cover the entire wall behind the altar. In English churches, notable examples survive at Winchester Cathedral and St Albans Cathedral. A reredos differs from a retable, which is typically a smaller panel or shelf placed on or immediately behind the altar itself, though usage of the terms overlaps.

History

Elaborate reredoses became common in the later Middle Ages. Many English examples were damaged or destroyed during the Reformation and the Civil War, and the Gothic Revival of the 19th century produced many new ones, often richly coloured and gilded.
