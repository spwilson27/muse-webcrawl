Horatio Gates

Horatio Lloyd Gates
Born: July 26, 1727, Maldon, Essex, England
Died: April 10, 1806, New York City
Allegiance: Great Britain; United States
Service: British Army; Continental Army
Rank: Major general

Horatio Lloyd Gates (July 26, 1727 – April 10, 1806) was a British-born American soldier who served as a general in the Continental Army during the early years of the American Revolutionary War. He is best known for his victory at the Battle of Saratoga in 1777, a turning point of the war, and for his disastrous defeat at the Battle of Camden in 1780.

Early career

Gates served in the British Army during the War of the Austrian Succession and the French and Indian War, serving in North America under Edward Braddock and witnessing the disastrous defeat at the Monongahela in 1755. After the war he settled in Virginia, and at the outbreak of the Revolution he offered his experience to the American cause, receiving appointment as adjutant general of the Continental Army.

Saratoga and Camden

In 1777 Gates took command of the Northern Department and received the surrender of British General John Burgoyne's army at Saratoga, a victory that helped persuade France to enter the war. Gates's reputation suffered from his association with the alleged Conway Cabal against George Washington. In 1780 he took command in the South and led his army to a crushing defeat at Camden, South Carolina, after which he was removed from command. His flight from the battlefield drew ridicule, and though an inquiry was ordered, he was never restored to a field command.

Later life

Gates retired to his Virginia estate, freed his slaves, and moved to New York, where he served one term in the state legislature. He died in 1806.
