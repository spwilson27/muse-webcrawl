Charles M. Schulz

Charles Monroe "Sparky" Schulz (/ʃʌlts/; November 26, 1922 – February 12, 2000) was an American cartoonist, the creator of the comic strip Peanuts which features his two best-known characters, Charlie Brown and Snoopy. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential cartoonists in history, and cited by many cartoonists as a major influence, including Jim Davis, Murray Ball, Bill Watterson, Matt Groening, and Dav Pilkey.

Early life

Schulz was born in Minneapolis, Minnesota, and grew up in Saint Paul. His father, Carl Schulz, was a barber, and his mother, Dena, was a housewife. He was nicknamed "Sparky" after the horse Spark Plug in the Barney Google comic strip. A shy teenager, Schulz served in the U.S. Army during World War II as a squad leader in a machine-gun unit in Europe.

Career

After the war Schulz worked as an art instructor and sold the single-panel feature Li'l Folks to the St. Paul Pioneer Press. United Feature Syndicate relaunched it as the four-panel strip Peanuts on October 2, 1950. Schulz wrote, pencilled, inked, and lettered every one of the strip's 17,897 installments until ill health forced his retirement in late 1999. At its peak Peanuts ran in more than 2,600 newspapers in 75 countries and in 21 languages.

Death and legacy

Schulz was diagnosed with colorectal cancer in November 1999. He died in his sleep at his home in Santa Rosa, California, on February 12, 2000 — the night before his final Sunday strip was published. The Charles M. Schulz Museum opened in Santa Rosa in 2002. Schulz received numerous honors, including a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame and induction into the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame.
