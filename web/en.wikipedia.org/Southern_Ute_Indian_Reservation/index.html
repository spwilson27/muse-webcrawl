The Southern Ute Indian Reservation is a federally recognised American Indian reservation in south-western Colorado, home to the Southern Ute Indian Tribe, descendants of the Mouache and Capote bands of the Ute people. Its capital is Ignacio. The reservation stretches in a band roughly 15 miles wide and 70 miles long along the Colorado–New Mexico border.

History

The Ute people historically ranged across much of Colorado and Utah. Treaties in 1868 established a large reservation in western Colorado, but mining and settler pressure shrank Ute lands repeatedly. Under the Brunot Agreement of 1874 and subsequent agreements, the Southern Utes were confined to the southern strip that forms today's reservation, while the allotment policy of the 1890s broke much of it into individual parcels, leaving a checkerboard of tribal, allotted and non-Indian land. The eastern part remained in tribal ownership as a consolidated block, while the western part was allotted.

Government and economy

The tribe is governed by an elected tribal council under a constitution adopted in the 1930s. The Southern Ute have become one of the most economically successful tribes in the United States through energy development: oil and natural gas production on the reservation, channelled through the tribe's energy companies and its Growth Fund, has financed extensive investments in real estate, private equity and other ventures off the reservation. Tribal enterprises include the Sky Ute Casino Resort in Ignacio. The tribe also operates the Southern Ute Indian Cultural Center and Museum.

Culture

The Southern Ute maintain the Ute language (a Numic language of the Uto-Aztecan family) and ceremonial traditions including the annual Bear Dance and participation in the Sun Dance. The tribe celebrates its heritage at the Southern Ute Tribal Fair and powwows, and works to transmit language and customs to younger generations.

Geography

The reservation spans parts of Archuleta, La Plata and Montezuma counties, from the pine forests around Lake Vallecito and the Los Pinos River in the east to arid lands near the Four Corners in the west. Chimney Rock National Monument, an Ancestral Puebloan site, lies within the reservation's boundaries.
