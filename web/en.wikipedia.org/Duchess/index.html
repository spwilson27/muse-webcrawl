Duchess

A duchess in ceremonial dress (placeholder image)

A duchess is a member of nobility who ranks below a monarch and above a marchioness or marquess in European peerages. The title is held by a woman in her own right (suo jure) or is the title given to the wife of a duke. The territory ruled or held by a duke or duchess is a duchy.

Forms of address and precedence

In the British peerage, a duchess is addressed as "Your Grace" and formally styled "The Most Noble" (historically) or "Her Grace". Duchesses rank immediately below the royal family and above marchionesses. The wife of a duke uses her husband's title as a courtesy title; a woman granted a dukedom in her own right is a duchess suo jure, a rare occurrence.

Historical use

The term derives from the Latin dux, "leader", via the Old French duchesse. Medieval duchesses could wield substantial power, ruling duchies as regents or in their own right; notable examples include Eleanor of Aquitaine, Duchess of Aquitaine and later queen consort of France and England, and Anne of Brittany. In France, Germany and Italy, duchesses of sovereign duchies could be ruling heads of state.

Modern usage

Today, hereditary duchesses exist mainly in the United Kingdom and Spain, while reigning duchies survive in Luxembourg, whose monarch is a grand duke. Royal duchesses in the British royal family include the Duchess of Edinburgh and the Duchess of Sussex. The title is also widely used in historical fiction and popular culture.
