J. R. D. Tata

Born29 July 1904, Paris, France
Died29 November 1993 (aged 89), Geneva, Switzerland
OccupationIndustrialist, aviator
Known forChairman of Tata Sons; founder of Tata Airlines (later Air India)
AwardsBharat Ratna (1992); Padma Vibhushan (1955)

Jehangir Ratanji Dadabhoy Tata (29 July 1904 – 29 November 1993) was an Indian industrialist and aviator who chaired the Tata Group, India's largest conglomerate, for over half a century. A pioneer of Indian aviation, he founded India's first commercial airline, Tata Airlines, in 1932, which later became Air India. In 1992 he received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.

Early life

J. R. D. Tata was born in Paris to Ratanji Dadabhoy Tata, a cousin of Tata Group founder Jamsetji Tata, and his French wife Suzanne Brière. He was educated in France, Japan and England, and served briefly in the French Army. He returned to India in 1925 and joined the family firm, Tata Sons.

Aviation

Fascinated by flying from childhood — he was inspired by the French aviator Louis Blériot — Tata obtained the first pilot's licence issued in India in 1929. In 1932 he founded Tata Airlines and personally flew its first flight, carrying mail from Karachi to Bombay (via Ahmedabad) in a de Havilland Puss Moth. The airline was renamed Air India in 1946; after nationalisation in 1953 Tata remained its chairman until 1977. In 1962, at the age of 58, he re-enacted his pioneering 1932 flight to mark its thirtieth anniversary.

Head of the Tata Group

Tata became chairman of Tata Sons in 1938, aged 34, and held the post until 1991 — one of the longest tenures in Indian business history. Under his leadership the group expanded from 14 companies to around 95, moving into chemicals, automobiles (TELCO, later Tata Motors), tea, software (TCS), watches (Titan) and many other fields. He also championed employee welfare measures well ahead of Indian law, including the eight-hour working day and free medical aid, and supported institutions such as the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and the Tata Memorial Hospital.

Honours and death

Tata received the Padma Vibhushan in 1955 and the Bharat Ratna in 1992. He died in Geneva on 29 November 1993 and was buried in Paris. A biography, Beyond the Last Blue Mountain by R. M. Lala, remains a standard account of his life.
