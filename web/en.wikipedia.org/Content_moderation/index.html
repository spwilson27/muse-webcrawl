Content moderation

Content moderation is the process by which online platforms monitor, review, and act on user-generated content to enforce community standards, terms of service, and legal requirements. Actions range from labeling and reduced distribution to removal of content and suspension of accounts.

Methods

Moderation may occur before publication (pre-moderation), after publication in response to user reports, or reactively through automated systems. Platforms combine machine-learning classifiers and hash-matching tools (such as PhotoDNA for child sexual abuse material) with human reviewers, many of them contract workers reviewing large volumes of disturbing material — a labor practice that has drawn criticism over psychological harm.

Debates

Content moderation sits at the center of controversies over free expression, misinformation, harassment, and platform power. Regulatory frameworks such as the EU Digital Services Act impose transparency and due-process obligations, while in the United States, Section 230 generally shields platforms from liability for user content and for good-faith moderation decisions.
