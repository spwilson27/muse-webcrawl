Opioid overdose
Symptoms: Pinpoint pupils, unconsciousness, respiratory depression
Causes: Opioids such as morphine, heroin, fentanyl
Treatment: Naloxone, supportive care, assisted ventilation

An opioid overdose is toxicity due to excessive consumption of opioids, such as morphine, codeine, heroin, fentanyl, tramadol, or methadone. This results in decreased levels of consciousness, pinpoint pupils, and respiratory depression; untreated, the resulting lack of oxygen can cause brain damage and death. The risk rises when opioids are combined with alcohol or sedatives such as benzodiazepines, and when tolerance has fallen after a period of abstinence.

Signs and symptoms

The classic triad is coma, pinpoint pupils (miosis), and respiratory depression. Other signs include blue lips or fingertips, slow or absent pulse, and gurgling or snoring sounds from airway obstruction.

Treatment

Immediate treatment is to support breathing and administer naloxone, an opioid antagonist that rapidly reverses the effects; it is available as an injection or nasal spray and is increasingly carried by first responders and laypeople under harm-reduction programs. Because naloxone's duration of action may be shorter than that of the opioid, victims require observation and sometimes repeated doses or an infusion.
