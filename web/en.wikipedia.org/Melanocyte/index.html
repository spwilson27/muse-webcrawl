Melanocytes are melanin-producing cells of neural crest origin located mainly in the basal layer of the skin's epidermis, in hair follicles, the uvea of the eye, the inner ear and the meninges. They produce the pigment melanin inside organelles called melanosomes, which they transfer to surrounding keratinocytes, colouring the skin and hair and protecting the skin from ultraviolet radiation.

Function

Each melanocyte supplies melanin to roughly 30–40 keratinocytes (the "epidermal melanin unit"). Melanin granules sit like caps over keratinocyte nuclei, absorbing ultraviolet light and scavenging free radicals. Differences in human skin colour result mainly from the amount, type (brown-black eumelanin versus red-yellow pheomelanin) and distribution of melanin, not from large differences in melanocyte number. Production is stimulated by ultraviolet exposure and by melanocyte-stimulating hormone acting on the MC1R receptor.

Development

Melanocytes develop from neural crest cells that migrate during embryogenesis under the control of genes including MITF, SOX10 and KIT. Defects in this pathway cause pigmentary and hearing disorders such as Waardenburg syndrome and piebaldism.

Clinical significance

Loss or autoimmune destruction of melanocytes causes vitiligo; absence of functional melanin synthesis causes albinism; benign proliferations form moles (naevi), and malignant transformation produces melanoma, the most dangerous form of skin cancer.
