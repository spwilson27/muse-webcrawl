Argonauts

The Argonauts (Greek: Ἀργοναῦται) were a band of heroes in Greek mythology who, in the years before the Trojan War, accompanied Jason to Colchis in his quest for the Golden Fleece. Their name comes from their ship, the Argo, named after its builder, Argus. The voyage of the Argonauts is one of the most famous adventure tales of Greek myth, retold most fully in the Argonautica of Apollonius of Rhodes.

The quest

Jason, the rightful heir of Iolcus, was commanded by the usurper Pelias to fetch the Golden Fleece from Colchis on the eastern shore of the Black Sea — a task expected to be fatal. Jason assembled the greatest heroes of the age: among them Heracles, Orpheus, Castor and Pollux, Atalanta, Peleus, Theseus (in some versions), and the winged sons of Boreas. The Argo, built under the guidance of Athena with a speaking oak beam from Dodona in its prow, carried the crew through many adventures, including the Harpies, the clashing Symplegades rocks, and the island of Lemnos.

Colchis and return

In Colchis, King Aeëtes set Jason impossible tasks — yoking fire-breathing bulls, ploughing a field, and sowing dragon's teeth. The king's daughter Medea, a sorceress, fell in love with Jason and aided him with her magic; she later helped him seize the Fleece, guarded by a sleepless dragon, and fled with the Argonauts. The return voyage, in different accounts, took the Argo through a variety of miraculous routes before Jason reached Iolcus.

Legacy

The tale influenced literature from antiquity onward, and elements of it have been compared with the epic of Gilgamesh and other Near Eastern voyage stories. The story has been adapted many times in modern literature and film, notably Ray Harryhausen's 1963 film Jason and the Argonauts.
