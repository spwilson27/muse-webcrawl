Lord Atkin

James Richard Atkin, Baron Atkin
Born: 28 November 1867, Brisbane, Queensland
Died: 25 June 1944, Aberdyfi, Wales
Occupation: Judge; Lord of Appeal in Ordinary (1928–1944)

James Richard Atkin, Baron Atkin (1867–1944), known as Lord Atkin, was a lawyer and judge of Australian-Welsh origin who served as a Law Lord. He is one of the most influential figures in the development of the modern law of negligence.

Donoghue v Stevenson

In Donoghue v Stevenson [1932], Atkin formulated the “neighbour principle”: persons are under a duty to take reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions which they can reasonably foresee would be likely to injure their neighbour — those so closely and directly affected by their acts that they ought reasonably to be in contemplation. This laid the foundation for the modern tort of negligence throughout the Commonwealth.

Other work

Atkin's dissent in Liversidge v Anderson [1942], defending judicial scrutiny of executive detention in wartime against the majority's deference, was vindicated by later generations and is celebrated as a defence of the rule of law.
