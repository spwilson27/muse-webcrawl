Bill Monroe

William Smith Monroe (September 13, 1911 – September 9, 1996) was an American mandolinist, singer, and songwriter who created the bluegrass music genre. Because of this, he is often called the "Father of Bluegrass". The genre takes its name from his band, the Blue Grass Boys, named for Monroe's home state of Kentucky.

Career

Monroe performed with his brothers Birch and Charlie as the Monroe Brothers in the 1930s before forming the Blue Grass Boys in 1939 and joining the Grand Ole Opry, where he remained a member for more than 50 years. The classic 1945–48 lineup, with Lester Flatt on guitar, Earl Scruggs on banjo, Chubby Wise on fiddle, and Cedric Rainwater on bass, defined the bluegrass sound: high, lonesome harmonies over virtuosic, driving string-band instrumentation.

Monroe's compositions — including "Blue Moon of Kentucky" (later recorded by Elvis Presley), "Uncle Pen", "Footprints in the Snow", and "Raw Hide" — became bluegrass standards. He founded one of the first bluegrass festivals at Bean Blossom, Indiana, in 1967. Monroe was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1970, the Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1971, and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award.
