Digital root

The digital root (also called the repeated digital sum) of a natural number in a given number base is the single-digit value obtained by an iterative process of summing digits, where each iteration uses the result from the previous iteration in order to compute a digit sum.

Definition

For example, the digital root of 65,536 in base 10 is 7, because 6 + 5 + 5 + 3 + 6 = 25, and 2 + 5 = 7. The process continues until a single-digit number is reached.

Properties

The digital root is congruent to the number modulo 9 (with 0 corresponding to 9 for multiples of 9), a fact historically used in the bookkeeping check known as casting out nines. Digital roots form repeating patterns in multiplication tables and are related to the concept of additive persistence, the number of iterations required to reach the root.
