Basis (linear algebra)


The same vector expressed in the standard basis and in another basis

In mathematics, a set B of vectors in a vector space V is called a basis (plural bases) if every element of V may be written in a unique way as a finite linear combination of elements of B. Equivalently, a basis is a set of vectors that is linearly independent and spans the space. The coefficients of this linear combination are referred to as components or coordinates of the vector with respect to B.

Every vector space admits a basis, a result that relies on the axiom of choice. All bases of a given vector space have the same cardinality, called the dimension of the space; this is the content of the dimension theorem. A basis is called an ordered basis when an ordering of its elements matters, as it does for coordinates and matrices of linear transformations.

Standard basis

The standard basis of ℝn consists of the unit vectors along the coordinate axes; for example, in ℝ3 it is {(1,0,0), (0,1,0), (0,0,1)}. A vector (x, y, z) is the linear combination xe1 + ye2 + ze3. For polynomial spaces, the monomials 1, x, x², … form a basis of the space of polynomials of bounded degree.

Change of basis

Coordinates of the same vector in two different bases are related by an invertible change-of-basis matrix; linear transformations transform accordingly by similarity. In inner product spaces, an orthonormal basis, in which the vectors are mutually perpendicular unit vectors, is especially convenient.
