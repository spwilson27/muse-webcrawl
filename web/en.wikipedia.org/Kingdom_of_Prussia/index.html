The Kingdom of Prussia (German: Königreich Preußen) was a German state that existed from 1701 to 1918. It originated in the union of the Margraviate of Brandenburg and the Duchy of Prussia, ruled by the Hohenzollern dynasty, and grew from a modest north-European power into the dominant state of Germany. Prussia spearheaded the unification of Germany in 1871, and the Prussian king simultaneously held the title of German Emperor until the monarchy's collapse at the end of the First World War.

Origins

Elector Frederick III of Brandenburg secured the consent of Emperor Leopold I and Poland to crown himself "King in Prussia" — the careful wording reflecting that Royal Prussia remained Polish territory — at Königsberg on 18 January 1701, taking the name Frederick I. His successor, Frederick William I, the "Soldier King," built a famously disciplined army and a frugal, centralised administration.

Frederick the Great and expansion

Frederick II ("the Great," r. 1740–1786) seized Silesia from Austria in the War of the Austrian Succession and defended the conquest through the Seven Years' War, elevating Prussia to the rank of a European great power. Prussia also gained territory in the Partitions of Poland (1772, 1793, 1795), acquiring West Prussia and the Posen region. Frederick styled himself an enlightened absolutist, patronised philosophy and the arts, and codified much Prussian law.

Napoleonic era and reform

Crushing defeats by Napoleon at Jena and Auerstedt in 1806 triggered the Stein–Hardenberg reforms: abolition of serfdom, municipal self-government, army reorganisation under Scharnhorst and Gneisenau, and the founding of the University of Berlin (1810). Prussian forces contributed decisively to Napoleon's defeat, including at Waterloo in 1815. The Congress of Vienna awarded Prussia the Rhineland and Westphalia, giving it a large western, industrialising territory.

Unification of Germany

Under Minister President Otto von Bismarck (from 1862), Prussia won wars against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–71). The North German Confederation (1867) and then the German Empire, proclaimed at Versailles on 18 January 1871, made the Prussian king German Emperor. Prussia retained its own parliament, army administration, and — through its size — decisive influence within the Empire, including a three-class franchise for its Landtag that favoured wealthy voters.

First World War and end

Prussia supplied the bulk of the German war effort in 1914–1918. The November Revolution of 1918 forced Wilhelm II's abdication; Prussia became the Free State of Prussia within the Weimar Republic, for decades a stronghold of democratic government under Otto Braun, until Franz von Papen's 1932 Preußenschlag coup. The Allied Control Council formally dissolved the Prussian state by Law No. 46 on 25 February 1947, citing it as "a bearer of militarism and reaction."
