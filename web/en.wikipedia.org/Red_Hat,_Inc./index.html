Red Hat, Inc. is an American multinational software company that provides open-source software products for the enterprise community. Founded in 1993 and headquartered in Raleigh, North Carolina, Red Hat is the world's leading provider of enterprise open-source solutions, best known for Red Hat Enterprise Linux (RHEL). In July 2019, IBM acquired Red Hat for approximately US$34 billion, the largest software acquisition of its time; Red Hat operates as a distinct unit within IBM, retaining its headquarters and brand.

History

Red Hat was formed from Marc Ewing's Red Hat Linux distribution and Bob Young's ACC Corporation, which merged in 1995. The company went public in 1999 in one of the most successful IPOs of the dot-com era. It shifted to a subscription enterprise model with RHEL in the early 2000s, sponsoring the community Fedora Project as its upstream. Subsequent acquisitions included JBoss (2006), and products grew to include the OpenShift container platform (based on Kubernetes), Ansible automation, and OpenStack and storage offerings.

Business model

Red Hat sells subscriptions for support, updates, certification and indemnification around open-source software rather than selling proprietary licences — a model that made it the first open-source company to exceed US$1 billion in annual revenue (2012) and US$2 billion (2016).
