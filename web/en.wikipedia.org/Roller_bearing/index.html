Roller bearing

A roller bearing, also known as a rolling-element bearing, is a bearing which carries a load by placing rolling elements (such as balls or rollers) between two bearing rings called races. The relative motion of the races causes the rolling elements to roll with very little rolling resistance and with little sliding.

Design

Roller bearings use cylinders of slightly greater length than diameter as rolling elements. Compared with ball bearings, they have a larger contact area with the races and hence a higher radial load capacity, though typically a lower maximum speed. A cage or separator keeps the rollers evenly spaced, and bearing rings are usually made of hardened steel.

Types

  • Cylindrical roller bearings — cylinders parallel to the axis; high radial capacity.
  • Spherical roller bearings — barrel-shaped rollers that tolerate misalignment.
  • Tapered roller bearings — truncated cones that support combined radial and axial loads.
  • Needle roller bearings — very thin rollers for compact designs.
  • Thrust roller bearings — designed primarily for axial loads.

Applications

Roller bearings are used in gearboxes, axle boxes on railway vehicles, wind turbines, rolling mills, conveyor systems, and heavy machinery. Their failure modes include fatigue spalling, brinelling, and contamination wear, and their service life is commonly estimated using the L10 rating-life model.
