Profanity

Profanity, also called swearing or cursing, is the use of language considered offensive, vulgar, or taboo in a given culture. Originally referring to irreverence toward sacred things, the term now covers obscenities, blasphemy, and slurs.

Categories and function

Profanities draw on religion, excrement, sex, disease, and group identity. Research in psychology and neurology shows swearing can express emotion, relieve pain, and signal group solidarity; it is processed partly in older brain structures, which is why some aphasia patients retain the ability to swear. Social norms, broadcasting regulation (such as George Carlin's "seven dirty words" case, FCC v. Pacifica), and workplace rules govern its public use, varying widely by culture and era.
