Sacred grove

Sacred groves are patches of forest or woodland that communities protect for religious or spiritual reasons, leaving them largely undisturbed. Because felling, hunting and sometimes entry are restricted by taboo, sacred groves often preserve old-growth vegetation and biodiversity long after surrounding landscapes are cleared.

Around the world

Thousands of sacred groves survive in India — such as the devrai of Maharashtra, the kavu of Kerala and the groves of Meghalaya — dedicated to local deities and ancestral spirits. Similar institutions existed in ancient Greece and Rome (the lucus), among the Celts and Germanic peoples, in Shinto Japan (shrine forests, chinju no mori), and in West Africa, including the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove in Nigeria, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Conservation value

Ecologists value sacred groves as refuges for endemic species, seed sources for restoration and indicators of historical vegetation, though many are threatened by urbanisation and weakening of traditional beliefs.
