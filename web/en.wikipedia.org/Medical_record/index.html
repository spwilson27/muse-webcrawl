Medical record

The terms medical record, health record and medical chart are used somewhat interchangeably to describe the systematic documentation of a single patient's medical history and care across time within one particular health care provider's jurisdiction. A medical record includes a variety of types of "notes" entered over time by health care professionals, recording observations and administration of drugs and therapies, orders for the administration of drugs and therapies, test results, X-rays, reports, and so on.

Contents and use

Records document presenting complaints, diagnoses, medications, allergies, immunisations, laboratory and imaging results, and treatment plans. They serve clinical communication, continuity of care, billing, quality review, research, and as legal evidence of care provided. Traditionally kept on paper, records are now predominantly electronic: the electronic medical record (EMR) and electronic health record (EHR) enable sharing across providers, decision support, and patient portals.

Ownership and access

The physical record is usually the property of the provider or facility, while patients in many jurisdictions have legal rights to inspect and copy their information and to request corrections. Privacy laws such as HIPAA in the United States and the GDPR in Europe regulate disclosure of protected health information.
