Routemaster

The AEC Routemaster is a front-engined double-decker bus designed by London Transport and built by the Associated Equipment Company (AEC) and Park Royal Vehicles from 1954 until 1968. Introduced into service in 1956, the Routemaster — with its lightweight aluminium body, half-cab, and open rear platform — became the standard London bus and one of the city's most enduring icons. Around 2,876 were built; the last were withdrawn from regular service in December 2005, though refurbished examples returned on a heritage route.

Design

The Routemaster was designed specifically for London's stop-start urban operation. Its aluminium monocoque construction made it significantly lighter than the buses it replaced, improving fuel economy. The open platform at the rear allowed passengers to board and alight quickly — including, informally, between stops — while the separate driver's half-cab and a conductor collecting fares made for fast operation. The engine, originally an AEC AV590 diesel, was mounted at the front beside the driver. Standard RM buses seated 64 passengers; the longer RML seated 72.

Service history

Prototypes entered service in 1956 and production vehicles from 1959, progressively replacing London's trolleybuses and older RT-type buses. Routemasters worked across the capital for nearly five decades. Under the privatised operators of the 1990s the fleet dwindled, and the last scheduled Routemasters on a standard route ran on route 159 on 9 December 2005, displaced by modern accessible buses with driver-only operation. A heritage service on route 15, later renumbered and reduced, kept conductor-operated Routemasters running in central London for tourists until 2019, with special services thereafter.

Successors and legacy

The New Routemaster ("Borismaster"), introduced in 2012 under Mayor Boris Johnson, was explicitly designed in homage to the original, with a rear platform (later generally kept closed). The original Routemaster remains an international symbol of London, appears on countless souvenirs, and many preserved examples operate tours and private hires worldwide.
