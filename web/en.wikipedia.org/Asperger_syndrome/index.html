Asperger syndrome

Asperger syndrome (AS), also known as Asperger's, was formerly a diagnosis for a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by significant difficulties in social interaction and nonverbal communication, along with restricted and repetitive patterns of behavior and interests, but without significant delays in language or cognitive development.

Asperger syndrome is now considered part of the autism spectrum.

History

The syndrome was named after Austrian pediatrician Hans Asperger, who described children in his practice in 1944 who lacked nonverbal communication skills, demonstrated limited empathy, and were physically clumsy. The diagnosis was popularised in the English-speaking world by Lorna Wing in the 1980s and was included in the DSM-IV in 1994.

Reclassification

In 2013, the DSM-5 folded Asperger syndrome into the umbrella diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder (ASD), and the ICD-11 followed suit. Many people previously diagnosed with Asperger's continue to identify with the term, and debate persists about the change, as well as about Asperger's own historical record during the Nazi era.

Characteristics

People with the profile formerly labelled Asperger's may pursue intense, focused interests, prefer routines, and experience sensory sensitivities, while typically having average or above-average language and intellectual abilities. Support is individualized and focuses on social communication skills, occupational therapy, and treatment of co-occurring conditions such as anxiety.
