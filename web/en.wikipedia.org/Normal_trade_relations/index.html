Normal trade relations

AbbreviationNTR
Former nameMost favoured nation (MFN) status (in U.S. law, until 1998)
MeaningThe recipient receives trade advantages equal to the best given to any partner
Key useU.S. trade law; WTO most-favoured-nation principle

Normal trade relations (NTR) is the term used in the law of the United States for the status known internationally as most favoured nation (MFN) treatment. A country granted NTR receives the same tariff rates and trade concessions that the United States gives to its best trading partners; it confers no special favour beyond non-discriminatory treatment.

Terminology

In 1998 the United States renamed "most favoured nation" status to "normal trade relations" in its domestic law, because the older phrase was widely misunderstood as implying special privileges rather than equal treatment. The international term remains MFN, enshrined in Article I of the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT 1947) and carried into the World Trade Organization agreements.

United States practice

For countries not members of the WTO, and historically for communist states under the Jackson–Vanik amendment (1974), NTR was subject to annual presidential waivers and congressional review, linking trade status to emigration and human-rights conditions. "Permanent normal trade relations" (PNTR) was granted to China in 2000 in connection with its accession to the WTO, and to Russia and Moldova in 2012. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, the United States suspended NTR for Russia and Belarus in 2022.
