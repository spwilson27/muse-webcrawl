Chihuahua (state)

Chihuahua, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Chihuahua, is the largest state in Mexico, covering about 247,455 square kilometres (95,543 sq mi) in the country's northwest. Its capital is the city of Chihuahua, and its largest city is Ciudad Juárez, across the Rio Grande from El Paso, Texas.

Geography

The state spans three great regions: the Sierra Madre Occidental mountains in the west, with the vast Copper Canyon system; the central plateau of grasslands and valleys; and the Chihuahuan Desert in the east, part of the largest desert in North America. The Sierra Tarahumara is home to the indigenous Rarámuri (Tarahumara) people, renowned as long-distance runners.

History

Chihuahua was a Spanish colonial mining frontier before becoming a Mexican state in 1824. During the Mexican Revolution, Chihuahua was the principal base of Pancho Villa's División del Norte, and the Battle of Ciudad Juárez in 1911 helped launch the revolution. The state later became a center of maquiladora manufacturing along the U.S. border.

Economy

Border manufacturing in Juárez, mining, agriculture (apples, pecans, cattle), and tourism to Copper Canyon — served by the Chihuahua al Pacífico ("Chepe") railway — drive the economy.
