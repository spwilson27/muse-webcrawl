The Jesus and Mary Chain

The Jesus and Mary Chain are a Scottish alternative rock band formed in East Kilbride in 1983 around the songwriting partnership of brothers Jim Reid and William Reid. Their sound fused pop melodies in the tradition of the Beach Boys and the Shangri-Las with waves of guitar feedback indebted to the Velvet Underground and the Stooges, creating a template that proved foundational for noise pop and shoegaze.

History

Signed by Alan McGee to Creation Records, the band's debut single "Upside Down" (1984) became an indie landmark. Their early gigs were brief, chaotic and sometimes ended in disturbances; drummer Bobby Gillespie later found fame with Primal Scream. The debut album Psychocandy (1985), with "Just Like Honey" and "Never Understand", buried sweet melodies under feedback and is ranked among the great debut albums. Darklands (1987) was more restrained; Automatic (1989) yielded the US alternative hit "Head On" (later covered by the Pixies). The band scored a top-ten single with "Sometimes Always" (1994), a duet with Hope Sandoval.

Breakup and reunion

Internal friction, especially between the brothers, led to the band's onstage collapse and breakup in 1999. They reunited for Coachella in 2007 and have toured since, releasing Damage and Joy (2017), their first album in 19 years, and Glasgow Eyes (2024). Their influence runs through shoegaze, noise pop and indie rock worldwide.
