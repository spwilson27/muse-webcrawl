Sultanate of Maguindanao

The Sultanate of Maguindanao (Maguindanaon: Kasultanan nu Magindanaw) was a sultanate that ruled parts of the island of Mindanao in the southern Philippines, especially in the modern provinces of Maguindanao del Norte and Maguindanao del Sur, from around 1500 to the early twentieth century. Centred on the Pulangi (Rio Grande de Mindanao) river basin near present-day Cotabato City, it was one of the most powerful Muslim states in the archipelago.

History

Islam reached the region through traders and missionaries; Sharif Kabungsuwan, traditionally said to have come from Johor around the early 1500s, is credited with founding the sultanate and spreading Islam among the Maguindanao people. Under rulers such as Sultan Muhammad Kudarat (reigned c. 1625–1671), the sultanate resisted Spanish attempts at conquest, controlled trade in forest and sea products, and extended its influence over much of Mindanao and into the Sulu archipelago.

Spanish expeditions reduced its power during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, though the sultanate was never fully conquered by Spain. Following the Spanish–American War, the United States dismantled the sultanate's authority in the early 1900s during the Moro campaigns.
