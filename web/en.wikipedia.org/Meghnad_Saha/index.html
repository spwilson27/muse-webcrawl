Meghnad Saha

Meghnad Saha FRS (6 October 1893 – 16 February 1956) was an Indian astrophysicist best known for developing the Saha ionization equation, which describes the physical and chemical conditions governing the ionization of elements and became a cornerstone of stellar spectroscopy and the interpretation of stellar spectra.

Life and career

Saha was born in Shaoratoli village near Dhaka, in present-day Bangladesh, into a poor family. He studied at Presidency College, Calcutta, where his classmates included Satyendra Nath Bose and his teachers included Jagadish Chandra Bose and Prafulla Chandra Ray. He taught at the University College of Science in Calcutta and later at Allahabad University, before returning to Calcutta as Palit Professor of Physics. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1927.

Work

Published in 1920–21, Saha's thermal ionization theory allowed astronomers to relate the spectral classes of stars to their temperatures and compositions, laying the foundation of modern astrophysics. Saha also founded the journal Science and Culture, established the Institute of Nuclear Physics in Calcutta (later renamed the Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics), championed scientific and industrial planning in independent India, promoted river-valley development projects such as the Damodar Valley Corporation, and led the reform of the Indian calendar. He was elected to the Indian Parliament in 1952. The lunar crater Saha is named after him.
