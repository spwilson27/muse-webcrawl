Hunahpu

Hunahpu (pronounced [hunaxˈpu], roughly "One Hunahpu") is one of the Maya Hero Twins in the mythology of the K'iche' Maya people, as recorded in the Popol Vuh. Together with his brother Xbalanque, Hunahpu is a central figure of the K'iche' creation narrative and one of the most prominent deities in Maya mythology. The twins also appear in Classic-period Maya art centuries before the Popol Vuh was written down.

Mythology

Hunahpu and Xbalanque were conceived miraculously: their father, Hun Hunahpu, had been defeated and sacrificed by the lords of the underworld, Xibalba, and his severed head, hung in a calabash tree, spat into the hand of the maiden Xquic, who became pregnant with the twins.

Raised under the hostility of their elder half-brothers, the twins became skilled hunters and ballplayers. Summoned to Xibalba to answer for the noise of their ballgame, they survived a series of deadly trials in the underworld's houses of darkness, razors, cold, jaguars, fire, and bats. In the House of Bats, Hunahpu was decapitated by the bat demon Camazotz; Xbalanque temporarily replaced his brother's head with a squash until the real head could be recovered.

Ultimately the twins defeated the lords of death, One Death and Seven Death, through trickery, performing miraculous dances and sacrifices and refusing to be tricked in turn. After their victory they revived their father as the Maize God, and the twins themselves ascended into the sky — in some accounts becoming the sun and the moon.

In Classic Maya art

The Hero Twins appear widely in Classic-period ceramics, often shown as youthful ballplayers, hunters with blowguns, or scribes. The blowgunner Hunahpu is frequently depicted shooting the bird demon Vucub Caquix (Seven Macaw) from a tree, an episode also narrated in the Popol Vuh.
