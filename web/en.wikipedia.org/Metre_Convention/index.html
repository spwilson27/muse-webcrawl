Metre Convention

The Metre Convention (Convention du Mètre), also known as the Treaty of the Metre, is an international treaty that created the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM), an intergovernmental organisation under the authority of the General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) and the supervision of the International Committee for Weights and Measures (CIPM). The treaty was signed in Paris on 20 May 1875 by representatives of 17 nations; 20 May is now celebrated as World Metrology Day. Membership has since grown to over 60 Member States, with additional associates.

Purpose

The Convention was created to establish a permanent, internationally agreed system of measurement units — originally the metre and the kilogram, whose platinum-iridium prototypes the BIPM made and kept at Sèvres, outside Paris — and to maintain and disseminate them. It is the legal foundation of the International System of Units (SI).

Organisations

The CGPM, meeting every four to six years, ratifies decisions such as the 2019 redefinition of the SI base units in terms of fundamental constants. The CIPM oversees the BIPM's laboratories, which coordinate international comparisons of national measurement standards and maintain facilities such as the international time reference.
