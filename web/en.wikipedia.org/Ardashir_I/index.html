Ardashir I (Middle Persian: Ardašīr), also known as Ardashir the Unifier (180 – February 242), was the founder of the Sasanian Empire and its first shahanshah ("King of Kings"), reigning from 224 to 242. He was the ruler of the southwestern Iranian region of Persis and a vassal of the Parthian Empire before he defeated the last Parthian king, Artabanus IV, at the Battle of Hormozdgan in 224.

Rise to power

Ardashir was the son of Papak, the ruler of Istakhr, and grandson of Sasan, after whom the dynasty is named. After Papak's death, Ardashir succeeded his brother Shapur as ruler of Persis and gradually subdued neighbouring princes, Fars and Kerman, building the city of Ardashir-Khwarrah (modern Firuzabad) as his capital.

Overthrow of the Parthians

In April 224 Ardashir met Artabanus IV in battle at Hormozdgan; Artabanus was killed and Parthian rule ended. Ardashir was crowned shahanshah at Ctesiphon in 226 and took the title "King of Kings of Iran". He subsequently campaigned against Rome, contesting Armenia, Mesopotamia and Hatra, which he captured in 240.

Administration and religion

Ardashir reorganised the empire with a centralised administration and a hierarchy of kings, princes and nobles subordinate to the shahanshah. He promoted Zoroastrianism as a pillar of the state, supporting the priest Kartir, and is credited in later tradition with collecting the sacred texts of the Avesta. His maxim — "there is no king without an army, no army without wealth, no wealth without taxes, and no taxes without agriculture" — summarises his programme of state-building.

Legacy

The empire Ardashir founded lasted more than four centuries, until the Muslim conquest in 651, and remained Rome's and Byzantium's principal eastern rival. He was succeeded by his son Shapur I.
