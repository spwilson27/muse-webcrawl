Mass media

Mass media refers to the forms of media that reach large audiences via mass communication: broadcast television and radio, film, print publishing (newspapers, magazines, books), recorded music, and digital and online media. The term arose in the 1920s as national broadcasting and mass-circulation newspapers transformed public communication.

Sectors and functions

Mass media serve to inform (news and current affairs), entertain, educate, and persuade (advertising and propaganda). Media industries are organized around these channels, historically dominated by large corporations and public broadcasters, and financed by advertising, subscriptions, or public funding. The rise of the internet and social media since the 2000s has blurred the line between mass and interpersonal communication, enabling many-to-many publishing and user-generated content alongside traditional outlets.

Study and effects

Media studies examines how mass media influence culture, politics, and behavior — through agenda-setting, framing, cultivation of worldviews, and the political economy of media ownership. Regulation varies by country and medium, balancing freedom of the press against rules on ownership concentration, content standards, and broadcasting licenses.
