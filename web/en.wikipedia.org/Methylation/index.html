Methylation

Methylation, in the chemical sciences, is the addition of a methyl group (CH3) to a substrate, or the substitution of an atom or group by a methyl group. In biochemistry the term is used most prominently for the enzymatic transfer of methyl groups, usually donated by S-adenosyl methionine (SAM), onto DNA, RNA, proteins and small molecules.

DNA methylation

DNA methylation typically occurs at cytosine bases in CpG sequences in mammals and is a central mechanism of epigenetic regulation: it helps control gene expression, genomic imprinting, X-chromosome inactivation and the silencing of transposable elements. Aberrant methylation patterns are associated with cancer and developmental disorders.

Other biological methylation

Histone methylation on lysine and arginine residues modulates chromatin structure; RNA methylation (such as m6A) regulates RNA processing and stability; and methylation reactions in the liver detoxify many compounds, including arsenic and various drugs.

In organic chemistry

In the laboratory, methylation is performed with reagents such as methyl iodide, dimethyl sulfate and diazomethane. Industrial methylation processes include the production of methyl esters for biodiesel and the synthesis of pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals.
