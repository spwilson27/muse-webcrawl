RMS Queen Mary 2 (QM2) is a British transatlantic ocean liner operated by Cunard. She was built in 2003 by the Chantiers de l'Atlantique shipyard in Saint-Nazaire, France, and was named by Queen Elizabeth II after the earlier liner RMS Queen Mary. At the time of her construction she was the largest passenger ship ever built, and she remains the largest ocean liner ever built—as distinct from cruise ships—designed specifically for regular transatlantic crossings between Southampton and New York.

Design

Unlike most modern cruise ships, QM2 was built with a strengthened hull, deeper draught, and a high service speed of 30 knots for North Atlantic service. She is powered by a combined diesel-electric and gas turbine plant driving four propulsion pods. Her interiors evoke the golden age of ocean travel, with an Art Deco–inspired Grand Lobby, the largest ballroom at sea (the Queens Room), a planetarium in the Illuminations theatre, and a library of some 8,000–10,000 volumes.

Service

Queen Mary 2 took over the flagship role and the transatlantic schedule from Queen Elizabeth 2 in 2004. She also undertakes world voyages and cruises. In 2016 she underwent a major "remastering" refit that added single staterooms and expanded kennel facilities—QM2 is known for carrying dogs and cats across the Atlantic. The ship carries the RMS (Royal Mail Ship) prefix by virtue of Cunard's mail contract tradition.
