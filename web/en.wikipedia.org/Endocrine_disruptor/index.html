Endocrine disruptor

Endocrine disruptors, sometimes also referred to as hormonally active agents, endocrine disrupting chemicals, or endocrine disrupting compounds, are chemicals that can interfere with endocrine (or hormonal) systems. These disruptions can cause cancerous tumors, birth defects, and other developmental disorders.

Mechanism

Endocrine disruptors can mimic naturally occurring hormones such as estrogen, block hormone receptors, or alter the synthesis and metabolism of hormones. Because hormones act at very low concentrations, even small exposures may produce biological effects.

Common examples

Well-studied endocrine disruptors include bisphenol A (BPA), certain phthalates used in plastics, polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs), the pesticide DDT, and some flame retardants. They are found in plastics, pesticides, cosmetics, and industrial byproducts.

Regulation

The European Union and the United States Environmental Protection Agency maintain screening and regulatory programs for endocrine disrupting chemicals, though the scientific and policy debate over low-dose effects remains active.
