Gravity is a fundamental interaction that causes all objects with mass or energy to be attracted (or gravitate) toward one another. Gravity is by far the weakest of the four fundamental interactions, approximately 10³⁸ times weaker than the strong interaction, yet it dominates the structure of the universe on large scales because it is always attractive and acts over unlimited range. On Earth, gravity gives weight to physical objects and causes the tides.

Newtonian gravity

Isaac Newton's law of universal gravitation (1687) states that every mass attracts every other mass with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. It accurately predicts planetary orbits, comets and tides, and remains an excellent approximation for most purposes.

General relativity

Albert Einstein's general theory of relativity (1915) describes gravity not as a force but as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy. It explains anomalies such as Mercury's perihelion precession and predicts black holes, gravitational lensing, gravitational time dilation and gravitational waves, all since confirmed by observation.

Open questions

Reconciling gravity with quantum mechanics — a theory of quantum gravity — remains one of the great unsolved problems in physics. Phenomena attributed to dark matter and dark energy may also point to modifications of gravity on galactic and cosmological scales.
