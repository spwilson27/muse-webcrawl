Deicing

Deicing is the process of removing snow, ice or frost from a surface. Anti-icing is the application of chemicals that not only deice but also remain on a surface and continue to delay the reformation of ice for a certain period of time, or prevent adhesion of ice to make mechanical removal easier.

Methods

Deicing can be accomplished by mechanical methods (scraping or pushing), through the application of heat, by use of dry or liquid chemicals designed to lower the freezing point of water, or by a combination of these techniques. Common deicing chemicals include sodium chloride (rock salt), calcium chloride, magnesium chloride and potassium acetate. Urea and glycols are used where chlorides would cause unacceptable corrosion.

Aircraft deicing

In aviation, ground deicing of aircraft is critical, because even a thin layer of frost on a wing can disrupt airflow and reduce lift. Heated mixtures of propylene glycol or ethylene glycol and water are sprayed onto the aircraft; thicker Type II–IV fluids provide anti-icing protection for a limited "holdover time" before takeoff.

Roads and environment

Salt spread on roads lowers the freezing point of water and melts ice, but runoff can damage vegetation, contaminate water supplies and corrode vehicles and infrastructure. These concerns have encouraged reduced-salt strategies, pre-wetting, brine application and alternative chemicals.
