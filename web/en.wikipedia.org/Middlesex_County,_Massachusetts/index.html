Middlesex County, Massachusetts

Middlesex County is a county in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, in the United States. As of the 2020 census, the population was 1,632,002, making it the most populous county in Massachusetts and in New England, and among the most populous counties in the United States without county government (the county government was abolished in 1997). The county's traditional seats are Cambridge and Lowell.

The county was created by the Massachusetts General Court on May 10, 1643, and named after the English county of Middlesex. It includes much of Boston's northwestern and western suburbs, including Cambridge, Somerville, Newton, Waltham, Lowell, Framingham, and Malden, and is home to institutions such as Harvard University, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, and Tufts University. The Route 128 corridor within the county is a major high-technology and biotechnology centre.
