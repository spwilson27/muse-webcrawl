Virginia Dare (born 18 August 1587) was the first English child born in a New World English colony. She was born in the Roanoke Colony in what is now North Carolina to Ananias and Eleanor Dare, the daughter of the colony's governor, John White. Her fate is unknown, as the colony — the "Lost Colony" — was found deserted when White returned in 1590.

Birth and the Lost Colony

Dare was born nine days after the colonists' arrival at Roanoke Island and was baptised the following Sunday. Governor White sailed for England in late 1587 to obtain supplies, but the Anglo-Spanish War delayed his return until August 1590. He found the settlement abandoned, with the word "CROATOAN" carved on a post — suggesting the colonists had moved to Croatoan Island — but weather prevented a search, and no trace of Virginia Dare was ever found.

Theories and legends

Historians generally suppose the colonists dispersed and were absorbed into, or killed by, neighbouring Native American peoples. Legends grew around Dare, including tales that she survived among the Croatoan or other tribes; the so-called Dare Stones of the 1930s, purporting to record her life and death, are widely regarded as forgeries.

Cultural legacy

Virginia Dare became a romantic symbol of early America, commemorated in poems, plays, place names (Dare County, North Carolina) and product brands, including Virginia Dare wines and flavour extracts. She is depicted in a statue at the Elizabethan Gardens on Roanoke Island and in the bronze doors of the North Carolina museum collections.
