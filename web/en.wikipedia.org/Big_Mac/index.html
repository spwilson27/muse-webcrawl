Big Mac

The Big Mac is a hamburger sold by the international fast food restaurant chain McDonald's. It was introduced in the Greater Pittsburgh area in 1967 and across the United States in 1968. It is one of the company's flagship products and signature dishes.

Composition

The Big Mac consists of two 1.6 oz (45.4 g) beef patties, "Big Mac sauce" (a Thousand Island dressing variant), iceberg lettuce, American cheese, pickles, and onions on a three-part sesame seed bun, with the middle bun layer known as the "club". A famous 1974 advertising jingle listed the ingredients: "Two all-beef patties, special sauce, lettuce, cheese, pickles, onions on a sesame seed bun."

History

The sandwich was created by Jim Delligatti, a Pittsburgh franchisee, and named by Esther Glickstein Rose, a secretary in McDonald's advertising department. It was modelled on double-deck burgers sold by chains such as Big Boy. The Big Mac is sold in more than one hundred countries, with regional variations such as the chicken and Maharaja Mac versions in India.

Big Mac Index

Since 1986, The Economist has published the Big Mac index as a light-hearted measure of purchasing power parity, comparing the price of a Big Mac in different currencies to judge whether currencies are under- or over-valued against the US dollar.
