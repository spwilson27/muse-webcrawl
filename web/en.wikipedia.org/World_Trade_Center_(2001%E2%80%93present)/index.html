World Trade Center (2001–present)

LocationLower Manhattan, New York City, United States
OwnerPort Authority of New York and New Jersey
Tallest buildingOne World Trade Center, 1,776 ft (541 m)

The World Trade Center is a mostly completed complex of buildings in Lower Manhattan, New York City, built to replace the original complex destroyed in the September 11 attacks of 2001. The new complex includes the tallest building in the United States, One World Trade Center, as well as the National September 11 Memorial & Museum.

History

After the destruction of the original Twin Towers on September 11, 2001, an extensive planning and design process followed. The Lower Manhattan Development Corporation organized a master plan competition won by architect Daniel Libeskind. Construction proceeded over more than a decade amid debates over design, financing, and security.

Buildings

One World Trade Center opened in November 2014, rising 104 stories to a symbolic height of 1,776 feet. The complex also includes 3 World Trade Center, 4 World Trade Center, 7 World Trade Center, the Oculus transportation hub designed by Santiago Calatrava, and the National September 11 Memorial, whose twin reflecting pools occupy the footprints of the original towers. Two World Trade Center remains the last tower planned but unfinished.

Memorial and museum

The National September 11 Memorial opened on September 11, 2011, the tenth anniversary of the attacks, and the museum opened in 2014. The names of the victims are inscribed on bronze parapets surrounding the memorial pools.
