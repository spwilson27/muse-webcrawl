Veterans Health Administration

The Veterans Health Administration (VHA) is the component of the United States Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) that implements the healthcare program of the VA in the United States. It is the largest integrated healthcare system in the country, providing care at more than 1,300 facilities, including 171 medical centres and over 1,100 outpatient sites, and serving roughly nine million enrolled veterans each year.

History

Federal care for veterans began with early soldiers' homes and expanded after the Civil War and World War I. The Veterans Bureau (1921) and then the Veterans Administration (1930) consolidated these programs; the Department of Medicine and Surgery, the VHA's predecessor, was established in 1946 and built a nationwide hospital system affiliated with medical schools. In 1989 the VA was elevated to a cabinet department. Under Under Secretary Kenneth Kizer in the 1990s, the VHA was reorganised into regional Veterans Integrated Service Networks (VISNs), shifted emphasis toward primary and outpatient care, and became an early leader in electronic health records with its VistA system.

Services

Eligible veterans receive a standard medical benefits package covering preventive, primary, specialty, mental health, surgical, rehabilitation and long-term care, with copayments depending on priority group and service connection. The VHA operates notable programs in mental health and suicide prevention, spinal cord injury, polytrauma, prosthetics, and treatment of conditions such as PTSD. It also has a large research program and trains a substantial share of American physicians through affiliations with more than 100 medical schools.

Controversies

The VHA has faced recurring criticism over waiting times and quality variation. In 2014, revelations that staff at the Phoenix VA and other facilities had kept unofficial waiting lists, while some veterans died awaiting care, led to a national scandal, the resignation of Secretary Eric Shinseki, and the Veterans Access, Choice and Accountability Act of 2014. The subsequent VA MISSION Act of 2018 expanded veterans' options to receive community (private) care paid for by the VA.
