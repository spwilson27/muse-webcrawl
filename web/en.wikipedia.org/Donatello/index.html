Donato di Niccolò di Betto Bardi (c. 1386 – 13 December 1466), better known as Donatello, was an Italian sculptor of the Renaissance period. Born in Florence, he studied classical sculpture and developed a complete mastery of bronze, wood, stone and other media. He is remembered as one of the greatest sculptors of all time and the founder of modern sculpture, pioneering techniques such as rilievo schiacciato ("flattened relief") and reviving the free-standing nude and the equestrian monument.

Life and career

Donatello was apprenticed in the workshop of Lorenzo Ghiberti and worked with Filippo Brunelleschi, with whom tradition says he travelled to Rome to study ancient ruins. His marble Saint George (c. 1415–17) for Orsanmichele already showed a new psychological intensity. For the Medici he created the bronze David (c. 1440s), the first free-standing nude male sculpture since antiquity. In Padua (1443–1453) he executed the high altar of the Basilica of Saint Anthony and the bronze equestrian monument to the condottiere Gattamelata, the first of its kind since ancient Rome. His late wooden Magdalene Penitent is among the most expressive works of the Renaissance.

Style and influence

Donatello's figures combine classical naturalism with intense emotion and dramatic movement. His innovations in perspective relief and expressive modelling profoundly influenced Italian sculpture, including the young Michelangelo.
