Varicose veins

Specialty: Vascular surgery, dermatology
Symptoms: Enlarged twisted veins, aching legs, swelling
Causes: Weak or damaged vein valves
Risk factors: Age, pregnancy, obesity, prolonged standing, family history

Varicose veins, also known as varicoses, are a medical condition in which superficial veins become enlarged and twisted. They typically develop in the legs just under the skin, where the veins must carry blood upward against gravity. When valves in the veins weaken or fail, blood pools, the veins stretch and become visible and bulging.

Signs and symptoms

Varicose veins are often mainly a cosmetic concern, but they may cause aching, heaviness, itching, ankle swelling and night cramps. Complications can include skin changes and lipodermatosclerosis, venous ulcers, superficial thrombophlebitis, and bleeding. They are more common in women and increase in frequency with age and pregnancy.

Treatment

Conservative measures include exercise, leg elevation, weight management and compression stockings. Procedural treatments include sclerotherapy, endovenous laser treatment and radiofrequency ablation, and surgical ligation and stripping of the affected vein. Spider veins are a smaller, milder related condition.
