Tanker (ship)

A tanker (or tank ship) is a ship designed to transport or store liquids or gases in bulk. Major types include oil tankers (carrying crude oil and refined products), chemical tankers, liquefied natural gas (LNG) carriers, and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) carriers. Tankers range from small coastal vessels of a few hundred tonnes deadweight to very large crude carriers (VLCCs) and ultra-large crude carriers (ULCCs) exceeding 300,000 DWT.

History

The first successful oil tanker, the Zoroaster, was built in 1878 for the Nobel brothers' operations on the Caspian Sea; the first ocean-going tanker, the Glückauf (1886), established the basic layout of hull-as-tank. Post-Second World War growth in oil trade drove tanker sizes upward through the Suezmax, VLCC, and ULCC classes, culminating in the Seawise Giant (1979), the largest ship ever built. Disasters such as the Exxon Valdez spill (1989) led under MARPOL rules to the phase-out of single-hull tankers in favour of double hulls.

Design and operation

Tankers divide their cargo space with longitudinal and transverse bulkheads into separate tanks, loaded and discharged by pipelines and pumps rather than cranes. Inert gas systems blanket cargo tanks to prevent explosions. Product tankers carry refined fuels with coated tanks; chemical tankers have stainless-steel tanks for corrosive cargoes; gas carriers use pressurized, refrigerated, or membrane containment systems to keep gas liquefied.

Fleet and markets

Tankers move roughly a third of world seaborne trade by tonnage. Charter rates are notoriously volatile, quoted on the Worldscale system. Size classifications — Handysize, MR, LR1/LR2, Aframax, Suezmax, VLCC — reflect the ports and canals each class can use.
