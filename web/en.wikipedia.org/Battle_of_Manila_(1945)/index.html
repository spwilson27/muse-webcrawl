Battle of Manila (1945)

Battle of Manila (1945)
Date3 February – 3 March 1945
LocationManila, Philippines
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsUnited States and Filipino forces; Empire of Japan
CommandersDouglas MacArthur, Oscar Griswold; Sanji Iwabuchi

The Battle of Manila (3 February – 3 March 1945) was a major battle of the Pacific War, fought by American and Filipino forces against Japanese naval infantry commanded by Rear Admiral Sanji Iwabuchi. It was the scene of the worst urban fighting in the Pacific theatre and one of the most destructive battles of the Second World War.

American units raced to Manila in early February, liberating the civilian internees at Santo Tomas University, but Iwabuchi's Manila Naval Defense Force refused orders to evacuate and fought from fortified positions in the south of the city. A month of artillery bombardment and house-to-house fighting reduced much of Manila to rubble. During the battle, Japanese troops committed widespread atrocities against Filipino civilians — the Manila massacre — and an estimated 100,000 civilians died in the fighting.

Aftermath

By 3 March organized resistance had ended. The Japanese garrison was virtually annihilated, American forces lost about 1,000 killed, and Manila, once known as the "Pearl of the Orient", was among the most devastated Allied capitals of the war. The battle completed the liberation of the Philippine capital as MacArthur returned governmental authority to the Commonwealth.
