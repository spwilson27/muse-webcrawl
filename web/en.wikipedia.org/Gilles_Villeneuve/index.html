Gilles Villeneuve

Joseph Gilles Villeneuve (18 January 1950 – 8 May 1982) was a Canadian racing driver who competed in Formula One for McLaren and Ferrari. Renowned for his spectacular car control, fearless style and never-give-up attitude, he won six Grands Prix and finished runner-up in the 1979 World Championship to his Ferrari teammate Jody Scheckter. He was killed in a crash during qualifying for the 1982 Belgian Grand Prix at Zolder.

Career

Villeneuve began racing snowmobiles in Quebec before moving to Formula Ford and Formula Atlantic, winning the Canadian and US Atlantic titles. A guest appearance in Formula One for McLaren at the 1977 British Grand Prix so impressed that Ferrari signed him before season's end. His first victory, at his home race in Montreal in 1978, made him a national hero. In 1979 he fought for the title and famously dueled René Arnoux wheel-to-wheel at Dijon, a battle still cited among the greatest in Grand Prix history. Despite uncompetitive cars in 1980–81, he won brilliantly at Monaco and Jarama in 1981.

Death and legacy

In 1982, after a bitter dispute with teammate Didier Pironi over team orders at Imola, Villeneuve crashed fatally at Zolder when his Ferrari struck the slower car of Jochen Mass in qualifying. The Montreal circuit was named Circuit Gilles Villeneuve in his honour, and his son Jacques won the 1997 Formula One World Championship. Gilles Villeneuve remains one of the most beloved drivers in Ferrari and Formula One history.
