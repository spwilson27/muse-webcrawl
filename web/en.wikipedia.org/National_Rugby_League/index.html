National Rugby League

The National Rugby League (NRL) is the premier professional rugby league competition in Australasia, run by the Australian Rugby League Commission. Founded in 1998 out of the reunification that ended the Super League war, the NRL premiership is contested by teams from Australia and New Zealand and is one of the most watched sporting competitions in the region.

History

Rugby league in Australia dates to 1908, when the New South Wales Rugby League premiership began in Sydney. The competition nationalised in the 1980s and 1990s before splitting in 1997 between the Australian Rugby League and the breakaway Super League. The 1998 NRL season reunited the game as a partnership, with the competition coming fully under the ARL Commission in 2012.

Teams and format

The league expanded to seventeen teams with the admission of the Dolphins of Redcliffe in 2023, joining clubs such as the Melbourne Storm, Penrith Panthers, Brisbane Broncos, Sydney Roosters, South Sydney Rabbitohs and the New Zealand Warriors. The season runs from March to October, ending with the NRL Grand Final in Sydney. The State of Origin series between New South Wales and Queensland is played mid-season. An affiliated women's competition, the NRL Women's Premiership, began in 2018.
