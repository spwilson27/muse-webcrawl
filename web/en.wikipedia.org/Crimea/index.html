Crimea

Crimea is a peninsula in Eastern Europe, on the northern coast of the Black Sea, almost entirely surrounded by the Black Sea and the smaller Sea of Azov. The peninsula comprises the Autonomous Republic of Crimea (Ukraine) and the city of Sevastopol. Its status has been disputed since 2014, when Russia annexed Crimea following a referendum conducted under Russian military control; most countries continue to recognise it as part of Ukraine. The peninsula is connected to the Ukrainian mainland by the Isthmus of Perekop.

Geography

Crimea divides into three zones: the flat steppe of the north and centre, used for grain and sunflowers; the Crimean Mountains along the south-east coast, rising to 1,545 m at Roman-Kosh; and the narrow southern coast, with a mild, Mediterranean-like climate, historically a resort region of palaces and sanatoria. The peninsula has few rivers; water supply long depended on the North Crimean Canal from the Dnieper.

History

Known to the Greeks as Taurica, Crimea hosted Greek colonies such as Chersonesus, then came under Roman, Byzantine, Khazar, Mongol, and Genoese influence. The Crimean Khanate, a Turkic state under Ottoman suzerainty, ruled from the fifteenth century until Russian annexation in 1783 under Catherine the Great. The Crimean War (1853–56) pitted Russia against Britain, France, and the Ottoman Empire. In 1954 the USSR transferred Crimea from the Russian SFSR to the Ukrainian SSR. The Crimean Tatars, deported en masse by Stalin in 1944, were permitted to return only from the late 1980s. After Ukrainian independence in 1991, Crimea remained an autonomous republic within Ukraine, with Russia's Black Sea Fleet based at Sevastopol under lease agreements.

Annexation and aftermath

In February–March 2014, in the wake of Ukraine's Euromaidan revolution, Russian forces seized the peninsula; a referendum on joining Russia was held under occupation and condemned as illegitimate by the UN General Assembly. Russia's annexation triggered international sanctions. The 19-km Crimean Bridge across the Kerch Strait opened in 2018. After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Crimea became a major military staging area and itself a target of Ukrainian strikes.
