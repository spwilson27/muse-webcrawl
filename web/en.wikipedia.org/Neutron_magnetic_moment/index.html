Neutron magnetic moment

The neutron magnetic moment is the intrinsic magnetic dipole moment of the neutron. Protons and neutrons, both nucleons, behave as small magnets whose strengths are measured by their magnetic moments. The neutron, despite having no net electric charge, has a magnetic moment of approximately −1.913 nuclear magnetons.

Significance

The existence of a magnetic moment for an electrically neutral particle was an early indication that the neutron is not an elementary particle but is composed of charged quarks whose charges cancel overall while their magnetic moments do not. The measured value is now known to a relative precision of a few parts in 10^8 and is a cornerstone quantity in nuclear and particle physics.

Measurement

It is measured by neutron spin resonance and beam techniques developed from the work of Alvarez and Bloch (1940), and by comparing the neutron's Larmor precession frequency with that of the proton in the same magnetic field.
