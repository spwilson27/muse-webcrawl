The Pentagon is the headquarters building of the United States Department of Defense, located in Arlington County, Virginia, across the Potomac River from Washington, D.C. It is named for its shape, a regular pentagon, and as a symbol of the U.S. military the phrase "the Pentagon" is often used as a metonym for the Department of Defense and its leadership.

The building was designed by American architect George Bergstrom and built by contractor John McShain. Ground was broken on 11 September 1941 and the building was dedicated on 15 January 1943, an exceptionally fast construction driven by the demands of the Second World War. It consolidated 17 scattered War Department buildings.

Layout

Despite having five floors above ground plus mezzanine and basement levels, some 28 km (17.5 miles) of corridors and a five-acre central courtyard, it takes at most seven minutes to walk between any two points in the building because of its concentric ring design — five rings (A through E) connected by ten radial corridors. The building houses roughly 23,000 military and civilian employees and about 3,000 support personnel. By floor area it was the world's largest office building until surpassed by India's Surat Diamond Bourse in 2023.

September 11 attacks

On 11 September 2001, exactly 60 years after ground-breaking, American Airlines Flight 77 was hijacked and flown into the western side of the building as part of the September 11 attacks, killing 184 people: 59 passengers and crew aboard the aircraft and 125 in the Pentagon. The damaged section had recently been renovated with blast-resistant features, which limited casualties. The rebuilt area includes the Pentagon Memorial and a chapel at the impact point.

Renovation

The Pentagon Renovation Program (PENREN), carried out from the 1990s to 2011, gutted and rebuilt the entire building wedge by wedge, modernising systems, improving security and removing hazardous materials including asbestos.
