Geography

The Chartreuse Mountains (French: massif de la Chartreuse) are a mountain range in southeastern France, forming the southernmost part of the French Prealps. They lie north of Grenoble and south of Chambéry, within the departments of Isère and Savoie. The range is a folded limestone massif about 42 km long, bounded by deep gorges carved by the Guiers and Isère rivers.

Peaks

The highest summit is Chamechaude at 2,082 metres. Other prominent peaks include the Dent de Crolles (2,062 m), famous for one of Europe's deepest cave systems; the Granier (1,933 m), whose northern face collapsed in a catastrophic landslide in 1248 and again in smaller falls in 2016; and Charmant Som (1,867 m). Much of the range is protected within the Chartreuse Regional Natural Park, established in 1995.

The Grande Chartreuse

The range gives its name to the Grande Chartreuse, the mother house of the Carthusian Order, founded by Saint Bruno in 1084 in a remote valley near Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse. The monks there produce Chartreuse liqueur, distilled in nearby Voiron, whose green and yellow varieties named the color chartreuse. The area is also a centre for hiking, caving and cross-country skiing.
