Buzzcocks

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Buzzcocks are an English punk rock band formed in Manchester in 1976 by singer-songwriter Pete Shelley and guitarist Howard Devoto. They are regarded as pioneers of the punk and independent music movements, combining fast, melodic pop songcraft with lyrics about love, boredom and everyday frustration.

History

Inspired by seeing the Sex Pistols, Shelley and Devoto organised the Pistols' famous Lesser Free Trade Hall gigs in Manchester in 1976. After Devoto left to form Magazine, Shelley became frontman. The band's self-released Spiral Scratch EP (1977) was one of the first punk records issued on an independent label and helped launch the DIY indie movement.

Music and success

Signing to United Artists, Buzzcocks released a string of acclaimed singles, including "Orgasm Addict", "What Do I Get?" and their biggest hit, "Ever Fallen in Love (With Someone You Shouldn't've)" (1978), which reached number 12 in the UK. Their albums Another Music in a Different Kitchen, Love Bites and A Different Kind of Tension blended punk energy with sharp pop hooks. The compilation Singles Going Steady (1979) is widely considered a classic.

Later years

The band split in 1981 but re-formed in 1989 and toured and recorded for decades. Pete Shelley died in December 2018; the band has continued in his memory with Steve Diggle as frontman. Their name, incidentally, gave the BBC panel show Never Mind the Buzzcocks its title.
