Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences (Swedish: Kungliga Vetenskapsakademien) is one of the royal academies of Sweden. Founded in 1739 by, among others, the naturalist Carl Linnaeus, the botanist Jonas Alströmer and the mechanical engineer Mårten Triewald, it is an independent, non-governmental scientific organisation dedicated to promoting the sciences, particularly the natural sciences and mathematics.

Nobel Prizes

The Academy is best known internationally for awarding the Nobel Prizes in Physics and in Chemistry, and the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, under the terms of Alfred Nobel's will of 1895. Its Nobel Committees evaluate nominations and propose laureates, whom the Academy's members confirm in October each year. The Academy also awards its own prizes, including the Crafoord Prize in fields not covered by the Nobel Prizes, such as astronomy and mathematics, geosciences, biosciences and polyarthritis research.

Activities

The Academy publishes scientific journals including Ambio and Acta Mathematica (founded 1882), runs research institutes such as the Beijer Institute of Ecological Economics and the Abisko Scientific Research Station, and advises the Swedish government and public on scientific matters, including climate and environment policy. Members are elected in classes covering mathematics, astronomy and space science, physics, chemistry, geosciences, biological sciences, medical sciences, engineering, social sciences and a class for humanities and outstanding service to science.
