Gaffer (filmmaking)

A gaffer supervises the lighting crew on set

In film and television production, a gaffer is the head electrician, responsible for the execution (and sometimes the design) of the lighting plan for a production. The gaffer works under the director of photography (cinematographer), who designs the overall look of the lighting; together they translate that vision into the placement, powering, and control of lights and related equipment. The term is sometimes used for the chief lighting technician in theatre as well.

Role

The gaffer's duties include scouting locations for power availability, planning the electrical distribution and generator needs, selecting and rigging lighting instruments, and directing the electrical crew during the shoot. The gaffer's chief assistant is the best boy (best boy electric), who manages the electrical department's equipment, crew schedules, and paperwork. In contrast, the key grip heads the separate grip department, which handles rigging, flags, diffusion frames, and camera support movement.

Etymology

The origin of the word is uncertain. One long-standing explanation derives it from "gaffer" as an old term for a foreman or elderly supervisor (itself a contraction of "godfather" or "grandfather"), applied to the head of a work gang. Another tradition holds that early film electricians moved overhead lights with a long hooked pole called a "gaff", a tool also used in fishing and sailing.
