Mobile payment


Image placeholder: contactless mobile payment

Mobile payment, also referred to as mobile money, mobile money transfer, and mobile wallet, is any of various payment processing services operated under financial regulations and performed from or via a mobile device. Instead of paying with cash, cheque, or physical credit cards, a consumer can use a mobile phone to pay for a wide range of services and digital or hard goods.

Models

Direct mobile billing charges purchases to the user's phone bill. Mobile web payments use a browser or app and conventional card details. Contactless payments use near-field communication (NFC): the phone is waved or tapped near a point-of-sale terminal, as in Apple Pay (launched 2014), Google Pay, and Samsung Pay; these systems typically use tokenisation, replacing card numbers with one-time device codes. SMS and USSD-based systems, such as Kenya's M-Pesa (launched 2007 by Safaricom), allow money transfer and banking on basic feature phones and have dramatically expanded financial inclusion in East Africa and elsewhere. QR-code-based systems, such as Alipay and WeChat Pay in China, became dominant in many Asian markets.

Adoption

Mobile payment use grew rapidly in the 2010s; in China, mobile payments came to account for the majority of retail transactions, while in North America and Europe contactless card and phone payments surged, helped by their speed and convenience. India’s Unified Payments Interface (UPI, 2016) processes billions of transactions monthly.

Security

Major platforms combine tokenisation, device biometrics (fingerprint or face recognition), and transaction monitoring. Risks include device theft, phishing, and malware, mitigated by authentication requirements at the point of sale.
