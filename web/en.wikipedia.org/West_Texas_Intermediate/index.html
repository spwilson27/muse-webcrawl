West Texas Intermediate (WTI)
Grade of crude oil; benchmark
API gravity: about 39.6°
Sulfur content: about 0.24% (sweet)
Delivery point: Cushing, Oklahoma
Traded on: NYMEX (symbol CL)

West Texas Intermediate (WTI), also known as Texas light sweet, is a grade of crude oil used as a benchmark in oil pricing. It is a light crude oil — with an API gravity of around 39.6 — and sweet, containing about 0.24% sulfur, which makes it high quality and relatively easy to refine into gasoline and other products.

Benchmark pricing

WTI futures contracts are traded on the New York Mercantile Exchange (NYMEX), and the price of WTI is one of the three principal benchmarks reported in the media, alongside Brent Crude from the North Sea and Dubai Crude. The physical delivery point for WTI futures is Cushing, Oklahoma, a major pipeline and storage hub known as the "Pipeline Crossroads of the World".

Price history

WTI historically traded at a small premium to Brent, reflecting its lighter, sweeter quality. In the 2010s a glut at Cushing caused WTI to trade at a significant discount to Brent for several years. On 20 April 2020, in the midst of the COVID-19 demand collapse and with storage nearly full, the front-month WTI futures contract settled at a negative price (−$37.63 per barrel) for the first time in history, as holders paid to avoid taking physical delivery.
