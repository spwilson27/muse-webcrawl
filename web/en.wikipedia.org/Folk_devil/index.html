Folk devil

Coined byStanley Cohen (1972)
FieldSociology, criminology, media studies
Related conceptMoral panic

A folk devil is a person or group portrayed in the media as outsiders and deviants who are blamed for crimes or other social problems. The term was coined by the sociologist Stanley Cohen in his 1972 book Folk Devils and Moral Panics, which studied the media reaction to the Mods and Rockers youth subcultures in 1960s Britain.

Characteristics

Folk devils are typically stereotyped and presented as a threat to societal values and interests. Media coverage exaggerates and distorts their behaviour, symbolises them through distinctive clothing or style, and predicts further disorder. Cohen argued that the societal reaction itself can amplify the deviance it condemns: increased policing and surveillance push the targeted group into further marginality, a process known as deviancy amplification.

Examples

Groups cast as folk devils in different periods have included punks, ravers associated with acid house parties, video-game players after school shootings, asylum seekers, hooded youths ("hoodies"), and various immigrant communities. More recent analyses have applied the concept to online phenomena, such as moral panics over social-media challenges.

Criticism

Later scholars have debated how far folk devils are passive victims of labelling; some groups embrace or perform their stigmatised image. Others note that in a fragmented social-media environment, panics may be shorter-lived and folk devils may answer back more easily than in the broadcast era Cohen described.
