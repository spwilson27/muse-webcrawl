Swan Lake

Swan Lake (Russian: Лебединое озеро, Lebedinoye ozero), Op. 20, is a ballet composed by Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky in 1875–76. It tells the story of Odette, a princess turned into a swan by an evil sorcerer's curse, and is one of the most popular ballets of all time.

History

The original 1877 Bolshoi production, choreographed by Julius Reisinger, was poorly received. The ballet was transformed after Tchaikovsky's death in the 1895 Mariinsky Theatre revival in St Petersburg, with choreography by Marius Petipa and Lev Ivanov and revisions to the score by Riccardo Drigo. This version became the basis of nearly all later productions.

Synopsis

Prince Siegfried, hunting on his birthday, discovers a lake where swans transform into maidens at night. Their queen, Odette, explains that the sorcerer Rothbart's spell can be broken only by a vow of eternal love. At the palace ball, Rothbart's daughter Odile — disguised as Odette, the famous "Black Swan" — tricks Siegfried into betraying his vow. Endings vary: in many productions the lovers die and are united in an apotheosis; others end in triumph over Rothbart.

Music and choreography

The score is celebrated for its sweeping melodies, including the haunting oboe "Swan Theme". The dual role of Odette/Odile, with its 32 fouettés in the third act, is a supreme technical test for prima ballerinas. The dances of the cygnets and the corps of swans remain icons of classical ballet.

Legacy

Swan Lake is a staple of every major company and has inspired adaptations from Matthew Bourne's all-male production to the 2010 film Black Swan.
