Compass Players

The Compass Players, a Chicago improvisational theatre troupe

The Compass Players was an American improvisational theatre group founded in Chicago in 1955 by David Shepherd and Paul Sills. The troupe is widely regarded as the birthplace of modern improvisational comedy, and many of its members later founded or joined The Second City, the influential comedy revue theatre established in 1959.

History

David Shepherd, a student at the University of Chicago, developed the idea of "theatre games" with Viola Spolin, Paul Sills's mother, who had been experimenting with improvisational exercises for actors. Shepherd and Sills formed the Compass Players in 1955, performing initially in a bar near the University of Chicago campus. The troupe's format combined Spolin's theatre games with scenario-based improvisation drawn from commedia dell'arte and from audience suggestions.

The name came from the Compass Tavern, the Hyde Park bar where the group first performed. The company quickly attracted talented young performers, including Mike Nichols, Elaine May, Severn Darden, Barbara Harris, Shelley Berman, and Del Close, several of whom became major figures in American comedy and theatre.

Style and influence

Unlike stand-up comedy of the era, Compass performances were ensemble-based and often sharply satirical, addressing politics, psychoanalysis, academia, and contemporary social manners. Nichols and May developed their celebrated two-person improvisational routines within the group before leaving for New York, where their Broadway show An Evening with Mike Nichols and Elaine May (1960) brought the Compass style to national attention.

When the Compass Players dissolved in the late 1950s, Paul Sills, Bernard Sahlins, and Howard Alk founded The Second City in December 1959, carrying the improvisational format forward. The Second City in turn became a training ground for generations of performers on Saturday Night Live and in film and television comedy.
