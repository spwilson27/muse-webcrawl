Metric tensor

In differential geometry, a metric tensor (or simply a metric) is a structure on a manifold that allows distances and angles to be measured. Formally it is a symmetric, nondegenerate (0,2) tensor field g, varying smoothly from point to point, which defines an inner product on the tangent space at each point. The line element ds² = gij dxi dxj gives the infinitesimal squared distance, from which lengths of curves, areas, volumes and angles are computed by integration.

Examples

On Euclidean space in Cartesian coordinates the metric is the identity matrix and ds² = dx² + dy² + dz²; in polar or spherical coordinates the components differ, encoding the geometry of the coordinate system. On a sphere of radius R, ds² = R²(dθ² + sin²θ dφ²). Metrics induced on surfaces embedded in space arise from the first fundamental form.

General relativity

In general relativity, spacetime carries a Lorentzian metric of signature (−,+,+,+) (or the opposite convention), which determines the light-cone structure, proper time along worldlines, and geodesics followed by free-falling bodies. The metric is the dynamical field of the theory, related to matter and energy by the Einstein field equations; famous solutions include the Minkowski, Schwarzschild and FLRW metrics. The inverse metric gij raises indices and defines gradients.
