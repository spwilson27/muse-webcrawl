Polycrates (Greek: Πολυκράτης; died 522 BC) was the tyrant of the Aegean island of Samos from about 538 BC to 522 BC. He established himself as one of the most powerful rulers of the Greek world, built a large navy, and made Samos a centre of trade, engineering and the arts.

Rule

Polycrates seized power together with his brothers Pantagnotus and Syloson, then exiled Syloson and killed Pantagnotus to rule alone. He built a fleet of about one hundred penteconters and, according to Herodotus, plundered friend and foe alike, dominating much of the Aegean. He allied himself with Amasis II of Egypt and recruited the physician Democedes of Croton to his court.

Public works and culture

His reign is associated with three celebrated engineering works described by Herodotus: the great temple of Hera (the Heraion), the harbour mole, and the Tunnel of Eupalinos, an aqueduct driven through a mountain from both ends. The poets Anacreon and Ibycus were attached to his court.

The ring of Polycrates

Herodotus tells the famous story that Amasis, alarmed by Polycrates' unbroken good fortune, advised him to throw away his most prized possession to avert divine envy. Polycrates cast a precious signet ring into the sea — only for it to be returned days later in the belly of a fish served at his table. Amasis, concluding that such luck must end in catastrophe, broke off the alliance.

Death

In 522 BC Polycrates was lured to Magnesia on the Maeander by the Persian satrap Oroetes, who promised him treasure. He was murdered there and his body crucified — an end Herodotus presents as the fulfilment of ominous dreams reported by his daughter.
