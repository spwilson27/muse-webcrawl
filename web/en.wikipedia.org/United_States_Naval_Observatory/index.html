United States Naval Observatory

The United States Naval Observatory (USNO) is a scientific and military facility that produces geopositioning, navigation, and timekeeping data for the United States Navy and the United States Department of Defense. Established in 1830 as the Depot of Charts and Instruments, it is one of the oldest scientific agencies in the United States. Its headquarters in northwest Washington, D.C., at Observatory Circle, includes the official residence of the Vice President of the United States.

Timekeeping

The Observatory operates the USNO Master Clock, an ensemble of atomic clocks that provides the precise time standard for the Department of Defense and a major contribution to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC). Its time signals underpin GPS synchronization, communications networks, and financial and scientific applications; USNO and the National Institute of Standards and Technology jointly maintain the U.S. time scale.

Astronomy and almanacs

Since the nineteenth century the USNO has published The Nautical Almanac (jointly with the United Kingdom) and The Astronomical Almanac, supplying the ephemerides used in celestial navigation. Its astronomers discovered the moons of Mars, Phobos and Deimos, in 1877 (Asaph Hall) and have maintained fundamental star catalogs and the celestial reference frame. Optical observing largely moved to the darker skies of the Naval Observatory Flagstaff Station in Arizona, which hosts the Navy Precision Optical Interferometer.

Grounds

The observatory moved to its present 74-acre site in 1893. Since 1974, the Queen Anne-style house at Number One Observatory Circle on the grounds has served as the official residence of the Vice President.
