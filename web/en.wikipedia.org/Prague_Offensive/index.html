Prague offensive
Date6–11 May 1945
LocationPrague, Czechoslovakia
ResultSoviet–Allied victory; end of major combat in Europe

The Prague offensive (Russian: Пражская стратегическая наступательная операция) was the last major military operation of World War II in Europe. The offensive was fought on the Eastern Front from 6 May to 11 May 1945. Fought concurrently with the Prague uprising, the offensive significantly helped the liberation of Czechoslovakia and its capital. The offensive was one of the last engagements of World War II in Europe and continued after Nazi Germany's unconditional capitulation on 8/9 May.

Background

After the fall of Berlin, Army Group Centre under Ferdinand Schörner, with roughly 600,000–900,000 men, remained in Bohemia and Moravia and sought to fight its way west to surrender to the Americans rather than the Red Army. On 5 May, Czech resistance fighters launched the Prague uprising, seizing much of the city.

The offensive

Three Soviet fronts — the 1st, 2nd, and 4th Ukrainian Fronts under Ivan Konev, Andrei Yeremenko, and others — advanced on Prague from several directions with over two million men. Soviet troops entered Prague on 9 May 1945, largely unopposed after German forces began withdrawing westward. The last German formations surrendered by 11 May, though some SS units continued fighting briefly.

Aftermath

The offensive completed the liberation of Czechoslovakia and brought an end to large-scale fighting in Europe, three days after the general German surrender.
