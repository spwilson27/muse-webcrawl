Priestley Medal

The Priestley Medal is the highest honour conferred by the American Chemical Society (ACS), awarded annually to recognize distinguished service to chemistry. It is named for Joseph Priestley, the 18th-century chemist credited with the discovery of oxygen, and was first awarded in 1923 to Ira Remsen.

History and recipients

The medal, made of gold, is presented at the ACS national meeting, and recipients traditionally deliver an address published in Chemical & Engineering News. Laureates include many of the most influential chemists of the 20th and 21st centuries, among them Linus Pauling, Dorothy Hodgkin, Glenn T. Seaborg, Robert Burns Woodward, Mary Lowe Good, and Mostafa El-Sayed. The award was initially given every three years and became annual in 1944.

Distinguished from awards for a single discovery, the Priestley Medal recognizes lifetime achievement and service to the chemical enterprise, including research, education, and public engagement.
