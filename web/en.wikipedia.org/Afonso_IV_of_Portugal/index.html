Afonso IV (8 February 1291 – 28 May 1357), nicknamed the Brave (o Bravo), was King of Portugal from 1325 until his death. The son of King Denis and Elizabeth of Aragon, he fought Castile alongside his allies at the Battle of Río Salado (1340), a major Christian victory over the Marinids.

Reign

Afonso centralized royal justice and administration, curtailed the privileges of the nobility, and promoted maritime exploration; expeditions to the Canary Islands under his patronage were later disputed with Castile. His reign saw the devastation of the Black Death in 1348–49.

Inês de Castro

Afonso is remembered for ordering the murder of Inês de Castro, the beloved of his heir Peter, in 1355, fearing Castilian influence through her family. After Afonso's death, Peter's vengeance against her killers became one of Portugal's most famous legends.
