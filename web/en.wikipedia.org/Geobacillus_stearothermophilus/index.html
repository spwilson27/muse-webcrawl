Geobacillus stearothermophilus

Geobacillus stearothermophilus (formerly Bacillus stearothermophilus) is a Gram-positive, rod-shaped, spore-forming bacterium in the phylum Bacillota. It is a thermophile, growing optimally at about 55–65 °C, and is widely distributed in soil, hot springs, ocean sediment and decaying plant material.

Biological indicator

The heat-resistant endospores of G. stearothermophilus make it the standard biological indicator for validating steam sterilisation (autoclaving). Spore strips or ampoules with a known population (typically 106 spores) are run through a sterilisation cycle and then incubated at 55–60 °C: growth indicates the cycle failed to sterilise. Because its spores are more resistant to moist heat than most pathogens, their destruction assures an adequate sterility margin.

Food spoilage and biotechnology

The organism is a classic cause of "flat sour" spoilage in canned foods: it ferments carbohydrates producing acid without gas, souring low-acid canned products without swelling the can. Conversely, its thermostable enzymes are exploited in biotechnology — notably Bst DNA polymerase, used in loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and other isothermal DNA amplification methods, and thermostable α-amylases used in starch processing.
