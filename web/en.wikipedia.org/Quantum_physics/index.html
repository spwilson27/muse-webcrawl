Quantum physics

Quantum physics, also called quantum mechanics or quantum theory, is the branch of physics that describes nature at the scale of atoms and subatomic particles. It departs from classical physics in that energy, momentum and other quantities are often restricted to discrete values (quantization), objects exhibit both wave and particle characteristics, and outcomes of measurements are fundamentally probabilistic.

Overview

The theory was developed in the first decades of the 20th century, beginning with Planck's solution to the black-body radiation problem (1900) and Einstein's explanation of the photoelectric effect (1905), and reaching mathematical maturity in the work of Heisenberg, Schrödinger, Born and Dirac. Its formalism uses wave functions and operators, with the Schrödinger equation governing time evolution.

Branches and applications

Quantum mechanics underlies quantum field theory, quantum chemistry, semiconductor electronics, lasers, MRI, and the emerging technologies of quantum computing and quantum cryptography.
