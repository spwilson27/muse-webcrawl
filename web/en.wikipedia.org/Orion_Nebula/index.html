The Orion Nebula (also known as Messier 42, M42, or NGC 1976) is a diffuse nebula situated in the Milky Way, south of Orion's Belt in the constellation Orion, in the middle of the "sword" hanging from the belt. It is one of the brightest nebulae in the sky and is visible to the naked eye as a fuzzy patch on a clear night. At a distance of about 1,344 light-years, it is the closest region of massive star formation to Earth.

Structure and star formation

The nebula is a stellar nursery about 24 light-years across, containing some 2,800 young stars. At its heart lies the Trapezium Cluster, a tight group of massive hot stars whose ultraviolet radiation ionizes the surrounding gas, creating the glowing H II region. Hubble Space Telescope observations have revealed hundreds of protoplanetary disks ("proplyds") within the nebula, showing solar systems in the process of formation.

Observation history

Although the stars of the sword were known since antiquity, the nebulosity was first noted telescopically by Nicolas-Claude Fabri de Peiresc in 1610 and independently by Johann Baptist Cysat in 1617. Christiaan Huygens published the first detailed drawing in 1659. Charles Messier catalogued it in 1769. In 1880 Henry Draper took the first photograph of the nebula, a landmark in astrophotography.
