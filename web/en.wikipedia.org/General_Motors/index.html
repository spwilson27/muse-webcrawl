General Motors

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

General Motors Company (GM) is an American multinational automotive manufacturing company headquartered in Detroit, Michigan, United States. It is the largest automaker in the United States and was the largest in the world for 77 years before losing the top spot to Toyota in 2008. GM's core brands include Chevrolet, Buick, GMC, and Cadillac.

History

General Motors was founded in 1908 by William C. Durant as a holding company for Buick, and quickly acquired other manufacturers including Oldsmobile, Cadillac, and Oakland (later Pontiac). Under president Alfred P. Sloan in the 1920s, GM pioneered the multi-brand strategy of "a car for every purse and purpose" and the practice of annual model changes, overtaking Ford as the largest American automaker.

GM grew into one of the largest corporations in the world, but rising costs, import competition, and the 2008 financial crisis drove it into Chapter 11 bankruptcy on 1 June 2009. The company emerged 40 days later, on 10 July 2009, following a government-backed restructuring in which the U.S. Treasury took a majority stake, and brands including Pontiac, Saturn, Hummer, and Saab were discontinued or sold.

Electrification

GM launched the Chevrolet Volt plug-in hybrid in 2010 and the all-electric Chevrolet Bolt EV in 2016. The company has committed heavily to electric vehicles based on its Ultium battery platform, announcing plans to phase out internal-combustion light-duty vehicles by 2035, and owns the autonomous-vehicle subsidiary Cruise.

Operations

Beyond North America, GM operates major joint ventures in China (including SAIC-GM) and sells vehicles in markets around the world, though it has withdrawn from Europe (selling Opel and Vauxhall to PSA Group in 2017) and several other regions to focus on its most profitable markets.
