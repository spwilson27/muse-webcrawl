Non-Catholic Cemetery, Rome

LocationTestaccio, Rome, Italy
Established18th century (c. 1716 onwards)
Notable burialsJohn Keats, Percy Bysshe Shelley

The Non-Catholic Cemetery (Italian: Cimitero Acattolico), also called the Protestant Cemetery, is a historic burial ground in the Testaccio district of Rome, beside the ancient Pyramid of Cestius. It serves as the resting place for non-Catholics—especially Protestants and Orthodox Christians—who died in Rome, as well as for many writers, artists, and scholars.

History

Burials of non-Catholics, who could not be interred in consecrated Catholic ground, began in the early 18th century near the pyramid for members of the exiled Stuart court and foreign travellers. The cemetery expanded in the 19th century and today is administered with the involvement of foreign embassies. Its cypress trees and quiet grounds have long made it a place of literary pilgrimage.

Notable burials

The Romantic poets John Keats (died 1821) and Percy Bysshe Shelley (died 1822) are buried here, as are the writer's son Joseph Severn, Edward John Trelawny, August von Goethe, and the Italian Marxist thinker Antonio Gramsci.
