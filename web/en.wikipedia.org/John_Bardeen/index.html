John Bardeen

John Bardeen (May 23, 1908 – January 30, 1991) was an American physicist and electrical engineer, the only person to win the Nobel Prize in Physics twice.

At Bell Labs he co-invented the transistor in 1947 with William Shockley and Walter Brattain, sharing the 1956 Nobel Prize. At the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign he developed, with Leon Cooper and John Robert Schrieffer, the BCS theory of superconductivity, earning a second Nobel Prize in 1972. His work underpins modern electronics and medical imaging.
