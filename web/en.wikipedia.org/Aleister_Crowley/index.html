Aleister Crowley

Aleister Crowley (born Edward Alexander Crowley; 12 October 1875 – 1 December 1947) was an English occultist, ceremonial magician, poet, novelist, mountaineer, and painter. He founded the religion of Thelema, identifying himself as the prophet entrusted with guiding humanity into the Æon of Horus in the early 20th century.

A prolific writer, he published widely over the course of his life. Born to a wealthy family in Royal Leamington Spa, Warwickshire, Crowley rejected his parents' fundamentalist Christian Plymouth Brethren faith to pursue an interest in Western esotericism. He was educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he focused his attention on mountaineering and poetry.

Thelema

In 1904, while in Egypt, Crowley claimed to have been contacted by a supernatural entity named Aiwass, who dictated The Book of the Law (Liber AL vel Legis), the central sacred text of Thelema. Its core doctrine is summed up in the phrase "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law."

Occult career

Crowley joined the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1898, where he was trained in ceremonial magic by Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers and Allan Bennett. He later became involved with the Ordo Templi Orientis (O.T.O.), eventually leading its British branch, and founded the A∴A∴, an occult order through which he propagated Thelema. In 1920 he established the Abbey of Thelema in Cefalù, Sicily, a commune from which he was expelled by Mussolini's government in 1923.

Mountaineering

Crowley was an accomplished mountaineer, taking part in expeditions to K2 in 1902 and Kangchenjunga in 1905, and making notable ascents in the Alps and elsewhere.

Legacy

Crowley remains a highly controversial figure, denounced in the popular press of his day as "the wickedest man in the world." He has been an enduring influence on modern occultism, esotericism, and popular culture, appearing on the cover of the Beatles' album Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band.
