The Chronicles of Narnia is a series of seven children's fantasy novels by the British author C. S. Lewis. Written between 1949 and 1954 and published from 1950 to 1956, the series is set in the fictional realm of Narnia, a world of talking animals and mythical creatures ruled by the great lion Aslan.

The books

In publication order, the series comprises The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe (1950), Prince Caspian (1951), The Voyage of the Dawn Treader (1952), The Silver Chair (1953), The Horse and His Boy (1954), The Magician's Nephew (1955), and The Last Battle (1956), which won the Carnegie Medal. Most of the books follow English children — the Pevensies and their friends — who are drawn into Narnia to aid Aslan against evil forces.

Themes and legacy

The stories draw on Greek and Roman mythology, British and Irish fairy tales, and Lewis's Christian theology. The Chronicles have sold over 100 million copies and have been adapted for radio, television, stage, and film, notably the Walden Media film series beginning in 2005.
