Initiation

Initiation
TypeRite of passage
PurposesAdmission to a group, transition of status, transmission of knowledge
ExamplesReligious initiation, fraternity initiation, coming-of-age rites

Initiation is a rite of passage marking entrance or acceptance into a group or society. It can also be a formal admission to adulthood in a community or one of its formal components. In an extended sense, initiation can also denote the transmission of knowledge, status, or spiritual power from one person or generation to another.

Characteristics

Anthropologists such as Arnold van Gennep described rites of passage as having three phases: separation, liminality (transition), and incorporation. The initiate is separated from their former status, undergoes a period of testing, instruction, or ordeal, and is then reincorporated into the community with a new identity or rank. Mircea Eliade wrote extensively on initiation in traditional religions, treating it as a symbolic death and rebirth.

Religious initiation

Many religions have initiation rites: baptism and confirmation in Christianity, bar and bat mitzvah in Judaism, upanayana in Hinduism, and various forms of initiation in mystery religions of the ancient world, such as the Eleusinian Mysteries. In some traditions, initiation confers membership; in others it grants access to esoteric teachings, as in certain schools of Buddhism, Sufism, and Western esotericism.

Secular and social initiation

Fraternities, sororities, military units, gangs, and clubs often require initiations, which may range from harmless ceremonies to dangerous hazing. Many jurisdictions and institutions prohibit hazing because of injuries and deaths associated with it. Workplace and professional initiations are usually milder, such as formal induction ceremonies.
