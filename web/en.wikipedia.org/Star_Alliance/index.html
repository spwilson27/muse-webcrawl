Star Alliance is the world's first and one of the largest global airline alliances, founded on 14 May 1997 by Air Canada, Lufthansa, Scandinavian Airlines, Thai Airways International and United Airlines. Its member airlines coordinate schedules, share airport facilities, and offer passengers reciprocal frequent-flyer benefits across a network spanning over 180 countries.

History

The alliance's creation marked the beginning of the modern era of airline alliances, prompting rivals Oneworld (1999) and SkyTeam (2000). Membership expanded to include All Nippon Airways, Singapore Airlines, Air China, Turkish Airlines and many others. Members' aircraft often carry the alliance's five-pointed star logo alongside their own livery, and a number fly aircraft painted entirely in Star Alliance colours.

Benefits and structure

Passengers can earn and redeem frequent-flyer miles across all members, with elite tiers (Star Alliance Silver and Gold) granting lounge access, priority handling and extra baggage. The alliance pioneered common IT platforms, co-located airport terminals — such as London Heathrow Terminal 2, "the Queen's Terminal" — and joint connection centres. It is managed by a central company based in Singapore, with a chief executive board drawn from member airlines.

Membership changes

Membership has evolved as airlines merge, fail or shift alliances; notable departures include US Airways (to Oneworld after merging with American) and founding member SAS, which left for SkyTeam in 2024 following Air France–KLM's investment, while other carriers joined via the Connecting Partner programme.
