Kenyan cuisine

Ugali served with sukuma wiki and grilled meat, a staple Kenyan meal.

Kenyan cuisine reflects the country's many ethnic groups and its history of trade along the Indian Ocean coast. There is no single national cuisine; instead, regional diets vary between the pastoralist communities of the north, the agricultural peoples of the central highlands, the fishing communities around Lake Victoria, and the Swahili coast, whose cooking shows Arab, Persian, Indian, and Portuguese influences.

Staples

The most widespread staple is ugali, a stiff porridge of maize flour cooked in water, typically eaten by hand with vegetables or stew. It is commonly accompanied by sukuma wiki (collard greens sautéed with onions and tomatoes). Other common dishes include githeri (boiled maize and beans), irio (mashed peas, potatoes, and maize), rice dishes such as pilau and biryani on the coast, and matoke (cooked bananas) in western regions.

Meat and nyama choma

Grilled meat, known as nyama choma (Swahili for "roasted meat"), is a popular social food, usually goat or beef, served in informal restaurants and roadside eateries with kachumbari, a salad of tomato and onion. Fish, especially tilapia from Lake Victoria and seafood on the coast, is also widely eaten.

Drinks

Kenya is one of the world's largest producers of black tea, and chai — tea boiled with milk, sugar, and spices — is drunk throughout the day. Coffee from the highlands is prized internationally, and traditional beverages include mursik, fermented milk of the Kalenjin people.
