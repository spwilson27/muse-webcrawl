Sir Georg Solti (born György Stern; 21 October 1912 – 5 September 1997) was a Hungarian-British orchestral and operatic conductor, best known for his appearances with opera companies in Munich, Frankfurt, and London, and as music director of the Chicago Symphony Orchestra (1969–1991). One of the most recorded conductors of the twentieth century, he won 31 Grammy Awards, more than any other artist at the time of his death.

Early life and career

Solti studied piano and composition at the Franz Liszt Academy in Budapest under teachers including Béla Bartók, Leó Weiner, and Ernő Dohnányi. He made his conducting debut in 1938 with The Marriage of Figaro at the Hungarian State Opera. As a Jew, he spent the war years in Switzerland, where he won the 1942 Geneva International Piano Competition. After the war he became music director of the Bavarian State Opera in Munich (1946–1952) and then of the Frankfurt Opera.

London and Chicago

As music director of the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden (1961–1971), Solti raised the company to international standing. His first complete studio recording of Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen with the Vienna Philharmonic for Decca (1958–1965) is widely regarded as a landmark of the gramophone era. In Chicago he formed a celebrated partnership with the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, touring internationally and making numerous recordings; he was later its music director laureate. He was knighted in 1972 and became a British citizen the same year.

Legacy

Known for his energetic, precise podium manner and fiery interpretations, Solti left an enormous discography of operas and orchestral works. The Solti Conducting Academy and competitions named after him support young conductors.
