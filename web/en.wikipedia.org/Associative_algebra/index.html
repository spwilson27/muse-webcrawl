Associative algebra


An associative algebra is a vector space equipped with a bilinear, associative product.

In mathematics, an associative algebra A over a commutative ring R is a ring that is also an R-module, in such a way that the ring operations are compatible with scalar multiplication. When R is a field, an associative algebra is a vector space together with an associative bilinear multiplication.

Definition

An associative algebra is a set with three operations — addition, multiplication and scalar multiplication — such that it is simultaneously a ring and a module over R, and the scalar multiplication satisfies r(xy) = (rx)y = x(ry). Many authors also require the algebra to possess a multiplicative identity compatible with the scalars, in which case it is equivalent to a ring homomorphism from R into the centre of A.

Examples

  • The ring of n × n matrices over a field
  • Polynomial rings in one or several variables
  • Group algebras of finite groups
  • Endomorphism rings of vector spaces and modules
  • Quaternions and other division algebras

The representation theory of finite-dimensional associative algebras, and in particular of quivers, is a major area of modern algebra.
