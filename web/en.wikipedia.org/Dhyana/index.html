Dhyana

Dhyana (Sanskrit: ध्यान, dhyāna) is a Sanskrit term meaning meditation, contemplation, or profound abstract meditation. It is a central practice in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism, denoting a state of concentrated, unbroken attention in which the mind becomes absorbed in its object.

In Hindu traditions

In Patanjali's Yoga Sutras, dhyana is the seventh of the eight limbs of yoga, following dharana (concentration) and preceding samadhi (absorption). Whereas dharana fixes the mind on a single point, dhyana is the continuous, effortless flow of awareness toward that point. Dhyana is also discussed extensively in the Bhagavad Gita and the Upanishads.

In Buddhism

In Pali the term is jhana; the Buddha's discourses describe a series of progressively deeper meditative absorptions. Through Chinese transmission of the word (chan) and Japanese (zen), the term gave its name to the Chan/Zen school of Buddhism.

Meaning

The term derives from the Sanskrit root dhyai, "to contemplate, reflect upon". Across traditions, dhyana is regarded as a means of calming the mind, gaining insight into the nature of reality, and progressing toward liberation.
