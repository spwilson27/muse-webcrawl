The Last Supper is the final meal that, in the Gospel accounts, Jesus shared with his apostles in Jerusalem before his crucifixion. It is commemorated by Christians on Maundy Thursday (Holy Thursday), the fifth day of Holy Week. During the meal Jesus broke bread and shared wine with the words identifying them as his body and blood, thereby instituting the Eucharist (Holy Communion), which Christian churches continue to celebrate in obedience to his command, "Do this in remembrance of me."

Gospel accounts

The Synoptic Gospels (Matthew, Mark, and Luke) present the meal as a Passover meal at which Jesus predicted that one of the Twelve would betray him and instituted the Eucharist. John's Gospel instead places the meal before Passover, omits the bread-and-cup institution narrative, and emphasises the washing of the disciples' feet, the farewell discourses (chapters 14–17), and the new commandment to "love one another". Paul's First Letter to the Corinthians (c. AD 55) contains the earliest written account of the institution. Whether the Last Supper was a Passover Seder, and on which day of the week and year it occurred, are long-standing subjects of scholarly debate (the "Synoptic problem" of chronology).

In art

The Last Supper is one of the most frequently depicted subjects in Christian art. Leonardo da Vinci's mural (1495–1498) in the refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan — showing the moment after Jesus announces the coming betrayal — is the most famous version, with notable others by Tintoretto, Veronese, and Salvador Dalí.
