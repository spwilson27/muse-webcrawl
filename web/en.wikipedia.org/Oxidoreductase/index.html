Oxidoreductase

In biochemistry, an oxidoreductase is an enzyme that catalyses the transfer of electrons from one molecule, the reductant (electron donor), to another, the oxidant (electron acceptor). Such reactions are known as oxidation–reduction, or redox, reactions.

Nomenclature

Oxidoreductases constitute EC 1 in the Enzyme Commission classification of enzymes. Proper names are formed as "donor:acceptor oxidoreductase", while common names often take the form "donor dehydrogenase" or "acceptor reductase". Cofactors commonly involved include NAD+/NADH, NADP+/NADPH, and FAD/FADH2.

Examples

Important oxidoreductases include alcohol dehydrogenase, lactate dehydrogenase, glucose oxidase, cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV of the respiratory chain), and the enzymes of photosynthesis and cellular respiration that move electrons through electron transport chains to generate ATP.

Biological role

Redox enzymes are central to energy metabolism: catabolic pathways oxidise nutrients and transfer the electrons to carriers, whose reoxidation drives oxidative phosphorylation.
