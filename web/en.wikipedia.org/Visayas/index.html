The Visayas (Cebuano: Kabisay-an), or the Visayan Islands, are one of the three principal geographical divisions of the Philippines, along with Luzon and Mindanao. Located in the central part of the archipelago, they consist of several islands, primarily surrounding the Visayan Sea, although the Visayas are also considered the northeast extremity of the entire Sulu Sea.

The major islands of the Visayas are Panay, Negros, Cebu, Bohol, Leyte and Samar. The region may also include the provinces of Palawan and Romblon, depending on definitions, though Romblon was transferred to the Mimaropa region in 2017.

History

The Visayas were among the first parts of the Philippines contacted by Europeans: Ferdinand Magellan's expedition reached Cebu in 1521, and Miguel López de Legazpi established the first Spanish settlement at Cebu in 1565. The precolonial inhabitants, known collectively as Visayans, were seafaring peoples organised into barangays, practised tattooing — leading Spaniards to call them pintados, "the painted ones" — and maintained trade links across Southeast Asia.

Language

The Visayan languages form a group of closely related Philippine languages. The most widely spoken are Cebuano, Hiligaynon (Ilonggo) and Waray, alongside smaller languages such as Kinaray-a, Aklanon and Capiznon.

Festivals

The Visayas are famous for their January festivals honouring the Santo Niño (Child Jesus), including the Sinulog in Cebu City, the Ati-Atihan in Kalibo, Aklan — often called the "Mother of All Philippine Festivals" — and the Dinagyang in Iloilo City.
