Elisha Otis

Elisha Graves Otis (August 3, 1811 – April 8, 1861) was an American industrialist, founder of the Otis Elevator Company, and inventor of a safety device that prevents elevators from falling if the hoisting cable fails.

Otis's safety elevator made practical passenger elevators — and hence skyscrapers — possible.

The safety elevator

In 1852, while working in Yonkers, New York, Otis devised a hoist fitted with a safety brake: spring-loaded pawls that engaged a ratchet if the rope went slack. He demonstrated it dramatically at the Exhibition of the Industry of All Nations in New York in 1854, riding a platform high above the crowd and ordering the rope cut; the platform dropped only a few inches before the brake caught, and Otis declared, "All safe, gentlemen, all safe!"

Company

Otis founded his company in 1853. In 1857 the first passenger elevator with his safety device was installed at 488 Broadway in New York, in the Haughwout Department Store. After his death in 1861, his sons Charles and Norton continued the firm, which became the world's largest elevator manufacturer; it later became part of United Technologies and is now Otis Worldwide.

Legacy

By making tall buildings safe and practical for passengers, Otis's invention was a key enabling technology of the skyscraper and the modern city.
