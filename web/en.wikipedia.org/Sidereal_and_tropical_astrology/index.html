Sidereal and tropical astrology

Sidereal and tropical are terms used to describe two different definitions of the year, and they are also used as terms for two distinct zodiacal coordinate systems used in astrology. The difference arises from the precession of the equinoxes.

The two zodiacs

The tropical zodiac, used by most Western astrologers, is fixed to the seasons: 0° Aries begins at the March equinox. The sidereal zodiac, used in Hindu astrology and by some Western schools, is fixed relative to the stars. Because the equinox precesses westward by about one degree every 72 years, the two systems have drifted roughly 24 degrees apart since they coincided around the third century CE.

Ayanamsa

The angular difference between the systems is called the ayanamsa in Vedic astrology. Several values are in use, the Lahiri ayanamsa being the most common in India.

Practice

A natal chart cast in the sidereal system will often place planets in the sign preceding the one they occupy in a tropical chart, leading to differing interpretations between the traditions.
