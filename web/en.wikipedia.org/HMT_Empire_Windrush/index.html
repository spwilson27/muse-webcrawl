HMT Empire Windrush

Launched1930 (as Monte Rosa)
TypePassenger liner / troopship
Tonnage14,651 GRT
FateSank 30 March 1954 after fire in the Mediterranean

HMT Empire Windrush, originally MV Monte Rosa, was a passenger liner and cruise ship launched in Germany in 1930. Seized as a war prize by Britain, she was operated as a troopship. In 1948 she brought one of the first large groups of post-war West Indian immigrants to the United Kingdom, landing 1,027 passengers — among them several hundred Caribbean migrants — at Tilbury, Essex, on 22 June 1948. The passengers became known as the Windrush generation.

The 1948 voyage

The Windrush picked up passengers in Jamaica, Trinidad and elsewhere in the Caribbean; many were ex-servicemen who had served Britain in the Second World War, and others came seeking work in a Britain short of labour. Among those aboard were the calypsonians Lord Kitchener and Lord Beginner; newsreel footage shows Lord Kitchener singing "London Is the Place for Me" on arrival. The arrival is widely regarded as a founding moment of modern multicultural Britain and of the Black British community, and 22 June is commemorated as Windrush Day.

Later history

The ship continued trooping service, including evacuations during the Korean War era. In March 1954, on a voyage from Japan, an explosion and fire in the engine room killed four crewmen; the passengers and crew were rescued, but the burning ship sank in the Mediterranean west of Algiers while under tow. Her name later became attached to the 2018 "Windrush scandal", in which members of the Windrush generation were wrongly detained, denied services and in some cases deported by the Home Office.
