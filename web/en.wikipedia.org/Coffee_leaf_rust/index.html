Coffee leaf rust

Coffee leaf rust is a devastating fungal disease of coffee plants caused by Hemileia vastatrix. It primarily attacks Coffea arabica, producing yellow-orange, powdery lesions on the undersides of leaves, severe defoliation and major yield losses. It is considered the most economically important coffee disease worldwide.

Causal agentHemileia vastatrix
HostsCoffea species
Common namesLa roya (Spanish)

History

The rust was first reported in Sri Lanka (then Ceylon) in the 1860s, where it destroyed the island's coffee industry within a few decades and drove planters to switch to tea. It spread through Asia and Africa and reached Brazil in 1970, thereafter spreading throughout the Americas. A major epidemic beginning around 2012 in Central America and Colombia caused billions of dollars in losses and displaced many farm workers.

Control

Management relies on fungicides (especially copper-based and systemic products), shade and nutrition management, and — most durably — resistant varieties such as Catimor and Sarchimor lines and Colombia's Castillo cultivar. Warm, wet conditions favour the fungus, and climate change is expanding the elevations at which it can thrive.
