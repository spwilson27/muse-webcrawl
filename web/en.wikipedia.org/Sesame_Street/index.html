Sesame Street is an American educational children's television series that combines live action, sketch comedy, animation, and puppetry. It is produced by Sesame Workshop (formerly Children's Television Workshop) and was created by Joan Ganz Cooney and Lloyd Morrisett. The show premiered on 10 November 1969 and became a landmark of educational media, using the techniques of commercial television — catchy songs, fast cuts, and humour appealing to adults as well as children — to teach preschoolers letters, numbers, and social skills.

Format and characters

The programme is set on a fictional urban street centred on the 123 Sesame Street brownstone. Its Muppet characters were created with Jim Henson, whose company built and performed many of them. Big Bird and Oscar the Grouch were original cast members; Elmo rose to prominence in the 1980s and 1990s, and the segment "Elmo's World" ran from 1998. The show is structured around a curriculum developed with educational researchers, and each episode traditionally centres on a letter and number of the day, with celebrity guests appearing frequently.

Impact and research

Sesame Street was the first children's programme built around a detailed curriculum tested through formative research. Studies have repeatedly found educational benefits, especially for disadvantaged children — its original target audience. It has won more Emmy Awards than any other children's series and has been adapted in more than 120 countries through local co-productions such as Plaza Sésamo and Takalani Sesame.

Broadcast history

The show aired on PBS for its first 45 years. In 2015 Sesame Workshop announced a deal under which new episodes would premiere on HBO (later Max) before airing on PBS; in 2025, first-run episodes moved to Netflix with same-day PBS availability. See also the Muppets and Jim Henson.
