The Island of Montreal (French: Île de Montréal) is an island in south-western Quebec, Canada, at the confluence of the Saint Lawrence and Ottawa rivers. It is the largest island of the Hochelaga Archipelago and home to the city of Montreal, which occupies most of its area, together with several suburban municipalities.

Geography

The island is roughly 50 kilometres long and 16 kilometres wide, shaped like a boomerang with the Saint Lawrence River along its southern shore and the Rivière des Prairies along the north. Lake Saint-Louis borders its western end, and Lake Saint-Pierre lies downstream to the east. Mount Royal, a small volcanic hill (a Monteregian intrusion), rises 233 metres above the city that surrounds it.

History

The island was inhabited by the St. Lawrence Iroquoians, whose village of Hochelaga stood near Mount Royal when Jacques Cartier visited in 1535. Samuel de Champlain explored the area in the early 1600s, and in 1642 Paul de Chomedey, Sieur de Maisonneuve, and Jeanne Mance founded Ville-Marie, the mission that became Montreal.

Administration

Since municipal reorganisations in 2002–2006, the island comprises the city of Montreal — covering about 85 percent of the territory — and fifteen independent suburbs, all coordinated in some services through the Urban Agglomeration of Montreal. The island contains the bulk of the Montreal metropolitan population and its downtown core, port, and two of its major universities.
