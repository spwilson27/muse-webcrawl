Peer Gynt


Peer Gynt, Ibsen's verse drama of 1867.

Peer Gynt is a five-act verse play by the Norwegian dramatist Henrik Ibsen, published in 1867 and first performed in 1876 in Christiania (now Oslo) with incidental music by Edvard Grieg. Written in Danish–Norwegian, it is a sprawling, fantastical counterpart to Ibsen's earlier verse drama Brand.

Plot

Peer Gynt, a boastful young peasant who lives in a world of fantasy, abducts a bride, flees into the mountains, encounters the Troll King (in whose hall he refuses to become a troll by living only "unto himself"), and spends decades wandering the world as a merchant and self-styled emperor. Old and shipwrecked, he returns home, where the Button-Molder threatens to melt him down for having been nothing, and he is redeemed by the patient love of Solveig, who has waited for him. Famous scenes include Åse's death, the hall of the Mountain King, and the onion monologue, in which Peer peels an onion seeking a kernel that is not there.

Music and legacy

Grieg's incidental music, later arranged into two suites, includes "Morning Mood", "In the Hall of the Mountain King", "Anitra's Dance", and "Solveig's Song", and is among the most recognisable of all theatre music.
