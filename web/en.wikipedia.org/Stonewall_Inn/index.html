Stonewall Inn

The Stonewall Inn, Greenwich Village, New York City

The Stonewall Inn is a gay bar and recreational tavern at 53 Christopher Street in the Greenwich Village neighborhood of Manhattan, New York City. It was the site of the Stonewall riots of 1969, which are widely considered the single most important event leading to the gay liberation movement and the modern fight for LGBT rights in the United States.

History

The building originally housed stables in the 19th century and later a restaurant. In 1966, members of the Mafia bought and converted it into a gay bar — a private club, in part to circumvent liquor licensing rules that effectively banned serving gay patrons. The bar was subject to frequent police raids, as were most gay establishments of the era.

The 1969 uprising

In the early hours of 28 June 1969, patrons resisted a routine police raid, and the confrontation escalated into several nights of demonstrations. The uprising galvanised activists; within a year, Gay Pride marches were held in several cities, and organisations such as the Gay Liberation Front and the Gay Activists Alliance had formed.

Legacy and designation

The Stonewall Inn and surrounding streets were designated a New York City landmark in 2015 and, in 2016, the Stonewall National Monument — the first U.S. national monument dedicated to LGBT rights — was established by President Barack Obama.
