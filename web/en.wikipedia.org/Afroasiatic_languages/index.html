Afroasiatic languages


Distribution of the Afroasiatic language family

The Afroasiatic languages (also Afro-Asiatic, Afrasian, or Hamito-Semitic in older sources) are a language family of about 300 languages spoken predominantly in the geographic subregion of Afro-Asiatic — West Asia, North Africa, the Horn of Africa, and parts of the Sahara and Sahel — by roughly 500 million people.

Branches

The family is conventionally divided into six branches:

  • Semitic — including Arabic, Hebrew, Amharic and the extinct Akkadian;
  • Egyptian — Ancient Egyptian and its descendant Coptic (now liturgical only);
  • Berber (Amazigh) — spoken across North Africa;
  • Cushitic — including Oromo and Somali;
  • Chadic — including Hausa;
  • Omotic — spoken in southwestern Ethiopia.

Characteristics

Shared features include a system of consonantal roots (especially in Semitic and Egyptian), emphatic consonants in several branches, and grammatical gender in singular nouns. The family's homeland is debated, with proposals ranging from the Levant to the Red Sea coast of Africa; Afroasiatic includes the earliest attested languages in the world, Egyptian and Akkadian, documented from about 3000 BCE.
