Cluny Abbey

Cluny Abbey is a former Benedictine monastery in Cluny, Saône-et-Loire, France. Founded in 910 by William I, Duke of Aquitaine, it became the center of the Cluniac movement, one of the most influential monastic reforms of the Middle Ages.

History

William placed the abbey directly under the authority of the Pope, freeing it from local secular and episcopal control — an unusual arrangement that helped Cluny build a vast network of dependent priories across Europe. Its abbots, including Berno, Odo, Odilo, and Hugh of Semur, were major figures in medieval church life.

The church

The third abbey church, Cluny III, begun in 1088, was the largest church in Christendom until the rebuilding of St Peter's Basilica in Rome. Most of the abbey was destroyed during the French Revolution; only the southern transept arm and bell tower (the Clocher de l'Eau Bénite) survive.

Legacy

Cluny's liturgical elaboration, care for the dead, and role in promoting pilgrimage and the Peace of God movement shaped medieval Latin Christianity.
