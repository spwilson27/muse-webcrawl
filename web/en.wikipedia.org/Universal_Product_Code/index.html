Universal Product Code

A UPC-A barcode, the most common form of the Universal Product Code.

TypeBarcode symbology / product identifier
First usedJune 26, 1974
Digits12 (UPC-A)
Administered byGS1 US

The Universal Product Code (UPC) is a barcode symbology widely used in North America and other countries for tracking trade items in stores. It consists of a scannable strip of black bars and white spaces above a sequence of 12 numeric digits. The first product scanned with a UPC at a retail checkout — a pack of Wrigley's chewing gum at a Marsh supermarket in Troy, Ohio, on June 26, 1974 — is often taken as the beginning of the modern barcode era.

Structure

A UPC-A code has twelve digits. The first digit is a number-system character, the following five digits form the manufacturer identification number, the next five are the item number assigned by the manufacturer, and the final digit is a modulo-10 check digit used to detect scanning errors. The bars encode the digits in a pattern of bars and spaces of varying widths, readable by optical scanners from either direction.

History

The grocery industry began coordinating on a standard product code in the early 1970s, selecting a design based on a proposal developed by George J. Laurer at IBM. The system spread rapidly through supermarkets during the late 1970s and 1980s, transforming inventory management and checkout speed, and is now administered in the United States by GS1 US as part of the global GS1 system.

Variants

Shorter and specialized forms include UPC-E, a zero-suppressed six-digit version for small packages, and add-on codes (UPC-2 and UPC-5) used for periodicals and books. The UPC is closely related to the 13-digit EAN used in much of the rest of the world; a UPC-A can be represented as an EAN-13 with a leading zero.
