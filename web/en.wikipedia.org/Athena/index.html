Athena (Ancient Greek: Ἀθηνᾶ) is an ancient Greek goddess associated with wisdom, warfare and handicraft, later syncretised with the Roman goddess Minerva. She was one of the Twelve Olympians and the patron deity of the city of Athens, from which she most likely received her name. The Parthenon on the Athenian Acropolis was dedicated to her.

Mythology

Athena was born fully grown and armoured from the forehead of Zeus, after Zeus swallowed her mother, the Titaness Metis, embodiment of cunning wisdom. In the contest with Poseidon for patronage of Athens, Athena won by offering the olive tree; Poseidon gave a salt spring (or the horse). She was a virgin goddess (Parthenos) and helper of heroes: she guided Perseus against Medusa, whose head she thereafter bore on her aegis; she aided Odysseus throughout the Odyssey; and she supported the Greeks in the Trojan War, in contrast to Ares, who embodied war's violence — Athena represented its strategy and discipline.

Cult and depiction

Her principal festival was the Panathenaea, and Phidias's colossal gold-and-ivory statue Athena Parthenos stood in the Parthenon. In art she wears a Corinthian helmet and the aegis with the Gorgoneion, carries a spear and shield, and is accompanied by her owl (the source of the phrase "owls to Athens") and by Nike, goddess of victory.
