Euromaidan

Euromaidan was a wave of demonstrations and civil unrest in Ukraine, which began on the night of 21 November 2013 with public protests in Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Independence Square) in Kyiv. The protests were sparked by the Ukrainian government's sudden decision not to sign the European Union–Ukraine Association Agreement, instead choosing closer ties to Russia and the Eurasian Economic Union.

Course of the protests

The demonstrations grew after a violent police crackdown on student protesters on 30 November 2013, expanding into a broad movement calling for the resignation of President Viktor Yanukovych and his government, an end to corruption, and closer integration with Europe. Protesters occupied Kyiv's central square and government buildings through the winter, building barricades and self-defence units. Clashes in January and February 2014 escalated into deadly violence; around one hundred protesters were killed, most of them in the shootings of 18–20 February 2014.

Outcome

On 21 February 2014 an agreement between Yanukovych and the opposition was signed, but Yanukovych fled Kyiv that night and was removed from office by parliament the following day. The Euromaidan, together with the subsequent Revolution of Dignity, was followed by Russia's annexation of Crimea and the outbreak of war in Donbas. Ukraine went on to sign the Association Agreement with the European Union in 2014.
