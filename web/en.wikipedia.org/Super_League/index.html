Super League

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Super League (officially the Betfred Super League for sponsorship reasons) is the top-level European rugby league club competition, administered by the Rugby Football League in England. The league currently consists of twelve teams, predominantly from northern England, with clubs also having participated from France and Canada.

History

The Super League was founded in 1996 as part of the shift of the British game to a summer season, following Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation offering substantial funding amid the Super League war in Australia. The competition replaced the Rugby Football League Championship as the top tier. Its champions are decided by a play-off series culminating in the Super League Grand Final at Old Trafford, Manchester.

Format

Teams play each other home and away, plus additional fixtures including the Magic Weekend, before the top clubs enter play-offs; the lowest-ranked club is relegated to the Championship, subject to changes in league structure introduced through grading criteria in the 2020s. The club finishing top of the regular season table receives the League Leaders' Shield.

Champions

The competition has been dominated by a small group of clubs. St Helens are the most successful team, with Leeds Rhinos and Wigan Warriors also winning multiple titles; Bradford Bulls and Hull Kingston Rovers have also been champions. Winners of the Grand Final have gone on to contest the World Club Challenge against the champions of Australia's National Rugby League.
