Praetorian prefecture of Africa

The praetorian prefecture of Africa (Latin: praefectura praetorio Africae) was a major administrative division of the Eastern Roman Empire located in North Africa. It was established after the reconquest of the region from the Vandals in 533–534 by the general Belisarius, during the reign of Emperor Justinian I.

Establishment

Following the Vandalic War, Justinian reorganised the recovered provinces — roughly modern Tunisia, northeastern Algeria and northwestern Libya, together with Sardinia, Corsica and the Balearic Islands — into a new prefecture with its capital at Carthage. The first praetorian prefect was Archelaus, and the soldier Solomon, who combined civil and military responsibilities, played a leading role in the province's early defence against Moorish (Berber) tribes.

Administration and history

The prefecture was divided into provinces including Zeugitana (Proconsularis), Byzacena, Numidia, the Mauretanias and Tripolitania. Its prefects governed from Carthage and were responsible for taxation, justice and supply of the army. The region was troubled by recurrent Berber revolts, military mutinies and, in the seventh century, the first Arab raids. Around 590 Emperor Maurice reorganised the territory into the Exarchate of Africa, placing civil and military authority in the hands of an exarch in response to the growing external threat.

End

The exarchate that succeeded the prefecture survived until the Arab conquest of Carthage in 698, which ended Byzantine rule in Africa.
