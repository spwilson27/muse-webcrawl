Dyck path

In combinatorics, a Dyck word is a string consisting of n X's and n Y's such that no initial segment of the string has more Y's than X's. Geometrically, the corresponding Dyck path is a lattice path from (0,0) to (2n, 0) made of up-steps (1,1) and down-steps (1,−1) that never dips below the x-axis. The structures are named after the German mathematician Walther von Dyck.

Counting

The number of Dyck paths of length 2n is the n-th Catalan number, Cn = (1/(n+1)) · (2n choose n). The reflection principle gives a classic proof: invalid paths that cross below the axis are in bijection with all paths from a reflected starting point.

Applications

Dyck paths encode many Catalan-counted structures: correctly matched parentheses, ways to triangulate a polygon, binary trees with n+1 leaves, and stack-sortable permutations. They also model the trajectory of a simple random walk conditioned to stay non-negative, linking combinatorics with probability theory and the study of Brownian excursions.

Generalizations

Ballot sequences, Łukasiewicz paths and Fuss–Catalan (Raney) numbers generalise Dyck paths by allowing different step sets or endpoint heights.
