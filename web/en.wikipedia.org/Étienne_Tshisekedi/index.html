Étienne Tshisekedi

Étienne Tshisekedi (1932–2017), the historic leader of the Congolese opposition.

Étienne Tshisekedi wa Mulumba (14 December 1932 – 1 February 2017) was a Congolese politician and leader of the Union for Democracy and Social Progress (UDPS), the main opposition party in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. A veteran opponent of Mobutu Sese Seko and later of the Kabila governments, he served briefly as Prime Minister of Zaire three times in the early 1990s during the country's attempted transition to democracy.

Career

Tshisekedi was one of the first Congolese to earn a law degree, graduating in 1961. He initially served in Mobutu's government but broke with him and in 1980 co-authored an open letter criticising the regime; in 1982 he co-founded the UDPS, enduring years of imprisonment, internal exile and harassment. Chosen as prime minister by the Sovereign National Conference in 1992, he was repeatedly undermined by Mobutu.

After Mobutu's fall he opposed both Laurent-Désiré Kabila and Joseph Kabila. He ran for president in 2011, officially losing to Joseph Kabila amid widespread allegations of irregularities. He died in Brussels in 2017; his body was repatriated in 2019, after his son Félix Tshisekedi became president.
