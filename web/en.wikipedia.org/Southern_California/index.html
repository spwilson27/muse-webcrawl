Southern California (commonly abbreviated SoCal) is a geographic and cultural region in the southern portion of the U.S. state of California. It is anchored by the metropolitan areas of Los Angeles, San Diego and the Inland Empire, and is home to well over half of California's population, making it one of the most populous regions in the United States.

Geography and climate

The region spans coastal plains, transverse and peninsular mountain ranges, and deserts including the Mojave and Colorado deserts. Its Mediterranean climate, with mild wet winters and long dry summers, gives way inland to arid conditions. Southern California is seismically active, crossed by the San Andreas Fault system, and its chaparral landscapes are subject to periodic wildfires driven by Santa Ana winds.

Economy and culture

The economy is one of the largest regional economies in the world, encompassing the entertainment industry centred on Hollywood, the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach (among the busiest container ports in the Americas), aerospace, technology, tourism, trade and agriculture. The region is famous for its beaches, surf culture, freeway network, and sprawling suburban development, as well as leading universities such as UCLA, USC, UC San Diego and Caltech.
