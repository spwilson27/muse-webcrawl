McDonnell Douglas Corporation was a major American aerospace manufacturer and defence contractor. It was formed by the merger of McDonnell Aircraft and the Douglas Aircraft Company in 1967, and merged into Boeing in 1997 to form The Boeing Company, ending nearly a century of Douglas and McDonnell aircraft production.

The company produced many famous aircraft, including the F-4 Phantom II, F-15 Eagle and F/A-18 Hornet fighters, the DC-9, DC-10 and MD-11 airliners, the C-17 Globemaster III transport, and the Harpoon and Tomahawk missiles. It also built spacecraft and components for the Mercury and Gemini programmes, Skylab, and the Space Shuttle, and operated the Delta family of launch vehicles through its heritage companies.

History

James Smith McDonnell founded his aircraft company in 1939, building fighters such as the FH-1 Phantom and F2H Banshee for the U.S. Navy. Donald Douglas's company, founded in 1920, built the DC series of airliners and the C-47/DC-3. By the 1960s Douglas was in financial difficulty because of the cost of the DC-8 and DC-9 programmes, leading to the 1967 merger. McDonnell Douglas struggled in the commercial market against Boeing and Airbus in the 1990s, and after defence-industry consolidation it merged with Boeing in a $13 billion stock swap in August 1997.
