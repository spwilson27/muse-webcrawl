Pipe Rolls

The Pipe Rolls, sometimes called the Great Rolls of the Pipe, are a collection of financial records of the English Exchequer, recording the accounts of royal sheriffs and other officials. The series runs almost continuously from 1155 to 1833, with a single earlier roll surviving for 1130 — the oldest continuous series of public records in England.

Contents and name

Each roll, written on parchment membranes sewn together, summarised the annual audit at the Exchequer of sums owed to and by the Crown. The name is traditionally said to derive from the pipe-like rolls of parchment or the "pipe" through which documents were passed. The roll for 1130 is the only surviving financial record of the reign of Henry I.

Scholarly use

The Pipe Roll Society, founded in 1883, has published editions of the rolls, which remain a fundamental source for medieval English administrative, economic and local history.
