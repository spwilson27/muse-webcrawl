LibriVox

LibriVox is an online digital library of free public domain audiobooks read by volunteers from around the world. Founded in August 2005 by Hugh McGuire, a Montreal-based writer and web developer, LibriVox's objective is "to make all books in the public domain available, for free, in audio format on the internet".

Volunteers record chapters of public domain texts — drawn largely from Project Gutenberg — and the recordings are released into the public domain themselves, so that anyone may download, copy, and redistribute them without restriction. Recordings are available through the LibriVox website, the Internet Archive, and podcast feeds such as iTunes.

History

McGuire launched the project in 2005 with a blog post asking whether it would be possible to harness podcasting and volunteers to record the classics. The first recorded book was Joseph Conrad's The Secret Agent. By 2006 the catalogue had grown to hundreds of titles, and LibriVox became one of the largest producers of free audiobooks on the web. The project is coordinated entirely by volunteers who manage recording projects through an online forum, using a "magic window" workflow to track claimed and completed sections.

Recording model

Any volunteer may record, requiring only a computer, a microphone, and free recording software such as Audacity. Projects include solo recordings, collaborative group readings, dramatic readings with casts, and weekly poetry collections. Because the source texts must be in the public domain in the United States, LibriVox generally uses works published before 1930. Every LibriVox recording begins with a standard disclaimer announcing that the recording is in the public domain.
