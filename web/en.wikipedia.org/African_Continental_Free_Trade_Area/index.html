The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) is a free trade area encompassing most of Africa. It was established by the African Continental Free Trade Agreement, signed in Kigali, Rwanda, on 21 March 2018, and is the largest free trade area in the world by number of participating countries since the formation of the World Trade Organization.

Goals

Administered by the African Union with a secretariat in Accra, Ghana, the AfCFTA aims to boost intra-African trade—which has historically been low at roughly 15–18% of the continent's total trade—by eliminating tariffs on most goods, reducing non-tariff barriers, and creating a single market of some 1.4 billion people with a combined GDP of over US$3 trillion.

Implementation

Trading under the agreement officially began on 1 January 2021, though full tariff liberalization is phased in over years. Protocols on investment, competition, intellectual property, and digital trade were negotiated in later phases.
