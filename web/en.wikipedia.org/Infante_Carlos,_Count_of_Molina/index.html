Infante Carlos, Count of Molina

Infante Carlos of Spain, Count of Molina (29 March 1788 – 10 March 1855), known to his followers as Don Carlos (and claimed by them as Carlos V), was the second son of King Charles IV of Spain and the younger brother of Ferdinand VII. After Ferdinand changed the succession law to allow his daughter Isabella to inherit the throne, Don Carlos claimed the crown for himself, sparking the Carlist Wars that convulsed Spain through much of the 19th century.

The succession dispute

In 1830 Ferdinand VII published the Pragmatic Sanction, repealing the Salic-law succession introduced by the Bourbons and restoring the older Castilian rule permitting female succession. When Ferdinand died in 1833, the three-year-old Isabella II was proclaimed queen under the regency of her mother, Maria Christina, while Don Carlos and his supporters — the Carlists — proclaimed him king.

Carlist Wars and exile

Don Carlos led his forces during the First Carlist War (1833–1840), campaigning in the Basque Country and Navarre with the support of defenders of regional fueros, the Church, and traditionalist absolutism. Defeated after the Convention of Vergara (1839), he went into exile in France. In 1845 he abdicated his claim in favor of his son, Carlos Luis, Count of Montemolín, and died in Trieste in 1855. Carlism persisted as a political movement well into the 20th century.
