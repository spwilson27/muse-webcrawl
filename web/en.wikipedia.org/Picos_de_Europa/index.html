Picos de Europa

Location
Northern Spain (Asturias, Cantabria, León)
Highest peak
Torre Cerredo, 2,650 m
Protected area
Picos de Europa National Park

The Picos de Europa ("Peaks of Europe") are a limestone mountain range in northern Spain, extending about 40 km across Asturias, Cantabria, and León, only some 20 km inland from the Bay of Biscay. Tradition holds that their pale summits were the first sight of Europe for ships returning from the Americas.

Geography

The range consists of three massifs separated by deep gorges, including the Cares Gorge. Its highest summit is Torre Cerredo (2,650 m); the most iconic is the sheer monolith of Naranjo de Bulnes (Picu Urriellu), a mecca for Spanish alpinism. The area forms the core of Picos de Europa National Park, created in 1918 as Spain's first national park.

Nature and culture

The mountains support Cantabrian brown bears, wolves, chamois, and bearded vultures, and are famous for their cave-aged blue cheeses, including Cabrales, traditionally matured in limestone caves. The Lakes of Covadonga are a major visitor destination.
