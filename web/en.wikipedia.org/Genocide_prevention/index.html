Genocide prevention

Genocide prevention is the set of activities by states, international organizations, and civil society intended to stop genocide and related mass atrocities before they occur or while they are under way. It is grounded in the 1948 Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide, under which states parties undertake to prevent as well as punish the crime, and it was reinforced by the "responsibility to protect" principle endorsed at the 2005 UN World Summit.

Risk factors and early warning

Scholars such as Gregory Stanton have modelled genocide as a process developing through predictable stages — including classification, dehumanization, polarization, and preparation — arguing that preventive measures can be applied at each stage. Early-warning systems monitor indicators such as hate speech, exclusionary ideologies, armed conflict, prior unpunished atrocities, and the targeting of identity groups.

Preventive tools

Measures range from diplomacy, mediation, and targeted sanctions to arms embargoes, peacekeeping deployments, humanitarian assistance, and, as a last resort, military intervention. Judicial deterrence through bodies such as the International Criminal Court, and domestic measures against incitement, are also part of the preventive toolkit.

Institutions

The United Nations maintains a Special Adviser on the Prevention of Genocide, established in 2004 after the failures to prevent the Rwandan genocide of 1994 and the Srebrenica massacre of 1995. Regional bodies, national atrocity-prevention boards, and non-governmental organizations also conduct risk assessments and advocacy.
