The 2006 Southern Leyte mudslide was a mass wasting event that occurred on 17 February 2006 in the Philippine province of Southern Leyte, burying the entire village of Guinsaugon in the municipality of Saint Bernard. An estimated 1,126 people were killed.

The disaster

After ten days of heavy rain, the side of a mountain above Guinsaugon collapsed shortly after a magnitude 2.6 earthquake. The slide, estimated at up to 30 metres deep in places, covered the village including its elementary school, where some 246 children and teachers were gathered for classes; only a handful of people in the school area survived. Rescue efforts by Philippine forces, aided by teams from the United States, Taiwan and Malaysia, were hampered by rain, deep mud and unstable terrain.

Causes and aftermath

Investigations pointed to a combination of prolonged rainfall, deforestation and the loss of deep-rooted coconut trees, the geology of the Philippine fault zone and the minor tremor. The disaster prompted a review of hazard mapping across the Philippines, and Guinsaugon was permanently abandoned, with survivors resettled in new villages nearby.
