Bulova is an American timepiece manufacturing company founded in 1875 by Joseph Bulova, a Bohemian immigrant, in New York City. It is now owned by Japan's Citizen Watch Co. Bulova is known historically for pioneering standardized watch production and mass advertising, and for the Accutron, the first fully electronic watch, introduced in 1960.

History

Joseph Bulova opened a small jewelry store on Maiden Lane in 1875 and began manufacturing table clocks and later wristwatches. In 1912 he opened a plant in Biel, Switzerland, dedicated to standardized production of movements. Bulova aired what is regarded as the first television commercial in the United States on July 1, 1941, and sponsored pioneering radio and TV programming.

Accutron

The Accutron, developed by engineer Max Hetzel, used a tuning fork vibrating 360 times per second instead of a balance wheel, giving unprecedented accuracy for its time. Accutron movements were used in US space program instrumentation, and a Bulova Accutron timing device was left on the Moon by Apollo 15 commander David Scott in 1971; Scott's personal Bulova chronograph, worn during that mission, later sold at auction.

Modern era

Bulova was acquired by the Loews Corporation in 1979 and sold to Citizen in 2007. The brand today sells quartz, automatic, and high-frequency Precisionist watches under the Bulova name and related brands.
