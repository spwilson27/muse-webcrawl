Ironman 70.3

Ironman 70.3, also known as a Half Ironman, is one of a series of long-distance triathlon races organised by the World Triathlon Corporation (WTC). The "70.3" refers to the total distance in miles (113.0 km) covered in the race, consisting of a 1.2-mile (1.9 km) swim, a 56-mile (90 km) bike ride, and a 13.1-mile (21.1 km) run — each segment half the distance of the corresponding leg of a full Ironman Triathlon.

History

The first Half Ironman race was held in the United Kingdom in 2001. The 70.3 series was launched by the WTC in 2005, expanding rapidly as an accessible step between Olympic-distance racing and the full Ironman. Today dozens of Ironman 70.3 events are held worldwide each year, serving as qualifiers for the annual Ironman 70.3 World Championship.

World Championship

The Ironman 70.3 World Championship was first held in Clearwater, Florida, in 2006, and the location has rotated among venues including Las Vegas, Mont-Tremblant, Zell am See, the Sunshine Coast, Chattanooga, Port Elizabeth, Nice, Taupō and St. George, Utah. Since 2017 men's and women's championship races have been held on separate days. Professional winning times are typically around 3 hours 40 minutes for men and 4 hours for women, depending on course conditions.

Format

Races are held over a single day with mass or wave starts. Competitors must complete the course within cutoff times, generally around 8 hours 30 minutes. Age-group athletes compete for slots at the World Championship awarded according to participation in each category.
