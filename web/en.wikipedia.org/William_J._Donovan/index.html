William Joseph "Wild Bill" Donovan (January 1, 1883 – February 8, 1959) was an American soldier, lawyer, and intelligence officer, best known as the wartime head of the Office of Strategic Services (OSS), the predecessor of the Central Intelligence Agency.

Career

Donovan earned the Medal of Honor for heroism with the "Fighting 69th" Infantry Regiment in the First World War. Between the wars he built a successful Wall Street law practice and served as a U.S. attorney and assistant attorney general. In 1941 President Franklin D. Roosevelt appointed him Coordinator of Information, and in 1942 he became director of the OSS, running espionage and special operations worldwide. After the war he advocated for a permanent central intelligence service and later served as U.S. ambassador to Thailand.
