Bilge Khagan (Old Turkic: 𐰋𐰃𐰠𐰏𐰀 𐰴𐰍𐰣, Bilgä Qaghan; 683–734) was the fourth khagan of the Second Turkic Khaganate. His reign, conducted together with his younger brother Kul Tigin and the statesman Tonyukuk, is remembered as the height of the khaganate's power and for the Orkhon inscriptions, the oldest known texts written in a Turkic language.

Early life

Bilge was the elder son of Ilterish Khagan, the founder of the Second Turkic Khaganate, and the nephew of Qapaghan Khagan. Born Ashina Mojilian, he campaigned alongside his father and uncle from a young age and was appointed shad (governor) of the eastern wing of the empire.

Reign

After Qapaghan's death in 716, his son Inel was placed on the throne, but Bilge and Kul Tigin staged a coup in which Inel and his supporters were killed. Bilge took the throne and made Kul Tigin crown prince and effective commander of the army. With Tonyukuk as adviser, the brothers campaigned against rebellious tribes, the Türgesh, and Tang dynasty China, stabilising the steppe empire. Bilge favoured diplomacy with Tang China, sought trade and a marriage alliance, and reportedly considered adopting Chinese-style cities, Buddhism and Taoism — a plan Tonyukuk famously argued against in the inscriptions.

Death and legacy

Kul Tigin died in 731 and Tonyukuk around 725. In 734 Bilge was poisoned by a courtier named Buyruk Chor, though he survived long enough to have the poisoner and his family executed. The Orkhon inscriptions erected in memory of Bilge and Kul Tigin recount the history of the Turks and contain Bilge's counsel to his people, and they remain central sources for early Turkic history.
