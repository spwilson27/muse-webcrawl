Ondansetron

Trade nameZofran, others
Class5-HT3 receptor antagonist (antiemetic)
RoutesOral, intravenous, intramuscular
First approved1990

Ondansetron, sold under the brand name Zofran among others, is a medication used to prevent nausea and vomiting caused by chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and surgery. It is a serotonin 5-HT3 receptor antagonist: it blocks serotonin receptors in the gut and the chemoreceptor trigger zone of the brain, reducing the vomiting reflex. It was developed by Glaxo and first approved in 1990.

Medical uses

Ondansetron is widely used for chemotherapy-induced and postoperative nausea and vomiting, and is also used off-label for gastroenteritis in children and for nausea in pregnancy in some settings. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is available as a generic in tablets, orally disintegrating tablets, and injectable forms.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, constipation, and dizziness. Ondansetron can prolong the QT interval of the heart, which led regulators to limit high single intravenous doses; caution is advised in people with congenital long QT syndrome or electrolyte abnormalities. It is generally ineffective for motion sickness, since that pathway is mediated mainly by histamine and acetylcholine rather than serotonin.
