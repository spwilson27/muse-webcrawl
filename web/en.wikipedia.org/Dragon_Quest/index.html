Dragon Quest

Creator: Yuji Horii
Developer: Square Enix (formerly Enix)
First release: Dragon Quest, May 27, 1986
Genre: Role-playing

Dragon Quest, previously published in North America as Dragon Warrior, is a series of role-playing video games created by Japanese game designers Armor Project (Yuji Horii), Bird Studio (Akira Toriyama) and Sugiyama Kobo (Koichi Sugiyama), published by Square Enix (formerly Enix). The first game in the series was released in Japan in 1986 for the Famicom, and the series has since spawned eleven main instalments and numerous spin-offs.

The series introduced many of the conventions of the console role-playing genre and has had a significant impact on the development of other RPGs. Its recurring features include turn-based combat, random encounters, text-based menus, a party of heroes commanded by the player, and signature monsters such as the blue Slime, the series mascot designed by Toriyama.

Development and influences

Yuji Horii drew inspiration from Wizardry, Ultima, and his own earlier adventure game The Portopia Serial Murder Case, aiming to make the RPG accessible to a broad console audience. Akira Toriyama, creator of Dragon Ball, has provided character and monster designs for every main game, and Koichi Sugiyama composed the music until his death in 2021.

Cultural impact

Dragon Quest is enormously popular in Japan, where new releases have traditionally been scheduled on weekends after reports (part fact, part urban legend) of mass absenteeism on weekday launch dates. The series has sold more than 85 million copies worldwide. Dragon Quest III (1988) is frequently cited as one of the most influential games of all time, and Dragon Quest XI (2017) received wide international acclaim.
