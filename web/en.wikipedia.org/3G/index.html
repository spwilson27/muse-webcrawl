3G

3G is the third generation of wireless mobile telecommunications technology. It was the upgrade over 2G and preceding 2.5G networks, providing faster data transfer rates and greater network capacity. The first commercial 3G networks were introduced in 2001, and the technology eventually gave way to 4G and later 5G systems.

History

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) defined the requirements for 3G networks in the late 1980s under the IMT-2000 framework. NTT DoCoMo launched the first commercial 3G network in Japan in October 2001, based on W-CDMA technology. Networks then spread through Europe, South Korea, and the United States during the early and mid-2000s.

Standards

Several standards fall under the 3G umbrella, most notably UMTS (with HSPA and HSPA+ evolutions), CDMA2000 (with EV-DO), and China's TD-SCDMA. Typical peak data rates ranged from 384 kbit/s for early UMTS to several megabits per second for HSPA+, with theoretical maxima above 40 Mbit/s in later releases.

Features

Compared with 2G, 3G enabled practical mobile internet access, video calling, mobile television, and richer multimedia services. The launch of smartphones in the late 2000s drove 3G subscriptions to billions worldwide.

Transition and shutdown

As LTE networks matured, operators began refarming 3G spectrum for 4G and 5G. Many carriers shut down their 3G networks in the early 2020s, including the major US carriers in 2022, ending service for older handsets, vehicle telematics, and some alarm systems that still depended on the technology.
