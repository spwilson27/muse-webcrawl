Araneus diadematus

Cross orbweaver
OrderAraneae
FamilyAraneidae
GenusAraneus
SpeciesA. diadematus

Araneus diadematus, the cross orbweaver (also known as the European garden spider, diadem spider, orangie, cross spider and crowned orb weaver), is an orb-weaver spider found in Europe and North America. The species was first described by Carl Alexander Clerck in 1757.

Description

Individual spiders' colourings can range from extremely light yellow to very dark grey, but all A. diadematus have mottled markings across the dorsal abdomen, with four or more segments forming a cross—a pattern reflected in the species name, derived from the Latin diadematus, "wearing a crown/diadem". Adult females grow to about 6.5–20 mm in body length; males are smaller, at 5.5–13 mm.

Behaviour

The spider builds a classic orb web, typically in shrubs, fences and garden vegetation, and often consumes and rebuilds the web each day. It sits at the hub or hides nearby with a thread signalling vibrations from trapped prey, which it wraps in silk before eating. Like most orb-weavers, its bite is not medically significant to humans. Females lay egg sacs in sheltered places in autumn and die shortly afterwards; the spiderlings emerge the following spring.
