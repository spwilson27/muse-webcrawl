Pritzker Architecture Prize

The Pritzker Architecture Prize is an international award presented annually "to honor a living architect or architects whose built work demonstrates a combination of those qualities of talent, vision and commitment, which has produced consistent and significant contributions to humanity and the built environment through the art of architecture." Founded in 1979 by Jay A. Pritzker and his wife Cindy, the award is often called "architecture's Nobel Prize".

History

The Pritzker family, owners of the Hyatt hotel chain, established the prize through the Hyatt Foundation. The first laureate was Philip Johnson. Laureates receive $100,000, a citation, and (since 1987) a bronze medallion based on designs by Louis Sullivan. The jury changes over time and selects a living architect whose body of work, rather than a single building, is honoured.

Laureates

Recipients include I. M. Pei, Richard Meier, Frank Gehry, Robert Venturi, Zaha Hadid (the first woman laureate, 2004), Rem Koolhaas, Norman Foster, Renzo Piano, Jean Nouvel, Peter Zumthor, Toyo Ito, Shigeru Ban, Alejandro Aravena, Balkrishna Doshi (the first Indian laureate, 2018), and Diébédo Francis Kéré (2022). Denise Scott Brown was controversially not included when her husband and partner Robert Venturi won in 1991, prompting later petitions for recognition.

Criticism

The prize has been criticised for favouring "starchitects", for the historical under-representation of women, and for sometimes overlooking collaborative practices. It remains the most prestigious award in the profession, and hosting cities use the annual ceremony — held at architecturally significant sites worldwide — as a cultural showcase.
