A pride parade (also known as a pride event, pride festival, pride march, or pride protest) is an outdoor event celebrating lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer (LGBTQ) social and self-acceptance, achievements, legal rights, and pride. The events also serve as demonstrations for legal rights such as same-sex marriage, and most occur annually in June to commemorate the 1969 Stonewall riots in New York City, a pivotal moment in modern LGBTQ social movements.

Origins

Before Stonewall, the Annual Reminder pickets (1965–1969) in Philadelphia advocated discreet protest. After the riots of 28 June 1969, activists including Craig Rodwell, Fred Sargeant, Ellen Broidy and Linda Rhodes proposed a commemorative march in New York. On 28 June 1970, the Christopher Street Liberation Day march drew thousands from Greenwich Village to Central Park; parallel events took place in Chicago, Los Angeles and San Francisco. The word "pride" — as a counter to shame — is associated with early organizer L. Craig Schoonmaker.

Growth

Parades spread internationally through the 1970s and 1980s. The largest events now attract millions: São Paulo's parade has been among the world's biggest, and events in New York, Madrid (WorldPride 2017), Toronto, San Francisco, London and Sydney draw very large crowds and extensive corporate and civic participation. WorldPride is licensed by InterPride to a host city every few years.

Character and debate

Pride events range from explicitly political marches — still necessary, organizers argue, where LGBTQ rights are contested — to large carnivalesque festivals with floats, music and sponsorship. The balance between protest and party, and the role of corporations and police in parades, are recurring subjects of debate within LGBTQ communities. Some events, such as Dyke Marches and Trans Marches, focus on particular constituencies. In some countries pride marches remain banned or face violent opposition, leading to court battles over freedom of assembly.
