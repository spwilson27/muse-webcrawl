The God Delusion is a 2006 bestselling non-fiction book by British evolutionary biologist Richard Dawkins, in which he argues that a supernatural creator almost certainly does not exist and that belief in a personal god qualifies as a delusion — a persistent false belief held in the face of strong contradictory evidence.

Contents

Dawkins distinguishes between Einsteinian "religion" (awe at the universe) and supernatural religion, criticises agnosticism he calls "poverty of agnosticism", and examines arguments for God's existence including the argument from design, which he counters with the "Ultimate Boeing 747 gambit": a designer capable of creating complex life would itself have to be at least as complex and improbable. He explores the roots of religion as a possible evolutionary by-product, the roots of morality outside religion, and criticises the religious indoctrination of children. A recurring theme is that consciousness-raising about atheism, modelled on feminist consciousness-raising, can overcome the stigma attached to non-belief.

Reception

The book was a major commercial success, selling millions of copies and translated into more than 35 languages, and became a central text of the New Atheism movement alongside works by Sam Harris, Christopher Hitchens, and Daniel Dennett. It drew both praise for its accessibility and criticism from theologians and philosophers — including Alvin Plantinga, Alister McGrath, and Terry Eagleton — who disputed its characterisations of religious belief and its philosophical arguments.
