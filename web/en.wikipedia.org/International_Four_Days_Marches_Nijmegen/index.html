The International Four Days Marches Nijmegen (Dutch: Internationale Vierdaagse Afstandsmarsen Nijmegen), commonly known as the Vierdaagse (Four Days March), is the largest multiple-day walking event in the world. It is organised every year in Nijmegen, Netherlands in mid-July as a means of promoting sport and exercise. Participants walk 30, 40 or 50 kilometres daily depending on their age and gender, and, on completion, receive a royally approved medal, the Four Days Cross (Vierdaagsekruis).

History

The event began in 1909, when marches were held from several Dutch garrison towns, and Nijmegen became the permanent seat in 1916. It grew from a mainly military exercise into a mass civilian event. The Marches were suspended during the world wars; in 1946 they resumed. Military contingents from many nations still take part, staying at Camp Heumensoord near the city.

The marches and festivities

The walking takes place over four consecutive days, each with a route named after the main town it passes through, such as Elst, Wijchen and Groesbeek. The final day's entry into Nijmegen along the Sint Annastraat—transformed into the "Via Gladiola"—draws huge crowds who present gladioli to the finishers. The Vierdaagsefeesten, a week of festivals held alongside the marches, attracts well over a million visitors to the city.
