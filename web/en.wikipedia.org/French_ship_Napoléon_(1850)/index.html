French ship Napoléon (1850)

Launched16 May 1850, Toulon
Type90-gun steam ship of the line
DesignerHenri Dupuy de Lôme

FateStricken 1876; broken up 1887

Napoléon was a 90-gun ship of the line of the French Navy, launched in 1850. She was the first true steam battleship — the first warship designed and built from the keel up to operate primarily under steam power with a screw propeller — and her success triggered a naval arms race that transformed every major fleet within a decade.

Design

Designed by the young naval architect Henri Dupuy de Lôme, Napoléon combined a full sailing rig with a two-cylinder steam engine driving a single screw. Unlike earlier auxiliary steamers, her machinery was integral to the design, and she could sustain around 13 knots under steam — faster than any sailing ship of the line. She carried ninety guns on three decks, comparable to the best sailing battleships, while freeing warships from dependence on the wind for manoeuvre.

Career and impact

Napoléon served with the French fleet during the Crimean War, where steam battleships demonstrated decisive mobility in operations against Russia. Her performance prompted Britain to accelerate its own screw battleship programme and hastened the end of the purely sailing capital ship. She remained in service into the 1870s before being stricken in 1876.

Significance

Naval historians treat Napoléon as a turning point: within ten years France and Britain had launched ironclads (Gloire and HMS Warrior), rendering wooden steam ships of the line obsolete almost as soon as the type had been perfected.
