Constitution of Apatzingán

The Constitution of Apatzingán, formally the Constitutional Decree for the Liberty of Mexican America (Decreto Constitucional para la Libertad de la América Mexicana), was promulgated on 22 October 1814 in Apatzingán, Michoacán, by the Congress of Chilpancingo during the Mexican War of Independence. It was the first constitution drafted for Mexico, largely inspired by the ideas of José María Morelos as expressed in his Sentimientos de la Nación.

Content

The decree declared the sovereignty of the people, equality before the law, the abolition of slavery and caste distinctions, freedom of speech and of the press, and Roman Catholicism as the sole religion. It divided government into legislative, executive (a three-person Supreme Government), and judicial powers, and provided for a congress of deputies from the provinces.

Legacy

Because royalist forces soon overran the insurgent congress, the constitution was never fully implemented, but it became a foundational text of Mexican constitutionalism, anticipating the federal Constitution of 1824 and later liberal charters.
