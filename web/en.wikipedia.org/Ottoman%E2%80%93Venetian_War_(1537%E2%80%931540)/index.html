Ottoman–Venetian War (1537–1540)

Date1537–1540
LocationMediterranean, Aegean, Ionian, Dalmatia
ResultOttoman victory
BelligerentsOttoman Empire vs. Venice and the Holy League

The Ottoman–Venetian War of 1537–1540, also called the Third Ottoman–Venetian War, was fought between the Ottoman Empire of Suleiman the Magnificent and the Republic of Venice, which was joined by a papal-sponsored Holy League including Spain and other Italian states.

Course

The war began with the Ottoman siege of Corfu in 1537 and campaigns in Dalmatia and the Aegean, where the admiral Hayreddin Barbarossa seized most of the Venetian-ruled Cyclades and other islands. The Christian fleet, commanded by Andrea Doria, fought Barbarossa at the Battle of Preveza in September 1538, an Ottoman victory that confirmed Ottoman naval dominance in the eastern Mediterranean.

Settlement

Venice made a separate peace in 1540, paying an indemnity of 300,000 ducats and ceding its remaining possessions in the Peloponnese (Monemvasia and Nauplia) and most of the Aegean islands it had lost.
