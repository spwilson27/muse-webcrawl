Navigable aqueduct

A navigable aqueduct is a water bridge that carries a navigable waterway—a canal—across a river, valley, road, railway or another obstacle. Such structures were built in large numbers during the canal age, allowing boats to cross terrain without descending and climbing locks.

Construction

Early aqueducts were masonry structures; later examples used cast-iron troughs, such as the Pontcysyllte Aqueduct in Wales (completed 1805), designed by Thomas Telford, which carries the Llangollen Canal high over the Dee valley. Modern examples include the Magdeburg Water Bridge in Germany, opened in 2003.

Examples

Notable navigable aqueducts include the Pont du Gard (a Roman water aqueduct, not navigable), the Barton Aqueduct, the Briare Aqueduct in France, and the Pontcysyllte Aqueduct, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
