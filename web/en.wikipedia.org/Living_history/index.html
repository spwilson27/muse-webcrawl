Living history

Living history is an activity that incorporates historical tools, activities and dress into an interactive presentation that seeks to give observers and participants a sense of stepping back in time. Although it does not necessarily seek to reenact a specific event, it often involves interpreters performing the everyday activities of a past era.

Practice

Living history is practised at open-air museums such as Skansen in Stockholm (founded 1891), Colonial Williamsburg, Plimoth Patuxet and Beamish, and by reenactment societies devoted to periods from the Roman era to the Second World War. Interpreters may use first-person interpretation, speaking as a historical character, or third-person demonstration.

Education

Museums and schools use living history as experiential education: visitors observe or take part in farming, cooking, crafts and military drills using period techniques. Practitioners debate the balance between authenticity and accessibility, including contested questions about presenting difficult subjects such as slavery and war.
