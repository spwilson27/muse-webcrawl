Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern (Mecklenburg–Western Pomerania) is a state (Land) in north-eastern Germany, with its capital at Schwerin and largest city Rostock. It borders the Baltic Sea, Poland, and the German states of Brandenburg, Lower Saxony and Schleswig-Holstein.

Geography

The state is Germany's least densely populated, characterised by the Mecklenburg Lake District, thousands of lakes formed by glaciation, the islands of Rügen and Usedom, and Hanseatic port cities such as Wismar and Stralsund, whose old towns are UNESCO World Heritage sites.

History

Formed after the Second World War from the historic region of Mecklenburg and the western part of Pomerania, it was dissolved into districts in East Germany in 1952 and re-established on German reunification in 1990. Tourism, agriculture, shipbuilding and renewable energy anchor its economy.
