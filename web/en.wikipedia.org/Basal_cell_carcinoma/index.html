Basal cell carcinoma

Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is the most common type of skin cancer. It arises from the basal cells of the epidermis and rarely metastasises, but can be locally invasive if untreated.

Signs and causes

BCC often appears as a painless raised, pearly or waxy bump with visible blood vessels, or as a flat, scar-like lesion, typically on sun-exposed skin such as the face and neck. The principal risk factor is ultraviolet radiation from sunlight and tanning beds; fair skin and a history of sunburn increase risk.

Treatment

Treatment is usually by surgical excision, including Mohs micrographic surgery for high-risk sites. Other options include cryotherapy, topical chemotherapy, and, for advanced cases, hedgehog pathway inhibitors such as vismodegib. Prevention focuses on sun protection and skin surveillance.
