Biscay

Biscay (Basque: Bizkaia; Spanish: Vizcaya) is a province of Spain located on the Bay of Biscay in the autonomous community of the Basque Country, in northern Spain. Its capital city is Bilbao. The province is named after the bay, and the bay in turn takes its name from the province.

Geography

Biscay covers an area of about 2,217 square kilometres, making it one of the smallest provinces of Spain. It borders the Cantabrian Sea to the north, the provinces of Cantabria to the west, Burgos to the south, and Gipuzkoa and Álava to the east and southeast. The terrain is mountainous, cut by deep valleys running down to a rugged coastline.

History

The territory was historically organised as the Lordship of Biscay, a semi-autonomous lordship whose lords swore to uphold the local charters, the fueros, beneath the Oak of Gernika. These privileges survived in modified form until the nineteenth century. In the modern era Biscay became one of Spain's most industrialised regions, centred on iron mining, steelmaking, and shipbuilding around Bilbao.

Demographics

With well over a million inhabitants, Biscay is the most populous province of the Basque Country. Metropolitan Bilbao accounts for most of the population. Both Spanish and Basque (Euskara) are official languages in the province.
