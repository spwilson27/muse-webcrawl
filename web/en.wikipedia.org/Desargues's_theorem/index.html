Desargues's theorem

In projective geometry, Desargues's theorem, named after Girard Desargues, states that two triangles are in perspective axially if and only if they are in perspective centrally.

Statement

If the lines joining corresponding vertices of two triangles meet in a single point (central perspectivity), then the intersection points of corresponding sides are collinear (axial perspectivity), and vice versa.

Significance

The theorem holds in the real projective plane and in any projective space of dimension greater than two, but can fail in some abstract projective planes (non-Desarguesian planes).
