Woodes Rogers (c. 1679 – 15 July 1732) was an English sea captain, privateer, and colonial administrator. He completed a circumnavigation of the globe as a privateer (1708–1711), rescuing the castaway Alexander Selkirk, and later became the first Royal Governor of the Bahamas, where he suppressed piracy.

Circumnavigation

Backed by Bristol merchants, Rogers commanded the ships Duke and Duchess on a privateering voyage against Spanish commerce. In February 1709 he rescued Alexander Selkirk from the Juan Fernández Islands, an episode recorded in Rogers' bestselling account A Cruising Voyage Round the World (1712) and commonly linked to the inspiration for Robinson Crusoe.

Governor of the Bahamas

Appointed governor in 1718, Rogers arrived at Nassau offering the King's pardon to pirates who surrendered; many accepted, while Charles Vane famously escaped. Rogers fortified Nassau and hanged several pirates who returned to their old trade, earning the motto Expulsis Piratis, Restituta Commercia ("Pirates expelled, commerce restored"). He died in Nassau in 1732 during his second term.
