War with the Newts (Válka s mloky in the original Czech) is a satirical science fiction novel by the Czech writer Karel Čapek, published in 1936. One of the most famous works of interwar Czech literature, it satirizes fascism, colonialism, capitalism, racism, and the appeasement politics of the 1930s through the story of an intelligent species of giant sea newts discovered in the Pacific.

Plot

Sea captain J. van Toch discovers a population of docile, intelligent giant salamanders near Sumatra and trades them knives and tools for pearls. Industrialists, led by the magnate G. H. Bondy and the Salamander Syndicate, breed and export the newts worldwide as cheap labour for underwater construction, harbours, and land reclamation. The newts multiply explosively, form their own civilization under a Supreme Salamander, demand living space, and begin demolishing the continents' coasts to extend their shallow seas. Humanity, divided and commercially entangled, appeases them until war and flooding engulf the world; the novel ends with the author arguing with himself about whether humanity can still be saved — concluding that the newts, like humans, will eventually destroy themselves through their own conflicts.

Style and reception

The novel is narrated partly through the scrapbook of Mr. Povondra, a doorman who collects newspaper clippings about the newts, giving it a collage structure of reports, scientific papers, and conference minutes. It is frequently compared with Čapek's play R.U.R. (1920), which introduced the word "robot", and is regarded as a classic of dystopian and satirical fiction, translated into many languages.
