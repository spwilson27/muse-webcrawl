Video hosting service

A video hosting service is a type of online service that allows individuals to upload video clips to a website, which stores them on its servers and allows other users to view them, typically through streaming media. Video hosting services are a form of online video platform, and commonly also provide sharing, commenting, and discovery features.

History

Early video sharing faced high bandwidth costs and limited home internet speeds. ShareYourWorld, founded in 1997, was among the first sites to let users upload video clips. The spread of broadband and Adobe Flash in the mid-2000s enabled mainstream services: Vimeo launched in 2004, and YouTube, founded in 2005, grew to dominate the market and was acquired by Google in 2006. Later services include Dailymotion, and short-form platforms such as TikTok.

Technology

Uploaded videos are transcoded into multiple formats and resolutions (such as H.264, VP9, and AV1 at various bitrates) and delivered adaptively via content delivery networks using protocols such as HLS and MPEG-DASH. Services typically provide embedded players so videos can be displayed on other websites.

Purposes and impact

Video hosting enables user-generated content, video blogging (vlogging), citizen journalism, education, marketing, and entertainment. Platforms monetize through advertising, subscriptions, and revenue sharing with creators. Issues associated with the services include copyright infringement (addressed by systems such as YouTube's Content ID), content moderation, misinformation, and the privacy of uploaded material. Some services specialize in niches: privacy-focused or decentralized hosting (e.g. PeerTube), live streaming (Twitch), and professional hosting for businesses.
