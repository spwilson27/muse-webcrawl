Remanence

Remanence (or remanent magnetization) is the magnetization left behind in a ferromagnetic material after an external magnetic field is removed. It is the property exploited by permanent magnets: high remanence, combined with high coercivity, keeps a magnet magnetized in everyday use.

Hysteresis

On a hysteresis loop (B–H curve), remanence is the value of magnetic flux density B remaining when the applied field H is reduced to zero after saturation. The squareness ratio (remanence divided by saturation magnetization) is a figure of merit for magnetic recording media, which require a square loop for stable bit storage.

Geological remanence

Rocks acquire natural remanent magnetization as they form — thermoremanent magnetization when igneous rocks cool through the Curie temperature, and detrital remanence in sediments. Because this remanence records the direction of Earth's field at the time, paleomagnetism uses it to reconstruct past plate motions and geomagnetic reversals, providing key evidence for seafloor spreading and plate tectonics.
