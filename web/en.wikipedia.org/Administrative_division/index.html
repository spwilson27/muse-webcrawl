Administrative division

SynonymsSubnational entity, constituent unit, subdivision
Highest levelFederated state, province, region (varies)
ExamplesUS states, Canadian provinces, German Länder, French régions, Japanese prefectures

Administrative divisions (also known as subnational entities, constituent divisions, or administrative units) are the territorial subunits into which a country is divided for the purposes of administration and government. Below the national government, divisions may form a multi-level hierarchy: a state or province may be subdivided into counties or districts, which are in turn divided into municipalities, towns and villages.

The powers and character of divisions depend on a country's constitutional structure. In federations such as the United States, Germany, India and Brazil, the highest-level divisions — states, Länder, provinces — have their own governments, legislatures and constitutions or extensive statutory powers, and their existence is constitutionally guaranteed. In unitary states such as France and Japan, divisions are created and can be altered by the central government, though many have elected councils and substantial devolved responsibilities.

Terminology

Names for divisions vary widely: states, provinces, regions, prefectures, counties, districts, departments, oblasts, governorates, cantons, parishes and municipalities all serve as administrative labels in different countries. Because equivalent terms can imply very different powers, comparative tables distinguish divisions by level (first, second, third) rather than by name alone.

Statistics

Administrative divisions underpin census-taking, elections, taxation and the delivery of public services, and international standards such as ISO 3166-2 assign codes to the principal subdivisions of each country for use in data processing.
