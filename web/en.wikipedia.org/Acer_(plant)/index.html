Acer (plant)

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Sapindales
Family:Sapindaceae
Genus:Acer L.

Acer is a genus of trees and shrubs commonly known as maples. The genus has about 132 species, most of which are native to Asia, with a number also appearing in Europe, northern Africa, and North America. Only one species, Acer laurinum, extends to the Southern Hemisphere. The type species of the genus is the sycamore maple, Acer pseudoplatanus, one of the most common maple species in Europe.

Maples usually have easily recognisable palmate leaves (with a few exceptions) and distinctive winged fruits called samaras. The closest relatives of the maples are the horse-chestnuts. Maple syrup is made from the sap of some maple species. The maple leaf is the national arboreal emblem of Canada, and it appears on the country's flag.

Description

Most maples are trees growing to a height of 10–45 metres (33–148 feet), while others are shrubs less than 10 metres tall bearing a number of small trunks originating at ground level. Most species are deciduous; a few in southern Asia and the Mediterranean region are evergreen. Leaves are opposite, and in most species palmately lobed. The flowers are regular, pentamerous, and borne in racemes, corymbs, or umbels. The fruit is a pair of winged samaras that spin as they fall, carrying the seeds a considerable distance — popularly called "helicopters".

Uses

Maples are important as sources of syrup and wood. Sugar maple (Acer saccharum) sap is boiled down to make maple syrup, principally in Canada and the northeastern United States. The hard wood of some species is prized for flooring, furniture, and musical instruments, and maples are widely planted as ornamental trees valued for their autumn colour. Several species, such as Acer palmatum (Japanese maple), are staples of horticulture, with thousands of cultivars.
