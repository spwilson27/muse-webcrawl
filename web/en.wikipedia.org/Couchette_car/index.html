Couchette car

A couchette car is a railway passenger coach offering basic sleeping accommodation with fold-down berths, positioned as an intermediate between seated accommodation and a full sleeping car. The name derives from the French verb se coucher, "to lie down".

A couchette compartment typically contains four or six berths arranged in two or three tiers. By day the compartment provides ordinary seating; at night the seat backs fold down and upper berths are lowered to form bunks, with bedding usually limited to a blanket and pillow. Washrooms are shared at the end of the coach. Couchette cars are a mainstay of European night trains, including the ÖBB Nightjet network, and were widely used on long-distance trains in the Soviet Union and its successor states.

History

Couchettes were popularised in the mid-twentieth century as railways sought a cheap overnight product between third-class seating and the Wagons-Lits sleeping car. Although many night trains were withdrawn in Western Europe in the 1990s and 2000s, couchette services have seen a revival with the growth of climate-conscious travel since the late 2010s.
