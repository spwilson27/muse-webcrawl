Residency show

A residency show is a series of concerts or performances by an artist or act performed at a single venue over an extended period, rather than as part of a tour of many cities. Concert residencies are especially associated with Las Vegas, Nevada, where casinos and theatres host long-running engagements by headline performers.

Notable venuesThe Colosseum at Caesars Palace; Park Theater; Zappos Theater
PioneersLiberace, Elvis Presley, Rat Pack era acts

History

Las Vegas residencies began in the 1940s and 1950s with performers such as Liberace and later Elvis Presley, whose 1969–1976 engagement at the International Hotel (later the Las Vegas Hilton) helped define the format. Once considered a destination for artists past their commercial peak, residencies were reinvented in the 2000s by Celine Dion's A New Day... (2003–2007) at the Colosseum at Caesars Palace, which grossed over US$385 million and proved a contemporary superstar could anchor a residency.

Format and economics

Residencies let productions use elaborate, permanently installed staging that touring shows cannot transport. Performers benefit from a fixed home base and predictable schedule, while venues gain a marquee attraction that draws visitors who also spend on hotels, dining and gaming.
