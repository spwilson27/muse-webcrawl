Mass appraisal

Mass appraisal is the process of valuing a group of properties as of a given date, using standardized procedures, statistical models, and common data. It is the method used by tax assessors to set values for property taxation, in contrast to the single-property appraisal prepared for a sale or mortgage.

Methods

Mass appraisal systems — often called CAMA (computer-assisted mass appraisal) — build valuation models from sales data, property characteristics, and location factors. Hedonic regression and other statistical techniques estimate how features such as living area, lot size, age, and condition contribute to value across a whole market area. Assessors then test accuracy and equity with ratio studies comparing appraised values to actual sale prices.

Standards and appeals

The International Association of Assessing Officers (IAAO) publishes standards for model performance, including the coefficient of dispersion and price-related differential. Because mass-appraised values are estimates, owners typically have a right to appeal individual assessments, supplying evidence that their property's value is out of line with the model or with comparable properties.
