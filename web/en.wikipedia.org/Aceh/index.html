Aceh is a province of Indonesia at the northern end of Sumatra, with Banda Aceh as its capital and largest city. It is bordered by the Indian Ocean to the west, the Strait of Malacca to the northeast, and the province of North Sumatra to the southeast. Aceh is known for its political independence in earlier centuries, its rich natural resources, and its strongly Islamic culture; it is the only Indonesian province that officially implements Sharia law.

History

The Aceh Sultanate, founded in the early 16th century, became a major regional power and a centre of Islamic scholarship and trade, at its height under Sultan Iskandar Muda in the early 17th century. The Dutch colonial war in Aceh (1873–1904 and beyond) was one of the longest and bloodiest of the Dutch East Indies campaigns. After Indonesian independence, Acehnese resentment of Jakarta led to the Darul Islam rebellion in the 1950s and later to the Free Aceh Movement (GAM) separatist insurgency from 1976.

2004 tsunami and peace

Aceh was the region closest to the epicentre of the 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and was devastated by the ensuing tsunami, which killed well over 100,000 people in the province. Reconstruction aid and the disaster's aftermath helped bring about the August 2005 Helsinki peace agreement between the Indonesian government and GAM, ending the insurgency and granting Aceh special autonomy, including its own regional political parties and implementation of Sharia.

Economy

Aceh has significant reserves of oil and natural gas, and agriculture — including coffee (notably Gayo coffee), rice, palm oil, and spices — is a mainstay of the provincial economy.
