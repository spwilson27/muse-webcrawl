Brix

Degrees Brix (symbol °Bx) is a measure of the dissolved solids in a liquid, and is commonly used to measure dissolved sugar content of an aqueous solution. One degree Brix is 1 gram of sucrose in 100 grams of solution and represents the strength of the solution as percentage by mass.

If the solution contains dissolved solids other than pure sucrose, then the °Bx only approximates the dissolved solid content. For example, when one adds equal amounts of salt and sugar to water, the °Bx refractometric changes. The scale was developed by Adolf Brix in the 19th century and is widely used in the wine, sugar, carbonated beverage, fruit juice, maple syrup and honey industries.

Measurement

Brix can be measured using a hydrometer, which infers the reading from the specific gravity of the solution, or more commonly with a refractometer, which measures the refractive index. Modern digital instruments give precise readings and compensate for temperature. Approximate relations exist between Brix, specific gravity, and the older Baumé and Plato scales.

Uses

Winemakers use Brix to estimate the potential alcohol content of wine, since sugars are converted to alcohol during fermentation. Fruit growers use it as an indicator of ripeness and quality, and the sugar industry uses it to control refining processes.
