Wu-style tai chi

FounderWu Quanyou (1834–1902)
Developed byWu Jianquan (1870–1942)
OriginBeijing, China

Wu-style tai chi (Wu-style t'ai chi ch'uan) is one of the five major family styles of tai chi, the Chinese internal martial art. It derives from the Yang style through Wu Quanyou, a Manchu imperial guard officer who studied under Yang Luchan and his son Yang Banhou, and was refined and popularized by Wu Quanyou's son Wu Jianquan.

Characteristics

Wu style is distinguished by a distinctive forward-leaning posture, compact medium-sized stances, and an emphasis on parallel footwork and upright balance through the central equilibrium. Its forms use smaller circles and subtle internal movements compared with the larger frame of Yang style, and the style is noted for sophisticated push hands (tui shou) practice.

History

Wu Jianquan taught in Beijing and later in Shanghai, where he co-founded the Jianquan Tai Chi Association in 1935. His children Wu Gongyi and Wu Yinghua, and later his granddaughter Wu Yanxia and grandson Wu Da-k'uei, spread the style through Hong Kong, Macau, and Southeast Asia. Another branch, descending from Wu Quanyou's disciple Wang Maozhai, remained in northern China.
