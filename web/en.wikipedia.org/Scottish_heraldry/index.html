Scottish heraldry

A Scottish armorial achievement with crest and motto above the helm.

Scottish heraldry is the form of coats of arms and armorial bearings used in Scotland. It is regulated by the Lord Lyon King of Arms under Scottish heraldic law and differs from English heraldry in several distinctive ways. All arms in Scotland must be recorded in the Public Register of All Arms and Bearings in Scotland, established by an Act of the Parliament of Scotland in 1672.

Distinctive features

In Scottish heraldry the crest is of special importance: it sits within a strap-and-buckle badge worn by clan members to show allegiance to their chief, while the chief alone uses the full crest. The motto is placed above the crest, unlike the English practice of placing it below the shield. Most importantly, Scottish arms are the personal property of one individual at a time: other family members must "matriculate" their arms with differences (marks of cadency), so undifferenced arms legally belong to the head of the family alone.

The Lord Lyon

The Lord Lyon King of Arms is both a royal officer and a judge of the Court of the Lord Lyon, with statutory power to grant arms, matriculate differences, and prosecute the unauthorised use of arms in Scotland — a power of enforcement unique among heraldic authorities. The heraldic executive also includes heralds and pursuivants such as Rothesay Herald and Unicorn Pursuivant.

Clan heraldry

Highland clan chiefs' arms structure much of Scottish heraldry: many families bear arms derived from their chief's, differenced appropriately. Common charges include the stag's head, the lymphad (galley), the boar's head, and the hand holding a cross crosslet associated with the Lord of the Isles. Regional styles, especially in the Highlands and Islands, preserve influences from Gaelic and Norse tradition.
