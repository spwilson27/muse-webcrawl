Postumus

Postumus

Reign: 260–269 AD

Title: Emperor of the Gallic Empire

Capital: Cologne (Colonia Agrippina)

Successor: Marcus Aurelius Marius

Marcus Cassianius Latinius Postumus was a Roman commander who founded and ruled the breakaway Gallic Empire from 260 to 269 AD, during the Crisis of the Third Century. A Batavian by birth, he governed the western provinces of Gaul, Germania, Britain and (for a time) Spain in defiance of the central government in Rome.

Postumus rose to prominence as a senior officer on the Rhine frontier under the emperor Gallienus. In 260, after a dispute over the spoils of a victory against the Juthungi — and the execution of Gallienus's young son Saloninus by troops at Cologne — the army proclaimed Postumus emperor.

Reign

Unlike many usurpers, Postumus did not march on Rome. Instead he consolidated the frontier provinces into a functioning Roman state with its own senate, consuls, praetorian guard and mint, and successfully defended the Rhine against Germanic incursions. His coinage, initially of notably fine quality, proclaimed him "Restorer of the Gauls". In 268 he defeated a bid by Gallienus's general Aureolus and survived a revolt by his own officer Laelianus at Mainz.

Death and legacy

After defeating Laelianus in 269, Postumus refused his troops permission to sack Mainz, and they murdered him. His Gallic Empire survived him by five years until Aurelian reunited it with Rome in 274. Historians regard Postumus as an able ruler whose secession was defensive and administrative rather than a bid for the whole empire.
