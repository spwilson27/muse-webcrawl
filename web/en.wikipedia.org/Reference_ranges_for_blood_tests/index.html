Reference ranges for blood tests

Reference ranges for blood tests are sets of values used by health professionals to interpret results from laboratory analyses of blood samples. A reference range is typically defined as the interval within which 95% of values from a healthy reference population fall, so about 5% of healthy people have results outside it.

Examples

Common adult reference ranges include fasting glucose of roughly 70–100 mg/dL, sodium of 135–145 mmol/L, hemoglobin of about 120–155 g/L in women and 135–175 g/L in men, and total cholesterol below 200 mg/dL. Ranges vary between laboratories and depend on the assay method, age, sex, and population studied.

Interpretation

Results outside a reference range are not by themselves diagnostic; clinicians interpret them alongside symptoms, history, and other tests. Some quantities, such as electrolytes and blood counts, are conventionally displayed in large reference tables sorted by mass and molar concentrations in textbooks and laboratory reports.
