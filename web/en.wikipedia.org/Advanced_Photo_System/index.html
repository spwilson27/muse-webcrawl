Advanced Photo System

The Advanced Photo System (APS) is a film format for still photography introduced in 1996 by Kodak, Fujifilm, Canon, Nikon, and Minolta. The 24 mm film is housed in a sealed plastic cartridge (called IX240 by the photo industry) and introduced drop-in loading, mid-roll film changes in some cameras, and magnetic/optical data exchange (IX) recording exposure information for improved prints.

Formats

APS offered three image formats selectable at exposure: H (High Definition, 16:9), C (Classic, 3:2), and P (Panoramic, 3:1). The film itself records a full-frame image; the selected aspect ratio is flagged in the data track and applied at printing.

History and decline

APS cameras ranged from point-and-shoots to SLRs, but the smaller negative gave lower image quality than 35 mm, and the rapid rise of digital photography in the early 2000s ended the format's growth. Kodak ceased APS film production in 2011. The "APS-C" designation for digital camera sensors is a legacy of the format's Classic size.
