IUPAC nomenclature of inorganic chemistry

The IUPAC nomenclature of inorganic chemistry is a systematic method of naming inorganic chemical compounds, as recommended by the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC). It is published in Nomenclature of Inorganic Chemistry, informally known as the Red Book. The most recent edition was published in 2005, succeeding the 1990 edition.

Types of nomenclature

The Red Book recognises several complementary naming systems:

  • Compositional nomenclature — names built simply from the composition of a substance, using multiplicative prefixes (mono-, di-, tri-…) and, for electropositive elements with variable charge, Stock numbers giving the oxidation state in Roman numerals, e.g. iron(III) chloride for FeCl3.
  • Substitutive nomenclature — based on parent hydrides (such as silane or phosphane) with substituents replacing hydrogen atoms, in the style of organic nomenclature.
  • Additive nomenclature — used particularly for coordination compounds, in which ligand names are added to the name of the central atom, e.g. pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride.

Key rules

The system prescribes names for elements (including systematic placeholder names for undiscovered elements), binary compounds, oxoacids and their salts, hydrides, boranes, and coordination and organometallic compounds. It regulates the ordering of elements in names and formulae — essentially by electronegativity, with the more electronegative element conventionally named last and given an "-ide" ending for binary compounds — and the use of multiplicative prefixes and locants (including the Greek-letter and κ/η conventions for ligand bonding modes in coordination chemistry).

Some retained traditional names remain sanctioned: for example water and ammonia are retained for general use, although their systematic names (oxidane and azane) are preferred within systematic nomenclature.
