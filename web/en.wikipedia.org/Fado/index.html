Fado

Fado (Portuguese: "destiny, fate") is a music genre which can be traced to the 1820s in Lisbon, Portugal, but probably has much earlier origins. Fado historian Rui Vieira Nery says the "only reliable information" about its history is that of oral tradition. The genre is characterised by mournful tunes and lyrics, often about the sea or the life of the poor, and infused with a sentiment of resignation, fate and melancholy known as saudade.

Stylistic originsPortuguese folk music
Cultural originsEarly 19th century, Lisbon, Portugal
Typical instrumentsPortuguese guitar, Spanish guitar, vocals

History

Fado emerged in the working-class districts of Lisbon, such as Alfama and Mouraria, in taverns and streets. Its first great star was Maria Severa Onofriana in the 19th century. In the 20th century, Amália Rodrigues, the "Rainha do Fado" (Queen of Fado), became its most famous performer and ambassador.

Recognition

In 2011 fado was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. A distinct style, Coimbra fado, is associated with the university city of Coimbra and is traditionally sung by men.
