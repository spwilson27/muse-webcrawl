Imre Lakatos

Imre Lakatos (Hungarian: Lakatos Imre [ˈlɒkɒtoʃ ˈimrɛ]; 9 November 1922 – 2 February 1974) was a Hungarian philosopher of mathematics and science, known for his thesis of the fallibility of mathematics and its "methodology of proofs and refutations" in its pre-axiomatic stages of development, and also for introducing the concept of the "research programme" in his methodology of scientific research programmes.

Life

Lakatos was born Imre Lipschitz in Debrecen, Hungary, in 1922, changing his surname during and after the Second World War to escape persecution and later for political reasons. He studied at the University of Debrecen and in Moscow, was active in Hungarian communist politics after the war, fell from favor, and later emigrated to England after the Hungarian Revolution of 1956. He completed his PhD at Cambridge in 1961 and taught at the London School of Economics from 1960 until his sudden death in 1974.

Philosophy of science

Lakatos sought a theory of scientific growth that would capture the history of science without violating Karl Popper's falsificationist criterion of scientific rationality. His methodology of scientific research programmes evaluates series of theories by whether they form progressive programmes (predicting novel facts) or degenerating ones (offering only post-hoc accommodations). Each programme has a "hard core" of assumptions protected by a "protective belt" of auxiliary hypotheses.

Philosophy of mathematics

His work Proofs and Refutations (published posthumously in 1976, based on his doctoral thesis) presented mathematics as a quasi-empirical, fallible enterprise driven by conjecture, proof, counterexample, and refinement, modeled on the history of Euler's polyhedron formula.
