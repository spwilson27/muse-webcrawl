Artificial island

An artificial island is an island that has been constructed by people rather than formed by natural processes. Artificial islands are usually built on existing reefs or by expanding existing islets, often through land reclamation using sand, rock and other fill, although some consist of floating structures or platforms.

History

Artificial islands have been created for millennia: examples include the crannogs of prehistoric Scotland and Ireland, the ceremonial island city of Nan Madol in Micronesia, and the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan, expanded on chinampas in Lake Texcoco. The Netherlands' polder engineering and harbour islands such as Dejima in Nagasaki Bay are early modern examples.

Modern examples

Large modern projects include airport islands such as Kansai International Airport and Hong Kong International Airport, the Palm Islands and World Islands off Dubai, and the reclaimed Flevopolder in the Netherlands — the largest artificial island by area. China has built several artificial islands on reefs in the South China Sea, a programme that has drawn international controversy. Artificial islands are also built for ports, offshore wind facilities, military bases and real-estate development.

Legal status

Under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, artificial islands do not possess the status of islands: they have no territorial sea of their own and their construction does not affect maritime delimitation. They fall under the jurisdiction of the coastal state in whose waters they are built.

Environmental concerns

Reclamation can destroy marine habitats, alter currents and sediment flows, and cause subsidence; Kansai Airport, for example, has sunk more than predicted. Projects are increasingly subject to environmental impact assessments.
