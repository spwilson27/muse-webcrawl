First Partition of Poland

The First Partition of Poland took place in 1772 as the first of three partitions that eventually ended the existence of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth in 1795. Growing powerlessness under Russian influence led Russia, the Kingdom of Prussia and the Habsburg monarchy to fix their territorial gains at the Commonwealth's expense.

Background

The Bar Confederation (1768–1772) fought Russian domination; a resulting Russo-Turkish war alarmed Austria. Frederick the Great of Prussia proposed partition as compensation.

Terms

By the treaty of 5 August 1772: Russia took Polish Livonia and eastern Belarus; Prussia took Royal Prussia (linking Brandenburg with East Prussia); Austria took Galicia. The Partition Sejm ratified the cession under duress in 1773.

Aftermath

The shock spurred reform, culminating in the Constitution of 3 May 1791, but two further partitions (1793, 1795) erased Poland from the map until 1918.
