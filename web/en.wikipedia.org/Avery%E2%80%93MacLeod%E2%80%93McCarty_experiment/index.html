Avery–MacLeod–McCarty experiment

ScientistsOswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, Maclyn McCarty
InstitutionRockefeller Institute, New York
Published1944
SubjectDNA as hereditary material

The Avery–MacLeod–McCarty experiment, reported in 1944 by Oswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, and Maclyn McCarty, demonstrated that DNA is the substance that causes bacterial transformation—the process by which one strain of bacterium can be changed by material from another. It was a landmark in establishing DNA, rather than protein, as the molecule of heredity.

Background

The work built on Frederick Griffith's 1928 "transforming principle" experiment with Streptococcus pneumoniae, in which dead virulent bacteria could transform living harmless bacteria into a virulent form. At the time, most scientists believed protein, with its greater chemical complexity, must carry genetic information.

The experiment

The team purified the transforming material from virulent bacteria and systematically eliminated candidate components. Extracts retained transforming activity after treatments that destroyed proteins, lipids, and RNA, but lost activity when treated with the DNA-destroying enzyme deoxyribonuclease. Chemical analysis showed the active fraction matched DNA.

Reception and legacy

Though initially met with skepticism—some critics argued trace protein contamination could explain the results—the experiment paved the way for the Hershey–Chase experiment of 1952 and the determination of DNA's structure in 1953.
