National parks of England and Wales

The national parks of England and Wales are thirteen protected landscapes designated under the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949 for their natural beauty, wildlife and cultural heritage, and for the recreational opportunities they offer. Unlike the publicly owned parks of many countries, most land within them is privately owned and farmed; independent National Park Authorities manage planning and conservation.

The parks

The ten English parks are the Peak District (1951, the first), Lake District, Dartmoor, North York Moors, Yorkshire Dales, Exmoor, Northumberland, The Broads, New Forest and South Downs (the newest, 2009–10). Wales has three: Snowdonia (Eryri), Pembrokeshire Coast and Brecon Beacons (Bannau Brycheiniog). Together they cover about 9.3% of England and nearly 20% of Wales.

Purposes

Park authorities pursue two statutory purposes: conserving and enhancing natural beauty, wildlife and cultural heritage, and promoting public understanding and enjoyment. Where the two conflict, the Sandford Principle gives conservation priority. The Lake District and Snowdonia receive millions of visitors a year, while the system as a whole faces debate over farming, housing affordability, visitor pressure and nature recovery.
