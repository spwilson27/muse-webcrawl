Editing

Editing is the process of selecting and preparing written, visual, audible, or cinematic material used by a person or entity to convey a message or information. The editing process can involve correction, condensation, organisation, and many other modifications performed with an intention of producing a correct, consistent, accurate and complete piece of work.

Types

In publishing, editing ranges from developmental editing (shaping a work's structure and content), through copy editing (grammar, style, consistency) and line editing, to proofreading (final error checking). The person who performs editing is called an editor. In film and video, editing is the art of assembling shots into a coherent sequence, a craft dating from the earliest cinema; in audio and music, editing involves selecting, splicing, and mixing recorded material.

Digital editing

Digital tools transformed editing: word processors made revision nearly costless, non-linear editors replaced physical film splicing, and collaborative platforms allow real-time editing by many people. Wikis such as Wikipedia are built on open editing by volunteers, with version histories recording every change. Photo and audio editing software likewise made manipulations — and disputes about authenticity — commonplace.
