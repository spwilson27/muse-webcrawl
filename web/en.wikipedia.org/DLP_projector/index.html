DLP projector

A DLP projector is a video projector that creates images using Digital Light Processing (DLP) technology, developed by Texas Instruments. The core of the system is an optical semiconductor called the Digital Micromirror Device (DMD), invented by Larry Hornbeck in 1987, which contains an array of up to millions of tiny mirrors, each corresponding to a pixel.

How it works

Each micromirror tilts thousands of times per second toward or away from the light source, reflecting light through the projection lens or onto a light absorber, producing shades of grey through pulse-width modulation. In single-chip projectors, colour is added by a spinning colour wheel placed between the lamp and the DMD; three-chip designs, used in digital cinema, split the light with a prism into red, green and blue paths, each with its own DMD.

Applications

DLP projectors are used in home theatre, business presentations, classrooms and the majority of the world's digital cinema screens. Single-chip designs can exhibit a "rainbow effect" for some viewers, an artefact of the sequential colour system.

Comparison with other technologies

Compared with LCD projectors, DLP models generally offer higher contrast and smoother images without a visible pixel grid, though modern LCD and LCoS systems have narrowed the differences.
