Princess Irina Alexandrovna of Russia

Princess Irina Alexandrovna of Russia (15 July 1895 – 26 February 1970) was the only daughter of Grand Duke Alexander Mikhailovich and Grand Duchess Xenia Alexandrovna, and a niece of Tsar Nicholas II. She married Prince Felix Yusupov, heir to one of Russia's greatest fortunes and later famous as one of the assassins of Grigori Rasputin. She spent most of her adult life in exile in France.

Princess Irina Alexandrovna, niece of Nicholas II and wife of Felix Yusupov.

Early life and marriage

Irina was born at Peterhof and grew up largely at Ai-Todor, her parents' Crimean estate. As the eldest of seven children and the Tsar's only niece by blood, she was considered a possible bride for foreign princes, but in February 1914 she married Prince Felix Yusupov at the Anichkov Palace, in the last great imperial wedding before the First World War. The couple's only child, Irina Felixovna ("Bébé"), was born in 1915.

Revolution and exile

Felix Yusupov's role in Rasputin's murder in December 1916 led to his exile to his estates, which inadvertently saved the couple from the fate of many Romanovs. In April 1919 they left Russia aboard the British battleship HMS Marlborough with other members of the imperial family. In Paris they founded the fashion house Irfé, named for Irina and Felix, and later won a notable libel suit against MGM over the film Rasputin and the Empress, whose portrayal of a character based on Irina established the practice of blanket fictional-character disclaimers in films. Irina died in Paris in 1970 and was buried at Sainte-Geneviève-des-Bois Russian Cemetery beside her husband.
