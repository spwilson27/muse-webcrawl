The International Federation of Journalists (IFJ) is the world's largest global union federation of journalists' trade unions, representing around 600,000 media workers in more than 140 countries through over 180 affiliated unions and associations. It promotes international action to defend press freedom and social justice through strong, free and independent trade unions of journalists.

History

The IFJ was founded in Paris in 1926. Its activities were disrupted by the Second World War, and after a Cold War split in 1950–52 — when many Western unions left a rival body — the IFJ was relaunched in Brussels in 1952. It later reunified with journalists' unions from the former communist bloc after 1990, becoming a genuinely global federation.

Activities

The IFJ campaigns against violence and impunity targeting journalists, publishing an annual record of journalists and media staff killed in the line of duty. It issues the IFJ International Press Card, a widely recognized credential for professional journalists, and maintains a safety fund for journalists in danger. The federation promotes ethical journalism through its Global Declaration of Principles on the Conduct of Journalists (the Bordeaux Declaration, 1954, amended 1986), and works on gender equality, authors' rights, and the employment conditions of freelance journalists. Regional groups cover Europe (through the European Federation of Journalists), Africa, Asia-Pacific, Latin America, and the Middle East.

Structure

The IFJ is governed by a congress held every three years, which elects a president and executive committee; its secretariat is in Brussels. Host cities of recent congresses include Muscat (2022). The IFJ holds consultative status with UN bodies and works alongside organizations such as UNESCO on the safety of journalists.
