Grapheme

Field: Linguistics, orthography

Notation: graphemes are conventionally written between angle brackets, e.g. ⟨a⟩

In linguistics, a grapheme is the smallest functional unit of a writing system. Graphemes are the fundamental units of written language, analogous to phonemes in spoken language. A grapheme may be a single letter, such as ⟨m⟩ or ⟨T⟩, or a combination of letters that functions as a single unit, such as the English digraph ⟨sh⟩ or the Spanish ⟨ch⟩.

Definition and notation

Graphemes are abstract units: a grapheme can have several physical realisations, called allographs, which include different typefaces, handwriting styles and the distinction between upper- and lower-case forms. For example, ⟨a⟩, ⟨A⟩ and the handwritten forms of "a" are allographs of the same grapheme in most analyses of English. By convention, graphemes are enclosed in angle brackets: ⟨...⟩, while phonemes are written between slashes and glyphs or characters between braces.

Graphemics and writing systems

The study of graphemes and writing systems is called graphemics or graphology (in one of its senses). Writing systems differ in how graphemes relate to language. In alphabetic systems, graphemes roughly correspond to phonemes, though the correspondence may be deep (as in Finnish or Spanish) or highly irregular (as in English). In syllabaries, each grapheme represents a syllable; in logographic systems, graphemes represent words or morphemes. Some scripts also use punctuation marks, spaces and other symbols as graphemes.

Grapheme clusters and digital text

In computing, particularly in the Unicode standard, a user-perceived character may consist of several code points; such a sequence is called a grapheme cluster. For example, an accented letter may be encoded as a base character plus a combining diacritic. Text-processing rules for counting characters, moving a cursor and breaking lines typically operate on grapheme clusters rather than individual code points.
