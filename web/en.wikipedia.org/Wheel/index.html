Wheel

Wheel
TypeSimple machine component
InventedLate Neolithic / early Bronze Age, ~4th millennium BC
UsesTransport, pottery, machinery, mills

A wheel is a rotating circular component intended to turn on an axle bearing. Together with the axle, the wheel is one of the key components of the wheel and axle, one of the six classical simple machines. Wheels enable heavy objects to be moved easily by facilitating movement or transportation while supporting a load, by drastically reducing friction through rolling motion.

History

The wheel was invented in the late Neolithic period, possibly in Mesopotamia or Eastern Europe; the potter's wheel predates its use for transport and dates to around 3500 BC or earlier. The oldest known evidence for wheeled vehicles — wagon depictions and the wooden Ljubljana Marshes Wheel (c. 3150 BC) — comes from the 4th millennium BC. Wheels spread across Europe and Asia, though wheeled transport was not developed in the Americas, Australia or sub-Saharan Africa before contact with Eurasia.

Early wheels were solid wooden discs; spoked wheels, appearing by around 2000 BC on war chariots, were lighter and faster; iron and later pneumatic rubber tires further improved performance.

Mechanics

By rolling rather than sliding, wheels overcome friction at the ground contact; remaining resistance is mainly rolling resistance in the bearing. In machinery, wheels appear as gears, pulleys, flywheels and turbine rotors. The wheel's combination with the axle provides mechanical advantage when force is applied to either element.
