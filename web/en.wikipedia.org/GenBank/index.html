GenBank is the NIH genetic sequence database, an annotated collection of all publicly available DNA sequences. It is maintained by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), part of the United States National Library of Medicine. GenBank is a founding member of the International Nucleotide Sequence Database Collaboration (INSDC), exchanging data daily with the European Nucleotide Archive and the DNA Data Bank of Japan so that the three archives share essentially the same content.

Contents and growth

GenBank contains sequences from tens of thousands of organisms, submitted by individual laboratories and by large-scale sequencing projects. Each record includes the sequence itself together with bibliographic and biological annotation, such as the source organism and the locations of genes and coding regions. The archive has grown exponentially, doubling roughly every 18 months for much of its history; periodic releases numbering in the hundreds of millions of records also feed the separate whole-genome shotgun (WGS) assemblies. Access is free via the NCBI website, Entrez and tools such as BLAST.

History

GenBank originated in 1979 as the Los Alamos Sequence Database, built by Walter Goad and colleagues at Los Alamos National Laboratory, and by 1982 had become GenBank under contract to the National Institutes of Health, later moving to the NCBI. Journals typically require authors to deposit sequences and cite the accession number before publication.
