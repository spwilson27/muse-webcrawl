Religion in the United States

Religion in the United States is characterised by a diversity of beliefs and practices, with Christianity historically the dominant faith and the First Amendment guaranteeing free exercise of religion while prohibiting an establishment of religion.

Demographics

In recent surveys, roughly six to seven in ten Americans identify as Christian, divided among Protestants (evangelical, mainline, and historically Black traditions) and Roman Catholics, together with smaller Mormon and Orthodox communities. Jews, Muslims, Hindus, Buddhists, and other faiths together account for several percent of the population. A rapidly growing share — around a quarter to a third of adults — is religiously unaffiliated, including atheists and agnostics.

History

Religious life in colonial America was shaped by Protestant dissenters and by established churches in several colonies; disestablishment followed independence. Waves of immigration brought Catholics, Jews, and later Muslims, Hindus, and Buddhists, while revival movements such as the Great Awakenings profoundly influenced American culture and politics.
