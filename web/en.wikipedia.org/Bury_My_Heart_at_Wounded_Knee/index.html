Bury My Heart at Wounded Knee: An Indian History of the American West is a 1970 non-fiction book by American writer Dee Brown. It documents the history of the United States' westward expansion during the late 19th century as experienced by Native Americans, drawing on tribal histories, council records, and first-hand accounts. The book covers the period from the Long Walk of the Navajo in 1864 to the Wounded Knee Massacre of 1890.

Title

The title is taken from a line in Stephen Vincent Benét's 1930 poem "American Names":

"I shall not be there. I shall rise and pass.
Bury my heart at Wounded Knee."

Wounded Knee was the site of the last major confrontation between Native Americans and the United States Army, in South Dakota in December 1890.

Content

Each chapter focuses on a particular tribe or leader — including the Navajo, Apache under Geronimo, Cheyenne, Sioux under Red Cloud, Sitting Bull and Crazy Horse, and the Nez Perce under Chief Joseph — recounting broken treaties, forced removals, and armed resistance. Brown's narrative foregrounds Native American perspectives, quoting extensively from recorded speeches and testimony.

Reception and influence

The book was a major bestseller, spending over a year on bestseller lists, and has been translated into many languages and never out of print. It is credited with transforming popular understanding of the American West and contributing to the Native American rights movement of the 1970s. An illustrated edition appeared in 2000, and a 2007 HBO television film adaptation, starring Adam Beach and Aidan Quinn, won several Emmy Awards. Some historians have criticised Brown for presentism and for occasionally loose sourcing, but the book's influence on public history is widely acknowledged.
