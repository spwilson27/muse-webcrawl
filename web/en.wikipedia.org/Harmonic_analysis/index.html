Harmonic analysis

FieldMathematics (analysis)
Key toolsFourier series, Fourier transform, group representations
ApplicationsSignal processing, PDEs, number theory, quantum mechanics, data compression

Harmonic analysis is a branch of mathematics concerned with the representation of functions and signals as the superposition of basic waves, and the study of the generalisation of Fourier series and Fourier transforms. In the past two centuries, it has become a vast subject with applications in areas as diverse as number theory, representation theory, signal processing, quantum mechanics, tidal analysis and neuroscience.

Core ideas

The basic waves are the harmonics — sinusoidal functions whose frequencies are integer multiples of a fundamental frequency. Any periodic function can be approximated by a sum of harmonics (its Fourier series), and a broad class of functions can be represented by a continuous superposition (its Fourier transform). Harmonic analysis studies the convergence of such representations, the operators associated with them, and the recovery of a function from its transform.

Abstract harmonic analysis

On locally compact topological groups, the theory generalises: Fourier analysis on groups studies functions via the unitary representations of the group, with the Peter–Weyl theorem for compact groups and Pontryagin duality for abelian groups as landmarks. This "abstract harmonic analysis" links analysis to algebra and underlies much of modern representation theory.

Applications

The fast Fourier transform is one of the most widely used algorithms in computing, enabling efficient audio and image compression (MP3, JPEG), medical imaging, solution of partial differential equations, and analysis of time series from seismology to finance. Results such as the uncertainty principle also play a foundational role in physics.
