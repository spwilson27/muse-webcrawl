Sejong (15 May 1397 – 8 April 1450), personal name Yi Do, was the fourth king of the Joseon dynasty of Korea. He is regarded as one of the greatest rulers in Korean history and is universally known as Sejong the Great. His reign saw major advances in science, technology, agriculture, literature, and medicine, and he is credited with personally creating Hangul, the Korean alphabet, promulgated in 1446 in the document Hunminjeongeum.

Reign

Sejong ascended the throne in 1418 after his father, King Taejong, abdicated in his favour. He staffed his court with talented scholars through the Hall of Worthies (Jiphyeonjeon) and promoted merit over birth. Early in his reign he strengthened Korea's northern border against Jurchen incursions and ordered the Ōei invasion of Tsushima Island in 1419 to suppress Japanese pirates.

Hangul

Finding Chinese characters ill-suited to Korean speech and literacy confined to a small elite, Sejong devised a featural alphabet of 28 letters (now 24) representing the sounds of Korean, publishing it in 1443–1446 as the Hunminjeongeum ("The Proper Sounds for the Instruction of the People"). The script faced opposition from Confucian officials but eventually became Korea's national script; its promulgation is celebrated as Hangul Day.

Science and other achievements

Sejong sponsored the astronomer Jang Yeong-sil, whose inventions included water clocks, a rain gauge, and celestial globes, and ordered works on agriculture (Nongsa jikseol), medicine, and music. His government reformed taxation, codified law, and advanced movable-type printing. In his later years, suffering from diabetes and failing eyesight, he shared duties with his son Munjong before his death in 1450.

Legacy

Sejong's portrait appears on the South Korean 10,000-won banknote, and his statue stands in Gwanghwamun Square, Seoul. Sejong City, a planned administrative capital, bears his name.
