Proclamation of the Irish Republic

The Proclamation of the Irish Republic (Irish: Forógra na Poblachta), also known as the 1916 Proclamation or the Easter Proclamation, was a document issued by the Irish Volunteers and the Irish Citizen Army during the Easter Rising in Ireland on 24 April 1916. It proclaimed Ireland's independence from the United Kingdom.

Text and principles

Addressed "in the name of God and of the dead generations", the Proclamation announced a provisional government, guaranteed "religious and civil liberty, equal rights and equal opportunities to all its citizens", and declared its resolve "to pursue the happiness and prosperity of the whole nation and of all its parts, cherishing all of the children of the nation equally". Its language of equality is often read as extending to women, unusual for its time.

Signatories and reading

The document was signed by seven members of the Military Council of the Irish Republican Brotherhood: Thomas J. Clarke, Seán Mac Diarmada, Thomas MacDonagh, P. H. Pearse, Éamonn Ceannt, James Connolly, and Joseph Plunkett. Patrick Pearse read it aloud outside the General Post Office on Sackville Street (now O'Connell Street) in Dublin at noon on Easter Monday.

Printing and survival

The Proclamation was printed in difficult conditions at Liberty Hall on a Summit Wharfdale press, in two halves because of a shortage of type. Original copies are now prized historical artefacts; one is displayed in the GPO museum. The text is read aloud at official commemorations each Easter.
