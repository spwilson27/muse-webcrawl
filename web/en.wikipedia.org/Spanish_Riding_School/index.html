Spanish Riding School

Spanish Riding School
German: Spanische Hofreitschule
Location: Hofburg, Vienna, Austria
Founded: 1572 (first mention)
Horses: Lipizzaner stallions

The Spanish Riding School (German: Spanische Hofreitschule) is an Austrian institution dedicated to the preservation of classical dressage and the training of Lipizzaner horses, based in the Hofburg in Vienna. It is one of the oldest riding academies in the world, claiming a continuous tradition of more than 450 years. Its white stallions and their performances in the baroque Winter Riding School are an emblem of Vienna.

History

The school is named for the Spanish horses that formed the foundation of the Lipizzan breed, established by Archduke Maximilian II at Lipica (Lipizza) in 1580. A wooden riding arena was first documented in 1565, and the school itself is first mentioned in 1572. The present Winter Riding School, an elegant baroque hall designed by Joseph Emanuel Fischer von Erlach, was commissioned by Emperor Charles VI and opened in 1735.

The Lipizzaners

The school uses only Lipizzaner stallions, bred at the federal stud farm at Piber in Styria. Foals are born dark and grey with age, most becoming the famous white. Training follows the classical system of the haute école, progressing through stages over years; the celebrated "airs above the ground" — the levade, courbette and capriole — derive from Renaissance military horsemanship.

Twentieth century and UNESCO

During the Second World War the stallions were evacuated, famously being protected in 1945 with the involvement of the school's director Alois Podhajsky and the US Army under General George S. Patton. In 2015, the classical horsemanship of the Spanish Riding School was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
