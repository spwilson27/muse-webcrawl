Gene Sharp, scholar of nonviolent action.

Gene Sharp (21 January 1928 – 28 January 2018) was an American political scientist best known for his writings on nonviolent struggle. He founded the Albert Einstein Institution in Boston in 1983 and held posts at the University of Massachusetts Dartmouth and Harvard's Center for International Affairs.

Work

Sharp's theory holds that political power depends on the consent and obedience of the governed; when subjects withdraw cooperation, rulers' sources of power collapse. His three-volume The Politics of Nonviolent Action (1973) catalogued 198 methods of nonviolent action — from protest and persuasion to noncooperation and intervention. His short book From Dictatorship to Democracy (1993), written for Burmese dissidents, was translated into dozens of languages and used by pro-democracy movements in Serbia, Ukraine, Egypt, and elsewhere.

Sharp was sometimes accused by governments, including Venezuela's and Iran's, of acting as an agent of US regime-change policy; he denied such ties. He was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize several times and received the Right Livelihood Award in 2012.
