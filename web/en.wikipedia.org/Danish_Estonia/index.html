Danish Estonia (Danish: Dansk Estland) refers to the territories in present-day northern Estonia ruled by the Kingdom of Denmark, chiefly from 1219 to 1346, with the island of Ösel (Saaremaa) remaining Danish until 1645.

Conquest

Danish involvement began with crusading expeditions to the eastern Baltic. In 1219 King Valdemar II of Denmark landed with a fleet near present-day Tallinn and founded a fortress after the Battle of Lyndanisse — the battle at which, according to legend, the Danish flag, the Dannebrog, fell from the sky. The Danes established the town of Reval (Tallinn) and ruled the provinces of Harria and Vironia, compiling the Liber Census Daniae, a valuable register of Estonian settlements.

End of Danish rule

After the St. George's Night Uprising of 1343, King Valdemar IV sold the Estonian duchy to the Teutonic Order for 19,000 marks in 1346, and it passed to the Livonian Order. Danish rule nevertheless left lasting marks: Tallinn's name is traditionally derived from Taani linn ("Danish town/castle"), and its coat of arms echoes the Danish arms.
