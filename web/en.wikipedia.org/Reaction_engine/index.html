Reaction engine

A reaction engine is an engine that produces thrust by expelling reaction mass, in accordance with Newton's third law of motion. Unlike engines that push against an external medium, a reaction engine carries (or collects) its working mass and accelerates it backwards to propel the vehicle forwards.

Types

The most familiar reaction engines are rockets, which carry all their propellant aboard and can operate in vacuum. Jet engines form a broader family: air-breathing turbojets, turbofans, ramjets, and scramjets ingest air as most of their reaction mass, while ducted fans and propellers accelerate external air but are conventionally classed separately. Other examples include ion thrusters, which expel ionised gas at very high exhaust velocity but low thrust, and waterjets for boats.

Performance

Thrust equals mass flow multiplied by exhaust velocity; efficiency is measured by specific impulse. The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation governs reaction engines that carry their mass with them, linking achievable velocity change to exhaust velocity and mass ratio.
