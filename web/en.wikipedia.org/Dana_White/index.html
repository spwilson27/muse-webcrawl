Dana Frederick White Jr. (born 28 July 1969) is an American businessman who serves as the chief executive officer and president of the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC), the world's largest mixed martial arts organization. Under White's stewardship, the UFC has grown from a struggling promotion bought for US$2 million in 2001 into a global sports business sold to Endeavor in 2016 for about US$4 billion.

Early life

White was born in Manchester, Connecticut, and spent much of his childhood in Las Vegas and Maine. He worked as a bellman and ran boxing aerobics classes, then became a manager for fighters including Tito Ortiz and Chuck Liddell. In that role he learned that the UFC's then-owner, Semaphore Entertainment Group, was looking to sell the promotion.

UFC career

White approached his childhood friend Lorenzo Fertitta, a casino executive, and in January 2001 the Fertittas purchased the UFC through their company Zuffa, installing White as president with a minority ownership stake. White led the campaign for sanctioning by state athletic commissions and championed the reality series The Ultimate Fighter (2005), widely credited with saving the promotion and igniting the mainstream boom of mixed martial arts. After the 2016 sale of the UFC to WME-IMG (Endeavor), White remained president; in 2023 he became CEO as the UFC was combined with WWE into TKO Group Holdings. He has also launched side ventures including Dana White's Contender Series, the slap-fighting league Power Slap, and the boxing promotion Zuffa Boxing.

Public image

White is known for a blunt, combative public style, frequent feuds with fighters, promoters and journalists, and recurring controversy over fighter pay. He is a prominent supporter of Donald Trump, speaking at Republican National Conventions and maintaining a close public friendship with the former president.
