.30-40 Krag

The .30-40 Krag, also known in United States service as the .30 Army and commercially as the .30 Government, is a rimmed centre-fire rifle cartridge adopted by the US Army in 1892 together with the Krag–Jørgensen bolt-action rifle. Its designation reflects the contemporary convention of calibre and black-powder-era charge notation: a .30 calibre bullet propelled by 40 grains of (smokeless) powder.

Service history

The cartridge served through the Spanish–American War, the Philippine–American War, and the Boxer Rebellion. Standard loading used a 220-grain round-nose bullet at about 2,000 feet per second. After the Army adopted the .30-03 and then the .30-06 Springfield, the .30-40 passed into sporting use, where it remained popular for deer and larger game, chambered in Krag rifles, Winchester Model 1895 lever actions, and single-shot rifles.

Characteristics

The rimmed case operates at relatively modest pressures, suiting it to the Krag action. Factory sporting loads typically drive 150- to 180-grain bullets at roughly 2,400–2,700 ft/s, performance comparable to other classic medium rifle cartridges of its era. It remains in production as a hunting cartridge more than a century after adoption.
