Das Rheingold

Das Rheingold (German: The Rhinegold) is the first of the four music dramas that constitute Richard Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen (The Ring of the Nibelung). It premiered as a single opera at the National Theatre Munich on 22 September 1869, and received its first performance as part of the complete Ring cycle at the Bayreuth Festspielhaus on 13 August 1876.

Conceived by Wagner as a "preliminary evening" (Vorabend) to the main trilogy, the opera lasts about two and a half hours and is performed without an interval. It sets up the central conflicts of the cycle: the theft of the Rhine gold, the forging of the all-powerful ring, and the curse placed upon it.

Synopsis

In the depths of the Rhine, the dwarf Alberich renounces love and steals the gold guarded by the Rhinemaidens, forging from it a ring that grants world dominion. Meanwhile Wotan, chief of the gods, has promised the goddess Freia to the giants Fasolt and Fafner as payment for building Valhalla. To ransom her, Wotan and Loge capture Alberich and seize his hoard, including the ring, which Alberich curses. Fafner kills Fasolt over the treasure — the curse's first victim — and the gods cross a rainbow bridge into Valhalla as the Rhinemaidens lament their lost gold.

Music

The opera opens with a famous 136-bar prelude built on an E-flat major arpeggio depicting the primal flow of the Rhine. Wagner's system of leitmotifs — musical themes associated with characters, objects, and ideas — is already fully operative, including motifs for the ring, Valhalla, and the sword.
