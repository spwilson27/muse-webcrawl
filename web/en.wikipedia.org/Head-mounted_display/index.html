Head-mounted display

A head-mounted display (HMD) is a display device, worn on the head or as part of a helmet, that has a small display optic in front of one (monocular HMD) or each eye (binocular HMD). An HMD has many uses, including in virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), aviation, engineering, medicine, and entertainment. Head-mounted displays are the primary components of virtual reality headsets.

Types and technology

HMDs typically use miniature LCD or OLED microdisplays viewed through magnifying optics that place a wide-field image apparently at a comfortable distance. Optical see-through HMDs use transparent combiners or waveguides to overlay imagery on the real world (as in Microsoft HoloLens and Google Glass), while video see-through designs capture the world with cameras and display it on opaque screens (as in most VR headsets). Modern consumer devices such as the Meta Quest series, HTC Vive, PlayStation VR, and Apple Vision Pro combine the displays with inside-out positional tracking using cameras and inertial sensors, offering six degrees of freedom of head motion.

Key technical parameters include field of view, resolution per eye, refresh rate, latency between head motion and the updated image (motion-to-photon latency; high values cause simulator sickness), and interpupillary distance adjustment. Military helmet-mounted displays, such as those integrated with the F-35 fighter helmet, project flight and targeting information onto the visor and allow pilots to "look through" the airframe using external cameras.

History

Precursors include the stereoscope and Edward Link's flight simulator displays. Ivan Sutherland created the first computer-driven HMD, the "Sword of Damocles" (1968), with head tracking. Military and NASA research sustained development through the 1970s and 1980s, and the term "virtual reality" was popularized by Jaron Lanier's VPL Research, which sold commercial HMDs in the 1980s. Consumer interest surged after the 2012 Oculus Rift Kickstarter campaign, leading to a mass market for VR and mixed-reality headsets in the 2010s and 2020s.
