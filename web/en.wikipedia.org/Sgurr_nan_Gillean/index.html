Sgurr nan Gillean

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sgurr nan Gillean is a mountain in the northern section of the Cuillin range on the Isle of Skye in Scotland. With a height of 966.1 metres (3,169 ft), it is one of eleven Munros in the Cuillin and one of the most recognisable peaks on Skye, its sharp, pyramidal profile dominating views from Sligachan and the road to Portree. The name is Scottish Gaelic, commonly rendered "peak of the young men" (or "of the gillies").

Geology and setting

Like the rest of the Black Cuillin, the mountain is composed largely of gabbro, an exceptionally rough and grippy rock that also carries magnetic anomalies capable of disturbing compasses. Sgurr nan Gillean stands at the head of Glen Sligachan, ringed by the subsidiary tops of Sgurr Beag and Sgurr na h-Uamha and connected by ridge to Am Basteir and the Basteir Tooth.

Ascent

The usual route starts at the Sligachan Hotel, crosses the glen, and climbs the south-east ridge — a long, increasingly rocky scramble graded among the easier Cuillin Munro ascents, though still exposed near the summit, where the ridge narrows and the "window" and summit pinnacles are negotiated. The Tourist Route was pioneered in the era of Forbes and the early Skye guides; the first recorded ascent was by Professor James Forbes in 1836. Walkers are warned that mist, magnetic rock, and the complex ridge system make navigation demanding.
