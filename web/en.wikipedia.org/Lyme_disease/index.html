Lyme disease

Lyme disease, also known as Lyme borreliosis, is a tick-borne infectious disease caused by bacteria of the Borrelia genus, principally Borrelia burgdorferi in North America, transmitted by black-legged ticks (Ixodes scapularis and I. pacificus).

Signs and symptoms

The earliest sign, in about 70–80% of cases, is erythema migrans, an expanding red rash often resembling a bull's-eye at the bite site, accompanied by fever, headache, and fatigue. Untreated infection can spread to joints, heart, and nervous system, causing arthritis, carditis, facial palsy, and meningitis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is clinical in early disease with characteristic rash; later stages use serological testing. Early Lyme disease is treated with doxycycline; later manifestations may require ceftriaxone. Most patients recover fully, though some report persistent symptoms.

Epidemiology and prevention

Lyme is the most common vector-borne disease in the United States, with tens of thousands of reported cases annually, concentrated in the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic, and upper Midwest. Prevention includes protective clothing, repellents, and prompt tick removal.
