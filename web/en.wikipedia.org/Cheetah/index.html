The cheetah (Acinonyx jubatus) is a large cat native to Africa and central Iran. It is the fastest land animal, capable of reaching speeds of 80 to 98 km/h (50 to 61 mph) in short bursts while chasing prey, with exceptional acceleration and manoeuvrability. Its lightly built, long-legged body, semi-retractable claws, flexible spine and long tail are all adaptations for high-speed pursuit.

Characteristics

The cheetah has a tawny to creamy-white coat marked with round black spots and characteristic black "tear marks" running from the eyes to the mouth. Unlike other big cats, it hunts mainly by sight during the day, stalking prey such as Thomson's gazelles and other small-to-medium antelopes before a short explosive chase. It cannot roar, but vocalises with purrs, chirps and hisses.

Distribution and conservation

Cheetahs once ranged across Africa and much of Asia but have lost most of their historic range. The African population is scattered across eastern and southern Africa, while the critically endangered Asiatic cheetah survives only in Iran, with a small population reintroduced to India in 2022. Threats include habitat loss, conflict with farmers, prey depletion and the illegal pet trade. The species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN, and is noted for its unusually low genetic diversity, the legacy of population bottlenecks at the end of the last ice age.
