National Book Award

The National Book Awards are a set of annual U.S. literary awards administered by the National Book Foundation. Established in 1950, they are among the most prestigious literary prizes in the United States.

Categories

Awards are currently given in five categories: Fiction, Nonfiction, Poetry, Translated Literature, and Young People's Literature. Winners are selected by panels of five judges for each category, drawn from writers and literary professionals.

History

The awards were founded by a consortium of publishing groups. In 1980 they were briefly restructured as the American Book Awards with many more categories before returning to the National Book Award name and a smaller format in the following years.

Notable winners

Fiction winners have included William Faulkner, Ralph Ellison (Invisible Man), and Jesmyn Ward. The Foundation also presents a Medal for Distinguished Contribution to American Letters.
