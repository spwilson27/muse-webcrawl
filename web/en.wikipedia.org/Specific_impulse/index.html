Specific impulse

Specific impulse (usually abbreviated Isp) is a measure of how efficiently a reaction mass engine, such as a rocket using propellant or a jet engine, creates thrust. For engines whose reaction mass is only the fuel they carry, specific impulse is exactly proportional to exhaust velocity.

Specific impulse
Common symbolsIsp
SI unitmetre per second (m/s)
Other unitssecond (s)
Dimensionvelocity or time

Definition

Specific impulse is defined as the thrust produced divided by the mass flow rate of propellant (multiplied, in the "seconds" convention, by standard gravity g0 = 9.80665 m/s²). When expressed in seconds, it equals the exhaust velocity divided by g0. Typical values range from about 250 s for solid rocket motors to roughly 450 s for liquid hydrogen/oxygen engines, while ion thrusters can exceed 3,000 s at very low thrust.

Role in rocketry

Through the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation, the change in velocity a rocket can achieve grows exponentially with the ratio of initial to final mass, with specific impulse as the proportionality factor. Higher specific impulse therefore means a spacecraft can carry more payload or reach more distant destinations with the same propellant mass.

Air-breathing engines

For jet engines, which take in atmospheric air, specific impulse is often defined in terms of fuel consumption only, yielding much larger values — turbofans can exceed 6,000 s — because the reaction mass includes air not carried aboard.
