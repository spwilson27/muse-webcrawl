Seismic analysis

Seismic analysis is a branch of structural analysis concerned with calculating the response of buildings, bridges and other structures to earthquakes. It is a central part of earthquake engineering, providing the loads and deformations against which structures in seismically active regions are designed under codes such as ASCE 7, Eurocode 8 and national standards worldwide.

Methods

The simplest procedure is the equivalent lateral force method, which represents earthquake action as a static horizontal force proportional to the building's weight and a seismic coefficient that depends on the site's hazard, soil and the structure's period and ductility. Response spectrum analysis models the structure's modes of vibration and combines their peak responses — read from a design response spectrum — statistically, using rules such as SRSS or CQC. Time-history analysis applies recorded or simulated ground-motion accelerograms directly and integrates the equations of motion step by step; in its nonlinear form it can capture yielding, degradation and collapse, but demands careful selection and scaling of records. Pushover analysis applies monotonically increasing lateral loads to a nonlinear model to estimate capacity and target displacement in performance-based design.

Key concepts

Analysis rests on the natural periods and mode shapes of the structure, its mass and damping, and the characterisation of ground motion through peak ground acceleration and spectral acceleration. Modern practice frames results probabilistically: hazard curves give the annual frequency of exceeding ground-motion levels, and design aims to limit damage in frequent events while preventing collapse in rare ones.
