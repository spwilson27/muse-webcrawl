The Stone Roses were an English rock band formed in Manchester in 1983. One of the pioneering groups of the Madchester movement in the late 1980s and early 1990s, the band's classic and most prominent lineup consisted of vocalist Ian Brown, guitarist John Squire, bassist Mani, and drummer Reni. Their eponymous debut album (1989) is widely considered one of the greatest British albums ever made.

History

After building a following with singles such as "Sally Cinnamon" and "Elephant Stone," the band released The Stone Roses in 1989 on Silvertone Records, featuring songs including "I Wanna Be Adored," "She Bangs the Drums," "Waterfall," and the climactic "I Am the Resurrection." In May 1990 they headlined a landmark open-air concert at Spike Island, Widnes, before 27,000 fans. A protracted legal battle with Silvertone stalled their career; the second album, Second Coming (1994), arrived on Geffen to mixed reviews. Reni left in 1995 and Squire in 1996; the band dissolved after a disastrous Reading Festival performance that year.

Reunion

The classic lineup reunited in 2011, playing stadium shows including three nights at Manchester's Heaton Park, and released two new singles, "All for One" and "Beautiful Thing," in 2016. Brown later indicated the band had split again in 2017. Bassist Mani (Gary Mounfield) died in 2025.

Legacy

The Stone Roses profoundly influenced Britpop acts such as Oasis, and their fusion of indie rock, psychedelia, and dance rhythms helped define the sound and style — baggy clothing, bucket hats — of a generation of British music.
