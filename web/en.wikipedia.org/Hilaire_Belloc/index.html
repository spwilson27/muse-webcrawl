Hilaire Belloc

Joseph Hilaire Pierre René Belloc
Born: 27 July 1870, La Celle-Saint-Cloud, France
Died: 16 July 1953 (aged 82), Guildford, England
Occupation: Writer, historian, poet, politician
Nationality: French-born British

Joseph Hilaire Pierre René Belloc (27 July 1870 – 16 July 1953) was a French-English writer, historian, and one of the most prolific authors in England during the early 20th century. A prominent Catholic apologist, he was President of the Oxford Union and a Member of Parliament for Salford South from 1906 to 1910. Belloc, together with G. K. Chesterton, formed a famous literary partnership — George Bernard Shaw dubbed them the "Chesterbelloc".

Works

Belloc wrote over 150 books, including histories and biographies, travel writing such as The Path to Rome (1902), the novel Mr Burden, the political-economic work The Servile State (1912), which influenced distributism; Europe and the Faith (1920), with its famous epigram "the Faith is Europe and Europe is the Faith"; and the beloved children's verse Cautionary Tales for Children (1907), including "Jim, who ran away from his Nurse, and was eaten by a Lion" and the tale of Matilda "who told lies and was burned to death". His light verse and his histories of England and France remained widely read for decades. Belloc's writings also include antisemitic themes that have been widely criticised, notably in The Jews (1922).
