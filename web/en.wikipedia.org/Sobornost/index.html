Sobornost

Sobornost
MeaningSpiritual community / conciliar unity
TraditionEastern Orthodox theology; Slavophilism
Key thinkerAleksey Khomyakov

Sobornost (Russian: соборность) is a theological and philosophical term in Russian thought denoting the spiritual unity of a community — a free association of persons united by love and faith rather than by external authority or legal compulsion. It is derived from sobor, meaning council or assembly, and is closely related to the word for cathedral.

Origins

The concept was developed by the Slavophile thinker Aleksey Khomyakov (1804–1860), who contrasted the organic unity of the Orthodox Church with what he saw as the external legalism of Roman Catholicism and the individualism of Protestantism. In Khomyakov's ecclesiology, the Church is united "in love" and knows the truth corporately, not through a single infallible office.

Development

Sobornost influenced later Russian religious philosophy, including Vladimir Solovyov, Nikolai Berdyaev, and Sergei Bulgakov, and was sometimes extended from ecclesiology to social and political ideals of communal life. Critics have noted that the term can be vague and has been invoked both for conciliar democracy within the church and as a quasi-nationalist slogan.

Related ideas

The term parallels the Greek notion of catholicity (katholikotēs) and the conciliar practice of the early church. In contemporary Orthodox theology, sobornost is frequently cited in discussions of synodality and church governance.
