Chadic languages

FamilyAfroasiatic
RegionSahel: Nigeria, Niger, Chad, Cameroon
Number of languagesNearly 200
Largest languageHausa

The Chadic languages are a branch of the Afroasiatic language family spoken across the Sahel belt of west and central Africa, in northern Nigeria, Niger, Chad and northern Cameroon. The branch contains nearly 200 languages, most with small numbers of speakers.

Classification

Chadic is conventionally divided into four subgroups: West Chadic, Biu–Mandara (Central Chadic), East Chadic and Masa. By far the most widely spoken Chadic language is Hausa, a West Chadic language used as a lingua franca by tens of millions across Nigeria and Niger; other significant languages include Ngambay's relatives in the east and the many small languages of the Mandara Mountains.

Features

Many Chadic languages are tonal and preserve consonant features typical of Afroasiatic, such as implosives and lateral fricatives. Because most are spoken by small communities and are little documented, a large proportion are considered endangered.
