Family Compact

The Family Compact was a small closed group of men who exercised most of the political, economic and judicial power in Upper Canada (modern Ontario) from the 1810s to the 1840s. It was noted for its Tory and anti-American views and for its political conservatism.

Membership and influence

The Compact centred on figures such as John Strachan, the influential Anglican bishop, and included senior officials, judges, bankers, and members of the Executive and Legislative Councils linked by family, business, and patronage. Centred in York (Toronto), it controlled land grants, the Bank of Upper Canada, and appointments, and promoted the interests of the Church of England through the clergy reserves.

Opposition and decline

Reformers led by William Lyon Mackenzie denounced the Compact's monopoly, and grievances against it helped provoke the Upper Canada Rebellion of 1837. Its dominance faded with the union of the Canadas in 1841 and the advent of responsible government. The name echoed the earlier Château Clique in Lower Canada.
