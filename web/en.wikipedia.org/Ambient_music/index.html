Ambient music is a genre of music that emphasises tone and atmosphere over traditional musical structure or rhythm. A form of slow instrumental music, it is often said to evoke an "atmospheric", "visual", or "unobtrusive" quality. The genre is commonly traced to the English musician Brian Eno, who popularised the term with his 1978 album Ambient 1: Music for Airports, and who wrote in its liner notes that ambient music "must be as ignorable as it is interesting."

History

Early antecedents include Erik Satie's musique d'ameublement ("furniture music") of 1917, intended to be heard without being listened to, and the minimalist drones of La Monte Young and Terry Riley in the 1960s. In the 1970s, German kosmische musik artists such as Tangerine Dream and Popol Vuh, the Japanese composer Hiroshi Yoshimura's kankyō ongaku ("environmental music"), and Eno's own work with Robert Fripp developed the style further.

Eno coined "ambient music" to distinguish such works from the background music piped into commercial spaces. In the late 1980s and 1990s, UK acts such as The Orb, Aphex Twin (with Selected Ambient Works Volume II, 1994), Boards of Canada, and Biosphere carried ambient into rave culture's chill-out rooms and into the home-listening mainstream. The 2010s saw a streaming-era revival, with ambient playlists marketed for focus, sleep, and relaxation.

Characteristics

Ambient music typically features long, sustained tones, slowly evolving textures, tape loops, and sparse or absent melody and rhythm. Field recordings, piano, strings, and synthesised pads are common. Although generally quiet, the genre also includes loud drone-based works. Subgenres include dark ambient, ambient techno, psybient, lowercase, and isolationism.

Influence

Ambient techniques have been absorbed into film scoring, video game soundtracks, and wellness audio, and the genre remains a significant part of electronic music culture.
