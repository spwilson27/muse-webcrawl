Erdos–Szekeres theorem

The Erdos–Szekeres theorem (commonly written Erdős–Szekeres theorem) is a result in Ramsey theory proved by Paul Erdős and George Szekeres in 1935. It gives a precise bound on the size of a sequence that forces the existence of a long monotone subsequence.

Statement

For positive integers r and s, any sequence of distinct real numbers of length (r − 1)(s − 1) + 1 contains a monotonically increasing subsequence of length r or a monotonically decreasing subsequence of length s. Equivalently, the Ramsey-type number for monotone subsequences is N(r, s) = (r − 1)(s − 1) + 1, and this bound is tight: sequences of length (r − 1)(s − 1) exist with no such subsequence.

Proof ideas

One proof assigns to each term the pair (a, b), where a is the length of the longest increasing subsequence ending at that term and b the length of the longest decreasing one; distinct terms receive distinct pairs, so with more than (r − 1)(s − 1) terms some pair must exceed the bounds. The result is regarded as one of the founding results of Ramsey theory, and it also motivated the "happy ending problem" about points in general position forming convex polygons.
