Ted Turner

Robert Edward Turner III (born November 19, 1938) is an American entrepreneur, television producer, media proprietor, and philanthropist. As a businessman he founded the Cable News Network (CNN), the first 24-hour cable news channel, and the Turner Broadcasting System, which later merged with Time Warner. He also founded WTBS, whose satellite "superstation" signal helped pioneer cable television, and channels including TNT, TBS, and Cartoon Network. Known for his outspokenness, he was nicknamed "the Mouth of the South".

Career

Turner took over his father's billboard business after his father's suicide in 1963 and expanded into radio and television, buying a failing Atlanta UHF station in 1970. He bought the Atlanta Braves (1976) and Atlanta Hawks (1977) to supply programming, beamed his station — renamed WTBS — to cable systems nationwide by satellite in 1976, and on 1 June 1980 launched CNN, a bet widely derided at the time ("the Chicken Noodle Network"). He also created the Goodwill Games in 1986 as a Cold War sporting bridge, and in 1986 bought the MGM/UA film library, seeding TNT and Turner Classic Movies.

Turner Broadcasting merged with Time Warner in 1996, making Turner the company's largest shareholder and vice chairman; the subsequent AOL merger of 2001 badly eroded his fortune, and he left the board in 2006. As a yachtsman, Turner won the America's Cup in 1977 skippering Courageous. He was for a time the largest private landowner in the United States, with around two million acres devoted substantially to bison ranching and conservation, and founded the Ted's Montana Grill restaurant chain.

Philanthropy

In 1997 Turner pledged US$1 billion to the United Nations Foundation, which he founded to support UN causes — one of the largest charitable gifts then made. Through the Turner Foundation and the Nuclear Threat Initiative (co-founded with Sam Nunn) he has funded environmental causes, population issues, and nuclear disarmament; he is also a prominent supporter of the Giving Pledge.
