Nordic Passport Union

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nordic Passport Union is an arrangement between the Nordic countries — Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden, including the Faroe Islands — that allows their citizens to travel between the member states without a passport and to reside and work in any member state without residence or work permits.

History

Passport requirements between the Nordic countries were abolished in steps during the 1950s: the union took effect in 1954 (with Finland joining the passport-freedom arrangements by 1966) alongside the common Nordic labour market established the same year. The union predates and partly anticipated the Schengen Area; when the Nordic EU members and later Norway and Iceland joined Schengen, the passport union was retained within the Schengen framework.

Scope

Citizens of the member states may in principle travel within the union using any acceptable proof of identity, and in practice often none at all for overland travel, though carriers may require identification. Greenland and Svalbard are outside parts of the arrangement, and temporary border controls reintroduced during the 2015 migration situation and afterwards have qualified the freedom in practice.
