Air medical services

Air medical services are the use of aircraft — helicopters and fixed-wing aeroplanes — to transport patients and provide emergency medical care, including scene response, inter-hospital transfer, and the transport of organs and medical teams. Providers commonly operate as air ambulances or MEDEVAC services in military usage.

Operations

Helicopter emergency medical services (HEMS) typically carry a pilot, a flight paramedic, and a flight nurse, and sometimes a physician, delivering advanced life support at trauma scenes and rapid transport to trauma centres. Fixed-wing air ambulances cover longer distances and can fly in weather that grounds helicopters.

History

Air evacuation began with balloon and aeroplane experiments in the First World War, expanded greatly with helicopter casualty evacuation in the Korean and Vietnam Wars, and passed into civilian trauma systems in the 1970s, notably with programmes such as Germany's Christoph network and hospital-based services in the United States.

Considerations

Air transport is faster but far more expensive than ground ambulances, and its benefit is debated for short distances; safety, weather minimums, and appropriate triage are central concerns for operators and regulators.
