Michael VIII Palaiologos (Greek: Μιχαὴλ Δούκας Ἄγγελος Κομνηνὸς Παλαιολόγος; 1223/24 – 11 December 1282) was the founder of the Palaiologan dynasty, the last ruling family of the Byzantine Empire. Ruling from 1259 (at first as co-emperor and regent of the Empire of Nicaea), he recovered Constantinople from the Latins on 25 July 1261, restoring the Byzantine Empire after 57 years of exile, and reigned until his death in 1282.

Rise to power

A brilliant and ambitious general of aristocratic birth, Michael rose under the Laskarid emperors of Nicaea. After the death of Theodore II Laskaris in 1258 he manoeuvred against the regent George Mouzalon — who was murdered by troops — and became regent, then co-emperor (crowned 1 January 1259), for the child emperor John IV. His victory over a coalition of Epirotes, Sicilians, and Achaeans at the Battle of Pelagonia in 1259 secured Nicaea's position.

Recovery of Constantinople and usurpation

In July 1261 Michael's general Alexios Strategopoulos, finding Constantinople nearly undefended, retook the city almost by accident; Michael entered in triumph on 15 August 1261 and was crowned again in Hagia Sophia. He then had the young John IV blinded and confined — an act that brought his excommunication by Patriarch Arsenios and a lasting schism (the Arsenite schism) in the church.

Reign

Michael rebuilt Constantinople and its defences, restored its population, and pursued an energetic diplomacy in the West. To avert a new crusade by Charles of Anjou he negotiated the Union of Lyons (1274), a formal submission of the Greek church to Rome that was widely hated at home and eventually repudiated after his death. His diplomacy helped encourage the Sicilian Vespers of 1282, which destroyed Charles's plans. In the east, however, he neglected the Anatolian frontier in favour of European concerns, allowing Turkish beyliks to advance — a fateful choice for the empire's future. He died on campaign in Thrace in December 1282, and, because of the Union, was denied a full imperial funeral.
