BALCO scandal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The BALCO scandal was a major sports-doping affair centred on the Bay Area Laboratory Co-operative (BALCO), a California company founded by Victor Conte. From the late 1990s until a federal raid in 2003, BALCO supplied elite athletes in baseball, track and field and other sports with performance-enhancing drugs, most famously the designer steroid tetrahydrogestrinone (THG), known as "the clear," which existing tests could not detect.

Investigation

In 2003 an anonymous coach — later identified as Trevor Graham — sent a syringe containing THG to the U.S. Anti-Doping Agency, enabling a test that implicated numerous athletes. A federal investigation led by IRS agent Jeff Novitzky raided BALCO's offices, and a grand jury heard testimony from baseball stars including Barry Bonds and Jason Giambi. Conte pleaded guilty to conspiracy and money laundering in 2005 and served prison time; Bonds was later convicted of obstruction of justice (a conviction overturned on appeal in 2015).

Impact

The scandal implicated sprinter Marion Jones, who admitted steroid use in 2007, surrendered her Olympic medals and served a prison sentence for perjury. It pushed Major League Baseball to strengthen drug testing, fuelled public suspicion of the era's home-run records, and became a central episode in the debate over doping in professional sport. The reporting of San Francisco Chronicle journalists Mark Fainaru-Wada and Lance Williams, in the book Game of Shadows, chronicled the affair.
