Xianxia (simplified Chinese: 仙侠; traditional Chinese: 仙俠; pinyin: xiānxiá; lit. 'immortal heroes') is a genre of Chinese fantasy influenced by Chinese mythology, Taoism, Buddhism, Chinese martial arts, traditional Chinese medicine, Chinese folk religion and other traditional elements. It is closely related to, and sometimes confused with, wuxia, but generally contains more overt supernatural elements.

Characteristics

Protagonists in xianxia are usually "cultivators" (修者 xiūzhě, 修士 xiūshì, or 修仙者 xiūxiānzhě) who seek to cultivate their internal energy, known as qi, through meditation, martial practice, alchemy and spiritual discipline in order to become xian — immortal beings of immense power. Stories commonly feature demons, ghosts, monsters, magical treasures, flying swords, celestial bureaucracies and multiple realms of existence, and often draw on Taoist ideas of self-transcendence and balance.

Cultivation typically proceeds through ranked stages, and characters may ascend from a mortal world to higher planes of existence. Conflict often centres on sects, clans and rivalries over resources, techniques and heavenly mandates.

History

The genre's roots lie in classical Chinese tales of immortals and in Republican-era martial arts writing. Shushan swordsman fiction of the 1930s, notably Huanzhulouzhu's Legend of the Swordsmen of the Mountains of Shu, is often cited as a formative work. The modern genre crystallised on Chinese web-novel platforms such as Qidian in the 2000s, with works like Zhu Xian and Mortal's Journey to Immortality popularising its conventions.

In other media

Xianxia has spread into television dramas, films, animation (donghua), manhua and video games, and translations on platforms such as Wuxiaworld have brought it a substantial international readership. English-language usage sometimes applies "xianxia" loosely to high-fantasy cultivation stories generally, though purists distinguish it from the more Western-influenced xuanhuan genre.
