Alice Springs

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Location: Northern Territory, Australia

Established: 1872 (telegraph station); town proclaimed 1933

Population: approx. 25,000

Arrernte name: Mparntwe

Alice Springs (Arrernte: Mparntwe) is a town in the southern Northern Territory of Australia, close to the geographical centre of the country. It is the third-largest town in the Territory and the principal service centre for the vast Central Australian outback, lying on the Todd River between the East and West MacDonnell Ranges.

History

The area is the traditional land of the Arrernte people, whose connection to Mparntwe stretches back tens of thousands of years. Europeans arrived with the construction of the Overland Telegraph Line linking Adelaide to Darwin; a repeater station was built in 1872 beside a waterhole in the Todd River, named Alice Springs after Alice Todd, wife of telegraph superintendent Charles Todd. The town that grew up nearby was originally called Stuart, and was renamed Alice Springs in 1933. Gold discoveries at Arltunga in the 1880s and the arrival of the railway from Adelaide in 1929 spurred growth; Afghan cameleers were central to transport in the early decades.

Geography and climate

Alice Springs has a hot desert climate, with summer temperatures regularly exceeding 40 °C and cold winter nights that can fall below freezing. Rainfall is low and highly variable, and the usually dry Todd River occasionally floods. The town sits near Heavitree Gap, the pass through which the river, road and railway enter from the south.

Economy and attractions

Tourism, government services and support for surrounding Aboriginal communities and cattle stations drive the local economy. The town is a base for visiting Uluru–Kata Tjuta and Kings Canyon national parks. Landmarks include the historic Telegraph Station, Anzac Hill, the Royal Flying Doctor Service base and museum, the School of the Air, and the Araluen Cultural Precinct. Nearby, the Pine Gap joint defence facility is operated by Australia and the United States. Events include the Henley-on-Todd Regatta, a dry-riverbed boat race, and the Finke Desert Race.
