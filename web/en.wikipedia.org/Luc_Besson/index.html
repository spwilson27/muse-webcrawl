Luc Paul Maurice Besson (born 18 March 1959) is a French film director, screenwriter and producer. One of the leading figures of the Cinéma du look movement of the 1980s, he is known for visually flamboyant, internationally successful French films, many of them in English. He founded the production company EuropaCorp and the Cité du Cinéma studio complex near Paris.

Early life

Besson was born in Paris to parents who worked as scuba-diving instructors for Club Med, and spent much of his childhood in seaside resorts around the Mediterranean. He hoped to become a marine biologist, but a diving accident at 17 ended that ambition, and he turned to cinema, working as an assistant director in France and the United States.

Career

His first feature, the post-apocalyptic Le Dernier Combat (1983), won awards at festivals. Subway (1985) established his reputation; The Big Blue (1988), about free-diver Jacques Mayol, became a cult hit in France. La Femme Nikita (1990) was an international success and was remade in Hollywood. Léon: The Professional (1994), starring Jean Reno and a young Natalie Portman, is among his most acclaimed works. The Fifth Element (1997), with Bruce Willis and Milla Jovovich, was for years the most expensive European film ever made and a worldwide hit; Besson won the César Award for Best Director for it.

Producer and EuropaCorp

Through EuropaCorp, founded in 1999, Besson wrote and produced numerous action franchises including Taxi, The Transporter and Taken. As director he later made The Lady (2011), Lucy (2014) — which grossed over $460 million worldwide — and the ambitious space opera Valerian and the City of a Thousand Planets (2017), whose commercial underperformance contributed to EuropaCorp's financial difficulties. He subsequently directed Anna (2019) and Dogman (2023).
