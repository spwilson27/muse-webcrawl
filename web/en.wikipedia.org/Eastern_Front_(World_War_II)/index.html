Eastern Front (World War II)

The Eastern Front was a theatre of World War II fought between the European Axis powers and Finland against the Soviet Union and Poland, extending from June 1941 to May 1945 over the vast territories of Eastern Europe. It was by far the largest and deadliest land theatre of the war. The conflict began with Operation Barbarossa, Germany's invasion of the USSR on 22 June 1941, breaking the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact.

Background

Nazi ideology proclaimed the need for Lebensraum in the East and the destruction of "Judeo-Bolshevism". Stalin, having purged the Red Army officer corps in the late 1930s, relied on the 1939 non-aggression pact to buy time; Germany conquered Poland (1939), France (1940), and the Balkans, then turned east.

Barbarossa and the advance on Moscow

Over three million Axis troops invaded in three army groups: North toward Leningrad, Centre toward Moscow, and South toward Ukraine. Encirclements at Białystok–Minsk, Smolensk, and Kyiv captured millions of Soviet prisoners, but Soviet resistance, winter weather, and Hitler's diversions saved Moscow (Battle of Moscow, October 1941 – January 1942). Leningrad endured a siege of 872 days. In 1942 the German offensive (Case Blue) drove toward the Caucasus oilfields and the Volga.

Stalingrad and Kursk

The Battle of Stalingrad (August 1942 – February 1943) ended with the encirclement and destruction of the German Sixth Army—the war's decisive turning point in the east. At Kursk in July 1943, the largest tank battle in history, the Red Army repelled the German summer offensive; thereafter the strategic initiative remained Soviet.

Bagration and the advance to Berlin

Operation Bagration (June 1944) annihilated German Army Group Centre in Belorussia, the Wehrmacht's worst defeat of the war. Soviet offensives liberated the Balkans, Poland, and eastern Germany. The Battle of Berlin (April–May 1945) culminated in the Reichstag's capture; Hitler's suicide on 30 April and Germany's surrender (7–9 May 1945) ended the war in Europe. In the USSR and Russia the war is commemorated as the Great Patriotic War.

Casualties and occupation

The Eastern Front accounted for the great majority of German military deaths (roughly four of every five German soldiers killed in combat died there) and catastrophic Soviet losses: about 8.7 million Soviet military dead and 13–14 million civilian deaths, including victims of Nazi occupation policies, the Hunger Plan, and the Holocaust by bullets. Total deaths on the front are estimated at 30 million or more.
