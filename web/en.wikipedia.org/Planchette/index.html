Planchette

Planchette
[ Image: Heart-shaped planchette with pencil holder ]
TypeDivination / spirit-communication device
OriginFrance, c. 1853
MaterialsWood, casters or wheels, pencil (early form)
RelatedOuija board, automatic writing

A planchette (French for "little plank") is a small, usually heart-shaped board or platform, supported by two casters and a vertical pencil-holder at its tip, designed to move or write messages in spirit-communication practices such as séances and automatic writing. In its later and now most familiar form—without the pencil—the planchette serves as a movable pointer for a Ouija or talking board, indicating letters and words on the board's surface through a small window or pointer.

History

The planchette emerged in Paris around 1853 amid the European craze for table-turning and Spiritualism imported from the United States. The original device held a pencil so that the spirits were thought to write out messages directly as participants rested their fingers lightly on the board. Planchettes quickly became popular parlour novelties across France, Britain, and America during the 1850s and 1860s.

Talking boards

In the late 1880s and 1890s, American manufacturers combined the planchette with a board printed with the alphabet, numbers, and the words "yes," "no," and "good bye." The Kennard Novelty Company's patented board of 1891—the Ouija—popularized this format, and the pencil was dropped in favour of a viewing window in the planchette. The rights passed to William Fuld and later (1966) to Parker Brothers, and in 1991 to Hasbro, which still markets the Ouija board with a plastic planchette.

Explanation

Scientific accounts attribute the planchette's movement to the ideomotor effect—unconscious, involuntary muscular movements by the participants—as demonstrated in the 1850s by investigators such as Michael Faraday for table-turning and later studied by William Benjamin Carpenter and others. Participants typically believe the movement is guided by external or spiritual agency despite generating the motion themselves.

In culture

Planchettes appear frequently in literature, film, and occult practice, and heart-shaped wooden planchettes have become decorative motifs associated with Spiritualism and the Victorian séance.
