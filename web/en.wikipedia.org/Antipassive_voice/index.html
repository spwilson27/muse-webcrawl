Antipassive voice

The antipassive voice is a type of grammatical voice that either does not include the object or includes the object as an oblique argument of a transitive verb, focusing instead on the agent. It is found in many ergative languages, including Dyirbal, Basque, Mayan languages, and Inuit languages.

Function

The antipassive is the mirror image of the passive voice: where the passive demotes or deletes the agent of a transitive clause, the antipassive demotes or deletes the patient. In ergative–absolutive languages, the antipassive converts a transitive clause into an intransitive one, placing the agent in the absolutive case, which often allows it to participate in syntactic operations — such as coordination — that are restricted to absolutive arguments.

Example

In Dyirbal, a basic transitive sentence has an ergative-marked subject and an absolutive-marked object. In the antipassive, marked by a verbal suffix, the subject appears in the absolutive and the object appears in the dative or instrumental case, or is omitted entirely.
