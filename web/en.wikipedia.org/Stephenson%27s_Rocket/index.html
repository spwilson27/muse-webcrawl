Stephenson's Rocket

Rocket
Designer: George and Robert Stephenson
Built: 1829, Forth Street Works, Newcastle
Wheel arrangement: 0-2-2
Famous for: Winning the Rainhill Trials

Rocket was an early steam locomotive of 0-2-2 wheel arrangement, designed by George Stephenson and his son Robert Stephenson and built in 1829 at their Forth Street Works in Newcastle upon Tyne.

Rainhill Trials

In October 1829 the directors of the Liverpool and Manchester Railway held the Rainhill Trials to choose locomotives for their new line. Rocket was the only locomotive to complete the trials successfully, averaging about 12 mph and reaching roughly 30 mph, and it won the £500 prize.

Design innovations

Rocket combined several features that became standard: a multi-tubular boiler with 25 copper fire tubes for efficient heat transfer, a separate firebox, a blast pipe to draw the fire, and cylinders connected directly to the driving wheels — set at an angle rather than vertical. The preserved locomotive is now in the Science Museum, London.
