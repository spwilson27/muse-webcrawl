Brunswick is a city in and the county seat of Glynn County in the U.S. state of Georgia. As the seat of its county, it is the principal city of the Brunswick metropolitan area. As of the 2020 census, the population of the city proper was 15,210; the metropolitan area had a population of roughly 113,000. Brunswick is home to an important port city on the Atlantic Coast, and is located on a narrow coastal plain on the eastern seaboard of the United States.

The city was founded in 1771 by the Province of Georgia and named after the German duchy of Brunswick-Lüneburg, the ancestral home of King George III. It was laid out following the Oglethorpe Plan, a grid pattern of streets and squares also used in Savannah. During the American Civil War, the city was abandoned and burned. Later, in the late 19th century, Brunswick emerged as a major center for the lumber and naval stores industries, and its deepwater port became one of the busiest on the southeastern coast.

History

The area around Brunswick was inhabited for thousands of years by Indigenous peoples, including the Mocama and Guale. In 1738, James Oglethorpe established plantations near the site. The city was formally laid out in 1771, with its first lots recorded that year. Brunswick's grid of streets and public squares mirrored the design of Savannah.

During World War II, Brunswick's shipyards, operated by the J.A. Jones Construction Company, produced Liberty ships for the war effort, and a blimp base, Naval Air Station Glynco, was established nearby. The Brunswick Golden Isles Airport now occupies part of the former base.

Geography

Brunswick is located on the Atlantic coastal plain, at the confluence of several tidal rivers including the Turtle River and East River, opposite the Golden Isles of Georgia — St. Simons Island, Sea Island, Little St. Simons Island, and Jekyll Island. The city sits at a low elevation, and marshes of Glynn surround much of the area. The Sidney Lanier Bridge, a cable-stayed bridge completed in 2003, carries U.S. Highway 17 over the Brunswick River and is one of the tallest bridges in Georgia.

Economy

The Port of Brunswick, operated by the Georgia Ports Authority, is a major deepwater port specializing in roll-on/roll-off cargo such as automobiles and heavy machinery, as well as bulk and breakbulk cargo. Tourism, seafood processing, and manufacturing also contribute to the local economy. The Federal Law Enforcement Training Center (FLETC), headquartered at nearby Glynco, is a significant employer.
