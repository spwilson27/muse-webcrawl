Stand in the Schoolhouse Door

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Governor Wallace standing in the doorway of Foster Auditorium

The Stand in the Schoolhouse Door took place at Foster Auditorium at the University of Alabama on 11 June 1963. George Wallace, the Governor of Alabama, in a symbolic attempt to keep his inaugural promise of "segregation now, segregation tomorrow, segregation forever", stood at the door of the auditorium to try to block the entry of two African American students, Vivian Malone and James Hood.

Events

The Kennedy administration had federalised the Alabama National Guard. After Deputy Attorney General Nicholas Katzenbach confronted Wallace, Wallace delivered a statement and, when General Henry Graham formally asked him to step aside, he did so; Malone and Hood were enrolled. President John F. Kennedy that evening delivered his landmark civil rights address proposing what became the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

Legacy

The incident became an emblem of resistance to desegregation in the United States. Wallace later renounced segregationism. Vivian Malone became the university's first African American graduate in 1965. The site was designated a National Historic Landmark, and the doorway features a commemorative plaza.
