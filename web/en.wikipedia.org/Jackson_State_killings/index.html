The Jackson State killings occurred on Friday, 15 May 1970, at Jackson State College (now Jackson State University) in Jackson, Mississippi. City and state police opened fire on a crowd of students outside a women's dormitory, killing two young men and wounding twelve others. The shooting took place eleven days after the Kent State shootings, amid nationwide student unrest over the Vietnam War and the US invasion of Cambodia, but received far less national attention.

Background

Jackson State was a historically black college. Tensions in Jackson included protests against racial discrimination and the draft; on 14 May a disturbance along Lynch Street, which bisected the campus, included a dump truck being set alight. Police cordoned off the area on the night of 14–15 May as students gathered outside Alexander Hall, a women's dormitory.

The shooting

Shortly after midnight, police advanced on the crowd. Officers claimed a sniper had fired at them — a claim the subsequent federal investigation did not substantiate — and opened fire with shotguns, rifles, and a submachine gun, discharging over 150 rounds in about 30 seconds into the crowd and the dormitory behind it. Phillip Lafayette Gibbs, a 21-year-old junior and father of two, and James Earl Green, a 17-year-old local high-school student walking home, were killed. Twelve others were wounded, some trampled in the panic.

Aftermath

The President's Commission on Campus Unrest concluded that the police firing was an "unreasonable, unjustified" response, but a Hinds County grand jury declined to indict any officer, and no one was ever prosecuted. A civil suit was ultimately settled. The Gibbs–Green Plaza at Jackson State University now commemorates the victims. Historians frequently contrast the relative obscurity of the Jackson State killings with the national trauma of Kent State, attributing the difference in part to the race of the victims and the region where the shooting occurred.
