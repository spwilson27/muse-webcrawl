Handicap race

A handicap race is a race in which competitors are given different advantages or disadvantages — a handicap — intended to equalise their chances of winning. Handicapping is used in horse racing, golf, sailing, motorsport, cycling and many other sports.

Horse racing

In horse racing, a handicap is a race in which each horse carries a different weight, assigned by an official handicapper according to the horse's assessed ability: better horses carry more weight ("weight-for-age" adjustments and penalties for previous wins also apply). The aim is for all runners, in theory, to have an equal chance, producing competitive racing and attractive betting. Famous handicaps include the Grand National, the Melbourne Cup and the Epsom Handicap. In North America such races are graded by the size of the purse and the quality of the field.

Other sports

In sailing, handicap systems such as IRC and PHRF adjust boats' elapsed times by rating factors. In motorsport, success ballast and balance-of-performance rules handicap winning cars. In foot racing and cycling, handicap events may use staggered starts, with slower competitors starting first. In golf, the handicap index serves a related equalising purpose in amateur competition rather than within a single race.

Strategy

Because a horse's official rating rises with success, connections sometimes campaign a horse to obtain a favourable handicap mark before a major target race — a practice known as "plotting" a handicap — within the rules set by racing authorities.
