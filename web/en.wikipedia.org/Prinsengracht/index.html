The Prinsengracht ("Prince's Canal") is a canal in central Amsterdam, the Netherlands. Named after the Prince of Orange, it is the fourth and longest of the main canals of the city's canal belt, the Grachtengordel, running roughly 3.2 kilometres parallel to and west of the Keizersgracht and Herengracht.

History

Construction of the canal belt began in 1612 as part of Amsterdam's great early-17th-century expansion during the Dutch Golden Age. The Prinsengracht, the outermost of the three main canals, was lined with somewhat more modest houses, warehouses, and workshops than the grander Herengracht, and the Jordaan working-class district developed just to its west. The canal belt was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2010.

Notable sites

The best-known building on the canal is the Anne Frank House at Prinsengracht 263, where Anne Frank and her family hid during the Second World War and where her diary was written; the building is now a museum. Nearby stands the Westerkerk, whose tower is a landmark of the canal. Other notable features include the Noorderkerk, the Houseboat Museum, and the annual Prinsengrachtconcert, an open-air classical concert performed on a pontoon in the canal. Many of the canal's quays are lined with houseboats and with Amsterdam's famously narrow, gabled canal houses.
