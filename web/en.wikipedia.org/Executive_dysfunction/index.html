Executive dysfunction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Executive dysfunction is a disruption of the executive functions — the cognitive processes, associated largely with the prefrontal cortex, that regulate, control, and manage other cognitive processes such as planning, working memory, attention, inhibition, mental flexibility, problem solving, and the initiation and monitoring of action. When these systems are impaired, a person may have difficulty starting tasks, organizing steps toward a goal, switching between tasks, controlling impulses, or judging the consequences of behaviour, even though basic intelligence and memory for facts may be relatively intact.

Causes and associated conditions

Executive dysfunction is not a diagnosis in itself but a pattern of impairment seen in many neurological and psychiatric conditions. It is classically associated with frontal lobe injury, stroke, and traumatic brain injury, and with neurodegenerative diseases such as frontotemporal dementia and Parkinson's disease. It is also a prominent feature of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and occurs in autism spectrum disorder, schizophrenia, depression, and obsessive–compulsive disorder.

Assessment and management

Clinicians assess executive functioning with neuropsychological tests such as the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test, the Stroop test, and trail-making tasks, together with rating scales of everyday behaviour. Management depends on the underlying condition and may include medication, cognitive rehabilitation, external organizational aids, structured routines, and psychotherapy approaches that break goals into small, concrete steps.
