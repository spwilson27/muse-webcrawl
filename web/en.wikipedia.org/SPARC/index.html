SPARC

SPARC (Scalable Processor ARChitecture) is a reduced instruction set computer (RISC) instruction set architecture originally developed by Sun Microsystems in the mid-1980s. Its design was strongly influenced by the pioneering RISC projects at UC Berkeley (RISC I and II) and it introduced features such as register windows to speed procedure calls. SPARC processors powered Sun's workstations and servers — the SPARCstation and UltraSPARC lines — and were also made by Fujitsu, Texas Instruments and others.

An UltraSPARC processor, Sun's 64-bit SPARC implementation.

Architecture

SPARC is a load/store architecture with a large register file organised as overlapping register windows: at any time a procedure sees 32 integer registers, of which adjacent windows share eight, reducing memory traffic for arguments and return values. The architecture is big-endian by nature (later versions allow bi-endian operation). SPARC Version 9 (1993) defined a full 64-bit architecture, implemented from 1995 in the UltraSPARC.

History and licensing

Sun launched its first SPARC systems, the Sun-4 workstations, in 1987. In 1989 Sun founded SPARC International to license the architecture openly to other vendors and prevent fragmentation; the SPARC trademark and specifications were administered by that body. After Oracle's acquisition of Sun in 2010, SPARC development continued in the SPARC M-series servers, though Oracle progressively wound down hardware development in the late 2010s; Fujitsu's SPARC64 line, used in Japan's Fugaku-era predecessors such as the K computer, was a major independent implementation.
