Jacques Delors

Jacques Lucien Jean Delors
Born: 20 July 1925, Paris
Died: 27 December 2023, Paris
Offices: President of the European Commission (1985–1995); French Minister of Finance (1981–1984)

Jacques Lucien Jean Delors (20 July 1925 – 27 December 2023) was a French politician who served as the eighth President of the European Commission from 1985 to 1995, the longest tenure in that office. He is widely regarded as one of the principal architects of the modern European Union, driving the creation of the single market and the path to the euro.

Early career

Delors worked at the Banque de France and in French economic planning, and was a social affairs adviser to Prime Minister Jacques Chaban-Delmas. A member of the Socialist Party from 1974, he served in the European Parliament (1979–1981) before becoming Minister of Economy and Finance under President François Mitterrand, where he oversaw the 1983 "tournant de la rigueur" (austerity turn).

European Commission

As Commission President, Delors championed the Single European Act (1986), which set 1992 as the deadline for completing the single market, and the Delors Report (1989), which laid out the three-stage plan for Economic and Monetary Union realised in the Maastricht Treaty (1992). He also expanded structural funds and promoted a "social dimension" for the Community, including the Social Charter. His federalist ambitions brought him into conflict especially with British Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher, whose 1988 Bruges speech was directed partly against his agenda.

Later life

Frontrunner to stand as the Socialist candidate in the 1995 French presidential election, Delors declined to run and retired from the Commission. He founded the think tank Notre Europe and remained an influential commentator on European affairs until his death in 2023.
