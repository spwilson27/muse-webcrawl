The Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria (Coptic: Ϯⲉⲕ̀ⲕⲗⲏⲥⲓⲁ ⲛ̀ⲣⲉⲙⲛ̀ⲭⲏⲙⲓ ⲛ̀ⲟⲣⲑⲟⲇⲟⲝⲟⲥ) is an Oriental Orthodox Christian church based in Egypt, serving Africa and the Middle East. The head of the church is the Pope of Alexandria and Patriarch of the See of St Mark, who resides in Cairo and presides over a global diaspora. As of the early twenty-first century, it is the largest Christian denomination in Egypt, where Copts constitute a significant minority of the population.

The church belongs to the family of Oriental Orthodox churches, which includes the Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian, Eritrean, and Malankara churches. It has been a distinct body since the Council of Chalcedon in 451 AD, when it took a position on Christology that differed from the imperial church, affirming the formula of Cyril of Alexandria: "one incarnate nature of God the Word" (mia physis).

History

Tradition holds that the church was founded by Saint Mark the Evangelist, author of the second Gospel, who preached in Alexandria and was martyred there around 68 AD. Alexandria became one of the great centres of early Christianity, home to the catechetical school associated with Clement and Origen, and to theologians such as Athanasius, who defended Nicene orthodoxy against Arianism, and Cyril, whose Christological teaching shaped the church's identity.

After Chalcedon, the non-Chalcedonian patriarchate of Alexandria developed separately from the Byzantine (Melkite) patriarchate. The Arab conquest of Egypt in the seventh century placed the church under Muslim rule, and over the following centuries the Coptic language gradually gave way to Arabic in daily life, while remaining the liturgical language. Egyptian monasticism — founded by figures such as Anthony the Great and Pachomius — profoundly influenced Christian practice worldwide and remains central to Coptic spirituality.

Modern era

Under Pope Cyril VI (1959–1971) and Pope Shenouda III (1971–2012), the church experienced a Sunday school revival, monastic renewal, and extensive expansion abroad as Copts emigrated to North America, Europe, and Australia. Pope Tawadros II became the 118th pope in November 2012. The church has suffered periodic persecution and attacks, including bombings of churches, and Copts have been targeted by extremists in Egypt and Libya.

Beliefs and practices

The church celebrates seven sacraments and a rich liturgical cycle, centred on the Divine Liturgy attributed to Saint Basil, Saint Gregory, and Saint Cyril. Fasting occupies a large part of the Coptic calendar — over 200 days a year for the strictest observance — including the Fast of the Apostles, the Fast of the Virgin Mary, and Great Lent. The Coptic calendar, reckoned from the Diocletian "Era of the Martyrs" (284 AD), is still used liturgically.
