The National Reorganization Process (Spanish: Proceso de Reorganización Nacional, often simply el Proceso) was the military dictatorship that ruled Argentina from the coup of 24 March 1976, which deposed President Isabel Perón, until the restoration of democracy in 1983.

Rule and repression

Successive juntas, led initially by Jorge Rafael Videla and later by Roberto Viola, Leopoldo Galtieri and Reynaldo Bignone, dissolved Congress, banned political activity and imposed censorship. The regime waged the "Dirty War" against suspected leftists and dissidents: between about 9,000 (the figure documented by the CONADEP commission) and 30,000 people (the figure long maintained by human rights groups) were forcibly disappeared and killed in clandestine detention centres such as ESMA.

Economy and the Falklands War

Economy minister José Alfredo Martínez de Hoz pursued liberalisation and financial deregulation that ended in a severe debt and banking crisis. In April 1982 the junta invaded the Falkland Islands (Islas Malvinas); Argentina's defeat by the United Kingdom in June 1982 discredited the regime and hastened elections in October 1983, won by Raúl Alfonsín.

Trials and legacy

The 1985 Trial of the Juntas convicted the dictatorship's leaders, a landmark in transitional justice. Amnesty laws and pardons later shielded many perpetrators until the laws were annulled in the 2000s and trials resumed.
