Dene-Yeniseian languages

Dene-Yeniseian
Proposed byEdward Vajda (2008)
BranchesNa-Dene (North America); Yeniseian (Siberia)
Key evidenceVerb morphology, cognate sets
StatusWidely discussed; accepted by many specialists, not proven to all

Dene-Yeniseian is a proposed language family linking the Na-Dene languages of North America with the Yeniseian languages of central Siberia. The hypothesis was presented in detail by linguist Edward Vajda in 2008 and is regarded as the first credible demonstration of a genealogical relationship between language families of the Old and New Worlds.

The proposal

Earlier scholars, including Alfredo Trombetti and Merritt Ruhlen, had suggested connections between Yeniseian and various American families, but Vajda's work was the first to present systematic correspondences. His evidence includes cognates in basic vocabulary, shared patterns in verb prefix morphology — the complex templatic verb structure found in both Ket (the only surviving Yeniseian language) and Na-Dene languages such as Navajo and Tlingit — and correspondences in tone and phonology.

Reception

At a 2008 symposium and in subsequent publications, many specialists in Na-Dene and Yeniseian languages judged the evidence promising. The hypothesis remains under investigation, with some linguists reserving judgement pending fuller reconstruction of proto-Yeniseian and proto-Na-Dene.

Implications

If correct, Dene-Yeniseian would imply that the ancestors of Na-Dene speakers migrated from Asia to North America in a later movement separate from the first peopling of the Americas — a scenario compatible with archaeological and genetic evidence of a back-migration of Ket-related ancestry into Siberia as well.
