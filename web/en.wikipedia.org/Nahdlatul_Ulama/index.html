Nahdlatul Ulama

Nahdlatul Ulama (NU; Indonesian for "Revival of the Ulama") is an Islamic organisation in Indonesia, founded on 31 January 1926 in Surabaya by the scholar Hasyim Asy'ari and a group of traditionalist ulama. It is frequently described as one of the largest independent Islamic organisations in the world, with membership estimates ranging from tens of millions to around 90–120 million people.

History

NU was founded in response to reformist movements and the rise of Wahhabism in Arabia, which traditionalist Javanese scholars feared threatened local Islamic practices. The organisation established a vast network of pesantren (Islamic boarding schools) and mosques. Prominent leaders have included Abdurrahman Wahid ("Gus Dur"), grandson of the founder, who chaired NU from 1984 to 1999 and served as President of Indonesia from 1999 to 2001.

Beliefs and activities

NU follows the Shafi'i school of jurisprudence and Ash'ari-Maturidi theology, and emphasises tolerance, pluralism and local culture within an Indonesian nationalist framework — expressed in the slogan Islam Nusantara ("Islam of the Archipelago"). It runs schools, universities, hospitals and charities, and holds that loyalty to the Indonesian state is compatible with — indeed required by — Islamic teaching. In 2019 NU announced it would abandon the religious use of the term kafir for non-Muslim citizens of Indonesia.
