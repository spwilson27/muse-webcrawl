Bay Psalm Book

The Bay Psalm Book, formally The Whole Booke of Psalmes Faithfully Translated into English Metre, is a psalter produced in the Massachusetts Bay Colony and printed in Cambridge, Massachusetts, in 1640. It is recognised as the first book printed in British North America, and surviving copies are among the most valuable printed books in the world.

The first book printed in British North America (1640).

Production

The Puritan settlers of Massachusetts Bay sang metrical psalms in worship but were dissatisfied with the versions in use, which they considered loose renderings of the Hebrew. Leading ministers of the colony — including Thomas Weld, John Eliot and Richard Mather — prepared a new translation aiming at fidelity to the original text rather than poetic elegance, using a press brought from England by Stephen Daye. Around 1,700 copies were printed in 1640.

Contents

The book contains metrical versions of the 150 Psalms for congregational singing, without printed music in the first edition (tunes were drawn from a small stock of well-known melodies); later editions added tunes. Its preface, attributed to John Cotton, defended the translation. Revised editions followed, and the psalter remained in use in New England churches well into the eighteenth century.

Rarity and value

Only eleven copies of the first edition are known to survive, in varying completeness. In November 2013 a copy sold at Sotheby's in New York for $14.2 million, at the time a world auction record for a printed book. Another copy was sold by Boston's Old South Church, one of two institutions that each held two copies.
