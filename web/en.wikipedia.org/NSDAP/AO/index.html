The NSDAP/AO was the foreign organisation (German: Auslands-Organisation) of the Nazi Party. It organised and supervised Nazi Party members who were German citizens living outside the Reich, and it coordinated propaganda and party activity among ethnic German communities abroad.

History

The party's foreign department was established in 1931 and was reorganised in 1934 as an independent Gau-level organisation under Ernst Wilhelm Bohle, who held the rank of Gauleiter. The AO maintained local groups (Ortsgruppen) and country organisations in dozens of states, concentrated in the Americas, Europe, and parts of Africa and Asia. Membership was restricted to Reich citizens; local sympathisers of German descent were handled by other bodies.

Activities

The AO published propaganda, organised rallies and schooling, monitored emigrant communities, and in some countries became entangled in espionage and diplomatic controversies that strained Germany's relations with host states. Most branches were shut down or interned by host governments once war began. The organisation ceased to exist with the collapse of Nazi Germany in 1945, and Bohle was later convicted at the Nuremberg Ministries Trial.
