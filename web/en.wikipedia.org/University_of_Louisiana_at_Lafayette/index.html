The University of Louisiana at Lafayette (UL Lafayette) is a public research university in Lafayette, Louisiana, United States. It is the largest campus of the University of Louisiana System and the second-largest university in the state. Founded in 1898 as the Southwestern Louisiana Industrial Institute, it became a university in 1960 and adopted its present name in 1999.

Academics

UL Lafayette offers over 240 programs and achieved "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" status in 2021. It has historic strengths in computer science — it awarded one of the first American master's degrees in the field — and in Francophone studies, Cajun and Creole folklife, and Louisiana culture through the Center for Louisiana Studies and the archives of Acadiana. The university operates research centres in energy, water resources, and advanced computer modeling.

Campus

The main campus is famed for Cypress Lake, a natural swamp habitat with cypress trees, Spanish moss, alligators, and egrets in the middle of the university grounds. The campus adjoins the University Research Park and the Cajundome sports and convention arena.

Athletics

Its teams are the Louisiana Ragin' Cajuns, competing in the Sun Belt Conference. The baseball and softball programs are perennial national contenders, and the football team has won multiple New Orleans Bowl titles.
