Hairstyle

A hairstyle, hairdo, or haircut refers to the styling of hair, usually on the human scalp. The fashioning of hair can be considered an aspect of personal grooming, fashion, and cosmetics, although practical, cultural, and popular considerations also influence some hairstyles. Hairstyles vary widely by culture, period and gender, and may signal social status, religious affiliation, age, or membership of a subculture.

History

Elaborate hairstyling dates to antiquity: Egyptian wigs, Roman women's sculpted curls, and the topknots and queues of various Asian societies all carried social meaning. In 18th-century Europe, powdered wigs and tall coiffures were fashionable among the aristocracy. The 20th century saw rapid changes — the bob of the 1920s, the beehive of the 1960s, the punk mohawk of the 1970s, and the voluminous styles of the 1980s.

Types

Common styles include short cuts such as the crew cut, buzz cut and pixie cut; medium styles such as the bob; and long hair worn loose, in braids, ponytails, buns or dreadlocks. Processes used to achieve styles include cutting, blow-drying, curling, straightening, perming, colouring, bleaching and the use of extensions and wigs.

Social and cultural significance

Hair has ritual and symbolic importance in many religions: tonsure in Christian monasticism and Buddhism, uncut hair (kesh) in Sikhism, sidelocks in Orthodox Judaism, and head-covering practices in Islam. Military and institutional dress codes frequently regulate hair length. Some states have historically imposed or restricted hairstyles, and discrimination based on hairstyles associated with particular ethnic groups has prompted legislation such as the CROWN Act in parts of the United States.
