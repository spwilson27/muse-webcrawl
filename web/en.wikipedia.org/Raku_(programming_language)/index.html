Raku (programming language)

Raku is a member of the Perl family of programming languages, formerly known as Perl 6. It was designed by Larry Wall and developed by a large community of volunteers. Unlike Perl 5, from which it diverged significantly, Raku was created as a complete redesign intended to be a "second sister" language rather than a successor.

History

The language design process began in 2000 with a series of community "Requests for Comments" (RFCs), followed by Wall's "Apocalypses" documents describing the design. Damian Conway's "Exegeses" explained the Apocalypses with examples. The first official release, version 6.c ("Christmas"), arrived in December 2015. In 2019 the language was renamed from Perl 6 to Raku to emphasise its distinct identity.

Features

Raku supports object-oriented, functional, and procedural programming, gradual typing, a powerful regular-expression and grammar engine, multiple dispatch, junctions, lazy lists, and extensive Unicode support. Its main implementation is Rakudo, which runs on the MoarVM virtual machine.
