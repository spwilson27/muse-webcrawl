Smart city


Amsterdam is an example of a smart city

A smart city is a technologically modern urban area that uses different types of electronic methods and sensors to collect specific data. Information gained from that data is used to manage assets, resources and services efficiently; in return, that data is used to improve operations across the city. This includes data collected from citizens, devices, buildings and assets that is processed and analyzed to monitor and manage traffic and transportation systems, power plants, utilities, urban forestry, water supply networks, waste, criminal investigations, information systems, schools, libraries, hospitals, and other community services.

Smart cities are defined as smart both in the way in which their governments interact with and respond to the needs of their communities and in how their society and infrastructure operate. The smart city concept integrates information and communication technology (ICT), and various physical devices connected to the Internet of things (IoT) network to optimize the efficiency of city operations and services and connect to citizens.

Technology

Smart city technologies include networks of sensors and cameras, geographic information systems, open data platforms, smart grids, intelligent transport systems, and digital services delivered through mobile applications. Proponents argue these tools can reduce energy use and congestion, improve public safety, and make government more responsive.

Criticism

Critics raise concerns about mass surveillance and the privacy implications of pervasive data collection, the role of large technology vendors in shaping public policy, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and the risk that technology-led development neglects social equity. High-profile projects such as Sidewalk Labs' cancelled Quayside development in Toronto became focal points for these debates.

Examples

Frequently cited smart cities include Singapore, Barcelona, Amsterdam, Seoul, and Copenhagen, which have implemented programmes ranging from sensor-based waste collection and smart lighting to integrated mobility platforms and citywide open-data portals.
