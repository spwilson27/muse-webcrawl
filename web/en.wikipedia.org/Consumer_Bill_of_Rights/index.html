Consumer Bill of Rights

The Consumer Bill of Rights is a set of consumer protections articulated by United States President John F. Kennedy in a special message to Congress on 15 March 1962. Kennedy set out four rights of consumers, which became a foundational statement of the consumer movement; consumer organizations, notably Consumers International, later expanded the list to eight rights.

The original four rights

Kennedy's 1962 message declared that consumers, "by definition, include us all," and listed: (1) the right to safety — protection against products hazardous to health or life; (2) the right to be informed — protection against fraudulent or misleading information and the provision of facts needed to make informed choices; (3) the right to choose — access to a variety of products at competitive prices; and (4) the right to be heard — assurance that consumer interests receive full consideration in government policy.

Expansion to eight rights

Consumers International later added four more: the right to satisfaction of basic needs, the right to redress, the right to consumer education, and the right to a healthy environment. These eight rights informed the United Nations Guidelines for Consumer Protection, first adopted in 1985 and expanded in 1999 and 2015.

Impact

The 1962 message helped launch the modern consumer protection era in the United States, preceding legislation on product safety, truthful labelling and lending disclosure, and the creation of the Consumer Product Safety Commission in 1972. World Consumer Rights Day is observed annually on 15 March, the anniversary of Kennedy's address.
