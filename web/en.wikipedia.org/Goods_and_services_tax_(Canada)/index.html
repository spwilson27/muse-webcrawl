Goods and services tax (Canada)


GST shown on a Canadian sales receipt.

The goods and services tax (GST; French: taxe sur les produits et services, TPS) is a value-added tax introduced by the federal government of Canada on January 1, 1991. It is levied on most goods and services sold in Canada, with businesses collecting the tax and remitting it to the Canada Revenue Agency, claiming credits for GST paid on their own inputs.

History

The GST replaced the hidden federal manufacturers' sales tax of 13.5%, which economists criticized for distorting production and hurting exports. Introduced under Prime Minister Brian Mulroney's Progressive Conservative government, it was deeply unpopular at launch and contributed to the government's defeat in 1993; the succeeding Liberal government, despite campaigning against it, retained the tax. In several provinces the GST was later combined with the provincial sales tax into the harmonized sales tax (HST).

The rate was cut from 7% to 6% in July 2006 and to 5% on January 1, 2008, under the government of Stephen Harper. Basic groceries, prescription drugs, medical devices and residential rent are zero-rated or exempt. Low-income individuals receive a quarterly GST/HST credit to offset the tax's regressivity.

Quebec

Quebec administers its own value-added tax, the Quebec sales tax (QST), alongside the GST; uniquely, Revenu Québec collects the federal GST within the province on behalf of the Canada Revenue Agency.
