Sabah (Malay pronunciation: [saˈbah]) is a state of Malaysia located on the northern portion of the island of Borneo. It is the second-largest Malaysian state by area and is bordered by the Malaysian state of Sarawak to the southwest, the Indonesian province of North Kalimantan to the south, and surrounded on its remaining sides by the South China Sea, Sulu Sea and Celebes Sea. Its capital is Kota Kinabalu.

Geography and nature

Sabah is dominated by the Crocker Range and by Mount Kinabalu (4,095 m), the highest mountain in Malaysia and the centrepiece of Kinabalu Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for its extraordinary plant diversity. The state is famous for its rainforests, coral reefs and wildlife, including orangutans, proboscis monkeys and pygmy elephants, and for world-class diving at Sipadan Island. The Danum Valley and Maliau Basin conservation areas protect some of Borneo's last primary lowland rainforest, though logging and oil-palm plantations have converted large areas.

History

Historically under the influence of the Bruneian Empire and the Sultanate of Sulu, northern Borneo was leased in 1878 to British interests and administered by the British North Borneo Chartered Company until Japanese occupation during the Second World War (1942–1945). Sabah became a British Crown Colony in 1946 and joined the Federation of Malaysia on 16 September 1963, with safeguards embodied in the 20-point agreement. The Philippines has historically asserted a claim to eastern Sabah based on the former Sulu sultanate's rights — a claim Malaysia rejects, though it was linked to the 2013 Lahad Datu incursion.

People and economy

Sabah is ethnically diverse, with indigenous groups including the Kadazan-Dusun, Bajau and Murut, alongside Malay, Chinese and other communities. The economy rests on petroleum and natural gas, palm oil, timber, cocoa and tourism.
