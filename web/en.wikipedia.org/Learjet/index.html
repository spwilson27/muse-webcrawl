Learjet was an American manufacturer of business jets, founded by inventor and entrepreneur Bill Lear in Wichita, Kansas. Its aircraft effectively created the market for small, fast, private jet travel in the 1960s, and "Learjet" became a generic cultural shorthand for the private jet of the wealthy.

History

Bill Lear based his design on the wing of the Swiss FFA P-16 strike fighter project, retaining its distinctive tip fuel tanks. The first model, the Learjet 23, flew on 7 October 1963 and entered service in 1964, carrying six to eight passengers at speeds then unmatched by civilian aircraft. Successive models — the 24, 25, 28/29, 35/36, 55, 60, and later 31, 40, 45, 70/75, and 85 — enlarged the range from light jets to midsize types. More than 3,000 Learjets were built in total.

Bombardier era and end of production

Bombardier purchased Learjet in 1990 and continued production in Wichita alongside its Challenger and Global lines. In February 2021 Bombardier announced the end of Learjet production to concentrate on its larger, more profitable families; the final aircraft, a Learjet 75, was delivered in 2022. Support for the in-service fleet continues from Wichita.

Cultural impact

The Learjet became a symbol of celebrity and corporate luxury, immortalized in popular songs and film, and set speed and altitude records for its class. Its aircraft also served in military and special-mission roles with air forces around the world.
