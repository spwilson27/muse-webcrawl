Present arms (command)

Present arms is a two-part drill command used by many armed forces around the world, by which soldiers are ordered to render a salute with their weapons. On the preparatory command "Present", troops bring their rifles to a position in front of the body; on the execution command "Arms", the salute is completed according to the drill manual of the service concerned.

Use

The present arms is the principal salute rendered by armed personnel under arms, used when rendering honours to senior officers, national colours, heads of state, or at ceremonies such as parades, inspections, guard mounting and funerals. Troops not carrying weapons instead render a hand salute on the equivalent command "Salute".

Variations

The precise movements differ by country and by weapon. In United States drill (for example, per Marine Corps drill manuals), the rifle is brought to a position centred in front of the body with the muzzle up and the right hand at the small of the stock. In British drill, the rifle is brought to the centre of the body and then lowered into the "present" position with the left hand supporting the forestock. Swords, where carried by officers and guards, have their own form of the present arms.

Related commands

After the salute, troops return to their previous position on commands such as "Order arms" or "Shoulder arms". The present arms is one of a family of movements that also includes slope arms, trail arms and shoulder arms.
