World War I (also called the First World War or the Great War) was a global conflict between two coalitions: the Allies and the Central Powers. Fighting took place mainly in Europe from 28 July 1914 to 11 November 1918, mobilising more than 70 million military personnel and causing an estimated 9–10 million combatant deaths and around 10 million civilian deaths.

Causes and outbreak

The immediate trigger was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria by Gavrilo Princip in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. Alliance commitments, imperial rivalry, militarism and nationalism turned the Austro-Serbian crisis into a general European war within weeks.

Course of the war

After Germany's advance through Belgium was halted at the Marne, the Western Front settled into trench warfare, with enormous battles at Verdun and the Somme in 1916 and Passchendaele in 1917. On the Eastern Front, Russia's collapse after the 1917 revolutions took it out of the war. The United States entered in April 1917; its fresh troops helped the Allies repel Germany's 1918 Spring Offensive and win the Hundred Days Offensive. Germany signed an armistice on 11 November 1918.

Consequences

The Treaty of Versailles (1919) redrew Europe's map — much as Italian unification had reshaped the peninsula two generations earlier — dissolved empires, and imposed reparations on Germany. The war's unresolved grievances fed directly into the Second World War, while conflicts such as the Yugoslav Wars of the 1990s traced some of their roots to the post-1918 settlement in the Balkans.
