Memorial

A memorial is an object, structure, place or event established to commemorate and preserve the memory of a person, group or event. Memorials range from small plaques and gravestones to monumental architecture such as the Lincoln Memorial in Washington, D.C., the Taj Mahal (a mausoleum), and commemorative landscapes like the National September 11 Memorial in New York.

Types

Common forms include war memorials honouring fallen soldiers (cenotaphs, triumphal arches, memorial walls bearing names), tombs and grave markers, statues and obelisks, memorial museums and halls, named institutions such as scholarships, buildings and hospitals, and living memorials such as trees and gardens. Online memorials and virtual cemeteries have also become common. Memorial services and days of remembrance — services or ceremonies held after a death, separate from funerals — serve the same commemorative function in time rather than in physical form.

Function

Scholars of collective memory note that memorials do not merely preserve the past: they shape public understanding of it, serving as sites of mourning, civic ritual, education and sometimes political contest, as debates over removing monuments to controversial figures have shown.
