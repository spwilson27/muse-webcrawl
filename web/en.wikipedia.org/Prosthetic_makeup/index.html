Prosthetic makeup

Foam latex and silicone appliances transform an actor's appearance.

Prosthetic makeup, also called special makeup effects, is the process of using sculpting, moulding, and casting techniques to create cosmetic appliances that are glued to an actor's skin to alter their appearance — ageing a young actor, creating wounds, or transforming a performer into a creature. It is a major branch of special effects in film, television, and theatre.

Process and materials

Artists typically take a life cast of the actor, sculpt the desired features in clay over it, mould the sculpture, and cast appliances in foam latex, silicone, or gelatin. The pieces are then adhered with medical or spirit-gum adhesives, blended at the edges, and painted. Modern workflows may begin with digital scanning and 3D printing of moulds.

Notable artists

Pioneers include Jack Pierce, creator of the classic Universal monster makeups; Dick Smith, whose ageing makeup for The Godfather and work on Amadeus advanced the field; and Rick Baker, whose transformation effects in An American Werewolf in London helped prompt the Academy to create the Oscar for Best Makeup. Application can take many hours — some full-body makeups require teams of artists and most of a working day.
