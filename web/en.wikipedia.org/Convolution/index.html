Convolution
Notation(f ∗ g)(t)
PropertiesCommutative, associative, distributive
Key theoremConvolution theorem (Fourier transform)
ApplicationsSignal processing, probability, image processing, CNNs

In mathematics (in particular, functional analysis), convolution is a mathematical operation on two functions (f and g) that produces a third function, expressing how the shape of one is modified by the other. The term convolution refers to both the result function and the process of computing it. It is defined as the integral of the product of the two functions after one is reflected about the y-axis and shifted:

(f ∗ g)(t) = ∫ f(τ) g(t − τ) dτ

Properties and the convolution theorem

Convolution is commutative, associative, and distributive over addition, and the derivative of a convolution is the convolution of one function with the derivative of the other. The convolution theorem states that the Fourier transform converts convolution into pointwise multiplication, which is the basis for fast convolution algorithms via the FFT and for the design of filters in signal processing.

Discrete convolution

For sequences, the discrete convolution (f ∗ g)[n] = Σm f[m] g[n − m] underlies digital filtering, polynomial multiplication, and the "convolution" operation (technically cross-correlation) of convolutional neural networks in image recognition.

Applications

In probability, the distribution of the sum of two independent random variables is the convolution of their distributions. In engineering, the output of any linear time-invariant system is the convolution of its input with the system's impulse response. In optics and imaging, blurring is convolution with a point-spread function.
