Kalmyk language

Kalmyk
Native to: Kalmykia, Russia
Family: Mongolic
Writing: Cyrillic; formerly Clear Script

Kalmyk (Хальмг келн) is a Mongolic language spoken by the Kalmyks, a people of Oirat Mongol origin living mainly in the Republic of Kalmykia in southern Russia, on the western shore of the Caspian Sea. It is the only Mongolic language with a substantial speaker community in Europe. UNESCO classifies it as definitely endangered, as many younger Kalmyks now speak Russian primarily.

History

The Kalmyks migrated from Dzungaria to the Volga steppe in the early 17th century, and their language diverged from other Oirat varieties. In 1648 the monk Zaya Pandita devised the Clear Script (Todo Bichig), adapted from the Mongolian script, for writing Oirat. In the Soviet period the script was changed first to Latin and then, in 1938, to Cyrillic, which remains in use. The deportation of the Kalmyks to Siberia in 1943–1957 severely disrupted transmission of the language.

Features

Kalmyk is agglutinative, with vowel harmony, subject–object–verb word order, and a case system of around nine cases. It preserves some archaic Mongolic features while showing influences from Turkic and Russian. Dialects include Torgut, Derbet, and Buzava.
