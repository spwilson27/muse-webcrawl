Law and society

Law and society is an interdisciplinary field — also called socio-legal studies — that studies law as a social institution: how legal rules, courts and professions interact with culture, politics, economics and everyday life. It draws on sociology, anthropology, history and political science rather than treating law as a closed doctrinal system.

Approaches

Scholars examine the "gap" between law on the books and law in action, access to justice, legal consciousness, dispute resolution outside courts, and how social movements use litigation. Foundational figures include Max Weber, Émile Durkheim, Eugen Ehrlich ("living law") and Roscoe Pound, who contrasted law in books with law in action. The Law and Society Association, founded in 1964, and its journal anchor the field in the United States.

Topics

Research spans policing, punishment, regulation, legal pluralism in colonial and postcolonial settings, and the legal profession. See also Law school and Lawsuit.
