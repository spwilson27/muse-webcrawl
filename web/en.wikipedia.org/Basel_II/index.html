Basel II

Basel II is the second of the Basel Accords, published by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision in June 2004. It refined and expanded the capital rules of Basel I, intending to align regulatory capital more closely with banks' underlying risks and to improve supervisory review and market discipline.

The three pillars

Basel II rests on three mutually reinforcing pillars:

  • Pillar 1 — Minimum capital requirements: retains the 8% minimum ratio but makes risk weights far more sensitive to credit quality, allowing banks to use external ratings (the standardised approach) or their own internal models (internal ratings-based approaches). It also adds an explicit capital charge for operational risk.
  • Pillar 2 — Supervisory review: requires supervisors to evaluate banks' internal capital adequacy assessment processes and to intervene when capital is judged insufficient for the risks taken.
  • Pillar 3 — Market discipline: sets disclosure requirements intended to let market participants assess a bank's risk profile and capital adequacy.

Implementation and criticism

Implementation proceeded unevenly: the European Union applied Basel II through its Capital Requirements Directive from 2007, while the United States applied the advanced approaches only to its largest banks. Critics argued that reliance on banks' internal models and credit rating agencies contributed procyclicality, and the framework was heavily revised after the 2007–2009 financial crisis by Basel III.
