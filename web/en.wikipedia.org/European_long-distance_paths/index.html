The European long-distance paths are a network of long-distance walking routes crossing Europe, designated by the European Ramblers' Association (ERA, formerly the European Ramblers' Association / Europäische Wandervereinigung). There are twelve paths, designated E1 through E12, with a combined length of roughly 75,000 km. Each is assembled from existing national and regional trails and waymarked, in principle, across the countries it traverses.

The routes

  • E1 — North Cape (Norway) to Capo Passero, Sicily (~8,000 km)
  • E2 — Scotland to Nice, France, via England, the Low Countries and the Alps (~4,850 km)
  • E3 — Spain (Santiago de Compostela) to Bulgaria/Turkey (~6,950 km)
  • E4 — Spain to Cyprus via France, the Alps, the Balkans and Greece (~10,000 km)
  • E5 — Brittany to Verona, Italy (~3,200 km)
  • E6 — Finland to Greece (~5,200 km)
  • E7 — Portugal to Ukraine (~4,330 km)
  • E8 — Ireland to Bulgaria, toward Istanbul (~4,390 km)
  • E9 — the International Friendship Trail along the former Iron Curtain and Baltic–Atlantic coasts (~9,880 km)
  • E10 — Baltic (Rügen) to Spain (~2,880 km)
  • E11 — Netherlands to Lithuania (~2,070 km)
  • E12 — Mediterranean trail around Spain, France, Italy and the western Balkans

History and waymarking

The E-paths were conceived from the 1970s as symbols of European unity, deliberately crossing borders; the E9 follows much of the former Iron Curtain and relates to the later Iron Curtain Trail concept. Waymarking quality varies greatly by country: sections in Germany, France (where they coincide with GR routes), and Switzerland are well maintained, while some eastern sections are incomplete or only nominally designated. Few walkers attempt an entire E-path; most hike regional segments built from famous national trails such as France's GR5, which underlies parts of the E2.
