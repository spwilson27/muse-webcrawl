Minimum cut

Minimum cut
FieldGraph theory, network flows
Related problemMaximum flow
Key resultMax-flow min-cut theorem

In graph theory, a minimum cut or min-cut of a graph is a cut (a partition of the vertices of a graph into two disjoint subsets) that is minimal in some metric. In a weighted graph, the metric is typically the sum of the weights of the edges crossing the cut; in an unweighted graph, it is the number of crossing edges.

Minimum cuts are closely tied to the problem of computing the maximum flow through a network. The celebrated max-flow min-cut theorem states that the maximum amount of flow that can pass from a source to a sink equals the total weight of the edges in a minimum s–t cut.

Variants

The s–t cut problem separates a specified source s from a specified sink t. The global minimum cut separates the graph into any two nonempty parts; it can be found in polynomial time, for example by the Stoer–Wagner algorithm, or randomized by Karger's algorithm, which contracts random edges and succeeds with probability at least 2/(n(n−1)). Repeating Karger's algorithm enough times finds the min cut with high probability.

Applications

Minimum cuts are used in image segmentation, community detection in networks, VLSI design, and the analysis of network reliability and connectivity. The edge connectivity of a graph is the weight of its global minimum cut.
