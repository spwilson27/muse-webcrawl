Romance films centre on the emotional journey of a couple in love.

Romance films are movies centred on the passion, emotion and romantic involvement of the main characters — typically the development, obstacles and resolution of a love relationship. The genre spans romantic drama, romantic comedy ("rom-com"), romantic thrillers and historical romance, and frequently overlaps with melodrama.

Characteristics

A romance film plot usually follows two people who meet, are drawn together, and must overcome obstacles — class differences, family opposition, misunderstandings, rivals, distance or their own fears — before a resolution that unites them (or, in tragic variants, separates them definitively). Common devices include the "meet-cute", love triangles, mistaken identity and the climactic declaration of love.

History

Romance was present from cinema's earliest decades, in adaptations of novels such as Wuthering Heights and Pride and Prejudice. Hollywood's Golden Age produced classics including Casablanca (1942) and Roman Holiday (1953), while the screwball comedies of the 1930s shaped the romantic comedy. The genre had a commercial resurgence in the late 1980s and 1990s with films such as When Harry Met Sally..., Pretty Woman, Four Weddings and a Funeral and Titanic (1997), the latter long the highest-grossing film of all time. In the 21st century theatrical romances declined in Hollywood as studios favoured franchise films, and the genre migrated substantially to streaming services and to international industries such as Bollywood, where romance remains central.

Subgenres

Romantic comedy treats love humorously, usually with a happy ending. Romantic drama emphasises emotional seriousness and may end unhappily, as in Brief Encounter. Romantic thrillers combine love stories with suspense. Historical and costume romances adapt classic literature or set stories in earlier eras.
