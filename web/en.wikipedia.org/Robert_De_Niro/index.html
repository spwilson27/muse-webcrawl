Robert Anthony De Niro (born 17 August 1943) is an American actor and film producer. Known for his collaborations with Martin Scorsese, he is considered one of the greatest and most influential actors of his generation. De Niro is the recipient of various accolades, including two Academy Awards, a Golden Globe Award, the Cecil B. DeMille Award, and a Screen Actors Guild Life Achievement Award.

Career

De Niro studied acting at HB Studio, the Stella Adler Conservatory, and Lee Strasberg's Actors Studio. His first collaboration with Scorsese was Mean Streets (1973). He won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for playing the young Vito Corleone in The Godfather Part II (1974) and the Academy Award for Best Actor for portraying boxer Jake LaMotta in Raging Bull (1980), for which he famously gained 60 pounds.

His other celebrated roles include Travis Bickle in Taxi Driver (1976), Michael Vronsky in The Deer Hunter (1978), Rupert Pupkin in The King of Comedy (1982), Jimmy Conway in Goodfellas (1990), Max Cady in Cape Fear (1991), and Frank Sheeran in The Irishman (2019). He received an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actor for his role in Killers of the Flower Moon (2023).

Business and festival work

De Niro co-founded the Tribeca Film Festival in 2002 in the aftermath of the September 11 attacks, to promote economic and cultural revitalization of Lower Manhattan. He is also a co-founder of the production company Tribeca Productions and of the Nobu restaurant and hotel group.
