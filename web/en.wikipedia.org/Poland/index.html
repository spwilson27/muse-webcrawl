Poland

Poland, officially the Republic of Poland, is a country in Central Europe. It is divided into 16 administrative voivodeship provinces, covering an area of 312,696 km², and has a population of over 38 million, making it the fifth-most populous member state of the European Union. Its capital and largest metropolis is Warsaw; other major cities include Kraków, Gdańsk, Wrocław, Poznań, and Łódź. Poland shares borders with Germany to the west; the Czech Republic and Slovakia to the south; Ukraine and Belarus to the east; and Lithuania and Russia to the northeast, with the Baltic Sea to the north.

History

The Kingdom of Poland emerged under the Piast dynasty around 1025, following the Christianisation of Poland in 966. From 1569, the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth was one of Europe's largest and most populous states. The partitions of the late 18th century erased Poland from the map for 123 years until independence was regained in 1918. Invaded by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union in September 1939, Poland suffered catastrophic losses in the Second World War. Post-war Poland became a communist satellite state; the Solidarity movement of the 1980s helped bring about the peaceful transition to democracy in 1989.

Geography and economy

Poland's terrain ranges from the Baltic coast and northern lakelands to the Carpathian and Sudetes mountains in the south; the Vistula is its longest river. It has a developed, high-income economy, the EU's sixth largest by GDP (nominal), with strong manufacturing, agriculture, and service sectors.

Culture

Poland has produced figures of global renown, including Nicolaus Copernicus, Marie Curie, Frédéric Chopin, and Pope John Paul II, and is known for its medieval Old Towns, 17 UNESCO World Heritage Sites, and hearty cuisine.
