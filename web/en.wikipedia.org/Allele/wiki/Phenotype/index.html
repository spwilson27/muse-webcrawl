Phenotype

An organism's phenotype is the set of its observable characteristics or traits — its morphology, development, biochemical and physiological properties, and behaviour. The phenotype results from the expression of the organism's genetic code (its genotype) together with the influence of environmental factors, and from interactions between the two.

The shells of Donax variabilis show striking phenotypic variation within one species.

Genotype–phenotype distinction

Wilhelm Johannsen's 1909 distinction between genotype and phenotype is foundational to genetics: inheritance transmits the genotype, while natural selection acts on phenotypes. Two organisms with the same genotype may differ in phenotype through phenotypic plasticity — the capacity of one genotype to produce different outcomes in different environments, as when water fleas grow defensive helmets in the presence of predators.

Variation and study

Phenotypic variation underlies evolution by natural selection. Modern biology studies phenotypes at every scale, from molecules (gene expression profiles) to whole organisms and behaviour. Large-scale "phenomics" and deep phenotyping in medicine aim to characterise phenotypes as systematically as genomes, linking them to genotypes in genome-wide association studies.
