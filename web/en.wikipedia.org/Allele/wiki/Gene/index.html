Gene

In biology, the word gene has several related meanings. The Mendelian gene is a basic unit of heredity: a discrete factor passed from parent to offspring that influences a trait. The molecular gene is a sequence of nucleotides in DNA (or RNA, in some viruses) that is transcribed to produce a functional product, either a protein or a functional RNA molecule. Most living things encode their genes in DNA; the human genome contains roughly 20,000 protein-coding genes.

History

The concept descends from Mendel's "factors" (1865), rediscovered in 1900. Wilhelm Johannsen coined the word "gene" in 1909. The one gene–one enzyme hypothesis of Beadle and Tatum (1941), the identification of DNA as the hereditary material, and the double helix of 1953 progressively gave the gene a molecular identity.

Structure and expression

A typical protein-coding gene includes a promoter, exons that survive in the mature transcript, introns that are spliced out, and untranslated regions. Gene expression — transcription followed by translation — is tightly regulated, allowing cells with identical genomes to differentiate into specialised types. Changes in gene sequence, called mutations, are the source of alleles and of heritable variation.
