Locus (genetics)

In genetics, a locus (plural loci) is a specific, fixed position on a chromosome where a particular gene or genetic marker is located. Each chromosome carries many genes, each at its own locus; the complete set of loci for an organism is its genome. A variant of the DNA sequence at a given locus is called an allele.

Each gene occupies a characteristic locus on a chromosome.

Nomenclature

Cytogenetic notation specifies a locus by chromosome, arm (p for the short arm, q for the long arm), and band numbers counted from the centromere: for example, the cystic fibrosis gene CFTR is at 7q31.2 — chromosome 7, long arm, region 3, band 1, sub-band 2. Loci used in forensics and linkage analysis need not be genes; any identifiable DNA sequence, such as a microsatellite, can serve as a marker locus.

Use in mapping

Genetic mapping determines the relative order of loci along a chromosome from recombination frequencies: loci far apart assort nearly independently, while closely linked loci are inherited together. A quantitative trait locus (QTL) is a locus whose alleles contribute to variation in a measurable trait.
