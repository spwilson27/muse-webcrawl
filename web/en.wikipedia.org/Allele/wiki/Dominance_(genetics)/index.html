Dominance (genetics)

In genetics, dominance is the phenomenon in which one variant (allele) of a gene masks or overrides the effect of a different variant of the same gene in a heterozygote. The allele that is expressed is called dominant, and the allele that is masked is called recessive. Dominance is a relationship between alleles, not an inherent property of a single allele, and it applies to a particular phenotype.

A Punnett square showing a monohybrid cross with complete dominance.

Complete and incomplete dominance

Under complete dominance, the heterozygote's phenotype is indistinguishable from that of the dominant homozygote — as with Mendel's purple-flowered pea plants. Under incomplete dominance, the heterozygote shows an intermediate phenotype, such as pink flowers in snapdragons crossed from red and white parents. In codominance, both alleles are fully expressed, as in the AB blood type, where both A and B antigens appear on red blood cells.

Mechanisms

Many recessive alleles are loss-of-function mutations: one working copy of the gene still produces enough functional protein, so the heterozygote appears normal, while the homozygote lacks the protein and shows the trait. Dominant disorders such as Huntington's disease arise when a single copy of a harmful allele is sufficient to cause disease, often through gain-of-function or dominant-negative effects.
