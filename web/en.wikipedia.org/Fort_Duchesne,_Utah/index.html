Fort Duchesne, Utah

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Duchesne is a census-designated place (CDP) in Duchesne County, Utah, United States. It is located within the Uintah and Ouray Reservation of the Northern Ute Tribe and serves as the headquarters of the Ute Tribe of the Uintah and Ouray Reservation.

History

Fort Duchesne was established in August 1886 by Major Frederick W. Benteen with two companies of the 9th Cavalry, the African-American "Buffalo Soldiers", during the period of the Ute conflicts in the Uintah Basin. The post was garrisoned until 1912. After the Army abandoned the fort, the site became the agency headquarters for the Northern Ute Tribe.

The community remains the seat of tribal government for the Ute Tribe of the Uintah and Ouray Reservation, one of the largest reservations in the northern United States. Tribal offices, the Ute Indian Tribe administration, and related services are centered there.

Geography

Fort Duchesne lies in the Uintah Basin of northeastern Utah, near the confluence of the Duchesne and Strawberry river drainages, roughly eight miles southeast of Roosevelt, along U.S. Route 40. The elevation is approximately 4,950 feet (1,510 m) above sea level, in high desert terrain between the Uinta Mountains to the north and the Book Cliffs to the south.

Demographics

The population of the CDP was a few hundred residents at recent censuses, the large majority of whom are Native American, principally members of the Ute Tribe. The economy is supported by tribal government, oil and gas activity in the Uintah Basin, ranching and services.
