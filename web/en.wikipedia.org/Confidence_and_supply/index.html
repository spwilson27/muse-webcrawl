Confidence and supply

In a parliamentary democracy, confidence and supply (C&S) is an arrangement under which a party or independent legislators agree to support the government in motions of confidence and appropriation (supply) votes, by voting in favour or abstaining. In return, the governing party may consult the supporting party on policy or offer concessions.

How it works

Unlike a formal coalition, the supporting party does not join the cabinet and remains free to vote against the government on other legislation. The arrangement guarantees the government can pass budgets and survive no-confidence motions — the two votes whose loss traditionally forces a government's resignation or an election.

Examples

United Kingdom

The 1977–78 Lib–Lab pact sustained James Callaghan's Labour government. In 2017, after losing her majority, Theresa May's Conservative government reached a confidence and supply agreement with the Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) of Northern Ireland, accompanied by additional funding for the region.

Canada, Australia and New Zealand

Confidence and supply deals are common for minority governments in Canada (for instance the 2022 Liberal–NDP agreement, ended in 2024) and in Australia. In New Zealand, under mixed-member proportional representation, such agreements — sometimes with ministers appointed outside cabinet — are a standard feature of government formation.

Spain

Spanish minority governments have relied on analogous investiture and budget pacts with regional parties.
