Plant breeding

Plant breeding is the science of changing the traits of plants in order to produce desired characteristics, such as higher yield, disease resistance, drought tolerance or improved quality. It has been practised since the beginning of agriculture through selection of the best plants, and was placed on a scientific footing with the rediscovery of Mendel's laws of inheritance around 1900.

Techniques

Breeders use selection, hybridisation between varieties or species, backcrossing, induced mutation and, in modern programmes, marker-assisted selection and genetic engineering. The Green Revolution of the mid-20th century, led by Norman Borlaug, produced dwarf, high-yielding wheat and rice varieties that greatly increased food production.

Goals

Modern breeding targets yield stability under climate change, nutritional quality, resistance to pests and pathogens, and reduced need for fertiliser and water.
