Plant hunting

Plant hunting is the activity of searching for, collecting and transporting new or rare plants for cultivation and scientific study. At its height in the Victorian era, expeditions sponsored by botanic gardens and commercial nurseries introduced rhododendrons, orchids, conifers and countless other plants from Asia, the Americas and Africa into Western gardens.

Methods

Collectors gathered seeds, bulbs and living specimens, pressing samples for herbaria and keeping detailed field notes. Transport was revolutionised by the sealed Wardian case, which protected plants from salt spray and conserved fresh water on long voyages.

Legacy

Many familiar garden plants descend from these introductions, but some introductions became invasive species. Modern collecting follows access and benefit-sharing rules under international biodiversity agreements.
