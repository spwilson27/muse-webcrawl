Gavrilo Princip (25 July 1894 – 28 April 1918) was a Bosnian Serb student who assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir presumptive to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg, in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. The assassination set in motion a chain of events — the July Crisis — that led within weeks to the outbreak of the First World War.

Early life

Princip was born in the village of Obljaj in western Bosnia, then under Austro-Hungarian rule, into a poor peasant family. He studied in Sarajevo and Tuzla, and in 1912 moved to Belgrade, where he became involved with Young Bosnia, a loosely organised movement of students seeking the liberation and unification of South Slavs. He was recruited, along with Nedeljko Čabrinović and Trifko Grabež, to carry out the assassination during the Archduke's visit, and was armed and trained with help from members of the Serbian Black Hand organisation.

The assassination

After Čabrinović's bomb attack failed earlier that morning, Princip encountered the Archduke's car by chance near the Latin Bridge when it stopped after taking a wrong turn. He fired two shots with an FN Model 1910 pistol, killing both Franz Ferdinand and Sophie. Too young for the death penalty under Austro-Hungarian law, Princip was sentenced to 20 years' imprisonment.

Death and legacy

Princip died of tuberculosis in the prison at Terezín in April 1918, months before the war ended. His legacy remains sharply contested: he is regarded by many Serbs as a national hero and freedom fighter, while others view him as a terrorist whose act unleashed catastrophe.
