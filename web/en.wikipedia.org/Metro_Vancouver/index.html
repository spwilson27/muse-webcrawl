Metro Vancouver is a metropolitan area and regional district in the Lower Mainland of British Columbia, Canada, centred on the city of Vancouver. The Metro Vancouver Regional District (formerly the Greater Vancouver Regional District) is a federation of 21 municipalities, one electoral area and one treaty First Nation that provides regional services such as drinking water, sewage, solid waste management, regional parks and housing.

Geography and members

The region lies between the Coast Mountains and the Strait of Georgia, around the mouth of the Fraser River. Its major municipalities include Vancouver, Surrey (nearly equal in population to Vancouver), Burnaby, Richmond, Coquitlam, Langley, Delta, North Vancouver and Maple Ridge. The University of British Columbia lands make up Electoral Area A. The Tsawwassen First Nation joined the regional district as a treaty member.

Regional governance

Created in 1967, the regional district delivers water from mountain reservoirs in the Capilano, Seymour and Coquitlam watersheds, treats wastewater, operates regional parks, runs the affordable housing corporation, and plans regional growth through a growth strategy that protects agricultural and industrial land and concentrates development in urban centres. Public transport is handled separately by TransLink, which runs the SkyTrain rapid transit network, buses and the West Coast Express commuter railway.

Population and economy

With about 2.6 million people, Metro Vancouver is Canada's third-largest metropolitan area. It is exceptionally multicultural — roughly half its residents belong to a visible minority group and large communities speak Punjabi, Cantonese and Mandarin. The Port of Vancouver is Canada's largest port; other economic pillars include film and television production ("Hollywood North"), technology, tourism and international education. The region consistently ranks among the world's most liveable — and most expensive — cities, with housing affordability a dominant political issue.
