Florence Griffith Joyner

Florence Delorez Griffith Joyner (born Florence Delorez Griffith; December 21, 1959 – September 21, 1998), also known as Flo-Jo, was an American track and field athlete and the fastest woman ever recorded. She set world records in 1988 for both the 100 m and 200 m that still stand. During the late 1980s she became a popular figure in international sport for her record-breaking performances as well as her flamboyant personal style, including one-legged racing suits and long, decorated fingernails.

Career

Griffith was born in Los Angeles, California, and ran track at UCLA, where she was coached by Bob Kersee. She won a silver medal in the 200 m at the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics and another silver with the 4 × 400 m relay team at the 1987 World Championships. In 1987 she married Al Joyner, the 1984 Olympic triple jump champion; her sister-in-law was the heptathlon and long jump star Jackie Joyner-Kersee.

Her breakthrough came at the 1988 US Olympic Trials in Indianapolis, where she ran the 100 m in 10.49 seconds, smashing the world record — a mark that has never been approached since. At the Seoul Olympics later that year she won gold medals in the 100 m, 200 m (in a world record 21.34 seconds) and the 4 × 100 m relay, plus silver in the 4 × 400 m relay. She was named AP Female Athlete of the Year and won the Sullivan Award for 1988.

Later life and death

Griffith Joyner retired from athletics in February 1989. She worked in business, design and fitness promotion, and co-chaired the President's Council on Physical Fitness under President Bill Clinton. She died in her sleep on September 21, 1998, aged 38, at her home in Mission Viejo, California; the cause was an epileptic seizure caused by a congenital cerebral vascular malformation (cavernous angioma). Her sudden improvement in 1988 had prompted persistent speculation about performance-enhancing drugs, but she passed all drug tests during her career and no evidence of doping was ever produced.
