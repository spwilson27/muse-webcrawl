Astoria, Queens

Astoria
Neighborhood of Queens, New York City
Borough: Queens
Community Board: Queens CB 1
Population: approx. 78,000 (2010 census area estimate)

Astoria is a neighborhood in the western portion of the borough of Queens in New York City. It is bounded by the East River and is adjacent to three other Queens neighborhoods: Long Island City to the southwest, Sunnyside to the southeast, and Woodside to the east. As of 2019, Astoria has an estimated population of 95,446.

Originally the site of a village called Hallet's Cove, which was incorporated on April 12, 1839, Astoria was renamed for John Jacob Astor, then the wealthiest man in America, in order to persuade him to invest in the neighborhood. He only invested $500, but the name stayed. During the second half of the 19th century, economic and commercial growth brought increased immigration from Germany, followed by Italians and many other ethnicities.

History

The area now known as Astoria was originally called Hallet's Cove, after its first landowner William Hallet, who settled there in 1652 with his wife, Elizabeth Fones. Beginning in the early 19th century, wealthy New Yorkers constructed large residences around 12th and 14th streets, an area that later became known as Astoria Village.

Incorporated as a village on April 12, 1839, the settlement was renamed for John Jacob Astor. From 1860 to 1890 the area grew as a center for German immigrants, and the Steinway family established a settlement for the workers of their piano factory, including the Steinway Mansion. Astoria and several other surrounding villages, including Steinway, were incorporated into Long Island City in 1870, which itself was incorporated into the City of Greater New York in 1898.

Demographics

Astoria is known for its ethnic diversity. It has long been home to one of the largest Greek communities outside Greece, concentrated around 30th Avenue and Ditmars Boulevard. Italian, Irish, Egyptian, Bangladeshi, and Latin American communities are also well represented, and the Steinway Street area is sometimes called "Little Egypt" for its concentration of hookah lounges and Arab businesses.

Transportation

Astoria is served by the New York City Subway's BMT Astoria Line (N and W trains), terminating at Ditmars Boulevard. The M60 Select Bus Service connects the neighborhood to LaGuardia Airport, and the NYC Ferry's Astoria route began service in 2017.
