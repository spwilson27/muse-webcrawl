The seven seals are a series of seals securing the scroll or book in the Book of Revelation (chapters 5–8) of the New Testament. In John's vision, a scroll in the right hand of God is sealed with seven seals, and only the Lamb of God — Christ — is found worthy to open them. Breaking each seal unleashes events and judgments on the earth, forming the first of Revelation's three great cycles of seven (seals, trumpets, bowls).

The seals

  • First seal: a white horse; its rider, holding a bow and given a crown, goes out conquering. Often interpreted as conquest.
  • Second seal: a red horse; its rider takes peace from the earth, and people slay one another — war.
  • Third seal: a black horse; its rider holds scales — famine and economic scarcity.
  • Fourth seal: a pale horse; its rider is Death, followed by Hades, with authority to kill a quarter of the earth.
  • Fifth seal: the souls of martyrs under the altar cry out for justice and are given white robes and told to wait.
  • Sixth seal: a great earthquake; the sun turns black, the moon blood-red, stars fall, and the sky recedes like a scroll — cosmic upheaval.
  • Seventh seal: silence in heaven for about half an hour, followed by the giving of the seven trumpets to seven angels.

The first four riders are collectively known as the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse.

Interpretation

Interpretations vary by school: preterists link the seals to first-century events such as the fall of Jerusalem; historicists to successive ages of church history; futurists to end-time tribulation; and idealists to timeless patterns of suffering and judgment. In popular culture, "breaking the seventh seal" became proverbial through Ingmar Bergman's 1957 film The Seventh Seal.
