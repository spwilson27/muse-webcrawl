Pole of inaccessibility

A pole of inaccessibility with respect to a geographical criterion of inaccessibility marks the location that is the most challenging to reach according to that criterion. Often it refers to the most distant point from the coastline, implying a maximum degree of continentality or oceanity. The term "pole of inaccessibility" usually is not applied in its literal sense, but rather as a conceptual construct, due to formal definitions of distance measurement as well as political and geographical peculiarities.

Oceanic pole of inaccessibility

The oceanic pole of inaccessibility, popularly known as Point Nemo (Latin for "nobody"), is located in the South Pacific Ocean at approximately 48°52.6′S 123°23.6′W, some 2,688 km (1,670 mi) from the nearest land. It is so remote that the closest humans are often astronauts aboard the International Space Station when it passes overhead. The area is also used as a "spacecraft cemetery" for the disposal of decommissioned satellites and space stations.

Continental poles of inaccessibility

The continental pole of inaccessibility of Eurasia, the landmass's point farthest from any ocean, lies in northwestern China near the border with Kazakhstan, in the Xinjiang region, at roughly 2,645 km from the nearest coastline. Other continents have their own continental poles: North America's lies in South Dakota, South America's in Brazil, Africa's near the border of the Central African Republic and South Sudan, and Australia's near the centre of the continent.

Antarctic pole of inaccessibility

The southern pole of inaccessibility, the point on Antarctica farthest from the Southern Ocean, is notoriously difficult to pin down owing to the ice sheet, but is commonly placed near the former Soviet Pole of Inaccessibility research station, where a bust of Vladimir Lenin still stands atop a building buried in snow.
