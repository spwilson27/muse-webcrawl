Military band

A military band is a group of personnel that performs musical duties for military functions, usually ceremonial, but also for morale and public relations. A typical military band consists of a wind ensemble — brass, woodwind and percussion sections — and performs marches, national anthems, and ceremonial music at parades, guard mountings, state occasions and tattoos.

[Image: A military band marching on parade in full dress uniform]

History

Music has accompanied armies since ancient times through trumpets, drums and fifes used for signalling. The organised military band grew out of the Janissary (mehter) bands of the Ottoman Empire, whose instrumentation — large drums, cymbals, triangles and Turkish crescents — was adopted across 18th-century Europe. By the 19th century bands had standardised around the brass-and-reed instrumentation that composers such as John Philip Sousa, "The March King", later made famous with his own professional band.

Roles

  • Ceremonial duties: parades, trooping the colour, state funerals, changing of the guard
  • Morale and entertainment of troops, historically including deployment zones
  • Public engagement: concerts, tattoos (such as the Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo) and recordings
  • Field music roles: buglers, drummers and pipers for signals and commemorations

Notable bands

Among the most prominent are the bands of the British Household Division, the United States Marine Band ("The President's Own", established 1798), the United States Army Band "Pershing's Own", and the French Republican Guard Band. Some services also maintain full orchestras, choirs, and bagpipe bands (pipes and drums).
