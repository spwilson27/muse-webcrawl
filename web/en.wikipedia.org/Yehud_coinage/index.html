Yehud coinage

RegionYehud Medinata (Persian province of Judah)
Periodc. 4th century BC – early Hellenistic period
MaterialSilver (small denominations)

The Yehud coinage is a series of small silver coins minted in the Persian province of Yehud (Judah) during the late Persian and early Hellenistic periods, beginning around the mid-fourth century BC. They are among the earliest coins struck in the southern Levant.

Types

The coins bear the Aramaic inscription yhd or yhwd ("Yehud") in Aramaic or paleo-Hebrew script. Designs imitate Athenian coinage, including the head of Athena and her owl, alongside local motifs such as lilies, falcons, and the head of a Persian king. Some rare issues name officials, including a high priest and a governor.

Significance

Because of their tiny size — many weigh less than half a gram — the coins likely served local small transactions. Their findspots cluster around Jerusalem, and they provide important evidence for the administration and economy of Persian-period Judah.
