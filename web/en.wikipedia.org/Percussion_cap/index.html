Percussion cap

The percussion cap is a small cylinder of copper or brass containing a shock-sensitive explosive, such as mercury fulminate, used to ignite the propellant in a firearm. Placed over a hollow nipple on the breech, it is struck by the hammer, explodes, and sends flame through the nipple to the main charge.

Percussion cap
Invented: early 19th century
Material: copper or brass cup
Compound: mercury fulminate, later chlorate mixtures
Replaced: flintlock ignition

History

Percussion ignition developed in the early 1800s following Alexander Forsyth's 1807 patent using fulminates. Small caps became practical in the 1820s and were widely adopted by the 1830s–1840s, replacing flintlocks in military and sporting arms because they were faster, more reliable in wet weather, and simpler to use.

Use

Caplock muzzleloaders were loaded with powder and ball from the muzzle, then a cap was placed on the nipple before firing. Percussion caps also underpin the later self-contained metallic cartridge, where the priming compound is integrated into the cartridge case as a primer.
