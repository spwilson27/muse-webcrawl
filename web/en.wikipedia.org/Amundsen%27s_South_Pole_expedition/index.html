The South Pole expedition of 1910–1912, led by the Norwegian explorer Roald Amundsen, was the first to reach the geographic South Pole. Amundsen and four companions arrived at the pole on 14 December 1911, five weeks before Robert Falcon Scott's British party.

Preparation

Amundsen originally planned to reach the North Pole but, after Frederick Peary's claim in 1909, secretly redirected his expedition south. He established his base, Framheim, at the Bay of Whales on the Ross Ice Shelf — closer to the pole than Scott's base at McMurdo Sound — and relied on skis and Greenland dogs, laying supply depots along the route in the autumn of 1911.

The polar journey

The polar party — Amundsen, Oscar Wisting, Helmer Hanssen, Sverre Hassel, and Olav Bjaaland — set out on 19 October 1911, crossed the Transantarctic Mountains via the Axel Heiberg Glacier, and reached 90°S on 14 December. They planted the Norwegian flag, named the site Polheim, and returned to Framheim on 25 January 1912, having lost none of the party.

Aftermath

Amundsen announced the achievement publicly from Hobart, Tasmania, in March 1912. Scott's party reached the pole on 17 January 1912 and perished on the return journey.
