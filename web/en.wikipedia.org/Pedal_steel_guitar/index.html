Pedal steel guitar

The pedal steel guitar is a console-type of steel guitar with pedals and knee levers that change the pitch of certain strings, enabling more varied and complex passages than on a non-pedal steel guitar. Developed from Hawaiian and lap steel traditions, it became a signature sound of American country music.

Pedal steel guitar
Type: Electric console steel guitar
Common tunings: E9, C6
Associated genres: Country, Western swing

Mechanism

The player sits at a horizontal console, sliding a polished steel bar along the strings with one hand while plucking with the other. Foot pedals and knee levers pull or release strings through a mechanical linkage, raising or lowering pitches mid-phrase to produce the instrument's characteristic smooth chord changes and crying glissandi.

History

Pedal mechanisms appeared in the 1940s; players such as Bud Isaacs, whose playing on Webb Pierce's "Slowly" (1953) popularized the sound, and innovators like Buddy Emmons and Jimmy Day standardized the E9 "Nashville" tuning and copedent in the 1950s–60s.

Use

Beyond country, pedal steel appears in rock, jazz, gospel ("sacred steel") and ambient music, valued for its vocal, sustaining tone.
