Ornament (music)

In music, an ornament or embellishment is a decorative flourish added to a melody, either written by the composer or improvised by the performer. Ornaments were especially important in Baroque music, where performers were expected to embellish the written score.

Types

Common ornaments include the trill (rapid alternation with the note above), the mordent, the turn, the appoggiatura (a leaning note taking half the value of the main note), the acciaccatura or grace note, the glissando, and the tremolo. Each has its own notation symbol, usually placed above or beside the note it decorates.

History

Ornamentation was described in treatises by composers such as C. P. E. Bach. In the Baroque era, French composers used tables of ornaments (agréments). By the Classical and Romantic periods, composers increasingly wrote out embellishments in full, though improvised ornamentation remained central in traditions such as jazz and Indian classical music.
