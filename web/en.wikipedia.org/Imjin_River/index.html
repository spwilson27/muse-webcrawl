Imjin River

Length: about 273 km
Source: Mount Aho, North Korea
Mouth: Han River estuary, Yellow Sea

The Imjin River (Korean: 임진강) is a river in North and South Korea. It flows from north to south, crossing the Korean Demilitarized Zone, and joins the Han River downstream of Seoul before emptying into the Yellow Sea. At about 273 kilometres it is the seventh-longest river on the Korean Peninsula.

Geography

The Imjin rises in the Rangrim Mountains of North Korea and flows southwest through the central peninsula. Its basin includes tributaries such as the Hantan River. Because the river crosses the DMZ, access to much of its length is restricted, and flooding during the monsoon — including releases from North Korean dams — has periodically caused damage and loss of life in the South.

History

The river was a major strategic barrier during the Imjin War of 1592–1598, the Japanese invasions of Korea, and again during the Korean War. The Battle of the Imjin River in April 1951, in which the British Gloucestershire Regiment famously held a position against Chinese forces, is one of the best-known engagements of that war.
