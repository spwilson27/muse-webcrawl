Edam is a town in the northwest Netherlands, in the province of North Holland. Combined with Volendam, it forms the municipality of Edam-Volendam. Edam is the namesake of Edam cheese, traditionally sold in the town's markets in round, red- or yellow-waxed forms.

History

The town grew around a dam on the E or IJe river — "E-dam". It received city rights in 1357 and prospered through trade, shipbuilding, and herring fishery. In the Dutch Golden Age Edam was an important port with one of the largest shipyards in Holland. The cheese market, held since the 16th century, was revived as a tourist demonstration: farmers bring cheese by boat and horse-drawn cart, and carriers in traditional dress weigh and trade the cheeses during summer market days.

Landmarks

The Grote Kerk (Great Church) of St. Nicholas, begun around 1400, is notable for its stained-glass windows donated by neighbouring towns after a fire. Other sights include the leaning Carillon tower, the town hall (1737), and the Edams Museum, partly housed in a floating cellar.
