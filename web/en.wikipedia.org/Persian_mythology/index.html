Persian mythology

Persian mythology is the body of myths originally told by the ancient Persians and other Iranian peoples. It centres on the cosmic struggle between good and evil, rooted in Zoroastrian dualism, and on the heroic legends of kings and champions preserved in the Shahnameh (Book of Kings), composed by Ferdowsi around 1010 CE.

Sources

The primary religious source is the Avesta, the scriptures of Zoroastrianism, containing hymns (the Gathas) attributed to the prophet Zoroaster. Middle Persian (Pahlavi) texts such as the Bundahishn recount creation and cosmology, while the Shahnameh preserves the legendary history of Iran from the first king to the Arab conquest.

Key figures

  • Ahura Mazda — the wise lord, supreme god of good and light
  • Angra Mainyu (Ahriman) — the destructive spirit, source of evil
  • Mithra — god of covenant, light and oaths
  • Rostam — the greatest hero of the Shahnameh, famed for his seven labours (Haft Khan) and his tragic battle with his son Sohrab
  • Zahhak — the serpent-shouldered tyrant overthrown by Fereydun
  • Simurgh — the wise, benevolent giant bird
  • Jamshid — legendary king who fell through hubris

Themes

Central themes include the dualism of asha (truth/order) versus druj (falsehood), the sanctity of fire and water, the hero's trials, and eschatology: the coming of the saviour Saoshyant and the final renovation of the world, Frashokereti.
