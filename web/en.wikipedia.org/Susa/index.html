Susa

The archaeological site of Susa, one of the oldest settlements in the region.

Susa was an ancient city in the lower Zagros Mountains about 250 km (160 mi) east of the Tigris, in modern-day Iran. One of the most important cities of the Ancient Near East, Susa served as the capital of Elam and the winter capital of the Achaemenid Empire, and remained a strategic centre under the Parthian and Sasanian empires.

The modern Iranian town of Shush is located on the site of ancient Susa. Shush is identified as Shushan, mentioned in the Book of Esther and other biblical books.

History

Susa was inhabited from the late 5th millennium BCE and was a major centre of the Elamite civilization. It came under Persian control under Cyrus the Great and was rebuilt by Darius I, who made it a royal capital with a great palace. Alexander the Great captured the city in 331 BCE, and it later passed through Seleucid, Parthian, and Sasanian hands before the Muslim conquest in the 7th century CE.

Excavations

French-led excavations in the late 19th and 20th centuries uncovered major finds, including the Code of Hammurabi stele (brought to Susa by an Elamite king as war booty), the Victory Stele of Naram-Sin, and extensive Achaemenid palace remains.
