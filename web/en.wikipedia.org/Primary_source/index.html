Primary source

In the study of history as an academic discipline, a primary source (also called an original source) is an artifact, document, diary, manuscript, autobiography, recording, or any other source of information that was created at the time under study. It serves as an original source of information about the topic. Similar definitions can be used in other fields, such as the natural sciences, where a primary source might be the first formal report of experimental results.

Contrast with secondary sources

Primary sources are distinguished from secondary sources, which cite, comment on, or build upon primary sources. A secondary source is a document or recording that relates or discusses information originally presented elsewhere, generally at some remove in time and often involving analysis, interpretation, or evaluation. Tertiary sources compile and summarise secondary sources — encyclopedias and textbooks are typical examples.

Examples

Typical primary sources in history include letters, diaries, speeches, interviews, official records, court transcripts, census data, photographs, newspaper reports written by eyewitnesses, and physical artifacts. In the sciences, a journal article reporting new experimental data is a primary source; in literature, the work itself — a novel, poem, or play — is the primary source.

Use in scholarship

Historians prize primary sources as the closest evidence to the events, people, and cultures they study, but treat them critically: a source's status as "primary" depends on the question being asked, and every source reflects the perspective, purpose, and limitations of its creator. The same document may serve as a primary source for one inquiry and a secondary source for another.
