Mass psychogenic illness

Other namesMass hysteria; epidemic hysteria
FieldPsychiatry, sociology
SymptomsRapid spread of symptoms without organic cause

Mass psychogenic illness (MPI), also called mass hysteria or mass sociogenic illness, is the rapid spread of illness symptoms through a population where there is no infectious or toxic agent capable of explaining them. The symptoms are real to those affected but are driven by psychological and social factors.

Characteristics

Outbreaks often occur in cohesive groups such as schools or workplaces, spreading by line of sight and social communication, and typically resolve once the group is separated or reassured.

Examples

Historical examples include the dancing plagues of medieval Europe, the 1962 Tanganyika laughter epidemic, and episodes of fainting or twitching spreading through schools.
