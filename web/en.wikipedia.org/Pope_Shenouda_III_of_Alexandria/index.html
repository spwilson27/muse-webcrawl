Pope Shenouda III (3 August 1923 – 17 March 2012) was the 117th Pope of Alexandria and Patriarch of the See of St. Mark, head of the Coptic Orthodox Church, serving for over forty years from 1971 until his death.

Life and papacy

Born Nazeer Gayed in Asyut, he became a monk under the name Antonios, lived as a hermit in the Wadi El Natrun desert, and was consecrated Bishop of Christian Education in 1962 before his election as pope. His reign saw great expansion of the Coptic Church in Egypt and the diaspora, growth of monasticism, and prolific preaching and writing.

Conflict and exile

In 1981 President Anwar Sadat banished Shenouda to a monastery amid tensions over sectarian violence and the peace treaty with Israel; he returned to Cairo in 1985 under President Mubarak. Shenouda died in 2012 and was succeeded by Pope Tawadros II.
