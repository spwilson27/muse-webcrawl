Vielle
Type: bowed string instrument
Period: medieval Europe, 10th–15th centuries
Related to: fiddle, viol, rebec, hurdy-gurdy (vielle à roue)

The vielle is a European bowed stringed instrument used in the medieval period, an ancestor of the modern violin family. Shaped variously — oval, waisted or guitar-like — it typically carried three to five strings tuned in fifths and fourths, played on the shoulder, against the chest, or resting on the lap.

History

The vielle (from Latin fidula, source also of "fiddle") was the principal bowed instrument of troubadours, trouvères and jongleurs from roughly the 10th to the 15th century, accompanying song, dance and epic recitation. Iconography in cathedral sculpture and illuminated manuscripts shows its many forms. As the viol and later the violin families developed in the Renaissance, the vielle declined, though its name survives in the wheel-bowed vielle à roue — the hurdy-gurdy.
