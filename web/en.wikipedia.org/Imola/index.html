Imola

Imola (Italian: [ˈiːmola]) is a city and comune in the Metropolitan City of Bologna, located on the river Santerno, in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy. The city is traditionally considered the western entrance to the historical region of Romagna. It has a population of around 70,000 and is internationally known for the Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari motor racing circuit.

History

The area was settled in antiquity and known in Roman times as Forum Cornelii, founded around 82 BC on the Via Aemilia. In the Middle Ages the city was contested by the Lombards, the papacy, and local lordships; it later came under the Manfredi and, famously, the Sforza — Caterina Sforza ruled Imola in the late 15th century before it was taken by Cesare Borgia. It subsequently formed part of the Papal States until Italian unification in the 19th century.

Main sights

Landmarks include the Rocca Sforzesca, a well-preserved 13th–15th-century fortress; the cathedral of San Cassiano; the Palazzo Tozzoni; and the public library's historic collections. Leonardo da Vinci drew a famous plan of Imola in 1502 while in Borgia service.

Motorsport

The Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari, opened in 1953, hosted the Formula One San Marino Grand Prix from 1981 to 2006 and the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix from 2020. The circuit is remembered with sorrow for the deaths of Roland Ratzenberger and Ayrton Senna during the 1994 race weekend.
