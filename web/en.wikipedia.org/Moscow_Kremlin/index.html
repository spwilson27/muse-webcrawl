Moscow Kremlin

The Moscow Kremlin (Russian: Московский Кремль) is a fortified complex in the centre of Moscow, overlooking the Moskva River to the south, Saint Basil's Cathedral and Red Square to the east, and Alexander Garden to the west. It is the best known of the kremlins (Russian citadels) and includes five palaces, four cathedrals, and the enclosing Kremlin Wall with Kremlin towers. The complex serves as the official residence of the President of the Russian Federation and as a museum. The word "Kremlin" is often used metonymically for the Russian government.

History

A fortified settlement existed on Borovitsky Hill from at least the 11th century; Yuri Dolgorukiy is credited with fortifying Moscow in 1156. The first stone walls were built under Dmitry Donskoy in 1366–1368. The present red-brick walls and towers were built by Italian architects between 1485 and 1495 under Ivan III. The Kremlin was the seat of the Grand Dukes of Moscow and the tsars until Peter the Great moved the capital to Saint Petersburg in 1712; it regained central importance after 1918, when the Soviet government moved back to Moscow.

Buildings

Cathedral Square is ringed by the Dormition (Uspensky) Cathedral, where tsars were crowned; the Archangel Cathedral, burial place of the rulers of Muscovy; and the Annunciation Cathedral. The Ivan the Great Bell Tower (81 m) long marked the city's skyline. Palaces include the Grand Kremlin Palace, the Terem Palace, and the Kremlin Senate, the presidential working residence. The Armoury Chamber museum holds the imperial regalia and the famous Fabergé eggs. The Tsar Cannon and Tsar Bell — the largest bell in the world — stand within the grounds.

The Kremlin today

Parts of the complex are open to visitors as the Moscow Kremlin Museums, while other areas remain restricted as the centre of Russian state power.
