Abuja is the capital and ninth most populous city of Nigeria. Located in the centre of the country within the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), it is a planned city built mainly in the 1980s based on a master plan by International Planning Associates of Pittsburgh. It replaced Lagos, the country's most populous city, as the capital city on 12 December 1991.

Abuja's geography is defined by Aso Rock, a 400-metre (1,300 ft) monolith left by water erosion, around which much of the city was built. The Presidential Complex, National Assembly, Supreme Court and much of the city extend to the south of the rock.

History

In the early 20th century, Abuja was the name of a nearby town, now called Suleja. The indigenous people of the region are the Gbagyi (Gwari), whose land was chosen in 1976 for a new capital because of its central location, easy accessibility, healthy climate, low population density and availability of land for future expansion. A decree in 1976 created the Federal Capital Territory, and construction began in earnest in the late 1970s and 1980s, led by the Federal Capital Development Authority.

On 12 December 1991, the seat of government was formally moved from Lagos to Abuja, although Lagos remains Nigeria's economic capital and largest city.

City districts

The city is divided into districts developed in phases. Phase 1 comprises the Central District, Maitama, Asokoro, Wuse and Garki, which host government ministries, diplomatic missions and upscale residences. Later phases include Gwarinpa, Utako, Jabi and satellite towns such as Kubwa, Lugbe and Nyanya, where much of the working population lives.

Landmarks

Notable landmarks include the Nigerian National Mosque, the National Christian Centre, the National Assembly Complex, the Presidential Villa at Aso Rock, Millennium Park, and the Abuja National Stadium (now Moshood Abiola National Stadium). Zuma Rock, a large igneous intrusion just north of the city on the road to Kaduna, is often depicted on the 100 naira note and is sometimes called the "Gateway to Abuja".

Climate

Abuja has a tropical wet and dry climate (Köppen Aw), with a rainy season from April to October and a dry season dominated for part of the year by the dusty harmattan trade winds from the Sahara.
