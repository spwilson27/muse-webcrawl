Multiverse

The multiverse is the hypothetical set of all universes. Together, these universes comprise everything that exists: the entirety of space, time, matter, energy, information, and the physical laws and constants that describe them. The different universes within the multiverse are called parallel universes, flat universes, or "alternate universes".

History

The idea of multiple worlds dates back to ancient Greek atomism. In modern physics, multiverse hypotheses arise in several theories: the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics proposed by Hugh Everett III in 1957, cosmic inflation theory, and string theory's landscape of possible vacua.

Classification

Physicist Max Tegmark proposed a hierarchy of multiverse levels, from universes beyond our cosmic horizon (Level I) through inflationary bubble universes (Level II), quantum many-worlds (Level III), to ultimate ensembles with different mathematical structures (Level IV). Critics argue such hypotheses are not testable and therefore fall outside empirical science, while supporters contend they are natural consequences of well-tested theories.
