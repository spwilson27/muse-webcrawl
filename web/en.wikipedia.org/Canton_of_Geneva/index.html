Canton of Geneva

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The canton of Geneva, officially the Republic and Canton of Geneva, is one of the 26 cantons of Switzerland. Located in the French-speaking western part of the country (Romandy), it surrounds the city of Geneva at the southwestern end of Lake Geneva, bordering France on nearly all sides.

History

Geneva was an independent city republic for centuries and a centre of the Reformation under John Calvin. It joined the Swiss Confederation in 1815 following the Congress of Vienna. In the 20th century Geneva became a major centre of international diplomacy.

International organisations

The canton hosts the European headquarters of the United Nations, the Red Cross, the World Health Organization, the World Trade Organization, and many other international bodies, as well as CERN near its border.

Demographics

With a population of around 500,000, Geneva is one of the most densely populated cantons. A large proportion of residents are foreign nationals, reflecting its international character. French is the official language.
