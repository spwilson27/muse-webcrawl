Weasel

Weasels are mammals of the genus Mustela of the family Mustelidae. The genus includes the least weasel, the stoat (ermine), and the long-tailed weasel, among others. Weasels are small, active predators with long, slender bodies and short legs, adapted for hunting rodents in burrows and dense cover.

Species

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Mammalia
Order: Carnivora
Family: Mustelidae
Genus: Mustela

The genus Mustela includes about seventeen species, among them the least weasel (M. nivalis), the stoat (M. erminea), the long-tailed weasel (M. frenata), the European polecat (M. putorius)—ancestor of the domestic ferret—and the critically endangered European mink (M. lutreola). The name "weasel" is also applied loosely to other small mustelids such as fishers and martens.

Biology

Weasels range in length from about 13 cm (least weasel, the smallest living carnivoran) to over 40 cm. Most species have brown upperparts and white underparts; several northern species, including the stoat and least weasel, turn white in winter. They are solitary, mostly nocturnal hunters that take mice, voles, rabbits, and birds, caching surplus prey. Their high metabolic rate requires them to eat a large fraction of their body weight daily.

Relation to humans

Weasels have been both valued as controllers of rodent pests and persecuted as predators of poultry and game birds. The stoat's winter pelt, ermine, was historically prized for ceremonial robes. In folklore and idiom, "weasel" has acquired a sly connotation, reflected in expressions such as "weasel words".
