Almost prime

In number theory, an almost prime is a natural number containing at most k prime factors, where k is fixed. More precisely, a number is called k-almost prime if it has exactly k prime factors, counted with multiplicity (Ω(n) = k). A 1-almost prime is simply a prime number; a 2-almost prime is a semiprime.

Almost prime
Notation: Ω(n) = k
k=1: prime; k=2: semiprime
Field: Analytic number theory

Examples

The 2-almost primes (semiprimes) begin 4, 6, 9, 10, 14, 15, 21, 22, 25, 26… The 3-almost primes begin 8, 12, 18, 20, 27, 28, 30, 42, 44, 45, 50…

Results

Almost primes are central to sieve theory and to progress toward Goldbach's conjecture and the twin prime conjecture. Chen's theorem (1973) states that every sufficiently large even number is the sum of a prime and a 2-almost prime (a product of at most two primes). The concept generalises in results about bounded gaps between primes and in the study of smooth numbers.

Formal definition

A natural number n is a k-almost prime if and only if Ω(n) = k, where Ω is the prime omega function counting total prime factors with multiplicity.
