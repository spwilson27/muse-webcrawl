Koi-Koi


Koi-Koi is a Japanese card game played with hanafuda cards.

Koi-Koi (Japanese: こいこい) is a popular card game in Japan played with hanafuda ("flower cards"), a deck of 48 cards divided into twelve suits of four, one for each month of the year, decorated with flowers and other seasonal imagery. The object of the game is to form scoring combinations (yaku) by matching cards by month.

Gameplay

Two players are each dealt eight cards, with eight more face-up on the table. On a turn, a player matches a card from hand with one of the same month on the table (or discards), then draws from the stock and matches again. When a player completes a yaku — such as collecting the three "light" cards with the rain man, or the boar, deer and butterfly set (ino-shika-chō) — they may stop and claim the points, or say "koi-koi" ("come on") to continue the round and risk losing their score in pursuit of more points.

History

Hanafuda evolved from Portuguese playing cards introduced to Japan in the 16th century, adapted during the Edo period when gambling with foreign-style cards was banned. Koi-Koi is one of several games played with the deck and appears widely in Japanese popular culture and video games.
