Pather Panchali (Bengali: পথের পাঁচালী, "Song of the Little Road") is a 1955 Indian Bengali-language drama film written and directed by Satyajit Ray, his directorial debut. Based on Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay's 1929 novel of the same name, it tells the story of Apu, a young boy in a poor village family in rural Bengal, and his elder sister Durga. The first part of Ray's Apu Trilogy (followed by Aparajito and Apur Sansar), it is widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made and a landmark of parallel cinema.

Plot

In the village of Nishchindipur in early 20th-century Bengal, the priest Harihar Roy struggles to support his wife Sarbajaya, his spirited daughter Durga and his young son Apu, along with the aged widowed aunt Indir. Durga steals fruit from a neighbour's orchard, the family sinks deeper into debt, and Harihar leaves to seek work. During his absence Durga falls ill after being caught in a monsoon storm and dies. When Harihar returns, unaware, and shows the sari he has bought for his daughter, Sarbajaya breaks down. The family leaves the ancestral home for Benares as a train carries them away.

Production

Inspired by Italian neorealism — especially Vittorio De Sica's Bicycle Thieves — Ray shot the film on location in the village of Boral with mostly non-professional actors, cinematographer Subrata Mitra and art director Bansi Chandragupta, on a shoestring budget assembled over several years. When funds ran out, the Government of West Bengal financed completion. The score was composed by Ravi Shankar.

Reception and legacy

The film premiered at the Museum of Modern Art in New York in May 1955 and was released in Calcutta that August. At the 1956 Cannes Film Festival it won the Best Human Document award, launching Ray's international reputation. Critics have consistently placed it among the finest films in cinema history; it appeared in the Sight and Sound critics' poll of the greatest films. It established the template of Indian art cinema and made Apu one of world cinema's enduring figures.
