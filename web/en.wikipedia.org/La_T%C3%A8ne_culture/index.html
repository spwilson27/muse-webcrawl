La Tène culture

The La Tène culture was a European Iron Age culture, named after the type site La Tène on the shore of Lake Neuchâtel in Switzerland, where thousands of objects were deposited in the lake from the 19th century onward. It developed out of the earlier Hallstatt culture from roughly 450 BCE until the Roman conquest era in the 1st century BCE.

Extent and society

Associated with the Celts, La Tène material culture spread across Gaul, Britain and Ireland, Iberia, the Danube basin and into Anatolia. It is noted for its curvilinear art — elaborate metalwork, torcs, decorated weapons and coinage — and for oppida, large fortified settlements that emerged in the later period.

Burials and deposits

Characteristic finds include chariot burials, weapons and jewellery deposited in rivers and lakes as votive offerings, and cemeteries with both inhumation and cremation.
