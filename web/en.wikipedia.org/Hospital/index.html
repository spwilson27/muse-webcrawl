Hospital

A hospital is a healthcare institution providing patient treatment with specialised health science and auxiliary healthcare staff and medical equipment. The best-known type is the general hospital, which typically has an emergency department to treat urgent health problems ranging from fire and accident victims to sudden illness, along with inpatient beds and surgical facilities. Other types include specialist hospitals for children, psychiatry, trauma, or rehabilitation, and teaching hospitals affiliated with universities.

History

Institutions caring for the sick existed in the ancient world, including temples in Egypt and Greece and hospitals in India and Persia. In medieval Europe, monasteries and religious orders maintained hospitals attached to abbeys, and the Islamic world developed sophisticated bimaristans offering free care. The modern hospital emerged in the 18th and 19th centuries with the professionalisation of medicine and nursing, antiseptic and anaesthetic surgery, and purpose-built pavilion-plan buildings such as those advocated by Florence Nightingale.

Organisation

Hospitals are generally staffed by physicians, nurses, and allied health professionals, and organised into departments such as internal medicine, surgery, obstetrics, paediatrics, radiology, and pathology, supported by laboratories and pharmacies. They may be funded publicly, run as private for-profit businesses, or operated by charities and religious organisations. District and regional hospitals form tiers within health systems, referring complex cases to larger centres.

Modern developments

Recent decades have seen shorter inpatient stays, growth of outpatient and day surgery, and increasing use of electronic health records and telemedicine. Hospitals remain central to acute and emergency care in virtually every health system.
