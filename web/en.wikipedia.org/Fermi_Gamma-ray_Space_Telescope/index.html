Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope

The Fermi observatory in Earth orbit

The Fermi Gamma-ray Space Telescope (FGST, previously the Gamma-ray Large Area Space Telescope, or GLAST) is a space observatory developed by NASA and international partners to perform gamma-ray astronomy. It was launched on 11 June 2008 aboard a Delta II rocket and named in honour of physicist Enrico Fermi.

Instruments

Fermi carries two main instruments: the Large Area Telescope (LAT), which images gamma rays from about 20 MeV to over 300 GeV, and the Gamma-ray Burst Monitor (GBM), which detects transient bursts across a wider field of view. The LAT scans the entire sky every three hours.

Discoveries

The mission has catalogued thousands of gamma-ray sources, including pulsars, active galactic nuclei and gamma-ray bursts. It discovered the enormous gamma-ray structures known as the Fermi bubbles extending from the centre of the Milky Way, and its observations have constrained the nature of dark matter and tested fundamental physics, including the constancy of the speed of light across energies.
