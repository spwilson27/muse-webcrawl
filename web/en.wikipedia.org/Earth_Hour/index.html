Earth Hour

Earth Hour is a worldwide movement organised by the World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF). Held annually, it encourages individuals, communities and businesses to turn off non-essential electric lights for one hour, from 8:30 to 9:30 p.m. local time on a Saturday in March, as a symbol of commitment to the planet.

History

Earth Hour began in Sydney in 2007, when 2.2 million people and more than 2,000 businesses turned off their lights. In 2008 the event went global, with landmarks such as the Golden Gate Bridge and the Colosseum joining in. By the 2010s participation extended to more than 7,000 cities and towns.

Observance

Iconic landmarks including the Eiffel Tower, Sydney Opera House, Empire State Building and Burj Khalifa routinely switch off floodlighting. Beyond the lights-out gesture, WWF uses the event to promote campaigns on nature loss and climate change.

Criticism

Critics argue the direct energy savings are negligible and may even be offset by candle use, and that the event risks substituting symbolism for policy. Supporters respond that Earth Hour is an awareness campaign, not an energy-reduction measure.
