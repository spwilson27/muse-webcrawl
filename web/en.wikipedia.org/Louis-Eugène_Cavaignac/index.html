Louis-Eugène Cavaignac

Louis-Eugène Cavaignac (15 October 1802 – 28 October 1857) was a French general and politician who, as head of the executive power of the French Second Republic in 1848, suppressed the June Days uprising in Paris. He subsequently stood as a candidate in the presidential election of December 1848 but was decisively defeated by Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte.

Career

Born in Paris, the son of a revolutionary politician, Cavaignac trained as a military engineer and served in Algeria, where he rose to prominence and became governor-general in early 1848. Recalled to Paris during the Revolution of 1848, he was given command of the forces confronting the workers' insurrection of June 1848. Granted near-dictatorial emergency powers by the National Assembly, he crushed the revolt in three days of street fighting at a cost of thousands of lives.

Later life

After the June Days, Cavaignac led the government until the presidential election, in which his reputation for stern republican moderation could not match Louis-Napoléon's popular appeal. Following the coup d'état of December 1851, he refused to swear allegiance to the new regime, was briefly imprisoned, and retired from public life, dying in 1857.
