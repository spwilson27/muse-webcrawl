Maracanã Stadium

LocationRio de Janeiro, Brazil
Opened16 June 1950
Capacity~78,800

The Maracanã Stadium (officially Estádio Jornalista Mário Filho) is a football stadium in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, and one of the most famous sporting venues in the world. It takes its popular name from the Maracanã River and the surrounding neighbourhood.

History

Built for the 1950 FIFA World Cup, the stadium hosted the deciding match in which Uruguay defeated Brazil before a crowd estimated at nearly 200,000 — a result remembered in Brazil as the Maracanaço. It went on to host the 2014 World Cup final, the opening and closing ceremonies of the 2016 Summer Olympics, and numerous Copa América finals. Clubs Flamengo and Fluminense play their home matches there.

Renovation

The stadium was extensively rebuilt for the 2014 World Cup, with a new roof and a single-tier bowl, reducing capacity from its historic maximum while modernising facilities.
