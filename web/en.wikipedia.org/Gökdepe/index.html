Gökdepe (also rendered Geok Tepe or Gokdepe) is a town in the Ahal Region of Turkmenistan, about 45 kilometres northwest of the capital, Ashgabat. It lies at the foot of the Kopet Dag mountains on the historic route across the Karakum Desert.

Battle of Geok Tepe

Gökdepe is best known for the siege and battle of 1880–81, in which Russian imperial forces under General Mikhail Skobelev stormed the fortress of the Akhal-Teke Turkmens after an earlier Russian expedition had been repulsed in 1879. The fortress fell on 24 January 1881, and thousands of defenders and civilians were killed in the assault and ensuing massacre. The conquest opened the way to the Russian annexation of Transcaspia and the eventual submission of Merv in 1884. In Turkmenistan the battle is commemorated as a national day of mourning.

Modern town

Today Gökdepe is a district centre in a farming area irrigated from the Karakum Canal. Within the old fortress walls stands the Saparmurat Hajji Mosque, built in the 1990s to commemorate the 1881 defenders and named for President Saparmurat Niyazov's pilgrimage (hajj). A museum at the site preserves artefacts of the battle, and memorial services are held each January.
