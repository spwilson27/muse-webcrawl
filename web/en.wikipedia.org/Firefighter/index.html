Firefighter

A firefighter (or fireman) is a first responder trained in firefighting, primarily to control and extinguish fires that threaten life and property, as well as to rescue persons from dangerous situations such as collapsed or burning buildings and crashed vehicles. Modern firefighters also respond to medical emergencies, hazardous materials incidents and natural disasters.

Duties

The core duties of firefighters include fire suppression, rescue operations, emergency medical response, fire prevention and public education, and hazardous materials mitigation. Firefighters work from fire stations equipped with fire engines, ladder trucks and rescue vehicles, and typically work long shifts, often of 24 hours, while on call.

History

Organized firefighting dates to ancient Rome, where the Vigiles patrolled the city and fought fires with buckets, blankets and pumps. After the Great Fire of London in 1666, insurance companies formed private fire brigades. Municipal departments developed in the 19th century; Edinburgh established the world's first municipal fire service in 1824. Steam-powered and later motorized apparatus transformed the profession in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Training and equipment

Firefighters complete academy training covering fire behavior, hose and ladder operations, ventilation, search and rescue, and emergency medical care. Structural firefighters wear personal protective equipment including a helmet, turnout coat and trousers made of fire-resistant fabrics, gloves, boots and a self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) for work in smoke-filled environments. Wildland firefighters use lighter gear and hand tools to combat wildfires.

Hazards

Firefighting is among the most dangerous occupations. Hazards include burns, smoke inhalation, structural collapse, heat stress and long-term exposure to carcinogens; sudden cardiac events are a leading cause of on-duty deaths. Safety procedures, incident command systems and improved protective equipment have steadily reduced casualties.
