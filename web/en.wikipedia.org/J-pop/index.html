J-pop

J-pop (Japanese pop) is a musical genre that entered the musical mainstream of Japan in the 1990s. Modern J-pop has its roots in traditional Japanese music, but significantly in 1960s pop and rock music such as the Beatles and the Beach Boys, and in the kayōkyoku popular songs that preceded it.

History

The term "J-pop" was coined in the late 1980s by the radio station J-WAVE to distinguish Japanese music from foreign music. The genre boomed in the 1990s with artists such as Namie Amuro, Hikaru Utada, and Ayumi Hamasaki, and idol groups managed by agencies such as Johnny & Associates became a dominant force. Japan developed the world's second-largest music market, driven largely by domestic J-pop sales.

Characteristics and culture

J-pop encompasses idol pop, rock-influenced acts, hip hop, and electronic music, and is closely tied to anime theme songs, television dramas, and advertising. The idol system — young performers trained and promoted as multi-talented entertainers — is a distinctive feature of the industry, and fan culture around J-pop groups is highly organised.
