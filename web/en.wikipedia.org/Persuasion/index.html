Persuasion

Persuasion is communication intended to influence the beliefs, attitudes, intentions, motivations or behaviours of others. It is a symbolic process in which persuaders attempt to convince others to change, without coercion. Persuasion is central to rhetoric, advertising, marketing, politics, law and everyday social interaction.

Classical rhetoric

Aristotle, in his Rhetoric, identified three modes of persuasion: ethos (the credibility or character of the speaker), pathos (appeal to the audience's emotions) and logos (appeal to logic and evidence). Roman rhetoricians such as Cicero and Quintilian codified the canons of rhetoric — invention, arrangement, style, memory and delivery.

Modern theories

Elaboration likelihood model

The elaboration likelihood model (Petty and Cacioppo) proposes two routes: a central route, in which audiences carefully evaluate argument quality, producing durable attitude change; and a peripheral route, relying on superficial cues such as attractiveness, credibility or mood, producing weaker, temporary change.

Principles of influence

Robert Cialdini identified recurring principles used in compliance: reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, and scarcity (later adding unity). Other strategies include the foot-in-the-door technique, door-in-the-face, framing effects, repetition and narrative persuasion.

Methods and domains

Persuasion operates through media campaigns, propaganda, advertising, negotiation, preaching, courtroom advocacy and interpersonal requests. Research also examines resistance to persuasion, including inoculation theory — pre-exposing audiences to weakened counterarguments builds resistance to later attacks — and psychological reactance, the tendency to reject perceived threats to one's freedom.

Ethics

Persuasion is distinguished from manipulation and coercion by transparency and the audience's freedom to reject the appeal, though the boundaries are debated, especially in political propaganda, advertising to children, and "dark pattern" design.
