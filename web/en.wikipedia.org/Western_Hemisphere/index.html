Western Hemisphere
DefinitionHalf of Earth west of the prime meridian and east of the antimeridian (180°)
ContinentsNorth America, South America (and parts of others)
Population~1 billion

The Western Hemisphere, also called the American Hemisphere, is the half of planet Earth that lies west of the prime meridian (which crosses Greenwich, London, United Kingdom) and east of the antimeridian (the meridian 180°). The other half is called the Eastern Hemisphere. In geographical terms, the Western Hemisphere consists primarily of the Americas (North America and South America), their islands, and the surrounding waters of the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans.

Because the dividing meridians do not follow continental boundaries, parts of Europe, Africa, Asia and Oceania (including the United Kingdom, Ireland, most of West Africa, eastern Russia's Chukotka region, Fiji and parts of Antarctica) also lie in the Western Hemisphere by longitude.

Usage

The term is used in geopolitics and international relations to refer to the Americas as a sphere, a usage popularised by the Monroe Doctrine of 1823, which asserted that the hemisphere was closed to further European colonisation. The Organization of American States groups nearly all independent states of the Americas.
