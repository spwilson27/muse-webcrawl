White supremacy

TypeRacist ideology
Core beliefWhite people are superior to other races and should dominate them

White supremacy is the belief that white people are superior to those of other races and thus should dominate them. The belief favours the maintenance and defence of any power and privilege held by white people. Historically, white supremacist ideology underpinned colonialism, slavery, segregation, and apartheid.

History

Scholars trace modern white supremacist thought to pseudo-scientific racism of the 18th and 19th centuries, which classified human populations hierarchically. It provided justification for transatlantic slavery, European colonial empires, and, in the United States, Jim Crow laws after Reconstruction. The Ku Klux Klan, founded after the American Civil War and revived in 1915, became the most prominent American white supremacist organisation. In South Africa, apartheid (1948–1994) institutionalised white minority rule.

Contemporary forms

Contemporary white supremacist movements include neo-Nazi groups, white nationalist movements, and elements of the alt-right. Extremist violence motivated by white supremacist ideology has been identified by security agencies in several countries as a significant domestic terrorism threat. Academic usage also extends the term to structural and systemic patterns of advantage, a usage that is debated.

Opposition

Civil rights movements, anti-racist organisations, and legal frameworks such as the U.S. Civil Rights Act of 1964 and South Africa's post-apartheid constitution have dismantled many formal white supremacist institutions, while monitoring groups continue to track extremist networks.
