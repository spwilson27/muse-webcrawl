Tuscan Archipelago

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tuscan Archipelago (Italian: Arcipelago Toscano) is a chain of islands between the Ligurian Sea and Tyrrhenian Sea, west of Tuscany, Italy. According to legend, the islands are jewels that fell from the necklace of Venus into the sea. Most of the archipelago is protected as the Tuscan Archipelago National Park, the largest marine park in Italy and in the Mediterranean.

Islands

The seven main islands, from largest to smallest, are:

  • Elba — the largest, famous as the site of Napoleon Bonaparte's first exile in 1814–15
  • Giglio — off the Argentario promontory; scene of the Costa Concordia disaster in 2012
  • Capraia — the only volcanic island of the group besides the smaller islets
  • Montecristo — a strictly protected nature reserve, immortalised by Dumas's novel The Count of Monte Cristo
  • Pianosa — flat island, formerly a penal colony and site of Agrippa Postumus's exile
  • Giannutri — southernmost, with Roman villa ruins
  • Gorgona — the smallest and northernmost, still an agricultural penal colony

Nature

The islands host Mediterranean maquis, endemic plants, peregrine falcons, mouflon and rich marine life including dolphins and monk seals. Access to Montecristo is tightly rationed to protect its ecosystem, while Elba and Giglio support substantial summer tourism.
