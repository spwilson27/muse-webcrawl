John Chadwick (21 May 1920 – 24 November 1998) was an English linguist and classical scholar who, with architect Michael Ventris, deciphered Linear B, the syllabic script used for Mycenaean Greek in the Bronze Age.

Life

Educated at St Paul's School and Corpus Christi College, Cambridge, Chadwick served in the Royal Navy during the Second World War, working on Italian naval codes and learning Japanese. After the war he worked on the Oxford Latin Dictionary before returning to Cambridge as a lecturer in classics, later becoming Perceval Maitland Laurence Reader in Classics.

Linear B

Ventris, building on the work of Alice Kober and Emmett Bennett, announced in 1952 that Linear B encoded an early form of Greek. Chadwick, a specialist in early Greek dialects, recognised the linguistic plausibility of Ventris's grid and joined him in validating and refining the decipherment. Their joint paper "Evidence for Greek Dialect in the Mycenaean Archives" (1953) and book Documents in Mycenaean Greek (1956) established the decipherment, confirmed by newly excavated tablets from Pylos whose readings matched predictions. After Ventris's death in a car accident in 1956, Chadwick carried the work forward, publishing the popular account The Decipherment of Linear B (1958) and The Mycenaean World (1976), and contributing the transliteration volume of the Knossos tablets corpus.

Legacy

Chadwick was elected a Fellow of the British Academy in 1958. His work transformed understanding of Bronze Age Greece, proving that the Mycenaean palace civilisation was Greek-speaking centuries before the classical age.
