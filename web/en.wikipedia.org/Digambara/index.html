Digambara

Digambara ("sky-clad") is one of the two major schools of Jainism, the other being Śvētāmbara ("white-clad"). The Digambara tradition holds that its monks must renounce all possessions, including clothing, as a practice of complete non-attachment (aparigraha), and that women must be reborn as men to attain liberation.

History

The schism between the two traditions developed in the centuries after Mahavira (6th–5th century BC), hardening by around the first century CE. Traditional accounts link the split to a famine in Magadha during which a group of monks under Bhadrabahu migrated south to Karnataka, while those who remained adopted white garments. Digambaras hold that the original Jain canon was lost, whereas Śvētāmbaras preserve texts the Digambaras regard as incomplete or spurious.

Practices and beliefs

Digambara monks carry only a peacock-feather broom (to sweep insects from their path without harming them) and a wooden water gourd. They eat once a day, standing, from their cupped hands. Important Digambara texts include the Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama and the works of Kundakunda. Major centres include Shravanabelagola, site of the great statue of Bahubali (Gommateshwara), consecrated in 981 CE.
