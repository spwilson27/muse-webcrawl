Sixaxis

The Sixaxis (stylized as SIXAXIS) is a wireless gamepad produced by Sony as the original controller for the PlayStation 3 console. It was introduced at E3 2006 and released alongside the console in November 2006, remaining the standard controller until 2008, when it was succeeded by the DualShock 3, which added vibration feedback.

Sixaxis

Manufacturer: Sony Computer Entertainment
Type: Gamepad
Released: November 2006
Connectivity: Bluetooth, USB

Features

The name refers to the controller's motion-sensing capability along six axes: the three linear axes (X, Y, Z) and three rotational axes (pitch, roll, yaw). The Sixaxis could detect movement and tilt, which games such as Lair and MotorStorm used for steering and flight control. Unlike its DualShock predecessors, it shipped without rumble motors, a decision Sony attributed to interference with the motion sensors; critics suspected the omission related to a patent dispute with Immersion Corporation, later settled.

Design

The shell closely follows the DualShock 2's shape but adds a central "PS" home button, four shoulder buttons including analogue L2/R2 triggers, and LED indicators for the player number. It connects via Bluetooth and charges over a mini-USB cable.
