1924 Winter Olympics

The 1924 Winter Olympics, officially the I Olympic Winter Games, were held from 25 January to 5 February 1924 in Chamonix, France. Originally organised as an "International Winter Sports Week" in association with the 1924 Paris Summer Games, the event was retroactively recognised by the IOC as the first Winter Olympic Games.

Competition

Some 258 athletes from 16 nations competed in 16 events across sports including bobsleigh, curling, ice hockey, figure skating, speed skating, ski jumping, cross-country skiing, Nordic combined and military patrol. Canada's ice hockey team, the Toronto Granites, dominated the tournament, outscoring opponents 110–3. Norwegian skiers shone, with Thorleif Haug winning all three Nordic skiing events then contested (a medal in ski jumping was later reassigned after an error was discovered decades later).

Highlights

Eleven-year-old Sonja Henie of Norway made her Olympic debut in figure skating, finishing last but beginning a career that would bring three consecutive gold medals. The first Winter Olympic champion was American speed skater Charles Jewtraw, winner of the 500 metres. Norway topped the medal table, followed by Finland.

Legacy

The success of the Chamonix week convinced the IOC to formally create the Winter Games, staged every four years in the same year as the Summer Games until 1992, after which they were offset by two years.
