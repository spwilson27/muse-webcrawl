Kepler space telescope

The Kepler space telescope was a NASA space telescope launched on 7 March 2009 to discover Earth-sized planets orbiting other stars using the transit method. Named after astronomer Johannes Kepler, it observed a fixed field in Cygnus, Lyra and Draco.

Mission

Kepler monitored the brightness of about 150,000 stars, detecting periodic dips caused by transiting exoplanets. After failures of reaction wheels ended its original mission in 2013, the K2 mission used solar pressure for stabilisation. The spacecraft was retired on 30 October 2018.

Discoveries

Kepler confirmed more than 2,600 exoplanets, including Kepler-186f, the first Earth-size planet in a habitable zone, fundamentally changing understanding of planetary system diversity.
