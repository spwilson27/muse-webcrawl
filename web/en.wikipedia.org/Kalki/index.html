Kalki

Kalki (Sanskrit: कल्कि), also called Kalkin, is the prophesied tenth and final incarnation (avatāra) of the Hindu god Vishnu. According to Vaishnava cosmology, Kalki will appear at the end of the present age, the Kali Yuga, riding a white horse and wielding a flaming sword, to destroy the wicked, end the age of degradation and restart the cycle of time with a new Satya Yuga.

Scriptural accounts

Kalki is described in the Mahābhārata and in the Purāṇas, most extensively in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa and in the dedicated Kalki Purāṇa. He is prophesied to be born in the village of Shambhala to a Brahmin named Vishnuyashas (and his wife Sumati, in later texts). He will be instructed by the immortal Parashurama, marry the princess Padmavati, and defeat the forces of the demon Kali, whose rule characterises the degenerate age. After his victory, he will restore dharma and inaugurate a renewed golden age before the cosmic cycle begins again.

Timing

Traditional calculations hold that the Kali Yuga lasts 432,000 human years and began in 3102 BCE, so Kalki's advent lies hundreds of thousands of years in the future. Some religious movements and claimants have nonetheless identified Kalki with figures of their own time.

In other traditions

Kalki is also found in Buddhist texts, notably the Kalachakra tradition, where the kings of Shambhala — culminating in a final king often equated with Kalki — wage war against barbarism. In Sikh scripture, Kalki appears as an incarnation of God sent to end the age of evil.
