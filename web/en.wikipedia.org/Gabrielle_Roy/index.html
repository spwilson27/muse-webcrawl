Gabrielle Roy, CC, FRSC (22 March 1909 – 13 July 1983) was a French Canadian author, regarded as one of the most important writers in Canadian literature. Her novel Bonheur d'occasion (1945), translated as The Tin Flute, is considered a landmark of Quebec realism and of the urban novel in French Canada.

Life

Roy was born in Saint-Boniface, Manitoba, into a large Franco-Manitoban family that fell on hard times. She trained as a teacher and taught in rural Manitoba schools before saving enough to travel to Europe in 1937, where she studied drama in London and wrote journalism. Returning to Montreal at the outbreak of war, she worked as a journalist and sketch writer while composing her first novel.

Work

Bonheur d'occasion, set in the working-class Montreal district of Saint-Henri during the Second World War, won the Prix Femina in Paris and was the first Canadian book selected by the Literary Guild of America. Later works include Alexandre Chenevert (1954), a portrait of a bank clerk's inner life; Rue Deschambault (1955), drawing on her Manitoba childhood; and Ces enfants de ma vie (1977), fictionalised recollections of her teaching years, which won the Governor General's Award. Her unfinished autobiography, La Détresse et l'Enchantement, appeared posthumously.

Honours

Roy won the Governor General's Award three times, was the first woman admitted to the Royal Society of Canada's French section, and was made a Companion of the Order of Canada. Her image appeared on the Canadian twenty-dollar bill in 2004 alongside other celebrated Canadian artists.
