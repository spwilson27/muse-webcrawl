Interislander is the brand of the New Zealand ferry service that crosses Cook Strait between Wellington in the North Island and Picton in the South Island. Operated by the state-owned rail company KiwiRail, it carries passengers, road vehicles and rail freight on one of the world's more notoriously rough short sea crossings, a route of about 92 kilometres (58 nautical miles) taking roughly three hours, the last part winding through the Marlborough Sounds.

History

The service began in 1962 with the roll-on/roll-off rail ferry Aramoana, which for the first time let trains and vehicles move between the islands without transhipment, effectively joining New Zealand's two rail networks. Successive ships included Arahanga, Aratika, Arahura and the chartered Kaitaki (formerly of Irish Ferries), joined in 2021 by the former Stena Baltica, renamed Kaiarahi. The Cook Strait ferries are a critical freight link, and the route has a history of severe weather incidents, most tragically the sinking of the competing Union Company's Wahine in 1968.

Operations

Interislander competes with Bluebridge, operated by StraitNZ. Rail wagons are loaded at Wellington onto the rail-capable vessels and unloaded at Picton to continue on the Main North Line. Successive governments have studied replacing the ageing fleet; an iReX programme to buy two large rail-enabled ferries and rebuild both terminals was cancelled in late 2023 on cost grounds, and plans for smaller replacement ships followed.
