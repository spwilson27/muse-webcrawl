Cruiser

A cruiser is a type of warship. Modern cruisers are among the largest and most powerful surface combatants, generally equipped with guided missiles for air defence, anti-ship, and land-attack roles. Historically, the term covered a wide range of fast, independent warships used for scouting, raiding commerce, and showing the flag on distant stations; from the late 19th century it denoted armoured and protected warships graded between battleships and destroyers.

History

The term "cruiser" derives from the cruising role of frigates and sloops in the age of sail. In the ironclad era, navies developed armoured cruisers and protected cruisers; the battlecruiser concept of the early 1900s combined battleship guns with cruiser speed at the cost of armour, with mixed results at Jutland in 1916. The Washington Naval Treaty (1922) standardised "light" and "heavy" cruisers by gun calibre. In the Second World War, cruisers fought in nearly every theatre, providing anti-aircraft screens, shore bombardment, and commerce raiding and defence.

Modern cruisers

After 1945, guns gave way to missiles, and the "guided-missile cruiser" emerged. The United States Navy's Ticonderoga class, built around the Aegis combat system, and Russia's Slava and nuclear-powered Kirov classes are the principal surviving types; many former cruisers have been reclassified or replaced by large destroyers. As of the 21st century only a handful of navies operate vessels formally classified as cruisers.
