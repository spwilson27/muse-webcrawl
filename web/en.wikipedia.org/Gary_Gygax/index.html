Gary Gygax

Ernest Gary Gygax (July 27, 1938 – March 4, 2008) was an American game designer and author best known for co-creating the pioneering tabletop role-playing game Dungeons & Dragons with Dave Arneson. He co-founded the company TSR, Inc. in 1973 and is widely regarded as the father of the role-playing game hobby.

Early life and wargaming

Born in Chicago, Gygax grew up in Lake Geneva, Wisconsin, where he became an avid wargamer. In 1971, with Jeff Perren, he wrote Chainmail, a set of medieval miniatures wargame rules whose fantasy supplement suggested rules for magic and monsters. Arneson's experiments merging these rules with individual character play in his Blackmoor campaign led the two men to collaborate on a new kind of game.

Dungeons & Dragons and TSR

Dungeons & Dragons was published in 1974 by Tactical Studies Rules (TSR), the company Gygax founded with Don Kaye. The game was a commercial breakthrough and spawned an industry; Gygax authored or oversaw the Monster Manual (1977), Players Handbook (1978), and Dungeon Masters Guide (1979) of Advanced Dungeons & Dragons. He also founded Gen Con, the major gaming convention, in 1968. Following internal disputes he lost control of TSR in 1985 and left the company.

Later work and legacy

Gygax went on to create the Dangerous Journeys and Lejendary Adventure role-playing systems and wrote fantasy novels, including the Gord the Rogue series. He died in Lake Geneva in 2008. His influence on video games — from hit points and character classes to experience points and dungeon exploration — is pervasive, and he is commemorated by a memorial at Gen Con and by numerous tributes within the hobby.
