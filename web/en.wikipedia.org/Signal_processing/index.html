Signal processing is an electrical engineering subfield that focuses on analysing, modifying, and synthesising signals, such as sound, images, potential fields, seismic signals, altimetry processing, and scientific measurements. A signal is a function conveying information about a phenomenon; signal processing techniques are used to improve transmission and storage efficiency and to extract information, including by filtering noise, compression, and feature extraction.

Foundations

The mathematical backbone is Fourier analysis: any practical signal can be decomposed into sinusoids, letting engineers work in the frequency domain via the Fourier transform. The Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem states that a band-limited signal can be perfectly reconstructed from samples taken at more than twice its highest frequency — the basis of all digital audio and imaging. Claude Shannon's 1948 information theory quantified channel capacity and source coding. Digital signal processing (DSP) became practical with the rediscovery of the Fast Fourier Transform by Cooley and Tukey in 1965 and the arrival of dedicated DSP chips; algorithms such as linear filters (FIR and IIR), convolution, the Kalman filter, and adaptive filters underpin the field.

Applications

Signal processing is everywhere: MP3/AAC audio and JPEG/H.264 image-video compression; error correction and modulation in mobile phones; radar and sonar detection; seismic surveys for oil exploration; electrocardiograms, MRI reconstruction, and ultrasound in medicine; speech recognition and synthesis; seismology and radio astronomy; and financial time-series analysis. Modern advances increasingly merge classical DSP with machine learning, for example in source separation and image denoising.
