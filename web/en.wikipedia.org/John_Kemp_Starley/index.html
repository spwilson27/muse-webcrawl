John Kemp Starley

John Kemp Starley (14 December 1854 – 5 October 1901) was an English inventor and industrialist who is widely considered the inventor of the modern bicycle. In 1885 he produced the Rover safety bicycle, a rear-wheel-drive, chain-driven bicycle with two similar-sized wheels, a design that has remained the basic pattern of the bicycle ever since.

Life

Starley was born in Walthamstow, Essex, the son of a gardener. In 1872 he moved to Coventry to work for his uncle, the inventor James Starley, at the Coventry Machinists Company. He worked on the production of bicycles and tricycles, and in 1877, with William Sutton, he founded the firm of Starley & Sutton. In 1881 he set up the Meteor Works in Coventry.

The Rover safety bicycle

The penny-farthing bicycle, with its huge front wheel, was fast but dangerous. Starley's Rover of 1885 used a chain drive to the rear wheel, a diamond-shaped frame, and wheels of roughly equal size, putting the rider lower and further back. The design, refined with a tangent-spoked wheel, pneumatic tyres (after Dunlop's 1888 patent), and a diamond frame, quickly displaced the ordinary bicycle. Starley's company became the Rover Cycle Company, which later evolved into the Rover car company. He died in Coventry in 1901, aged 46.
