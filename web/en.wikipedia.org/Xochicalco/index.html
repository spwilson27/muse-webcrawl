Xochicalco is a pre-Columbian archaeological site in the state of Morelos, Mexico. A fortified hilltop city, it rose to prominence in the Epiclassic period (c. 650–900 CE) following the decline of Teotihuacan, and it interacted with Maya, Zapotec, Mixtec and Gulf Coast cultures.

The site

The city was built on a series of artificially levelled terraces on a hill about 38 kilometres southwest of Cuernavaca, ringed by walls and ditches that reflect the unsettled politics of the era. Major structures include ball courts, temples, an observatory cave in which a shaft of sunlight falls on the equinoxes, and elite residential compounds around the central acropolis.

Pyramid of the Feathered Serpents

The site's masterpiece is the Pyramid of the Feathered Serpents (Pirámide de Quetzalcóatl), whose talud-tablero façades carry superb stone reliefs of undulating feathered serpents, warriors and calendrical glyphs. The iconography is a major source for the spread of the Quetzalcoatl cult in highland Mexico.

Decline

Around 900 CE much of the city was burned and abandoned, possibly amid internal revolt or conflict with neighbouring powers. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1999.
