Pookkalam

A pookkalam is an intricate floral carpet or arrangement of flowers laid on the floor, traditionally created in the Indian state of Kerala during the festival of Onam. The word derives from Malayalam poov (flower) and kalam (colour sketch or pattern). Fresh flower petals of many colours are arranged in concentric, geometric and figurative designs in courtyards and before homes and temples.

TypeFloral design / floor art
RegionKerala, India
OccasionOnam festival
MaterialsFresh flower petals, leaves, coloured powder

Tradition

According to tradition, pookkalam are laid for ten days, from Atham to Thiruvonam, the days of the Onam festival, to welcome the mythical King Mahabali, whose annual visit to his former subjects Onam celebrates. A small design is made on the first day and a new ring of flowers is added each day, so that the pookkalam grows progressively larger and more elaborate, reaching its fullest on Thiruvonam day. Traditionally, girls and women of the household collected flowers — including thumba, mukkutti, tulasi (holy basil), marigold, and hibiscus — early in the morning and arranged the design.

Modern practice

Today pookkalam competitions are held in schools, colleges, offices, and public venues across Kerala, judged on design, colour combination, and neatness. Large public pookkalam may use hundreds of kilograms of petals. The art form is comparable to other South Asian floor-art traditions such as rangoli and kolam, though the pookkalam is distinguished by its exclusive use of fresh flowers and its association with Onam. A traditional clay figure of Mahabali or the deity Vamana (Thrikkakara Appan) is often placed at the centre.
