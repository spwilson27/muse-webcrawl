Thomas Telford (9 August 1757 – 2 September 1834) was a Scottish civil engineer, architect and stonemason, and a noted road, bridge and canal builder. He was the first president of the Institution of Civil Engineers (1820) and was dubbed the "Colossus of Roads" by Robert Southey.

Major projects

As surveyor for Shropshire, Telford built bridges in iron at Buildwas and Coalport and a series of canals. His Ellesmere Canal (now Llangollen Canal) includes the Pontcysyllte Aqueduct (1805), which carries the canal 38 metres above the River Dee on cast-iron troughs — the longest and highest aqueduct in Britain. For the government he built the Caledonian Canal through the Great Glen, over 1,500 miles of roads and more than a thousand bridges in the Scottish Highlands, and harbour works across Scotland.

Later works

His Menai Suspension Bridge (1826), linking Anglesey to mainland Wales, had the world's longest span of its day, and the London–Holyhead road (now the A5) integrated bridges, embankments and tollhouses into a unified design. The town of Telford in Shropshire was named after him in 1968.
