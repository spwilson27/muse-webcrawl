Kidney failure is treated with dialysis or transplantation.

Kidney failure, also known as end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) or end-stage renal disease (ESRD), is a medical condition in which the kidneys can no longer adequately filter waste products from the blood, functioning at less than 15% of normal levels. Kidney failure is classified as either acute kidney failure, which develops rapidly and may resolve, or chronic kidney failure, which develops slowly and is generally irreversible.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms can include leg swelling, feeling tired, vomiting, loss of appetite, and confusion. As waste products accumulate, the syndrome of uraemia develops, which may cause nausea, itching (pruritus), pericarditis, and encephalopathy. Complications of chronic failure also include high blood pressure, anaemia due to reduced erythropoietin production, weakened bones (renal osteodystrophy), fluid overload, high potassium (hyperkalaemia), and cardiovascular disease.

Causes

Causes of acute kidney failure include low blood pressure, blockage of the urinary tract, certain medications, muscle breakdown, and haemolytic uraemic syndrome. Causes of chronic kidney failure include diabetes, high blood pressure, glomerulonephritis, and polycystic kidney disease. Diagnosis is based on blood tests for creatinine and urea together with estimation of the glomerular filtration rate (GFR), and urine testing for protein.

Treatment

Treatment of acute failure depends on the underlying cause and may be supportive until function recovers. Treatment of chronic failure may include haemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, or a kidney transplant, along with management of blood pressure, anaemia, acidosis, and mineral and bone disorders. Dietary changes, such as limiting salt, potassium, and phosphorus, are commonly recommended. Some patients, particularly the elderly with multiple comorbidities, may choose conservative (non-dialytic) management.

Prognosis and epidemiology

Kidney failure requiring dialysis carries a substantially increased mortality, largely from cardiovascular disease, though transplantation offers the best survival and quality of life among replacement therapies. Millions of people worldwide receive maintenance dialysis, and its prevalence continues to rise with the global increase in diabetes and hypertension.
