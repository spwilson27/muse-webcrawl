Peritoneal dialysis uses the lining of the abdomen as the dialysis membrane.

Peritoneal dialysis (PD) is a type of dialysis that uses the peritoneum — the thin membrane lining the abdomen — as the membrane through which fluid and dissolved substances are exchanged with the blood. It is a home-based treatment for kidney failure, an alternative to haemodialysis, and is performed by the patient (or a helper) rather than in a dialysis centre.

Principle and procedure

A soft silicone catheter (commonly a Tenckhoff catheter) is permanently placed in the abdomen. Sterile dialysate fluid — a glucose- or icodextrin-based solution — is infused into the peritoneal cavity and left to dwell for several hours. During the dwell, waste solutes diffuse from peritoneal capillaries into the fluid and excess water is drawn in by osmosis; the fluid is then drained and replaced with fresh solution, a cycle called an exchange.

Types

  • Continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD): manual exchanges, typically four per day, each taking about 30 minutes, with long dwells between.
  • Automated peritoneal dialysis (APD): a cycler machine performs multiple exchanges overnight while the patient sleeps; also called continuous cycler-assisted PD (CCPD).

Advantages and disadvantages

PD offers greater lifestyle flexibility, steady (continuous) clearance with gentler fluid shifts, preservation of residual kidney function for longer, and no need for vascular access or routine anticoagulation. Drawbacks include the daily treatment burden, risk of body-image concerns, glucose absorption from dialysate, protein losses, and the possibility of technique failure over years due to peritoneal membrane changes.

Complications

The most important complication is peritonitis — infection of the peritoneal cavity, usually from touch contamination at connection, presenting with cloudy effluent and abdominal pain. Other complications include catheter exit-site and tunnel infections, hernias, leaks, and the rare but serious encapsulating peritoneal sclerosis.
