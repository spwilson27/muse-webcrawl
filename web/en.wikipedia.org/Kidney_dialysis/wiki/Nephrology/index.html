Nephrology concerns the kidneys, their physiology, and their diseases.

Nephrology (from Greek nephros, "kidney", plus -logy, "study of") is a specialty of adult internal medicine and of paediatric medicine concerned with the study of the kidneys: their normal function (renal physiology), diseases of the kidney, the preservation of kidney health, and the treatment of kidney disease, from dietary and pharmacological therapy to renal replacement therapy (dialysis and kidney transplantation). A physician specialising in nephrology is called a nephrologist.

Scope

Nephrology covers acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease, glomerular diseases (glomerulonephritis and glomerular syndromes), tubulointerstitial diseases, kidney involvement in systemic diseases such as diabetes, lupus, and vasculitis, disorders of fluid, electrolyte, and acid–base balance, hypertension (especially secondary and resistant forms), kidney stones and cystic diseases, and the care of patients before and after kidney transplantation. Most nephrological conditions are systemic disorders not limited to the kidney itself.

Diagnosis

Assessment typically combines history and examination with laboratory studies of blood (creatinine, urea, electrolytes) and urine (urinalysis, protein and albumin excretion, sediment microscopy), estimation of glomerular filtration rate, imaging by ultrasound and other modalities, and, when needed, percutaneous kidney biopsy for histopathological diagnosis. Genetic testing plays a growing role in inherited kidney diseases.

Treatment

Management includes treatment of the underlying cause, blood-pressure and glycaemic control, immunosuppression for immune-mediated disease, management of anaemia and mineral-bone disease, and preparation for renal replacement therapy. Nephrologists supervise haemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis units and work closely with transplant surgeons, dietitians, and other specialists.

History

The discipline crystallised in the mid-20th century, after Richard Bright's 19th-century descriptions of kidney disease ("Bright's disease") and following the inventions of practical haemodialysis by Willem Kolff and of kidney biopsy, which together transformed kidney medicine. Professional bodies such as the International Society of Nephrology (founded 1960) and journals including Kidney International support the field.
