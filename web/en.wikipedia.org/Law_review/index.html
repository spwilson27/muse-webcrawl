Law review

A law review (or law journal) is a scholarly journal focused on legal issues, usually published by a law school and — distinctively in the United States — edited largely by students. Flagship reviews such as the Harvard Law Review (founded 1887), Yale Law Journal and Columbia Law Review are among the most cited legal publications in the world.

Content and selection

Reviews publish long "articles" by professors and judges, shorter essays, and student-written "notes" and case comments, all meticulously footnoted under the Bluebook citation system. Membership is typically earned through grades and a writing competition ("writing on"), and service as an editor is a prestigious credential for clerkships and law firm hiring.

Criticism

The student-edited model is criticised for slow publication, excessive length and citation-checking burdens, yet law reviews remain central to American legal scholarship and are frequently cited by courts, including the Supreme Court. See Law school.
