Boston Public Library

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Boston Public Library is a municipal public library system in Boston, Massachusetts, founded in 1848. The Boston Public Library is also Massachusetts' library for the Commonwealth, a legal depository for state documents, and (until 2021) one of the five national "libraries of last recourse." With some 24 million volumes, it is surpassed among American public libraries only by the New York Public Library, and its research collections — including the library of John Adams and major holdings of incunabula, maps, and music — are of national significance.

History

The library was established in 1848 by a statute of the Massachusetts legislature supported by Boston mayor Josiah Quincy Jr., and championed by trustee George Ticknor. It opened in 1854 in a former schoolhouse on Mason Street — the first large free municipal library in the United States, and the first public library to allow people to borrow books and take them home.

The McKim Building

The central library's McKim Building in Copley Square, opened in 1895, was designed by Charles Follen McKim of McKim, Mead and White in the Renaissance Revival style and dubbed a "palace for the people." It features murals by John Singer Sargent (the Triumph of Time cycle), Pierre Puvis de Chavannes, and Edwin Austin Abbey, a courtyard modeled on a Roman palazzo, and the famous lion statues of the main staircase. The building is a National Historic Landmark. In 1972 it was joined by the modernist Johnson Building, designed by Philip Johnson, which underwent a major renovation completed in 2016.

Services and branches

The Boston Public Library operates the Central Library in Copley Square and more than two dozen branch libraries across the city's neighborhoods. In 2018 it eliminated overdue fines on many materials, and it offers extensive digital collections, exhibitions, lectures, and the Norman B. Leventhal Map Center.
