Axel Oxenstierna

Born 16 June 1583, Fånö, Uppland
Died 28 August 1654 (aged 71), Stockholm
Office Lord High Chancellor of Sweden (1612–1654)

Axel Gustafsson Oxenstierna (1583–1654) was a Swedish statesman and Count of Södermöre. He served as Lord High Chancellor of Sweden from 1612 until his death, and became one of the most influential figures in Swedish history, playing a central role in establishing Sweden as a great power during the Thirty Years' War.

Career under Gustavus Adolphus

Oxenstierna entered royal service under Charles IX and was appointed Chancellor by the young Gustav II Adolf in 1612. The two men worked in close partnership: the king led Sweden's armies in Germany while Oxenstierna administered the state, negotiated with foreign powers, and managed finances and logistics. He was the architect of the 1634 Instrument of Government, a landmark in Swedish constitutional and administrative history.

After 1632

Following Gustavus Adolphus's death at the Battle of Lützen in 1632, Oxenstierna assumed direction of Swedish policy in Germany, forming the Heilbronn League with German Protestant states and continuing the war in alliance with France. He also headed the regency for the young Queen Christina from 1632 to 1644.

Legacy

Oxenstierna is credited with founding Sweden's postal service, reforming its administration and local government, and founding several towns. His letters and state papers remain a major source for seventeenth-century European history. He is regarded as one of the ablest statesmen of his era.
