Ragnarök

In Norse mythology, Ragnarök is a prophesied series of events, including a great final battle, that leads to the death of many major gods and the destruction of the world, followed by its renewal. The name is Old Norse for "fate of the gods" (or "twilight of the gods" in the related form Götterdämmerung).

The prophecy

Sources including the Poetic Edda poem Völuspá and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda describe portents: a three-year Fimbulvetr winter, social collapse, and the loosening of the wolf Fenrir. At the battle on the field of Vígríðr, Odin is devoured by Fenrir and avenged by Víðarr; Thor kills the world-serpent Jörmungandr but dies from its venom; Freyr falls to the fire giant Surtr, who engulfs the world in flame. The earth sinks into the sea.

Renewal

Afterwards the earth rises green from the waters. Surviving gods — including Baldr returned from the dead — meet again, and two humans, Líf and Lífthrasir, who sheltered in the wood Hoddmímis holt, repopulate the world.
