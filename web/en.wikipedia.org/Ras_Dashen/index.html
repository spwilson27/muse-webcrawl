Ras Dashen

Ras Dashen (Amharic: ራስ ዳሸን) is the highest mountain in Ethiopia, rising to 4,550 metres (14,928 ft) in the Simien Mountains of the Amhara Region. It is the fourth-highest mountain in Africa when measured by prominence, and the highest point of the Ethiopian Highlands.

Geography

Ras Dashen is the highest of a cluster of jagged volcanic peaks in Simien Mountains National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for its dramatic escarpments and endemic wildlife such as the gelada baboon and walia ibex. The mountain is an eroded volcano; glaciers once covered its summit plateau. The first recorded ascent by a European was made in 1841 by the French officers Ferret and Galinier.

Climbing

The standard route approaches from the east through the park, typically from Debarq and the village of Ambiko, over two or three days. The climb is a high-altitude trek and scramble rather than a technical ascent, and permits and park scouts are required.
