3LCD

3LCD is a projection technology that uses three liquid crystal display panels — one each for red, green and blue — to form a projected image. The name derives from the three LCD panels used in the optical engine. The technology was pioneered by Epson, which remains the major manufacturer of 3LCD panels, and 3LCD is also the name of a licensing and marketing consortium formed in 2005.

How it works

White light from a lamp or laser source is split by dichroic mirrors into red, green and blue components. Each beam passes through its own LCD panel, where the image for that colour channel is formed, and the three beams are then recombined in a dichroic prism and passed through the projection lens. Because all three colours are projected simultaneously and continuously, 3LCD projectors are promoted as delivering equal white and colour brightness ("colour light output") and as being free of the "rainbow effect" sometimes seen with single-chip DLP systems.

Applications

3LCD projectors have been widely used in education, business presentation and home theatre, competing principally with DLP and LCoS technologies.
