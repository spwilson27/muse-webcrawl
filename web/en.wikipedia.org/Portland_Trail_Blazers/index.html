The Portland Trail Blazers are an American professional basketball team based in Portland, Oregon, competing in the Western Conference of the National Basketball Association (NBA). Founded as an expansion team in 1970, the franchise has played at the Moda Center (formerly the Rose Garden) since 1995. The team was owned for thirty years by Microsoft co-founder Paul Allen, from 1988 until his death in 2018.

History

The Trail Blazers' defining triumph came in 1977, when, in only their first-ever playoff appearance, Bill Walton and coach Jack Ramsay led them to the NBA championship, defeating the Philadelphia 76ers — an upset celebrated by "Blazermania" across Oregon. Strong teams built around Clyde Drexler reached the NBA Finals again in 1990 and 1992, losing to Detroit and Chicago respectively. The early-2000s "Jail Blazers" era damaged the franchise's image; later teams centred on Brandon Roy, LaMarcus Aldridge, and then Damian Lillard, who led Portland to the 2019 Western Conference finals and became the franchise's all-time leading scorer before his 2023 trade to Milwaukee.

Fans and identity

Portland is one of the smallest NBA markets yet has historically recorded some of the league's best attendance, including a sellout streak of 814 consecutive games from 1977 to 1995. The team's name, chosen in a public contest, refers to the trailblazers of the Oregon Trail; its pinwheel logo is one of the most recognisable in American sport.
