Benchmark dose

A benchmark dose (BMD) is a dose or concentration of a substance that produces a predetermined change in the response rate of an adverse effect — the benchmark response (BMR) — compared with background.

Definition

The lower confidence limit on the BMD, the BMDL, is commonly used as a point of departure in chemical risk assessment. The approach was proposed by Kenny Crump in 1984 as an alternative to the NOAEL approach.

Advantages

Unlike a NOAEL, a BMD uses all the dose-response data, accounts for study sensitivity through confidence limits, and is not restricted to the experimental doses actually tested.

Use

Regulatory bodies including the US EPA and EFSA use BMD modelling software to derive reference doses and health-based guidance values.
