Packard was an American luxury automobile marque built by the Packard Motor Car Company of Detroit, Michigan. The company produced some of the most prestigious cars of the first half of the twentieth century, competing with Cadillac, Lincoln, and European luxury brands.

History

Brothers James Ward and William Doud Packard built their first automobile in Warren, Ohio, in 1899. The company moved to Detroit in 1903 into a factory designed by Albert Kahn that was among the first reinforced-concrete automobile plants. Packard gained fame for engineering innovations, notably the Twin Six of 1915, the first production V12 engine, and later the straight-eight engines that powered its senior cars.

During the Second World War, Packard built Rolls-Royce Merlin aircraft engines under license — more than 55,000 of them — powering aircraft such as the P-51 Mustang. After the war, Packard struggled against the "Big Three," losing its cachet with increasingly dated designs and costly retooling.

Decline

In 1954 Packard merged with Studebaker to form the Studebaker-Packard Corporation. The last Detroit-built Packards rolled off the line in 1956; badge-engineered models based on Studebakers were sold through 1958, after which the Packard name was retired.
