Panarchy

Panarchy is a term with several related uses. In political theory, coined by the Belgian botanist and economist Paul Émile de Puydt in 1860, it denotes a system in which individuals may freely choose which government to belong to without moving territory. In ecology, panarchy describes the nested, cross-scale interactions of adaptive cycles in social-ecological systems, developed by C. S. Holling, Lance Gunderson and colleagues.

Political panarchy

De Puydt proposed that competing governmental offices, like competing churches, would let citizens subscribe to the political system of their choice, fostering competition and liberty.

Ecological panarchy

In resilience theory, panarchy links cycles of growth, conservation, release and reorganisation across scales of time and space, explaining how systems transform and persist.
