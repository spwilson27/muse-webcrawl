Pet food

Pet food is plant or animal material intended for consumption by pets, typically sold as dry kibble, wet (canned) food, or treats formulated for species such as dogs and cats. It is a major global industry distinct from livestock feed.

History

Commercial pet food began in the 1860s with James Spratt's dog biscuits in England. Canned dog food and dry kibble expanded in the 20th century, and extruded kibble became dominant after the 1950s. Feeding standards are set in the US with reference to AAFCO nutrient profiles and regulated by the FDA and state authorities.

Nutrition and debate

Dogs are omnivorous while cats are obligate carnivores requiring nutrients such as taurine from animal tissue. Debates concern grain-free diets (investigated for a possible link to canine dilated cardiomyopathy), raw feeding, meat by-products and premium/natural marketing. Recalls, notably the 2007 melamine contamination, have driven tighter safety rules.
