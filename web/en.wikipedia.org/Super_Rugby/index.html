Super Rugby is a men's professional rugby union club competition involving teams from Australia, New Zealand, Fiji and the Pacific Islands. It began in 1996 as the Super 12 with teams from Australia, New Zealand and South Africa, expanded to Super 14 in 2006 and Super Rugby (15 teams) in 2011, and at its peak featured 18 teams across five countries including Argentina and Japan.

History

The competition was created following the professionalisation of rugby union in 1995, under a landmark broadcast deal between the SANZAR unions and News Corporation. The Crusaders of Christchurch became the competition's dominant team, winning three consecutive titles from 1998 to 2000 and a record seven straight finals appearances; they later won three consecutive championships again from 2017 to 2019, plus further titles in the regionalised competitions of 2020–2023.

Restructuring

The COVID-19 pandemic ended the conference-based 15-team format in 2020. South African teams departed for Europe's United Rugby Championship, and from 2022 the competition continued as Super Rugby Pacific with 12 teams: five each from New Zealand and Australia plus the Fijian Drua and Moana Pasifika. The Blues won the 2024 title, their first full championship since 2003.

Format

Teams play a round-robin regular season followed by knockout finals; the top eight qualify for quarter-finals. The champion is decided in a grand final.
