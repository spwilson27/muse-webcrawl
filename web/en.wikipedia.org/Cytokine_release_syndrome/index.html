Cytokine release syndrome

Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a form of systemic inflammatory response syndrome arising when large numbers of white blood cells are activated and release inflammatory cytokines, which in turn activate yet more white blood cells. It is a potentially life-threatening toxicity most commonly associated with immunotherapies such as CAR T-cell therapy and some monoclonal antibodies, and it can also occur in severe infections.

SymptomsFever, fatigue, nausea, headache, rash, dyspnoea, tachycardia, hypotension
CausesCAR T-cell therapy, monoclonal antibodies (e.g. TGN1412), severe infection, sepsis
Key mediatorInterleukin-6 (IL-6)
TreatmentSupportive care, tocilizumab, corticosteroids

Signs and symptoms

CRS usually begins with fever, myalgia, and fatigue, and in severe cases progresses to high fever, vascular leak, hypotension, hypoxia, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and multi-organ failure. Neurological symptoms, when present, are classified separately as immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS).

Causes and mechanism

During CAR T-cell therapy, engineered T cells engaging tumour cells trigger a cascade involving macrophages releasing interleukin-6, interleukin-1, and other cytokines. The 2006 TGN1412 trial, in which a CD28 superagonist antibody caused near-fatal CRS in six healthy volunteers, transformed safety practices for first-in-human dosing.

Grading and treatment

The ASTCT consensus grades CRS from 1 (fever alone) to 5 (death) based on fever, hypotension, and hypoxia. Management includes antipyretics and fluids for mild cases, and the IL-6 receptor blocker tocilizumab — approved for CAR T-cell-induced CRS — plus corticosteroids for severe cases.
