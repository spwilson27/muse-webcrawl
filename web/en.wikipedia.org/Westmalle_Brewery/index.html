Westmalle Brewery (Dutch: Brouwerij der Trappisten van Westmalle) is a Trappist brewery in the Westmalle Abbey, Belgium. It produces three beers designated as Trappist beer by the International Trappist Association — Westmalle Dubbel, Westmalle Tripel and Westmalle Extra — and is one of the largest and most influential Trappist breweries in the world.

The abbey of Westmalle was founded in 1794 by Trappist monks fleeing the French Revolution, and brewing began in 1836, with beer initially made only for the monks' own consumption. Sales to the public began in earnest in the later 19th century.

Beers

The Westmalle Dubbel (7% ABV), a dark reddish-brown ale first brewed in its modern form in 1926, established the "dubbel" style. The Westmalle Tripel (9.5% ABV), a clear golden strong ale introduced in 1934 and reformulated in 1956, is regarded as the archetype of the tripel style and is sometimes called the "mother of all tripels." The lighter Extra (4.8% ABV) is the monks' own table beer, sold publicly only occasionally. The brewery's yeast strain is widely used by other brewers.

Trappist tradition

Like all Trappist breweries, Westmalle operates within the abbey walls under the supervision of monks, and its income supports the monastery and charitable works. Much of the production work today is performed by lay employees in a modern brewhouse.
