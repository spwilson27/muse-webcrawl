EPFL

The École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) is a public research university and institute of technology in Lausanne, Switzerland. One of the two Swiss Federal Institutes of Technology (the other being ETH Zurich), it specialises in natural sciences and engineering and is regularly ranked among the leading technical universities in Europe and the world. Its campus, on the shore of Lake Geneva, hosts more than 12,000 students and about 370 professors, along with affiliated research centres.

History

EPFL traces its origins to the École spéciale de Lausanne, founded in 1853 with eleven students. It became part of the University of Lausanne as an engineering school before being taken over by the Swiss federal government in 1969, when it received its present name and status as an autonomous federal institute. Since then it has expanded into life sciences, computer science, architecture and the social sciences, and developed the EPFL Innovation Park and satellite campuses in Valais, Fribourg, Neuchâtel and Geneva.

Campus

The main campus in Ecublens is notable for landmark buildings including the Rolex Learning Center (opened 2010, designed by SANAA), the ArtLab pavilion by Kengo Kuma, and the SwissTech Convention Center. EPFL is associated with facilities such as the Swiss Plasma Center's tokamak research and the Blue Brain Project in computational neuroscience.
