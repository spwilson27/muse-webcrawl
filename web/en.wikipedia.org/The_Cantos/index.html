The Cantos

AuthorEzra Pound
LanguageEnglish (with many others)
GenreEpic poetry, modernism
Published1917–1969

The Cantos is a long modernist epic poem by Ezra Pound, written over more than fifty years, from 1913 until his death in 1972. The incomplete poem comprises 116 sections, each a "canto", and runs to some 800 pages.

Overview

Pound described the work as a "poem including history", drawing on Confucian texts, Chinese and Japanese history, the writings of John Adams, economics, Provençal troubadour poetry, and the Odyssey. Using his "ideogrammic method", Pound juxtaposes fragments of text, quotation, and languages — including Chinese characters — rather than narrating in a continuous line.

Sections

Early cantos explore Renaissance Italy and the Malatesta family; the middle sequences include the Chinese History Cantos and the Adams Cantos; The Pisan Cantos (1948), written while Pound was held in a detention camp near Pisa, won the Bollingen Prize in 1949, a decision that provoked lasting controversy because of Pound's wartime broadcasts for fascist Italy.

Legacy

Unfinished and notoriously difficult, The Cantos remains a central — and contested — monument of literary modernism.
