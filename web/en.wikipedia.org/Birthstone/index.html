Birthstone

A birthstone is a gemstone associated with a person's month of birth. Wearing one's birthstone is a popular custom in many cultures, and the modern list of stones by month was standardised in 1912 by the American National Association of Jewelers (now Jewelers of America), with a few later additions. The tradition is often traced to the twelve stones of the Breastplate of Aaron described in the Book of Exodus and to their association with the signs of the zodiac.

Origins

The historian Josephus, in the 1st century AD, connected the twelve stones of the high priest's breastplate with the twelve months and zodiac signs, and by the 8th century the custom of wearing a stone for one's birth month was emerging in Europe. Earlier, wearers were encouraged to own all twelve stones and wear each in its month. "Traditional" lists, drawn from older sources, differ in places from the 1912 modern list — for example assigning lapis lazuli to December and ruby to December in some older schemes.

Cultural variants

Ayurvedic tradition in India associates gemstones with the nine celestial influences of Navagraha astrology rather than birth months. Western astrology also assigns stones by zodiac sign, which do not align exactly with calendar months. Birthstones are commercially significant in jewellery, particularly for birthday gifts and mother's jewellery set with the birthstones of children.
