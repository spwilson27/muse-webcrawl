Tennessee is a landlocked state in the southeastern United States, bordered by eight other states. The Tennessee River divides it into three culturally distinct Grand Divisions — East, Middle, and West Tennessee. Its capital and largest city is Nashville, famed as a center of country music, and its other major cities include Memphis, Knoxville, and Chattanooga.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Cherokee and Chickasaw, inhabited the region for millennia before European settlement. Tennessee became the 16th state in 1796. It was the last state to join the Confederacy and the first readmitted after the Civil War, during which major battles were fought at Shiloh, Chattanooga, and Franklin. In the 20th century the federal Tennessee Valley Authority transformed the region with dams and electrification, and Oak Ridge played a key role in the Manhattan Project. Martin Luther King Jr. was assassinated in Memphis in 1968.

Geography and economy

The state stretches from the Mississippi River lowlands in the west to the Great Smoky Mountains — the most visited U.S. national park — in the east. Agriculture, automotive manufacturing (with plants of Nissan, Volkswagen, and General Motors), healthcare, logistics (FedEx is headquartered in Memphis), and tourism anchor the economy. Nashville's Grand Ole Opry and Memphis's Beale Street and Graceland make music a defining cultural export, from country and blues to rock and roll and soul.
