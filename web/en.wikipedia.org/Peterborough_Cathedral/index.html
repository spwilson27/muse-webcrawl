Peterborough Cathedral

Peterborough Cathedral, properly the Cathedral Church of St Peter, St Paul and St Andrew, is a cathedral in Peterborough, Cambridgeshire, England. It is the seat of the Bishop of Peterborough and one of the finest surviving examples of Norman architecture in England, renowned for its unique west front and painted wooden nave ceiling.

History

A monastery was founded on the site around 655 by Peada of Mercia, and an abbey church of the Benedictines flourished in Anglo-Saxon times before being destroyed by Vikings in 870 and refounded in 972. The present building was begun in 1118 after a fire, consecrated in 1238, and built largely in a remarkably uniform late Norman style. At the Dissolution of the Monasteries the abbey survived by becoming, in 1541, a cathedral of the new Diocese of Peterborough.

Architecture

The west front (c. 1200), with its three enormous arches, is an iconic and unconventional design in Early English Gothic. The nave retains a rare painted wooden ceiling of c. 1220–1240, decorated with kings, saints and musicians. The retrochoir, known as the New Building (c. 1500), has superb fan vaulting.

Burials

The cathedral is the burial place of Catherine of Aragon, first queen of Henry VIII, interred in 1536; her tomb remains a site of pilgrimage marked by pomegranates, her emblem. Mary, Queen of Scots was also buried here in 1587 before being moved to Westminster Abbey in 1612.
