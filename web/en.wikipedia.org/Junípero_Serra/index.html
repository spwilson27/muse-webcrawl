Saint Junípero Serra

Junípero Serra (1713–1784) was a Spanish Franciscan friar who founded a series of missions in Alta California, in what is now the U.S. state of California. He established nine of the 21 Spanish missions in California, beginning with Mission San Diego de Alcalá in 1769, and is often called the "Apostle of California". He was canonised by Pope Francis in 2015, the first saint canonised on U.S. soil.

Life

Born Miquel Josep Serra in Petra, Mallorca, he became a Franciscan, took the name Junípero, and taught philosophy before sailing to New Spain (Mexico) in 1749. He worked in the Sierra Gorda missions of Querétaro and in Baja California before being appointed president of the California missions after the Jesuits were expelled.

Missions

Serra personally founded nine missions, including San Diego (1769), San Carlos Borromeo de Carmelo (1770, his headquarters), San Antonio de Padua, San Gabriel Arcángel, San Luis Obispo, San Francisco de Asís, San Juan Capistrano, Santa Clara de Asís and San Buenaventura. He died at Mission San Carlos in 1784 and is buried there.

Legacy and controversy

Serra's legacy is contested. The Catholic Church honours him as a tireless evangeliser who defended indigenous people against some colonial abuses. Many Native American critics and historians, however, emphasise that the mission system involved forced labour, corporal punishment, suppression of indigenous cultures and catastrophic population decline from disease. During protests in 2020, several statues of Serra in California were toppled or removed.
