Confession of faith

For the sacrament of confession, see Sacrament of Penance.

A confession of faith is a statement of the shared beliefs of a community of religious believers. Confessions of faith in Christianity, known as creeds in their shorter liturgical forms, define the doctrines of a church or denomination. Adherents may be required to affirm the confession as a condition of membership, ordination or office.

An early modern printed confession of faith

Christianity

Early creeds

The earliest confessional statements include the Apostles' Creed and the Nicene Creed (325, expanded 381), which remain authoritative for most Christian traditions. These creeds summarise belief in the Trinity, the incarnation, crucifixion and resurrection of Christ.

Reformation confessions

During the Protestant Reformation, churches produced lengthy systematic confessions, including the Augsburg Confession (1530) for Lutherans, the Belgic Confession (1561), the Heidelberg Catechism (1563) and the Westminster Confession of Faith (1646) for Reformed and Presbyterian churches. Baptist confessions such as the London Baptist Confession of 1689 adapted Reformed statements. The Roman Catholic Church issued the decrees and canons of the Council of Trent as its confessional standard.

Modern statements

Many contemporary churches and evangelical organisations adopt concise "statements of faith" covering scripture, God, salvation and ethics. In some traditions the confession is understood as subordinate to, and always revisable in light of, the Bible (norma normata).

Other religions

Analogous declarations exist elsewhere: the Islamic shahada, the Maimonidean Thirteen Principles of Faith in Judaism, and affirmations in various Hindu and Buddhist communities, though these function differently from Christian doctrinal confessions.
