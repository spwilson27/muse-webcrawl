Informbiro period

The Informbiro period was an era in the history of Yugoslavia following the Tito–Stalin split in 1948. Named after the Informbiro — the Information Bureau of the Communist and Workers' Parties (Cominform) — the period lasted roughly from 1948 into the mid-1950s and was marked by Yugoslavia's break with the Soviet Union and the Eastern Bloc, purges of pro-Soviet communists, and the country's gradual reorientation toward the West and, later, non-alignment.

Background

In June 1948 the Cominform, at Soviet instigation, passed a resolution condemning the leadership of the Communist Party of Yugoslavia, and Yugoslavia was expelled from the organisation. Josip Broz Tito refused to submit to Stalin's demands, and the split became one of the defining events of the early Cold War.

Purges and Goli Otok

Within Yugoslavia, the regime moved against real and perceived supporters of the Cominform resolution, known as ibeovci. Thousands were arrested, expelled from the party, or sent to labour camps. The most notorious was the camp on Goli Otok ("Barren Island") in the Adriatic, established in 1949, where thousands of political prisoners were held in harsh conditions.

Consequences

Cut off from the Eastern Bloc, Yugoslavia accepted Western economic and military aid while developing its own model of "socialist self-management" and workers' councils, beginning with the 1950 law handing factories to workers' collectives. Relations with the USSR were normalised after Stalin's death, culminating in Khrushchev's visit to Belgrade in 1955, but Yugoslavia never rejoined the Soviet bloc and went on to co-found the Non-Aligned Movement in 1961.
