Benetton family

The Benetton family is an Italian family of entrepreneurs from Treviso, in the Veneto region, best known as the founders and owners of the Benetton Group clothing empire. Four siblings — Luciano (born 1935), Giuliana (born 1937), Gilberto (1941–2018), and Carlo (1943–2018) — founded the company in 1965, building a knitwear business into one of the world's most recognisable fashion brands. Through their holding company Edizione, the family diversified into motorway concessions (Atlantia/Autostrade per l'Italia), roadside restaurants (Autogrill), airports, agriculture, and real estate, becoming one of Italy's wealthiest families.

The four founders

The siblings' father, Leone Benetton, ran a bicycle rental business and died when Luciano was fourteen. Luciano worked as a clothing salesman, Giuliana knitted sweaters at home, and the brothers raised capital by selling a bicycle and an accordion. Gilberto became the family's financier and architect of its diversification, while Carlo oversaw production. Each of the four held an equal share of Edizione.

Edizione and diversification

Founded in 1981, Edizione invested the clothing profits in infrastructure and services: it acquired control of Autostrade, operator of most of Italy's toll motorways, and of Autogrill. After the 2018 collapse of Genoa's Morandi Bridge, operated by an Atlantia subsidiary, the family faced intense political pressure and agreed to cede control of Autostrade to a consortium led by the state lender Cassa Depositi e Prestiti in 2021–22.

Second generation

Members of the next generation have taken increasing roles. Alessandro Benetton, Luciano's son, chaired Benetton Group from 2012 to 2014 and founded the private-equity firm 21 Invest; in 2022 he became chairman of Edizione as the holding reorganised its governance.
