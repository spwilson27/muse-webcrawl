Bessarabia is a historical region in Eastern Europe, bounded by the Dniester River on the east and the Prut River on the west, with the Danube and the Black Sea to the south. About two-thirds of Bessarabia lies within modern Moldova, with the Ukrainian oblast of Chernivtsi in the north and the Budjak area of Odesa Oblast in the south.

History

The territory formed the eastern part of the Principality of Moldavia before the Ottoman Empire's expansion; in 1812, by the Treaty of Bucharest ending a Russo-Turkish war, the Russian Empire annexed Bessarabia. After the Crimean War, a southwestern strip was returned to Moldavia in 1856 but regained by Russia in 1878. Following the Russian Revolution, Bessarabia's assembly, the Sfatul Țării, proclaimed union with Romania in 1918, a status recognised by the Treaty of Paris (1920) though disputed by the Soviet Union. In 1940, under the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact's secret protocol, the USSR annexed Bessarabia, organising most of it as the Moldavian SSR; after a Romanian-German occupation during the Second World War, Soviet control was restored in 1944 and lasted until Moldova's independence in 1991.

Geography and people

Bessarabia is a fertile agricultural region of steppe and rolling hills, known for grain, sunflowers, wine and fruit production. Its population has long been ethnically mixed — Moldovans/Romanians forming the majority, alongside Ukrainians, Russians, Bulgarians, Gagauz, Germans (until 1940) and a formerly substantial Jewish population largely destroyed in the Holocaust.
