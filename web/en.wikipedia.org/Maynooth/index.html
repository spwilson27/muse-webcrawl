Maynooth (Irish: Maigh Nuad) is a university town in north County Kildare, Ireland, about 25 kilometres west of Dublin. Its name derives from the plain of Nuada, a figure of Irish mythology. It is home to Maynooth University and St Patrick's College, the national seminary of the Roman Catholic Church in Ireland.

History

The town grew around Maynooth Castle, a stronghold of the FitzGerald earls of Kildare founded in the twelfth century near the meeting of the Lyreen and Joan Slade rivers. The castle was besieged during the Silken Thomas rebellion of 1535. In 1795 St Patrick's College was founded by an act of the Irish parliament as a seminary for Catholic priests, and the Royal Canal and later the railway connected the town to Dublin.

Education

Maynooth University, established formally in 1997 from the colleges on the site, is one of Ireland's youngest universities and gives the town a large student population relative to its size. St Patrick's College continues to train seminarians, and its Pugin-designed Gothic revival buildings are among the town's landmarks.

Transport and amenities

Maynooth railway station is served by Dublin commuter trains on the Western Commuter line, and the town lies just off the M4 motorway. Carton House, a historic estate hotel and golf resort, lies to the east of the town.
