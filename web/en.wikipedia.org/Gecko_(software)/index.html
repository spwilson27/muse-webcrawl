Gecko (software)

Gecko is a browser engine developed by the Mozilla Foundation and its contributors. It is used in the Firefox web browser and in a range of other products, including the Thunderbird email client, to render web pages and, historically, application user interfaces written in XUL. Gecko is written in C++ and Rust and is cross-platform, running on Windows, macOS, Linux, and Android.

History

Gecko began development at Netscape in 1997 after the company acquired DigitalStyle and its layout engine technology. Netscape named the project "Raptor" and later "NGLayout" before settling on Gecko. In 1998 the engine formed the basis of the Mozilla Application Suite, and it was first shipped commercially in Netscape 6 in November 2000. AOL's acquisition-era decisions and the long development cycle meant Internet Explorer dominated the market, but Gecko's strong standards support helped drive the revival of browser competition in the 2000s.

In 2017 Mozilla shipped Quantum Flow and other parts of Project Quantum, incorporating components from the experimental Servo engine, including the Stylo style system written in Rust. Servo's parallel layout work had begun in 2013 as a research project with Samsung.

Standards support

Gecko supports HTML, CSS, the Document Object Model, XML, XHTML, SVG, ECMAScript (JavaScript, executed by the SpiderMonkey engine), and MathML. Its Acid2 and Acid3 test results were widely used in the 2000s as benchmarks of standards compliance among browser engines.
