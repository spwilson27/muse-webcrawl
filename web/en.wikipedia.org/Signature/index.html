Signature

A signature (from Latin: signare, "to sign") is a handwritten (and often stylised) depiction of someone's name, nickname, or even a simple "X" or other mark that a person writes on documents as a proof of identity and intent. The writer of a signature is a signatory. Similar to a handwritten signature, a signature work describes the work as readily identifying its creator.

Function and legal status

Traditionally, the function of a signature is to permanently attach to a document a person's uniquely identifying mark, attesting that they authored, approved or agreed to be bound by it. Signatures appear on contracts, cheques, wills, letters and works of art. In many legal systems any mark made with intent to sign is valid, even if it is not a legible name. Forgery of a signature is a crime in most jurisdictions.

History

Personal seals and signet rings served as signatures in the ancient world and remain important in East Asia, where stamps (such as the Japanese hanko, Chinese yìnzhāng and Korean dojang) are still used in place of, or alongside, handwritten signatures. The flourished penmanship of early modern Europe made the handwritten signature the standard personal mark in the West, and celebrated examples — such as John Hancock's large signature on the United States Declaration of Independence — became famous.

Electronic and digital signatures

Modern law increasingly recognises electronic signatures (typed names, click-to-sign records, or signature images attached to a document) and digital signatures, which use public-key cryptography to bind a signer's identity to a document and detect tampering. Laws such as the ESIGN Act in the United States and the eIDAS Regulation in the European Union give such signatures legal effect.

Other uses

The word is used metaphorically in many fields: in music, signature may refer to a key signature or time signature; in computing, a type signature describes a function's inputs and outputs; and in mathematics the signature of a quadratic form or metric counts its positive and negative directions.
