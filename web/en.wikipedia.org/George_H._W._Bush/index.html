George Herbert Walker Bush
Born: 12 June 1924, Milton, Massachusetts
Died: 30 November 2018, Houston, Texas
41st President of the United States (1989–1993)
43rd Vice President (1981–1989)

George Herbert Walker Bush (12 June 1924 – 30 November 2018) was an American politician and diplomat who served as the 41st president of the United States from 1989 to 1993. A Republican, he previously served as vice president under Ronald Reagan, and held posts including congressman, ambassador to the United Nations, envoy to China, and director of the CIA.

Early life and career

Bush enlisted in the US Navy on his eighteenth birthday and became one of its youngest aviators; he was shot down over the Pacific in 1944 and rescued by a submarine. After graduating from Yale, he moved to Texas and co-founded an oil business before entering politics in the 1960s.

Presidency

Bush's presidency was dominated by foreign policy at the end of the Cold War: he managed the peaceful reunification of Germany and the dissolution of the Soviet Union, ordered the invasion of Panama (1989), and led a broad coalition that expelled Iraqi forces from Kuwait in the Gulf War (1991). At home, he signed the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Clean Air Act amendments, but broke his "no new taxes" pledge in a 1990 budget deal, damaging him politically. Amid recession, he lost the 1992 election to Bill Clinton. His son George W. Bush became the 43rd president.
