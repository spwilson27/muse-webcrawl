Toothbrush

A toothbrush is an oral hygiene tool used to clean the teeth, gums, and tongue. It consists of a head of tightly clustered bristles, onto which toothpaste is applied, mounted on a handle that facilitates the cleaning of hard-to-reach areas of the mouth. They are usually used alongside flossing for maintaining proper oral hygiene. Toothbrushes are available in a variety of sizes and bristle firmness; dentists generally recommend soft-bristled brushes used twice daily.

History

Various forms of tooth-cleaning tools have been used since ancient times, including chew sticks used by the Egyptians and Babylonians, and twigs with frayed ends. The bristle toothbrush, similar to the type used today, was invented in China, traditionally credited to the Hongzhi Emperor in 1498, using hog bristles attached to bamboo or bone handles. Mass production began in England in the 1780s under William Addis. Nylon bristles were introduced by DuPont in 1938, and the first electric toothbrush was developed in Switzerland in 1954.

Types

Manual toothbrush

The standard manual toothbrush has a plastic handle and nylon bristles graded as soft, medium, or hard. Soft brushes are generally recommended to avoid damaging enamel and gums.

Electric toothbrush

Electric toothbrushes use powered oscillation or rotation of the head to clean teeth. Studies suggest oscillating-rotating brushes may remove slightly more plaque than manual brushing.

Interdental brushes and chewable brushes

Interdental brushes clean between teeth, while chewable toothbrushes are disposable brushes used without water or toothpaste, common in travel and vending contexts.

Technique and guidance

Common guidance is to brush for two minutes twice a day with a fluoride toothpaste, holding the brush at a 45-degree angle to the gum line and using gentle circular motions. Brushes should be replaced approximately every three to four months or sooner if bristles are frayed.
