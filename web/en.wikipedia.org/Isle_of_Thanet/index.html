Isle of Thanet

Thanet forms the north-eastern tip of Kent, England.

The Isle of Thanet lies at the most easterly point of Kent, England. While in the past it was separated from the mainland by the Wantsum Channel, it is no longer a true island, the channel having silted up by the 16th century. Archaeological evidence shows the area was an early site of human settlement and Roman and Anglo-Saxon landings.

Geography

Thanet is bounded by the sea on three sides and by the River Stour to the south-west. Its principal towns are the seaside resorts of Margate, Broadstairs and Ramsgate, together with inland villages such as Minster and Birchington-on-Sea. The chalk cliffs around North Foreland and Botany Bay are notable coastal features.

History

The island was the landing place of the legendary Anglo-Saxon leaders Hengist and Horsa in 449 and of St Augustine's mission to England in 597, which landed near Ebbsfleet. Thanet developed as a holiday destination in the 18th and 19th centuries, and Ramsgate was a major embarkation port during the Napoleonic Wars and the Dunkirk evacuation.
