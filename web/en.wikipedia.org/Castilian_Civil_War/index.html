Castilian Civil War

Date1366–1369
LocationCrown of Castile (Iberian Peninsula)
CombatantsPeter I of Castile vs. Henry of Trastámara (Henry II)
Foreign involvementEngland (for Peter); France and Aragon (for Henry)
ResultVictory for Henry of Trastámara; founding of the House of Trastámara

The Castilian Civil War (1366–1369) was a conflict between King Peter I of Castile and his illegitimate half-brother Henry, Count of Trastámara, for the Castilian throne. The war became intertwined with the Hundred Years' War, drawing in England on Peter's side and France on Henry's, and ended with Henry killing Peter and founding a new royal dynasty.

Background

Peter I, called "the Cruel" by his enemies and "the Just" by his supporters, alienated much of the Castilian nobility through his favouritism, his treatment of his French queen Blanche of Bourbon, and his reliance on Jewish and Muslim officials. Henry of Trastámara, an illegitimate son of Alfonso XI, built a coalition of discontented nobles backed by Aragon and by French free companies led by Bertrand du Guesclin.

Course of the war

In 1366 Henry invaded and was crowned at Burgos while Peter fled. Peter secured English support through the Treaty of Libourne with Edward, the Black Prince, whose army decisively defeated Henry at the Battle of Nájera (3 April 1367), where du Guesclin was captured. But the alliance collapsed over unpaid costs and sickness in the English army; the Black Prince withdrew, and Henry returned in 1368. After the Battle of Montiel in March 1369, Peter was trapped in the castle of Montiel, where Henry killed him with his own hand, according to tradition with du Guesclin's help.

Consequences

Henry II became the first king of the House of Trastámara, which ruled Castile until the Catholic Monarchs and, through succession, shaped the later Spanish monarchy. The war also strengthened Castile's alliance with France, committing its powerful fleet against England in the Hundred Years' War.
