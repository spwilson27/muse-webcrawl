The Bovidae comprise the biological family of cloven-hoofed, ruminant mammals that includes cattle, bison, buffalo, antelopes (including gazelles), sheep and goats. With more than 140 living species, it is the largest family within the order Artiodactyla. Wild bovids occur naturally on every continent except Australia and Antarctica.

Characteristics

All male bovids — and females in many species — bear horns: unbranched bony cores sheathed in keratin that, unlike antlers, are never shed. Bovids are specialised grazers and browsers with four-chambered stomachs that ferment cellulose, and most live in herds with complex social hierarchies.

Subfamilies and importance

The family is usually divided into subfamilies such as Bovinae (cattle, buffalo, bison), Caprinae (sheep, goats, ibex), and the many antelope groups (Antilopinae, Alcelaphinae, Hippotraginae and others). Domesticated bovids — cattle, sheep, goats, water buffalo and yak — underpin human agriculture worldwide, providing meat, milk, hides, wool and draught power.
