Austin Seven

Manufacturer: Austin Motor Company

Production: 1923–1939

Class: Economy car

Production total: approx. 290,000

The Austin Seven is an economy car that was produced from 1923 until 1939 in the United Kingdom by the Austin Motor Company. Nicknamed the "Baby Austin", it was one of the most popular cars ever produced for the British market and sold well abroad. Its effect on the British market was similar to that of the Model T Ford in the United States, replacing most other British economy cars and cyclecars of the early 1920s.

Design and production

Designed principally by Herbert Austin with the young draftsman Stanley Edge, the Seven used a small 696 cc (later 747 cc) side-valve four-cylinder engine in an A-frame chassis. It was built under licence abroad: it became the first BMW car (the Dixi), the basis of the French Rosengart, and was copied by Nissan in Japan. Coachbuilt and sports versions, including the supercharged Ulster, achieved notable competition success.

Legacy

The Seven's design underpinned many later specials and racing cars; Colin Chapman built his first car, the Lotus Mark I, on an Austin Seven chassis. Many examples survive and are supported by an active owners' club movement.
