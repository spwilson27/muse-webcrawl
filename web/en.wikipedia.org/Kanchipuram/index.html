Kanchipuram

Kanchipuram
State: Tamil Nadu, India
Nickname: City of a Thousand Temples
Famous for: Kanchipuram silk sarees

Kanchipuram is a city in the Indian state of Tamil Nadu, about 72 kilometres southwest of Chennai. One of the seven sacred cities (Sapta Puri) of Hinduism, it is renowned for its many temples — earning it the name "city of a thousand temples" — and for its handwoven silk sarees.

History

Kanchipuram was the capital of the Pallava kingdom from the 3rd to the 9th century CE, and the Pallavas raised some of the earliest stone temples in South India here, including the Kailasanathar temple and the shore temples of their port at Mahabalipuram. The city later flourished under the Cholas and the Vijayanagara Empire. It was a centre of learning and religious debate, associated with Buddhism and Jainism as well as Hinduism, and was visited by the Chinese monk Xuanzang in the 7th century.

Temples and silk

Major temples include Ekambareswarar, dedicated to Shiva as the element earth; Kamakshi Amman; Varadaraja Perumal, dedicated to Vishnu; and the Vaikunta Perumal temple. The city is divided between Shaivite and Vaishnavite quarters. Kanchipuram's silk sarees, woven with pure mulberry silk and gold zari in contrasting borders, hold a Geographical Indication tag and are a staple of South Indian weddings.
