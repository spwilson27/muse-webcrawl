Bronchitis is inflammation of the bronchi in the lungs that causes coughing. Bronchitis usually begins as an infection in the nose, ears, throat, or sinuses and then spreads to the bronchi. Symptoms include coughing up mucus, wheezing, shortness of breath and chest discomfort. It is divided into two types: acute and chronic.

Acute bronchitis

Acute bronchitis is a short-term inflammation of the bronchi, most often caused by the same viruses that cause the common cold and influenza. It is characterised by a cough, often productive of sputum, lasting typically one to three weeks. It is one of the most common reasons for medical visits. Because the cause is almost always viral, antibiotics are not recommended for otherwise healthy people; treatment is supportive — rest, fluids and cough suppressants or honey for symptom relief.

Chronic bronchitis

Chronic bronchitis is defined as a productive cough lasting at least three months in each of two successive years, after other causes are excluded. It is one of the two main forms of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), along with emphysema. The dominant cause is long-term exposure to tobacco smoke; occupational dusts, air pollution and repeated infections also contribute. Chronic inflammation narrows the airways and damages the lungs' mucus-clearing mechanisms.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the history and examination; a chest X-ray or lung function tests (spirometry) are used when pneumonia or COPD is suspected. The foundation of chronic bronchitis management is removing the irritant — above all, stopping smoking — supplemented by bronchodilators, inhaled steroids, pulmonary rehabilitation and vaccination against influenza and pneumococcus.

Epidemiology

Acute bronchitis affects about 5% of adults annually. Chronic bronchitis/COPD affects hundreds of millions worldwide and is among the leading causes of death globally.
