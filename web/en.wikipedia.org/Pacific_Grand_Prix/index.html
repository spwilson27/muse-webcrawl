The Pacific Grand Prix (Japanese: パシフィックグランプリ) was a round of the Formula One World Championship twice in the mid-1990s and three times non-World Motorcycle Grand Prix. Non-championship Pacific Grand Prix were also held in the United States in the early 1960s. The name was later used for rounds of the Super GT and MotoGP world championships held in Japan.

Formula One races

The event was established so that Japan would host two Formula One races, alongside the Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka. Both editions were held at the remote Tanaka International Circuit (now Okayama International Circuit) in Aida, Okayama Prefecture. Michael Schumacher won both races for Benetton. The 1994 race is remembered for a first-lap collision involving Ayrton Senna, and the 1995 race was moved from April to October following the Great Hanshin earthquake; Schumacher's victory there clinched his second World Championship. The event was dropped for 1996 owing to low attendance, a consequence of the circuit's remote location.

Winners

1994: Michael Schumacher (Benetton-Ford); 1995: Michael Schumacher (Benetton-Renault).
