Alfonso XII of Spain

Alfonso XII of Spain (28 November 1857 – 25 November 1885) was King of Spain from 1874 to 1885. Known as "the Peacemaker," he restored the Bourbon dynasty and brought stability after the turmoil of the Democratic Sexennium and the First Spanish Republic.

Accession

Born in Madrid to Isabella II, Alfonso was exiled at age ten. Educated at the Theresianum in Vienna and Sandhurst in England, he issued the Sandhurst Manifesto in 1874 promising a constitutional monarchy. A military rising at Sagunto proclaimed him king on 29 December 1874.

Achievements

His reign saw the Constitution of 1876, the end of the Third Carlist War, and peace in Cuba under the Pact of Zanjón (1878). The political system designed by Cánovas del Castillo, with alternating Conservative and Liberal parties, lasted for decades.

Death

Always delicate in health, Alfonso concealed a recurrence of tuberculosis. He died at the Royal Palace of El Pardo three days short of his 28th birthday, leaving his widow pregnant with the future Alfonso XIII. A monument to him stands in Madrid's Retiro Park.
