Edward Norton Lorenz (23 May 1917 – 16 April 2008) was an American mathematician and meteorologist, known as the founder of modern chaos theory and the originator of the concept of the butterfly effect. He spent most of his career at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.

Life and career

Lorenz studied mathematics at Dartmouth College and Harvard, and earned a PhD in mathematics from Harvard in 1948, turning to meteorology during wartime service as a weather forecaster for the United States Army Air Corps. He joined MIT's meteorology department in 1955 and remained there for the rest of his career, becoming head of the department in 1962. He was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 1975.

Work

His 1963 paper "Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow" showed that a simplified model of fluid convection could behave nonperiodically and unpredictably despite being fully deterministic, founding the study of chaotic systems. The Lorenz equations remain a canonical model of chaos. Lorenz's work established a theoretical limit to weather prediction and influenced fields far beyond meteorology, from ecology to economics. He received the Crafoord Prize in 1983 and the Kyoto Prize in 1991.
