Tapi River

Tapi (Tapti) River
SourceSatpura Range, Multai, Madhya Pradesh
MouthGulf of Khambhat, Arabian Sea (near Surat, Gujarat)
LengthAbout 724 km

The Tapi River, also spelled Tapti, is a river in central India, about 724 kilometres long. One of the few major peninsular rivers that flow westward, it rises in the Satpura Range near Multai in Madhya Pradesh and empties into the Gulf of Khambhat of the Arabian Sea near Surat in Gujarat.

Course

The Tapi flows west through the Khandesh region of Maharashtra, between the Satpura Range to the north and the Ajanta hills, passing Burhanpur and Jalgaon before entering the Gujarat plain. It is unusual in flowing west through a rift valley, in contrast to most Deccan rivers, which flow east to the Bay of Bengal. Its major tributaries include the Purna, Girna, and Waghur rivers.

Significance

The Ukai Dam, one of Gujarat's largest reservoirs, provides irrigation and hydroelectric power. Surat, at the river's mouth, was historically a major port of the Mughal Empire.
