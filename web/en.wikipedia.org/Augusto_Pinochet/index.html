Augusto Pinochet

Augusto Pinochet

Born: 25 November 1915, Valparaíso, Chile

Died: 10 December 2006 (aged 91), Santiago, Chile

Office: President of Chile, 1974–1990; head of the military junta from 1973

Augusto José Ramón Pinochet Ugarte (25 November 1915 – 10 December 2006) was a Chilean army officer and dictator who ruled Chile from 1973 to 1990. He came to power in the military coup of 11 September 1973 that overthrew the democratically elected government of President Salvador Allende, ending the nearly four-decade Latin American tradition of constitutional government in Chile.

Rule

Pinochet's junta dissolved Congress, banned political parties, and carried out a campaign of repression against leftists and dissidents. Chile's official truth commissions later documented more than 3,000 people killed or forcibly disappeared and tens of thousands tortured or imprisoned. Economically, his government applied free-market policies advised by the "Chicago Boys", privatising state enterprises and pensions; the reforms were accompanied by sharp recessions, high unemployment, and increased inequality, though Chile later experienced strong growth.

Transition and later years

A 1988 plebiscite, held under the 1980 constitution, rejected a further eight-year term for Pinochet. Free elections followed in 1989, and he handed over the presidency to Patricio Aylwin in March 1990, remaining commander-in-chief of the army until 1998 and then senator-for-life. In October 1998 he was arrested in London on a Spanish extradition request concerning human rights abuses; after lengthy legal proceedings he was released on medical grounds in 2000. In Chile he later faced multiple prosecutions and investigations into corruption before his death in 2006, without conviction.
