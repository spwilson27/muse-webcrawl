Regina caeli

Regina caeli (Latin for "Queen of Heaven") is a musical antiphon addressed to the Blessed Virgin Mary, used by the Latin Church in place of the Angelus during Eastertide, from Compline on Holy Saturday until midday on the Saturday after Pentecost. It is one of the four seasonal Marian antiphons, alongside the Alma Redemptoris Mater, the Ave Regina caelorum and the Salve Regina.

Text

The Latin text begins: Regina caeli, laetare, alleluia; quia quem meruisti portare, alleluia; resurrexit, sicut dixit, alleluia; ora pro nobis Deum, alleluia — "Queen of Heaven, rejoice, alleluia; for he whom you were worthy to bear, alleluia; has risen, as he said, alleluia; pray for us to God, alleluia."

History and music

The antiphon's author is unknown; it dates to the 12th century or earlier and was sung by Franciscans in the 13th century. A legend associates its origin with Pope Gregory the Great, who was said to have heard angels singing its first lines during a procession in Rome. Composers from the Renaissance onward — including Victoria, Charpentier and Mozart (KV 127 and KV 276) — have set the text many times.
