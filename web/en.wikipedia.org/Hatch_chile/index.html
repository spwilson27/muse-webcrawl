Hatch chile

Hatch chiles are a group of cultivars of the New Mexico chile pepper (Capsicum annuum) grown in the Hatch Valley of southern New Mexico, centered on the village of Hatch. The chiles are harvested both green and red, and are a defining ingredient of New Mexican cuisine. Strictly speaking, "Hatch chile" refers to peppers grown in the Hatch Valley — an appellation tied to the region's soil, altitude and climate — though the name is used more loosely in commerce, which has prompted certification efforts.

History and cultivation

Chile peppers were cultivated in the region for centuries, and modern New Mexico pod types were developed beginning in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, notably by Fabian Garcia at what is now New Mexico State University, who released 'New Mexico No. 9' in 1913, a foundational cultivar. The Hatch Valley, irrigated by the Rio Grande below Elephant Butte Reservoir, became known for producing chiles with a distinctive balance of sweetness and heat. Popular commercial cultivars grown there include 'Big Jim' — once holder of the record for the world's largest chile pod — and varieties rated from mild to extra-hot on the Scoville scale.

Culinary use

Green Hatch chiles are typically fire-roasted until the skin blisters and blackens, then peeled and used in stews, sauces, enchiladas and the ubiquitous green chile cheeseburger. Red chiles, the fully ripened fruit, are dried in ristras or ground into sauces. Roasting season in late summer is a cultural event: roasters appear outside grocery stores throughout New Mexico and increasingly across the United States, and the village of Hatch hosts the Hatch Chile Festival each Labor Day weekend, drawing tens of thousands of visitors.

Protection of the name

In 2012, New Mexico enacted a law requiring that chiles marketed as "New Mexico" chiles actually be grown in the state, and producers in the Hatch Valley have pursued trademark and certification programs to prevent out-of-state and foreign-grown peppers from being sold under the Hatch name.
