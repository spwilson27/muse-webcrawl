G.I. Joe

G.I. Joe figures from the "A Real American Hero" line

G.I. Joe is an American media franchise and line of action figures owned and produced by the toy company Hasbro. The initial product offering represented four of the branches of the U.S. armed forces with the Action Soldier (U.S. Army), Action Sailor (U.S. Navy), Action Pilot (U.S. Air Force), and Action Marine (U.S. Marine Corps). The name is derived from the usage of "G.I." as a generic term for a U.S. soldier, derived from the term "Government Issue".

History

G.I. Joe was created by toy designer Stan Weston and developed by Hasbro executive Donald Levine. It was introduced in 1964 as a line of 11.5-inch (about 30 cm) poseable figures — a format for which Hasbro coined the term "action figure" because the word "doll" was thought to be unacceptable for a boys' toy. The line was a major commercial success through the 1960s.

In 1982 the franchise was relaunched as G.I. Joe: A Real American Hero, with smaller 3.75-inch figures, vehicles, and playsets. The relaunch was supported by a Marvel Comics series written largely by Larry Hama and an animated television series (1983–1986) famous for its closing public-service segments ending in "And knowing is half the battle." In the new storyline, the G.I. Joe team is an elite special-missions force battling the terrorist organisation Cobra, led by Cobra Commander and including characters such as Destro and the Baroness. Popular heroes include Duke, Snake Eyes, Scarlett, and Roadblock.

Later media

The franchise has expanded into multiple comic book series, animated series, video games, and live-action films, including G.I. Joe: The Rise of Cobra (2009), G.I. Joe: Retaliation (2013), and Snake Eyes (2021). G.I. Joe remains one of Hasbro's flagship brands.
