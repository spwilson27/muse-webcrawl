Hardfacing

Hardfacing is a metalworking process where harder or tougher materials are applied to a base metal. It is welded to the base material, and generally takes the form of specialized electrodes for arc welding or filler rod for oxyacetylene and gas tungsten arc welding welding. Powders are used in plasma thermal spray, plasma-spray hardfacing, thermal spray, and cold spray processes. Hardfacing is the most economical method of modifying the surfaces of metals without changing the properties of the underlying components.

The process is used to reduce wear on components subject to abrasion, impact, erosion, cavitation and corrosion, thereby extending the service life of machinery and reducing replacement costs. Hardfacing materials are applied as a relatively thick layer (typically 1 to 10 mm) rather than as a thin coating.

Methods

Hardfacing can be applied by a variety of welding and spraying techniques, including:

  • Shielded metal arc welding (SMAW), commonly using covered electrodes
  • Gas metal arc welding (GMAW) and flux-cored arc welding (FCAW)
  • Submerged arc welding (SAW), often for large flat surfaces and rolls
  • Oxyacetylene welding, which gives low dilution and good control
  • Plasma transferred arc (PTA) welding, used for precise, low-dilution deposits
  • Laser cladding and thermal spraying with alloys such as cobalt-based Stellite and nickel-based alloys

Materials

Hardfacing alloys are generally classified by their composition and the type of wear they resist. Common groups include low-alloy steels, high-chromium irons, cobalt-based alloys (such as Stellite), nickel-based alloys, and composites containing tungsten carbide particles in a metallic matrix. Cobalt-based alloys retain their hardness at elevated temperatures and resist galling and corrosion, while tungsten carbide composites resist severe abrasion but relatively low impact.

Applications

Hardfacing is widely used in industries where equipment operates in abrasive environments: earth-moving and mining machinery (bucket teeth, dozer blades, crusher jaws), agricultural implements, dredging equipment, valves and valve seats, pump components, and steel mill rolls. The process can be applied both to new components, to build in wear resistance, and to worn parts, to rebuild them to their original dimensions.
