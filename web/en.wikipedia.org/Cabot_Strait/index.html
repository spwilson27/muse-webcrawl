Cabot Strait

LocationBetween Cape Breton Island and Newfoundland, Canada
WidthAbout 110 km (68 mi)
ConnectsGulf of St. Lawrence and the Atlantic Ocean

The Cabot Strait (French: Détroit de Cabot) is a strait in eastern Canada, lying between Cape Breton Island, Nova Scotia, to the south and the island of Newfoundland to the north. At roughly 110 kilometres wide, it is the largest of the three outlets joining the Gulf of St. Lawrence to the Atlantic Ocean, the others being the Strait of Belle Isle and the Strait of Canso.

The strait is named for the Italian explorer Giovanni Caboto (John Cabot), who reached the region in 1497. It is a major shipping artery and is crossed by Marine Atlantic ferries between North Sydney, Nova Scotia, and Channel-Port aux Basques, Newfoundland, a constitutionally significant link uniting Newfoundland with mainland Canada. Communication and power cables also cross the strait.

History

During the Second World War the strait was a hunting ground for German U-boats in the Battle of the St. Lawrence; in October 1942 U-69 torpedoed and sank the ferry SS Caribou in the strait with the loss of 137 lives, one of Canada's worst wartime maritime disasters at home.
