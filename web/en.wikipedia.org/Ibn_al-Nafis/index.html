Ibn al-Nafis

Born 1213, Damascus
Died 1288, Cairo
Known for Pulmonary circulation

Ala-al-Din Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Abi-Hazm al-Qarshi (1213 – 17 December 1288), known as Ibn al-Nafis, was an Arab polymath whose areas of work included medicine, surgery, physiology, anatomy, biology, Islamic studies, jurisprudence, and philosophy. He is best known for being the first to describe the pulmonary circulation of the blood.

Biography

Ibn al-Nafis was born in 1213 in Damascus and studied medicine at the Nuri Hospital there. He later moved to Cairo, where he served as chief physician at the Al-Nasiri Hospital and later at the Al-Mansouri Hospital, and as personal physician to the sultan.

Pulmonary circulation

In his Commentary on Anatomy in Avicenna's Canon (1242), Ibn al-Nafis described how blood flows from the right ventricle of the heart to the left through the lungs, rejecting the Galenic idea of invisible pores in the interventricular septum. His account predates the European descriptions by William Harvey and others by several centuries.

Other works

He wrote the Comprehensive Book on Medicine, one of the largest medical encyclopedias of its time, as well as commentaries on Hippocrates and Avicenna, and a work of logic and theology.
