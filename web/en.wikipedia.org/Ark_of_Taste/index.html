Ark of Taste

The Ark of Taste (Italian: Arca del Gusto) is an international catalogue of endangered heritage foods maintained by the Slow Food movement. Founded in 1996 and headquartered in Bra, Piedmont, Italy, it documents distinctive regional foods, crop varieties, and livestock breeds that are at risk of extinction.

Purpose and criteria

The Ark exists to point out the existence and uniqueness of these products and to draw attention to the risk of their disappearance, encouraging action to protect them — by eating them, supporting their producers, or conserving their genetic stock. To be listed, a product must be of distinctive quality, linked to the culture, history, or tradition of a region, produced in limited quantities, and at risk of extinction.

Contents

Some 6,000 products from more than 150 countries have been catalogued. Examples include the Çakmak cheese of Turkey, the Tequila agave landscapes' traditional products, American heritage livestock breeds, the British Greengage and numerous artisan cheeses, cured meats, breads, and fermented drinks. Entries describe the product, its history, production area, and producers.

Related programmes

The Ark is complemented by Slow Food Presidia, projects that work directly with small-scale producers to safeguard specific Ark products, and by the broader Slow Food Foundation for Biodiversity, established in 2003 to fund such work. The Ark inspired comparable seed- and breed-saving efforts and was showcased prominently at the Salone del Gusto and Terra Madre events in Turin.
