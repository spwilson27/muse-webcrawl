Emergency locator beacon

An emergency locator beacon is a radio beacon used to locate aviators, mariners, and land-based users in distress and in need of immediate rescue. It is a battery-powered radio transmitter designed to be activated in a life-threatening emergency; when activated, it transmits a continuous distress signal that is detected by the international Cospas-Sarsat satellite system and relayed to search and rescue authorities.

Types

  • ELT (Emergency Locator Transmitter) — carried aboard aircraft and activated automatically by impact or manually by the crew.
  • EPIRB (Emergency Position-Indicating Radio Beacon) — carried aboard ships and boats; many models float free and activate automatically when submerged.
  • PLB (Personal Locator Beacon) — carried by individuals on land, such as hikers and pilots, and activated manually.

Operation

Modern beacons transmit digitally on the 406 MHz frequency, encoding a unique identifier registered to the vessel, aircraft, or user, and in many cases a GNSS position. Cospas-Sarsat satellites in low Earth orbit and geostationary orbit detect the signal; newer medium-orbit (MEOSAR) payloads on navigation satellites allow near-instantaneous detection and location. Older analog beacons transmitting only on 121.5 MHz are no longer monitored by satellite.

Regulation

Carriage and registration of beacons are mandated by international conventions and national regulators, including SOLAS requirements for ships and aviation authorities' rules for aircraft. Registration of the beacon's unique identifier is essential so rescuers can verify alerts and obtain information about the craft in distress.
