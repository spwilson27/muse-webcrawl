Protein fold

The term protein fold refers to the general three-dimensional architecture of a protein, that is, the spatial arrangement of its secondary structure elements independent of the details of side chains and loops. Proteins sharing the same fold have the same major arrangement of α-helices and β-sheets with similar connectivity, even when their sequences differ greatly.

Classification

Fold classifications organize known structures hierarchically. The SCOP database groups structures into classes, folds, superfamilies, and families; CATH uses Class, Architecture, Topology, and Homologous superfamily. Common folds include the Rossmann fold, the immunoglobulin fold, the TIM barrel, and the globin fold. Estimates of the number of distinct folds in nature range around one to a few thousand.

Evolution and design

Fold similarity can reflect common ancestry or convergent evolution to a stable, favorable architecture. Fold space informs protein structure prediction and de novo protein design, where new sequences are created to adopt target folds.
