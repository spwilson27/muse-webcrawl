Anders Hejlsberg (born 2 December 1960) is a Danish software engineer who co-designed several influential programming languages and development tools. He was the original author of Turbo Pascal and the chief architect of Delphi at Borland, and at Microsoft he led the design of C# and later created TypeScript. He is a Technical Fellow at Microsoft.

Early career

Hejlsberg studied engineering at the Technical University of Denmark but left before graduating to work in software. In the early 1980s he wrote a Pascal compiler, sold as Turbo Pascal by Borland, whose speed and low price made it a landmark product. He remained at Borland, becoming chief architect of its successor, Delphi, a rapid application development environment built on Object Pascal.

Microsoft

Hejlsberg moved to Microsoft in 1996, after one of the software industry's most famous acqui-hires. There he worked on J++ and the Windows Foundation Classes before becoming lead architect of C#, the flagship language of the .NET platform, released in 2000. In 2012 Microsoft released TypeScript, a superset of JavaScript adding static types, which Hejlsberg designed to support large-scale application development; it became one of the most widely adopted languages in web development.

Awards and honours

Hejlsberg has received numerous awards, including the Dr. Dobb's Excellence in Programming Award (2001, jointly with the C# team) and Popular Science's "Best of What's New" recognition. He frequently presents at developer conferences and is regarded as one of the most influential language designers of his generation.
