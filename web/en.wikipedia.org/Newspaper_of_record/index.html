Newspaper of record

A newspaper of record is a major national newspaper with a large circulation whose editorial and news-gathering functions are considered authoritative and independent. The term also has a narrower legal meaning: a publication designated by a government to publish public or legal notices.

Journalistic usage

Examples commonly described as newspapers of record include The New York Times in the United States, The Times in the United Kingdom, Le Monde in France, the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung in Germany and The Hindu in India. Such papers are expected to maintain large reporting staffs, extensive archives, corrections practices and influence on the wider news agenda; other media often follow their reporting.

Origins

The phrase derives from The New York Times's marketing of its published index and its slogan-like reputation for printing a complete record; the paper won an early legal dispute over the phrase "newspaper of record" usage. The designation is informal and sometimes criticised as elitist or self-appointed.

Legal notices

In the legal sense, courts and governments designate newspapers in which foreclosure notices, probate filings and other official announcements must be published to be legally effective. Shrinking print circulation has prompted debate about moving such notices online.
