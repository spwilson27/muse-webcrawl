Dust mite

House dust mites are microscopic arachnids belonging to the family Pyroglyphidae, principally the species Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus and Dermatophagoides farinae. They live in house dust and feed on flakes of shed human and animal skin.

Biology

Dust mites measure about 0.2–0.3 mm in length and are effectively invisible to the naked eye. They thrive in warm, humid environments, particularly in bedding, upholstered furniture and carpets, where food is plentiful. They do not bite or burrow into skin.

Allergy

Proteins in dust mite faeces and body fragments are among the most common indoor allergens worldwide. Exposure can trigger allergic rhinitis, asthma and eczema in sensitized individuals; the major allergens are known as Der p 1 and Der f 1.

Control

Recommended measures include encasing mattresses and pillows in mite-proof covers, washing bedding at high temperature, reducing indoor humidity below about 50 percent, and regular vacuuming with HEPA filtration.
