Radeon

Radeon is a brand of computer products, including graphics processing units (GPUs), random-access memory, RAM disk software, and solid-state drives, produced by Radeon Technologies Group, a division of AMD. The brand was launched in 2000 by ATI Technologies, which was acquired by AMD in 2006 for US$5.4 billion.

History

The first Radeon product, the Radeon DDR (R100), competed with Nvidia's GeForce 256. The Radeon 9700 Pro (2002) is widely regarded as a landmark GPU that gave ATI a decisive performance lead. Successive architectures included TeraScale, Graphics Core Next (GCN), Polaris, Vega, and RDNA (introduced in 2019 with the RX 5000 series), followed by RDNA 2 in the RX 6000 series — which also powers the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S consoles — RDNA 3 (RX 7000 series, 2022), the first consumer GPU to use a chiplet design, and RDNA 4 (RX 9000 series, 2025).

Products

Radeon GPUs serve consumer (Radeon RX), professional (Radeon Pro), and data centre markets, and AMD's APUs combine Ryzen CPU cores with integrated Radeon graphics. Software support is provided through AMD Software: Adrenalin Edition on Windows and drivers in the Linux kernel.
