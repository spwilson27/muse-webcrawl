MHC class II molecules are a class of major histocompatibility complex proteins found mainly on professional antigen-presenting cells — dendritic cells, macrophages and B cells. They present peptides derived from extracellular proteins to CD4+ T helper cells, linking the innate detection of pathogens to adaptive immune responses.

Structure and loading

Each molecule is a heterodimer of α and β chains, each contributing one half of an open-ended peptide-binding groove, which accommodates peptides of about 13–25 residues whose ends can protrude. In the endoplasmic reticulum the groove is occupied by the invariant chain; after transport to endosomal compartments, the invariant chain is trimmed to the CLIP fragment, and HLA-DM catalyses its exchange for antigenic peptides generated by proteolysis of endocytosed material.

Function and genetics

Recognition of peptide–MHC class II by the T-cell receptor, with CD4 as co-receptor, activates helper T cells that coordinate antibody production and macrophage activation. The human genes (HLA-DP, HLA-DQ, HLA-DR) are among the most polymorphic in the genome, and particular alleles are strongly associated with autoimmune diseases such as type 1 diabetes and coeliac disease.
