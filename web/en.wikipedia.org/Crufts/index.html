Crufts

LocationNational Exhibition Centre, Birmingham, England
Inaugurated1891
OrganiserThe Kennel Club

Crufts is an international dog show held annually in the United Kingdom, organised by The Kennel Club. It is the largest dog show in the world by number of entries, regularly attracting more than 20,000 competing dogs and around 150,000 visitors over four days. It has been held at the National Exhibition Centre in Birmingham since 1991.

History

The show is named after its founder, Charles Cruft, a travelling salesman for a dog food manufacturer, who held the first show under his name in 1891 at the Royal Agricultural Hall in Islington, London. After a wartime interruption the show transferred to The Kennel Club in 1948 and later moved to Earls Court before settling in Birmingham.

Competition

Dogs compete in breed judging across seven groups — Gundog, Hound, Pastoral, Terrier, Toy, Utility, and Working — with group winners advancing to the final for the Best in Show title. The programme also includes agility, obedience, flyball, heelwork to music, and events such as Scruffts for crossbreed dogs and the Friends for Life award.

Criticism

The show has faced criticism over the breeding of pedigree dogs for extreme conformation at the expense of health, notably after the 2008 BBC documentary Pedigree Dogs Exposed, which led the BBC to drop coverage; Channel 4 has broadcast the event since 2010.
