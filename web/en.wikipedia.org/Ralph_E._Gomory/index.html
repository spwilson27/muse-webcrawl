Ralph Edward Gomory (7 May 1929 – December 2024) was an American applied mathematician and executive. He was a long-serving senior vice president for science and technology at IBM and later president of the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, and he is known in mathematics for his work in integer programming.

Mathematical work

Gomory received his PhD from Princeton University in 1954 under Solomon Lefschetz. Shortly afterwards, while at Princeton, he solved a long-standing problem by inventing the cutting-plane method for integer linear programming — the "Gomory cut" — one of the foundational results of the field. With T. C. Hu he also developed the Gomory–Hu tree, a compact representation of minimum cuts in a network.

IBM and the Sloan Foundation

Gomory joined IBM in 1959 and became director of research in 1970, overseeing the company's worldwide research laboratories for nearly two decades. Under his leadership IBM researchers won Nobel Prizes for the scanning tunnelling microscope and high-temperature superconductivity. From 1989 to 2007 he served as president of the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation.

Trade and technology policy

In later years Gomory became a prominent critic of prevailing assumptions about free trade. With William Baumol he co-authored Global Trade and Conflicting National Interests (2000), arguing that economies of scale can make trade outcomes harmful to a nation's overall welfare.
