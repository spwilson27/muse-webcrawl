St Nazaire Raid

The St Nazaire Raid, also known as Operation Chariot, was a British amphibious attack on the docks of Saint-Nazaire in German-occupied France on 28 March 1942. The objective was the Normandie dry dock — the only dock on the Atlantic coast of France large enough to service the German battleship Tirpitz. By destroying it, the British hoped to ensure that Tirpitz could never use French ports for repairs after raiding in the Atlantic.

The attack

The obsolete destroyer HMS Campbeltown, disguised as a German vessel and packed with delayed-action explosives in her bows, was rammed into the dock gates under heavy fire, escorted by motor launches carrying Commandos who landed to demolish pumping stations, winding gear and other dock machinery. The covering force suffered heavily: of 612 men who took part, 169 were killed and 215 captured, and most of the small boats were destroyed. The raiders who could not be evacuated fought until their ammunition ran out.

Results

Late the following morning, Campbeltown's hidden charges exploded, wrecking the dock gates and killing hundreds of Germans — including officers and sightseers touring the ship. The Normandie dock was put out of action for the rest of the war and was not repaired until 1947. Tirpitz never ventured into the Atlantic. Five Victoria Crosses were awarded for the raid, and Saint-Nazaire honoured it with the nickname "the greatest raid of all."
