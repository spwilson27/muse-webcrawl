Volcanic gas

Volcanic gases are gases given off by active (and sometimes dormant) volcanoes. They are dissolved in magma at depth and released as pressure falls during ascent and eruption. The most abundant volcanic gas is water vapour (steam), typically making up more than 60% of emissions, followed by carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide; other significant gases include hydrogen sulfide, hydrogen chloride and hydrogen fluoride.

Role and hazards

Gas pressure drives explosive eruptions, and monitoring changes in gas composition and emission rates is a key tool for forecasting volcanic activity. Volcanic gases can be directly lethal — the 1986 limnic eruption at Lake Nyos in Cameroon released a cloud of carbon dioxide that asphyxiated about 1,700 people — and sulfur emissions converted to sulfate aerosols can cool the global climate, as after the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo. Acid gases also produce acid rain and volcanic smog ("vog").
