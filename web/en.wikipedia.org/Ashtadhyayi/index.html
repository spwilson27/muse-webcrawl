Ashtadhyayi

The Aṣṭādhyāyī (Sanskrit: अष्टाध्यायी, "Eight Chapters") is a celebrated grammar of Sanskrit composed by the ancient Indian grammarian Pāṇini, generally dated to the 6th–4th centuries BCE. Consisting of roughly 3,959 aphoristic rules (sūtras) arranged in eight chapters, it provides a complete and systematic description of the phonology, morphology and syntax of Sanskrit, and is widely regarded as one of the most remarkable intellectual achievements of the ancient world.

Structure and method

The sūtras are written in an extremely compressed style that relies on a technical metalanguage: abbreviations formed from the fourteen Śivasūtras (which order the sounds of Sanskrit), markers (anubandhas) attached to roots and affixes, rules of rule-interaction and inheritance of elements across rules (anuvṛtti). The grammar is generative in character: from a lexicon of verbal roots (the Dhātupāṭha) and nominal stems, its ordered rules derive the correct forms of the language, while excluding incorrect ones. Later commentators, notably Kātyāyana and Patañjali (author of the Mahābhāṣya), discussed and refined Pāṇini's rules.

Significance

The Aṣṭādhyāyī fixed the standard of Classical Sanskrit and preserved the older Vedic language described in some of its rules. In the modern era it has attracted attention from linguists and computer scientists for its formal rigour, which has been compared to that of a programming language or a Turing machine; its system influenced the development of modern linguistics and the study of formal grammars.
