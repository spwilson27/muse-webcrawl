General aviation

General aviation (GA) is defined by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) as all civil aviation aircraft operations with the exception of commercial air transport or aerial work. However, for statistical purposes, ICAO uses a definition of general aviation which includes aerial work. The vast majority of the world's air traffic falls into this category, and most of the world's airports serve general aviation exclusively.

Definition

General aviation covers a diverse range of activities, both commercial and non-commercial, including flying clubs, flight training, agricultural aviation, light aircraft manufacturing, business aviation, personal flying, air ambulance services, and aerial firefighting. The United States has more than 19,000 airports, heliports, seaplane bases, and other landing facilities, the vast majority of which serve general aviation.

Aircraft

The largest numbers of general aviation aircraft are light piston-engine aeroplanes such as the Cessna 172, the most-produced aircraft in history. The sector also encompasses turboprops, business jets, rotorcraft, gliders, balloons, and ultralight aircraft. Experimental and homebuilt aircraft form a significant subsector, supported by organisations such as the Experimental Aircraft Association (EAA).

Regulation and safety

In the United States, general aviation is regulated by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), while in Europe it falls under the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA). Most GA aircraft operate under visual flight rules (VFR) into small airfields without scheduled airline service, providing connectivity to communities that commercial aviation does not reach.
