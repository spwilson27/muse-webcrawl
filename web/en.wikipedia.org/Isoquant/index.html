Isoquant

An isoquant (derived from Greek iso, "equal", and Latin quantitas, "quantity") is a curve through the set of all combinations of production inputs — typically capital and labour — that yield the same level of output. Isoquants are a central tool of microeconomic production theory, analogous to indifference curves in consumer theory.

Properties

Isoquants are usually drawn convex to the origin, reflecting diminishing marginal rates of technical substitution: as more of one input is used, increasingly large amounts of it are needed to replace a unit of the other. A family of isoquants (an isoquant map) represents a production function; isoquants farther from the origin represent higher output. The slope of an isoquant is the marginal rate of technical substitution (MRTS).

Cost minimisation

A firm minimising cost for a given output chooses the input combination where an isoquant is tangent to an isocost line (a line of equal total cost), so that the MRTS equals the ratio of input prices. Special cases include straight-line isoquants for perfect substitutes and L-shaped isoquants for fixed-proportions (Leontief) technology.
