Southern Peninsula (Iceland)

The Southern Peninsula (Icelandic: Suðurnes) is a region in southwestern Iceland and, in the geographical sense, the Reykjanes Peninsula that juts into the North Atlantic west of Reykjavík. The region is one of Iceland's eight statistical regions, with a population of roughly 28,000–30,000, concentrated in the towns of Keflavík, Njarðvík, and Grindavík.

Geography and geology

The peninsula sits astride the Mid-Atlantic Ridge where it comes ashore, and consists of young, porous lava fields feeding geothermal areas such as Svartsengi and Krýsuvík. Keflavík International Airport, Iceland's main international gateway, is located at its western tip, beside the former NATO base at Keflavík. The Blue Lagoon geothermal spa is one of Iceland's best-known attractions.

Volcanic activity

After roughly 800 years of quiet, the peninsula entered a new eruptive period in March 2021 with the Fagradalsfjall eruption, followed by further eruptions near Sundhnúkur from late 2023 that forced the evacuation of Grindavík and repeatedly threatened the Svartsengi power plant and Blue Lagoon. The events mark the reawakening of the Reykjanes volcanic systems.
