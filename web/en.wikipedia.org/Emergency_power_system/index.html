Emergency power system

Emergency power system
PurposeBackup electrical power during utility failure
ComponentsGenerator, UPS, transfer switch, batteries
ApplicationsHospitals, data centers, airports, emergency services

An emergency power system is an independent source of electrical power that supports important electrical systems on loss of normal power supply. A standby power system may include a standby generator, batteries and other apparatus. Emergency power systems are installed to protect life and property from the consequences of loss of primary electric power supply. It is a type of continual power system.

History

Emergency power systems were used as early as World War II on naval ships. In combat, a ship may lose the function of its steam boilers, which power the steam turbines for the ship's generator. In such a case, one or more diesel engines are used to drive back-up generators. Early transfer switches relied on manual operation; two switches would be placed horizontally, in line and the "on" position facing each other. To change from one source to another, the operator must turn off one switch and turn on the other.

Operation

For some classes of emergency power systems, the load must be restored within a specified time. Emergency lighting, emergency power, smoke control and fire alarm systems are required in many public buildings. Diesel engine-driven generators are commonly used because they can reach full power within about ten seconds of a mains failure, whereas battery-based uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) bridge the gap for loads, such as computers, that cannot tolerate even a brief interruption.

Types

Emergency power systems include standby generators fuelled by diesel or gas, UPS systems using lead-acid or lithium-ion batteries, and automatic transfer switches that detect a mains failure and start the backup source. Codes such as NFPA 110 in the United States specify performance requirements, including how quickly the system must accept load and how long it must run.
