Volunteered geographic information
Coined byMichael F. Goodchild (2007)
Major examplesOpenStreetMap, Wikimapia, geotagged photos, citizen science
Related conceptsCrowdsourcing, citizen science, neogeography

Volunteered geographic information (VGI) is the harnessing of tools to create, assemble, and disseminate geographic data provided voluntarily by individuals. The term was coined by geographer Michael F. Goodchild in 2007 to describe the explosion of user-generated spatial content enabled by Web 2.0, GPS-equipped devices, and online mapping platforms — famously characterising citizens as a network of "sensors."

Examples

The flagship example is OpenStreetMap (founded 2004), a free, editable map of the world built by millions of contributors whose local knowledge proved decisive in crises — notably the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team's mapping after the 2010 Haiti earthquake. Other VGI includes Wikimapia, geotagged photographs (Flickr, Panoramio), location reviews, fitness-app traces, and biodiversity observations on platforms such as iNaturalist and eBird.

Issues

Research on VGI examines data quality and accuracy (often surprisingly comparable to authoritative data in well-mapped areas), coverage biases reflecting contributor demographics and wealth, vandalism and edit wars, licensing, and privacy. Goodchild and others frame VGI as part of a broader participatory/citizen-science shift in geography, contrasting with traditionally authoritative, top-down geographic information.
