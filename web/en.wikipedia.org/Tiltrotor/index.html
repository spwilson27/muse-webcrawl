Tiltrotor

A tiltrotor is an aircraft that generates lift and propulsion by means of powered rotors (proprotors) mounted on rotating engine nacelles, usually at the wingtips. The nacelles rotate between vertical (for takeoff and landing like a helicopter) and horizontal (for fast, efficient forward flight like a turboprop aeroplane), giving the aircraft the vertical-lift capability of a helicopter combined with the speed and range of a fixed-wing aircraft.

History

The concept dates to the 1930s, but practical development began with the Bell XV-3 of 1955 and the more successful XV-15 of 1977, which proved the concept for NASA and the US military. This led to the Bell Boeing V-22 Osprey, which first flew in 1989 and entered service in 2007. Other designs include the Leonardo AW609 civil tiltrotor and numerous unmanned designs.

Cross-shafting connects the two proprotors so that either engine can drive both rotors in case of failure. Flight control is complex: in helicopter mode the rotors are controlled like those of a tandem helicopter; in aeroplane mode conventional control surfaces take over; the conversion corridor between the two regimes must be managed carefully. Disadvantages include high cost, mechanical complexity, and high disc loading with strong downwash compared with a helicopter.
