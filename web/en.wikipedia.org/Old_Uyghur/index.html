Old Uyghur is an extinct Turkic language spoken in the Uyghur kingdoms of Qocho (near modern Turfan) and Ganzhou between the 9th and 14th centuries. It developed from Old Turkic after the fall of the Uyghur Khaganate in 840 drove Uyghur groups west into the Tarim Basin oases. It is not the direct ancestor of the modern Uyghur language, which descends from the Chagatai branch — a common source of confusion.

Literature

Old Uyghur possesses one of the richest literatures of the pre-modern Turkic world, preserved thanks to the dry climate of Turfan and Dunhuang. The corpus includes Buddhist sutras translated from Sanskrit, Chinese and Tocharian; Manichaean and Church of the East Christian texts; medical, legal and commercial documents; and original works such as the Buddhist narrative texts Altun Yaruk (Golden Light Sutra). Most manuscripts were excavated by German, Russian, British and Japanese expeditions in the early twentieth century.

Script and features

The cursive Old Uyghur alphabet, adapted from Sogdian writing and written vertically under Mongol influence, was adopted by the Mongols and survives today in the classical Mongolian script. The language is agglutinative with vowel harmony, like other Turkic languages, and its vocabulary shows heavy borrowing from Sanskrit, Sogdian, Tocharian and Chinese reflecting its multilingual Silk Road setting.
