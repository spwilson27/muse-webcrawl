Kora (instrument)

A kora, the 21-string bridge-harp of West Africa.

The kora is a stringed instrument used extensively in West Africa. A kora typically has 21 strings, which are played by plucking with the fingers, and combines features of the lute and a harp.

Description

The kora is a large calabash (gourd) cut in half and covered with cow skin to make a resonator, with a long hardwood neck. Its soundboard has two handholds in the form of spiked handles, and the strings run in two divided ranks, allowing the player to pluck melody and accompaniment simultaneously with both hands. The bridge, called the konso, holds the strings in a notched vertical position.

History

The kora is traditionally associated with the Mandinka people of the Gambia, Senegal, Mali, Guinea-Bissau and Guinea. According to oral tradition, the instrument dates back centuries and was central to the culture of the jali (griot), hereditary musicians and historians of the Mandé world. Famous modern players include Toumani Diabaté of Mali and Sona Jobarteh of the Gambia.

Playing technique

The player holds the instrument upright, supporting it with the middle, ring and little fingers of each hand on the handgrips, while plucking with thumbs and index fingers. Traditional playing distinguishes between the kumbengo (accompaniment) and birimintingo (improvised solo runs).
