The Outsiders (novel)

The Outsiders is a coming-of-age novel by American author S. E. Hinton, first published in 1967 by Viking Press. Hinton began writing it at fifteen and completed it at sixteen, drawing on the real rivalry between two social groups at her Tulsa, Oklahoma high school: the working-class "Greasers" and the affluent "Socs" (short for Socials).

Plot

The story is narrated by fourteen-year-old Ponyboy Curtis, who lives with his brothers Darry and Sodapop after their parents' deaths. When Ponyboy's friend Johnny kills a Soc in self-defence, the two boys flee to an abandoned church, where a fire later forces a reckoning. The novel explores class conflict, loyalty, violence, and the loss of innocence, closing on the injunction to "stay gold", drawn from Robert Frost's poem "Nothing Gold Can Stay".

Reception and legacy

The Outsiders is credited with launching the modern young adult genre, presenting teenagers realistically rather than through the sanitised depictions then common. It has sold millions of copies and is a staple of American school syllabi, though it has also frequently been challenged for its violence and themes. Francis Ford Coppola's 1983 film adaptation, starring a young ensemble cast, further cemented its reputation.
