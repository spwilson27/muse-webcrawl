RIM-66 Standard

The RIM-66 Standard MR (medium range) is a medium-range surface-to-air missile (SAM), with a secondary role as an anti-ship missile, originally developed for the United States Navy. It is a member of the Standard missile family. The RIM-66 surface-to-air missile is a solid-fuelled, tail-controlled, fragmentation warhead missile that uses a semi-active radar homing guidance system.

History

Work began in the 1950s and 1960s to replace the Terrier and Tartar missiles with a common design; the Standard entered service in 1967. Successive blocks improved the seeker, fuze, and resistance to electronic countermeasures, and adaptation to the Mk 41 Vertical Launching System permitted wider deployment. The SM-2 variant, integrated with the Aegis combat system, became the principal fleet air-defence missile of the US Navy and many allied navies, with a range in excess of 100 nautical miles in later versions.

Service

The missile has been used in operations including the 1988 engagement in which USS Vincennes, firing SM-2s, shot down Iran Air Flight 655 under contested circumstances, and numerous interceptions of aircraft, anti-ship missiles, and drones in the Red Sea in 2023–24. Later Standard family members include the RIM-67 (extended range), RIM-156, RIM-161 (SM-3, ballistic missile defence), and RIM-174 (SM-6).
