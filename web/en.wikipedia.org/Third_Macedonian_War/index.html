Third Macedonian War

Date171–168 BC
LocationMacedon, Thessaly, Illyria

The Third Macedonian War (171–168 BC) was a war fought between the Roman Republic and King Perseus of Macedon. It ended in a decisive Roman victory at the Battle of Pydna in 168 BC, which brought the Antigonid dynasty and the Macedonian kingdom to an end, and greatly increased Roman hegemony over Greece and the Hellenistic world.

Background

Perseus succeeded his father Philip V in 179 BC and worked to restore Macedonian influence in Greece, renewing alliances, intervening in Thrace and Illyria, and marrying into the Seleucid royal house. Pergamum's king Eumenes II, Rome's ally and Macedon's rival, denounced Perseus to the Roman Senate in 172 BC. After diplomatic exchanges failed, Rome declared war in 171 BC.

Course of the war

The war's first years went badly for Rome: Perseus won a cavalry victory at Callinicus in 171 BC, and successive Roman commanders failed to break into Macedon. In 168 BC the experienced consul Lucius Aemilius Paullus took command. On 22 June 168 BC, at the Battle of Pydna, the Macedonian phalanx initially drove the Romans back, but as the phalanx advanced over broken ground its formation opened up, and Roman maniples infiltrated the gaps, where their short swords proved deadly at close quarters. The Macedonian army was annihilated, losing perhaps 20,000–25,000 killed, while Roman losses were minimal. Perseus fled and was captured; he was paraded in Paullus's triumph in Rome and died in captivity.

Aftermath

Rome abolished the Macedonian monarchy and divided the kingdom into four nominally independent republics (merides) forbidden to intermarry or trade across their borders. The settlement was enforced harshly: 500 leading Achaeans were deported to Italy, and in Epirus Roman forces enslaved some 150,000 people. Macedon was finally annexed as a province after the Fourth Macedonian War in 148 BC.
