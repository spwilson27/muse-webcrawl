Middlemarch

Middlemarch, A Study of Provincial Life is a novel by the English author Mary Ann Evans, who wrote under the pen name George Eliot. Published in eight installments in 1871–72, it is widely considered her masterpiece and one of the greatest novels in English literature.

Plot and characters

Set in the fictional Midlands town of Middlemarch around 1829–1832, in the years before the Reform Act of 1832, the novel weaves together several interlocking stories. The idealistic Dorothea Brooke marries the aging scholar Edward Casaubon, whose pedantic "Key to All Mythologies" project and emotional coldness trap her in a loveless marriage; after his death she eventually finds happiness with his cousin Will Ladislaw. The ambitious young doctor Tertius Lydgate, hoping to reform medicine, is undone by his marriage to the pretty, extravagant Rosamond Vincy and entanglement with the banker Bulstrode, whose respectable façade hides a corrupt past. Other threads follow Fred Vincy, Mary Garth and the Featherstone inheritance.

Themes

The novel explores marriage, ambition, idealism, education, political reform, religion and the position of women, depicting provincial society as a "web" of interdependent lives. Its omniscient narrator combines psychological depth with philosophical reflection in famous passages such as the "pier-glass" parable.

Reception

Middlemarch was admired by contemporaries and has grown steadily in reputation. Virginia Woolf called it "one of the few English novels written for grown-up people." It topped the BBC's 2015 critics' poll of the greatest British novels, and a 1994 BBC television adaptation brought it to a wide modern audience.
