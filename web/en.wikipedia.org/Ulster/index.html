Ulster (Irish: Ulaidh; Ulster Scots: Ulstèr) is one of the four traditional provinces of Ireland, located in the north of the island. It is made up of nine counties: six of these — Antrim, Armagh, Down, Fermanagh, Londonderry and Tyrone — form Northern Ireland, part of the United Kingdom, while the remaining three — Cavan, Donegal and Monaghan — form part of the Republic of Ireland.

History

In early Ireland, Ulster was the territory of the Ulaid, and it features prominently in the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, including the epic Táin Bó Cúailnge. The province long resisted English control, but after the Flight of the Earls in 1607 the Plantation of Ulster brought large numbers of English and Scottish settlers, whose descendants form much of the unionist community. Ulster was partitioned in 1921 when Ireland was divided. The term "Ulster" is frequently used, loosely, as a synonym for Northern Ireland.

Geography

Ulster includes the island's northernmost point, Malin Head in County Donegal, and its largest lake, Lough Neagh. The Mourne Mountains, the Sperrins and the Antrim Coast and Glens are notable landscapes. Belfast, on the River Lagan, is the province's largest city and the capital of Northern Ireland; Derry is the second city of the northwest.

Languages and culture

English is the main language, with Irish spoken in Gaeltacht areas, chiefly in Donegal, and Ulster Scots spoken in parts of Antrim, Down and Donegal. The province has its own traditions in Gaelic games, rugby, music and literature.
