Karl Landsteiner

Born14 June 1868, Baden bei Wien, Austria
Died26 June 1943, New York City, USA
Known forDiscovery of blood groups and the Rh factor
AwardsNobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (1930)

Karl Landsteiner (14 June 1868 – 26 June 1943) was an Austrian-American biologist, physician, and immunologist. He discovered the major blood groups in 1900 and, with Alexander S. Wiener, identified the Rhesus (Rh) factor in 1937, making blood transfusion a routine and safe medical practice. He also developed the modern classification of blood groups and won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1930.

Early life and work in Vienna

Landsteiner studied medicine at the University of Vienna, graduating in 1891, and spent five years in laboratories in Zurich, Würzburg, and Munich. Returning to Vienna, he worked at the Hygienic Institute and the Wilhelmina Hospital. In 1900 he published a footnote observing that blood serum from some individuals agglutinated the red cells of others; by 1901 he had distinguished the groups A, B, and C (later O), and his colleague's work soon added the rare AB group.

Later research

Landsteiner conducted important research on poliomyelitis, demonstrating its viral nature, and on syphilis and immunochemistry, introducing the concept of haptens. After the First World War he moved to the Netherlands and then, in 1922, to the Rockefeller Institute in New York, where he became a US citizen. With Wiener he discovered the Rh factor in 1937, explaining many transfusion reactions and cases of hemolytic disease of the newborn.

Legacy

Landsteiner is regarded as the father of transfusion medicine; his birthday, 14 June, is observed as World Blood Donor Day.
