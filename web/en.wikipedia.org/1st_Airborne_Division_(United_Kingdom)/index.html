1st Airborne Division (United Kingdom)

Active1941–1945
CountryUnited Kingdom
BranchBritish Army
TypeAirborne forces
NicknameRed Devils

The 1st Airborne Division was an airborne infantry division of the British Army during the Second World War. The division was formed in late 1941 during the Second World War, after the British prime minister, Winston Churchill, demanded an airborne force, and was initially under command of Major-General Frederick A. M. Browning.

Formation

The division was one of two airborne divisions raised by the British Army during the war, the other being the 6th Airborne Division. Its first operation was in Norway, followed by actions in North Africa and Italy. The division's most famous battle was at Arnhem in the Netherlands during Operation Market Garden in September 1944.

Operation Market Garden

During Operation Market Garden, the division, commanded by Major-General Roy Urquhart, was dropped at Arnhem to seize a bridge over the Lower Rhine. The division held the northern end of the bridge against heavy German opposition for far longer than planned, but was eventually forced to withdraw across the river, having suffered very heavy casualties.

Disbandment

The division was never fully reformed after Arnhem. It took part in operations in Norway in 1945 to supervise the disarmament of German forces and was disbanded later that year.
