Dany Laferrière (born 1953) is a Haitian-Canadian novelist and journalist who writes in French. Exiled from Haiti under the Duvalier dictatorship after the murder of a colleague, he settled in Montreal in 1976, working factory jobs before achieving success with his provocative debut novel Comment faire l'amour avec un nègre sans se fatiguer ("How to Make Love to a Negro Without Getting Tired", 1985).

Career

Laferrière's work — an interwoven cycle of autobiographical novels about Haiti, exile, and life in Montreal and Miami — includes L'Odeur du café, Le Goût des jeunes filles, and L'Énigme du retour (2009), written after his father's death and winner of the Prix Médicis. In December 2013 he was elected to the Académie française, the first Haitian and the first Canadian to receive that honour, occupying seat 2, once held by Montesquieu.
