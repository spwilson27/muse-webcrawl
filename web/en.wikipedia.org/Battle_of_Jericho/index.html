Battle of Jericho

Battle of Jericho
Location: Jericho, Canaan
Result: Israelite victory (biblical account)
Described in the Book of Joshua

The Battle of Jericho is an episode described in the biblical Book of Joshua, in which the Israelites, led by Joshua, capture the walled city of Jericho — the first city taken in the conquest of Canaan after the Exodus from Egypt and the crossing of the Jordan River.

The biblical account

According to Joshua 6, God instructed Joshua to march the Israelite army, accompanied by priests carrying the Ark of the Covenant and blowing rams' horns (shofars), around the walls of Jericho once a day for six days. On the seventh day they circled the city seven times; at the priests' trumpet blast and the people's shout, the walls collapsed, and the army stormed the city. Everything in Jericho was placed under the herem (ban) and destroyed, except Rahab — who had hidden Joshua's spies — and her household, who were spared. A curse was pronounced on anyone who rebuilt the city.

Historicity and archaeology

Jericho (Tell es-Sultan) is one of the oldest continuously inhabited places on earth, with famous Neolithic and Bronze Age remains, including a massive stone tower. Excavations by John Garstang in the 1930s seemed to confirm collapsed walls, but Kathleen Kenyon's work in the 1950s dated the main destruction of the walled city to around 1550 BC, well before any conventional date for Joshua (13th century BC), and the Late Bronze Age settlement was small and unfortified. Consequently most archaeologists regard the conquest narrative as an etiological or legendary account rather than a historical report, though the subject remains debated.
