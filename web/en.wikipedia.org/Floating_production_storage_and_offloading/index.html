Floating production storage and offloading

FPSO
TypeOffshore production vessel
FunctionProcessing, storage and offloading of oil and gas
First unitShell Castellón (1977), offshore Spain

Floating production storage and offloading (FPSO) is a type of floating vessel used by the offshore oil and gas industry to produce, process, store, and offload hydrocarbons. An FPSO receives fluids from subsea wells, separates them into oil, gas, and water on deck, stores the stabilised crude in its hull tanks, and periodically transfers it to shuttle tankers. Because it needs no pipeline to shore, the FPSO is the production system of choice for deepwater and remote fields.

Design

Most FPSOs are converted oil tankers — often very large crude carriers — fitted with a topside processing plant, though purpose-built hulls are increasingly common. The vessel is held on station by a mooring system; many use an internal or external turret around which the ship can weathervane with wind and current while connected to risers from the seabed. Storage capacities commonly range from several hundred thousand to over two million barrels. Produced gas may be exported by pipeline, reinjected, used as fuel, or flared where regulations permit.

Operation

Crews live aboard in accommodation for typically 80–200 people. Offloading takes place through a stern or bow hose to a shuttle tanker positioned nearby (tandem mooring), usually every few days to weeks depending on production rate and storage. Units are designed to stay on field for 20 years or more without dry-docking.

History and use

The first FPSO, Shell's Castellón, was converted in 1977 for a field offshore Spain. The concept expanded rapidly in the North Sea, West Africa, Brazil's pre-salt fields, Southeast Asia, and Australia. Brazil's Petrobras operates one of the world's largest FPSO fleets. Related vessel types include FSUs (storage only), FSOs (storage and offloading without production), FLNG units for floating liquefied natural gas, and semi-submersible production platforms.

Advantages and risks

FPSOs offer fast development schedules, reusability, and independence from export pipelines. Their challenges include high day rates, motion effects on process equipment, and the safety and environmental risks of storing large volumes of crude at sea — fire, explosion, and oil spill — managed under classification society and flag-state rules.
