Natal astrology

Also called: Genethlialogy, natal chart reading

Based on: Date, time and place of birth

Natal astrology, sometimes called genethlialogy, is a branch of astrology based on the concept of a natal chart (also called a birth chart), a diagram of the positions of the Sun, Moon, planets and other celestial points at the exact moment of an individual's birth, as seen from the place of birth. Practitioners hold that this chart describes the individual's character, temperament, aptitudes and the broad pattern of their life, and that it can be used to interpret personality and anticipate tendencies and life themes.

The natal chart

A natal chart is divided into twelve houses, determined by the ascendant — the zodiac sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth. The positions of the planets in signs and houses, and the angular relationships between them known as aspects (such as conjunctions, squares, trines and oppositions), form the basis of interpretation. The Sun sign, Moon sign and rising sign are commonly regarded as the most important factors in popular astrology.

History

Natal astrology developed in the Hellenistic world, combining Babylonian omen astrology with Greek ideas, and reached a mature form in works such as Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos (2nd century CE). It was practised throughout the medieval Islamic world and Europe, and later formed part of the Western astrological tradition. Other cultures developed parallel systems, including Vedic astrology (Jyotisha) in India and Chinese astrology.

Scientific status

Astrology, including natal astrology, is regarded by the scientific community as a pseudoscience. Controlled studies, such as the double-blind test published by Shawn Carlson in Nature in 1985, have found that astrologers cannot match natal charts to personality profiles better than chance. Critics also point to the Barnum effect — the tendency of people to accept vague, general statements as personally meaningful — as an explanation for astrology's perceived accuracy.
