Oyster farming

Oyster farming is an aquaculture practice in which oysters are bred and raised for their pearls, shells, and meat. Cultivation dates to Roman times—Sergius Orata is credited with early ponds in the first century BC—and was refined in Japan and France in the nineteenth century.

Methods

Oysters begin as larvae that settle ("spat") on cultch such as old shells. Grow-out methods include bottom culture on intertidal beds, rack-and-bag systems, floating rafts and longlines, and cages. Farmers protect stock from predators such as starfish and drills, and manage diseases including MSX and Dermo in North America.

Industry

Major cultivated species include the Pacific oyster (Magallana gigas), the eastern oyster, and the Sydney rock oyster. China is by far the largest producer. Farmed oysters filter large volumes of water, and reef restoration projects use aquaculture techniques for ecological benefit.
