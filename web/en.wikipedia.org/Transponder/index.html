A transponder (portmanteau of transmitter and responder) is a device that, on receiving a radio signal (the interrogation), automatically transmits a response. Transponders are used in aviation, satellite communications, maritime navigation, toll collection and keyless entry systems.

FunctionAutomatic radio response to interrogation

Aviation

Aircraft transponders reply to secondary surveillance radar on 1090 MHz with a four-digit squawk code identifying the flight; Mode C adds pressure altitude, while Mode S and ADS-B (automatic dependent surveillance–broadcast) add aircraft identity, position and velocity, forming the backbone of modern air traffic control. In-flight squawk 7700 indicates a general emergency, 7600 a radio failure and 7500 unlawful interference. Transponder failure or deactivation — as in the disappearance of Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 in 2014 — can dramatically hamper tracking.

Satellites and communications

On a communications satellite, a transponder receives the uplink signal, translates it to a downlink frequency, amplifies it and retransmits it — the basic repeating unit that leases bandwidth to broadcasters. C-band and Ku-band satellites carry dozens of transponders, each typically of 36 or 54 MHz bandwidth.

Other uses

Passive RFID transponders draw power from the reader's signal and are used in livestock tagging, contactless payment and inventory control. Active transponders with their own batteries serve in electronic toll collection (e.g. E-ZPass), marine AIS collision avoidance, and vehicle immobilisers, where the key's chip must authenticate before the engine starts.
