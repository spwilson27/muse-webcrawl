Cross-country skiing is a form of skiing in which skiers rely on their own locomotion to move across snow-covered terrain, rather than using ski lifts or other forms of assistance. It is widely practised as a sport and recreational activity and is one of the most physically demanding endurance sports, engaging the muscles of both the upper and lower body. Cross-country skiing is part of the Nordic skiing family, which also includes ski jumping, Nordic combined, and biathlon.

Techniques

Two principal techniques are used. In the classic technique, skiers move forward using a diagonal stride in parallel grooves cut into the snow, propelling themselves with poles and with kick wax (or a mechanical grip zone) under the middle of the ski. In the freestyle or skate technique, skiers push off the edge of the ski at an angle like ice skaters, on a wide groomed surface. Most elite competitions specify one technique or the other; the skiathlon combines both in a single race.

History

Skiing originated thousands of years ago in Scandinavia and Central Asia as a means of winter transport and hunting. The word "ski" derives from the Old Norse skíð, meaning a split piece of wood. In the nineteenth century, figures such as Sondre Norheim of Telemark, Norway, developed the equipment and techniques that turned skiing into a sport, and Norwegian explorers — most famously Fridtjof Nansen, who crossed Greenland on skis in 1888, and Roald Amundsen on his South Pole expedition — demonstrated its utility. Cross-country skiing has featured at the Winter Olympics since the first Games in Chamonix in 1924.

Competition

Races are held over distances from sprints of about 1.5 km to the 50 km marathon, in interval-start and mass-start formats, along with relays and team sprints. The FIS Cross-Country World Cup, running since the early 1980s, is the premier annual circuit, and events such as Sweden's Vasaloppet (90 km, first held in 1922) and Norway's Birkebeinerrennet draw tens of thousands of recreational participants. Norway has historically dominated international competition; Bjørn Dæhlie, with eight Olympic gold medals, and Marit Bjørgen, the most decorated Winter Olympian, are among the sport's most successful athletes.

Recreational skiing

Beyond racing, cross-country skiing ranges from touring on groomed trail networks at ski centres to backcountry and off-track travel. It is recognised as excellent aerobic exercise with low impact on joints, and in Nordic countries it remains a commonplace winter activity for all ages.
