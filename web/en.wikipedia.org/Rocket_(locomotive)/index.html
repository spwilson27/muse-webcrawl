Stephenson's Rocket

Rocket
DesignerGeorge and Robert Stephenson, with Henry Booth
Built1829, Robert Stephenson and Co., Newcastle
Type0-2-2 steam locomotive

Rocket is an early steam locomotive designed by George and Robert Stephenson for the Rainhill Trials of October 1829, a competition held by the Liverpool and Manchester Railway to choose its motive power. Rocket won convincingly and became the template for steam locomotive design for the next 150 years.

[Image placeholder: Stephenson's Rocket on display at the Science Museum]

Design

Built at the Stephensons' Forth Street works in Newcastle with input from railway treasurer Henry Booth, Rocket combined several innovations: a multi-tube boiler (25 copper fire-tubes) giving a large heating surface; a blast pipe directing exhaust steam up the chimney to draw the fire; cylinders set at an angle driving the wheels directly rather than through gears; and a separate firebox. It weighed only 4¼ tons yet hauled a 13-ton train at up to 24 mph (39 km/h) during the trials.

Rainhill Trials

At Rainhill, Rocket was the only entrant to complete all the required runs without failing, its rivals Novelty and Sans Pareil breaking down; the horse-powered Cycloped was not a serious contender. The Stephensons won the £500 prize and the locomotive contract.

Service and preservation

Rocket hauled the opening train of the Liverpool and Manchester Railway on 15 September 1830 — the day MP William Huskisson was struck and killed by it, becoming the first widely reported railway fatality. After some years of service, heavily rebuilt, it was donated to the Patent Office Museum in 1862 and is now displayed by the Science Museum Group. A working replica, built in 1979, runs at the National Railway Museum in York.
