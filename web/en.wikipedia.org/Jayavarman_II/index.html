Jayavarman II (c. 770 – c. 835) was a Khmer prince and king who is widely regarded as the founder of the Khmer Empire, the state that dominated Southeast Asia for centuries and built the temples of Angkor.

Founding of the empire

According to the famous Sdok Kak Thom inscription of 1052 — the main source for his reign, written two centuries after his death — Jayavarman returned from "Java" (a place whose identity is debated) and in 802 CE, on Phnom Kulen (Mount Mahendra), was proclaimed a chakravartin, a universal monarch, in a ceremony conducted by the Brahman Hiranyadama. The rite declared Kambuja independent of Javanese overlordship and established the devaraja (god-king) cult at the center of Khmer kingship.

Reign

Jayavarman established several capitals, including Indrapura, Hariharalaya (near present-day Roluos), and Mahendraparvata on Phnom Kulen, where recent lidar archaeology has revealed a substantial early city. He unified competing Khmer principalities, laying the political and religious foundations on which successors, culminating in the builders of Angkor Wat, would raise their empire. He died around 835 and received the posthumous name Paramesvara.
