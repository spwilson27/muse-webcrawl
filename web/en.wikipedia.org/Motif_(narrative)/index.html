Motif (narrative)

In narrative, a motif is a recurring element — an image, object, phrase, situation, or idea — that has symbolic significance in a work and contributes toward the development of a theme. Unlike a theme, which is an abstract idea, a motif is concrete and appears repeatedly in a text.

Use in folklore studies

The systematic study of motifs is central to folkloristics. Stith Thompson's Motif-Index of Folk-Literature catalogues thousands of recurring narrative elements — such as magic objects, tests of identity, and forbidden chambers — across world folklore, and the Aarne–Thompson–Uther Index classifies tale types in which such motifs cluster. Vladimir Propp's Morphology of the Folktale took a related approach, analysing the recurrent functions of characters in Russian fairy tales.

Motif and theme

A motif can be a recurring image (such as light and darkness), a repeated phrase, or a structural pattern like the journey of descent and return. Through repetition, motifs accumulate meaning and help an author develop themes without stating them directly. The term is also used in music for a short recurring melodic figure (the leitmotif of Wagnerian opera being the famous example) and in the visual arts for a recurring decorative element.
