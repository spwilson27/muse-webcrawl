Reasonable accommodation

Reasonable accommodations are adjustments that enable a qualified person with a disability to perform a job.

A reasonable accommodation is an adjustment made in a system to accommodate or make fair the same system for an individual based on a proven need. The term originates in United States law and is used principally in two settings: disability rights, under the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 (ADA), and religious freedom, under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. Similar concepts exist in the law of many other countries and in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, which uses the term "reasonable accommodation" expressly.

Disability and employment

Under Title I of the ADA, a covered employer must provide reasonable accommodations to a qualified employee or applicant with a disability unless doing so would cause an undue hardship — significant difficulty or expense. Accommodations may include making facilities accessible, restructuring a job, modifying work schedules, acquiring or adapting equipment, providing readers or interpreters, and modifying training or examinations. The determination is made through an informal "interactive process" between employer and employee, and agencies such as the Job Accommodation Network publish guidance on common solutions, many of which cost little or nothing.

Religion

Under Title VII, employers must reasonably accommodate employees' sincerely held religious beliefs and practices — such as schedule changes for Sabbath observance, dress and grooming rules, and prayer breaks — unless the accommodation imposes an undue hardship. In Groff v. DeJoy (2023), the U.S. Supreme Court clarified that an employer must show that the burden of granting an accommodation would result in substantial increased costs in relation to the conduct of its particular business, rejecting the long-used "de minimis" reading.

Other contexts

Reasonable accommodation duties also arise in housing under the Fair Housing Act, in education for students with disabilities, and in public services. Denial of a legally required accommodation is a form of discrimination in its own right under the CRPD.
