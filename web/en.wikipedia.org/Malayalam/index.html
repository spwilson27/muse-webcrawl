Malayalam is a Dravidian language spoken in the Indian state of Kerala and the union territories of Lakshadweep and Puducherry (Mahé district), principally by the Malayali people. It has about 38 million native speakers and is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India and one of six languages designated a Classical Language of India.

History

Malayalam diverged from Tamil-related dialects of the west coast around the 9th century CE, developing under heavy Sanskrit influence that gave it a large borrowed vocabulary and a script adapted from Grantha. Its literature came of age with poets such as Thunchaththu Ezhuthachan, regarded as the father of modern Malayalam. The first Malayalam newspaper and a rich print culture helped make Kerala India's most literate state.

Features

Malayalam is agglutinative and distinguishes a large inventory of sounds, including several retroflex and dental consonants that make it famously difficult for learners; its script has one of the largest character sets of any Indian writing system, though a simplified orthography was promoted in the 1970s–80s. The word "Malayalam" itself is a palindrome in English transliteration. The language's word order is subject–object–verb, and its dialects vary by region and community, including the Arabi Malayalam tradition written in an adapted Arabic script.
