Dhrupad

Dhrupad is a vocal genre in Hindustani classical music, said to be the oldest still-in-use style of singing in North Indian classical music. The name derives from dhruva ("fixed, immovable") and pada ("verse"). The term denotes both the poetry form of the composition and the style of singing in which it is set.

Structure

A dhrupad performance typically begins with an extended, unmetered alap in which the singer explores the raga using syllables such as "nom tom", accompanied only by the tanpura. This leads into the composed bandish, set to a rhythmic cycle (tala) such as chautal (12 beats) or dhamar (14 beats), with accompaniment on the pakhawaj. The vocalist then improvises rhythmic variations (layakari) over the composition.

History and gharanas

Dhrupad evolved from temple and court music, reaching a high point in the court of Akbar, where the legendary Tansen sang. Major schools (banis) include Dagar, Darbhanga, Bettiah, and Talwandi. The Dagar family, including the brothers Zahiruddin and Faiyazuddin Dagar and later Zia Mohiuddin and Zia Fariduddin Dagar, were central to dhrupad's twentieth-century revival, alongside the Mallik family of Darbhanga.

Instruments

Besides voice, dhrupad is performed on the rudra veena and surbahar, with the pakhawaj providing rhythmic accompaniment.
