Black-body radiation

Black-body radiation
Governing law: Planck's law
Peak wavelength: Wien's displacement law
Total power: Stefan–Boltzmann law

Black-body radiation is the thermal electromagnetic radiation emitted by an idealized opaque, non-reflective body in thermodynamic equilibrium. Its spectrum depends only on the body's temperature and follows Planck's law.

History

Classical physics predicted infinite emission at short wavelengths (the ultraviolet catastrophe). In 1900 Max Planck derived the correct spectrum by assuming energy is quantized, an insight that launched quantum mechanics.

Applications

The cosmic microwave background is an almost perfect black-body spectrum at 2.725 K. Black-body curves are used to estimate the surface temperatures of stars and in infrared thermometry.
