Kuala Lumpur City Centre (Malay: Pusat Bandar Kuala Lumpur), universally known by its initials KLCC, is a multipurpose development area and the central business district of Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. Built on the former site of the Selangor Turf Club racecourse, the 100-acre development is home to the Petronas Twin Towers, once the world's tallest buildings, and is regarded as a city-within-a-city and a symbol of modern Malaysia.

Development

The master plan, drawn up in the 1990s under the direction of the state oil company Petronas, combined offices, a major shopping mall, hotels, a convention centre, a mosque and a large urban park on the old racecourse site, which had been relocated to Sungai Besi. The twin towers, designed by César Pelli and completed in 1998, held the title of world's tallest buildings until 2004 and remain the tallest twin towers in the world.

Components

  • Petronas Twin Towers — 88-storey headquarters towers linked by a skybridge, housing Petronas and other corporations.
  • Suria KLCC — a six-storey luxury shopping mall at the towers' base, opened in 1998.
  • KLCC Park — a 50-acre landscaped park designed by Roberto Burle Marx, with a jogging track, children's pool and the Lake Symphony water fountains.
  • Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre and the adjoining Aquaria KLCC oceanarium.
  • Asy-Syakirin Mosque, hotels including the Mandarin Oriental and Traders Hotel, and luxury residential towers.

Transport

KLCC is served by its own station on the Kelana Jaya LRT line, by an underground pedestrian walkway to Bukit Bintang (the Pavilion KL link), and by elevated air-conditioned walkways connecting to the wider city-centre district.
