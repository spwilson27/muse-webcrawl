Longbridge plant

The Longbridge plant is a vehicle manufacturing facility in Longbridge, in the south-west of Birmingham, England, on the border with Worcestershire. For most of the 20th century it was one of Britain's largest car factories, home at various times to Austin, British Leyland, Rover Group and MG Rover, and the birthplace of famous models including the Austin Seven and the Mini.

History

The site was developed by Herbert Austin, who founded the Austin Motor Company there in 1905 in a former print works. Longbridge expanded rapidly during both world wars, building aircraft and munitions as well as vehicles, and shadow production continued at nearby Cofton Hackett. In the post-war decades it became the flagship plant of the British Motor Corporation and then British Leyland, building the Mini (launched 1959, designed by Alec Issigonis), the 1100/1300 range, Marina, Metro and other volume models.

The plant was frequently at the centre of Britain's industrial relations struggles in the 1970s. Successive restructurings under British Aerospace and BMW ownership shrank the site; after BMW sold Rover in 2000, MG Rover continued production until its collapse in April 2005, with the loss of some 6,000 jobs at Longbridge and many more in the supply chain.

MG era and redevelopment

MG's new owner, SAIC, retained part of the site, assembling MG models including the MG6 and MG3 from kits, before ending production in 2016 and keeping design and engineering functions there. Much of the former factory area has since been redeveloped for housing, retail, offices and a technology park, with a new Longbridge town centre.
