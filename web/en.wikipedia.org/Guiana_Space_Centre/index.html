The Guiana Space Centre (French: Centre spatial guyanais, CSG), also called Europe's Spaceport, is a European spaceport near Kourou in French Guiana, a French overseas department. Operational since 1968, it is particularly suitable as a location for a spaceport because of its equatorial location and open sea to the east and north.

Location advantages

The centre lies at latitude 5°10′ north, only about 500 km from the equator. Earth's rotation gives rockets launched eastward from near the equator a significant boost, and geostationary transfer orbits require minimal plane change. Launch trajectories pass over the Atlantic Ocean, so spent stages fall harmlessly into the sea. The region is sparsely populated and largely free of tropical cyclones and earthquakes.

History

France selected Kourou in 1964 after leaving its Algerian test ranges. The first launch, a Véronique sounding rocket, took place in 1968. The European Space Agency (ESA) began using the site in 1975 and funded much of its development; the first Ariane 1 launch occurred on 24 December 1979. Since then Ariane 4, Ariane 5 and the Vega rocket have flown from the site, joined by Soyuz launches between 2011 and 2022 and the Ariane 6, which made its maiden flight in July 2024.

Facilities

The spaceport covers about 700 km² and includes launch complexes for Ariane 6 (ELA-4) and Vega (ELV), payload preparation buildings, and a tracking station network. Arianespace conducts commercial operations from the site; a substantial share of the world's commercial communication satellites have been launched from Kourou.
