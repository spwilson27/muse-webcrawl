Loess Plateau

The Loess Plateau is a plateau in north-central China covering some 640,000 km², blanketed by deep deposits of wind-blown silt called loess. It is drained by the middle reaches of the Yellow River.

Formation

Over millions of years, winds carried dust from the Gobi Desert and other arid regions, depositing loess tens to hundreds of metres thick. The easily eroded soil gives the Yellow River its name and heavy sediment load.

History

The fertile plateau was a cradle of early Chinese civilisation; cave dwellings (yaodong) are still carved into its soft cliffs. Millennia of farming and deforestation caused severe erosion and gully formation.

Restoration

Since the 1990s, large projects — terracing, check dams, grazing bans and re-vegetation, including the Grain for Green programme — have greatly reduced erosion and sediment reaching the Yellow River.
