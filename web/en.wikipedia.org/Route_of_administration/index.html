Route of administration

A route of administration is the path by which a drug, fluid, poison, or other substance is taken into the body. Routes are classified by the location at which the substance is applied and determine the speed and extent of absorption.

Enteral routes

Enteral administration delivers substances via the digestive tract: oral (swallowed, the most common route), sublingual and buccal (absorbed through the mouth, bypassing first-pass metabolism), and rectal.

Parenteral routes

Parenteral routes bypass the gut, usually by injection: intravenous (fastest onset), intramuscular, subcutaneous, and intradermal. Other routes include inhalation, topical and transdermal application to the skin, intranasal, ophthalmic, and intrathecal injection into the spinal canal.

Choice of route

The route chosen depends on the drug's properties, the desired speed of action, the site of action, and patient factors. Oral drugs may be degraded by stomach acid or liver first-pass metabolism, while intravenous delivery gives complete bioavailability.
