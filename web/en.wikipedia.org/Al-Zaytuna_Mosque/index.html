Al-Zaytuna Mosque

Location: Medina of Tunis, Tunisia

Founded: c. 698 CE; rebuilt 9th century

Style: Aghlabid and later Maghrebi architecture

University: Ez-Zitouna University

Al-Zaytuna Mosque (Arabic: جامع الزيتونة, "Mosque of the Olive Tree"), also known as the Ez-Zitouna Mosque, is the main mosque of Tunis and one of the oldest and most important mosques in the Islamic world. Located in the Medina of Tunis, it is traditionally said to have been founded around 698 CE, on a site where an olive tree stood, and was substantially rebuilt by the Aghlabids in the 9th century.

Architecture

The mosque covers about 5,000 square metres and has a large courtyard enclosed by galleries, a hypostyle prayer hall with columns reused from ancient Carthage, and a square minaret in the Almohad style, completed in 1894 and rising 43 metres. Its library and teaching spaces developed over centuries.

University

For over a millennium, al-Zaytuna functioned as a great centre of Islamic learning — often described as one of the oldest universities in the world — attracting scholars from across North Africa and the Islamic world. Teachers at Zaytuna included the historian and philosopher Ibn Khaldun, who studied there. In the modern era its teaching was reorganized as Ez-Zitouna University under the Tunisian state, and the mosque's educational role was curtailed during the Bourguiba era before a partial revival after 2011.
