Klein–Nishina formula

The Klein–Nishina formula, derived by Oskar Klein and Yoshio Nishina in 1928, gives the differential cross section for photons scattered by a free electron — Compton scattering — calculated from quantum electrodynamics using the Dirac equation.

Content

Unlike the classical Thomson scattering formula, the Klein–Nishina result predicts that the scattering cross section decreases as photon energy increases, and that high-energy photons are scattered preferentially in the forward direction. It reduces to the Thomson formula at low energies.

Significance

The formula was one of the earliest successful applications of relativistic quantum mechanics to a real process, and it remains standard in X-ray and gamma-ray physics, radiation transport and astrophysics.
