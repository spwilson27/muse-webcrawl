Telecine

Telecine is the process of transferring motion picture film into video and is performed in a laboratory by a film colorist using a telecine machine. Telecine also refers to the equipment used in the process. The term originated from a combination of "television" and "cinema".

The pulldown process

Film is normally shot and projected at 24 frames per second. Television standards historically ran at different rates — 29.97 frames per second for NTSC and 25 for PAL — so converting film to video requires adapting the frame rate. For NTSC, the 3:2 pulldown spreads four film frames across five video frames by alternating fields shown twice and three times. PAL conversions typically speed the film up slightly to 25 frames per second, shortening the running time by about 4 percent and raising audio pitch unless it is corrected.

Uses

Telecine transfer was essential for broadcasting films on television and for home video releases. In modern post-production, film is often scanned at high resolution into data files (a process sometimes called datacine) for digital intermediate workflows, but the telecine stage remains the point at which colour timing decisions shape the look of the final video master. The term is also used informally for pirated copies of films made by recording a projection with a video camera, though those are more properly called "telesync" copies.
