Semantics (computer science)


Formal semantics assigns mathematical meanings to programs.

In programming language theory, semantics is the rigorous mathematical study of the meaning of programming languages. It does so by evaluating the meaning of syntactically valid strings defined by a specific programming language, showing the computation involved. Whereas syntax concerns the form of programs, semantics concerns their behaviour and meaning; formal semantics is used in compiler design, program verification, and the comparison of programming languages.

Overview

The field of formal semantics came into its own in the 1960s and 1970s, with the work of researchers including Christopher Strachey, Dana Scott, John McCarthy, and Robert Floyd. Broadly, three principal approaches are distinguished:

  • Operational semantics describes the meaning of a program by the execution steps it performs on an abstract machine, often given as transitions between configurations of the machine.
  • Denotational semantics assigns to each phrase of a language a mathematical object (a denotation), typically a function between domains of values, so that the meaning of a compound phrase is composed from the meanings of its parts.
  • Axiomatic semantics defines the meaning of program fragments by the logical assertions (preconditions and postconditions) that hold of them, as in Hoare logic.

Applications

Formal semantics supports the proof of program correctness, the specification of language standards, and reasoning about program equivalence and optimization. Semantics also underpins static analysis, type systems, and abstract interpretation, and it provides the mathematical basis for comparing the expressiveness of different language constructs.

History

Early work by McCarthy laid the foundations of a mathematical theory of computation. Floyd's 1967 method of assigning meanings to flowcharts and Hoare's 1969 axiomatic basis for programming languages led to axiomatic semantics, while Scott and Strachey developed denotational semantics using domain theory in the early 1970s. Gordon Plotkin's structural operational semantics (1981) popularized rule-based operational descriptions.
