2 Tone (music genre)

2 Tone (also written Two Tone) is a music genre that fused Jamaican ska and reggae rhythms with the energy and attitude of punk rock and new wave. It emerged in Coventry, England, in the late 1970s and took its name from 2 Tone Records, the record label founded in 1979 by Jerry Dammers of The Specials.

History

The genre grew out of the West Midlands, where young musicians of both British and Jamaican heritage had grown up listening to 1960s ska, rocksteady, and reggae alongside punk. Bands such as The Specials, Madness, The Selecter, The Beat (known as The English Beat in North America), and Bad Manners combined upbeat offbeat guitar rhythms, prominent horn sections, and organ with socially conscious lyrics addressing unemployment, racial tension, and the threat of nuclear war.

2 Tone Records released a string of hit singles in 1979 and 1980, including "Gangsters" by The Specials, "The Prince" by Madness, and "On My Radio" by The Selecter. The label's black-and-white chequerboard artwork and its mascot, the rude boy figure "Walt Jabsco", symbolised the movement's commitment to racial unity at a time of rising far-right activity in Britain.

Influence

Although the original 2 Tone boom faded by the mid-1980s, the genre was hugely influential on the American third wave of ska in the 1990s, inspiring bands such as The Mighty Mighty Bosstones, No Doubt, and Reel Big Fish. The Specials' debut album, produced by Elvis Costello, is frequently cited among the finest British albums of its era.
