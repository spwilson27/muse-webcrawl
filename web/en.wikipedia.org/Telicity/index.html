Telicity

In linguistics, telicity (from Greek télos, "end, goal") is the property of a verb or verb phrase that presents an action or event as having a specific endpoint. A telic verb phrase describes an action that is completed once its goal is reached, as in "build a house"; an atelic phrase describes an action with no inherent endpoint, as in "walk in the park".

Tests for telicity

Common diagnostics include compatibility with "in an hour" (telic) versus "for an hour" (atelic), the imperfective paradox — "was building a house" does not entail "built a house", whereas "was walking" entails "walked" — and whether an event can be described as finished or stopped.

Relation to aspect

Telicity is part of lexical aspect (Aktionsart), alongside distinctions such as state, activity, accomplishment and achievement in Zeno Vendler's classification. Telicity often depends not only on the verb but on its arguments: "drink beer" is atelic, while "drink a beer" is telic, because the quantized object supplies an endpoint.
