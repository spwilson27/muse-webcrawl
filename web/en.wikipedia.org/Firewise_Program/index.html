Firewise Program

The Firewise USA program (the Firewise Program) is a cooperative initiative, administered by the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) and co-sponsored by the USDA Forest Service and the National Association of State Foresters, that encourages local solutions for safety by involving homeowners in shared responsibility for preparing their homes and neighbourhoods to adapt to wildfire.

Approach

Firewise teaches people living in the wildland–urban interface how to reduce their wildfire risk: creating defensible space by clearing flammable vegetation around structures, using fire-resistant landscaping and building materials, and maintaining access for fire appliances. Communities that complete a wildfire risk assessment, form an action plan and invest a set amount of resident effort per dwelling can gain recognition as Firewise USA sites, renewed annually.

Growth

The program began in the early 2000s and has grown to well over a thousand recognised communities across the United States. Research after destructive fires has repeatedly shown that homes prepared to Firewise standards — particularly with cleared zones immediately around the structure and ember-resistant vents — survive at substantially higher rates.
