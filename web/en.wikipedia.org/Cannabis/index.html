Cannabis

Cannabis is a genus of flowering plants in the family Cannabaceae, native to Central and South Asia. It has long been cultivated for its fibre (hemp), seeds and seed oil, and as a source of psychoactive and medicinal compounds. The plant's chemical constituents include over one hundred cannabinoids, most notably tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and cannabidiol (CBD).

Taxonomy and description

The genus is commonly divided into Cannabis sativa, Cannabis indica, and Cannabis ruderalis, though some botanists treat all as a single species, C. sativa. Cannabis plants are annual, dioecious herbs with serrated palmate leaves and hollow stems. Female plants produce the resinous flower clusters ("buds") richest in cannabinoids.

History

Cannabis is among humanity's oldest cultivated crops. Archaeological evidence from Asia documents its use for fibre, food, ritual, and medicine for thousands of years; the ancient Chinese pharmacopoeia and the writings of Herodotus both mention it. Hemp was a strategic material for rope and sails in the age of exploration, while psychoactive use spread through Asia, Africa, and the Americas.

Uses

Industrial hemp, bred for low THC content, is used for textiles, paper, bioplastics, building materials, and food. Recreational cannabis is consumed for its euphoric and relaxing effects, typically by smoking, vaporizing, or in edibles. Medical cannabis is prescribed or recommended for conditions including chronic pain, chemotherapy-induced nausea, and certain seizure disorders, though evidence varies by condition.

Effects and risks

THC acts on the endocannabinoid system, altering mood, perception, appetite, and short-term memory. Adverse effects can include anxiety, impaired driving, dependence in a minority of users, and an association with psychosis in susceptible individuals, particularly with high-potency products and adolescent use.

Legal status

Cannabis was prohibited in most countries during the twentieth century and remains controlled under international treaties, but since the 2010s a growing number of jurisdictions — including Canada, Uruguay, Malta, Germany, Thailand (briefly), and many US states — have legalized recreational use, while medical programs are more widespread.
