The Mercury Prize, formerly the Mercury Music Prize, is an annual music prize awarded for the best album released in the United Kingdom by a British or Irish act. It was created by Jon Webster and Robert Chandler in association with the British Phonographic Industry and British Association of Record Dealers in 1992 as an alternative to the Brit Awards. The prize is open to all musical genres, and albums are chosen by an independent panel of musicians, presenters, and journalists purely on musical merit, regardless of sales.

Format and reputation

A shortlist of twelve albums is announced each summer, with the winner revealed at an awards ceremony in the autumn. Winning — and even being shortlisted — typically produces a large boost in album sales, the so-called "Mercury effect". The prize has a reputation for rewarding adventurous or unexpected choices and for occasional controversy when favourites lose to outsiders. PJ Harvey is the only artist to have won twice (2001 and 2011).
