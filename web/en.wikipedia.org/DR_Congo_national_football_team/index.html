DR Congo national football team

DR Congo
Nickname: The Leopards (Les Léopards)
Association: FECOFA
Confederation: CAF (Africa)
Home stadium: Stade des Martyrs, Kinshasa

The DR Congo national football team, nicknamed the Leopards, represents the Democratic Republic of the Congo in men's international football and is controlled by the Congolese Association Football Federation (FECOFA). The team is a member of FIFA and the Confederation of African Football (CAF).

History

The team has played under several names reflecting the country's history, including Congo-Kinshasa and Zaire. As Zaire, the team became the first Sub-Saharan African nation to qualify for the FIFA World Cup, appearing at the 1974 tournament in West Germany. That campaign is remembered for the famous incident in which defender Mwepu Ilunga rushed out of the defensive wall and kicked away a Brazilian free kick before it was taken.

Honours

DR Congo has won the Africa Cup of Nations twice: in 1968, as Congo-Kinshasa, and in 1974, as Zaire, defeating Zambia in a replayed final. The country is also one of the most successful nations in the African Nations Championship (CHAN), the tournament for home-based players, winning the inaugural edition in 2009 and again in 2016.

Stadium

The team's home ground is the Stade des Martyrs in Kinshasa, one of the largest stadiums in Africa.
