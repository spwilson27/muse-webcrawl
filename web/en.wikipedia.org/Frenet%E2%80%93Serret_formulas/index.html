Frenet–Serret formulas

Frenet–Serret formulas
FieldDifferential geometry
Named afterJean Frédéric Frenet, Joseph Alfred Serret
Introduced1847 (Frenet), 1851 (Serret)

In differential geometry, the Frenet–Serret formulas describe the kinematic properties of a particle moving along a continuous, differentiable curve in three-dimensional Euclidean space, or the geometric properties of the curve itself.

The frame

At each point of a curve one defines an orthonormal frame of three vectors: the unit tangent T, the unit normal N, and the binormal B = T × N. The formulas express the derivatives of these vectors with respect to arc length in terms of the curve's curvature κ and torsion τ.

The formulas

dT/ds = κN; dN/ds = −κT + τB; dB/ds = −τN. Curvature measures how sharply the curve bends; torsion measures how it twists out of its osculating plane.

Applications

The formulas are fundamental in the theory of space curves and appear in physics, robotics, and computer graphics wherever the local geometry of a trajectory matters.
