Documenta

Documenta (stylised as documenta) is an exhibition of contemporary art which takes place every five years in Kassel, Germany. It was founded in 1955 by artist, teacher and curator Arnold Bode as part of the Bundesgartenschau (Federal Horticultural Show) held in Kassel that year. The first documenta was a retrospective of the major movements and artists of early 20th-century art — art that the Nazi regime had suppressed as "degenerate" — and it became an unexpected success, establishing the exhibition as one of the most important events in the international art world.

History

Bode organized the first exhibition in the restored Fridericianum, one of continental Europe's oldest museums, which remains documenta's central venue. Successive editions were directed by figures including Harald Szeemann (documenta 5, 1972), which transformed the format into a themed "hundred-day event"; Jan Hoet (documenta IX, 1992); Catherine David (documenta X, 1997), the first woman and non-German artistic director; Okwui Enwezor (Documenta11, 2002), which extended the exhibition to platforms on several continents; Carolyn Christov-Bakargiev (dOCUMENTA (13), 2012); and Adam Szymczyk (documenta 14, 2017), held jointly in Kassel and Athens. Documenta fifteen (2022) was curated by the Indonesian collective ruangrupa under the concept of lumbung (communal rice barn).

Venues and format

Each edition lasts 100 days and spreads across museums, cinemas, warehouses, outdoor sites and public spaces throughout Kassel. Visitor numbers for recent editions have approached or exceeded 900,000. Between exhibitions, the documenta archiv preserves the records of each edition.

Significance

Alongside the Venice Biennale, documenta is regarded as one of the most prestigious recurring exhibitions of contemporary art, notable for curatorial ambition and for defining debates within the art world — much as movements such as Divisionism once redefined painting.
