Gorilla Glass

Gorilla Glass is a brand of chemically strengthened glass developed and manufactured by Corning Inc. It is designed to be thin, light, and damage-resistant, and is used primarily as cover glass for portable electronic devices such as smartphones, tablet computers, laptops, and televisions.

Manufacturer: Corning Inc.

Type: Alkali-aluminosilicate sheet glass

Introduced: 2007

Primary uses: Smartphone and tablet cover glass

History

Corning's research into toughened glass dates to the 1960s with its Chemcor project. The material was revived in the mid-2000s when Apple CEO Steve Jobs sought a durable glass screen for the first iPhone; Corning adapted its earlier work and delivered the first Gorilla Glass in 2007. Successive generations followed: Gorilla Glass 2 (2012) was thinner, Gorilla Glass 3 introduced "native damage resistance", and later versions (4, 5, 6) emphasized survival of drops from greater heights. Gorilla Glass Victus, released in 2020, claimed improved drop and scratch resistance together, and has been used in flagship Samsung and other Android phones. By the early 2020s Corning said the glass had been used on more than eight billion devices.

Manufacture

The glass is an alkali-aluminosilicate formed by Corning's fusion draw process, which produces pristine thin sheets. It is then chemically strengthened by ion exchange: the glass is immersed in a molten potassium salt bath at about 400 °C, so smaller sodium ions leave the glass and are replaced by larger potassium ions, creating a layer of compressive stress at the surface that resists scratching and cracking.

Related products

Corning also makes Willow Glass, a flexible glass, and Lotus Glass for displays. Competitors include Asahi's Dragontrail glass and Schott's Xensation Cover. Sapphire and plastic covers are alternatives for some applications.
