Voice acting in Japan (seiyū)

A seiyū (Japanese: 声優, "voice actor") is a Japanese voice actor. In Japan, voice actors work in radio, television, film, video games, and narration, and are especially prominent as the voices of characters in anime. Because the anime industry is so large, Japanese voice acting has developed into a distinct profession with its own training schools, talent agencies, awards, and celebrity culture, in contrast to many countries where voice acting is a smaller branch of acting.

History

Voice performance in Japan grew out of radio drama troupes organized by the broadcaster NHK in the 1920s and 1930s. The term seiyū was coined by a journalist in the 1940s, originally for actors specializing in radio and dubbing. With the rise of television anime from the 1960s — including the work of pioneers such as Yasuo Yamada — and the dubbing of foreign films and series, the profession expanded rapidly. By the 1970s, anime magazine coverage had turned leading voice actors into public personalities with fan followings.

Industry

Aspiring seiyū typically attend specialized academies and sign with talent agencies; pay scales for anime work have traditionally been based on a ranking system tied to years of experience rather than starring role. Successful voice actors diversify into singing, live events, radio hosting, and "idol" activities, and popular series hold concerts and fan events. The annual Seiyu Awards, established in 2007, honour outstanding performances.

Notable figures

Renowned seiyū include Megumi Hayashibara, Masako Nozawa (the voice of Goku in Dragon Ball), Mamoru Miyano, and Kana Hanazawa. The profession's visibility has made "seiyū" a recognized career aspiration in Japan, though the industry is also noted for intense competition and modest starting pay.
