Tokyo Metropolis (東京都, Tōkyō-to) is one of Japan's 47 prefectures and its most populous, consisting of the 23 special wards of central Tokyo together with 26 cities, towns and villages in the Tama region to the west and the Izu and Ogasawara island chains stretching far into the Pacific. The metropolis is governed by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, headed by an elected governor and the Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly. "Tokyo Metropolis" is distinct from the broader Greater Tokyo Area, the world's most populous metropolitan region, which spills into the neighbouring prefectures of Kanagawa, Chiba and Saitama.

History

Tokyo Prefecture was created in 1868 from the former Edo, seat of the Tokugawa shogunate, which the Meiji government renamed Tokyo ("eastern capital"). In 1889 the City of Tokyo was established within the prefecture, with fifteen wards. In 1943, during the Second World War, the city and prefecture were merged into a single metropolitan entity, Tōkyō-to, abolishing the city government in order to strengthen wartime control; the city itself was never re-established. The special wards gained increased self-government under the post-war Local Autonomy Law.

Geography and subdivisions

The mainland portion of the metropolis stretches about 90 km east to west, from the Okutama mountains to Tokyo Bay. The 23 special wards (特別区) form the urban core and former City of Tokyo; each ward elects its own mayor and assembly and functions much like a city. The Tama area contains commuter cities such as Hachioji, Tachikawa and Musashino. The island groups include the Izu Islands and the remote Ogasawara Islands, some 1,000 km south, which include Chichijima and Iwo Jima (Iōtō).

Government and economy

The Metropolitan Government, headquartered in the twin-towered Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building in Nishi-Shinjuku (designed by Kenzo Tange), administers police (the Metropolitan Police Department), fire services, waterworks, public transport including the Toei subway, and markets for the whole metropolis. Tokyo's metropolitan economy is among the largest of any subnational jurisdiction in the world; the metropolis hosts the headquarters of most of Japan's largest corporations, the Tokyo Stock Exchange, and national government institutions, since Tokyo is also the capital of Japan.
