Cullinan Diamond

The Cullinan Diamond is the largest gem-quality rough diamond ever found, weighing 3,106 carats (621.20 g). It was discovered at the Premier No. 2 mine in Cullinan, South Africa, on 26 January 1905 and named after Thomas Cullinan, the chairman of the mining company. The Transvaal Colony government purchased the stone and presented it to King Edward VII of the United Kingdom.

Cutting

The diamond was studied for months before being cut by Joseph Asscher in Amsterdam in 1908. It was cleaved into nine principal stones (Cullinan I–IX) and 96 smaller brilliants. The task of the first blow was so stressful that Asscher is said to have fainted after it succeeded.

The principal stones

Cullinan I, the "Great Star of Africa", is a 530.2-carat pendant diamond set in the Sovereign's Sceptre with Cross; it was the largest clear-cut diamond in the world until the Golden Jubilee Diamond surpassed it. Cullinan II, the "Second Star of Africa" (317.4 carats), is set in the Imperial State Crown. The other numbered stones are in brooches, pendants, and rings held by the British royal family as part of the Crown Jewels.
