Sonora is a state in northwestern Mexico, bordered by the U.S. states of Arizona and New Mexico to the north, Chihuahua to the east, Sinaloa to the south, and the Gulf of California (Sea of Cortés) and Baja California to the west. Its capital and largest city is Hermosillo.

Geography

Sonora is Mexico's second-largest state by area. Much of its west lies within the Sonoran Desert, one of the most biodiverse deserts in the world, known for the saguaro cactus. The Sierra Madre Occidental runs along the eastern border, and the Yaqui, Mayo, Sonora, and Colorado rivers drain toward the Gulf of California.

History

The region was home to peoples including the Yaqui, Mayo, Seri, and Tohono O'odham. Spanish missionaries led by the Jesuit Eusebio Francisco Kino established missions in the late 17th century. Sonora became a state in 1830. Four Mexican presidents of the revolutionary era — Álvaro Obregón, Plutarco Elías Calles, Abelardo Rodríguez, and Adolfo de la Huerta — came from Sonora, giving the state outsized influence in post-revolutionary politics.

Economy

Agriculture (wheat, grapes, vegetables) in irrigated river valleys, cattle ranching, copper mining (including the great Cananea mine), fishing and shrimp farming along the coast, and manufacturing in border cities are the principal economic activities. Hermosillo hosts a major Ford assembly plant.
