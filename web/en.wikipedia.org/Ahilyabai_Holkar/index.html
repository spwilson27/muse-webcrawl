Ahilyabai Holkar

Born31 May 1725, Chondi
Died13 August 1795, Maheshwar
Reign1767–1795 (Holkar state, Indore)
DynastyHolkar
SpouseKhanderao Holkar

Ahilyabai Holkar (31 May 1725 – 13 August 1795) was the ruler of the Holkar state of Indore in central India from 1767 until her death. Widowed young and bereaved of her father-in-law Malhar Rao Holkar and her son, she petitioned the Peshwa for permission to rule herself, and governed for nearly three decades with a reputation for justice, piety, and administrative wisdom that made her one of the most celebrated women rulers in Indian history.

Reign

Ruling from Maheshwar on the Narmada River, Ahilyabai maintained peace within her territories while Maratha power elsewhere fragmented. She appointed Tukoji Rao Holkar as her military commander, kept efficient records, held daily public audiences, and was famed for impartial justice and personal austerity. Under her patronage Indore grew prosperous as a trading center and Maheshwar became a center of textile weaving — the origin of the Maheshwari sari.

Building and charity

Ahilyabai spent her personal fortune on public works across India: temples, ghats, wells, rest houses, and roads. Most famously she rebuilt the Kashi Vishwanath Temple in Varanasi (1780) and contributed to temples and ghats from Somnath and Ayodhya to Rameswaram, building far beyond her own domain. She is revered in Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh; Indore's airport is named Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Airport in her honor.
