WrestleMania

WrestleMania is a professional wrestling event held annually between mid-March and early April by WWE, the world's largest professional wrestling promotion. First held in 1985, it is the company's flagship event and one of the most successful and longest-running professional wrestling events in history, often described as the Super Bowl of professional wrestling.

History

The first WrestleMania was held on 31 March 1985 at Madison Square Garden in New York City, headlined by Hulk Hogan and Mr. T against Roddy Piper and Paul Orndorff. Conceived by Vince McMahon as a make-or-break spectacle, its success on closed-circuit television and pay-per-view helped propel the WWF's national expansion. WrestleMania III (1987) at the Pontiac Silverdome drew a claimed 93,173 fans, long cited as an indoor attendance record.

Format and significance

WrestleMania culminates WWE's major storylines and typically features celebrity involvement, elaborate entrances, and the company's biggest matches of the year. Since WrestleMania 36 (2020), held over two nights during the COVID-19 pandemic, the event has been staged across two evenings. The Undertaker's undefeated streak at WrestleMania, which reached 21–0 before ending at WrestleMania XXX in 2014, is one of the event's most famous storylines.

Economic impact

Host cities compete to stage WrestleMania, and WWE has claimed cumulative economic impacts exceeding two billion dollars for host communities.
