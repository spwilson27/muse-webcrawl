Moral anti-realism

Moral anti-realism is the metaethical view that there are no objective moral facts or properties. Anti-realists deny that moral judgements describe a mind-independent moral reality, holding instead that moral claims are false (error theory), express attitudes rather than beliefs (non-cognitivism), or are true only relative to a standpoint or culture (some forms of relativism).

FieldMetaethics
Opposed toMoral realism
FormsError theory, non-cognitivism, subjectivism

Forms

Error theory

J. L. Mackie argued that moral judgements aim to state objective facts but that no such facts exist, so positive moral claims are systematically false. Mackie supported this with the argument from queerness and the argument from relativity.

Non-cognitivism

Non-cognitivists such as A. J. Ayer, Charles Stevenson and Simon Blackburn hold that moral utterances primarily express emotions, prescriptions or plans rather than describing the world, so they are not straightforwardly truth-apt.

Arguments

Anti-realists commonly appeal to persistent moral disagreement, the explanatory redundancy of moral facts, and evolutionary debunking arguments suggesting our moral beliefs are shaped by selection pressures rather than objective truths.
