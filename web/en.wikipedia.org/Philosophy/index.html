Philosophy

Philosophy (from Greek philosophia, "love of wisdom") is the systematic study of general and fundamental questions concerning topics such as existence, reason, knowledge, values, mind, and language. It is a critical, largely non-empirical discipline distinguished from other ways of addressing fundamental questions by its questioning approach and its reliance on rational argument.

Branches

Philosophy's principal branches include metaphysics, which studies the fundamental nature of reality; epistemology, the study of knowledge; ethics, concerning moral principles; logic, the study of valid reasoning and argument; and aesthetics, concerning art and beauty. Major subfields include philosophy of language, philosophy of science, and political philosophy.

History

Historically, philosophy encompassed all bodies of knowledge, and a practitioner was known as a philosopher. From the time of ancient Greek philosophy to the nineteenth century, "natural philosophy" was the name for the branch that became the physical sciences. Philosophy also has long traditions in India, China, and the Islamic world, among other cultures.

Methods

Philosophical methods include questioning, critical discussion, rational argument, and systematic presentation. Classic philosophical questions include whether it is possible to know anything and to prove it, what is most real, and whether there is a best way to live.
