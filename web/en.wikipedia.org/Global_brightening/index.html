Global brightening

Global brightening is the observed increase in the amount of solar radiation reaching the Earth's surface that has occurred since roughly the late 1980s and 1990s. It is the counterpart to the earlier period of global dimming, in which surface sunlight declined from the 1950s to the 1980s.

Causes

The brightening is attributed largely to reductions in aerosol pollution following clean-air legislation in Europe and North America: fewer sulfate and soot particles mean less sunlight is scattered and absorbed in the atmosphere, and clouds become less reflective. Changes in cloud cover also contribute, and brightening has been observed most clearly over industrialised land regions.

Effects

Measured increases are on the order of a few watts per square metre per decade in affected regions. Brightening may have unmasked some greenhouse-gas warming previously offset by dimming, influenced evaporation and the hydrological cycle, and affected solar energy generation and agriculture. It is tracked by surface radiation networks such as the Baseline Surface Radiation Network and by satellite instruments.
