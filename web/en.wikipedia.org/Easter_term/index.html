Easter term

Easter term is the second of four terms in the legal year in England and Wales (the others being Michaelmas, Hilary, and Trinity terms), during which the superior courts traditionally sat. In the modern court calendar, sittings run from after the Easter break until late May.

Legal year

The division of the legal year into terms derives from the medieval period, when the royal courts sat around the feasts of the Church. Easter term historically began after the Easter vacation and ran to the week of Ascension or Pentecost, with dates varying because Easter is a moveable feast. Under the modern Civil Procedure Rules timetable, Easter sittings begin on the second Tuesday after Easter and end on the Friday before the spring bank holiday.

Other uses

The name is also used for the spring academic term in some schools and universities in the United Kingdom, though universities such as Cambridge instead divide the year into Michaelmas, Lent, and Easter terms, with Easter term being the third term, running from April to June and encompassing the main examination period.
