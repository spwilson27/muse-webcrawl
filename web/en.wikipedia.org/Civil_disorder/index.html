Civil disorder
Also called: Civil unrest, civil strife
Includes: Riots, protests, strikes, demonstrations
Response: Police, crowd control, emergency powers

Civil disorder, also known as civil unrest or civil strife, is a broad term used by law enforcement to describe unrest caused by a group of people, ranging from lawful mass demonstrations and strikes to riots and violent confrontation with authorities. Civil disorder typically involves the disruption of public order and may include property damage, looting, arson, and clashes between crowds and police.

Causes

Causes of civil disorder include political grievances, economic hardship, unemployment, racial or ethnic tensions, disputed elections, unpopular government policies, and incidents of police misconduct. Sociologists have studied collective behaviour and crowd dynamics to understand how gatherings escalate; modern research emphasizes that crowd violence is usually shaped by intergroup dynamics, including interactions between crowds and police, rather than by irrational "mob" psychology alone.

Notable examples

Historically significant episodes include the Gordon Riots of 1780 in London, the urban riots in the United States in the 1960s, the Los Angeles riots of 1992, the 2005 French riots, the 2011 England riots, and mass protest movements such as those of the Arab Spring in 2011.

Response

Authorities respond with a graduated range of measures, from negotiation and facilitation of lawful protest, to riot police, curfews, states of emergency, and in extreme cases military deployment. Public order law in many countries criminalizes riot while protecting rights of peaceful assembly.
