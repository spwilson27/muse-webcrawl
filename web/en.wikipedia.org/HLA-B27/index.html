HLA-B27


The HLA-B27 antigen-presenting molecule

Human leukocyte antigen B27 (HLA-B27) is a class I surface antigen encoded by the B locus in the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) on chromosome 6. It presents antigenic peptides to CD8+ T cells. HLA-B27 is strongly associated with a group of inflammatory diseases known as the seronegative spondyloarthropathies, most famously ankylosing spondylitis.

Disease associations

Around 90 percent of people with ankylosing spondylitis carry HLA-B27, compared with roughly 8 percent of the general population of European ancestry, making it one of the strongest known HLA–disease associations. HLA-B27 is also associated with reactive arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease–related arthritis, and acute anterior uveitis. Most HLA-B27-positive individuals never develop these diseases, so the antigen is neither necessary nor sufficient, and its presence alone is a poor screening test.

Conversely, HLA-B27 appears protective against certain viral infections: carriers are over-represented among people who control HIV infection without treatment ("elite controllers") and among those who spontaneously clear hepatitis C virus. This is thought to reflect efficient presentation of viral peptides to cytotoxic T cells.

Mechanism

Several hypotheses seek to explain the disease association: the "arthritogenic peptide" hypothesis, in which B27 presents a self-peptide that mimics a bacterial peptide; the misfolding hypothesis, in which B27 heavy chains fold slowly and trigger endoplasmic-reticulum stress; and free heavy chains or homodimers at the cell surface engaging killer immunoglobulin-like receptors (such as KIR3DL2) on immune cells.

Prevalence

HLA-B27 prevalence varies widely by population: it is common among some Indigenous peoples of the circumpolar north and the Haida of the Pacific Northwest (up to 50 percent), moderate in Northern Europeans, and rare in most African and Japanese populations, in patterns that roughly track the prevalence of ankylosing spondylitis.
