World Chess Championship

The World Chess Championship is played to determine the world champion in chess. The first generally recognised championship match was held in 1886 between Wilhelm Steinitz and Johannes Zukertort; Steinitz won and became the first official world champion.

History

Before 1948 the title was the champion's personal property, contested in privately arranged matches; champions included Emanuel Lasker, José Raúl Capablanca, Alexander Alekhine, and Max Euwe. After Alekhine's death, FIDE took control, organising a tournament in 1948 won by Mikhail Botvinnik and thereafter a cycle of Candidates tournaments and matches. Between 1993 and 2006 the title split between FIDE and the "classical" line founded by Garry Kasparov, before reunification in 2006 under Vladimir Kramnik.

Recent champions

Viswanathan Anand held the title from 2007 to 2013, followed by Magnus Carlsen (2013–2023). After Carlsen declined to defend his title, Ding Liren defeated Ian Nepomniachtchi in 2023; Gukesh Dommaraju became the youngest undisputed champion by beating Ding in 2024.

Format

Modern championships are matches between the champion and the winner of the Candidates Tournament, with classical games followed by rapid and blitz tiebreaks if scores are level.
