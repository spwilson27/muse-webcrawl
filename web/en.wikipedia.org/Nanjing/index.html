Nanjing

Nanjing (Chinese: 南京; pinyin: Nánjīng, "southern capital") is the capital of Jiangsu Province in eastern China and one of the country's great historic cities. A sub-provincial city of over nine million people on the lower Yangtze River, it has served as the capital of China under several dynasties and governments.

History

Founded in antiquity as Jinling, Nanjing became a capital under the Wu kingdom of the Three Kingdoms era and again for a succession of southern dynasties. The Hongwu Emperor made it the first capital of the Ming dynasty in 1368, building its great city wall and the Xiaoling Mausoleum, before the capital moved to Beijing in 1421. It was the Treaty of Nanking (1842) that ended the First Opium War.

Under the Republic of China, Nanjing was again the national capital from 1927. In December 1937, invading Japanese forces perpetrated the Nanjing Massacre. Today the city is a major centre of education, research and culture, home to Nanjing University and numerous other institutions.
