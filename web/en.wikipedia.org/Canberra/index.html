Canberra

Canberra is the capital city of Australia. With a population of over 460,000, it is Australia's largest inland city and the eighth-largest city overall. Canberra is located at the northern end of the Australian Capital Territory, about 280 kilometres southwest of Sydney.

History

The area was inhabited by Indigenous peoples, principally the Ngunnawal, for at least 21,000 years. Following federation in 1901, a site for the national capital was selected in 1908 as a compromise between rivals Sydney and Melbourne. An international design competition was won by American architects Walter Burley Griffin and Marion Mahony Griffin, and construction began in 1913. The provisional Parliament House opened in 1927, and the city grew steadily after the Second World War as government departments transferred from Melbourne.

Geography and layout

Canberra sits on a plain at the foot of the Brindabella Ranges, at an elevation of about 580 metres. The Griffins' plan featured geometric street patterns, garden-city landscaping, and a central artificial lake, Lake Burley Griffin, formed by damming the Molonglo River in 1963. Major landmarks — Parliament House, the Australian War Memorial, the National Gallery, and the National Library — are arranged around the lake's Parliamentary Triangle.

Government and economy

Canberra is the seat of the Government of Australia and home to many national institutions and foreign embassies. The public service is the largest employer, and the city also hosts the Australian National University and the University of Canberra. The territory has had self-government since 1988.

Culture

The city is known for its planned greenery, earning it the nickname "the Bush Capital." Annual events include Floriade, a spring flower festival, and the National Multicultural Festival. Canberra's residents have the highest average income and education levels of any Australian capital.
