The atmosphere of Earth is the layer of gases, known collectively as air, retained by Earth's gravity that surrounds the planet and forms its planetary atmosphere. It protects life by absorbing ultraviolet solar radiation, retaining heat (the greenhouse effect), reducing temperature extremes, and burning up most incoming meteoroids.

Composition

Dry air contains roughly 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.9% argon, and 0.04% carbon dioxide, with variable water vapour (about 0–4%). Carbon dioxide levels have risen above 420 parts per million due to human activity, driving climate change.

Layers

The troposphere (0–12 km) contains most of the mass and nearly all weather. The stratosphere (to ~50 km) contains the ozone layer. Above are the mesosphere (to ~85 km), the thermosphere (to ~600 km), where auroras occur, and the exosphere, which fades into space.

Evolution

Earth's early atmosphere was transformed by volcanic outgassing and later by photosynthetic cyanobacteria, whose oxygenation of the air about 2.4 billion years ago (the Great Oxidation Event) made aerobic life possible.
