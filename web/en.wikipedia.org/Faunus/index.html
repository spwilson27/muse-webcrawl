Faunus

Faunus, the horned god of the forest, plains and fields.

In ancient Roman religion and myth, Faunus was the horned god of the forest, plains and fields; when he made cattle fertile he was known as Inuus. A god of nature and fertility, he was one of the oldest Roman deities, counted among the di indigetes, and was closely associated with the Greek god Pan.

Mythology and cult

Faunus was credited with a prophetic voice heard in sacred groves, particularly at his sanctuary on the Aventine Hill and at Albunea near Tibur; he was said to reveal the future in dreams to those who slept on the fleeces of sacrificed lambs, a practice called incubation. In myth he appears as a legendary king of Latium, son of Picus and grandson of Saturn, who taught agriculture and cattle-raising to his people.

Festivals

His festival, the Faunalia, was celebrated by country people on 5 December with merrymaking in the fields. Faunus was also linked with the Lupercalia festival in February: the Luperci, priests who ran through Rome striking bystanders with goatskin thongs, were associated with Faunus in his aspect as Lupercus, the averter of wolves from the flocks.

Fauna

The god's name survives in the zoological term fauna — the animals of a region — coined from the notion of Faunus and his companion fauns (half-human, half-goat spirits of the untamed woodland) as presiding powers over wild creatures.
