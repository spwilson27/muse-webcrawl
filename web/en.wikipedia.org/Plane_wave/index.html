Plane wave

In physics, a plane wave is a wave whose wavefronts (surfaces of constant phase) are infinite parallel planes. It is a special case of a wave or field that now (at a moment in time) takes a constant value over every plane that is perpendicular to a fixed direction in space; the value at any point in space then depends only on the projection of that point onto the direction vector, and on time.

A monochromatic plane wave can be written using a complex exponential with a wave vector k whose magnitude is the wavenumber and whose direction is the direction of propagation. For a scalar wave, the field takes the form A·exp(i(k·x − ωt)), where ω is the angular frequency.

Properties

True plane waves cannot exist physically, because they would require infinite energy and would have to extend infinitely in space; nevertheless, they are an extremely useful idealisation. Any real wave can be decomposed into a superposition of plane waves (a Fourier decomposition), which underlies much of wave optics, electromagnetism, acoustics, and quantum mechanics — where a free particle with definite momentum is described by a plane wave, following the de Broglie relations.

Applications

Plane waves are used as the basis of solutions to the wave equation in homogeneous media, in describing electromagnetic radiation far from a source (where spherical wavefronts are locally approximately planar), and in scattering theory, where an incoming particle or wave is modelled as a plane wave incident on a target.
