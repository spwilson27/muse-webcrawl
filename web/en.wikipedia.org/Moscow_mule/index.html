Moscow mule

[Image: Moscow mule in a copper mug with lime]

The Moscow mule is a cocktail made with vodka, spicy ginger beer and lime juice, garnished with a slice or wedge of lime and typically served in a copper mug. Its name links the drink to Moscow (through vodka) and to the "kick" of the ginger beer, though it was invented in the United States.

History

The drink was created in 1941 in Los Angeles, at the Cock 'n Bull pub in Hollywood. John G. Martin, an executive of the Heublein drinks company who had acquired the American rights to Smirnoff vodka, joined Jack Morgan, the pub's owner and producer of Cock 'n Bull ginger beer, in devising a drink that would sell both products - vodka then being little known in the United States. The copper mug, associated with Sophie Berezinski, whose family had manufactured copper goods in Russia, became the drink's trademark vessel through an energetic marketing campaign in which Martin photographed bartenders holding the mug and a bottle of Smirnoff. The Moscow mule helped popularize vodka in America during the 1950s.

Variations

The mule template - spirit, ginger beer and citrus - has spawned many variants, including the Kentucky mule (bourbon), Mexican mule (tequila), gin-gin mule, and the Dark 'n' Stormy (rum), which is a related trademarked drink. Concerns have periodically been raised about acidic drinks leaching copper from unlined mugs; food codes generally require mugs used for such cocktails to be lined.
