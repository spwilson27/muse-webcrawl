World Rogaining Championships

The World Rogaining Championships are an international rogaining competition held under the auspices of the International Rogaining Federation (IRF). Rogaining is the sport of long-distance cross-country navigation in which teams of two to five competitors visit checkpoints in a set order within a time limit, typically 24 hours.

History

The first World Rogaining Championships were held in 1992 at Beechworth in Victoria, Australia, the state where the sport originated in the 1970s. The championships were initially held every two years and later became an annual event as the sport spread internationally, with strong participation from Australia, New Zealand, Russia, the Czech Republic, the Baltic states, and North America.

Format

Teams navigate on foot using only a map and compass, planning their own route among checkpoints of differing point values spread over an area that may exceed 100 square kilometres. Categories are contested by gender and age class, including open, veteran, and junior divisions.
