Savusavu

Savusavu is a town in the Fijian province of Cakaudrove, on the southern coast of Vanua Levu, Fiji's second-largest island. Built around a sheltered natural harbour on Savusavu Bay, it has a population of roughly 5,000–7,000.

History and economy

Savusavu developed in the 19th century as a port for the sandalwood and bêche-de-mer trades and later for copra. Today it is a popular stop for cruising yachts, with a marina and customs port of entry, and a centre for tourism, diving, and pearl farming in the bay. The town sits over geothermal activity; hot springs are used locally for cooking, and geothermal power potential has been investigated.

Attractions

Nearby attractions include the Namena Marine Reserve, known for soft-coral diving, and Jean-Michel Cousteau's resort. Savusavu is connected to the rest of Vanua Levu by road and to Suva by ferry and by flights to Savusavu Airport.
