Rasgulla

CourseDessert
RegionOdisha and West Bengal, India
Main ingredientsChhena (curdled milk), semolina, sugar syrup

Rasgulla (also spelled rosogolla) is a popular syrupy dessert from the eastern part of the Indian subcontinent. It consists of soft, spongy, ball-shaped dumplings made from chhena (an Indian cottage cheese) and a small amount of semolina dough, cooked in a light syrup of sugar and water, often flavoured with rose water or cardamom.

Preparation

Fresh chhena is kneaded with semolina until smooth, rolled into small balls, and simmered in boiling sugar syrup. As they cook, the balls expand and absorb the syrup, becoming light and spongy. Rasgulla is usually served chilled or at room temperature, and canned rasgulla is widely sold commercially.

Origin dispute

The origin of rasgulla has long been contested between the states of Odisha and West Bengal. Odisha associates the sweet with the centuries-old tradition of offering rasgulla at the Jagannath Temple in Puri, while West Bengal credits confectioner Nobin Chandra Das of Kolkata with popularising a spongy version in the 19th century. Both states have received Geographical Indication (GI) tags for their distinct variants: "Odisha Rasagola" and "Banglar Rosogolla".

Variations

Variants include baked rasgulla, rasmalai (rasgulla flattened and soaked in flavoured milk), and regional versions made with different syrups or fillings.
