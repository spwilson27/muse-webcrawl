Fourth Industrial Revolution

A "smart factory": cyber-physical systems at the heart of Industry 4.0

The Fourth Industrial Revolution, also known as Industry 4.0, is a collective term for the ongoing automation and digitalisation of manufacturing and industrial practices, characterised by the fusion of technologies across the physical, digital, and biological spheres. The concept was popularised by Klaus Schwab, founder of the World Economic Forum, in 2015–2016, building on the German government's Industrie 4.0 strategic initiative.

Key technologies

  • Cyber-physical systems and the Internet of Things — networked sensors and machines communicating in real time.
  • Big data and analytics, cloud computing, and edge computing.
  • Artificial intelligence and machine learning applied to production, logistics, and design.
  • Advanced robotics, additive manufacturing (3D printing), and augmented reality.
  • Biotechnology advances such as gene editing.

Relation to earlier revolutions

The first Industrial Revolution mechanised production with water and steam power; the second enabled mass production with electricity; the third automated production with electronics and information technology. Proponents argue the fourth is distinguished by unprecedented speed, scope, and systems impact. Critics contend it is largely an extension of the digital (third) revolution, or a marketing and policy framework rather than a distinct historical epoch.

Impacts and debate

Debate centres on employment displacement and new job creation, inequality, privacy, data governance, and the reshaping of supply chains and business models. Governments worldwide have adopted Industry 4.0 strategies to promote smart manufacturing.
