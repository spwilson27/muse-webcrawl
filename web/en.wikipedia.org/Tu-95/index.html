Tupolev Tu-95


A Tu-95MS of the Russian Air Force

The Tupolev Tu-95 (NATO reporting name: "Bear") is a large, four-engine turboprop-powered strategic bomber and missile platform developed by the Soviet Union during the Cold War. First flown in 1952 and introduced in 1956, it remains in service with Russia, making it one of the longest-serving military aircraft designs in the world.

Design

The Tu-95 is powered by four Kuznetsov NK-12 turboprops driving enormous contra-rotating propellers, which make it one of the fastest propeller-driven aircraft ever built—and reputedly the loudest. Its swept wing and intercontinental range allowed it to strike targets in the United States from Soviet bases. Variants include the Tu-95MS cruise-missile carrier and the Tu-142 maritime patrol derivative.

Operational history

A Tu-95 dropped the Tsar Bomba, the most powerful nuclear weapon ever tested, in 1961. During the Cold War, naval versions routinely shadowed Western carrier groups. Modernized Tu-95MS aircraft carrying Kh-101 cruise missiles have been used in Russian operations over Syria and Ukraine, and the type is expected to remain in service into the 2040s.
