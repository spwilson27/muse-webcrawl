Memel Territory

The Memel Territory (German: Memelland or Memelgebiet; Lithuanian: Klaipėdos kraštas) was a region separated from the German Reich by the Treaty of Versailles in 1920. It comprised the northern part of East Prussia lying north of the Neman (Memel) River, centred on the port city of Memel, now Klaipėda in Lithuania.

History

Placed under French administration on behalf of the League of Nations, the territory was seized by Lithuanian forces and local insurgents in the Klaipėda Revolt of January 1923. The League accepted Lithuanian sovereignty, and the Klaipėda Convention of 1924 granted the territory autonomy within Lithuania, with its own diet and directorate.

Return to Germany and aftermath

Under pressure after the Munich crisis, Lithuania returned the territory to Germany in March 1939 following an ultimatum by Adolf Hitler — Germany's last territorial acquisition before the Second World War. After 1945 the area was incorporated into the Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic, its German population expelled, and Memel regained its Lithuanian name, Klaipėda.
