Torres del Paine National Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Torres del Paine) is a national park in the Última Esperanza Province of Chile's Magallanes Region, in southern Patagonia. It is famed for its soaring granite towers and horns, turquoise lakes, glaciers and steppe, and is one of South America's most visited protected areas.

Geography

The park's centrepiece is the Paine massif, an eastern spur of the Andes. Its best-known formations are the three Towers of Paine (Torres del Paine), the Cuernos del Paine ("Horns"), and Cerro Paine Grande, the highest summit at about 2,884 metres. Glaciers such as Grey, Pingo and Tyndall descend from the Southern Patagonian Ice Field into the park, and lakes include Pehoé, Nordenskjöld, Sarmiento and Grey.

Wildlife

The park supports guanacos, pumas — for which it is a noted viewing destination — Andean condors, rheas, foxes and the endangered South Andean deer (huemul). Vegetation ranges from Patagonian steppe to Magellanic subpolar forest.

Tourism

The park was established in 1959 and designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve in 1978. Popular trekking routes include the "W" trek and the full "O" circuit around the Paine massif. Repeated wildfires caused by visitors, notably in 1985, 2005 and 2011, have led to strict fire regulations.
