Renting

Renting is an agreement where a payment is made for the temporary use of a good, service, or property owned by another person or company. The owner, generally known as the landlord in property transactions, receives rent from the tenant in exchange for exclusive possession for a defined period. Renting occurs for housing, vehicles, tools and equipment, consumer goods, and many other items.

Residential renting

Residential tenancy is governed in most countries by housing law that defines the rights and obligations of landlords and tenants: habitability standards, security deposits, notice periods, protection from eviction, and remedies for non-payment. Common forms include assured shorthold tenancies in England and Wales, and month-to-month or fixed-term leases in the United States, subject to state and local law. Rent controls or rent stabilisation apply in some cities, limiting how much rents may increase.

Renting of goods and vehicles

Car rental, tool hire, and equipment leasing let customers use expensive or infrequently needed items without purchasing them. Rental agreements typically address insurance, damage, late returns, and liability. Rent-to-own contracts combine rental with an option to purchase, and are regulated in many places because of their high effective cost.

Economic aspects

Renting gives flexibility and avoids capital outlay and maintenance costs, but builds no equity. The choice between renting and buying housing is a common personal-finance question influenced by prices, interest rates, expected length of stay, and local market conditions (often summarised by the price-to-rent ratio).
