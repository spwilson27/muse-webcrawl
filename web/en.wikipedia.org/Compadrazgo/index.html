Compadrazgo

Compadrazgo (Spanish for "co-parenthood") is a system of ritual kinship found in Spain, Latin America, the Philippines, and other Catholic-influenced societies. It arises from the institution of godparenthood (padrinazgo) in the Roman Catholic Church: when a child is baptized, the godparents (padrino and madrina) enter into a special relationship not only with the child (their ahijado or godchild) but also with the child's parents, who become their compadres (co-parents).

Social functions

Compadrazgo ties carry strong obligations of mutual aid, loyalty, hospitality, and respect. Families may choose godparents strategically: selecting social equals strengthens horizontal bonds within a community, while selecting wealthier or more powerful patrons creates vertical ties that can provide access to jobs, credit, protection, and influence. Anthropologists have studied compadrazgo extensively as a mechanism of social cohesion, reciprocity, and patron–client relations.

Occasions and variations

Although baptism is the classic occasion, compadrazgo relationships may also be created at confirmation, first communion, weddings, and, in Mexico and elsewhere, at secular celebrations such as a girl's fifteenth birthday (quinceañera) or even the blessing of a new house or business. In Tagalog-speaking Philippines, the equivalent terms are kumpare and kumare, derived from the Spanish.
