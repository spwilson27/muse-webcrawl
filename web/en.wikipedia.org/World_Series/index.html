The World Series is the annual championship series of Major League Baseball (MLB) in the United States and Canada. Contested since 1903 between the champion teams of the American League (AL) and the National League (NL), it is a best-of-seven playoff played in October — hence its nickname, the "Fall Classic."

History

The first World Series in 1903 saw the Boston Americans (now the Red Sox) defeat the Pittsburgh Pirates in a best-of-nine series. The 1904 series was cancelled after the New York Giants refused to play, but the event resumed in 1905 and has been held every year since except 1994, when a players' strike wiped out the postseason. The series adopted its permanent best-of-seven format in 1922, following the best-of-nine experiments of 1919–1921, a period overshadowed by the Black Sox scandal.

Format

The series follows a 2–3–2 home-field format. Since 2003, apart from the neutral-site 2020 series, home-field advantage has gone to the league whose team won the All-Star Game in some eras, and since 2017 to the pennant winner with the better regular-season record. The winner receives the Commissioner's Trophy, first awarded in 1967.

Records and notable series

The New York Yankees have won a record 27 championships in 41 appearances. Memorable series include the 1975 clash between Cincinnati and Boston, the 1986 Mets comeback against the Red Sox, the 1991 Twins–Braves series, the 2001 Diamondbacks' upset of the Yankees, the 2016 Chicago Cubs victory ending a 108-year drought, and the 2024 series in which the Los Angeles Dodgers defeated the Yankees. Don Larsen remains the only pitcher to throw a perfect game in the World Series, in 1956.
