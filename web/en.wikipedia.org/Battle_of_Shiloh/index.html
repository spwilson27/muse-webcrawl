The Battle of Shiloh, also known as the Battle of Pittsburg Landing, was a major battle in the American Civil War fought on April 6–7, 1862, in southwestern Tennessee. Confederate forces under Generals Albert Sidney Johnston and P. G. T. Beauregard launched a surprise attack against the Union Army of the Tennessee commanded by Major General Ulysses S. Grant.

First day

The Confederate attack caught many Union troops in their camps and drove them back toward the Tennessee River. General Johnston was mortally wounded during the afternoon. A stubborn Union stand in a sunken road — remembered as the "Hornets' Nest" — bought time for Grant to establish a final defensive line near Pittsburg Landing, supported by gunboats on the river.

Second day

Reinforced overnight by the Army of the Ohio under Don Carlos Buell, Grant counterattacked on April 7 and retook the lost ground. Beauregard, recognizing his army was outnumbered and exhausted, ordered a retreat to Corinth, Mississippi.

Aftermath

Shiloh was the bloodiest battle in American history up to that time, with roughly 23,000 total casualties, shocking public opinion in both North and South. The Union victory ended Confederate hopes of blocking the Federal advance into northern Mississippi and opened the way toward Corinth and the Mississippi River.
