Weill Cornell Medicine, officially the Joan & Sanford I. Weill Medical College of Cornell University, is the biomedical research unit and medical school of Cornell University. Located on the Upper East Side of Manhattan in New York City, it is affiliated with NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital, the Hospital for Special Surgery, and Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, among other institutions.

History

The medical college was founded in 1898 as Cornell University Medical College, chartered with an endowment from Colonel Oliver Hazard Payne. In 1998 it was renamed in honour of Joan and Sanford I. Weill following major philanthropic gifts. The institution adopted the umbrella name "Weill Cornell Medicine" in 2015 to encompass its medical college, graduate school of medical sciences, and physician organisation.

Education and research

Weill Cornell Medicine enrols several hundred medical students and graduate students, including students in the Tri-Institutional PhD Program run jointly with Rockefeller University and Memorial Sloan Kettering. It also operates Weill Cornell Medicine–Qatar in Doha, established in 2001 as the first American medical school outside the United States. Research strengths include cancer biology, neuroscience, cardiovascular medicine, and global health.
