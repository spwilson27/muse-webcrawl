Alexander Andreyevich Baranov

Alexander Andreyevich Baranov (Russian: Алекса́ндр Андре́евич Бара́нов; 3 February [O.S. 23 January] 1747 – 16 April [O.S. 4 April] 1819), sometimes spelled Aleksandr or Alexandr and Baranof, was a Russian trader and merchant who worked for the Shelikhov-Golikov Company and the Russian-American Company. He was the first governor of Russian America, serving from 1790 to 1818.

Career in Alaska

Baranov arrived in Russian America in 1790 and took charge of the company's operations from Kodiak Island. He founded settlements including Pavlovskaya (Kodiak) and, in 1804, New Archangel (present-day Sitka), which became the capital of Russian Alaska. After Tlingit forces destroyed the earlier Sitka settlement in 1802, Baranov retook the site in the Battle of Sitka in 1804.

Under his administration the Russian-American Company expanded the sea otter trade down the Pacific coast; his deputy Ivan Kuskov founded Fort Ross in California in 1812. Baranov also oversaw shipbuilding, agriculture, schools, and attempts at trade with Spanish California, Hawaii, and China, though his rule relied on the coercion of Alaska Native peoples, whose labour the fur trade exploited.

Death and legacy

Baranov was replaced in 1818 by naval officers sent from Russia and died at sea on the voyage home, near Batavia (Jakarta), in April 1819; he was buried at sea. Baranof Island in Alaska's Alexander Archipelago is named for him, as is Baranov Museum in Kodiak.
