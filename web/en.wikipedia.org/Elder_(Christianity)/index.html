Elder (Christianity)

Elder
Greek termPresbyteros (πρεσβύτερος)
RoleLeader/teacher in a local church
Key textsActs 14:23; 1 Timothy 3 & 5; Titus 1; 1 Peter 5
Related officesBishop (overseer), deacon, pastor

In Christianity, an elder is a person valued for wisdom and recognised as a leader in a local church or denomination. The English word derives from the New Testament Greek presbyteros, meaning an older or senior person, and gives rise to the words priest and presbyter. In the New Testament, elders were appointed in congregations (for example in Acts 14:23) to shepherd, teach and oversee the community, alongside overseers (episkopoi) and deacons.

By tradition

In Presbyterian churches, elders form the governing session of a congregation: teaching elders (ordained ministers) and ruling elders (laypeople) together, with higher courts of presbytery, synod and general assembly. Many Baptist, Brethren and independent evangelical churches practise elder-led or elder-ruled congregational government. In the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Anglican traditions, the Greek term survives in "presbyter", the second order of ordained ministry — the priest — while leadership of a diocese rests with bishops. In the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, Elder is an office in the Melchizedek priesthood and a title for male missionaries; Jehovah's Witnesses are led locally by a body of elders.

Qualifications

The pastoral epistles (1 Timothy 3, Titus 1) list character qualifications for overseers and elders — self-control, hospitality, freedom from greed and a well-managed household — which most traditions read as requirements for appointment. Whether women may serve as elders is a long-standing point of difference among denominations.
