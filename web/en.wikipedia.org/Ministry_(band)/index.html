Ministry (band)

OriginChicago, Illinois, U.S.
Formed1981
GenresIndustrial metal, industrial rock, thrash metal
FounderAl Jourgensen
Key albumsThe Land of Rape and Honey; The Mind Is a Terrible Thing to Taste; Psalm 69

Ministry is an American industrial metal band founded in Chicago in 1981 by producer, singer, and instrumentalist Al Jourgensen. Beginning as a synth-pop act with the album With Sympathy (1983), Ministry radically changed direction toward aggressive, sample-laden industrial music, achieving a breakthrough with The Land of Rape and Honey (1988) and The Mind Is a Terrible Thing to Taste (1989).

The band's commercial peak came with Psalm 69: The Way to Succeed and the Way to Suck Eggs (1992), featuring tracks such as "N.W.O." and "Just One Fix," which fused thrash-metal guitars with drum machines, tape loops, and political film samples. Bassist Paul Barker was a key collaborator from 1986 to 2003. Ministry became a major influence on industrial metal and bands such as Nine Inch Nails, Marilyn Manson, and Rammstein.

Later years

After a trilogy of albums fiercely critical of the George W. Bush administration, Jourgensen dissolved the band in 2008, then revived it in 2011. Later albums include From Beer to Eternity (2013), AmeriKKKant (2018), Moral Hygiene (2021), and Hopiumforthemasses (2024), with farewell touring announced for the mid-2020s.
