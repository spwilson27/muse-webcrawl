Principal curvature

FieldDifferential geometry
Notationκ1, κ2
Derived quantitiesGaussian curvature K = κ1κ2; mean curvature H = (κ1 + κ2)/2

In differential geometry, the principal curvatures of a surface at a point are the maximum and minimum values of the normal curvature of the surface in different directions at that point. They are the eigenvalues of the shape operator (Weingarten map), and the corresponding eigendirections — the principal directions — are mutually orthogonal (except at umbilical points, where the principal curvatures coincide).

Definitions

For a surface in three-dimensional Euclidean space, the normal curvature in a given tangent direction measures how sharply the surface bends in that direction. By a theorem of Euler (1760), the normal curvature in a direction making angle θ with the first principal direction is κn = κ1 cos²θ + κ2 sin²θ.

Derived curvatures

The product of the principal curvatures is the Gaussian curvature, an intrinsic quantity by Gauss's Theorema Egregium, while half their sum is the mean curvature, important in the theory of minimal surfaces and in physics (for example, in the Young–Laplace equation for surface tension). A point with κ1 = κ2 is called umbilical; on a sphere every point is umbilical.
