The Governor of Tokyo (Japanese: 東京都知事, Tōkyōto chiji) is the head of government of Tokyo Metropolis, Japan's capital prefecture, home to roughly 14 million residents in its 23 special wards and surrounding municipalities. Directly elected for a four-year term, the governor oversees one of the largest metropolitan budgets in the world and is among the most prominent elected officials in Japan.

History

The modern office dates to 1947, when Seiichiro Yasui became the first governor of the newly formed Tokyo Metropolis under the postwar Local Autonomy Law; Tokyo Metropolis itself was created in 1943 by merging Tokyo City with Tokyo Prefecture. Earlier, appointed governors administered the prefecture during the Meiji and wartime periods.

Notable governors

Prominent postwar governors include Ryokichi Minobe (1967–1979), a socialist-backed reformer who introduced free medical care for the elderly; Shunichi Suzuki (1979–1995), who oversaw the construction of the new metropolitan government headquarters in Shinjuku; Yukio Aoshima (1995–1999); Shintaro Ishihara (1999–2012), a nationalist novelist-politician; Naoki Inose (2012–2013); Yoichi Masuzoe (2014–2016), who resigned over an expenses scandal; and Yuriko Koike, elected in 2016 as the first woman to hold the office and re-elected in 2020 and 2024. Koike's tenure included the postponed Tokyo 2020 Olympic and Paralympic Games and the metropolis's COVID-19 response.

Powers

The governor proposes the metropolitan budget and ordinances to the Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly, directs the metropolitan bureaus (covering waterworks, transport including the Toei subway, and public health), and represents Tokyo internationally. The office is elected by universal suffrage of metropolitan residents over 18.
