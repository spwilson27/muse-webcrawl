Electronic countermeasure

An electronic countermeasure (ECM) is an electrical or electronic device designed to trick or deceive radar, sonar or other detection systems, and forms part of electronic warfare. Countermeasures may be used offensively, to support attacking forces, or defensively, to protect friendly aircraft, ships and vehicles from radar-guided weapons.

Radar countermeasures

Noise jamming floods a radar receiver with strong radio energy, masking genuine echoes. Deception jamming instead transmits manipulated signals that cause the radar to report false ranges, bearings or velocities. Chaff — clouds of small metallic strips cut to resonate at radar wavelengths — creates a profusion of false targets. Expendable decoys and towed decoys lure missiles away from their targets, while infrared countermeasures such as flares seduce heat-seeking missiles.

Platforms and systems

Dedicated electronic-warfare aircraft such as the EA-6B Prowler and its successor the EA-18G Growler carry powerful jamming pods like the AN/ALQ-99. Self-protection suites fitted to combat aircraft combine radar warning receivers with jammers, chaff and flare dispensers that react automatically to threats. Naval countermeasures include offboard decoys such as Nulka, which hovers away from a ship while broadcasting a seductive radar signal.

Counter-countermeasures

Opposing ECM are electronic counter-countermeasures (ECCM), including frequency agility, spread-spectrum waveforms and home-on-jam guidance, in the continuing measure-and-countermeasure contest of electronic warfare.
