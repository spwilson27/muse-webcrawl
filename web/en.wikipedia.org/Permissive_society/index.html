Permissive society

A permissive society is a society in which previously restricted social norms — especially concerning sexuality, censorship and authority — become progressively more liberal. The phrase is most often applied to Western societies in the 1960s and 1970s.

Usage in Britain

In Britain the term described reforms under the 1960s Labour governments: the legalisation of abortion and homosexual acts in 1967, the abolition of theatre censorship, divorce reform and the end of capital punishment. Critics, including campaigner Mary Whitehouse, used "permissiveness" pejoratively, blaming it for social breakdown; supporters saw the changes as humane modernisation.

Debate

Historians debate whether the era represented a genuine moral revolution, a catch-up of law with existing behaviour, or changes concentrated among urban elites. The concept remains a reference point in cultural and political arguments about liberalisation.
