Beer–Lambert law

The Beer–Lambert law, also known as Beer's law, relates the attenuation of light to the properties of the material through which the light is travelling. It states that absorbance is proportional to the concentration of the absorbing species and the path length.

Formulation

Absorbance A = ε·l·c, where ε is the molar attenuation coefficient, l is the optical path length and c is the concentration. Transmittance falls exponentially with distance.

History

The law combines the work of Pierre Bouguer, Johann Heinrich Lambert and August Beer in the 18th and 19th centuries.

Applications

It underlies spectrophotometry in chemistry and medicine, including pulse oximetry and analysis of atmospheric gases. See Spectroscopy.
