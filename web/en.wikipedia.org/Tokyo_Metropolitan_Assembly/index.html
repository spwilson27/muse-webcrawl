The Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly (東京都議会, Tōkyō-to Gikai) is the prefectural parliament of Tokyo Metropolis, Japan. Its 127 members are elected every four years by single non-transferable vote in 42 electoral districts corresponding to the special wards, cities, towns and villages of the metropolis. Together with the directly elected Governor of Tokyo, the Assembly forms the core of metropolitan self-government: it enacts metropolitan ordinances, approves the budget — one of the largest subnational budgets in the world — and oversees the enormous metropolitan bureaucracy.

History

The Assembly's origins lie in the Tokyo Prefectural Assembly established in 1879 under the Meiji system of prefectural assemblies. When Tokyo City and Tokyo Prefecture were merged into the Metropolis in 1943, the wartime assembly lost effective power, but it was restored as a directly elected body under the post-war constitution and the Local Autonomy Law of 1947. Metropolitan assembly elections are held in unified local election cycles offset from most other prefectures — Tokyo votes in a different year from the standard unified elections, together with a few other assemblies.

Powers and composition

The Assembly votes on ordinances, the metropolitan budget and major appointments, interpellates the governor and bureau chiefs in plenary and committee sessions, and can pass a vote of no confidence in the governor, who may respond by dissolving the Assembly. Party politics largely mirror the national scene: the Liberal Democratic Party has historically been the largest force, but the Assembly has seen major swings, notably in 2009 when the Democratic Party of Japan became the largest party, in 2017 when Governor Yuriko Koike's new regional party Tomin First no Kai won the most seats, and in 2025 when Tomin First again emerged as the largest party.
