Ai-Todor

Location: Gaspra, Crimea, Black Sea
Known for: Swallow's Nest castle; Charax Roman fort

Ai-Todor (Crimean Tatar: Ay Todor, from Greek Ἅγιος Τόντορος, "Saint Theodore") is a cape on the southern coast of Crimea, near the settlement of Gaspra, about 8.5 kilometres from Yalta. It is one of the most recognisable landmarks of the Crimean coast, crowned by the Swallow's Nest castle.

Features

The cape has three rocky spurs. The central spur carries the ruins of Charax, a Roman fortified camp founded under Vespasian in the 1st century CE, which guarded the coast and the route between Chersonesus and the eastern Black Sea ports. Excavations at Charax have revealed walls, a water reservoir, baths and a sanctuary.

On the Aurora Cliff of the cape stands the Swallow's Nest (Lastochkino Gnezdo), a small neo-Gothic decorative castle built in 1911–1912 for the Baltic German oil magnate Baron von Steingel to designs by the architect Leonid Sheremetev. Perched 40 metres above the sea, it became the emblem of Crimea despite frequent earthquake damage — a quake in 1927 sheared off part of the rock beneath it.

In the nineteenth century the Ai-Todor estate belonged to Grand Duke Alexander Mikhailovich of Russia, and the area around Gaspra and Koreiz became a fashionable aristocratic resort zone. A lighthouse operates on the cape, and the nearby Ai-Todor palace and park are now part of a sanatorium complex.
