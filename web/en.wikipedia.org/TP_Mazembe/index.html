TP Mazembe

Full name: Tout Puissant Mazembe
Founded: 1939
Ground: Stade TP Mazembe, Lubumbashi
Nickname: Les Corbeaux (The Ravens)
League: Linafoot (DR Congo)

Tout Puissant Mazembe, commonly known as TP Mazembe and formerly Englebert, is a professional football club based in Lubumbashi, in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Founded in 1939, it is one of the most successful clubs in African football, playing in black and white striped shirts and nicknamed "Les Corbeaux" (The Ravens).

History

Mazembe rose to continental prominence in the late 1960s, winning the African Cup of Champions Clubs in 1967 and 1968. After a long quieter period, investment under owner Moïse Katumbi in the 2000s restored the club to the top of African football.

Honours

The club has won the CAF Champions League five times (1967, 1968, 2009, 2010, 2015) and the CAF Confederation Cup in 2016 and 2017. In 2010 Mazembe became the first African club to reach the final of the FIFA Club World Cup, defeating Brazil's Internacional before losing to Inter Milan — still the best performance by an African side in the competition. Domestically, Mazembe has won numerous Linafoot league titles and Congolese Cups.

Stadium

The club plays at the Stade TP Mazembe in the Kamalondo suburb of Lubumbashi, a modern stadium opened in 2011 with a capacity of about 18,000.
