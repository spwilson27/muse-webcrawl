Shape of the universe

Shape of the universe
FieldPhysical cosmology
AspectsLocal geometry (curvature); global topology
Curvature parameterΩk ≈ 0 (flat within measurement error)

Possible formsFlat (Euclidean), spherical (closed), hyperbolic (open); topologies include torus, Poincaré dodecahedral space

The shape of the universe is a question in physical cosmology concerning the geometry of the universe, both its local curvature — whether space is flat, positively curved (spherical), or negatively curved (hyperbolic) — and its global topology — whether space is finite or infinite, and simply connected or multiply connected. General relativity relates local curvature to the total energy density, but leaves the global shape undetermined, so the question must be answered by observation.

Local geometry

In the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker model, the curvature density parameter Ωk summarises the geometry: Ωk = 0 means flat Euclidean space, in which parallel lines stay parallel and triangle angles sum to 180°; positive curvature closes space like the surface of a sphere, so the universe could be finite yet unbounded; negative curvature gives an infinite saddle-shaped (hyperbolic) geometry. Cosmic inflation predicts the observable universe should be extremely close to flat, because expansion drives curvature toward zero.

Global topology

Even with flat local geometry, the universe may be finite through nontrivial topology: space could be a three-dimensional torus, in which travelling far enough in one direction returns one to the starting point, or a more exotic manifold. Proposed closed spherical topologies include the Poincaré dodecahedral space, once suggested to explain patterns in the cosmic microwave background (CMB). In a small closed universe, the same object might be seen in several directions at different ages, and searches for matching circles in the CMB sky test this possibility.

Observational evidence

Measurements of the CMB by WMAP and the Planck satellite, combined with baryon acoustic oscillation data, constrain spatial curvature to be very close to zero — Planck 2018 results give Ωk = 0.0007 ± 0.0019 — consistent with a flat universe. No evidence for nontrivial topology on scales comparable to the observable universe has been found, so if the universe is closed or multiply connected, its fundamental domain is at least comparable to, and probably larger than, the observable universe, whose diameter is about 93 billion light-years.
