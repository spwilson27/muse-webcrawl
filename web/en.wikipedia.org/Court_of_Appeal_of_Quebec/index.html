Court of Appeal of Quebec

The Court of Appeal of Quebec (French: Cour d'appel du Québec) is the highest judicial court in Quebec, Canada. It hears appeals from the Superior Court of Quebec and the Court of Québec, as well as certain administrative tribunals.

The court sits in Montreal and Quebec City and is composed of a chief justice (the Chief Justice of Quebec), a body of puisne judges appointed by the federal government, and supernumerary judges; appeals are normally heard by panels of three judges. Its origins date to 1849, when the Court of Queen's Bench was created for Lower Canada; the modern court took its present name in 1974.

Jurisdiction

As Quebec's appellate court for a civil-law jurisdiction within a federal state, the court decides both civil and criminal appeals, and the provincial government may refer questions of law to it for advisory opinions. Judgments may be appealed, usually with leave, to the Supreme Court of Canada.
