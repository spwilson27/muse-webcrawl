Christmas cake

Christmas cake is a type of cake, often a rich fruitcake, served at Christmas in the United Kingdom, Ireland, and many Commonwealth countries. A traditional British Christmas cake is a dark, dense fruitcake made with dried and candied fruits soaked in brandy or rum, covered first with marzipan and then with white royal icing, and decorated with festive motifs. Other countries have their own distinct Christmas cakes, from German stollen to Japanese sponge Christmas cakes.

British tradition

The cake descends from plum porridge and the Twelfth Night cakes of earlier centuries. In the Victorian era it took its modern form: a matured fruitcake, traditionally made on "Stir-up Sunday" (the last Sunday before Advent), when each family member stirred the mixture and made a wish. The cake is baked weeks or months ahead and periodically "fed" with brandy to keep it moist and improve its flavor. Silver charms or coins were once baked inside for luck. Slices of the same style of cake are used as wedding cake in Britain.

Christmas cakes around the world

  • Germany — Stollen, a yeasted fruit bread dusted with icing sugar, associated with Dresden.
  • Italy — Panettone from Milan and pandoro from Verona, tall leavened cakes.
  • Japan — a light sponge cake layered with whipped cream and strawberries, sold widely at Christmas.
  • Philippines — fruitcakes influenced by both British and American traditions.
  • Caribbean — black cake, a very dark rum-soaked fruitcake derived from British pudding.
