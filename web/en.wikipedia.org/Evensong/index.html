Evensong

TypeEvening prayer service
TraditionAnglican, Lutheran, Roman Catholic
Principal elementsPsalms, Magnificat, Nunc dimittis, anthem
Related servicesVespers, Compline, Morning Prayer

Evensong is a church service traditionally held in the late afternoon or evening, centred on sung prayer, psalms, and canticles. In the Anglican tradition, Evensong combines elements of the older monastic offices of Vespers and Compline into a single service, as set out in the Book of Common Prayer. Choral Evensong, sung by a choir with organ accompaniment, remains one of the most distinctive and widely attended forms of worship in cathedrals and collegiate churches, particularly in England.

History

The service was created during the English Reformation under Archbishop Thomas Cranmer. When the first Book of Common Prayer appeared in 1549, Cranmer condensed the eight daily monastic offices into two: Morning Prayer (Matins) and Evening Prayer (Evensong). The evening office drew its psalms and readings from Vespers and its closing prayers from Compline. The 1662 prayer book gave the service the form still familiar today, with the canticles Magnificat (the Song of Mary) and Nunc dimittis (the Song of Simeon) as its fixed musical pillars.

Structure of the service

A typical Choral Evensong begins with an introit or opening hymn, followed by the Preces (opening responses), psalms appointed for the day, and two lessons from the Old and New Testaments. After the first lesson the choir sings the Magnificat; after the second, the Nunc dimittis. The Apostles' Creed, the Responses, and a series of collects follow, and the service usually concludes with a choral anthem, prayers, and a final hymn. Congregations often attend in a largely listening, contemplative role.

Music

Evensong has inspired a large repertoire of settings by composers including Thomas Tallis, William Byrd, Orlando Gibbons, Henry Purcell, Charles Villiers Stanford, Herbert Howells, and William Walton. Settings of the Magnificat and Nunc dimittis — known collectively as a "Service" — range from Tudor polyphony to contemporary works. The BBC has broadcast Choral Evensong weekly since 1926, making it the longest-running outside broadcast programme in the corporation's history.
