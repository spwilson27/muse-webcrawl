Nicolas Steno

Nicolas Steno (Danish: Niels Steensen; 1 January 1638 – 25 November 1686) was a Danish scientist, a pioneer in anatomy and geology, who later converted to Catholicism and became a bishop. He was beatified by Pope John Paul II in 1988.

Science

In anatomy, Steno discovered the parotid duct (Stensen's duct) and argued that the heart is a muscle. In geology, his 1669 work De solido intra solidum naturaliter contento ("On a solid naturally enclosed in a solid"), prompted by dissecting a shark's head and recognizing that "tongue stones" were fossil shark teeth, laid down foundational principles of stratigraphy: the law of superposition, the principle of original horizontality, and the principle of lateral continuity. He also formulated Steno's law of crystallography — that the angles between crystal faces of a given mineral are constant.

Religious life

Steno abandoned science in the 1670s, converted from Lutheranism to Catholicism, was ordained a priest in 1675, and served as titular bishop and apostolic vicar in northern Germany and Scandinavia, living in extreme asceticism until his death in Schwerin.
