Peace Palace

The Peace Palace (Dutch: Vredespaleis) is an international law administrative building in The Hague, Netherlands. Opened on 28 August 1913, it houses the International Court of Justice (the principal judicial body of the United Nations), the Permanent Court of Arbitration, the Hague Academy of International Law, and the Peace Palace Library.

History

The idea of the palace began at the First Hague Peace Conference of 1899, convened to discuss disarmament and international arbitration. The Permanent Court of Arbitration, established by that conference, needed a permanent home. American philanthropist Andrew Carnegie donated $1.5 million through the Carnegie Foundation to build it. An international design competition was won by French architect Louis M. Cordonnier, whose Neo-Renaissance design was adapted by Dutch architect Johan van der Steur.

Contents and gifts

Many nations contributed artworks and materials to the building: marble from Italy, tapestries from Japan, a fountain from Denmark, stained glass from the United Kingdom, and a large vase from Russia. The Peace Palace Library is one of the world's leading collections on international law.

Institutions

The International Court of Justice has sat in the palace since 1946, succeeding the Permanent Court of International Justice, which was housed there from 1922. The building remains a symbol of international law and diplomacy and is a popular visitor attraction in The Hague.
