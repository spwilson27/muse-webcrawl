North Sea flood of 1953

The North Sea flood of 1953 was a major flood caused by a heavy storm at the end of Saturday, 31 January 1953, and the morning of the next day. A combination of a high spring tide and a severe European windstorm caused a storm tide; in combination with a tidal surge of the North Sea, the water level locally exceeded 5.6 metres (18.4 ft) above mean sea level. The flood and waves overwhelmed sea defences and caused extensive flooding, killing 2,551 people in the Netherlands, Belgium and the United Kingdom.

The storm and surge

A deep Atlantic depression moved across the northern North Sea during 31 January 1953, driving strong north-westerly winds that pushed water southwards into the narrowing southern basin. Because the surge coincided with a high spring tide, sea levels rose far above predicted values, overtopping and breaching dikes and sea walls along the Dutch, Belgian and English coasts during the night.

Netherlands

The Netherlands suffered the greatest losses: 1,836 people died, mostly in the provinces of Zeeland, South Holland and North Brabant. Around 150,000 hectares of land were flooded, tens of thousands of livestock drowned, and thousands of buildings were destroyed. The disaster prompted the Dutch government to launch the Delta Works, one of the largest hydraulic engineering projects in history, to shorten the coastline and protect the south-western delta.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, 307 people died. The east coast of England, particularly Lincolnshire, Norfolk, Suffolk, Essex and Kent, was badly affected; the town of Canvey Island in the Thames estuary lost 58 residents. In Scotland, the town of Coldingham suffered severe damage. The disaster spurred construction of the Thames Barrier, completed in 1982, and major improvements to coastal warning systems.

Legacy

The flood is remembered in the Netherlands as the Watersnoodramp (flood disaster) and remains the country's defining natural catastrophe of the 20th century. It directly led to modern storm-surge forecasting services in the affected countries.
