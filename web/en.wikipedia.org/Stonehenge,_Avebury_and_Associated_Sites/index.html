Stonehenge, Avebury and Associated Sites

Stonehenge, Avebury and Associated Sites is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Wiltshire, England, inscribed in 1986. It comprises two clusters of Neolithic and Bronze Age monuments about 30 kilometres apart: Stonehenge and its surroundings on Salisbury Plain, and Avebury and its associated sites to the north.

The Stonehenge landscape

Stonehenge itself — a ring of standing sarsen stones capped with lintels, enclosing bluestone arrangements — was built in stages between about 3000 and 2000 BC. Around it lie the Avenue, the Cursus, barrow cemeteries, and sites such as Durrington Walls and Woodhenge, which together form a dense ceremonial landscape along the River Avon.

The Avebury landscape

Avebury contains the largest megalithic stone circle in the world, enclosing two smaller circles and parts of the village itself, dating to roughly 2600 BC. Associated monuments include Silbury Hill, the largest artificial prehistoric mound in Europe; the West Kennet Long Barrow; the Sanctuary; and the Kennet Avenue.

Significance

UNESCO recognised the sites for their outstanding prehistoric monuments, the sophistication of their design and construction, and their continuing influence on archaeology, astronomy and popular culture. The properties are managed jointly under a single management plan, with most land in the care of English Heritage and the National Trust.
