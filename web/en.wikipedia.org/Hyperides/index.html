Hyperides

Hyperides
Born: c. 390 BC, Athens
Died: 322 BC, Aegina
Athenian logographer and orator, one of the ten canonical Attic orators.

Hyperides (Greek: Ὑπερείδης, Hypereidēs; c. 390 – 322 BC) was an Athenian logographer (speechwriter for the law courts) and politician. He was counted among the ten canonical Attic orators, and was renowned in antiquity for the charm, wit and subtlety of his style.

Life

Hyperides was a pupil of Isocrates and possibly of Plato. He first gained prominence prosecuting the statesman Autocles in 360 BC, and built a lucrative practice writing speeches for litigants. Like Demosthenes, he was a leading opponent of Macedonian expansion; after Alexander the Great's destruction of Thebes in 335 BC, Hyperides helped stiffen Athenian resolve. He was closely associated with the courtesan Phryne, whom he famously defended on a charge of impiety.

Death and rediscovery

After Athens' defeat in the Lamian War, Hyperides fled the city but was captured on Aegina in 322 BC and executed by the Macedonians — tradition says his tongue was cut out first, so the orator could not speak in his own defence. His speeches were thought lost until papyrus fragments were recovered from Egyptian mummy cartonnage in the 19th century, allowing six speeches to be read in whole or part today, including the funeral oration for Leosthenes and the speech Against Athenogenes.
