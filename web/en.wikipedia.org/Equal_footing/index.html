Equal footing

Equal footing doctrine

Field
U.S. constitutional law
Key cases
Pollard's Lessee v. Hagan (1845); Coyle v. Smith (1911)

The equal footing doctrine is a principle of United States constitutional law holding that all states admitted to the Union enter on an equal basis with the original thirteen states, possessing the same sovereignty and rights. It traces to the Northwest Ordinance of 1787 and the Admissions Clause of the U.S. Constitution.

Application

In Pollard's Lessee v. Hagan (1845), the Supreme Court held that Alabama, on admission, acquired title to the beds of its navigable waters just as the original states had. The doctrine has been applied to submerged lands, water rights, and public land grants. In Coyle v. Smith (1911), the Court ruled that Congress could not, as a condition of Oklahoma's admission, restrict the state's power to move its capital — a condition limiting a political power the original states enjoyed was unconstitutional, although economic conditions (such as land grants) may still be enforced.
