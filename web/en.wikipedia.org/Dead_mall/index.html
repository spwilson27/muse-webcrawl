Dead mall

A dead mall (also known as a greyfield) is a shopping mall with high vacancy rates, low consumer traffic, or visible deterioration, often following the departure of its anchor department stores. The phenomenon, most widespread in the United States, grew as older enclosed malls lost shoppers to newer centers, big-box power centers, and online retail.

Causes

Malls commonly decline when an anchor tenant such as Sears, JCPenney, or Macy's closes, triggering co-tenancy clauses that let smaller stores leave. Regional overbuilding during the mall boom of the 1970s and 1980s, demographic shifts, and the rise of e-commerce in the 2000s and 2010s accelerated closures. The bankruptcy of major chains during the so-called "retail apocalypse" left hundreds of properties largely empty.

Fates of dead malls

Some dead malls are demolished and replaced by open-air "lifestyle centers," warehouses, or mixed-use developments. Others have been converted to offices, medical centers, schools, churches, or housing. The Hawthorne Plaza in California and Randall Park Mall in Ohio — once billed as the world's largest — are well-known examples of malls that closed and were demolished or redeveloped.

In popular culture

Dead malls have attracted documentary and artistic interest, including the websites and photography projects chronicling abandoned retail spaces, and a nostalgia-driven online community drawn to their distinctive architecture and atmosphere.
