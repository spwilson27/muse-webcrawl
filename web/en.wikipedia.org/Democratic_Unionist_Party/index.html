The Democratic Unionist Party (DUP) is a unionist, loyalist and national conservative political party in Northern Ireland. It was founded in 1971 by Ian Paisley and Desmond Boal during the Troubles and was for many years the most hardline of the unionist parties, opposing power-sharing with Irish nationalists. From 2003 until the early 2020s it was the largest unionist party and generally the largest party in the Northern Ireland Assembly.

History

The DUP grew out of the Protestant Unionist Party. Under Paisley it campaigned against the Sunningdale Agreement and the Anglo-Irish Agreement. Following the Good Friday Agreement of 1998 — which the DUP opposed — the party overtook the Ulster Unionist Party as the leading unionist voice. In 2007 Paisley entered a power-sharing executive with Sinn Féin as First Minister, alongside Martin McGuinness.

Recent history

From 2017 to 2019 the DUP held the balance of power at Westminster and supported Theresa May's Conservative government through a confidence-and-supply agreement. The party opposed the Brexit withdrawal arrangements applying distinct rules to Northern Ireland, and from 2022 to 2024 it boycotted the Assembly over the Northern Ireland Protocol/Windsor Framework, returning to Stormont in February 2024. Leaders after Paisley have included Peter Robinson, Arlene Foster, Edwin Poots, Jeffrey Donaldson and Gavin Robinson.

Policies

The DUP supports Northern Ireland remaining part of the United Kingdom, socially conservative positions including historical opposition to abortion liberalisation and same-sex marriage, and a strong record of Euroscepticism.
