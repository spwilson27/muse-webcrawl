Finland–Russia relations concern the bilateral relationship between the Republic of Finland and the Russian Federation, shaped by a 1,340 km shared border and a long, often fraught history.

History

Finland was part of the Russian Empire from 1809 until independence in 1917. The two countries fought the Winter War (1939–40) and Continuation War (1941–44), after which Finland ceded Karelia and adopted a policy of neutrality — sometimes called "Finlandization" — under the 1948 Finno-Soviet Treaty.

Post-Cold War

After 1991 Finland joined the European Union (1995) while maintaining workable relations with Russia. Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Finland applied for and joined NATO in April 2023, and closed its eastern border crossings in late 2023 amid a dispute over instrumentalised migration.
