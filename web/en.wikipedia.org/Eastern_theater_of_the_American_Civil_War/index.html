Eastern theater of the American Civil War

Eastern theater
Date: 1861–1865
Location: Virginia, Maryland, Pennsylvania, West Virginia, North Carolina coast
Principal armies: Army of the Potomac vs. Army of Northern Virginia

The eastern theater of the American Civil War comprised the major military and naval operations in the states of Virginia, West Virginia, Maryland and Pennsylvania, the District of Columbia, and the coastal fortifications and seaports of North Carolina. It was dominated by the struggle between the Union Army of the Potomac and Robert E. Lee's Confederate Army of Northern Virginia.

Overview

The proximity of the rival capitals — Washington, D.C., and Richmond, Virginia, barely 100 miles apart — made the eastern theater the focus of public attention and the war's most famous campaigns. Early Union efforts to capture Richmond through the Peninsula Campaign and repeated Overland offensives were frustrated by Lee's defensive skill.

Major campaigns

Key engagements included the First and Second Battles of Bull Run, Antietam, Fredericksburg, Chancellorsville, Gettysburg (1863, in Pennsylvania), and the Wilderness. In 1864–65, Ulysses S. Grant's Overland Campaign and the siege of Petersburg ground down Lee's army, culminating in the fall of Richmond and Lee's surrender at Appomattox Court House on 9 April 1865.
