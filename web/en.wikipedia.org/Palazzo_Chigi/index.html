Chigi Palace

Palazzo Chigi
Location: Piazza Colonna, Rome, Italy
Construction: 1562–1580
Architects: Giacomo della Porta, Carlo Maderno
Current use: Official residence of the Prime Minister of Italy

The Chigi Palace (Italian: Palazzo Chigi, IPA: [paˈlattso ˈkiːdʒi]) is a palace and former noble residence in Rome which is the seat of the Council of Ministers and the official residence of the Prime Minister of Italy. Since December 14, 1962, the Chigi Palace is also the seat of the Italian Government.

History

Construction began in 1562 under Giacomo della Porta for the Aldobrandini family and was completed by Carlo Maderno in 1580. In 1659 the palace was purchased by the Chigi family of Siena — the family of Pope Alexander VII — who gave it its present name. It later passed to the Odescalchi family and was leased to various embassies before being bought by the Italian state in 1916. It initially housed the Ministry of Foreign Affairs; in 1961 it became the official meeting place of the Council of Ministers and residence of the prime minister, exchanging roles with the Viminale Palace.

The palace overlooks Piazza Colonna, named for the Column of Marcus Aurelius, and stands near Palazzo Montecitorio, seat of the Chamber of Deputies.
