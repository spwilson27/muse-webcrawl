Shing-Tung Yau

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: portrait of mathematician Shing-Tung Yau]

Shing-Tung Yau (Chinese: 丘成桐; born 4 April 1949) is a Chinese-American mathematician. He is a professor emeritus at Harvard University and, since 2022, a professor at Tsinghua University in Beijing. Yau is known for fundamental contributions to differential geometry, partial differential equations, and geometric analysis.

Born in Shantou, Guangdong, and raised in Hong Kong, Yau studied at the Chinese University of Hong Kong and earned his PhD at the University of California, Berkeley, under Shiing-Shen Chern in 1971. In 1976 he proved the Calabi conjecture, showing that compact Kähler manifolds with vanishing first Chern class admit Ricci-flat metrics; the resulting spaces are now called Calabi–Yau manifolds and became central objects in string theory.

Other contributions

With Richard Schoen, Yau proved the positive mass theorem in general relativity. His work with William Meeks on minimal surfaces, the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem in gauge theory, and the Li–Yau gradient estimates have all been influential. He was awarded the Fields Medal in 1982 and has also received the MacArthur Fellowship, the National Medal of Science, the Wolf Prize, and the Shaw Prize.

Yau has founded mathematical institutes in China and championed mathematics education there; his role in publicising the proof of the Poincaré conjecture brought him wide attention outside mathematics.
