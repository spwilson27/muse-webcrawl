Gamblers Anonymous

Founded1957, Los Angeles, California
TypeTwelve-step fellowship
PurposeMutual aid for problem gambling
Modeled onAlcoholics Anonymous

Gamblers Anonymous (GA) is a fellowship of people who share their experience, strength and hope with each other that they may solve their common problem and help others to recover from a gambling problem. Founded in Los Angeles in 1957 by Jim W., a member of Alcoholics Anonymous who recognized that the compulsion to gamble mirrored alcoholism, GA adapts the Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions of AA to compulsive gambling.

Program

The only requirement for membership is a desire to stop gambling. Meetings are free, anonymous, and self-supporting; members share their stories, work the steps with a sponsor, and commit to abstinence from gambling, "one day at a time." GA's literature poses twenty questions — including whether gambling has made one's home life unhappy or affected one's reputation — to help newcomers assess whether they are compulsive gamblers; answering yes to seven or more is presented as a sign of a problem.

Related fellowships

Gam-Anon, founded in 1960, serves family and friends of compulsive gamblers, paralleling Al-Anon for families of alcoholics. GA meetings now take place in many countries, and the fellowship gained renewed attention as sports betting and online gambling expanded.
