Nabonidus was the last king of the Neo-Babylonian Empire, reigning from 556 BC until the Persian conquest of Babylon in 539 BC. An unusual figure among Babylonian monarchs, he came to the throne in a coup despite not being of the royal line, and his reign is remembered for his religious devotion to the moon god Sin, his long self-imposed residence at the oasis of Tayma in Arabia, and his antiquarian interest in earlier Mesopotamian history.

Reign

Nabonidus elevated Sin of Harran above Marduk, Babylon's traditional chief god, restoring Sin's temple (with his mother Adad-guppi, a lifelong devotee of Sin, influential at court) and reportedly neglecting the New Year festival at Babylon. For about a decade he lived at Tayma, leaving his son Belshazzar as coregent in Babylon — an absence later tradition, including the biblical Book of Daniel's account of "Nebuchadnezzar's madness" and the Dead Sea Scrolls' Prayer of Nabonidus, seems to reflect.

Fall

In 539 BC Cyrus the Great of Persia defeated the Babylonian army at Opis and entered Babylon, ending the empire. Nabonidus was captured; his ultimate fate is uncertain, with later accounts claiming he was exiled to Carmania. His excavation and restoration of ancient temples, and his collection of antiquities, have led some modern writers to call him an early archaeologist.
