Boltzmann distribution

FieldStatistical mechanics
Named afterLudwig Boltzmann
Key variablesEnergy E, temperature T, Boltzmann constant kB

In statistical mechanics and mathematics, a Boltzmann distribution (also called the Gibbs distribution) is a probability distribution that gives the probability that a system will be in a certain state as a function of that state's energy and the temperature of the system. The probability of a state with energy E is proportional to exp(−E / kBT), where kB is the Boltzmann constant and T the absolute temperature.

Definition

For a system in thermal equilibrium, the probability pi of occupying state i is pi = (1/Z) e−Ei/kBT, where Z is the partition function, the normalising sum over all states. Higher-energy states are exponentially less likely, and the distribution underlies the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution of molecular speeds.

Applications

The Boltzmann distribution describes the populations of atomic energy levels, reaction rates in chemistry, carrier densities in semiconductors, and the atmospheric density profile. In machine learning, the softmax function and Boltzmann machines generalise the same exponential form.

History

Ludwig Boltzmann derived the distribution in the 1860s–1870s during his development of the kinetic theory of gases; Josiah Willard Gibbs gave it a general formulation in his 1902 Elementary Principles in Statistical Mechanics.
