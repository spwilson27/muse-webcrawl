Harness racing

Harness racing is a form of horse racing in which horses race at a specific gait — either the trot or the pace — while pulling a driver in a two-wheeled cart called a sulky (or bike). Races are held on dirt or synthetic oval tracks, most commonly over one mile, and the sport is popular in North America, Australasia, and parts of Europe, especially Scandinavia and France.

Gaits

Trotters move their legs in diagonal pairs, while pacers move both legs on the same side together; pacers usually wear hobbles to help maintain gait. A horse that breaks into a gallop must be steered to the outside and slowed until it resumes its gait, effectively ending its chance of winning.

Breeds and major races

In North America harness racing is dominated by the Standardbred breed, developed from the Thoroughbred foundation sire Messenger and named for its ability to race a mile within a "standard" time. Premier events include the Hambletonian (trotters) and the Little Brown Jug (pacers), part of the Triple Crowns for each gait, and the Breeders Crown series. France's Prix d'Amérique, raced at Vincennes, is the world's richest trotting race; trotting under saddle (trot monté) is also common in France.
