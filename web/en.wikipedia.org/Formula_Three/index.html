Formula Three

Formula Three (F3) is a third-tier class of open-wheel formula racing. Originating in post-war Britain from 500 cc motorcycle-engined cars, it became one of the principal stepping stones to Formula One. Since 2019 the top level has been the FIA Formula 3 Championship, which supports Formula One Grands Prix, with regional series such as the Formula Regional championships below it.

History

Formula Three began in the late 1940s with lightweight cars powered by 500 cc motorcycle engines, built by firms such as Cooper. After periods with 1000 cc and 1600 cc engines, the 2.0-litre formula established in 1974 endured for decades, with chassis dominated by Dallara. National championships in Britain, Germany, Italy and Japan, plus the Macau Grand Prix, became famous proving grounds.

Modern structure

The FIA Formula 3 Championship, created in 2019 from the merger of the GP3 Series and European Formula 3, uses a single Dallara chassis with a 3.4-litre engine of about 380 horsepower. Its champions earn Super Licence points toward Formula One.

Notable graduates

Formula Three champions and race winners who reached Formula One include Ayrton Senna, Mika Häkkinen, Lewis Hamilton, Max Verstappen and Charles Leclerc.
