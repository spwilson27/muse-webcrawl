Antioquia Department

The mountainous terrain of Antioquia in northwestern Colombia

Antioquia is a department of Colombia located in the northwestern part of the country. Its capital is Medellín, Colombia's second-largest city. Antioquia is one of the most populous and economically important departments of Colombia, with a strong industrial base centred on Medellín and significant production of coffee, bananas, flowers, and gold.

Geography

Antioquia covers roughly 63,600 square kilometres. Much of the department is mountainous, crossed by the Western and Central ranges of the Andes, with river valleys such as the Cauca and the Atrato. It has a short coastline on the Caribbean Sea in the Gulf of Urabá. The department is divided into nine subregions and 125 municipalities.

History

The region was inhabited in pre-Columbian times by Indigenous peoples including the Nutabe, Tahamí, and Emberá. Spanish conquistadors arrived in the sixteenth century, and the colonial town of Santa Fe de Antioquia was founded in 1541. After independence, Antioquia was one of the original states of the United States of Colombia; its colonizing farmers—the antioqueños or paisas—spread settlement through the coffee-growing regions of west-central Colombia in the movement known as the Antioquian colonization.

Economy and culture

Medellín is a centre for textiles, finance, energy, and technology, while the surrounding region is known for coffee cultivation, cut flowers exported worldwide, and hydropower. Paisa culture is strongly associated with Antioquia, including its distinctive accent, cuisine (such as the bandeja paisa), and the annual Flower Festival in Medellín.
