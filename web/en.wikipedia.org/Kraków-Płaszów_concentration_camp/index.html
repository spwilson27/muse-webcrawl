Kraków-Płaszów concentration camp

The Kraków-Płaszów concentration camp (German: Konzentrationslager Plaszow) was a Nazi concentration and forced-labour camp in Płaszów, a southern suburb of Kraków, Poland, operating from 1942 to 1945 on the grounds of two Jewish cemeteries.

History

Established as a forced-labour camp in October 1942, it became a concentration camp in January 1944. Its commandant from 1943 was Amon Göth, notorious for brutality and tried and hanged in 1946. At its peak the camp held about 25,000 prisoners, mostly Polish Jews; an estimated 8,000 people were murdered at the camp itself, many at the Hujowa Górka execution site, in addition to those deported to extermination camps.

Prisoners worked in quarries and factories; some were employed by Oskar Schindler, whose enamel factory workers were later transferred to safety. As the Red Army advanced, the camp was liquidated in early 1945. The site is now a memorial area; the camp was depicted in the film Schindler's List (1993).
