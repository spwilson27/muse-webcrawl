Xiphosura is an order of marine chelicerate arthropods that includes the horseshoe crabs. Despite their name, they are not true crabs but are more closely related to arachnids such as spiders and scorpions. Only four species survive today, in the family Limulidae: the Atlantic horseshoe crab (Limulus polyphemus) and three Indo-Pacific species of the genera Tachypleus and Carcinoscorpius.

Anatomy and life history

Xiphosurans have a large horseshoe-shaped prosoma (cephalothorax), a smaller opisthosoma, and a long rigid tail spine, the telson, used to right themselves if overturned. They breathe through book gills and feed on molluscs and worms in shallow coastal waters, migrating inshore in spring to lay eggs on sandy beaches; their eggs are a critical food source for migratory shorebirds such as the red knot.

Evolution and medical importance

The group has an exceptionally long fossil record, extending back about 450 million years, and living forms are often called "living fossils". Their blue, copper-based blood contains Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL), which clots in the presence of bacterial endotoxins and is used worldwide to test the sterility of medicines and medical devices — a practice that has raised conservation concerns for the species harvested for it.
