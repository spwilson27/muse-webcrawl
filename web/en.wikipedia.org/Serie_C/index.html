Serie C

The Serie C is the third-highest division in the Italian football league system, below Serie B and above Serie D. It is organised by the Lega Pro and currently consists of 60 teams divided into three regional groups (Girone A, B, and C) of 20 teams each. The division traces its history to 1935, when a national third tier was first established.

Format

Each group plays a double round-robin. The winner of each group is promoted directly to Serie B, and a fourth promotion place is decided by extensive play-offs involving up to 28 teams from the three groups — one of the largest play-off systems in European football. At the bottom, the last-placed team in each group is relegated directly to Serie D, with further relegations decided by play-outs between the teams just above.

History

A third national division named Serie C was created in 1935 under the Fascist-era reorganisation of Italian football. Over the decades the league was repeatedly restructured: from 1978 it split into Serie C1 and Serie C2, and in 2008 those became Lega Pro Prima Divisione and Seconda Divisione. In 2014 the two were re-merged into a single Lega Pro, which in 2017 resumed the historic name Serie C. Clubs failing financial checks are frequently excluded or refounded, and the league has admitted reserve (Under-23) teams of top clubs, beginning with Juventus Next Gen in 2018, followed by Atalanta U23 and Milan Futuro.

Clubs

Serie C mixes historic clubs that have played in Serie A — such as Catania, Vicenza, Padova, Perugia, and Avellino — with small provincial and island sides, and serves as a proving ground for young players loaned from top-flight teams. Its cup competition is the Coppa Italia Serie C.
