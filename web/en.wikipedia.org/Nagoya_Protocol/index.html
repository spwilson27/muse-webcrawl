Nagoya Protocol

The Nagoya Protocol on Access to Genetic Resources and the Fair and Equitable Sharing of Benefits Arising from their Utilization to the Convention on Biological Diversity is an international agreement which aims at sharing the benefits arising from the utilisation of genetic resources in a fair and equitable way. It is a supplementary agreement to the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD).

Adopted on 29 October 2010 in Nagoya, Aichi Prefecture, Japan, the Protocol entered into force on 12 October 2014, 90 days after the fiftieth instrument of ratification was deposited.

Provisions

The Protocol sets out rules for access to genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge, requiring the prior informed consent of the providing country (and of indigenous and local communities, where applicable) and mutually agreed terms for benefit-sharing. Benefits may be monetary, such as royalties, or non-monetary, such as research collaboration and technology transfer.

Parties must designate national focal points and competent authorities, take measures to ensure that genetic resources used within their jurisdiction were accessed lawfully, and support compliance through checkpoints and the Access and Benefit-sharing Clearing-House, an online information-sharing platform.

Scope and debates

The Protocol applies to genetic resources within the scope of Article 15 of the CBD and to traditional knowledge associated with them; it interacts with specialised regimes such as the International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources and WHO arrangements on pathogen sharing. Discussion continues over its application to digital sequence information (DSI) — genetic data stored in databases — which the CBD's fifteenth conference of the parties addressed in 2022 by agreeing to establish a multilateral benefit-sharing mechanism.
