Punjab (Punjabi: ਪੰਜਾਬ, pronounced [pəɲˈdʒaːb]) is a state in northwestern India, forming part of the wider Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent. It borders the Indian states of Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, and Rajasthan, the union territory of Chandigarh, and the Pakistani province of Punjab to the west. Its capital is Chandigarh, a union territory shared with Haryana, and its largest city is Ludhiana. The name Punjab means "land of five waters", from the Persian panj ("five") and āb ("water"), referring to the five rivers — Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, and Sutlej — that flow through the historic region.

History

The Punjab was a heartland of the Indus Valley Civilisation and later the cradle of Sikhism, founded by Guru Nanak in the 15th century. After centuries of Mughal and Afghan rule, the Sikh Empire of Maharaja Ranjit Singh (1799–1849) unified the region from Lahore until the British annexed it in 1849 following the Anglo-Sikh Wars. Punjab province became a major recruiting ground for the British Indian Army and a focus of canal-colony agricultural settlement.

The Partition of 1947 split Punjab between India and Pakistan amid mass migration and violence in which hundreds of thousands died. In 1966, the Indian Punjab was reorganised on linguistic lines: the Hindi-speaking areas became Haryana and parts of Himachal Pradesh, leaving a Punjabi-speaking, Sikh-majority state. The 1970s–1990s saw the Green Revolution, which made Punjab India's breadbasket, and a violent insurgency linked to the Khalistan separatist movement, including Operation Blue Star (1984) and the assassination of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi.

Geography and economy

Punjab's flat alluvial plains are intensively farmed: the state produces a large share of India's wheat and rice, contributing disproportionately to the central food grain pool, though intensive irrigation has caused alarming groundwater depletion. Industry centres on Ludhiana (textiles, bicycles, machine tools), Jalandhar (sports goods), and Amritsar (tourism around the Golden Temple, Sikhism's holiest shrine). The state has among India's highest per-capita incomes historically, though growth has slowed relative to other states.

Demographics and culture

Punjab is the only Indian state with a Sikh majority (about 58% of the population in 2011), with Hindus forming about 39%. Punjabi, written in the Gurmukhi script, is the official language. Punjabi culture — bhangra music and dance, a celebrated cuisine, and a large global diaspora in Canada, the UK, and the US — is internationally prominent. The state is also renowned for its contribution to Indian sport, especially field hockey and kabaddi, and to the Indian armed forces.
