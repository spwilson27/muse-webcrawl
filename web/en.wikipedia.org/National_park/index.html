National park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Yellowstone, established in 1872, was the world's first national park.

A national park is a nature park designated by a national government for conservation purposes, generally a reserve of natural, semi-natural, or developed land declared or owned by a sovereign state. National parks are typically established to protect outstanding scenery, wildlife, and ecosystems while allowing public recreation under regulation. The IUCN classifies national parks as Category II protected areas: large natural areas set aside to protect large-scale ecological processes and ecosystems characteristic of the area.

History

The idea of preserving wilderness for the public is often credited to the artist George Catlin (1832) and the writer Henry David Thoreau. The United States created the world's first national park, Yellowstone, on 1 March 1872, signed into law by President Ulysses S. Grant, followed by Mackinac (1875, later a state park) and Yosemite (1890). Australia's Royal National Park (1879) and Canada's Banff (1885) followed, and the model spread worldwide. The US National Park Service was created in 1916; today there are more than 6,500 national parks in over 100 countries, covering millions of square kilometres. The largest is Northeast Greenland National Park, at about 972,000 km².

Management and purposes

National parks balance preservation with access: zoning separates wilderness areas from visitor facilities, and activities such as mining, logging, hunting, and development are excluded or tightly controlled. Parks protect biodiversity, watersheds, and geological features, serve as benchmarks for environmental change, and generate substantial tourism revenue, though they also face pressures from overcrowding, invasive species, poaching, funding shortfalls, and climate change — and, historically, the displacement of Indigenous inhabitants, an issue increasingly addressed through co-management.

Famous national parks

  • Yellowstone, Yosemite, Grand Canyon, and Denali (United States)
  • Banff and Jasper (Canada)
  • Serengeti (Tanzania) and Kruger (South Africa)
  • Fiordland (New Zealand)
  • Sagarmatha (Nepal, containing Mount Everest)
  • Galápagos (Ecuador)
  • Torres del Paine (Chile)
