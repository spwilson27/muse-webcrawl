Sphere of influence

In the field of international relations, a sphere of influence is a spatial region or concept division over which a state or organisation has a level of cultural, economic, military, or political exclusivity. While there may be a formal alliance or other treaty obligations between the influenced nation and the greater power, such formal arrangements are not necessary to the existence of a sphere of influence.

Historical examples

The concept was prominent in the era of European colonialism, when powers divided regions such as Africa and China into exclusive spheres. During the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union each maintained spheres of influence — in Western and Eastern Europe respectively — a division dramatised by the Monroe Doctrine's earlier assertion of American primacy in the Western Hemisphere and by Soviet interventions in Hungary (1956) and Czechoslovakia (1968) to preserve its bloc.

Usage

Spheres of influence may be established by treaty, by tacit great-power agreement, or simply by the balance of power and geography. The term is also used metaphorically in business and celestial mechanics (the gravitational sphere of influence of a planet).
