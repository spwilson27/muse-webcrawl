Tensor

In mathematics and physics, a tensor is an algebraic object that describes a multilinear relationship between sets of algebraic objects related to a vector space. Tensors can map between different objects such as vectors and scalars, and even other tensors. A tensor can be represented as an array of numbers whose components transform in a definite way under changes of coordinates. Scalars are tensors of order zero, vectors tensors of order one, and matrices (representing linear maps) tensors of order two; higher-order tensors generalise these.

Definitions

Several equivalent approaches define a tensor. In the component approach, a tensor of type (r, s) is an array of components that transforms under a change of basis with r factors of the transformation matrix and s factors of its inverse — distinguishing contravariant and covariant indices. In the modern coordinate-free approach, a tensor is an element of a tensor product of a vector space and its dual space. Tensor fields assign such an object to each point of a manifold, varying smoothly from point to point.

History

The word tensor was introduced by William Rowan Hamilton in 1846 in a different sense, and the modern usage grew out of the absolute differential calculus developed by Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro and Tullio Levi-Civita around 1890–1901 — "tensor analysis". Albert Einstein's adoption of the notation made tensors famous: general relativity is formulated entirely in the language of tensor fields such as the metric tensor and the Einstein tensor. Woldemar Voigt used the term in its modern sense in 1898 in crystallography.

Applications

Tensors are central wherever anisotropy or multi-directional quantities arise: the Cauchy stress tensor and strain tensor in continuum mechanics, moments of inertia in dynamics, the electromagnetic field tensor in relativity, and diffusion tensors in medical imaging. In computer science and machine learning, "tensor" commonly denotes a multidimensional array, and libraries such as TensorFlow take their name from tensor computations on such arrays.
