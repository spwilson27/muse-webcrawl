Funeral

A funeral is a ceremony connected with the final disposition of a corpse, such as a burial or cremation, with the attendant observances. Funerary customs comprise the complex of beliefs and practices used by a culture to remember and respect the dead, from interment to memorial services and prayers.

History

Burial of the dead with grave goods is among the earliest detectable forms of religious practice, evidenced by Neanderthal and early modern human burials over 100,000 years old, some containing red ochre and flower pollen. Ancient Egyptian funerals involved elaborate mummification and tomb goods; Greek and Roman funerals featured processions, orations, and cremation or inhumation.

Religious funerals

In Christianity, funerals typically include a requiem or funeral Mass or a funeral service with hymns, scripture, and a eulogy, followed by burial or cremation. In Islam, the janaza prayer is performed and burial follows quickly, ideally within 24 hours, with the body washed, shrouded, and buried facing Mecca. Jewish funerals are likewise prompt, with the body watched over (shmira) and simple burial. Hindu funerals usually involve cremation on a pyre or in a crematorium, followed by rites for the soul's journey and later immersion of ashes in a sacred river. Buddhist and Sikh funerals also commonly involve cremation.

Types of service

Modern practice includes traditional funerals, memorial services held without the body present, graveside services, direct cremation or burial without ceremony, state funerals for heads of state, and "green" or natural burials that minimise environmental impact. A growing trend is the pre-planned or pre-paid funeral arranged by the deceased during life.

Funeral industry

In much of the Western world funerals are organised by funeral directors (morticians) operating funeral homes, which prepare the body, supply the coffin or casket, arrange transport such as a hearse, and coordinate with cemeteries, crematoria, and clergy.
