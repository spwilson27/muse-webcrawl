Finger-four

The finger-four formation is a flight formation used by fighter aircraft, named because the positions of the four planes resemble the fingertips of an outstretched hand. It consists of two pairs (elements), each a leader and a wingman, offset so the formation leader sits at the front like the middle fingertip.

History

The formation was developed in the 1930s, notably by the Finnish Air Force and independently by German Luftwaffe pilots including Werner Mölders during the Spanish Civil War, where it formed the basis of the Schwarm. It replaced rigid three-aircraft "vic" formations and proved superior in flexibility and mutual lookout.

Advantages

Finger-four gives each pilot a good field of view, allows the formation to split into two fighting pairs, and concentrates firepower while retaining manoeuvrability. It remained a standard tactical formation through the Second World War and into the jet age.
