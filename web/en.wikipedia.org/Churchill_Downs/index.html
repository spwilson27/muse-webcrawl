Churchill Downs

Churchill Downs is a horse racing complex in Louisville, Kentucky, United States, famed for hosting the annual Kentucky Derby. It officially opened in 1875 and was named for Samuel Churchill, whose family provided the land for the racetrack. The complex is a National Historic Landmark and covers 175 acres.

History

The track was founded by Meriwether Lewis Clark Jr., grandson of explorer William Clark, who raised money in England and France and leased land from his uncles John and Henry Churchill. The first Kentucky Derby and Kentucky Oaks were run in 1875. In 1895 a new grandstand opened crowned by the twin spires designed by Joseph Dominic Baldez, which became the symbol of the track and the Derby.

Facilities

Churchill Downs has a one-mile dirt oval, a seven-furlong turf course, and stabling for more than 1,400 horses. Attendance at the Derby regularly exceeds 150,000, with the infield famously hosting a festival atmosphere. The Kentucky Derby Museum is located on the grounds.

Major races

Besides the Kentucky Derby — the first leg of the American Triple Crown — the track hosts the Kentucky Oaks, the Stephen Foster Handicap, the Clark Handicap, and the Woodford Reserve Turf Classic. It has hosted the Breeders' Cup multiple times.
