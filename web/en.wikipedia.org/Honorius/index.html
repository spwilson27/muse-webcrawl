Honorius

Honorius (9 September 384 – 15 August 423) was Roman emperor in the West from 393 to 423. The younger son of Theodosius I, he became augustus of the western empire as a child on his father's death in 395, while his brother Arcadius ruled the East. His reign saw the sack of Rome by the Visigoths under Alaric in 410 — the first time the city had fallen to a foreign army in nearly 800 years.

Reign

Effective power long rested with the general Stilicho, Honorius's guardian and father-in-law, until his execution in 408 amid court intrigue. Thereafter the court at Ravenna lurched between factions while Visigoths, Vandals, and usurpers such as Constantine III eroded imperial control of Gaul, Spain, and Britain; Roman administration in Britain effectively ended around 410.

Sack of Rome

After failed negotiations and two sieges, Alaric's Visigoths entered Rome on 24 August 410 and plundered it for three days. The event shocked the Mediterranean world and prompted Augustine's City of God. Honorius, safe in marsh-guarded Ravenna, is said by Procopius to have initially misunderstood the news as concerning his pet chicken, also named Roma.

Death and succession

Honorius died childless of dropsy in 423. After the brief usurpation of Joannes, his nephew Valentinian III was installed as western emperor.
