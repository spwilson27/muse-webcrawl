Horticulture is the art, science and business of cultivating plants in gardens and other intensive settings, encompassing fruits, vegetables, herbs, ornamental trees, flowers and turf. The word combines the Latin hortus (garden) with cultura (cultivation). Unlike agronomy, which manages broad-acre field crops, horticulture typically works at smaller, more intensively managed scales.

Branches

Major branches include pomology (fruit growing), olericulture (vegetables), floriculture (flowers and ornamentals), arboriculture (trees and shrubs), viticulture (grapes) and landscape horticulture, which designs and maintains parks and gardens. Postharvest handling, nurseries and seed production are also part of the industry.

Practice and science

Horticulturalists apply knowledge of plant physiology, soil science, breeding and pest management — increasingly including hydroponics, greenhouse climate control, tissue culture and precision irrigation. Urban horticulture and community gardening have grown with interest in local food, green infrastructure and therapeutic gardens.
