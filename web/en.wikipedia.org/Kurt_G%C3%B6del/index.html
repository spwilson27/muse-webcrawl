Kurt Friedrich Gödel (1906–1978) was a logician, mathematician, and philosopher, widely regarded as one of the most significant logicians in history. Born in Brünn (Brno) in Austria-Hungary, he studied at the University of Vienna and emigrated to the United States in 1940, becoming a member of the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.

Incompleteness theorems

In 1931 Gödel published his incompleteness theorems: any consistent formal system rich enough for arithmetic contains true statements it cannot prove, and such a system cannot prove its own consistency. The results ended Hilbert's programme of a complete, consistent foundation for mathematics and had lasting philosophical impact.

Other work

Gödel proved the completeness theorem for first-order logic, showed the relative consistency of the axiom of choice and the continuum hypothesis with set theory, and in 1949 produced rotating-universe solutions of Einstein's field equations (Gödel universes) allowing closed timelike curves. He was a close friend of Albert Einstein at Princeton.
