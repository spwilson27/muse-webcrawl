Protestantism in Ireland

Protestantism is a Christian community on the island of Ireland. In the 2021 census of the Republic of Ireland, 4.2% of the population identified as Protestant or other Christian denominations, while in Northern Ireland Protestants formed a plurality of the population for most of its history. The main Protestant churches in Ireland are the Church of Ireland (Anglican), the Presbyterian Church in Ireland, and the Methodist Church in Ireland, alongside smaller denominations such as Baptists, Brethren, and Pentecostal churches.

History

Protestantism arrived in Ireland with the Tudor conquest and the Reformation of the 16th century, when the Church of Ireland was established as the state church. Large-scale Protestant settlement followed during the Plantations of Ireland, especially the Plantation of Ulster in the early 17th century, which brought Scottish Presbyterians and English Anglicans to the north of the island.

Confessional divisions shaped Irish politics for centuries, through the Williamite War, the Penal Laws against Catholics and Dissenters, and the sectarian tensions of the 19th and 20th centuries. In 1921 the island was partitioned: the predominantly Protestant northeast formed Northern Ireland within the United Kingdom, while the rest became the Irish Free State, later the Republic of Ireland.

Demographics

Protestants are concentrated in Northern Ireland, particularly in counties Antrim, Down, Armagh, and Londonderry/Derry. In the Republic, Protestant communities are found especially in Dublin, Cork, and the border counties of Donegal, Cavan, and Monaghan.
