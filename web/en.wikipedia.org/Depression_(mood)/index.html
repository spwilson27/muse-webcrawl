Depression (mood)

Depression is a mental state of low mood and aversion to activity. It commonly involves feelings of sadness, emptiness, hopelessness, helplessness, worthlessness and loss of interest in activities once enjoyed. It can affect a person's thoughts, behaviour, motivation and sense of well-being, and may be a normal temporary reaction to life events such as loss, or a symptom of physical or psychiatric disorders.

Clinical depression

When low mood is persistent (at least two weeks), pervasive and accompanied by symptoms such as disturbed sleep and appetite, fatigue, poor concentration and low self-esteem, it may be diagnosed as a depressive disorder such as major depressive disorder. Depression is a leading cause of disability worldwide.

Causes and treatment

Contributing factors include genetics, brain chemistry, life stress, chronic illness and certain medications. Treatments include psychotherapy (such as cognitive behavioural therapy), antidepressant medication, exercise and, in severe cases, electroconvulsive therapy. Support from others and maintaining daily routines can also help.
