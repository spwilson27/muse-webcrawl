Somerset


Glastonbury Tor, Somerset

Somerset (archaically Somersetshire) is a ceremonial county in South West England. It is bordered by the Bristol Channel and Gloucestershire to the north, Wiltshire to the east, Dorset to the south-east, and Devon to the south-west. The county town is Taunton, and the city of Bath is a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for its Roman-built baths and Georgian architecture.

Geography

Somerset is a predominantly rural county of rolling hills, including the Mendip Hills, Quantock Hills, and Exmoor — the latter a national park — separated by extensive low-lying wetlands known as the Somerset Levels. The county is known for cider production and for Cheddar cheese, which originates in the village of Cheddar, site of Cheddar Gorge.

History

The name derives from the Old English Sumorsǣte, the people living at or dependent on Somerton. Somerset has been inhabited since Palaeolithic times; Sweet Track, a timber causeway on the Levels, dates to around 3800 BC. The county was part of the Anglo-Saxon kingdom of Wessex, and Alfred the Great took refuge in the marshes at Athelney in 878 before defeating the Danes. During the English Civil War and the Monmouth Rebellion of 1685, which ended at the Battle of Sedgemoor, Somerset saw significant fighting.

Places of interest

Notable places include Wells Cathedral, Glastonbury Abbey and Glastonbury Tor — the site of the Glastonbury Festival — and the Roman Baths in Bath. The county's coastline on the Bristol Channel includes the resort towns of Weston-super-Mare and Minehead.
