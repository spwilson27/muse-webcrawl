CaMKII (calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II) is a serine/threonine-specific protein kinase regulated by the calcium/calmodulin complex. It is one of the most abundant proteins in the brain and plays a central role in synaptic plasticity, learning and memory.

Structure and activation

The enzyme assembles into a large holoenzyme of 12 subunits arranged in two stacked rings. Each subunit has a catalytic domain, a regulatory segment and an association domain. When calcium/calmodulin binds, the regulatory segment is displaced and the kinase is activated. Neighbouring subunits can then phosphorylate each other at threonine 286 (in the α isoform), a reaction called autophosphorylation. This modification traps calmodulin on the enzyme and renders the kinase partially active even after calcium levels fall — a molecular switch frequently proposed as a mechanism of memory storage.

Role in synaptic plasticity

CaMKII is required for the induction of long-term potentiation (LTP) at many synapses. Upon activation it translocates to the postsynaptic density, phosphorylates AMPA-type glutamate receptors and promotes their insertion into the postsynaptic membrane, strengthening the synapse. Genetic disruption of CaMKIIα impairs spatial learning in mice, while mutations in the human genes are associated with intellectual disability.

Other functions

Outside the brain, CaMKII isoforms regulate cardiac muscle contraction, vascular tone, insulin secretion and fertility. In the heart, chronic CaMKII activation contributes to arrhythmia and heart failure, making the enzyme a target of drug-development interest.
