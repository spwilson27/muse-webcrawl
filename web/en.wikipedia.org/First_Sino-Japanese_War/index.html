First Sino-Japanese War

The First Sino-Japanese War (25 July 1894 – 17 April 1895) was fought between Qing China and the Empire of Japan, primarily for influence over Korea. It marked Japan's emergence as a major world power and demonstrated the weakness of the Qing military, accelerating reform and revolutionary movements in China.

Course

Japanese forces defeated the Qing in Korea at Pyongyang, won naval supremacy at the Battle of the Yalu River, and captured Port Arthur and Weihaiwei. The war ended with the Treaty of Shimonoseki, in which China recognised Korean independence, ceded Taiwan, the Penghu Islands and the Liaodong Peninsula to Japan, and paid a large indemnity. Russia, France and Germany, in the Triple Intervention, forced Japan to retrocede Liaodong, embittering Japanese opinion.

Significance

The war shifted the regional balance of power decisively toward Japan and opened a period of intensified foreign encroachment on China known as the "scramble for concessions".
