Carhop

A carhop is a waiter or waitress who brings food to customers in their cars at a drive-in restaurant. The term is a compound of "car" and "hop", as in bellhop or soda jerk-era slang for serving staff; it is said to derive from the practice of hopping onto the running board of a moving car to take an order. Carhops became an iconic feature of American roadside culture in the mid-20th century.

History

Drive-in service began in 1921 at Kirby's Pig Stand in Dallas, Texas, which pioneered curb service. The format spread widely in the 1930s–1950s; chains such as A&W and Sonic became known for carhop service, sometimes delivered on roller skates, with trays that attached to a partly rolled-down car window. Carhops — often young women in distinctive uniforms — were a fixture of the cruising culture of postwar American teenagers.

Decline and revival

From the 1960s, drive-through windows and indoor fast food displaced the carhop at most chains, though Sonic Drive-In retains carhops (many on skates) at thousands of locations. During the COVID-19 pandemic some restaurants revived carhop service as a way to serve customers without indoor dining, and nostalgic independent drive-ins continue the tradition.
