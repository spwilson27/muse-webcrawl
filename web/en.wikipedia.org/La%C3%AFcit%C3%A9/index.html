Laïcité

Laïcité (roughly "secularity") is the French concept of a strict separation between religion and the state, enshrined in the 1905 French law on the Separation of the Churches and the State and in the constitution of the Fifth Republic, which declares France a république laïque.

Principles

Laïcité rests on freedom of conscience, the equality of all citizens before the law regardless of belief, and the neutrality of public institutions: the state neither recognises nor funds any religion (with historical exceptions such as Alsace-Moselle), and public servants must not display religious affiliation on duty.

Debates

The concept has been at the centre of controversies over religious symbols in public schools — leading to the 2004 law banning conspicuous religious symbols for pupils — and the 2010 ban on face coverings. Supporters view laïcité as protecting both the state and religious freedom; critics argue some applications disproportionately affect Muslim citizens.
