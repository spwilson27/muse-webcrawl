ImageNet

ImageNet is a large visual database designed for use in visual object recognition research. Created by researchers led by Fei-Fei Li, it organises images according to the WordNet hierarchy of nouns: more than 14 million images have been hand-annotated to indicate the objects pictured, and bounding boxes are provided for over one million images across more than 20,000 categories.

The ImageNet Challenge

From 2010 to 2017 the project ran the ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge (ILSVRC), an annual competition in which algorithms classified images into 1,000 categories. In 2012 a deep convolutional neural network, AlexNet, developed by Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever and Geoffrey Hinton, won by a wide margin — a result widely credited with sparking the modern deep learning revolution in computer vision and artificial intelligence. Error rates in the challenge fell steadily thereafter, dropping below the estimated human level on the task by the mid-2010s.

Impact and criticism

ImageNet became the standard benchmark and pretraining corpus for computer vision. It has also attracted criticism over labelling errors, biases inherited from WordNet categories, and privacy concerns, leading the project to blur faces and revise problematic categories.
