Ski boot

Ski boots are footwear used in skiing that provide a way to attach the skier to skis using ski bindings. They combine a rigid outer shell that transfers the skier's movements to the ski with a padded inner boot (liner) that provides warmth and fit. Ski boots are sized in Mondopoint, based on foot length in centimetres.

Alpine boots

Modern alpine ski boots have a hard plastic shell, usually of polyurethane, with a hinged cuff, several buckles and a power strap. Their stiffness is described by a flex index; racing boots are much stiffer than recreational boots. Boots traditionally came in rear-entry and front-entry designs; most are now front-entry with an overlapping shell. Their soles conform to ISO standards so that bindings release predictably.

Other disciplines

Nordic (cross-country) boots are much softer, attaching only at the toe to allow a striding motion. Telemark boots have a bellows at the forefoot for flexing. Alpine touring boots are lighter, have a walk mode that unlocks the cuff, rubberised lugged soles for scrambling, and may carry tech inserts for pin bindings. Snowboard boots are a separate category designed for use with snowboard bindings.

History

Early ski boots were leather hiking-style boots. Plastic shells, pioneered in the 1960s by companies such as Lange, revolutionised the sport by giving skiers far greater lateral control.
