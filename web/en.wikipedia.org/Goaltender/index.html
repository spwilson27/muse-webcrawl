Goaltender

In ice hockey, the goaltender (colloquially the goalie, netminder or tendie) is the player responsible for preventing the hockey puck from entering their team's net, thus preventing the opposing team from scoring. The goaltender plays in or near the goal crease and wears specialised equipment including a mask, leg pads, a catching glove and a blocker, and uses a distinctive wide-bladed stick.

Role and privileges

Within the goal crease, the goaltender is protected by special rules: opposing players may not make avoidable contact with the goalie, and goals scored while the goalie is interfered with can be disallowed. The goalie may cover ("freeze") the puck to stop play, and is the only player permitted to handle the puck with the hands in certain ways, though passing it to a teammate with the hand is restricted. In the National Hockey League, a trapezoid behind the net (introduced in 2005) limits where the goalie may play the puck.

Styles and technique

Goaltending styles evolved from the stand-up style of the early game to the butterfly style, popularised by Glenn Hall and refined by Patrick Roy, in which the goalie drops to the knees with pads flared along the ice to seal the bottom of the net, now dominant in combination with hybrid techniques. Key statistics include goals against average (GAA), save percentage, wins and shutouts.

History

Early goaltenders played bare-faced; Clint Benedict pioneered a leather mask in 1930, but it was Jacques Plante who made the mask standard after 1959, later also transforming the position by roaming to play the puck. Other innovators include Ken Dryden, Dominik Hašek, known for an acrobatic "flopper" style, and Martin Brodeur, the NHL's all-time leader in wins and shutouts and a noted puck-handler. Teams may "pull the goalie" late in a game, replacing the goaltender with an extra attacker when trailing.
