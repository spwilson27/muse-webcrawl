Harvard Square is a triangular plaza at the intersection of Massachusetts Avenue, Brattle Street, and John F. Kennedy Street in Cambridge, Massachusetts, United States, and the historic center of the city. The Square is adjacent to Harvard Yard, the historic heart of Harvard University. It functions as a commercial district, a major MBTA transit hub (Harvard station on the Red Line), and a gathering place known for street performers, chess players, bookstores and cafés.

History

The area was the center of the settlement of Newtowne, founded in 1630–31; the town was renamed Cambridge in 1638 when Harvard College, the oldest institution of higher education in the United States, was established nearby. Roads radiating from the Square follow 17th-century paths to surrounding towns. In the 19th and 20th centuries the Square grew into a dense retail and publishing district serving the university.

Features

Landmarks include the Harvard Coop (founded 1882), the Harvard Book Store (founded 1932), the Brattle Theatre (a repertory cinema since 1953), and the landmark Out of Town News kiosk (the Sheldon Cohen Island), listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Brattle Street — "Tory Row" — preserves colonial-era mansions, including the Longfellow House–Washington's Headquarters National Historic Site. Harvard Square is known for its chess matches in front of the Coop, its street musicians (singer-songwriter Tracy Chapman performed there as a student), and annual events such as the MayFair and Oktoberfest. The Square has been the subject of ongoing debate over chain stores versus independent businesses and the gentrification of its retail mix.
