Skopje is the capital and largest city of North Macedonia, home to roughly a quarter of the country's population. It lies in the north of the country on the upper Vardar River, in a valley ringed by mountains, and has been inhabited since at least 4000 BC.

History

The city began as the Roman settlement of Scupi. It was ruled in turn by the Byzantine Empire, the Bulgarian and Serbian empires — under Stefan Dušan it briefly served as a capital — and, from the late 14th century, by the Ottoman Empire for more than 500 years. The Old Bazaar and the Stone Bridge across the Vardar date from the Ottoman period. After the Balkan Wars Skopje passed to Serbia and later Yugoslavia.

The 1963 earthquake

On 26 July 1963 a powerful earthquake destroyed about 80 per cent of the city and killed more than 1,000 people. Skopje was rebuilt with extensive international aid — it became known as the "city of international solidarity" — following a master plan influenced by the Japanese architect Kenzō Tange.

Modern city

Skopje is North Macedonia's political, economic and cultural centre. Landmarks include the Kale Fortress, the Memorial House of Mother Teresa — who was born in the city in 1910 — and Mount Vodno, crowned by the Millennium Cross. The controversial "Skopje 2014" project added many neoclassical buildings and monumental statues to the centre, reshaping its appearance and sparking debate about cost and national identity.
