Israeli–Palestinian conflict

The Israeli–Palestinian conflict is an ongoing political and armed conflict between Israel and Palestinians over land, sovereignty, and national self-determination in the territory of former Mandatory Palestine. It is part of the wider Arab–Israeli conflict and is widely regarded as one of the world's most intractable disputes, with deep religious, historical, and political dimensions.

Origins

The conflict's roots lie in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, with the rise of Zionism and Arab nationalism under Ottoman rule. Under the British Mandate (1920–1948) tensions grew between Jewish immigrants and the Arab population. The UN Partition Plan of 1947 proposed two states; its rejection by Arab leaders was followed by the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, the establishment of Israel, and the flight or expulsion of some 700,000 Palestinians — commemorated by Palestinians as the Nakba.

Occupation and uprisings

In the Six-Day War of 1967 Israel occupied the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and the Gaza Strip. The First Intifada (1987–1993) led to the Oslo Accords, in which Israel and the PLO mutually recognized each other and established the Palestinian Authority, but final-status issues were deferred. The Second Intifada (2000–2005) followed the failure of the Camp David Summit. In 2007 Hamas took control of Gaza, leading to a blockade and repeated wars between Israel and Hamas, most devastatingly beginning with the Hamas-led attack on Israel of 7 October 2023 and Israel's subsequent large-scale military campaign in Gaza.

Core issues and peace efforts

Proposed resolutions, most commonly the two-state solution, have turned on borders along the 1967 lines, the status of Jerusalem, Israeli settlements in the West Bank, the right of return of Palestinian refugees, and security arrangements. Despite decades of mediation by the United States, the UN, and others, no final agreement has been reached.
