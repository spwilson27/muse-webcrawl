Red-green color blindness

Red-green color blindness is the most common form of colour vision deficiency, affecting the ability to distinguish red and green hues. It is usually inherited as an X-linked recessive trait via opsin genes on the X chromosome, affecting about 8% of males and 0.5% of females of Northern European ancestry.

Red-green colour blindness
Types: Protan, Deutan
Inheritance: X-linked recessive
Diagnosis: Ishihara test

Types

Protan defects affect the red (L) cone pigment — protanopia (absent) and protanomaly (shifted). Deutan defects affect the green (M) pigment — deuteranopia and deuteranomaly, the latter being the single most common colour vision defect.

Causes and diagnosis

Unequal crossing over between the adjacent OPN1LW and OPN1MW genes creates hybrid pigments. Diagnosis uses Ishihara plates and anomaloscopes. There is no cure; tinted lenses and apps can aid discrimination, and gene therapy has been demonstrated in primates.
