Allier

Allier (French: [a.lje]; Occitan: Alèir) is a department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of central France. It is named after the river Allier, which flows through it. Its prefecture is Moulins, and its population was about 334,000 in the early 2020s. The department corresponds largely to the former province of Bourbonnais, the historic seat of the dukes of Bourbon.

History

Allier was one of the original 83 departments created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790. During the Second World War, the spa town of Vichy, in the south of the department, became the seat of the collaborationist French State government headed by Marshal Philippe Pétain from 1940 to 1944, giving the regime the name “Vichy France”.

Geography

The department is crossed by the Loire and Allier rivers. The north is part of the Bourbonnais bocage, the west includes the Combrailles uplands, and the south rises toward the Montagne Bourbonnaise. Its largest towns are Montluçon, Vichy, and Moulins.

Economy and tourism

The economy is mainly agricultural, with metallurgy and logistics around Montluçon and spa tourism around Vichy, whose Great Spa Towns of Europe listing is recognized by UNESCO. The Pal and other local attractions draw visitors from across France.
