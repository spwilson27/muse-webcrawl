Black Friday (shopping)

Black Friday is the Friday after Thanksgiving in the United States. It traditionally marks the start of the Christmas shopping season and is widely regarded as the busiest shopping day of the year, with retailers offering heavily promoted sales, early store hours, and "doorbuster" deals. The term originated in Philadelphia in the 1950s–60s to describe the chaos of crowds and traffic on that day; retailers later promoted a more positive association with accounts moving "into the black" (turning a profit).

History

The use of Thanksgiving parades to launch Christmas shopping dates to the early twentieth century. By the 1980s, Black Friday had become a nationwide retail phenomenon, and in the 2000s and 2010s stores opened progressively earlier—some on Thanksgiving evening itself. The rise of e-commerce created a parallel event, Cyber Monday, and extended sales periods across the whole week. The tradition has spread to many other countries, often promoted by multinational retailers.

Violence and criticism

Black Friday has drawn criticism for consumerism, dangerous crowd crushes, and incidents of violence as shoppers compete for limited discounted goods, including a 2008 incident in which a store employee was trampled to death. Others criticize the erosion of the Thanksgiving holiday for retail workers.

Economic significance

Retail analysts and the media closely watch Black Friday spending as a predictor of the holiday season and overall retail performance, though its importance has diminished as sales spread across longer periods and online channels.
