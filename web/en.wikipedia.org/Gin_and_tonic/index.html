Gin and tonic

A classic gin and tonic

A gin and tonic is a highball cocktail made with gin and tonic water poured over a large amount of ice, usually garnished with a slice or wedge of lime or lemon. The ratio of gin to tonic varies according to taste, the strength of the gin, and other mixers being added; a common proportion is between 1:1 and 1:3.

History

The cocktail was popularised in the 19th century among British officers and administrators in India. Tonic water contains quinine, which was used to prevent and treat malaria; because the tonic water of the period was extremely bitter, it was combined with gin, sugar and citrus to make it palatable. As modern tonic waters contain far less quinine, the drink is now far less bitter. The combination became an emblematic drink of the British Raj and spread worldwide.

Preparation and variations

The drink is traditionally served in a highball glass over ice with a wheel of lime. In Spain, the gin-tonic developed into an elaborate serve in a large copa (balloon) glass, with aromatic garnishes such as juniper berries, herbs or peppercorns chosen to complement the botanicals of a particular gin. The craft-gin revival of the 21st century greatly expanded the variety of gins and premium tonic waters on the market. A gin and tonic also fluoresces under ultraviolet light owing to the quinine.
