Italy, officially the Italian Republic, is a country in Southern and Western Europe. It consists of a long peninsula shaped like a boot, bordered by the Alps to the north and surrounded by the Mediterranean Sea, together with the islands of Sicily and Sardinia and many smaller islands. Italy shares land borders with France, Switzerland, Austria and Slovenia, and entirely encloses the microstates of San Marino and Vatican City.

History

The Italian peninsula was the heartland of ancient Rome, whose republic and empire shaped law, engineering, language and governance across Europe. After the fall of the Western Roman Empire, Italy fragmented into city-states and kingdoms, yet flourished culturally during the Renaissance, which began in Florence and gave the world artists such as Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo. Unification (the Risorgimento) was completed in 1861 under the Kingdom of Italy, proclaimed by Victor Emmanuel II with Giuseppe Garibaldi and Count Cavour as key figures. Italy became a republic in 1946 after a referendum abolishing the monarchy.

Geography and climate

The Apennine Mountains run the length of the peninsula; the Po Valley in the north is the country's main agricultural and industrial plain. Italy has active volcanoes, including Vesuvius near Naples and Etna in Sicily. The climate ranges from alpine in the north to Mediterranean along the coasts and in the south.

Economy and culture

Italy is a founding member of the European Union and the eurozone, with a highly developed economy strong in manufacturing, fashion, automotive engineering, food and tourism. It has the most UNESCO World Heritage Sites of any country. Italian cuisine, art, opera and cinema are influential worldwide, and Italian is spoken by tens of millions in a global diaspora.
