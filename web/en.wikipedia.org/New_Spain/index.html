New Spain

New Spain, officially the Viceroyalty of New Spain, was an integral territorial entity of the Spanish Empire, established by the Spanish Crown during the Spanish colonisation of the Americas and having its capital in Mexico City. It existed from 1521, after the fall of Tenochtitlan, until Mexican independence in 1821.

Territory

At its greatest extent it encompassed Mexico, Central America, much of what is now the southwestern and central United States, Florida, the Spanish Caribbean, Venezuela's coast at times, and the Philippines — administered through the Captaincy General of the Philippines — making it the largest viceroyalty of the empire.

Society and economy

Silver mining at Zacatecas, Guanajuato and Potosí (in Peru) underpinned the imperial economy, worked through the encomienda and repartimiento systems imposed on indigenous peoples, whose population collapsed from disease. A hierarchical casta society of peninsulares, creoles, mestizos and indigenous peoples developed, administered from Mexico City and shaped by the Catholic Church.
