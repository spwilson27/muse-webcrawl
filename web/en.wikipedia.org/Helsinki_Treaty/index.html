Helsinki Treaty

Treaty of Cooperation between Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden
Signed23 March 1962, Helsinki
Entered into force1 July 1962
PartiesDenmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden
SubjectNordic cooperation

The Helsinki Treaty, formally the Treaty of Cooperation between Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden, is the foundational agreement governing official cooperation among the Nordic countries. Signed in Helsinki on 23 March 1962 and in force from 1 July 1962, it provides the legal framework for the work of the Nordic Council and the Nordic Council of Ministers. The date of its signing, 23 March, is celebrated annually as Nordic Day.

Contents

The treaty commits the five countries to cooperate across a wide range of fields: legal affairs and the harmonisation of legislation; culture, education and research; social policy and labour markets; transport and communications; economic policy; and environmental protection. It underpins well-known Nordic arrangements such as the common labour market (established 1954), the Nordic Passport Union, which abolished passport controls between the member states, and conventions on social security and mutual assistance.

Revisions

The treaty has been amended several times, notably in 1971, when the Nordic Council of Ministers was created as the intergovernmental forum of cooperation, and in connection with later changes to the Nordic institutions. Proposals to update the treaty in the 2020s have addressed new areas such as security and defence cooperation, crisis preparedness and the position of the autonomous territories — Greenland, the Faroe Islands and Åland — within the cooperation framework.

Significance

Together with the Nordic Council (founded 1952), the Helsinki Treaty institutionalised one of the world's oldest and most extensive systems of regional cooperation. Its preamble declares the aim of promoting close relations between the Nordic peoples in culture as well as in legal and social matters, and of working toward uniform regulation wherever possible.
