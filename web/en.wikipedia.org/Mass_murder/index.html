Mass murder
Definition: Murder of multiple people in a single event or short period
FBI threshold (older): four or more victims in one incident
Related terms: Mass shooting, spree killing, serial killing

Mass murder is the act of killing a number of people, typically simultaneously or over a relatively short period of time and in close geographic proximity. Definitions vary by jurisdiction and agency: the United States FBI historically defined mass murder as the killing of four or more people in a single incident with no cooling-off period, while some more recent statutes use a threshold of three victims. Mass murder may be committed by individuals or by organizations, and in criminology it is distinguished from spree killing (multiple locations over a longer period with no cooling-off interval by traditional definitions) and serial killing (killings separated by cooling-off periods).

Motives and perpetrators

Studies of mass murderers identify common factors including perceived grievances, revenge, notoriety-seeking, ideological extremism, severe mental disturbance in some cases, and personal crises such as job loss or relationship breakdown. A large share of incidents involve firearms, though vehicles, explosives, arson and bladed weapons are also used. Most perpetrators are male, and many die at the scene, often by suicide or by police intervention.

Mass shootings and public policy

Mass shootings — incidents in which several people are shot — receive intense media attention and feature prominently in debates over gun control, mental-health policy and media coverage of offenders, including concerns about copycat effects. Researchers debate whether coverage should minimize naming perpetrators ("No Notoriety" campaigns) to reduce contagion. At the extreme end, state-perpetrated mass murders, such as genocides and wartime atrocities, are studied separately as mass killings under international law.
