Annan Plan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Annan Plan was a United Nations proposal to reunify Cyprus, divided since 1974, as a single state — the United Cyprus Republic — organised as a federation of two constituent states, one Greek Cypriot and one Turkish Cypriot. It was named after UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan and was finalised on 31 March 2004 after years of negotiations.

Provisions

The plan provided for a weak federal government with a rotating presidential council, return of some territory from the Turkish Cypriot to the Greek Cypriot side (including the return of Varosha and Morphou), phased withdrawal of Greek and Turkish troops, and complex arrangements on property restitution and the rights of settlers. Cyprus would join the European Union as a whole.

Referendums

On 24 April 2004 the plan was put to simultaneous referendums. Turkish Cypriots approved it by about 65%, but Greek Cypriots rejected it by about 76%, after President Tassos Papadopoulos campaigned for a "resounding no". The plan therefore lapsed, and the Republic of Cyprus joined the EU a week later representing the whole island in law but only the south in practice. The Annan Plan remains a reference point in later reunification talks.
