Sopoćani

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Sopoćani monastery church

The Sopoćani Monastery (Serbian: Сопоћани), an endowment of King Stefan Uroš I of Serbia, was built in the second half of the 13th century near the source of the Raška River in the region of Ras, the centre of the Serbian medieval state. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, listed as part of the Stari Ras and Sopoćani complex.

Architecture and frescoes

The church, dedicated to the Holy Trinity, combines Romanesque and Byzantine elements, characteristic of the Raška school of architecture. Its frescoes, painted around 1263–1268, are considered among the finest achievements of Byzantine and Serbian medieval painting; the Dormition of the Mother of God on the west wall of the nave is famous for its monumental, classically balanced composition and luminous colour.

History

The monastery flourished for centuries but was burned and abandoned after an Ottoman incursion in 1689, remaining roofless for some two hundred years. Conservation and restoration were carried out in the 20th century, and a monastic community was re-established.
