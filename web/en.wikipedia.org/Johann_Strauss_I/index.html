Johann Strauss I

[Image placeholder: portrait of Johann Strauss I]

Johann Baptist Strauss I (German: [ˈjoːhan bapˈtɪst ʃtʁaʊs]; 14 March 1804 – 25 September 1849) was an Austrian composer of the Romantic period. He was famous for his light music, namely waltzes, and popularised the form alongside Josef Lanner, especially throughout the Viennese Court. Strauss is best known for composing the "Radetzky March".

Born in Vienna, Strauss lost both parents young and learned the viola while apprenticed to a bookbinder. He played in the orchestras of Michael Pamer and Josef Lanner before forming his own orchestra in 1825. His ensemble toured widely in Europe, and Strauss became known as the "father of the waltz", elevating the dance from peasant origins to entertainment fit for royalty.

Family and rivalry

Strauss was the father of three composing sons — Johann II, Josef, and Eduard — the Strauss musical dynasty. Ironically, he opposed his sons pursuing musical careers and was furious when Johann II formed his own rival orchestra in 1844; the conflict divided Vienna's dance-music world between father and son camps. Strauss died of scarlet fever in Vienna in 1849, after which Johann II took over and united the orchestras.

Works

Strauss composed more than 250 works, including over 150 waltzes, plus polkas, gallops, marches, and quadrilles. His most enduring piece, the "Radetzky March" (Op. 228, 1848), written in honour of Field Marshal Joseph Radetzky, remains a fixture of New Year's concerts and is traditionally accompanied by audience clapping led by the conductor.
