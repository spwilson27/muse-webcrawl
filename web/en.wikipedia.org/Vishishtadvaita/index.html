Vishishtadvaita

Ramanuja (1017–1137), systematizer of Vishishtadvaita

School: Vedanta

Founder: Ramanuja

Core text: Sri Bhasya

Vishishtadvaita (Sanskrit: विशिष्टाद्वैत, "qualified non-dualism") is a school of Vedanta philosophy that teaches that the supreme reality, Brahman, exists as one but with attributes — qualified by sentient souls and insentient matter, which form the "body" of God. It is the main philosophy of Sri Vaishnavism and identifies the supreme being with Vishnu (Narayana).

Philosophy

Ramanuja (1017–1137) systematized Vishishtadvaita in works such as the Sri Bhasya, his commentary on the Brahma Sutras. Against Shankara's Advaita, which treats the world of plurality as ultimately illusory, Ramanuja held that individual selves (chit) and the material world (achit) are real and eternally distinct, yet inseparably dependent on Brahman as the body depends on the soul. Brahman alone exists independently; everything else exists as his attribute or mode (prakara).

Liberation

The goal of human life is moksha — release from samsara and eternal service to God in Vaikuntha. This is attained through bhakti (devotion), karma and jnana yoga, and above all through prapatti, complete self-surrender to God's grace. Predecessors of Ramanuja include Nathamuni and Yamunacharya of the Alvars' devotional tradition.

Later developments

After Ramanuja, Sri Vaishnavism split into the Vadakalai (northern) and Tenkalai (southern) schools, differing over the nature of grace and surrender. Vedanta Desika and Pillai Lokacharya were leading later teachers.
