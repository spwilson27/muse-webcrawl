Stochastic differential equation

A stochastic differential equation (SDE) is a differential equation in which one or more of the terms is a stochastic process, resulting in a solution which is also a stochastic process. SDEs have many applications throughout pure mathematics and are used to model various behaviours of stochastic models such as stock prices or physical systems subject to thermal fluctuations.

Formulation

A common form of an SDE is dXt = μ(Xt, t) dt + σ(Xt, t) dWt, where Wt is a Wiener process (Brownian motion), μ is the drift coefficient, and σ is the diffusion coefficient. SDEs are typically interpreted as integral equations involving the Itô integral or the Stratonovich integral, which differ because of the quadratic variation of Brownian motion.

Itô calculus

Itô's lemma is the fundamental tool for differentiating functions of stochastic processes. For a function f(Xt, t), it gives df = (∂f/∂t + μ ∂f/∂x + ½σ² ∂²f/∂x²) dt + σ ∂f/∂x dWt, the extra second-order term distinguishing it from ordinary chain-rule calculus.

Examples and applications

Famous examples include the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process for mean-reverting behaviour and geometric Brownian motion, used in the Black–Scholes model of option pricing. SDEs are used in mathematical finance, physics, biology, and engineering. Numerical methods such as the Euler–Maruyama method approximate their solutions.
