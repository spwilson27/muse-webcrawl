The Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, officially the Treaty of Non-Aggression between Germany and the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics, was a neutrality pact signed on 23 August 1939 by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union. It is named after its signatories, Soviet foreign minister Vyacheslav Molotov and German foreign minister Joachim von Ribbentrop.

Terms

Publicly, each party promised neutrality if the other were attacked by a third party, and neither would join any grouping of powers directed against the other. A secret additional protocol divided Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of influence, covering Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Finland and Romania.

Consequences

On 1 September 1939 Germany invaded Poland, starting the Second World War in Europe; the Soviet Union invaded eastern Poland on 17 September. Under the protocol and a follow-up Boundary and Friendship Treaty, the two powers partitioned Poland, and the USSR went on to occupy the Baltic states, attack Finland in the Winter War, and annex Bessarabia.

Termination

The pact ended on 22 June 1941 when Germany launched Operation Barbarossa, the invasion of the Soviet Union. The existence of the secret protocol was denied by the Soviet Union until 1989.
