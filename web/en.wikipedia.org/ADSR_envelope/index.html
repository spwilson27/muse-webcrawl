ADSR envelope

The ADSR envelope is a fundamental component of many synthesisers and electronic instruments, used to control a parameter — most often amplitude — over the duration of a note. ADSR stands for the envelope's four stages: attack, decay, sustain, and release.

Stages

  • Attack — the time taken to rise from zero to the peak level after the key is pressed.
  • Decay — the time taken to fall from the peak to the sustain level.
  • Sustain — the level held while the key remains pressed (a level, not a time).
  • Release — the time taken to fade from the sustain level to zero after the key is released.

Use

By adjusting the four parameters, musicians shape sounds from percussive plucks (fast attack, no sustain) to slow pads (long attack and release). ADSR envelopes can also modulate filter cutoff or pitch. Analogue envelope generators became standard on synthesisers in the 1960s, and the concept persists in virtually all software synthesisers today.
