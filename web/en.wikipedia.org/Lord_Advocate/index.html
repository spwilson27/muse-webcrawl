The Lord Advocate, also known as His Majesty's Advocate, is the principal legal adviser to the Scottish Government and the Crown in Scotland for civil and criminal matters that fall within the devolved powers of the Scottish Parliament. The Lord Advocate is the head of the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service, Scotland's public prosecution service, and as such is responsible for the investigation and prosecution of crime in Scotland.

History

The office dates to the late fifteenth century; the first recorded holder was John Ross of Montgrenan, appointed in 1483. Over the centuries the Lord Advocate became the dominant figure in Scottish legal and political life, effectively governing Scotland in the period after the Union of 1707 when no Secretary of State for Scotland existed. The office was placed on a statutory devolved footing by the Scotland Act 1998.

Functions

The Lord Advocate is a member of the Scottish Government, appointed by the monarch on the recommendation of the First Minister with the agreement of the Scottish Parliament. The office-holder leads the prosecution system through the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service, represents the Scottish Government in litigation, and advises ministers on Scots law. By convention the Lord Advocate is not normally a member of the Scottish Cabinet but attends when legal business is discussed.
