Delta blues

Delta blues is one of the earliest styles of blues music, originating in the Mississippi Delta — the flat, fertile floodplain between the Mississippi and Yazoo rivers in the American South. It is characterised by raw vocals, rhythmic acoustic guitar, slide playing and harmonica.

Characteristics and artists

Delta blues songs grew out of field hollers, work songs and spirituals in the sharecropping communities of the Delta, performed at house parties, juke joints and plantation dances. Pioneering artists include Charley Patton, often called the founder of the Delta blues; Son House; and Robert Johnson, whose 1936–37 recordings and the legend that he sold his soul at a crossroads made him the genre's most mythologised figure. Other key names are Skip James, Tommy Johnson, Bukka White and Mississippi John Hurt.

Influence

As African Americans migrated north during the Great Migration, Delta musicians such as Muddy Waters and Howlin' Wolf electrified the style in Chicago, creating Chicago blues, a direct ancestor of rock and roll. Delta blues was extensively rediscovered during the 1960s folk and blues revival and remains foundational to American popular music.
