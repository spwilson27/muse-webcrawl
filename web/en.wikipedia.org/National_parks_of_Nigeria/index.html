National parks of Nigeria

Nigeria's national parks are managed by the Nigeria National Park Service, established by decree in 1991. The system comprises eight national parks covering roughly 3 percent of the country's total land area, protecting ecosystems that range from Sahel savanna in the north to lowland rainforest in the south.

The parks

The parks include Kainji Lake, Nigeria's oldest national park (established 1979), encompassing sectors around the Kainji reservoir; Yankari, famous for its elephants, Wikki Warm Springs, and birdlife; Cross River, a rainforest park of exceptional primate diversity and an important refuge for the Cross River gorilla; Gashaka Gumti, Nigeria's largest national park, on the border with Cameroon; Okomu, a small rainforest park protecting forest elephants and white-throated monkeys; Kamuku and Chad Basin in the northern savannas; and Old Oyo, which preserves the ruins of the former Oyo Empire capital.

Conservation challenges

Nigerian national parks face pressure from poaching, logging, encroachment, and insecurity, and conservation organizations work with the park service on ranger training and anti-poaching efforts, particularly in Cross River and Gashaka Gumti.
