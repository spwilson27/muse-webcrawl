Reichstag (Weimar Republic)

The Reichstag met in the Reichstag building in Berlin from 1919 to 1933.

The Reichstag was the lower house of the legislature of the Weimar Republic (1919–1933), the parliamentary chamber elected by universal suffrage under a system of proportional representation. Together with the Reichsrat, which represented the federal states (Länder), it formed the Reich legislature under the Weimar Constitution of 11 August 1919.

Powers

The Reichstag enacted federal law, approved the budget, and could withdraw confidence from the Chancellor and government, who were responsible both to it and to the Reich President. Deputies were elected for four-year terms, with one seat for roughly every 60,000 votes; women voted and sat in the Reichstag from its first election in January 1919. The chamber could be dissolved by the President, a power used repeatedly in the Republic's final crisis years.

Political development

Early Reichstags were dominated by the "Weimar coalition" of Social Democrats, the Centre Party and the German Democratic Party. Proportional representation produced fragmented multi-party parliaments and unstable coalition ministries — more than twenty cabinets in fourteen years. After 1930, Chancellor Heinrich Brüning governed largely through President Hindenburg's emergency decrees under Article 48 because no stable Reichstag majority could be formed. The elections of July and November 1932 made the Nazi Party the largest single party, though without a majority.

End

Following the Reichstag fire of 27 February 1933, the chamber — meeting in the Kroll Opera House — passed the Enabling Act of 23 March 1933, effectively surrendering legislative power to Hitler's government. The Reichstag continued to exist formally throughout the Nazi period but met only rarely, as a rubber-stamp body of uniformed party nominees.
