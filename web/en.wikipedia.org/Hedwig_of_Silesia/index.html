Hedwig of Silesia

Hedwig of Silesia (Polish: Jadwiga Śląska; German: Heilige Hedwig von Andechs; 1174 – 15 October 1243) was a Duchess consort of Silesia and Greater Poland, and is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church. Born at Andechs Castle in Bavaria to Count Berthold IV of Andechs, she was married at about the age of twelve to Henry I the Bearded, Duke of Silesia, of the Polish Piast dynasty.

Life

Hedwig and Henry had seven children, among them Henry II the Pious. She was renowned for her piety, austerity, and charity: she founded hospitals, cared for the sick and poor, and walked barefoot even in winter. In 1202 the couple founded the Cistercian convent at Trzebnica, the first women's religious house in Silesia, where Hedwig withdrew after her husband's death in 1238. She endured the deaths of most of her children, including Henry II, who was killed fighting the Mongols at the Battle of Legnica in 1241.

Veneration

Hedwig died at Trzebnica in 1243 and was canonised by Pope Clement IV in 1267. She is the patron saint of Silesia, of brides, widows, and exiles. Her shrine at Trzebnica remains a pilgrimage site, and she is remembered as a bridge figure between Polish and German cultures; her niece was Saint Elizabeth of Hungary. The illustrated Vita of Saint Hedwig (the Lubin Codex, c. 1353) is a notable work of Silesian Gothic art.
