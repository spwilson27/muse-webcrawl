Trade paperback (comics)

Trade paperback collections

In comics in the United States, a trade paperback (often shortened to trade or TPB) is a collection of stories originally published in single-issue comic books, reprinted in book format under a single cover. A trade paperback typically collects a complete miniseries or a single story arc from an ongoing series — commonly four to twelve issues — and is printed on higher-quality paper than the original issues, bound with a flexible card cover.

History

Reprint collections existed for decades, but the format took off in the 1980s with landmark collections such as The Dark Knight Returns and Watchmen, which remained continuously in print and reached bookstore audiences beyond comics shops. Through the 1990s and 2000s trades became central to publishers' economics: single issues increasingly functioned as serial openings for the collected edition, which could be sold indefinitely through the book trade and libraries.

Distinction from graphic novels

A trade paperback reprints material first serialized elsewhere, whereas a graphic novel, strictly defined, is original in book form — though in common usage "graphic novel" is applied to both. Related formats include the hardcover collection, the oversized "absolute" or "omnibus" edition gathering many issues, and the digest-sized volume, including translated manga tankōbon.

Extras

Collections frequently add material absent from the original issues: author introductions, sketchbook pages, character designs, and supplementary essays, intended to make the book the definitive edition of the story.
