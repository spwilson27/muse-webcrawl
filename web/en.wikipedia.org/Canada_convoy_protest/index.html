Canada convoy protest

The Canada convoy protest, known by participants as the Freedom Convoy (French: Convoi de la liberté), was a series of protests and blockades in Canada in January and February 2022. Sparked by a federal requirement that cross-border truck drivers be vaccinated against COVID-19, convoys converged on Ottawa, where demonstrators occupied streets around Parliament Hill for about three weeks, while related blockades disrupted border crossings including the Ambassador Bridge at Windsor and the crossing at Coutts, Alberta.

Background

On 15 January 2022 a Canadian rule took effect requiring unvaccinated truckers crossing from the United States to quarantine; the United States imposed a similar rule days later. Although most Canadian truckers were vaccinated, organizers framed the mandate as the catalyst for a wider protest against pandemic restrictions generally.

Occupation of Ottawa

Convoys arrived in Ottawa on 28–29 January. Hundreds of vehicles blocked Wellington Street and surrounding areas; protesters demanded the end of all mandates and, in some statements, the replacement of the government. Residents complained of noise, harassment, and closures. The protest drew international attention and copycat movements abroad, and fundraising campaigns raised millions of dollars, some of which were frozen or blocked.

Emergencies Act and clearance

On 14 February 2022, Prime Minister Justin Trudeau invoked the Emergencies Act for the first time in its history, granting authorities powers to clear blockades, compel tow services, and freeze financial accounts. Police operations from 18 to 20 February cleared the Ottawa occupation with hundreds of arrests; the Ambassador Bridge blockade had been cleared on 13 February. The emergency declaration was revoked on 23 February.

Aftermath

The Public Order Emergency Commission (Rouleau Commission), reporting in February 2023, concluded that the legal threshold for invoking the Emergencies Act had been met, while criticizing failures of policing and intergovernmental coordination. Leading organizers faced criminal charges including mischief and counselling offences. A federal court ruling in January 2024 later held the invocation unreasonable, a decision subject to appeal.
