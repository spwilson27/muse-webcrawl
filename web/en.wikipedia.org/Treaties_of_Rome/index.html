Treaties of Rome

The Treaties of Rome, or the Rome Treaties, are two treaties signed on 25 March 1957 in Rome, Italy, by Belgium, France, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, and West Germany, which entered into force on 1 January 1958. One treaty established the European Economic Community (EEC); the other established the European Atomic Energy Community (EAEC, or Euratom). The signing took place at the Palazzo dei Conservatori on the Capitoline Hill.

Contents

The Treaty establishing the European Economic Community set out to create a common market through the elimination of customs duties and quantitative restrictions, a common agricultural policy, free movement of goods, persons, services, and capital, and common policies on transport and competition. It created the EEC's institutions, including the Council of Ministers, the Commission, the Parliamentary Assembly, and the Court of Justice. The Euratom Treaty aimed to coordinate the member states' research and development programmes for the peaceful use of nuclear energy.

Legacy

The EEC Treaty, later renamed the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, remains one of the two primary treaties of the European Union. The 50th anniversary of the signing was marked in 2007 by the Berlin Declaration.
