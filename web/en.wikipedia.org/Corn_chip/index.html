Corn chip

A corn chip is a snack food made from cornmeal fried in oil or baked, usually in the shape of a small noodle or scoop. Corn chips are thick, rigid and crunchy, and are often eaten with dips such as salsa, guacamole or cheese dip. They differ from tortilla chips in that corn chips are made directly from cornmeal dough shaped and fried, while tortilla chips are cut from corn tortillas.

History

The corn chip was popularised in the United States in the 1930s. Charles Elmer Doolin purchased a recipe and equipment in San Antonio, Texas, and began selling Fritos corn chips in 1932, founding what became the Frito Company. Fritos later merged with H.W. Lay & Company to form Frito-Lay in 1961. Doritos, a flavoured triangular corn chip, were introduced in the 1960s.

Production

Corn chips are made by cooking corn in lime water (nixtamalization), grinding it into masa, shaping the dough, and frying it in oil. The resulting chips are salted and sometimes seasoned with cheese, chili or other flavourings.

Varieties

Brands and styles include scoop-shaped chips designed for dipping, flavoured varieties such as nacho cheese and cool ranch, and regional styles in Mexican cuisine where similar preparations are known as totopos.
