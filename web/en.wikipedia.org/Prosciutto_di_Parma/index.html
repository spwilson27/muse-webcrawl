Prosciutto di Parma

Country of originItaly
RegionProvince of Parma, Emilia-Romagna
TypeDry-cured ham
CertificationPDO (1996)

Prosciutto di Parma, also called Parma ham, is a dry-cured ham produced in the hills surrounding the city of Parma in the Emilia-Romagna region of Italy. It is one of the most famous Italian prosciutti crudi, protected by a Denominazione di Origine Protetta (PDO) under European Union law since 1996 and regulated by the Consorzio del Prosciutto di Parma, founded in 1963. Genuine Parma ham bears the consortium's ducal crown brand seared into the skin.

Production

Parma ham is made from the hind legs of heavy pigs of approved breeds raised in north-central Italy. The curing uses only sea salt — no nitrates, nitrites, or other additives are permitted. After salting and resting, the hams are washed, hung to dry, and coated on the exposed flesh with sugna, a mixture of lard, salt, and rice flour, to keep them from drying too quickly. The minimum maturing period is 12 months, and many hams age 18 to 30 months. The hills around Langhirano, the centre of production, benefit from cool breezes said to impart the ham's characteristic sweet, delicate flavour.

Characteristics and use

The finished ham has a deep pink-red colour, a thin layer of white fat, and a sweet, lightly salty taste, with a softer texture than the leaner Tuscan style. It is typically sliced very thin and eaten raw as an antipasto, often with melon or figs, wrapped around grissini, or used in dishes such as saltimbocca and tortellini fillings. Annual production runs to roughly nine million hams, a significant share of which is exported pre-sliced in sealed packs.
