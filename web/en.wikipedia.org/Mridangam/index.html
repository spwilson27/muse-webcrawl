Mridangam

The mridangam is a percussion instrument of ancient origin. It is the primary rhythmic accompaniment in Carnatic music ensembles, and also in Dhrupad and Hindustani music. A related South Asian percussion instrument is the khol of Bengal.

ClassificationMembranophone, hand drum
RelatedPakhawaj, khol, maddalam

Description

The mridangam is a barrel-shaped drum whose body is traditionally hollowed out of a single block of wood (often jackfruit wood). The two heads are made of layered goat/cow skin held by leather straps; the right head carries a permanent black spot of iron filings and rice paste (the syoru or satham) which gives the drum its distinctive pitched resonance, allowing it to be tuned to the tonic of the soloist. A paste of semolina and water is applied to the left head before performance to lower its pitch.

Playing technique

The mridangam is played with the fingers and palms of both hands while held across the player's lap, producing a large vocabulary of strokes named by solfa syllables. Legendary players include Palghat Mani Iyer, Palani Subramania Pillai, and Umayalpuram K. Sivaraman.
