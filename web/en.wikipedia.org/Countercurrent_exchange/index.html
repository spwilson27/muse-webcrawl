Countercurrent exchange

Countercurrent exchange is a mechanism in which two fluids flow in opposite directions across an exchange surface, maximising transfer of heat or substances between them.

Examples in biology

Fish gills use countercurrent flow of water and blood to extract oxygen efficiently. The loop of Henle in kidneys creates an osmotic gradient to concentrate urine. Rete mirabile networks in limbs, tuna muscles and bird legs conserve heat. Countercurrent multiplication amplifies the gradient.

Engineering

The same principle is used in heat exchangers, chemical processing and dialysis.
