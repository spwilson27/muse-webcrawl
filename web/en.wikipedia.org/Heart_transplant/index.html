Heart transplant

A heart transplant, or cardiac transplantation, is a surgical transplant procedure performed on patients with end-stage heart failure or severe coronary artery disease when other treatments no longer suffice. The patient's diseased heart is removed (orthotopic procedure) and replaced with a healthy heart from a recently deceased organ donor.

History

The first human-to-human heart transplant was performed on 3 December 1967 by South African surgeon Christiaan Barnard at Groote Schuur Hospital in Cape Town; the recipient, Louis Washkansky, survived 18 days. Early results were poor because of organ rejection, but the introduction of the immunosuppressant ciclosporin in the early 1980s transformed outcomes, and heart transplantation became an established therapy. Several thousand transplants are now performed worldwide each year, limited mainly by donor availability.

Procedure and aftercare

The donor heart is usually implanted in the anatomical position after the recipient's heart is excised, with the patient on cardiopulmonary bypass. Recipients require lifelong immunosuppressive therapy to prevent rejection, monitored by periodic endomyocardial biopsy and imaging. Complications include acute rejection, infection, and cardiac allograft vasculopathy, a chronic narrowing of the graft's arteries.

Outcomes

Median survival after heart transplantation now exceeds 12–14 years in many series, with one-year survival around 90 per cent. Because donor hearts are scarce, alternatives such as ventricular assist devices (artificial pumps) are used as a bridge to transplant or as destination therapy.
