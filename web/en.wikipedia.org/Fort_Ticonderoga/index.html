Fort Ticonderoga

Fort Ticonderoga, formerly Fort Carillon, is a large 18th-century star fort built by the French at a narrows near the south end of Lake Champlain, in northern New York. It overlooks the portage between Lake Champlain and Lake George, a route of immense strategic importance in the colonial wars of North America. Its name comes from the Iroquois word tekontaró:ken, meaning "it is at the junction of two waterways."

French and Indian War

The Marquis de Lotbinière began construction of Fort Carillon in 1755. In July 1758, in the Battle of Carillon, a French force of about 3,600 under the Marquis de Montcalm repelled a frontal assault by some 16,000 British troops under General James Abercrombie — one of the bloodiest battles of the war. The following year the British returned under Jeffrey Amherst, and the French destroyed the fort's powder magazine and withdrew. The British repaired the fort and renamed it Ticonderoga.

American Revolution

On 10 May 1775, in one of the first offensive actions of the Revolutionary War, Ethan Allen and the Green Mountain Boys, accompanied by Benedict Arnold, surprised the small British garrison and captured the fort "in the name of the Great Jehovah and the Continental Congress." Its cannon were hauled by Henry Knox overland to Boston, forcing the British evacuation of that city in March 1776. In July 1777 a British army under General John Burgoyne placed cannon on Mount Defiance, the unoccupied height overlooking the fort, and the American garrison evacuated without a fight. The Americans briefly reoccupied the fort after Burgoyne's surrender at Saratoga, and the British finally abandoned it in 1781.

Museum

After the Revolution the fort fell into ruin and was quarried for building stone. In 1909 the Pell family, owners of the site, began restoring it, and Fort Ticonderoga opened as one of America's earliest restored historic sites. It is now operated by the Fort Ticonderoga Association as a museum and education centre, with restored barracks, a museum of colonial military artifacts, gardens, and the battlefields of the surrounding 2,000-acre preserve.
