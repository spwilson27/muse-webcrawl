Spoken word

Spoken word is an oral poetic performance art that is based mainly on the poem, the performer's aesthetic qualities, and the audience's engagement. It is a 20th- and 21st-century continuation of older oral artistic traditions including Greek poetry, the West African griot tradition, and the Harlem Renaissance.

History

Modern spoken word has roots in the poetry readings of the Beat Generation, the Black Arts Movement, and the work of poets such as Gil Scott-Heron and The Last Poets, who fused poetry with music and political comment. The poetry slam, founded in Chicago in the 1980s by Marc Smith, popularized competitive performance poetry.

Characteristics

Spoken word pieces are written to be performed aloud, using rhythm, improvisation, rhymes, word play, and audience participation. Themes often include identity, politics, social justice, race, and personal experience. The art form overlaps with hip hop, dub poetry, and performance poetry.

Spread

Television programs such as Def Poetry Jam, youth organizations, and online video platforms spread spoken word widely in the 2000s and 2010s, making it a global youth arts movement.
