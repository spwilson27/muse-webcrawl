The Yamuna (Hindi: यमुना) is the second-largest tributary of the Ganges and the longest tributary in India. Rising in the Yamunotri Glacier of the Himalayas in Uttarakhand, it travels 1,376 kilometres south past Delhi, Mathura and Agra before merging with the Ganges at the Triveni Sangam in Prayagraj.

Course and tributaries

After descending from the mountains at Yamunanagar, the river crosses the Indo-Gangetic Plain, where it receives major tributaries including the Chambal — its longest — as well as the Sindh, Betwa and Ken. A series of barrages divides the river; at the Hathnikund Barrage much of its flow is diverted into the Western and Eastern Yamuna Canals for irrigation.

Religious significance

The Yamuna is one of the most sacred rivers of Hinduism, worshipped as the goddess Yamuna, daughter of the sun god Surya and sister of Yama. Its banks at Mathura and Vrindavan are central to the legends of Krishna, and the confluence at Prayagraj hosts the Kumbh Mela festival.

Pollution

Despite its sanctity, the Yamuna is severely polluted, especially along the Delhi stretch, where untreated sewage and industrial effluents have often left the water unfit even for bathing. Repeated government clean-up programmes, including the Yamuna Action Plan and court-supervised efforts, have had limited success.
