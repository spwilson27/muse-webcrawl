Fragonard (perfume house)

Fragonard's historic factory in Grasse, the perfume capital of France.

Parfumerie Fragonard is a French perfume house founded in 1926 in Grasse, Provence, by Eugène Fuchs. Fuchs named the company after Jean-Honoré Fragonard (1732–1806), the celebrated Rococo painter who was a native of Grasse, and opened his first factory in one of the town's oldest perfume workshops, originally built in 1782.

History and museums

The company remains family-owned and is run by descendants of the founder. Fragonard operates factories and museums in Grasse and in the village of Èze on the French Riviera, where visitors can see traditional copper stills and perfume-making equipment, and it runs the Musée du Parfum Fragonard in Paris, near the Opéra Garnier, opened in 1983 in a Napoleon III-era town house. Its museum collections of perfume bottles, toiletry articles and distillation apparatus trace 3,000 years of perfumery.

Products

Fragonard produces perfumes, eaux de toilette, soaps, cosmetics and home fragrances, sold mainly through its own boutiques and mail order rather than department stores. Well-known fragrances include the floral Belle de Nuit and the citrus-and-ginger Eau de Hong Kong line; each year the house releases a "flower of the year" collection celebrating a single floral note such as jasmine, lilac or lily of the valley.
