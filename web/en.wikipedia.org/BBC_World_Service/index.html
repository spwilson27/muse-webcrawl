BBC World Service

Type Radio broadcasting network
Launch 19 December 1932 (as Empire Service)
Owner BBC

The BBC World Service is an international broadcaster owned and operated by the BBC. It is the world's largest external broadcaster in terms of reception area, language selection and audience reach, providing news, speech and discussion in more than 40 languages to a global audience of several hundred million people.

History

The service began in 1932 as the Empire Service, broadcasting on shortwave to English speakers across the British Empire. During the Second World War, its news — including broadcasts in many languages to occupied Europe — built a reputation for accuracy and impartiality. The famous opening notes of Beethoven's Fifth Symphony, echoing the Morse code "V for Victory", became its emblem.

Services

The World Service broadcasts in languages including Arabic, Persian, Hausa, Swahili, Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Chinese, Russian, Ukrainian, Spanish and Portuguese, as well as an English-language all-news radio service. Output is distributed by shortwave, FM partner stations, satellite, and increasingly online and through podcasts.

Funding

Historically funded by a grant-in-aid from the UK Foreign Office, the World Service has been funded primarily from the television licence fee since 2014, with additional government funding for expansion projects. Its editorial independence from government is enshrined in the BBC's charter.
