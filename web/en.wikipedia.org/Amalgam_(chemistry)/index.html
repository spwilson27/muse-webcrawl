Amalgam (chemistry)

An amalgam is an alloy of mercury with another metal. Depending on the proportion of mercury, amalgams may be liquid, soft paste, or solid. Almost all metals form amalgams with mercury; iron and platinum are notable exceptions.

Uses

Dental amalgam — an alloy of mercury with silver, tin, and copper — has been used for tooth fillings since the 19th century, though its use is declining over environmental concerns about mercury. Sodium amalgam is a reagent in organic chemistry, and amalgams of gold were historically central to extracting gold and silver from ores. Mercury's tendency to amalgamate is also a hazard: spilled mercury can embrittle aluminium structures, which is why mercury thermometers are banned aboard aircraft.
