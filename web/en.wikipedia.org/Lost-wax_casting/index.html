Lost-wax casting

Lost-wax casting (also called investment casting, or cire perdue) is a process for making metal objects in which a wax model is encased in refractory material to form a mould; the wax is melted out and molten metal poured into the cavity. The technique is at least 6,000 years old, with early examples from the Indus Valley and Mesopotamia.

Steps typically include making the wax original (or a wax copy of a model), attaching sprues, building the investment mould, burning out the wax, pouring metal, and breaking the mould to reveal the casting, which is then finished. It permits fine detail and complex shapes and is used for sculpture — famously for bronze statues and Benin Bronzes — jewellery, dentistry and precision industrial parts.
