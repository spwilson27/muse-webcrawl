The Battle of Kinsale, also known as the Siege of Kinsale, was the ultimate battle in England's conquest of Gaelic Ireland, beginning in October 1601, near the end of Queen Elizabeth I's reign, and at the climax of the Nine Years' War — a campaign by Hugh O'Neill, Hugh Roe O'Donnell and other Irish lords against English rule.

Background

In September 1601 a Spanish expedition under Don Juan de Águila landed at Kinsale in the south of Ireland to support the Irish rebellion. The English lord deputy, Charles Blount, Lord Mountjoy, besieged the town, while O'Neill and O'Donnell marched the length of Ireland through winter to relieve it.

The battle

On 24 December 1601 (Old Style) the Irish army attempted a coordinated night attack with a Spanish sortie, but the attack miscarried in darkness and confusion. English cavalry routed the Irish foot, and the Spanish sortie was repulsed. Águila surrendered Kinsale on terms in early January 1602.

Aftermath

The defeat ended Spanish intervention in Ireland and effectively broke the rebellion. O'Donnell left for Spain, and the subsequent Flight of the Earls in 1607 marked the end of the old Gaelic order in Ireland.
