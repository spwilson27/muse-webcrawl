Minicomputer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

ClassMid-range multi-user computer
Era1960s – 1990s
Role todaySuperseded by the server

A minicomputer is a class of multi-user computer in the middle range of the computing spectrum — more powerful than a microcomputer, less powerful and expensive than a mainframe. Minicomputers were, in effect, the departmental servers of their era: from the mid-1960s through the 1980s they provided shared computing to laboratories, engineering groups, and businesses.

When client–server computing took hold, the surviving minicomputer lines were simply marketed as servers. DEC's VAX systems running VMS or Ultrix, IBM's AS/400, and HP's minicomputer descendants performed file, print, and application serving for workgroups, and the distinction between "minicomputer" and "server" dissolved as microprocessor-based servers running Unix, NetWare, and Windows NT took over the role entirely.

Notable systems

  • DEC PDP-8, PDP-11, and VAX
  • Data General Nova and Eclipse MV
  • HP 3000 and HP 9000
  • IBM System/36, System/38, and AS/400
  • Prime Computer and Wang VS systems
