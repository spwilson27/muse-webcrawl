Anarcho-syndicalism

Anarcho-syndicalism is a political philosophy and anarchist school of thought that views revolutionary industrial unionism, or syndicalism, as a method by which workers can bring about a free, egalitarian society. It holds that labour unions are both the means of improving workers' conditions under capitalism and the embryonic institutions of a future stateless society based on workers' self-management.

History

The movement emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, influenced by the French CGT and theorists such as Rudolf Rocker, whose Anarcho-Syndicalism: Theory and Practice (1938) remains a defining text. Its largest organization was Spain's Confederación Nacional del Trabajo (CNT), which played a central role in the Spanish Revolution of 1936, when factories and land were collectivized in Catalonia and Aragón.

Principles and tactics

Anarcho-syndicalists favour direct action — strikes, boycotts, and the general strike — over parliamentary politics. Their unions are organized federally, without paid officials, with decisions made by assemblies of members. The goal is a general strike that would allow workers to take over production and reorganize society through federations of self-managed workplaces and communes.
