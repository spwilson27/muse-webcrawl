The Urmia Lake Bridge (Persian: پل شهید کلانتری), officially the Shahid Kalantari Bridge, is a bridge and causeway across Lake Urmia in northwestern Iran, linking the cities of Tabriz and Urmia and the provinces of East Azerbaijan and West Azerbaijan. Completed in November 2008, it shortened the road journey between the two cities from roughly 200 km around the lake to about 130 km across it.

Structure

The crossing consists mainly of an earth causeway built across the shallow lake, with a steel bridge section — the main span of about 1.4 km, including a tied-arch central span of roughly 100 metres — providing an opening for water circulation between the northern and southern arms of the lake.

Environmental controversy

The causeway has been controversial since its conception. Environmentalists argue that the embankment impedes circulation between the two halves of the hypersaline lake, altering salinity and ecology and contributing to the lake's severe shrinkage, alongside drought and agricultural water use. Iranian authorities have studied enlarging the bridge opening as part of Lake Urmia restoration efforts.

History

A crossing of the lake was first proposed in the 1970s and abandoned after the 1979 revolution; construction resumed in the 2000s under President Mohammad Khatami's and Mahmoud Ahmadinejad's governments, and the bridge was inaugurated in November 2008.
