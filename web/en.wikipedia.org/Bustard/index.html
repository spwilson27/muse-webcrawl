Bustard

Bustards are large, terrestrial birds of the family Otididae, living mainly in dry open country and steppes of the Old World and Australia. They include some of the heaviest flying birds, such as the great bustard and the kori bustard.

Description and behaviour

Bustards have long legs, broad bodies and cryptic brown plumage. They are omnivorous, feeding on insects, seeds and small vertebrates, and are generally shy and wary. Males of many species perform spectacular courtship displays, inflating throat sacs and transforming their plumage into a ball of white feathers; in lekking species males gather at traditional display sites.

Conservation

Many bustards are threatened by hunting and the loss and disturbance of grassland habitat. The great bustard, once extinct as a breeding bird in Britain, has been reintroduced to Salisbury Plain; the great Indian bustard is critically endangered.
