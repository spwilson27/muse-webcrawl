The October Manifesto, formally the Manifesto on the Improvement of the State Order, was a document issued by Emperor Nicholas II of Russia on 30 October 1905 (17 October Old Style), under the influence of his minister Sergei Witte. Drafted in response to the revolutionary unrest and general strike paralysing the empire, it promised basic civil liberties and an elected legislative assembly, the State Duma.

Background

The Revolution of 1905 began with the Bloody Sunday massacre of January 1905 and was intensified by Russia's defeats in the Russo-Japanese War. By October a general strike had shut down railways, factories and universities. Witte presented the Tsar with a stark choice: appoint a military dictator or grant constitutional concessions.

Provisions

The Manifesto pledged:

  • Fundamental civil freedoms: inviolability of person, freedom of conscience, speech, assembly and association;
  • Extension of the franchise toward universal male suffrage for elections to the State Duma;
  • A rule that no law would take effect without the Duma's approval.

Effects

The Manifesto split the opposition: liberals of the Constitutional Democratic Party (Kadets) and the moderate Octobrists accepted it as the basis of a constitutional order, while the Bolsheviks and Socialist Revolutionaries dismissed it as insufficient and continued agitation; the St. Petersburg Soviet was suppressed in December. Political parties were legalised and the first Duma convened in April 1906.

Retreat

The Fundamental Laws of 1906, issued just before the Duma opened, reasserted the Tsar's "supreme autocratic power", retained his control of ministers and foreign policy, and allowed him to dissolve the Duma and legislate by decree under Article 87. Nicholas dissolved the first two Dumas and, in June 1907, altered the electoral law to favour landowners — an act widely regarded as breaching the Manifesto's spirit and a step on the road to 1917.
