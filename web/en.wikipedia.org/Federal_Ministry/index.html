Federal Ministry

A federal ministry is a specialised government department at the federal level in countries such as Germany and Austria.

A federal ministry is a specialised administrative body at the level of a federal government, headed by a federal minister who is usually a member of the cabinet. The term is used especially in Germany (German: Bundesministerium) and Austria for the departments of the federal administration.

In Germany, federal ministries are the supreme federal authorities (oberste Bundesbehörden). They are responsible for policy development, drafting legislation, and oversight of subordinate federal agencies within their remit. Most ministries have their principal seat in Berlin, with some retaining a significant presence in Bonn, the former capital of West Germany.

German federal ministries

Depending on the cabinet, Germany has around fourteen to seventeen federal ministries, including the Federal Ministry of Finance, the Federal Ministry of the Interior, the Federal Foreign Office, the Federal Ministry of Defence, the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs, the Federal Ministry of Health, and the Federal Ministry for Digital and Transport. The number, names, and portfolios of ministries can be changed by the Federal Chancellor through organisational decrees at the formation of a government.
