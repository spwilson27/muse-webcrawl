Yoweri Kaguta Museveni (born 15 September 1944) is a Ugandan politician and military officer who has served as President of Uganda since 1986, making him one of Africa's longest-serving leaders.

Early life and insurgency

Museveni was born in Ntungamo in southwestern Uganda and studied economics and political science at the University of Dar es Salaam in Tanzania, where he was influenced by pan-Africanist and Marxist ideas. After Idi Amin seized power in 1971, Museveni joined the resistance and fought in the Uganda–Tanzania War of 1978–79. Following the disputed 1980 elections, he founded the National Resistance Army (NRA) and waged a five-year guerrilla war — the Ugandan Bush War — against the government of Milton Obote and then the military junta of Tito Okello. The NRA captured Kampala in January 1986.

Presidency

Museveni's government initially won praise for restoring security, pursuing market reforms, and running an effective early campaign against HIV/AIDS. Uganda adopted a new constitution in 1995; Museveni won elections in 1996 and 2001 under the no-party "Movement" system, and again in 2006, 2011, 2016, and 2021 after multiparty politics was restored and presidential term and age limits were removed by constitutional amendments.

Criticism

Critics cite Uganda's military involvement in the Congo wars, corruption, restrictions on opposition and the press, and the 2023 Anti-Homosexuality Act as stains on his record, while supporters credit him with decades of relative stability and economic growth.
