Secunda, Mpumalanga

The Sasol Secunda complex, around which the town was built, is one of the largest synthetic fuel plants in the world.

Secunda is a town in the Mpumalanga province of South Africa, built around the Sasol synthetic fuels and chemicals complex. It lies roughly 130 kilometres east of Johannesburg, on the Highveld at an elevation of about 1,600 metres. The name Secunda is Latin for "second", referring to the fact that the plant was the second oil-from-coal installation built by Sasol after the original works at Sasolburg.

History

Secunda was established in the 1970s after the South African government decided, in response to the oil crises of that decade and international sanctions, to expand domestic production of liquid fuels from coal. Construction of the Sasol Two plant began in 1976, followed by Sasol Three, and the town was laid out to house the construction workforce and later the plant's permanent employees. The first synthetic fuel was produced in 1980. Streets in the town are named after trees, and the residential areas were planned with extensive green belts.

Economy

The economy of Secunda is dominated by the Sasol complex, which converts coal from surrounding collieries, together with natural gas, into petrol, diesel, and a wide range of chemical products using Fischer–Tropsch technology. The plant is among the largest single-point sources of greenhouse-gas emissions in the world, a fact that has made Secunda a focus of debate about South Africa's energy transition. Surrounding agriculture includes maize and livestock farming.

Geography and climate

Secunda has a subtropical highland climate with warm summers, frequent afternoon thunderstorms, and cold, dry winters with frost. The town falls within the Govan Mbeki Local Municipality, whose seat is Secunda itself. Nearby towns include Bethal, Trichardt and Evander.
