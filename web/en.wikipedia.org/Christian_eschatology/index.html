Christian eschatology

Christian eschatology is a major branch of study within Christian theology dealing with the "last things" (eschata). It concerns doctrines about death, the intermediate state, the Second Coming of Christ (Parousia), the resurrection of the dead, the Last Judgment, heaven and hell, and the renewal of creation in the new heaven and new earth.

The Last Judgment is a central theme of Christian eschatology

Principal themes

Central eschatological teachings include the return of Christ in glory; the general resurrection; the final judgment of the living and the dead; and the ultimate destiny of humankind. Christian traditions differ over the millennium — the reign of Christ described in Revelation 20 — giving rise to the broad frameworks of premillennialism (including dispensational premillennialism), amillennialism, and postmillennialism. They also differ over the rapture, the Great Tribulation, and the nature of hell, including doctrines of eternal conscious punishment, annihilationism, and universal reconciliation.

Sources

The principal biblical sources are the prophetic books of the Old Testament, especially Daniel; the Olivet Discourse of Jesus in the Synoptic Gospels; the letters of Paul; and the Book of Revelation. Statements of doctrine appear in historic creeds, which confess that Christ "will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead."

History

Early Christians generally expected the return of Christ imminently. Over the centuries, interpretations shifted: Augustine's allegorical reading of the millennium dominated Western theology for a thousand years, while waves of millenarian expectation recurred repeatedly. In the 19th century, dispensationalism systematized a futurist premillennial scheme that became highly influential in evangelicalism, including beliefs about the rapture popularized in the 20th and 21st centuries.
