Edgeworth series

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Successive terms of the Edgeworth expansion approximate a distribution around the normal curve.

The Edgeworth series, named after Francis Ysidro Edgeworth, expands a probability distribution in terms of the normal distribution, with correction terms expressed through the cumulants of the distribution — principally its skewness and kurtosis. It refines the central limit theorem by approximating the distribution of a standardised sum of random variables with an asymptotic series in powers of 1/√n.

Form of the expansion

For a standardised variable with density f(x), the expansion begins from the standard normal density φ(x) and multiplies by a polynomial whose coefficients are built from the cumulants κ₃ (skewness) and κ₄ (excess kurtosis), typically involving Hermite polynomials. The first correction term adjusts for skewness; the second for kurtosis and the square of the skewness.

Properties and limitations

Unlike a convergent series, the Edgeworth expansion is asymptotic: for fixed sample size, adding many terms eventually worsens the approximation, and the approximated "density" can become negative in the tails. It is nonetheless widely used in statistics and econometrics to improve normal approximations to sampling distributions, and it underlies the Cornish–Fisher expansion for quantiles.

Related expansions

The Gram–Charlier series, of which the Edgeworth series is a rearrangement, expands directly in derivatives of the normal density. Edgeworth's ordering of terms by powers of 1/√n makes his version asymptotically consistent.
