Cedilla

A cedilla (from Spanish cedilla, "small ceda", i.e. small Z), also known as cedilha (Portuguese) or cédille (French), is a hook or tail ( ¸ ) added under certain letters as a diacritical mark to modify their pronunciation. In Catalan, French, and Portuguese it is used only on the letter C (forming Ç), where it changes the sound from hard /k/ to soft /s/ before the vowels a, o, and u.

Origin

The cedilla originated in Visigothic Spain as a small z written beneath or after a c to indicate the affricate sound /ts/, which later softened to /s/. The name for the mark is a diminutive of the old Spanish name of the letter z, ceda.

Use in other languages

Beyond Ç, cedilla-like marks appear on other letters: Latvian uses ģ, ķ, ļ, ņ (technically comma marks); Marshallese and some transliterations use ş and ţ — though in Romanian, the correct mark under S and T is a comma below, not a cedilla, a distinction that caused longstanding character-encoding problems. Turkish uses ç and ş. In the International Phonetic Alphabet, a cedilla-like diacritic indicates other modifications of sound.

Encoding

In Unicode, the combining cedilla is U+0327 and the spacing cedilla is U+00B8. Precomposed characters such as Ç (U+00C7) are widely used in French, Portuguese, Catalan, and Turkish text.
