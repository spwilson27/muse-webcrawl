Class reductionism

Class reductionism is a pejorative term, used mainly within left-wing and social-movement debates, for the view that social class and economic relations are the fundamental axis of oppression and that other forms of inequality—based on race, gender, sexuality, caste, or disability—are secondary, derivative, or will be resolved automatically once class inequality is abolished.

Usage and debate

Critics argue that a class-first approach neglects the distinct mechanisms and lived realities of racism, patriarchy, and other systems of domination, and risks sidelining the concerns of marginalized groups within coalitions. Defenders of class-centred politics reply that the label is applied unfairly to any emphasis on material inequality, that universal economic programs (jobs, housing, health care) benefit all oppressed groups, and that fragmentation by identity undermines the solidarity needed to win structural change.

Related concepts

The debate is closely connected to intersectionality, a framework associated with Kimberlé Crenshaw, which analyses how systems of oppression interlock rather than reducing one to another. It also echoes older disputes within Marxism and feminism—for example, the "dual systems" arguments of socialist feminists who treated capitalism and patriarchy as distinct but interacting structures—and contemporary arguments about "identity politics" in electoral strategy.
