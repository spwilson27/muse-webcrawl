The Battle of Austerlitz, also known as the Battle of the Three Emperors, was fought on 2 December 1805 near the town of Austerlitz in Moravia. It was one of Napoleon Bonaparte's greatest victories: the French Grande Armée decisively defeated a larger combined Russian and Austrian army under Emperors Alexander I and Francis I, effectively ending the War of the Third Coalition.

Background

After forcing the surrender of an Austrian army at Ulm in October 1805, Napoleon advanced into Austria and occupied Vienna, then pursued the retreating Allies into Moravia. Eager for a decisive battle before Prussia might intervene and before winter, Napoleon feigned weakness, deliberately abandoning the commanding Pratzen Heights and weakening his right flank to lure the Allies into attacking it.

Battle

On the morning of 2 December, in thick fog, the Allied columns descended from the Pratzen Heights to turn the French right. As Napoleon had intended, this thinned the Allied centre. At about 9 a.m., as the mist lifted — the celebrated "sun of Austerlitz" — Marshal Soult's IV Corps stormed the Heights and split the Allied army in two. The French then rolled up both wings; Allied troops fleeing south across the frozen Satschan ponds suffered heavily, though accounts of thousands drowning through broken ice were exaggerated by French propaganda.

Aftermath

The battle shattered the Third Coalition. Austria signed the Treaty of Pressburg (26 December 1805), ceding territory and paying an indemnity, and within a year the Holy Roman Empire was dissolved. Russia withdrew its armies eastward. Austerlitz is studied as a tactical masterpiece of deception, timing, and concentration of force.
