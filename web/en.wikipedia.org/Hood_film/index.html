Hood film

Hood filmGenre: drama / crime
Setting: inner-city United States
Key period: early 1990s
Notable directors: John Singleton, the Hughes brothers, Spike Lee, F. Gary Gray

A hood film is a film genre originating in American cinema, centred on African American and Latino urban life, typically in Los Angeles, New York City or Chicago. Hood films portray life in ghettos and housing projects, with recurring themes of poverty, gang violence, drug dealing, police brutality, racism and the struggle to escape one's environment.

History

The genre crystallised in the early 1990s "New Black Cinema" boom, following Spike Lee's Do the Right Thing (1989). John Singleton's Boyz n the Hood (1991), which made Singleton the youngest and first Black nominee for the Academy Award for Best Director, is considered the defining work, alongside Menace II Society (1993) by the Hughes brothers, Juice (1992), New Jack City (1991) and Friday (1995), a comedic take on the genre. Hip hop music and artists frequently feature, with rappers such as Ice Cube and Tupac Shakur in leading roles.

Themes and legacy

Many hood films function as social commentary on systemic inequality and the crack epidemic, while critics have debated whether some entries glamorise violence. The genre's influence persists in later works such as Moonlight (2016) and Straight Outta Compton (2015).
