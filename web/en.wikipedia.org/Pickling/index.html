Pickling

Purpose
Food preservation
Methods
Vinegar immersion; brine fermentation
Examples
Pickled cucumbers, kimchi, sauerkraut, achaar

Pickling is the process of preserving or extending the shelf life of food by either anaerobic fermentation in brine or immersion in vinegar. The low pH of the resulting product inhibits spoilage bacteria and gives pickled foods their characteristic sour taste.

Methods

In vinegar pickling, food is submerged in an acidic solution, often with salt, sugar, and spices, and may be heat-processed for shelf stability. In fermentation pickling, vegetables are kept in salt brine, allowing lactobacillus bacteria to produce lactic acid; classic examples include sauerkraut, kimchi, and traditional kosher dill cucumbers.

History and variety

Pickling is among the oldest food preservation techniques, practised for thousands of years from Mesopotamia to East Asia. Every culinary culture has its pickles: Indian achaar in oil and spices, Japanese tsukemono, German gherkins, Middle Eastern torshi, and the pickled onions and eggs of Britain.

Safety

Proper acidity (below pH 4.6) prevents the growth of Clostridium botulinum. Home canning of low-acid pickles requires tested recipes and pressure or water-bath processing to avoid botulism.
