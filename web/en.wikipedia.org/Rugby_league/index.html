Rugby league football, commonly called just rugby league and sometimes league football football, is a full-contact sport played by two teams of thirteen players on a rectangular field measuring 68 metres wide and 112–122 metres long. It is one of the two major codes of rugby football, the other being rugby union.

History

Rugby league originated in 1895 at the George Hotel in Huddersfield, England, when 22 clubs split from the Rugby Football Union over payments to working-class players for time lost at work ("broken time" payments). The new Northern Union soon changed its laws: the line-out and contested scrums were de-emphasised, teams were reduced to thirteen players in 1906, and the play-the-ball replaced the ruck, creating a faster, more open game. The sport spread to Australia and New Zealand, where it became the dominant rugby code in New South Wales and Queensland.

Gameplay

Teams carry the ball upfield in sets of six tackles, after which possession passes to the opposition. Points are scored by a try (4 points) grounded beyond the goal line, a conversion or penalty goal (2 points) and a drop goal (1 point; 2 points from beyond the 40-metre line in the NRL). Scrums are largely uncontested and serve mainly to restart play.

Major competitions

The premier club competitions are Australia's National Rugby League (NRL) and the British Super League. Internationally, the Rugby League World Cup, first held in 1954, and Australia's State of Origin series between New South Wales and Queensland are flagship events. In Australia, the annual State of Origin is among the country's most-watched sporting events.
