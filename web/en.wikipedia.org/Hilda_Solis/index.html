Hilda Lucia Solis (born 20 October 1957) is an American politician who served as the 25th United States Secretary of Labor from 2009 to 2013, in the administration of President Barack Obama. A member of the Democratic Party, she previously represented California's 31st and 32nd congressional districts in the US House of Representatives. Since 2014 she has served on the Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors, representing the 1st District.

Early life and education

Solis was born in Los Angeles to immigrant parents — her mother from Nicaragua and her father from Mexico, a union shop steward. She was the first in her family to attend college, graduating from California State Polytechnic University, Pomona, and earning a master's degree in public administration from the University of Southern California.

Political career

Solis served on the Rio Hondo Community College Board of Trustees, then in the California State Assembly (1992–1994) and State Senate (1994–2000), where in 1995 she became the first Latina elected to the state senate. She gained national attention for environmental-justice legislation and for raising California's minimum wage. In 2000 she received the John F. Kennedy Profile in Courage Award, the first woman to be so honoured.

Elected to Congress in 2000, Solis served on the Energy and Commerce Committee and championed labour, health and green-jobs legislation. As Secretary of Labor she emphasised enforcement of wage-and-hour and workplace-safety laws and job training. After leaving the Cabinet in 2013 she was elected to the Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors in 2014, where she has focused on homelessness, housing and county services in the San Gabriel Valley and eastern Los Angeles.
