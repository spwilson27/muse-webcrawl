Provincetown, Massachusetts

Provincetown is a New England town at the very tip of Cape Cod in Barnstable County, Massachusetts, United States. With a year-round population of roughly 3,700, it swells to as many as 60,000 in the summer. Famous as the landing place of the Mayflower Pilgrims in 1620, it is renowned as a historic fishing port, one of the oldest art colonies in America, and a popular LGBTQ+ resort destination.

History

The area was inhabited by the Nauset people and named Meeshawn. In November 1620 the Pilgrims anchored in Provincetown Harbor and signed the Mayflower Compact before moving on to Plymouth; the 252-foot Pilgrim Monument (completed 1910) commemorates the landing. Provincetown was incorporated in 1727 and grew as a fishing and whaling port, attracting a large Portuguese community. From the late 19th century it became an artists' and writers' colony — home to playwright Eugene O'Neill and the Provincetown Players — and later a theatre and tourist centre.

Geography and culture

The town curls around a natural harbour at the end of the Cape's "fist," surrounded by the dunes and beaches of the Cape Cod National Seashore. Commercial Street is its main thoroughfare of galleries, restaurants, and guesthouses. Provincetown is noted for its tolerance and vibrant gay community, and much of its population is seasonal.
