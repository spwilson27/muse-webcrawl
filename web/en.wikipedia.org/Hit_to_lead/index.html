Hit to lead

Hit to lead (H2L) is a stage of early drug discovery in which small-molecule "hits" found by screening are evaluated and refined into "lead compounds" — drug candidates that show activity and selectivity against a biological target together with drug-like physicochemical properties. Hit to lead bridges high-throughput screening (HTS) and the subsequent lead optimisation phase, and its goal is to deliver one or more chemically tractable series worth investing in.

Hit identification

Hits originate from several sources: high-throughput screening of corporate or commercial compound libraries (often hundreds of thousands of compounds) against an assay for the target; fragment-based screening, which finds small, weak binders of high ligand efficiency; virtual screening and structure-based design; and literature or natural-product leads. Raw HTS hit rates are typically 0.01–1%, and many apparent hits are artefacts — aggregators, fluorescent compounds, redox cyclers, and "pan-assay interference compounds" (PAINS) — so confirmation in orthogonal assays is essential.

Hit triage and validation

In hit to lead, confirmed hits are re-synthesised or repurchased and characterised: dose–response curves establish potency; counter-screens measure selectivity against related targets and cytotoxicity; and physicochemical profiling checks aqueous solubility, chemical stability, lipophilicity (logP/logD), and permeability. Clustering hits into chemical series lets chemists judge structure–activity relationships (SAR) — a series in which small structural changes produce interpretable potency changes is far more valuable than a singleton of equal potency.

Hit expansion and early SAR

Chemists then synthesise analogues around the most promising scaffolds ("hit expansion" or "SAR by synthesis", supplemented by "SAR by catalogue" purchases of near neighbours). Objectives include improving potency into the sub-micromolar range, demonstrating ligand efficiency and lipophilic ligand efficiency (LLE) headroom, removing reactive or toxicophore groups, confirming a plausible binding mode (ideally with a crystal or cryo-EM structure of the target–ligand complex), and showing activity in a cell-based assay. Simple in vitro ADME tests — microsomal stability, plasma protein binding, hERG liability — flag problems early.

Criteria for a lead

A series graduates to lead status when it offers reproducible potency with a clear SAR, acceptable selectivity, drug-like properties consistent with guidelines such as Lipinski's rule of five, freedom to operate (patentability), and synthetic tractability for rapid analogue production. Projects commonly carry two or three chemically distinct lead series forward into lead optimisation as insurance against late-stage failure, reflecting the high attrition of drug development overall.
