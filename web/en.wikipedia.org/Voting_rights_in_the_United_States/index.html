Voting rights in the United States


Key amendments: 15th, 19th, 23rd, 24th, 26th
Key statutes: Voting Rights Act of 1965; National Voter Registration Act of 1993; Help America Vote Act of 2002

Voting rights in the United States have evolved from a franchise limited largely to white male property owners at the founding to near-universal adult suffrage, through a long series of constitutional amendments, federal statutes, court decisions and state-level struggles. Elections in the United States are administered primarily by state and local governments, so rules on registration, identification and eligibility vary.

Expansion of the franchise

  • 1870 — The Fifteenth Amendment prohibits denying the vote on account of race, colour or previous condition of servitude, though Jim Crow laws — poll taxes, literacy tests and grandfather clauses — disenfranchised most Black voters in the South for decades.
  • 1920 — The Nineteenth Amendment extends the vote to women nationwide.
  • 1961 — The Twenty-third Amendment grants presidential electors to the District of Columbia.
  • 1964 — The Twenty-fourth Amendment bans poll taxes in federal elections; the Supreme Court extended the ban to state elections in 1966.
  • 1965 — The Voting Rights Act outlaws discriminatory voting practices, suspends literacy tests and requires certain jurisdictions with histories of discrimination to obtain federal "preclearance" before changing voting rules.
  • 1971 — The Twenty-sixth Amendment lowers the voting age to 18.

Modern issues

Contemporary debates concern voter identification laws, the restoration of voting rights to people with felony convictions (felony disenfranchisement affects millions of Americans, with rules varying widely by state), the voting status of residents of US territories, partisan gerrymandering, and election administration. In Shelby County v. Holder (2013), the Supreme Court struck down the coverage formula that determined which jurisdictions required preclearance, effectively suspending that part of the Voting Rights Act.
