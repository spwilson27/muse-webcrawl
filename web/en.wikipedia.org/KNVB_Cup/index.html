The KNVB Cup (Dutch: KNVB Beker) is the national knockout cup competition in Dutch football, organised by the Royal Dutch Football Association (KNVB) since 1898. It is the second most prestigious domestic competition in the Netherlands after the Eredivisie. For sponsorship reasons it has been known as the TOTO KNVB Cup.

Format

The competition is open to professional clubs from the Eredivisie, Eerste Divisie and Tweede Divisie, top amateur clubs and district cup winners. Ties are single matches, generally played at the ground of the lower-division team in early rounds; the final is traditionally held at De Kuip in Rotterdam. The winner qualifies for the UEFA Europa League (and for the Johan Cruyff Shield against the league champion), unless it has already qualified for the Champions League.

History and records

First contested in the 1898–99 season, when RAP Amsterdam won, the cup's silver trophy is nicknamed the dennenappel ("pine cone"). Ajax is the most successful club with 20 titles, followed by Feyenoord (14) and PSV (11). Famous giant-killings and amateur finalists remain rare, though lower-division sides such as IJsselmeervogels have reached the semi-finals. The 2020 final between Utrecht and Feyenoord was cancelled due to the COVID-19 pandemic.
