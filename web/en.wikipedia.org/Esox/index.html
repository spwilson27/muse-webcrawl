Esox is a genus of freshwater fish commonly known as pike or pickerel. It is the only living genus in the family Esocidae, the pikes. The type species is the northern pike (Esox lucius). These fish are found across the Northern Hemisphere in North America and Eurasia.

Species

Currently recognized species in the genus include:

  • Esox lucius — northern pike
  • Esox masquinongy — muskellunge
  • Esox niger — chain pickerel
  • Esox americanus — American pickerel (including the redfin and grass pickerels)
  • Esox reichertii — Amur pike
  • Esox aquitanicus — Aquitaine pike
  • Esox cisalpinus — southern pike

Description

Pikes are ambush predators with elongated bodies, flattened duckbill-like snouts, and large mouths armed with sharp teeth. Their dorsal and anal fins are set far back on the body near the tail, an arrangement suited to sudden bursts of acceleration from vegetation cover. Two of the larger species — the northern pike and the muskellunge — are prized sport fish and apex predators in many lakes and slow rivers.

Hybrids and fishing

The tiger muskellunge is a well-known hybrid of the northern pike and muskellunge. Pikes feature prominently in angling lore and in heraldry; in British angling, pike fishing has a long tradition, and large specimens exceeding 20 kg (44 lb) have been recorded historically.
