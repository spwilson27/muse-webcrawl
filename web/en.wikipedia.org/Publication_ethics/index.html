Publication ethics

Publication ethics is the set of principles, standards and guidelines governing the conduct of authors, editors, peer reviewers and publishers in scholarly publishing. It addresses issues such as authorship, plagiarism, data fabrication and falsification, duplicate publication, conflicts of interest and the correction or retraction of published work.

Guidelines

The Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE), founded in 1997, provides guidance and flowcharts for editors handling suspected misconduct. Other bodies, including the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) and the World Association of Medical Editors, publish recommendations on authorship criteria and responsible research reporting.

Research misconduct

Common violations include plagiarism, fabrication or falsification of data, redundant publication, undisclosed conflicts of interest, peer-review manipulation and the use of paper mills. Journals may respond with corrections, expressions of concern or retractions; the Retraction Watch database tracks retractions across the literature.
