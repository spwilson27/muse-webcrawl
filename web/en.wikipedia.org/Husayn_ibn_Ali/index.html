Husayn ibn Ali (626–680) was an Arab religious and political leader, the younger son of Ali and Fatimah and grandson of the Prophet Muhammad. He is revered in Islam, especially by Shia Muslims as the third Imam, and is remembered for his stand against the Umayyad caliph Yazid I, ending in his death at the Battle of Karbala in 680.

Early life

Husayn was born in Medina. Tradition holds that Muhammad named him and his elder brother Hasan, loved them dearly, and called them leaders of the youth of Paradise. After Muhammad's death in 632, Husayn lived through the caliphates of Abu Bakr and Umar, his father's caliphate (656–661), and Hasan's brief caliphate and peace treaty with Mu'awiya I, founder of the Umayyad Caliphate.

Revolt and death

When Mu'awiya died in 680, his son Yazid demanded Husayn's allegiance. Husayn refused, left Medina for Mecca, and then set out toward Kufa in Iraq, whose people had invited him. Intercepted by Umayyad forces, his small caravan was forced to camp at Karbala, cut off from water, and on 10 Muharram — Ashura — Husayn and most of his companions and family were killed. His infant son Ali Zayn al-Abidin survived and continued the line of Imams in Twelver Shi'ism.

Legacy

Husayn's martyrdom is a defining event in Shia identity, commemorated annually during Muharram with mourning rituals, passion plays (ta'ziya) and pilgrimage to his shrine in Karbala. Sunnis also honour him as a member of the Prophet's household (Ahl al-Bayt) and many condemn his killing. His slogan-like legacy — resistance to tyranny and sacrifice for justice — has inspired movements across the Muslim world.
