Yam (vegetable)

Freshly harvested yam tubers

Yam is the common name for some plant species in the genus Dioscorea (family Dioscoreaceae) that form edible tubers. The tubers of some other species in the genus, being toxic when unprocessed, are not included in the scope of this article. Yams are perennial herbaceous vines native to Africa, Asia, and the Americas, cultivated for the consumption of their starchy tubers in many temperate and tropical regions. The tuber itself, also called a "yam", varies in form and can be up to 1.5 metres (5 feet) in length.

Yams were likely first domesticated in West Africa and Southeast Asia thousands of years ago. Nigeria is the world's largest producer, followed by Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire. Yams are a primary agricultural and culturally important commodity in West Africa, where the annual "new yam festival" marks the beginning of the harvest season in many communities.

Description

Yam plants are climbing vines with heart-shaped leaves; their tubers grow underground and have rough, scaly skin ranging from light pink to dark brown, with flesh that may be white, yellow, purple, or pink depending on the species. Important cultivated species include the white yam (Dioscorea rotundata), yellow yam (D. cayenensis), water yam (D. alata), and Chinese yam (D. polystachya).

Cultivation and use

Yams are typically propagated vegetatively from tuber pieces ("seed yams") and require a long, warm growing season. The harvested tubers are rich in starch and are eaten boiled, roasted, fried, or pounded into the West African dish fufu. Unlike the sweet potato, which is often marketed as a "yam" in North America, true yams belong to a different botanical order and are rarely sweet.

Confusion with sweet potato

In the United States and Canada, orange-fleshed sweet potatoes are frequently labeled "yams" in commerce, a usage dating to marketing practices of the 1930s. True yams are seldom sold in typical North American supermarkets, though they are common in markets serving African, Caribbean, and Asian communities.
