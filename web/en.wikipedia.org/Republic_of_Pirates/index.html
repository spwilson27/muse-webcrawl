Republic of Pirates

Nassau harbour on New Providence, the pirates' base from about 1706 to 1718.

The Republic of Pirates was the base and stronghold of a loose confederacy of privateers-turned-pirates operating from Nassau on the island of New Providence in the Bahamas during the early eighteenth century, roughly from 1706 to 1718. After the British colonial government effectively collapsed during the War of the Spanish Succession, former privateers such as Henry Jennings, Charles Vane, Edward Teach (Blackbeard), and Benjamin Hornigold turned the harbour into a self-governing pirate haven — sometimes remembered as the "Flying Gang" — at the height of the post-war Golden Age of Piracy.

Governance

The pirate community at Nassau numbered perhaps a thousand men at its peak and operated without formal British authority. Pirate crews themselves practised a rough internal democracy, electing captains and quartermasters and sharing plunder according to written articles; contemporary accounts, including the best-selling A General History of the Pyrates (1724), portrayed Nassau as a place where pirates administered their own affairs while the Bahamas' appointed authorities were absent or powerless.

Suppression

In 1718 the Crown appointed Woodes Rogers as royal governor, and he arrived with a naval force and offers of a royal pardon. Many pirates, including Hornigold — who became a pirate hunter — accepted the pardon; those who refused, such as Vane, fled, and several recalcitrants were hanged at Nassau in December 1718, ending the republic. The episode has been popularised by Colin Woodard's book The Republic of Pirates (2007) and the television series Black Sails.
