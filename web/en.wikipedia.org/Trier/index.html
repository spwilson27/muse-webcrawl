Trier is a city on the banks of the Moselle in Germany. It lies in a valley between low vine-covered hills in the west of the state of Rhineland-Palatinate, near the border with Luxembourg. Founded by the Romans in the late 1st century BC as Augusta Treverorum, it claims to be Germany's oldest city and was a major centre of the late Roman Empire, serving at times as an imperial residence.

History

The city was founded on the territory of the Celtic Treveri tribe. Under Rome it grew into a prosperous provincial capital, and in the late 3rd and 4th centuries it was one of the capitals of the Tetrarchy and a residence of emperors including Constantine the Great. In the Middle Ages Trier became an important ecclesiastical centre ruled by archbishop-electors. In 1818 Karl Marx was born in the city; his birthplace is now a museum.

Sights

Trier preserves outstanding Roman monuments, collectively designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site. These include the Porta Nigra, the largest Roman city gate north of the Alps, the Imperial Baths, an amphitheatre, the Basilica of Constantine, and Roman bridges. The Roman Monuments, together with Trier Cathedral and the Church of Our Lady, were inscribed in 1986.

Economy and wine

The city is a centre of the Moselle wine region, known particularly for Riesling. Tourism, wine production and the University of Trier are important to the local economy.
