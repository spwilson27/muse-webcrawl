London Calling is the third studio album by the English rock band the Clash, released as a double album in the United Kingdom on 14 December 1979 by CBS Records, and in the United States in January 1980 by Epic Records. Produced by Guy Stevens, it marked a broadening of the band's sound beyond punk rock into reggae, rockabilly, ska, hard rock, pop and R&B.

Background and recording

After the success of Give 'Em Enough Rope (1978), the band, managed by Bernie Rhodes, parted ways with their management and recorded in the summer of 1979 at Wessex Sound Studios in London. Songs addressed unemployment, racial conflict, drug use, and the responsibilities of adulthood. The title track takes its name from the BBC World Service station identification "This is London calling," broadcast during the Second World War, and its lyrics warn of nuclear error, police brutality and a coming "ice age."

Track listing

Highlights include "London Calling," "Brand New Cadillac" (a Vince Taylor cover), "Jimmy Jazz," "Rudie Can't Fail," "Spanish Bombs," "The Right Profile," "Lost in the Supermarket," "Clampdown," "The Guns of Brixton" (written and sung by bassist Paul Simonon), "Death or Glory," "Train in Vain," and "Revolution Rock." "Train in Vain," not listed on the original sleeve, became the band's first US top-30 single.

Reception and legacy

The album reached number nine on the UK Albums Chart and is routinely ranked among the greatest albums of all time: Rolling Stone placed it number eight on its 2003 and 2012 lists of the 500 greatest albums, and number 16 on the 2020 revised list. Pennie Smith's cover photograph — with lettering modelled on Elvis Presley's debut album — was named the best rock and roll photograph of all time by Q magazine, and the image was later issued as a Royal Mail postage stamp. Critics praised the album for demonstrating that punk could absorb the whole tradition of rock and roll while retaining its political urgency.
