Maroons

Maroons are descendants of Africans in the Americas and islands of the Indian Ocean who escaped from slavery and formed their own settlements. The name derives from the Spanish cimarrón, meaning "wild" or "untamed".

Maroon communities formed in Jamaica, Suriname, Brazil (where their settlements were called quilombos, most famously Palmares), and elsewhere, often blending African, Indigenous, and European cultural elements and resisting colonial forces.

Jamaican Maroons won treaties with the British in 1739 recognising their autonomy, and Maroon heritage is celebrated in the Caribbean and South America today.
