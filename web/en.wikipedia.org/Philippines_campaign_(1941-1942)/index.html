Philippines campaign (1941–1942)

The Philippines campaign (8 December 1941 – 8 May 1942) was the invasion of the Commonwealth of the Philippines by the Empire of Japan and the defence of the islands by Filipino and American forces. It began hours after the attack on Pearl Harbor and ended with the fall of Corregidor, resulting in the surrender of all remaining United States and Philippine forces in the archipelago.

Background

American war plans (War Plan Orange) envisaged a fighting withdrawal to the Bataan Peninsula and the island fortress of Corregidor at the entrance to Manila Bay. General Douglas MacArthur commanded the United States Army Forces in the Far East (USAFFE), which included the Philippine Army, still in the process of mobilisation when war came.

The invasion

Japanese forces under General Masaharu Homma landed at Lingayen Gulf and Lamon Bay on Luzon in December 1941. Much of the Far East Air Force was destroyed on the ground at Clark Field. MacArthur declared Manila an open city and withdrew his forces into Bataan.

Bataan and Corregidor

The defenders of Bataan, weakened by disease and starvation rations, surrendered on 9 April 1942; some 75,000 Filipino and American prisoners were then subjected to the Bataan Death March. Corregidor held out under heavy bombardment until its surrender on 6 May 1942 under Lieutenant General Jonathan Wainwright.

Aftermath

The campaign was a major Japanese victory, but the prolonged resistance in Bataan and Corregidor delayed Japanese timetables and became a rallying symbol. Guerrilla resistance continued throughout the occupation until American forces returned in 1944–45.
