Charity Commission for England and Wales

Charity Commission
Type: Non-ministerial government department
Formed: 1853 (Charity Commissioners)
Jurisdiction: England and Wales
Function: Registrar and regulator of charities
Register: ~170,000 registered charities

The Charity Commission for England and Wales is a non-ministerial department of the Government of the United Kingdom that regulates registered charities in England and Wales and maintains the Central Register of Charities. It is the independent registrar and regulator of charities, reporting directly to Parliament rather than to a minister.

Functions

The Commission decides whether organisations qualify for charitable status and registration, provides guidance to charity trustees, investigates misconduct and mismanagement, and can take regulatory action such as suspending trustees, appointing interim managers, or opening statutory inquiries. Its statutory objectives, set out in the Charities Act 2011, include increasing public trust and confidence in charities and promoting the effective use of charitable resources.

History

The origins of the Commission lie in the Charitable Trusts Act 1853, which created the Charity Commissioners in the wake of the Brougham Commission's inquiries into abuses of charitable endowments in the early nineteenth century. Its modern framework was reshaped by the Charities Acts of 1993, 2006, 2011, and 2022.

Scope

Charities in Scotland are regulated by the Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator (OSCR), and those in Northern Ireland by the Charity Commission for Northern Ireland. Some charities in England and Wales, such as certain universities and national museums, are "exempt" from registration and are regulated by other principal regulators.
