Irving Janis

Born: Irving Lester Janis, May 26, 1918, Buffalo, New York

Died: November 15, 1990 (aged 72)

Known for: Groupthink

Fields: Social psychology

Institutions: Yale University

Irving Lester Janis (May 26, 1918 – November 15, 1990) was an American research psychologist at Yale University and a professor emeritus at the University of California, Berkeley, most famous for his theory of "groupthink", which described the systematic errors made by groups when making collective decisions.

Work

Janis initially researched persuasion and attitude change, co-authoring work on the "sleeper effect", and studied fear appeals and psychological stress in patients facing surgery. In Victims of Groupthink (1972, revised 1982 as Groupthink) he analysed American foreign-policy fiascos — the Bay of Pigs invasion, the failure to anticipate Pearl Harbor, the escalation of the Vietnam War — arguing that cohesive decision-making groups develop concurrence-seeking tendencies that override realistic appraisal of alternatives. Symptoms he identified include illusions of invulnerability, collective rationalisation, self-censorship, an illusion of unanimity, and "mindguards".

Legacy

Janis recommended procedures for improving decision-making, such as assigning devil's advocates and consulting outside experts, in Decision Making (1977, with Leon Mann) and Crucial Decisions (1989). Groupthink remains a widely taught concept in psychology, management and political science, and is regularly invoked in post-mortems of corporate and governmental failures.
