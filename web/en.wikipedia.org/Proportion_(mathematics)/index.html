Proportion (mathematics)

In mathematics, two sequences of numbers, often experimental data, are proportional or directly proportional if their corresponding elements have a constant ratio, called the coefficient or constant of proportionality. A proportion is traditionally an equality of two ratios, written a/b = c/d.

Direct and inverse proportion

Two variables are directly proportional when one is a constant multiple of the other (y = kx); their graph is a straight line through the origin. They are inversely proportional when their product is constant (xy = k), so that one decreases as the other increases.

Properties

From the proportion a/b = c/d follow the rules of cross-multiplication (ad = bc), alternation, inversion, and composition. Proportions were extensively developed in Book V of Euclid's Elements, attributed to Eudoxus, which gave a rigorous theory of ratios for both commensurable and incommensurable magnitudes.
