Bill Nichols (film critic)

Bill Nichols (born 1942) is an American film critic and theoretician best known for his pioneering work in the study of documentary film. He is Professor Emeritus of Cinema at San Francisco State University and is widely regarded as a founder of modern documentary studies.

Documentary modes

In works such as Representing Reality (1991) and Introduction to Documentary (2001, with later editions), Nichols proposed a taxonomy of documentary "modes" — poetic, expository, observational, participatory, reflexive, and performative — describing the characteristic conventions by which documentaries organize their representation of the historical world. The framework, analogous to literary genres, became standard in film studies teaching.

Other work

Nichols edited the influential anthologies Movies and Methods (1976, 1985) and wrote Ideology and the Image (1981) and Blurred Boundaries (1994), addressing ideology, representation, and the boundaries between documentary and fiction. He founded the Visible Evidence conference series on documentary studies and taught for many years at Queen's University in Canada and San Francisco State University.
