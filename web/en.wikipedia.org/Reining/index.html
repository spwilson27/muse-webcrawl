Reining

Reining is a western riding competition in which riders guide their horses through a precise pattern of circles, spins and stops. It is recognised by the International Federation for Equestrian Sports (FEI) as a western discipline and is governed internationally by the FEI and in the United States by the National Reining Horse Association (NRHA), founded in 1966. Reining is sometimes described as the western equivalent of dressage.

The pattern

Each reining pattern is a prescribed sequence of manoeuvres performed at the lope and gallop, including large fast circles and small slow circles, lead changes, rollbacks over the hocks, 360-degree spins, and the signature sliding stop, in which the horse brings its hindquarters deep under itself and slides on its hind feet — wearing special sliding plates — while continuing to move its front legs. Horses are judged on smoothness, finesse, attitude and speed, with credits and penalties assessed for each manoeuvre; each horse-and-rider pair starts with a score of 70.

Horses

American Quarter Horses dominate the sport, though Paints, Appaloosas and other stock breeds also compete. Reining horses are trained to be guided "on a loose rein" with light cues, responding willingly to the rider's seat, legs and one hand on the reins.

Competition

Reining was added to the FEI World Equestrian Games in 2002, the first western discipline so recognised. The NRHA Futurity, Derby and major aged events award millions of dollars in prize money annually, and leading sires and riders have achieved celebrity within the horse industry.
