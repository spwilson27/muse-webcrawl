The Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus' is the primate and head of the Russian Orthodox Church, the largest autocephalous Eastern Orthodox church in the world. The patriarchate was first established in 1589, when Metropolitan Job of Moscow was elevated to the rank of patriarch with the recognition of Constantinople.

History

Christianity came to Kievan Rus' in 988, and the church was originally governed by metropolitans appointed from Constantinople. As Moscow rose, its metropolitans gained increasing independence; in 1448 the Russian bishops elected their own metropolitan without Constantinople's approval, a de facto autocephaly later formalized by the creation of the patriarchate.

In 1721 Peter the Great abolished the patriarchate and replaced it with the Holy Governing Synod, placing the church under state administration for nearly two centuries. The office was restored in 1917 by the All-Russian Local Council, which elected Tikhon as patriarch amid the Russian Revolution.

The office today

The patriarch is elected for life by the Local Council of the Russian Orthodox Church. Patriarch Kirill, enthroned on February 1, 2009, oversees a church with dioceses across Russia, Ukraine (historically), Belarus, Central Asia, and a worldwide diaspora. The patriarch's principal cathedral is the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour in Moscow, and his residence includes the Danilov Monastery.
