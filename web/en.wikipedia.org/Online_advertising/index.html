Online advertising

Online advertising, also known as digital advertising or internet advertising, is a form of marketing and advertising that uses the Internet to promote products and services to audiences and platform users. Online advertising includes email marketing, search engine marketing (SEM), social media marketing, many types of display advertising (including web banner advertising), and mobile advertising. Advertisements are increasingly delivered by automated software systems operating on multiple platforms and delivered across a variety of websites, media, and platforms.

History

The first clickable web banner ad was sold in 1993, and in 1994 the first widely noted banner advertisement — AT&T's campaign on HotWired.com — reportedly achieved an extraordinary click-through rate of around 44%. The founding of Google in 1998 and the launch of its AdWords (now Google Ads) platform in 2000 established pay-per-click search advertising as the industry's engine. Social networks later added powerful behavioural targeting, and programmatic advertising automated the buying and selling of display inventory through real-time auctions. By the late 2010s, global spending on digital advertising surpassed that on television and print combined.

Major formats

  • Search advertising — text ads shown alongside search engine results, matched to user queries.
  • Display advertising — banner, rich media, and video ads on websites and apps.
  • Social media advertising — sponsored content on platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and X.
  • Video advertising — pre-roll, mid-roll, and outstream ads, prominent on platforms such as YouTube.
  • Email marketing — promotional messages sent to opted-in mailing lists.
  • Affiliate marketing — commissions paid to publishers for referred sales or leads.
  • Influencer marketing — endorsements by creators with large followings.

Targeting, measurement and pricing

Online advertising is bought under pricing models including cost per mille (CPM), cost per click (CPC), and cost per action (CPA). Advertisers target audiences by demographics, location, device, browsing behaviour, and inferred interests, and can measure impressions, clicks, and conversions in near real time — capabilities that distinguish it from traditional media.

Criticism and regulation

The industry faces criticism over privacy and large-scale data collection, the spread of ad fraud and malvertising, the use of ad blockers by consumers, and effects on competition. Regulations such as the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and ePrivacy rules, and platform changes such as the phasing out of third-party cookies, have reshaped tracking practices.
