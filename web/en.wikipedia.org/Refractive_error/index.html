Refractive error

A refractive error is a problem with focusing light accurately on the retina due to the shape of the eye or cornea. The main types are myopia (near-sightedness), hyperopia (far-sightedness), astigmatism (distorted focus from an irregular cornea) and presbyopia (age-related loss of near focus).

Refractive errors are the most common cause of visual impairment worldwide and are easily corrected with glasses, contact lenses or refractive surgery such as LASIK. Uncorrected, they can cause eyestrain, headaches and, in children, amblyopia. The World Health Organization estimates at least 2.2 billion people have a vision impairment, a large share from uncorrected refractive error.
