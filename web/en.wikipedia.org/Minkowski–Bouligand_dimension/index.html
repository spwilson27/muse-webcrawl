Minkowski–Bouligand dimension

In fractal geometry, the Minkowski–Bouligand dimension, also known as the Minkowski dimension or box-counting dimension, is a way of determining the fractal dimension of a set S in a Euclidean space, or more generally in a metric space. It is named after Hermann Minkowski and Georges Bouligand.

[Image: The coastline of Britain covered by grids of decreasing box size for box-counting]

Definition

To calculate the dimension, cover the set with a grid of boxes of side ε and count the number N(ε) of boxes that intersect the set. The box-counting dimension is defined, when the limit exists, as

dimbox(S) = limε→0 log N(ε) / log(1/ε).

If the limit does not exist, one may still speak of the upper and lower box dimensions defined with limit superior and limit inferior. In practice, the dimension is estimated from the slope of a log–log plot of N(ε) against 1/ε — the method famously applied to the coastline of Britain.

Properties

The box-counting dimension is always greater than or equal to the Hausdorff dimension, and the two coincide for many well-behaved fractals. For a smooth curve it equals 1 and for a region of the plane it equals 2; the Sierpiński triangle has box dimension log 3 / log 2 ≈ 1.585. Unlike Hausdorff dimension, however, box dimension is not countably stable: a countable dense set such as the rationals in [0,1] has box dimension 1.
