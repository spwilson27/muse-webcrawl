Li Bai


Li Bai (701–762), courtesy name Taibai, was a Chinese poet of the Tang dynasty, revered as one of the greatest poets in Chinese history.

Li Bai (Chinese: 李白; pinyin: Lǐ Bái; 701–762), also known as Li Po, courtesy name Taibai (太白), art name Qinglian Jushi (青蓮居士), was a Chinese poet, acclaimed from his own time to the present as a brilliant and romantic figure who took traditional poetic forms to new heights.

Life

Li Bai spent much of his life travelling China, drinking wine, and writing poetry celebrating nature, friendship, and solitude. He served briefly at the court of Emperor Xuanzong in Chang'an around 742–744, but left and resumed his wanderings. He was implicated in a rebellion late in life and exiled, though later pardoned. He died in Dangtu, Anhui, in 762; legend holds that he drowned reaching for the moon's reflection in the Yangtze River.

Poetry

Around a thousand poems attributed to Li Bai survive, including the famous "Quiet Night Thought" (靜夜思) and "Drinking Alone by Moonlight". Along with Du Fu, he is considered one of the two greatest poets of the Tang dynasty. His work influenced poets across East Asia and, through translations, Western modernists such as Ezra Pound.
