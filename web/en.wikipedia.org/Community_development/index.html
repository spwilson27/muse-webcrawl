Community development

Community development is a process where community members come together to take collective action and generate solutions to common problems. It is a broad concept applied to the practices of civic leaders, activists, involved citizens, and professionals to improve various aspects of local communities, typically aiming to build stronger and more resilient local communities.

Definitions and scope

The United Nations defines community development as "a process where community members come together to take collective action and generate solutions to common problems." Community wellbeing — economic, social, environmental, and cultural — often evolves from this type of collective action being taken at a grassroots level. Community development ranges from small initiatives within a small group to large-scale interventions involving whole communities.

Approaches

Common approaches include asset-based community development, which maps and mobilizes existing local skills and resources; needs-based approaches, which target identified deficits; and participatory approaches that involve community members in planning, implementing, and evaluating projects. Practitioners work in areas such as affordable housing, public health, education, economic development, infrastructure, and environmental sustainability.

History

Community development emerged as a professional field in the mid-20th century, influenced by colonial administration programs, postwar reconstruction, settlement houses such as Hull House in Chicago, and the organizing traditions associated with Saul Alinsky. International development agencies, governments, and nonprofit organizations such as community development corporations (CDCs) in the United States now employ large numbers of community development workers.
