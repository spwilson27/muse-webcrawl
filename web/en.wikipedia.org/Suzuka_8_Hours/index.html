Suzuka 8 Hours

The Suzuka 8 Hours (Japanese: 鈴鹿8時間耐久ロードレース) is a motorcycle endurance race held annually at the Suzuka Circuit in Ino, Suzuka City, Mie Prefecture, Japan. First run in 1978, it is one of the most prestigious motorcycle races in the world and forms part of the FIM Endurance World Championship. The race is held over eight hours, typically in late July or early August, in the heat and humidity of the Japanese summer.

History

The race was established in 1978, when American riders Wes Cooley and Mike Baldwin won the inaugural event on a Suzuki. During the 1980s and 1990s the event grew into a major fixture of the Japanese racing calendar, attracting star riders from the Grand Prix and Superbike world championships, for whom victory at Suzuka carried enormous prestige with the Japanese manufacturers Honda, Yamaha, Suzuki and Kawasaki.

The race was notable for the participation of Valentino Rossi, who won in 2001 alongside Colin Edwards for Honda, and for multiple victories by riders such as Wayne Gardner, Mick Doohan and Tohru Ukawa, who holds the record for the most wins by a rider. Honda has historically been the most successful manufacturer at the event.

Format

Teams of two or three riders share a single motorcycle, alternating stints over the eight-hour duration. Pit stops for fuel, tyres and rider changes are a crucial part of the strategy. The race runs from late morning into the evening, finishing in darkness, and the combination of heat, traffic from slower teams and the technical Suzuka layout makes it one of the most demanding events in endurance racing.

Significance

The Suzuka 8 Hours is considered one of the "big three" motorcycle endurance races alongside the Bol d'Or and the Le Mans 24h Moto. Because the Japanese factories treat it as a home event of exceptional importance, factory entries are often stronger than at other rounds of the championship.
