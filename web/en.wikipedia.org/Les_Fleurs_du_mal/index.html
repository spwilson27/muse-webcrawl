Les Fleurs du mal

Les Fleurs du mal (The Flowers of Evil) is a volume of French poetry by Charles Baudelaire, first published in 1857. It was important in the symbolist and modernist movements, and its poems dealt frankly with themes of decadence and eroticism.

Author: Charles Baudelaire
Published: 1857
Language: French
Genre: Lyric poetry, Decadent literature

Publication and trial

On publication, Baudelaire and his publishers were prosecuted for obscenity. Six poems were suppressed and formally banned in France, a ban not lifted until 1949. The scandal enhanced the book's fame, and Baudelaire added 35 new poems to the expanded 1861 edition.

Themes and structure

The collection is organised into sections including "Spleen et Idéal", "Tableaux parisiens" and "La Mort". Baudelaire sought to extract beauty from horror, sin and the squalor of modern Paris — "flowers" grown from "evil". His preface imagines the reader as a "hypocrite lecteur, — mon semblable, — mon frère!". Famous poems include "L'Albatros", "Correspondances", "Hymne à la Beauté" and "Une Charogne".

Influence

The volume profoundly influenced French poetry, Verlaine and Rimbaud among others, and English-language poets including T. S. Eliot. It is generally considered one of the greatest collections of poetry in French literature.
