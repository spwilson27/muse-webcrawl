Glossary of mathematical symbols


Common mathematical notation.

A mathematical symbol is a figure or combination of figures used to represent a mathematical object, an action on mathematical objects, a relation between them, or to structure formulas. This glossary explains the meaning and use of symbols sorted by topic and by their introduction into mathematics.

Set theory and logic

Symbols such as ∈ (is an element of), ⊆ (subset), ∪ (union), ∩ (intersection), ∅ (empty set), ∀ (for all), ∃ (there exists), ⇒ (implies) and ⇔ (if and only if) form the vocabulary of modern set theory and mathematical logic.

Calculus and analysis

The integral sign ∫, the summation sign Σ, the product sign Π, infinity ∞, the partial derivative ∂ and the limit notation lim are standard in analysis. Greek letters such as π, ε, δ and φ are used extensively as constants and variables.

History

Mathematical notation developed over centuries: the + and − signs appeared in the 15th century, = was introduced by Robert Recorde in 1557, and much of the modern symbolic apparatus was standardised by the 18th and 19th centuries, notably through the work of Leibniz and Euler.
