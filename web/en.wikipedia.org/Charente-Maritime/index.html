Charente-Maritime

Charente-Maritime (French pronunciation: [ʃaʁɑ̃t maʁitim] ⓘ; Poitevin-Saintongeais: Chérente-Marine; Occitan: Charanta Maritima) is a department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region on the southwestern coast of France. Named after the river Charente and its maritime border with the Atlantic Ocean, its prefecture is La Rochelle. It had a population of about 660,000 in the early 2020s.

History

Previously a part of Saintonge and Aunis, Charente-Inférieure was one of the 83 original departments created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790. On 4 September 1941 it was renamed Charente-Maritime to avoid the supposedly pejorative connotation of "inférieure" (lower) and to reflect its Atlantic coastline. Saintes was the prefecture until 1810, when La Rochelle took over.

Geography

The department lies on the Atlantic coast and includes several islands: Île de Ré, Île d'Aix, Île d'Oléron, and Île Madame. It borders the departments of Gironde, Charente, Deux-Sèvres, and Vendée. Major towns include La Rochelle, Saintes, Rochefort, and Royan. The Gironde estuary, the largest estuary in Western Europe, forms part of its southern boundary.

Economy and tourism

Charente-Maritime is one of France's most popular tourist destinations, known for its beaches, oyster farming (Marennes-Oléron oysters are famous), Cognac production in the east, and historic port towns. The Zoo de la Palmyre near Royan is one of the most visited zoos in France, and Fort Boyard, off the Île d'Aix, became known internationally through the television game show of the same name.
