National League (division)

The National League is the fifth tier of the English football league system and the top division of the National League, the competition formerly known as the Football Conference. It sits immediately below the English Football League (League Two) and above the National League North and National League South.

History

The competition was founded in 1979 as the Alliance Premier League, uniting leading clubs from the Northern Premier League and Southern League to create a single national non-League division. Automatic promotion to the Football League began in 1987, ending the old re-election system for the bottom league clubs; a second promotion place via play-offs was added in 2003. It was rebranded as the National League in 2015 and has carried sponsor names including Blue Square, Skrill, Vanarama and Enterprise.

Promotion and relegation

The champions are promoted automatically to League Two, with a second club promoted through play-offs involving the next six teams. The bottom four clubs are relegated to the National League North or South, depending on geography. Notable former members that climbed into the Football League include Wimbledon, Wigan Athletic (via the old system), Luton Town and Wrexham.
