Croscore fonts

The Croscore fonts are a collection of open-source typefaces developed by Google and designed by Steve Matteson of Ascender Corporation. They were created to be metrically compatible with three widely used Microsoft core fonts — Arial, Times New Roman, and Courier New — so that documents render with identical line breaks and pagination when the proprietary fonts are unavailable. The fonts are distributed under the Apache License 2.0 and are widely used in ChromeOS and Linux distributions.

The fonts

The collection comprises three families: Arimo, a humanist sans-serif metrically compatible with Arial; Tinos, a serif design compatible with Times New Roman; and Cousine, a monospace design compatible with Courier New. Each is available in regular, bold, italic, and bold italic styles and supports Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic scripts.

Use

Because of their metric compatibility and free licence, the Croscore fonts serve as drop-in substitutes in office software such as LibreOffice when documents created with Microsoft fonts are opened on systems without them. They are related to the broader "core fonts for the Web" tradition and to Google's later Noto and ChromeOS font projects.
