News magazine

FormatPrint or digital periodical, usually weekly
ExamplesTime, The Economist, Newsweek, Der Spiegel, The Week
ContentCurrent events, analysis, commentary

A news magazine is a typed, printed, and published magazine, radio or television programme, usually published weekly, consisting of articles about current events. News magazines generally discuss stories in greater depth than newspapers do, and aim to give the reader an understanding of the important events beyond the basic facts.

History

The modern news magazine was pioneered in the United States by Henry Luce and Briton Hadden, who founded Time in 1923 to summarise the week's news for busy readers. Newsweek followed in 1933 and U.S. News & World Report in 1948. In Britain, The Economist (founded 1843) styles itself a newspaper but follows the magazine format, while The Spectator and New Statesman combine news with commentary. Germany's Der Spiegel, founded in 1947, became known for investigative reporting.

Television and radio

The term also applies to broadcast formats such as 60 Minutes and Dateline, which present long-form reported segments. Many print titles have shifted to digital-first or digital-only publication as print circulations declined in the 21st century — see magazine for the broader format.
