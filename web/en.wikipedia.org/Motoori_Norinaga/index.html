Motoori Norinaga (本居 宣長; 21 June 1730 – 5 October 1801) was a Japanese scholar of kokugaku (national learning) active during the Edo period. He is best known for his monumental commentary on the Kojiki, the Kojiki-den, which occupied him for some 35 years.

Life and work

Born in Matsusaka in Ise Province, Norinaga studied medicine in Kyoto and practised as a physician while pursuing classical studies. A brief meeting with the kokugaku scholar Kamo no Mabuchi in 1763 inspired him to devote himself to the study of Japan's ancient texts. Rejecting what he saw as the distortions of Confucian and Buddhist ("Chinese") modes of thought, Norinaga sought the authentic spirit of ancient Japan through philological study of the Kojiki and early poetry such as the Man'yōshū.

Ideas

Norinaga praised the aesthetic sensibility of mono no aware — a sensitivity to the pathos of things — which he developed in his studies of The Tale of Genji. His work elevated the status of the imperial institution and native Shinto and profoundly influenced later nativist thought and, indirectly, the ideology of the Meiji Restoration.
