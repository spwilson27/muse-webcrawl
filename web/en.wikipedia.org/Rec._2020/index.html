ITU-R Recommendation BT.2020, commonly known as Rec. 2020, is a set of standards for ultra-high-definition television (UHDTV) published by the International Telecommunication Union in 2012. It defines picture parameters including resolution, frame rate, bit depth, and—most notably—a very wide colour gamut far exceeding that of the earlier Rec. 709 standard used for HDTV.

Colour space

The Rec. 2020 colour space uses monochromatic primary colours at wavelengths of 630 nm (red), 532 nm (green), and 467 nm (blue) with the CIE D65 white point. Its triangular gamut covers about 75.8% of the CIE 1931 colour space visible to the human eye, compared with about 35.9% for Rec. 709. Because the primaries are monochromatic, no physical display can fully reproduce the gamut, but modern wide-gamut displays—often marketed by their coverage of the DCI-P3 space—approach it increasingly closely. Rec. 2020 also specifies a non-constant luminance YCbCr encoding and the PQ and HLG electro-optical transfer functions are associated with it through Rec. 2100 for HDR television.

Resolutions and frame rates

Rec. 2020 defines two resolutions: 3840×2160 ("4K UHD") and 7680×4320 ("8K UHD"), both with a 16:9 aspect ratio and square pixels. Frame rates range from 23.976 to 120 frames per second, progressive scan only. Bit depth is 10 or 12 bits per sample.

Adoption

Rec. 2020 is used as the container colour space for most 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray discs and HDR streaming content, even though current consumer displays cannot reproduce its full gamut. The Japanese 8K broadcasts by NHK are based on the standard.
