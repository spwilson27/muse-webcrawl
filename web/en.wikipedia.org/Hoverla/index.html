Hoverla

Mount Hoverla
Ukrainian: Говерла
Elevation: 2,061 m (6,762 ft)
Range: Chornohora, Carpathian Mountains
Location: Ivano-Frankivsk / Zakarpattia Oblasts, Ukraine
First ascent: 1880

Hoverla (Ukrainian: Говерла; Hungarian: Hóvár) is the highest mountain in Ukraine, with an elevation of 2,061 metres (6,762 ft) above sea level. It lies in the Chornohora range of the Eastern Carpathians, on the border between Ivano-Frankivsk and Zakarpattia oblasts, within the Carpathian National Nature Park.

Geography

The mountain has a distinctive domed, pyramidal form. Its slopes are covered with beech and spruce forests up to the treeline, above which lie subalpine meadows (polonyna). Snow can persist on the summit well into summer, and the peak is frequently shrouded in cloud. The sources of the Prut River lie on its slopes, including the Prut waterfall.

Tourism and symbolism

Hoverla is Ukraine's most popular mountain climb, with marked trails ascending from the Zarosliak sports base; the ascent takes roughly two to three hours. Since Ukrainian independence, climbing Hoverla on national holidays has become a patriotic tradition, and a Ukrainian flag and trident emblem mark the summit, together with a cross and a plaque commemorating the proclamation of the Carpatho-Ukrainian state in 1939. The first recorded ascent was in 1880.
