The Magnificent Mile, sometimes shortened to the Mag Mile, is an upscale section of Chicago's Michigan Avenue, running from the Chicago River to Oak Street in the Near North Side. The district is home to luxury shops, department stores, hotels, restaurants, and some of Chicago's tallest and most famous buildings. The name was coined by real-estate developer Arthur Rubloff in the 1940s as part of a promotional campaign.

History

Michigan Avenue north of the river was originally the quiet, residential Pine Street. The opening of the Michigan Avenue Bridge (now the DuSable Bridge) in 1920 linked the street to the Loop and transformed it, as envisioned by Daniel Burnham's 1909 Plan of Chicago, into a grand commercial boulevard. Landmark buildings rose quickly: the Wrigley Building (1921–24), Tribune Tower (1925), and the Allerton Hotel. After the Second World War, Rubloff's development vision replaced mansions with modern retail and office towers, cementing the avenue's identity as a premier shopping street. The John Hancock Center (1969) and Water Tower Place (1975) anchored its northern end.

Landmarks

  • Chicago Water Tower (1869) — one of the few structures to survive the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, at the avenue's northern section.
  • Wrigley Building and Tribune Tower — facing each other across the river at the district's southern gateway.
  • 875 North Michigan Avenue (the former John Hancock Center) — a 100-storey X-braced skyscraper with an observation deck.
  • Water Tower Place and 900 North Michigan — vertical malls anchors.
  • Drake Hotel — historic hotel at the northern end, by Lake Michigan.

Retail

The Magnificent Mile hosts flagship stores for international luxury brands alongside department stores such as Bloomingdale's and Nordstrom, and it is routinely ranked among the most prestigious retail streets in the United States. Retail rents on the avenue have historically been among the nation's highest outside New York's Fifth Avenue.

Events

The district is the site of the Magnificent Mile Lights Festival, held each November, in which more than a million lights along the avenue are illuminated with a parade and fireworks over the river, marking the start of Chicago's holiday season.
