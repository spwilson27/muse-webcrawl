Coffee

Coffee is a beverage brewed from roasted coffee beans. Darkly colored, bitter, and slightly acidic, coffee has a stimulating effect on humans, primarily due to its caffeine content. It has the highest sales in the world market for hot drinks. The seeds of the Coffea plant's fruits are separated to produce unroasted green coffee beans. The beans are roasted and then ground into fine particles that are typically steeped in hot water before being filtered out, producing a cup of coffee. It is usually served hot, although chilled or iced coffee is common.

History

Coffee drinking originated in the Ethiopian highlands, where legend credits a goatherd named Kaldi with noticing the energizing effect of the cherries. By the 15th century coffee was cultivated and traded in Yemen, and coffee houses spread through Mecca, Cairo, and Constantinople. Venetian merchants brought coffee to Europe in the 17th century; despite initial suspicion, it was blessed by Pope Clement VIII, and coffee houses flourished as centres of news and debate—London's became known as "penny universities." Colonial powers spread cultivation to the Caribbean, Latin America, and Asia, making coffee a global commodity built historically on enslaved and exploited labour.

Production

Brazil is the world's largest producer, followed by Vietnam and Colombia. After harvesting—by hand stripping or selective picking—the seeds are extracted by dry or washed processing, dried, milled, and exported as green coffee. Roasting transforms the beans chemically, developing aroma and flavour; ground coffee is brewed by methods including espresso, filter, French press, and Turkish preparation. Decaffeinated coffee removes most caffeine, and instant coffee is produced by freeze- or spray-drying brewed extract.

Effects and culture

Caffeine is a central nervous system stimulant that increases alertness and reduces fatigue; moderate coffee consumption is associated in observational research with several health benefits, though excessive intake can cause insomnia and anxiety. Coffee is woven into social rituals worldwide, from Ethiopian coffee ceremonies to Swedish fika and Italian espresso bars, and its trade raises ongoing debates about fair prices and sustainability for growers.
