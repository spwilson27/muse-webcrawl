Comirnaty

TypemRNA vaccine
DevelopersBioNTech and Pfizer
TargetSARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19)
First authorisedDecember 2020 (United Kingdom)

Comirnaty is the brand name of the COVID-19 vaccine developed by the German company BioNTech in partnership with the American pharmaceutical firm Pfizer. Also known by its development code BNT162b2, it was the first mRNA vaccine ever authorised for use in humans.

Technology

The vaccine delivers messenger RNA encoding the spike protein of the SARS-CoV-2 virus, packaged in lipid nanoparticles that carry it into cells. The recipient's cells temporarily produce the spike protein, prompting an immune response that prepares the body to fight the real virus. The mRNA does not enter the cell nucleus or alter DNA and is broken down within days.

Authorisation and use

Following a phase III trial showing about 95 per cent efficacy against symptomatic COVID-19, the United Kingdom authorised the vaccine on 2 December 2020, with the United States and European Union following within weeks. It became one of the most widely administered COVID-19 vaccines worldwide, with billions of doses delivered, and was later adapted into bivalent formulations targeting Omicron variants. Reported side effects are mostly mild, such as sore arm and fatigue; rare cases of myocarditis, mainly in young males, have been documented.
