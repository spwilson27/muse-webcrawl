Kingston is a city in Ontario, Canada, on the northeastern shore of Lake Ontario, at the mouth of the St. Lawrence River and the southern entrance to the Rideau Canal. Located midway between Toronto and Montreal, it is known as the "Limestone City" for its many 19th-century buildings constructed from local limestone.

History

The site was an Indigenous settlement and meeting place long before European contact. In 1673 the French built Fort Frontenac there; the fort was destroyed and rebuilt several times in the Anglo-French struggle for North America, and was captured by the British in 1758 during the Seven Years' War. United Empire Loyalists resettled the town in 1783–84, and Kingston was named in honour of King George III. Its strategic position made it a major naval base on Lake Ontario during the War of 1812, and Fort Henry was built to guard the dockyards.

Kingston was chosen as the first capital of the united Province of Canada in 1841; Sir John A. Macdonald, Canada's first prime minister, was Kingston's long-serving MP and is buried in the city. Kingston Penitentiary, opened in 1835, was for generations Canada's most famous maximum-security prison before closing in 2013.

Present day

Modern Kingston's economy is anchored by the public sector: Queen's University (founded 1841), the Royal Military College of Canada, St. Lawrence College, Kingston General Hospital, and Canadian Forces Base Kingston. Fort Henry and the Kingston Fortifications form part of the Rideau Canal UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city is a gateway to the Thousand Islands, and ferries link it to Wolfe Island.
