Baahubali 2: The Conclusion is a 2017 Indian epic action film directed by S. S. Rajamouli, who co-wrote the film with V. Vijayendra Prasad. It is the second and concluding part of the Baahubali franchise, following Baahubali: The Beginning (2015), and was shot simultaneously in Telugu and Tamil. The film stars Prabhas in the title role, alongside Rana Daggubati, Anushka Shetty, Tamannaah Bhatia, Ramya Krishna and Sathyaraj.

Plot

The film continues the story of the kingdom of Mahishmati. Shiva (Mahendra Baahubali), raised among a tribal community, learns of his royal heritage and hears the story of his father Amarendra Baahubali, a beloved prince betrayed and killed on the orders of his jealous cousin Bhallaladeva, at the hands of the loyal slave-warrior Kattappa. The film answers the first part's famous cliffhanger — "Why did Kattappa kill Baahubali?" — before Shiva returns to Mahishmati to defeat Bhallaladeva and free his mother Devasena, who has been held captive for 25 years.

Production

The two films were produced together by Arka Media Works with a combined budget reported at around ₹430 crore, making the project the most expensive Indian film production at the time. The Conclusion was filmed largely at Ramoji Film City in Hyderabad, with extensive visual effects work by Indian and international studios.

Release and reception

Baahubali 2 was released worldwide on 28 April 2017 on more than 9,000 screens. It received highly positive reviews for Rajamouli's direction, the performances and the visual effects. The film grossed over ₹1,800 crore (approximately US$270 million) worldwide, becoming the highest-grossing Indian film of all time at that point, and it remains among the highest-grossing Indian films ever. Its Hindi dubbed version set records for a dubbed film in the Hindi market. The film won several National Film Awards, including Best Popular Film Providing Wholesome Entertainment.

Legacy

The film's success is credited with expanding the pan-Indian market for big-budget regional films and demonstrating that a film in a South Indian language could achieve record-breaking success across India and overseas.
