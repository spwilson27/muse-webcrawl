Annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Date20 February – 26 March 2014 (recognised by Russia from 18 March 2014)
LocationCrimean Peninsula, Ukraine
ResultRussian annexation of the Autonomous Republic of Crimea and Sevastopol; not recognised by most of the international community
PartiesRussian Federation; Ukraine

The annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation took place in February and March 2014, during the aftermath of the Ukrainian Revolution of Dignity. Russian forces without insignia — widely dubbed "little green men" — seized control of strategic sites across the Crimean Peninsula, including the parliament in Simferopol, airports, and Ukrainian military bases. A referendum held on 16 March 2014 under Russian military presence reported an overwhelming vote in favour of joining Russia, and on 18 March a treaty of accession was signed in Moscow incorporating the Republic of Crimea and the federal city of Sevastopol into the Russian Federation.

The annexation was condemned by the United Nations General Assembly in Resolution 68/262, which affirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity and declared the referendum invalid. Most countries continue to regard Crimea as Ukrainian territory occupied by Russia, and the annexation triggered international sanctions against Russia and its expulsion from the G8.

Background

Crimea had been transferred from the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic to the Ukrainian SSR in 1954 by decree of Nikita Khrushchev. After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, Crimea remained within Ukraine as an autonomous republic, while Russia's Black Sea Fleet remained based at Sevastopol under a leasing agreement extended in 2010 by the Kharkiv Pact. The peninsula's population was majority ethnic Russian, and its status was a recurring source of tension between Kyiv and Moscow.

Events

Following the flight of President Viktor Yanukovych on 22 February 2014, armed men seized the Crimean parliament building on 27 February and a new pro-Russian regional government was installed under Sergey Aksyonov. Ukrainian bases were blockaded, and the referendum date was moved forward twice. On 18 March 2014, President Vladimir Putin signed the accession treaty; the Russian Constitutional Court approved it the next day and the Federation Council ratified it on 21 March.

International reaction

The United States, the European Union, and other states imposed sanctions on Russian officials and entities. The UN General Assembly vote on 27 March 2014 recorded 100 nations in favour of Ukraine's territorial integrity, 11 against, and 58 abstentions. Russia justified its actions by citing the protection of Russian speakers and the precedent of Kosovo's declaration of independence.

Aftermath

Russia integrated Crimea into its Southern Federal District, built the Crimean Bridge across the Kerch Strait (opened to road traffic in 2018), and faced persistent issues including water supply after Ukraine blocked the North Crimean Canal. The annexation formed part of the wider Russo-Ukrainian War that began in 2014 and escalated with Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.
