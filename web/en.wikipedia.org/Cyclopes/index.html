Cyclopes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cyclopes (singular Cyclops; Greek: Κύκλωπες, "circle-eyed") are a race of giants in Greek and Roman mythology, each having a single eye in the middle of the forehead. The name is commonly taken to mean "round eye" or "circle eye".

The three traditions

Ancient authors describe three distinct groups:

  • The primordial Cyclopes of Hesiod's Theogony — Brontes, Steropes, and Arges, sons of Uranus and Gaia, who forged Zeus's thunderbolts.
  • The pastoral Cyclopes of Homer's Odyssey — lawless shepherd giants, most famously Polyphemus, who was blinded by Odysseus.
  • The builder Cyclopes — legendary masons credited with the massive "Cyclopean" walls of Mycenae and Tiryns.

Origins and interpretations

Scholars have proposed that the Cyclops myth may have been inspired by the fossil skulls of dwarf elephants found in Sicily and other Mediterranean islands, whose large central nasal cavity could be mistaken for a single eye socket. Others connect the smith-Cyclopes with early metalworking communities. The Cyclopes remain among the most recognisable figures of Greek myth, frequently reinterpreted in later literature, art, and film.
