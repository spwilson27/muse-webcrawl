Medical sign

A medical sign is an objective indication of a disease, injury, or abnormal physiological state that may be detected during a physical examination of a patient. These signs are visible or otherwise detectable such as a rash or bruise. Medical signs, or clinical signs, are distinguished from symptoms, which are subjective experiences the patient reports, such as pain or fatigue.

Examples

Classic signs include fever measured by thermometer, hypertension measured with a sphygmomanometer, clubbing of the fingers, jaundice, Kernig's sign in meningitis, Babinski's sign in upper motor neuron lesions, and Murphy's sign in acute cholecystitis. Many signs are eponymous, named after the clinicians who described them. Vital signs — body temperature, pulse, respiratory rate and blood pressure — are the most routinely recorded.

Signs versus symptoms

Some features can be both: nausea is a symptom, while vomiting observed by a clinician is a sign. A constellation of signs and symptoms characteristic of a particular disease is called a syndrome. Eliciting and interpreting signs remains a core clinical skill, supplemented by laboratory tests and imaging.
