The Durham Bulls are a Minor League Baseball team of the International League and the Triple-A affiliate of the Tampa Bay Rays. They are located in Durham, North Carolina, and play their home games at Durham Bulls Athletic Park, which opened in 1995. The team is best known internationally for the 1988 film Bull Durham, which starred Kevin Costner, Susan Sarandon, and Tim Robbins and was filmed largely at the team's former home, Durham Athletic Park.

History

Baseball in Durham dates to 1902, when the Durham Tobacconists played in the North Carolina League. The Bulls name was adopted in 1913, referencing the city's famous Bull Durham tobacco brand. Over the decades the club played in various leagues, including the Piedmont League and the Carolina League, and was affiliated with several major league organizations, most notably the Atlanta Braves during the era of the Bull Durham film.

In 1998 the Bulls moved up to Triple-A as an expansion franchise in the International League, aligning with the then-new Tampa Bay Devil Rays. The Bulls won the Governors' Cup, the International League championship, in 2002, 2009, 2013, 2017, 2018, and 2022, and captured the Triple-A National Championship in 2009 and 2021.

Ballparks

The Bulls played at Durham Athletic Park from 1926 until 1994; the park, built in 1939 after a fire destroyed the original grandstand, became a beloved landmark after the film's success. In 1995 the team moved to the new Durham Bulls Athletic Park downtown, designed by the firm HOK, with a 10,000-seat capacity and a signature bull sign beyond the left-field wall whose eyes light up and tail wags when a Bull hits a home run.

In popular culture

The 1988 romantic comedy Bull Durham, written and directed by Ron Shelton, a former minor league player, is widely regarded as one of the greatest sports films ever made. Its success dramatically raised the profile of both the team and minor league baseball generally in the late 1980s.
