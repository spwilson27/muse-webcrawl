El Alto International Airport (Spanish: Aeropuerto Internacional El Alto) is an international airport serving La Paz, the seat of government of Bolivia, located in the neighbouring city of El Alto. At an elevation of 4,061 metres (13,325 ft) above sea level, it is one of the highest international airports in the world.

Operations at altitude

The thin air at El Alto reduces engine performance and lift, so aircraft require unusually long take-off runs — the main runway is 4,000 metres long — and often operate with payload restrictions. Wide-body aircraft historically used the airport for certification testing of high-altitude performance, and only specially rated crews and aircraft with modified oxygen systems serve some routes.

History

An airfield existed at El Alto from the early twentieth century; the modern airport was inaugurated in 1965 and has since been extensively renovated. It was a hub for the former national airline Lloyd Aéreo Boliviano and is now the principal hub of Boliviana de Aviación (BoA).

Access

The airport is connected to La Paz by motorway and by the Mi Teleférico cable-car network, the Yellow and Purple lines serving stations near the terminal. Passenger numbers are roughly two million per year, with domestic services to Santa Cruz, Cochabamba and Sucre and international links within South America and to Madrid.
