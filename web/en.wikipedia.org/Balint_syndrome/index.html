Bálint syndrome

Bálint syndrome is a rare neurological disorder caused by bilateral damage to the posterior parietal and occipital cortex, described by the Hungarian neurologist Rezső Bálint in 1909. It is characterized by a triad of visuospatial deficits.

The triad

The three cardinal features are simultanagnosia (inability to perceive more than one object at a time), optic ataxia (inability to guide hand movements using visual information), and ocular apraxia (inability to voluntarily direct gaze). Patients may fail to notice most of a scene despite intact elementary vision.

Causes

Common causes include watershed strokes affecting both parietal lobes, posterior cortical atrophy, and certain neurodegenerative diseases.
