Epirus (region)

Epirus is a geographical and historical region in southeastern Europe, now shared between Greece and Albania. It lies between the Pindus Mountains to the east and the Ionian Sea to the west. The modern Greek region of Epirus has its capital at Ioannina; the northern part of historical Epirus, sometimes called Northern Epirus, lies in southern Albania.

The Vikos Gorge in the Zagori area of Epirus, Greece

History

In antiquity Epirus was inhabited by Greek tribes including the Molossians, Thesprotians, and Chaonians, and was home to the oracle of Zeus at Dodona, one of the oldest in Greece. Under King Pyrrhus of Epirus (319/318–272 BC), whose costly victories over Rome gave rise to the phrase "Pyrrhic victory", the region briefly became a major power. It later passed through Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman rule; the medieval Despotate of Epirus was one of the successor states of the Byzantine Empire after 1204.

Southern Epirus became part of Greece in 1913 after the Balkan Wars. The mountainous terrain, stone-built villages of Zagori, and the Vikos–Aoös National Park are noted features of the Greek region today.

Geography

Epirus is rugged and mountainous, with the Pindus range forming its spine. Major rivers include the Acheron, Arachthos, and Aoös (Vjosë). The climate is wetter than most of Greece, supporting dense forests.
