The fancy rat (Rattus norvegicus domestica) is the domesticated form of the brown rat, and the most common species of rat kept as a pocket pet. The name "fancy rat" derives from the idea of animal fancy, or the promotion of domesticated animals. Fancy rats are bred in a wide variety of coat colours and patterns, including hooded, Berkshire, albino, and hairless (sphynx) varieties, and in two ear types: standard and "Dumbo," with larger, lower-set ears.

History

Rat fancy began in 18th- and 19th-century Britain, where rat-catchers such as Jack Black supplied unusual-coloured rats caught or bred from the animals used in rat-baiting pits to wealthy customers as pets. The world's first rat fancy organization, the National Mouse and Rat Club, was founded in England in 1912. Today, rat clubs and shows exist worldwide, and fancy rats are popular companion animals valued for their intelligence, cleanliness, and sociability.

Health and behaviour

Fancy rats typically live two to three years. They are social animals and are generally kept in same-sex pairs or groups. Common health problems include respiratory infections (often associated with Mycoplasma pulmonis), mammary and pituitary tumours in older females, and hind-limb degeneration in aged rats. Rats are highly trainable and can learn their names, come when called, and perform simple tricks; laboratory studies show they display empathy-like helping behaviour toward cage mates.

Care

Fancy rats require a large, well-ventilated wire cage with multiple levels, a staple diet of formulated rat blocks supplemented with fresh foods, free-roaming exercise time, and enrichment such as hammocks, tubes, and chew toys. Because they are prey animals, they need hiding places and gentle handling from a young age.
