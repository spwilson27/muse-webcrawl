Synchronized swimming

Synchronized swimming (artistic swimming)
Olympic debut: Los Angeles 1984
Governing body: World Aquatics
Events: Duet, team, solo, mixed duet

Synchronized swimming, officially renamed artistic swimming in 2017, is a hybrid form of swimming, dance, and gymnastics in which swimmers perform choreographed routines of acrobatic movements in the water, accompanied by music, in perfect unison with one another.

History

The sport developed from "water ballet" performances in the early twentieth century and was popularized in the United States by swimmer and film star Esther Williams. Katherine Curtis organized early competitions and helped codify rules. Synchronized swimming became an Olympic sport at the 1984 Los Angeles Games; long dominated by the United States, Canada, Russia, and later China and Japan, the programme includes women's duet and team events, and men became eligible in team competition from the Paris 2024 Games.

Competition

Routines are judged on execution, artistic impression, and difficulty. Competitors must hold demanding positions — verticals, lifts, throws, and hybrids — while never touching the pool bottom, requiring exceptional breath control, strength, flexibility, and timing. A 2017 rebrand by the world governing body renamed the sport "artistic swimming," though the older name remains widely used.
