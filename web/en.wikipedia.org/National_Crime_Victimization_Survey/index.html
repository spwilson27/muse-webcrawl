National Crime Victimization Survey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The National Crime Victimization Survey (NCVS) is a national survey conducted by the United States Bureau of Justice Statistics (BJS), with data collected by the U.S. Census Bureau. It is the primary source of information on the frequency, characteristics, and consequences of criminal victimisation in the United States, and one of the government's two main crime data collections alongside the FBI's police-reported statistics.

Methodology

The NCVS surveys a nationally representative rotating sample of households — roughly 150,000 households and over 240,000 persons aged 12 and older in recent designs. Respondents are interviewed every six months for three years about victimisations they experienced in the preceding six months, including crimes not reported to the police. This allows the survey to measure the "dark figure" of unreported crime.

Coverage and findings

The survey covers violent crimes (rape and sexual assault, robbery, aggravated and simple assault) and property crimes (burglary, motor vehicle theft, and other theft). It collects details on offenders, weapons, injuries, and whether the crime was reported. NCVS data show that a large share of violent victimisations — typically between 40 and 60 percent depending on the crime — are never reported to police.

History

The survey began in 1973 as the National Crime Survey and was substantially redesigned in 1992, when its name changed to the National Crime Victimization Survey and its screening questions and methods were improved, notably increasing measured rates of sexual assault and domestic violence.
