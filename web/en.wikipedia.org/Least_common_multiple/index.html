Least common multiple

In arithmetic and number theory, the least common multiple (LCM, also lowest common multiple or smallest common multiple) of two integers a and b is the smallest positive integer that is divisible by both a and b. For example, the least common multiple of 4 and 6 is 12, since 12 is the smallest number that is a multiple of both.

The LCM can be computed from prime factorisations — taking each prime to the highest power appearing in either number — or via the greatest common divisor using the identity lcm(a, b) = |a × b| / gcd(a, b). The concept extends to any finite set of integers and, more generally, to elements of a commutative ring.

Least common multiples are used when adding or subtracting fractions with different denominators, where the least common denominator is the LCM of the denominators, and in problems involving periodic events coinciding.
