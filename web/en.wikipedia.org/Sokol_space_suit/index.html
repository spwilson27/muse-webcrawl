Sokol space suit

The Sokol (Russian: Сокол, "falcon") is a series of Soviet and Russian space suits used by crews aboard Soyuz spacecraft. Introduced in 1973, the Sokol is a rescue pressure suit: it is worn during launch, docking, and landing to protect the wearer in the event of accidental cabin depressurization, but it is not designed for spacewalks (extravehicular activity), which use the Orlan suit instead.

History

Development of a rescue suit followed the Soyuz 11 tragedy of 1971, in which three cosmonauts died when their capsule depressurized during re-entry; the crew had flown without pressure suits. The Sokol-K was developed by NPP Zvezda and first flown on Soyuz 12 in 1973. Successive versions — Sokol-KR, Sokol-KM, and Sokol-KV — improved comfort and operation, culminating in the Sokol-KV2, which has been standard since Soyuz TM and remains in use on Soyuz MS flights to the International Space Station.

Description

The Sokol-KV2 consists of an inner pressure bladder of rubberized fabric and an outer layer of white nylon canvas. It has an integral soft helmet with a clear polycarbonate visor that opens upward. The suit is entered through an opening in the torso that is sealed by rolling and tying the fabric, and it connects to the spacecraft's life-support system through hoses that supply oxygen and cooling air. Gloves are detachable, and boots are integral; leather boots are worn over the suit for walking before launch.

The suit weighs about 10 kilograms (22 lb) and is custom-fitted to each crew member, who also has a molded seat liner in the Soyuz descent module.

Operational use

Crews wear the Sokol during dynamic flight phases. It has protected crews in real emergencies, notably Soyuz MS-10 in 2018, when the crew survived an in-flight booster failure and ballistic re-entry. Every person flying on Soyuz — including space tourists and visiting astronauts of other nationalities — wears a Sokol suit.
