Peanut butter

Peanut butter is a food paste or spread made from ground, dry-roasted peanuts. It commonly contains additional ingredients that modify the taste or texture, such as salt, sweeteners, or emulsifiers. Consumed in many countries, it is especially popular in the United States, where it is a staple sandwich filling—famously paired with jelly.

Peanut butter
Type: Spread
Main ingredient: Peanuts
Origin: North America (modern form, late 19th century)

History

Ground peanut pastes were made by the Aztecs and Incas. The modern product emerged in the late 19th century: Marcellus Gilmore Edson patented a peanut paste in 1884, and John Harvey Kellogg patented a process for making peanut butter from raw peanuts in 1895. It was popularized at the 1904 St. Louis World's Fair.

Production and types

Peanuts are roasted, cooled, blanched and ground. "Crunchy" varieties contain coarsely ground fragments; "smooth" varieties are uniformly ground. Natural peanut butter, containing only peanuts and sometimes salt, tends to separate and requires stirring.

Nutrition

Peanut butter is energy-dense, rich in protein, monounsaturated fat, vitamin E, magnesium and niacin. It is also a major allergen for those with peanut allergy.
