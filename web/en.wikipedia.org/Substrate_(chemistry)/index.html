Substrate (chemistry)

In chemistry, a substrate is the chemical species that is observed in, or acted upon by, a chemical reaction — typically the molecule that a catalyst or enzyme transforms. The usage is somewhat context-dependent, but the substrate is generally the reactant whose conversion the reaction is about; the substance produced is the product.

In biochemistry

In enzymology, the substrate is the molecule upon which an enzyme acts. The substrate binds to the enzyme's active site, forming an enzyme–substrate complex, and is converted to one or more products, which are released so the enzyme can repeat the cycle. Enzymes are typically highly specific for their substrates — a specificity explained first by Emil Fischer's "lock and key" model and refined by the induced-fit model. The dependence of reaction rate on substrate concentration is described by Michaelis–Menten kinetics, characterized by the constants Vmax and Km.

In general and surface chemistry

In organic synthesis, "substrate" often simply denotes the principal starting compound of interest, as distinct from reagents added to transform it. In heterogeneous catalysis and surface science, the substrate may instead refer to the solid surface on which reactions occur or films are deposited — a sense shared with materials science and electronics, where a substrate is the base material (such as a silicon wafer) supporting a device or coating.
