No-communication theorem

In physics, the no-communication theorem (also called the no-signalling principle) is a no-go theorem from quantum information theory which states that, during measurement of an entangled quantum state, it is not possible for one observer, by making a measurement of a subsystem of the total state, to communicate information to another observer.

Significance

Although quantum entanglement produces correlations between distant measurements that cannot be explained classically — as shown by Bell's theorem — the theorem shows these correlations cannot be exploited to transmit information faster than light. The outcomes at each side are individually random; only when the two sets of results are later compared through a classical, light-speed-limited channel do the correlations appear. The theorem is thus essential for the compatibility of quantum mechanics with special relativity, and it underlies the security analysis of quantum communication protocols.
