Refeeding syndrome

Refeeding syndrome is a metabolic disturbance that occurs when food is reintroduced too rapidly after a period of starvation or severe undernourishment. It is characterised by potentially fatal shifts in fluids and electrolytes, particularly a sharp fall in serum phosphate (hypophosphataemia).

Mechanism

During starvation the body adapts to fat metabolism and intracellular electrolyte stores are depleted. Renewed carbohydrate intake triggers insulin release, driving phosphate, potassium and magnesium into cells and increasing thiamine demand. The resulting imbalances can cause cardiac failure, respiratory failure, seizures and death.

Prevention and treatment

The syndrome was recognised after the Second World War, when starved prisoners and famine victims died after rapid refeeding. Prevention involves identifying at-risk patients — including those with anorexia nervosa, chronic alcoholism or prolonged fasting — starting nutrition slowly, and supplementing phosphate, potassium, magnesium and thiamine with close monitoring.
