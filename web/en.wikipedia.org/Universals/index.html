Universals

In philosophy, universals are general entities — properties, kinds and relations — that can be instantiated by many particular things. The redness shared by many red objects, or the humanness shared by many people, would be universals, contrasted with the particulars (individual objects) that exemplify them.

The problem of universals

The problem of universals asks how different objects can share the same property, and what ontological status such shared features have. Plato held that universals (Forms) exist independently in a separate realm. Aristotle located forms within things themselves. Medieval scholasticism debated the issue intensely.

Positions

Realism affirms that universals exist, either before things (Platonism), in things (Aristotelian realism) or as abstract objects. Nominalism denies that universals exist, explaining resemblance by names, concepts or primitive similarity. Conceptualism places universals in the mind as concepts. The debate remains central in metaphysics and the philosophy of language.
