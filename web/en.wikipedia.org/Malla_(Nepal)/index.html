Malla (Nepal)

The Malla dynasty was a ruling dynasty of the Kathmandu Valley in Nepal, reigning from the 13th century until 1769. The Malla period is often described as a golden age of Nepalese art, architecture, and culture.

History

Malla kings initially ruled a unified valley kingdom, but by the late 15th century the valley was divided among three rival Malla kingdoms: Kathmandu, Bhaktapur, and Patan (Lalitpur). Their competition spurred lavish building of palaces, temples, and durbar squares. The era ended with the conquest of the valley by Prithvi Narayan Shah of Gorkha in 1768–1769.

Legacy

The Mallas introduced the pagoda style of temple architecture to its height, developed the Newari calendar and coinage, and produced masterpieces such as the palaces and temples of the three Durbar Squares, now UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
