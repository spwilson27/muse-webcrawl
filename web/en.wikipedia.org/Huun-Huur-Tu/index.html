Huun-Huur-Tu

Huun-Huur-Tu (Tuvan: Хүн Хүртү, literally "sun propeller" — the vertical separation of light rays seen at dawn and dusk on the steppe) is a music ensemble from Tuva, a republic of Russia in southern Siberia. Founded in 1992 as the Tuvan Folk Ensemble by Kaigal-ool Khovalyg, Alexander Bapa, Sayan Bapa, and Albert Kuvezin, the group is the best-known exponent of Tuvan throat singing (khöömei) in the West.

Music

The group's sound centres on throat singing techniques — including the deep drone of kargyraa, the whistling harmonics of sygyt, and khöömei itself — in which a single vocalist produces two or more pitches simultaneously. They perform with traditional instruments such as the igil (two-stringed horsehead fiddle), byzaanchy, doshpuluur (lute), khomus (jaw harp), and the adai horse skull. Many songs imitate the sounds of nature — wind, water, birds, and horses — reflecting the shamanic and pastoral heritage of Tuva.

Career

Renamed Huun-Huur-Tu in 1993 under manager Alexander Bapa, the ensemble toured extensively in the United States and Europe, recording for Shanachie Records beginning with 60 Horses in My Herd (1993) and The Orphan's Lament (1994). The group has collaborated with artists including Frank Zappa, the Kronos Quartet, Ry Cooder, and The Bulgarian Voices Angelite, and its music appeared in films such as Genghis Blues (1999). Albert Kuvezin left to form the rock-influenced Yat-Kha; later members include Radik Tyulyush and Alexey Saryglar.
