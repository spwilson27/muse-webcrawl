Iverson bracket

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the Iverson bracket, named after Kenneth E. Iverson, is the notation that denotes a number that is 1 if the condition in square brackets is satisfied, and 0 otherwise. More exactly, for a proposition P:

[P] = 1 if P is true; 0 otherwise.

Uses

The notation, introduced by Iverson in the programming language APL and popularized in mathematics by Donald Knuth, lets sums and identities be written without separate cases or explicit limits: for example, the Kronecker delta is δij = [i = j], and a sum over elements satisfying a condition can be written by multiplying the terms by the bracket.

Relation to other notation

The Iverson bracket generalizes the Kronecker delta and is closely related to the indicator function, which is written with a bold or blackboard-bold 1 rather than brackets.
