Surakarta, known colloquially as Solo (Javanese: ꦯꦴꦭ, Sala), is a major city in Central Java, Indonesia. The 46.72 km² city adjoins Karanganyar Regency and Boyolali Regency to the north, Karanganyar Regency and Sukoharjo Regency to the east and south, and Klaten Regency to the west. On the eastern side of Solo lies the Bengawan Solo River, the longest river on the island of Java. Together with Yogyakarta, Surakarta is the heir of the Mataram Sultanate that was split into two kingdoms in 1755: Kasunanan Surakarta and Kesultanan Yogyakarta.

The metropolitan area, called Subosukawonosraten, had a population of nearly 6.9 million at the 2020 census. Surakarta is a centre of Javanese culture, renowned for its batik production, gamelan music, and refined court dance traditions.

History

The city was founded in 1745, when the susuhunan Pakubuwono II moved the capital of the Mataram kingdom from Kartasura to a new palace on the bank of the Bengawan Solo, following the destruction of Kartasura during a rebellion. The new city took its name from the nearby village of Sala. By the Treaty of Giyanti in 1755, the kingdom was divided, creating the Kasunanan of Surakarta and the Sultanate of Yogyakarta. A further division in 1757 produced the junior court of the Mangkunegaran within Surakarta itself, giving the city two royal palaces.

During the Indonesian National Revolution, Surakarta was a stronghold of republican forces. After independence, the city lost its special-region status in 1946 following an anti-swapraja movement, but its two courts retained cultural roles.

Geography and climate

Surakarta lies in a lowland basin at about 100 metres above sea level, flanked by Mount Merapi and Mount Meru to the west and Mount Lawu to the east. It has a tropical monsoon climate, with a rainy season from roughly November to April and a drier season from May to October.

Culture

Surakarta is considered, alongside Yogyakarta, the centre of classical Javanese culture. The Kraton Surakarta Hadiningrat and the Pura Mangkunegaran palaces preserve court arts including wayang kulit shadow puppetry, gamelan orchestras, and bedhaya sacred dance. The city is one of Indonesia's principal batik centres; the districts of Laweyan and Kauman are historic batik-producing kampungs, and Solo batik is characterised by its sogan brown dyeing. Annual events include the Solo Batik Carnival and the Solo International Performing Arts festival.

Economy and transport

Textiles, batik, furniture, and food processing are major industries. The city is served by Adisumarmo International Airport, located to the northwest, and by Solo Balapan station, one of Java's principal railway stations on the line between Jakarta and Surabaya. The Bengawan Solo, from which the city takes its common name, has been celebrated in the famous keroncong song composed by Gesang Martohartono in 1940.

Education

Surakarta is home to Sebelas Maret University (UNS), a major public university, as well as the Indonesian Institute of the Arts (ISI Surakarta) and several other higher education institutions.
