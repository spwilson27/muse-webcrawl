Common Security and Defence Policy

The Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP), formerly the European Security and Defence Policy (ESDP), is the European Union's course of action in the fields of defence and crisis management, and a main part of the EU's Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP). The policy includes provisions for mutual defence among member states and for military and civilian missions outside the Union.

History

The policy originated in the 1998 Saint-Malo declaration by France and the United Kingdom and was formalised by the Treaty of Nice. The first EU crisis-management missions were deployed in 2003, including police and military operations in the Balkans. The Treaty of Lisbon (2009) renamed the policy CSDP, added a mutual defence clause, and created the framework for Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO), activated in 2017.

The EU has conducted more than thirty civilian and military missions on three continents, from anti-piracy operations off Somalia (EU NAVFOR Atalanta) to training missions in Africa and border assistance missions. The European Defence Agency supports capability development, and EU Battlegroups have been maintained on standby since 2007.
