USS Holland (SS-1) was the United States Navy's first modern submarine and the first submarine to be formally commissioned by the service. Designed by Irish-American engineer John Philip Holland, she was built as a private venture — the Holland VI — by the Crescent Shipyard in Elizabeth, New Jersey, for the Holland Torpedo Boat Company.

Purchased by the Navy on 11 April 1900 for $150,000 after a series of trials, she was commissioned on 12 October 1900 at Newport, Rhode Island, under the command of Lieutenant Harry H. Caldwell. Her success led directly to orders for the Plunger-class submarines and to the founding of the Electric Boat Company, and she is regarded as a foundational vessel of the US submarine force; her commissioning date is celebrated as the birthday of the force.

Design

Holland combined features that became standard in submarine design: a teardrop-shaped hull for submerged running, internal ballast tanks, hydroplanes for depth control, a dual propulsion system — a gasoline engine for surface running and battery charging, and an electric motor for submerged propulsion — and a reloadable torpedo tube in the bow, supplemented by a pneumatic dynamite gun. She could dive to about 75 feet and make roughly 6–7 knots on the surface and 5 knots submerged.

Service

The submarine spent her brief commissioned career at Newport and Annapolis as an experimental and training vessel, testing tactics and training the Navy's first submariners. She made a notable submerged run and demonstrated torpedo firing for observers, though her small size, limited endurance and gasoline engine made her hazardous and quickly obsolete as larger boats entered service.

She was decommissioned in 1905 and struck in 1910. After a period as a museum exhibit at the Paterson Museum in New Jersey — her designer's home town museum — she was scrapped in 1932 during the Great Depression, though her periscope and other fittings survive in collections, and a full display about her is maintained at the Submarine Force Library and Museum.
