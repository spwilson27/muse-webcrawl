Politburo Standing Committee

The Politburo Standing Committee (PSC) is the highest decision-making body of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and, in practice, of the People's Republic of China. Its members — currently seven — are the country's top leaders, ranked in a strict hierarchy, with the CCP General Secretary first.

Role and selection

Formally, the Standing Committee is elected by the CCP Central Committee at each Party Congress, held every five years; in practice its composition is settled through consultation among senior leaders and retired elders. The PSC meets regularly and decides major questions of national policy, personnel, and security. Its members concurrently hold the top state and party posts: the presidency, the premiership, the chairmanships of the National People's Congress Standing Committee and the CPPCC, and leadership of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection.

History

A Standing Committee of the Politburo has existed in some form since 1927 and was formalised at the 8th Party Congress in 1956. Its size has varied from five to eleven members; it has had seven members since the 18th Congress in 2012. The current committee was seated at the 20th Party Congress in October 2022, headed by General Secretary Xi Jinping. See also Chinese Communist Party and Politburo.
