Anarchism

Anarchism is a political philosophy and movement that is sceptical of all justifications of authority and seeks to abolish the institutions it claims maintain unnecessary coercion and hierarchy, including the state, capitalism, and patriarchy. Anarchists advocate self-governed societies based on voluntary, cooperative institutions — free association, mutual aid, and direct democracy — in place of rulers and ruling classes.

History

Precursors can be found in Taoist, Stoic, and radical Reformation thought, and William Godwin's Enquiry Concerning Political Justice (1793) is often called the first modern anarchist text. Pierre-Joseph Proudhon was the first to call himself an anarchist, declaring "property is theft" in 1840. The classical anarchist movement (c. 1860s–1930s) developed through the First International, where Mikhail Bakunin's collectivist anarchism split from Marxism, and produced major currents: Peter Kropotkin's anarcho-communism, the anarcho-syndicalism of revolutionary trade unions (such as Spain's CNT), and individualist anarchism. Anarchists played a major role in the labour movements of Europe and the Americas, in the Mexican and Russian revolutions, and most dramatically in the Spanish Revolution of 1936, when large parts of Catalonia and Aragón were collectivised before defeat in the Civil War.

Schools of thought

Major tendencies include anarcho-communism, collectivist anarchism, mutualism, anarcho-syndicalism, insurrectionary anarchism, and individualist currents such as egoism; later developments include anarcha-feminism, green anarchism, and post-anarchism. Anarchists disagree over violence, markets, organisation, and technology, but share opposition to domination and a commitment to prefigurative politics — embodying desired social relations in present practice.

Contemporary movement

Since the 1960s anarchist ideas have influenced the New Left, feminist, environmental, and anti-globalisation movements, including the alter-globalisation protests of 1999 onward, Occupy Wall Street, and experiments in horizontal organising. Anarchist-influenced autonomous regions such as the Zapatista territories and Rojava are often cited in discussions of stateless governance.
