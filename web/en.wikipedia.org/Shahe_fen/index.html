Shahe fen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shahe fen (Chinese: 沙河粉; pinyin: shāhé fěn) or simply he fen (河粉) is a type of flat, broad Chinese rice noodle originating in Shahe Town, in the Tianhe District of Guangzhou, Guangdong. According to local tradition, the noodles were created in the Qing dynasty by a street vendor in the town, from which they spread throughout Cantonese cuisine.

Production and texture

Shahe fen are made by steaming a thin sheet of rice flour batter, which is then oiled, folded, and cut into ribbons roughly a centimetre wide. Fresh noodles are prized for their smooth, elastic, slightly chewy texture and clean rice flavour, and they are highly perishable, traditionally made and eaten the same day. A dried version is sold widely but is considered inferior.

Dishes

The best-known preparation is gon chow ngauh ho (beef chow fun), dry-fried with beef, bean sprouts, and chives over fierce heat to impart wok hei. Shahe fen are also served wet in soups and gravy sauces such as wat tan ho (with egg gravy, seafood, and vegetables, a style popular in Malaysia and Singapore), and rolled as a base for rice noodle rolls (cheung fun), a close relative made by the same steaming technique.
