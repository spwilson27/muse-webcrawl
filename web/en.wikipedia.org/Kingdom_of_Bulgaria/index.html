The Kingdom of Bulgaria, also called the Tsardom of Bulgaria, was a constitutional monarchy in Southeastern Europe that existed from Bulgaria's declaration of full independence from the Ottoman Empire on 5 October 1908 until the abolition of the monarchy by referendum on 15 September 1946.

History

Ferdinand of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha, prince since 1887, proclaimed himself tsar (king) in 1908. Bulgaria fought in the Balkan Wars (1912–13) and on the side of the Central Powers in the First World War, suffering territorial losses under the Treaty of Neuilly (1919). Under Boris III the country experienced coups, authoritarian royal rule from 1935, and alignment with the Axis in the Second World War, though Bulgaria protected its Jewish citizens while deporting Jews from occupied territories. Boris died suddenly in 1943 and was succeeded by his six-year-old son Simeon II.

After the Soviet-backed Fatherland Front coup of September 1944, a referendum held under communist control abolished the monarchy in 1946 and Bulgaria became a people's republic.
