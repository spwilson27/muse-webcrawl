Guinness World Records

Guinness World Records, originally the Guinness Book of Records, is a reference book published annually listing world records in many fields. First published in 1955, it has become one of the best-selling books of all time and a global brand certifying record attempts.

Origins

The book grew out of an argument in 1951 between Sir Hugh Beaver, managing director of the Guinness brewery, and his shooting companions about which was the fastest game bird in Europe. Realising that pub debates everywhere lacked a reliable source of answers, Beaver commissioned twin brothers Ross and Norris McWhirter to compile a book of facts. The first edition, given away free in pubs, proved so popular that it went on sale in 1955 and topped bestseller lists.

Records and adjudication

Guinness World Records maintains tens of thousands of records, from the tallest human (Robert Wadlow, 2.72 m) to the longest fingernails, and adjudicates public record attempts with official judges. Records must be measurable, standardisable, verifiable and breakable, and the organisation declines dangerous categories. Spin-offs include the annual illustrated edition, television programmes, museums and the Gamer's Edition.
