Cucurbitales is an order of flowering plants within the fabid rosids of the eudicots. It contains around 2,600 species in eight families under the APG IV classification, distributed mainly in tropical and subtropical regions. The order is economically important chiefly for the gourd family, Cucurbitaceae, and the begonia family, Begoniaceae.

Families and notable members

Cucurbitaceae contributes cucumbers, melons, watermelons, pumpkins, squashes and gourds — fast-growing vines typically with tendrils, unisexual flowers and fleshy pepo fruits. Begoniaceae, the order's largest family by species count, includes the many ornamental begonias. Other families are Anisophylleaceae, Apodanthaceae (parasitic plants), Corynocarpaceae, Coriariaceae, Datiscaceae and Tetramelaceae.

Relationships

Molecular phylogenetics places Cucurbitales in the "nitrogen-fixing clade" of the fabids, as sister to Fagales; several members form associations with nitrogen-fixing bacteria. Members generally share inferior ovaries and a tendency toward unisexual flowers.
