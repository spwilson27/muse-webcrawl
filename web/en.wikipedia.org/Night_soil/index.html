Night soil

Night soil is the historical term for human excrement collected at night from privies, cesspools and bucket toilets, traditionally used as fertiliser in agriculture. In cities without sewers, "night soil men" removed waste under cover of darkness, a practice reflected in the name.

Historical use

Night soil was widely used in China, Japan and parts of Europe for centuries to maintain soil fertility; in Edo-period Japan it was a valuable commodity traded between cities and farms. Its use declined with water-carriage sewerage, chemical fertilisers and recognition of the health risks: untreated human waste can transmit cholera, typhoid, helminths and other diseases. Modern approaches such as composting toilets and treated biosolids seek the nutrient benefits with pathogen control.
