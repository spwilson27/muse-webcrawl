Abai Qunanbaiuly

Born10 August 1845, Abay District, Russian Empire
Died6 July 1904 (aged 58)
OccupationPoet, philosopher, composer
LanguageKazakh, Russian

Abai Qunanbaiuly (Kazakh: Абай Құнанбайұлы; 1845–1904), commonly known simply as Abai (also transliterated Abay), was a Kazakh poet, composer and philosopher. He is regarded as the founder of modern written Kazakh literature and one of the most important figures in Kazakh culture.

Life

Abai was born in the Chingiz Mountains region of what is now the Abay Region of Kazakhstan, the son of Qunanbai, a wealthy and influential clan leader. Educated first by a mullah and later at a Russian school in Semipalatinsk, he read widely in Russian and European literature as well as in Persian, Arabic and Turkic classics. He served for a time as a volost administrator before devoting himself to study and writing.

Works

Abai's poetry, much of it written in the last decades of his life, criticised ignorance and social ills among the Kazakhs and urged education and moral self-improvement. His best-known prose work is the Book of Words (Qara sözderi), a collection of 45 short parables and philosophical reflections. He also translated works by Pushkin, Lermontov and Krylov into Kazakh, and composed melodies that remain popular.

Legacy

Abai is commemorated throughout Kazakhstan: the city of Abay, Abai Kazakh National Pedagogical University, monuments and museums bear his name, and his image appears on Kazakh currency. The year 2020 marked the 175th anniversary of his birth with celebrations in Kazakhstan and abroad.
