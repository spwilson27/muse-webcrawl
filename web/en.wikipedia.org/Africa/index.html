Africa is the world's second-largest and second-most populous continent, after Asia. At about 30.37 million km² including adjacent islands, it covers about 20% of Earth's land area. With roughly 1.4 billion people, it accounts for about 18% of the world's human population. The continent is surrounded by the Mediterranean Sea to the north, the Suez Canal and the Red Sea to the northeast, the Indian Ocean to the southeast, and the Atlantic Ocean to the west. It is home to 54 fully recognized sovereign states.

Geography

Africa straddles the equator and comprises many climate zones, from the Sahara — the world's largest hot desert — through the Sahel and the savannas to the rainforests of the Congo Basin and the temperate coasts of the north and south. Major rivers include the Nile, Congo, Niger, and Zambezi. The East African Rift is splitting the continent's tectonic plate over geological time.

History

Africa is widely regarded as the cradle of humanity: hominins and anatomically modern humans evolved there, and all non-African populations descend from migrations out of Africa beginning roughly 60,000–70,000 years ago. Ancient Egypt, Nubia, Carthage, and Axum were among the world's early major civilizations; medieval Africa saw the Ghana, Mali, and Songhai empires, Great Zimbabwe, and extensive trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean trade. From the 15th century, European colonization and the Atlantic slave trade transformed the continent; the "Scramble for Africa" of the late 19th century partitioned nearly all of it among European powers. Decolonization after the Second World War, peaking in the "Year of Africa" (1960), produced the modern map of states.

Demographics and economy

Africa is the youngest continent, with a median age under 20. It has the greatest linguistic diversity of any continent, with an estimated 1,500–3,000 languages across the Niger–Congo, Afroasiatic, Nilo-Saharan, and Khoisan families, plus colonial languages. Its economies are rich in minerals and hydrocarbons; Nigeria, South Africa, and Egypt are among the largest economies. The African Union, founded in 2002 and headquartered in Addis Ababa, coordinates continental policy.
