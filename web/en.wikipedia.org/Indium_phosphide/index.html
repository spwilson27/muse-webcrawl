Indium phosphide

FormulaInP
StructureZinc blende (cubic)
Band gap1.34 eV (direct, 300 K)
Melting point1,062 °C

Indium phosphide (InP) is a binary semiconductor composed of indium and phosphorus. It has a direct band gap of about 1.34 eV and high electron velocity, making it a key material for high-speed and optoelectronic devices, especially in fibre-optic communications.

Properties

InP crystallises in the zinc blende structure with a lattice constant of about 5.87 Å. Its electron mobility and peak electron velocity exceed those of silicon and gallium arsenide under many conditions, suiting it to high-frequency transistors. It is also relatively resistant to radiation and has a high thermal conductivity for a III–V semiconductor.

Applications

InP is the substrate of choice for lasers and photodetectors operating at the 1.3 µm and 1.55 µm wavelengths used in long-haul optical fibre networks, because InGaAsP alloys lattice-matched to InP emit and absorb in those bands. InP high-electron-mobility transistors and heterojunction bipolar transistors serve in millimetre-wave and terahertz electronics, and InP-based devices are used in photonic integrated circuits and some space solar cells.

Production

Single crystals are grown by the Czochralski method under high phosphorus pressure or by vertical gradient freeze techniques, then sliced into wafers. InP is classed as a probable carcinogen in dust form, and its production is more costly than silicon, limiting it to performance-critical niches.
