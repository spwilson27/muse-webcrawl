Japanese language

Japanese is written with a mixture of kanji and two kana syllabaries.

Japanese (日本語, Nihongo) is the principal language of the Japonic language family, spoken by about 125 million people, primarily in Japan, where it is the national language. It is an agglutinative, mora-timed language with a phonemic vowel system and a pitch-accent system.

Writing system

Japanese is written with a combination of three scripts: kanji, logographic characters borrowed from Chinese; hiragana, a syllabary used for native words and grammatical elements; and katakana, a syllabary used mainly for foreign loanwords, emphasis, and onomatopoeia. The Latin alphabet (rōmaji) is also used in some contexts.

Grammar

Japanese word order is subject–object–verb, and particles mark the grammatical function of words. Verbs and adjectives conjugate for tense, politeness, and mood, and an elaborate system of honorific speech (keigo) encodes social relationships between speaker, listener, and subject.

History

Old Japanese is attested from the 8th century, in texts such as the Kojiki and Man'yōshū. Middle and Early Modern Japanese developed over the following centuries, and the modern standard language is based on the dialect of Tokyo. Japanese has borrowed extensively from Chinese and, since the 19th century, from English and other European languages.
