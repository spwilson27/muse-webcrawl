Olympiastadion (Berlin)

LocationWestend, Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf, Berlin
Opened1936
ArchitectWerner March
Capacity74,475
TenantsHertha BSC

The Olympiastadion is a stadium in Berlin, Germany, built for the 1936 Summer Olympic Games. Designed by Werner March, it formed the centrepiece of the Reichssportfeld complex and hosted the Games at which Jesse Owens won four gold medals.

History

The stadium replaced an earlier venue built for the cancelled 1916 Olympics. Its monumental neoclassical stone facade reflected the architectural ambitions of the Nazi regime, though the structure itself survived the Second World War nearly intact. It subsequently served in divided Berlin's western sector and, after reunification, underwent a major renovation completed in 2004, adding a distinctive cantilevered roof and lowering the pitch.

Major events

The Olympiastadion hosted matches of the 1974 and 2006 FIFA World Cups — including the 2006 final, infamous for Zinedine Zidane's headbutt on Marco Materazzi — and the 2015 UEFA Champions League Final. It stages the DFB-Pokal final every year, the 2009 and 2023 World Athletics Championships events, and large concerts. Usain Bolt set his 100 m and 200 m world records here at the 2009 World Championships, times that still stand.

Today

It remains the home ground of Hertha BSC and Germany's largest stadium by capacity for international fixtures, and a protected historic monument.
