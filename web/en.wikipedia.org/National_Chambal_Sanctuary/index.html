National Chambal Sanctuary

National Chambal Sanctuary
Location: Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan, India
Established: 1979
River: Chambal
Key species: Gharial, mugger crocodile, Ganges river dolphin, Indian skimmer

The National Chambal Sanctuary, also known as the National Chambal Gharial Wildlife Sanctuary, is a large tri-state protected area in northern India, established in 1979 to protect the critically endangered gharial, the red-crowned roof turtle and the endangered Ganges river dolphin. It covers a long, narrow eco-reserve along about 425 km of the Chambal River and its ravines across Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh.

Wildlife

The sanctuary holds one of the largest remaining wild populations of gharials (several hundred adults), together with mugger crocodiles, seven species of freshwater turtles, smooth-coated otters, striped hyenas, Indian wolves, and over 300 bird species including large breeding colonies of the vulnerable Indian skimmer. Its relatively clean river — the Chambal is one of India's least polluted major rivers — supports rich fisheries.

Threats and events

Threats include sand mining, fishing nets, water extraction and habitat disturbance. In the winter of 2007–08 more than a hundred gharials died in the sanctuary from a mysterious nephrotoxic illness, an event that drew international attention to the species' precarious status. Captive-reared gharials from centres such as Kukrail have been released into the river to reinforce the population.
