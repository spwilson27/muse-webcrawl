Tres Zapotes

LocationSantiago Tuxtla, Veracruz, Mexico
RegionGulf Coast of Mexico
CulturesOlmec, Epi-Olmec
PeriodsFormative to Classic

Tres Zapotes is a Mesoamerican archaeological site located in the south-central Gulf lowlands of Mexico in the Papaloapan River plain. Tres Zapotes is sometimes referred to as the third major Olmec capital, after San Lorenzo Tenochtitlán and La Venta, though its Olmec phase accounts for only a portion of the site's long occupation. The site's occupation continued into the Epi-Olmec and Classic cultures, a longevity not seen elsewhere in the Olmec region.

Overview

Located in the rolling hills bordering the Sierra de los Tuxtlas, along a small tributary of the Papaloapan River known as the Río Hueyapan, Tres Zapotes covered a total area of about 2.5 square kilometres, with roughly 100 mounds of various sizes and styles. Much of the site is on and around the ranch of Tres Zapotes, in the municipality of Santiago Tuxtla, Veracruz.

Discoveries

The site is famous for Colossal Head 2 (called "Monument Q" or the "Nestepe head"), one of the seventeen known Olmec colossal heads, and for Stela C, one of the earliest monuments bearing a Long Count calendar date: its partial date corresponds to 32 BCE, a reading that helped establish the antiquity of the Long Count calendar. The backed portion of Stela C was recovered decades after the front, completing the date.
