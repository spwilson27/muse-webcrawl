Liberal Democratic Party (Japan)


The Liberal Democratic Party (自由民主党, Jiyū-Minshutō), abbreviated LDP, is a conservative political party in Japan, founded in 1955. It has governed Japan for most of the postwar era.

The Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) is a major conservative and nationalist political party in Japan. It has been in power almost continuously since its foundation in 1955, with brief periods in opposition in 1993–1994 and 2009–2012.

History

The LDP was formed on 15 November 1955 through the merger of the Liberal Party and the Japan Democratic Party, uniting Japan's conservative forces against the Japan Socialist Party. Its long dominance shaped the "1955 System" of Japanese politics.

Ideology and factions

The party is broadly conservative, supporting a market economy, a close alliance with the United States, and constitutional revision debates. Internally it is organised into factions (habatsu) that compete over the party presidency, whose holder typically becomes prime minister.
