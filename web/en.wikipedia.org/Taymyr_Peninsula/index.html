Taymyr Peninsula

The Taymyr Peninsula (Russian: Таймырский полуостров) is a large peninsula in the Russian Far North, in Krasnoyarsk Krai, forming the northernmost part of mainland Asia. Cape Chelyuskin, at its tip (77°43′N), is the northernmost point of any continental landmass.

Taymyr Peninsula
Location: Siberia, Russia
Area: approx. 400,000 km²
Highest point: Byrranga Mountains, ~1,125 m

Geography

The peninsula lies between the Kara Sea and the Laptev Sea, with the Byrranga Mountains running across its centre and Lake Taymyr, one of Russia's largest lakes, to their south. The climate is severe Arctic tundra, home to reindeer herds and one of the last wild musk ox populations, reintroduced from Canada and Greenland. Indigenous peoples include the Dolgans, Nenets and Nganasans. The region is administered as the Taymyrsky Dolgano-Nenetsky District, with its centre at Dudinka.
