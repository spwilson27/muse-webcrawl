Timeline of LGBT history

The following is a timeline of lesbian, gay, bisexual, and transgender (LGBT) history. It records selected events concerning same-sex love, gender variance, law, activism, and culture from the ancient world to the present. Historians caution that modern identity labels do not map neatly onto earlier societies, but records of same-sex affection and gender nonconformity appear across many cultures.

Ancient world

  • c. 2400 BCE – Khnumhotep and Niankhkhnum, ancient Egyptian royal servants, buried together in a shared tomb, sometimes interpreted as a same-sex couple.
  • c. 630 BCE – Sappho of Lesbos writes lyric poetry celebrating love between women; her name and island later give rise to the words "sapphic" and "lesbian".
  • 342 BCE – The Sacred Band of Thebes, an elite unit said to consist of pairs of lovers, is destroyed at the Battle of Chaeronea.
  • 27 BCE–14 CE – Roman emperor Augustus reigns; Roman attitudes toward same-sex relations vary by status and role.
  • 342 CE – Roman emperors Constantius II and Constans issue a law against same-sex marriage.

Medieval and early modern periods

  • 390 CE – Christian emperors Valentinian II, Theodosius I and Arcadius issue an edict condemning homosexual acts.
  • 1533 – England's Buggery Act makes sodomy a capital offence under secular law.
  • 1791 – Revolutionary France decriminalizes sodomy in its new penal code.
  • 1861 – England reduces the penalty for buggery from death to life imprisonment.
  • 1867 – Karl Heinrich Ulrichs publicly defends love between men before the Congress of German Jurists in Munich — one of the first public speeches for homosexual rights.
  • 1895 – Oscar Wilde is convicted of gross indecency in London and sentenced to two years' hard labour.

20th century

  • 1924 – Henry Gerber founds the Society for Human Rights in Chicago, the first documented gay rights organization in the United States.
  • 1933–1945 – Nazi Germany persecutes homosexuals; thousands are sent to concentration camps wearing the pink triangle.
  • 1950 – The Mattachine Society is founded in Los Angeles; the Daughters of Bilitis follows in 1955 in San Francisco.
  • 1967 – The Sexual Offences Act partially decriminalizes homosexual acts in England and Wales.
  • 1969 – The Stonewall riots in New York City catalyze the modern gay liberation movement; the first Pride marches follow in 1970.
  • 1973 – The American Psychiatric Association removes homosexuality from the DSM.
  • 1981 – The first reports of the disease later named AIDS emerge; the epidemic devastates gay communities and spurs activism such as ACT UP (founded 1987).
  • 1989 – Denmark becomes the first country to legally recognize same-sex registered partnerships.

21st century

  • 2001 – The Netherlands becomes the first country to legalize same-sex marriage.
  • 2011 – "Don't Ask, Don't Tell" is repealed in the United States.
  • 2015 – The U.S. Supreme Court rules in Obergefell v. Hodges that same-sex marriage is a constitutional right nationwide; Ireland legalizes same-sex marriage by popular referendum.
  • 2017–2023 – Marriage equality is enacted in Germany, Australia, Taiwan (2019, the first in Asia), Switzerland, Mexico, and others.
