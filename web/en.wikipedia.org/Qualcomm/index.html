Qualcomm

Qualcomm headquarters, San Diego

Qualcomm Incorporated is an American multinational corporation headquartered in San Diego, California, that creates semiconductors, software and services related to wireless technology. Founded in 1985 by Irwin M. Jacobs and six others, it owns patents critical to the CDMA, LTE and 5G mobile communications standards, and its Snapdragon systems-on-chip power a large share of the world's Android smartphones.

History

Qualcomm's early business centred on the OmniTRACS satellite tracking system for trucking and on promoting the CDMA digital cellular standard it had developed despite widespread industry scepticism. In 1999 it sold its handset business to Kyocera and its infrastructure business to Ericsson to focus on chips and licensing. Its technology licensing division (QTL) — collecting royalties on handsets using its patent portfolio — has been highly profitable and repeatedly controversial, attracting antitrust cases and fines in China, South Korea, the EU and the United States, and a landmark licensing dispute with Apple that was settled in 2019.

Products

The company's main product lines are Snapdragon processors and modems for phones, automotive and Internet-of-things devices, RF front-end components, and (as a fabless company) chips manufactured by foundries such as TSMC and Samsung. Qualcomm has also expanded into laptop processors with its Snapdragon X series based on the Oryon CPU acquired with Nuvia in 2021.
