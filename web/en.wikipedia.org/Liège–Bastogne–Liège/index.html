Liège–Bastogne–Liège


Liège–Bastogne–Liège, nicknamed La Doyenne ("The Old Lady"), is a one-day professional road cycling race held each April in Belgium. First edition: 1892. It is one of the five Monuments of cycling.

Liège–Bastogne–Liège, also known as La Doyenne, is a European one-day professional road bicycle racing race, first run in 1892. It is considered one of the most prestigious single-day events in cycling and is one of the five Monuments of the European professional road cycling calendar, and one of the 24 races that make up the UCI World Tour.

Route

The race runs from Liège in Wallonia, south to Bastogne and back, covering roughly 250 km through the hilly Ardennes. The route features a succession of short, steep climbs known as côtes, including the Côte de la Redoute and the Côte de la Roche-aux-Faucons, which often decide the race in the final kilometres.

History

First held in 1892 for amateurs, the race became professional in 1894. Eddy Merckx holds the record with five victories. The race traditionally closes the spring Ardennes classics week, following the Amstel Gold Race and La Flèche Wallonne.
