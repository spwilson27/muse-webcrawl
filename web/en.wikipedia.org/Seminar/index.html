Seminar

A seminar is a form of academic instruction, either at an academic institution or offered by a commercial or professional organization. It has the function of bringing together small groups for recurring meetings, focusing each time on some particular subject, in which everyone present is requested to participate. This is often accomplished through an ongoing Socratic dialogue with a seminar leader or instructor, or through a more formal presentation of research.

The word seminar is derived from the Latin seminarium, meaning "seed plot"—a place where ideas are sown and cultivated.

In universities

In American and European universities, seminars are a common method of instruction, especially at the graduate level: participants are expected to read assigned materials in advance, present papers or research in progress, and engage in discussion guided by a professor. Seminars contrast with lectures, in which instruction is largely one-way, and are typically small, advanced classes with enrolments limited to allow discussion.

Specialized forms include the research seminar, in which scholars present current work; the pro-seminar for beginning graduate students; and the doctoral seminar focused on dissertation research.

Commercial and professional seminars

Outside academia, "seminar" also refers to professional development events: one-off or short-series meetings at which experts present current knowledge, skills training, or business topics such as management, marketing, or personal finance. Such events range from free public lectures to paid conferences, and they may be delivered in person, by telephone (teleseminars), or online (webinars).
