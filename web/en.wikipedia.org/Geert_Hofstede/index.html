Geert Hofstede, Dutch social psychologist.

Gerard Hendrik (Geert) Hofstede (2 October 1928 – 12 February 2020) was a Dutch social psychologist, IBM employee, and Professor Emeritus of Organizational Anthropology and International Management at Maastricht University. He is best known for developing one of the earliest and most influential frameworks for describing national cultures, Hofstede's cultural dimensions theory.

Research

Working at IBM, Hofstede analysed a large survey database of employee values collected from more than 70 countries. His 1980 book Culture's Consequences identified four dimensions along which national cultures vary: power distance, individualism versus collectivism, masculinity versus femininity, and uncertainty avoidance. Later work with Michael Harris Bond and Michael Minkov added long-term versus short-term orientation and indulgence versus restraint, making six dimensions.

The framework became standard in cross-cultural psychology, international management, and marketing, though it has been criticised for treating nations as uniform cultures, for relying on data from a single company, and for the age of the original data.
