The Gospel of Matthew (Greek: Εὐαγγέλιον κατὰ Μαθθαῖον, romanized: Euaggélion katà Maththaîon) is the first book of the New Testament and one of the three Synoptic Gospels. It tells how Israel's Messiah, Jesus, comes to his people, is rejected by them, and finally commissions his disciples to proclaim the gospel to all the nations. It opens with a genealogy of Jesus through Joseph back to Abraham, and an infancy narrative including the visit of the Magi, the flight into Egypt and the massacre of the innocents.

Matthew structures Jesus's teaching into five great discourses, echoing the five books of Moses: the Sermon on the Mount (chapters 5–7), the mission discourse, the parables of the kingdom, the discourse on the community, and the apocalyptic discourse on the Mount of Olives. Frequent "formula quotations" present events as fulfilments of Hebrew scripture, and the gospel is the most Jewish in outlook of the four, while ending with the universal commission of the risen Jesus: "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations" (28:19).

Authorship and sources

The gospel is anonymous; the title attributing it to Matthew the tax-collector apostle appears in the 2nd century. The majority scholarly view is that it was composed in Greek around 80–90 AD, probably in Syria (Antioch is often suggested), by an author who drew on the Gospel of Mark and a sayings collection known as Q. Because of its orderly arrangement and prominent place in early Christian teaching, Matthew became the most frequently quoted and, in many periods, the most influential of the gospels.
