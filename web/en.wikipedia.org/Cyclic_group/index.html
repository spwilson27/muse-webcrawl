Cyclic group

In abstract algebra, a cyclic group or monogenous group is a group that can be generated by a single element. That is, it is a set of invertible elements with a single associative binary operation, and it contains an element g such that every other element of the group may be obtained by repeatedly applying the group operation to g or its inverse. Cyclic groups are among the simplest groups and are completely classified: for each positive integer n there is exactly one cyclic group of order n (up to isomorphism), and one infinite cyclic group.

NotationZn, Z/nZ, Cn
Generatorsφ(n) for a group of order n
PropertiesAlways abelian; all subgroups cyclic

Examples

The integers under addition form the unique infinite cyclic group, generated by 1 (or −1). The integers modulo n under addition form a cyclic group of order n. In geometry, the rotational symmetry group of a regular polygon is cyclic. The multiplicative group of a finite field is cyclic, as is the group of n-th roots of unity under multiplication.

Properties

Every cyclic group is abelian. Every subgroup of a cyclic group is itself cyclic, and for each divisor of the order there is exactly one subgroup of that order. An element gk generates a cyclic group of order n precisely when k and n are coprime, so the number of generators equals Euler's totient φ(n).

Applications

Cyclic groups underlie the Diffie–Hellman key exchange and ElGamal encryption in cryptography, where security rests on the difficulty of the discrete logarithm problem in a large cyclic group. They also appear in number theory through primitive roots, and in Fourier analysis through cyclic convolutions.
