Directive Principles of State Policy

The Directive Principles of State Policy of India are guidelines set out in Part IV of the Constitution of India (Articles 36–51) for the framing of laws by the government. Borrowed from the Irish Constitution, they embody the aims of social and economic democracy that were to complement the political democracy granted by the Fundamental Rights.

Content

The principles direct the state to secure a social order promoting the welfare of the people, to minimise inequalities in income, to ensure equitable distribution of material resources, free and compulsory education for children (originally Article 45), public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age and sickness, a uniform civil code for citizens (Article 44), the organisation of village panchayats, the promotion of international peace, and the separation of the judiciary from the executive.

Non-justiciability

Unlike the Fundamental Rights, the Directive Principles are non-justiciable: they cannot be enforced by any court. Article 37 nonetheless declares them "fundamental in the governance of the country" and makes it the duty of the state to apply them in making laws.

Relationship with Fundamental Rights

The balance between Part III (Fundamental Rights) and Part IV has been a recurring theme in Indian constitutional law, notably in the Minerva Mills case (1980), in which the Supreme Court held that the two parts must be read in harmony.
