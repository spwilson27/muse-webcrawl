Uzbek is a Turkic language spoken by the Uzbeks and the official language of Uzbekistan. It is the most widely spoken Turkic language of Central Asia after Kazakh, with some 34 million native speakers. Uzbek belongs to the Karluk branch of the Turkic family and is closely related to Uyghur.

History

The literary precursor of modern Uzbek is Chagatai, the Turkic literary language of Central Asia under the Timurids, used by poets such as Ali-Shir Nava'i in the 15th and 16th centuries. Modern Uzbek standardized in the Soviet period largely on the dialects of Tashkent and Fergana. The language was written first in Arabic script, then in Latin (1928–1940), then Cyrillic during the Soviet era, and officially returned to a Latin alphabet after Uzbekistan's independence in 1991 — though Cyrillic remains in wide use.

Features

Unlike most Turkic languages, standard Uzbek has largely lost vowel harmony under the influence of Persian (Tajik), with which it has coexisted for centuries. It retains the agglutinative morphology typical of Turkic, a subject–object–verb word order, and heavy Persian and Arabic vocabulary.
