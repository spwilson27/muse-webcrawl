Committee on the Rights of the Child

The Committee on the Rights of the Child (CRC) is a body of independent experts that monitors implementation of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child by its state parties, together with the Convention's optional protocols on children in armed conflict, the sale of children, and communications. It is one of the treaty bodies of the United Nations human rights system and meets in Geneva, Switzerland.

Membership and sessions

The committee consists of 18 members of high moral standing and recognised competence in the field of children's rights, elected by state parties for four-year terms and serving in their personal capacity. It normally holds three sessions per year at the Palais Wilson and the Palais des Nations in Geneva.

Functions

States that ratify the Convention must submit an initial report within two years and periodic reports every five years on the measures they have taken to implement children's rights. The committee examines each report in dialogue with the state's delegation and issues concluding observations with recommendations. It also publishes general comments interpreting provisions of the Convention — for example on the best interests of the child, the right to be heard, and freedom from violence — and holds days of general discussion. Since the third optional protocol entered into force in 2014, children (or their representatives) from states that have ratified it may also bring individual complaints to the committee.
