Lisa del Giocondo (née Gherardini; 1479–1542) was an Italian noblewoman from Florence, best known as the sitter for Leonardo da Vinci's Mona Lisa. She was a member of the Gherardini family and married the Florentine cloth and silk merchant Francesco del Giocondo, with whom she had five children.

Life

Lisa was born in Florence in 1479 and married Francesco del Giocondo in 1495 at the age of fifteen. The couple lived a comfortable middle-class life; Francesco was a successful merchant who served in Florentine civic offices. Lisa died in 1542 and was buried at the convent of Sant'Orsola in Florence, where she had spent her final years.

The portrait

Francesco is believed to have commissioned the portrait around 1503, possibly to celebrate the birth of a child or the purchase of a family house. Leonardo worked on the painting intermittently for years and kept it with him until his death in France in 1519. The portrait's Italian title, La Gioconda, is both her married name and a play on the word for "jovial"; the French title La Joconde derives from the same source.

Identification

Although other sitters were proposed over the centuries, a marginal note dated 1503 by the Florentine official Agostino Vespucci, discovered in 2005, confirmed that Leonardo was painting Lisa del Giocondo, settling the identification for most scholars.
