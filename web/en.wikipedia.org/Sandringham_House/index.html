Sandringham House is a country house in the parish of Sandringham, Norfolk, England. It is the privately owned country retreat of the British monarch — currently Charles III — and sits at the heart of a 20,000-acre estate that has belonged to the Royal Family since 1862.

History

The estate was purchased in 1862 for Albert Edward, Prince of Wales (later Edward VII), for £220,000. The existing house was demolished and replaced between 1870 and 1892 by the present red-brick Jacobethan mansion designed by A. J. Humbert and Samuel Teulon, with its distinctive gables, turrets and ballroom. Sandringham was the birthplace of Alexander John, youngest son of Edward VII, and the place of death of George V (1936) and George VI (1952).

The estate

The estate includes working farms, commercial forestry, the Sandringham gardens designed in part by Geoffrey Jellicoe, the parish church of St Mary Magdalene — where the Royal Family traditionally worships on Christmas Day — and the villages of West Newton and Wolferton. Anmer Hall, on the estate, is the country home of the Prince and Princess of Wales. Some 600 acres of country park are open to the public, and the house's ground-floor rooms open seasonally, except during royal residence.

Royal residence

Sandringham is traditionally where the monarch spends Christmas and remains in residence until February, marking the anniversary of George VI's death. The Christmas broadcast has frequently been recorded there, and the royal shooting parties held on the estate are among the most famous in Britain.
