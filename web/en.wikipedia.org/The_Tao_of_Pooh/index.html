The Tao of Pooh is a book written by Benjamin Hoff and published in 1982. The book is intended as an introduction to the Eastern belief system of Taoism for Western readers, allegorically using A. A. Milne's Winnie-the-Pooh stories to explain its principles.

Synopsis

Hoff presents Pooh as an exemplar of wu wei (effortless action) and the Uncarved Block (p'u), contrasting him with fretful characters: Owl the scholar, Rabbit the clever planner, and Eeyore the complainer. Dialogues between Hoff and the characters frame excerpts from Milne and Taoist texts such as the Tao Te Ching.

Reception

The book spent 49 weeks on the New York Times bestseller list and is used in some university courses. Hoff followed it with The Te of Piglet (1992). Some sinologists criticised its simplifications of Taoism.
