Foreign relations of the United Kingdom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The foreign relations of the United Kingdom are governed by the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO), headed by the Foreign Secretary. The United Kingdom is a permanent member of the United Nations Security Council, a founding member of NATO, a member of the G7, G20, the Commonwealth of Nations, the Council of Europe, the OECD, and the World Trade Organization. Having joined the European Economic Community in 1973, the UK left the European Union in 2020 following the 2016 Brexit referendum.

Alliances and partnerships

The United Kingdom maintains a "Special Relationship" with the United States, encompassing close cooperation in defence, intelligence (the Five Eyes alliance, shared also with Canada, Australia, and New Zealand), and nuclear technology under the 1958 US–UK Mutual Defence Agreement. The UK is one of five recognised nuclear-weapon states under the Non-Proliferation Treaty and maintains a continuous at-sea nuclear deterrent aboard Vanguard-class submarines.

Global presence

The UK operates one of the largest diplomatic networks in the world, with embassies, high commissions, and consulates in most countries. It remains constitutionally linked to 14 British Overseas Territories and, through the Commonwealth realms historically and the Commonwealth of Nations, to many former parts of the British Empire. King Charles III is head of the Commonwealth.

Recent policy

Following Brexit, British policy has emphasised a "Global Britain" agenda: joining the CPTPP trade bloc, the AUKUS security partnership with Australia and the United States (announced 2021), sanctions coordination after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine — of which the UK has been one of the largest military donors — and an "Indo-Pacific tilt" in defence and trade strategy.
