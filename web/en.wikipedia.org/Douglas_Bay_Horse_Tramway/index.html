Douglas Bay Horse Tramway

The Douglas Bay Horse Tramway (Manx: Raad Yiarn Cabbyl Vaie Ghoolish) is a horse-drawn tramway running along the seafront promenade of Douglas, the capital of the Isle of Man. Opened in 1876, it is the last surviving horse tramway in the British Isles in regular public service and one of the oldest horse-drawn tram services in the world still operating on its original route.

History

The tramway was built by retired civil engineer Thomas Lightfoot and opened in 1876 to serve the growing Victorian tourist trade along Douglas Bay. It was later owned by the Douglas & Laxey Coast Electric Tramway interests, then by Douglas Corporation, which operated it from 1902. The service was suspended during both World Wars and the horses requisitioned. Despite recurring debates over cost, the tramway survived as a much-loved symbol of the island; in 2016 its operation transferred to the Isle of Man Government, and the track was relaid as part of the Douglas Promenades regeneration scheme, with the line temporarily shortened during the works and restored to its full length along the promenade.

Route and stock

The single, mostly double-track line runs for about 1.6 miles (2.6 km) from the Victoria Pier/Sea Terminal area at the south end of the promenade to Derby Castle at the north end, where it connects with the Manx Electric Railway. A fleet of open and enclosed toast-rack and saloon cars, some dating from the 19th century, is drawn by heavy horses ("trammers") stabled at Summerhill; horses typically work short shifts and are retired to the island's Home of Rest for Old Horses.
