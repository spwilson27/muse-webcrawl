Coxeter group

In mathematics, a Coxeter group, named for H. S. M. Coxeter, is an abstract group that admits a formal description in terms of reflections (or kaleidoscopic mirrors). Precisely, Coxeter groups are the groups that can be presented by generators that are all involutions, with relations specifying the orders of products of pairs of generators. The theory includes finite groups generated by reflections of Euclidean or spherical space, such as the symmetry groups of regular polytopes, and infinite groups such as affine and hyperbolic reflection groups.

Definition

A Coxeter group is a group W with a generating set S and relations of the form (st)m(s,t) = 1, where m(s,s) = 1 and m(s,t) = m(t,s) ≥ 2 for distinct s, t (m may be infinite, meaning no relation). The pair (W, S) is called a Coxeter system. The data are conveniently encoded in a Coxeter–Dynkin diagram, whose nodes are the generators and whose labelled edges record the values of m.

Examples and classification

The finite Coxeter groups were completely classified: they are the families Aₚ, Bₚ, Dₚ, the exceptional groups E₆, E₇, E₈, F₄, G₂, the dihedral groups I₂(m), and the non-crystallographic groups H₃ and H₄. They arise as the symmetry groups of regular polytopes, root systems, and the Weyl groups of Lie algebras. Replacing the Euclidean condition gives the affine Weyl groups, which act as symmetry groups of periodic tilings.

Applications

Coxeter groups underpin large areas of geometry and algebra: Lie theory, the geometry of polytopes and tessellations, buildings in group theory, and the Hecke algebras that generalize their group algebras. Every Coxeter group can be realized as a group generated by reflections in a suitable space, a theorem at the heart of Coxeter's original work.
