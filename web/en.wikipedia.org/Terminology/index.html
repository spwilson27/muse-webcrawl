Terminology

Terminology is the study of terms and their use; more broadly, the word also denotes the body of specialised words and expressions used in a particular field, profession, or subject. Terms are words and compound words that, in a specific context, have precise meanings differing from or narrower than their everyday senses — as "force" does in physics or "consideration" in contract law. The discipline of terminology studies how such terms are created, defined, standardised, and translated.

Scope and principles

Modern terminology science was founded by the Austrian engineer Eugen Wüster in the 1930s, who sought unambiguous technical communication through standardised vocabularies. Terminology work is concept-oriented: the terminologist starts from a concept within a subject field, delimits it against neighbouring concepts, and assigns designations in one or more languages. Core principles include monosemy within a domain, transparency of term formation, and consistency across texts. Standards such as ISO 704 govern methods, and terminologists maintain termbases used by translators, technical writers, and regulators.

Terminology versus lexicography

Unlike general lexicography, which describes a language's vocabulary from words to meanings, terminology proceeds from concepts to designations (the onomasiological approach) and is usually prescriptive or normalising, aiming to fix usage within a domain. Domain-specific terminologies range from anatomical nomenclature — the long-regulated Terminologia Anatomica — to the rapidly evolving vocabularies of computing and biotechnology.

Practice

Terminology management is a recognised step in translation and localisation workflows: terms are extracted from texts, validated with subject experts, stored with definitions and contexts, and distributed via terminology databases such as the EU's IATE. Poorly controlled terminology is a measurable source of error in medicine, engineering, and law, which has made terminology work a component of quality management in multinationals and international organisations.
