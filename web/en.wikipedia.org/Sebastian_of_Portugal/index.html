Sebastian of Portugal

Sebastian (Portuguese: Sebastião; 20 January 1554 – 4 August 1578), often called the Desired, was King of Portugal from 1557 until his death. He was the posthumous son of João Manuel, Prince of Portugal, and Joanna of Austria, and grandson of John III, whom he succeeded as an infant.

Sebastian
Reign: 11 June 1557 – 4 August 1578
Born: 20 January 1554, Lisbon
Died: 4 August 1578, Alcácer Quibir, Morocco

Reign

Sebastian was raised by his grandmother Catherine of Austria and his great-uncle Cardinal Henry, who served as regents, and was educated by Jesuits. He grew up devout, austere and obsessed with crusading warfare against Muslim Morocco.

Battle of Alcácer Quibir

In 1578 Sebastian led a large expedition into Morocco in support of a deposed sultan. At the Battle of Alcácer Quibir (the Battle of the Three Kings) on 4 August 1578, the Portuguese army was destroyed and Sebastian was killed, though his body was never conclusively identified.

Legacy

Childless, Sebastian was succeeded by Cardinal Henry, whose death in 1580 triggered a succession crisis and the Iberian Union under Philip II of Spain. The mystery of his fate gave rise to Sebastianism, a popular belief that the king would one day return to save Portugal.
