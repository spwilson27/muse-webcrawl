Moléson

Elevation: 2,002 m (6,568 ft)
Location: Canton of Fribourg, Switzerland
Range: Swiss Prealps (Bernese Alps foothills)

Moléson is a mountain of the Swiss Prealps, rising to an elevation of 2,002 metres (6,568 ft) above sea level in the canton of Fribourg, Switzerland. It overlooks the region of Gruyères and is one of the most recognisable peaks of the Fribourg Prealps, dominating the skyline west of the town of Gruyères.

Geography

The Moléson summit lies in the Saane/Sarine valley region and offers panoramic views over the Gruyère countryside, Lake Gruyère, and, on clear days, from the Jura Mountains to Mont Blanc and Lake Geneva. The mountain forms part of the chain separating the valley of the Sarine from the Veveyse district.

Tourism and access

Moléson is a popular destination for hiking, paragliding, and winter sports. A funicular railway runs from the village of Moléson-sur-Gruyères to Plan Francey, from where a cable car (gondola lift) continues to the summit station. The summit area has a restaurant and an astronomical observatory. In winter, the slopes host a small ski area, and in summer the mountain is known for via ferrata routes and alpine cheese-making demonstrations at nearby farms, where the famous Gruyère cheese tradition is maintained.

Name

The name Moléson is thought to derive from Latin roots relating to a mill or millstone, though its precise etymology is debated.
