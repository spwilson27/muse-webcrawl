Diode bridge

A diode bridge is a bridge rectifier circuit of four diodes that converts alternating current (AC) input into direct current (DC) output, a process known as rectification. Its key feature is that the output polarity is the same regardless of the input polarity.

Operation

On each half-cycle of the AC input, two of the four diodes conduct, steering current through the load in a consistent direction. The result is full-wave rectification with a pulsating DC waveform, usually smoothed by a capacitor filter.

Characteristics

The output voltage is reduced by two diode forward voltage drops. The arrangement is also called a Graetz bridge after Leo Graetz. Diode bridges are sold as single packaged bridge rectifier components and are found in most AC power supplies. A related circuit using thyristors or transistors allows controlled or active rectification.
