Gustavo Gutiérrez

Gustavo Gutiérrez Merino (8 June 1928 – 22 October 2024) was a Peruvian philosopher, Catholic theologian, and Dominican priest, regarded as one of the founders of Latin American liberation theology. His 1971 book A Theology of Liberation gave the movement its name and its foundational argument: that the Church must show a "preferential option for the poor" and address the structural causes of poverty and injustice.

Life

Born in Lima to a mestizo family, Gutiérrez suffered from osteomyelitis as a youth, which left him with a limp and frail health. He studied medicine and philosophy at the National University of San Marcos before pursuing theology in Europe, earning degrees in Belgium, France, and Rome. Ordained a priest in 1959, he returned to Peru, taught at the Pontifical Catholic University of Peru, and worked with poor parish communities in Lima. He joined the Dominican order in 1998.

Theology

Gutiérrez argued that poverty is not merely material deprivation but a "scandalous condition" contrary to human dignity, caused in part by unjust social structures. Theology, in his account, is "critical reflection on praxis" — it must begin from the lived experience of the oppressed and commit itself to their liberation, understood in three dimensions: political liberation, the liberation of human beings throughout history, and liberation from sin. His work was influential at the 1968 Medellín conference of Latin American bishops and spread worldwide, though it also drew scrutiny from the Vatican's Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith in the 1980s; Gutiérrez himself was never condemned. Later books include We Drink from Our Own Wells (1983) and On Job: God-Talk and the Suffering of the Innocent (1986).

Death and legacy

Gutiérrez died in Lima on 22 October 2024, aged 96. Pope Francis, who had met and corresponded with him, was among those who had rehabilitated liberation theology's standing within the Church; Gutiérrez is remembered as one of the most influential theologians of the twentieth century.
