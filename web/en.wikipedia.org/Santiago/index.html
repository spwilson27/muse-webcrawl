Santiago (Spanish pronunciation: [sanˈtjaɣo]), also known as Santiago de Chile, is the capital and largest city of Chile and one of the largest cities in the Americas. It is located in the country's central valley, at an elevation of roughly 520 m (1,706 ft) above sea level, between the Chilean Coastal Range to the west and the Andes — whose snow-capped peaks, including the 6,570-metre Tupungato, dominate the eastern skyline.

History

Santiago was founded on 12 February 1541 by the Spanish conquistador Pedro de Valdivia, who named it Santiago del Nuevo Extremo after Saint James and Extremadura. Destroyed in an indigenous uprising within its first year, the city was rebuilt and grew slowly as the colonial capital of the Captaincy General of Chile. Bernardo O'Higgins proclaimed Chilean independence in Santiago in 1818. In the 20th century the city industrialised and absorbed waves of rural migration. La Moneda Palace was bombed during the 1973 military coup that brought Augusto Pinochet to power; democracy returned in 1990.

Geography and climate

The city sprawls across the Maipo River basin, divided into 37 communes. Santiago has a cool semi-arid to Mediterranean climate: hot, dry summers, mild winters with most rain between May and August, and frequent thermal inversions that trap air pollution against the Andes — a persistent environmental challenge. The Gran Torre Santiago in Providencia, at 300 metres, is the tallest building in South America.

Economy and transport

Santiago generates roughly 40 percent of Chile's GDP and hosts the headquarters of major mining, banking and retail corporations, the Santiago Stock Exchange and most government ministries in the civic quarter around La Moneda. The Santiago Metro, opened in 1975, is one of South America's largest rapid-transit systems, integrated with the Red Metropolitana de Movilidad bus network; Arturo Merino Benítez International Airport is the country's main gateway.
