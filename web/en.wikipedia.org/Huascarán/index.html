Huascarán is a mountain in the Cordillera Blanca of the Peruvian Andes and, at 6,768 metres above sea level, the highest peak in Peru and the fourth highest in South America. Its snow-covered massif, protected within Huascarán National Park, rises above the Callejón de Huaylas valley in the Ancash Region. The mountain has two principal summits: Huascarán Sur (south, 6,768 m) and Huascarán Norte (north, 6,655 m).

Climbing history

The north peak was climbed in 1908 by an expedition led by Annie Smith Peck, who long promoted the mountain's height. The higher south peak was first ascended on 20 July 1932 by a German-Austrian expedition including Hans Kinzl and Erwin Schneider.

Disasters

On 10 January 1962 a huge ice avalanche from Huascarán Norte destroyed the town of Ranrahirca and surrounding villages, killing about 4,000 people. On 31 May 1970 the Ancash earthquake triggered an even larger debris avalanche that buried the town of Yungay, killing some 20,000 people in one of the deadliest natural disasters in Peruvian history.

Gravity

Because of the equatorial bulge, the summit of Huascarán experiences the weakest surface gravity on Earth, about 9.7639 m/s².
