Cut of pork

The cuts of pork are the different parts of the pig which are served as food. The most commonly recognised primal cuts are the shoulder, loin, belly, and leg (ham), though butchery traditions differ between countries in how the carcass is divided and named.

Primal cuts

The shoulder (including the Boston butt and picnic shoulder) is well marbled and suited to slow cooking and pulled pork. The loin yields chops, loin roasts, tenderloin, and back ribs, and is the source of Canadian bacon. The belly produces bacon and pancetta as well as spare ribs. The leg is usually cured and smoked as ham, or sold fresh for roasting.

Other cuts

The head is used for head cheese and guanciale (from the jowl); trotters, hocks, ears, and offal feature in many traditional cuisines. Fatback and lard are rendered from the pig's fat deposits.
