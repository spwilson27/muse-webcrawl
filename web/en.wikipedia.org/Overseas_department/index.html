Overseas department

An overseas department (French: département d'outre-mer, or DOM) is a department of France that is outside European France. Overseas departments have the same political status as departments in metropolitan France and are fully integrated into the French Republic, forming part of the European Union as outermost regions.

List

France has five overseas departments: Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Réunion, and Mayotte (which became a department in 2011). Each also functions as an overseas region, and since 2003 their institutional arrangements have been largely aligned with those of other French regions.

Status and administration

Residents are French citizens who vote in national elections and use the euro. Laws and regulations of France apply, though adaptations are permitted to account for local circumstances. Each department is administered by a prefect representing the state, alongside an elected departmental (and regional) council.

History

The status was created in 1946, when the old colonies of Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, and Réunion were transformed into departments, ending their colonial status in law and granting their inhabitants full French citizenship rights.
