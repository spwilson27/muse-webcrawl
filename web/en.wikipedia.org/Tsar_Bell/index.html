The Tsar Bell (Russian: Tsar'-kolokol) is the largest bell in the world, on display on the grounds of the Moscow Kremlin. It was cast in bronze in 1733–1735 by the foundry masters Ivan Motorin and his son Mikhail at the order of Empress Anna.

History

The bell was damaged before it was ever rung: during a fire in 1737, guards threw cold water on the hot bell, causing a slab of about 11.5 tonnes to break off. It remained in its casting pit for nearly a century before being raised in 1836 and placed on a stone pedestal beside the Ivan the Great Bell Tower.

Description

The bell weighs almost 202 tonnes, stands 6.14 metres tall and is decorated with relief portraits of Empress Anna, Tsar Alexei, angels, saints and ornamental plants.
