Firestorm

Firestorm
TypeSelf-sustaining conflagration with its own wind system
CausesExtremely intense fires over a large area: bombing raids, wildfires, volcanic events
Notable casesHamburg (1943), Dresden (1945), Tokyo (1945), Peshtigo (1871), Canberra (2003)

A firestorm is a conflagration which attains such intensity that it creates and sustains its own wind system. It is most commonly a natural phenomenon, created during some of the largest bushfires and wildfires, although it has also been deliberately produced by incendiary bombing of cities.

[Image placeholder: A towering convective column rising above a firestorm]

During a firestorm, the intense heat of the fire creates a powerful updraft. Cooler air is pulled in at ground level from all directions toward the centre of the fire, fanning the flames further and creating violent, hurricane-force inflow winds. Temperatures can rise so high that everything flammable combusts, and people in shelters may die from heat and carbon monoxide even without direct contact with flames. The rising column can generate pyrocumulonimbus clouds, lightning, and fire whirls.

Mechanism

A firestorm differs from an ordinary mass fire in that the fire, rather than being driven by the ambient wind, generates its own weather. The buoyant plume of hot gases rises, and air rushes inward at the surface to replace it. Because the inflow converges from all sides, the fire does not spread steadily in one direction but consumes the fuel within a defined area at extraordinary intensity, with temperatures exceeding 1,000 °C. Once started, a firestorm persists until the available fuel within the area is exhausted.

Historical examples

Second World War bombing

Firestorms were produced by Allied bombing raids on Hamburg (Operation Gomorrah, July 1943, killing roughly 40,000 people), Dresden (February 1945), and Tokyo (the firebombing of 9–10 March 1945, the single deadliest air raid in history). The atomic bombing of Hiroshima also produced a firestorm. The phenomenon drove post-war strategic debate about area bombing and later informed civil-defence planning for nuclear war.

Wildfires

Natural firestorms include the Peshtigo fire of 1871 in Wisconsin, the deadliest wildfire in recorded history, and the 2003 Canberra bushfires in Australia, during which a pyrocumulonimbus cloud and a fire-generated tornado were documented. The 2017 and later Californian and Australian fires have produced firestorm-scale fire behaviour and fire whirls.
