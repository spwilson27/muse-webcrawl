Gordion

Location: Ankara Province, Turkey

Coordinates: 39.65°N 32.01°E

Type: Settlement

Condition: In ruins

Gordion was the capital city of ancient Phrygia. It was located at the site of the modern village of Yassıhöyük, about 70–80 km southwest of Ankara, in the immediate vicinity of Polatlı, Turkey. Gordion's location at the confluence of the Sakarya and Porsuk rivers gave it a strategic location with control over fertile land.

History

The site was occupied from the Bronze Age, but rose to prominence in the early Iron Age as the capital of the Phrygian kingdom, flourishing in the 9th and 8th centuries BC under kings such as Gordias and Midas. Gordion is famous as the setting of the legend of the Gordian Knot, which Alexander the Great is said to have cut in 333 BC. The city was destroyed by the Cimmerians around 700 BC and later rebuilt under Lydian and Persian rule; it declined in the Hellenistic period and was abandoned in Roman times.

Archaeology

Excavations began in 1900 under Gustav and Alfred Körte, and the University of Pennsylvania Museum has conducted work at the site since 1950, initially under Rodney Young. The great burial mound known as the Midas Mound (Tumulus MM), with a diameter of about 300 metres, contained a wooden burial chamber — among the oldest standing wooden structures in the world — dated to about 740 BC. In 2023, Gordion was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
