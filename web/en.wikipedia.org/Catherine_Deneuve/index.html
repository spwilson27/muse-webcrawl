Catherine Deneuve

Catherine Deneuve
Born: October 22, 1943, Paris, France
Occupation: Actress
Notable films: The Umbrellas of Cherbourg, Belle de Jour, Indochine

Catherine Fabienne Dorléac (born 22 October 1943), known professionally as Catherine Deneuve, is a French actress regarded as one of the greatest European film stars. She gained recognition in Jacques Demy's musical The Umbrellas of Cherbourg (1964) and achieved international fame in Roman Polanski's Repulsion (1965) and Luis Buñuel's Belle de Jour (1967).

Career

Deneuve has worked with many of Europe's leading directors, including François Truffaut (The Last Metro, for which she won the César Award for Best Actress), André Téchiné, and Buñuel again in Tristana. She received an Academy Award nomination for Indochine (1992), which won the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film. Her later work includes Dancer in the Dark (2000) and 8 Women (2002).

Public image

Deneuve's likeness was used as the model for Marianne, the national symbol of France, from 1985 to 1989. She remains an enduring icon of French cinema and style.
