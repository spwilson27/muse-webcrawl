Dress code

A dress code is a set of standards that companies and institutions develop in relation to what employees and participants may wear. Dress codes are also common at schools, religious services, social events, and private clubs.

Western dress codes

Modern Western social dress codes form a hierarchy of formality:

  • White tie — the most formal evening dress: tailcoat, white bow tie, and waistcoat for men; full-length evening gown for women.
  • Black tie — dinner jacket (tuxedo) with black bow tie; evening gown or cocktail dress.
  • Business formal — suit and tie; equivalent office attire.
  • Business casual — collared shirts, trousers, or skirts without ties or jeans (varies by employer).
  • Smart casual — neat, informal clothing such as polo shirts and chinos.
  • Casual — jeans, T-shirts, and trainers.

School and workplace codes

Many schools enforce uniform or dress-code rules, often justified by safety, equality, or focus on study, and sometimes criticised on grounds of self-expression or gender equity. Workplace codes may be imposed for branding, professionalism, or safety — for example, high-visibility clothing and protective footwear in industrial settings.

Religious and cultural codes

Many religions prescribe modes of dress, such as the hijab in Islam, the kippah in Judaism, and monastic robes in Buddhism. Dress codes at places of worship commonly require modest clothing and uncovered or covered heads depending on tradition.
