Chen Changxing (1771–1853), courtesy name Yunting, was a 14th-generation master of the Chen family martial arts of Chenjiagou in Wen County, Henan. He is credited with synthesizing the older Chen routines into the "Old Frame" (Laojia) forms of what became Chen-style tai chi, and he is best known as the teacher of Yang Luchan, founder of Yang-style tai chi.

Life and teaching

Chen worked for much of his life as an armed escort (biaoshi), guarding merchant convoys, a profession that gave him a formidable practical reputation. Tradition holds that he combined routines attributed to Chen Wangting with newer material into two Old Frame routines and systematized principles such as silk-reeling energy (chansijing).

Yang Luchan

According to the famous account, Yang Luchan, a servant in a pharmacy owned by Chen family members, secretly observed Chen's teaching before being accepted as a student. Yang later taught in Beijing and softened the art for broader practice, creating the most widely practised tai chi style; because of this transmission, Chen Changxing is regarded as a pivotal figure linking the village Chen art to the modern tai chi families.

Legacy

Chen's other notable student, Chen Qingping, transmitted a lineage leading to the Zhaobao and small-frame styles. Later writers attached cosmological diagrams and theory texts to Chen Changxing's name, though the exact authorship of the "Taijiquan illustrated" materials associated with him remains debated among historians of the art.
