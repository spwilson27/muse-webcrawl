Battle of Chaeronea (338 BC)

Date2 August 338 BC (traditional date)
LocationChaeronea, Boeotia, Greece
ResultDecisive Macedonian victory
BelligerentsMacedon vs Athens, Thebes and allies

The Battle of Chaeronea was fought in 338 BC, near the city of Chaeronea in Boeotia, between the Macedonians led by Philip II and an alliance of Greek city-states led by Athens and Thebes. The battle was a decisive Macedonian victory and resulted in Macedonian supremacy over Greece.

Philip commanded the Macedonian right wing, while his 18-year-old son Alexander led the cavalry on the left. According to ancient accounts, Philip feigned a retreat to draw the Athenians out of position, stretching the allied line; Alexander then smashed through the gap and fell upon the Thebans. The Sacred Band of Thebes, the elite unit of 150 pairs of lovers, was annihilated where it stood — the mass grave excavated at Chaeronea, marked by the Lion of Chaeronea statue, is traditionally identified as its burial place. About 1,000 Athenians were killed and 2,000 captured.

After the battle Philip treated Athens generously but garrisoned Thebes. The victory led to the League of Corinth (337 BC), under which the Greek states accepted Macedonian hegemony and joined the planned invasion of Persia — the campaign Alexander would execute after Philip's assassination. Chaeronea thus marks the end of the independent classical Greek city-state.
