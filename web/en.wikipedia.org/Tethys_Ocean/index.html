Tethys Ocean

The Tethys Ocean was an ancient ocean that existed between the continents of Gondwana and Laurasia during much of the Mesozoic era, before the opening of the Indian and Atlantic oceans. It is named after the Greek sea goddess Tethys, sister and consort of Oceanus.

History

The geologist Eduard Suess coined the name in 1893 for the vanished sea whose sediments now form the mountain belts from the Alps to the Himalayas. Successive phases are distinguished: the Palaeo-Tethys, which closed during the late Paleozoic and early Mesozoic, and the Neo-Tethys, which widened as Gondwana broke apart and then shrank as Africa, Arabia, and India drifted north into Eurasia.

Remnants

The Mediterranean Sea, Black Sea, Caspian Sea, and Aral Sea are considered remnants of the Tethys, as are the marine sedimentary rocks uplifted in the Alpide belt. The Paratethys was a large shallow sea that split off along its northern margin during the Oligocene and Miocene.
