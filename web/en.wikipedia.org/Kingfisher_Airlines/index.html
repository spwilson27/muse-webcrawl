Kingfisher Airlines Limited was an Indian airline based in Bangalore. Founded by Vijay Mallya’s United Breweries Group and named after its Kingfisher beer brand, it launched in 2005 as a premium full-service carrier, grew rapidly through the acquisition of Air Deccan, and collapsed in 2012 under heavy debts, becoming one of India’s most prominent corporate failures.

Rise

Kingfisher distinguished itself with spacious cabins, in-flight entertainment and high service standards, winning international awards and ordering wide-body aircraft including the Airbus A380 for planned long-haul expansion. In 2007 it acquired a controlling stake in the low-cost pioneer Air Deccan, rebranded as Kingfisher Red, which saddled it with large losses.

Collapse

Rising fuel prices, the 2008 financial crisis and an ill-suited mixed business model left the airline with debts of roughly 7,000 crore rupees owed to banks, airports, lessors, employees and tax authorities. Flights were suspended in October 2012 after strikes by unpaid staff, its licence was suspended and later allowed to lapse, and Mallya left India in 2016 amid loan-fraud allegations and extradition proceedings in the United Kingdom.
