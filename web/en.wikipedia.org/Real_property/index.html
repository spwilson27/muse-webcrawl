Real property

In English common law, real property, real estate, or immovable property is land, together with any permanent structures attached to it — buildings, and the rights associated with the land, such as crops, minerals, and water rights. It is distinguished from personal property (movables) such as vehicles, furniture, and money.

Historical background

The term reflects the feudal origins of English land law, in which all land was ultimately held of the Crown and disputes over land could be resolved by "real actions" recovering the thing itself, whereas personal property claims were remedied only in damages. The doctrine of estates classifies the interests that can be held in land — fee simple, fee tail, life estate, and leasehold among them.

Interests and transfer

Ownership of real property is evidenced and transferred by deeds, recorded in public land registries or under Torrens title systems. Rights in another's land include easements, covenants, liens, and mortgages. Adverse possession allows a long-term possessor, under strict conditions, to acquire title.

Modern usage

Today real property law governs buying, selling, leasing, zoning, and taxation of land and buildings, and underpins the real estate industry. In civil law jurisdictions the corresponding category is immovable property.
