Abraham–Lorentz force

The Abraham–Lorentz force (also known as the Lorentz–Abraham force, the radiation reaction force, or the self-force) is the force that an accelerating charged particle experiences due to its own emitted electromagnetic radiation. It is the classical (non-relativistic) expression of radiation reaction: because an accelerated charge radiates energy according to the Larmor formula, a recoil force must act on the charge.

Formula

The force is proportional to the time derivative of the acceleration (the "jerk"): Frad = (μ0 q² / 6πc) · (da/dt), often written using the characteristic time τ0 = μ0q²/6πmc, which for an electron is about 6 × 10−24 s. The relativistic generalisation is the Abraham–Lorentz–Dirac force.

Problems

The Abraham–Lorentz equation of motion is notorious for pathological solutions: runaway solutions, in which a charge accelerates exponentially without external force, and pre-acceleration, in which the charge begins to move before a force is applied, apparently violating causality. These arise because the equation is third-order in time and treats the electron as a point particle. Physicists have proposed resolutions including the Landau–Lifshitz reduction of order, extended (non-point) charge models, and the view that classical electrodynamics simply breaks down at these scales, where quantum electrodynamics takes over. In practice, radiation reaction is significant in high-intensity laser–plasma interactions and in synchrotron radiation damping in particle accelerators.

History

The force was derived in the work of Max Abraham and Hendrik Lorentz in the early 1900s, and Paul Dirac extended it relativistically in 1938.
