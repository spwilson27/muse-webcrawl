Statutory instrument

In many jurisdictions, a statutory instrument (SI) is the principal form of delegated (secondary) legislation made under powers granted by an Act of Parliament (primary legislation). Statutory instruments allow governments and ministers to fill in the details of frameworks established by Acts, and to amend or implement laws without a new Act for every change.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, statutory instruments are governed by the Statutory Instruments Act 1946. Around 3,000 are made each year. Depending on the parent Act, an SI may be subject to the affirmative resolution procedure (requiring approval by Parliament before taking effect) or the negative resolution procedure (taking effect unless Parliament resolves against it). They include orders, regulations, rules, and schemes. Devolved legislatures in Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland make their own subordinate legislation.

Other jurisdictions

Equivalent instruments exist in Ireland (under the Statutory Instruments Act 1947), Canada (regulations and orders in council registered as statutory instruments), and Australia (legislative instruments). Critics periodically raise concerns about the volume of delegated legislation and the adequacy of parliamentary scrutiny, especially where "Henry VIII clauses" allow SIs to amend primary legislation itself.
