TypeInput device
FunctionConverts physical images/documents into digital form
Sensor typesCCD, CIS (contact image sensor)
Common formsFlatbed, sheet-fed, handheld, drum

An image scanner—often abbreviated to just scanner—is a device that optically scans images, printed text, handwriting or an object and converts it to a digital image. Commonly used in offices are variations of the desktop flatbed scanner where the document is placed on a glass window for scanning.

History

Modern scanners descend from early telephotography and fax input devices. Drum scanners, using photomultiplier tubes, long provided the highest quality for professional reproduction, while desktop CCD scanners became widespread in the 1980s and 1990s.

Types

Flatbed scanners are the most common; sheet-fed scanners pull paper past a fixed sensor; handheld scanners are moved manually over the page; and specialised devices scan film, books, and three-dimensional objects.

Quality and software

Scanner output is characterised by optical resolution (in dots per inch), colour depth, and dynamic range. Scanned text is often processed with optical character recognition (OCR) software to produce editable documents.
