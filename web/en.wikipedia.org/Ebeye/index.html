Ebeye (Marshallese: Epjā) is the most populous island of Kwajalein Atoll in the Marshall Islands, and the second most populous island in the country after Majuro. Despite its tiny land area of roughly 80 acres (0.36 km²), it is home to around 10,000 to 15,000 people, making it one of the most densely populated places on Earth.

History

Ebeye's density stems from the United States military presence at the nearby Reagan Test Site on Kwajalein Island. During the Cold War, Marshallese workers employed at the missile range, and islanders displaced from other atolls because of nuclear testing in the Pacific, were settled on Ebeye. The island is connected to Kwajalein by a ferry used by daily commuters who work at the base but are not permitted to live there.

Living conditions

The island has long struggled with overcrowding, ageing infrastructure, intermittent power and fresh water, sanitation problems and high rates of infectious disease. It has sometimes been described in the media as a "slum of the Pacific". Marshallese and US-funded projects have sought to improve water supply, sewerage, electricity and housing, and a causeway and infrastructure upgrades have been undertaken in recent decades.
