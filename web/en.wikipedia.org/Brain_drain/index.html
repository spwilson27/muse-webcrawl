Brain drain

Brain drain is the emigration of highly trained or qualified people from a country, region, or field, typically for better pay, working conditions, or quality of life elsewhere. The term was popularised in the 1960s with reference to scientists and technologists leaving the United Kingdom for North America.

Brain drain can deprive source countries of scarce skills — notably in medicine, where wealthy nations recruit health workers trained in poorer ones — and of the return on public investment in education. Offsetting effects include remittances sent home, diaspora networks, and "brain circulation", in which emigrants return with new skills and capital or collaborate across borders.

The concept also applies within countries, as when rural areas or smaller cities lose graduates to metropolitan centres.
