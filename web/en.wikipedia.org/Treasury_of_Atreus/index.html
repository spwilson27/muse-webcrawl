Treasury of Atreus

The Treasury of Atreus or Tomb of Agamemnon is a large tholos or beehive tomb constructed around 1250 BC near Mycenae in Greece. It is the largest and most elaborate of the nine tholos tombs at Mycenae and one of the most impressive monuments surviving from Bronze Age Greece. The name derives from Pausanias, who described underground "treasuries" built by Atreus, father of Agamemnon, to house his wealth, though the structure was in fact a royal tomb.

Architecture

The tomb consists of a sloping entrance passage (dromos) about 36 m long, a doorway some 5.4 m high once framed by columns of green limestone, and a circular chamber 14.5 m in diameter roofed by a corbelled dome 13.5 m high — the largest dome in the world until the Roman Pantheon. Above the doorway sits a lintel stone weighing around 120 tonnes, with a relieving triangle above it. A smaller side chamber, partly cut into rock, likely held burials. Despite the name, no direct link to Atreus or Agamemnon can be proven, and the tomb was robbed in antiquity.
