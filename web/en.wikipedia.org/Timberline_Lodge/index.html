Timberline Lodge

Timberline Lodge is a mountain lodge on the south side of Mount Hood in Clackamas County, Oregon, United States, at an elevation of about 5,960 feet (1,817 m) within the Mount Hood National Forest. Built during the Great Depression by the Works Progress Administration (WPA), it is celebrated as a masterpiece of Cascadian rustic architecture and hand craftsmanship.

History

Construction began in 1936; unemployed craftspeople employed by the WPA carved the massive timber beams, built the great central stone fireplace, and produced hand-woven textiles, furniture, paintings and ironwork, much of it from local and recycled materials. President Franklin D. Roosevelt dedicated the lodge on 28 September 1937. It struggled financially in its early decades and was closed briefly in 1955 before being taken over by operator Richard Kohnstamm, whose family long ran the ski area.

Ski area and culture

Timberline offers the only year-round lift-served skiing in North America, with summer skiing on the Palmer Glacier; its lifts include the Magic Mile, first opened in 1939. The lodge's exterior is famous worldwide as the establishing shot of the Overlook Hotel in Stanley Kubrick's film The Shining (1980).
