Barycenter

The barycenter (from Greek barús "heavy" and kéntron "centre") is the center of mass of two or more bodies that are orbiting each other. It is the point about which the bodies orbit.

Examples

The Earth and Moon orbit a barycenter about 4,671 km from Earth's center, inside the Earth. The Sun and Jupiter orbit a barycenter just outside the Sun's surface.

Mathematics

For two bodies, the distance of the primary from the barycenter is determined by the ratio of the masses and their separation.

Importance

Barycentric coordinates are fundamental in astronomy, orbital mechanics and the detection of exoplanets by the wobble method.
