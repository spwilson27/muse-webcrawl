Marion Mahony Griffin (née Marion Lucy Mahony; 14 February 1871 – 10 August 1961) was an American architect and artist. She was one of the first licensed female architects in the world, and is regarded as an original member of the Prairie School. Her celebrated architectural renderings, executed in a distinctive style influenced by Japanese prints, helped define the visual identity of Frank Lloyd Wright's early work and of the Prairie School generally.

Early life and career

Mahony graduated from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1894, only the second woman to earn its architecture degree. She worked for her cousin Dwight Perkins and then, from 1895, in Frank Lloyd Wright's studio, where she designed buildings, interiors, furniture and murals, and produced the luminous watercolor perspectives — most famously for the Wasmuth Portfolio of 1910 — that carried Wright's designs to a European audience.

Partnership with Walter Burley Griffin

In 1911 she married Walter Burley Griffin, a colleague from Wright's studio. Together they won the international competition to design Canberra, the new capital of Australia, in 1912; Mahony's presentation drawings are credited as decisive in the win. The couple worked in Australia for over two decades on projects including the Sydney suburb of Castlecrag, then moved to India in 1935, where Walter died in 1937. Marion returned to Chicago and later wrote an unpublished memoir, The Magic of America, a major source on the Prairie School circle.
