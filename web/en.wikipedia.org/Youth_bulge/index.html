Youth bulge

A youth bulge is a demographic pattern in which a country or region has a disproportionately large share of its population between the ages of roughly 15 and 29. The term was popularised by scholars studying the links between demography and political instability.

Theory

Researchers such as Gunnar Heinsohn and Henrik Urdal have argued that a large youth cohort, especially of young men facing unemployment and delayed prospects, can increase the risk of unrest, conflict, and rapid social change. Youth bulges have been invoked to help explain phenomena from historical revolutions to the Arab Spring.

Responses and criticism

Others caution that youth bulges are not destiny: outcomes depend on education, labour markets, and institutions. When absorbed productively, a large young workforce can instead produce a "demographic dividend" of rapid economic growth.
