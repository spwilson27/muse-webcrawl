International auxiliary language

An international auxiliary language (IAL) is a language meant for communication between people from different nations who do not share a common native language. Historically such languages have often been constructed, including Esperanto (the most widely spoken), Ido, Interlingua, Volapük, and Latino sine flexione.

History and types

Natural languages have also served as auxiliary languages — Latin, French, and today English act as global lingua francas. Constructed IALs are typically classed as schematic (a priori) or naturalistic (a posteriori, drawing vocabulary from existing languages). The IAL movement peaked in the early 20th century; Esperanto was proposed for official use at the League of Nations and UNESCO has passed resolutions acknowledging it.
