Balmer series

The Balmer series is the set of spectral emission lines of the hydrogen atom that result from electron transitions from higher energy levels down to the second energy level (n = 2). Its lines lie largely in the visible spectrum.

The Balmer formula

Johann Balmer discovered in 1885 an empirical formula predicting the wavelengths of the four visible lines — H-alpha (656.3 nm, red), H-beta (486.1 nm), H-gamma (434.0 nm), and H-delta (410.2 nm). The formula was later explained by the Rydberg formula and the Bohr model of the atom.

Astronomical importance

Balmer lines are prominent in the spectra of A-type stars and are widely used in astronomy to estimate stellar temperatures, and H-alpha emission traces ionized gas in nebulae and star-forming regions.
