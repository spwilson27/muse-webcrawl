UCI WorldTeam

A UCI WorldTeam is a professional road cycling team of the highest category licensed by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI). WorldTeams are obliged to compete in all races of the UCI World Tour, including the three Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia and Vuelta a España — and are in turn guaranteed invitations to those events.

Licensing

Licences are awarded in multi-year cycles following assessment of sporting, ethical, financial, administrative and organisational criteria. Since the 2023 season, promotion and relegation between WorldTeams and UCI ProTeams has been determined by the UCI World Team Ranking accumulated over a three-year period. Teams typically employ between 27 and 30 riders under contract and must meet minimum salary and staffing standards.

History

The top division originated with the UCI ProTour in 2005, when the licensed teams were called UCI ProTeams. After a power struggle between the UCI and Grand Tour organisers, the World Tour calendar was reconstituted, and from 2015 the top-tier teams became known as UCI WorldTeams. Women's top-tier squads are separately licensed as UCI Women's WorldTeams.

Events

WorldTeams compete in the World Tour calendar of one-day classics (such as Paris–Roubaix and the Tour of Flanders) and stage races (such as Paris–Nice, the Critérium du Dauphiné and the three Grand Tours). They may also accept invitations to races in the UCI ProSeries and continental circuits.
