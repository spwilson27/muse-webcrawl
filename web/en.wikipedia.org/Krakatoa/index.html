Krakatoa

Krakatoa (Indonesian: Krakatau) is a caldera in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra, Indonesia, and the site of one of the most violent volcanic eruptions in recorded history.

1883 eruption

On 26–27 August 1883, Krakatoa erupted with a force estimated at about 200 megatons of TNT. The climactic explosion was heard nearly 3,000 miles away, ejected some 20 cubic kilometres of rock and ash, and generated tsunamis up to about 35 metres that killed more than 36,000 people along the coasts of Java and Sumatra. Two-thirds of the island was destroyed, leaving a caldera. Global temperatures fell by more than 1 °C in the following years, and vivid sunsets were reported worldwide.

Anak Krakatau

A new volcanic island, Anak Krakatau ("Child of Krakatoa"), emerged from the caldera in 1927 and has grown since. Its flank collapse in December 2018 triggered a tsunami that killed more than 400 people.
