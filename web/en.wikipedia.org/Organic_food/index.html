Organic food, also known as ecological food or biological food, is food and drinks produced by methods complying with the standards of organic farming. Standards vary worldwide, but organic farming features practices that cycle resources, promote ecological balance, and conserve biodiversity. Organisations regulating organic products may restrict the use of certain pesticides and fertilisers in the farming methods used to produce such products.

Organic foods typically are not processed using irradiation, industrial solvents, or synthetic food additives. Livestock raised for organic meat, eggs and dairy must have access to the outdoors and be fed organic feed, and may not be given routine antibiotics or growth hormones.

History

The organic movement began in the early 20th century in reaction to industrialising agriculture: Sir Albert Howard's work in India, Lady Eve Balfour's The Living Soil (1943), and J. I. Rodale's publishing in the United States laid the foundations. Legal definitions arrived later — the US Organic Foods Production Act (1990) and USDA National Organic Program (2002), and EU regulations from 1991 onwards. IFOAM – Organics International coordinates standards internationally.

Health, environment and debate

Consumers often buy organic food out of concern over pesticide residues, additives and environmental impact. Scientific reviews find that organic farming generally supports greater on-farm biodiversity but requires more land per unit of output, and that evidence for significant nutritional superiority of organic food is limited and contested. Organic products usually command price premiums, and certification fraud is an ongoing regulatory concern.
