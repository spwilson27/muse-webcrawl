Chrysler

ParentStellantis
HeadquartersAuburn Hills, Michigan, United States

Chrysler is an American automobile manufacturer and brand, and the name of the historic American automotive company founded by Walter Chrysler in 1925. For most of the twentieth century Chrysler was one of Detroit's "Big Three" automakers alongside General Motors and Ford. Its United States operations are now part of the multinational group Stellantis, formed in 2021.

History

Walter Chrysler built the company from the remnants of the Maxwell Motor Company, launching the Chrysler Six in 1924 and quickly expanding by acquiring Dodge Brothers in 1928 and launching the Plymouth and DeSoto brands. The company was known for engineering innovations such as hydraulic brakes, and later for the "Forward Look" styling of the 1950s and the muscle cars and minivans of subsequent decades — the 1984 Plymouth Voyager and Dodge Caravan essentially created the minivan segment.

Crises and mergers

Chrysler narrowly avoided bankruptcy in 1979 with federal loan guarantees under chairman Lee Iacocca. In 1998 it merged with Daimler-Benz to form DaimlerChrysler, a union dissolved in 2007 when the company was sold to Cerberus Capital Management. In 2009 Chrysler entered Chapter 11 bankruptcy and emerged under the control of Italy's Fiat, eventually becoming Fiat Chrysler Automobiles (FCA), which merged with PSA Group in 2021 to form Stellantis.

The brand today

As a marque, Chrysler now sells principally the Pacifica minivan in North America, having discontinued the 300 sedan in 2023, while the group's American operations continue to market Dodge, Jeep and Ram vehicles.
