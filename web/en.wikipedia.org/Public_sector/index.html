Public sector

Public sector
Comprises: central government, local government, public corporations
Contrasted with: private sector, voluntary sector
Key functions: public services, infrastructure, welfare, defence

The public sector, also called the state sector, is the part of the economy composed of both public services and public enterprises. It includes goods and services provided by national, regional and local governments, such as national defence, public roads, primary education, public healthcare and policing.

Organisation

The organisation of the public sector can take several forms, including direct administration by government departments, wholly or partially state-owned enterprises, and agencies funded wholly or partly by government. Activities that would not survive purely on commercial grounds, or which are considered socially essential, are commonly provided publicly. The precise boundary varies between countries and over time; in many states the late twentieth century saw large-scale privatisation of formerly public enterprises such as telecommunications, rail and energy utilities.

Employment

Public sector employment is a major component of total employment in most developed economies. Workers include civil servants, teachers, healthcare staff, police officers and members of the armed forces. Employment conditions, pay-setting mechanisms and pension arrangements often differ from those in the private sector and are frequently the subject of political debate.

Role in the economy

Economists associate the public sector with the provision of public goods — goods that are non-rival and non-excludable — and with the correction of market failures, redistribution of income, and macroeconomic stabilisation through taxation and public spending.
