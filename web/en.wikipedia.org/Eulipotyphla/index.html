Eulipotyphla is an order of mammals suggested by molecular methods of phylogenetic reconstruction, comprising the hedgehogs and gymnures, shrews, moles and desmans, the solenodons, and the extinct West Indian nesophontes. The name means "truly fat and blind," a reference to the fact that members were long lumped among the "insectivores" — historically grouped in the now-abandoned order Insectivora.

Taxonomic history

Traditionally, small insect-eating mammals were united in Insectivora (or Lipotyphla), including tenrecs and golden moles. Molecular studies in the late 1990s, notably by Waddell and colleagues (1999), showed that tenrecs and golden moles belong to the Afrotheria, while the remaining "lipotyphlans" form a distinct laurasiatherian clade for which the name Eulipotyphla was proposed. Subsequent genomic studies have confirmed the group, placing it among the earliest-diverging lineages of Laurasiatheria. Uncertainty persists about whether shrews or hedgehogs are its earliest branch.

Characteristics

Eulipotyphlans are mostly small, nocturnal mammals with long pointed snouts, small eyes, and diets dominated by invertebrates. The order includes some remarkable specializations: the star-nosed mole's tentacled nose, the venomous bite of the solenodons and some shrews, and the Etruscan shrew — among the smallest mammals on Earth by mass. Several species, such as the Cuban and Hispaniolan solenodons, are endangered relicts.
