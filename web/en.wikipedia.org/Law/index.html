Law

Law is a set of rules that are created and are enforceable by social or governmental institutions to regulate behaviour. It has been described variously as a science and as the art of justice. State-enforced laws can be made by a legislature, resulting in statutes; by the executive through decrees and regulations; or established by judges through precedent, usually in common law jurisdictions. Private individuals may create legally binding contracts, and constitutional law, administrative law, and international law form further major branches.

Legal systems

The two principal traditions are civil law, based on comprehensive codified statutes and descending from Roman law as systematised in the Corpus Juris Civilis and the Napoleonic Code, and common law, developed in England and based on binding judicial precedent alongside statutes. Other systems include religious law, such as Islamic Sharia and Jewish Halakha, customary law, and mixed systems combining several traditions.

Subjects

Law is divided into many fields. Public law includes constitutional, administrative, and criminal law, governing the relationship between individuals and the state. Private law includes contract, tort, and property law, governing dealings between private parties. Major further areas include company and commercial law, labour law, family law, environmental law, tax law, and international law. The philosophy of law (jurisprudence) examines questions such as the nature of law, its relation to morality, and the authority of legal institutions, from natural law theories to legal positivism and legal realism.

Institutions and history

Legal systems are administered by judiciaries, executives, legislatures, and the legal profession. Written law is ancient: early codes include the Mesopotamian Code of Hammurabi (c. 1750 BC), and Roman law, canon law, and the English common law profoundly shaped later systems. The rule of law — the principle that all persons and institutions are accountable to law that is publicly promulgated and equally enforced — is a central ideal of modern constitutional states.
