John Paul Meier (August 8, 1942 – October 18, 2022) was an American biblical scholar and Roman Catholic priest. He was a professor of theology at the University of Notre Dame and previously taught at The Catholic University of America. His five-volume work A Marginal Jew: Rethinking the Historical Jesus (1991–2016) is one of the most comprehensive modern studies of the historical Jesus.

Education and career

Meier was educated at St. Joseph's Seminary, at the Gregorian University and the Pontifical Biblical Institute in Rome, where he earned his doctorate in Sacred Scripture, and ordained a priest in 1967. He taught New Testament at Catholic University from 1985 to 1998 and at Notre Dame from 1998 until his retirement in 2013.

A Marginal Jew

In A Marginal Jew Meier set out to reconstruct what can be known of Jesus of Nazareth by strictly historical methods, famously imagining an "unpapal conclave" of Catholic, Protestant, Jewish and agnostic scholars locked in the basement of Harvard Divinity School and forced to reach a consensus document. Across five volumes he examined the roots of the problem and the sources, the mentor and the message, companions and competitors, the secret of Jesus, and the authentic parables. He concluded, among other things, that Jesus was an eschatological prophet in the tradition of Elijah, that a small core of the parables and sayings is authentic, and that certain miracles likely have a basis in events of Jesus's life.

Other work and death

Meier's earlier books include The Vision of Matthew (1979) and Antioch and Rome (with Raymond E. Brown, 1983). He died in New York in October 2022.
