Parshall Oil Field

The Parshall Oil Field is an oil field in Mountrail County, North Dakota, United States, near the town of Parshall on the Fort Berthold Indian Reservation. Discovered in 2006 in the Bakken Formation, it is regarded as the discovery that launched the modern Bakken tight-oil boom: the field demonstrated that horizontally drilled, hydraulically fractured wells could produce oil economically from the thin, low-permeability middle Bakken member, transforming North Dakota into one of the largest oil-producing states in the US.

Discovery

Oil had been produced from the Bakken since 1951, but the formation's tight rock yielded only modest wells for decades, and earlier horizontal drilling in the 1990s was concentrated in the upper Bakken shale. In 2006, EOG Resources completed the Parshall #1-36H well into the middle Bakken dolomite with a single-stage fracture treatment and reported strong production; a follow-up well, the Parshall #2-36H, was even better. The wells revealed a large, oil-charged "fairway" in the middle member east of the main shale depocentre, where the rock was brittle enough to fracture effectively.

Development and significance

The Parshall results triggered a leasing and drilling rush across the Williston Basin. Operators combined long horizontal laterals with multi-stage hydraulic fracturing, and North Dakota's oil production climbed from under 100,000 barrels per day in the mid-2000s to over a million barrels per day within a decade. The field lies partly within the Fort Berthold Reservation of the Mandan, Hidatsa and Arikara Nation, whose members and tribal government received substantial mineral income but also bore much of the boom's impact on roads, housing, and the environment.
