Shiva Purana

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Shiva in meditation]

The Shiva Purana is one of eighteen major Puranas, collections of Sanskrit texts in Hinduism, and is dedicated to the Hindu god Shiva. The surviving manuscripts exist in many different versions and content, with varying numbers of chapters and verses; published editions commonly present around 24,000 verses arranged in six samhitas (sections).

The text discusses cosmology, mythology, the relationship between the gods, ethics, yoga, pilgrimage (tirtha) sites, and the theory and practice of devotion to Shiva. It narrates such famous episodes as the marriage of Shiva and Parvati, the birth of Kartikeya, the churning of the ocean, and the destruction of the demon Tripurasura. Twelve chapters of the Vidyeśvara Samhita and parts of other sections are centred on the worship of the Shiva lingam and the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines.

Contents and dating

Scholars estimate that the oldest core of the Shiva Purana dates from around the 10th to 11th centuries CE, with later additions. The text was probably composed in central and northern India. It also catalogues sacred geography, listing temples and rivers important to Shaiva pilgrims, and it contains discussions of dharma, festivals such as Maha Shivaratri, and methods of worship.
