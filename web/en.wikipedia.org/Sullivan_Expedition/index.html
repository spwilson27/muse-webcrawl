Sullivan Expedition

The Sullivan Expedition (also known as the Sullivan-Clinton Campaign) was a military campaign of the American Revolutionary War conducted in the summer of 1779 against the Iroquois (Haudenosaunee) nations that had sided with the British, and against Loyalist forces in what is now upstate New York and northern Pennsylvania.

Background and campaign

In retaliation for Iroquois and Loyalist raids on frontier settlements — including the Wyoming Valley and Cherry Valley massacres of 1778 — General George Washington ordered Major General John Sullivan to carry out a campaign of total destruction against Iroquois settlements, crops, and food stores. Sullivan's army of about 3,200 men advanced up the Susquehanna River, joined forces with Brigadier General James Clinton's brigade descending from Otsego Lake, and defeated a combined Iroquois-Loyalist force at the Battle of Newtown on 29 August 1779.

Destruction and aftermath

Over the following weeks the expedition destroyed some forty Iroquois towns and enormous quantities of corn and orchards around the Finger Lakes and Genesee Valley. Though it fought only one major battle, the campaign shattered the Iroquois Confederacy's capacity to sustain war and feed its people; thousands of refugees fled to British protection at Fort Niagara, where many starved in the harsh winter of 1779–80. The expedition opened the region to post-war American settlement and is commemorated by numerous markers along the route in New York.
