Holiness movement

The Holiness movement is a Christian movement that emerged chiefly in 19th-century Methodism and teaches that believers can experience entire sanctification, a second work of grace cleansing them from original sin and empowering holy living.

Origins

Rooted in John Wesley's doctrine of Christian perfection, the movement spread through camp meetings, publications, and figures such as Phoebe Palmer in the United States. Many adherents separated from Methodist churches to form Holiness denominations.

Churches and influence

Holiness bodies include the Church of the Nazarene, the Salvation Army in its early ethos, the Wesleyan Church, and the Free Methodist Church. The movement strongly influenced Pentecostalism, which emerged in part from Holiness circles in the early 20th century.

Beliefs

Emphases include conversion, entire sanctification, modest living, and social reform including abolitionism and temperance.
