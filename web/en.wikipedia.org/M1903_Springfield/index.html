The M1903 Springfield is an American five-round, magazine-fed, bolt-action service repeating rifle, manufactured by the Springfield Armory and Rock Island Arsenal. It was formally adopted by the United States armed forces in 1903.

History

The rifle's design was influenced by the German Mauser Model 1893, encountered during the Spanish–American War; the United States eventually paid Mauser patent royalties. The M1903 was first chambered for the .30-03 cartridge and then, from 1906, for the famous .30-06 Springfield round.

Service

The Springfield served as the primary infantry rifle of the U.S. Army in the First World War. Although the semi-automatic M1 Garand replaced it as the standard issue rifle in 1936, M1903s remained in widespread use throughout the Second World War, especially in sniper and grenade-launching roles, and saw service in Korea and early in Vietnam.
