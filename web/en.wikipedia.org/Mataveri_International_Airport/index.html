Mataveri International Airport

Airport serving Easter Island, Chile (IATA: IPC, ICAO: SCIP)

Mataveri International Airport (Spanish: Aeropuerto Internacional Mataveri) is the airport serving Hanga Roa on Easter Island (Rapa Nui), Chile. It is the main point of entry for tourists visiting the island's famous moai statues and is often described as one of the most remote airports in the world: the nearest continental airport, at Santiago, is about 3,500 kilometres away.

History

Construction began in 1965, and the first flight arrived in 1967. The runway was extended in the 1980s with United States involvement so that it could serve as an emergency landing site for NASA's Space Shuttle; the lengthened runway, about 3.3 kilometres long on a narrow corner of the island, was inaugurated in 1987, though the shuttle never used it. The airport displaces part of the archaeological landscape southwest of Rano Kau and has long been a focus of discussion about tourism pressures on the island.

Services

LATAM Airlines operates the principal scheduled services, linking Easter Island with Santiago and (seasonally and historically) with Papeete, Tahiti. The single terminal handles both domestic and international arrivals.
