The Seeing Eye

The Seeing Eye, Inc. is a guide dog school located in Morristown, New Jersey, in the United States. Founded in 1929, it is the oldest existing guide dog school in the United States and one of the largest.

Its name derives from the Biblical verse "hear, ye deaf; and look, ye blind, that ye may see" — and the term "Seeing Eye dog" is a registered trademark for its graduates.

History

American dog trainer Dorothy Harrison Eustis, working in Switzerland, wrote a 1927 Saturday Evening Post article about German guide dog schools for blinded veterans. Morris Frank, a blind young man from Nashville, persuaded her to train a dog for him; his dog Buddy became the first American guide dog, and Frank co-founded The Seeing Eye in Nashville in 1929 before it moved to New Jersey.

Training

Puppies — mainly Labrador and golden retrievers and German shepherds — are raised by volunteer families, then receive about four months of formal training before roughly three weeks of instruction with their blind handlers at the Morristown campus.
