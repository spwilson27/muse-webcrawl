Prevalence

In epidemiology, prevalence is the proportion of a particular population found to be affected by a medical condition (typically a disease or a risk factor such as smoking) at a specific time. It is calculated by dividing the number of people with the condition by the total number of people in the population, and is commonly expressed as a percentage or as the number of cases per 10,000 or 100,000 people.

Types

Point prevalence is the proportion of a population that has the condition at a specific point in time. Period prevalence is the proportion that has the condition at any time within a given period, while lifetime prevalence is the proportion that has ever had the condition up to the time of assessment.

Relationship to incidence

Prevalence differs from incidence, which measures only new cases arising in a period. For a condition in a steady state, prevalence is approximately the product of incidence and average disease duration: conditions that last longer accumulate higher prevalence even at low incidence. Prevalence figures are essential for planning health services, allocating resources, and describing the overall burden of disease in a population.
