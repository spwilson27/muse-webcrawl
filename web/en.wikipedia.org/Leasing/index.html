Leasing

Leasing is a contractual arrangement calling for the user (the lessee) to pay the owner (the lessor) for the use of an asset. Broadly put, a lease agreement is a contract between two parties: the lessor and the lessee. The lessor is the legal owner of the asset, while the lessee obtains the right to use the asset in return for regular rental payments. Property, buildings, vehicles and industrial or business equipment are typical leased assets.

Types of lease

A finance lease (or capital lease) effectively transfers the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee over most of the asset's economic life; an operating lease is shorter than the asset's useful life and the lessor retains more of the ownership risks. Other forms include sale-and-leaseback arrangements, leveraged leases, and short-term rentals such as car rental and equipment hire. In real estate, a lease is the contract by which a landlord grants a tenant exclusive possession of land or buildings for a term in return for rent.

Accounting treatment

Under IFRS 16 and the corresponding US standard ASC 842, most leases must now be recognised on the lessee's balance sheet as a right-of-use asset and a lease liability, largely eliminating the off-balance-sheet advantage that operating leases once conferred.
