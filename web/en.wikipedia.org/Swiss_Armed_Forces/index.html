Swiss Armed Forces

The Swiss Armed Forces (German: Schweizer Armee) are the land and air military forces of Switzerland. Structured as a militia system, they consist of a small professional cadre and a large body of conscript citizen-soldiers, reflecting Switzerland's long tradition of armed neutrality.

Organisation

The armed forces comprise the Land Forces, the Air Force, and supporting joint commands, under the Federal Department of Defence, Civil Protection and Sport. In peacetime there is no standing commander-in-chief above the rank of corps commander; in the event of war or national emergency, the Federal Assembly elects a general to command the army, a rank held only a handful of times in Swiss history.

Militia system and conscription

Military service is compulsory for able-bodied male Swiss citizens, with women able to volunteer. Conscripts complete recruit school and then serve periodic refresher courses, keeping their personal equipment and, historically, their service rifle at home. Those who decline military service may perform civilian service (Zivildienst) of longer duration. Switzerland's high readiness, extensive fortifications, and prepared demolitions on key Alpine routes long formed the core of its deterrence doctrine; reforms since the end of the Cold War (Armee 95, Armee XXI, and later development steps) have progressively reduced the force's size and professionalized parts of it.

Neutrality

Switzerland has maintained a policy of perpetual neutrality since the Congress of Vienna in 1815, and the armed forces' missions are defined as the defence of the country's territory and population, support to the civil authorities, and peace promotion abroad on a small scale, such as the Swisscoy contingent in Kosovo.
