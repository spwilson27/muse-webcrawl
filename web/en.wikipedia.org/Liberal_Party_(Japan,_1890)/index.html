The Liberal Party (自由党, Jiyūtō) was a political party in Imperial Japan, founded in 1890 by Itagaki Taisuke and other leaders of the Freedom and People's Rights Movement. It was one of the principal parties in the early Diet of Japan.

Background

The party's roots lay in the Aikokusha and the original Jiyūtō of 1881, the first nationwide political party in Japan, which demanded a national assembly and constitutional government. After that party dissolved in 1884, its veterans regrouped as the Daidō Club and, in August 1890, ahead of the first general election, re-established the Liberal Party.

Political activity

The Liberal Party won the largest bloc of seats in the first election to the House of Representatives in 1890 and allied with other opposition parties to resist the oligarchic governments of the Meiji genrō. It advocated reductions in land tax, economy in government, and expanded civil liberties. Under Itagaki, the party briefly entered government in 1898 in coalition with the Constitutional Party in the Ōkuma–Itagaki cabinet, Japan's first party cabinet.

Dissolution and legacy

In 1900 Itagaki retired and the party was absorbed into the Rikken Seiyūkai founded by Itō Hirobumi. The Jiyūtō name was revived by several later parties, most notably the post-war Liberal Party that merged into the Liberal Democratic Party in 1955.
