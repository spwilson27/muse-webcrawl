The wakizashi (Japanese: 脇差, "side inserted [sword]") is one of the traditionally made Japanese swords (nihontō) worn by the samurai in feudal Japan. Its size is similar to that of a long dagger or short sword, with a blade generally between 30 and 60 cm (12 and 24 in) long — between 1 and 2 shaku. Wakizashi close to the length of a katana are called ō-wakizashi and those closer to tantō length are called ko-wakizashi.

Use

The wakizashi was worn together with the katana as the daishō, the symbol of the samurai class. It served as a backup or auxiliary sword, for close-quarters fighting, and for decapitating a defeated opponent. Because it was shorter, the wakizashi could be worn indoors when the katana was left at the entrance of a house or castle. It was also the blade used in the ritual suicide of seppuku.

When worn together, the pair of swords were thrust through the sash (obi) with their cutting edges facing up. The wakizashi was worn on the left side of the body, close to the centre.

History

The production of wakizashi began in the Muromachi period. In the Edo period, Tokugawa regulations defined which members of society could wear the daishō — a privilege of the samurai class — and specified permissible blade lengths. Famous swordsmiths produced wakizashi in the same traditions as longer blades, and antique examples are collected today as art objects under Japanese law governing traditionally made swords.
