Indian Americans

Indian Americans are Americans with ancestry from India. The Indian American community, numbering over five million people, is among the largest and fastest-growing Asian American groups. The United States Census Bureau classifies them within the Asian Indian category, distinct from Native Americans, who are also sometimes called American Indians.

History

Small numbers of Indians arrived in the United States from the eighteenth century, and Punjabi farmers settled in California in the early twentieth century despite restrictive laws such as the Immigration Act of 1917 and the Supreme Court's 1923 decision in United States v. Bhagat Singh Thind. Large-scale immigration followed the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965, which favoured skilled professionals; later waves included family migrants and technology workers on H-1B visas.

Demographics and society

Indian Americans have among the highest median household incomes and educational attainment levels of any ethnic group in the United States, with strong representation in medicine, engineering, information technology, academia and business. Major population centres include the New York metropolitan area, the San Francisco Bay Area, Chicago, Houston and Dallas. The community is religiously diverse — predominantly Hindu, with significant Muslim, Christian, Sikh and Jain minorities — and celebrates festivals such as Diwali, which has received growing official recognition.
