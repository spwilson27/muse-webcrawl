Hydrology

Hydrology
Field: Earth science
Studies: Movement, distribution and management of water
Branches: Surface hydrology, hydrogeology, hydrometeorology, ecohydrology, hydroinformatics

Hydrology is the scientific study of the movement, distribution and management of water on Earth and other planets, including the water cycle, water resources and drainage basin sustainability. Hydrologists work in environmental science, civil and environmental engineering, water supply, flood forecasting, irrigation and climate research.

The water cycle

Hydrology centres on the hydrological cycle: evaporation and transpiration lift water to the atmosphere, where it condenses, falls as precipitation, and returns via surface runoff, rivers and groundwater flow to lakes and oceans. The water balance equation — precipitation equals runoff plus evapotranspiration plus change in storage — is fundamental to quantitative hydrology.

Branches

Surface-water hydrology studies rivers, lakes and floods; hydrogeology studies groundwater in aquifers; hydrometeorology links weather and water; ecohydrology examines interactions between water and ecosystems; and catchment hydrology studies how drainage basins transform rainfall into streamflow. Isotope hydrology uses natural tracers to date and trace water.

Methods and applications

Hydrologists measure streamflow with gauges, model basins with rainfall–runoff and distributed physical models, use remote sensing of snow, soil moisture and groundwater, and apply statistics to estimate flood frequencies. Applications include dam and levee design, drought planning, water allocation, land-use planning and predicting climate-change impacts on water resources.

History

Ancient civilisations engineered irrigation and flood control, but quantitative hydrology began with Perrault and Mariotte's 17th-century measurements showing rainfall could supply the Seine's flow, and Halley's evaporation studies. The 20th century added unit hydrograph theory, Horton's infiltration work and modern computational models.
