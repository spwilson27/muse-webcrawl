Treblinka extermination camp

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Treblinka was an extermination camp built and operated by Nazi Germany in occupied Poland during World War II. It was located in a forest north-east of Warsaw, near the village of Treblinka. The camp, built as part of Operation Reinhard — the deadliest phase of the Holocaust — operated between 23 July 1942 and 19 October 1943. During this time, it is estimated that between 700,000 and 900,000 Jews were murdered in its gas chambers, along with 2,000 Romani people. More Jews were murdered at Treblinka than at any other Nazi extermination camp apart from Auschwitz-Birkenau.

Operation

Victims arrived by rail, mostly from the Warsaw Ghetto and other ghettos in occupied Poland. They were killed with engine-exhaust carbon monoxide in gas chambers disguised as shower rooms; the bodies were buried, and later exhumed and cremated on open-air pyres to conceal the crime. The camp staff consisted of a small SS cadre and Trawniki guards, while Jewish prisoners were forced to perform much of the labour.

Uprising and closure

On 2 August 1943, prisoners staged a revolt, seizing weapons and setting buildings ablaze; a few hundred escaped, though most were hunted down and only some dozens survived the war. The camp was dismantled in late 1943 and the site disguised as a farm. A memorial of rough-hewn stones and symbolic railway tracks was erected at the site in the 1960s.
