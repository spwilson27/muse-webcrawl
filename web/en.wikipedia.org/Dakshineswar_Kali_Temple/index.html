Dakshineswar Kali Temple

Dakshineswar Kali Temple is a Hindu navaratna temple in Dakshineswar, Kolkata, West Bengal, India, situated on the eastern bank of the Hooghly River. The presiding deity is Bhavatarini, a form of Parashakti Adya Kali. The temple was built in 1855 by Rani Rashmoni, a Zamindar, philanthropist and devotee of Kali. It is closely associated with the 19th-century Bengal mystic Ramakrishna and his wife Sarada Devi, who spent much of their lives there.

History

Legend holds that Rani Rashmoni, about to depart on a pilgrimage to Varanasi in 1847, dreamt of the goddess Kali, who told her to build a temple on the banks of the Ganges instead. She purchased a 30,000-acre plot at Dakshineswar — then partly a Muslim burial ground and European-owned land — and construction ran from 1847 to 1855. The temple was consecrated on 31 May 1855. Ramkumar Chatterjee was appointed head priest; after his death his younger brother Ramakrishna succeeded him, serving the goddess there for some thirty years and making the temple a centre of his spiritual practice. Ramakrishna's room in the north-western part of the courtyard is preserved as a shrine.

Architecture and complex

Built in the traditional navaratna style of Bengal architecture, the three-storeyed, south-facing temple stands on a raised platform reached by a flight of steps, rising about 46 metres with nine spires. The sanctum houses the idol of Kali standing on the chest of Shiva. The complex includes twelve shrines dedicated to Shiva along the riverfront, a temple to Radha-Krishna (Radhakanta), a large natmandir (music hall), bathing ghats, and the Panchavati garden where Ramakrishna practised.

Significance

The temple is one of the most visited pilgrimage sites in Kolkata. The nearby Belur Math, headquarters of the Ramakrishna Math and Mission founded by Swami Vivekananda, lies across the river, and the two are linked by ferry.
