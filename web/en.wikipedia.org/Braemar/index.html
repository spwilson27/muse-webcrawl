Braemar

Braemar (Scottish Gaelic: Bràigh Mhàrr) is a village in Aberdeenshire, Scotland, around 58 miles (93 km) west of Aberdeen in the Highlands. It is situated in the upper valley of the River Dee, within the Cairngorms National Park, at an elevation of about 340 metres (1,100 ft), surrounded by pine forest and mountains.

History

The village developed around Braemar Castle, built in 1628 by the Earl of Mar and later used as a Hanoverian garrison after the Jacobite rising of 1745, in which the standard was raised at Braemar in 1715. In the 19th century the area became fashionable with the royal family's attachment to nearby Balmoral Castle, bought by Queen Victoria and Prince Albert in 1852.

Braemar Gathering

Braemar is best known for the Braemar Gathering, a Highland games held on the first Saturday of September, traditionally attended by members of the royal family. Highland games are said to have been held in the area for centuries; the modern gathering features piping, dancing, heavy events such as tossing the caber, and hill races.

Climate and geography

Braemar is one of the coldest inhabited places in the United Kingdom: it shares the UK's lowest recorded temperature of −27.2 °C, measured in 1895 and again in 1982. It is a base for walking and climbing in the Cairngorms and the Linn of Dee, a nearby gorge on the River Dee.
