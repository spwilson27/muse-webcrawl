Christogram

Type: Christian monogram

Examples: Chi Rho, IHS, IC XC

A Christogram is a monogram or combination of letters forming an abbreviation for the name of Jesus Christ. Christograms have been used since early Christianity on coins, seals, tombs, manuscripts, vestments, architecture, and devotional objects.

Major forms

The Chi Rho combines the Greek letters chi and rho, the first letters of Christos. The IHS monogram derives from the first letters of the Greek spelling of Jesus and was later interpreted in Latin phrases. IC XC abbreviates “Jesus Christ” using first and last letters, often paired with NIKA, meaning “conquers.”

History

After Constantine's adoption of the Chi Rho in the fourth century, Christograms became prominent imperial and ecclesiastical symbols. Medieval scribes and religious orders developed many decorative forms, which remain common in Christian art.
