The VT52 is a CRT-based computer terminal introduced by Digital Equipment Corporation (DEC) in 1975. It displayed 24 lines of 80 columns of text and supported both uppercase and lowercase characters.

Features

The VT52 used a proprietary set of escape sequences for cursor positioning, erasing, and graphics characters, including a simple graphics mode plotting at 64×32 resolution aspects. It communicated over serial lines and included a printer port on some models for hardcopy output.

Legacy

The VT52 established DEC's terminal line and was followed in 1978 by the VT100, which adopted ANSI standard escape codes. VT52 emulation modes persisted in later terminals and software for compatibility.
