The cucumber (Cucumis sativus) is a widely cultivated creeping vine in the gourd family, Cucurbitaceae, bearing cylindrical fruits used as a culinary vegetable. Originating in South Asia, where it has been cultivated for at least 3,000 years, it spread along trade routes to China, the Middle East and Europe, and is now grown worldwide, with China producing the large majority of the world crop.

Cultivation and varieties

Cucumbers are typically about 95% water, with a mild flavour and cool, crisp flesh. Varieties fall into three broad groups: slicing cucumbers, eaten fresh; pickling cucumbers, brined or vinegar-pickled as gherkins and cornichons; and seedless or "burpless" greenhouse types such as the long English cucumber. The bitterness some cucumbers show comes from compounds called cucurbitacins, bred out of most modern cultivars.

Uses

Beyond salads and sandwiches, cucumbers appear in Greek tzatziki, Indian raita, cold soups, sushi and infused water, and slices are a classic cosmetic eye treatment. Although botanically a fruit (a pepo berry), the cucumber is treated as a vegetable in the kitchen.
