New Left

The New Left was a broad political movement in Western countries during the 1960s and 1970s. Its adherents, many of them students and intellectuals, campaigned on issues including civil rights, feminism, gay rights, drug policy and opposition to the Vietnam War, while distancing themselves from the older labour movement and orthodox Marxist parties.

Origins

The term was popularised in Britain around the journal New Left Review and in the United States by Students for a Democratic Society, whose 1962 Port Huron Statement became a manifesto. Thinkers such as Herbert Marcuse and C. Wright Mills influenced its critique of consumer capitalism and bureaucracy.

Legacy

The protests of 1968 marked its high point; the movement subsequently fragmented, but its cultural politics shaped later activism around identity, the environment and participatory democracy.
