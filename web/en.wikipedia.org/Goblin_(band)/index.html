Goblin (band)

Goblin is renowned for its progressive rock scores for Italian horror films.

Goblin (also styled the Goblins) is an Italian progressive rock band known for its film scores for horror movies, most famously its collaborations with director Dario Argento. The band formed in Rome in the early 1970s, evolving from earlier groups including Cherry Five, and its key members have included Claudio Simonetti (keyboards), Massimo Morante (guitar), Fabio Pignatelli (bass), and Agostino Marangolo (drums).

Career

Goblin's breakthrough came in 1975 when Argento, dissatisfied with the original jazz score for Deep Red (Profondo Rosso), hired the band to rewrite and perform the music at short notice. The resulting album topped the Italian charts. The band went on to score Argento's Suspiria (1977), widely regarded as one of the most terrifying film soundtracks ever made, and George A. Romero's Dawn of the Dead (1978, credited as "The Goblins"), as well as Tenebrae and numerous other Italian genre films.

Style and legacy

Goblin's music blends progressive rock with funk, jazz, and electronic textures, driven by prominent synthesizers, electric piano, and insistent rhythms, creating an atmosphere of menace that became a signature of 1970s Italian horror. Their scores have been heavily sampled and covered, and the band has enjoyed a major revival since the 2000s, with reunions, extensive international touring (often performing scores live to film screenings), and new studio albums.
