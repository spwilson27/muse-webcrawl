Rosa Luxemburg

Rosa Luxemburg
Born: March 5, 1871, Zamość, Congress Poland
Died: January 15, 1919, Berlin
Occupation: Economist, revolutionary socialist
Works: The Accumulation of Capital (1913)

Rosa Luxemburg (1871–1919) was a Polish-German Marxist economist, philosopher, and revolutionary socialist. A leading figure in the Social Democratic Party of Germany, she co-founded the anti-war Spartacus League, which became the Communist Party of Germany.

Life and thought

Born in Zamość to a Jewish family, Luxemburg became involved in socialist politics as a teenager and fled to Switzerland, earning a doctorate in political economy at the University of Zurich. Moving to Germany in 1898, she became a prominent voice on the left of the SPD, defending revolutionary mass action against Eduard Bernstein's revisionism in Reform or Revolution (1899). Her major economic work, The Accumulation of Capital (1913), argued that capital accumulation depends on expansion into non-capitalist markets.

Death

Imprisoned for opposing the First World War, Luxemburg was released during the German Revolution of 1918. In January 1919, after the suppression of the Spartacist uprising in Berlin, she and Karl Liebknecht were arrested and murdered by Freikorps officers; her body was thrown into the Landwehr Canal.

Legacy

Luxemburg's writings on spontaneity, democracy, and imperialism remain influential, and her insistence that socialism must be democratic — summarised in the phrase "freedom is always the freedom of the one who thinks differently" — is widely quoted.
