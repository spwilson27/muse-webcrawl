Uluç Ali Reis

Uluç Ali Reis (1519 – 21 June 1587), later known as Uluç Ali Pasha and Kılıç Ali Pasha, was an Italian-born Ottoman corsair and admiral. Born Giovanni Dionigi Galeni in Calabria, he was captured by Barbary corsairs as a youth, converted to Islam, and rose through the ranks of the Ottoman fleet to become one of its most celebrated commanders.

Career

Uluç Ali served under the famous corsair Turgut Reis (Dragut) and became bey (governor) of Tripoli and later of Algiers. At the Battle of Lepanto in 1571 he commanded the Ottoman left wing and was the only Ottoman commander to escape the disastrous defeat with his squadron largely intact, even capturing the flagship of the Knights of Malta. In recognition, Sultan Selim II appointed him Kapudan Pasha (Grand Admiral) of the Ottoman Empire, and he rebuilt the fleet within a single year.

Later life

As grand admiral he recaptured Tunis in 1574 and campaigned in the Mediterranean for over a decade. He died in Constantinople in 1587 and is buried in the Kılıç Ali Pasha Mosque complex in Tophane, designed by the architect Sinan. Miguel de Cervantes, who was a captive in Algiers, mentions him in Don Quixote.
