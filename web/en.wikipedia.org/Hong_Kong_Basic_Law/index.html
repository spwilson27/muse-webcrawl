Hong Kong Basic Law

The Basic Law of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China is a Chinese national law that describes the principles and systems of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR). Enacted by the National People's Congress on 4 April 1990, it came into effect on 1 July 1997, when sovereignty over Hong Kong was transferred from the United Kingdom to China. It serves as the de facto constitution of Hong Kong, implementing the Sino-British Joint Declaration of 1984 and the policy of "one country, two systems".

Contents

The Basic Law consists of 160 articles in nine chapters, together with three annexes. It guarantees that Hong Kong's capitalist system and way of life remain unchanged for 50 years (until 2047), provides for the independence of the judiciary including the Court of Final Appeal, protects fundamental rights and freedoms of residents, and sets out the structure of the government: the Chief Executive, the Executive Council, the Legislative Council, the judiciary, district organisations and the public service.

Drafting

Drafting began in 1985 with the establishment of the Basic Law Drafting Committee, comprising mainland and Hong Kong members, and a consultative committee in Hong Kong. Two drafts were published for public consultation, in 1988 and 1989. The process was shaken by the Tiananmen Square protests of 1989, after which provisions concerning national security (Article 23) and the relationship between the central government and the region were tightened, and several Hong Kong committee members resigned or were expelled.

Interpretation and controversies

The power of final interpretation of the Basic Law rests with the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (Article 158), while Hong Kong courts are authorised to interpret the law in adjudicating cases. The Standing Committee has issued several interpretations, including on the right of abode (1999), electoral reform procedures (2004), the Chief Executive's term (2005), and legislator oath-taking (2016). Disputes over Article 23 national-security legislation, the pace of democratisation toward universal suffrage (Articles 45 and 68), and the 2020 imposition of the Hong Kong National Security Law by the Standing Committee have made the Basic Law a continuing focus of political debate.
