False discovery rate

In statistical hypothesis testing, the false discovery rate (FDR) is the expected proportion of rejected null hypotheses that are actually true (false positives) among all rejections. It provides a less conservative approach to multiple comparisons than family-wise error rate control.

Benjamini–Hochberg procedure

The most widely used method to control the FDR was proposed by Yoav Benjamini and Yosef Hochberg in 1995. Given m p-values sorted in increasing order, one finds the largest k such that p(k) ≤ (k/m)·q, and rejects the k smallest hypotheses, controlling the FDR at level q under independence or certain forms of positive dependence.

Applications

FDR methods are standard in genomics, neuroimaging, and other fields where thousands or millions of hypotheses are tested simultaneously, such as differential gene expression studies using microarrays or RNA sequencing.
