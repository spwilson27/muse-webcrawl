A procurator fiscal, sometimes called simply a fiscal, is a public prosecutor in Scotland, who has the power to impose fiscal fines and who investigates all sudden and unexplained deaths. Procurators fiscal are lawyers employed by the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS), which is headed by the Lord Advocate and managed by the Crown Agent. Unlike the position in England and Wales, where investigation and prosecution are divided between police and the Crown Prosecution Service, in Scotland the fiscal directs police investigations and alone decides whether, and in what court, a case proceeds.

Functions

All crime reported by the police in Scotland is submitted to the local procurator fiscal, who decides on the evidence whether to prosecute in the public interest. Alternatives to prosecution include a formal warning, a fiscal fine (a monetary penalty offered without a court appearance), fiscal work orders, compensation offers, and diversion to social-work or treatment programmes. Serious ("solemn") cases are prosecuted by Advocate Deputes in the High Court of Justiciary, while fiscals conduct summary prosecutions in sheriff and justice of the peace courts.

Fiscals also investigate all sudden, suspicious or unexplained deaths, and may hold a Fatal Accident Inquiry — a public fact-finding hearing before a sheriff — which is mandatory for deaths in custody and at work and discretionary in other cases where the Lord Advocate considers it in the public interest.

History

The office dates to the 16th century, when fiscals acted as financial officers of the sheriff courts; prosecutorial functions took precedence during the 19th century, and the modern unified national service was created in its present form through the 20th century. The office's name and structure reflect Scotland's distinct, partly civil-law-influenced legal system.
