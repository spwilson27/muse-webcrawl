Julius Nepos (died 9 May 480) was a Roman emperor who ruled the Western Roman Empire from 474 to 475, and continued to be recognised as emperor in exile in Dalmatia until his murder in 480. He is often regarded by historians as the last de jure Western Roman emperor, since his successor in Italy, Romulus Augustulus, was never recognised by the Eastern court at Constantinople.

Background

Nepos was the son of Nepotianus, a general who had served in Dalmatia, and his mother was a sister of Marcellinus, the semi-independent ruler of Dalmatia. Through his marriage to a niece of the Eastern empress Aelia Verina, wife of Leo I, Nepos gained powerful connections at the Eastern court. On Marcellinus's death in 468, Nepos succeeded him as ruler of Dalmatia.

Accession

In 474 the Eastern emperor Leo I appointed Nepos to depose the Western emperor Glycerius, who had been raised to the throne by the Burgundian magister militum Gundobad without Constantinople's approval. Nepos landed at Portus in June 474; Glycerius surrendered without a fight and was made bishop of Salona. Nepos was proclaimed emperor in Rome.

Reign

Nepos attempted to preserve the remaining imperial territories in Gaul and Italy. He negotiated with the Visigoths under Euric, ceding the Auvergne in 475 in exchange for recognition and peace, a concession that angered many Gallo-Romans. He also relied on military commanders such as Ecdicius and Syagrius. His relations with his magister militum Orestes deteriorated, and in August 475 Orestes rebelled and marched on Ravenna. Nepos fled by ship to Salona in Dalmatia on 28 August 475.

Exile and death

From Dalmatia, Nepos continued to issue coins and to be recognised by the Eastern emperor Zeno as the legitimate Western emperor. After Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus in 476 and sent the imperial insignia to Constantinople, Zeno pointedly referred the Senate's envoys to Nepos as their emperor. Nepos was murdered on 9 May 480 near Salona, possibly at the instigation of Glycerius or of Odoacer, who invaded Dalmatia soon afterwards.

Legacy

Because the Eastern court never recognised Romulus Augustulus, some historians treat Nepos's death in 480, rather than the deposition of 476, as the true end of the Western Roman Empire's imperial line.
