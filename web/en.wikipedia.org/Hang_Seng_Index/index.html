Hang Seng Index

The Hang Seng Index (HSI) is a freefloat-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted stock-market index of the largest companies traded on the Hong Kong Exchange. It is the principal gauge of the Hong Kong stock market.

History and composition

The index was created by banker Stanley Kwan and launched on 24 November 1969 by Hang Seng Indexes Company, a subsidiary of Hang Seng Bank, with a base of 100 points set at 31 July 1964. The number of constituents, originally 33, has grown over time — reaching the low 80s in the 2020s — and now includes major Hong Kong and mainland Chinese companies such as Tencent, Alibaba and HSBC, organised also into sub-indices for finance, utilities, properties and commerce and industry.

Role

The index underlies futures and options traded on the Hong Kong Exchange and numerous tracker funds and ETFs. Its largest historical peaks and crashes mirror events such as the 1997 Asian financial crisis and the 2007–08 global financial crisis.
