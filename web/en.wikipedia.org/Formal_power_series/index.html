Formal power series

Formal power series
Field: Abstract algebra
Notation: R[[X]]
Key property: Ring structure without convergence requirements
Related: Generating function

In mathematics, a formal power series is an algebraic structure that generalizes the notion of a power series by ignoring questions of convergence. Formal power series are manipulated purely formally: addition and multiplication are defined coefficient-wise, and two series are equal precisely when all corresponding coefficients are equal.

Definition

Given a commutative ring R, the ring of formal power series R[[X]] consists of sequences (a0, a1, a2, …) with entries in R, written as the infinite sum ∑ anXn. The subset of series with nonzero constant term forms the group of units; a series has a multiplicative inverse exactly when its constant term is a unit of R.

Algebraic operations

Addition is term-wise. Multiplication is the Cauchy product. Composition (substitution) of formal power series is defined when the substituted series has zero constant term. Formal differentiation works term-wise as for ordinary power series.

Applications

Formal power series are fundamental in combinatorics as generating functions, in number theory (e.g. p-adic expansions), and in algebra as completions of polynomial rings.
