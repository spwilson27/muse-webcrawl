The Grenadines are a chain of small islands that lie on a line between the larger islands of Saint Vincent and Grenada in the Lesser Antilles. The northern two-thirds of the chain, including Bequia, Mustique, Canouan, Mayreau, Union Island, Palm Island and Petit Saint Vincent, belong to the country of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, while the southern third, including Carriacou, Petite Martinique and Grenada itself, belongs to the country of Grenada.

Geography

The Grenadines stretch for roughly 100 kilometres between Saint Vincent in the north and Grenada in the south. Most are small, low-lying and sparsely populated, with white sand beaches, coral reefs and sheltered anchorages that make the chain one of the world's premier sailing destinations. The Tobago Cays, a small group of uninhabited islets protected as a marine park, are celebrated for their lagoon and sea turtles.

Islands

Bequia, the largest of the Grenadines administered by Saint Vincent, is known for its boat-building and whaling traditions. Mustique is a private island associated with luxury tourism and celebrity residents. Canouan and Union Island have small airports serving regional traffic. Carriacou, the largest Grenadine in the Grenadian portion, is known for its boat-building villages and traditional festivals.

History

The islands were originally inhabited by Carib peoples. European colonisation by the French and British brought sugar, cotton and indigo plantations worked by enslaved Africans. The Grenadines were divided between the two modern states following colonial boundary settlements, and both gained independence from Britain in the 1970s.

Economy

Tourism, yachting, fishing and small-scale agriculture dominate the economy. The Grenadines are a centre for charter sailing and bareboat cruising in the Caribbean.
