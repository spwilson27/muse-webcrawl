Demographics of Afghanistan

The demographics of Afghanistan are examined by the National Statistics and Information Authority. Afghanistan's population was estimated at around 41 million in the early 2020s, though no complete modern census has been conducted since 1979, making all figures estimates. The population is young and growing, with a median age under 20 and a fertility rate among the highest in Asia.

Afghanistan has one of the youngest populations in the world

Ethnic groups

Afghanistan is a multi-ethnic society. The largest groups are the Pashtuns, the Tajiks, the Hazaras, and the Uzbeks, with smaller populations of Turkmen, Baloch, Nuristanis, Aimaqs, Kyrgyz, and others. Precise shares are contested, as ethnic statistics are politically sensitive and no reliable census data exist.

Languages

The official languages are Pashto and Dari (Afghan Persian), both widely spoken; Dari serves as a lingua franca in much of the country. Uzbek, Turkmen, Balochi, and dozens of smaller languages are also spoken regionally.

Religion

Almost the entire population is Muslim — roughly 85–90% Sunni and 10–15% Shia, the latter including most Hazaras and the Ismaili community. Small minorities of Hindus and Sikhs, once numbering in the hundreds of thousands, have dwindled to a few hundred through emigration.

Urbanization and displacement

About a quarter of Afghans live in cities; Kabul, the capital, is by far the largest, with several million inhabitants. Decades of war have driven large refugee populations to Pakistan and Iran, as well as substantial internal displacement.
