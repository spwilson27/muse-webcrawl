Aimé Fernand David Césaire (June 26, 1913 – April 17, 2008) was a Francophone Martinican poet, playwright, and politician, and one of the founders of the négritude movement in Francophone literature.

Life and work

Studying in Paris at the École Normale Supérieure, Césaire, together with Léopold Sédar Senghor and Léon-Gontran Damas, developed négritude as an affirmation of Black identity and culture against French colonial assimilation. His long poem Cahier d'un retour au pays natal (Notebook of a Return to the Native Land, 1939) is a landmark of surrealist and anti-colonial poetry.

His prose polemic Discourse on Colonialism (1950) fiercely indicted European colonialism and influenced generations of anti-colonial thinkers, including Frantz Fanon, his student. Césaire also wrote plays such as A Tempest (1969), a postcolonial reworking of Shakespeare.

Political career

Césaire served as mayor of Fort-de-France for 56 years (1945–2001) and as deputy for Martinique in the French National Assembly; in 1946 he was a principal author of the law departmentalizing Martinique. He founded the Martinican Progressive Party in 1958 after breaking with the French Communist Party.
