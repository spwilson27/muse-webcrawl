Internet Engineering Task Force

The Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) is an open standards organization which develops and promotes voluntary Internet standards, in particular the technical standards that comprise the Internet protocol suite (TCP/IP). It is organised loosely as a community of network designers, operators, vendors and researchers, and famously works by "rough consensus and running code" — a phrase coined by David Clark in 1992 that captures its practical, bottom-up approach to standardisation.

Organisation

The IETF has no formal membership and no board in the corporate sense; participation is open to anyone. Work is done in working groups organised into areas (such as routing, transport, security and applications), each overseen by area directors who collectively form the Internet Engineering Steering Group (IESG). Oversight of the standards process is provided by the Internet Architecture Board (IAB). The IETF held its first meeting in San Diego in January 1986 and now meets three times a year, with most work continuing on mailing lists between meetings.

Standards and publications

The IETF publishes its work in the RFC (Request for Comments) series, a document tradition dating to 1969. Proposed standards begin as Internet Drafts; approved documents are published as RFCs, some of which become Internet Standards (STD). Standards produced by the IETF include IP, TCP, HTTP, TLS, DNS extensions, SMTP, and IPv6. Humorous April Fools' Day RFCs are a long-standing tradition of the series. Administrative and legal support for the IETF is provided by the IETF Administration LLC, established in 2018 under the Internet Society.

Chairs

Recent IETF chairs have included Jari Arkko (2013–2017), Alissa Cooper (2017–2021), and Lars Eggert, who took office in 2021. The IETF chair heads the IESG and serves as the general area director.
