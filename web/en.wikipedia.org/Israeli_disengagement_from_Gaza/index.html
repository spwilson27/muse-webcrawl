Israeli disengagement from Gaza

Israel withdrew all settlers and military personnel from Gaza in August–September 2005.

The Israeli disengagement from Gaza was the unilateral withdrawal in 2005 of the Israeli military and all Israeli settlers from the Gaza Strip, implemented by the government of Prime Minister Ariel Sharon. Israel evacuated all 21 Jewish settlements in Gaza and four smaller settlements in the northern West Bank.

Background

Israel had occupied the Gaza Strip since the Six-Day War of 1967. About 8,000 Israeli settlers lived among more than a million Palestinians, requiring a large military presence to protect isolated enclaves. Citing demographic and security arguments, Sharon proposed "disengagement" in 2003; the plan was approved by the Knesset in October 2004 amid fierce domestic controversy.

Implementation and aftermath

The evacuation took place between 15 August and 12 September 2005; unresisting settlers were removed by unarmed soldiers and police, and settlement buildings were demolished. Israel retained control of Gaza's airspace, coastline and most border crossings. Following Hamas's victory in the 2006 Palestinian elections and its takeover of Gaza in 2007, Israel and Egypt imposed a blockade on the territory, and the disengagement became a lasting point of contention in Israeli and Palestinian politics.
