New Romantic

New Romantic was an underground subculture movement that originated around 1979 in the United Kingdom, centered on London nightclubs such as Billy's and the Blitz. It arose as a flamboyant, fashion-led reaction against the austerity of punk, drawing on glam rock, David Bowie, and Roxy Music.

Music and fashion

Associated bands included Spandau Ballet, Duran Duran, Visage, Culture Club, and ABC. The sound combined synthesizers with danceable pop, while fashion featured frilled shirts, dandy and romantic historical dress, dramatic makeup, and androgynous styling for both sexes. Steve Strange, doorman of the Blitz club, became an emblematic figure, and club regulars were dubbed the "Blitz Kids."

Legacy

By the mid-1980s the movement had been absorbed into mainstream pop as its leading acts achieved international success, particularly in the United States during the "Second British Invasion."
