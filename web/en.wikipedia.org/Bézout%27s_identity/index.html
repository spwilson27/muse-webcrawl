Bézout's identity

In elementary number theory, Bézout's identity (also called Bézout's lemma) states that, for integers a and b with greatest common divisor d, there exist integers x and y such that ax + by = d. Moreover, the integers of the form ax + by are exactly the multiples of d. The integers x and y are called Bézout coefficients.

History

The identity is named after the French mathematician Étienne Bézout, who proved it for polynomials; the statement for integers was already known to Claude Gaspard Bachet de Méziriac in the 17th century.

Computation

The Bézout coefficients can be computed with the extended Euclidean algorithm. They are not unique: if (x, y) is one pair, all solutions are given by x + (b/d)k and y − (a/d)k for any integer k.

Applications

Bézout's identity underlies the theory of Euclidean domains, the proof of the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, and the computation of modular inverses used in cryptography.
