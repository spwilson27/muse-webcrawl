The Cancer Moonshot is a United States federal initiative to accelerate cancer research and improve prevention, diagnosis, and treatment. It was announced by President Barack Obama in his 2016 State of the Union address and placed under the leadership of Vice President Joe Biden, whose son Beau Biden had died of brain cancer the previous year. The name deliberately evokes the Apollo "moonshot" as a metaphor for a bold, coordinated national effort.

Original initiative (2016)

The initiative aimed to achieve in five years what would ordinarily take ten, focusing on immunotherapy, genomics, data sharing, early detection, and breaking down institutional silos. A Blue Ribbon Panel of scientific experts issued recommendations, and the 21st Century Cures Act, signed in December 2016, authorised $1.8 billion over seven years for the programme through the National Cancer Institute. Projects included the Cancer Immunotherapy Trials Network investments, the National Cancer Data Ecosystem, and veteran-focused lung cancer screening partnerships.

Reignition (2022)

As president, Biden relaunched the Cancer Moonshot in February 2022 with the goal of reducing the age-adjusted cancer death rate by at least 50 per cent over 25 years and improving the experience of people living with and surviving cancer. The relaunch created a White House Cancer Cabinet, emphasised screening missed during the COVID-19 pandemic, and supported programmes on environmental exposures, rare and childhood cancers, and multi-cancer early-detection tests.
