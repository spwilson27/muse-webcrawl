Tiki-taka

Tiki-taka (Spanish: tiqui-taca) is a style of play in association football characterised by short passing and movement, working the ball through various channels, and maintaining possession. The style is primarily associated with the Spain national team of 2006 to 2012 and with FC Barcelona under Pep Guardiola.

Origins

The term was popularised by Spanish television commentator Andrés Montes during coverage of the 2006 FIFA World Cup, though similar possession-based approaches have earlier roots in Johan Cruyff's Barcelona of the early 1990s and in the "Total Football" of the Netherlands. Guardiola, a former Cruyff player, refined the method around midfielders such as Xavi, Andrés Iniesta and Sergio Busquets.

Characteristics

Tiki-taka relies on short, quick, often one-touch passing in tight triangles, constant off-the-ball movement to offer options, pressing high and collectively to recover the ball immediately after losing it, and positional interchange so that possession itself becomes a defensive tool. Critics have at times called it sterile when passing becomes an end in itself without penetration.

Success

Spain won UEFA Euro 2008, the 2010 FIFA World Cup and UEFA Euro 2012 playing in this style, an unprecedented run of three consecutive major international titles. Barcelona won numerous domestic and European trophies in the same period, including two UEFA Champions League titles under Guardiola (2009 and 2011).
