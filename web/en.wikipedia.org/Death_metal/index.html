Death metal

Death metal is characterised by guttural "death growl" vocals, down-tuned distorted guitars and fast, complex drumming.

Death metal is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music. It typically employs heavily distorted and low-tuned guitars, played with techniques such as palm muting and tremolo picking; deep growling vocals; aggressive, powerful drumming, featuring double-kick and blast beat techniques; minor keys or atonality; abrupt tempo, key, and time signature changes; and chromatic — sometimes dissonant — chord progressions. The lyrical themes of death metal may include slasher film-style violence, political conflict, death, murder, religion (sometimes with anti-religious angles), nature, horror, and science fiction.

History

Building on the speed and aggression of thrash metal and hardcore punk, death metal emerged in the mid-1980s. Florida became the genre's epicentre: Possessed, whose 1985 album Seven Churches is often cited as the first death metal record (their 1984 demo was titled Death Metal), and Death, led by Chuck Schuldiner — often called the "godfather of death metal" — defined the sound alongside Morbid Angel, Deicide and Obituary, many recording at Morrisound Recording in Tampa. In England, Napalm Death, Bolt Thrower and Carcass developed grindcore and goregrind offshoots, while in Sweden, Entombed, Dismember and later the Gothenburg melodic death metal bands (In Flames, Dark Tranquillity, At the Gates) forged a distinct regional style.

Characteristics

The standard death metal vocal style is the low-pitched "death growl," achieved through guttural vocal techniques. Instrumentation favours speed and technicality: rapid alternate picking, complex riffing, blast beats and frequent tempo changes. Subgenres include melodic death metal, technical death metal (e.g., Necrophagist, Nile), brutal death metal, death-doom, and the metalcore-hybrid deathcore.
