Vulpes is a genus of the subfamily Caninae, within the family Canidae. Its members are commonly called "true foxes", distinguishing them from fox-like members of other canid genera such as Lycalopex (South American "foxes") and the bat-eared fox (Otocyon). The genus comprises about twelve living species distributed across the Northern Hemisphere and Africa.

Characteristics

Species of Vulpes are small to medium-sized canids, generally slighter than wolves, dogs, and jackals, with long narrow snouts, large pointed ears, and long bushy tails often tipped with white. Their skulls are relatively flattened, and they possess partially retractable claws (notably in the gray fox's close relatives). They are opportunistic omnivores, feeding on small mammals, birds, insects, eggs, fruit, and carrion, and they characteristically hunt rodents by pouncing after locating prey by sound. Foxes of this genus are typically solitary, forming pairs mainly during the breeding season and rearing litters of kits in dens.

Distribution and evolution

The genus is thought to have originated in North America during the late Miocene and dispersed to Eurasia and Africa. Today its species occupy habitats from Arctic tundra (the Arctic fox) to the Sahara Desert (the fennec). The red fox has one of the widest ranges of any terrestrial mammal and was introduced to Australia, where it is an invasive pest. Several fossil species are known, and domesticated silver foxes derive from farmed red foxes, famously selected for tameness in the Russian farm-fox experiment begun in 1959.
