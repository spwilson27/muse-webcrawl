Health equity

Equity, unlike simple equality, distributes resources according to need to achieve fair outcomes.

Health equity is the principle and goal that everyone should have a fair and just opportunity to attain their highest level of health. It arises from the observation that differences in health outcomes between groups — health disparities or health inequalities — are often systematic, avoidable, and unfair, reflecting unequal distributions of power, income, goods, and services rather than biology alone.

Concepts

Scholars including Margaret Whitehead (1992) defined health inequities as differences that are unnecessary and avoidable as well as unjust. Health equity is distinguished from health equality: equality gives everyone the same resources, while equity allocates according to differing needs and circumstances. Research into the social determinants of health — housing, education, employment, environment, discrimination, and access to care — underpins the field, notably the work of Michael Marmot and the WHO Commission on Social Determinants of Health (2008), which documented steep social gradients in life expectancy within and between countries.

Measurement and policy

Health equity is assessed by comparing outcomes such as life expectancy, infant mortality, and disease burden across groups defined by income, race and ethnicity, sex, geography, disability, and other characteristics. Policies aimed at equity include universal health coverage, targeted prevention in disadvantaged communities, proportionate universalism (Marmot's principle that services be universal but scaled to need), and "health in all policies" approaches. The COVID-19 pandemic, which disproportionately affected low-income and minority populations in many countries, brought health equity to renewed prominence in public health practice and research funding.

Criticism and debate

Debates concern how much inequality counts as inequity, the relative roles of individual behaviour and structural conditions, and the proper targets and metrics for interventions.
