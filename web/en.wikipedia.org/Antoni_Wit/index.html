Antoni Wit (born 7 February 1944) is a Polish conductor, known for his interpretations of Polish music and for an exceptionally large catalogue of recordings, particularly for the Naxos label. He studied conducting with Henryk Czyż at the Academy of Music in Kraków, composition with Krzysztof Penderecki, and later with Nadia Boulanger in Paris; he was also influenced by Witold Lutosławski.

Career

In 1971 Wit was a joint prize-winner at the Herbert von Karajan conducting competition. He became artistic director of the Pomeranian Philharmonic and later led the Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra in Katowice (1983–2000), one of Poland's leading ensembles. From 2002 to 2013 he was artistic director of the Warsaw Philharmonic Orchestra, and he subsequently served as music director of the Orquesta Sinfónica de Navarra in Spain.

Recordings

Wit's more than 200 recordings include complete cycles of the symphonies and other works of Lutosławski, Penderecki, Szymanowski, and other Polish composers, as well as widely praised recordings of Mahler, Prokofiev, and Olivier Messiaen — his recording of the Turangalîla-Symphonie won the Cannes Classical Award. His recording of Penderecki's works also received a Grammy Award in 2013.
