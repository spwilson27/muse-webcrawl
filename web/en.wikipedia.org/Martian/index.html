Martian

A Martian is a hypothetical native inhabitant of the planet Mars. The word is also used as an adjective meaning "of or relating to Mars" — as in Martian soil, Martian atmosphere, or the Martian surface — and, in fiction, for any extraterrestrial invader by extension.

In science

No life has been confirmed on Mars, but the possibility of past or present microbial life has driven much exploration of the planet. The 1996 claim of possible fossil microbes in the Martian meteorite ALH84001 sparked wide debate, and missions continue to search for biosignatures. In planetary science, "Martian" routinely describes the planet's geology, meteorites (the SNC group), and dust.

In fiction

Martians became a staple of science fiction after Percival Lowell's popularization of "canals" on Mars. H. G. Wells's The War of the Worlds (1898) portrayed Martians invading Earth; Edgar Rice Burroughs's Barsoom series and Ray Bradbury's The Martian Chronicles (1950) offered romanticized Martian civilizations. The stereotypical "little green man" and the flying-saucer era cemented the Martian as pop culture's archetypal alien. Andy Weir's novel The Martian (2011) instead applies the word to a stranded human astronaut surviving on Mars.
