Fujifilm Holdings Corporation, commonly known as Fujifilm, is a Japanese multinational conglomerate headquartered in Tokyo. Founded in 1934 as Fuji Photo Film Co., Ltd., a spin-off of Dai-Nippon Celluloid, it grew into one of the world's largest photographic film manufacturers and the principal rival of Kodak.

Surviving the digital transition

Unlike Kodak, which went bankrupt in 2012, Fujifilm survived the collapse of the film market by aggressively diversifying: applying its expertise in collagen, antioxidants, and thin-film chemistry to cosmetics (the Astalift line), pharmaceuticals, regenerative medicine, and high-performance materials such as films for LCD displays. Healthcare — medical imaging, endoscopy, and biopharmaceutical manufacturing — is now its largest business segment.

Cameras and film today

Fujifilm remains a significant camera maker through its X-series APS-C and GFX medium-format mirrorless cameras, valued by enthusiasts for their film-simulation colour modes. Its Instax instant cameras became a major commercial success in the 2010s, and the company continues to manufacture photographic film for a revived analogue market.
