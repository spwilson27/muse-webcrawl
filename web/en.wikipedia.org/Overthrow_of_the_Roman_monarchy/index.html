Overthrow of the Roman monarchy

Datec. 509 BC
LocationRome
ResultRoman Republic established

The overthrow of the Roman monarchy was the political revolution in ancient Rome, traditionally dated to about 509 BC, that ended the Roman Kingdom and led to the establishment of the Roman Republic. According to Roman tradition, the revolt was triggered by the rape of Lucretia by Sextus Tarquinius, son of the king Lucius Tarquinius Superbus.

Events

Led by Lucius Junius Brutus and Lucius Tarquinius Collatinus, the aristocracy expelled the royal family after Tarquin's tyranny provoked widespread resentment. The king's property was confiscated, the Campus Martius was consecrated to Mars, and the office of king was replaced by two annually elected consuls.

Historical assessment

Much of the narrative comes from Livy and other later sources, and details such as the Lucretia story are generally regarded as legendary. Nevertheless, an abrupt political change in late sixth-century BC Rome is plausible, and attempts by Tarquin to regain the throne — including the war with Lars Porsena — continued for years afterward.
