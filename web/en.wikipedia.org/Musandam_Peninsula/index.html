Musandam Peninsula

The Musandam Peninsula (Arabic: رؤوس الجبال) is a peninsula forming the northeastern tip of the Arabian Peninsula, overlooking the Strait of Hormuz. Most of it belongs to the Musandam Governorate of Oman, separated from the rest of the country by territory of the United Arab Emirates; a small part, Dibba, is divided between Oman and the UAE emirates of Sharjah and Fujairah.

The peninsula's jagged coastline, carved by the sinking of the land into the sea, forms steep fjord-like inlets called khors, most famously Khor ash Sham. The rugged mountains rise to over 2,000 metres at Jebel Harim. This scenery has led the region to be called the "Norway of Arabia."

Geography and people

Khasab is the governorate's capital and main port. The indigenous Shihuh people traditionally moved between mountain villages in winter and coastal fishing and date-farming settlements in summer. Because of its position at the entrance to the Persian Gulf, the peninsula is of great strategic importance, and a major share of the world's seaborne oil passes through the strait just offshore.
