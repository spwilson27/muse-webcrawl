Soil science

Examining a soil profile in the field

Soil science is the study of soil as a natural resource on the surface of the Earth, including soil formation, classification, and mapping; the physical, chemical, biological, and fertility properties of soils; and these properties in relation to the use and management of soils. The two main branches are pedology, which studies soil as a natural body in the landscape, and edaphology, which studies soil in relation to plant growth.

History

The Russian geologist Vasily Dokuchaev (1846–1903) is regarded as the founder of modern soil science. Studying the chernozem soils of the Russian steppe, he recognised soil as an independent natural body formed by the interaction of climate, organisms, relief, parent material, and time. His ideas were spread in the West through the work of Curtis Marbut and were formalised by Hans Jenny in Factors of Soil Formation (1941). Earlier agricultural chemists such as Justus von Liebig had studied soil fertility, and Charles Darwin documented the role of earthworms in soil formation.

Areas of study

Soil science spans soil physics (water and air movement, structure), soil chemistry (nutrient cycling, acidity, salinity), soil biology and ecology, soil genesis and classification, land surveying and mapping, and soil conservation. Modern practice increasingly uses digital soil mapping, remote sensing, and geographic information systems.

Classification

International classification systems include USDA soil taxonomy, with its twelve soil orders (such as Alfisols, Mollisols, and Oxisols), and the FAO/UNESCO World Reference Base for Soil Resources.
