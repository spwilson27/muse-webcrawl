A longbow is a type of tall bow that makes a fairly long draw possible. Longbows for hunting and warfare have been made from many materials by many cultures, but in Europe they are particularly associated with the English and Welsh archers of the medieval period, whose massed volleys decided battles such as Crécy (1346) and Agincourt (1415) during the Hundred Years' War.

Design

The English longbow was usually made from a single stave of yew, combining strong heartwood resisting compression with elastic sapwood resisting tension. Surviving bows from the wreck of the Mary Rose (1545) suggest draw weights of 100–185 pounds, requiring a lifetime of training; English law repeatedly mandated archery practice for able-bodied men.

Decline and revival

Firearms displaced the longbow in warfare by the end of the 16th century, though advocates praised its rate of shooting into the 18th century. It survives today in target archery and as a popular symbol of medieval England.
