Adenosine receptor

The adenosine receptors (or P1 receptors) are a class of purinergic G protein-coupled receptors with adenosine as their endogenous ligand. There are four known types of adenosine receptors in humans: A1, A2A, A2B, and A3, each encoded by a different gene.

Function

Adenosine receptors are widely expressed and mediate the effects of adenosine, which accumulates during metabolic stress and promotes sleep, dilates coronary blood vessels, slows heart rate (via A1 receptors in the AV node), and modulates neurotransmitter release and immune function. The A2A receptor in the striatum interacts with dopamine D2 receptors and is implicated in arousal and movement.

Pharmacology

Caffeine and theophylline are non-selective antagonists of adenosine receptors, which accounts for much of their stimulant effect. Adenosine itself is used clinically to terminate supraventricular tachycardia. Selective A2A antagonists such as istradefylline are used as adjunct therapy in Parkinson's disease, and A2A agonists such as regadenoson are used in cardiac stress imaging.
