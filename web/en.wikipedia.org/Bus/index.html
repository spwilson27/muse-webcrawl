Bus

A bus is a motor vehicle designed to carry many passengers in addition to the driver. Buses can seat between about ten and several hundred passengers in articulated or double-decker configurations, and are used for public transport, school transport, touring, and private charter. The word derives from the Latin omnibus, "for all", via the French voiture omnibus.

History

Horse-drawn omnibuses appeared in cities such as Nantes and Paris in the 1820s and in London in 1829. Steam buses followed in the nineteenth century, and motor buses became common in the early twentieth century, rapidly replacing horse-drawn services and competing with trams and trolleybuses.

Types

Common types include single-decker and double-decker city buses, articulated "bendy" buses, intercity coaches with luggage holds and reclining seats, minibuses, school buses, and bus rapid transit vehicles operating on dedicated lanes. Modern buses may be powered by diesel, natural gas, hybrid-electric systems, batteries, or hydrogen fuel cells.

Role in transport

Buses are the most widely used form of public transport worldwide, offering flexible routing at lower infrastructure cost than rail. Transit agencies operate scheduled networks with fare collection increasingly handled by smart cards and mobile payment.
