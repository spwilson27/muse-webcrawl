Nikolaus Harnoncourt

Nikolaus Harnoncourt (born Johann Nikolaus Graf de la Fontaine und d'Harnoncourt-Unverzagt; 6 December 1929 – 5 March 2016) was an Austrian conductor, cellist and a pioneer of the historically informed performance movement.

Career

A cellist with the Vienna Symphony from 1952, Harnoncourt founded the Concentus Musicus Wien with his wife Alice in 1953 to perform early music on period instruments. The ensemble's recordings of Bach, Monteverdi, Rameau and others transformed expectations of Baroque style. From the 1970s he conducted mainstream orchestras — including the Berlin and Vienna Philharmonics and the Royal Concertgebouw — in repertoire from Monteverdi to Berg, applying rhetorical, historically aware interpretation. With Nikolaus's Concentus and the Chamber Orchestra of Europe he recorded the complete Beethoven symphonies, and he led many productions at the Salzburg Festival.

Legacy

Harnoncourt's writings, including Music as Speech, and his famously intense rehearsal style influenced generations of performers. He retired in December 2015 and died in Vienna the following March.
