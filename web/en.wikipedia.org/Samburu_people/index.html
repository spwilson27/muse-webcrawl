Samburu people

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Samburu

Total population: roughly 300,000

Regions: Samburu County, Rift Valley, Kenya

Language: Samburu (a Maa language), Swahili, English

Religion: Traditional monotheism (Nkai/Engai); Christianity

Related groups: Maasai, Turkana, Rendille

The Samburu are a Nilotic pastoralist people inhabiting north-central Kenya, principally Samburu County in the Rift Valley region. They are closely related to the Maasai, who speak the closely related Maa language, and Samburu oral tradition holds that the two peoples were once a single group that divided generations ago. The Samburu refer to themselves as Lokop or Loikop, “owners of the land”.

Culture and society

Samburu society is organised by a gerontocratic age-set system. Boys are circumcised in cohorts and pass into the warrior grade of moran, serving for years as protectors of the herds before becoming junior and then senior elders. Marriage is polygynous and arranged, with bridewealth paid in cattle. Samburu are renowned for their elaborate beadwork and ornaments, red-dyed hair and red cloth (shuka), and for the singing wells at which herders draw water for their animals.

Livelihood

Traditionally semi-nomadic cattle herders, the Samburu depend on their livestock for milk, meat and blood, which together form the core of the diet, supplemented today by maize meal and other purchased foods. Cattle are the central measure of wealth and social standing. Drought, pressure on grazing land, and the restrictions of colonial and post-colonial land policy have made the pastoralist livelihood increasingly precarious, and many Samburu now combine herding with wage work, trade or conservancy employment in the wildlife tourism sector.

Religion

Traditional Samburu religion is monotheistic, centred on the god Nkai (Engai), who is associated with the sky and rain and is believed to dwell on Mount Nyiru, sacred to the Samburu. Ritual specialists and prophets mediate between the people and Nkai; blessings, and the cursing power of elders, remain important features of social life, alongside a growing Christian minority.
