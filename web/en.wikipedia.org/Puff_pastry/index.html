Puff pastry

Puff pastry, also known as pâte feuilletée, is a light and flaky laminated pastry made from a dough (détrempe) and butter or another solid fat (beurrage). The butter is layered into the dough by repeated rolling and folding (turns), which produces hundreds of alternating layers that separate and puff when baked as water in the butter turns to steam.

Production

Traditional puff pastry requires six single turns, producing 729 layers. Variants include inverse puff pastry, in which the butter layer encloses the dough, and rough puff pastry, in which pieces of butter are incorporated more quickly. Commercial puff pastry is made with continuous laminating machinery and often uses margarine.

Uses

Puff pastry is used for vol-au-vent, mille-feuille, croissants (which use a yeasted laminated dough), beef Wellington, sausage rolls, palmiers, turnovers and the Eccles cake. It is related to the Middle Eastern filo and to strudel doughs.
