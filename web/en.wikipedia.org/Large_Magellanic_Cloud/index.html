Large Magellanic Cloud

The Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) is a dwarf galaxy and satellite of the Milky Way, about 163,000 light-years away in the constellations Dorado and Mensa. With a diameter of roughly 14,000 light-years and a mass about one hundredth of the Milky Way's, it is visible to the naked eye from the southern hemisphere.

Features

The LMC contains the Tarantula Nebula, the largest known star-forming region in the Local Group, and hosted supernova SN 1987A, the nearest observed supernova in the modern era. Together with the Small Magellanic Cloud it is connected by the Magellanic Bridge and trailing Magellanic Stream of gas.

History

Known to southern peoples for millennia, it was described by Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi in 964 and named for Ferdinand Magellan after his 1519 voyage. Henrietta Leavitt's period–luminosity work on its Cepheid variables helped establish cosmic distances.
