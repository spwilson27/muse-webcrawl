Schönbrunn Palace (German: Schloss Schönbrunn) is a former imperial summer residence in Vienna, Austria. The 1,441-room Baroque palace is one of the most important architectural, cultural, and historical monuments in the country, and one of Austria's most visited tourist attractions.

History

The estate took its name from a "beautiful spring" (schöner Brunnen) found there by Emperor Matthias. After the Ottoman siege of 1683, Emperor Leopold I commissioned Johann Bernhard Fischer von Erlach to design a grand hunting lodge and palace for his son Joseph. Under Maria Theresa in the mid-18th century, architect Nicolò Pacassi gave the palace much of its present form; it became the centre of imperial court life. Emperor Franz Joseph I, born at Schönbrunn in 1830, spent much of his life there and died in the palace in 1916. In 1918, Charles I renounced participation in state affairs at Schönbrunn, marking the end of the Habsburg monarchy.

The palace and gardens

The principal rooms include the Great Gallery, the Mirror Room where the six-year-old Mozart performed, and the Vieux Laque Room. The palace park, opened to the public in 1779, contains the Gloriette, the Neptune Fountain, a maze, Roman ruins, and the world's oldest surviving zoo, the Tiergarten Schönbrunn, founded in 1752. The palace and gardens were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996.
