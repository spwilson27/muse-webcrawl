First Barons' War
Date: 1215–1217
Location: England
Combatants: Rebel barons and France vs. King John, then Henry III
Result: Royal victory; Treaty of Lambeth (1217)

The First Barons' War (1215–1217) was a civil war in England between King John and a coalition of rebel barons, arising from John's refusal to abide by Magna Carta, the charter he had sealed at Runnymede in June 1215.

Course

The barons renounced allegiance, seized London, and invited Prince Louis of France to take the English throne; Louis landed in 1216 and was proclaimed king in London. John's death in October 1216 changed the war's character: the nine-year-old Henry III was crowned, and his regent William Marshal reissued Magna Carta, winning moderates back.

Outcome

Royal forces defeated the rebels at Lincoln and a French fleet at Sandwich in 1217. The Treaty of Lambeth confirmed Louis's withdrawal, and Magna Carta was reissued again in 1225, becoming a lasting part of English law.
