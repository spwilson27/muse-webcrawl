Marriage at Cana

The Marriage at Cana, also known as the Wedding at Cana or the Wedding Feast at Cana, is the first miracle attributed to Jesus in the Gospel of John (John 2:1–11). At a wedding feast in Cana in Galilee, when the wine ran out, Jesus turned water into wine.

The account

Jesus, his mother Mary and his disciples attend a wedding. When the host's wine fails, Mary tells Jesus, "They have no wine." After initially responding that his hour has not yet come, Jesus directs servants to fill six stone water jars with water; when the steward tastes it, it has become wine of superior quality. John calls the event the first of Jesus' "signs" and says his disciples believed in him.

Interpretation

The episode appears only in John's Gospel among the canonical gospels. Interpretations include a celebration of marriage, a Eucharistic foreshadowing, and a sign of the abundance of the messianic age. The location of Cana is usually identified with Kafr Kanna in the Galilee.

In art

The feast became a popular subject for large-scale painting, most famously The Wedding at Cana (1563) by Paolo Veronese, one of the largest canvases in the Louvre, depicting the scene as a lavish Venetian banquet.
