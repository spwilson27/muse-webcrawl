Komi-Permyak Okrug


Komi-Permyak Okrug, an administrative territory within Perm Krai, Russia; administrative centre Kudymkar.

Komi-Permyak Okrug (Russian: Ко́ми-Пермя́цкий о́круг; Komi-Permyak: Коми-Пермяцкöй округ) is a territory with special status (an okrug) within Perm Krai, Russia. Its administrative centre is the town of Kudymkar. It is the historic homeland of the Komi-Permyak people, a Finno-Ugric group related to the Komi.

History

The territory was established in 1925 as the Komi-Permyak National Okrug, the first national okrug created in the Soviet Union, and later became the Komi-Permyak Autonomous Okrug, a federal subject of Russia in its own right. Following a referendum, on 1 December 2005 it merged with Perm Oblast to form Perm Krai, retaining special status as an okrug within the new krai — the first such merger of Russian federal subjects.

Geography and population

The okrug covers about 32,800 square kilometres in the upper Kama River basin, west of the Ural Mountains, a landscape of taiga forest. Its population was roughly 116,000 at the 2010 census, with ethnic Komi-Permyaks forming a majority in rural districts. The Komi-Permyak language is written in Cyrillic and co-official locally, though its use has declined.
