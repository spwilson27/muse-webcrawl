Our Lady of Fátima

LocationFátima, Portugal
Date13 May – 13 October 1917
WitnessesLúcia dos Santos, Francisco and Jacinta Marto
ShrineSanctuary of Fátima

Our Lady of Fátima is a Catholic title of Mary, mother of Jesus, based on the Marian apparitions reported in 1917 by three shepherd children at the Cova da Iria in Fátima, Portugal. The three children were Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto.

The apparitions

Beginning on 13 May 1917, the children reported a series of apparitions of a lady "brighter than the sun" who urged prayer, especially the rosary, penance, and devotion to her Immaculate Heart. The final apparition on 13 October 1917 was accompanied by the "Miracle of the Sun", witnessed by tens of thousands of onlookers.

The Three Secrets

Lúcia later recorded three "secrets": a vision of hell; a prediction concerning Russia and a second world war; and a third secret, kept sealed by the Vatican until its publication in 2000, describing a vision of a "bishop in white" being killed.

Devotion

The Catholic Church declared the apparitions "worthy of belief" in 1930. Fátima is one of the most important Marian shrines in the world, drawing millions of pilgrims annually. Francisco and Jacinta were canonised in 2017.
