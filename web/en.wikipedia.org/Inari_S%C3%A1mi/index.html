Inari Sámi
Native name: anarâškielâ
Region: Inari, Finnish Lapland
Speakers: a few hundred
Family: Uralic, Sámi languages

Inari Sámi (anarâškielâ) is a Sámi language spoken by the Inari Sámi people around Lake Inari in northern Finland. It is the only Sámi language spoken exclusively in Finland and has a few hundred speakers.

History

The first books in Inari Sámi appeared in the nineteenth century, including a translation of the catechism. Use of the language declined sharply during the twentieth century as children were educated in Finnish.

Revitalisation

Since the 1990s, language nests — immersion kindergartens modelled on the Māori and Hawaiian movements — have produced a new generation of young speakers, and the language is taught in schools in the municipality of Inari.

Writing system

Inari Sámi is written in a Latin-based alphabet with additional letters such as á, č, đ, ŋ, š, ž and â.
