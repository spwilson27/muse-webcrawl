Labarum

The labarum (Greek: λάβαρον) was a vexillum (military standard) displaying the Chi-Rho symbol (☧), formed from the first two Greek letters of "Christ" (ΧΡΙΣΤΟΣ). It was adopted by the Roman emperor Constantine the Great after his victory at the Battle of the Milvian Bridge in 312 CE.

Origin

According to the church historian Eusebius, Constantine saw a vision of the cross (or Chi-Rho) in the sky with the words "in this sign, conquer" (in hoc signo vinces) before the battle against Maxentius. The labarum was carried by a dedicated guard of fifty men and became the imperial battle standard.

Later use

The labarum remained in use by Christian Roman and Byzantine emperors; its precise etymology is uncertain, with proposed derivations from Latin and Celtic roots. The Chi-Rho on the standard is often shown flanked by the Greek letters Alpha and Omega.
