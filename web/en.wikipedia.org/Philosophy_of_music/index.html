Philosophy of Music

The philosophy of music is the study of fundamental questions about the nature and value of music: what music is, how it expresses or arouses emotion, how musical works are to be identified and performed, and why music matters. It is a branch of aesthetics.

What is music?

Definitions of music typically appeal to organized sound, but noise music, silence (as in John Cage's 4′33″), and electronically generated sound strain simple definitions. Philosophers debate whether music is a purely sonic phenomenon or essentially involves intention, culture, and listening practices.

Music and emotion

A central puzzle is how abstract patterns of sound can express emotions such as sadness or joy. Theories include arousal theory (music stirs feeling in listeners), resemblance or contour theory (music's dynamics mirror expressive behavior), and persona theory (listeners imagine a persona expressing emotion in the music). Eduard Hanslick famously argued for formalism: music's content is "tonally moving forms" and it expresses no definite emotions at all.

Ontology and performance

Musical works raise ontological questions: is a symphony a physical object, an abstract type, or a class of performances? What makes a performance an authentic instance of a work, and how much deviation can improvisation or interpretation allow? Philosophers such as Nelson Goodman and Jerrold Levinson have offered influential accounts.
