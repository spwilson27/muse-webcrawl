A snack food (or simply a snack) is a small portion of food generally eaten between meals. Snacks range from fresh fruit, nuts, and yogurt to highly processed packaged products such as potato chips, pretzels, crackers, candy, and chocolate. In many countries a large commercial industry produces packaged snacks designed for convenience and long shelf life.

History

Snacking is ancient — roasted nuts, dried fruit, and popcorn were eaten for millennia — but the packaged snack industry dates from the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when products such as Cracker Jack (1896) and industrially produced potato chips spread in the United States. Vending machines, supermarkets, and convenience stores made snacks ubiquitous in the postwar era.

Types

Savory snacks include potato and tortilla chips, pretzels, popcorn, roasted nuts, jerky, and rice crackers. Sweet snacks include candy, chocolate, cookies, cakes, and ice cream. Many cultures have distinctive snack traditions, such as tapas in Spain, dim sum items in China, or chaat in India.

Nutrition and health

Processed snack foods are often high in salt, sugar, and fat and low in fiber and micronutrients, and frequent snacking is associated in dietary research with excess calorie intake, obesity, and tooth decay. Health authorities generally recommend whole-food snacks such as fruit, vegetables, and nuts. Manufacturers have responded with reduced-fat, baked, and portion-controlled products, though their health benefits are debated.

Industry

The global snack market is worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually and is dominated by large multinational food companies. Marketing, especially to children, and the placement of snacks at checkouts are recurrent subjects of public-health debate and regulation.
