Sacsayhuamán

Sacsayhuamán (from Quechua Saqsaywaman, "satisfied falcon") is an Inca citadel on a steep hill overlooking Cusco, Peru. Built in the 15th century under Pachacuti and later Inca rulers, it is renowned for megalithic dry-stone walls of enormous, closely fitted boulders.

Architecture

Three parallel zigzag walls of limestone blocks form the principal ramparts; the largest stones have been estimated at well over 100 tonnes. Within the enclosure stood towers and storehouses, and the great plaza could hold thousands of people. Spanish chroniclers attributed the work to Inca engineering using vast corvée workforces of the mit'a system.

Use and later history

The complex served ceremonial, administrative and military purposes. After the conquest, much of the structure was dismantled for building stone. Since 1944 the site has hosted the re-created Inti Raymi sun festival, and it forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the City of Cusco (1983).
