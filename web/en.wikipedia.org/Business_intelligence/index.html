Business intelligence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (recreated from memory)

Business intelligence (BI) comprises the strategies and technologies used by enterprises for the data analysis and management of business information. BI technologies provide historical, current, and predictive views of business operations, supporting better business decision-making through reporting, online analytical processing, dashboards, data mining, and benchmarking.

Definition and history

The term was popularized by Gartner analyst Howard Dresner in 1989, though an earlier usage appeared in an 1865 book by Richard Millar Devens. Modern BI evolved from decision support systems of the 1960s–1980s, and from executive information systems and data warehousing in the 1990s. In the 2010s and 2020s, self-service BI tools and cloud platforms made interactive analysis accessible to non-technical users.

Technologies

Common BI components include data warehouses and data marts, extract-transform-load (ETL) processes, OLAP cubes, reporting and query tools, scorecards and dashboards, data visualization, and increasingly embedded machine learning for forecasting and anomaly detection. BI differs from business analytics in emphasis: BI is often described as descriptive — what happened and what is happening — while business analytics adds predictive and prescriptive analysis.

Applications

Organizations use BI to measure performance against goals, identify market trends, improve supply chain efficiency, support compliance reporting, and increase revenue. Effective BI programs depend on data quality, governance, and alignment between metrics and strategy.
