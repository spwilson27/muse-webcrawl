The Emirate of Transjordan (Arabic: إمارة شرق الأردن) was a British protectorate established on 11 April 1921, in territory east of the Jordan River that formed part of the Mandate for Palestine. It was ruled by the Hashemite emir Abdullah I, though it was administered by Britain until it gained nominal independence in 1946, when it became the Hashemite Kingdom of Transjordan (later Jordan).

Establishment

After the collapse of Faisal's Arab kingdom in Syria in 1920, Abdullah moved north toward Ma'an and then Amman, and Britain, seeking to stabilise the region, recognised him as ruler of Transjordan at the Cairo Conference of 1921 under Colonial Secretary Winston Churchill. In 1923 Britain formally separated Transjordan's administration from Palestine and excluded it from the provisions of the mandate concerning a Jewish national home.

Government and society

The emirate was one of the poorest and least populated states in the Middle East, with a population of roughly 200,000–300,000, largely settled farmers, townspeople and Bedouin tribes. Its security rested on the Arab Legion, a British-subsidised force commanded by British officers including John Bagot Glubb. In 1928 Britain and the emirate signed an agreement recognising a constitutional government, and an Organic Law was promulgated.

Independence

By the Treaty of London of 22 March 1946, Britain recognised Transjordan as a fully independent state. On 25 May 1946 Abdullah was proclaimed king, and the country was renamed the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan in 1949 after it occupied the West Bank during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.
