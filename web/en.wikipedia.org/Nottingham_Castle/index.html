Nottingham Castle is a Stuart Restoration-era ducal palace, built on the site of a Norman castle, on Castle Rock, a high promontory overlooking the city of Nottingham, England. It is famous in legend as the base of the Sheriff of Nottingham in the tales of Robin Hood.

History

The original castle was ordered by William the Conqueror in 1068 and rebuilt in stone under Henry II. It was a major royal fortress: Roger Mortimer was captured there in 1330 when Edward III entered through a secret tunnel, and Richard III set out from the castle to the Battle of Bosworth in 1485. Charles I raised his standard at Nottingham in August 1642, beginning the English Civil War. After the war the ruined castle was demolished by order of Parliament in 1651.

In 1674–79 William Cavendish, 1st Duke of Newcastle, built the present ducal palace on the site. The palace was burned by rioters in 1831, during protests after the Duke of Newcastle opposed the Reform Bill, and stood as a roofless shell until it was restored and opened in 1878 as the first municipal museum of art outside London. Beneath Castle Rock runs a network of man-made caves dating back to the Middle Ages.

Today

The castle, museum, and grounds underwent a major £30 million redevelopment and reopened in June 2021 with new galleries on Robin Hood, the city's rebellions, and its world-famous lace and textile industries. A statue of Robin Hood stands in the gardens below the walls.
