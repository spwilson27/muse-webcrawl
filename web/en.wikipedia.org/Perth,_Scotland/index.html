Perth (Scottish Gaelic: Peairt) is a city in central Scotland on the banks of the River Tay. Historically the county town of Perthshire, it was granted city status again in 2012 as part of the Queen's Diamond Jubilee celebrations. Perth is known as "The Fair City" after Walter Scott's novel The Fair Maid of Perth.

History

Perth grew around a crossing of the Tay — Scotland's longest river — and received royal burgh status from William the Lion around 1210. Nearby Scone Abbey was the traditional crowning place of Scottish kings, where monarchs were enthroned upon the Stone of Scone (Stone of Destiny). The city suffered floods over the centuries, responded to in modern times by major flood defences. In 1559 John Knox preached a sermon at St John's Kirk that helped ignite the Scottish Reformation. Perth later became a centre for linen, leather and whisky blending, and was a major railway hub.

Today

Modern Perth is an administrative and service centre with attractions including St John's Kirk, the Perth Museum (opened 2024 in the former City Hall, housing the Stone of Destiny), the Fergusson Gallery devoted to artist J. D. Fergusson, and gardens at Branklyn. The city serves as a gateway to the Highlands.
