Secondary metabolite

Secondary metabolites, also called specialised metabolites or secondary products, are organic compounds produced by plants, fungi, bacteria, and other organisms that are not directly involved in normal growth, development, or reproduction (the roles of primary metabolites). Instead they mediate ecological interactions—defence against herbivores and pathogens, attraction of pollinators, competition with other organisms—and thereby improve survival.

Representative classes of secondary metabolites.

Major classes

Secondary metabolites are commonly grouped into three large classes based on their biosynthetic origins: terpenoids (built from isoprene units; e.g., menthol, carotenoids, rubber), phenolic compounds (from the shikimate pathway; e.g., flavonoids, tannins, lignin precursors), and nitrogen-containing compounds such as alkaloids (e.g., caffeine, nicotine, morphine) and cyanogenic glycosides. Microorganisms produce antibiotics such as penicillin, a secondary metabolite of the fungus Penicillium.

Importance to humans

Many pharmaceuticals derive from secondary metabolites, including morphine, quinine, aspirin precursors, the anticancer drug paclitaxel (Taxol), and statins. They are also sources of pigments, flavours, fragrances, and pesticides, and are a major focus of natural-products chemistry and drug discovery.
