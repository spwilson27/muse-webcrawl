Australian Catholic University (ACU) is a public Catholic university in Australia. Established in 1991 through the amalgamation of four Catholic tertiary institutions in eastern Australia, it has campuses in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Canberra, Ballarat and Adelaide, as well as a campus in Rome, Italy.

History

ACU's predecessor institutions trace their origins to teacher-training and nursing colleges run by religious orders in the nineteenth century, including the Sisters of Mercy and the Christian Brothers. The university opened on 1 January 1991.

Faculties

The university is organised into faculties of education and arts; health sciences; law and business; and theology and philosophy. It is known in particular for nursing, education and health programs, and for research institutes such as the Mary MacKillop Institute for Health Research.

Identity

ACU is a member of the Association of Catholic Colleges and Universities and maintains a Catholic identity expressed through its core curriculum in philosophy, theology and ethics.
