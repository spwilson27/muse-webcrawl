Battle of Bosworth Field

Date22 August 1485
LocationNear Ambion Hill, Leicestershire, England
ResultVictory for Henry Tudor; death of Richard III; end of the Wars of the Roses

The Battle of Bosworth Field (or Battle of Bosworth) was the last significant battle of the Wars of the Roses, the civil war between the Houses of Lancaster and York that extended across England in the 15th century. It was fought on 22 August 1485, and the Lancastrian Henry Tudor, Earl of Richmond, defeated the army of the Yorkist King Richard III.

Richard had deployed his larger army on and around Ambion Hill near Market Bosworth in Leicestershire, while the forces of the Earl of Northumberland held back and the powerful Stanley brothers waited, uncommitted, on the sidelines. After an initial exchange in which Henry's vanguard under the Earl of Oxford withstood the Duke of Norfolk's attack, Richard led a direct cavalry charge against Henry's position to kill his rival in person. At that moment the Stanleys intervened — on Henry's side. Richard was unhorsed and killed in the marshy ground, the last English king to die in battle.

Henry was crowned Henry VII, founding the Tudor dynasty, and he consolidated his claim by marrying Elizabeth of York. Richard's body was taken to Leicester and buried at Greyfriars; his remains were famously rediscovered beneath a car park in 2012 and reinterred in Leicester Cathedral in 2015.
