Francisco de Almeida

Dom Francisco de Almeida (c. 1450 – 1 March 1510) was a Portuguese nobleman, soldier and explorer, and the first Viceroy of Portuguese India. He is credited with establishing Portuguese maritime hegemony in the Indian Ocean through his "blue water" policy of naval supremacy rather than extensive territorial occupation.

Viceroy of India

In 1505 King Manuel I appointed Almeida to lead a large fleet to India, where he strengthened Portuguese positions on the Malabar Coast, built fortresses, and intervened in local politics. His son Lourenço de Almeida explored Ceylon and was killed fighting a Mamluk–Gujarati fleet at Chaul in 1508. In February 1509 Almeida avenged him at the Battle of Diu, decisively defeating the combined Mamluk Egyptian, Ottoman-supported and Gujarati fleet and securing Portuguese dominance of Indian Ocean trade routes.

Death

Reluctant to hand over power to his successor, Afonso de Albuquerque, Almeida briefly imprisoned him before being ordered to release him. On the return voyage to Portugal, Almeida was killed in a skirmish with Khoikhoi cattle herders at Table Bay, near the Cape of Good Hope, on 1 March 1510.
