Signal Corps (United States Army)

The United States Army Signal Corps is a branch of the U.S. Army responsible for military communications and information systems. Founded in 1860 by Albert J. Myer, it has managed everything from Civil War flag-and-torch signalling to modern satellite networks, radar, and combat photography.

History

Major Albert J. Myer, an Army surgeon, developed a system of aerial telegraphy using flags by day and torches by night, known as wig-wag signalling. Congress authorised the Signal Corps in 1860, and it saw extensive service in the Civil War. The Corps later ran the Army's weather service, pioneered military aviation, and operated the telegraph lines of the expanding United States.

World wars and beyond

In the First and Second World Wars the Signal Corps built vast telephone and radio networks, developed radar in cooperation with civilian laboratories, and produced combat film and photographs. During the Cold War it operated global communications systems, and today it provides the Army's cyber-age backbone of networks, satellites, and information services.
