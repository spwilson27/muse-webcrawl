Trauma (medicine)

Trauma
FieldEmergency medicine, trauma surgery
TypesBlunt, penetrating, thermal, blast
AssessmentPrimary survey (ABCDE), ATLS protocols

In medicine, trauma is any serious injury to the body caused by an external force — violence, accidents, falls, burns, or other physical impact. Major trauma is a leading cause of death and disability worldwide, particularly among people under 45, and is managed through organised trauma systems and trauma centres.

Classification and mechanism

Trauma is divided by mechanism into blunt trauma (the most common, e.g. road traffic collisions and falls), penetrating trauma (gunshot and stab wounds), thermal injury (burns), and blast injury. Severity is quantified with scoring systems such as the Injury Severity Score.

Management

Initial management follows the primary survey (Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure) of Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS). The first hour after injury is often called the "golden hour". Concepts such as damage control surgery and permissive hypotension aim to interrupt the lethal triad of hypothermia, acidosis, and coagulopathy. Trauma-induced coagulopathy and massive transfusion protocols are central to modern care.

Epidemiology

Injuries kill several million people each year globally; road traffic injuries are among the leading causes of death for children and young adults, prompting WHO prevention programmes.
