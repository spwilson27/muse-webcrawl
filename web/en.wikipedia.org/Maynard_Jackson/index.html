Maynard Jackson

Maynard Holbrook Jackson Jr. (March 23, 1938 – June 23, 2003) was an American attorney and politician who served as the 54th mayor of Atlanta, Georgia, from 1974 to 1982 and again from 1990 to 1994. He was the first African American mayor of Atlanta and of a major southern city.

Mayoralty

Jackson was elected in 1973 at age 35. As mayor he promoted affirmative action in city contracts, greatly increasing the share of business going to minority-owned firms, notably in the expansion of Hartsfield Atlanta International Airport. He also oversaw the expansion of the MARTA transit system and the airport's growth into one of the world's busiest.

Later life and legacy

After leaving office Jackson remained influential in Democratic politics and business. He died in 2003 in Arlington, Virginia. Atlanta's airport was renamed Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport in his honour.
