Baker's yeast

Baker's yeast is the common name for strains of yeast commonly used in baking bread and other bakery products, serving as a leavening agent which causes the bread to rise. The most common species is Saccharomyces cerevisiae, the same species used for alcoholic fermentation in beer and wine.

Use in baking

Yeast ferments sugars present in dough, producing carbon dioxide and ethanol. The carbon dioxide forms gas bubbles that expand the dough, while the ethanol evaporates during baking. Baker's yeast is sold as fresh compressed cakes, active dry yeast, or instant yeast.

History

Leavened bread dates to ancient Egypt, but commercial yeast production began in the 19th century, when the Vienna process and later refrigerated distribution made fresh yeast widely available to bakers.
