Crown Heights, Brooklyn

Crown Heights
Borough: Brooklyn, New York City
Known for: Eastern Parkway, Lubavitch world headquarters, West Indian Day Parade

Crown Heights is a neighbourhood in the central portion of Brooklyn, New York City, bounded broadly by Washington Avenue to the west, Atlantic Avenue to the north, Ralph Avenue to the east and Clarkson Avenue to the south. It is known for its brownstone blocks, cultural institutions along Eastern Parkway, and its large Hasidic Jewish and Caribbean communities.

History

The area developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as a wealthy residential district. From the 1920s the Chabad-Lubavitch movement centred itself on Eastern Parkway at 770 Eastern Parkway, its world headquarters. After the Second World War, Caribbean immigrants made Crown Heights a centre of West Indian culture in New York.

Culture

The West Indian Day Parade, one of New York's largest annual events, runs along Eastern Parkway each Labour Day. The Brooklyn Museum, Brooklyn Botanic Garden, Brooklyn Children's Museum and the central branch of the Brooklyn Public Library sit at or near the neighbourhood's western edge. In 1991 the neighbourhood was the scene of the Crown Heights riot, a pivotal event in the city's modern history.
