Dual basis

In linear algebra, given a basis of a vector space V, the dual basis is a set of vectors in the dual space V* — the space of linear functionals on V — that act as coordinate extractors for the original basis.

Definition

Let {e1, …, en} be a basis of a finite-dimensional vector space V over a field F. The dual basis is the set of linear functionals {e1, …, en} ⊂ V* defined by the relation:

ei(ej) = δij

where δ is the Kronecker delta. The dual basis is itself a basis of V*, and every vector and every functional can be expanded in terms of the two: a vector v = Σ ei(v) ei, and a functional f = Σ f(ei) ei.

In inner product spaces

When V carries an inner product, each dual basis vector can be represented by an ordinary vector via the Riesz representation theorem, giving the reciprocal basis used in crystallography to define the reciprocal lattice. For an orthonormal basis, the dual basis coincides with the basis itself.

Applications

Dual bases underlie the distinction between covariant and contravariant components of tensors, the construction of biorthogonal wavelet systems, and dual codes in coding theory.
