Abhisheka

Abhisheka (Sanskrit: अभिषेक, abhiṣeka) is a Hindu ritual of consecration in which a deity's image (murti), a sacred object, or a person is ceremonially bathed by pouring liquids over it. The term combines abhi ("towards, upon") and seka ("sprinkling, pouring"). Abhisheka is a central part of temple worship and of royal coronation ceremonies in Indian tradition.

Practice

The substances poured over the image typically include water, milk, curd, honey, sugar or sugarcane juice, ghee, coconut water, fruit juices, sandalwood paste and sacred ash, accompanied by the chanting of mantras. When five substances are used together (milk, curd, ghee, honey and sugar) the mixture is called panchamrita. Rudra Abhisheka, performed for Shiva, centres on the lingam, over which the Rudram hymns are recited while water is poured continuously.

Significance

The ritual signifies purification, nourishment and the invocation of the deity's presence and blessings. Historically, royal abhisheka — the anointing of a king with water from sacred rivers — legitimised sovereignty, and the rite also gives its name to the consecration rituals of Jain and Buddhist images and to initiatory empowerments in Vajrayana Buddhism.
