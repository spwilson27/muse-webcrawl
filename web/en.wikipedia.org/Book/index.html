Book


Printed books in codex form

A book is a medium for recording information in the form of writing or images. Books are typically composed of many pages bound together along one edge and protected by a cover. The transition from scrolls to the codex form occurred in late antiquity, and the invention of the printing press in the 15th century transformed books into mass-produced objects. In the 21st century, books are also distributed as e-books and audiobooks.

History

Early writing media included clay tablets in Mesopotamia, papyrus scrolls in Egypt, and bamboo and silk in China. The codex—leaves folded and sewn at the spine—was popularized by the Romans and adopted widely by early Christians for scripture. Medieval manuscript books were laboriously copied by scribes, often in monastic scriptoria. Around 1450, Johannes Gutenberg's movable-type printing press made books far cheaper, helping spread literacy, science, and the Renaissance. Paperbacks democratized reading further in the 20th century.

Structure

A modern book typically includes front matter (title page, copyright page, dedication, table of contents), the body of the text organized into chapters, and back matter (appendices, index, bibliography). Physical components include the spine, boards, endpapers, and the text block. Hardcover and paperback differ chiefly in binding durability and price.

Publishing

Books are produced by an industry chain of authors, literary agents, publishers, printers, distributors, and booksellers. Each edition is identified by an ISBN (International Standard Book Number). Public and research libraries collect and preserve books; the largest, the British Library and the Library of Congress, each hold over 170 million items.
