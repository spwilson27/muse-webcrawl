Restless legs syndrome

Restless legs syndrome (RLS), also called Willis–Ekbom disease, is a neurological disorder characterised by an irresistible urge to move the legs, usually accompanied by uncomfortable sensations, that worsens during rest and in the evening or night and is relieved by movement.

SymptomsUrge to move legs, worse at rest and at night, relieved by movement
AssociationsIron deficiency, pregnancy, kidney failure, periodic limb movements in sleep

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is clinical, based on the essential criteria: an urge to move the legs, onset or worsening during rest, relief with activity, and a circadian pattern (worse in the evening/night), not explained by another condition. Low iron stores (ferritin) are a common secondary cause and are routinely checked.

Treatment

Management includes treating iron deficiency, sleep hygiene, and avoiding aggravating substances such as caffeine and certain medications. For persistent symptoms, alpha-2-delta ligands (gabapentinoids) are commonly used; dopamine agonists are effective but carry a risk of "augmentation", a worsening of symptoms with long-term use.
