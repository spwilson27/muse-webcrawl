Life science

The life sciences are the branches of science that study living organisms — microorganisms, plants, animals, and human beings — together with related considerations such as bioethics. Biology is the central life science; the field also encompasses a large number of specialised disciplines distinguished by the type of organism studied or the scale and method of inquiry.

Branches

Major life-science disciplines include biochemistry, molecular biology, cell biology, genetics and genomics, microbiology, botany, zoology, physiology, anatomy, neuroscience, immunology, pharmacology, ecology, evolutionary biology, and marine biology. Interdisciplinary fields such as bioinformatics, biotechnology, and systems biology apply computation and engineering to living systems.

Applications

Life-science research underpins medicine, agriculture, and environmental management: drug and vaccine development, crop improvement, disease surveillance, and conservation all draw on it. The "life sciences industry" — pharmaceutical, biotechnology, and medical-device companies — is a major economic sector, and governments fund the field through agencies such as the US National Institutes of Health and comparable bodies worldwide.
