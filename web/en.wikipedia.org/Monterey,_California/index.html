Monterey, California

Monterey is a city on the southern shore of Monterey Bay on California's Central Coast, in Monterey County. It had a population of about 28,000 at the 2020 census. Founded by the Spanish in 1770, it served as the capital of Alta California under both Spain and Mexico, and was the site of California's constitutional convention in 1849.

History

Monterey Bay was named in 1602 by Sebastián Vizcaíno in honour of the Count of Monterrey, Viceroy of New Spain. Permanent settlement began in 1770 when Gaspar de Portolá and Junípero Serra established the Presidio of Monterey and the Mission San Carlos Borromeo de Carmelo. Monterey became the capital of the Californias and its only port of entry for taxable goods. In 1846, during the Mexican–American War, Commodore John D. Sloat raised the United States flag over the Custom House. In 1849 delegates met at Colton Hall to draft California's first constitution.

Cannery Row and the sardine era

In the early twentieth century Monterey became the centre of a vast sardine-canning industry along the street later immortalised as Cannery Row in John Steinbeck's 1945 novel of the same name. The sardine fishery collapsed in the 1950s from overfishing, and the canneries closed one by one. The waterfront was redeveloped for tourism; the Monterey Bay Aquarium opened in 1984 on the site of the largest cannery.

Institutions

Monterey hosts the Naval Postgraduate School, the Defense Language Institute at the Presidio of Monterey, and the Middlebury Institute of International Studies. The surrounding marine waters form the Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary, one of the largest in the United States.
