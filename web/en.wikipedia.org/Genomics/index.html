Genomics

Genomics is an interdisciplinary field of molecular biology focusing on the structure, function, evolution, mapping and editing of genomes — the complete set of DNA within a single cell of an organism. Unlike classical genetics, which studies individual genes, genomics characterises and quantifies an organism's genes collectively, and their interrelations and influence on the organism. It relies on high-throughput DNA sequencing and bioinformatics to sequence, assemble and analyse genomes.

History

The term genomics was coined in 1986 by geneticist Tom Roderick. The first complete genome of a free-living organism, the bacterium Haemophilus influenzae (1.8 million base pairs), was sequenced in 1995 by the Institute for Genomic Research using whole-genome shotgun sequencing. The Human Genome Project produced a draft human genome in 2001 and a finished sequence in 2003; a complete, gapless human genome was finally reported by the Telomere-to-Telomere consortium in 2022. Advances in next-generation sequencing cut costs from hundreds of millions of dollars per genome to under a thousand.

Subfields and applications

Structural genomics determines genome organisation and content; functional genomics studies gene expression and function (using transcriptomics and related "omics" methods); comparative genomics compares genomes across species to reveal evolution. Applications include disease-gene discovery, personalised medicine, pharmacogenomics, pathogen surveillance, and crop and livestock improvement.
