IG Farben

Founded1925
Dissolved1952 (liquidation completed 2012)
HeadquartersFrankfurt am Main, Germany
IndustryChemicals, pharmaceuticals

IG Farben (Interessengemeinschaft Farbenindustrie AG) was a German chemical and pharmaceutical conglomerate formed in 1925 from the merger of BASF, Bayer, Hoechst, Agfa, and other firms. It was the largest company in Europe and the largest chemical company in the world.

Second World War

IG Farben worked closely with the Nazi regime, building the Buna-Werke plant at Auschwitz to produce synthetic rubber and fuel using concentration camp labour. Its subsidiary Degesch distributed Zyklon B. After the war, the company was seized and broken up by the Allies, and 23 executives were tried in the IG Farben Trial at Nuremberg.

Successors

The original constituent companies — including BASF, Bayer, and Hoechst (now part of Sanofi) — re-emerged as independent firms. The IG Farben Building in Frankfurt later served as a U.S. Army headquarters and is now part of Goethe University.
