FCMA Treaty

The FCMA Treaty (Treaty of Friendship, Commerce and Maritime Assistance), commonly associated with the agreements between the United States and the Kingdom of Siam, refers to a class of friendship, commerce and navigation (FCN) treaties concluded in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. The best-known example is the Treaty of Amity and Commerce between the United States and Siam signed on 20 March 1833, and its successors.

Background

Friendship, commerce and maritime assistance (FCMA) agreements were a standard instrument of nineteenth-century diplomacy, granting most-favoured-nation trading status, consular representation, and protections for merchants and mariners. The 1833 Roberts Treaty between the United States and Siam was the first treaty between the United States and an Asian nation, negotiated by Edmund Roberts.

Provisions

Such treaties typically regulated port duties, the treatment of shipwrecked crews, extraterritorial jurisdiction for foreign nationals, and the freedom of commerce. Later revisions in the twentieth century removed unequal provisions, including extraterritoriality.
