Wu Zetian (624 – 16 December 705) was the only legitimate female emperor in the history of China. She rose from imperial concubine to empress consort of Emperor Gaozong of the Tang dynasty, ruled as empress dowager and regent, and in 690 proclaimed her own Zhou dynasty, reigning until her deposition shortly before her death in 705.

Wu Zetian, the sole woman to rule China as emperor in her own name.

Rise to power

Wu entered the palace of Emperor Taizong as a junior concubine. After his death she became a concubine — then wife — of his son Gaozong, becoming empress in 655. As Gaozong's health failed she governed in practice, and after his death in 683 she controlled the succession of her sons Zhongzong and Ruizong before taking the throne herself.

Reign

Her rule expanded the civil-service examination system, promoted Buddhism, supported large construction projects such as the Longmen Grottoes additions, and extended Tang influence in Central Asia. She is also remembered for harsh purges of rivals and officials carried out through secret police in the early years of her reign.

Legacy

Traditional historians portrayed her negatively, emphasizing her methods, while modern assessments also credit her administrative ability and the stability and prosperity of her reign. She is buried with Gaozong at the Qianling Mausoleum, famed for its wordless stele.
