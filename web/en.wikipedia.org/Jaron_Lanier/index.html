Jaron Lanier

Jaron Zepel Lanier
Born: 3 May 1960, New York City, U.S.
Occupation: Computer scientist, writer, musician, visual artist
Known for: Virtual reality pioneer; founder of VPL Research
Notable works: You Are Not a Gadget (2010), Who Owns the Future? (2013), Ten Arguments for Deleting Your Social Media Accounts Right Now (2018)

Jaron Zepel Lanier (born May 3, 1960) is an American computer scientist, visual artist, computer philosophy writer, technologist, futurist, and composer of contemporary classical music. Considered a founder of the field of virtual reality, Lanier and Thomas G. Zimmerman left Atari in 1985 to form VPL Research, Inc., the first company to sell VR goggles (the EyePhone) and wired gloves (the DataGlove). He is widely credited with popularizing — and by some accounts coining — the term "virtual reality."

Career

Lanier worked at Atari in the early 1980s and developed the cult video game Moondust. After VPL Research, he led teams at Silicon Graphics, Microsoft Research, and other organizations; he joined Microsoft in 2009, where he has worked on projects including the Kinect motion sensor. He has also been a scholar at institutions such as the International Computer Science Institute in Berkeley.

Writing and criticism of technology

Although an insider of Silicon Valley, Lanier became one of its most prominent critics. In You Are Not a Gadget (2010) he attacked the dehumanizing effects of Web 2.0 design and "hive mind" thinking. In Who Owns the Future? (2013) he argued that networked platforms concentrate wealth while eroding the middle class, proposing micropayment-based data compensation as a remedy. Ten Arguments for Deleting Your Social Media Accounts Right Now (2018) contends that social media manipulation harms users and politics alike. His memoir and history of VR, Dawn of the New Everything, appeared in 2017.

Music

Lanier is also a musician and collector of rare instruments, performing on piano and a wide range of world instruments, and has composed contemporary classical works including symphonies and the ballet Amazing Voyage.
