Kenneth Koch

Kenneth Koch (27 February 1925 – 6 July 2002) was an American poet, playwright, and professor, active from the 1950s until his death. He was a prominent member of the New York School of poetry, alongside John Ashbery, Frank O'Hara, James Schuyler, and Barbara Guest.

Life and career

Koch was born in Cincinnati, Ohio. He served in the Second World War in the Philippines, then studied at Harvard University and Columbia University, where he earned a PhD in 1959. He taught at Columbia for more than forty years, influencing generations of poets.

His poetry is noted for its exuberance, humour, and surface brilliance, in collections including Thank You and Other Poems (1962), The Art of Love (1975), and the long comic epic Ko, or A Season on Earth (1960). He also wrote plays and collaborated with painters of the New York School.

Teaching writing

Koch became widely known beyond poetry circles for his pioneering work teaching children to write poetry, documented in Wishes, Lies, and Dreams (1970) and Rose, Where Did You Get That Red? (1973), and later for teaching poetry to the elderly in nursing homes, described in I Never Told Anybody (1977). His books remain central texts in creative writing pedagogy.

Death

Koch died of leukemia in New York City on 6 July 2002. His posthumous collection A Possible World appeared the same year.
