André Bazin

André Bazin (18 April 1918 – 11 November 1958) was a renowned and influential French film critic and film theorist. Bazin co-founded the film magazine Cahiers du Cinéma in 1951, and his writings on the ontology of the photographic image and the virtues of realism shaped generations of filmmakers and critics.

Life and career

Bazin was born in Angers and studied to become a teacher at Saint-Cloud. During the German occupation of France he began organizing film screenings and writing criticism. After the war he wrote for newspapers and journals including L'Écran français and Le Parisien libéré, and became a central figure in the flourishing post-war French film culture of ciné-clubs and journals.

Film theory

Bazin argued that cinema's essence lies in its photographic basis: the camera records reality with a degree of objectivity unavailable to other arts. He championed directors who respected the spatial unity of a scene through long takes and deep focus, such as Jean Renoir and Orson Welles, and he was an early and passionate advocate of Italian neorealism. His essays were collected posthumously in the multi-volume Qu'est-ce que le cinéma? (What Is Cinema?).

Legacy

Bazin mentored the young critics who became the directors of the French New Wave, including François Truffaut, Jean-Luc Godard and Éric Rohmer. He died of leukemia in 1958, on the first day of shooting of Truffaut's The 400 Blows, which was dedicated to his memory.
