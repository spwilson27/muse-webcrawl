Cadillac

Cadillac Motor Car Division, or simply Cadillac, is a division of the American automobile manufacturer General Motors (GM) that designs and builds luxury vehicles. Founded in 1902, it is among the oldest automobile brands in the world and has long been positioned as GM's flagship luxury marque.

History

Cadillac was formed from the remnants of the Henry Ford Company in 1902 and named after Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac, the French explorer who founded Detroit. Under Henry Leland, the company became known for precision manufacturing and interchangeable parts, winning the Dewar Trophy in 1908. General Motors purchased Cadillac in 1909.

Cadillac introduced many automotive innovations, including the first mass-produced V8 engine (1914) and the first production electric starter (1912). Through the mid-twentieth century its large, richly styled cars became symbols of American prosperity, and "Cadillac" entered the language as a byword for the best of a kind.

Modern era

After losing ground to European and Japanese luxury rivals in the 1980s and 1990s, Cadillac repositioned itself in the 2000s with sharper styling and performance models such as the CTS-V. In the 2020s the brand moved toward electrification, launching the Lyriq crossover and the hand-built Celestiq sedan while continuing models such as the Escalade SUV.
