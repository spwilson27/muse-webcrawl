Thermidorian Reaction

The Thermidorian Reaction was the coup d'état of 9 Thermidor Year II (27 July 1794) in revolutionary France, in which Maximilien Robespierre and his allies were overthrown and the Reign of Terror brought to an end.

Events

Fearing for their own lives, deputies of the National Convention — including Tallien and Billaud-Varenne — turned on Robespierre during the session of 9 Thermidor. Robespierre, Saint-Just, Couthon, and their supporters were arrested, briefly rescued by the Paris Commune, then retaken; Robespierre was guillotined the next day with twenty-one others.

Aftermath

The Thermidorians dismantled the machinery of the Terror, released suspects, closed the Jacobin Club, and produced the more conservative Constitution of Year III and the Directory. The term "Thermidor" has since been used generally for the moment a revolution recoils from its radical phase — notably by Trotsky comparing Stalin's rise to Thermidor.
