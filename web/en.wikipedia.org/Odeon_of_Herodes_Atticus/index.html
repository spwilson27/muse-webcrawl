The Odeon of Herodes Atticus (Greek: Ωδείο Ηρώδου του Αττικού; also called the Herodeion) is a stone Roman theatre structure on the southwest slope of the Acropolis of Athens, Greece. It was built in AD 161 by the wealthy Athenian magnate and Roman senator Herodes Atticus in memory of his wife, Aspasia Annia Regilla.

History

The odeon was the third such hall built in Athens, after those of Pericles and Agrippa. Its steep auditorium, hollowed into the hillside, held about 5,000 people in 32 rows of Pentelic marble seats. The stage building rose three storeys and was faced with marble; the whole auditorium was roofed with expensive cedar of Lebanon wood, a remarkable feat of engineering. The building was destroyed during the Herulian invasion of AD 267 and left in ruins.

Modern use

The seating and stage were restored in the 1950s with Pentelic marble. Since then the Herodeion has been the principal venue of the Athens Festival, hosting concerts, opera and ballet each summer. Performers there have included Maria Callas, Frank Sinatra, Nana Mouskouri, Luciano Pavarotti and Leonard Cohen. Its acoustics and setting beneath the Acropolis make it one of the most celebrated open-air performance spaces in the world.
