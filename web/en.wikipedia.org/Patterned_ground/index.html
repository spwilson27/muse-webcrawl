Patterned ground


Sorted stone circles in Svalbard

Patterned ground is the distinct, geometric arrangement of surface material found in periglacial environments — regions subject to repeated freezing and thawing. The patterns include circles, polygons, nets, steps, and stripes formed from stones and soil, and occur in Arctic and alpine regions and even, in fossil form, in areas formerly covered by ice sheets.

Formation

Several mechanisms contribute to patterned ground. Frost heave lifts stones vertically through the soil as needle ice forms beneath them, while repeated freeze–thaw cycles sort particles by size: fine material accumulates in the centers of patterns and coarse stones migrate to the borders, producing sorted circles and polygons. On slopes, the same sorting stretched downhill by gravity produces stone stripes. Frost cracking of frozen ground also creates polygonal networks, sometimes filled by ice wedges.

Types

Common forms include sorted and non-sorted circles, nets, and polygons on flat terrain, and steps and stripes on slopes. Scales range from a few centimeters to several meters across. Similar polygonal patterns observed on Mars by orbiting spacecraft have been interpreted as evidence of past or present ground ice on that planet.
