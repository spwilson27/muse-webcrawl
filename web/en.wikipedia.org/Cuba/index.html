Cuba, officially the Republic of Cuba, is an island country comprising the island of Cuba, Isla de la Juventud, and several minor archipelagos. Located where the northern Caribbean Sea, Gulf of Mexico and Atlantic Ocean meet, it is the largest island in the Caribbean and has a population of around 11 million. Havana is the capital and largest city.

History

The island was inhabited by Taíno and Ciboney peoples before Christopher Columbus claimed it for Spain in 1492. Cuba remained a Spanish colony until 1898, when the Spanish–American War led to nominal independence in 1902 under strong United States influence. After decades of political instability and the dictatorship of Fulgencio Batista, Fidel Castro's 26th of July Movement seized power in the Cuban Revolution of 1959, establishing a one-party socialist state aligned with the Soviet Union. The ensuing Cold War confrontation produced the failed Bay of Pigs invasion (1961) and the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962). The collapse of the USSR triggered the severe "Special Period" economic crisis of the 1990s.

Geography and economy

The main island is 1,250 km long, with the Sierra Maestra mountains in the east. The economy historically centred on sugar, tobacco and rum; today tourism, remittances, nickel and biotechnology are major earners, within a system combining state planning with limited private enterprise. A long-standing United States embargo shapes trade and politics. Cuba is internationally noted for its universal healthcare and education systems, its music — son, rumba, salsa, and the Buena Vista Social Club phenomenon — and its classic-car streetscape in Havana.
