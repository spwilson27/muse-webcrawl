Biblioteca Marciana

The Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana (Biblioteca Marciana, "Library of Saint Mark") is a public library in Venice, Italy. It is one of the earliest surviving public libraries and repositories for manuscripts in Italy and holds one of the world's most significant collections of classical texts. It is housed in a Renaissance building, designed by Jacopo Sansovino, on the Piazzetta San Marco opposite the Doge's Palace.

History

The library grew from the gift of Cardinal Bessarion, who in 1468 donated his collection of some 750 Greek and Latin codices to the Republic of Venice for public use. Additions came from the Grimani collection and later bequests. Construction of Sansovino's building began in 1537; Andrea Palladio called it "the richest and most ornate building that there has been since antiquity". The reading room ceiling and walls were decorated by leading Venetian artists including Titian, Tintoretto, Veronese, and Palma il Giovane.

Holdings

Treasures of the Marciana include the Grimani Breviary, the will of Marco Polo, early printed editions (incunabula), the world map of Fra Mauro dating from about 1450, and important Greek manuscripts fundamental to the Renaissance recovery of classical antiquity.
