Daemonorops
Family: Arecaceae
Subfamily: Calamoideae
Tribe: Calameae
Type species: Daemonorops melanochaetes

Daemonorops is a genus of rattan palms in the family Arecaceae, native to tropical Asia from India and southern China through Southeast Asia to New Guinea. The genus contains over one hundred described species, most of them climbing palms of lowland and hill rainforest.

Description

Species of Daemonorops are typically spiny, clustering or solitary climbing palms whose stems provide some of the finest commercial rattan canes. The fruits are covered in overlapping scales and, in many species, exude a bright red resin.

Uses

The red resin obtained from the fruit scales of species such as Daemonorops draco and D. propinquus is the source of "dragon's blood", historically used as a dye, varnish, incense and traditional medicine. The canes are harvested for furniture and handicrafts throughout Southeast Asia.

Taxonomy

Recent phylogenetic studies of the subtribe Calaminae have led some botanists to merge Daemonorops into an enlarged genus Calamus, though the name remains widely used in floras and the rattan trade.
