Yuri Gagarin

Yuri Alekseyevich Gagarin
Born: 9 March 1934, Klushino, USSR
Died: 27 March 1968, near Novosyolovo
Mission: Vostok 1, 12 April 1961
Distinction: First human in space

Yuri Alekseyevich Gagarin (1934–1968) was a Soviet cosmonaut who became the first human to travel into outer space, completing one orbit of Earth aboard Vostok 1 on 12 April 1961.

Early life and flight

Gagarin was born in the village of Klushino, worked as a foundryman, learned to fly at a local aeroclub, and joined the Soviet Air Force, flying MiG-15s in the Arctic. Selected in 1960 among the first group of cosmonauts, the 1.57 m (5 ft 2 in) pilot suited the cramped Vostok capsule. His 108-minute flight — one 327 km-apogee orbit — began with his famous "Poyekhali!" ("Let's go!") and included the remark, often rendered, "I see Earth! It is so beautiful." He ejected and parachuted separately from the capsule near Engels, as planned but then kept secret.

Fame and death

Gagarin became a global celebrity and Soviet ambassador of goodwill, touring dozens of countries. Protective of their hero, Soviet authorities kept him from further spaceflights for years; he served as deputy training director and backed up Soyuz 1. On 27 March 1968 he died in the crash of a MiG-15UTI trainer during a routine flight — the exact cause (likely a jet wake or weather balloon encounter combined with a flawed altimeter reading) remains debated. His ashes were placed in the Kremlin Wall Necropolis.

Legacy

12 April is celebrated as Cosmonautics Day and the UN's International Day of Human Space Flight; "Yuri's Night" parties mark the anniversary worldwide. A lunar crater and asteroid 1772 Gagarin bear his name.
