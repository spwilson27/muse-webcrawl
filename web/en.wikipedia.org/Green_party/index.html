Green party

Green parties campaign on environmental protection, social justice, and grassroots democracy.

A green party is a formally organised political party based on the principles of green politics, such as social justice, environmentalism, and nonviolence. Greens generally affirm the "Four Pillars" first articulated by the German Greens — ecological wisdom, social justice, grassroots democracy, and nonviolence — and the six guiding principles of the Global Greens charter, which add sustainability and respect for diversity. Green parties exist in nearly 90 countries and are organised internationally in the Global Greens federation.

History

The first parties calling themselves "green" emerged in the 1970s: the United Tasmania Group (1972) in Australia and the Values Party (1972) in New Zealand were early precursors, and Britain's PEOPLE party (later the Green Party) was founded in 1973. The West German party Die Grünen, founded in 1980, became the movement's flagship, entering the Bundestag in 1983 and joining a federal coalition government in 1998 with Joschka Fischer as foreign minister. Greens have since participated in governments in many European countries, and green parties achieved a breakthrough in the 2019 European Parliament election. Petra Kelly, a co-founder of the German party, was an internationally prominent figure in the movement's formative years.

Policies

Green parties typically advocate rapid decarbonisation and renewable energy, public transport, protection of biodiversity, progressive taxation and welfare, electoral reform (especially proportional representation), decentralisation, and pacifist or restrained foreign policies, though positions vary by country and some green parties are centrist or combine environmentalism with liberal economics.
