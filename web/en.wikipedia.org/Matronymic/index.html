Matronymic

A matronymic is a component of a personal name based on the given name of one's mother, grandmother, or another female ancestor — the counterpart of a patronymic, which is based on the father's name. Matronymic surnames and name elements occur in many cultures, though less commonly than patronymics.

Examples

In Iceland, where most surnames are patronymic, some people bear matronymics, for example when the father is unknown or by choice; a son of a woman named Elín may be Elínarson. Arabic ibn names occasionally use a famous mother. In medieval Europe, matronymic surnames arose when a mother was a widow, of higher status, or an heiress; English surnames such as Marriott, Dyson (from Dye) and Margaretson have matronymic origins. Hebrew names in religious contexts traditionally include the mother's name in prayers for the sick, as in "Ploni ben [mother]". Matronymics also appear in various African, Southeast Asian and Indigenous naming traditions.

Modern usage

Some jurisdictions allow parents to choose a matronymic for a child as readily as a patronymic, and feminist naming movements have promoted matronymics to balance patrilineal customs. In Spain and Latin America, every person traditionally bears both a paternal and a maternal surname.
