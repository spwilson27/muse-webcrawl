Józef Klemens Piłsudski (1867–1935) was a Polish statesman widely regarded as the principal architect of the Second Polish Republic. A former socialist revolutionary and commander of the Polish Legions in the First World War, he led Poland to independence in 1918 and directed its defense in the Polish–Soviet War.

Early life

Born into a Polish-Lithuanian noble family in the Russian Empire, Piłsudski was exiled to Siberia for alleged involvement in a plot against Tsar Alexander III. He later led the Polish Socialist Party and its paramilitary wing, organizing operations such as the Bezdany raid of 1908 to fund the independence movement.

Independence and war

In 1914 Piłsudski formed the Polish Legions under Austro-Hungarian auspices; his refusal to swear an oath to the Central Powers led to his imprisonment at Magdeburg in 1917. Released in November 1918, he became Chief of State of reborn Poland. As commander in the Polish–Soviet War he won the Battle of Warsaw (1920), the "Miracle on the Vistula", halting the Red Army's advance.

Later years

Piłsudski retired in 1923 but returned to power in the May Coup of 1926, ruling behind the scenes through the Sanation regime while holding posts including Minister of Military Affairs. He promoted the idea of a Polish-led federation (Prometheism) and signed non-aggression pacts with the USSR (1932) and Germany (1934). He died in 1935 and remains a towering, debated figure in Polish memory.
