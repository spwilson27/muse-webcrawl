Motion controller

TypeInput device / game controller
SensorsAccelerometer, gyroscope, infrared/optical tracking, cameras
Notable examplesWii Remote, PlayStation Move, Kinect (camera-based), VR controllers
Popularised2006 (Nintendo Wii)

A motion controller is an input device that tracks its position, orientation or acceleration in physical space and uses that movement as input, most commonly for video games and virtual reality. Motion controllers allow players to interact through natural gestures — swinging, pointing, tilting or reaching — rather than only pressing buttons or moving sticks.

Technology

Motion sensing typically combines accelerometers, which measure acceleration and tilt, gyroscopes, which measure rotation, and external references for absolute position: infrared sensor bars (as with the Wii Remote), LED tracking by cameras (PlayStation Move), structured-light and time-of-flight cameras (Kinect, which tracks the body without a held controller), and inside-out camera tracking on modern VR headsets and controllers.

History

Early experiments included light guns and peripherals of the 1980s–90s, but motion control entered the mainstream with Nintendo's Wii Remote in 2006, whose intuitive gestural play broadened the gaming audience and made the Wii a major commercial success. Sony and Microsoft responded with PlayStation Move (2010) and Kinect (2010). From 2016, virtual reality systems such as the Oculus Rift, HTC Vive and PlayStation VR made tracked hand controllers standard for immersive gaming.

Applications

Beyond games, motion controllers are used in VR design and training, rehabilitation and physical therapy, drone control, presentation pointers, and as accessibility devices.
