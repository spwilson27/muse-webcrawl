The State Fair of Texas is an annual state fair held in Dallas, Texas, at the historic Fair Park. Running for 24 days from late September through October, it is one of the largest state fairs in the United States, regularly drawing around two million visitors. The fair has been held since 1886, with interruptions for the two world wars and the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.

Attractions

The fair's best-known symbol is Big Tex, a 55-foot-tall cowboy figure that has greeted visitors since 1952, wearing a recorded welcome in English and Spanish. The original Big Tex was destroyed by an electrical fire in 2012 and was replaced by a new version the following year. Other highlights include the Texas Star Ferris wheel, for many years the largest in North America, the midway, creative arts competitions, livestock shows and auctions, concerts, an auto show, and the Red River Showdown football game between the University of Texas and the University of Oklahoma, played at the Cotton Bowl stadium on the fairgrounds during the fair.

Food

The fair is famous for its fried foods; it claims to be the home of the corn dog, introduced by the Fletcher brothers in the early 1940s. Since 2005 the Big Tex Choice Awards have honoured the most creative new fair foods, with winners including fried butter, fried beer, and fried Thanksgiving dinner.

Fair Park

Fair Park, a 277-acre complex of Art Deco exhibition buildings built for the 1936 Texas Centennial Exposition, is a National Historic Landmark and a major attraction in its own right.
