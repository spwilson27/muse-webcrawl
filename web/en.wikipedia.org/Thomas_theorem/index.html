Thomas theorem

Thomas theorem
Formulated1928
AuthorsW. I. Thomas and Dorothy Swaine Thomas
FieldSociology

The Thomas theorem is a theory of sociology formulated in 1928 by William Isaac Thomas and Dorothy Swaine Thomas: "If men define situations as real, they are real in their consequences." In other words, interpretations of a situation cause the action; subjective definitions, whether or not objectively correct, shape behaviour and outcomes.

Explanation

The Thomases introduced the theorem in The Child in America (1928) to emphasize that societies must be understood through the meanings people attach to events, not only through objective conditions. A classic illustration is a bank run: even if a bank is solvent, the belief that it is failing leads depositors to withdraw funds, making the failure real.

Influence

The theorem is a foundational statement of the social construction of reality and anticipates Robert K. Merton's concept of the self-fulfilling prophecy (1948). It underlies symbolic interactionism and remains a standard starting point in sociological treatments of perception, labelling, and collective definition of situations.
