Leaf spring

Leaf spring
Type: Suspension spring
Material: Spring steel (or composite)
Forms: Semi-elliptic, elliptic, parabolic, transverse

A leaf spring is a simple form of spring commonly used for the suspension in wheeled vehicles. It consists of one or more long, narrow, arc-shaped plates (leaves) of spring steel attached to the axle and chassis, flexing to absorb road shocks.

Construction

In a multi-leaf spring, several leaves of decreasing length are stacked and clamped together, giving progressive stiffness. Mono-leaf springs use a single plate, often tapered. Inter-leaf friction provides some damping, though modern designs minimise it and rely on separate shock absorbers.

History and use

Leaf springs were used on horse-drawn carriages by the 17th century and were standard on early automobiles. Today they persist mainly on trucks, vans and trailers, where they combine springing and axle location cheaply, and transverse composite leaf springs have been used in some sports cars.
