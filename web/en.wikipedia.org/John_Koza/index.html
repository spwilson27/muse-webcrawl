John Koza

Born: 1943, Rochester, New York, U.S.
Fields: Computer science
Institutions: Stanford University; Genetic Programming Inc.
Known for: Genetic programming; co-founding Scientific Games Corporation

John R. Koza (born 1943) is an American computer scientist and businessman known for inventing genetic programming, a method for evolving computer programs automatically, and as co-founder of Scientific Games Corporation, which popularised instant lottery tickets.

Scientific Games

Koza earned his PhD in computer science from the University of Michigan in 1972, where he studied under John Henry Holland. With Daniel Bower he founded Scientific Games in 1973, which developed the first secure instant ("scratch-off") lottery game in 1974 and became a major supplier to state lotteries.

Genetic programming

Drawing on Holland's genetic algorithms, Koza proposed evolving entire programs represented as tree structures (often in Lisp) using selection, crossover and mutation. His books Genetic Programming (1992) and its successors reported numerous "human-competitive" results, including evolved designs for antennas and electronic circuits, some of which received patents. An evolved antenna flew on NASA's ST5 spacecraft in 2006.

Other activities

Koza was a consulting professor at Stanford and led the annual Genetic and Evolutionary Computation Conference community's Humies awards for human-competitive evolved results.
