Diffie–Hellman key exchange

Diffie–Hellman (DH) key exchange is a mathematical method of securely generating a symmetric cryptographic key over a public channel. It was one of the first public-key protocols, published by Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman in 1976, based on a concept by Ralph Merkle; an equivalent system had earlier been developed secretly at GCHQ by Malcolm Williamson.

Protocol

Two parties publicly agree on a large prime p and a generator g. Each chooses a secret integer (say a and b) and sends the other ga mod p and gb mod p respectively. Each can then compute the shared secret gab mod p, while an eavesdropper faces the computationally hard discrete logarithm problem to recover it.

Security

Diffie–Hellman provides no authentication by itself and is vulnerable to man-in-the-middle attacks unless combined with signatures or certificates. Modern practice uses large groups or elliptic-curve variants (ECDH), and ephemeral keys provide forward secrecy in protocols such as TLS.
