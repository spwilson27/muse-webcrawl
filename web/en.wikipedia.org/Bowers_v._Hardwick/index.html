Bowers v. Hardwick

Bowers v. Hardwick, 478 U.S. 186 (1986), was a United States Supreme Court decision that upheld, in a 5–4 ruling, the constitutionality of a Georgia sodomy law criminalizing oral and anal sex in private between consenting adults, in this case with respect to homosexual sodomy.

The case arose after Michael Hardwick was arrested in 1982 when an Atlanta police officer, serving an unrelated warrant, observed him engaging in consensual sexual activity in his own bedroom. Hardwick challenged the statute as a violation of the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.

Writing for the majority, Justice Byron White framed the issue narrowly as whether the Constitution conferred a fundamental right to engage in homosexual sodomy, and concluded it did not. Justice Harry Blackmun's dissent argued the case was about the right to privacy in one's home and intimate life.

The decision was widely criticized and was overruled seventeen years later by Lawrence v. Texas (2003), which struck down sodomy laws nationwide and repudiated Bowers' reasoning.
