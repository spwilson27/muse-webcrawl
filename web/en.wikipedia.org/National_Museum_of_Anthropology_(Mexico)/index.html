National Museum of Anthropology (Mexico)

Established: 1964 (current building)

Location: Chapultepec Park, Mexico City, Mexico

Spanish name: Museo Nacional de Antropología (MNA)

Administered by: Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia (INAH)

The National Museum of Anthropology (Spanish: Museo Nacional de Antropología, MNA) is a national museum in Mexico City, located within Chapultepec Park. It is the largest and most visited museum in Mexico, and one of the most important museums of anthropology in the world. It houses the world's largest collection of ancient Mexican art and ethnographic exhibits on Mexico's indigenous peoples.

Building and history

The current building, opened on 17 September 1964, was designed by architects Pedro Ramírez Vázquez, Jorge Campuzano and Rafael Mijares. Its vast central courtyard is covered by an enormous concrete umbrella fountain, El Paraguas, supported by a single column carved with reliefs. The museum's origins date to 1790, when the naturalist José Longinos Martínez established a museum of natural history; its anthropological collections developed through the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Collection

Ground-floor galleries present the archaeology of Mexico's pre-Hispanic civilisations, arranged by region and culture. Famous exhibits include the Aztec Stone of the Sun (often called the Aztec calendar stone), the giant Olmec colossal heads from La Venta and San Lorenzo, the jade mask and treasures from the tomb of the Maya king Pakal of Palenque, the Aztec Coatlicue statue, and a replica of the Codex and the feather headdress (Penacho) associated with Moctezuma II. Upper-floor galleries cover the ethnography of Mexico's contemporary indigenous peoples, including costumes, crafts, dwellings and ritual objects.
