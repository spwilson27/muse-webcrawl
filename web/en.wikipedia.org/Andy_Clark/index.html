Andy Clark

Andy Clark
Born: 1957
Fields: Philosophy of mind, cognitive science
Known for: The extended mind thesis

Andy Clark (born 1957) is a British philosopher and cognitive scientist best known for championing the idea that cognition is not confined to the brain. With David Chalmers, he co-authored the influential 1998 paper “The Extended Mind”, and his books Being There (1997), Natural-Born Cyborgs (2003), and Surfing Uncertainty (2016) have shaped embodied and predictive accounts of the mind.

The extended mind

In “The Extended Mind”, Clark and David Chalmers argued by the “parity principle” that if an external resource plays the same functional role as an internal cognitive process, it counts as part of that process: Otto, a patient with Alzheimer's disease, uses a notebook the way others use biological memory, so the notebook is literally part of his mind. The thesis launched the active externalism debate in philosophy of mind and the wider 4E cognition movement—embodied, embedded, enactive, and extended.

Predictive processing

Clark's later work presents the brain as a prediction machine that continually forecasts sensory input and minimises prediction error, unifying perception, action, and learning. Surfing Uncertainty develops this predictive processing framework and its implications for embodiment and technology.

Career

Clark has held chairs at Washington University in St. Louis, Indiana University, and the University of Edinburgh, and is a professor of cognitive philosophy at the University of Sussex. He is a Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and of the British Academy.
