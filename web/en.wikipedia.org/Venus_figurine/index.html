Venus figurine

A Venus figurine is a small statuette of a woman dating from the Upper Paleolithic, mostly found in Europe and parts of Asia. The figurines, made between roughly 40,000 and 10,000 years ago, typically emphasise the breasts, abdomen, hips and thighs, and are often carved from materials such as mammoth ivory, soft stone, ceramic or bone.

Notable examples

The oldest known example is the Venus of Hohle Fels (c. 40,000–35,000 BP), carved from mammoth ivory and found in Germany. The most famous is the Venus of Willendorf (c. 28,000–25,000 BP), an 11 cm limestone figure discovered in Austria in 1908 and tinted with red ochre. Other notable examples include the Venus of Dolní Věstonice, one of the oldest known ceramic objects, and the Venus of Lespugue.

Interpretation

The purpose of the figurines remains debated: proposed interpretations include fertility symbols, mother-goddess images, self-representations by women, or more general expressions of health and survival. The name "Venus" is a 19th-century coinage referencing the Roman goddess of love.
