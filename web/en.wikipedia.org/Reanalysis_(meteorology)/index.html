Reanalysis (meteorology)

A meteorological reanalysis is a climate data set produced by combining past weather observations with a modern numerical weather prediction model using data assimilation, run in a fixed, unchanging configuration over a long historical period. The result is a spatially complete, dynamically consistent reconstruction of the state of the atmosphere (and often the ocean and land surface) at regular intervals — for example, hourly on a global grid extending back decades.

Purpose

Raw historical observations are irregular in space and time and come from instruments that have changed over the decades, making them difficult to use directly for climate research. Reanalysis provides homogeneous gridded fields of variables such as temperature, wind, pressure, humidity and precipitation, enabling studies of climate variability and change, case studies of past weather events, and the calibration of other models.

Major reanalyses

Well-known products include the NCEP/NCAR Reanalysis (begun in the 1990s), the ECMWF series (ERA-40, ERA-Interim and ERA5), NASA's MERRA and MERRA-2, and JMA's JRA-55. ERA5, produced by the Copernicus Climate Change Service, provides hourly data from 1940 to the present at about 31 km resolution.

Limitations

Reanalyses inherit model biases and are affected by changes in the observing system — for example, the introduction of satellite data around 1979 — which can introduce spurious jumps or trends, particularly in data-sparse regions and for precipitation and clouds.
