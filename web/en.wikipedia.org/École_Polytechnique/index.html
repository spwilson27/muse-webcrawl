The main buildings of École Polytechnique on its campus at Palaiseau, south of Paris.

École Polytechnique, often known simply as Polytechnique or by the nickname l'X, is a French grande école and a constituent member of the Institut Polytechnique de Paris. Founded in 1794 during the French Revolution, it is one of France's most prestigious and selective institutions of higher education in science and engineering.

History

The school was founded by Lazare Carnot and Gaspard Monge as the École centrale des travaux publics to train engineers and scientists for the revolutionary state. Napoleon gave it a military status in 1804 — students are still French military officer cadets in the flagship engineering programme — and its motto, Pour la Patrie, les Sciences et la Gloire ("For the Fatherland, Science and Glory"). In 1976 the school moved from Paris's Latin Quarter to Palaiseau.

Education and alumni

Admission to the cycle ingénieur is by a famously competitive examination after classes préparatoires. Graduates enter state technical corps, the military, research, or industry. Alumni include scientists such as Cauchy, Fresnel, Poincaré and Becquerel, as well as numerous business leaders and politicians.
