The Moscow Metro is a rapid transit system serving Moscow, Russia, and the neighbouring Moscow Oblast cities. Opened in 1935 under the Soviet Union, it was the first underground railway system in the USSR. The Metro is famous worldwide for the lavish architecture of its stations — marble halls, chandeliers, mosaics, and stained glass — which were conceived as "palaces for the people."

History

The first line, the Sokolnicheskaya line, opened on 15 May 1935 between Sokolniki and Park Kultury, with a branch to Smolenskaya. Construction employed tens of thousands of workers and was a flagship project of Stalin's industrialisation drive. During the Second World War, stations served as air-raid shelters and command posts. Post-war stations on the Koltsevaya (Circle) line, opened in stages between 1950 and 1954, represent the apex of the system's ornate "Stalinist Empire" style.

Network

The network is predominantly radial lines crossing the city and connecting to the Koltsevaya circle line (Line 5). The Bolshaya Koltsevaya line (Line 11), the Big Circle Line, was completed in 2023 and at about 70 km is among the longest metro circle lines in the world. The system also integrates with the Moscow Central Circle (Line 14) and the Moscow Central Diameters suburban rail services. Trains run at intervals as short as 90 seconds at peak hours.

Architecture

Celebrated stations include Mayakovskaya, with its stainless-steel arches and ceiling mosaics of the Soviet sky; Komsomolskaya, with baroque-style stucco and mosaics of Russian military heroes; Ploshchad Revolyutsii, lined with bronze statues of workers, soldiers, and collective farmers; and Novoslobodskaya, famous for its glowing stained-glass panels.
