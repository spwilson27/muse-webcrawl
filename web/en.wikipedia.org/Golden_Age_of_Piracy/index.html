Golden Age of Piracy

Periodc. 1650 – 1730 (peak 1713 – 1726)
RegionsCaribbean Sea, North American east coast, West African coast, Indian Ocean
Key figuresBlackbeard, Bartholomew Roberts, Anne Bonny, Henry Morgan, Captain Kidd

The Golden Age of Piracy is a common designation for the period in maritime history when piracy flourished in the Caribbean Sea, along the eastern coast of North America, off West Africa, and in the Indian Ocean. The period is generally dated from about 1650 to 1730, with its most intense phase in the decade following the end of the War of the Spanish Succession in 1713.

Phases

Historians commonly divide the era into three overlapping phases. The buccaneering period (c. 1650–1680) saw English, French, and Dutch freebooters based in Jamaica and Tortuga raiding Spanish settlements in the Caribbean. The Pirate Round (c. 1690s) involved long-distance voyages from the Americas to rob shipping in the Indian Ocean, notably the capture of the Mughal treasure ship Ganj-i-Sawai by Henry Every in 1695. The final phase, from 1713 to 1726, followed the demobilisation of thousands of privateers and sailors after the Treaty of Utrecht, many of whom turned to piracy around bases such as Nassau in the Bahamas.

Causes

The boom in colonial shipping provided rich targets, while weak colonial governance and corrupt officials offered pirates safe markets for stolen goods. Many pirates were former naval sailors or privateers accustomed to harsh discipline and low wages; aboard pirate vessels they found comparatively democratic practices, including elected captains and shares of plunder distributed by written articles.

Decline

From about 1718, coordinated campaigns by the Royal Navy, colonial governors such as Woodes Rogers in the Bahamas, and a wave of trials and executions suppressed the pirate havens. By the mid-1720s the great pirate fleets had been destroyed, and the era had effectively ended. The period left a lasting legacy in popular culture, shaping the modern stereotype of the pirate found in works from Treasure Island to modern films.
