Korean architecture

Key features: Hanok, ondol, curved roofs, dancheong painting
Notable sites: Changdeokgung, Bulguksa, Hwaseong

Korean architecture refers to the building traditions of Korea, shaped over millennia by the peninsula's climate, materials, and influences from China adapted to local conditions, Confucian and Buddhist thought, and the philosophy of harmony with nature.

Traditional architecture

Traditional Korean buildings are characterised by curved tiled roofs with upturned eaves, wooden bracket complexes (pogyeok), and the ondol underfloor heating system. Hanok houses arrange rooms around a courtyard, with floor seating reflected in low furniture. Stone pagodas and wooden temples such as Bulguksa and Haeinsa (housing the Tripitaka Koreana) exemplify Buddhist architecture of the Silla and Goryeo periods.

Palaces and fortresses

Joseon-dynasty palaces in Seoul — Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung (a UNESCO site) — and the Hwaseong Fortress in Suwon survive as landmarks of the late tradition. Modern Korean architecture blends high-rise development with revival of traditional motifs.
