Alois Senefelder

Johann Alois Senefelder (6 November 1771 – 26 February 1834) was a German actor and playwright who invented the printing technique of lithography in the 1790s.

Alois Senefelder
Born: 6 Nov 1771, Prague
Died: 26 Feb 1834, Munich
Known for: Inventing lithography (1796)

Life

Born in Prague, then in the Kingdom of Bohemia, Senefelder studied law in Munich and turned to playwriting. Unable to afford publication of his plays, he experimented with cheap reproduction methods, etching on copper and then writing with a greasy crayon on smooth Solnhofen limestone.

Invention of lithography

In 1796 Senefelder discovered that a design drawn with a fatty substance on limestone, when the stone was wetted and inked, would print repeatedly — the principle that oil and water repel. He developed the process for text, music, and art, obtained privileges in Bavaria, and published his Vollständiges Lehrbuch der Steindruckerey (Complete Textbook of Lithography) in 1818.

Legacy

Lithography revolutionised commercial printing, mapmaking, and fine-art printmaking, and evolved into modern offset lithography, still the dominant printing process for books, packaging and newspapers.
