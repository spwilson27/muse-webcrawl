Faisal I bin al-Hussein bin Ali al-Hashemi (20 May 1883 – 8 September 1933) was King of Iraq from 1921 to 1933, and briefly King of Syria in 1920. A member of the Hashemite family, he was a leader of the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire during the First World War.

Arab Revolt and Syria

Faisal, the third son of Hussein bin Ali, Sharif of Mecca, commanded Arab forces in the revolt alongside T. E. Lawrence. In 1920 he was proclaimed King of Syria, but was expelled by French forces after the Battle of Maysalun in July of that year.

King of Iraq

With British support, Faisal was installed as King of Iraq under the British Mandate in 1921. He balanced relations with Britain, Iraqi nationalists, and the country's Sunni, Shia, and Kurdish communities, and oversaw Iraq's admission to the League of Nations as an independent state in 1932. He died in Bern, Switzerland, in 1933 and was succeeded by his son Ghazi.
