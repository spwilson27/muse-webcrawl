Imperial German Navy

The Imperial German Navy (German: Kaiserliche Marine) was the navy of the German Empire, which existed from 1871 to 1919. It grew out of the Prussian Navy and the navy of the North German Confederation, and its expansion under Kaiser Wilhelm II and Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz was a major factor in the Anglo-German naval arms race before the First World War.

Build-up

Although Germany's strategy was traditionally land-based, Wilhelm II's accession in 1888 brought a drive for a battle fleet. The Navy Laws of 1898 and 1900, championed by Tirpitz, authorized a fleet strong enough — under his "risk theory" — to deter even the Royal Navy. By 1914 Germany possessed the world's second-largest battle fleet, centered on the High Seas Fleet based at Wilhelmshaven and Kiel.

First World War

During the war, the High Seas Fleet's surface actions were limited; its largest engagement was the Battle of Jutland (May 31 – June 1, 1916) against the British Grand Fleet, which ended tactically inconclusive and left the strategic situation unchanged. Germany increasingly relied on U-boats, including unrestricted submarine warfare from 1917, which helped provoke the United States' entry into the war.

End

In October 1918, a planned final fleet sortie triggered the Kiel mutiny, sparking the German Revolution. After the Armistice, the fleet was interned at Scapa Flow, where on June 21, 1919, German crews scuttled most of the ships rather than surrender them. The Imperial Navy was formally dissolved, and the Treaty of Versailles permitted Germany only a much-reduced successor force, the Reichsmarine.
