Pulaar is a variety of the Fula language spoken by the Fula people of the Senegal River valley — the Futa Toro region of northern Senegal and southern Mauritania — and by communities in Mali, Guinea-Bissau, and the diaspora. Its speakers call themselves Haalpulaar'en ("those who speak Pulaar"), a group that includes the Toucouleur people. Pulaar is one of the major national languages of Senegal and is used in radio, primary education, and literacy programmes.

Classification and features

Pulaar belongs to the western branch of Fula within the Atlantic languages of the Niger–Congo family. Like other Fula varieties it has a large system of noun classes (over twenty, marked by suffixes and by initial consonant alternation), implosive consonants (ɓ, ɗ, ƴ), prenasalised stops, and a distinction between long and short vowels. Its closest relatives among the Fula continuum are Pular of Guinea and Maasina Fulfulde of Mali; mutual intelligibility across the whole Fula dialect chain is partial, and the western and eastern varieties can be difficult for each other's speakers.

Writing and status

Pulaar has been written for centuries in an Arabic-based script called Ajami, used for religious poetry and correspondence by the region's Islamic scholars. A standardised Latin orthography was adopted in Senegal in the later 20th century for official and educational use, and Pulaar-language newspapers, literature, and broadcasting (including large audiences in Mauritania's south) have grown since. In Senegal, Pulaar is among the six languages first codified for use in national-language education and media.
