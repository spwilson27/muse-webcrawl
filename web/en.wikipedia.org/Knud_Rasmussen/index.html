Knud Johan Victor Rasmussen (1879–1933) was a Greenlandic-Danish polar explorer and anthropologist, often called the "father of Eskimology". Born in Greenland to a Danish missionary and an Inuit-Danish mother, he spoke Greenlandic as his first language and became uniquely able to document Inuit cultures from within.

Early expeditions

Rasmussen joined the Danish Literary Expedition to Greenland (1902–04) and in 1910 co-founded the Thule Trading Station at Cape York (Uummannaq), naming it after the legendary Thule. The station financed his research through trade in furs.

The Thule expeditions

Between 1912 and 1933 Rasmussen led seven Thule expeditions across Greenland and Arctic Canada and Alaska. The Fifth Thule Expedition (1921–1924) was his greatest achievement: travelling by dog sled some 32,000 km from Greenland to the Bering Strait, his team collected vast ethnographic material on Inuit groups, published in ten volumes.

Work and legacy

Rasmussen recorded myths, songs and shamanic practices, co-produced the film Palos Brudefærd, and advocated for Greenlanders' welfare. His house in Hundested, Denmark, is now a museum. He died in Copenhagen in 1933 after an illness contracted during the Seventh Thule Expedition.
