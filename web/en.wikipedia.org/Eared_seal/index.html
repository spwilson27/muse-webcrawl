Eared seal

An eared seal, or otariid, is any member of the marine mammal family Otariidae, one of three groupings of pinnipeds. They comprise 15 extant species in seven genera, commonly known either as sea lions or fur seals, and are distinguished from true (earless) seals by external ear flaps, larger foreflippers and the ability to rotate their hind flippers forward to walk on land.

Anatomy and locomotion

Otariids propel themselves mainly with long, largely hairless foreflippers, steering with the hind flippers, whereas phocid seals swim primarily with their hindquarters. On land, eared seals can support themselves on their foreflippers and move with a bounding gait. Males are typically much larger than females, especially in sea lions.

Distribution and behaviour

Otariids live in oceans worldwide except the northern Atlantic, from subpolar to tropical waters. They feed on fish, squid, krill and sometimes penguins. Most species are highly polygynous, with dominant males defending territories or female groups during the breeding season on island rookeries.

Conservation

Several fur seal species were hunted nearly to extinction for their pelts in the 18th and 19th centuries and have since recovered under protection; others, such as the Galápagos fur seal, remain threatened, and the Japanese sea lion is extinct.
