Stress (mechanics)

In continuum mechanics, stress is a physical quantity that describes forces present during deformation. For an object being pushed on all sides, such as a submerged submarine, the stress is called pressure; for an object being pulled apart, it is tensile stress, and for material resisting sliding, shear stress. More generally, stress is defined as the force divided by the area over which it acts: σ = F / A. Stress is expressed in the SI unit of pascals (Pa).

More precisely, stress is a tensor quantity: the Cauchy stress tensor, introduced by Augustin-Louis Cauchy in the 1820s, completely describes the state of stress at a point. It has nine components, of which symmetry of the tensor reduces the independent components to six — three normal stresses and three shear stresses. Transformation of the tensor between coordinate systems yields principal stresses, the maximum and minimum normal stresses at a point, computed with the aid of Mohr's circle.

Types of simple stress

Normal stresses act perpendicular to a surface (tension and compression) while shear stresses act parallel to it. Uniaxial stress arises in bars under axial loading; biaxial and triaxial (hydrostatic) states arise in pressure vessels and deep underground materials respectively.

Stress analysis

Stress analysis determines internal stresses from external loads, geometry, and material properties, and is essential in mechanical, civil, and aerospace engineering. Methods include analytical solutions, the finite element method, and experimental techniques such as photoelasticity and strain gauges. Stress is related to strain through constitutive laws such as Hooke's law for elastic materials.
