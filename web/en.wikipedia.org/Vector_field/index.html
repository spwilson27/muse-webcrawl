Vector field

In vector calculus and physics, a vector field is an assignment of a vector to each point in a space, most commonly Euclidean space. A vector field on a plane can be visualised as a collection of arrows with given magnitudes and directions, each attached to a point on the plane.

Examples

Vector fields model quantities that have direction at every point: the velocity of a moving fluid, the force of gravity or electromagnetism at each location, or wind patterns on a weather map. The gradient of a scalar field is a vector field; the curl and divergence characterise a field's rotation and outflow, respectively.

Operations and theory

Line integrals measure work done along curves in a field; flux measures flow through a surface. Fundamental results include Green's theorem, Stokes' theorem, and the divergence theorem. A field with zero curl is conservative and can be written as the gradient of a potential. In differential geometry, vector fields are sections of the tangent bundle of a manifold, and their flows describe the motion of points along the field.
