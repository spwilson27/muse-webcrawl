Off-Broadway

Off-Broadway refers to professional theatre venues in New York City with seating capacities between 100 and 499, and to the plays, musicals, and revues staged in them. By this definition, determined by contract agreements with theatrical unions, Off-Broadway venues are smaller than Broadway theatres (500+ seats) but larger than Off-Off-Broadway venues (fewer than 100 seats). The category does not refer to geography: an "Off-Broadway" theatre may be located in the Broadway theatre district itself.

History

The movement emerged in the 1950s as artists sought alternatives to the commercial pressures of Broadway, staging more experimental and affordable work in Greenwich Village and elsewhere. Landmark productions included the 1952 revival of Summer and Smoke at Circle in the Square and the long-running The Threepenny Opera. The Obie Awards were created in 1955 by The Village Voice to honour Off-Broadway achievement.

Role in theatre

Many successful Broadway shows began Off-Broadway, including Hair, A Chorus Line, Rent, Avenue Q, and Hamilton, which premiered at the Public Theater in 2015. Major Off-Broadway companies include Playwrights Horizons, Manhattan Theatre Club, Signature Theatre, the Roundabout Theatre Company, and the Public Theater.
