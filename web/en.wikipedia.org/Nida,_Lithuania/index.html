Nida (German: Nidden; Curonian: Nīde) is a resort town in Lithuania, the administrative center of Neringa Municipality on the Curonian Spit. Situated between the Curonian Lagoon and the Baltic Sea near the border with Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast, Nida is famous for its towering shifting sand dunes, weather vanes, fishermen's cottages, and its history as an artists' colony. The Parnidis Dune, rising about 52 metres above the town, is one of the largest drifting dunes in Europe.

The Parnidis Dune overlooking Nida and the Curonian Lagoon

History

Nida was first mentioned in Teutonic Order documents in the early 15th century as a settlement on the road between Königsberg and Memel. The original village was buried by drifting sand and relocated to its present site in the 18th century. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Nida became a celebrated artists' colony (Künstlerkolonie Nidden), attracting German Expressionist painters such as Max Pechstein and Karl Schmidt-Rottluff. The Nobel Prize-winning writer Thomas Mann built a summer house here in 1930, where he wrote parts of Joseph and His Brothers; the house is now a museum and cultural center.

Geography

Nida lies on the Curonian Spit, a 98 km UNESCO World Heritage peninsula shared with Russia. Great dunes, some exceeding 60 metres, dominate the landscape; the Parnidis Dune carries a sundial and an observation deck overlooking both the lagoon and the Baltic Sea.

Tourism

Today Nida is one of Lithuania's premier seaside resorts, known for quiet beaches, cycling paths along the spit, smoked fish, amber, and its colourful carved weather vanes (vėtrungės) derived from Kuršininkai fishing-boat markers. Ferries connect it across the lagoon, and the town is reached by road from Klaipėda via the Smiltynė ferry.
