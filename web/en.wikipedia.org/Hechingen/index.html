Hechingen is a town in the Zollernalbkreis district of Baden-Württemberg, Germany, about 60 km south of Stuttgart. It lies below the Swabian Jura near Hohenzollern Castle, the ancestral seat of the House of Hohenzollern, which overlooks the town from Mount Hohenzollern.

History

Hechingen was first documented in 786. It became the residence of the counts of Zollern and later the capital of the principality of Hohenzollern-Hechingen, one of the lines of the Hohenzollern dynasty that also produced the rulers of Brandenburg, Prussia and the German Empire. In 1850 the principality was absorbed into Prussia as part of the province of Hohenzollern. The Jewish community of Hechingen, documented from the sixteenth century, built a synagogue in 1767 that survives as a memorial and cultural venue.

Sights

Hohenzollern Castle, rebuilt in its present neo-Gothic form in the nineteenth century, remains partly owned by the Hohenzollern family and holds the Prussian royal crown and relics of Frederick the Great. In the town itself, the collegiate church of St James (Stiftskirche) and the Old Town's half-timbered houses are notable. Nearby, the excavated Roman villa rustica at Stein is preserved under a museum shelter.
