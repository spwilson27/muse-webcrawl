Anita Mui

Anita Mui Yim-fong (10 October 1963 – 30 December 2003) was a Hong Kong singer and actress who made major contributions to the Cantopop music scene and received numerous awards and honours. Known as the "Daughter of Hong Kong" and often called the "Madonna of the East", she was famed for her contralto voice, flamboyant stage costumes, and ever-changing image.

Early life and career

Born in Hong Kong to a poor family, Mui began singing in nightclubs and amusement parks as a small child to help support her family. In 1982 she won the first New Talent Singing Awards, launching her recording career. Her deep voice, unusual for a female singer, and her bold image set her apart; albums such as Bad Girl (1985) made her Cantopop's leading female star through the 1980s.

Acting

Mui starred in more than 40 films, winning the Golden Horse Award for Best Actress for her role as the ghost Fleur in Rouge (1987) opposite Leslie Cheung. Other notable roles included A Better Tomorrow III (1989), The Heroic Trio (1993), and July Rhapsody (2002), for which she won further acting honours.

Activism and final years

Mui was active in charitable work, founding a nursing home and supporting SARS relief in 2003. After announcing she had cervical cancer, she performed a final series of concerts in November 2003, famously ascending a staircase in a wedding gown — "marrying" the stage — in her farewell. She died on 30 December 2003, aged 40. Her funeral drew huge crowds, and a statue of her stands on Hong Kong's Avenue of Stars.

Legacy

Mui's life was dramatised in the 2021 biopic Anita. She remains an icon of Hong Kong popular culture, admired for her artistry, generosity, and advocacy for democracy.
