Mountain biking

Mountain biking (MTB) is a sport of riding bicycles off-road, often over rough terrain, using specially designed mountain bikes. Mountain bikes share similarities with other bikes but incorporate features designed to enhance durability and performance in rough terrain, such as air or coil-sprung shocks, wide knobby tires, durable wheels, more powerful brakes, and lower gear ratios for climbing steep grades.

History

The sport is generally traced to Marin County, California, in the 1970s, where riders including Gary Fisher, Charlie Kelly, and Joe Breeze adapted heavy "klunker" bicycles for downhill runs on Mount Tamalpais, later building purpose-designed frames. The first purpose-built production mountain bike, the Specialized Stumpjumper, appeared in 1981. Mountain biking grew rapidly in the 1990s; cross-country racing became an Olympic sport at the 1996 Atlanta Games.

Disciplines

  • Cross-country (XC): racing over varied terrain, emphasizing endurance and climbing
  • Downhill (DH): timed descents on steep, technical courses, usually with lift access
  • Enduro: timed downhill stages with untimed uphill transfers
  • Trail riding: recreational riding on natural and purpose-built trails
  • Dirt jumping, freeride, trials, and four-cross: jump-, stunt-, and head-to-head-focused formats

The sport is governed internationally by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), which runs the Mountain Bike World Cup.
