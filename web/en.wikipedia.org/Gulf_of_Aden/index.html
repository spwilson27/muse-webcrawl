The Gulf of Aden is a deepwater gulf of the Arabian Sea in the Indian Ocean, located between Yemen on the south coast of the Arabian Peninsula and Somalia and Djibouti in the Horn of Africa. In the northwest, it connects with the Red Sea through the Bab el-Mandeb strait. It is a vital waterway for shipping, especially for oil and container traffic travelling between Europe and Asia through the Suez Canal; some 21,000 ships transit the gulf annually, carrying a significant share of world trade and of Europe's oil supplies.

Geography and geology

The gulf is about 900 km long and varies in width from roughly 150 to 480 km. Geologically it is a young ocean basin formed by the separation of the Arabian Plate from the African Plate beginning about 35 million years ago; its central trough contains an active spreading ridge continuous with the East African Rift and the Red Sea rift system. The Yemeni island of Socotra lies at its eastern mouth.

History and trade

The gulf's ports have been trading entrepôts since antiquity — the frankincense ports of southern Arabia and the Somali trading towns described in the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea. The port of Aden, a British colony from 1839 to 1967, became one of the world's busiest bunkering ports after the Suez Canal opened in 1869.

Piracy

From about 2005 to 2012, Somali pirates operating from the gulf's coasts hijacked numerous commercial vessels for ransom, prompting international naval patrols (including EU NAVFOR Atalanta, Combined Task Force 151 and NATO operations), industry best-management practices and the use of armed guards, after which successful hijackings fell sharply. Attacks resumed sporadically in the 2020s, alongside missile and drone attacks on shipping in the adjacent Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb by Houthi forces from late 2023, which caused many shipping companies to reroute vessels around the Cape of Good Hope.

Environment

The gulf is biologically productive, with monsoon-driven upwelling supporting major fisheries for tuna, sardines and other species, though illegal fishing and dumping have been persistent concerns along the Somali coast.
