The Secret Intelligence Service (SIS), commonly known as MI6 (Military Intelligence, Section 6), is the foreign intelligence agency of the United Kingdom. It is responsible for the covert overseas collection and analysis of human intelligence in support of the UK's national security.

History

The service was formed in 1909 as part of the Secret Service Bureau, under Captain (later Sir) Mansfield Smith-Cumming, who signed his letters with the initial "C" written in green ink — a tradition retained by every subsequent chief. During the Second World War the service ran agents and networks across occupied Europe and worked alongside the codebreakers of Bletchley Park. Its existence was not officially avowed by the British government until 1994, the same year it moved into the SIS Building at Vauxhall Cross.

In popular culture

MI6 is the employer of Ian Fleming's fictional spy James Bond, arguably the most famous intelligence officer in literature and film, and of John le Carré's George Smiley.
