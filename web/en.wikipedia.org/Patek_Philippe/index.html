Patek Philippe SA is a Swiss luxury watch and clock manufacturer, founded in 1839 in Geneva. It is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious watchmakers in the world, known for its complicated mechanical movements, traditional craftsmanship, and the fact that it has remained a family-owned company under the Stern family since 1932.

History

The company began when Polish watchmaker Antoni Patek started making pocket watches in Geneva in 1839. In 1845 he partnered with French watchmaker Adrien Philippe, inventor of the keyless winding and hand-setting mechanism, and in 1851 the firm took the name Patek, Philippe & Cie. The company supplied watches to royalty, including Queen Victoria, and pioneered numerous horological innovations, including the perpetual calendar, split-seconds hand, chronograph, and minute repeater in wristwatches.

In 1932, during the Great Depression, the company was purchased by brothers Charles and Jean Stern, whose family has owned it ever since. Thierry Stern became president in 2009.

Notable watches

Famous models include the Calatrava (introduced 1932), the Nautilus sports watch designed by Gérald Genta (1976), the Aquanaut, and grand complications such as the Sky Moon Tourbillon and the Grandmaster Chime, one of the most complicated wristwatches ever made. The Henry Graves Supercomplication pocket watch of 1933 held the record as the world's most complicated watch for decades and sold at auction for record prices.

Production and philosophy

Patek Philippe produces roughly 60,000–70,000 watches per year, all with in-house movements. Since 1996 its advertising has used the slogan "You never actually own a Patek Philippe. You merely look after it for the next generation." The Patek Philippe Museum in Geneva houses one of the world's most important collections of timepieces.
