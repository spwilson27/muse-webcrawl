The Mali War is an ongoing armed conflict that started in January 2012 between the northern and southern parts of Mali in Africa. In January 2012, the National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad (MNLA), an alliance of Tuareg rebels bolstered by fighters and weapons returning from Libya after the fall of Muammar Gaddafi, launched an insurgency in the north. Frustration with the government's handling of the rebellion led soldiers under Captain Amadou Sanogo to overthrow President Amadou Toumani Touré in March 2012, and in the resulting chaos the rebels seized the northern two-thirds of the country, including Timbuktu, Gao, and Kidal, declaring the independent state of Azawad.

The MNLA was soon displaced by its Islamist allies — Ansar Dine, al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb (AQIM), and MUJAO — which imposed a harsh form of sharia law, destroyed Sufi shrines in Timbuktu, and in January 2013 advanced south toward the capital. At Mali's request, France launched Operation Serval on 11 January 2013, halting the advance and retaking the northern cities within weeks, with support from African forces.

International involvement

The African-led AFISMA force was absorbed in July 2013 into the UN Multidimensional Integrated Stabilization Mission in Mali (MINUSMA), which became one of the UN's deadliest peacekeeping missions. France followed Serval with the regional counterterrorism Operation Barkhane (2014–2022) across the Sahel. The 2015 Algiers peace agreement between the government and northern armed groups was never fully implemented.

Escalation and realignment

Violence spread from the north into central Mali, fuelled by jihadist groups — including the al-Qaeda coalition JNIM (formed 2017) and the Islamic State in the Greater Sahara — and by intercommunal conflict between Fulani herders and Dogon and Bambara farming communities. Military coups in August 2020 and May 2021 brought a junta led by Assimi Goïta to power, which turned to Russian Wagner Group (later Africa Corps) mercenaries, demanded the withdrawal of French forces (completed 2022) and then of MINUSMA (completed December 2023), and in late 2023 retook Kidal with Wagner support. The conflict remains unresolved, with large areas outside effective state control and a severe humanitarian crisis.
