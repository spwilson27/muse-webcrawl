Side cap

A side cap is a military cap that can be folded flat when not being worn. It is also known as a garrison cap or flight cap in the United States, a field service cap in the United Kingdom, or an overseas cap. Its distinctive shape resembles an envelope or the hull of a boat.

History

The side cap derives from the Scottish Glengarry bonnet and from folding field caps worn during the First World War, when steel helmets made stiff peaked caps impractical at the front. The style was adopted by many armies and air forces during the Second World War and remains part of several dress uniforms.

Use

Because it folds flat, the cap can be tucked under a belt or shoulder strap when a helmet or headset is worn. It is commonly worn by air force personnel, soldiers in barracks dress, veterans' organizations, and uniformed youth movements.

Variants

National variants include the German Schiffchen ("little ship"), the Soviet pilotka, and the Spanish gorrillo. Colours and piping vary by service branch and unit.
