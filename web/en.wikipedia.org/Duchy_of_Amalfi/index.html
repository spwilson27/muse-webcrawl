The Duchy of Amalfi was a de facto independent state centred on the seaside city of Amalfi and its surrounding territory on the Sorrentine Peninsula in southern Italy during the Early and High Middle Ages. Amalfi, in the region of Campania, became one of Italy's great maritime republics, rivalling Venice, Pisa and Genoa.

History

Amalfi broke away from the Duchy of Naples around 839 and elected its own rulers, first prefects and later dukes (doges). Its merchants traded across the Mediterranean, maintaining fondachi in Constantinople, Antioch, Egypt and North Africa, and Amalfitan sailors are traditionally credited with refining the mariner's compass. The city's maritime code, the Tavole Amalfitane, influenced Mediterranean sea law for centuries.

The duchy's power waned in the eleventh century. It fell under Norman control, was sacked by Pisa in 1135–1137, and was absorbed into the Kingdom of Sicily, ending its independence, though Amalfi retained its fame in trade and law long afterwards.
