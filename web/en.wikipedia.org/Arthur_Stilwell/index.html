Arthur Stilwell

Born: 21 October 1859, Rochester, New York

Died: 26 September 1928, New York City

Occupation: Railroad builder, entrepreneur

Known for: Founding the Kansas City Southern Railway and Port Arthur, Texas

Arthur Edward Stilwell (21 October 1859 – 26 September 1928) was an American entrepreneur and railroad promoter who built several railroads in the United States and Mexico, founded numerous towns, and is best known for creating the Kansas City Southern Railway and the city of Port Arthur, Texas.

Railroad building

Stilwell began in insurance and printing before turning to railroad promotion in Kansas City in the 1880s. He built the Kansas City Suburban Belt Railway and then the Kansas City, Pittsburg and Gulf Railroad, driving a line south to the Gulf of Mexico. Having decided — on a hunch he attributed to his "unseen forces" — to avoid Galveston, which was later devastated by the 1900 hurricane, he founded the port city of Port Arthur on Sabine Lake as his terminus. After losing control of the railroad in receivership, it was reorganised as the Kansas City Southern Railway.

Other ventures

Stilwell went on to promote the Kansas City, Mexico and Orient Railway, intended to reach the Pacific at Topolobampo, and founded towns along his lines, many named whimsically — sometimes, he claimed, suggested in dreams. He wrote novels, plays and an autobiography, Live and Grow Young, and maintained a lifelong belief in guidance from spirits, which he said influenced his business decisions.
