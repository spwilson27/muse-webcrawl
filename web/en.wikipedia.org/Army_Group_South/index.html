Army Group South

Army Group South
German: Heeresgruppe Süd
Active: 1939–1945 (various formations)
Country: Nazi Germany
Campaigns: Poland; France; Operation Barbarossa; Case Blue; Stalingrad; Ukraine

Army Group South (German: Heeresgruppe Süd) was the name of several major German Army formations during the Second World War, principally responsible for operations in Ukraine and southern Russia.

Early war

Under Gerd von Rundstedt, Army Group South led the main effort in the invasion of Poland in 1939 and served in the campaign in France in 1940. For Operation Barbarossa in 1941 it advanced into Ukraine, capturing Kyiv and encircling huge Soviet forces in the Uman and Kyiv pockets.

Case Blue and Stalingrad

In summer 1942 the army group was split into Army Groups A and B for Case Blue, the offensive toward Stalingrad and the Caucasus oil fields. The destruction of the Sixth Army at Stalingrad in early 1943 was a turning point of the war.

Later formations

A re-formed Army Group South under Erich von Manstein fought the Battle of Kursk and the long retreat across Ukraine in 1943–44, before the formation was renamed Army Group North Ukraine and later reorganized again; a final Army Group South operated in Hungary and Austria in 1945.
