Ostia Antica

Ostia Antica is an ancient Roman city and archaeological site near the modern town of Ostia, about 25 kilometres southwest of Rome. Founded traditionally in the 7th century BC and developed as Rome's seaport at the mouth of the Tiber, it became a thriving commercial centre of perhaps 50,000 inhabitants at its peak in the 2nd century AD.

History

Ostia began as a fortified colony guarding the river mouth and nearby salt pans. During the Republic it served as Rome's naval base and grain port. When silting made the old harbour inadequate, the emperors Claudius and Trajan built new basins at Portus to the north, but Ostia remained the administrative and residential heart of the harbour district, filled with warehouses (horrea), guild offices and apartment blocks (insulae).

The ruins

Decline in late antiquity and the shifting coastline left the city buried in silt, preserving it remarkably. Excavations, especially under Benito Mussolini for the 1942 exposition, exposed the theatre, the Forum, the Capitolium temple, baths with fine mosaics, the Square of the Corporations with its guild mosaics, and everyday buildings such as bakeries, fulleries and taverns. Together with Pompeii, Ostia Antica gives one of the fullest pictures of ordinary urban life in the Roman Empire.
