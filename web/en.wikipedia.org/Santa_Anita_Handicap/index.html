The Santa Anita Handicap is an American Thoroughbred horse race held annually at Santa Anita Park in Arcadia, California. Run over 1¼ miles on dirt for horses aged four and older, usually in early March, it was for decades one of the richest and most prestigious races in the United States — celebrated as the "Big 'Cap" — and remains a Grade I event.

History

The race was inaugurated in 1935, the first season of the newly built Santa Anita Park, with a then-spectacular purse of $100,000 that made it the richest race in America and a nationwide sensation; the winner was Azucar. It quickly attracted the finest handicap horses of the era. Seabiscuit, whose story became a symbol of the Depression-era United States, was beaten narrowly in the 1937 and 1938 renewals before finally winning the Santa Anita Handicap in 1940 in his comeback from injury — the climactic race of his career.

Other famous winners include Citation, Round Table, Affirmed, Spectacular Bid, John Henry (twice), Alysheba, Best Pal, Tiznow, Lava Man (twice), Game On Dude (three times) and Accelerate. The race was not run in 1942–44 during the Second World War, when the track was closed. In 1958, the race's finish between Silky Sullivan and other contenders became part of racing folklore, and in 1985 the filly Lady's Secret won en route to a Horse of the Year campaign the following season.

Records and character

Santa Anita's handicap tradition historically required the best older horses to concede weight, producing famous duels. In the modern era the race's purse and status have fluctuated relative to newer rich events such as the Pegasus World Cup, but the "Big 'Cap" endures as one of American racing's classic tests of stamina for older horses.
