Executive compensation, or executive pay, is composed of the financial compensation and other non-financial awards received by an executive from their firm for their service. It is typically a mixture of fixed salary, variable performance-based bonuses, equity (stock options and restricted shares), benefits, and perquisites, designed to align executives' incentives with shareholders' interests under agency theory.

Structure

Base salary is usually a minority of total pay at large public companies. Annual bonuses depend on yearly performance metrics, while long-term incentive plans vest over several years. Stock options grant the right to purchase shares at a set strike price; restricted stock units vest over time. Deferred compensation, supplemental executive retirement plans, and "golden parachute" change-of-control payments are common additional elements.

Controversy

Executive pay levels, and especially the ratio of CEO pay to median worker pay, have been widely debated. Critics argue boards are captured by management and pay consultants ratchet packages upward, while defenders contend pay reflects a competitive market for scarce managerial talent. Reforms include mandatory disclosure, clawback provisions for restated earnings, and shareholder "say on pay" votes introduced in the United States by the Dodd–Frank Act of 2010.
