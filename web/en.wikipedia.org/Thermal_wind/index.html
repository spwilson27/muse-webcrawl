Thermal wind

The thermal wind is the vertical change (shear) of the geostrophic wind in the atmosphere, arising from horizontal temperature gradients. It is not an actual wind but the difference between geostrophic winds at two pressure levels, and it explains why the jet stream strengthens with height in the presence of a pole-to-equator temperature contrast.

Relation

The thermal wind blows parallel to lines of constant temperature (isotherms), with cold air on its left in the Northern Hemisphere, and its magnitude is proportional to the horizontal temperature gradient. The concept underlies the polar-front theory of mid-latitude cyclones and the behaviour of fronts.
