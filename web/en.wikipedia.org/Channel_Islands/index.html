Channel Islands


The Channel Islands, an archipelago in the English Channel off the Normandy coast.

The Channel Islands (Norman: Îles d'la Manche; French: îles Anglo-Normandes) are an archipelago in the English Channel off the French coast of Normandy. They are divided into two Crown Dependencies: the Bailiwick of Jersey and the Bailiwick of Guernsey, the latter including Alderney, Sark and several smaller islands. The islands are not part of the United Kingdom, but the British monarch is their head of state and the UK is responsible for their defence and international representation. Their combined population is about 170,000.

History

The islands were attached to the Duchy of Normandy in the 10th century and came under the English Crown with the Norman Conquest of 1066. When King John lost mainland Normandy in 1204, the islands remained loyal to the English Crown and were granted self-government, forming the last remnant of the duchy. During the Second World War the Channel Islands were the only part of the British Isles occupied by German forces, from 1940 until liberation on 9 May 1945.

Economy

Finance is the dominant industry, the islands having developed as offshore financial centres from the 1960s. Tourism, agriculture (including Jersey Royal potatoes and Guernsey dairy cattle) and light industry also contribute. Both bailiwicks issue their own pound sterling notes and coins, pegged to the British pound.

Geography

The largest island, Jersey, covers 118 square kilometres and lies about 22 kilometres from the Cotentin Peninsula; Guernsey lies further west. The islands enjoy a mild maritime climate influenced by the Gulf Stream, with some of the largest tidal ranges in the world.
