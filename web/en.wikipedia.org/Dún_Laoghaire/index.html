Dún Laoghaire

Dún Laoghaire is a suburban coastal town in County Dublin, Ireland, about 12 km south of Dublin city centre. It is the administrative centre of the county of Dún Laoghaire–Rathdown and is best known for its large artificial harbour, built in the nineteenth century.

Name and history

The name means "fort of Laoghaire", after the fifth-century High King Laoghaire mac Néill, who is said to have kept a fort here. The town grew around its harbour, whose foundation stone was laid in 1817; the East and West Piers were built of granite from nearby Dalkey quarry. Between 1821 and 1920 the town was officially called Kingstown, in honour of a visit by George IV, before reverting to its Irish name.

The harbour and ferry port

For generations Dún Laoghaire was Ireland's principal mail and passenger port to Britain, with ferries to Holyhead in Wales. Regular ferry services ended in 2015, and the harbour is now used mainly for leisure sailing, cruise ship tenders and marine activities. The town's two yacht clubs and long piers make it a centre for sailing on Dublin Bay.

Landmarks

Notable sights include the James Joyce Tower at nearby Sandycove, where the opening of Ulysses is set, the National Maritime Museum in the former Mariners' Church, and the Pavilion Theatre. The town is served by the DART railway, which follows the coast into Dublin.
