Alexey Pajitnov

Alexey Leonidovich Pajitnov (born 16 April 1955) is a Russian-American computer engineer and video game designer best known for creating the puzzle game Tetris in 1984, while working at the Dorodnitsyn Computing Centre of the Soviet Academy of Sciences in Moscow. Tetris became one of the best-selling and most influential video games of all time.

Alexey Pajitnov, creator of Tetris.

Tetris

Pajitnov developed Tetris on an Elektronika 60 computer, inspired by pentomino puzzles; the name combines the Greek prefix tetra- (four, for the four-block pieces, or tetrominoes) with tennis, his favourite sport. With Dmitry Pavlovsky and Vadim Gerasimov he adapted it for the IBM PC, from where it spread across the world. Because he was a Soviet state employee, Pajitnov initially received no royalties; rights were managed by the state agency ELORG.

Later career

Pajitnov emigrated to the United States in 1991 and worked at Microsoft on puzzle collections including Microsoft Entertainment Pack: The Puzzle Collection and the Xbox game Hexic HD. In 1996 he co-founded The Tetris Company with Henk Rogers to manage licensing, and he finally began receiving royalties from the game.
