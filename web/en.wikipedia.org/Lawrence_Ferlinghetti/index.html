Lawrence Ferlinghetti

Lawrence Ferlinghetti (24 March 1919 – 22 February 2021) was an American poet, painter, publisher and activist, best known as co-founder of City Lights Booksellers & Publishers in San Francisco, a hub of the Beat Generation.

Life and work

Born in Yonkers, New York, Ferlinghetti served in the US Navy in the Second World War and took a doctorate at the Sorbonne. In 1953 he and Peter D. Martin opened City Lights, the first all-paperback bookshop in the US. Its Pocket Poets series published Allen Ginsberg's Howl (1956); Ferlinghetti was tried, and acquitted, for obscenity in a landmark free-speech case. His own collection A Coney Island of the Mind (1958) became one of the best-selling poetry books of the century. He was San Francisco's first poet laureate and lived to 101.
