Chazzan

A chazzan (Hebrew: חַזָּן, also transliterated hazzan; plural chazzanim) or cantor is a Jewish musician, or a person with a fine voice, trained in Jewish liturgical music, who leads the congregation in songful prayer in the synagogue. In larger congregations the chazzan is a salaried professional, and in many communities the role is a recognised form of clergy.

Role

The chazzan chants the prayers in traditional modes (nusach), often with considerable improvisation, and leads responsive readings and congregational singing. On Sabbaths and festivals the cantor's music is a central feature of worship, and the great cantors of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries — the so-called "golden age of chazzanut" — were celebrated concert artists as well.

History

The office developed in the geonic period, when fixed liturgy required a skilled prayer leader. In modern times, Reform and Conservative movements ordain trained cantors through seminaries, and women have served as cantors in liberal movements since the late twentieth century. Where no professional is available, a lay prayer leader is called shaliach tzibbur ("emissary of the congregation").

Qualifications

Traditional requirements emphasise a pleasing voice, mastery of the liturgy and its modes, and personal piety. Contemporary cantorial schools add pastoral, educational and musical training.
