Axis–angle representation

In mathematics, the axis–angle representation of a rotation parameterizes a rotation in a three-dimensional Euclidean space by two quantities: a unit vector indicating the direction of an axis of rotation, and an angle describing the magnitude of the rotation about the axis. The angle and axis determine a rotation of the plane perpendicular to the axis.

Only two numbers, not three, are needed to define the direction of a unit vector, and the angle is a third number; however, the axis–angle representation is often handled as a three-component rotation vector whose direction is the axis and whose magnitude is the angle.

Euler's rotation theorem

The representation rests on Euler's rotation theorem, which states that any rotation or sequence of rotations of a rigid body about a fixed point is equivalent to a single rotation by a given angle about a fixed axis (the Euler axis) that runs through the fixed point.

Rodrigues' formula

Rodrigues' rotation formula gives the rotation of a vector about an axis: for a vector v rotated by angle θ about a unit axis k, the result is v cos θ + (k × v) sin θ + k(k · v)(1 − cos θ). The corresponding rotation matrix can be written compactly using the exponential map from the Lie algebra so(3) of skew-symmetric matrices to the rotation group SO(3).

Uses

Axis–angle representations are used in computer graphics, robotics, rigid body dynamics and the theory of the rotation group. They are closely related to Euler angles, quaternions and rotation matrices, and can be converted between these forms.
