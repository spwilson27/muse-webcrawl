Eliyahu M. Goldratt

Eliyahu Moshe Goldratt (March 31, 1947 – June 11, 2011) was an Israeli business management thinker, physicist by training, and originator of the Theory of Constraints (TOC). He introduced his ideas to a wide audience through his business novel The Goal (1984, with Jeff Cox), which follows a plant manager applying constraint-focused thinking to rescue a failing factory.

Theory of Constraints

Goldratt argued that any system's output is limited by a small number of constraints, and proposed the Five Focusing Steps: identify the constraint, exploit it, subordinate everything else to it, elevate it, and repeat as constraints shift. He later developed applications for production scheduling (drum-buffer-rope), project management (critical chain), distribution, marketing and sales, presented in books including It's Not Luck, Critical Chain and The Choice.

Career

Goldratt founded Creative Output, which marketed the OPT scheduling software, and later the Avraham Y. Goldratt Institute to promote TOC education. His work has been widely adopted in manufacturing and beyond, though debated within operations management scholarship.
