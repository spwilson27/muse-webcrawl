Tadeusz Kościuszko

Andrzej Tadeusz Bonawentura Kościuszko (4 or 12 February 1746 – 15 October 1817) was a Polish military engineer, statesman, and military leader who became a national hero in Poland, Lithuania, Belarus, and the United States. He fought in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth's struggles against Russia and Prussia, and on the U.S. side in the American Revolutionary War, where he rose to brigadier general. In 1794 he led the Kościuszko Uprising against the partitioning powers.

Early life and America

Kościuszko was born into a moderately wealthy szlachta family and educated at the Royal Military Academy in Warsaw and in Paris. Unable to buy a commission at home, he sailed for America in 1776 and was commissioned a colonel of engineers in the Continental Army. His fortifications at Bemis Heights contributed to the American victory at Saratoga, and he designed the defences of West Point. Congress promoted him to brigadier general in 1783 and granted him U.S. citizenship.

Uprising of 1794

Returning to Poland, Kościuszko fought in the 1792 war against Russia. On 24 March 1794, in Kraków's market square, he proclaimed an insurrection and took dictatorial powers as Supreme Commander. His scythemen-peasant infantry won at Racławice, and his Połaniec Manifesto promised limits on serfdom. Wounded and captured at Maciejowice in October, he was imprisoned in St. Petersburg until pardoned by Tsar Paul I in 1796.

Later years and legacy

Kościuszko spent his final years in exile in France and Switzerland, declining to serve Napoleon, whom he distrusted. His American will directed that his U.S. assets be used to free and educate enslaved people — a bequest administered, imperfectly, by Thomas Jefferson's executors. He is commemorated by monuments worldwide; Mount Kosciuszko, Australia's highest peak, bears his name, as does Kościuszko National Park.
