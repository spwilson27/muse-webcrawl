Physician

Occupation
Medical doctor
Education
Medical degree (MD, MBBS, DO)
Activity
Diagnosis and treatment of disease

A physician, medical practitioner, or doctor is a health professional who practices medicine, which is concerned with promoting, maintaining, or restoring health through the study, diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of disease, injury, and other physical and mental impairments.

Roles and responsibilities

Physicians may focus their practice on certain disease categories, types of patients, or methods of treatment, known as specialist medical practitioners, or assume responsibility for continuing, comprehensive care as general practitioners. Their duties include taking patient histories, performing examinations, ordering and interpreting diagnostic tests, prescribing medication, and performing procedures or surgery within their specialty.

Education and regulation

Becoming a physician typically requires an undergraduate degree, completion of medical school, and supervised postgraduate training (residency or internship), followed by licensing by a medical regulatory authority. Medical practice is governed by legal and ethical frameworks that vary by jurisdiction but generally require practice within a recognized standard of care.

History

Medicine has ancient roots in Egyptian, Greek, Indian, and Chinese traditions. The Hippocratic tradition in Greece emphasized observation and prognosis, and medieval and Renaissance universities formalized medical education. The 19th and 20th centuries brought germ theory, anesthesia, vaccines, and antibiotics, transforming physicians' effectiveness.
