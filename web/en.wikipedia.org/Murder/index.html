Murder

A murder trial in session

Murder is the unlawful killing of another human being without justification or valid excuse, committed with intent to kill or with the intent to cause serious bodily injury that results in death. It is generally distinguished from manslaughter by the presence of malice aforethought, and from other forms of homicide by its unlawfulness.

Elements

In common law jurisdictions, murder typically requires an act (or culpable omission) causing death, a mental state of malice aforethought, and the absence of defences such as self-defence. Many jurisdictions divide murder into degrees: first-degree murder usually involves premeditation or killings during certain felonies (the felony murder rule), while second-degree murder covers other intentional killings.

Penalties

Murder is among the most severely punished crimes. Sentences commonly include life imprisonment, and in some jurisdictions the death penalty. Some countries impose mandatory life sentences for murder.

Statistics

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime estimates several hundred thousand intentional homicides worldwide each year. Murder rates vary greatly between countries, from under 1 per 100,000 inhabitants in much of Europe and East Asia to several dozen per 100,000 in parts of Latin America and the Caribbean.
