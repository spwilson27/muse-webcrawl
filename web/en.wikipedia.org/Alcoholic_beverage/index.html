Alcoholic beverage

An alcoholic beverage (also called an alcoholic drink, adult beverage, or simply a drink) is a beverage containing ethanol, a type of alcohol also known as ethyl alcohol, produced by the fermentation of grains, fruits, or other sources of sugar. The consumption of alcoholic drinks, often called "drinking", plays an important social role in many cultures.

Types

The main classes are beer (typically 3–7% alcohol by volume, brewed from cereals), wine (roughly 9–16%, fermented from grapes or other fruits), and spirits (commonly 35–50%, produced by distilling fermented material), together with cider, mead, sake, and fortified wines. Alcohol content is usually expressed as alcohol by volume (ABV).

History and regulation

Fermented drinks are among humanity's oldest beverages: chemical evidence of a mixed fermented drink dates to the Neolithic village of Jiahu in China (c. 7000 BC). Alcohol production, sale, and consumption are regulated by law in most countries, with minimum drinking ages, taxation, and licensing; some jurisdictions and religions prohibit alcohol entirely. Globally, alcohol use is a major risk factor for disease and injury, and the World Health Organization estimates it causes millions of deaths annually.
