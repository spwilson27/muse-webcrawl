Hero of Socialist Labour (Russian: Герой Социалистического Труда, Geroy Sotsialisticheskogo Truda) was the highest degree of distinction in the Soviet Union for exceptional achievements in national economy and culture. It provided a similar status to the title Hero of the Soviet Union, which was awarded for heroic deeds, but unlike the latter, it was not awarded to foreign citizens.

History

The title was introduced by decree of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet on 27 December 1938. It superseded the earlier title Hero of Labour, established in 1928. Recipients were awarded the Order of Lenin, a diploma of the Presidium of the Supreme Soviet, and, from 1940, the "Hammer and Sickle" gold medal designed by Sergey Merkulov.

Criteria and privileges

The title was conferred on citizens whose labour activity contributed especially outstanding innovative results to industry, agriculture, transport, trade, science or technology, or who rendered exceptional service to the Soviet state. Holders received material privileges including a pension supplement, priority housing, and free public transport. A person twice awarded the title could have a bronze bust erected in their home town; the title was awarded twice to 201 people and three times to 15 people, among them nuclear physicist Igor Kurchatov and aircraft designer Andrei Tupolev.

Recipients and abolition

The first recipient was Joseph Stalin, awarded the title in December 1939 on his sixtieth birthday. Altogether 20,605 awards were made before the title was abolished following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991. Similar titles existed in other socialist states, including East Germany's Held der Arbeit and variants in Bulgaria, Romania, China and North Korea.
