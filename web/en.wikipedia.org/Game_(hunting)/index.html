Game (hunting)

Game or quarry is any wild animal hunted for animal products, for recreation, or as a trophy. The species of animal chosen as game varies in different parts of the world and is governed by local law. Game meat is typically eaten by the hunter or sold, and game animals are managed through hunting seasons, bag limits and licensing.

Types of game

Game is commonly divided into big game — large mammals such as deer, elk, moose, wild boar and bear — and small game, including rabbits, hares, squirrels and game birds. Game birds include upland birds such as pheasant, grouse and partridge, and waterfowl such as ducks and geese hunted over wetlands.

In Africa, the so-called "Big Five" — lion, leopard, rhinoceros, elephant and Cape buffalo — were historically the most prized trophy animals. In the United Kingdom, the term "game" has a specific legal meaning under the Game Acts, covering pheasants, partridges, grouse, hares and deer during defined seasons.

Hunting and management

Regulated hunting is used as a wildlife management tool: licence fees and excise taxes on equipment fund conservation in many countries, notably under the North American Model of Wildlife Conservation. Conversely, overhunting and illegal poaching have driven declines in many game populations, leading to protected status for some species.

Game meat

The meat of game animals — venison being the best known — is generally leaner and more strongly flavoured than domesticated meat. Traditional preparation includes hanging game birds and mammals to tenderise the flesh. Food-safety rules in many jurisdictions require inspection of game sold commercially.
