Mutagen

In genetics, a mutagen is a physical or chemical agent that permanently changes genetic material, usually DNA, in an organism, thus increasing the frequency of mutations above the natural background level. Mutagens that also promote cancer are termed carcinogens, though not all mutagens are carcinogenic and vice versa.

Types

  • Physical mutagens include ionizing radiation (X-rays, gamma rays, alpha particles) and ultraviolet light, which damages DNA by breaking strands or forming pyrimidine dimers.
  • Chemical mutagens include base analogues that substitute for normal bases during replication, intercalating agents such as ethidium bromide that insert between bases, alkylating agents such as mustard gas derivatives, and reactive oxygen species.
  • Biological mutagens include transposons and some viruses that insert their genetic material into host DNA.

Discovery

Hermann Joseph Muller demonstrated in 1927 that X-rays dramatically increased mutation rates in fruit flies, work that earned him the Nobel Prize. Charlotte Auerbach later showed that chemical agents, including mustard gas, were also mutagenic.

Testing

The Ames test, developed by Bruce Ames, uses bacteria to screen chemicals for mutagenicity quickly and inexpensively, and remains a standard early screen for carcinogenic potential.
