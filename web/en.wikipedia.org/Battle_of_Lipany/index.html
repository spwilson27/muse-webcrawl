Battle of Lipany

The Battle of Lipany (Czech: Bitva u Lipan), also called the Battle of Český Brod, was fought on 30 May 1434 near Lipany, east of Prague in Bohemia. It was the decisive final battle of the Hussite Wars, in which a coalition of moderate Hussites (Utraquists) and Bohemian Catholics defeated the radical Taborites and Orphans led by Prokop the Great, who was killed in the fighting.

Background

The Hussite Wars had raged since 1420, after the execution of Jan Hus, with Hussite armies repeatedly defeating crusades launched against them. By the 1430s, however, war-weariness, economic devastation and diplomatic openings at the Council of Basel drove the moderate Utraquist nobility toward a settlement. The radical Taborites and the "Orphans" rejected the negotiations, and the conflict became a civil war between Hussites themselves. In May 1434 the league of Bohemian lords fielded an army under Diviš Bořek of Miletínek, himself a former Hussite commander.

The battle

The radical army, numbering perhaps 10,000 with several hundred war wagons, occupied a strong position near Lipany. The baronial army feigned retreat with its cavalry after an initial exchange of cannon fire, drawing the Taborites out of their wagon fort (wagenburg) in pursuit. When the radicals had opened their formation, the concealed enemy cavalry and reserves attacked, stormed the wagons and crushed the Taborite army. Prokop the Great and many of his captains died on the field; radical losses may have reached 1,200 or more, with hundreds taken prisoner, many of whom were afterwards burned in barns at Český Brod.

Aftermath

Lipany ended the military power of radical Hussitism. Negotiations concluded with the Compacts of Basel (1436), which legalised communion under both kinds (bread and wine) for the laity in Bohemia, and Sigismund was finally accepted as king of Bohemia. In Czech memory the battle is mourned as a national tragedy—Hussites destroyed by Hussites—though it opened the way to peace.
