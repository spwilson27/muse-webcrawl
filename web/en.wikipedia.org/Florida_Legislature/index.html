Florida Legislature

The Florida Legislature is the legislature of the U.S. state of Florida. It is organized as a bicameral body composed of an upper house, the Florida Senate, and a lower house, the Florida House of Representatives. Article III of the Florida Constitution defines the terms and responsibilities of the Legislature.

Composition

The Florida Senate has 40 members, each elected from a single-member district to a four-year term, with elections staggered so that roughly half the seats are contested every two years (all seats following redistricting years). The Florida House of Representatives has 120 members elected to two-year terms. Legislators are limited to eight consecutive years in each chamber under term limits approved by voters in 1992. The Senate is presided over by its President and the House by its Speaker.

Sessions

The Legislature meets at the Florida State Capitol in Tallahassee. Regular sessions last 60 days, beginning on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in March in odd-numbered years and in January in even-numbered years; special sessions may be called by the governor or by the presiding officers. Its most important annual task is passing the state's General Appropriations Act — the only bill the state constitution requires it to enact — with a state budget exceeding $100 billion in recent years.

History

The first legislative body of Florida Territory met in 1822. The modern legislature's influence shifted dramatically after the reapportionment revolution of the 1960s ended the era of the "Pork Chop Gang", a bloc of rural legislators who dominated state politics. Florida has had a Republican majority in both chambers since the late 1990s.
