Women's Army Corps

The Women's Army Corps (WAC) was the women's branch of the United States Army. It was created as the Women's Army Auxiliary Corps (WAAC) on 15 May 1942 and converted to full military status as the WAC on 1 July 1943. It remained a distinct branch until 1978, when women were fully integrated into the Army's other branches.

History

Sponsored by Congresswoman Edith Nourse Rogers, the WAAC allowed women to serve in non-combat roles such as clerks, drivers, mechanics, and communications specialists, freeing men for combat duty. As full WACs after 1943, members received the same pay, ranks, and benefits as male soldiers. At its peak strength, around 150,000 women served during the Second World War.

Later service

WACs served in the Korean and Vietnam Wars. The corps was disestablished in 1978, and its remaining members were transferred to other branches as the Army moved to a fully integrated force.
