Canaan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Canaan was a Semitic-speaking civilisation and region in the Ancient Near East during the late 2nd millennium BC. It encompassed roughly the territory of today's Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, and Israel, centred on Phoenicia along the coast and the inland areas later known as the Land of Israel in Jewish tradition. The Canaanites are best known from the Hebrew Bible, Egyptian records such as the Amarna letters, and archaeology at sites including Ugarit, Hazor, Megiddo, and Byblos.

History

The name "Canaan" appears in cuneiform and Egyptian texts of the 2nd millennium BC; in the New Kingdom period the region was an Egyptian province contested with the Hittite Empire. Canaanite society was organised into city-states ruled by local kings under Egyptian oversight. In the Late Bronze Age collapse around 1200 BC, many Canaanite cities were destroyed or declined, and new peoples — Israelites in the highlands, Philistines on the coast, and the Phoenician city-states in the north — emerged from and succeeded the Canaanite world.

Language and culture

Canaanite languages, a branch of Northwest Semitic, include Phoenician, Hebrew, Moabite, and Ammonite. The Canaanites of Ugarit developed an early alphabetic cuneiform script, and the Phoenician alphabet, adapted from Canaanite letter forms, became the ancestor of the Greek, Latin, Hebrew, and Arabic alphabets. Canaanite religion centred on deities such as El, Baal, Asherah, and Anat, whose myths are preserved in the Ugaritic texts and which form the background against which biblical Israelite religion developed.
