Downhill (ski competition)


Downhill is the fastest and most dangerous alpine ski discipline.

Downhill is a form of alpine skiing competition in which the racer who completes a steep, long course in the fastest time wins. It is the fastest discipline in alpine skiing, with racers reaching speeds above 130 km/h on World Cup courses, and it carries the highest risk of injury.

Course and technique

Downhill courses typically drop 800–1,100 metres in vertical for men and are marked by widely spaced gates, requiring few turns per unit distance. Skiers adopt an aerodynamic tuck, absorb jumps and compressions, and use long, stiff skis of at least 218 cm for men with minimal sidecut to maximise stability at speed. Safety netting lines the course.

Events

Downhill is one of the original alpine events at the Winter Olympics and the FIS Alpine Ski World Cup, where the season's most consistent performer wins the downhill crystal globe. Famous courses include the Hahnenkamm at Kitzbühel and the Lauberhorn at Wengen. Unlike the technical events of slalom and giant slalom, each competitor makes a single run.
