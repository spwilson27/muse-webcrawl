Bernard John Taupin (born 22 May 1950) is an English lyricist and visual artist. He is best known for his songwriting partnership with Elton John, one of the most successful in music history, for whom he has written the lyrics to most of John's songs since 1967.

Partnership with Elton John

In 1967 Taupin answered a Liberty Records advertisement for songwriters in the New Musical Express; the label paired the 17-year-old lyricist with pianist Reginald Dwight (Elton John). Their working method became famous: Taupin writes lyrics first — often quickly, in longhand — and John sets them to music, the two rarely working in the same room. Their catalogue includes "Your Song", "Rocket Man", "Tiny Dancer", "Candle in the Wind", "Goodbye Yellow Brick Road", "Don't Let the Sun Go Down on Me" and "I'm Still Standing", spanning more than thirty albums and hundreds of millions of sales.

Other work

Taupin has written songs for other artists, including Starship's "We Built This City" (co-written, 1985) and Heart's "These Dreams" (with Martin Page), released solo albums, published poetry and a memoir, Scattershot (2023), and pursued a career as a visual artist in the United States, where he has lived since the 1970s. He also has a long involvement in horse racing and ranching in California.
