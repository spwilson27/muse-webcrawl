Distributed generation

Distributed generation, also called distributed energy or decentralised generation, refers to electricity generation and storage performed by a variety of small, grid-connected or distribution-system-connected devices referred to as distributed energy resources (DER). Conventional power stations, such as coal-fired, gas, and nuclear plants, are centralised and often require electricity to be transmitted over long distances; distributed resources generate power close to where it is used.

Technologies

Distributed energy resources include rooftop solar photovoltaic panels, small wind turbines, fuel cells, microturbines, reciprocating engines, and battery storage systems. Combined heat and power (cogeneration) units capture waste heat for local heating, raising overall efficiency.

Advantages and challenges

Proponents cite reduced transmission losses, improved reliability, lower peak demand on the grid, and easier integration of renewable energy. Challenges include voltage regulation, protection coordination, reverse power flows on distribution feeders designed for one-way flow, and the need for smart grid controls to coordinate very large numbers of devices.

Grid integration

Microgrids aggregate distributed resources so that a neighbourhood, campus, or facility can operate connected to the main grid or islanded from it during outages. Virtual power plants similarly coordinate fleets of DERs to provide grid services.
