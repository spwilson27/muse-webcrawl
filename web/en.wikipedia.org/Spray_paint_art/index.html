Spray paint art

Spray paint art, also known as graffiti art in some contexts, is an art form using spray paint, usually performed on poster board, wood, canvas or other non-porous surfaces, and distinct from graffiti performed on walls. It became especially popular with street performers in tourist areas, who create landscape, space and fantasy scenes rapidly before an audience.

Technique

Artists build images in layers, using lids, plates, torn paper and other objects as stencils and masks, scraping with palette knives or newspaper to create textures for planets, mountains and water. Because spray paint dries quickly, a piece can be finished in minutes.

History

The form is often associated with Mexico City street artists of the 1980s, such as Ruben Sadoval, and with performers at fairs and boardwalks worldwide. It is unrelated in intent, though not in medium, to the spray-can writing culture of graffiti.
