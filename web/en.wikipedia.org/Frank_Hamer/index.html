Francis Augustus Hamer (17 March 1884 – 10 July 1955) was an American law enforcement officer and Texas Ranger who led the 1934 posse that tracked down and killed the outlaws Bonnie Parker and Clyde Barrow. Renowned as a tracker and marksman, he is one of the most celebrated Rangers in Texas history.

Career

Hamer, who worked as a cowboy in his youth, joined the Rangers in 1906 and served intermittently for decades, patrolling the Mexican border during the era of Pancho Villa, enforcing Prohibition, and confronting the Ku Klux Klan. He was wounded several times and credited with surviving numerous gunfights. He also worked as a city marshal, a special investigator for cattlemen's associations, and briefly for the federal government.

Bonnie and Clyde

In February 1934, after retiring from the Rangers, Hamer was commissioned as a special investigator for the Texas prison system to stop Clyde Barrow, whose gang had humiliated the state in the Eastham prison breakout. Working with former Ranger Manny Gault and lawmen from Texas and Louisiana, Hamer studied Barrow's movements for over three months. On 23 May 1934 the posse ambushed Parker and Barrow on a rural road near Gibsland, Louisiana, killing both.

Legacy

Hamer returned to private security work and died in Austin in 1955. He was portrayed by Kevin Costner in the 2019 film The Highwaymen, which retold the Bonnie and Clyde hunt from the lawmen's perspective.
