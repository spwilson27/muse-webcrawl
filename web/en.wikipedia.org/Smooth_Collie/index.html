Smooth Collie

A sable and white Smooth Collie

The Smooth Collie is a breed of dog developed in the Scottish Highlands as a herding dog. It is the short-coated variety of the Collie, sharing its breed standard with the long-coated Rough Collie in most countries, including the United Kingdom; in the United States the two are registered as varieties of a single breed by the American Kennel Club. The Smooth Collie differs from its rough counterpart only in coat: short, dense and harsh-textured, lying flat against the body.

Temperament and use

Smooth Collies are intelligent, active and trainable herding dogs, generally friendly and good with children. Although now kept mostly as companions, they compete in herding, agility, obedience and tracking, and are sometimes used as assistance and search-and-rescue dogs. The short coat requires far less grooming than the Rough Collie's, which advocates cite as one of the variety's practical advantages.

Health

Like the Rough Collie, the Smooth Collie can be affected by collie eye anomaly, progressive retinal atrophy, and the MDR1 gene mutation, which causes sensitivity to certain drugs including ivermectin. Responsible breeders screen for these conditions. The variety is comparatively uncommon and has been classed as a vulnerable native breed in the UK in some years.
