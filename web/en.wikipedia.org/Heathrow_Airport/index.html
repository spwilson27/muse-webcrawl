Heathrow Airport (IATA: LHR) is the main international airport serving London, England, and the largest of the city's airports. Located about 23 km (14 miles) west of central London in the London Borough of Hillingdon, it is one of the busiest airports in the world by international passenger traffic and the primary hub for British Airways, with Virgin Atlantic also based there.

History

The airport began as a small airfield near the village of Heathrow in the 1930s and was requisitioned during the Second World War. It opened for civil aviation as London Airport on 1 January 1946 and was renamed Heathrow in 1966. Terminal 5 opened in 2008 and the rebuilt Terminal 2 in 2014. Proposals for a third runway have been debated for decades and remain politically contentious because of noise, air quality and climate concerns.

Operations

Heathrow has two parallel east–west runways and operates close to its permitted capacity of about 480,000 flights a year. It serves well over 200 destinations, with transatlantic routes to New York among the world's busiest international air corridors. The airport is connected to London by the Piccadilly line of the Underground, the Elizabeth line, the Heathrow Express, and the M4 and M25 motorways.
