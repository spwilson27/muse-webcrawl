Coherent population trapping

Coherent population trapping (CPT) is a quantum interference phenomenon in atomic physics in which atoms illuminated by two coherent laser fields are pumped into a superposition state that no longer absorbs the light — a "dark state". It arises in a three-level system, typically a lambda (Λ) configuration, where two lower states are coupled to a common excited state by two optical fields.

Mechanism

When the difference between the two laser frequencies matches the energy splitting of the two lower states (the two-photon or Raman resonance condition), the atoms accumulate in a particular superposition of the lower states whose interaction with the fields cancels by destructive interference. The medium then becomes transparent at the resonance, producing a narrow dip in absorption — or, viewed the other way, a sharp spectral feature whose width can be far below the natural linewidth of the transition. CPT is closely related to electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT), in which one of the fields is much stronger than the other.

Applications

The extremely narrow CPT resonance is exploited in chip-scale atomic clocks, in which a vapour cell of rubidium or caesium atoms provides a stable frequency reference small and economical enough for portable devices. CPT also underlies sensitive magnetometers used in geophysics and medicine, and is studied in laser cooling (velocity-selective coherent population trapping, which can cool atoms below the recoil limit) and in quantum optics and quantum information research.
