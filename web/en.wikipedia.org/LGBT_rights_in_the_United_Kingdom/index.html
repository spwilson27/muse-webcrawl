LGBT rights in the United Kingdom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The rights of lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender people in the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland are among the most extensive in the world. Homosexual acts between men were partially decriminalised in England and Wales by the Sexual Offences Act 1967, following the Wolfenden Report of 1957; Scotland followed in 1980 and Northern Ireland in 1982.

Legal milestones

Key milestones include the equalisation of the age of consent at 16 in 2001; the repeal of Section 28 (which had banned the "promotion" of homosexuality by local authorities) in 2003; the Civil Partnership Act 2004, granting same-sex couples nearly identical rights to marriage; the Gender Recognition Act 2004; the Equality Act 2010, which protects against discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender reassignment; and the Marriage (Same Sex Couples) Act 2013, which introduced same-sex marriage in England and Wales, followed by Scotland in 2014 and Northern Ireland in 2020.

Present situation

LGBT people serve openly in the armed forces (since 2000), may adopt jointly, and are protected in employment and services. Remaining areas of debate include reform of gender recognition procedures, a proposed ban on conversion practices, and access to gender-related healthcare.
