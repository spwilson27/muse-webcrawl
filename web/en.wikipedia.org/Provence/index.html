Provence is a geographical region and historical province of south-eastern France, extending from the left bank of the lower Rhône westward to the Italian border and south to the Mediterranean Sea. It corresponds largely with the modern departments of Alpes-de-Haute-Provence, Bouches-du-Rhône, Var and Vaucluse, within the region of Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur. Its name derives from Latin Provincia, as it was the first Roman province established beyond the Alps.

History

The coast was colonised by Greek sailors from Phocaea, who founded Massalia (modern Marseille) around 600 BCE. Rome annexed the area in the 2nd century BCE, making it the first province of Transalpine Gaul — hence simply "the Province". Roman monuments survive at Arles, Orange and Nîmes, including amphitheatres, the theatre at Orange and the aqueduct of the Pont du Gard.

In the Middle Ages Provence passed between Frankish, Burgundian and local counts; in the 14th and 15th centuries its counts also ruled Naples. It was gradually incorporated into France, a process completed when it was formally annexed in 1486 after the death of René of Anjou's heir. The papal enclave of Avignon, seat of the popes in the 14th century, remained separate until 1791.

Geography and climate

Provence ranges from the Camargue wetlands of the Rhône delta and the limestone hills of the Alpilles and Luberon to the foothills of the southern Alps and Mont Ventoux. The climate is Mediterranean, famously windy in the Rhône valley because of the mistral, a cold dry north wind. Lavender, olives, vines and sunflowers are emblematic crops; the lavender fields around Valensole bloom in early summer.

Culture

The traditional language is Provençal, a variety of Occitan, celebrated by the 19th-century Félibrige movement of Frédéric Mistral, who won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904. The region inspired painters such as Van Gogh and Cézanne, and writers including Peter Mayle, whose A Year in Provence popularised it for English readers. Provençal cuisine features olive oil, garlic, herbs (herbes de Provence), ratatouille, bouillabaisse and tapenade.
