The Zulu Kingdom, sometimes referred to as the Zulu Empire, was a Southern African kingdom in what is now South Africa. The small Zulu clan of the Nguni people was transformed under King Shaka (reigned 1816–1828) into a powerful state through conquest and assimilation during the upheavals of the Mfecane. At its height in the early 19th century the kingdom controlled the coastal region between the Phongolo and Tugela rivers.

Zulu warriors of the kingdom, armed with the short stabbing spear (iklwa) and large war shields introduced under Shaka.

Military system

Shaka reorganised the army into regiments (amabutho) organised by age, replaced the long throwing spear with the short stabbing spear (iklwa) used with a large cowhide shield, and introduced close-order tactics, including the famous "buffalo horns" formation: a strong centre, two horns enveloping the enemy, and a reserve. This system let the Zulu overwhelm neighbouring chiefdoms and resist European encroachment for decades.

Later kings and the Anglo-Zulu War

Shaka was assassinated in 1828 and succeeded by Dingane, then Mpande, and then Cetshwayo. In 1879 British forces invaded Zululand, beginning the Anglo-Zulu War. The Zulu won a famous victory at the Battle of Isandlwana but were defeated at Ulundi later that year. The kingdom was broken into 13 chiefdoms, plunged into civil war, and its territory was annexed first to Natal (1897) and later incorporated into the Union of South Africa.

Legacy

The Zulu monarchy continues today as the traditional kingship within the South African province of KwaZulu-Natal, and the Zulu are the largest ethnic group in South Africa.
