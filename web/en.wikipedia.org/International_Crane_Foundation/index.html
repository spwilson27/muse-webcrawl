International Crane Foundation

The International Crane Foundation (ICF) is a non-profit conservation organisation dedicated to the conservation of cranes and the ecosystems, watersheds and flyways on which they depend. It was founded in 1973 by ornithologists Ron Sauey and George Archibald. Its world headquarters and visitor centre are in Baraboo, Wisconsin, United States, and the ICF maintains regional programmes in Asia and Africa, including a partnership with the Endangered Wildlife Trust in South Africa.

Work

The ICF is the only place in the world where all fifteen species of crane can be seen together. Its Crane City breeding centre houses the most important captive population of whooping cranes, one of the world's rarest birds, and the foundation played a major role in efforts to rebuild whooping crane numbers and to reintroduce birds into the wild in the eastern United States. It has also worked extensively on the conservation of the critically endangered Siberian crane, the red-crowned crane in East Asia, and the blue crane, wattled crane and grey crowned crane in Africa.

The organisation's approach combines captive breeding and reintroduction with field research, wetland protection, and community-based conservation in the regions where cranes live, from the crane breeding grounds of Siberia and the Korean Demilitarized Zone to the floodplains of Zambia.

History

Sauey and Archibald established the foundation on the Sauey family farm near Baraboo in 1973. Archibald famously spent three years living and dancing with a human-imprinted female whooping crane named Tex to encourage her to breed, an effort that succeeded in 1982. In 2023 the foundation marked its fiftieth anniversary. Its visitor centre reopened in 2021 after extensive renovation of its crane exhibits and trails.
