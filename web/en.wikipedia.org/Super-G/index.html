Super-G

Super giant slalom skiing, usually called super-G, is a racing discipline of alpine skiing that combines elements of the speed of downhill with the tighter turning of giant slalom. Along with downhill, it is regarded as one of the "speed events", in contrast to the "technical events" of giant slalom and slalom.

Course and rules

A super-G course is set with gates spaced more widely than in giant slalom but more closely than in downhill, requiring a minimum vertical drop of 400 metres for men and women. Competitors make a single run; unlike in downhill, there are no full training runs, only a one-hour course inspection, so skiers must partly memorise the course. Winning speeds commonly exceed 90 km/h.

History

The discipline was introduced to the World Cup calendar in the early 1980s and approved by the International Ski Federation in 1982. It became a World Championship event in 1987 and an Olympic event at the 1988 Winter Olympics in Calgary.
