Batak peoples

Total populationAbout 8–9 million (2010 census)
RegionsNorth Sumatra, Indonesia
LanguagesBatak languages (Toba, Karo, Simalungun, Pakpak, Angkola, Mandailing), Indonesian
ReligionChristianity (majority among Toba, Karo, Simalungun), Islam (majority among Mandailing, Angkola), traditional Parmalim

The Batak peoples are a collection of closely related Austronesian ethnic groups native to the highlands of North Sumatra, Indonesia, centred on Lake Toba. The term "Batak" embraces several groups speaking distinct but related languages: the Toba, Karo, Simalungun, Pakpak, Angkola, and Mandailing. Each group has its own traditions, clan (marga) system, and dialect, while sharing a common core of social organization built on patrilineal clans and the philosophy of Dalihan Na Tolu, the "three-legged hearth" governing relations between clan, affines, and wife-givers.

Culture

Traditional Batak architecture is famous for its massive wooden houses with soaring, saddle-backed roofs, built without nails and adorned with carved gables. Batak societies historically maintained their own Indic-derived script, used chiefly by ritual specialists (datu) for divination and magical texts written on bark books called pustaha. Music, weaving of ulos cloth, and wood carving are prominent arts. The Toba Batak around Lake Toba are well known for the HKBP (Protestant Christian Batak Church), one of the largest Lutheran denominations in Asia.

History

The Batak highlands were long isolated from coastal trade networks. In the 19th century, the Padri War brought Islam to the Mandailing and Angkola in the south, while German Rhenish missionaries, led by Ludwig Ingwer Nommensen from 1862, evangelized the Toba in the north. Dutch colonial rule was only extended over the interior after prolonged resistance, including the long guerrilla war of the Toba priest-king Sisingamangaraja XII, killed in 1907. After Indonesian independence, Batak migrants spread throughout Indonesia, and Batak individuals became prominent in the military, law, and politics.
