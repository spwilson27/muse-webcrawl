Kingston is a village and census-designated place in the town of South Kingstown, Washington County, Rhode Island, United States. Originally known as Little Rest, it is best known as the home of the University of Rhode Island, whose main campus occupies much of the village.

History

The settlement was called Little Rest in the colonial era. Tradition holds that during King Philip’s War (1675–76) colonial forces rested there before the Great Swamp Fight against the Narragansett, and the name Kingston was adopted later. The village preserves a notable collection of eighteenth- and nineteenth-century buildings as the Kingston Village Historic District.

University of Rhode Island

The institution that became the University of Rhode Island was founded in Kingston in 1892 as the Rhode Island College of Agriculture and the Mechanic Arts. The campus, built around a central quadrangle of granite buildings, dominates village life; the village’s Kingston station on Amtrak’s Northeast Corridor serves both the university and southern Rhode Island.
