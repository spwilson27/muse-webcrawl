The State University System of Florida (SUSF or SUS) is a system of twelve public universities in the U.S. state of Florida. As of recent years it enrolls more than 430,000 students, making it one of the largest public university systems in the United States.

Governance

The system is governed by the Florida Board of Governors, a seventeen-member body created by a 2002 constitutional amendment, most of whose members are appointed by the Governor of Florida. Each university is additionally overseen by its own board of trustees. The Chancellor of the State University System serves as the system's chief executive.

Member institutions

The twelve member universities are the University of Florida, Florida State University, Florida A&M University, the University of South Florida, Florida Atlantic University, Florida International University, the University of Central Florida, the University of North Florida, the University of West Florida, Florida Polytechnic University, New College of Florida, and Florida Gulf Coast University.

Rankings and research

The system is noted for high performance in state metrics-based funding and for the University of Florida's membership in the Association of American Universities, joined by the University of South Florida in 2023. U.S. News & World Report has repeatedly ranked Florida's public university system No. 1 in the nation.
