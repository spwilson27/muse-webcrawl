John C. Frémont

John Charles Frémont (January 21, 1813 – July 13, 1890) was an American explorer, military officer and politician. Nicknamed "The Pathfinder," he led celebrated expeditions mapping the American West in the 1840s, played a leading role in the conquest of California during the Mexican–American War, and in 1856 became the first presidential candidate of the new Republican Party.


John C. Frémont

Explorations

Frémont led five expeditions into the West between 1842 and 1853, guided on several by Kit Carson. His vivid reports, prepared with his wife Jessie Benton Frémont, publicised the Oregon Trail, the Great Basin (a term he coined) and California, and became essential guides for emigrants.

California and politics

During the Mexican–American War, Frémont led the California Battalion and accepted the surrender of Mexican forces in northern California; his quarrel over authority with General Stephen Kearny led to a court-martial and his resignation from the Army. Briefly one of California's first U.S. Senators, he ran for President in 1856 on the Republican slogan "Free Soil, Free Men, and Frémont," losing to James Buchanan.

Civil War

As Union commander of the Department of the West, Frémont issued an unauthorised emancipation order in Missouri in 1861, which President Lincoln revoked; he was removed and later resigned after being placed under a junior officer. He lost much of his fortune in railroad ventures and served as Governor of Arizona Territory (1878–1881).
