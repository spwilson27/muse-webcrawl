Orthopoxvirus

Orthopoxvirus is a genus of viruses in the family Poxviridae and the subfamily Chordopoxvirinae. Humans, mammals and arthropods serve as natural hosts. There are twelve species in the genus, including the type species Variola virus, the causative agent of smallpox.

Known species

Recognised species include variola virus (smallpox), vaccinia virus, cowpox virus, monkeypox virus (the cause of mpox), camelpox virus, ectromelia virus, raccoonpox virus, skunkpox virus, taterapox virus, volepox virus, uasin gishu virus and abatin macacapox virus.

Structure

Orthopoxvirus virions are generally enveloped and brick-shaped, measuring roughly 200 by 250 nanometres. Their genomes are linear double-stranded DNA of about 170–250 kilobases, encoding around 200 genes. The replication cycle takes place entirely in the cytoplasm of the host cell, unusual among DNA viruses.

Disease and immunity

Several orthopoxviruses infect humans. Smallpox was eradicated worldwide in 1980 following vaccination with the related vaccinia virus, which confers cross-protective immunity across the genus. Mpox, caused by monkeypox virus, produces a rash illness and became the subject of a global outbreak beginning in 2022. Cowpox is usually a mild, localised infection acquired from animals.

History

The genus has been studied since Edward Jenner's 1796 demonstration that cowpox inoculation protected against smallpox. Vaccinia, the vaccine virus, became one of the most intensively studied model viruses and a vector for recombinant vaccines.
