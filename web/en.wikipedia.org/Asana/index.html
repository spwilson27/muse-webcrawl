Asana

An asana (Sanskrit: आसन, āsana, "seat") is a body posture, originally and still a general term for a sitting meditation pose, and later extended in hatha yoga and modern yoga to any type of position — standing, inverted, balancing, twisting, or reclining — in which the practitioner sits down. The Yoga Sutras of Patanjali define asana as a position that is "steady and comfortable".

History

The earliest asanas were seated meditation postures such as Padmasana (lotus pose). Medieval hatha yoga texts, including the Hatha Yoga Pradipika (15th century), describe 15 asanas, mostly seated. Over subsequent centuries the repertoire expanded; the Gheranda Samhita mentions 32 useful asanas and the Shiva Samhita 84. In the 20th century, teachers such as Tirumalai Krishnamacharya, B. K. S. Iyengar, and K. Pattabhi Jois developed dynamic systems with hundreds of poses, including standing and sun salutation sequences.

In modern yoga

In contemporary yoga as exercise, asanas are the central component of practice, often combined with breathing techniques (pranayama) and meditation. Well-known asanas include Adho Mukha Svanasana (downward-facing dog), Trikonasana (triangle pose), Virabhadrasana (warrior poses), and Savasana (corpse pose), used for final relaxation.
