Ganymede

Ganymede is a natural satellite of Jupiter and the largest and most massive moon in the Solar System. It is the ninth-largest object in the Solar System, larger than the planet Mercury (though less massive), and the only moon known to possess an internal magnetic field. Ganymede orbits Jupiter in roughly seven days and is one of the four Galilean moons discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610.

Physical characteristics

Ganymede has a mean radius of about 2,634 km. It is composed of roughly equal amounts of silicate rock and water ice. Its surface divides into dark, heavily cratered regions and lighter grooved terrain formed by tectonic activity. The moon is believed to be fully differentiated, with a molten iron-rich core that generates its magnetic field, and it harbours a subsurface saltwater ocean that may contain more water than all of Earth's surface oceans combined.

Atmosphere and exploration

Ganymede has a thin oxygen atmosphere and an ionosphere, first detected by the Hubble Space Telescope. The moon was studied by the Pioneer and Voyager probes and extensively by the Galileo orbiter in the 1990s. The European Space Agency's JUICE spacecraft, launched in 2023, is en route to Jupiter and is planned to enter orbit around Ganymede in the 2030s, which would make it the first spacecraft to orbit a moon other than Earth's.
