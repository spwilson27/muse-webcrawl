Linear span

In mathematics, the linear span (also called the linear hull) of a set of vectors in a vector space is the set of all linear combinations of those vectors. It is the smallest linear subspace containing the set: equivalently, it is the intersection of all subspaces containing the set. The span of an empty set is the zero subspace {0}.

Definition

The span of vectors v₁, …, vk consists of all vectors of the form a₁v₁ + … + akvk, where the ai are scalars from the ground field. A set of vectors is said to span a space if its span equals the whole space. A spanning set that is linearly independent is a basis, and every vector in the space then has a unique expression as a combination of basis vectors.

Examples

In three-dimensional real space, the span of two non-parallel vectors is the plane through the origin containing both; the span of a single nonzero vector is the line through the origin in its direction. The standard unit vectors e₁, e₂, e₃ span all of R³. In the space of polynomials, monomials span the polynomial space.
