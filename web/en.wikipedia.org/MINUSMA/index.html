The United Nations Multidimensional Integrated Stabilization Mission in Mali (French: Mission multidimensionnelle intégrée des Nations Unies pour la stabilisation au Mali, MINUSMA) was a United Nations peacekeeping mission in Mali, established by Security Council Resolution 2100 on 25 April 2013 to stabilise the country after the Tuareg rebellion and the 2012–2013 northern Mali conflict, and to support the political process and the implementation of the 2015 Algiers peace agreement.

History

MINUSMA took over from the African-led AFISMA force on 1 July 2013, following the French military intervention Operation Serval that had halted armed groups' advance on Bamako. The mission was mandated to protect civilians, support the extension of state authority, promote human rights, and assist humanitarian delivery.

Operations and challenges

The mission operated in a dangerous asymmetric environment; attacks by jihadist armed groups made MINUSMA one of the deadliest UN peacekeeping missions, with more than 300 peacekeepers killed over its decade of deployment. Its bases in Gao, Kidal, Timbuktu, and Ménaka were repeatedly attacked with improvised explosive devices and indirect fire.

Termination

Following deteriorating relations with Mali's transitional military authorities, Mali's foreign minister demanded the mission's withdrawal in June 2023. On 30 June 2023 the Security Council adopted Resolution 2690, terminating the mandate and ordering an orderly withdrawal, which MINUSMA completed by 31 December 2023, handing over or closing its bases across the country.
