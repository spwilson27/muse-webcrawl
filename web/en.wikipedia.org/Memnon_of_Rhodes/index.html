Memnon of Rhodes

Memnon of Rhodes (c. 380 – 333 BC) was a Greek commander from Rhodes who served the Persian Empire during the early years of Alexander the Great's conquest. He and his brother Mentor were mercenary captains in Persian service; through Mentor's influence at court, Memnon rose to command Persian forces in western Asia Minor.

Career

Memnon had earlier been exiled and spent time at the Macedonian court of Philip II, giving him firsthand knowledge of Macedonian methods. When Alexander invaded in 334 BC, Memnon advised the Persian satraps to adopt a scorched-earth policy and fight at sea and in Alexander's rear, but the satraps rejected his counsel and fought at the Granicus, where they were defeated. Memnon then organised the defence of Halicarnassus and, appointed supreme commander in the Aegean, began capturing islands such as Chios and Lesbos and threatened to carry the war into Greece and cut Alexander's supply lines.

Death

In 333 BC, while besieging Mytilene, Memnon fell ill and died. His death removed Persia's most effective commander in the west; his widow Barsine later became Alexander's mistress. Ancient sources regarded Memnon as the ablest general opposed to Alexander in the early campaigns.
