Loch Oich is a freshwater loch in the Great Glen of the Scottish Highlands, lying between Loch Lochy to the south-west and Loch Ness to the north-east. Together with those larger lochs it forms part of the chain of waters linked by Thomas Telford's Caledonian Canal, and its surface—around 32 metres above sea level—is the summit level of the canal.

Geography and the canal

The loch is fed principally by the River Garry, which enters at its south-western end near Invergarry, and drains north-east through the River Oich, which flows past Fort Augustus into Loch Ness. When the Caledonian Canal was constructed in the early 19th century, the loch's level was raised and its channel deepened so that vessels could pass directly through it between Laggan Locks (toward Loch Lochy) and Cullochy and Kytra locks (toward Fort Augustus). The canal company also straightened sections of shore. The A82 road and the Great Glen Way long-distance path run along its eastern shore.

History

The loch's shores saw clan conflict during the 17th century. Near its northern end stands the Well of the Seven Heads (Tobar nan Ceann), a monument commemorating the 1665 "Keppoch murders" justice episode, in which the heads of seven men involved in the murder of the young Keppoch chief and his brother were washed in the spring before being presented to MacDonald of Glengarry. The ruined Invergarry Castle, a former seat of the MacDonells of Glengarry, overlooks the loch from its western shore; it was burned after the Jacobite rising of 1745 and later partly rebuilt as a hotel.
