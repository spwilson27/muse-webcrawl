Wyoming

Wyoming is a state in the Mountain West subregion of the Western United States. It is bordered by Montana to the north, South Dakota and Nebraska to the east, Idaho to the west, Utah to the southwest, and Colorado to the south. Wyoming is the least populous U.S. state, with a population of about 580,000, and the second most sparsely populated after Alaska. Its capital and most populous city is Cheyenne.

Geography

Wyoming's landscape is defined by the Rocky Mountains in the west and the High Plains in the east. The state contains most of Yellowstone National Park — established in 1872 as the world's first national park — and all of Grand Teton National Park. Major ranges include the Tetons, Wind River Range, Bighorn Mountains, and Absaroka Range. The Continental Divide runs through the state, and the Great Divide Basin forms an endorheic region in the centre-south.

History

The region was home to Indigenous peoples including the Shoshone, Arapaho, Crow, Lakota, and Cheyenne. Parts of Wyoming were acquired by the United States through the Louisiana Purchase (1803) and later treaties. Wyoming Territory was organised in 1868; in 1869 it became the first jurisdiction in the world to grant women the right to vote on equal terms with men, earning it the nickname "Equality State". Wyoming entered the Union as the 44th state on 10 July 1890.

Economy

Wyoming's economy is dominated by mineral extraction — it is the leading U.S. producer of coal and a major producer of oil, natural gas, trona, and uranium — together with agriculture, especially cattle and sheep ranching, and tourism drawn to its national parks.

Demographics

With fewer than six people per square mile, Wyoming has the second-lowest population density of any state. Its largest cities are Cheyenne, Casper, Laramie, and Gillette.
