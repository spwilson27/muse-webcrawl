Poliovirus

Virus classification

Realm: Riboviria
Family: Picornaviridae
Genus: Enterovirus
Species: Enterovirus C

Poliovirus, the causative agent of polio (also known as poliomyelitis), is a serotype of the species Enterovirus C, in the family of Picornaviridae. There are three poliovirus serotypes: types 1, 2, and 3.

Poliovirus is composed of an RNA genome and a protein capsid. The genome is a single-stranded positive-sense RNA (+ssRNA) genome that is about 7500 nucleotides long. The viral particle is about 30 nm in diameter with icosahedral symmetry. Because of its short genome and its simple composition—only RNA and a nonenveloped icosahedral protein coat that encapsulates it—poliovirus is widely regarded as the simplest significant virus.

Transmission and disease

Poliovirus infects human cells by binding to the CD155 receptor (the poliovirus receptor) on the cell membrane. It is transmitted primarily by the faecal–oral route and replicates in the gastrointestinal tract; in a small proportion of cases it invades the central nervous system and destroys motor neurons, causing flaccid paralysis. Most infections are asymptomatic or cause only mild illness.

Eradication

Following the development of the polio vaccines by Jonas Salk (inactivated vaccine) and Albert Sabin (oral live-attenuated vaccine), the Global Polio Eradication Initiative, launched in 1988, has reduced wild poliovirus cases by over 99%. Wild poliovirus type 2 was declared eradicated in 2015 and type 3 in 2019; type 1 remains endemic only in Afghanistan and Pakistan, though circulating vaccine-derived poliovirus outbreaks occur occasionally elsewhere.
