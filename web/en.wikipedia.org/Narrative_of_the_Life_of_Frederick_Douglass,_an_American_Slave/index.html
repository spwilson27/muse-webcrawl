Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, an American Slave

AuthorFrederick Douglass
Published1845
PublisherAmerican Anti-Slavery Society, Boston
GenreSlave narrative, memoir

Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, an American Slave is a memoir and abolitionist treatise published in 1845 by Frederick Douglass, who had escaped from slavery in Maryland. It is the best-known of the slave narratives written by fugitive slaves, and is credited with helping to advance the abolitionist cause in the United States and Britain.

Contents

Douglass recounts his childhood on Maryland plantations, his removal to Baltimore, where Sophia Auld secretly began teaching him to read before her husband forbade it, and his realisation that literacy was "the pathway from slavery to freedom." He describes brutal overseers — notably Edward Covey, with whom the sixteen-year-old Douglass physically fought back — and his eventual escape to the North in 1838. Prefaces by William Lloyd Garrison and Wendell Phillips vouch for the account's authenticity.

Reception and impact

The book sold thousands of copies within months and was translated into French and Dutch. Because Douglass named real people and places, the Narrative increased his risk of recapture; supporters in Britain raised funds to purchase his legal freedom in 1846. Douglass later expanded the work in My Bondage and My Freedom (1855) and Life and Times of Frederick Douglass (1881).
