Exodus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Exodus (from Greek exodos, "a going out" or "departure"), sometimes called the Exodus, is the founding myth of the Israelites, central to the Book of Exodus in the Hebrew Bible and retold annually at the Jewish festival of Passover. It describes the enslavement of the Israelites in ancient Egypt, their liberation under the leadership of Moses, and their journey through the wilderness to Mount Sinai, where they receive the Law from God, before ultimately entering the Promised Land of Canaan.

The biblical narrative

According to the Book of Exodus, the descendants of Jacob had settled in Egypt and multiplied, until a Pharaoh who "did not know Joseph" enslaved them. Moses, born to Hebrew parents but raised in Pharaoh's court, fled to Midian after killing an Egyptian overseer, and there God called him from the Burning Bush to lead his people out. When Pharaoh refused to let the Israelites go, God inflicted the Ten Plagues on Egypt, culminating in the death of the firstborn, from which the Israelites were spared — the origin of the Passover festival. Pharaoh released them, then pursued them; at the crossing of the Red Sea (or Sea of Reeds), the waters were parted for the Israelites and closed over the Egyptian army. In the wilderness, the people received manna and water, and at Mount Sinai Moses received the Ten Commandments and the covenant law, while the Ark of the Covenant and the Tabernacle were constructed.

Historical questions

The historicity of the Exodus is debated. Mainstream archaeology has found no material evidence of a mass migration of the scale described, and most scholars date the composition of the narrative to the first millennium BCE, possibly preserving memories of smaller movements, Egyptian imperial control of Canaan, or older traditions reshaped in the Babylonian exile. The story nonetheless became the paradigmatic account of liberation in Jewish, Christian, and later political traditions, and the term "exodus" has passed into general use for any large departure or emigration of people.
