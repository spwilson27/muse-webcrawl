Emerald

Emerald is a gemstone and a variety of the mineral beryl (Be3Al2(SiO3)6) coloured green by trace amounts of chromium or sometimes vanadium. Beryl has a hardness of 7.5–8 on the Mohs scale. Most emeralds are highly included, so their toughness (resistance to breakage) is classified as generally poor; their internal features, sometimes called the emerald's jardin ("garden"), are characteristic of the stone.

Sources

Colombia is by far the world's largest producer of emeralds, known for stones of fine, slightly bluish-green colour; other significant sources include Zambia, Brazil, Afghanistan and Ethiopia. Emeralds were mined in Egypt as early as 1500 BC, and the stone was prized in antiquity and by the Inca and Aztec civilizations. Synthetic emeralds have been produced since the mid-20th century.
