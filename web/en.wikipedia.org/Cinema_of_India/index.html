The Cinema of India, consisting of films produced across India, has been a major point of reference for global culture since the 20th century. Indian cinema encompasses several regional film industries, the most widely known being the Hindi-language industry in Mumbai (popularly called Bollywood), along with Telugu (Tollywood), Tamil (Kollywood), Bengali, Kannada (Sandalwood), and Malayalam cinema.

India has been one of the world's largest producers of films by number of titles, releasing well over a thousand features a year. Indian films are screened throughout South Asia and in diaspora communities worldwide, with growing audiences in the Middle East, Africa, and — through streaming — globally.

History

The first Indian feature film, Dadasaheb Phalke's Raja Harishchandra (1913), drew on Hindu mythology. The first Indian talkie, Alam Ara (1931), established the song-and-dance format that remains characteristic of popular Indian cinema. The post-independence "Golden Age" of the 1950s saw internationally acclaimed work by Satyajit Ray, whose Pather Panchali (1955) won worldwide recognition, alongside the popular successes of Raj Kapoor, Guru Dutt, and Mehboob Khan's Mother India (1957).

Regional industries

South Indian industries — Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, and Malayalam — produce a majority of India's films in recent years, and pan-Indian blockbusters such as Baahubali (2015–2017) and RRR (2022) achieved unprecedented nationwide and overseas success. Bengali cinema, centred in Kolkata, has a distinguished parallel-cinema tradition associated with Ray, Ritwik Ghatak, and Mrinal Sen.

Music and form

Music is integral to Indian popular cinema; most mainstream films include song sequences, and playback singers are major stars in their own right. Melodrama, family drama, romance, action, and comedy are frequently blended in the "masala" format.
