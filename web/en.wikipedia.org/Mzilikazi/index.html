Mzilikazi, founder of the Ndebele kingdom in what is now Zimbabwe.

Mzilikazi (c. 1790 – 9 September 1868), also spelled Mzilikazi Khumalo, was a Southern African king who founded the Ndebele (Matabele) kingdom in the region of present-day southwestern Zimbabwe. Originally a lieutenant of Shaka, king of the Zulu, he broke away in the early 1820s during the upheavals of the Mfecane and led his Khumalo followers on a long migration.

Migration and kingdom

After defying Shaka, Mzilikazi fled north, settling first in the Marico valley of the Transvaal, where he built a powerful state modelled partly on Zulu military organisation and clashed repeatedly with Griqua, Korana and Voortrekker parties; in 1837 Boer commandos defeated his forces and drove him across the Limpopo. In Matabeleland he consolidated his kingdom — known to the British as the Matabele kingdom — ruling from settlements around present-day Bulawayo, incorporating many Shona communities and exacting tribute. He maintained diplomatic relations with the missionary Robert Moffat, his long-time friend, and with traders and hunters from the south.

Death and succession

Mzilikazi died in 1868 near Ingama after a succession dispute had already split his people. Following a period of conflict, his son Lobengula was installed as king in 1870 and ruled until the kingdom was destroyed in the First Matabele War of 1893–94.
