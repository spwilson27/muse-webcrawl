Gerhard Karl Erich Gentzen (24 November 1909 – 4 August 1945) was a German mathematician and logician. He made major contributions to the foundations of mathematics, including proof theory, especially natural deduction and sequent calculus. He died of starvation in a Czech prison camp shortly after the end of the Second World War, having been arrested in Prague.

Life

Gentzen was born in Greifswald and studied mathematics at the universities of Greifswald, Göttingen, Munich, and Berlin. He received his doctorate from the University of Göttingen in 1933 under Paul Bernays, though Hilbert was his intellectual mentor and he worked as Hilbert's assistant from 1934 to 1943. His habilitation thesis of 1936 gave the first consistency proof for elementary number theory. During the war he worked at the German Mathematical Institute at the University of Prague; he joined the Nazi Party and the SA, a step biographers attribute to career necessity and a degree of conformity with the regime. Arrested by Czech forces in May 1945, he died in custody that August.

Work

Gentzen's doctoral work introduced two formal systems that transformed logic: natural deduction, which models the way mathematicians actually reason with assumptions, and the sequent calculus, designed for metamathematical investigation. For the latter he proved the celebrated Hauptsatz (cut-elimination theorem), showing that every proof can be transformed into a cut-free ("analytic") proof — the foundational result of structural proof theory.

In 1936 Gentzen proved the consistency of Peano arithmetic using transfinite induction up to the ordinal ε0, a principle not provable within arithmetic itself — thus measuring exactly the proof-theoretic strength of arithmetic and founding ordinal analysis. The result respected Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, since the induction principle used lies outside the system whose consistency is proved.

Legacy

Gentzen's systems are now standard tools in logic, computer science (where natural deduction underlies the Curry–Howard correspondence and proof assistants), and philosophy. The Gentzen–Takeuti tradition of ordinal analysis remains a central programme in proof theory.
