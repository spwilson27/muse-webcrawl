Soviet–Japanese border conflicts

The Soviet–Japanese border conflicts, also known as the Soviet–Japanese border war, were a series of undeclared border clashes between the Soviet Union (and its client state Mongolia) and the Empire of Japan (and its puppet state Manchukuo) from 1932 to 1939, fought along the ill-defined borders of Manchuria and Mongolia.

Background

After Japan occupied Manchuria in 1931–32 and created the puppet state of Manchukuo, its Kwantung Army confronted Soviet and Mongolian forces along thousands of kilometres of disputed frontiers. Between 1932 and 1934 dozens of small incidents were recorded; these escalated as Japan probed Soviet strength and sought to determine the true border lines.

Major battles

The first large-scale engagement was the Battle of Lake Khasan (July–August 1938), fought near the junction of the Korean, Manchurian and Soviet borders after Soviet troops occupied disputed heights. After heavy fighting the Japanese withdrew and a ceasefire restored the status quo. Far larger was the Battle of Khalkhin Gol (May–September 1939), known in Japan as the Nomonhan Incident. What began as a dispute over the Mongolian–Manchukuo border escalated into a full-scale battle involving tens of thousands of troops, tanks and aircraft. In August 1939 Soviet and Mongolian forces under Georgy Zhukov encircled and destroyed much of the Japanese Sixth Army, inflicting severe casualties and convincing Japanese planners that expansion northward against the USSR was unpromising.

Consequences

The defeat at Khalkhin Gol pushed Japan toward its "strike south" strategy against the European colonies and the United States, and toward diplomacy with Moscow. The two powers signed the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact in April 1941, which kept the peace between them until the Soviet Union declared war on Japan in August 1945.
