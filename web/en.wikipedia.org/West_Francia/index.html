West Francia (Medieval Latin: Francia occidentalis) was the western part of the Carolingian Empire, created by the Treaty of Verdun in 843, which divided Charlemagne's empire among the three sons of Louis the Pious. It was ruled first by Charles the Bald and is generally regarded as the historical predecessor of the Kingdom of France.

History

The Treaty of Verdun (843) granted Charles the Bald the lands west of a line roughly following the Meuse, Saône and Rhône rivers. His brother Lothair I received Middle Francia, while Louis the German received East Francia, the forerunner of Germany. The western kingdom included Aquitaine, Brittany's marches, and the county of Flanders.

West Francia's kings struggled against Viking raids throughout the ninth century; the Treaty of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte (911) granted Normandy to the Norse leader Rollo in return for defense against further raids. Internally, royal authority weakened as great aristocratic families — the Robertians, the counts of Flanders, Anjou, and Blois — built hereditary principalities.

In 987, on the death of the last Carolingian king, Louis V, the magnates elected Hugh Capet, founding the Capetian dynasty and marking the conventional end of West Francia and the beginning of the Kingdom of France.
