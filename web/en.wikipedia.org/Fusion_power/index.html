Fusion power

Fusion power is a proposed form of power generation that would produce electricity by using heat from nuclear fusion reactions — the process that powers the Sun and stars, in which light atomic nuclei combine to form heavier nuclei, releasing energy.

Principles

The easiest fusion reaction to achieve is between the hydrogen isotopes deuterium and tritium, producing helium-4, a neutron, and 17.6 MeV of energy. Deuterium is abundant in seawater; tritium is bred from lithium. Reacting plasma must reach temperatures above 100 million kelvin and be confined long enough and at sufficient density to satisfy the Lawson criterion for net energy gain.

Approaches

The leading approach is magnetic confinement, especially the tokamak — a toroidal device using powerful magnetic fields — exemplified by the international ITER project under construction in France, designed to produce 500 MW of fusion power from 50 MW of heating. The stellarator (such as Wendelstein 7-X in Germany) is an alternative magnetic design. Inertial confinement compresses fuel pellets with lasers; in December 2022 the U.S. National Ignition Facility first achieved target energy gain greater than one.

Advantages and challenges

Fusion promises abundant fuel, no carbon emissions, no long-lived high-level waste, and inherent safety against meltdown. However, no device has yet delivered net electrical power, and challenges remain in plasma stability, neutron-resistant materials, tritium breeding, and cost. A wave of private companies since the 2010s has pursued compact and alternative designs with ambitious timelines for commercial plants.
