La Amistad

La Amistad ("The Friendship") was a 19th-century Spanish schooner famous for a slave revolt in 1839, when kidnapped Mende Africans led by Sengbe Pieh (Joseph Cinqué) seized the ship off Cuba after being illegally transported across the Atlantic.

The revolt and trial

The Africans killed the captain and cook and ordered the surviving crew to sail back to Africa, but the Spaniards steered north at night, and the ship was seized by a US revenue cutter off Long Island. A celebrated legal case followed: the Africans were defended by abolitionists, with former president John Quincy Adams arguing before the US Supreme Court, which in United States v. The Amistad (1841) ruled the Africans were free people who had acted in self-defence.

Legacy

The survivors returned to Sierra Leone in 1842. The case became an emblem of the abolitionist movement and inspired Steven Spielberg's 1997 film Amistad; a replica vessel, launched in 2000, sails as a goodwill and educational ship.
