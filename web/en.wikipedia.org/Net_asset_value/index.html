Net asset value

Net asset value (NAV) is the value of an entity's assets minus the value of its liabilities, often in relation to the value of a fund's shares. The term is most commonly used for mutual funds, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), hedge funds, and other pooled investment vehicles, where it represents the per-share (or per-unit) price at which investors buy and sell.

NAV per share = (Total assets − Total liabilities) / Shares outstanding

Mutual funds

Open-end mutual funds calculate NAV once each business day, typically after the close of U.S. markets at 4:00 p.m. Eastern Time, valuing the fund's securities at market prices and dividing the net total by the number of shares outstanding. Orders placed during the day are executed at that day's closing NAV ("forward pricing"). A fund's NAV per share changes daily with the market value of its portfolio; total investment return, however, must also account for distributions of income and capital gains.

Closed-end funds and ETFs

Closed-end funds trade on exchanges at market prices that may stand at a premium or discount to their NAV, a persistent feature studied in finance. ETFs, by contrast, have an arbitrage mechanism through authorised participants that keeps their market price close to NAV, though small premiums and discounts still occur. For real-estate investment trusts and private funds, NAV depends on appraisals of illiquid assets and is calculated less frequently, sometimes quarterly.
