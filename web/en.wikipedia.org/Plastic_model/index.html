Plastic model

A finished 1:48 scale model aircraft.

A plastic model is a scale model manufactured from plastic, most commonly sold as an injection-moulded kit for assembly by hobbyists. Plastic model kits are available for subjects including aircraft, military vehicles, ships, automobiles, spacecraft, buildings, and figures. The hobby of building them, sometimes called plastic modelling, grew rapidly after the Second World War and remains popular worldwide.

History

The first plastic models were produced in the 1930s by companies such as Frog in the United Kingdom, using cellulose acetate. After the war, injection moulding in polystyrene made cheap, sharply detailed kits possible. British firm Airfix released its first kit, a tractor, in 1949 and a model of the Golden Hind in 1952; American companies including Revell, Monogram, and Aurora, and later Japanese firms such as Tamiya and Hasegawa, expanded the market. By the 1960s and 1970s plastic kits were a mass-market toy and hobby staple.

Kits and construction

Parts are supplied attached to frames called sprues or runners. The builder removes the parts, cleans up mould lines and ejector-pin marks, and cements them with plastic solvent cement, which welds the polystyrene. Models are typically painted, often with an airbrush, and finished with water-slide decals for markings. Advanced builders add aftermarket detail parts in photo-etched metal or resin, scratch-build components, and apply weathering techniques to simulate wear, dirt, and battle damage.

Scales

Common scales include 1:72 and 1:48 for aircraft, 1:35 for military vehicles, 1:24 and 1:25 for cars, and 1:350 and 1:700 for ships. Standardised scales allow models of different subjects to be displayed consistently together. Figure modelling often uses millimetre heights (e.g. 54 mm) instead of ratios.

Manufacturers and community

Major manufacturers include Airfix, Revell, Tamiya, Bandai (whose snap-fit Gundam kits are among the best-selling model lines in the world), Italeri, Trumpeter, and Eduard. The International Plastic Modellers' Society (IPMS), founded in Britain in 1963, organises chapters, conventions, and competitions internationally.
