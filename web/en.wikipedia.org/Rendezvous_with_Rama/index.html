Rendezvous with Rama

Rama, a vast cylindrical alien vessel, as imagined entering the Solar System.

Rendezvous with Rama is a science fiction novel by British author Arthur C. Clarke, first published in 1973. Set in the 2130s, the story involves a 50-kilometre-long cylindrical alien starship — dubbed "Rama" by humans — that enters the Solar System, and the crew of the survey ship Endeavour, sent to intercept and explore it before it swings around the Sun and departs.

Inside Rama the astronauts find a self-contained artificial world: a cylindrical sea, plains divided into geometric patterns, city-like clusters of structures, and, eventually, robotic "biots" that awaken as the ship approaches the Sun. The explorers map much of the interior but learn almost nothing about the Ramans themselves, and the novel ends with the ship leaving the Solar System and the reflection that "the Ramans do everything in threes" — a line that set up Clarke's later sequels, written with Gentry Lee.

Reception

The novel won both the Hugo Award and the Nebula Award for Best Novel, and is regarded as a classic of hard science fiction and a defining example of the "Big Dumb Object" subgenre. Its combination of rigorous extrapolation and cosmic mystery — the alien builders never appear — has influenced generations of writers and game designers.
