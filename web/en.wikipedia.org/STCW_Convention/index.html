STCW Convention

The International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers (STCW), adopted in 1978 by the International Maritime Organization (IMO), is the international treaty that sets minimum qualification standards for masters, officers and watch personnel on seagoing merchant ships.

The convention was the first to establish basic requirements on training, certification and watchkeeping for seafarers on an international level. Previously, standards were established individually by governments, usually without reference to practices in other countries, so standards and procedures varied widely. The STCW Convention entered into force on 28 April 1984.

History

The convention was adopted on 7 July 1978 and entered into force in 1984. It has been extensively revised since. The 1995 amendments, which entered into force on 1 February 1997, introduced a major overhaul, including a related STCW Code, quality standards systems, and expanded powers for the IMO to evaluate parties' compliance. The 2010 amendments, adopted in Manila, Philippines, are known as the "Manila Amendments" and entered into force on 1 January 2012.

Contents

The convention applies to seafarers serving on board seagoing ships entitled to fly the flag of a party, with certain exceptions such as warships and fishing vessels (the latter covered by the separate STCW-F Convention). It prescribes minimum standards relating to training, certification and watchkeeping, including rest hours for watchkeepers and requirements for certificates of competency and proficiency.
