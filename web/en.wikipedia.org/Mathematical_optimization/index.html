Mathematical optimization
Field: Applied mathematics
Problem: Minimize or maximize an objective function subject to constraints
Major classes: Linear, convex, nonlinear, integer, stochastic

Mathematical optimization (also spelled optimisation) is the selection of a best element, with regard to some criterion, from a set of available alternatives. In its simplest form, an optimization problem consists of maximizing or minimizing a real-valued objective function by systematically choosing input values from within an allowed (feasible) set defined by constraints:

minimize f(x) subject to gi(x) ≤ 0, hj(x) = 0

Optimization is foundational to operations research, engineering design, economics, logistics and machine learning.

Major subfields

Linear programming concerns linear objectives and constraints and is solvable in polynomial time; the simplex method of George Dantzig (1947) remains a workhorse. When the objective and feasible region are convex, the problem is a convex optimization problem, for which local optima are global and interior-point methods are efficient. Requiring variables to be integers yields integer programming, generally NP-hard but practically powerful through branch-and-bound. Other branches include quadratic programming, nonlinear programming, stochastic and robust optimization, combinatorial optimization, and global optimization.

Methods

For smooth unconstrained problems, gradient descent and Newton's method use first- and second-derivative information; the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions characterize optima of constrained problems. Where derivatives are unavailable or the landscape is rugged, derivative-free methods, simulated annealing, genetic algorithms and other metaheuristics are used. Training modern machine-learning models is an optimization problem in millions of variables, usually attacked with stochastic gradient descent and its variants (momentum, Adam).

History

The field grew from the calculus of variations of Euler and Lagrange, through linear programming developed during and after the Second World War, to the convex-optimization renaissance of the 1990s and today's large-scale computational practice, supported by solvers such as Gurobi, CPLEX and open-source alternatives.
