Barberton Greenstone Belt

The Barberton Greenstone Belt is an ancient geological structure in the Kaapvaal Craton of southeastern Africa, located in the Mpumalanga province of South Africa and extending into Eswatini. Situated in the Makhonjwa Mountains near the town of Barberton, it contains some of the oldest well-preserved rocks on Earth, dating from about 3.6 to 3.2 billion years ago (Paleoarchean era).

Geology and significance

The belt consists of sequences of volcanic and sedimentary rocks — including komatiites, basalts, cherts, and banded iron formations — that were later deformed and intruded by granitic plutons. Because of its exceptional preservation, the belt is a key site for studying the early Earth's crust, ocean chemistry, and the emergence of life. Its cherts contain some of the earliest evidence of life, including microfossils and stromatolites around 3.4 billion years old, and its sedimentary rocks record impacts of extraterrestrial bodies. The region was also the site of one of South Africa's earliest gold rushes in the 1880s. In 2018 the Barberton Makhonjwa Mountains were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
