QR decomposition

In linear algebra, a QR decomposition, also known as a QR factorization or QU factorization, is a decomposition of a matrix A into a product A = QR of an orthonormal matrix Q and an upper triangular matrix R. It is often used to solve the linear least squares problem and is the basis for the QR algorithm for computing eigenvalues.

Methods of computation

Three classical methods are used. The Gram–Schmidt process orthonormalizes the columns of A one at a time; a modified version improves numerical stability. Householder reflections zero out subdiagonal entries column by column using orthogonal reflectors and are the standard dense method in practice. Givens rotations zero out entries one at a time and are useful for sparse or structured matrices and for updating a factorization.

Existence and uniqueness

Every real square matrix has a QR decomposition. If A is invertible, the decomposition with positive diagonal entries in R is unique. For rectangular matrices A (m × n with m ≥ n), the "thin" or economy decomposition uses an m × n matrix Q with orthonormal columns.

Applications

QR decomposition solves overdetermined linear systems and least-squares problems by reducing Ax = b to the triangular system Rx = QTb. Iterating QR factorizations with shifts gives the QR algorithm, the standard method for computing all eigenvalues of a matrix. QR decomposition also generalizes to the Iwasawa decomposition of Lie groups.
