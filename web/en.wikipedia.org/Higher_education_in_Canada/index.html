Higher education in Canada

Higher education in Canada describes the system of post-secondary education provided by universities, colleges, and polytechnics across Canada's ten provinces and three territories. Responsibility for education is a provincial jurisdiction under the Constitution Act, 1867, so there is no federal department of education; the federal government supports the system mainly through research funding and transfer payments.

Universities

Canada has around 100 public universities, enrolling well over a million students. The largest include the University of Toronto, the University of British Columbia, and the Université de Montréal; McGill University in Montreal is noted for its large international enrolment. Universities grant bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees, and most are members of Universities Canada. Degree quality is overseen provincially, so there is no national accreditation system comparable to that of the United States.

Colleges and polytechnics

Public colleges — known as cégeps in Quebec — offer diplomas, certificates, applied degrees, and apprenticeships. Quebec's system is distinctive: students complete two years of pre-university or three years of technical study at a cégep after grade 11 before proceeding to a three-year university bachelor's degree. Colleges have expanded rapidly in applied research and degree-granting programs.

Funding and students

Canadian post-secondary education is publicly funded to a greater degree than in the United States, though tuition fees have risen steadily, averaging several thousand dollars per year for domestic undergraduates and far more for international students, who make up a large and growing share of enrolment. Canada has among the highest rates of tertiary educational attainment in the OECD, with a majority of adults aged 25–64 holding a college or university credential.
