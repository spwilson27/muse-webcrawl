613 commandments

The 613 commandments (Hebrew: taryag mitzvot) are the commandments (mitzvot) traditionally counted in the Torah in Jewish tradition. The number derives from a teaching of Rabbi Simlai preserved in the Talmud (Makkot 23b): 365 prohibitions, matching the days of the solar year, and 248 positive commandments, matching the limbs and organs of the human body.

Maimonides' enumeration

The most influential list is that of Maimonides (Rambam), set out in his Sefer Hamitzvot ("Book of the Commandments"), in which he first stated fourteen principles for deciding which statements count as separate commandments before enumerating all 613. His list was criticized and defended in turn — notably by Nachmanides — and remains the standard reference, printed in many editions of the Pentateuch.

Content

The commandments cover worship, the Temple service and sacrifices, ritual purity, dietary laws (kashrut), festivals, family life, charity, agriculture, civil and criminal law, and relations with others. Many — such as those concerning the Temple, sacrifices, and the agricultural laws of the Land of Israel — cannot be fully performed in the absence of the Temple in Jerusalem, a fact reflected in later abridged works such as the Sefer ha-Chinuch and modern compendia that organize the 613 by subject and applicability.
