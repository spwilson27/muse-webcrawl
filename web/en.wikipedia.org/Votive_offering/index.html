Votive offering

A votive offering is one or more objects displayed or deposited, without the intention of recovery or use, in a sacred place for broadly religious purposes. Such items are a feature of many societies, ancient and modern, and are often made in fulfilment of a vow or in gratitude for a favour granted — the latter being called an ex-voto when it commemorates a specific event such as recovery from illness or survival of an accident.

PurposesPetition, vow fulfilment, thanksgiving
Common formsStatuettes, plaques, candles, models of body parts, coins

History and examples

In the ancient world, worshippers dedicated statues, miniature weapons, jewellery, and anatomical models of healed body parts at Greek and Roman sanctuaries such as those of Asclepius. Wealth deposited in sacred contexts — from Bronze Age hoards in bogs and rivers to the treasuries of Delphi — explains many major archaeological finds. Inscriptions often record the vow explicitly in the formula votum solvit libens merito ("paid the vow willingly and deservedly").

The practice continues across religions: candles and milagros in Catholic shrines, painted ex-voto tablets in Mexico, votive stupas and tablets in Buddhism, ema plaques at Shinto shrines, and coins thrown into wells and fountains. Curators and archaeologists value votive deposits because, unlike refuse, they were deliberately selected and preserved, offering direct evidence of religious belief and practice.
