Wildlife conservation

Wildlife conservation refers to the practice of protecting wild species and their habitats in order to maintain healthy wildlife populations and restore, protect or enhance natural ecosystems. Major threats to wildlife include habitat destruction, degradation and fragmentation, overexploitation, poaching, pollution, climate change and invasive species. The IUCN estimates that tens of thousands of assessed species are threatened with extinction.

History

The modern conservation movement emerged in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, driven by figures such as John Muir, Theodore Roosevelt and Aldo Leopold, and by the creation of protected areas such as Yellowstone National Park (1872). In the United States, the Lacey Act (1900), the Migratory Bird Treaty Act (1918) and the Endangered Species Act (1973) established foundational legal frameworks; internationally, CITES (1973) regulates trade in wild species, and the Convention on Biological Diversity (1992) set global goals for protecting ecosystems.

Methods

Conservation practice combines in-situ protection — protected areas, habitat restoration, wildlife corridors and reintroduction of species such as wolves to Yellowstone — with ex-situ measures including captive breeding, seed banks and genome resource banks. Population monitoring, anti-poaching enforcement, environmental education and working with local communities are also central. Conservation biology supplies the scientific basis, including concepts such as minimum viable population and biodiversity hotspots.

Organisations

Major organisations include the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN), which maintains the Red List of Threatened Species, the World Wildlife Fund (WWF), Conservation International, the Wildlife Conservation Society and Fauna & Flora International, alongside government agencies and innumerable local groups.

Challenges

Conservation faces persistent funding gaps, conflicts between wildlife and human livelihoods (such as crop raiding and livestock predation), illegal wildlife trade, and the accelerating effects of climate change, which shifts species ranges faster than many can adapt.
