Date4 April – 22 June 1944

The Battle of Kohima was the turning point of the Japanese U-Go offensive into India in 1944 during the Second World War. The battle took place in three stages from 4 April to 22 June 1944 around the town of Kohima, the capital of Nagaland in northeast India.

Background

The Japanese 31st Division under Lieutenant General Kōtoku Satō advanced to cut the road between Imphal and Dimapur and isolate the British IV Corps. The small Kohima garrison — British, Indian and Nepalese troops under Colonel Hugh Richards — held the ridge above the town against repeated assaults, at one point fighting across the deputy commissioner's tennis court.

The fighting

Relieved by the British 2nd Division in mid-April, Allied forces fought to clear the ridge and reopen the road to Imphal, finally achieved on 22 June 1944. Starving and out of supplies, the Japanese withdrew, suffering catastrophic losses during the retreat.

Legacy

Together with the simultaneous Battle of Imphal, Kohima was voted Britain's greatest battle in a 2013 poll by the National Army Museum. The Kohima Epitaph at the war cemetery reads: "When you go home, tell them of us and say, for your tomorrow, we gave our today."
