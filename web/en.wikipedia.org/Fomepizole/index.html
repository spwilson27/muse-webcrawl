Fomepizole

Trade nameAntizol
Other name4-methylpyrazole
FormulaC4H6N2
RoutesIntravenous
UseAntidote for methanol and ethylene glycol poisoning

Fomepizole, also known as 4-methylpyrazole, is a medication used to treat poisoning by methanol and ethylene glycol. It may be used alone or together with hemodialysis, and is given by injection into a vein. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

Fomepizole is indicated for suspected or confirmed methanol or ethylene glycol poisoning, typically evidenced by an elevated osmolar gap, metabolic acidosis, visual symptoms (in methanol poisoning), or urinary calcium oxalate crystals (in ethylene glycol poisoning). Early administration prevents the accumulation of the toxic metabolites responsible for blindness, kidney failure, and death.

Mechanism of action

Fomepizole is a competitive inhibitor of the enzyme alcohol dehydrogenase, which catalyses the first step in the metabolism of ethylene glycol and methanol. By blocking this enzyme, fomepizole slows the production of glycolic acid, glyoxylic acid, and oxalic acid from ethylene glycol, and of formic acid from methanol, allowing the parent compounds to be excreted or removed by dialysis before serious injury occurs.

History and society

Fomepizole was approved for medical use in the United States in 1997. It largely replaced ethanol as the preferred antidote for toxic alcohol poisoning in many countries because it does not cause intoxication or hypoglycaemia and is easier to dose, although it is considerably more expensive per dose than ethanol.
