Béla IV of Hungary

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Béla IV (29 November 1206 – 3 May 1270) was King of Hungary and Croatia from 1235 to 1270, and Duke of Styria from 1254 to 1258. The son of Andrew II, he spent much of his reign first trying to reverse his father's grants of royal land, then, after the catastrophe of the Mongol invasion of 1241–42, rebuilding the kingdom — for which he is remembered in Hungary as the "second founder of the state".

Mongol invasion

Béla's attempts to restore royal authority alienated the barons, many of whom fought half-heartedly when the Mongols of Batu Khan invaded in 1241. The Hungarian army was annihilated at the Battle of Mohi, and Béla fled to the Dalmatian coast, pursued by a Mongol detachment. The invaders devastated the kingdom, killing a large share of its population, before withdrawing in 1242 on the death of Ögedei Khan.

Rebuilding

Drawing the lesson that stone fortifications had resisted best, Béla promoted the building of castles, granted town privileges and fortification rights, encouraged immigration to repopulate deserted regions, and founded or refounded towns including the new royal seat at Buda. He also resettled the Cumans, whose light cavalry strengthened his army, and prepared defences that helped Hungary repel a second Mongol invasion in 1285, after his death.

Later reign

His later years were marred by conflict with his son Stephen, whom he was forced to crown as junior king and to whom he ceded the eastern part of the kingdom. Béla died in 1270 on Margaret Island, named after his daughter St Margaret.
