Marital property

Illustration of jointly owned household property (image placeholder).

Marital property is property acquired by either spouse during a marriage that is subject to division between the spouses upon divorce or annulment, as distinct from separate property owned before the marriage or acquired by gift or inheritance and kept separate.

Legal regimes

Jurisdictions follow different regimes. In community property systems (such as those in nine U.S. states including California and Texas, and in many civil-law countries), most property acquired during the marriage is presumed owned equally by both spouses. In common-law or equitable distribution systems, courts divide marital property fairly — though not necessarily equally — considering factors such as the length of the marriage, each spouse's contributions, and economic circumstances.

Classification

Disputes commonly concern the classification of assets: commingling separate and marital funds, the appreciation of separate property, pensions and businesses, and the effect of prenuptial agreements can all change what counts as marital property.
