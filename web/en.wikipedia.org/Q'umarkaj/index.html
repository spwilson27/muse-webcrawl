Q'umarkaj

The ruins of Q'umarkaj

Q'umarkaj (K'iche': [qʔumarˈkah]), also known as Utatlán in Nahuatl, was the capital of the K'iche' Maya kingdom in the highlands of Guatemala just before the Spanish conquest. It was founded around 1400 by the K'iche' king Q'uq'kumatz on a defensible plateau surrounded by ravines, near the modern town of Santa Cruz del Quiché.

History

Under kings such as K'iq'ab, Q'umarkaj grew into the centre of a powerful expansionist kingdom that dominated much of the Guatemalan highlands, exacting tribute from neighbouring peoples. Its population at its height is estimated at around 15,000. In 1524 the Spanish conquistador Pedro de Alvarado defeated the K'iche' armies, was invited into the city, and, suspecting a trap, seized the K'iche' lords, had them burned, and razed the city; the inhabitants were resettled in nearby Santa Cruz del Quiché.

The ruins

The site comprised Temple pyramids (including the temple of Tohil, the patron god of the K'iche'), palaces, a ballcourt in the Mesoamerican style, and houses of the nobility, largely of stone that was stuccoed and painted. Much of the stone was later quarried to build Santa Cruz, so the surviving structures are mostly their earthen and rubble cores. Q'umarkaj remains a place of religious significance for K'iche' Maya people, and ceremonies are still performed at altars on the site.
