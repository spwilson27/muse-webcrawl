Wake

In fluid dynamics, a wake is the region of disturbed, often turbulent flow downstream of a solid body moving through a fluid, or of fluid moving past a stationary body. Wakes are caused by the drag the body exerts on the fluid and are marked by velocity deficits, recirculation, and shed vortices.

Characteristics

Behind bluff bodies the wake may organize into a regular pattern of alternating vortices known as a Kármán vortex street. A ship's surface wake has a characteristic V-shape described by Kelvin wake patterns, combining transverse waves behind the hull with diverging waves along arms whose half-angle is about 19.47 degrees in deep water. In aerodynamics, wakes behind aircraft wings contain powerful trailing vortices, the subject of wake turbulence separation rules in aviation.

Applications

Understanding wakes matters for reducing drag on vehicles, designing wind farms (where downstream turbines sit in the wakes of upstream ones), predicting pollutant dispersion, and ensuring safe aircraft spacing. Similar concepts appear in plasma physics and in the "wakes" of objects moving through other media.
