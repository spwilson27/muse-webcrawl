Body of Christ

The Body of Christ in Christian theology

In Christian theology, the term Body of Christ has two main meanings. It may refer to the words of Jesus Christ at the Last Supper — "This is my body" — when he gave bread to his disciples, a statement central to doctrines of the Eucharist. It may also refer, in the writings of the Apostle Paul, to the Christian Church as a single body whose head is Christ and whose members are all believers.

The Eucharistic body

During the Last Supper, according to the synoptic Gospels and Paul's First Letter to the Corinthians, Jesus took bread, broke it, and said, "This is my body, which is given for you." Christian denominations differ over how this statement is to be understood. Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox teaching affirms the real presence of Christ in the Eucharist, with Catholic doctrine specifying transubstantiation. Lutheran churches teach a sacramental union, while many other traditions understand the presence spiritually or the meal as a memorial.

The Feast of Corpus Christi ("Body of Christ"), instituted in the 13th century following the visions of Juliana of Liège, celebrates the real presence with processions and adoration of the Blessed Sacrament.

The Church as the Body of Christ

Paul develops the image at length in Romans 12 and 1 Corinthians 12: just as a body, though one, has many parts, so the community of believers forms one body in Christ, each member having distinct gifts and functions. Ephesians and Colossians further describe Christ as the head of the body, the Church. This theology underlies ecclesiological ideas such as the communion of saints and the mystical body.
