Adjustable wrench

TypeHand tool

An adjustable wrench (American English) or adjustable spanner (British English) is an open-ended wrench with a movable jaw, allowing it to be used with different sizes of fastener head, such as nuts and bolts. The jaw is adjusted by a knurled worm screw, so a single tool can replace a whole set of fixed-size wrenches.

History

Adjustable wrenches appeared in the 19th century; early designs such as the "monkey wrench" used a sliding jaw. The Swedish engineer Johan Petter Johansson patented an improved design in 1891 and founded Bahco, which became a major manufacturer. In North America, the Crescent Tool Company popularized the type, and "Crescent wrench" became a genericized trademark.

Design

The tool consists of a handle, a fixed jaw, and a movable jaw whose position is set by rotating a worm gear with the thumb. The jaws are typically angled about 15 degrees to the handle so the wrench can be flipped over to turn a fastener in a confined arc. Sizes commonly range from 4 inches (100 mm) to 24 inches (600 mm) in length.

Use

For best results the wrench should be pulled so that the load falls on the fixed jaw, the stronger side, and the jaws should fit snugly on the flats of the fastener to avoid rounding its corners. Adjustable wrenches are convenient but less precise than box-end or socket wrenches, so they are a common second choice for high-torque work.
