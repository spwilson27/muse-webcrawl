Michoacán, formally Michoacán de Ocampo, is one of the 32 states of Mexico, located in western Mexico on the Pacific coast. Its capital and largest city is Morelia. The state's name comes from Nahuatl Michhuahcān, "place of the fishermen", referring to the communities around Lake Pátzcuaro.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, Michoacán was the heartland of the Purépecha (Tarascan) state, one of the great pre-Columbian civilisations of Mesoamerica, centred at Tzintzuntzan on Lake Pátzcuaro and never conquered by the Aztecs. The independence leader José María Morelos was born in Morelia, and the priest and geographer Melchor Ocampo, for whom the state is named, came from the region.

Geography

The state spans the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt and the Sierra Madre del Sur, with lakes including Pátzcuaro and Cuitzeo. The Parícutin volcano famously rose from a cornfield in 1943. Michoacán contains part of the Monarch Butterfly Biosphere Reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage Site where millions of monarch butterflies overwinter.

Economy and culture

Michoacán is one of Mexico's leading agricultural states and the world's largest producer of avocados, centred on Uruapan. The port of Lázaro Cárdenas is a major Pacific gateway. The state is famed for its Day of the Dead celebrations around Lake Pátzcuaro, Purépecha crafts such as the copperwork of Santa Clara del Cobre, and traditional music and dance including the Dance of the Old Men.
