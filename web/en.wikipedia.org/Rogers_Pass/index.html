Rogers Pass

Elevation1,330 m (4,364 ft)
LocationGlacier National Park, British Columbia, Canada
RangeSelkirk Mountains
Traversed byTrans-Canada Highway; Canadian Pacific Railway (via Connaught and Mount Macdonald tunnels)

Rogers Pass is a high mountain pass through the Selkirk Mountains of British Columbia, Canada, used by the Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR) and the Trans-Canada Highway. It lies within Glacier National Park and was discovered in 1881 by Major Albert Bowman Rogers, a surveyor working for the CPR.

History

The pass made possible the completion of Canada's first transcontinental railway in 1885. Its steep approaches and extreme snowfall — among the heaviest in North America — caused frequent avalanches, leading the CPR to build the Connaught Tunnel (1916) and later the Mount Macdonald Tunnel (1988) beneath it. The Royal Canadian Artillery operates the world's largest mobile avalanche control programme along the highway through the pass.
