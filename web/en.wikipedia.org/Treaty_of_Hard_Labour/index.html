Treaty of Hard Labour

SignedOctober 1768
LocationHard Labour Creek, South Carolina
PartiesGreat Britain; Cherokee

The Treaty of Hard Labour was signed in October 1768 between the British and the Cherokee at Hard Labour Creek in South Carolina. Negotiated by John Stuart, the British superintendent of Indian affairs for the southern district, it established a boundary line between Cherokee hunting grounds and British colonial settlement.

Terms

The Cherokee ceded claims to lands in the southwestern part of present-day Virginia and West Virginia, and the boundary was fixed running from the mouth of the Kanawha River toward the headwaters of the Holston. The line was intended to implement the Royal Proclamation of 1763 and restrain settler encroachment west of the Appalachians.

Aftermath

The boundary proved unsatisfactory to both sides and was superseded almost immediately by the Treaty of Lochaber in 1770, which pushed the line further west. Settlers continued to move beyond the agreed limits, contributing to tensions that erupted in Lord Dunmore's War in 1774.
