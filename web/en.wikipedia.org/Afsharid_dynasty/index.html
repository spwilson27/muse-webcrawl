The Afsharid dynasty (Persian: افشاریان) was an Iranian dynasty founded by Nader Shah (r. 1736–1747) of the Qirqlu clan of the Turkoman Afshar tribe. At its height under Nader, the Afsharid Empire briefly controlled a vast territory stretching from the Caucasus and Central Asia to the Indus valley, becoming one of the most powerful empires of its era.

History

Nader rose to power in the chaos following the Afghan Hotak invasion that toppled the Safavid dynasty. After expelling the Afghans and restoring and then deposing the Safavid shah, he was crowned in 1736. His campaigns reconquered lost territories from the Ottomans and Russians, and in 1738–39 he invaded Mughal India, defeating the Mughal army at Karnal and sacking Delhi, carrying off immense treasure including the Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor diamond. Increasingly tyrannical and paranoid in his later years, Nader was assassinated by his own officers in 1747. His empire quickly fragmented; his successors ruled only Khorasan in the northeast, while Ahmad Shah Durrani founded the Durrani Empire in Afghanistan and Karim Khan Zand took power in most of Iran. The dynasty effectively ended by 1796.
