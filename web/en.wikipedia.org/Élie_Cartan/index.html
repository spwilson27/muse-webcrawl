Élie Cartan

Élie Cartan (1869–1951), founder of the modern theory of differential forms and a pioneer of Lie group theory.

Élie Joseph Cartan (9 April 1869 – 6 May 1951) was a French mathematician who did fundamental work in the theory of Lie groups, differential systems (PDEs) and differential geometry. He also made significant contributions to general relativity and indirectly to quantum mechanics. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians of the twentieth century.

Work

Cartan's 1894 thesis completed the classification of simple Lie algebras begun by Wilhelm Killing. He developed the exterior calculus of differential forms, the method of moving frames (repère mobile) for studying geometric structures, and the theory of spinors in arbitrary dimension. His notion of a Cartan connection generalised the geometry of Lie groups to curved spaces and became central to modern differential geometry and gauge theory. His son Henri Cartan was also a leading mathematician, a founding member of Bourbaki.

Recognition

Long under-appreciated outside France — partly because of his dense style — Cartan's ideas were absorbed into mainstream mathematics in the second half of the 20th century. The lunar crater Cartan and the asteroid 17917 Cartan are named after him.
