Sedenion

The sedenions are the 16-dimensional algebra obtained by one step of the Cayley–Dickson construction applied to the octonions.

The sedenions form a 16-dimensional noncommutative and nonassociative algebra over the real numbers, obtained by applying the Cayley–Dickson construction to the octonions. The name derives from the Latin sedecim, "sixteen". Each sedenion is a linear combination of the unit sedenions e0 through e15, where e0 is the scalar or real element.

Properties

Like the octonions, the sedenions are power-associative and flexible, but each step of the Cayley–Dickson process sacrifices a property: the complex numbers lose ordering, the quaternions lose commutativity, the octonions lose associativity, and the sedenions lose the division-algebra property. Consequently the sedenions contain zero divisors: pairs of non-zero elements whose product is zero. They are also not alternative, though they retain a multiplicative norm in a weaker sense, and conjugation and a notion of magnitude can still be defined.

Zero divisors

The zero divisors of the sedenions have been studied geometrically; they can be described in terms of pairs of imaginary unit elements and are related to the geometry of the exceptional Lie group G2 and to M-theory in physics. All 84 "assessor" triples of basis units that fail to associate in a particular patterned way generate families of zero divisors.

Applications

Sedenions have few mainstream applications but appear in abstract algebra, in attempts to describe particle physics symmetries, and in some work on neural networks and signal processing using hypercomplex numbers. Continuing the construction beyond sedenions gives 32-dimensional "trigintaduonions" and higher algebras with progressively weaker properties.
