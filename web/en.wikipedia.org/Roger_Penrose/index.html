Roger Penrose

Born: 8 August 1931, Colchester, Essex, England
Fields: Mathematical physics, geometry, cosmology
Known for: Penrose tiling, singularity theorems, twistor theory
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (2020), Wolf Prize (1988)

Sir Roger Penrose (born 8 August 1931) is an English mathematical physicist, mathematician, and philosopher of science. He is Emeritus Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics at the University of Oxford. Penrose shared the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics for the discovery that black hole formation is a robust prediction of general relativity, based on his 1965 singularity theorem.

Work in physics and mathematics

Penrose introduced twistor theory, Penrose diagrams for spacetime, and spin networks, the last becoming foundational for loop quantum gravity. With Stephen Hawking he proved the Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems. He proposed cosmic censorship and, later, conformal cyclic cosmology, in which the universe passes through successive aeons. In tiling theory he discovered the aperiodic Penrose tiling, work that anticipated the discovery of quasicrystals.

Consciousness and popular books

In books such as The Emperor's New Mind (1989) and Shadows of the Mind (1994), Penrose argued that conscious understanding is non-computational, and with Stuart Hameroff developed the Orch-OR theory linking consciousness to quantum processes in neuronal microtubules. His popular works also include The Road to Reality (2004), a comprehensive guide to mathematical physics. Penrose devised the impossible triangle (Penrose tribar) with his father Lionel Penrose, influencing the art of M. C. Escher.
