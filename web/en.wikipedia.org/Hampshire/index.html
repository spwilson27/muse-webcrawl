Hampshire

Hampshire
County town: Winchester
Population: approx. 1.9 million
Region: South East England

Hampshire is a ceremonial county in South East England on the English Channel coast. Its county town is the historic cathedral city of Winchester, once the capital of Wessex and of Anglo-Saxon England. The largest settlements are the port cities of Southampton and Portsmouth.

Geography

The county contains two national parks: the New Forest, created as a royal hunting ground by William the Conqueror in 1079 and famous for its free-roaming ponies, and part of the South Downs, a range of chalk hills. The rivers Test and Itchen are renowned chalk streams prized for fly fishing. Offshore lies the Isle of Wight, historically part of Hampshire and now a separate ceremonial county.

History and economy

Hampshire's coast has long been central to British naval history: Portsmouth is home to the Royal Navy's historic dockyard and HMS Victory, while Southampton was a great transatlantic liner port — the Titanic sailed from there in 1912. Jane Austen spent much of her life in the county, at Steventon and Chawton, and is buried in Winchester Cathedral. Today the economy combines maritime industries, services, tourism, and agriculture.
