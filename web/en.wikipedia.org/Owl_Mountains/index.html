Owl Mountains

Polish: Góry Sowie

German: Eulengebirge

Location: Lower Silesian Voivodeship, Poland

Highest point: Wielka Sowa, 1,015 m (3,330 ft)

Range: Central Sudetes

The Owl Mountains (Polish: Góry Sowie; German: Eulengebirge) are a mountain range of the Central Sudetes in south-western Poland, in the Lower Silesian Voivodeship. They run for about 26 kilometres between the towns of Wałbrzych and Kłodzko, forming a steep north-eastern escarpment above the Silesian lowland. The highest peak is Wielka Sowa ("Great Owl"), 1,015 metres above sea level, crowned by a 25-metre stone observation tower built in 1906.

Geology and landscape

The Owl Mountains are composed mainly of very old Precambrian gneiss of the Sowie Mountains Block, among the oldest rocks in Europe, with migmatites and smaller bodies of other metamorphic rocks. The range is largely forested with spruce and beech, and much of it is protected as the Owl Mountains Landscape Park (Park Krajobrazowy Gór Sowich), established in 1991 and covering some 8,000 hectares. Wildlife includes mouflon, introduced in the early 20th century, red deer and numerous bat species hibernating in old mines and tunnels.

Project Riese

The mountains are best known internationally for Project Riese ("Giant"), a vast system of underground tunnels and chambers excavated by Nazi Germany in 1943–45 using concentration-camp prisoners, principally from Gross-Rosen. Seven major complexes were begun — including Rzeczka, Włodarz, Osówka, Sokolec, Jugowice, Soboń and the tunnels beneath Książ Castle — but none was completed. Their purpose remains debated: proposed explanations include a protected Führer headquarters, underground armaments factories, and, in fringe speculation, weapons research. The Osówka and Włodarz complexes are now open to tourists, and the unfinished tunnels fuel persistent legends of a buried Nazi "gold train", which attracted international attention in 2015.

Tourism

The range is a popular area for hiking and mountain biking, with a network of marked trails radiating from Wielka Sowa and resorts in the foothill towns of Walim, Rzeczka, Sokolec and Pieszyce. Fortifications of the Srebrna Góra fortress, among the largest mountain fortresses in Europe, guard the Silver Pass at the south-eastern end of the range.
