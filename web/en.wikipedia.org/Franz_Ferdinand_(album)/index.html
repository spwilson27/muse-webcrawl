Franz Ferdinand is the debut studio album by the Scottish indie rock band Franz Ferdinand, released on 9 February 2004 by Domino Recording Company. Propelled by the hit single "Take Me Out", the album was a commercial and critical success, winning the 2004 Mercury Prize and helping to define the post-punk revival of the 2000s.

Background and recording

The band formed in Glasgow in 2002 and built a following through warehouse parties and early singles such as "Darts of Pleasure". The album was recorded largely in Malmö, Sweden, with producer Tore Johansson, giving its angular guitar pop a crisp, dance-oriented sound.

Singles and reception

"Take Me Out" reached number 3 on the UK Singles Chart and became the band's signature song, famous for its abrupt mid-song tempo change. Other singles included "The Dark of the Matinée", "Michael", and "This Fire". Critics praised the album's wit, rhythmic sharpness, and art-school sensibility; it appeared near the top of many 2004 year-end lists.

Legacy

The album has sold millions of copies worldwide and is frequently cited among the best debut albums of the decade. Its success established Franz Ferdinand as one of the leading bands of the 2000s indie rock movement.
