Southern Sámi (Southern Sami: Åarjelsaemien gïele) is the southernmost of the Sámi languages, spoken by a few hundred people in central Norway and Sweden. It is written in the Latin alphabet, using letters such as ï to represent a distinctive vowel.

Characteristics

Southern Sámi differs sharply from the much larger Northern Sámi. Notable features include the absence of consonant gradation, extensive vowel umlaut alternations in inflection, and a dual number in verbs alongside singular and plural. Because of these differences, Southern and Northern Sámi are not mutually intelligible.

Status and revitalisation

The language is classified as seriously endangered by UNESCO. Its speaker community is concentrated around Snåsa in Norway and in Swedish municipalities such as Vilhelmina and Tärnaby. Revitalisation efforts include language nests for children, teaching materials, a dictionary project, and media such as the Southern Sámi magazine and broadcasts.
