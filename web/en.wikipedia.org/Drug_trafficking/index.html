Drug trafficking

Drug trafficking is a global illicit trade involving the cultivation, manufacture, distribution, and sale of substances that are subject to drug prohibition laws. It is a major component of transnational organized crime, with the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime estimating the trade to be worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually.

Scope and routes

Trafficking networks move drugs such as cocaine, heroin, methamphetamine, cannabis, and synthetic opioids like fentanyl from production regions to consumer markets, often through long chains of intermediaries. Major routes include cocaine flows from the Andean region of South America to North America and Europe, heroin from Afghanistan and Myanmar, and chemicals for synthetic drugs shipped between Asia, North America, and Europe. Concealment methods include shipping containers, commercial aircraft, tunnels, submarines and semi-submersibles, drones, and human couriers ("mules").

Organizations

Trafficking is carried out by organizations ranging from loose networks to structured groups such as Mexican cartels (including the Sinaloa Cartel and Jalisco New Generation Cartel), Colombian organizations, Italian mafia groups such as the 'Ndrangheta, and various gangs. Corruption of officials and money laundering through banks, businesses, and cryptocurrencies support the trade.

Enforcement and consequences

International control rests on treaties including the 1961 Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs and the 1988 Vienna Convention. Enforcement strategies range from interdiction and eradication to kingpin targeting, while critics argue that prohibition-driven enforcement fuels violence, mass incarceration, and corruption; the "war on drugs" declared by the United States in the 1970s remains controversial. Trafficking-related violence has caused tens of thousands of deaths in Mexico and elsewhere, and overdose epidemics in consumer countries have intensified debate between prohibition, decriminalization, and harm-reduction approaches.
