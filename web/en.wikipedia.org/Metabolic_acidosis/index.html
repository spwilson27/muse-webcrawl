Metabolic acidosis

Metabolic acidosis is a condition in which the body produces too much acid, loses too much bicarbonate, or the kidneys cannot remove enough acid, lowering blood pH. It is characterized by a primary reduction in serum bicarbonate concentration.

Causes

Causes are commonly divided using the anion gap. High anion gap acidosis results from accumulation of acids such as lactate (lactic acidosis), ketones (diabetic ketoacidosis), or toxins (methanol, ethylene glycol), and from kidney failure. Normal anion gap acidosis arises from gastrointestinal bicarbonate loss (e.g., diarrhea) or renal tubular acidosis. The mnemonic MUDPILES summarizes common high-gap causes.

Signs and treatment

Symptoms may include rapid deep breathing (Kussmaul respirations), fatigue, headache, and, in severe cases, shock or arrhythmia. Diagnosis uses arterial blood gas and electrolyte measurements. Treatment addresses the underlying cause; intravenous sodium bicarbonate is used selectively in severe cases, and dialysis may be required for toxin ingestion or renal failure.
