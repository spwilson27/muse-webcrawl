Rajputana


Placeholder image: Rajputana

Rajputana, meaning "Land of the Rajputs", was a region in northwestern India that roughly corresponds to the modern state of Rajasthan. During the British Raj it was administered as the Rajputana Agency, a collection of princely states under British paramountcy.

Geography and states

The region lies between the Indus valley and the Gangetic plain, dominated by the Thar Desert and the Aravalli Range. Its principal princely states included Mewar (Udaipur), Marwar (Jodhpur), Jaipur, Bikaner, Jaisalmer and Kota, ruled by Rajput clans such as the Sisodias, Rathores and Kachhwahas.

History

Rajput kingdoms resisted, allied with and ultimately accepted first Mughal and then British suzerainty through treaties in 1817–18. After Indian independence in 1947, the states acceded to India and were integrated in stages into the state of Rajasthan, completed in 1956.
