Radiation burn

A radiation burn is damage to the skin or other biological tissue caused by exposure to ionising radiation. It is also known as radiodermatitis, or, in its chronic occupational form among early radiologists, as radiation dermatitis. Radiation burns differ from thermal burns in that symptoms may be delayed for hours to weeks after exposure, and damage can progress over time.

Causes and symptoms

Causes include accidents involving industrial or medical radiation sources (such as the Goiânia accident of 1987), nuclear accidents (Chernobyl, where firefighters suffered severe beta burns), radiotherapy side effects, and prolonged fluoroscopy. Course of injury typically includes an early transient redness (erythema), a latent period, then moist desquamation, ulceration, and in severe cases necrosis. High doses of beta radiation are a common cause of skin injury because beta particles deposit their energy superficially.

Treatment

Treatment is supportive and similar to thermal burn care — wound management, pain control, infection prevention — supplemented in severe cases by surgical excision and grafting. There is no specific antidote; prognosis depends on dose, area, and depth.
