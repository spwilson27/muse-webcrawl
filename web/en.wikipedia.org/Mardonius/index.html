Mardonius

Mardonius (died 479 BC) was a leading Persian military commander during the Greco-Persian Wars. The son of Gobryas and a nephew — and, through marriage to the king's daughter Artozostra, son-in-law — of Darius I, he was one of the most trusted figures of the Achaemenid court. According to Herodotus, Mardonius was a persistent advocate of invading Greece, hoping to become its satrap.

Early career

In 492 BC Mardonius led the first Persian expedition against Greece, reimposing Persian control over Thrace and Macedon, but his fleet was wrecked in a storm off Mount Athos and he was wounded in a night attack by the Brygi, forcing a retreat. He was replaced in command by Datis and Artaphernes, who were defeated at the Battle of Marathon in 490 BC.

Invasion of Xerxes

After the accession of Xerxes I, Mardonius urged the great invasion of 480 BC. When Xerxes returned to Asia after the Battle of Salamis, Mardonius was left in command of a picked army to continue the war. He wintered in Thessaly and attempted to win Athens over by diplomacy through Alexander I of Macedon, offering the Athenians autonomy and territorial gains; they refused.

Plataea and death

In 479 BC Mardonius reoccupied Athens, then withdrew into Boeotia to fight on ground favourable to his cavalry. At the Battle of Plataea his army was defeated by the allied Greeks under the Spartan regent Pausanias. Mardonius was killed in the fighting — Herodotus says by the Spartan Aeimnestus, who struck him on the head with a stone — and the Persian army was routed. His death effectively ended the Persian invasion of Greece.
