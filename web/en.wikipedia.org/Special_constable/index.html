Special constable

Special constable
Short title: Special
Service: Volunteer (usually unpaid)
Powers: Full constable powers (varies by jurisdiction)
Found in: UK, Canada, and other Commonwealth countries

A special constable or special police constable (SC or SPC, informally a "special") is generally an auxiliary or part-time member of a police force. In the United Kingdom, special constables are sworn volunteer police officers who hold the full powers of a constable while on (and in some circumstances off) duty, and who serve alongside regular officers in the Special Constabulary of each territorial force.

United Kingdom

The modern Special Constabulary in England and Wales was formalised by the Special Constables Act 1914, building on much older traditions of parish constables and wartime auxiliaries. Specials wear uniforms nearly identical to those of regular officers, receive structured training, and are expected to volunteer a minimum number of hours per month (commonly around 16–18). Numbers peaked during the world wars and again around 2011–2012 at some 20,000 in England and Wales, before declining to roughly half that figure. Specials patrol, respond to incidents and staff events, and some progress into specialist roles; many also use the experience as a route into the regular service.

Elsewhere

Comparable roles exist in Scotland, Northern Ireland (where part-time reserve officers were historically paid), Canada — where "special constables" include transit, campus and court officers with limited powers — and various other Commonwealth jurisdictions, as well as special police forces for railways, parks and ports.
