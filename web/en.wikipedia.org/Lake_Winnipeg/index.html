Lake Winnipeg

Lake Winnipeg is a very large, relatively shallow lake in Manitoba, Canada, about 55 km north of the city of Winnipeg. It is Canada's fifth-largest freshwater lake and the eleventh-largest in the world by surface area. The name is from Cree words meaning "muddy waters".

Geography

The long, narrow lake drains a watershed of nearly 1 million km², including parts of Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, Minnesota and North Dakota. It drains north through the Nelson River to Hudson Bay. Manitoba Hydro regulates the lake for hydroelectric generation, under an agreement dating from the 1970s.

Ecology

The commercial fishery — walleye (pickerel), lake whitefish and sauger — is valuable, supporting many lakeside and Indigenous communities; Gimli is a major fishing and resort town. Excess nutrients from the Red River and other tributaries have caused increasing algal blooms, a concern monitored by research on the lake including the research vessel Namao.

History

The lake was a central route in the fur trade, travelled by explorer La Vérendrye in the 1730s; posts and later Icelandic settlement at Gimli (New Iceland, 1875) shaped its shores.
