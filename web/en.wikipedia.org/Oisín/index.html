Oisín

Oisín (pronounced roughly "uh-SHEEN"), anglicised as Ossian, is a legendary Irish poet and warrior of the Fenian Cycle of Irish mythology. He is the son of Fionn mac Cumhaill, leader of the Fianna, and of Sadhbh, who had been transformed into a deer by a druid. Oisín is credited in tradition as the narrator and supposed author of many of the Fenian poems and tales.

The voyage to Tír na nÓg

The most famous tale tells how Niamh of the Golden Hair, a woman of the Otherworld, fell in love with Oisín and carried him on a white horse across the sea to Tír na nÓg, the land of eternal youth. After what seemed three years there, Oisín longed to visit Ireland; Niamh warned him not to dismount, for in Ireland three hundred years had passed. When his saddle girth broke and he touched the ground while helping men lift a stone, the centuries fell upon him and he became an old, blind man.

Legacy

In later tradition Oisín survives to meet Saint Patrick, to whom he recounts the deeds of the Fianna in the dialogue known as the Acallam na Senórach. His name was borrowed by James Macpherson for the supposed ancient bard "Ossian" whose poems, published in the 1760s, ignited a European literary sensation and controversy over their authenticity.
