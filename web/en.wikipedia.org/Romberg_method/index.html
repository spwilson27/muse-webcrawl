Romberg's method

In numerical analysis, Romberg's method (Romberg integration) is a technique for approximating a definite integral by applying Richardson extrapolation repeatedly to the trapezoidal rule or the midpoint rule. It is named after Werner Romberg, who published the method in 1955.

Method

Starting from trapezoidal estimates R(n,0) computed with successively halved step sizes, extrapolated values are built as R(n,m) = R(n,m−1) + (R(n,m−1) − R(n−1,m−1)) / (4^m − 1). Each column of the resulting triangular table eliminates a higher-order error term, so diagonal entries converge far faster than the raw trapezoidal estimates — effectively producing high-order accuracy from a simple rule.

Properties

The method works best for smooth integrands over finite intervals and was a standard library routine for quadrature before adaptive Gaussian quadrature became widespread. Endpoint singularities or non-smooth behaviour degrade the extrapolation. See also numerical integration and Richardson extrapolation.
