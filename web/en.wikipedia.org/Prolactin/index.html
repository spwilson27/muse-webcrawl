Prolactin (PRL), also known as lactotropin and mammotropin, is a protein best known for its role in enabling mammals to produce milk. It is influential in over 300 separate processes in various vertebrates, including osmoregulation in fish and parental behaviour in birds. Prolactin is secreted from the pituitary gland in response to eating, mating, estrogen treatment, ovulation and nursing.

The hormone was first discovered in non-human animals by Oscar Riddle around 1930 and was distinguished from growth hormone in humans in 1970 by Henry Friesen. Prolactin is a single-chain polypeptide of 199 amino acids with a molecular weight of about 23 kilodaltons; its structure is similar to that of growth hormone and placental lactogen.

Function

In mammals, prolactin's principal physiological action is to initiate and maintain lactation. It acts on the mammary glands to promote their growth during pregnancy and to stimulate milk production after childbirth. The suckling stimulus maintains prolactin secretion, and the hormone also contributes to maternal behaviour. Prolactin additionally has functions in metabolism, regulation of the immune system, and reproduction, including effects on the corpus luteum and on hair growth cycles.

Regulation

Unlike most pituitary hormones, prolactin secretion is under predominantly inhibitory control by the hypothalamus. Dopamine, released from tuberoinfundibular neurons, acts on D2 receptors of lactotroph cells to suppress prolactin release; drugs that block dopamine receptors therefore raise prolactin levels. Thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) and estrogen stimulate release. Prolactin exerts short-loop negative feedback on its own secretion by increasing dopamine turnover.

Clinical significance

Hyperprolactinemia, an abnormally high level of prolactin, is most commonly caused by a prolactinoma, a benign pituitary adenoma, but can also result from medications (especially antipsychotics), hypothyroidism, or pregnancy. In women it causes galactorrhea, amenorrhea and infertility; in men it can cause reduced libido, erectile dysfunction and gynecomastia. Treatment is usually with dopamine agonists such as cabergoline or bromocriptine. Abnormally low prolactin (hypoprolactinemia) can cause failure of lactation after childbirth.
