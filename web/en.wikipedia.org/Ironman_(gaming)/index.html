Ironman (gaming)

Ironman mode is an optional gameplay mode in many video games, especially strategy and role-playing games, that restricts or disables the player's ability to save and reload the game at will. Named after the endurance of the Ironman triathlon, the mode typically forces autosaving at a single point and automatically overwrites the save when the player makes a decision or suffers a defeat, so consequences cannot be undone.

Mechanics

In an ironman playthrough the game saves automatically and frequently — often whenever the player exits or a major event occurs — and manual save files are disabled. If a character dies or a campaign fails, the loss is permanent. Many implementations reward ironman play with achievements that cannot be earned otherwise, since the player cannot reload to reverse bad outcomes ("savescumming").

Examples

Grand strategy titles by Paradox Interactive, including Europa Universalis IV, Crusader Kings II and III, Hearts of Iron IV and Stellaris, require ironman mode for achievement eligibility, restricting saving to a single autosaved file stored in the cloud in some versions. The XCOM series offers an "Ironman" difficulty setting in which a single save is continuously overwritten. RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress and roguelike games embody similar design through permanent death (permadeath) mechanics.

Related concepts

Ironman mode is closely related to permadeath and hardcore modes, in which a game ends permanently upon the player's defeat. It appeals to players seeking higher stakes and is a common format for challenge runs and streamed playthroughs.
