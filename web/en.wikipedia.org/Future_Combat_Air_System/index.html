Future Combat Air System

The Future Combat Air System (FCAS; French: Système de combat aérien du futur, SCAF) is a European combat system of systems under development by France, Germany, and Spain, intended to enter service around 2040 and replace the Rafale and Eurofighter Typhoon fleets.

Components

The system's core is the New Generation Fighter (NGF), a sixth-generation crewed stealth fighter, which will operate as part of a networked "combat cloud" alongside uncrewed "remote carrier" drones acting as loyal wingmen and effectors, and existing aircraft, satellites, and command systems.

Development

France and Germany announced the programme in 2017, and Spain joined in 2019. Dassault Aviation leads the fighter, Airbus leads the remote carriers and combat cloud, and Spain's Indra coordinates Spanish participation; Safran and MTU Aero Engines develop the engine. The first phase of demonstrator work was contracted in 2022 after protracted negotiations over workshare and intellectual property, and disputes — particularly between Dassault and Airbus over leadership of the fighter pillar — have periodically threatened the programme.

Context

FCAS is a counterpart to the separate UK–Italy–Japan Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP), and both reflect European efforts to retain an independent combat-aircraft industry.
