Betwa River

The Betwa River is a river in central India, a tributary of the Yamuna. It rises in the Vindhya Range just north of Hoshangabad in Madhya Pradesh and flows generally north-east through Madhya Pradesh and the Bundelkhand region of Uttar Pradesh for about 590 kilometres before joining the Yamuna near Hamirpur.

Course and significance

The Betwa passes the cities of Vidisha, Sanchi's vicinity, Jhansi and Orchha; the historic town of Orchha, former capital of the Bundela Rajputs, stands on its banks, where cenotaphs (chhatris) of the Bundela kings line the river. In ancient texts the river was known as the Vetravati. The river is an important source of irrigation and hydroelectric power in the drought-prone Bundelkhand region.

Dams

Major projects on the Betwa include the Rajghat Dam, shared by Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh, the Matatila Dam, and the Parichha Dam. The Ken–Betwa river-linking project, approved by the Government of India, is intended to transfer surplus water from the Ken River to the Betwa basin for irrigation and drinking water.
