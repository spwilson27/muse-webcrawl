Akshobhya

Akshobhya (Sanskrit: Akṣobhya, "Immovable One"; Japanese: Ashuku Nyorai) is one of the Five Wisdom Buddhas, a group of five Dhyani Buddhas that evolved in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. Akshobhya presides over the eastern paradise Abhirati and is associated with the transformation of anger into mirror-like wisdom.

Origins

Akshobhya appears in the Akṣobhyatathāgatasyavyūha Sūtra, among the earliest Mahayana texts, which describes his vow, as a monk, never to feel anger or disgust toward any being — the steadfastness that earned him the name "Immovable". His pure land Abhirati is described as a paradise attained through devotion and practice.

Iconography

Akshobhya is usually depicted blue in colour, seated in the earth-touching (bhūmisparśa) mudra, and associated with the element water, the aggregate of consciousness, and the vajra. His consort is Locanā, and his mount is the elephant. In Tibetan Buddhism he is central to practices for purifying negative karma, and his mantra is widely recited.
