Battle of Blair Mountain

The Battle of Blair Mountain was the largest labour uprising in United States history and the largest armed conflict on American soil since the Civil War. Fought from 25 August to 2 September 1921 in Logan County, West Virginia, it pitted some 10,000 armed coal miners against roughly 3,000 deputies, mine guards and strikebreakers led by Sheriff Don Chafin.

Background

For decades southern West Virginia's coalfields had seen violent conflict between miners seeking to unionise with the United Mine Workers and coal operators who kept the region a non-union stronghold guarded by private detectives. The Matewan Massacre of 1920 and the assassination of Matewan police chief Sid Hatfield on 1 August 1921 inflamed the miners, who began an armed march on Logan and Mingo counties to free imprisoned union men and organise the coalfields.

The battle

Chafin's forces dug in along the ridgeline of Blair Mountain. Over several days of fighting, miners assaulted the positions while private planes dropped improvised bombs and gas on them — among the few instances of aircraft used against Americans on U.S. soil. President Warren G. Harding threatened federal intervention, and on 2 September U.S. Army troops arrived; the miners, many of them veterans, refused to fight the Army and dispersed.

Aftermath

Nearly a thousand miners were indicted, some for treason against the state, though few were convicted. Union membership in West Virginia collapsed for a decade, but the battle became an enduring symbol of the American labour movement, and the battlefield was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2009.
