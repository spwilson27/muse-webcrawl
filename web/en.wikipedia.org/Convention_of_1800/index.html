Convention of 1800

The Convention of 1800, also known as the Treaty of Mortefontaine, was a treaty between the United States of America and the French Republic that ended the Quasi-War, an undeclared naval conflict fought between 1798 and 1800.

Background

Relations between the United States and revolutionary France deteriorated after the XYZ Affair and French seizures of American merchant ships. President John Adams pursued a diplomatic settlement despite pressure from within his own Federalist Party for open war.

Terms

Signed on 30 September 1800 at Mortefontaine, France, the convention restored peaceful commercial relations, ended the 1778 Treaty of Alliance between France and the United States, and returned captured warships. Questions of compensation for seized American shipping were deferred and remained contentious for decades. The treaty helped preserve American neutrality and allowed Adams's successor, Thomas Jefferson, to negotiate the Louisiana Purchase with France in 1803.
