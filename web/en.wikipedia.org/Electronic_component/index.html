Electronic component

An electronic component is any basic discrete device or physical entity in an electronic system used to affect electrons or their associated fields. Components are the building blocks of electronic circuits and are typically packaged individually — with two or more connecting leads or terminals — and soldered onto a printed circuit board.

Classification

Components are classified as passive or active. Passive components cannot introduce net energy into a circuit: they include resistors, which dissipate energy, and capacitors and inductors, which store it temporarily in electric and magnetic fields. Active components rely on a source of energy to control or amplify signals; examples are transistors, diodes and integrated circuits. Electromechanical components, such as switches, relays and connectors, combine moving parts with electrical contacts.

Common components

  • Resistor — opposes the flow of electric current.
  • Capacitor — stores charge in an electric field between two conductors.
  • Inductor — stores energy in a magnetic field produced by a coil of wire.
  • Diode — conducts current primarily in one direction; includes the light-emitting diode.
  • Transistor — amplifies or switches electronic signals, the fundamental building block of modern electronics.
  • Integrated circuit — a complete circuit fabricated on a single piece of semiconductor, containing from a few to billions of transistors.

Packaging

Components are made in through-hole packages, which are mounted by inserting leads into drilled holes, and surface-mount packages (SMD), which are soldered directly onto pads. Miniaturisation has driven component sizes down to fractions of a millimetre, enabling compact consumer electronics.
