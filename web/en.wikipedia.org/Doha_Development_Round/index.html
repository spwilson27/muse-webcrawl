Doha Development Round

LaunchedNovember 2001, Doha, Qatar
ForumWorld Trade Organization
SubjectMultilateral trade liberalisation with development focus
StatusStalled; largely superseded

The Doha Development Round or Doha Development Agenda (DDA) is the trade negotiation round of the World Trade Organization (WTO) which commenced in November 2001 under the name of the Doha Ministerial. Its objective was to lower trade barriers worldwide, with a focus on making trade fairer for developing countries. It was the first WTO round to place development at its centre.

Agenda

The Doha Declaration mandated negotiations on agriculture (market access, export subsidies and domestic support), non-agricultural market access (NAMA), services, intellectual property (including access to medicines under TRIPS), trade facilitation, rules, and special and differential treatment for developing and least-developed countries. In parallel, the 2001 Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health affirmed members' rights to use compulsory licensing during health emergencies.

Stalled negotiations

Ministerial conferences at Cancún (2003), Hong Kong (2005) and Geneva (2008) failed to agree modalities, chiefly over agricultural subsidies and safeguards in the United States, the European Union, India and China. Talks collapsed in July 2008 over the special safeguard mechanism for developing-country farmers. Negotiations drifted thereafter; the 2013 Bali Ministerial produced the narrower Trade Facilitation Agreement, and at Nairobi in 2015 members publicly disagreed on whether the Doha mandate remained in force.

Assessment

The round's failure is widely attributed to the WTO's consensus rule and "single undertaking", shifting global economic power, and the rise of bilateral and regional agreements such as the Trans-Pacific Partnership.
