Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion

AuthorRobert B. Cialdini
Published1984 (revised editions since)
SubjectSocial psychology, persuasion, marketing
PublisherWilliam Morrow / Harper Business

Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion is a 1984 book by psychologist Robert B. Cialdini that examines the psychology of why people say "yes" and how to apply that knowledge ethically in business and everyday life. Drawing on experimental social psychology and on three years Cialdini spent undercover in sales, fundraising, and advertising organisations, the book identifies a small set of universal principles of persuasion.

The principles

The original edition described six principles. Reciprocity: people feel obliged to return favours. Commitment and consistency: once people take a stand, they align later behaviour with it. Social proof: people look to the behaviour of others to decide what to do. Liking: people are more easily persuaded by those they like. Authority: people defer to credible experts. Scarcity: opportunities seem more valuable when less available. In the later work Pre-Suasion (2016) and the revised Influence (2021), Cialdini added a seventh principle, unity — shared identity with the persuader.

Reception and influence

The book became a long-running bestseller, selling millions of copies and appearing on business bestseller lists decades after publication. It is widely assigned in marketing and psychology courses, and its principles are standard vocabulary in sales, negotiation, fundraising, and public-health campaign design. Cialdini frames the principles both as tools for persuaders and as defences for readers who want to recognise and resist manipulation.
