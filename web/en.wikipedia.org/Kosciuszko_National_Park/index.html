Kosciuszko National Park

Kosciuszko National Park
Location: New South Wales, Australia
Area: 6,900 km²
Established: 1944 as Kosciusko State Park; national park 1967

Kosciuszko National Park is a national park in the Snowy Mountains region of New South Wales, Australia. Covering about 6,900 square kilometres, it is the largest national park in New South Wales and contains Mount Kosciuszko, mainland Australia's highest peak at 2,228 metres.

Geography and ecology

The park encompasses alpine and subalpine environments, snow gum woodlands, montane forests, and the headwaters of the Murray, Murrumbidgee and Snowy rivers. It protects species such as the mountain pygmy possum, corroboree frog and broad-toothed rat, and extensive sphagnum bogs and fens.

Recreation

The park is a major destination for skiing at resorts including Thredbo, Perisher, Charlotte Pass and Guthega, and in summer for bushwalking, camping, fishing, mountain biking and climbing. The Main Range walk and the summit track to Mount Kosciuszko are popular.

History

Aboriginal people have long associations with the high country. European stock grazing, mining and the Snowy Mountains Hydro-electric Scheme (1949–1974) shaped the modern landscape. The park forms part of the Australian Alps National Parks and is heritage listed.
