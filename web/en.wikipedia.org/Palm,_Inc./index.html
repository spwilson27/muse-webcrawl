Palm, Inc.

Palm, Inc., was an American company that specialized in manufacturing personal digital assistants (PDAs) and developing software. Palm designed the PalmPilot, the first commercially successful PDA, and was the developer of several versions of Palm OS. The company was also responsible for the first versions of the Treo smartphone line and later developed the webOS operating system and the Palm Pre smartphone. Palm was acquired by Hewlett-Packard (HP) in July 2010 for approximately US$1.2 billion, after which the Palm brand was gradually retired.


Palm's products ranged from the PalmPilot PDA to the webOS-based Pre.

History

Founding and Pilot

Palm Computing was founded in 1992 by Jeff Hawkins, Donna Dubinsky, and Ed Colligan with initial funding from Tandy Corporation. The company first developed software for the Zoomer PDA marketed by Casio, and worked with GeoWorks and Apple before being acquired by U.S. Robotics in 1995. Hawkins had concluded that earlier handhelds failed because they tried to do too much; the PalmPilot, launched in 1996, was deliberately simple — a pocket-sized organizer that synchronized with a PC at the touch of a button, using an economical handwriting-recognition system called Graffiti. The Pilot 1000 and 5000 were an immediate success, selling a million units within 18 months, and the follow-up PalmPilot models in 1997 cemented the company's dominance of the PDA market.

Independence, Palm OS licensing, and split

U.S. Robotics was acquired by 3Com in 1997. Hawkins, Dubinsky, and Colligan left in 1998 to found Handspring, which licensed Palm OS for its Visor handhelds. Palm became a separate publicly traded subsidiary of 3Com in 2000, at the height of the dot-com boom. In 2003 Palm merged with Handspring and split into two companies: PalmSource, responsible for Palm OS, and palmOne, responsible for hardware. In 2005 palmOne bought PalmSource's share of the Palm trademark and renamed itself Palm, Inc. PalmSource was subsequently acquired by ACCESS of Japan.

Smartphones and webOS

The Treo line, inherited from Handspring, moved Palm into smartphones; the Treo 600 (2003) and later models running Palm OS and Windows Mobile were commercially significant, but competition from BlackBerry and, after 2007, the iPhone and Android devices eroded Palm's position. Under CEO Jon Rubinstein, formerly of Apple, Palm developed a new Linux-based operating system, webOS, praised for its card-based multitasking interface and Synergy contact integration. The Palm Pre launched in June 2009 to critical acclaim but modest sales; the smaller Pixi followed. Despite an investment from Elevation Partners, Palm could not fund continued competition.

Acquisition by HP and end

HP announced its acquisition of Palm in April 2010 and completed it in July 2010, valuing the company at about $1.2 billion. HP released webOS products including the Veer and Pre 3 phones and the TouchPad tablet in 2011, but discontinued webOS devices that August after poor TouchPad sales; webOS was later open-sourced and sold to LG in 2013 for use in smart televisions. The Palm brand was sold to a subsidiary of TCL Corporation in 2014, which released a small companion "Palm" phone in 2018.

Products

Palm's hardware lines included the Pilot, PalmPilot, III, V, VII, m-series, Tungsten, Zire, LifeDrive, TX, and Centro handhelds, and the Treo, Pre, and Pixi smartphones. The company's Palm OS powered devices from licensees including Handspring, Sony (CLIE), IBM (WorkPad), and Symbol Technologies.
