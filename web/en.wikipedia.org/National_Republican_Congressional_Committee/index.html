National Republican Congressional Committee

The National Republican Congressional Committee (NRCC) is the campaign committee of the Republican Party devoted to electing Republicans to the United States House of Representatives. Founded in 1866, it is one of the Republican Party's "Hill committees", alongside the National Republican Senatorial Committee and the Republican National Committee.

Activities

The NRCC recruits candidates, raises funds, provides campaign strategy, polling, advertising and legal support, and runs independent expenditure campaigns in competitive districts. Its programs include the "Young Guns" initiative for promising challengers and open-seat candidates, and the "Patriot Program" supporting vulnerable incumbents.

Leadership

The committee is chaired by a Republican member of the House elected by the House Republican Conference. Recent chairs have included Tom Emmer (2019–2023) and Richard Hudson, who took over for the 2024 cycle. The NRCC is headquartered in Washington, D.C., near the Capitol.

History

The NRCC played a central role in the Republican capture of the House in 1994, 2010 and 2022, and in defending Republican majorities in other cycles. Its Democratic counterpart is the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC).
