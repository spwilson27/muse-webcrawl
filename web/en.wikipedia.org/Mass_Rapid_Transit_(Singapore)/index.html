The Mass Rapid Transit system, locally known by the initialism MRT, is a rapid transit system in Singapore and the island country's principal mode of railway transportation. The system commenced operations on 7 November 1987 with its first section between Yio Chu Kang and Toa Payoh, making it the second-oldest metro system in Southeast Asia. The network has since expanded to form the backbone of Singapore's public transport, alongside public buses and the Light Rail Transit (LRT) feeder systems.

Network

By the mid-2020s the MRT comprised six lines — the North South and East West lines (operated by SMRT), the fully automated North East and Circle lines, the Downtown line (operated by SBS Transit), and the Thomson–East Coast line — with further extensions and the Cross Island and Jurong Region lines under construction. Nearly all lines run underground in the central area and on elevated viaducts elsewhere; several lines are fully driverless, among the longest automated metro networks in the world.

History and operations

Debated since the 1960s, the MRT was championed by the government as the core of an all-day, high-capacity transport policy despite cost concerns; construction began in 1983 as Singapore's largest public works project. Trains are owned by the Land Transport Authority and operated under licence by SMRT Trains and SBS Transit. Fares are distance-based and paid with EZ-Link or SimplyGo cards. Daily ridership runs to well over three million trips on the rail network, and reliability, cleanliness, and bans on eating and drinking in stations and trains are hallmarks of the system.
