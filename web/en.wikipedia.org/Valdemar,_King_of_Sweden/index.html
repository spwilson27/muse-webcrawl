Valdemar Birgersson (1239 – 26 December 1302), also called Valdemar I, was King of Sweden from 1250 to 1275. The son of the powerful regent Birger Jarl, he was elected king as a child after the death of his uncle Eric XI.

Reign

Real power during Valdemar's minority lay with his father, who founded Stockholm and consolidated the kingdom. As an adult, Valdemar's reign was marked by disputes with his younger brothers Magnus, Eric and Bengt, who held large duchies. His marriage to Sophia of Denmark and later scandal involving her sister Jutta, a former nun, damaged his standing.

Deposition

In 1275 his brothers rose against him; after defeat at the Battle of Hova, Valdemar was forced to abdicate in favour of Magnus, who became King Magnus III ("Ladulås"). Valdemar spent years trying to recover parts of the kingdom with Danish and Norwegian support, holding Götaland intermittently.

Imprisonment and death

In 1288 Magnus had Valdemar arrested and confined to Nyköping Castle, where he lived under comfortable house arrest until his death in 1302. His deposition began a long period of rivalry within the Folkung dynasty between the lines of Magnus and Valdemar.
