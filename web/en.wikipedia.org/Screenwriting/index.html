Screenwriting (also called scriptwriting) is the art and craft of writing scripts for mass media such as feature films, television productions or video games. It is often a freelance profession. Screenwriters are responsible for researching the story, developing the narrative, writing the script, screenplay and dialogues, and delivering it, in the required format, to development executives. Screenwriters therefore have great influence over the creative direction and emotional impact of the screenplay and, arguably, of the finished film.

Screenwriters can pitch story ideas, be commissioned to write on assignment, adapt existing works such as novels and plays, or write spec scripts — screenplays written without a commission in the hope of selling them. In Hollywood, the vast majority of screenplays are first written on spec. Successful screenwriters may also be hired for uncredited "rewrites" and polishes of other writers' drafts; credit is determined by the Writers Guild of America under its credit system.

Process and theory

Development usually proceeds through a logline (a one-sentence summary), a synopsis, a treatment (a prose outline), a step outline, and finally the screenplay itself, which is then revised repeatedly — sometimes with different writers — through production drafts. The most widely taught structural model is the three-act structure set out by Syd Field in 1979; other prominent teachers include Robert McKee (Story, 1997), who emphasises "controlling idea" and scene value, and Linda Seger. Craft emphasis falls on externalising story in visual action and dialogue: unlike a novel, a screenplay is a blueprint for a collaborative industrial product, describing only what can be seen and heard.

Education and profession

Many screenwriters are self-taught; others attend film school programmes at institutions such as USC, NYU, AFI and UCLA. Since 1935, Hollywood screenwriters have been represented by the Writers Guild of America (WGA), which negotiates the Minimum Basic Agreement with studios, administers credits and residuals, and has mounted major strikes in 1988, 2007–08 and 2023, the last two largely over compensation for streaming ("new media") content.
