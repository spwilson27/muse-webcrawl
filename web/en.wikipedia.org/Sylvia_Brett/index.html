Sylvia Leonora, Lady Brooke (born Sylvia Leonora Brett; 25 February 1885 – 27 November 1971), also known as Sylvia, Ranee of Sarawak, was an English aristocrat who became the consort of Sir Charles Vyner Brooke, the third and last of the White Rajahs of Sarawak on the island of Borneo.

Early life

Sylvia Brett was the second daughter of Reginald Brett, 2nd Viscount Esher, a courtier and adviser to Edward VII and George V. She grew up in the orbit of the royal court at Windsor, in a family of considerable political and social influence, and moved in literary and artistic circles in London.

Marriage and Sarawak

In 1911 she married Charles Vyner Brooke, heir to the raj of Sarawak, a state ruled by the Brooke family since 1841. On his accession in 1917 she became Ranee of Sarawak. Sylvia travelled widely between England and Kuching, the Sarawak capital, and cultivated a flamboyant public image — she was an accomplished self-publicist, known for stylish dress and for keeping exotic pets. She wrote several books, including her autobiography Sylvia of Sarawak (1936) and Queen of the Headhunters (1970), which presented Sarawak to British readers in romantic terms.

The succession question and later life

Sylvia actively promoted the claims of her three daughters — known to the press as the "Sarawak princesses" — which fed disputes over the succession to the raj, since Sarawak's constitution favoured male heirs, notably her nephew Anthony Brooke. After the Second World War and the Japanese occupation of Sarawak, Vyner Brooke ceded Sarawak to Britain as a Crown Colony in 1946, ending a century of Brooke rule. Sylvia lived thereafter mainly in England and Barbados and died in London in 1971.
