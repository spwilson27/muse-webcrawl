Canadian Caper

The "Canadian Caper" was the joint covert rescue by the Government of Canada and the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) of six American diplomats who had evaded capture during the seizure of the United States embassy in Tehran, Iran, on 4 November 1979, when the American embassy was occupied by Iranian revolutionaries.

Background

When Iranian students stormed the U.S. embassy during the Iran hostage crisis, six diplomats escaped and eventually found refuge at the homes of Canadian officials, principally Ambassador Ken Taylor and immigration officer John Sheardown. The Canadian government agreed to shelter them and to help them leave Iran.

The operation

Canada issued genuine Canadian passports under false identities for the six Americans — an extraordinary step approved by Parliament in a secret session. The CIA, through officer Tony Mendez, built a cover story: the six were a Canadian film crew scouting locations in Iran for a science-fiction movie called Argo, to be produced by a fake Hollywood company, Studio Six Productions. On 27 January 1980, the group passed through Mehrabad Airport in Tehran and flew to Zurich, ending the caper successfully.

Aftermath and recognition

The Canadian role was kept quiet until the hostages' release in 1981 because of fears for the remaining captives. Ambassador Taylor was appointed to the Order of Canada and received the U.S. Congressional Gold Medal. The episode was later dramatised in the 2012 film Argo, which won the Academy Award for Best Picture; the film's fictionalised account drew criticism in Canada for minimising the Canadian contribution.
