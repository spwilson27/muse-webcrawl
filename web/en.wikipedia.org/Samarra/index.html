Samarra (Arabic: سامراء, Sāmarrāʾ) is a city in Iraq, standing on the east bank of the River Tigris in the Saladin Governorate, about 125 kilometres north of Baghdad. For much of the ninth century it served as the capital of the Abbasid Caliphate, and its extensive ruins — including the spiral Malwiya Minaret — form one of the world's great archaeological landscapes. The modern city is a major pilgrimage site for Shia Muslims.

History

The area has been settled since prehistoric times; the Samarra culture of the late Neolithic takes its name from finds nearby. In 836 the Abbasid caliph al-Mu'tasim, seeking to distance his Turkish military slaves from the population of Baghdad, founded a new capital at Samarra. His successors built an immense planned city of palaces, mosques, racecourses and military cantonments stretching for miles along the Tigris. The Great Mosque of Samarra, built under al-Mutawakkil around 848–852, was then the largest mosque in the world; its 52-metre spiral minaret, the Malwiya, remains the city's emblem.

After the "Anarchy at Samarra" and the caliphate's decline, the court returned to Baghdad in 892 and Samarra shrank drastically, though it retained religious importance. The city is sacred to Twelver Shia Islam: the al-Askari Shrine contains the tombs of the tenth and eleventh Imams, Ali al-Hadi and Hasan al-Askari, and the adjoining mosque marks the place associated with the occultation of the twelfth Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi. The bombing of the shrine's golden dome in February 2006 triggered widespread sectarian violence in Iraq; a second attack destroyed its minarets in 2007, and the shrine has since been rebuilt.

Archaeology

UNESCO inscribed the Samarra Archaeological City as a World Heritage Site in 2007, simultaneously placing it on the list of sites in danger. The Abbasid city's plan, largely unencumbered by later building, preserves palaces such as the Jawsaq al-Khaqani and the Qasr al-Ashiq, along with stucco styles — the "Samarra styles" — that influenced Islamic art across the medieval world.
