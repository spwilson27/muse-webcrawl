The River Thames is a river flowing through southern England, including London. At 346 km (215 miles) it is the longest river entirely in England and the second-longest in the United Kingdom. It rises in the Cotswolds and flows east through Oxford, Reading, Windsor and the capital before reaching the North Sea at the Thames Estuary.

Course

The Thames rises at Thames Head in Gloucestershire, flows through the university city of Oxford — where it is known locally as the Isis — and is joined by tributaries including the Cherwell, Kennet, Wey and Medway. Below Teddington Lock it becomes tidal (the Tideway) as it passes through central London, past Westminster, the City and Greenwich, to the estuary beyond the Thames Barrier flood defence.

History

The river was the making of London: the Romans founded Londinium at the lowest bridging point around AD 47–50. For centuries the Thames was the city's main highway and its port the busiest in the world. Industrial pollution made it a notorious health hazard — the Great Stink of 1858 forced the construction of Joseph Bazalgette's sewer network, and by 1957 the river was declared biologically dead; sustained cleanup since has brought back fish, seals and even seahorses. Frost fairs were held on the frozen river in the Little Ice Age, the last in 1814.

Crossings and culture

London Bridge, Tower Bridge, Westminster Bridge and the Millennium Bridge are among its many crossings. The annual Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race is rowed on the Championship Course between Putney and Mortlake, and the river hosts the Henley Royal Regatta. The Thames Path follows its length as a National Trail.
