Savannah River Site

The Savannah River Site (SRS) is a United States Department of Energy nuclear reservation near Aiken, South Carolina, on the Savannah River. Established in 1950 during the Cold War, the roughly 310-square-mile site produced weapons-grade plutonium and tritium for the US nuclear arsenal.

History

The Atomic Energy Commission selected the site in 1950; the towns of Ellenton and Dunbarton were displaced to make way for it. Five production reactors (R, P, L, K, and C) and two chemical separations plants ("canyons") operated from the 1950s. The last reactor shut down in the early 1990s as the Cold War ended.

Present day

SRS is now primarily an environmental cleanup site, managing legacy radioactive waste in underground tanks, vitrifying high-level waste at the Defense Waste Processing Facility, and operating the Savannah River National Laboratory. It also hosts tritium facilities supporting the current stockpile and the MOX/plutonium disposition programmes. Large parts of the site serve as a protected refuge for wildlife, including red-cockaded woodpeckers.
