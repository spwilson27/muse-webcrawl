Strategic planning

Strategic planning is an organization's process of defining its strategy or direction and making decisions on allocating its resources to attain strategic goals. It may also extend to control mechanisms for guiding the implementation of the strategy. Strategic planning became prominent in corporations during the 1960s and remains an important aspect of strategic management. It is executed by strategic planners or strategists, who involve many parties and research sources in their analysis of the organization and its relationship to the environment in which it competes.

The strategy includes a long-term vision of where the organization wants to be and objectives that are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART). Strategic planning is typically conducted over a three to five year horizon and is distinct from operational planning, which focuses on shorter-term activities.

Tools and approaches

Common analytical tools used in strategic planning include SWOT analysis (strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats), PEST analysis (political, economic, social, and technological factors), Porter's five forces analysis of industry competition, and scenario planning. Approaches such as the balanced scorecard translate strategy into operational objectives and measures across financial, customer, internal process, and learning perspectives.

Henry Mintzberg criticized rigid strategic planning, arguing that strategy often emerges from patterns of action rather than formal plans, and distinguishing deliberate strategy from emergent strategy. Other critics note that formal planning can stifle innovation and adapt poorly in rapidly changing environments, leading to interest in adaptive and agile approaches.

Process

A typical strategic planning process includes determining the current position, clarifying mission and vision, analyzing internal and external environments, formulating strategic options, choosing strategies, setting goals and objectives, developing action plans and budgets, and monitoring progress with periodic review and adjustment.
