Operation Hailstone was a massive naval airstrike and surface action launched by the United States Navy against the Japanese naval and logistics base at Truk Lagoon in the Caroline Islands on 17–18 February 1944. Truk, sometimes called the "Gibraltar of the Pacific", was the main anchorage of the Imperial Japanese Navy's Combined Fleet and a key forward base. The attack, carried out by Task Force 58, the fast carrier task force under Vice Admiral Marc Mitscher, devastated Japanese shipping and airpower in the lagoon and is often compared in scale and effect to the attack on Pearl Harbor.

Background

Following the capture of Kwajalein in the Marshall Islands, Admiral Raymond Spruance directed a carrier raid on Truk to cover the invasion of Eniwetok and to neutralize the threat Truk posed to Allied operations. Unknown to the Americans, Admiral Koga Mineichi had already withdrawn the Combined Fleet's heavy units westward to Palau and Singapore, but the lagoon still held dozens of warships, auxiliaries, merchant vessels and some 365 aircraft.

The attack

Before dawn on 17 February, fighters from nine carriers swept the atoll's airfields, destroying Japanese air cover. Waves of dive bombers and torpedo planes then attacked the anchored shipping, while American surface ships and submarines intercepted vessels attempting to escape through the lagoon's passes. Over two days the attackers sank roughly 45–50 ships totalling about 200,000 tons — including the light cruisers Agano, Katori and Naka, several destroyers and large quantities of fuel and supplies — and destroyed some 250–275 aircraft. American losses were about 25 aircraft and 40 men; the carrier Intrepid was damaged by a torpedo.

Aftermath

Hailstone effectively eliminated Truk as a major naval base. Rather than invade, the Allies bypassed and blockaded it, leaving its large garrison to wither for the rest of the war. The lagoon's sunken wrecks are today one of the world's most famous wreck-diving sites, often called the "Ghost Fleet of Truk Lagoon".
