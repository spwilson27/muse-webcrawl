Napoleon Bonaparte (1769–1821) was a French military and political leader who rose to prominence during the French Revolution, seized power in 1799, and crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804. Through a series of brilliant campaigns he dominated Europe for a decade, spread revolutionary legal and administrative reforms — and led France into wars that cost millions of lives.

Rise

Born in Corsica to a family of minor Italian nobility, Napoleon trained as an artillery officer and made his name at the siege of Toulon (1793) and by suppressing a royalist rising in Paris in 1795. His Italian campaign of 1796–97 and the expedition to Egypt (1798) made him a national hero. In the coup of 18 Brumaire (November 1799) he overthrew the Directory and became First Consul, consolidating power while reforming France: the Napoleonic Code (1804), the Bank of France, the lycée school system, and a reconciliation with the Catholic Church.

Empire and war

Crowned emperor in 1804, Napoleon crushed successive coalitions at Austerlitz (1805), Jena (1806), and Wagram (1809), redrawing the map of Europe and placing relatives on thrones. His Continental System tried to strangle British trade; the Peninsular War in Spain bled his armies. The invasion of Russia in 1812 was catastrophic — the Grande Armée of over 600,000 was destroyed by battle, cold, and hunger. Defeated at Leipzig in 1813, he abdicated in 1814 and was exiled to Elba. He escaped in 1815 for the "Hundred Days", but was finally defeated at Waterloo on 18 June 1815.

Exile and legacy

Napoleon spent his last years as a British prisoner on the remote Atlantic island of Saint Helena, dictating his memoirs, and died there in 1821; his remains were returned to Paris in 1840 and rest under the dome of Les Invalides. His legal code still underpins law in much of Europe and beyond, and his campaigns are studied in military academies worldwide — as is the controversy over his record: reformer and liberator to some, warmonger and tyrant who restored slavery in French colonies to others.
