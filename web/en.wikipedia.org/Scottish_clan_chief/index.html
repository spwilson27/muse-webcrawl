Scottish clan chief

A Scottish clan chief (Scottish Gaelic: ceannard) is the recognised head of a Scottish clan. Historically, the chief was the hereditary leader of the clan, holding authority over its members, lands and military following. The office combined kinship leadership with feudal landholding, and chiefs led their clansmen in the many conflicts of the Scottish Highlands, including the Jacobite risings.

Recognition

Today, clan chiefs are recognised by the Lord Lyon King of Arms, the heraldic authority of Scotland. Recognition is usually connected with the right to bear the chiefly coat of arms of the clan, undifferenced. A chief is typically addressed as "Chief" or by the territorial designation of the clan, and members of the chief's family and clan gentlemen historically wore the chief's crest within a strap-and-buckle badge.

Role today

Modern chiefs no longer hold legal jurisdiction over clan members — the last remnants of heritable jurisdictions were abolished after the Jacobite rising of 1745 — but many remain active ceremonial and social heads of their clans, presiding over clan societies, gatherings and Highland games worldwide.
