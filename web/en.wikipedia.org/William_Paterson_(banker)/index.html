William Paterson (banker)

William Paterson (April 1658 – 22 January 1719) was a Scottish trader and banker, a founder of the Bank of England, and the promoter of the ill-fated Darien scheme, Scotland's attempt to establish a colony in Panama.

Bank of England

Born in Tinwald, Dumfriesshire, Paterson prospered as a merchant in London and among the English factories of the Bahamas and the Netherlands. In 1691 he proposed a national bank for England modeled on Dutch and Italian examples; with the support of Charles Montagu, the plan was enacted in 1694, creating the Bank of England and lending the government £1.2 million at 8 percent to finance the war against France. Paterson served as a director in the Bank's first year but sold out after disputes with colleagues.

The Darien scheme

Paterson then persuaded Scotland to found the Company of Scotland (1695) and invest in a colony at Darien in the Isthmus of Panama, which he envisioned as a trading bridge between Atlantic and Pacific. The colony of New Caledonia, launched in 1698, collapsed under disease, Spanish hostility, and English indifference, consuming roughly a quarter of Scotland's liquid capital. The disaster fed directly into the political pressure for the Acts of Union 1707, under which investors were compensated. Paterson spent his last years lobbying for trade and colonial projects; he died in Westminster in 1719.
