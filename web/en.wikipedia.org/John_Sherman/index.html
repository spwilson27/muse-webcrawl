John Sherman

Born: 10 May 1823, Lancaster, Ohio, U.S.
Died: 22 October 1900 (aged 77), Washington, D.C.
Offices: U.S. Senator from Ohio; Secretary of the Treasury (1877–1881); Secretary of State (1897–1898)
Known for: Sherman Antitrust Act of 1890

John Sherman (May 10, 1823 – October 22, 1900) was an American politician from Ohio who served in both houses of Congress and as Secretary of the Treasury and Secretary of State. He was the principal author of the Sherman Antitrust Act of 1890.

Early career

Sherman was the younger brother of Civil War general William Tecumseh Sherman. A lawyer and Whig turned Republican, he entered the House of Representatives in 1855 and became a leading voice on finance. In 1861 he moved to the Senate, where he helped design the National Banking Acts and the financing of the Union war effort.

Treasury Secretary

As President Rutherford B. Hayes's Secretary of the Treasury (1877–1881), Sherman managed the resumption of specie payments, returning the dollar to the gold standard in 1879. He returned to the Senate in 1881 and repeatedly sought the Republican presidential nomination.

Antitrust and later service

The Sherman Antitrust Act, passed almost unanimously in 1890, outlawed combinations in restraint of trade and remains the foundation of U.S. antitrust law, though Sherman's own emphasis was on federal power to act against monopolies. He also sponsored the Sherman Silver Purchase Act of 1890.

In 1897 President McKinley made Sherman Secretary of State, partly to open his Senate seat for Mark Hanna; failing in health and memory, Sherman resigned in 1898 during the Spanish-American War crisis. He died in Washington in 1900.
