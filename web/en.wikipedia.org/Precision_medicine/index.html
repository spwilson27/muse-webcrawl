Precision medicine

Precision medicine is a medical model that proposes the customization of healthcare, with medical decisions, treatments, practices, or products tailored to a subgroup of patients, instead of a one-drug-fits-all model. In precision medicine, diagnostic testing is often employed for selecting appropriate therapies based on a patient's genetic content or other molecular or cellular analysis.

The approach overlaps with the older term personalized medicine, but emphasises population subgroups defined by biomarkers rather than treatment designed for a single individual. It depends on advances in genomics, proteomics, medical imaging and health informatics.

Applications

Oncology is the most developed field: tumours are profiled for mutations such as HER2 amplification in breast cancer or EGFR mutations in lung cancer, and matched to targeted drugs such as trastuzumab or osimertinib. Pharmacogenomics guides dosing of drugs including clopidogrel and warfarin according to variants in genes such as CYP2C19 and CYP2C9. National programmes, such as the United States' All of Us Research Program and Genomics England, collect genomic and health-record data from large volunteer cohorts.

Criticism

Critics note uneven clinical benefit outside oncology, high costs of sequencing and targeted drugs, risks to genetic privacy, and under-representation of non-European ancestries in reference databases, which can reduce accuracy for other populations.
