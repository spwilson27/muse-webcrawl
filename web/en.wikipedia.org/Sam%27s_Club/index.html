Sam's Club is an American chain of membership-only retail warehouse clubs owned and operated by Walmart Inc. Founded in 1983 and named after Walmart founder Sam Walton, the chain sells groceries, general merchandise and bulk goods at low margins to individual members and small businesses. It competes principally with Costco Wholesale and BJ's Wholesale Club.

History

The first Sam's Wholesale Club opened on April 7, 1983 in Midwest City, Oklahoma. The concept copied the warehouse-club model pioneered by Price Club in California: charge members an annual fee, stock a limited assortment of large-pack merchandise on pallet racking in no-frills warehouses, and pass purchasing scale into low prices. Sam's Club expanded rapidly across the United States through the 1980s and 1990s, absorbing rivals including Pace Membership Warehouse.

Sam's Club has historically ranked second in warehouse-club sales in the United States, behind Costco. In January 2018 Walmart abruptly closed 63 Sam's Club locations, converting some into e-commerce fulfilment centres, as part of a shift toward better-performing, higher-income trade areas. The chain has since invested in technology such as its Scan & Go mobile checkout, curbside pickup and an exit-arch system that verifies purchases using computer vision.

Operations

Clubs average around 134,000 square feet and carry roughly 4,000 stock-keeping units, far fewer than a Walmart supercenter. Two membership tiers, Club and Plus, offer tiered cash-back and shipping benefits. Sam's Club's principal private label is Member's Mark, spanning food, household goods, apparel and electronics. The chain also operates in Mexico in partnership with Walmart de México, and it withdrew from markets such as Canada and Brazil.
