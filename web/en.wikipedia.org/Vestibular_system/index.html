Vestibular system

The vestibular system is the sensory system that provides the brain with information about motion, head position, and spatial orientation, and is the main contributor to the sense of balance. It is located in the inner ear, in a structure called the vestibular apparatus adjacent to the cochlea.

Structure

The apparatus consists of two kinds of organs. The three semicircular canals, arranged roughly at right angles to one another, detect rotational acceleration of the head: turning movements displace the endolymph fluid within each canal, bending hair cells in a gelatinous structure called the cupula. The otolith organs — the utricle and saccule — detect linear acceleration and the direction of gravity, using small calcium carbonate crystals (otoconia) embedded in a gelatinous membrane over hair cells. Signals travel via the vestibular division of the vestibulocochlear nerve (cranial nerve VIII) to the vestibular nuclei of the brainstem and the cerebellum.

Function

The vestibular system drives reflexes that stabilise vision and posture, most notably the vestibulo-ocular reflex, which moves the eyes to compensate for head movement so that the visual image stays steady. Its input is combined with vision and proprioception to maintain equilibrium.

Disorders

Dysfunction produces vertigo, dizziness, nausea, and imbalance. Common disorders include benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (caused by displaced otoconia), Ménière's disease, labyrinthitis, and vestibular neuritis. Motion sickness arises from conflicting sensory signals involving vestibular input.
