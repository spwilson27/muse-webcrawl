Links (golf)

A links is the oldest style of golf course, first developed in Scotland. The word comes from the Scots links, meaning "rising ground" or "sandy hills", which in turn derives from the Old English hlinc (rising ground, ridge). Links land is typically characterised by dunes, ridges and depressions, and open exposed terrain with sandy soil that drains quickly and supports little more than coarse grass and gorse.

Characteristics

A true links course is located on the coast, on soil unfit for farming, and shaped by wind and sea. Features include undulating fairways, few if any trees, deep pot bunkers, thick rough of marram grass, and firm, fast greens. Because the ground is links land, golf is played "along the ground" as much as through the air, with the wind a major factor. Courses such as the Old Course at St Andrews, Royal St George's, and Royal Birkdale are classic examples and regular hosts of The Open Championship.

The term "links" has been loosely applied to any golf course, but purists reserve it for seaside courses on genuine links land; inland courses styled as links rarely share the true soil and conditions.
