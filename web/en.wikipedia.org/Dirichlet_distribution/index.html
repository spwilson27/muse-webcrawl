Dirichlet distribution

Samples on the 2-simplex for different concentration parameters.

In probability and statistics, the Dirichlet distribution (after Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet), often denoted Dir(α), is a family of continuous multivariate probability distributions parameterized by a vector α of positive reals. It is the multivariate generalization of the beta distribution and the conjugate prior distribution of the categorical and multinomial distributions in Bayesian statistics.

Definition

The Dirichlet distribution of order K ≥ 2 with parameters α₁, …, α_K > 0 has probability density function, on the (K−1)-dimensional probability simplex (vectors x with xᵢ ≥ 0 and Σxᵢ = 1),

f(x; α) = (1 / B(α)) Π xᵢ^(αᵢ−1)

where B(α) is the multivariate beta function, expressed in terms of gamma functions: B(α) = Π Γ(αᵢ) / Γ(Σ αᵢ). The parameters αᵢ are called concentration parameters; symmetric cases with all αᵢ equal give mass concentrated near the center (large α), near the corners (α < 1), or uniformly (α = 1).

Properties

Each marginal component Xᵢ follows a beta distribution, and components are negatively correlated. The distribution is closed under aggregation: merging categories of a Dirichlet vector yields another Dirichlet vector with summed parameters. It also arises by normalizing independent gamma-distributed random variables. If Yᵢ ~ Gamma(αᵢ, 1) independently, then the normalized vector (Y₁/ΣY, …, Y_K/ΣY) is Dirichlet distributed.

Applications

As the conjugate prior for multinomial data, the Dirichlet distribution is fundamental in Bayesian models of categorical data, Bayesian networks, and mixture models. It underlies latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA), a topic model for text, and appears in population genetics (as a model of allele frequencies closely related to the Balding–Nichols model), geology of compositional data, and Bayesian nonparametric statistics via the Dirichlet process.
