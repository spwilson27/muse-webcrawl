Marketing
Core framework: the 4 Ps (product, price, place, promotion)
Key figures: Philip Kotler, E. Jerome McCarthy, Theodore Levitt
Related: advertising, branding, market research

Marketing is the act of satisfying and retaining customers through the process of identifying, anticipating, and meeting their needs profitably. The American Marketing Association defines it as "the activity, set of institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large." Marketing spans market research, product development, pricing, distribution, advertising, and customer relationship management.


The marketing mix (four Ps)

History

Marketing practices are as old as trade itself, but the discipline emerged formally in the early 20th century as mass production created the need to stimulate demand. Orientations evolved from a production focus ("sell what we make") through a sales orientation to the modern marketing concept—popularized from the 1950s—that firms should make what customers need. Theodore Levitt's 1960 essay "Marketing Myopia" urged companies to define themselves by customer needs rather than products. Philip Kotler's textbooks codified the field for generations of managers.

The marketing mix

E. Jerome McCarthy grouped the marketer's tools into four controllable variables—the four Ps: product (the offering and its features, quality, and branding), price (list price, discounts, and financing), place (channels and distribution), and promotion (advertising, sales promotion, public relations, and personal selling). Later extensions added people, process, and physical evidence for services. Under the customer-oriented restatement proposed by Robert Lauterborn, these correspond to customer needs, cost, convenience, and communication.

Strategies and modern practice

Core strategic tools include market segmentation, targeting, and positioning (STP); branding; and the product life cycle. Relationship marketing and customer lifetime value shifted emphasis from single transactions to retention. The internet transformed practice through search and social media marketing, content marketing, influencer marketing, programmatic advertising, and data-driven personalization, while also raising concerns about privacy, surveillance advertising, and deceptive practices, leading to regulation such as the EU's GDPR.
