Geospatial intelligence

Satellite imagery is a principal source for geospatial intelligence.

Geospatial intelligence (GEOINT) is the intelligence discipline concerned with the exploitation and analysis of geographically determined information about human activity on Earth. It is derived from imagery, signals, measurement and signature intelligence, and other sources, and describes, assesses, and visually depicts physical features and geographically referenced activities.

Definition and sources

In United States law and practice, GEOINT is defined as the exploitation and analysis of imagery and geospatial information to describe, assess, and visually depict physical features and geographically referenced activities on Earth. Its sources include satellite and airborne imagery (IMINT), maps and terrain data, GPS-derived coordinates, and increasingly open-source geographic data.

Organisations

The principal U.S. agency for GEOINT is the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA), formed in 2003 from the National Imagery and Mapping Agency. Allied nations maintain comparable organisations and share products through intelligence alliances. GEOINT supports military operations, disaster response, treaty verification, and humanitarian relief.

Methods

Analysts combine remote sensing, photogrammetry, geographic information systems (GIS), and data fusion techniques. Modern practice uses automated change detection, three-dimensional terrain modelling, and machine learning to sift the enormous volumes of imagery produced by reconnaissance satellites, drones, and commercial providers.
