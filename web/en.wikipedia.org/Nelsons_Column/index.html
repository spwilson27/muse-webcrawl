Nelson's Column

Nelson's Column
Location: Trafalgar Square, London
Completed: 1843
Height: 51.6 m (169 ft)

Nelson's Column is a monument in Trafalgar Square in the City of Westminster, Central London, built to commemorate Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson, who died at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805.

Construction

The monument was designed by William Railton in 1838–1839 and built by Peto & Grissell between 1840 and 1843. The fluted Corinthian column, made of Dartmoor granite, is topped by a sandstone statue of Nelson sculpted by Edward Hodges Baily. Four bronze panel reliefs on the pedestal depict Nelson's great victories, and four bronze lions by Edwin Landseer were added at the base in 1867.

Significance

The column is one of London's best-known landmarks and the focal point of Trafalgar Square, used for public celebrations, protests, and the city's annual Christmas tree gift from Norway.
