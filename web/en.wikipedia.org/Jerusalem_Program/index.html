Jerusalem Program

Adopted1951 (revised 1968, 2004)
BodyWorld Zionist Organization
PredecessorBasel Program (1897)

The Jerusalem Program is the ideological platform of the World Zionist Organization, adopted in 1951 at the 23rd World Zionist Congress in Jerusalem, succeeding the Basel Program of 1897 as the defining statement of the aims of Zionism after the establishment of the State of Israel.

Contents

The original 1951 program set out tasks for the Zionist movement in the era of statehood: the strengthening of the State of Israel, the ingathering of exiles (aliyah) from all countries, and the fostering of Jewish national unity and identity in the Diaspora. A revised version adopted in 1968, after the Six-Day War, declared "the unity of the Jewish people and the centrality of Israel in Jewish life" and added aims including the strengthening of Israel as a Jewish, Zionist and democratic state, mass aliyah, Jewish education worldwide, defence of Jewish rights, and settlement as an expression of practical Zionism.

2004 revision

A further revision in 2004 reaffirmed Zionism's foundations: the Jewish people's historic connection to the Land of Israel, Israel as the national home of the Jewish people with Jerusalem as its capital, the promotion of aliyah, and the struggle against antisemitism and assimilation.
