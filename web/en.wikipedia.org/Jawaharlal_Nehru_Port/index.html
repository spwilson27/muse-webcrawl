Jawaharlal Nehru Port

Jawaharlal Nehru Port (JNPT)
Also known as: Nhava Sheva Port
Location: Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra, India
Operated by: Jawaharlal Nehru Port Authority
Opened: 26 May 1989
Type: Container port
Significance: One of India's largest container ports

Jawaharlal Nehru Port, also known as Nhava Sheva Port and abbreviated JNPT, is a major container port located at Nhava Sheva in Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra, on the eastern shore of Mumbai Harbour across from the city of Mumbai. Named after India's first Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, it was commissioned on 26 May 1989 and is administered by the Jawaharlal Nehru Port Authority under the Government of India.

Development

The port was developed to relieve congestion at the older Mumbai Port and to provide a modern, dedicated container-handling facility on India's west coast. It is connected by dedicated rail corridors and highways to the industrial hinterland of Maharashtra, Gujarat, and northern India.

Terminals

The port has multiple container terminals operated by a mix of the port authority and private concessionaires under public-private partnership models, including terminals run by DP World (Nhava Sheva International Container Terminal and Nhava Sheva India Gateway Terminal), APM Terminals (Gateway Terminals India), and PSA (Bharat Mumbai Container Terminals), along with the port's own Jawaharlal Nehru Port Container Terminal. A fourth terminal project has expanded capacity substantially.

Traffic

JNPT handles a large share of India's containerized trade — in recent years on the order of 6 million TEUs (twenty-foot equivalent units) annually — ranking among the country's busiest container gateways, alongside Mundra Port. Expansion plans, including the proposed deep-water port at Vadhavan, are intended to meet continued growth in trade.
