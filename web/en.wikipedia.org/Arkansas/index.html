Arkansas

Capital: Little Rock
Admitted to the Union: 15 June 1836 (25th)
Population: ~3.0 million
Nickname: The Natural State

Arkansas is a landlocked state in the West South Central region of the Southern United States. It is bordered by Missouri to the north, Tennessee and Mississippi to the east, Louisiana to the south, Texas to the southwest, and Oklahoma to the west. Its name derives from a French rendering of a Quapaw word meaning "land of downriver people."

Geography

The eastern border of Arkansas is formed by the Mississippi River. The state's geography ranges from the mountainous Ozark and Ouachita regions in the northwest to the low-lying Mississippi Alluvial Plain in the east. Mount Magazine, at 2,753 feet, is the state's highest point. Arkansas is known for its many rivers, lakes, and Hot Springs National Park, established in 1832 and the oldest area managed by the National Park Service.

History

Arkansas was part of the Louisiana Purchase of 1803 and became a separate territory in 1819. It was admitted to the Union as a slave state on 15 June 1836, the 25th state. Arkansas seceded during the American Civil War and was readmitted in 1868. In 1957, Little Rock became a focal point of the civil rights movement when Governor Orval Faubus attempted to block the integration of Little Rock Central High School, prompting President Dwight D. Eisenhower to send federal troops to enforce desegregation.

Economy

Agriculture — particularly rice, soybeans, and poultry — is a major part of the economy. The state is home to the headquarters of Walmart, the world's largest retailer, in Bentonville, and to Tyson Foods in Springdale.
