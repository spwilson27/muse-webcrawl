Complex manifold

FieldDifferential geometry, complex analysis
DefinitionManifold with an atlas of charts to ℂn with holomorphic transition functions
Real dimension2n (for complex dimension n)
ExamplesRiemann surfaces, complex projective space, complex tori

In differential geometry and complex analysis, a complex manifold is a manifold with an atlas of charts mapping into complex number space ℂn, such that the transition maps between overlapping charts are holomorphic functions. A complex manifold of complex dimension n is a real manifold of dimension 2n equipped with extra structure that makes complex-analytic methods available.

Definitions and structure

Equivalently, a complex manifold can be characterized by an integrable almost complex structure: an endomorphism J of the tangent bundle with J² = −1 whose Nijenhuis tensor vanishes (the Newlander–Nirenberg theorem). Complex manifolds are automatically orientable as real manifolds, and their tangent spaces carry a natural complex vector space structure at each point.

Examples

  • Riemann surfaces — one-dimensional complex manifolds, including the Riemann sphere ℂP1, elliptic curves, and higher-genus surfaces.
  • Complex projective space ℂPn and, more generally, projective algebraic varieties.
  • Complex tori ℂn/Λ, most of which are not algebraic (contrast abelian varieties).
  • Stein manifolds and complex Lie groups such as GL(n, ℂ).

Contrast with real manifolds

Not every smooth manifold can be a complex manifold: the sphere S4 admits no complex structure, and among spheres only S2 and S6 admit even almost complex structures (the existence of a complex structure on S6 is a famous open problem). Holomorphic rigidity also restricts geometry: by Liouville-type theorems, compact complex manifolds admit few global holomorphic functions, and classification questions — the Kodaira classification of surfaces, Kähler geometry, and Calabi–Yau manifolds of importance in string theory — form a rich area of research.
