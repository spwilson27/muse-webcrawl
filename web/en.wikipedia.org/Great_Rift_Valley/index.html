Great Rift Valley

LengthAbout 6,000–7,000 km (whole rift system)
ExtentsFrom Lebanon's Beqaa Valley to Mozambique
Formed byDivergent plate tectonics; separation of African (Nubian) and Somali plates
Named byJohn Walter Gregory (1890s expeditions)
Key featuresDead Sea, Red Sea, Afar Depression, Rift Valley lakes, Olduvai Gorge

The Great Rift Valley is a series of contiguous geographic trenches and rifts running about 6,000 to 7,000 kilometres from the Beqaa Valley in Lebanon south through the Red Sea and East Africa to Mozambique. The name was coined by the Scottish geologist John Walter Gregory after his 1892–93 expedition to British East Africa. The valley system records the ongoing splitting of the African continent as the Somali Plate pulls away from the Nubian Plate at a rate of a few millimetres per year.

Structure and branches

The northern part of the system includes the Dead Sea Transform and the Jordan Rift Valley, source of the Jordan River. The Red Sea and Gulf of Aden are young oceanic arms of the rift, meeting the continental East African Rift at the Afar Depression, one of the few places where an oceanic ridge can be studied on land. In Ethiopia the rift divides around the Ethiopian Highlands into the Western (Albertine) Rift — containing deep lakes Tanganyika and Albert — and the Eastern (Gregory) Rift through Kenya and Tanzania, famous for its alkaline lakes (Nakuru, Naivasha, Magadi) and volcanoes such as Kilimanjaro, Mount Kenya and Ol Doinyo Lengai.

Human origins

The rift's sediments and volcanic ash layers preserve an unmatched record of human evolution. Sites such as Olduvai Gorge (Tanzania), Koobi Fora and Turkana (Kenya), Hadar and the Middle Awash (Ethiopia) have yielded fossils of Australopithecus afarensis ("Lucy", found 1974), early Homo, and some of the oldest stone tools, earning the region its reputation as a "cradle of humankind"; several of its fossil sites are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Modern significance

The rift hosts rich geothermal resources (Kenya's Olkaria fields supply a large share of the country's electricity), soda-ash and other mineral extraction, spectacular wildlife in the Rift Valley lakes, and significant seismic and volcanic hazards for its growing population. In geological time the eastern rift will likely flood and form a new ocean basin, as the Red Sea did before it.
