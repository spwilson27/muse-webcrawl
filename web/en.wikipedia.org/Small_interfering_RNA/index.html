Small interfering RNA

An siRNA duplex guided into Argonaute within the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC).

Small interfering RNA (siRNA), sometimes known as short interfering RNA or silencing RNA, is a class of double-stranded RNA molecules, typically 20–24 base pairs in length, that interfere with the expression of specific genes through the pathway of RNA interference (RNAi). siRNAs guide the sequence-specific degradation of complementary messenger RNA, preventing its translation into protein.

Mechanism

Long double-stranded RNA is cleaved by the enzyme Dicer into short siRNA duplexes with characteristic two-nucleotide 3′ overhangs. One strand (the guide strand) is loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), whose core component is an Argonaute protein. The guide strand pairs with a fully complementary target mRNA, and Argonaute's endonuclease ("slicer") activity cleaves the target, which is then degraded by cellular exonucleases. Because the guide is perfectly matched, siRNA-mediated silencing is typically strong and specific, in contrast to microRNAs, which usually pair imperfectly and repress translation or promote decay.

Discovery

The phenomenon was first observed as post-transcriptional gene silencing in plants in the 1990s; in 1998 Andrew Fire and Craig Mello showed that double-stranded RNA triggers potent silencing in the worm Caenorhabditis elegans, work that won the 2006 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. In 2001, Thomas Tuschl's group showed that synthetic 21-nucleotide siRNAs could silence genes in mammalian cells without provoking an interferon response, opening the field to laboratory and therapeutic use.

Applications

Synthetic siRNAs are a standard research tool for knocking down gene expression. Therapeutically, the first siRNA drug, patisiran (Onpattro), was approved in 2018 for hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis, delivered in lipid nanoparticles; later approvals include givosiran, lumasiran and inclisiran, several using GalNAc conjugates that target the liver. Challenges include delivery to tissues beyond the liver, off-target effects, and immune stimulation.
