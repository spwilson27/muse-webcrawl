Comet Swift–Tuttle (formal designation 109P/Swift–Tuttle) is a large periodic comet with an orbital period of about 133 years. It is the parent body of the Perseid meteor shower, the most prolific annual meteor shower visible from Earth.

Discovery and orbit

The comet was discovered independently in July 1862 by Lewis Swift and Horace Parnell Tuttle. Its orbit was later linked to earlier apparitions, including Kegler's comet of 1737, and Chinese records may extend back to 188 BC. The comet was recovered in 1992 by Tsuruhiko Kiuchi and will return to perihelion in 2126.

Characteristics

With a nucleus some 26 kilometres across — more than twice the size of the object blamed for the extinction of the dinosaurs — Swift–Tuttle is the largest object to make repeated passes near Earth. As it approaches the Sun it sheds the stream of debris that Earth crosses each August, producing the Perseids. Long-term calculations show close approaches to Earth in coming millennia but no predicted impact.
