Speyer Cathedral (German: Speyerer Dom), officially the Imperial Cathedral Basilica of the Assumption and St Stephen, is a Roman Catholic cathedral in Speyer, Germany. Built of red sandstone, it is the largest Romanesque church in the world and a masterpiece of medieval architecture.

History

Construction began in 1030 under the Salian emperor Conrad II, who intended it as an imperial burial church; it was consecrated in 1061 under Henry IV, who later enlarged it with the first groin-vaulted nave of its scale in Europe. The crypt, the largest Romanesque columned hall crypt in Germany, holds the tombs of four Holy Roman Emperors and four kings of Germany, including Conrad II, Henry III, Henry IV and Rudolf of Habsburg. Damaged by fire in 1689 during the Nine Years' War, the cathedral was restored in the 18th and 19th centuries, when its west front was rebuilt and its interior frescoed by the Nazarene school.

Architecture

The cruciform basilica measures about 134 m in length with a nave vault 33 m high. Six towers, a dwarf gallery running around the exterior, and the monumental westwork give the building its distinctive silhouette along the Rhine.
