Lake Onega

Lake Onega (Russian: Онежское озеро, Onezhskoe ozero) is a lake in northwestern Russia, situated between Lake Ladoga and the White Sea, mostly within the Republic of Karelia with portions in Leningrad Oblast and Vologda Oblast. It is the second-largest lake in Europe after Lake Ladoga.

Geography

Lake Onega covers about 9,700 km² excluding its roughly 1,650 islands, which add some 250 km². The largest and most famous island is Kizhi, home to the Kizhi Pogost ensemble of wooden churches, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The lake is about 245 km long and 91.6 km wide, with an average depth of about 30 m. Its waters drain through the Svir River into Lake Ladoga and ultimately to the Baltic Sea via the Neva. The lake is part of the Volga–Baltic Waterway and the White Sea–Baltic Canal, making it navigable between the Baltic, the White Sea, and the Caspian basin.

Settlements and history

The principal city on the lake is Petrozavodsk, capital of Karelia, founded by Peter the Great in 1703 as an ironworks settlement. Other towns include Kondopoga and Medvezhyegorsk. The lake basin contains ancient petroglyphs on its eastern shore, carved between the 4th and 2nd millennia BC, near the Besov Nos headland. The lake's islands host historic wooden architecture, monasteries, and villages that draw tourists in summer.

Ecology

The lake supports a commercial fishery with species including vendace, smelt, perch, pike, burbot, and the endemic Onega salmon. Industrial pollution from lakeshore towns was a serious problem in the Soviet era, though water quality has improved since the 1990s.
