Nome, Alaska

State: Alaska
Region: Seward Peninsula
Population: about 3,700
Incorporated: 1901

Nome is a city in the Nome Census Area in the Unorganized Borough of the US state of Alaska. The city is located on the southern Seward Peninsula coast on Norton Sound of the Bering Sea. It lies 539 miles northwest of Anchorage and just south of the Arctic Circle region.

History

The area was long used by Iñupiaq people. Gold was discovered in Anvil Creek in 1898, sparking the Nome Gold Rush; by 1900 a tent city of some 10,000 lined the beaches, where "poor man's gold" could be panned from the sands. Nome is famous for the 1925 serum run to Nome — the "Great Race of Mercy" — in which dog sled teams relayed diphtheria antitoxin across Alaska, an event commemorated by the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race, whose finish line stands on Front Street.

Economy and transport

Nome is a regional hub for surrounding villages, with services, a hospital, and an airport; there are no roads connecting it to the rest of Alaska. Subsistence activities, mining, fishing, and tourism support the local economy.
