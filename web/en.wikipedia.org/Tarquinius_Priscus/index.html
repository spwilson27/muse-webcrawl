Lucius Tarquinius Priscus

Lucius Tarquinius Priscus, also known as Tarquin the Elder, was the legendary fifth king of Rome, reigning traditionally from 616 to 579 BC. The son of a Corinthian immigrant, Demaratus of Corinth, he was the first of Rome's Etruscan kings and is credited in the Roman tradition, preserved by Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, with major wars, public works, and institutional reforms.

Rise to power

Born Lucumo in the Etruscan city of Tarquinii, he migrated to Rome with his ambitious wife Tanaquil because Etruscan society barred the foreign-born from office. In Rome he became a confidant of King Ancus Marcius and guardian of his sons. On Ancus's death, Tarquin persuaded the people to elect him king, sending Ancus's sons away on a hunt.

Reign

Tradition credits Tarquin with victorious wars against the Latins, Sabines, and Etruscans, doubling the Senate by adding one hundred new members (minorum gentium), enlarging the cavalry, and reforming military organization. His building programme was immense: he is said to have laid out the Circus Maximus, begun the draining of the Forum valley through the Cloaca Maxima, built the Forum's shops and porticoes, and vowed the Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus on the Capitol, commenced in his reign.

Death and succession

After a reign of 38 years, Tarquin was assassinated by agents of Ancus Marcius's sons, who resented their exclusion. Tanaquil concealed his death long enough to secure the succession for her son-in-law, Servius Tullius, rather than the assassins. Tarquin's own sons, Lucius (Tarquin the Proud) and Arruns, later contended for the throne.
