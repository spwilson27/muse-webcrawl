CIELAB color space

The CIELAB color space, also known as CIE L*a*b*, is a color space defined by the International Commission on Illumination (CIE) in 1976. It expresses color as three values: L* for perceptual lightness, and a* and b* for the four unique colors of human vision — red, green, blue and yellow.

Coordinates

L* ranges from 0 (black) to 100 (white). The a* axis represents green at negative values and red at positive values; the b* axis represents blue at negative values and yellow at positive values. The coordinates are derived by a non-linear transformation of the CIE XYZ color space, designed so that equal distances correspond roughly to equal perceived color differences.

Uses

CIELAB is device-independent and widely used in printing, paint, plastics and textile industries for color specification and quality control. Color differences in Lab space are quantified by Delta E formulas, most recently CIEDE2000. It is also the working space of the ICC profile connection space and appears in software such as Adobe Photoshop's Lab mode.
