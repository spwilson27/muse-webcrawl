Nuxalk language

Nuxalk
Native to: Canada
Region: Bella Coola, British Columbia
Ethnicity: Nuxalk people
Language family: Language isolate (often grouped with Salishan)
Also known as: Bella Coola

The Nuxalk language (also known as Bella Coola) is a language isolate spoken by the Nuxalk people in and around the community of Bella Coola, British Columbia, at the mouth of the Bella Coola River. Although it is sometimes classified with the Salishan family, most specialists treat the relationship as distant or unproven; the language is surrounded geographically by Wakashan and Athabaskan languages.

Speakers and endangerment

Nuxalk is severely endangered. In the 21st century only a small number of fluent first-language speakers remain, most of them elderly, though language revitalisation efforts — including immersion classes, documentation, and teaching in the community school — are under way. In 2014 it was estimated that there were 17 fluent speakers.

Phonology

Nuxalk is famous among linguists for its complex consonant clusters and for allowing long syllabic strings with no vowels at all. A celebrated example is the word often transcribed as clhp'xwlhtlhplhhskwts', "then he had had in his possession a bunchberry plant", which contains no vowels. Nuxalk has a rich consonant inventory including ejectives and uvulars, and a small vowel system.

Grammar

Like neighbouring Northwest Coast languages, Nuxalk is polysynthetic, building long words from chains of suffixes, and has a complex system of demonstratives and person markers.
