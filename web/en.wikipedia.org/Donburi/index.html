Donburi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Donburi (丼, literally "bowl", also abbreviated to "-don" as a suffix) is a Japanese "rice-bowl dish" consisting of fish, meat, vegetables, or other ingredients simmered together and served over rice. Donburi meals are usually served in oversized rice bowls which are also called donburi. If one needs to distinguish, the dish is called donburi-mono (丼物) and the bowl is called donburi-bachi (丼鉢).

Common types

  • Gyūdon — beef and onion simmered in a sweet soy-based sauce.
  • Katsudon — a breaded, deep-fried pork cutlet (tonkatsu) simmered with egg and onions.
  • Oyakodon — chicken and egg simmered together in a dashi-based sauce; the name means "parent-and-child bowl".
  • Tendon — tempura shrimp and vegetables with a sweet sauce.
  • Unadon — grilled eel (unagi) glazed with a sweet soy sauce.
  • Kaisendon — raw seafood, similar to sashimi, served over rice.
  • Tekkadon and negitorodon — raw tuna, plain or with scallion.

Sauce and preparation

The simmering sauce varies according to season, ingredients, region, and taste. A typical sauce consists of dashi flavored with soy sauce and mirin. Donburi is a staple of casual restaurants and fast-food chains in Japan, valued for being filling, quick, and inexpensive.

History

Donburi developed during the Edo period, when serving toppings over rice in a single large bowl became a convenient meal for workers and theatre-goers. Unadon is often cited as among the earliest forms, followed by the many varieties that appeared in the Meiji era as meat eating became common in Japan.
