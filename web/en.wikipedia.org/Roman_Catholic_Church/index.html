The Catholic Church, also called the Roman Catholic Church, is the largest Christian church, with about 1.4 billion baptised members worldwide. Led by the pope, the Bishop of Rome, it teaches that it is the one, holy, catholic and apostolic church founded by Jesus Christ, with its bishops the successors of the apostles and the pope the successor of Saint Peter.

Beliefs and practice

Catholic doctrine is set out in the Catechism of the Catholic Church: belief in one God as Trinity, the Incarnation, salvation through Christ, and the seven sacraments — Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders and Matrimony. The Mass, centred on the Eucharist (in which Catholics affirm transubstantiation), is the principal act of worship. Devotion to Mary and veneration of saints, pilgrimage, monastic life and works of charity are characteristic features, as is the church's social teaching on the poor and the sanctity of life.

History

The church traces unbroken continuity to the first-century apostolic community. It became the state church of the Roman Empire in the 4th century, preserved learning through the medieval West, and split with the Eastern churches in the Great Schism of 1054. The Protestant Reformation of the 16th century divided Western Christianity; the church responded with the Council of Trent and the Catholic Reformation, and spread worldwide through missionary orders such as the Jesuits and Franciscans. The Second Vatican Council (1962–65) reformed the liturgy and opened ecumenical dialogue.

Organisation

The church comprises the Latin Church and 23 Eastern Catholic Churches in communion with Rome, organised into dioceses under bishops, parishes, and religious orders. Its central administration, the Roman Curia, sits in Vatican City, the world's smallest state. Catholic institutions run the world's largest non-governmental network of schools, universities, hospitals and charities. In recent decades the church has confronted a global clerical sexual-abuse crisis and sharply differing rates of practice between Europe and the growing churches of Africa and Asia.
