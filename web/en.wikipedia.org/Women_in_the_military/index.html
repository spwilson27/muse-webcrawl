Women in the military

Women in the military have served in armed forces throughout history, in roles ranging from camp followers and nurses to combat soldiers and senior commanders. The extent of women's formal integration varies widely by country and era.

Historical roles

Women fought disguised as men in early modern armies, staffed military nursing services formalised during the Crimean War, and served in large numbers in auxiliary corps during both world wars. The Soviet Union deployed women as snipers, pilots, and tank crews in the Second World War, including the "Night Witches" bomber regiments.

Post-1945 integration

Many Western states moved women from separate corps into the regular forces from the 1970s onward. The United States rescinded its combat exclusion policy in 2013, and Britain opened all ground close-combat roles in 2018. Israel, Norway, and several other states conscript women on equal or near-equal terms.

Debates

Integration has been accompanied by debate over physical standards, unit cohesion, and the prevention of sexual harassment and assault within armed forces.
