Buchan is an area of north-east Scotland, historically one of the provinces of Scotland, bounded by the River Deveron, the River Ythan, and the North Sea coast. It corresponds roughly to the northern part of modern Aberdeenshire. Buchan Ness, near Peterhead, is the most easterly point of mainland Scotland. The principal towns are Peterhead and Fraserburgh, two of the largest fishing ports in Europe.

History

Buchan was ruled in the Middle Ages by a line of mormaers and later earls, among the most powerful families in Scotland. The earldom passed through marriage to the Comyn family, whose power in Buchan was destroyed by Robert the Bruce during the Wars of Scottish Independence in the "Harrowing of Buchan" (1308). The earldom was later recreated and held by other families, including the Stewarts and the Erskines. The medieval Book of Deer, a 10th-century illuminated manuscript with early Gaelic notes, is associated with the old monastery of Deer in Buchan.

Geography and economy

The Buchan landscape is a low, undulating agricultural plateau ending in dramatic cliffs along the North Sea, including the Bullers of Buchan, a collapsed sea cave. Agriculture and fishing have traditionally dominated the economy; since the 1970s, North Sea oil and gas have been important, with a major gas terminal at St Fergus and Peterhead serving as a supply and support port. The area is known for its strong Doric dialect of Scots.
