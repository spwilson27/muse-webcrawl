Rabbit


Placeholder image: Rabbit

Rabbits are small mammals in the family Leporidae of the order Lagomorpha. The European rabbit (Oryctolagus cuniculus), native to southwestern Europe and northwest Africa, is the ancestor of all domestic rabbits and has been introduced worldwide.

Biology

Rabbits are herbivores with long ears, powerful hind legs adapted for hopping, and continuously growing incisors. They practise caecotrophy, re-ingesting soft faecal pellets to absorb nutrients. Most wild rabbits live in warrens — networks of burrows — and are social animals. Females (does) give birth to altricial kits after a gestation of about 30 days.

Domestication

Rabbits were first kept in Roman warrens and were domesticated in the Middle Ages, initially for meat. Today they are raised for meat, wool (Angora), fur, laboratory research, and as household pets; hundreds of breeds exist.

Ecology

Rabbits are important prey animals and grazers. Introduced populations, famously in Australia, have caused severe ecological damage, prompting control measures including the myxomatosis and rabbit haemorrhagic disease viruses.
