Ethylene (IUPAC name: ethene) is a hydrocarbon with the formula C2H4. It is a colourless, flammable gas with a faint sweet odour. Ethylene is the most produced organic compound in the world and the simplest alkene; in plants it acts as a hormone that regulates growth and fruit ripening.

Production

Ethylene is produced industrially by steam cracking of hydrocarbons such as ethane and naphtha at high temperature, and recovered from refinery gases. Major derivatives include polyethylene (the world's most common plastic), ethylene oxide, ethylene dichloride (for PVC), and ethylbenzene (for styrene).

Plant hormone

Plants synthesise ethylene from methionine. It promotes fruit ripening (bananas are shipped green and ripened with ethylene gas), leaf abscission, flower senescence, and responses to stress and wounding. The "triple response" in seedlings was an early assay for the hormone.

History

Ethylene was noted in the seventeenth century by Johann Joachim Becher and prepared by Dutch chemists in 1795 as "olefiant gas." Its role in illuminating-gas damage to plants in the nineteenth century led to recognition of its hormonal activity.
