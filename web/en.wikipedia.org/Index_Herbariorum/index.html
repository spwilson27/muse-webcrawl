Index Herbariorum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Index Herbariorum is a global directory of herbaria — the collections of preserved plant specimens maintained by botanical gardens, universities, museums, and research institutes. It assigns each participating herbarium a unique official code, known as a herbarium code, consisting of one to eight letters, which is used worldwide to cite specimens in scientific publications.

The index is maintained by the New York Botanical Garden and is now published as a continuously updated online database. Thousands of active herbaria in well over 150 countries are registered, collectively holding hundreds of millions of specimens.

History

Printed editions of Index Herbariorum were published from the 1950s onward under the auspices of the International Association for Plant Taxonomy. Editors including Patricia Kern Holmgren compiled successive volumes listing herbaria, their staff, holdings, and areas of specialisation. With the advent of the web, the print volumes were superseded by an online database that curators can update directly.

Herbarium codes

Famous codes include K for the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew; NY for the New York Botanical Garden; P for the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle in Paris; and MO for the Missouri Botanical Garden. When a botanist cites a type specimen, the citation typically gives the collector, number, and the herbarium code in which the specimen is deposited, allowing other researchers to locate and examine it.

Significance

By standardising herbarium abbreviations, Index Herbariorum underpins specimen citation in taxonomy, floristics, and increasingly in digitised biodiversity infrastructures such as GBIF, where herbarium codes serve as stable identifiers for collections.
