Vandalic War

The Vandalic War was a conflict fought in North Africa between the forces of the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire and the Vandalic Kingdom of Carthage in 533–534. It resulted in the Byzantine conquest of the Vandalic kingdom, and marked the first major victory of Emperor Justinian I's project of reconquering the lost western provinces of the Roman Empire (renovatio imperii).

Background

The Vandals, a Germanic people who had crossed into Africa in 429 under King Gaiseric, controlled the grain-rich provinces of North Africa and a powerful fleet that had sacked Rome in 455. By the 530s, relations between Constantinople and the Vandal king Gelimer had broken down, giving Justinian a pretext for invasion.

Campaign

The expedition, commanded by the general Belisarius, landed in Africa in the summer of 533 with about 15,000 troops. Belisarius defeated Gelimer at the Battle of Ad Decimum (13 September 533) and entered Carthage, then won a decisive victory at the Battle of Tricamarum in mid-December. Gelimer fled to Mount Papua in Numidia and surrendered in the spring of 534, ending a century of Vandal rule.

Aftermath

North Africa became the Byzantine praetorian prefecture of Africa. Although Moorish (Berber) revolts and mutinies plagued the province for years, the reconquest restored imperial control of the western Mediterranean's richest agricultural region and opened the way to Justinian's invasion of Ostrogothic Italy.
