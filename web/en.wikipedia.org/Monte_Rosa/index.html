Monte Rosa

Monte Rosa is a mountain massif in the eastern part of the Pennine Alps, on the border between Italy (Piedmont and Aosta Valley) and Switzerland (Valais). Its highest peak, the Dufourspitze, at 4,634 m (15,203 ft), is the second-highest mountain in the Alps and in western Europe, after Mont Blanc.

Monte Rosa
Elevation: 4,634 m (Dufourspitze)
Range: Pennine Alps
Countries: Switzerland, Italy
First ascent: 1855 (Dufourspitze)

Geography

The massif is heavily glaciated, including the Gorner Glacier, the second-largest glacier in the Alps. Principal summits include Dufourspitze, Zumsteinspitze, Signalkuppe (on which stands the Margherita Hut, the highest mountain hut in Europe at 4,554 m), Parrotspitze, and Ludwigshöhe.

Climbing

The Dufourspitze was first climbed in 1855 by a party guided by Matthäus zum Taugwald. Monte Rosa is popular with mountaineers and ski tourers; its east face, the Macugnaga wall, is one of the great walls of the Alps.
