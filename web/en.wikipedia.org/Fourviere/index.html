Fourvière is a district of Lyon, France, and the hill immediately west of the old part of the city, rising from the river Saône. It is the site of the Roman city of Lugdunum, founded in 43 BC, and of the Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière, whose white silhouette and gilded statue of the Virgin Mary dominate the Lyon skyline. The name is generally derived from the Latin Forum Vetus ("old forum").

History

Lugdunum, built on and around the hill, became the capital of Gallia Lugdunensis and one of the most important cities of the western Roman Empire; remains include two Roman theatres, among the oldest in France, and the Lugdunum museum. In the Middle Ages the hill became a centre of religious life, and after the city was spared worse effects in the Franco-Prussian War, Lyon's citizens fulfilled a vow by building the present basilica in the 1870s–1880s, designed by Pierre Bossan in a flamboyant neo-Byzantine style.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the basilica and its crypt, the Tour métallique de Fourvière (a smaller cousin of the Eiffel Tower, built 1892–94), the ancient theatres, and the funicular railways that have climbed the hill since the 19th century. Fourvière is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the historic site of Lyon.
