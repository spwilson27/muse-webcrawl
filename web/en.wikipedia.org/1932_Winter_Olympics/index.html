The 1932 Winter Olympics, officially known as the III Olympic Winter Games, were a winter multi-sport event held in Lake Placid, New York, United States, from February 4 to 15, 1932. They were the first Winter Olympics held in North America, and attendance and participation were reduced by the Great Depression.

Highlights

Seventeen nations and 252 athletes competed in 14 events across four sports: bobsleigh, ice hockey, figure skating, speed skating, cross-country skiing, ski jumping, Nordic combined, and demonstration sports including curling. The United States topped the medal table. Norwegian figure skater Sonja Henie won her second consecutive gold medal, and American speed skater Irving Jaffee won two golds.

Venues

Events used the new Olympic Arena — the first indoor venue for Olympic ice events — and the Mount Van Hoevenberg bobsleigh run. Lake Placid would host the Winter Games again in 1980.
