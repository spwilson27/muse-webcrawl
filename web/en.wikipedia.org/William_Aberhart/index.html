William Aberhart

William Aberhart (30 December 1878 – 23 May 1943) was a Canadian politician and radio evangelist who served as the seventh premier of Alberta from 1935 to 1943. A schoolteacher and lay preacher, he founded the Alberta Social Credit Party and led it to a landslide victory in the 1935 provincial election.

Early life and radio ministry

Born in rural Ontario, Aberhart became a school principal in Calgary and a popular fundamentalist preacher. His weekly "Back to the Bible Hour" radio broadcasts, heard across the Prairies, earned him the nickname "Bible Bill" and gave him an enormous audience. During the Great Depression he turned to the monetary reform theories of Major C. H. Douglas, known as Social Credit, and popularised them in Alberta.

Premier

In 1935 Aberhart's Social Credit League won 56 of 63 seats, promising a $25 monthly "basic dividend" to every citizen. His government attempted radical measures — prosperity certificates (a form of scrip), debt adjustment, and provincial banking legislation — but many were disallowed by the federal government or struck down by the courts, and the dividend was never paid. His press-regulation law was also invalidated. The government nonetheless introduced significant reforms in banking regulation, health and education. Aberhart died in office in 1943 and was succeeded by Ernest Manning.
