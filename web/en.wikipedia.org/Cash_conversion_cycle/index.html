Cash conversion cycle

The cash conversion cycle (CCC) is a metric in financial management that expresses the length of time, in days, that it takes for a company to convert its investments in inventory and other resources into cash flows from sales. A shorter cycle means the company's cash is tied up in working capital for less time.

Formula

CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO, where DIO is days inventory outstanding (how long inventory sits before being sold), DSO is days sales outstanding (how long customers take to pay), and DPO is days payable outstanding (how long the company takes to pay its suppliers). Increasing DIO or DSO lengthens the cycle; stretching payables (higher DPO) shortens it.

Interpretation

Some firms achieve a negative cash conversion cycle — collecting from customers before paying suppliers. Online retailer Amazon and computer maker Dell (in its direct-sales era) famously operated with negative cycles, effectively being financed by their supply chains. A lengthening cycle can signal deteriorating collections or obsolete inventory and is monitored by lenders and analysts as a liquidity indicator.
