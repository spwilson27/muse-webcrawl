Murder (United States law)

In the United States, murder is the unlawful killing of another human being with malice aforethought. Criminal law is mostly governed by the individual states, so the definition and punishment of murder vary by jurisdiction, though most follow the common-law tradition.

Degrees

Many states divide murder into degrees. First-degree murder generally involves a willful, deliberate and premeditated killing, or a killing committed during certain felonies under the felony murder rule. Second-degree murder covers other killings with malice that lack premeditation. The Model Penal Code instead distinguishes murder, manslaughter and negligent homicide.

Punishment

Murder carries the most severe penalties in American law, including life imprisonment and, in states retaining it and in the federal system, capital punishment. Aggravating and mitigating factors influence sentencing, particularly in death penalty cases.
