George Stevens

George Stevens (December 18, 1904 – March 8, 1975) was an American film director, producer, screenwriter, and cinematographer. During his career he directed a number of acclaimed dramas and comedies and won two Academy Awards for Best Director.

Career

Stevens began as a cameraman on Laurel and Hardy shorts, then directed comedies such as Alice Adams (1935), Swing Time (1936), and Woman of the Year (1942). During World War II he headed a U.S. Army Signal Corps film unit that shot footage of D-Day and the liberation of Dachau. His postwar epics — A Place in the Sun (1951) and Giant (1956), both of which won him the Best Director Oscar, plus Shane (1953) — are regarded as classics of American cinema.
