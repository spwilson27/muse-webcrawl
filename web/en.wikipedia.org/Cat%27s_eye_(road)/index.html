Cat's eye (road)

A cat's eye is a retroreflective road stud used in road marking, set into the road surface so that vehicle headlights are reflected back to the driver at night. Cat's eyes were invented in 1934 by Percy Shaw of Boothtown, Halifax, West Yorkshire, England, and became a standard feature of British roads.

Design

Shaw's original design consisted of a pair of reflective lenses mounted in a white rubber dome held in a cast-iron housing. When a vehicle passes over it, the dome is pressed down into the housing and the rubber wipes the lenses clean — a self-cleaning action that Shaw patented. Colours carry meanings in the UK: white studs mark lane lines, red the left edge, green slip roads and side junctions, and amber the central reservation.

Use

Cat's eyes are used worldwide, though in snow-ploughing regions recessed or alternative markers are often preferred. Modern variants include solar-powered LED "smart studs" that light actively and can change colour to warn of hazards.
