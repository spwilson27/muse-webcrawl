Scottish folk music

Scottish folk music (Scottish Gaelic: Ceòl tradaiseanta na h-Alba) encompasses the traditional music of Scotland, including ballads and songs in Scots and Gaelic, instrumental dance music for fiddle and pipes, and the pipe music of the Highlands. It has remained a living tradition, influencing and being influenced by classical composition and by the folk music of the wider British Isles, Ireland and North America.

A fiddler and a piper, two mainstays of Scottish traditional music

Forms

Instrumental traditions include reels, jigs, hornpipes and the stately strathspey, played on fiddle, accordion and bagpipes. Distinct regional fiddle styles survive, notably in the north-east and in Shetland, where Norse influence is audible. The great Highland bagpipe (pìob mhòr) has its own classical repertoire, ceòl mòr ("great music"), consisting of theme-and-variation compositions, alongside lighter ceòl beag marches and dance tunes.

Song traditions include the Border ballads, bothy ballads from the north-east farm workers, Jacobite songs, and the Gaelic traditions of waulking songs and psalm singing in the Hebrides. Robert Burns collected and reshaped many Scots songs in the 18th century.

Revival and modern scene

The folk revival of the 1950s and 1960s, associated with collectors such as Hamish Henderson and singers such as Jeannie Robertson, renewed interest. Groups including The Corries, The Incredible String Band, Battlefield Band and Capercaillie carried Scottish music to international audiences, and festivals such as Celtic Connections in Glasgow anchor a thriving contemporary scene.
