Anne of Austria (French: Anne d'Autriche; 22 September 1601 – 20 January 1666) was Queen of France as the wife of King Louis XIII, and regent of France during the minority of her son Louis XIV from 1643 until 1651. A Spanish infanta of the House of Habsburg, she became one of the most politically significant women of 17th-century France.

Marriage and queenship

Anne, eldest daughter of Philip III of Spain and Margaret of Austria, married Louis XIII in 1615 in a dynastic double marriage intended to cement peace between France and Spain. The marriage was distant for many years, strained by the Franco-Spanish rivalry and by the influence of Cardinal Richelieu, who suspected her of sympathy for her Spanish family. After more than two decades without a surviving child, she gave birth in 1638 to the future Louis XIV — an event celebrated as a miracle — and in 1640 to a second son, Philippe.

Regency

On Louis XIII's death in 1643, Anne had his will, which limited her powers, annulled by the Parlement of Paris and became regent in full. She governed with her chief minister, Cardinal Mazarin, whose influence and intimacy with her were the subjects of widespread speculation. Together they steered France through the final phase of the Thirty Years' War, concluded favourably by the Peace of Westphalia (1648) and the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659), and through the civil disturbances of the Fronde (1648–1653).

Later life

Anne formally handed power to Louis XIV in 1651 but remained influential until Mazarin's death in 1661. Known for her piety, she founded the church of Val-de-Grace in Paris in thanksgiving for her son's birth. She died of breast cancer in 1666. In popular culture she appears as a central character in Alexandre Dumas's novel The Three Musketeers.
