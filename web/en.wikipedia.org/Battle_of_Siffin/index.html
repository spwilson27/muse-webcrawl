Battle of Siffin

DateJuly 657 CE
LocationSiffin, near Raqqa, Syria
ResultInconclusive; arbitration
BelligerentsRashidun Caliphate (Ali) vs. Muawiyah's forces
CommandersAli ibn Abi Talib; Malik al-Ashtar vs. Muawiyah I; Amr ibn al-As

The Battle of Siffin (Arabic: معركة صفين) was fought in July 657 CE between the forces of the fourth Rashidun caliph, Ali ibn Abi Talib, and the rebellious governor of Syria, Muawiyah ibn Abi Sufyan. It was the central military confrontation of the First Fitna, the first civil war in Islamic history, and took place on the banks of the Euphrates near present-day Raqqa in Syria.

The plain of Siffin on the Euphrates, where the two armies camped for months.

Background

After the assassination of the caliph Uthman in 656, Ali succeeded to the caliphate but faced opposition from several quarters. Muawiyah, a kinsman of Uthman and long-serving governor of Syria, refused to pledge allegiance and demanded that Uthman's killers be punished first. Having defeated Aisha, Talha and Zubayr at the Battle of the Camel near Basra, Ali marched north into Syria to confront Muawiyah directly.

The battle

The two armies met at Siffin in the spring of 657. For weeks the fighting consisted mainly of skirmishes and duels between champions, punctuated by negotiations. A major engagement began on 26 July and raged for several days. Ali's forces, led by commanders such as Malik al-Ashtar and the veteran Ammar ibn Yasir — who was killed in the fighting — gradually gained the upper hand, and Muawiyah's position appeared close to collapse.

The arbitration

At this critical moment, on the advice of Amr ibn al-As, Muawiyah's soldiers raised copies of the Quran (or leaves from it) on their lances, calling for the dispute to be settled by the Book of God. Many of Ali's soldiers, unwilling to fight against the Quran, pressed him to accept arbitration. Against his better judgment Ali agreed. Abu Musa al-Ash'ari was chosen as Ali's arbitrator and Amr ibn al-As as Muawiyah's. The arbitration process dragged on inconclusively and is generally held to have weakened Ali's position while legitimising Muawiyah's claim.

Aftermath

The acceptance of arbitration split Ali's camp: a faction that became known as the Kharijites seceded, holding that judgment belonged to God alone, and later fought Ali at the Battle of Nahrawan in 658. Ali was assassinated by a Kharijite in 661, after which Muawiyah established the Umayyad Caliphate. Siffin thus stands as one of the defining events in the early schisms of Islam.
