Melinda French Gates

Melinda French Gates (born Melinda Ann French; August 15, 1964) is an American philanthropist and former computer scientist. She co-founded, with her former husband Bill Gates, the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, the world's largest private charitable organization, and founded the investment and incubation company Pivotal Ventures.

Early life and Microsoft career

Born and raised in Dallas, Texas, French Gates earned a bachelor's degree in computer science and economics from Duke University in 1986 and an MBA from Duke's Fuqua School of Business in 1987. She joined Microsoft that year, working on multimedia products including Publisher, Cinemania, Encarta and Expedia, and rose to general manager of information products. She married Bill Gates in 1994 and left Microsoft in 1996 to focus on family and philanthropy.

Philanthropy

The Gates Foundation, established in 2000, focuses on global health, poverty reduction and education, and French Gates has been a prominent advocate for women's health, family planning and gender equality; her memoir The Moment of Lift appeared in 2019. Following the couple's divorce in 2021, she resigned as co-chair of the foundation in June 2024 (the foundation was renamed the Gates Foundation) and committed $12.5 billion to her own charitable work through Pivotal Ventures, which she founded in 2015 to advance social progress for women and families in the United States. She has received the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2016, jointly with Bill Gates) and France's Legion of Honour, among other honours.
