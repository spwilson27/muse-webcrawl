Proxy fight

A proxy fight, proxy contest, or proxy battle is an unfriendly contest for control of a publicly held corporation. It occurs when a dissident group of shareholders seeks to persuade other shareholders to use their proxy votes to install new management or a new board of directors, typically in opposition to the incumbent leadership.

Mechanism

Shareholders who cannot attend an annual meeting may vote by proxy, assigning their vote to another party. Both management and dissidents solicit proxies by mailing proxy statements and, in contested solicitations, competing "cards" that shareholders sign and return. The side collecting the most proxies wins the contested board seats.

Activist investors

Proxy fights are a key tool of activist investors and hedge funds, which accumulate stakes in companies and campaign for strategic changes, asset sales, or board representation. Notable modern examples include the campaigns at companies such as ExxonMobil, where in 2021 the small activist fund Engine No. 1 won three board seats over climate strategy. Defences available to incumbents include staggered boards, poison pills, and settlement agreements granting dissidents some board seats.
