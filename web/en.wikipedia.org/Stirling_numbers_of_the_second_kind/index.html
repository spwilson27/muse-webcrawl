Stirling numbers of the second kind

In mathematics, particularly in combinatorics, a Stirling number of the second kind (or Stirling partition number) is the number of ways to partition a set of n objects into k non-empty subsets. It is denoted S(n, k) or by the brace notation {n \brace k}. Stirling numbers of the second kind occur in the study of partitions and appear in the expansion of powers in terms of falling factorials. They are named after James Stirling, who introduced them in the 18th century.

Definition

The Stirling number of the second kind S(n, k) counts the number of ways to partition a set of n labelled objects into k nonempty unlabelled subsets. For example, S(4, 2) = 7: the four elements {1,2,3,4} can be split into two nonempty subsets in seven ways.

Recurrence relation

Stirling numbers of the second kind satisfy the recurrence S(n+1, k) = k·S(n, k) + S(n, k−1), with boundary conditions S(0,0) = 1 and S(n,0) = S(0,k) = 0 for n, k > 0. The term k·S(n,k) counts placements of the (n+1)-th element into one of the k existing blocks, and S(n,k−1) counts placing it alone in a new block.

Explicit formula

By inclusion–exclusion, S(n, k) = (1/k!) Σj=0k (−1)k−j C(k, j) jn. The total number of partitions of an n-element set into any number of blocks is the Bell number Bn = Σk S(n, k).

Applications

These numbers appear in the identity xn = Σk S(n,k)·(x)k, relating ordinary powers to falling factorials, and in problems of occupancy, moments of distributions, and the analysis of algorithms. See also Stirling numbers of the first kind, Bell number, and partition of a set.
