Marinoan glaciation

The Marinoan glaciation was a period of worldwide glaciation that lasted from approximately 650 to 632 million years ago during the Cryogenian period. It was the second and last of the major "snowball Earth" glaciations of that period, following the Sturtian glaciation.

Evidence includes glacial deposits at low paleolatitudes and distinctive cap carbonates overlying them. The glaciation ended with the accumulation of greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide from volcanoes reaching a threshold that melted the ice.

It is named after Marino, a suburb of Adelaide, South Australia, near where its deposits were first described; the end of the Marinoan defines the start of the Ediacaran period.
