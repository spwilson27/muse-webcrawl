The Canadian Armed Forces (CAF; French: Forces armées canadiennes, FAC) are the unified military forces of Canada, including land, sea, and air commands referred to as the Canadian Army, Royal Canadian Navy (RCN), and Royal Canadian Air Force (RCAF). The Forces are managed by the Department of National Defence and overseen by the Armed Forces Council.

History

Canadian forces served in the Boer War, the First and Second World Wars, the Korean War, and numerous peacekeeping operations. On 1 February 1968, the Royal Canadian Navy, Canadian Army, and Royal Canadian Air Force were unified into a single service, the Canadian Armed Forces—a restructuring that was controversial but made Canada the first NATO country with a fully unified military structure. The distinct environmental commands formally restored their historic names (Royal Canadian Navy, Canadian Army, Royal Canadian Air Force) in 2011.

Role and operations

The CAF's core missions are the defence of Canada, cooperation with the United States in the defence of North America through NORAD, and contributions to international security through NATO and United Nations operations. Canadian personnel served in a combat role in Afghanistan from 2001 to 2014, Canada's longest war, and regularly deploy on operations in the Arctic, maritime patrols, and domestic emergency response such as flood and wildfire relief.

Structure

The professional head of the CAF is the Chief of the Defence Staff, who reports to the Minister of National Defence. Operational commands include Canadian Joint Operations Command, Canadian Special Operations Forces Command, and the binational NORAD. The Primary Reserve supplements the Regular Force, and the Canadian Rangers provide a military presence in sparsely settled northern and coastal regions.
