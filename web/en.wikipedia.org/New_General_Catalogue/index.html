New General Catalogue

The New General Catalogue of Nebulae and Clusters of Stars (abbreviated NGC) is an astronomical catalogue of deep-sky objects compiled by John Louis Emil Dreyer in 1888. The NGC contains 7,840 objects, including galaxies, star clusters and nebulae, and remains one of the most comprehensive general catalogues of its kind; its designations (such as NGC 224 for the Andromeda Galaxy) are still in everyday use by amateur and professional astronomers.

Origins

The catalogue grew out of William Herschel's catalogues of nebulae and clusters (1786–1802) and his son John Herschel's General Catalogue of Nebulae and Clusters (1864), which listed 5,079 objects. Commissioned by the Royal Astronomical Society to bring the General Catalogue up to date, Dreyer published the NGC as a memoir of the Society in 1888, adding objects discovered since 1864 and correcting positions and descriptions. He later issued two supplementary Index Catalogues (IC I in 1895 and IC II in 1908) adding a further 5,386 objects discovered photographically.

Modern revisions

Because many entries were based on visual observations with 19th-century telescopes, the NGC contains errors — misidentified, duplicated or non-existent objects. Modern projects, notably the NGC/IC Project and the Revised New General Catalogue (1973) by Sulentic and Tifft, have worked to identify and correct every entry against modern sky surveys.
