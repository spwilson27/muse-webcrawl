Tlaloc (Classical Nahuatl: Tlāloc) is the god of rain in Aztec religion and in the religions of other central Mexican peoples. One of the oldest and most widely worshipped deities in Mesoamerica, Tlaloc was a supreme god of the rains, water, lightning and agriculture, and was both a life-giving bringer of fertility and a feared wielder of storms, hail and disease. His cult, under various names, extends back many centuries before the Aztecs, appearing at Teotihuacan and among earlier cultures of the region.

Iconography

Tlaloc is instantly recognisable by his distinctive features: large round "goggle" eyes, prominent fangs, and a curled upper lip or snout, often rendered in blue and green tones associated with water and jade. He is frequently depicted holding lightning bolts or vessels from which rain pours, and accompanied by his assistants, the small rain dwarfs known as the Tlaloque. At Teotihuacan his image appears on murals and ceramics, sometimes in a debated female or "Great Goddess" form.

Tlalocan

The dead who died by water-related causes — drowning, lightning, dropsy and certain diseases — were believed to go not to Mictlan but to Tlalocan, Tlaloc's lush paradise, a place of eternal spring, abundance and greenery located in the mountains. Victims of these deaths were typically buried rather than cremated.

Worship and sacrifice

At the Templo Mayor of Tenochtitlan, twin shrines crowned the great pyramid: one to Huitzilopochtli, god of war and sun, and one to Tlaloc, reflecting the dual foundations of Mexica life in warfare and agriculture. Offerings recovered from Tlaloc's side of the temple include marine creatures, coral, shells and objects of blue stone. Festivals in his honour, including Atlcahualo and Huey Tozoztli, involved offerings to secure rain for the maize crop; some rites demanded the tears of sacrificed children, which were taken as an omen of coming rain.

Related deities

Tlaloc's consort was Chalchiuhtlicue, goddess of lakes and streams. Similar rain gods were worshipped across Mesoamerica: Chaac among the Maya, Cocijo among the Zapotecs and Dzahui among the Mixtecs, all sharing aspects of Tlaloc's goggle-eyed imagery and cult of rain-making.
