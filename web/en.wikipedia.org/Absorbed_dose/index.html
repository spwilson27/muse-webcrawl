QuantityEnergy deposited per unit mass

Absorbed dose is a dose quantity representing the mean energy imparted by ionizing radiation to a unit mass of matter. It is defined as the mean energy dE deposited per unit mass dm, and is used in radiation protection, radiotherapy and radiation physics.

Units

The SI unit is the gray (Gy), equal to one joule per kilogram, named after British physicist Louis Harold Gray. The older CGS unit, the rad, remains in use in some fields (1 Gy = 100 rad). Absorbed dose is distinct from equivalent dose and effective dose, which weight absorbed dose by radiation type and tissue sensitivity and are measured in sieverts.

Applications

Absorbed dose to tumour tissue (typically delivered in fractions totalling tens of grays) is the fundamental quantity prescribed in radiation therapy. In occupational and environmental settings, absorbed doses are usually far lower and are combined with weighting factors to estimate health risk. Whole-body absorbed doses of several grays delivered acutely can cause acute radiation syndrome.
