ABET

ABET is a non-governmental, non-profit accreditation organization for college and university programs in applied and natural science, computing, engineering, and engineering technology. Founded in 1932 as the Engineers' Council for Professional Development (ECPD), it adopted the name Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology in 1980 and rebranded simply as ABET in 2005.

Accreditation

ABET accredits more than 4,000 programs at over 800 institutions in more than 40 countries. Accreditation is voluntary and peer-reviewed: programs must demonstrate that students attain defined outcomes, that curricula meet standards set by professional societies, and that processes for continuous improvement are in place.

Criteria

Since the adoption of the EC2000 criteria, ABET evaluation emphasizes student outcomes — such as the ability to design systems, work on teams, and communicate — rather than prescriptive course counts. Its four commissions handle applied and natural science (ASAC), computing (CAC), engineering (EAC), and engineering technology (ETAC) programs.

International role

ABET is a founding signatory of the Washington Accord, an international agreement recognizing the substantial equivalence of engineering degrees accredited by its members, facilitating professional mobility of engineers across signatory countries.
