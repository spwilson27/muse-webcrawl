Rothko Chapel

The Rothko Chapel is a non-denominational chapel in Houston, Texas, founded by John and Dominique de Menil. The interior serves not only as a chapel, but also as a major work of modern art: on its walls are fourteen black but color-hued paintings by Mark Rothko.

History

In 1964 the de Menils commissioned Rothko to create a suite of paintings for a meditative chapel. Rothko, who had significant creative control over the octagonal building's design, produced fourteen canvases in deep maroons, blacks, and plums. He did not live to see the chapel completed: he died in 1970, and the chapel was dedicated in February 1971 as an interfaith sanctuary.

Broken Obelisk and activities

Outside the chapel, in a reflecting pool, stands Barnett Newman's sculpture Broken Obelisk (1963–67), dedicated to the memory of Martin Luther King Jr. The chapel has long been a centre for human rights activity; since 1973 it has presented the Óscar Romero Award to grassroots human rights defenders. The building underwent a major restoration completed in 2021, which improved the skylight system controlling the paintings' light.
