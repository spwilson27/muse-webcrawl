Sukhoi Su-27

The Sukhoi Su-27 (Russian: Сухой Су-27; NATO reporting name: Flanker) is a Soviet-origin twin-engine supermaneuverable fighter aircraft designed by Sukhoi. It was intended as a direct competitor for the large US fourth-generation fighters such as the Grumman F-14 Tomcat and F-15 Eagle, with a 3,530-kilometre (1,910 nmi) range, heavy aircraft ordnance, sophisticated avionics and high maneuverability. The Su-27 was designed for air superiority missions, and subsequent variants are able to perform almost all aerial warfare operations.

Development

Development began in 1969 in response to the United States' F-X program that produced the F-15. The first prototype, designated T-10, flew on 20 May 1977, but serious shortcomings led to a radical redesign (the T-10S), which flew in 1981. The production Su-27 entered service with the Soviet Air Forces in 1985, though it did not reach full operational capability until later in the decade.

Design

The Su-27 features a blended wing-body configuration, twin Saturn AL-31F turbofan engines spaced widely apart, fly-by-wire controls, and an infrared search and track (IRST) system mounted ahead of the cockpit, supplementing its radar. It is armed with a 30 mm cannon and up to ten air-to-air missiles. The aircraft became famous in the West for performing the Pugachev's Cobra maneuver at the 1989 Paris Air Show.

Variants and operators

The Su-27 spawned a large family: the Su-30 multirole fighter, the Su-33 carrier-based fighter, the Su-34 strike fighter, the Su-35, and Chinese-built derivatives such as the Shenyang J-11 and J-16. Su-27s have been operated by Russia, Ukraine, China, and a number of other air forces, and saw combat use including in the Russo-Ukrainian War.

Specifications (Su-27SK)

  • Crew: 1
  • Length: 21.9 m; wingspan: 14.7 m; height: 5.9 m
  • Maximum speed: Mach 2.35
  • Range: 3,530 km
  • Service ceiling: 19,000 m
  • Armament: 1 × 30 mm Gryazev-Shipunov GSh-30-1 cannon; up to 8,000 kg of stores on 10 hardpoints
