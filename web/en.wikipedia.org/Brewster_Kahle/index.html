Brewster Kahle

Brewster Lurton Kahle (born October 22, 1960) is an American digital librarian, computer engineer, and Internet entrepreneur. He founded the Internet Archive in 1996 and its Wayback Machine, and co-founded Alexa Internet and WAIS, Inc. A prominent advocate for universal access to knowledge, Kahle has campaigned for digitising the world's books, music, film and web pages and keeping them freely available, summarised in his maxim "Universal access to all knowledge". He was inducted into the Internet Hall of Fame in 2012.

Early career

Kahle graduated from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1982, where he worked on the WAIS (Wide Area Information Server) protocol for searching distributed databases; he also worked at Thinking Machines, a supercomputer company. In 1992 he co-founded WAIS, Inc., which created some of the first search tools for the early Internet and was sold to AOL in 1995 for a reported $15 million. In 1996 he co-founded Alexa Internet, which crawled the web to provide traffic data and related links; Alexa was acquired by Amazon in 1999 for about $250 million in stock.

Internet Archive

In 1996 Kahle founded the non-profit Internet Archive in San Francisco, funded initially from the Alexa sale. The Archive preserves snapshots of the World Wide Web via the Wayback Machine (opened to the public in 2001), which holds hundreds of billions of web pages, and has grown into a digital library of tens of millions of texts, plus large collections of audio, video, software and images. Kahle led projects to scan books at scale — including building the "Scribe" book scanners used by libraries worldwide — and launched the Open Library project aiming for "one web page for every book ever published". The Archive's former-church headquarters in San Francisco's Richmond District symbolises its "library" mission; Kahle also established a physical "backup" philosophy — the Archive maintains mirror copies, notably in Alexandria, Egypt, in the spirit of the ancient library.

Advocacy and controversies

Kahle has been an outspoken critic of excessive copyright terms and of the Google Books settlement, co-founding the Open Content Alliance as an open alternative. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the Archive's "National Emergency Library" (2020), which suspended waitlists on its lending of scanned books, provoked a copyright suit by four major publishers; in 2023 a federal court ruled against the Archive's controlled digital lending model, a decision the Archive appealed before settling aspects of the dispute. Kahle continues to argue that libraries must be able to own and lend digital copies if democratic access to knowledge is to survive the digital transition.
