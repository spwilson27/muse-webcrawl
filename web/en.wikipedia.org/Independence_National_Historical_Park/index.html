Independence National Historical Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Independence National Historical Park is a federally protected historic district in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, United States, that preserves several sites associated with the American Revolution and the nation's founding history. The 55-acre (22 ha) park, sometimes called "America's most historic square mile", is administered by the National Park Service.

Its centrepiece is Independence Hall, where the Declaration of Independence was debated and adopted in 1776 and where the United States Constitution was drafted in 1787. The park also protects the Liberty Bell, Congress Hall, Old City Hall, and numerous other 18th-century buildings.

History

The area around Independence Hall deteriorated during the early 20th century, prompting preservation campaigns. The park was authorised by Congress on 28 June 1948 and formally established on 4 July 1956. The National Park Service cleared several blocks north of Independence Hall to create Independence Mall, a landscaped open space leading toward the National Constitution Center, which opened in 2003.

Main sites

  • Independence Hall — completed in 1753 as the Pennsylvania State House; site of the Second Continental Congress and the Constitutional Convention.
  • Liberty Bell Center — home of the Liberty Bell, an iconic symbol of American independence bearing the inscription "Proclaim LIBERTY Throughout all the Land".
  • Congress Hall — seat of the United States Congress from 1790 to 1800, while Philadelphia was the temporary capital.
  • Carpenters' Hall — meeting place of the First Continental Congress in 1774.
  • Second Bank of the United States — a Greek Revival landmark housing a portrait gallery.

Visitation

The park receives several million visitors annually. Entry to Independence Hall is by timed ticket, distributed free of charge, while the Liberty Bell Center requires no ticket. In 1979 Independence Hall was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
