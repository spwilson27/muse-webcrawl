Lacquer

Lacquer is a type of hard, usually glossy coating or finish applied to materials such as wood or metal. Traditional East Asian lacquer is made from the sap of the lacquer tree (Toxicodendron vernicifluum), processed and applied in many thin layers that harden by oxidation and polymerisation in humid conditions, producing exceptionally durable, water-resistant surfaces used for furniture, tableware and art objects in China, Japan, Korea and Vietnam for thousands of years.

In modern usage, lacquers are solvent-based coatings that dry by evaporation, leaving a hard film; nitrocellulose lacquer was widely used for cars, guitars and furniture in the twentieth century. The word derives, via Portuguese and French, from Arabic lakk and Sanskrit lākṣā, referring to lac, the resinous secretion of scale insects also used to make shellac.
