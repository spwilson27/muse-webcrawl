Two-stroke engine

Animation of a two-stroke engine cycle.

A two-stroke (or two-cycle) engine is a type of internal combustion engine that completes a power cycle with two strokes (up and down movements) of the piston during one power cycle, this power cycle being completed in one revolution of the crankshaft. A four-stroke engine requires four strokes of the piston to complete a power cycle during two crankshaft revolutions.

In a two-stroke engine, the end of the combustion stroke and the beginning of the compression stroke happen simultaneously, with the intake and exhaust (or scavenging) functions occurring at the same time. Two-stroke engines often have a high power-to-weight ratio, power being available in a narrow range of rotational speeds called the power band.

Design

Simple two-stroke engines use ports in the cylinder wall, uncovered by the piston, for intake and exhaust, and use the crankcase as a scavenging pump. Because the oil used to lubricate the crankshaft is often mixed with the fuel in small engines, two-stroke engines historically produced high hydrocarbon emissions, which has led to their decline in many applications such as automobiles and, increasingly, handheld tools.

Applications

Two-stroke engines remain common in chainsaws, string trimmers, outboard motors, mopeds, small motorcycles, model aircraft, and large marine diesel engines (which use a highly efficient uniflow-scavenged design quite different from small petrol two-strokes).
