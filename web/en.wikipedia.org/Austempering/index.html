Austempering

Austempering is a heat treatment process for ferrous metals, most notably steel and cast iron. The part is heated to the austenitising temperature, quenched rapidly to a temperature above the martensite start point, and held there until the austenite transforms isothermally to bainite.

Process

Quenching is typically into a molten salt bath held between roughly 250 °C and 400 °C. Holding time ranges from minutes to hours depending on section size and desired properties, followed by air cooling.

Advantages

Austempering produces bainitic microstructures with high strength combined with good ductility and toughness, and with less distortion and cracking risk than conventional quench-and-temper treatments. Austempered ductile iron (ADI) is widely used for gears and automotive components.
