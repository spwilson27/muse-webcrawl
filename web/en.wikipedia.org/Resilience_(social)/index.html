Resilience (social)

Social resilience is the ability of communities, organisations, and societies to cope with external stresses and disturbances — such as disasters, economic shocks, environmental change, or political upheaval — while maintaining their essential functions, structures, and identity, and to adapt or reorganise in response to change.

Concepts

The concept draws on ecological resilience theory, particularly the work of C. S. Holling, and on social-ecological systems research. Key elements include social capital, institutional flexibility, diversity, redundancy, and the capacity for learning and self-organisation. Resilience thinking emphasises that systems may adapt within a regime or transform into a different regime when conditions become untenable.

Applications

Social resilience informs disaster risk reduction, climate adaptation planning, public health preparedness, and community development. Critics note the term can be used to shift responsibility for systemic risks onto communities themselves.
