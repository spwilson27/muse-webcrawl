Longleat House, one of the finest Elizabethan country houses in England

Longleat is an English stately home and the seat of the Marquesses of Bath, situated near Warminster in Wiltshire, on the border with Somerset. Longleat House, built between 1568 and 1580 for Sir John Thynne — replacing a priory destroyed by fire — is widely regarded as one of the finest examples of Elizabethan architecture in Britain, with its symmetrical facade of bay windows, classical orders and rooftop banqueting houses; the design is attributed to Robert Smythson.

The estate

The house is set in about 1,000 acres of parkland landscaped in the 18th century by Capability Brown, within a wider estate of some 9,800 acres, including Longleat Forest. The interior contains major collections of paintings, furniture, and one of the largest private libraries in Europe, alongside Elizabethan and later state rooms. The Longleat hedge maze, added in 1975, was for a time the world's longest, and the grounds contain a notable topiary and a large lake with a colony of sea lions and boat trips to a gorilla colony.

Safari park

In 1966 Longleat opened the first drive-through safari park outside Africa, a pioneering venture by the 6th Marquess of Bath in partnership with Jimmy Chipperfield. The "Lions of Longleat" became nationally famous, and the park has since expanded to include tigers, cheetahs, giraffes, zebras, monkeys, rhinos and a walk-through animal area. Longleat was also one of the first stately homes opened systematically to paying visitors, and it served as a long-running location for the BBC children's programme Animal Park.

The Thynn family

The estate has passed through the Thynne (later Thynn) family since the 16th century. Alexander Thynn, 7th Marquess of Bath (1932–2020), an eccentric artist and author, ran the estate for decades, painting the famous "Kama Sutra" murals in his private apartments; his son Ceawlin Thynn, 8th Marquess, succeeded in 2020.
