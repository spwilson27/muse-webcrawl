The Treo is a line of smartphones originally developed by Handspring and later produced by Palm, Inc. after its acquisition of Handspring in 2003. Combining a mobile phone with the personal digital assistant functions of the Palm OS platform, a small QWERTY thumb keyboard, and a touchscreen with stylus, the Treo was one of the defining smartphone lines of the mid-2000s.

History

Handspring, founded by Palm co-founders Jeff Hawkins and Donna Dubinsky, launched the first Treo models, the 180 and 180g, in 2002, following the earlier VisorPhone concept. The Treo 270 added a colour screen, and the Treo 600 (2003), the first model after the Palm merger, was a major commercial success with its integrated camera and five-way navigator. The Treo 650 (2004) added a high-resolution display, Bluetooth, and a removable battery. Later models included the 700w, the first Treo running Windows Mobile, the 700p and 755p on Palm OS, the 750, 800w, and the slim Treo Pro (2008). The cheaper Palm Centro (2007) carried the Treo formula to a younger market.

Features

Treos were known for one-handed usability, tight integration of phone, email, calendar, and contacts, threaded SMS messaging, and a large library of Palm OS applications. Business users valued their push email options and, on CDMA models, early EV-DO data speeds.

Legacy

The Treo line helped establish the keyboard-equipped smartphone category alongside BlackBerry devices, before touch-first phones such as the iPhone reshaped the market after 2007. Palm replaced the Treo line with the webOS-based Palm Pre in 2009.
