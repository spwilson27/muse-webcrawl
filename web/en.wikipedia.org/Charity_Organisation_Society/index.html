Charity Organisation Society

The Charity Organisation Society (COS) was a British organisation founded in London in 1869 with the aim of coordinating charitable relief and discouraging indiscriminate almsgiving. Its full original name was the Society for Organising Charitable Relief and Repressing Mendicity. The society promoted the idea of "scientific charity": relief should be given only after careful investigation of each applicant's circumstances, and should aim to restore people to independence rather than create dependence.

History

The COS arose from concern that the many overlapping charities and the outdoor relief provided under the Poor Law were demoralising the poor. Its district committees assessed applicants, referred deserving cases to the appropriate charity or the Poor Law guardians, and recorded details to prevent fraud and duplication. Influential figures associated with the society included Octavia Hill, who developed her system of housing management in close connection with it, and Charles Stewart Loch, its long-serving secretary.

Influence

The COS model spread widely: the first American Charity Organization Society was founded in Buffalo, New York, in 1877, and similar societies grew up across the United States. The society's emphasis on trained visitors and casework is often seen as an ancestor of modern professional social work, though its strict distinction between the "deserving" and "undeserving" poor drew criticism from contemporaries and later historians.

Successor

In 1946 the Charity Organisation Society changed its name to the Family Welfare Association, reflecting a shift towards family casework; the organisation later became Family Action, a national family support charity.
