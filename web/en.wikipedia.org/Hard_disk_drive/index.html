Hard disk drive

A hard disk drive (HDD), hard disk, or hard drive, is a non-volatile data storage device that stores digital data on rapidly rotating magnetic platters.

History

Introduced by IBM in 1956 with the RAMAC 350, early drives were refrigerator-sized and stored a few megabytes. Capacities grew steadily while sizes shrank to 3.5-inch and 2.5-inch form factors holding multiple terabytes.

Technology

Data is written and read by heads floating nanometres above platters spinning typically at 5,400 or 7,200 rpm, organised into tracks and sectors. Interfaces have included IDE/PATA, SCSI, SATA, and SAS. Although solid-state drives have displaced HDDs in many roles, hard drives remain widely used for bulk storage due to low cost per terabyte.
