Spermaceti

Spermaceti is a waxy substance found in the head cavities of the sperm whale (and, in smaller quantities, in the oils of other whales). It is extracted from sperm oil by crystallisation at 6 °C (43 °F), when treated by pressure and a chemical solution of caustic alkali. Spermaceti forms brilliant white crystals that are hard but oily to the touch, and are devoid of taste or smell.

Etymology

The name derives from Medieval Latin sperma ceti, "whale sperm", because the substance was originally mistaken for the whale's semen.

Function

Spermaceti is found in the spermaceti organ, a large structure in the whale's head. Biologists have proposed that it aids echolocation by focusing sound, and may help regulate buoyancy as its melting point changes with temperature during deep dives.

Uses

Historically, spermaceti was prized for candles (the standard "candlepower" was based on a spermaceti candle), cosmetics, ointments, and as an industrial lubricant. Demand for it was a major driver of sperm whaling in the 18th and 19th centuries; substitutes such as jojoba oil largely replaced it in the 20th century.
