Cape Town is the legislative capital of South Africa and the country's second-largest city, located on the shore of Table Bay at the northern end of the Cape Peninsula. It is the seat of the Parliament of South Africa, while Pretoria is the administrative capital and Bloemfontein the judicial capital. Cape Town is known for its harbour, for the flat-topped Table Mountain that overlooks the city bowl, and for landmarks such as Cape Point and Robben Island.

The city, founded in 1652 by Jan van Riebeeck as a supply station for the Dutch East India Company, is the oldest urban area in South Africa and is commonly called the "Mother City".

History

The Cape was inhabited by Khoisan peoples for millennia before Portuguese navigator Bartolomeu Dias rounded it in 1488 and Vasco da Gama recorded a landing in 1497. Dutch settlement from 1652 grew into the Cape Colony, supplied by free burghers and enslaved people imported from Indonesia, Madagascar and East Africa, whose descendants form much of the city's Cape Malay community. Britain seized the colony in 1795 and permanently in 1806; slavery was abolished in 1834. Cape Town became capital of the Union of South Africa in 1910.

Under apartheid the Group Areas Act forced non-white residents from inner districts such as District Six, which was declared a white area in 1966 and its more than 60,000 residents removed to the Cape Flats. Robben Island, in Table Bay offshore, held political prisoners including Nelson Mandela, who was imprisoned there for 18 of his 27 years. Mandela delivered his first speech as a free man from the balcony of Cape Town City Hall in February 1990.

Geography and climate

The city centre occupies the City Bowl, an amphitheatre between Table Mountain (1,085 m), Devil's Peak, Lion's Head and Signal Hill. Suburbs spread south along the Cape Peninsula and east across the Cape Flats to the Hottentots Holland mountains. Cape Town has a Mediterranean climate with warm, dry summers — accompanied by the strong south-easterly "Cape Doctor" wind — and cool, wet winters. The region lies within the Cape Floristic Region, a biodiversity hotspot of fynbos vegetation; Table Mountain alone has more plant species than the whole of the United Kingdom.

Economy and culture

Cape Town is the economic hub of the Western Cape, with finance, retail, tourism and a growing technology sector. Its port is among Africa's busiest, and the V&A Waterfront is one of the continent's most visited destinations. The city hosted matches of the 2010 FIFA World Cup at Cape Town Stadium. In 2018 Cape Town narrowly averted "Day Zero", when municipal taps were projected to run dry after a severe multi-year drought, following drastic water restrictions.
