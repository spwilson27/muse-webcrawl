Halicarnassus

Halicarnassus (Ancient Greek: Ἁλικαρνασσός, Halikarnassós) was an ancient Greek city in Caria, in southwestern Asia Minor, at the site of modern Bodrum in Turkey. It was the capital of Caria under the Hecatomnid dynasty and is famed for the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and as the birthplace of the historian Herodotus (c. 484 BC), often called the "Father of History".

History

Halicarnassus was founded by Dorian Greeks, traditionally from Troezen, and became a member of the Dorian Hexapolis. It passed under Lydian and then Persian rule. In the 4th century BC the satrap Mausolus (r. 377–353 BC) made it the capital of Caria, surrounding it with massive walls and building a harbour, palaces and temples. On his death, his wife and sister Artemisia II completed the great tomb known as the Mausoleum, from which all later grand tombs take their name.

The city withstood Alexander the Great only after a hard siege in 334 BC. It later came under Ptolemaic, Seleucid, and then Roman control, and persisted into the Byzantine era. In 1402 the Knights Hospitaller built the Castle of St. Peter at Bodrum, reportedly using stone from the by then ruined Mausoleum.
