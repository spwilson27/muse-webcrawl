Light novel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A light novel (Japanese: ライトノベル, raito noberu) is a style of Japanese young adult novel primarily targeted at high school and middle school students. "Light novel" is a wasei-eigo (Japanese-coined English) term. Light novels are commonly referred to in Japan by the abbreviation ranobe (ラノベ) or rainobe (ライノベ). They are typically published in bunkobon (paperback) format and feature manga-style illustrations, usually on the cover and in a small number of interior pages.

Characteristics

Light novels are character-driven prose fiction with comparatively simple, dialogue-heavy language intended for fast reading; volumes are relatively short (often around 200–300 pages) and series may run to many volumes. Typical genres include fantasy, science fiction, romance, school life, and the isekai (other-world) genre. Publishers issue them under dedicated imprints such as Dengeki Bunko, Fujimi Fantasia Bunko, Kadokawa Sneaker Bunko, Shueisha's Super Dash Bunko, and GA Bunko. New writers are commonly discovered through annual contests such as the Dengeki Novel Prize.

History

The form descends from Japanese pulp magazines and popular adventure fiction of the 1970s and 1980s. Yoshiki Tanaka's The Heroic Legend of Arslan (from 1986) and Baku Yumemakura's works were early successes; the genre was consolidated in the 1990s by series such as Slayers by Hajime Kanzaka (from 1990) and Record of Lodoss War novelizations. In the 2000s, series like Haruhi Suzumiya (2003) and A Certain Magical Index (2004) achieved major popularity. From the 2010s, web-fiction platforms — especially Shōsetsuka ni Narō ("Let's Become Novelists", founded 2004) — became a dominant source of new titles, with works such as Sword Art Online (originally a web novel), Re:Zero, and That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime moving from online serialization to print light novels and then to anime and manga adaptations.

Adaptations and international reception

Successful light novels form the core of multimedia franchises: they are routinely adapted into manga, anime television series, and video games, and these adaptations in turn drive book sales. Translations have spread worldwide; English-language publishers such as Yen On (Yen Press) license major series. In China, Taiwan, and South Korea, similar domestic categories of illustrated youth fiction have developed under the influence of the Japanese format.
