The Gulag Archipelago

The Gulag Archipelago is a three-volume non-fiction work by Russian writer Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn, published in Paris in 1973. Subtitled An Experiment in Literary Investigation, it documents the Soviet forced-labor camp system from 1918 to 1956.

Background

Solzhenitsyn, himself imprisoned in the Gulag from 1945 to 1953, based the work on his own experience, camp documents, and testimony from 227 fellow prisoners (zeks). The manuscript was smuggled to the West on microfilm; the KGB's seizure of a copy led to the suicide of his typist Elizaveta Voronyanskaya, prompting immediate publication abroad.

Impact

The book shattered Western left-wing illusions about the Soviet Union; Solzhenitsyn was arrested and expelled from the USSR in 1974. The word "archipelago" entered political vocabulary as a metaphor for the camp network scattered across the country. See also The Holocaust in Lithuania.
