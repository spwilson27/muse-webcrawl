Regions of Sweden

Regions (Swedish: regioner) are the self-governing county-level authorities of Sweden, responsible primarily for healthcare, public transport and regional development. There are 21 regions, broadly coterminous with the country's counties (län), each governed by an elected regional council (regionfullmäktige). Until 2020 they were formally known as county councils (landsting), and the term landsting is still common in everyday speech.

Responsibilities

Regions finance and organise most publicly funded healthcare, including hospitals and primary care, and are responsible for regional public transport together with municipalities. They may also support culture, education and business development. Healthcare accounts for the overwhelming majority of regional budgets, funded mainly by a proportional regional income tax.

History

County councils were created in 1862, separate from the state county administrative boards headed by governors. Large mergers created the modern Västra Götaland and Skåne regions in the late 1990s, and Gotland municipality also performs the functions of a region, being an island county without a separate council. Proposals to consolidate Sweden into a smaller number of large regions have repeatedly been debated but not enacted.
