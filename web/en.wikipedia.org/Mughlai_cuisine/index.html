Mughlai cuisine

Mughlai cuisine consists of the dishes developed in the medieval Indo-Persian cultural centres of the Mughal Empire. It represents a distinctive synthesis of Central Asian, Persian and Indian culinary traditions, and is known for its rich, creamy gravies, fragrant whole spices, use of dried fruits and nuts, and elaborate preparations originally created for the imperial court.

Characteristics and dishes

Signature Mughlai preparations include biryani and pulao rice dishes, kebabs such as seekh and shami kebab, rich curries like korma and pasanda, butter chicken, and the slow-cooked haleem. Desserts include shahi tukda, falooda, kulfi and phirni. Cooking techniques such as dum pukht (slow steaming in a sealed pot) and the use of saffron, cardamom, mace, kewra water and browned onions distinguish the style.

Influence

Centred on Delhi, Lucknow (Awadh) and Hyderabad, Mughlai cuisine spread throughout South Asia and, through migration, to restaurant cultures worldwide; it heavily shaped modern North Indian restaurant cooking and dishes later adapted abroad, including chicken tikka masala's predecessors in the imperial kitchen tradition.
