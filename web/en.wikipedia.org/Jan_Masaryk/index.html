Jan Garrigue Masaryk (September 14, 1886 – March 10, 1948) was a Czech diplomat and politician, foreign minister of Czechoslovakia from 1940 to 1948. The son of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, the first president of Czechoslovakia, he was a popular and internationally respected figure.

Career

Masaryk served as Czechoslovak ambassador to Britain from 1925 to 1938, resigning in protest at the Munich Agreement. During the Second World War he was foreign minister of the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London, broadcasting to occupied Czechoslovakia and helping secure Allied recognition. After the war he remained foreign minister in the coalition government, attempting to keep Czechoslovakia as a bridge between East and West. He reluctantly accepted Soviet pressure to reject the Marshall Plan in 1947.

Death

Weeks after the Communist coup of February 1948 — in which Masaryk, a non-Communist, controversially stayed on in the new government — he was found dead in the courtyard of the Foreign Ministry beneath his bathroom window on March 10, 1948. Official inquiries at the time concluded suicide, but suspicion of murder by the Communist security services has persisted; later investigations have reached conflicting or inconclusive results, and the circumstances remain a subject of debate.
