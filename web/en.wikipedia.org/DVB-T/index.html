DVB-T

DVB-T, short for Digital Video Broadcasting — Terrestrial, is the DVB European-based consortium standard for the broadcast transmission of digital terrestrial television. It transmits a compressed digital audio/video stream in MPEG transport stream format using coded orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (COFDM) modulation. First published in 1997, DVB-T was adopted across Europe, Asia, Africa, and Oceania.

Technical description

DVB-T uses COFDM with thousands of closely spaced subcarriers (2K or 8K modes), making it robust against multipath interference and suitable for single-frequency networks, in which multiple transmitters broadcast the same signal on one channel. A typical multiplex carries several television and radio services totalling roughly 24 Mbit/s in an 8 MHz channel, using MPEG-2 or, in later deployments, H.264 compression.

Adoption

The United Kingdom launched the world's first DVB-T services in 1998 (ONdigital), followed by Sweden and Spain. DVB-T underpinned digital switchover — the shutdown of analogue terrestrial TV — in most European countries during the 2000s and 2010s. Its second-generation successor, DVB-T2, offers substantially higher capacity and has replaced DVB-T in many markets.
