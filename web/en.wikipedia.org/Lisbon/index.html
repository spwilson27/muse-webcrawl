Lisbon (Portuguese: Lisboa) is the capital and largest city of Portugal, situated on the northern bank of the Tagus estuary near the Atlantic Ocean. It is one of the oldest cities in Europe, predating London, Paris and Rome, and served as the hub of Portugal's maritime empire during the Age of Discovery.

History

Known to the Romans as Olisipo, Lisbon was taken by Afonso Henriques in 1147 during the Reconquista. In the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries it became one of the world's great trading ports as Portuguese ships opened sea routes to India, Africa and Brazil. The catastrophic 1755 earthquake, followed by fire and tsunami, destroyed much of the city; it was rebuilt under the Marquis of Pombal with a pioneering grid plan and anti-seismic construction.

Geography and climate

The city is built across seven hills above the Tagus, Portugal's longest river. Lisbon has a Mediterranean climate with mild, rainy winters and warm, dry summers, and is among the sunniest capital cities in Europe.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Belém Tower and Jerónimos Monastery (UNESCO World Heritage Sites), São Jorge Castle, and the historic Alfama district, home of fado music. The city hosted Expo 98 and is a major centre for technology conferences and tourism.
