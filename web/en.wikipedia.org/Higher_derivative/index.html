Higher derivative

In mathematics, a higher derivative (or higher-order derivative) is the derivative of a function taken more than once. If f is a differentiable function, its first derivative f′ describes the rate of change of f; the second derivative f′′ is the derivative of f′, and so on. The n-th derivative is denoted f(n) or dnf/dxn in Leibniz notation.

Physical meanings

For a function giving the position of a moving object, the first derivative is velocity, the second derivative is acceleration, and the third derivative is called jerk. In economics, second derivatives describe concepts such as diminishing marginal utility and the curvature (convexity) of functions. In geometry, the second derivative relates to curvature.

Applications

Higher derivatives appear in Taylor's theorem, which approximates a function by a polynomial built from its derivatives at a point; in differential equations, where the order of the equation is the highest derivative it contains; and in tests for maxima and minima, where the sign of the second derivative helps classify critical points.
