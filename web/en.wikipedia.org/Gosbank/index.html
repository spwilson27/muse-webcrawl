Gosbank

Founded: 1921 (as State Bank of the RSFSR); 1923 as USSR State Bank

Headquarters: Moscow, Soviet Union

Type: Central bank

Successor: Central Bank of Russia (and other republican central banks)

Gosbank (Russian: Госбанк), the State Bank of the USSR, was the central bank of the Soviet Union and the sole bank in the country from the 1930s until the late 1980s. It was one of the three Soviet economic authorities, along with Gosplan (the State Planning Committee) and Gossnab (the State Committee for Material Technical Supply).

Functions

Gosbank combined the functions of a central bank and a commercial bank in a "monobank" system. It issued currency, held the deposits of state enterprises, extended short-term credit according to the plan, and operated the cashless payment system between enterprises. Cash and non-cash money circulated in separate spheres: households used cash, while enterprise transactions were recorded as transfers between Gosbank accounts, allowing the state to monitor compliance with production plans.

History

The State Bank was established in 1921 during the New Economic Policy, replacing the People's Bank of the RSFSR. It became the State Bank of the USSR in 1923. Banking reforms in 1930–32 eliminated commercial credit and concentrated nearly all short-term lending in Gosbank. Under perestroika, specialized banks were spun off from 1988 onward, and after the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, Gosbank's functions passed to the central banks of the successor states, principally the Central Bank of Russia.
