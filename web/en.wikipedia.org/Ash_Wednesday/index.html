Ash Wednesday

Ash Wednesday is a holy day of prayer and fasting in many Western Christian denominations. It is preceded by Shrove Tuesday and marks the first day of Lent, a season of penitence lasting six weeks before Easter Sunday. It is observed by Catholics, Lutherans, Moravians, Anglicans, Methodists, Nazarenes, and many other Christians.

Ashes are imposed on the forehead in the form of a cross.

Observance

The day takes its name from the practice of placing ashes on the foreheads of participants, accompanied by the words "Remember you are dust, and to dust you shall return" or "Repent, and believe in the Gospel". The ashes are traditionally prepared by burning the palm leaves of the previous year's Palm Sunday celebrations.

Date

Ash Wednesday falls 46 days before Easter (40 fasting days, excluding Sundays). Because Easter is a movable feast, Ash Wednesday can occur as early as 4 February and as late as 10 March.

History

The practice of imposing ashes has roots in ancient mourning and penitential customs described in the Hebrew Bible, and the association with the start of Lent was established by the early medieval period; Pope Gregory the Great is credited with fixing the beginning of Lent on this day in the Western calendar.
