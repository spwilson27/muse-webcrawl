Borough

A borough town centre

A borough is an administrative division in various English-speaking countries. In principle, the term borough designates a self-governing walled town, although in practice the term has had a variety of different meanings in different jurisdictions. The word derives from the Old English burh, meaning a fortified settlement, and is cognate with similar words in other Germanic languages.

Boroughs are found in the United Kingdom, Ireland, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and the United States, where the term is used in several states including Alaska, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, and Minnesota.

History

In medieval England, boroughs were settlements granted certain privileges, typically including the right to hold markets, elect their own officials, and be represented in Parliament. The Municipal Corporations Act 1835 reformed borough government in England and Wales, and subsequent legislation, including the Local Government Act 1972, reshaped or abolished many historic boroughs.

United Kingdom

In England, borough status is granted by royal charter and confers certain ceremonial privileges, including the right to call the council's chairman a mayor. London is divided into 32 London boroughs plus the City of London, each a local government district. In Scotland, burghs were the equivalent unit until 1975.

United States

In the United States the meaning varies by state. New York City is divided into five boroughs — Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island — each coextensive with a county. In Alaska, a borough is equivalent to a county. In Pennsylvania and New Jersey, a borough is a type of municipality, smaller than a city.
