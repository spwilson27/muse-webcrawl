Blood alcohol content

Blood alcohol content (BAC), also called blood alcohol concentration or blood alcohol level, is a measurement of alcohol intoxication used for legal and medical purposes. It is usually expressed as a mass of alcohol per volume of blood; in most jurisdictions a BAC of 0.10% means 0.10 grams of alcohol per 100 millilitres of blood.

Measurement

BAC can be measured directly from a blood sample or estimated from breath using a breathalyser, exploiting the fixed ratio between alcohol in the breath and alcohol in the blood. The Widmark formula provides a rough estimate from the amount consumed, body weight and sex. The body eliminates alcohol at a roughly constant rate of about 0.015% per hour in most people.

Effects by level

BACTypical effects
0.02–0.03%Mild relaxation, slight mood elevation
0.05–0.06%Lowered inhibition, impaired judgement beginning
0.08–0.10%Clear impairment of balance, speech and reaction time
0.15–0.20%Marked intoxication, nausea, blackouts possible
0.30%+Stupor, risk of coma
0.40%+Potentially fatal respiratory depression

Legal limits

Most countries set a legal limit for driving between 0.02% and 0.08%. The limit is 0.08% in most of the United States and in England and Wales, 0.05% in much of Europe and Australia, and 0.02% or effectively zero in several countries. Lower or zero limits commonly apply to novice and commercial drivers.
