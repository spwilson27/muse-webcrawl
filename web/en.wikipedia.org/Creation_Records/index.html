Creation Records was a British independent record label founded in 1983 by Alan McGee, Dick Green, and Joe Foster, named after the 1960s band The Creation. From indie-pop beginnings it became one of the defining labels of British guitar music, launching the shoegaze movement with My Bloody Valentine and breaking Britpop's biggest band, Oasis, before McGee wound it down in 1999–2000.

Creation defined the sound of British indie in the late 1980s and mid-1990s.

History

Creation began with singles by McGee's own band Biff Bang Pow! and early releases by The Jesus and Mary Chain, whose feedback-drenched debut single "Upside Down" (1984) became an indie landmark. The label championed The Weather Prophets, Primal Scream, Felt, The House of Love, and Teenage Fanclub, then bankrolled My Bloody Valentine's Loveless (1991), whose protracted, hugely expensive recording became industry legend. Facing bankruptcy, McGee sold half the label to Sony in 1992.

In 1993 McGee saw a young Manchester band, Oasis, playing at King Tut's Wah Wah Hut in Glasgow and signed them on the spot. Oasis's Definitely Maybe (1994) and (What's the Story) Morning Glory? (1995) sold in the tens of millions and made Creation briefly the centre of British pop culture during the Britpop era, its parties attended by politicians courting "Cool Britannia". Primal Scream's Screamadelica (1991) had already bridged indie and rave culture. In December 1999 McGee announced the label's closure, citing exhaustion and a desire to move on; its last significant releases appeared in 2000. He later founded the Poptones and 359 Music labels.

Legacy

Creation's story was dramatised in the 2021 film Creation Stories. Its roster — Ride, Slowdive, Super Furry Animals, The Boo Radleys, Swervedriver, Momus — embodied a romantic, chaotic model of the independent label that later British indie culture repeatedly mythologised.
