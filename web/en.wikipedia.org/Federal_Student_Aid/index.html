Federal Student Aid

Federal Student Aid (FSA) is an office of the United States Department of Education. It is the largest provider of student financial aid in the nation, administering the federal student financial assistance programs authorized under Title IV of the Higher Education Act of 1965. FSA oversees a loan portfolio of well over one trillion dollars owed by tens of millions of borrowers.

Programs

Federal Student Aid administers grants, loans, and work-study funds for students attending college or career school, including:

  • Pell Grants — need-based grants, primarily for low-income undergraduate students, which generally do not have to be repaid.
  • Direct Loans — subsidized and unsubsidized loans for students, and PLUS loans for parents and graduate students.
  • Federal Work-Study — part-time employment for students with financial need.
  • TEACH Grants and Iraq and Afghanistan Service Grants.

Applying: the FAFSA

Students apply for aid by completing the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA), which collects financial information used to calculate an eligibility index (formerly the Expected Family Contribution, now the Student Aid Index). The FAFSA Simplification Act led to a redesigned form that launched, with widely reported technical difficulties, for the 2024–25 award year.

Repayment

FSA oversees loan servicing through contracted servicers. Borrowers may choose among repayment plans, including income-driven repayment plans that tie payments to income and forgive remaining balances after a set period. The office also administers the Public Service Loan Forgiveness program and discharges for borrowers defrauded by their institutions.
