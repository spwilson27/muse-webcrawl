Allied Control Council

The Allied Control Council (ACC), also called the Four Powers, was the military occupation governing body of Germany after the end of the Second World War in Europe. Its members were the commanders-in-chief of the four occupying powers — the United States, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union and France — and it exercised supreme authority over matters concerning Germany as a whole.

Creation and work

The council was established under agreements reached by the European Advisory Commission in London and confirmed at the Potsdam Conference of 1945. It met for the first time in Berlin on 10 September 1945, sitting in the Kammergericht building in Schöneberg, which lay in the American sector. Decisions of the council required unanimity among the four powers.

Working through a Coordinating Committee and numerous directorates, the ACC issued laws and directives for occupied Germany, implementing the agreed "four Ds": demilitarisation, denazification, democratisation and decentralisation. Its proclamations included Control Council Law No. 1, which repealed Nazi laws, and Law No. 10, which provided a legal basis for prosecuting war criminals in each zone.

Breakdown

As Cold War tensions grew, four-power cooperation deteriorated. On 20 March 1948 the Soviet delegation walked out of the council, and it never functioned effectively again, although it was never formally dissolved; the Western powers continued to invoke its residual authority for Berlin questions. The quadripartite machinery for Berlin — though not the council itself — saw a limited revival in the Four Power Agreement on Berlin of 1971.
