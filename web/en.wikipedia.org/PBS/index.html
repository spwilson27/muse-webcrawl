The Public Broadcasting Service (PBS) is an American public broadcaster and non-commercial, free-to-air television network based in Arlington, Virginia. PBS is a publicly funded nonprofit organisation and the most prominent provider of educational programmes to public television stations in the United States, distributing shows such as PBS News Hour, Frontline, Nova, Antiques Roadshow, Masterpiece, and the children's line-up PBS Kids, home of Sesame Street for much of its history.

Structure

Unlike commercial networks or the BBC, PBS does not own its stations and produces few programmes itself. It is a membership organisation of more than 330 non-commercial, educational licensees — including state networks, universities, and community groups — and acts primarily as a distributor: programmes are supplied by member stations such as WGBH Boston, WNET New York, and WETA Washington, by independent producers, and by foreign broadcasters. Stations are free to schedule national programmes alongside local content.

History

PBS was established in 1969 under the framework of the Public Broadcasting Act of 1967, which also created the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, and it began operations on 5 October 1970, succeeding National Educational Television (NET). Over the following decades it became the main US outlet for British drama and documentaries, science and history programming (including Ken Burns's documentaries such as The Civil War), and acclaimed children's series.

Funding

PBS and its member stations are funded by a combination of federal appropriations channelled through the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, state and local government support, viewer memberships and donations, foundation grants, and corporate underwriting — the restrained form of sponsorship associated with the phrase "brought to you by". The appropriate levels of federal funding have been a recurring subject of political debate.
