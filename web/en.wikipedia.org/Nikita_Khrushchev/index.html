Nikita Khrushchev

Born: 15 April 1894, Kalinovka, Russian Empire

Died: 11 September 1971, Moscow

First Secretary: 1953–1964

Nikita Sergeyevich Khrushchev was the First Secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union from 1953 to 1964 and Chairman of the Council of Ministers from 1958 to 1964, leading the USSR through much of the early Cold War after the death of Joseph Stalin.

Rise

Born to a poor peasant family, Khrushchev worked as a metal fitter, fought in the Russian Civil War, and rose through the party in Ukraine and Moscow, participating in Stalin's purges.

Leadership

His 1956 "Secret Speech" denouncing Stalin's cult of personality began de-Stalinisation and the Khrushchev Thaw in cultural life. Domestic policies included the Virgin Lands campaign, mass housing construction (the khrushchyovka apartment blocks), and agricultural reforms. Abroad he proclaimed "peaceful coexistence", launched Sputnik-era space triumphs including Yuri Gagarin's flight, but also crushed the Hungarian uprising of 1956, built the Berlin Wall crisis of 1961, and brought the world to the brink in the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962.

Downfall

Removed by his colleagues in October 1964 and replaced by Leonid Brezhnev, he lived in retirement and dictated memoirs that were published in the West.
