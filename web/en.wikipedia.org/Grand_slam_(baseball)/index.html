Grand slam (baseball)

In baseball, a grand slam is a home run hit with all three bases occupied by baserunners — the bases are "loaded" — thereby scoring four runs, the most possible on a single play. A grand slam is credited with four runs batted in (RBIs) for the batter.

Notable grand slams

Don Mattingly set a major league record with six grand slams in a single season, in 1987. Alex Rodriguez holds the career record with 25 grand slams. One of the most famous came in the 1988 World Series, when Kirk Gibson, injured and limping, hit a walk-off home run — though not a grand slam — off Dennis Eckersley; true World Series grand slams include those by Moose Skowron (1956) and Tino Martinez (1998). In 1999 Robin Ventura hit a famous "grand slam single" in the National League Championship Series, so called because he was mobbed by teammates before completing his circuit of the bases and was credited only with a single.

Ultimate grand slam

A walk-off grand slam that erases a three-run deficit in the final inning is sometimes called an "ultimate grand slam", since it is the only play that can turn a three-run loss into a win in a single swing. A grand slam on the first pitch a batter ever faces in the major leagues is exceptionally rare; Kevin Kouzmanoff achieved the feat in 2006.

Pitcher grand slams

Grand slams by pitchers are rare in the modern era. Tony Cloninger hit two grand slams in a single game for the Atlanta Braves in 1966, a unique achievement for a pitcher.
