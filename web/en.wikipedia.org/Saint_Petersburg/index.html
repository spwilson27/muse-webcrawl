Saint Petersburg is the second-largest city in Russia and a federal city on the Neva River delta at the head of the Gulf of Finland. Founded by Tsar Peter the Great in 1703 as Russia's "window to Europe", it served as capital of the Russian Empire for more than two centuries (1713–1918, with a brief interruption). It was renamed Petrograd in 1914 and Leningrad in 1924 after Vladimir Lenin, reverting to Saint Petersburg following a referendum in 1991.

History

Peter laid the Peter and Paul Fortress on Hare Island in 1703, during the Great Northern War with Sweden, and built the city by forced labour at enormous human cost in the Neva marshes. Successive rulers embellished it with Baroque and Neoclassical palaces: the Winter Palace (now the Hermitage Museum), the Admiralty, St Isaac's Cathedral and the colonnaded Kazan Cathedral along Nevsky Prospekt, the main avenue. The city was the stage of the Decembrist revolt of 1825 and of the revolutions of 1905 and 1917; the October Revolution began here with the storming of the Winter Palace.

Siege of Leningrad

During the Second World War, German and Finnish forces encircled the city for 872 days (September 1941 – January 1944). The siege caused the deaths of well over a million civilians, most from starvation, and remains a central trauma and symbol of endurance in Russian memory; supplies crossed frozen Lake Ladoga on the "Road of Life".

Culture

Saint Petersburg is Russia's cultural capital. The Hermitage, in the Winter Palace complex, is one of the largest art museums in the world; the Mariinsky Theatre is renowned for ballet and opera; and the city's historic centre, a network of canals, bridges and palaces, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The short summer nights of June, the "White Nights", are celebrated with festivals.
