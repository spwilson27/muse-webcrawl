The United States Special Operations Command (USSOCOM or SOCOM) is the unified combatant command charged with overseeing the various special operations component commands of the Army, Marine Corps, Navy, and Air Force of the United States Armed Forces. The command is part of the Department of Defense and is the only unified combatant command created by an Act of Congress.

USSOCOM is headquartered at MacDill Air Force Base in Tampa, Florida. The idea of an American unified special operations command originated in the aftermath of Operation Eagle Claw, the disastrous attempted rescue of hostages at the American embassy in Iran in 1980. The ensuing investigation highlighted the need for a unified command structure for special operations forces.

History

Following the recommendations of the Cohen-Nunn Amendment to the Goldwater–Nichols Act, USSOCOM was activated on 16 April 1987. General James Lindsay served as its first commander. The command assumed responsibility for the training, equipping, and employment of America's special operations forces, including Army Special Forces ("Green Berets"), Navy SEALs, Army Rangers, Air Force special tactics units, and later Marine Corps Forces Special Operations Command (MARSOC).

Component commands

  • United States Army Special Operations Command (USASOC)
  • Naval Special Warfare Command (NAVSPECWARCOM)
  • Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC)
  • Marine Corps Forces Special Operations Command (MARSOC)
  • Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC) – a sub-unified command

Role

SOCOM's responsibilities include developing special operations strategy, doctrine, and tactics; preparing forces for assignment to regional combatant commanders; and conducting missions such as direct action, special reconnaissance, counter-terrorism, unconventional warfare, foreign internal defense, and civil affairs operations. The command's motto reflects its global remit, and its forces have participated in operations from Panama and the Gulf War to Afghanistan and Iraq.
