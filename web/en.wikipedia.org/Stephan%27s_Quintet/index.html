Stephan's Quintet is a visual grouping of five galaxies in the constellation Pegasus, discovered by Édouard Stephan in 1877 at the Marseille Observatory. It was the first compact galaxy group ever identified.

Members

Four of the galaxies — NGC 7317, 7318A, 7319 and (as a pair) 7318 — form a genuine compact group about 290 million light-years away and are gravitationally interacting. The fifth, NGC 7320, is a foreground galaxy only about 40 million light-years distant, a fact revealed by its much lower redshift, first noted by Edwin Hubble's successors; the discordant redshift of NGC 7320 was long cited in debates over redshift interpretation.

Observations

The group is a laboratory for galaxy interactions, featuring a large intergalactic shock front produced as NGC 7318B plunges through the group at high speed, heating gas that glows in X-rays. The quintet was among the first targets imaged by the James Webb Space Telescope, whose 2022 image revealed star formation and outflowing gas in unprecedented detail.
