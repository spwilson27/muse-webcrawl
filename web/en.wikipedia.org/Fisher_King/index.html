Fisher King

The Fisher King is a figure in Arthurian legend, the last in a long line of British kings tasked with guarding the Holy Grail. The Fisher King is both the protector and physical embodiment of his lands, but a wound renders him impotent and his kingdom barren. Unable to walk or ride a horse, he is carried about on a boat or stands fishing by a river, endlessly waiting for a "knight who can heal him" to arrive.

The Fisher King first appears in Chrétien de Troyes' Perceval, the Story of the Grail (late 12th century), the first work to connect the Grail with the Arthurian tradition. Later versions of the legend expand his story considerably; Robert de Boron's Joseph d'Arimathie gives him the name Bron, while Wolfram von Eschenbach's Parzival calls him Anfortas.

The wound and the wasteland

In most versions, the king's maiming — often a wound through the thighs — is bound up with the fate of his land: as the king weakens, his kingdom becomes a barren wasteland. Only the destined knight, usually Perceval, can restore both king and land by asking the right question about the grail procession, or by some other act of compassion or purity. In Chrétien's unfinished romance, Perceval fails to ask the question and the king remains unhealed; later continuations and adaptations resolve the story in different ways.

Origins and interpretation

Scholars have traced the Fisher King to Celtic mythology, particularly the Welsh figure Bran the Blessed, who possessed a cauldron of regeneration and, in the Mabinogion, is wounded in the foot and has his head carried to London after death. Jessie Weston, in From Ritual to Romance (1920), argued that the legend preserves elements of ancient fertility rituals — a thesis that strongly influenced T. S. Eliot's poem The Waste Land (1922), in which the Fisher King appears fishing "in the dull canal" and sitting "upon the shore / Fishing, with the arid plain behind me."

In modern culture

The figure gives his name to Terry Gilliam's 1991 film The Fisher King, starring Jeff Bridges and Robin Williams, in which a guilt-stricken radio DJ seeks redemption by helping a traumatised homeless man who believes himself a knight seeking the Grail in modern New York.
