Wang Bi (Chinese: 王弼; 226–249), courtesy name Fusi, was a Chinese philosopher of the state of Cao Wei during the Three Kingdoms period. Despite dying at only twenty-three, he became one of the most influential commentators in Chinese intellectual history, and a founding figure of Xuanxue ("Dark Learning"), often called Neo-Daoism. He is not to be confused with the much later Ming-dynasty philosopher Wang Yangming.

Thought

Wang Bi argued that all being arises from non-being (wu), a single, undifferentiated root underlying the multiplicity of things. His commentaries sought to harmonise Confucian social teaching with Daoist metaphysics, reading Confucius as a sage who embodied non-being precisely by not speaking of it.

Works

His commentaries on the Laozi (Daodejing) and the Zhou Yi (I Ching) became the standard editions studied for centuries. His interpretation of the I Ching displaced earlier cosmological readings by focusing on the meaning of the hexagrams' lines rather than on images and numerology. His commentary on the Analects survives only in fragments quoted by later scholars.

Life

A child prodigy from a family of scholars, Wang Bi held a minor court post under the Wei regent's circle and died during an epidemic in 249, the year of a major political upheaval at the Wei court.
