Stephen Joseph Harper (born April 30, 1959) is a Canadian politician who served as the 22nd prime minister of Canada from 2006 to 2015. He was the first leader of the modern Conservative Party of Canada, formed by merging the Canadian Alliance and the Progressive Conservatives.

Early political career

Harper was first elected to the House of Commons in 1993 as a Reform Party MP for Calgary West. After a spell heading the National Citizens Coalition, he won the leadership of the Canadian Alliance in 2002 and became Leader of the Opposition.

Prime Minister (2006–2015)

Harper won minority governments in 2006 and 2008, and a majority in 2011. His governments cut the GST from 7% to 5%, introduced income splitting for families, negotiated trade agreements including the EU's CETA (in principle), extended Canada's Afghan mission, recognised the Québécois as a nation within Canada, and steered the economy through the 2008 financial crisis with a stimulus programme.

Defeat and later career

The Conservatives lost the 2015 election to Justin Trudeau's Liberals. Harper resigned as leader, left Parliament in 2016, founded a consultancy, and became chairman of the International Democracy Union in 2018.
