Rimini

Rimini (Italian: [ˈriːmini]) is a city on the Adriatic coast of the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, capital of the Province of Rimini, with a population of about 150,000. Founded by the Romans in 268 BC as Ariminum, it is one of Italy's best-known seaside resorts, famous for its long sandy beaches and nightlife, and the birthplace of film director Federico Fellini.

The Arch of Augustus (27 BC), the oldest surviving Roman triumphal arch.

History

Roman Ariminum stood at the junction of the Via Flaminia from Rome and the Via Aemilia, a strategic Adriatic port and colony. Surviving Roman monuments include the Arch of Augustus, the Tiberius Bridge across the Marecchia river, and an amphitheatre. In the Middle Ages Rimini was contested among Byzantines, Lombards and the Papal States; under the Malatesta family in the 15th century it produced the Tempio Malatestiano, redesigned by Leon Battista Alberti for Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta.

Tourism

From the opening of the first bathing establishment in 1843, Rimini grew into a mass beach resort, with some 15 km of sandy shore lined with hotels in districts such as Marina Centro, Bellariva and Viserba. Fellini immortalised the town in films such as Amarcord (1973), and a museum and park now commemorate him.
