The Port of Marseille, officially the Grand Port Maritime de Marseille and marketed as Marseille Fos Port, is France's largest seaport and one of the biggest on the Mediterranean. It comprises the historic eastern basins within the city of Marseille — centred on the Old Port used since antiquity — and the modern industrial port zone at Fos-sur-Mer, some 50 km to the northwest, built from the 1960s in the Gulf of Fos.

Operations

The port handles crude oil and refined products, liquefied gas, dry bulk, containers, roll-on/roll-off traffic and passengers, including ferries to Corsica and North Africa and a large cruise business. The Fos site hosts deep-water container terminals and heavy industry (steel, petrochemicals), while the Marseille basins concentrate on passengers, repair yards and Mediterranean general cargo.

History

Marseille's Old Port served Greek Massalia from about 600 BC. In the 19th century the port expanded north with the Joliette docks; containerisation and oil traffic in the 20th century drove the move to Fos, planned as a European-scale industrial port. Today Marseille Fos regularly ranks among the top Mediterranean ports by tonnage and is France's leading cruise port.
