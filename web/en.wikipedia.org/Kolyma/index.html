Kolyma is a historical region in the Russian Far East, roughly corresponding to the basin of the Kolyma River in present-day Magadan Oblast and north-eastern Yakutia. One of the coldest inhabited places on Earth, it became notorious as the site of some of the harshest camps of the Soviet Gulag system.

Geography and climate

The region is mountainous taiga and tundra drained by the 2,129 km Kolyma River, which flows into the East Siberian Sea. Winters are extremely cold — temperatures below −50 °C are common inland — and the ground is permafrost. The port of Magadan on the Sea of Okhotsk is the gateway to the region.

Gold and Dalstroy

Gold was discovered in the 1920s, and in 1931 the state trust Dalstroy was created to exploit the region using forced labour. Prisoners of the Sevvostlag camps built Magadan, mined gold and tin in open pits and underground mines, and constructed the Kolyma Highway — the "Road of Bones" — toward Yakutsk. Mortality was very high; estimates of total deaths range into the hundreds of thousands.

Memory

Varlam Shalamov's Kolyma Tales, drawn from his seventeen years in the camps, is the classic literary testimony. After Stalin's death the camps were dismantled, though mining continued with free labour. Memorials in Magadan, including the Mask of Sorrow, commemorate the victims.
