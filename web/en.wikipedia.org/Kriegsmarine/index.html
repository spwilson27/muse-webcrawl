The Kriegsmarine ("War Navy") was the navy of Nazi Germany from 1935 to 1945. It superseded the Reichsmarine of the Weimar Republic, taking its new name when Germany openly repudiated the naval clauses of the Treaty of Versailles. During the Second World War it waged the Battle of the Atlantic with its U-boat arm and operated surface raiders and major warships including the battleship Bismarck.

Rebuilding and Plan Z

The 1935 Anglo-German Naval Agreement allowed Germany a fleet of 35 per cent of British tonnage. Grand Admiral Erich Raeder's Plan Z, approved in 1939, envisaged a large balanced fleet by the mid-1940s, but war came years early and construction of large ships was largely abandoned in favour of U-boats.

Second World War

Kriegsmarine forces supported the invasions of Poland, Norway and the Low Countries and fought the Royal Navy in the Atlantic and Arctic. Its surface fleet sank the battlecruiser HMS Hood in 1941 before Bismarck herself was sunk; the battleship Tirpitz tied down Allied forces in Norway until destroyed in 1944. The U-boat arm under Karl Dönitz nearly severed Allied supply lines but was defeated by mid-1943 through air cover, radar, codebreaking and convoy tactics, suffering the highest loss rate of any German service branch.

End

Dönitz succeeded Raeder in 1943 and, briefly, Hitler as head of state in April–May 1945. The Kriegsmarine was dissolved after Germany's surrender; surviving ships were divided among the Allies and German crews were used to clear mines.
