Gdansk

"Gdansk" is the ASCII rendering of the Polish name Gdańsk. This page summarises the city under its unaccented English spelling.

Gdansk (Polish: Gdańsk) is a city on the Baltic coast of northern Poland. With a population of about 486,000, it is the capital and largest city of the Pomeranian Voivodeship and forms, together with Gdynia and Sopot, the metropolitan area known as the Tricity. Its port is among the largest on the Baltic Sea.

History

Gdańsk was founded in the 10th century and first mentioned in records around 997. It grew into a wealthy Hanseatic trading port, passing between Polish, Teutonic Order, Prussian and German rule over the centuries; between the world wars it was the Free City of Danzig. The first shots of the Second World War were fired at Westerplatte on 1 September 1939. The largely destroyed city was rebuilt after 1945 as part of Poland.

In August 1980, strikes at the Gdańsk Shipyard led by Lech Wałęsa gave birth to the Solidarity trade union, a turning point in the collapse of communism in Central and Eastern Europe.
