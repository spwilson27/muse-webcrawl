First Intermediate Period of Egypt

First Intermediate Period
Period: c. 2181 – c. 2055 BC
Dynasties: 7th–11th (overlapping)
Capitals: Memphis; Heracleopolis; Thebes
Preceded by: Old Kingdom
Followed by: Middle Kingdom
Model soldiers from the tomb of Mesehti, Asyut, 11th Dynasty.

The First Intermediate Period, described by ancient Egyptians as a time of chaos, occurred between the end of the Old Kingdom and the beginning of the Middle Kingdom, roughly 2181 to 2055 BC. It spans the Seventh through part of the Eleventh dynasties and is characterised by the collapse of centralised royal authority and the rise of powerful provincial rulers, the nomarchs.

Causes and character

The period followed the exceptionally long reign of Pepi II and the fragmentation of power under weak successors. Low Nile floods, possibly linked to aridification around 2200 BC, are thought to have caused famine and undermined the crown. Provincial governors, once appointed officials, became hereditary local dynasts who maintained their own courts, armies, and monumental tombs.

The era was once portrayed as a "dark age" of anarchy, partly on the basis of literary works such as the Admonitions of Ipuwer; modern scholarship gives a more nuanced picture of regional prosperity and cultural creativity alongside political division.

Political division and reunification

Egypt divided broadly between a northern kingdom ruled from Heracleopolis (the Ninth and Tenth dynasties) and a southern kingdom based at Thebes (the Eleventh Dynasty). Conflict between the two spheres ended when the Theban king Mentuhotep II defeated the Heracleopolitan rulers and reunified Egypt around 2055 BC, founding the Middle Kingdom.
