The Brumadinho dam disaster occurred on 25 January 2019 when Dam I, a tailings dam at the Córrego do Feijão iron ore mine in Brumadinho, Minas Gerais, Brazil, suffered a catastrophic failure. The dam, owned by the mining company Vale, released a mudflow of mining waste that engulfed the mine's offices and cafeteria — where many workers were at lunch — as well as homes, farms, inns and roads in the valley below.

Impact

The disaster killed 270 people, most of them Vale employees and contractors. The mud, containing millions of cubic metres of tailings, flowed into the Paraopeba River, contaminating water supplies and devastating ecosystems downstream. It was one of the deadliest mining disasters in Brazilian history, occurring just over three years after the Mariana dam disaster, which also involved a Vale joint venture.

Investigations and aftermath

Investigations attributed the failure to liquefaction of the tailings and found that the dam had known stability problems; Vale executives and auditors from TÜV SÜD faced criminal charges including homicide. In February 2021 Vale agreed to pay about R$37.7 billion (around US$7 billion) in compensation and remediation to the state of Minas Gerais. The disaster prompted Brazil to ban upstream tailings dams and require the decommissioning of existing ones, and led to the creation of the Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management.
