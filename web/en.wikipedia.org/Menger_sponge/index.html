Menger sponge

In mathematics, the Menger sponge (also known as the Menger cube) is a three-dimensional fractal curve. It was first described by Karl Menger in 1926 while exploring the concept of topological dimension; it is a three-dimensional generalization of the Cantor set and the Sierpiński carpet.

Construction

Begin with a cube. Divide it into 27 equal smaller cubes, as in a Rubik's Cube, and remove the central cube of each face and the cube at the very centre — 7 cubes in total — leaving 20. Repeat the process on each of the remaining 20 cubes, and so on indefinitely. At each iteration the number of cubes multiplies by 20 while their size shrinks by a factor of 3.

Properties

In the limit, the sponge has zero volume (each step removes a fixed fraction of what remains) but infinite surface area. Its Hausdorff dimension is log 20 / log 3 ≈ 2.727. The Menger sponge is a "universal curve": every one-dimensional curve can be embedded in it, and it contains copies of every compact surface — a remarkable topological property that motivated Menger's original work.
