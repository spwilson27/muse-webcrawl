Villahermosa (Spanish pronunciation: [biʝaeɾˈmosa], lit. "Beautiful Village") is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Tabasco, and the seat of the municipality of Centro. Located in southeastern Mexico along the Grijalva River, it is a major centre of the country's petroleum industry and a gateway to the Olmec archaeological site of La Venta.

History

The city was founded in 1564 by Diego de Quijada under the name Villa Felipe II and later known as San Juan Bautista. It was repeatedly attacked by pirates in the colonial era and served as the state capital from the 19th century. Villahermosa grew rapidly after the discovery of large oil fields in Tabasco and the Gulf of Mexico in the 1970s.

Geography and climate

Villahermosa lies in a low, humid tropical plain crossed by the Grijalva and Carrizal rivers and numerous lagoons. It has a tropical monsoon climate with high temperatures and heavy rainfall year-round, and it is prone to flooding, most severely in 2007 when much of Tabasco was inundated.

Economy and sights

The economy is dominated by the state oil company Pemex and related services. The Parque-Museo La Venta displays colossal Olmec stone heads and other monuments moved from the site of La Venta. Other attractions include the Yumká nature reserve, the Museo Regional de Antropología Carlos Pellicer Cámara, and the Paseo Tabasco.
