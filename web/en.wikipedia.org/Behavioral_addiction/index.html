Behavioral addiction

Behavioral addiction
FieldPsychiatry, psychology
ExamplesGambling disorder, gaming disorder, compulsive buying
TreatmentCognitive behavioral therapy, motivational interviewing

Gambling is the most studied behavioral addiction.

Behavioral addiction is a form of addiction involving compulsive engagement in a rewarding non-substance-related behavior, despite significant harm or negative consequences. Like substance addictions, behavioral addictions involve craving, loss of control, tolerance-like escalation and continued use despite adverse outcomes.

Classification

Gambling disorder is classified with substance-related and addictive disorders in the DSM-5, and gaming disorder appears in the ICD-11. Other proposed behavioral addictions—such as compulsive shopping, sex addiction, food addiction and internet addiction—remain subjects of scientific debate about diagnostic boundaries.

Mechanisms

Research points to shared neurobiological pathways with substance addiction, particularly the mesolimbic dopamine reward system, impaired impulse control, and cue-induced craving. Genetic vulnerability, stress and co-occurring disorders such as depression and ADHD increase risk.

Treatment

Cognitive behavioral therapy is the mainstay of treatment, alongside self-help groups, motivational interviewing and, in some cases, medications targeting craving or co-occurring conditions.
