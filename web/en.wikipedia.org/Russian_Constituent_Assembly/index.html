Russian Constituent Assembly

The Russian Constituent Assembly (Russian: Всероссийское учредительное собрание) was a democratically elected constitutional body convened in Russia after the February Revolution of 1917. Elections held in November 1917 — among the largest democratic elections in history to that date, and among the first with universal suffrage including women — gave the Socialist Revolutionary Party the largest share of seats, reflecting its support among the peasantry.

The Constituent Assembly met for a single day at the Tauride Palace.

Background

The demand for a constituent assembly had been a central slogan of Russian liberal and socialist movements for decades. After the abdication of Nicholas II, the Provisional Government promised elections, but repeatedly delayed them. The Bolsheviks seized power in the October Revolution of 1917 partly in the name of the soviets, yet the elections to the Assembly went ahead on 25 November (12 November Old Style). Voter lists had been drawn up before the split of the Socialist Revolutionaries into Left and Right factions, so the Bolsheviks and Left SRs received far fewer seats than their current support suggested.

Single session and dispersal

The Assembly met in the Tauride Palace in Petrograd on 18–19 January 1918 (5–6 January O.S.). It elected the Socialist Revolutionary Viktor Chernov as president and refused to endorse the Bolshevik programme. After sitting through the night, the deputies were told by the guard commander — in a famous phrase attributed to the sailor Anatoly Zheleznyakov — that "the guard is tired", and the hall was closed. The following day the Bolshevik-controlled Central Executive Committee formally dissolved the Assembly. Demonstrations in its defence were fired upon. Its brief life has remained a symbol of Russia's unrealised democratic path during the revolution.
