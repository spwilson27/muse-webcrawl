Microsoft Store

The Microsoft Store is a digital distribution platform operated by Microsoft, serving as the app store for Windows. It began as the Windows Store with Windows 8 in 2012 and was later unified with Microsoft's other storefronts to offer apps, games, movies and TV shows in a single marketplace. (The name also formerly applied to Microsoft's chain of retail stores, which closed in 2020.)

History

The Windows Store launched alongside Windows 8, distributing "Metro-style" apps. With Windows 10 it merged content from Windows Phone Store, Xbox Video and Xbox Music. In 2017 the store was rebranded from Windows Store to Microsoft Store. Windows 11 (2021) brought a redesigned store with a more open policy: developers could submit Win32 and other traditional desktop applications, keep their own payment systems for non-game apps, and even feature third-party storefronts such as the Amazon Appstore and Epic Games Store.

Policies

Apps in the store are sandboxed and certified by Microsoft, and are updated automatically. Microsoft's revenue share is generally 15 percent for apps and 12 percent for games sold through its own commerce platform; developers using their own payment processing for apps can keep all revenue. Games on PC are also sold through the Xbox app and Game Pass subscriptions.

Retail stores

Microsoft opened its first physical Microsoft Store in 2009, eventually operating more than a hundred locations, mostly in the United States. In June 2020 the company announced the permanent closure of nearly all retail stores, retaining a few "Experience Centers."
