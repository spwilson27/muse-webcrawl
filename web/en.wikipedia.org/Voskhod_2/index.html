Voskhod 2

Voskhod 2 was a Soviet crewed space mission launched on 18 March 1965. The two-man crew, Pavel Belyayev and Alexei Leonov, completed 17 orbits of the Earth. During the flight Leonov performed the first spacewalk in history, spending about 12 minutes outside the spacecraft attached to an inflatable airlock — a landmark of the Space Race.

The spacewalk

Soon after reaching orbit, the inflatable airlock Volga was deployed and pressurised. Leonov, wearing the Berkut spacesuit with about 45 minutes of oxygen, exited the airlock and floated at the end of a 5.35-metre tether, filming and reporting that he felt well. When he tried to re-enter, his suit had ballooned in vacuum and would not fit through the airlock; he had to bleed off pressure to a dangerous level to squeeze back inside — an episode not publicly acknowledged for years.

Landing

The return was also troubled: the automatic landing system failed, forcing Belyayev to perform the first manual re-entry of a crewed Soviet spacecraft. Voskhod 2 landed off course in deep snow in the Ural Mountains near Perm, and the crew spent a night in the forest, threatened by wolves, before rescuers reached them the following day. Despite the difficulties, the mission was celebrated as a major Soviet achievement, preceding the first American spacewalk by nearly three months.
