The Boston Red Sox are an American professional baseball team based in Boston, Massachusetts. A charter member of the American League (founded 1901), the club plays at Fenway Park, the oldest stadium in Major League Baseball. The Red Sox have won nine World Series championships, tied for the third-most of any MLB team.

Early dominance and the "Curse"

Boston won the first modern World Series in 1903 (as the Boston Americans) and four more titles between 1912 and 1918 behind stars such as Cy Young, Tris Speaker, and the young pitcher-turned-outfielder Babe Ruth. After the 1919 sale of Ruth to the New York Yankees — a transaction blamed on owner Harry Frazee financing Broadway productions — the club entered an 86-year championship drought that fans dubbed the "Curse of the Bambino", marked by agonising near-misses in 1946, 1967, 1975, and 1986.

Breaking the curse

In the 2004 American League Championship Series, the Red Sox became the first team in MLB history to overcome a 3–0 series deficit, defeating the Yankees, and then swept the St. Louis Cardinals in the World Series to end the drought. Further titles followed in 2007, 2013 (a season of civic healing after the Boston Marathon bombing), and 2018, when the club won a franchise-record 108 regular-season games.

Fenway Park and culture

Fenway Park's quirks — the 37-foot Green Monster in left field, Pesky's Pole, and the hand-operated scoreboard — make it one of baseball's most beloved parks. The fan base, "Red Sox Nation", is among the most devoted in American sports, and the team's rivalry with the Yankees is one of the fiercest in professional sport.
