Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh (born Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark, later Philip Mountbatten; 10 June 1921 – 9 April 2021), was the husband of Queen Elizabeth II and, as such, the consort of the British monarch from Elizabeth's accession on 6 February 1952 until his death in 2021, making him the longest-serving royal consort in history.

Early life and naval career

Philip was born into the Greek and Danish royal families in Corfu, but his family was exiled from Greece when he was a child. Educated in France, Germany and the United Kingdom, he joined the Royal Navy in 1939 and served with distinction in the Mediterranean and Pacific fleets during the Second World War.

Marriage and role

He married Princess Elizabeth in 1947; they had four children: Charles, Anne, Andrew and Edward. Philip supported the Queen throughout her reign, founded the Duke of Edinburgh's Award in 1956, and was a patron of hundreds of organizations with interests in conservation, engineering and sport. He retired from public duties in 2017.
