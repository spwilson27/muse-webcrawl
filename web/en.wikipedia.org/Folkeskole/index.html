Folkeskole

The Folkeskole (Danish for "the people's school") is the Danish municipal primary and lower secondary school system, covering education from pre-school class (age 5–6) through the 9th or optional 10th grade. It educates the majority of Danish children, alongside a parallel sector of state-subsidised private schools.

History and structure

Compulsory education in Denmark dates to the school acts of 1814. The Folkeskole is governed by the Folkeskole Act and aims, in its statutory purpose clause, to give pupils knowledge and skills while preparing them for further education, fostering democratic spirit and all-round personal development. Teaching in most subjects follows a common curriculum with national goals; there are no examinations until the school-leaving examinations at the end of 9th and 10th grade.

Features

Distinctive features include class teachers who often follow the same class for several years, an emphasis on group work and oral participation, and (historically) no marks for pupils until the later grades. A major reform in 2014 lengthened the school day and introduced supported learning time and daily physical activity.
