Amazon River

Amazon River
Location: South America (Peru, Colombia, Brazil)
Length: ~6,400 km (disputed vs. Nile)
Discharge: ~209,000 m³/s — largest in the world
Basin: ~7,000,000 km²

The Amazon River (Spanish: Río Amazonas; Portuguese: Rio Amazonas) is the largest river in the world by discharge volume of water and the second-longest river system after the Nile. It flows eastward across northern South America from the Peruvian Andes to the Atlantic Ocean in Brazil, draining a basin of some 7 million km² covering much of the Amazon basin and its rainforest.

Course and tributaries

The most distant source is generally identified as the Apurímac River on Nevado Mismi in Peru. In Brazil, the river proper begins at the confluence of the Marañón and Ucayali near Nauta, and meets the Rio Negro at Manaus in the famous "Meeting of the Waters". Major tributaries include the Negro, Madeira, Tapajós, Xingu, Japurá and Putumayo. More than 1,100 tributaries together carry roughly one-fifth of all river water discharged into the world's oceans.

Ecology and people

The Amazon sustains the greatest diversity of freshwater life on Earth, including the Amazon river dolphin, piranhas, arapaima and thousands of other fish species. Millions of people live along its banks in cities such as Iquitos, Manaus and Belém; ocean-going ships can reach Manaus, 1,500 km inland. The river was named by Spanish explorer Francisco de Orellana, who descended it in 1541–42 and reported battles with women warriors, recalling the Amazons of Greek myth.
