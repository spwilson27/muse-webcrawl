Mergers and acquisitions (M&A) are business transactions in which the ownership of companies, business organisations, or their operating units are transferred to or consolidated with another company or business organisation. A merger combines two companies into a new or surviving entity, typically by mutual agreement; an acquisition (or takeover) occurs when one company purchases most or all of another company's shares or assets to assume control. M&A is a major activity of investment banks and a central tool of corporate strategy.

Motives

  • Synergies — cost savings and revenue gains from combining operations ("1 + 1 > 2").
  • Growth — faster expansion than organic growth; entry into new markets or product lines.
  • Market power — reduced competition and increased share (subject to antitrust limits).
  • Diversification — spreading risk across products or geographies.
  • Tax and financial motives — use of tax losses, cheaper financing, or undervalued targets.

Types

Horizontal mergers join competitors in the same industry; vertical mergers join firms at different stages of a supply chain; conglomerate mergers join unrelated businesses; congeneric mergers join related but non-competing firms. Acquisitions may be friendly (agreed with the target's board) or hostile (tender offers made directly to shareholders, often resisted with defences such as the poison pill).

Process and valuation

A typical deal proceeds through strategy and target screening, valuation (discounted cash flow, comparable companies, precedent transactions), negotiation of a purchase agreement, due diligence, financing, and regulatory clearance, before closing and integration. Research consistently finds that many acquisitions fail to create value for the acquirer's shareholders, owing to overpayment (the "winner's curse"), integration difficulties, and cultural clashes.

History

M&A activity comes in waves: the trust-building era around 1900, the conglomerate wave of the 1960s, the leveraged buyout boom of the 1980s, the strategic and stock-fuelled deals of the 1990s, and the private-equity and cross-border deals of the 2000s and 2010s. Landmark transactions include the AOL–Time Warner merger (2000), among the largest ever and later written down massively, and Exxon–Mobil (1999).
