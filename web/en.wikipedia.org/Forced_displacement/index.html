Forced displacement

Forced displacement (also called forced migration) is the involuntary or coerced movement of a person or group away from their home or home region, whether forced directly by violence or through indirect means such as environmental catastrophe or development projects. The United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) reported that the number of forcibly displaced people worldwide surpassed 100 million in 2022 and reached roughly 120 million by 2024, the highest level recorded.

Categories

Forcibly displaced populations include refugees, who cross international borders fleeing persecution or conflict; internally displaced persons (IDPs), who flee but remain within their own country and are protected in principle by the Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement; and asylum seekers awaiting determination of refugee status. Related concepts include population transfer, ethnic cleansing, and deportation. Forced displacement may be conflict-induced, disaster-induced (natural hazards, increasingly linked to climate change), or development-induced, as with large dams and urban clearance.

Legal framework

The 1951 Refugee Convention and its 1967 Protocol define refugee status and the principle of non-refoulement—that no one should be returned to a country where they face serious threats. Forced displacement is prohibited in many circumstances by international humanitarian law; forcible transfer of a civilian population can constitute a war crime or a crime against humanity under the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court.
