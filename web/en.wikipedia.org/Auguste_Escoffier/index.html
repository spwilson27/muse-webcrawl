Auguste Escoffier

Georges Auguste Escoffier (28 October 1846 – 12 February 1935) was a French chef, restaurateur and culinary writer who popularised and updated traditional French cooking methods. He is often called the "king of chefs and chef of kings". His codification of the recipes, techniques and kitchen organisation of classical French cuisine remains a foundation of professional cookery.

Escoffier modernised and codified classical French cuisine.

Career

Escoffier began his apprenticeship at the age of 13 in Nice, served as a chef in the Franco-Prussian War, and rose to direct the kitchens of César Ritz's hotels, including the Savoy in London (from 1890) and then the Ritz Paris and the Carlton in London. With Ritz he defined the luxury hotel restaurant for the Belle Époque, serving royalty, aristocracy and celebrities of the age.

Contributions

His book Le Guide Culinaire (1903) recorded some five thousand recipes and remains a standard reference. Escoffier reorganised the professional kitchen into the brigade de cuisine, a hierarchy of specialised stations that replaced the chaotic kitchens of earlier eras, and he simplified the elaborate dishes of Carême, refining the five mother sauces codified in French cuisine. He created famous dishes such as Peach Melba, named for the Australian soprano Nellie Melba, and Melba toast. He also campaigned to improve chefs' working conditions and professional discipline.
