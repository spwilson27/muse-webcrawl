John le Carré

David John Moore Cornwell (19 October 1931 – 12 December 2020), better known by his pen name John le Carré, was a British-Irish author, best known for his espionage novels, many featuring intelligence officer George Smiley. Having worked for the Security Service (MI5) and the Secret Intelligence Service (MI6) during the 1950s and 1960s, he became a full-time author after the success of his third novel, The Spy Who Came in from the Cold (1963).

Early life and intelligence career

Cornwell was born in Poole, Dorset. His father was a confidence trickster whose frauds and imprisonments haunted the author's life. He studied at the University of Bern and Lincoln College, Oxford, taught at Eton, and then served in MI5 and MI6; his cover in Hamburg was blown, he believed, by double agent Kim Philby, ending his intelligence career.

Novels

Le Carré's Smiley novels — Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy (1974), The Honourable Schoolboy (1977), and Smiley's People (1979) — depict a morally ambiguous Cold War espionage world contrasting with glamorous portrayals of spies. After the Cold War he turned to new subjects in works such as The Night Manager (1993), The Constant Gardener (2001), and A Most Wanted Man (2008). His novels have been widely adapted for film and television.
