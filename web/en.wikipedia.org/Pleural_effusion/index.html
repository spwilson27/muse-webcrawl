Pleural effusion

Pleural effusion
SpecialtyPulmonology
SymptomsShortness of breath, chest pain, cough
CausesHeart failure, pneumonia, cancer, pulmonary embolism

A pleural effusion is accumulation of excessive fluid in the pleural space, the potential space that surrounds each lung. Under normal conditions, pleural fluid is secreted by the parietal pleural capillaries and reabsorbed by the lymphatics in the visceral pleura. An effusion develops when production exceeds absorption or drainage is impaired.

Types

Pleural effusions are classically divided into transudates, caused by systemic factors such as heart failure and low serum protein, and exudates, caused by local processes such as infection, malignancy and inflammation. Light's criteria, comparing protein and lactate dehydrogenase levels in pleural fluid and serum, are used to distinguish the two.

Signs, diagnosis and treatment

Symptoms include breathlessness, pleuritic chest pain and cough. Examination may reveal reduced breath sounds and dullness to percussion. Diagnosis is by chest radiography, ultrasound or CT, and analysis of fluid obtained by thoracentesis. Treatment addresses the underlying cause; large or symptomatic effusions may be drained by thoracentesis, chest tube, pleurodesis or surgery.
