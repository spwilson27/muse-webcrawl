Serial killer

A serial killer (also serial murderer) is a person who murders three or more people, usually in service of abnormal psychological gratification, with the killings taking place over more than a month and including a significant interval (a "cooling-off period") between them. Some authorities set the threshold at two murders committed separately. Serial murder is distinguished from mass murder (many victims in a single event) and spree killing (multiple victims in a short burst at multiple locations).

Definition and history

The term "serial killer" is generally attributed to FBI investigator Robert Ressler in the 1970s, though German criminologist Ernst Gennat used Serienmörder earlier. Historical examples predate the term, including Jack the Ripper (1888) and H. H. Holmes in the 1890s. The FBI's Behavioral Science Unit at Quantico developed offender profiling partly in response to a perceived rise in serial murder in the 1970s and 1980s.

Typologies and motives

Investigators have classified serial killers by motive — including visionary, mission-oriented, hedonistic (lust, thrill, and comfort/profit), and power-or-control types — and as "organised" or "disorganised" offenders. Such typologies are widely used in popular culture, though researchers note their limited empirical precision. Motives frequently involve fantasy, compulsions, and a desire for dominance rather than material gain.

Prevalence

Serial murder is rare relative to homicide overall; estimates suggest serial killers account for well under one percent of murders in the United States in a typical year. Cases have been documented worldwide, and databases such as the Radford/FGCU Serial Killer Database track confirmed victims and offenders.

In popular culture

Serial killers are a staple of crime fiction, film, and true-crime media, from The Silence of the Lambs to numerous documentary series — a prominence that has drawn criticism for glamourising offenders.
