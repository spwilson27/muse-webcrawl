Eldfell

Eldfell
Elevation: 205 m
Location: Heimaey, Vestmannaeyjar, Iceland
Type: Volcanic cone
Last eruption: 1973

Eldfell ("Fire Mountain") is a volcanic cone on the island of Heimaey in the Vestmannaeyjar archipelago off the south coast of Iceland. It was formed during a famous eruption that began without warning in the early hours of 23 January 1973 and lasted until July of that year.

Eldfell looms over the harbour town it nearly destroyed in 1973.

The 1973 eruption

A fissure opened just east of the town of Vestmannaeyjabær, Iceland's most important fishing port. Within hours the entire population of about 5,300 was evacuated to the mainland, largely by the fishing fleet that happened to be in harbour because of a storm. Over the following months, lava and ash buried some 400 homes and added about 2.2 square kilometres of new land to the island.

Fighting the lava

In an unprecedented operation, workers pumped millions of tonnes of seawater onto the advancing lava front to cool and solidify it, slowing its progress toward the harbour. The effort is credited with saving the port; the cooled lava even improved the harbour by narrowing and sheltering its entrance.

Aftermath

Most residents returned after the eruption ended on 3 July 1973. The new cone, 205 metres high, remains warm in places, and its heat has been used for district heating. The Eldheimar museum, opened in 2014 around an excavated buried house, commemorates the eruption, sometimes called the "Pompeii of the North."
