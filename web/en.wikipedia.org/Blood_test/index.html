Blood test

A blood test is a laboratory analysis performed on a sample of blood, usually drawn from a vein in the arm by venipuncture or by a fingerstick. Blood tests are among the most common diagnostic procedures in medicine and are used to assess general health, detect infections and genetic conditions, measure organ function, and monitor the effects of treatment.

Common tests

The complete blood count (CBC) measures red blood cells, white blood cells, haemoglobin, haematocrit and platelets, helping to diagnose anaemia, infection and many other disorders. A blood chemistry panel (metabolic panel) measures electrolytes, glucose, kidney markers such as urea and creatinine, and liver enzymes. Other common investigations include lipid profiles for cholesterol, thyroid function tests, coagulation studies, blood cultures for infection, and specific markers such as troponin for heart attack or HbA1c for diabetes control.

Procedure and analysis

Blood is collected into tubes, some containing anticoagulants or other additives, labelled and sent to a medical laboratory, where automated analysers process most samples; serum or plasma is separated by centrifuge for chemistry tests. Results are compared against reference ranges, which vary with age, sex and the laboratory's methods.

Other uses

Blood testing is also used in blood typing for transfusion, screening of donated blood for transmissible infections, forensic analysis, doping control in sport, and prenatal screening. Legal blood alcohol measurement is a further specialised application.
