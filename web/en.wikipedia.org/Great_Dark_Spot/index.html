The Great Dark Spot (also known as GDS-89, for "Great Dark Spot – 1989") was one of a series of dark spots on Neptune similar in appearance to Jupiter's Great Red Spot. In 1989, GDS-89 was the first Great Dark Spot on Neptune to be observed by NASA's Voyager 2 space probe. Like Jupiter's spot, Great Dark Spots are anticyclonic storms — high-pressure regions — but Neptune's spots have relatively cloud-free interiors and last only a few years.

Characteristics

The original spot was roughly the size of the Earth (about 13,000 by 6,600 km), encircled by bright, high-altitude cirrus clouds of methane ice. Winds around it were measured at up to 2,400 km/h, the fastest in the Solar System. A smaller storm, Dark Spot 2, and a bright feature nicknamed "Scooter" accompanied it.

Disappearance and later spots

When the Hubble Space Telescope photographed Neptune in November 1994, the Great Dark Spot had vanished, while a similar new spot had appeared in the northern hemisphere. Hubble has since tracked several dark spots forming, drifting equatorward and dissipating, and in 2019 observed one large storm appear to reverse course away from the equator.
