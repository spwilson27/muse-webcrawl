Cryogenic fuel

Cryogenic fuels are fuels that require storage at extremely low temperatures to remain liquid, because their boiling points at normal pressure lie far below room temperature. In rocketry the term usually denotes cryogenic propellants such as liquid hydrogen, liquid oxygen and liquid methane.

Use in rockets

Liquid hydrogen with liquid oxygen offers the highest specific impulse of any practical chemical combination and powered the Space Shuttle main engines and the upper stages of many launch vehicles; Saturn V's upper stages and Ariane and H-II/H3 vehicles also use it. Liquid methane, used by newer engines such as SpaceX's Raptor and Blue Origin's BE-4, is denser, easier to handle and can in principle be produced on Mars from local water and carbon dioxide.

Challenges

Cryogenic propellants boil off during storage, embrittle or leak through materials (hydrogen especially), and require insulated tanks and careful ground handling, complicating long-duration missions.
