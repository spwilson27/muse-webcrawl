Isotopes of fluorine

Fluorine has one stable isotope, fluorine-19.

Fluorine (atomic number 9) has only one stable isotope, 19F, which accounts for essentially 100% of natural fluorine; fluorine is therefore a monoisotopic element. Eighteen radioisotopes have been characterised, with mass numbers from 13 to 31.

Fluorine-18

The most important radioisotope is fluorine-18, with a half-life of about 109.8 minutes. It decays by positron emission and is widely used as a radiotracer in positron emission tomography (PET), most notably incorporated into fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) for imaging glucose metabolism in oncology, cardiology and neurology. Because of its short half-life, 18F is produced in cyclotrons near the point of use.
