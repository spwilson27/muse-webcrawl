Second fundamental form

The second fundamental form is a quadratic form on the tangent plane of a smooth surface in three-dimensional Euclidean space, usually denoted II. Together with the first fundamental form, which defines the intrinsic metric of the surface, it completely describes the local extrinsic geometry of the surface up to rigid motion, and is a central object in differential geometry of surfaces.

The second fundamental form measures how a surface bends away from its tangent plane.

Definition

For a parametrized surface x(u, v) with unit normal n, the second fundamental form is

II = L du² + 2M du dv + N dv², where L = xuu·n, M = xuv·n, N = xvv·n.

Equivalently, II(v, w) = −⟨dn(v), w⟩, where dn is the differential of the Gauss map; the associated linear operator is the shape (Weingarten) operator.

Curvature

The normal curvature of the surface in a tangent direction is the ratio II(v, v)/I(v, v). Its maximum and minimum values are the principal curvatures κ1 and κ2, from which the Gaussian curvature K = κ1κ2 and mean curvature H = (κ1 + κ2)/2 are computed. Gauss's Theorema Egregium shows that K, though defined through both fundamental forms, depends only on the first.
