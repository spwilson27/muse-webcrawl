El Clásico (Spanish: El Clásico; Catalan: El Clàssic) is the name given to any football match between rival clubs FC Barcelona and Real Madrid CF. Originally referring only to the competitions held in the Spanish championship, the term now includes every match between the teams, including those in the UEFA Champions League, Copa del Rey and Supercopa de España.

The rivalry comes about as Madrid and Barcelona are the two largest cities in Spain, and the two clubs are among the richest and most successful football clubs in the world. The fixture is regarded as one of the biggest club games in world football, watched by hundreds of millions of viewers worldwide.

History

The clubs first met in the semi-final of the Copa de la Coronación on 13 May 1902, a match Barcelona won 3–1. The rivalry intensified during the twentieth century as it became entangled with wider cultural and political tensions between Catalonia and Castile: under the dictatorship of Francisco Franco, Barcelona came to be seen as a symbol of Catalan identity and resistance, famously described as més que un club ("more than a club"), while Real Madrid was associated with the centralist state.

The 1950s dispute over the transfer of Alfredo Di Stéfano, who ultimately joined Madrid and led them to five consecutive European Cups, remains one of the most contested episodes in the rivalry's history.

Modern era

The fixture reached a global peak during the years when Lionel Messi played for Barcelona and Cristiano Ronaldo for Madrid (2009–2018), when the two were widely regarded as the best players in the world. Their meetings drew record television audiences, and the pair pushed each other to unprecedented scoring feats.

Records

Messi is the all-time top scorer in El Clásico with 26 goals, and also holds the record for most appearances in the fixture alongside Sergio Ramos. The largest margin of victory includes Barcelona's 5–0 wins in 1974, 1994 and 2010, and Madrid's 5–0 win in 1995.
