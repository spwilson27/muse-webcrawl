Gaochang

Gaochang (also Karakhoja, Qocho or Qara-hoja) was an ancient oasis city on the northern rim of the Taklamakan Desert in present-day Xinjiang, China, about 30 km south-east of Turpan. Founded in the 1st century BC as a Han dynasty garrison, it became a flourishing Silk Road trading centre and, under the Uyghurs, the capital of the Qocho kingdom.

History

The site began as the Jushi kingdom's lands, then a Chinese military colony under the Han. After periods of local rule — including the Qu family kingdom of Gaochang (502–640) — the Tang dynasty conquered it in 640 and made it a prefecture. Following the An Lushan rebellion, control passed to Tibetans and then to Uyghurs fleeing the collapse of their khaganate in Mongolia, who established the Buddhist-Manichaean kingdom of Qocho.

The city survived under Mongol rule but was destroyed by fire and warfare in the late 13th–14th centuries, after which it was abandoned. Its dried ruins preserve rammed-earth walls, temples and stupas across roughly two square kilometres.

Religion and culture

Gaochang was a centre of Buddhism, Manichaeism and later Nestorian Christianity. German expeditions under Albert von Le Coq in the early 1900s removed murals and manuscripts in Old Uyghur, Chinese, Sogdian and Tocharian, many now in Berlin — removals that remain controversial.
