Teddy bear

A teddy bear is a stuffed toy in the form of a bear. Developed apparently simultaneously by toymakers Morris Michtom in the United States and Richard Steiff under Margarete Steiff's company in Germany in the early 20th century, the teddy bear, named after President Theodore "Teddy" Roosevelt, has become a popular children's toy, celebrated in story, song, and film.

Origin of the name

In November 1902, Roosevelt attended a bear hunt in Mississippi where, after a black bear was cornered and tied to a tree, he refused to shoot it as unsportsmanlike. Political cartoonist Clifford Berryman depicted the incident in The Washington Post, and Brooklyn shopkeeper Morris Michtom placed a small stuffed bear in his window labeled "Teddy's bear." At roughly the same time, the Steiff firm in Germany produced a jointed plush bear, the "Bear 55 PB," designed by Richard Steiff.

Design and manufacture

Traditional teddy bears are made of mohair plush with movable limbs and glass or button eyes; modern bears commonly use synthetic fabrics and safety eyes. Manufacturers such as Steiff, Gund, and the Vermont Teddy Bear Company became famous for their bears.

In culture

Famous fictional bears include Winnie-the-Pooh, Paddington Bear, and Rupert Bear. Songs such as "The Teddy Bears' Picnic" (melody 1907) helped cement the toy in popular culture. Antique teddy bears are valuable collectibles; arctophily is the hobby of collecting them.
