Inukai Tsuyoshi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Inukai Tsuyoshi (犬養 毅)
Born: 4 June 1855, Okayama, Japan
Died: 15 May 1932 (aged 76), Tokyo, Japan
Prime Minister of Japan: 1931–1932
Party: Rikken Seiyūkai

Inukai Tsuyoshi (4 June 1855 – 15 May 1932) was a Japanese politician and the prime minister of Japan from 1931 until his assassination in 1932. A journalist turned parliamentarian of the Taishō democracy era, he led the Rikken Seiyūkai party and attempted to restrain the military and restore Japan's finances during the Great Depression. His murder by naval officers in the May 15 Incident ended party government in pre-war Japan.

Early life and career

Born in Okayama to a samurai family, Inukai studied at Keio University and became a journalist before entering politics. He was first elected to the Imperial Diet in 1890, in Japan's first general election, and was re-elected a record number of times, serving more than forty years in the lower house. He held cabinet posts including minister of education and minister of communications, opposed the growing influence of the military and the Satsuma–Chōshū clan cliques, and protected exiled Chinese reformers and revolutionaries such as Sun Yat-sen.

Premiership

Inukai became prime minister in December 1931 after the fall of the Wakatsuki cabinet amid the Manchurian Incident, in which the Kwantung Army had invaded Manchuria without authorisation. His government, with Takahashi Korekiyo as finance minister, took Japan off the gold standard and began reflationary policies that helped ease the depression. Inukai sought to negotiate with China and to discipline the army, refusing to formally recognise the puppet state of Manchukuo, which deepened friction with the military.

Assassination

On 15 May 1932, a group of young Imperial Japanese Navy officers and army cadets stormed the prime minister's residence and shot Inukai, who reportedly asked to talk with his attackers ("If we talk, we'll understand"). The assassins received light sentences after public petitions for leniency. No party politician succeeded him; the following cabinets were dominated by the military and bureaucrats, accelerating Japan's path toward war.
