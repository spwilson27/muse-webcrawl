Rarotonga

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rarotonga is the largest and most populous island of the Cook Islands, a self-governing state in free association with New Zealand in the South Pacific Ocean. The island is home to about 10,000–13,000 people — the majority of the Cook Islands' resident population — and contains the capital, Avarua, on its northern coast, along with the parliament, the international airport and the main port. Tourism, offshore finance, marine pearls and agriculture are the main economic activities.

Geography

Rarotonga is a volcanic high island about 11 km by 7 km, ringed by a fringing reef enclosing a shallow lagoon. Its rugged interior rises to Te Manga at 652 metres, and includes the distinctive pinnacle of Te Rua Manga ("the Needle"). A single main road of about 32 km encircles the island following the coast; an older inland road, the Ara Metua, was built in the 11th century or earlier and is paved with stone slabs in places. The climate is tropical, with a cyclone season in the southern summer.

History

Polynesian voyagers settled Rarotonga by about 1000 CE; oral histories link it to migrations from French Polynesia and to departures for New Zealand (Aotearoa) — tradition holds that great voyaging canoes such as Takitumu left from Rarotonga. The first recorded European sighting was by the Spanish in 1606, and Captain James Cook visited the group in 1773 and 1777 without landing on Rarotonga itself. London Missionary Society missionaries arrived in 1821 and Christianity spread rapidly. The Cook Islands became a British protectorate in 1888, were annexed to New Zealand in 1901, and achieved self-government in 1965, with Rarotonga as the political centre.

Culture and tourism

Rarotonga is the main gateway for visitors to the Cook Islands, with beaches and lagoon resorts concentrated around Muri on the southeast coast. Cultural life centres on Cook Islands Māori language and dance, church music, and events such as the Te Maeva Nui constitutional celebrations. The island is also the starting point for trips to Aitutaki and the outer islands.
