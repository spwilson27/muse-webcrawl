Amenhotep II

Amenhotep II (sometimes read as Amenophis II) was the seventh pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty of ancient Egypt, reigning from about 1427 to 1401 BCE (chronologies vary). He was the son and successor of Thutmose III, with whom he may have shared a brief co-regency.

Reign

Like his father, Amenhotep II campaigned in Syria-Palestine to preserve Egypt's Asiatic empire: his annals record major campaigns in regnal years 7 and 9 against city-states in Retjenu and the Orontes valley, claiming enormous booty and thousands of prisoners, after which Mitanni, Babylon and the Hittites sent gifts recognising the balance of power. He cultivated an image of extraordinary physical prowess — stelae celebrate his feats of rowing, running, horsemanship and archery, including shooting arrows through copper targets. He also undertook building work at Karnak and throughout Egypt and Nubia.

Tomb and mummy

Amenhotep II was buried in tomb KV35 in the Valley of the Kings. In 1898 Victor Loret found his mummy still resting in its sarcophagus — unusual among New Kingdom royal tombs — and the tomb had served as a cache where priests had reburied other royal mummies, including Thutmose IV and Amenhotep III, to protect them from robbers. Some popular writers have proposed Amenhotep II as a candidate for the pharaoh of the Exodus, though this is not a mainstream scholarly position.
