United States Department of Health and Human Services

The United States Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) is a cabinet-level executive branch department of the U.S. federal government created to protect the health of Americans and provide essential human services. Its motto is "Improving the health, safety, and well-being of America." HHS is the largest grant-making agency in the federal government and, through Medicare and Medicaid, accounts for roughly a quarter of all federal outlays — a larger budget than any other department.

History

HHS originated as the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare (HEW), created by President Dwight D. Eisenhower in 1953. Oveta Culp Hobby was its first secretary. HEW oversaw the creation of Medicare and Medicaid in 1965. In 1980, the Department of Education was split off and HEW was renamed Health and Human Services. In 1995 the Social Security Administration became an independent agency.

Operating divisions

  • Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) — health coverage for well over 100 million people.
  • Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) — public health and disease control.
  • Food and Drug Administration (FDA) — safety of food, drugs, devices, and cosmetics.
  • National Institutes of Health (NIH) — the world's largest public funder of biomedical research.
  • Administration for Children and Families — Head Start, child welfare, and family assistance.
  • Indian Health Service, SAMHSA, HRSA, and the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality.

Role

Beyond insurance programs, HHS leads responses to public health emergencies (through the Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response and the CDC), funds biomedical research, regulates pharmaceuticals, and administers social services. Its decisions — from drug approvals to vaccine recommendations — have global impact.
