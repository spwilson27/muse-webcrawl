Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk of loss arising from a borrower's failure to repay a loan or meet contractual obligations. It is a central concern of banks and other lenders and a key input into the pricing of loans and bonds.

Measurement

Credit risk is commonly decomposed into the probability of default, the loss given default, and the exposure at default. Credit ratings assigned by agencies such as Moody's, S&P, and Fitch summarize assessments of borrower creditworthiness, and credit scores perform a similar function for individuals.

Management

Lenders manage credit risk through underwriting standards, diversification, collateral, covenants, and provisioning. Financial instruments such as credit default swaps allow risk to be transferred. Regulatory frameworks such as the Basel Accords require banks to hold capital against credit risk.
