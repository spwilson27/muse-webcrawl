Copley Square is a public square in the Back Bay neighborhood of Boston, Massachusetts, United States. Named for the colonial-era portrait painter John Singleton Copley, it is one of the most architecturally distinguished squares in the United States and a focal point of civic life in Boston.

History

The square stands on land created by the filling of Back Bay in the nineteenth century. Originally laid out in 1883 as Art Square, it was renamed for Copley in 1885. The square is bounded by Boylston Street, Dartmouth Street, St. James Avenue, and Huntington Avenue, and its surroundings were shaped by Boston's emergence as a centre of American culture and education.

Architecture

Several landmark buildings face the square. Trinity Church (1872–1877), designed by H. H. Richardson in the Romanesque Revival style that bears his name, is widely considered his masterpiece. Across Dartmouth Street stands the Boston Public Library's McKim Building (1895), designed by Charles Follen McKim in a Renaissance Revival manner and famously described as a "palace for the people." The Gothic Revival Old South Church (1873–1875) occupies the northern corner. The Copley Plaza Hotel opened on the southern side in 1912, and the John Hancock Tower, a 60-storey glass skyscraper completed in 1976, rises just southeast of the square.

Events

Copley Square hosts farmers' markets, concerts, and public gatherings. The finish line of the Boston Marathon has long been located on Boylston Street adjacent to the square, near the Boston Public Library. The square was also near the site of the second explosion of the 2013 Boston Marathon bombing.
