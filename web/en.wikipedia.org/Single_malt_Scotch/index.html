Single malt Scotch

TypeMalt whisky
OriginScotland
IngredientsWater and malted barley only
DistillationBatch, in pot stills at a single distillery
AgeingMinimum 3 years in oak casks in Scotland

Single malt Scotch is a type of single malt whisky produced in Scotland. By law, it must be made at a single distillery from a mash of 100 percent malted barley, distilled in pot stills, matured in oak casks in Scotland for at least three years, and bottled at a minimum of 40 percent alcohol by volume. The whisky in a bottle may be a marriage of many casks of different ages; any age statement refers to the youngest whisky included.

Production

Barley is malted — steeped, germinated and dried, sometimes over peat fires, which impart smoky phenolic flavours — then milled and mashed with hot water to extract sugars. The wort is fermented with yeast to a beer-like wash, which is distilled twice (occasionally three times) in copper pot stills. The spirit is then matured in oak casks, frequently ex-bourbon or ex-sherry wood, and must lose its raw character during at least three years of ageing; a small portion evaporates annually as the "angels' share".

Regions

Scotch whisky regions traditionally include the Highlands, Lowlands, Speyside, Islay and Campbeltown. Islay malts are famed for their peat smoke and maritime character, while many Speyside malts are noted for fruitiness and sherry cask influence. Famous single malt distilleries include The Macallan, Glenfiddich, Glenlivet, Laphroaig, Lagavulin, Ardbeg and Highland Park.
