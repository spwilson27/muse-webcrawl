Cottage cheese

Cottage cheese
TypeFresh cheese
Main ingredientsMilk, acid or rennet
TextureSoft curds in whey
Fat contentTypically 0–5%

Cottage cheese is a fresh cheese product with a mild flavour and a creamy, non-homogeneous, soupy texture. It is made by draining the cheese, as opposed to pressing it to separate the whey, whereby some whey is retained in the curds. The drained curd is typically washed to remove acidity, giving a sweeter taste.

Production

Cottage cheese is made by acidifying milk — either with bacterial cultures that convert lactose to lactic acid, or by adding an acid such as vinegar — until curds form. The curd is cut, gently cooked, drained, and often mixed with a cream dressing. Unlike many cheeses, it is not aged or coloured.

Culinary use

Cottage cheese is eaten on its own, with fruit, in salads, and in cooked dishes such as lasagne (as a substitute for ricotta) and cheesecakes. It is popular with dieters and bodybuilders because it is high in protein — especially slow-digesting casein — and relatively low in fat and calories.
