Unilateral contract

A reward advertisement is the classic example of an offer for a unilateral contract.

A unilateral contract is a contract in which only one party makes an express promise, and acceptance occurs not by a return promise but by performance of a specified act. The classic example is a reward offer: "I will pay $100 to whoever returns my lost dog." No one is obliged to search for the dog, but the offeror becomes bound once someone performs the act.

Distinguishing features

In a bilateral contract, by contrast, both parties exchange promises and each is bound as soon as the promises are made. Unilateral contracts commonly arise in reward offers, prize competitions, and certain insurance and option arrangements.

Revocation and performance

At common law, an offer for a unilateral contract historically could be revoked at any time before complete performance, even after the offeree had begun. Modern courts and the Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 45 soften this rule: once the offeree begins performance, the offer becomes irrevocable for a reasonable time, creating an option contract.

Famous cases

The leading English case is Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co [1893], in which a company's advertisement promising £100 to anyone who used its smoke ball and still caught influenza was held to be a unilateral offer accepted by performance.
