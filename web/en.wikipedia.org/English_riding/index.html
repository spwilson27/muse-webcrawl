English riding

English riding is a form of horse riding seen throughout the world. It involves a flat English saddle without the deep seat, high cantle, or saddle horn of a Western saddle, and a bridle typically with a cavesson noseband; the rider usually holds both reins in both hands and communicates through a combination of seat, leg, and rein aids. English riding encompasses the Olympic disciplines of dressage, eventing, and show jumping, as well as hunter seat riding, saddle seat, and hacking for pleasure.

Equipment

The English saddle places the rider close to the horse, with flaps shaped for the discipline — forward flaps for jumping, long straight flaps for dressage. Stirrup leathers are thin and adjustable, stirrup irons are metal, and bits commonly include snaffles and, in advanced dressage, the double bridle. Riders wear breeches or jodhpurs, tall boots or paddock boots with half chaps, and a hard helmet; formal disciplines prescribe jackets, stocks, and gloves.

Position and gaits

The classical position aligns ear, shoulder, hip, and heel. Riders post (rise) to the trot in many disciplines, ride two-point (jumping) position over fences, and perform collected and extended gaits in dressage. Terminology differs from Western riding in places: the canter's fast counterpart is the gallop, and a "hand gallop" is a controlled faster canter.

Disciplines

  • Dressage — precise gymnastic training movements performed in an arena.
  • Show jumping — jumping a course of fences against the clock and faults.
  • Eventing — dressage, cross-country, and show jumping combined over one to three days.
  • Hunter/jumper — judged on style and manners (hunters) or speed and accuracy (jumpers).
  • Saddle seat — high-stepping gaited and trotting breeds shown on the flat.
