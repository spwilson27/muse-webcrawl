Lubumbashi (Swahili: Lubumbashi; formerly Élisabethville) is the second-largest city in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the capital of the province of Haut-Katanga in the country's south-east. A major mining, industrial and commercial centre near the Zambian border, it is the hub of the Congolese Copperbelt and one of Africa's most important centres for copper, cobalt, zinc and uranium production.

History

The city was founded in 1910 by Belgian colonial authorities as a mining town serving the Union Minière du Haut-Katanga, and named Élisabethville after Queen Elisabeth of Belgium. It grew around copper smelting and the railways built to carry ore to ports in Angola, Mozambique, South Africa and (via the Congo River) the Atlantic. The Kipushi and Ruashi mines and the Shinkolobwe mine — source of the uranium used by the Manhattan Project — anchored the regional economy.

At Congolese independence in 1960, Moise Tshombe declared the secession of mineral-rich Katanga, with Lubumbashi as its capital; United Nations forces retook the city in 1963 after fierce fighting. Under President Mobutu Sese Seko's authenticité campaign the city was renamed Lubumbashi in 1966, after the Lubumbashi River (from umbashi, a type of clay used locally). The collapse of the state mining company Gécamines in the 1990s devastated the formal economy; since the 2000s a cobalt and copper boom, driven substantially by Chinese investment, has revived mining.

Geography and climate

Lubumbashi lies on a plateau at about 1,200 m elevation in the savanna of the south-eastern Congo Basin rim. It has a subtropical highland-influenced climate with a long dry season from May to September and rains from November to March. The city is organised into communes including Lubumbashi, Kamalondo, Katuba, Kampemba, Ruashi and Kenya, and is served by Luano International Airport and road and rail links to Zambia, Kolwezi and Kindu.

Economy and culture

Mining dominates: copper, cobalt, zinc, cadmium, germanium and tin are extracted and processed in and around the city by Gécamines and numerous international firms. The University of Lubumbashi, founded in 1955, is one of the country's leading universities. The city is home to TP Mazembe, one of Africa's most successful football clubs, five-time winners of the CAF Champions League, whose Stade TP Mazembe is a city landmark. Kiswahili is the main lingua franca, alongside French, the official language.
