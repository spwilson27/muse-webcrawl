Gonad

Gonad
TypesTestis (male), ovary (female)
ProductsGametes (sperm, ova); sex hormones
SystemReproductive system, endocrine system

A gonad is a mixed gland and sex organ that produces the gametes (ova and sperm) and sex hormones of an organism. In females of most species the gonads are the ovaries, and in males they are the testes. Besides producing reproductive cells, gonads are endocrine glands: the testes secrete androgens such as testosterone, and the ovaries secrete oestrogens and progesterone, which control the development of secondary sexual characteristics and the reproductive cycle.

Development

Gonads develop from the genital ridges of the intermediate mesoderm in the early embryo. In mammals the initially indifferent gonad differentiates into testis or ovary under the influence of genetic signals, most famously the SRY gene on the Y chromosome, which triggers testis formation. The embryonic gonad also gives rise to the somatic cell lines that support gametogenesis — Sertoli and Leydig cells in the testis, granulosa and theca cells in the ovary.

Function

Gamete production in the testis (spermatogenesis) proceeds continuously from puberty, whereas the ovary contains a finite stock of oocytes formed before birth, released cyclically after puberty. Gonadal function is regulated by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis through the gonadotropins LH and FSH. Disorders of the gonads include hypogonadism, gonadal dysgenesis, polycystic ovary syndrome and gonadal tumours.

Other animals

Gonads occur throughout the animal kingdom in varied forms; many fish and invertebrates are hermaphroditic, bearing both ovarian and testicular tissue, sometimes in a single combined gland called an ovotestis.
