Dario Argento (born 7 September 1940) is an Italian film director, screenwriter and producer, known for his influential work in horror, particularly the Italian giallo genre, during the 1970s and 1980s. Nicknamed the "Master of the Thrill" and the "Master of Horror," he has been called the Italian Alfred Hitchcock for his stylish, suspense-driven set pieces and bold use of color and music.

Early career

Argento was born in Rome to producer Salvatore Argento and photographer Elda Luxardo. He began as a film critic, then a screenwriter, co-writing Sergio Leone's Once Upon a Time in the West (1968) with Bernardo Bertolucci. His directorial debut, The Bird with the Crystal Plumage (1970), was an international hit that codified the giallo thriller — black-gloved killers, witnesses haunted by a misremembered detail, elaborate murder scenes — followed by The Cat o' Nine Tails and Four Flies on Grey Velvet (1971).

Peak years

Deep Red (Profondo Rosso, 1975), with its Goblin score and intricate set pieces, is often judged his finest thriller. Suspiria (1977), shot in vivid Technicolor about a coven of witches at a German ballet school, became his most famous film; it began the "Three Mothers" trilogy, continued with Inferno (1980) and Mother of Tears (2007). Other key works include Tenebrae (1982), Phenomena (1985), and Opera (1987). He also produced George A. Romero's Dawn of the Dead (1978) for its European cut and Lamberto Bava's Demons (1985).

Style and legacy

Argento's films are noted for roaming subjective camerawork, gloved first-person killer viewpoints, saturated color design, prog-rock and electronic scores, and dream logic over realism. His influence extends to directors such as John Carpenter, Guillermo del Toro, and Nicolas Winding Refn; Suspiria was remade by Luca Guadagnino in 2018. Later films, including The Stendhal Syndrome (1996) with his daughter Asia Argento, Sleepless (2001), and Dark Glasses (2022), received more mixed reviews but sustained his cult stature.
