Langdon Cheves

Langdon Cheves (September 17, 1776 – June 26, 1857) was an American politician, lawyer and businessman from South Carolina. He served as a member of the United States House of Representatives from 1810 to 1815, and was Speaker of the House from 1814 to 1815. He later served as president of the Second Bank of the United States from 1819 to 1822.

Early life and career

Cheves was born at Rocky River in the Abbeville District of South Carolina, the son of Alexander Cheves and Mary Langdon. He studied law and was admitted to the bar in Charleston, where he built a successful practice. He served in the South Carolina House of Representatives and as the state's attorney general from 1808 to 1810.

Congress

Elected to Congress in 1810, Cheves became a leading member of the "War Hawks," the young Republicans who pressed for war with Great Britain. He chaired the Ways and Means Committee and the Naval Affairs Committee. In January 1814 he was elected Speaker of the House, succeeding Henry Clay. He presided over the House during the War of 1812 and retired from Congress in 1815.

Bank of the United States

In 1819 Cheves became president of the Second Bank of the United States, succeeding William Jones. Faced with the aftermath of the Panic of 1819, he pursued a contractionary policy, calling in loans and demanding specie payments from state banks, which restored the Bank's finances but intensified the economic distress in the West and South. He resigned in 1822 and was succeeded by Nicholas Biddle.

Later life

Cheves later served as a commissioner under the Treaty of Ghent claims process and remained a prominent figure in South Carolina public life, generally opposing nullification while defending Southern interests. He died in Columbia, South Carolina, in 1857.
