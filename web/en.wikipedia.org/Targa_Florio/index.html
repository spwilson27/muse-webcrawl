Targa Florio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Targa Florio was a public road endurance automobile race held in the mountains of Sicily near the Italian mainland's capital of Palermo. Founded in 1906 by wealthy Sicilian wine producer and enthusiast Vincenzo Florio, it was the oldest sports car racing event, part of the World Sportscar Championship between 1955 and 1973. The race was held on closed public roads over circuits of varying length, most famously the 72 km (45 mi) Piccolo circuito delle Madonie, which twisted through mountain villages and included some 2,000 corners per lap.

History

The first Targa Florio in 1906 covered three laps of a 148 km course and was won by Alessandro Cagno in an Itala. Over the decades the circuit shrank while its hazards remained legendary: competitors raced at speed through towns, past spectators, livestock and sheer drops. Winners included Tazio Nuvolari, Stirling Moss, and Porsche, whose cars won eleven times — the manufacturer later used the name "Targa" for its semi-convertible 911 model after victories in the race.

End of the race

Safety concerns mounted as speeds rose; after several fatal accidents, the event lost world championship status and was discontinued as a competitive race following the 1977 edition, when a crash killed two spectators. It survives as the Targa Florio Classica, a regularity rally for historic cars run over the old Madonie roads.
