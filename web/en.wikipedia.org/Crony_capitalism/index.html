Crony capitalism

Crony capitalism, sometimes also called simply cronyism, is a term describing an economic system in which businesses thrive not as a result of free enterprise, but rather as a return on money amassed through collusion between a business class and a political class. This is often achieved by manipulation of relationships with state power by business interests, as money, particularly in the form of political campaign contributions or bribes, is exchanged for permission to pursue lucrative contracts, grants and special tax breaks, as well as the circumvention of regulations intended to protect the public.

Concept and usage

The term gained wide currency during the 1997 Asian financial crisis, when it was used to describe the close ties between governments and large business conglomerates in countries such as Indonesia, South Korea and Malaysia, which were blamed for misallocating capital and magnifying the collapse. It has since been applied to many economies, including Russia's oligarch system, China's state-connected enterprises, and aspects of the United States economy such as defence contracting, agricultural subsidies and financial-sector bailouts.

Mechanisms

Typical mechanisms include rent-seeking, regulatory capture, government subsidies and tax preferences granted to favoured firms, no-bid contracts, tariffs protecting incumbents, and the “revolving door” between regulatory agencies and regulated industries. Economists distinguish cronyism from ordinary lobbying in that the benefits accrue to specific firms rather than to an industry generally, and often in exchange for personal or political gain.

Criticism and debate

Critics across the political spectrum condemn crony capitalism: free-market advocates argue it corrupts competition and innovation, while progressives argue it demonstrates the power of wealth in politics. Some commentators object that the term is a rhetorical device used to attribute the failures of government intervention to capitalism itself; others respond that public choice theory explains cronyism as the predictable result of discretionary state power over the economy.
