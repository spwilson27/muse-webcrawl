Robert Ballard

Robert Ballard aboard an exploration vessel

Robert Duane Ballard (born June 30, 1942) is an American retired Navy officer and professor of oceanography at the University of Rhode Island who is most noted for his work in underwater archaeology: sea mapping and shipwreck research. He is best known for discovering the wreck of the RMS Titanic in 1985.

Career

Ballard earned degrees in chemistry and geology from the University of California, Santa Barbara, and a PhD in marine geology and geophysics from the University of Rhode Island in 1974. While at Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution he helped develop the deep-diving submersible Alvin's capabilities and took part in expeditions that discovered hydrothermal vents on the Galápagos Rift in 1977.

Famous discoveries

In September 1985 a joint French-American expedition led by Ballard located the wreck of Titanic, roughly 600 km southeast of Newfoundland at a depth of about 3,800 metres. He subsequently found the wrecks of the German battleship Bismarck (1989), the aircraft carrier USS Yorktown (1998) and John F. Kennedy's patrol boat PT-109 (2002).

Later work

Ballard founded the JASON Project for science education and the Ocean Exploration Trust, which operates the exploration vessel EV Nautilus and streams deep-sea exploration live to the public.
