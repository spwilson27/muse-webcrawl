Ivory Coast

The Plateau district of Abidjan
Capital: Yamoussoukro (political); Abidjan (economic)
Official language: French
Population: ~29 million
Independence: 7 August 1960 (from France)

Ivory Coast, officially the Republic of Côte d'Ivoire, is a country in West Africa bordered by Liberia and Guinea to the west, Mali and Burkina Faso to the north, Ghana to the east, and the Gulf of Guinea to the south. The government has requested since 1985 that the French name Côte d'Ivoire be used untranslated in all languages.

History

Pre-colonial Ivory Coast was home to several states, including the Kong Empire and Baoulé and Akan kingdoms. France established protectorates in the 19th century and made the territory a colony in 1893 within French West Africa. Independence came in 1960 under Félix Houphouët-Boigny, who led the country until 1993 through a period of rapid, cocoa-led growth — the "Ivorian miracle" — followed by economic decline, his death, a 1999 coup, and civil wars in 2002–2007 and 2010–2011 arising from disputed elections and the politics of ivoirité. Alassane Ouattara has been president since 2011.

Economy and geography

Ivory Coast is the world's largest producer and exporter of cocoa beans, and a major exporter of coffee and palm oil; agriculture employs much of the workforce. The south is coastal plain and rainforest, giving way to savanna in the north. Abidjan, on the Ébrié Lagoon, is the economic capital and one of West Africa's largest cities; Yamoussoukro, Houphouët-Boigny's birthplace, is the political capital and site of the Basilica of Our Lady of Peace, among the world's largest churches.

Culture

The country is ethnically diverse, with over 60 ethnic groups including the Baoulé, Bété, Guro and Mandé peoples; French is the official language alongside many indigenous languages. Ivorian popular music styles such as zouglou and coupé-décalé are known across francophone Africa.
