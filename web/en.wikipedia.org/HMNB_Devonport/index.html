His Majesty's Naval Base, Devonport (HMNB Devonport) is one of three operating bases of the Royal Navy in the United Kingdom, together with HMNB Clyde and HMNB Portsmouth. Located in the Devonport district of Plymouth on the River Tamar (the Hamoaze), it is the largest naval base in Western Europe and the sole nuclear repair and refuelling facility for Royal Navy submarines.

History

The royal dockyard at Plymouth Dock was founded in 1691, designed by Edmund Dummer, and grew through the age of sail and steam into a great industrial complex; the town that grew around it was renamed Devonport in 1824. The base was heavily bombed during the Plymouth Blitz of 1941. In 1977 the naval base and dockyard were brought under unified management, and the dockyard was privatised in 1987, latterly under Babcock.

Present role

Devonport is home port to the Devonport Flotilla, including Type 23 frigates, amphibious assault ships and hydrographic vessels, and hosts Flag Officer Sea Training (FOST) for the Royal Navy and allied navies. Its nuclear-licensed docks carry out deep maintenance and refuelling of Trafalgar and Vanguard-class submarines, and the decommissioned-submarine disposal programme is centred on the site. The base and dockyard together employ on the order of 11,000 people and underpin a large share of Plymouth's economy.
