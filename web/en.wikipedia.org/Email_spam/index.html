Email spam

Email spam, also referred to as junk email or simply spam, is unsolicited messages sent in bulk by email. The name comes from a Monty Python sketch in which the name of the canned pork product Spam is ubiquitous and unavoidable.

Most email spam messages are commercial in nature, advertising products or services, though a significant share carries scams, phishing attempts, or malware. Spam is economically attractive to senders because email imposes costs largely on recipients and Internet service providers rather than on the sender, allowing advertisers to operate at a very low marginal cost per message.

History

The first major commercial spam was sent in 1994 by the lawyers Laurence Canter and Martha Siegel, who advertised green card lottery services on thousands of Usenet newsgroups. As the consumer Internet grew through the 1990s and 2000s, spam volumes exploded, at times accounting for the large majority of all email traffic worldwide.

Anti-spam measures

Defences against spam include content-based filtering (increasingly using machine learning), sender authentication protocols such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, blocklists of known spam sources, and greylisting. Legislation such as the United States' CAN-SPAM Act of 2003, Canada's CASL, and the European Union's ePrivacy rules set requirements for commercial email. Despite these measures, spam remains a persistent feature of the email ecosystem, with much of it sent through botnets of compromised computers.
