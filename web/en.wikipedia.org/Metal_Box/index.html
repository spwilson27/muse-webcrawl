Metal Box is the second studio album by English post-punk band Public Image Ltd (PiL), released on 23 November 1979 by Virgin Records. It is widely regarded as a landmark of post-punk and experimental rock.

Background and recording

PiL was formed by vocalist John Lydon after the break-up of the Sex Pistols, together with guitarist Keith Levene, bassist Jah Wobble and drummer Jim Walker (later replaced). Following their 1978 debut, the band recorded Metal Box in a loose, improvisational style influenced by dub reggae, disco, krautrock and free jazz. Wobble's deep, rumbling basslines and Levene's metallic, textured guitar defined the sound, while Lydon's vocals ranged from chanting to keening on tracks such as "Poptones" and "Careering".

Packaging and release

The original album was famously packaged as three 12-inch 45 rpm records in a round metal canister resembling a film can — an expensive design that also inspired the album's title. In the United States it was reissued in 1980 as a conventional double LP titled Second Edition, with a different track order. The single "Death Disco" (also issued as "Swan Lake") preceded the album and reached the UK top twenty.

Reception and legacy

Though divisive on release, Metal Box is now consistently ranked among the greatest albums of all time, appearing on lists by Rolling Stone, NME and Pitchfork. Its fusion of dub space, abrasive guitar and rhythm-driven structures influenced generations of alternative and electronic musicians. The track "Albatross" opens the album with a ten-minute groove often cited as a high point of the post-punk era.

Track listing (original)

"Albatross", "Memories", "Swan Lake", "Poptones", "Careering", "No Birds", "Graveyard", "The Suit", "Bad Baby", "Socialist", "Chant" and "Radio 4".
