Manuel L. Quezon

Born19 August 1878, Baler, Philippines
Died1 August 1944, Saranac Lake, New York, USA
OfficePresident of the Philippines (1935–1944)

Manuel Luis Quezon y Molina (19 August 1878 – 1 August 1944) was a Filipino statesman and soldier who served as the first president of the Commonwealth of the Philippines from 1935 until his death in 1944. He is widely regarded as the second president of the Philippines and is known as the "Father of the National Language".

Early career

Born in Baler, Quezon fought in the Philippine Revolution and the Philippine–American War before entering politics. He became Resident Commissioner to the United States House of Representatives and later president of the Philippine Senate, dominating Philippine politics during the American colonial period.

Presidency

As Commonwealth president he promoted Filipino (based largely on Tagalog) as the national language, pursued social justice programmes, and prepared the country for independence. After the Japanese invasion of 1941–42 he headed a government-in-exile in the United States, where he died of tuberculosis in 1944. Quezon City, once the capital, and the province of Quezon are named after him.
