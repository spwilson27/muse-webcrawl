Baibars (Arabic: بيبرس; c. 1223 – 1 July 1277), full regnal name al-Malik al-Zahir Rukn al-Din Baibars, was the fourth sultan of the Mamluk Sultanate of Egypt. A Kipchak Turk by birth who rose from slavery to become one of the empire's greatest commanders, Baibars is celebrated in Egypt and Syria as the hero who halted the Mongol advance at the Battle of Ain Jalut (1260) and then systematically dismantled the Crusader states in the Levant.

Early life and rise

Born on the steppes north of the Black Sea, Baibars was captured and sold into slavery, eventually reaching Egypt, where he was bought by the Ayyubid sultan al-Salih Ayyub and trained in the Bahri regiment of mamluks stationed on the Nile. He distinguished himself at the Battle of Al Mansurah (1250), where the Mamluks defeated the Seventh Crusade of Louis IX of France, who was captured.

Ain Jalut and accession

In 1260, after the Mongols under Hulagu destroyed Baghdad and conquered Syria, Baibars served as a leading commander under Sultan Qutuz at the Battle of Ain Jalut in Palestine, where the Mamluks won the first major defeat inflicted on the Mongol Empire. On the return march to Egypt, Baibars assassinated Qutuz and seized the throne.

Reign

As sultan, Baibars created a standing professional army, built fortresses, roads and a swift postal relay system (the barid), and re-established the Abbasid caliphate in Cairo (1261) to legitimise Mamluk rule. He campaigned almost annually against the Crusader states, capturing Caesarea, Arsuf, Safed, Jaffa, Antioch (1268) and the great Krak des Chevaliers (1271), reducing Frankish territory to a strip around Acre. He also defeated the Mongol Ilkhanate at Elbistan in Anatolia in 1277, shortly before his death in Damascus on 1 July 1277.

Legacy

Baibars's exploits became the subject of the popular Arabic epic Sirat al-Zahir Baibars, recited by storytellers for centuries. His reign consolidated the Mamluk Sultanate into the dominant power of the eastern Mediterranean.
