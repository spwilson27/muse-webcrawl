Donald Norman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Donald Arthur Norman (born December 25, 1935) is an American researcher, professor, and author. Norman is the director of The Design Lab at the University of California, San Diego, and is best known for his books on design, especially The Design of Everyday Things, a foundational text of human-centred design.

Career

Norman earned degrees in electrical engineering and a PhD in psychology from the University of Pennsylvania. He was a founding member of the cognitive science department at UC San Diego, and later worked in industry as a vice president at Apple (where he popularised the title "user experience architect"), and as an executive at Hewlett-Packard and Encyclopaedia Britannica. With Jakob Nielsen he co-founded the Nielsen Norman Group, a leading usability consultancy.

Ideas

Norman introduced or popularised key design concepts: affordances and signifiers, mapping, feedback, constraints, the gulfs of execution and evaluation, and the idea that when users fail with a product, the design — not the user — is at fault. "Norman doors" — doors whose handles or flat plates confuse push and pull — became a byword for poor design. His later books include Emotional Design (2004), Living with Complexity (2010) and Design for a Better World (2023).
