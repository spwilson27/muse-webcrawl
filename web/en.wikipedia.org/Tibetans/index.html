Tibetans

Tibetans are an ethnic group native to Tibet, comprising some 6–7 million people, with large communities in the Tibetan Plateau, the Indian diaspora, and Nepal and Bhutan.

Culture

Tibetan Buddhism shapes art, festivals, medicine and literature. The Dalai Lama, now in exile in Dharamshala, India since the 1959 uprising, is the group's most prominent figure.

Political situation

China incorporated Tibet in 1951. Human rights groups report cultural repression, boarding schools and religious restrictions; Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch have documented abuses, which Beijing disputes.
