Victoria, British Columbia

Victoria is the capital city of the Canadian province of British Columbia, located on the southern tip of Vancouver Island off Canada's Pacific coast. The city has a population of about 92,000, with a metropolitan population of about 400,000.

History

The area was inhabited by the Lekwungen-speaking peoples for millennia. Victoria was founded in 1843 as Fort Victoria, a Hudson's Bay Company fur-trading post, and became the capital of the colony of Vancouver Island and later of British Columbia after the mainland and island colonies united in 1866.

Geography and culture

Victoria has a mild Mediterranean-influenced climate unusual for Canada, earning the nickname "the Garden City" for its gardens and year-round greenery. Major employers include provincial government, tourism, and the technology sector; the University of Victoria is nearby in Saanich.
