Columbia Records is an American record label owned by Sony Music Entertainment. Founded in 1889, it is one of the oldest surviving record labels in the world and was historically the first record company. Its name derives from the District of Columbia, where it was headquartered.

History

The Columbia Phonograph Company began by selling Edison phonographs and cylinders in Washington, D.C., and soon manufactured its own recordings. It played a central role in developing the disc record, the long-playing LP (introduced in 1948), and stereo recording. In 1938 Columbia was acquired by the Columbia Broadcasting System (CBS), and for fifty years it operated as CBS Records.

Under producers such as John Hammond and Mitch Miller, the label signed artists including Bessie Smith, Benny Goodman, Bob Dylan, Johnny Cash, Miles Davis, Barbra Streisand, and Bruce Springsteen. Sony purchased CBS Records in 1988 and renamed it Sony Music Entertainment, retaining Columbia as its flagship label.

Present day

Columbia remains one of the world's major labels, with a roster spanning pop, rock, hip hop, and country, and its "Walking Eye" logo is among the most recognisable in the recording industry.
