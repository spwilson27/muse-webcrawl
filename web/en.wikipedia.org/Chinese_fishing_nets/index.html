Chinese fishing nets

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Chinese fishing nets (Malayalam: cheenavala) are a type of stationary lift net used in India, especially in Kerala. They are large mechanical contraptions that hold horizontal nets of about 20 m across, operated from the shore by a team of four to six fishermen. The nets are permanently suspended on cantilevered wooden or bamboo frames; to fish, the team lowers the net into the water and raises it after a short wait, catching whatever swims over the net.

History

Popular belief holds that the nets were introduced to the Malabar Coast in the 14th century by the Chinese explorer Zheng He's fleet, though evidence is circumstantial. A competing theory traces them to a gift from the Chinese emperor to the Portuguese, or to their construction under Portuguese patronage. Whatever their true origin, they are closely associated with the port of Kochi (Cochin) and the Fort Kochi waterfront, where rows of the nets are a famous landmark and tourist attraction, though they remain working fishing gear.

Operation

The apparatus consists of a 10 m-high structure of teakwood and bamboo with long horizontal arms cantilevered over the water, counterweighted by stones (about 30 kg each) tied to ropes. A net about 20 m across hangs beneath the frame on ropes. The operator — traditionally a team working in coordination — lowers the net by walking on the counterweights; the mechanism requires careful balance, and the work demands considerable skill and timing. The nets catch fish, crabs, and prawns near the shore, particularly at high tide.

Modern relevance

Only a few hundred nets survive, mostly around Fort Kochi, Vypeen, and Kollam. Many are maintained primarily as cultural heritage and tourist draws, with the fish sold to visitors at nearby stalls, though a community of fishermen still depends on them for livelihood.
