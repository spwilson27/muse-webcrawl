LGBT community

The LGBT community, also known as the LGBTQ+ community, is a loosely defined grouping of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and other sexual and gender minority individuals united by a common culture and social movements. Communities may include gay villages, pride events, organisations, publications and shared spaces.

Terminology

The initialism LGBT, and variants such as LGBTQ, LGBTQIA+ and LGBTQ+, developed from the earlier term LGB in the late 1980s and 1990s. The rainbow flag, designed by Gilbert Baker in 1978, became a widely recognised symbol.

History and culture

Modern community institutions grew out of gay liberation and earlier homophile movements, bars and neighbourhoods in cities such as New York, San Francisco, London and Berlin, and responses to the HIV/AIDS crisis. Pride marches commemorate events including the Stonewall riots of 1969.

Criticism and diversity

Scholars note the community is not monolithic: members differ by race, class, religion, politics and geography, and the commercialisation of Pride and the inclusion of particular identities remain debated.
