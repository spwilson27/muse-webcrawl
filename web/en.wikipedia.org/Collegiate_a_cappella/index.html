Collegiate a cappella

Collegiate a cappella refers to a cappella singing groups run by students at colleges and universities, chiefly in the United States but also in Canada, the United Kingdom and elsewhere. Groups perform vocal arrangements of popular songs, standards and originals without instrumental accompaniment, using vocal percussion ("beatboxing") and voice-generated effects in place of instruments.

History

The tradition descends from college glee clubs and close-harmony groups such as the Yale Whiffenpoofs, founded in 1909 and generally regarded as the oldest collegiate a cappella group. Barbershop-influenced groups spread through American campuses in the early twentieth century. From the 1980s and 1990s the movement expanded rapidly, influenced by contemporary pop and by the success of groups such as Tufts University's Beelzebubs. There are now well over a thousand collegiate groups in the United States alone.

Organisation and culture

Groups are typically self-governing, holding competitive auditions each year, arranging their own repertoire, and recording albums. The International Championship of Collegiate A Cappella (ICCA), founded in 1996, runs a tournament-style competition that inspired the Pitch Perfect films. The Contemporary A Cappella Society and the annual Best of College A Cappella (BOCA) compilation support the community, and varsity-vocal competitions and festivals are held internationally.
