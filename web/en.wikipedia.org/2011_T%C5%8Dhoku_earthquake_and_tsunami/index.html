The 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, also called the Great East Japan Earthquake, struck off the Pacific coast of north-eastern Honshu on 11 March 2011. The undersea megathrust earthquake, among the most powerful ever recorded, triggered a tsunami with waves up to about 40 metres that inundated coastal towns in Iwate, Miyagi and Fukushima prefectures.

Earthquake and tsunami

The rupture, roughly 500 km long along the Japan Trench, displaced the seabed by tens of metres and shifted Honshu eastward. Some 45 minutes after the quake, tsunami waves overwhelmed seawalls and swept through Sendai and dozens of other communities, destroying ports, railways and hundreds of thousands of buildings. Early-warning systems and evacuations saved many lives, but entire neighbourhoods were erased.

Fukushima nuclear accident

The tsunami flooded the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant, knocking out cooling and causing meltdowns in three reactors, hydrogen explosions and large releases of radioactive material. Around 150,000 people were evacuated from the surrounding area, and the accident — rated level 7 on the INES scale — reshaped energy policy in Japan and abroad.

Aftermath

More than 19,000 people died, most by drowning. Reconstruction raised seawalls, relocated housing to high ground and decontaminated evacuated zones, though some districts remained restricted for years. The disaster remains the costliest natural catastrophe ever recorded.
