Zombi 3 is a 1988 Italian horror film directed by Lucio Fulci and Bruno Mattei. It is an in-name-only sequel to Fulci's Zombi 2 (1979). The film was shot in the Philippines, as were several other Italian zombie films of the period.

Plot

Terrorists steal a canister of a secret chemical compound, "Death One", developed by the military to reanimate dead soldiers. When the canister leaks during the theft and the contaminated body is cremated, the toxic ash rains down over a wide area, infecting birds and reviving the dead. A group of soldiers and vacationing young people must survive attacks by fast-moving zombies and infected animals while the military moves to contain — and cover up — the outbreak.

Production

Fulci began shooting the film in the Philippines but fell ill and left the production before completion. Producer Franco Gaudenzi brought in Bruno Mattei to finish the film, with screenwriter Claudio Fragasso also directing additional material; estimates suggest only part of the finished film is Fulci's work. The film is notorious for its flying zombie head sequence and for recycling elements from earlier Italian horror productions.

Reception and legacy

Released direct to video in many territories, Zombi 3 received poor reviews but developed a cult following among fans of Italian splatter cinema. It was followed by Zombi 4: After Death (1989) and Zombi 5: Killing Birds (1988), which are related in name only.
