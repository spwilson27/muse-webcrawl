The Indianapolis 500, formally known as the Indianapolis 500-Mile Race, and commonly called the Indy 500, is an annual automobile race held at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway (IMS) in Speedway, Indiana, United States, an enclave suburb of Indianapolis. The event is traditionally held over Memorial Day weekend, usually the last weekend of May. It is contested as part of the IndyCar Series, the top level of American open-wheel racing, an event that has long been formally known as the "Greatest Spectacle in Racing."

The inaugural race was held in 1911 and was won by Ray Harroun. The event celebrated its 100th running in 2016. The race is the premier event of the IndyCar calendar and attracts one of the largest single-day sporting audiences in the world, with grandstand and infield attendance estimated at more than 300,000.

History

Early years

The Indianapolis Motor Speedway complex was built in 1909 as a gravel-and-tar track and hosted a number of small events before the founders decided to focus on a single large race. The first 500-mile race took place on May 30, 1911, drawing 40 qualifying entries and a crowd of roughly 80,000. Ray Harroun, driving a Marmon Wasp and using a rear-view mirror instead of a riding mechanic, won in 6 hours, 42 minutes, and 8 seconds at an average speed of 74.602 mph.

AAA and USAC eras

The race was sanctioned first by the AAA Contest Board and later by the United States Auto Club (USAC). European drivers and cars occasionally challenged the American roadster contingent, most notably when Jim Clark won in 1965 in the rear-engined Lotus 38, accelerating the shift away from front-engined roadsters. A. J. Foyt became the first four-time winner in 1977; Al Unser and Rick Mears matched the feat, and Hélio Castroneves joined the group in 2021.

Modern era

Since 1996 the race has been part of the IndyCar Series. The event anchors the "Month of May," which includes practice sessions, Pole Day qualifying, and the Indianapolis 500 Festival Parade. Traditions include the winner drinking a bottle of milk in Victory Lane — a custom dating to Louis Meyer's victory in 1936 — and the singing of "Back Home Again in Indiana" before the start.

Race format

The field consists of 33 cars arranged in eleven rows of three, following the speedway's longstanding tradition. Qualifying is held over two days the weekend before the race, with each car making a four-lap timed run. The race itself covers 200 laps of the 2.5-mile (4.0 km) rectangular oval, totaling 500 miles (800 km). Since 2011, double points have been awarded for the race in the IndyCar championship standings.
