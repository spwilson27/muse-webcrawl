Game studies

Game studies, also known as ludology, is the study of games, the act of playing them, and the players and cultures surrounding them. It is a field of cultural studies that deals with all types of games throughout history. This field of research utilises the tactics of, at least, anthropology, sociology and psychology while examining aspects of the design of the game, the players in the game and finally, the role the game plays in its society or culture.

Approaches

Game studies is often distinguished by its focus on video games in the modern era, though its theories apply across media. Key debates in the field's early years included the (now largely set aside) narratology-versus-ludology controversy over whether games should be analysed primarily as stories or as rule systems. Other major approaches include platform studies, player studies and ethnography of online worlds, production studies, and formal analysis of rules and mechanics.

Institutions and publications

The journal Game Studies was founded in 2001, followed by venues such as Games and Culture and the Digital Games Research Association (DiGRA, founded 2003). Influential works include Johan Huizinga's Homo Ludens (1938), Roger Caillois's Man, Play and Games (1958), Espen Aarseth's Cybertext (1997), and Jesper Juul's Half-Real (2005).
