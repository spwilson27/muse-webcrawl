Harmonia axyridis

Harmonia axyridis is a large species of lady beetle in the family Coccinellidae, commonly known as the Asian lady beetle, multicoloured Asian lady beetle, or harlequin ladybird. Native to eastern Asia, it has been introduced around the world for the biological control of aphids and scale insects, and has become one of the world's most invasive insect species.

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Arthropoda
Class: Insecta
Order: Coleoptera
Family: Coccinellidae
Genus: Harmonia
Species: H. axyridis

Description

Adults are oval and strongly convex, typically 5.5–8.5 mm long. Colouration is extremely variable: elytra may be orange or red with up to 22 black spots, black with red spots, or nearly unmarked. The pronotum is cream or white with a black M- or W-shaped marking, a useful identifying feature.

Invasiveness

Introduced to North America and Europe in the twentieth century, the species established widely and now outcompetes many native ladybirds. Large aggregations overwinter inside buildings, where the beetles can stain surfaces and taint wine when harvested with grapes. They also bite humans occasionally, though the bites are harmless.
