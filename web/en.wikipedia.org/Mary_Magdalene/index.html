Mary Magdalene

Follower of Jesus in the New Testament

Mary Magdalene (Aramaic: Mariam Magdalēnē, "Mary of Magdala") was, according to the four canonical Gospels, a woman who travelled with Jesus as one of his followers and was a witness to his crucifixion and resurrection. She is mentioned by name more often than most of the apostles and is honoured as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican and Lutheran churches.

In the Gospels

Luke's Gospel relates that Jesus cast seven demons out of Mary Magdalene, and that she was among the women who supported his ministry out of their own means. She was present at the crucifixion when most of the male disciples had fled, and in John's Gospel she is the first person to see the risen Christ, who commissions her to announce the resurrection to the others — an account that led to her traditional title "Apostle to the Apostles."

Later traditions

In Western medieval tradition, Mary Magdalene was conflated with Mary of Bethany and with the unnamed "sinful woman" who anointed Jesus' feet; a famous sermon of Pope Gregory I in 591 consolidated this composite figure, embedding the image of Magdalene as a repentant prostitute in Western art and devotion. The Eastern churches never accepted the conflation, and the Roman Catholic Church separated the figures in its calendar revision of 1969, though the popular image persists.

Gnostic writings

Mary Magdalene figures prominently in several Gnostic texts, including the Gospel of Mary, the Gospel of Philip and Pistis Sophia, where she appears as a favoured disciple who receives secret teachings — a portrayal that has fuelled modern popular speculation, most famously in fiction such as The Da Vinci Code.
