The Yellowstone Caldera, sometimes called the Yellowstone supervolcano, is a volcanic caldera and supervolcano in Yellowstone National Park in the western United States. It was formed by a cataclysmic eruption about 640,000 years ago that ejected more than 1,000 km³ of rock and ash. The caldera measures roughly 72 by 55 kilometres and underlies much of the park's central plateau.

Eruptive history

The Yellowstone hotspot has produced three VEI-8 eruptions in the past 2.1 million years: the Huckleberry Ridge eruption (2.1 million years ago), the Mesa Falls eruption (1.3 million years ago) and the Lava Creek eruption (640,000 years ago) that formed the present caldera. Younger activity has been mostly rhyolitic lava flows, the most recent about 70,000 years ago.

Hydrothermal system

Heat from the magma system below drives the park's celebrated geysers and hot springs — more than 10,000 hydrothermal features, including Old Faithful. Two resurgent domes within the caldera rise and subside measurably, and earthquake swarms are common; the 1959 Hebgen Lake earthquake (M7.3) struck just west of the park.

Risk assessment

The USGS monitors Yellowstone continuously and states that another caldera-forming eruption is not imminent; annual probabilities are estimated around 0.00014%. Smaller eruptions and hydrothermal explosions are considered far more likely hazards.
