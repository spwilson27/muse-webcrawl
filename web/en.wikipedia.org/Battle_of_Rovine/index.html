Battle of Rovine

The marshy valley of the Argeș, where Mircea the Elder ambushed Bayezid I's army in 1395.

Date17 May 1395
LocationRovine, Argeș valley, Wallachia
ResultWallachian victory (disputed)
CommandersMircea the Elder vs. Bayezid I

The Battle of Rovine took place on 17 May 1395 between the army of Wallachia, led by Voivode Mircea the Elder, and an invading Ottoman army commanded by Sultan Bayezid I. It is one of the most celebrated battles of Romanian medieval history, immortalised in Mihai Eminescu's poem The Third Letter.

Background

After the Ottoman victory at Kosovo in 1389, Mircea's Wallachia stood in the path of Bayezid's advance toward Hungary. Mircea raided Ottoman territory south of the Danube, provoking Bayezid to invade in the spring of 1395 with a large army reinforced by Serbian vassal contingents, including troops of Stefan Lazarević and the lord Marko.

The battle

Mircea, heavily outnumbered, avoided open battle and harassed the invasion with scorched-earth tactics, drawing the Ottomans into unfavourable, marshy wooded ground in the Argeș valley at Rovine — "the ravines". Wallachian archers and ambushes from prepared positions inflicted heavy losses; Serbian sources mourned prominent nobles killed in the fighting, among them, by tradition, the hero Marko Mrnjavčević (Prince Marko), who is said to have declared he wished the Christians victory even at the cost of his own life. Bayezid withdrew across the Danube, having failed to subjugate Wallachia.

Consequences

Rovine preserved Wallachian autonomy for the moment, but Bayezid installed a rival voivode, Vlad, and Mircea had to fight for years to recover his throne; Wallachia eventually accepted paying tribute while keeping its internal independence. Some historians debate the battle's exact location and date, and Ottoman sources treat the campaign differently, but in Romanian memory Rovine endures as the archetype of the smaller nation defeating a great empire by cunning and terrain.
