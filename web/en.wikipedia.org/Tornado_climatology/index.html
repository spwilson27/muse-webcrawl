Tornado climatology

Tornado climatology is the study of where, when and how often tornadoes occur around the world, and how their frequency and intensity vary over time.

Distribution

The United States records the most tornadoes of any country — roughly 1,200 a year — concentrated in the Great Plains "Tornado Alley" and, increasingly recognised, the Southeast's "Dixie Alley". Other hotspots include the Pampas of Argentina, Bangladesh and eastern India (site of the deadliest tornado on record, at Daulatpur–Saturia in 1989), South Africa, Australia, and parts of Europe. Tornadoes peak in spring and early summer in mid-latitudes, following the clash of warm moist air with cool dry air and strong wind shear.

Records and trends

Intensity is rated on the Enhanced Fujita scale. Long records are biased by population and reporting changes, making climate-change attribution difficult; research suggests shifts in seasonality and clustering of outbreaks even where total counts are stable.
