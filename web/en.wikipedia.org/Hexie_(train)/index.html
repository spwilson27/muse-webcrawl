Hexie (Chinese: 和谐号; pinyin: Héxié Hào; literally "Harmony") is the Chinese name given to the China Railway High-speed (CRH) series of high-speed trains operated by China Railway. The Hexie trains were developed through technology transfer agreements with foreign manufacturers, including Bombardier, Alstom, Siemens and Kawasaki Heavy Industries.

Development

In the early 2000s China sought to build a high-speed rail network using proven foreign technology adapted to domestic production. The resulting families are the CRH1 (based on Bombardier's Regina), CRH2 (based on the E2 Series Shinkansen), CRH3 (based on the Siemens Velaro) and CRH5 (based on Alstom's Pendolino family, without tilting). Later, the CRH380A/B/C/D series were developed with greater domestic design input for speeds up to 380 km/h design and 350 km/h commercial operation.

Service

Hexie trains operate the world's largest high-speed network, serving lines such as the Beijing–Shanghai, Beijing–Guangzhou and Harbin–Dalian high-speed railways. Following the 2011 Wenzhou train collision, maximum speeds were temporarily reduced to 300 km/h before restoration of 350 km/h operation on major lines in 2017. From 2017 the newer indigenously designed Fuxing ("Rejuvenation") trains began to supplement and progressively replace Hexie services on flagship routes.
