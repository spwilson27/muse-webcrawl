Educational assessment

Educational assessment or educational evaluation is the systematic process of documenting and using empirical data on the knowledge, skills, attitudes and beliefs of learners, in order to refine programmes and improve student learning. Assessment data can be obtained from directly examining student work, from standardised tests, or from observations and other sources.

Types

Assessments are commonly classified as formative or summative. Formative assessment takes place during learning and provides feedback to guide teaching and study, while summative assessment evaluates achievement at the end of a course or programme, such as final examinations. Diagnostic assessment identifies learners' prior knowledge and misconceptions, and assessments may be norm-referenced (comparing candidates with each other) or criterion-referenced (comparing performance with fixed standards).

Validity and reliability

Key quality criteria for assessment include validity — whether the assessment measures what it claims to measure — and reliability, the consistency of results. Modern assessment theory also emphasises fairness, authenticity of tasks, and the consequences of assessment for teaching and learning, known as washback.

Methods

Methods include multiple-choice and constructed-response tests, essays, portfolios, practical examinations, oral examinations, performance assessment and peer assessment. Large-scale international studies such as PISA compare educational outcomes across countries.
