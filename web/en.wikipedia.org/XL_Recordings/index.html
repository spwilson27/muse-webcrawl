XL Recordings is a British independent record label founded in 1989. Originally an offshoot of Citybeat Records devoted to rave and dance music, it became one of the most successful independent labels in the world, with a small but influential roster, as a member of the Beggars Group of labels.

History

XL was launched by Tim Palmer, Nick Halkes, and Richard Russell as a dance label releasing records by acts of the UK rave scene. Its first major success was The Prodigy, signed in 1990, whose albums Experience and The Fat of the Land became landmarks of electronic music. Richard Russell took over leadership of the label and broadened its scope while keeping the roster deliberately small — at times only a handful of artists.

Artists and success

The label's breakthrough into the global mainstream came with Adele, whose albums 19, 21, and 25 — released on XL in most of the world — sold tens of millions of copies, with 21 becoming the best-selling album of the 21st century. Other notable XL artists have included Radiohead (whose In Rainbows and later albums came out on XL), the White Stripes (UK), M.I.A., Dizzee Rascal, Vampire Weekend, The xx, Jack White, King Krule, and Sampha. Russell himself records as part of Everything Is Recorded.

XL operates the imprint Young (formerly Young Turks), home to The xx, FKA twigs, and Sampha, and has been praised by the music press for an artist-friendly, quality-over-quantity approach that yields an unusually high rate of critically acclaimed releases.
