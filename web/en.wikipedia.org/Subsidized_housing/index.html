Subsidized housing

Subsidized housing is government-supported housing provided at below-market cost to low- and moderate-income households. Subsidies may be paid to the occupants (demand-side, such as housing vouchers), to the developers or owners of housing (supply-side), or embedded in publicly owned dwellings rented at reduced rates, as in public housing.

Forms

Common forms include direct public provision; project-based assistance tied to particular buildings; tenant-based vouchers — notably the United States Section 8 Housing Choice Voucher program administered by local housing authorities under the Department of Housing and Urban Development; and tax incentives for private developers, such as the U.S. Low-Income Housing Tax Credit. In the United Kingdom, council housing and housing associations provide social rented housing.

Eligibility and allocation

Eligibility is usually means-tested against local income limits, and demand frequently exceeds supply, producing long waiting lists. Rent in many programs is set as a proportion of household income (commonly about 30% in U.S. programs).

Debates

Supporters argue subsidized housing addresses market failures, homelessness, and housing insecurity; critics raise concerns about concentrated poverty in large projects, administrative costs, and disincentives. Policy has in many places shifted from large public estates toward vouchers and mixed-income developments.
