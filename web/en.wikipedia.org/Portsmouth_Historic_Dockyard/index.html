Portsmouth Historic Dockyard

Portsmouth Historic Dockyard is an area of HM Naval Base Portsmouth in Portsmouth, Hampshire, England, open to the public and containing several historic ships and the National Museum of the Royal Navy. The working naval base dates to 1495, when the world's first dry dock was built there under Henry VII.

Attractions

The dockyard's centrepieces are three famous ships. HMS Victory, launched 1765, is Nelson's flagship at Trafalgar (1805) and the world's oldest naval vessel still in commission, preserved in dry dock. HMS Warrior (1860) was Britain's first iron-hulled armoured battleship. The remains of the Mary Rose, Henry VIII's flagship sunk in 1545 and raised in 1982, are displayed in a dedicated museum opened in 2013.

Other attractions include the submarine HMS Alliance at the Royal Navy Submarine Museum in Gosport (reached by waterbus), Action Stations, the Trafalgar Sail, and harbour tours. Historic buildings include the Georgian storehouses, the Block Mills — pioneers of mass-production machine tools — and Boathouse No. 4.

Management

The historic dockyard is managed by the National Museum of the Royal Navy together with the Mary Rose Trust, the HMS Victory preservation bodies, and the Portsmouth Naval Base Property Trust, which cares for the historic buildings in the still-active naval base.
