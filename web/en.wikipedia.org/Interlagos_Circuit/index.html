The Autódromo José Carlos Pace, better known as Interlagos, is a 4.309 km (2.677 mi) motorsport circuit located in the city of São Paulo, Brazil, in the neighbourhood of Interlagos. It was inaugurated on 12 May 1940 and renamed in 1985 in honour of Brazilian Formula One driver José Carlos Pace, who died in a 1977 plane crash. It is the home of the São Paulo Grand Prix (formerly the Brazilian Grand Prix).

Layout

The circuit runs anticlockwise and is noted for its undulating terrain, long uphill pit straight, and the opening Senna 'S' corners (named for Ayrton Senna), which plunge downhill into the Curva do Sol. Other named corners include Descida do Lago, Ferradura, Laranjinha, Pinheirinho, Bico de Pato, Mergulho, Junção, and the sweeping Subida dos Boxes leading to Arquibancadas. The altitude of São Paulo (about 800 m) reduces engine power and downforce, and the bumpy surface and changeable weather frequently produce dramatic races.

Formula One history

Interlagos first hosted a World Championship Grand Prix in 1973, won by local hero Emerson Fittipaldi. After a period at Jacarepaguá in Rio de Janeiro, Formula One returned permanently in 1990 on a shortened circuit. Memorable races include Ayrton Senna's emotional home victory in 1991, driving much of the race stuck in sixth gear; the rain-hit 2003 race won by Giancarlo Fisichella; and title deciders in 2007, 2008 — when Lewis Hamilton overtook Timo Glock at the final corner to deny Felipe Massa the championship — and 2012.

Other events

Besides Formula One, the circuit hosts the FIA World Endurance Championship's 6 Hours of São Paulo, Brazilian stock car and touring car racing, and music festivals such as Lollapalooza Brazil on its grounds.
