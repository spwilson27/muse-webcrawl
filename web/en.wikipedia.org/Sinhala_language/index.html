Sinhala language

Sinhala
Native to: Sri Lanka
Speakers: ~16 million native
Family: Indo-European, Indo-Aryan
Script: Sinhala script (Brahmic)

Sinhala is an Indo-Aryan language primarily spoken by the Sinhalese people, the largest ethnic group in Sri Lanka, and an official language of the country alongside Tamil.

History

Sinhala developed in relative isolation from mainland Indo-Aryan languages after settlement of Sri Lanka in the first millennium BCE, absorbing Dravidian (Tamil) and later Portuguese, Dutch and English influences. Early inscriptions appear at Anuradhapura; classical literature drew heavily on Sanskrit and Pali.

Features

The language is diglossic, with distinct literary and spoken varieties, and its rounded script evolved for writing on ola palm leaves without tearing them. Language politics around Sinhala were a factor in the Sri Lankan civil war.
