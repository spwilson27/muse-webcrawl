Electronic paper

Electronic paper, also known as reflective display, is a display device that mimics the appearance of ordinary ink on paper. Unlike conventional flat-panel displays, which emit light, electronic paper reflects ambient light like ordinary paper, making it readable in bright sunlight and extremely power-efficient.

Electrophoretic displays are bistable: they retain an image without consuming power.

Technology

The most common technology is the electrophoretic display, developed from research at Xerox PARC in the 1970s and commercialized by E Ink Corporation (founded 1997). Millions of tiny microcapsules contain electrically charged white particles suspended in a dark fluid; applying a voltage moves the particles to or away from the viewing surface. Other approaches include electrowetting and cholesteric liquid crystal displays.

Applications

Electronic paper is best known in e-readers such as Amazon's Kindle, introduced in 2007. It is also used for electronic shelf labels, smartwatches (such as Pebble), signage, and digital notebooks. Colour e-paper exists but with muted colours and slower refresh than LCD or OLED displays.

Advantages and limitations

Advantages include low power consumption, wide viewing angle, and eye comfort. Limitations include slow refresh rates unsuitable for video, low brightness in the dark without a front light, and limited colour performance.
