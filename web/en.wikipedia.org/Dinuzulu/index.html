Dinuzulu kaCetshwayo (c. 1868 – 18 October 1913) was the king of the Zulu nation from 20 May 1884 until his death. He succeeded his father, Cetshwayo, but ruled over a kingdom already divided by civil war and British intervention.

Early reign

To defeat his rival Zibhebhu, the young Dinuzulu enlisted Boer volunteers, the "Dinuzulu's Volunteers", rewarding them with land that became the nucleus of the Nieuwe Republiek. Zululand was annexed by Britain in 1887 and later incorporated into Natal.

Exile and imprisonment

Accused of involvement in the 1906 Bambatha Rebellion, Dinuzulu was tried for high treason and sentenced to four years' imprisonment. Released in 1910 by his friend Prime Minister Louis Botha, he retired to a farm in the Transvaal, where he died in 1913. His son Solomon became the recognised Zulu king.
