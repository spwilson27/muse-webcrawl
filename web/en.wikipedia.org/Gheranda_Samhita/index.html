The Gheranda Samhita (Sanskrit: Gheraṇḍasaṁhitā, "Gheranda's collection") is a Sanskrit text of hatha yoga, probably written in the late 17th century. It is one of the three classic texts of hatha yoga, alongside the Hatha Yoga Pradipika and the Shiva Samhita, and one of the most encyclopaedic of all yoga texts.

Content

The text is framed as a dialogue between the sage Gheranda and his disciple Chanda Kapali. Unlike other hatha yoga texts, the Gheranda Samhita speaks of a sevenfold yoga (saptaṅga yoga), structured in seven chapters that teach, in sequence: the six purifications (shatkarma), thirty-two postures (asana), twenty-five seals (mudra), withdrawal of the senses (pratyahara), breath control (pranayama), meditation (dhyana), and absorption (samadhi).

The body is compared to an unbaked clay pot that must be hardened in the fire of yoga. The text's goal is physical perfection as a means to the highest spiritual attainment, culminating in isolation (kaivalya) of the self.

Manuscripts and commentaries

The Gheranda Samhita survives in numerous manuscripts from across India, including versions with differing numbers of verses. A well-known commentary was written by Khemaraja. The text was among the first hatha yoga works translated into English, appearing in the late 19th and early 20th centuries through the Theosophical Publishing House and translators such as Rai Bahadur Srisa Chandra Vasu.

Influence

Together with the Hatha Yoga Pradipika, the Gheranda Samhita formed the textual basis for much of modern postural yoga's knowledge of traditional asanas, shatkarmas and mudras, and it remains a standard reference in yoga teacher training.
