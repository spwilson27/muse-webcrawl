Veracruz (city)

Veracruz, officially the Heroica Veracruz, is a major port city and municipality on the Gulf of Mexico in the Mexican state of Veracruz. It is the state's most populous city and one of the country's oldest and most historically significant ports. The city is located along the coast in central Veracruz, about 90 km (56 mi) southeast of the state capital, Xalapa.

History

Veracruz was founded by Hernán Cortés on Good Friday, 22 April 1519, as La Villa Rica de la Vera Cruz, making it the first Spanish municipality in continental America. It served as the principal port of New Spain, through which the treasure fleets carried silver and goods between Mexico and Spain. The offshore fortress of San Juan de Ulúa guarded the harbour.

The city was attacked and occupied several times: by the United States during the Mexican–American War in 1847, by the French in 1861 at the start of the French intervention, and by the United States again in 1914. Its defence against these invasions earned Veracruz the title "four times heroic" (Cuatro Veces Heroica), reflected in its official name.

Port and economy

The Port of Veracruz is Mexico's oldest and largest port on the Gulf coast and a principal gateway for automobile imports and exports, container traffic, and bulk cargo. The city's economy also depends on tourism, fishing and services. A major expansion of the port in the bay of Vergara was inaugurated in 2019.

Culture

Veracruz is famous for its carnival, one of the largest in Mexico, and for its musical traditions, including son jarocho and the song "La Bamba". The city's cuisine blends Spanish, indigenous and Afro-Cuban influences; its coffee houses on the main square (zócalo), where marimba bands play, are a long-standing local institution.
