Gravity dam

A gravity dam is a dam constructed from concrete or stone masonry and designed to hold back water by using only the weight of the material and its resistance against the foundation to oppose the horizontal pressure of water pushing against it. Gravity dams are designed so that each section of the dam is stable and independent of any other dam section.

Characteristics

Gravity dams generally require stiff, strong foundations, ideally of unfractured rock. They are typically built in a wide, gently sloping valley, although most are built in narrow gorges. Because a gravity dam relies on its own weight to hold back water, it is essential that the foundation be strong enough to bear the load without excessive settlement or uplift pressure from water seeping beneath the structure.

The cross-section of a gravity dam is roughly triangular: nearly vertical on the upstream face and sloping on the downstream face, so that the resultant of the water pressure and the dam's own weight passes through the middle third of the base, preventing tension and overturning.

Classification

Gravity dams are classified by their construction material and structural action:

  • Concrete gravity dams, built of mass concrete, including roller-compacted concrete (RCC) dams.
  • Masonry gravity dams, built of stone or brick masonry.
  • Gravity dams combined with arches, known as arch-gravity dams, which derive part of their strength from arch action transferring loads to the abutments.

Examples

Notable gravity dams include the Grand Coulee Dam in the United States, the Bhakra Dam in India, and the Itaipu Dam on the border of Brazil and Paraguay (a combination of gravity, arch-gravity, and embankment sections). The Three Gorges Dam in China, the largest hydroelectric dam in the world by installed capacity, is a concrete gravity dam.

Advantages and disadvantages

Gravity dams are durable, require relatively low maintenance, and can be built in a wide range of valley shapes. However, they require very large volumes of concrete or masonry, firm foundations, and are vulnerable to uplift pressure and foundation weaknesses; failure of a gravity dam foundation contributed to disasters such as the St. Francis Dam failure of 1928.
