The Hawker Siddeley Nimrod was a British maritime patrol aircraft developed from the de Havilland Comet airliner — the first jet airliner — and operated by the Royal Air Force from 1969 to 2011. It served in anti-submarine warfare (ASW), anti-surface warfare and search-and-rescue roles, principally from RAF Kinloss in Scotland.

Design and variants

The Nimrod MR1, and its greatly improved successor the MR2, carried radar, magnetic anomaly detection, sonobuoys and a weapons bay for torpedoes and depth charges; MR2s later gained the ability to carry Harpoon and Sidewinder missiles. The R1 variant was a dedicated signals-intelligence aircraft. An AEW3 early-warning version suffered protracted development problems and was cancelled in 1986.

Replacement programme

A heavily rebuilt replacement, the Nimrod MRA4, was cancelled in the 2010 Strategic Defence and Security Review before entering service, leaving the UK without a dedicated maritime patrol aircraft until the arrival of the Boeing P-8 Poseidon in 2019–2020.
