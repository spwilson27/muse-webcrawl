Casimir III the Great (Polish: Kazimierz III Wielki; 30 April 1310 – 5 November 1370) was King of Poland from 1333 to 1370, the last king of the Piast dynasty. Widely considered one of Poland's greatest monarchs, he is remembered in the saying that he "found Poland built of wood and left it built of stone", reflecting his extensive programme of castle and town construction.

Reign

Succeeding his father Władysław I, Casimir preferred diplomacy to war where possible: he settled the dispute with the Teutonic Order (Treaty of Kalisz, 1343), renounced claims to Silesia in exchange for Bohemian recognition of his title, and expanded eastward into Red Ruthenia (Galicia). At home he codified Polish law (the Statutes of Casimir the Great at Piotrków and Wiślica), reformed taxation, the army, and the judiciary, founded or chartered many towns under Magdeburg law, and built dozens of castles and defensive walls.

Legacy

In 1364 Casimir founded the Kraków Academy — today the Jagiellonian University — the first university in Poland. He also extended royal protection to Jews, confirming and enlarging their privileges, which encouraged Jewish settlement in Poland. Having no surviving son, he arranged for the Polish crown to pass to his nephew, Louis I of Hungary, ending the Piast line of Polish kings on his death in 1370 from injuries suffered in a hunting accident.
