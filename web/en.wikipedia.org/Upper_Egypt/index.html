Ancient Egyptian nameTa-Shemau ("Land of the Reeds")

Upper Egypt (Arabic: صعيد مصر‎) is the southern portion of Egypt, encompassing the narrow strip of land on both sides of the Nile River that extends from south of the modern city of Cairo (near el-Aiyat) to Lake Nasser in the south, near the border with Sudan. The northern part of Egypt, including the Nile Delta, is called Lower Egypt — "upper" and "lower" refer to the flow of the Nile, which runs from south to north.

Geography

Upper Egypt follows the Nile valley, flanked by the Eastern Desert and the Western Desert. The valley is narrow — in places only a few kilometres of cultivable land lie between the river and the desert escarpments. Modern governorates of the region include Aswan, Luxor, Qena, Sohag, Asyut, Minya, and Beni Suef. Inhabitants of Upper Egypt are called Sa'idis and are distinguished by dialect and customs from Egyptians of the Delta.

History

In predynastic times, Upper Egypt was organized into nomes (districts) and was unified into a kingdom centred eventually at Thinis and then at Thebes. Around 3100 BC, the Upper Egyptian king Narmer is traditionally credited with the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt, founding Pharaonic civilization. Thebes (modern Luxor) served as capital during much of the Middle and New Kingdoms, and Upper Egypt preserves Egypt's most famous archaeological sites: the Valley of the Kings, the temples of Karnak and Luxor, Abu Simbel, Philae (near Aswan), and Abydos.
