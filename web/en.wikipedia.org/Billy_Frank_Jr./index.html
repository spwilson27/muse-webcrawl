Billy Frank Jr.

Died5 May 2014, Olympia, Washington, U.S.
NationalityNisqually Indian Tribe

Billy Frank Jr. (9 March 1931 – 5 May 2014) was a Native American environmental leader and treaty rights activist from the Nisqually Tribe in Washington state. He was a central figure in the "Fish Wars" of the 1960s and 1970s, during which Native Americans staged "fish-ins" to assert treaty-protected fishing rights.

Activism

Frank was arrested more than 50 times for fishing in defiance of state regulations on the Nisqually River. The protests helped lead to the landmark Boldt Decision of 1974, in which federal judge George Boldt affirmed that Washington treaty tribes were entitled to half the harvestable salmon in the state.

Environmental work

After the decision, Frank became chairman of the Northwest Indian Fisheries Commission, a position he held for more than 30 years, working on salmon habitat restoration and cooperative resource management.

Honours

Frank received the Albert Schweitzer Prize for Humanitarianism in 1992 and was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2015. The Billy Frank Jr. Nisqually National Wildlife Refuge was renamed in his honour.
