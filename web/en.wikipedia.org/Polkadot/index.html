Polkadot is a blockchain platform designed to allow different blockchains to interoperate. Founded by Ethereum co-founder Gavin Wood and developed by the Web3 Foundation and Parity Technologies, it launched in May 2020. Its native cryptocurrency is DOT, used for governance, staking and bonding new chains to the network.

Architecture

Polkadot consists of a central Relay Chain, which provides shared security and coordinates consensus, connected to parallel application-specific blockchains called parachains. Parachains lease slots on the Relay Chain and process transactions in parallel, while "bridges" aim to connect the network to external chains such as Bitcoin and Ethereum. The system originally used a nominated proof-of-stake consensus in which validators secure the Relay Chain and collators maintain parachains; later upgrades (including "Agile Coretime") revised how blockspace is allocated.

Governance

DOT holders vote on protocol changes through an on-chain governance system. The original model included a council and technical committee; the OpenGov system, activated in 2023, replaced it with multiple parallel referendum tracks with different thresholds and decision periods. Kusama, launched in 2019, serves as Polkadot's experimental "canary network".

History

Polkadot's white paper was published in 2016 and its first token sale took place in 2017; a large portion of the funds raised was frozen in the 2017 Parity wallet vulnerability. In 2020 the DOT token was redenominated by a factor of 100 following a community vote.
