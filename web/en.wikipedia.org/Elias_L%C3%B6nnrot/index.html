Elias Lönnrot

Born9 April 1802, Sammatti, Finland
Died19 March 1884 (aged 81), Sammatti, Finland
OccupationPhysician, philologist, collector of traditional Finnish oral poetry
Known forCompiling the Kalevala

Elias Lönnrot (9 April 1802 – 19 March 1884) was a Finnish physician, philologist and collector of traditional Finnish oral poetry. He is best known for creating the Finnish national epic, the Kalevala (1835, expanded 1849), from short ballads and lyric poems gathered from Finnish and Karelian oral tradition during a series of field expeditions in Finland and Karelia.

Early life and education

Lönnrot was born in Sammatti, in the province of Uusimaa, the son of a tailor. He studied medicine at the University of Turku (later the University of Helsinki), graduating in 1832 with a dissertation on folk medicine. He worked as a district doctor in Kajaani in eastern Finland, a remote post that put him in close contact with the rural singers of runos.

The Kalevala

Between 1828 and 1844 Lönnrot made eleven field trips through Finland, Karelia, Ingria and Estonia collecting poems. From this material he assembled the first edition of the Kalevala in 1835 and a greatly expanded edition in 1849 containing nearly 23,000 verses. The epic became a cornerstone of Finnish national identity and language. He also compiled the Kanteletar (1840), a collection of lyric poems, and worked extensively on Finnish lexicography, producing a major Finnish–Swedish dictionary.

Legacy

Lönnrot is regarded as one of the most influential figures in Finnish culture; his birthday, 9 April, is celebrated as the Day of the Finnish Language.
