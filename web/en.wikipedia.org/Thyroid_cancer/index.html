Thyroid cancer is cancer that develops from the tissues of the thyroid gland. It is a disease in which cells grow abnormally and have the potential to spread to other parts of the body. Symptoms can include a lump or swelling in the neck; most thyroid nodules, however, are benign.

Types

The main types of thyroid cancer are papillary thyroid cancer (the most common, about 80% of cases), follicular thyroid cancer, medullary thyroid cancer (arising from the parafollicular C cells and sometimes part of multiple endocrine neoplasia syndromes), and anaplastic thyroid cancer, a rare but highly aggressive form. Hürthle cell carcinoma is often considered a variant of follicular cancer.

Signs and symptoms

The first sign is often a painless thyroid nodule. As the disease progresses, there may be hoarseness from involvement of the recurrent laryngeal nerve, difficulty swallowing or breathing, and enlarged cervical lymph nodes.

Causes and risk factors

Exposure to ionizing radiation, particularly in childhood — as occurred after the Chernobyl disaster — is a well-established risk factor. Other factors include a family history of thyroid cancer, inherited conditions such as familial medullary thyroid cancer and MEN 2, iodine deficiency (associated with follicular cancer), and female sex; thyroid cancer is roughly three times more common in women.

Diagnosis

Evaluation typically involves neck ultrasound and fine-needle aspiration biopsy of suspicious nodules, guided by risk-stratification systems such as TI-RADS. Blood tests of thyroid function are usually normal.

Treatment

The mainstay of treatment is surgery — partial or total thyroidectomy, often with neck dissection for involved lymph nodes. Differentiated cancers may be treated postoperatively with radioactive iodine (I-131) to ablate remnant tissue. After total thyroidectomy, lifelong thyroid hormone replacement is required, and TSH suppression may reduce recurrence risk. Advanced or refractory disease may be treated with kinase inhibitors such as lenvatinib and sorafenib.

Epidemiology and prognosis

Thyroid cancer accounted for about 586,000 new cases worldwide in 2020. Rising incidence in many countries is attributed largely to increased detection of small papillary cancers by imaging. Prognosis for differentiated thyroid cancer is excellent, with five-year survival exceeding 98% for localized papillary disease, whereas anaplastic carcinoma has a very poor prognosis.
