Jean-Luc Godard

Born: 3 December 1930, Paris, France

Died: 13 September 2022, Rolle, Switzerland

Occupation: Film director, screenwriter, critic

Movement: French New Wave

Jean-Luc Godard (3 December 1930 – 13 September 2022) was a French-Swiss film director, screenwriter and critic, regarded as one of the most influential filmmakers of the post-war era. A pioneer of the French New Wave, he reshaped cinema with films such as Breathless (1960), Vivre sa vie (1962), Contempt (1963) and Pierrot le Fou (1965).

Career

Godard began as a critic for Cahiers du cinéma, arguing for an auteurist cinema. His debut feature Breathless, with its jump cuts, location shooting and improvisational energy, became the emblem of the New Wave. Through the 1960s his films grew increasingly political, culminating in the Dziga Vertov Group period after the events of May 1968.

Later work

From the 1980s onward, working from Rolle, Switzerland, Godard produced essayistic and formally experimental works, including the monumental video project Histoire(s) du cinéma, Film Socialisme (2010), the 3D film Goodbye to Language (2014), which won the Jury Prize at Cannes, and The Image Book (2018). He received an honorary Academy Award in 2010. Godard died by assisted suicide at his home in 2022, aged 91.
