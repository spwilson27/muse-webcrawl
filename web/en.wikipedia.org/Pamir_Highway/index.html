Pamir Highway

DesignationM41
RouteDushanbe (Tajikistan) – Osh (Kyrgyzstan)
LengthAbout 1,252 km (778 mi)
Highest pointAk-Baital Pass, 4,655 m (15,272 ft)

The Pamir Highway, officially the M41, is a road traversing the Pamir Mountains through Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and Kyrgyzstan in Central Asia. Running from Dushanbe, the capital of Tajikistan, to Osh in Kyrgyzstan via Khorog, it is one of the world's highest international roads, crossing the Ak-Baital Pass at 4,655 metres — the highest point on any former Soviet highway.

History

The route follows one branch of the ancient Silk Road. The first motorable sections were built by the Russian Empire at the end of the 19th century for military purposes; the Soviet Union constructed the modern highway in stages between the 1930s and 1940s, and it was numbered M41 in the Soviet road network.

Route and travel

East of Khorog the road climbs onto the Pamir plateau, passing Murghab, Lake Karakul, and remote Kyrgyz and Wakhi villages, with long stretches of rough, partially unpaved surface subject to landslides, avalanches, and erosion. The highway is celebrated by overland travellers and cyclists as one of the great adventure routes of the world, and a branch connects with the Wakhan Corridor of Afghanistan.
