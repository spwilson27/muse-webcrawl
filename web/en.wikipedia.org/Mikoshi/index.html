Mikoshi

Mikoshi (神輿)
Also called: shin'yo (divine palanquin)
Use: Shinto festivals (matsuri)
Purpose: Transporting a shrine's kami through the parish

A mikoshi (Japanese: 神輿) is a sacred religious palanquin in Shinto tradition. It serves as a portable shrine in which a kami (deity) is carried from a shrine through its neighbourhood during a festival, or matsuri, and on other ceremonial occasions.

Form

A mikoshi resembles a miniature shrine building mounted on carrying poles. It is typically lacquered black with gold ornament, has a roof that may be topped by a phoenix or other finial, and is hung with bells and tassels. The kami's spirit is ritually transferred into the mikoshi (in an object such as a mirror, sword or jewel) before the procession. Some mikoshi weigh over a tonne and require dozens of bearers.

Carrying

Teams of bearers in matching happi coats and headbands carry the poles on their shoulders, chanting calls such as "wasshoi" and deliberately jolting and swaying the shrine — a vigorous motion believed to amuse and pacify the deity and to shake blessings onto the community. Famous examples include the great mikoshi of Tokyo's Sanja Matsuri at Asakusa Shrine and the Kanda Matsuri.

History

Portable shrines are recorded in Japan from the Nara period; the first documented use is dated to 749 CE, when the deity Hachiman was carried from Usa to Nara. The practice became central to the great urban festivals of the Edo period.
