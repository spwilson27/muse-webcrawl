Art history

Art history is the study of artistic works in their historical development and stylistic contexts — that is, genre, design, format, and style. The study includes painting, sculpture, architecture, ceramics, furniture, and other decorative objects, as well as newer media such as photography, film, and digital art.

Development of the discipline

Giorgio Vasari's Lives of the Artists (1550) is often treated as the founding text of the discipline. Johann Joachim Winckelmann established systematic study of ancient art in the eighteenth century, and in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries figures such as Heinrich Wölfflin, Aby Warburg, and Erwin Panofsky developed methods including formalism, iconography, and iconology.

Scope

Art history traditionally concentrated on Western art from antiquity onward, periodised into movements such as Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, Neoclassicism, Romanticism, Impressionism, and Modernism. Since the late twentieth century the field has broadened to encompass global art traditions, feminist and postcolonial perspectives, and the social history of art.
