Kaiping (開平) is a county-level city in Guangdong Province, southern China, administered by the prefecture-level city of Jiangmen. Located west of the Pearl River Delta, it is best known internationally for the Kaiping Diaolou and Villages, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007.

Diaolou

The diaolou are fortified multi-storey watchtowers, some 1,800 of which survive in Kaiping and surrounding counties. Most were built in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with money sent home by overseas Chinese emigrants, many of whom had settled in North America. Combining Chinese and Western architectural features — including Greek columns, Roman domes, and Gothic arches rendered in reinforced concrete — the towers served as refuges against bandits and floods and as symbols of returning emigrants' success.

History and emigration

Kaiping was established as a county in 1649. Poverty, land pressure, and instability drove large-scale emigration from the mid-19th century onward, making the area part of the Siyi ("Four Counties") region whose diaspora strongly influenced Cantonese communities abroad. Remittances funded schools, roads, and the diaolou that define the local landscape today.
