Low-frequency effects

The low-frequency effects (LFE) channel is a dedicated audio track in surround sound formats that carries deep bass information, typically in the range of about 3 Hz to 120 Hz, and is reproduced by one or more subwoofers. It is the ".1" in notations such as 5.1 and 7.1.

Purpose

The LFE channel was introduced so that film soundtracks could deliver high-level bass effects — explosions, earthquakes, dinosaur footsteps — without overloading the main loudspeakers. Because the channel is recorded with an extra 10 dB of headroom, and bass management in a receiver redirects low frequencies from the main channels to the subwoofer, the LFE track is distinct from, though often confused with, the subwoofer feed itself.

History

Precursors include the Sensurround system of the 1970s, which used dedicated low-frequency generators for films such as Earthquake (1974). The modern LFE channel became standard with Dolby Digital on film and DVD, and is carried in formats including DTS, Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio. In a home theater, proper subwoofer placement and calibration determine how evenly the LFE content is heard across seats.
