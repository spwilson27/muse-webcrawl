A home theater is an audio-video system designed to reproduce the experience of a movie theater in the home, using a large display or projector, multichannel surround sound, and dedicated seating. The audio side of the system centers on an AV receiver driving loudspeakers arranged around the listener, plus one or more subwoofers.

The subwoofer's principal job is reproducing the low-frequency effects (LFE) channel — the ".1" track present in Dolby and DTS soundtracks — along with any deep bass redirected from the main speakers by bass management. Proper subwoofer placement and calibration are widely regarded as among the most consequential setup steps in a home theater, since room acoustics strongly affect low-frequency response.

Audio formats

Modern home theaters decode soundtracks in formats including Dolby Digital, Dolby TrueHD, DTS-HD Master Audio, and the object-based Dolby Atmos and DTS:X, all of which carry an LFE channel. Streaming services deliver surround sound primarily in Dolby Digital Plus, with or without Atmos metadata.
