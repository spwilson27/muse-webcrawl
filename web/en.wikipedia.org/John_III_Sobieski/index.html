John III Sobieski

John III Sobieski (Polish: Jan III Sobieski; 17 August 1629 – 17 June 1696) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1674 until his death. A brilliant military commander, he is famous for his victory over the Ottoman Empire at the Battle of Vienna in 1683.

Early career

Sobieski fought against the Cossacks, Sweden during the Deluge, and the Tatars and Turks. As Grand Crown Hetman he crushed the Ottomans at Khotyn (1673), a victory that carried him to election as king the following year.

Reign

His reign centred on the Ottoman war: the relief of Vienna (1683) made him a hero of Christendom — "Venimus, vidimus, Deus vicit" he wrote to the Pope. Poland joined the Holy League, but campaigns in Moldavia yielded little; Kamianets-Podilskyi was regained only after his death (Karlowitz, 1699). Domestically, magnate factions frustrated reform and his dynastic ambitions for his son James.

Legacy

Builder of Wilanów Palace, patron of scholars such as Hevelius (who named the constellation Scutum Sobiescianum for him), Sobieski exemplifies the late Commonwealth's martial glory.
