The Muppet Show

The Muppet Show is a variety sketch comedy television series created by Jim Henson and starring the Muppets. Produced in the United Kingdom, it ran for five seasons and 120 episodes from 1976 to 1981. The show is framed as a vaudeville-style theatre production hosted by Kermit the Frog, featuring musical numbers, sketches, and a celebrity guest star each week.

Format and characters

Kermit struggles to keep the chaotic show together backstage while acts unfold on stage. Regular characters include Fozzie Bear, the hapless stand-up comic; Miss Piggy, the porcine diva; Gonzo the Great; Rowlf the piano-playing dog; Dr. Teeth and the Electric Mayhem band; and Statler and Waldorf, the heckling old men in the balcony. Recurring sketches included "Pigs in Space", "The Swedish Chef", and "Veterinarian's Hospital".

Reception and legacy

The series was a worldwide success, broadcast in more than 100 countries and attracting guest stars such as Steve Martin, Elton John, Julie Andrews, and Rudolf Nureyev. It won Emmy, BAFTA, and Peabody awards, spawned the feature film The Muppet Movie (1979), and remains one of the most beloved television programmes of its era.
