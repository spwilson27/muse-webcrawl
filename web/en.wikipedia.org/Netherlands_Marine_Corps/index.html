The Netherlands Marine Corps (Dutch: Korps Mariniers) is the elite naval infantry corps of the Royal Netherlands Navy. Founded on 10 December 1665 by Grand Pensionary Johan de Witt and Admiral Michiel de Ruyter, it is one of the oldest marine corps in the world still in existence.

History

The corps distinguished itself during the Anglo-Dutch Wars, including the Raid on the Medway in 1667. In the Second World War, Dutch marines defended Rotterdam in May 1940, earning the nickname "Black Devils" from their German opponents. The corps has since served in Korea, Cambodia, Iraq, Afghanistan and anti-piracy operations off Somalia.

Organisation

The corps consists of two Marine Combat Groups, a maritime special operations force (NLMARSOF), a surface assault and training group, and supporting elements. It maintains a close relationship with the British Royal Marines through the UK/NL Landing Force.

Training

Marine training is conducted at the Van Ghent Barracks in Rotterdam and the Joost Dourleinkazerne on Texel, with arctic and mountain warfare training held in Norway.
