Allies of World War I

The Allies of World War I, or Entente Powers, were the coalition of countries led by France, the United Kingdom, Russia, Italy (from 1915), Japan, and the United States (from 1917) that fought against the Central Powers—Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria—during the First World War (1914–1918).

Formation

The core of the coalition grew out of the Triple Entente of France, Russia, and the United Kingdom, formalized by 1907 through the Franco-Russian Alliance, the Entente Cordiale, and the Anglo-Russian Convention. When war broke out in August 1914, Serbia and Belgium also became principal Allied belligerents following the Austro-Hungarian and German invasions.

Principal members

  • French Third Republic
  • British Empire (including Canada, Australia, India, New Zealand, and South Africa)
  • Russian Empire (until 1917)
  • Kingdom of Italy (from 1915)
  • Empire of Japan
  • United States (from 1917)
  • Kingdom of Serbia, Belgium, Romania (from 1916), Greece (from 1917), and others

War effort and outcome

The Allies coordinated strategy through conferences and supreme war councils, and their combined naval blockade and manpower eventually overwhelmed the Central Powers. The armistice of 11 November 1918 ended the fighting, and the Allied powers dictated the terms of the Treaty of Versailles in 1919.
