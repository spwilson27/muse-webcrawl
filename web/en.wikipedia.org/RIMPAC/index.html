RIMPAC

Rim of the Pacific Exercise
Type: Multinational naval exercise
Host: United States Navy (Third Fleet)
First held: 1971
Frequency: Biennial

The Rim of the Pacific Exercise (RIMPAC) is the world's largest international maritime warfare exercise. It is hosted and administered by the United States Navy's Third Fleet, headquartered at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii, and is held biennially during June and July of even-numbered years.

History

RIMPAC was first held in 1971 with the navies of the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia. It expanded steadily, and recent iterations have involved more than two dozen nations. China was invited and participated in 2014 and 2016 but was disinvited in 2018; Russia participated once, in 2012.

Activities

The exercise includes anti-submarine warfare, amphibious operations, live-fire missile and gunnery exercises, maritime interdiction, and disaster-relief training. A notable feature is the SINKEX, in which decommissioned vessels are sunk as targets. RIMPAC aims to promote stability in the Pacific region and strengthen interoperability among partner navies.
