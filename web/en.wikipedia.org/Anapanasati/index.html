Anapanasati

Ānāpānasati (Pali; Sanskrit: ānāpānasmṛti), meaning "mindfulness of breathing", is a core meditation practice in Buddhism. It involves attending to the sensations of the breath as a means of developing concentration (samādhi) and insight (vipassanā). The practice is described in the Ānāpānasati Sutta (Majjhima Nikāya 118).

The sixteen steps

The sutta sets out sixteen steps of practice arranged in four tetrads, correlated with the four foundations of mindfulness: contemplation of the body, feelings, mind, and mental phenomena. The meditator begins by knowing whether a breath is long or short, proceeds to experiencing the whole body and calming bodily formations, and advances through mindfulness of feelings and mind to contemplation of impermanence and letting go.

Role in Buddhist traditions

Ānāpānasati is practiced across Theravāda, Mahāyāna, and Tibetan traditions. In the Theravāda commentarial tradition, systematized in the Visuddhimagga, it is considered a meditation subject suitable for all temperaments and capable of leading to all four jhānas and to insight. Modern teachers have widely popularized breath mindfulness as an entry point to meditation.
