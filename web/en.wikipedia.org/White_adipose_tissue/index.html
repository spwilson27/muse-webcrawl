White adipose tissue

White adipose tissue (WAT) or white fat is one of the two types of adipose tissue found in mammals, the other being brown adipose tissue (BAT). In healthy, non-overweight humans, white adipose tissue makes up roughly 20% of the body weight of men and 25% of the body weight of women. Its cells contain a single large lipid droplet, which forces the nucleus to be squeezed into a thin rim at the periphery.

Structure and location

White adipose tissue is found beneath the skin (subcutaneous fat), around internal organs (visceral fat), in the yellow marrow of bones, and in the breast. The cells, called white adipocytes, are unilocular: they store energy mainly in the form of triglycerides within one large droplet. The tissue is highly vascularised and innervated, and it contains not only adipocytes but also preadipocytes, macrophages, endothelial cells, and fibroblasts in its stromal vascular fraction.

Function

The primary role of white adipose tissue is energy storage: it accumulates triglycerides when energy intake exceeds expenditure and releases fatty acids through lipolysis when energy is needed. It also provides mechanical cushioning, thermal insulation, and protection for internal organs.

Endocrine function

White adipose tissue is now recognised as an active endocrine organ. It secretes hormones and signalling molecules collectively termed adipokines, including leptin, which signals satiety to the brain; adiponectin, which modulates insulin sensitivity; resistin; and various inflammatory cytokines such as tumour necrosis factor alpha. Dysfunction of this endocrine activity is implicated in obesity-related metabolic diseases, including type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

Browning

Under certain stimuli, such as cold exposure or specific hormonal signals, some white adipocytes can acquire characteristics of brown adipocytes, a process called "browning" or "beiging." These beige adipocytes express uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1) and burn energy as heat, a phenomenon of considerable research interest for obesity therapy.
