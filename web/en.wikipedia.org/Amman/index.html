Amman (Arabic: عَمَّان, ʿAmmān) is the capital and the largest city of Jordan, and the country's economic, political, and cultural centre. With a population of over four million, Amman is Jordan's primate city and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with evidence of settlement dating back to the Neolithic period around 7250 BC at the site of ʿAin Ghazal.

History

In the Iron Age the city was known as Rabbath Ammon, capital of the Ammonite kingdom. In the Hellenistic period Ptolemy II Philadelphus rebuilt it as Philadelphia, a name it kept through the Roman and Byzantine eras, when it was a member of the Decapolis. After the Muslim conquest it reverted to the name Amman. The city declined in the later medieval period and was largely a small village until Circassian settlers arrived in 1878; it grew rapidly after being chosen as the capital of Transjordan in 1921.

Geography

Amman is situated in north-western Jordan on a hilly area originally spread over seven hills (jabals), and now spanning some nineteen hills, at elevations between 700 and 1,100 metres. It has a Mediterranean-influenced climate with hot, dry summers and cool, sometimes snowy winters.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Citadel (Jabal al-Qalʿa) with the Temple of Hercules and the Umayyad Palace, the well-preserved Roman Theatre seating about 6,000, the King Abdullah I Mosque, and the Jordan Museum, home to the Dead Sea Scrolls' Copper Scroll and the ʿAin Ghazal statues.

Economy and transport

Amman is a regional hub for banking, finance, healthcare, education and information technology, and hosts many multinational companies' Levant offices. It is served by Queen Alia International Airport, south of the city, and by the Amman Bus and Bus Rapid Transit services.
