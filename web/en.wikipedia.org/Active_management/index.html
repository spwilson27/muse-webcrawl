Active management

Active management (also called active investing) is an approach to investment management in which a manager or team makes specific investments with the goal of outperforming an investment benchmark index or target return. In passive management, investors expect a return mirroring a market index and do not attempt to outperform it.

Approach

An active manager may seek to beat the market through individual stock selection, sector allocation, market timing, or a combination of strategies, supported by fundamental analysis, technical analysis, and quantitative models. Active management involves taking risks that differ from the benchmark, often measured by tracking error or active share.

Criticism and evidence

Studies of fund performance, such as the SPIVA scorecards, have repeatedly found that a majority of actively managed funds underperform their benchmarks over long horizons, after fees. Supporters argue that skilled managers, less efficient markets, and risk management during downturns can justify active strategies.

Fees

Actively managed funds typically charge higher management fees than index funds, reflecting the cost of research and trading. Performance fees may also apply in hedge funds and some institutional mandates.
