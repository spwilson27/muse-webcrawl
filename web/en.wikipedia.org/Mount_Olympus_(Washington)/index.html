Mount Olympus (Washington)

Elevation7,980 ft (2,432 m)
Prominence7,838 ft (2,389 m)
LocationJefferson County, Washington, US
RangeOlympic Mountains
First ascent1907

Mount Olympus is the tallest and most prominent mountain in the Olympic Mountains of western Washington state and the central feature of Olympic National Park. Despite a modest elevation of 7,980 feet (2,432 m), it rises from near sea level and intercepts moisture-laden Pacific air, receiving enormous snowfall that sustains the largest glacier system in the contiguous United States outside the Cascade volcanoes.

Glaciers

Eight named glaciers cloak the summit massif, including the Hoh Glacier, over 4 kilometres long, the Blue, White, Hubert and Jeffers glaciers. These are among the southernmost glaciers in North America at their latitude to descend to such low elevations — some reach down into temperate rainforest near 1,000 metres. Like most glaciers in the region they retreated markedly through the twentieth and early twenty-first centuries.

History

The mountain was named in 1788 by the British fur trader John Meares, who judged it a fit home for the Greek gods. The first recorded ascent came in 1907, by a party of The Mountaineers led by L. A. Nelson, after earlier expeditions — including the Seattle Press Expedition of 1889–90, which crossed the interior and reported the mountain — had failed to climb it. Approaches are long: the standard route follows the Hoh River Trail some 27 kilometres through rainforest before reaching the glacier.
