Sam Manekshaw

Sam Hormusji Framji Jamshedji Manekshaw (3 April 1914 – 27 June 2008), widely known as Sam Bahadur ("Sam the Brave"), was an Indian military leader who served as Chief of the Army Staff of the Indian Army during the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971. He was the first Indian Army officer to be promoted to the rank of field marshal.

Career

Born into a Parsi family, Manekshaw was among the first cadets trained at the Indian Military Academy, Dehradun. He served in Burma during the Second World War, winning the Military Cross for gallantry after being grievously wounded at the Sittang River. After Partition he was reassigned to the 8th Gorkha Rifles and held senior staff and command appointments, including during the 1962 Sino-Indian War and the 1965 war with Pakistan.

The 1971 war

As army chief, Manekshaw resisted pressure to intervene prematurely in East Pakistan in April 1971, insisting on time to prepare; when war came in December, India's rapid campaign under his leadership ended in thirteen days with the surrender of over 90,000 Pakistani troops in Dhaka and the creation of Bangladesh. He was awarded the Padma Vibhushan and promoted to field marshal in January 1973. Famous for his wit, candour and devotion to his soldiers, he remains one of modern India's most admired military figures.
