Jadeite

CategoryPyroxene mineral
FormulaNaAlSi2O6
Hardness6.5–7 (Mohs)
ColourApple-green, emerald-green, white, lavender

Jadeite is a pyroxene mineral with composition NaAlSi2O6. It is one of the two minerals commonly called jade, the other being nephrite. Jadeite is the rarer and more valuable of the two, prized especially in its translucent emerald-green form known as Imperial jade.

Properties

Jadeite forms in high-pressure, low-temperature metamorphic rocks associated with subduction zones. It has a Mohs hardness of 6.5 to 7 and a dense, interlocking crystal structure that makes it exceptionally tough and suitable for carving.

Sources

The most important source is the jade tract of Kachin State in northern Myanmar (Burma), around Hpakan. Other deposits occur in Guatemala — the source of the jade used by the Maya and other Mesoamerican cultures — as well as in Japan, Russia, and California.

Cultural significance

Jadeite was carved by Olmec, Maya, and Aztec craftspeople for masks, ornaments, and ritual objects, and has long been treasured in China, where Burmese jadeite became fashionable at the Qing court in the 18th century.
