Al-Tabari

Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari
Born839 CE, Amol, Tabaristan (now Iran)
Died923 CE, Baghdad
FieldsHistory, Qur'anic exegesis (tafsir), jurisprudence
Notable worksHistory of the Prophets and Kings; Tafsir al-Tabari

Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn Jarīr ibn Yazīd al-Ṭabarī (839–923 CE) was a Persian historian and scholar from Amol, Tabaristan, who lived and worked in Baghdad under the Abbasid Caliphate. He is best known for two monumental works: his universal history and his commentary on the Qur'an, both of which remain foundational sources.

Tafsir al-Tabari

His Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān, commonly called the Tafsir al-Tabari, is the earliest major Qur'an commentary to survive in full. It compiles earlier interpretations with their chains of transmission (isnads) together with al-Tabari's own linguistic and theological analysis, and it shaped the Sunni exegetical tradition.

History of the Prophets and Kings

The Taʾrīkh al-rusul wa-l-mulūk ("History of the Prophets and Kings") is a universal history from Creation to 915 CE. Its volumes on the early Islamic community, the Umayyad and early Abbasid caliphates are an indispensable primary source, preserving many otherwise lost reports. A Persian abridgement by Bal'ami spread its influence widely.

Other work

Al-Tabari also wrote on Islamic jurisprudence, founding a short-lived legal school (the Jariri school). He declined official appointments and supported himself by his family's estate, teaching in Baghdad until his death in 923.
