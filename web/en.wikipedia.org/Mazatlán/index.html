Mazatlán

Mazatlán is a city in the Mexican state of Sinaloa, on the Pacific coast at the entrance to the Gulf of California. It is a major port, resort, and commercial fishing centre, famous for shrimp production and for having one of Mexico's largest carnivals.

History

Founded in the 1530s near an Indigenous settlement whose Nahuatl name means "place of deer," Mazatlán grew as a port exporting gold and silver from the Sierra Madre mines. In the 19th century it resisted French intervention forces, and German immigrants helped develop its brewing and commerce.

Tourism

Attractions include the historic old town (Centro Histórico), the Ángela Peralta theatre, the long malecón seawall, the Zona Dorada hotel district, and the El Faro lighthouse, among the tallest natural lighthouses in the world.
