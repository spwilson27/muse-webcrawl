Dalai Lama is a title given by the Tibetan people to the foremost spiritual leader of the Gelug (or "Yellow Hat") school of Tibetan Buddhism, the newest and most dominant of the four major schools. The 14th and incumbent Dalai Lama is Tenzin Gyatso, who has lived in exile in India since 1959. The Dalai Lamas are believed by Tibetan Buddhists to be manifestations of Avalokiteśvara, the bodhisattva of compassion, and are members of a lineage of tulkus — teachers reborn to continue their work.

History of the title

The title "Dalai Lama" was first bestowed in 1578 by the Mongol ruler Altan Khan on Sonam Gyatso, the third holder of the lineage, who is thus counted as the 3rd Dalai Lama; "Dalai" is the Mongolian translation of the Tibetan "Gyatso," meaning "ocean" (of wisdom). The 5th Dalai Lama, Ngawang Lobzang Gyatso (1617–1682), unified Tibet with Mongol support and established the Ganden Phodrang government, making the Dalai Lamas both spiritual and temporal rulers of Tibet from Lhasa's Potala Palace.

The 14th Dalai Lama

Tenzin Gyatso was born Lhamo Thondup in 1935 in Taktser, Amdo, and was recognized as the reincarnation of the 13th Dalai Lama at the age of two. He assumed political power in 1950, the year the People's Republic of China asserted control over Tibet. After the failed 1959 Tibetan uprising, he fled to India, where he established the Central Tibetan Administration in Dharamsala. He received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1989 for his non-violent advocacy of "genuine autonomy" for Tibet under the "Middle Way Approach." In 2011 he devolved his political authority to an elected leadership. The question of his succession — and Chinese government claims to control it — remains a major point of international contention.

Succession

Traditionally, after a Dalai Lama's death, senior lamas search for his reincarnation guided by visions, dreams, and tests in which a child identifies the previous Lama's possessions. The 14th Dalai Lama has stated that the institution may end with him, or that his successor may be found outside Tibet, or even be an adult or a woman — statements Beijing rejects.
