Rules of golf

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The rules of golf consist of a standard set of regulations and procedures by which the sport of golf should be played. They are jointly written and maintained by the R&A (formerly part of the Royal and Ancient Golf Club of St Andrews) and the United States Golf Association (USGA). The fundamental principle, stated in Rule 1, is that golf is to be played by striking the ball with a club from the teeing area into the hole by a stroke or strokes in succession — the player must "play the ball as it lies" and "play the course as you find it".

History

The earliest surviving rules were drawn up in 1744 by the Gentlemen Golfers of Leith, Scotland — thirteen articles covering matters such as teeing the ball within a club-length of the hole and forbidding the removal of "stones, bones or any break club" except on the green. The Royal and Ancient Golf Club issued its own code in 1754, and a single worldwide code was agreed in 1899. A major revision took effect in 2019, streamlining 34 rules into 24, shortening search time, permitting repair of spike marks on greens, and dropping several penalties; a further revision followed in 2023.

Main principles

The rules govern starting the hole, playing the ball, the putting green, relief procedures for balls in penalty areas, bunkers, and unplayable positions, and lost or out-of-bounds balls (generally stroke and distance). Breaches incur penalty strokes or, in match play, loss of hole; serious breaches or cheating lead to disqualification. A separate code of etiquette and the World Handicap System support fair competition, and local rules adapt the framework to particular courses.
