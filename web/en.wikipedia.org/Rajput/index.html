Rajput

Rajput (from Sanskrit rāja-putra, "son of a king") is a term for a large cluster of castes, clans and local groups in northern and western India historically associated with warriorhood, landholding and rulership. From the medieval period onward, Rajput clans founded numerous kingdoms in present-day Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh and neighbouring regions, and Rajput identity became linked to ideals of martial honour, loyalty and chivalry.

Origins

Historians debate the origins of the Rajputs. Between roughly the sixth and twelfth centuries, various groups — including local chiefs, pastoral and tribal communities, and descendants of earlier ruling lineages — were integrated into an emerging Rajput status through processes sometimes called "Rajputisation". Medieval bardic traditions later constructed elaborate genealogies, including the Agnikula myth of clans born from a fire sacrifice on Mount Abu, and claims of descent from the solar and lunar dynasties of epic kings.

Medieval kingdoms

Prominent Rajput dynasties included the Gurjara-Pratiharas, Chahamanas (Chauhans), Paramaras, Solankis (Chaulukyas), Guhilas of Mewar and Kachhwahas of Amber. Rajput rulers resisted, negotiated with, and served successively the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire; under Akbar, marriage alliances and imperial service bound many Rajput houses to the Mughal court, while Mewar under Maharana Pratap became a symbol of resistance. Rajput courts patronised distinctive painting schools, fortress architecture (as at Chittorgarh, Jaisalmer and Mehrangarh) and heroic literature.

Later history

During the Maratha ascendancy and then British rule, Rajput states became princely states of Rajputana and other agencies. After 1947 they were integrated into the Indian Union, forming much of the state of Rajasthan. Rajputs remain socially and politically significant in several northern states, and Rajput regiments are a noted part of the Indian Army.

Clans

Major clans traditionally enumerated include the Chauhan, Rathore, Sisodia, Kachhwaha, Parmar, Solanki, Tomar, Chandela and Bundela, among many others, organised by exogamous gotras and clan deities (kuldevi).
