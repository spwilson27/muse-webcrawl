Abbey of Gethsemani

Abbey of Our Lady of Gethsemani
Order: Trappists (OCSO)
Founded: 1848
Location: Nelson County, Kentucky, U.S.

The Abbey of Our Lady of Gethsemani is a Catholic monastery near Bardstown, Kentucky, in Nelson County. It is a member of the Order of Cistercians of the Strict Observance (Trappists). Founded in 1848 by monks from Melleray Abbey in France, it is the oldest monastery in the United States still operating on its original site.

Thomas Merton

Gethsemani is best known as the home of the monk and writer Thomas Merton, who entered the abbey in 1941 and lived there until his death in 1968. Merton's autobiography The Seven Storey Mountain (1948), which describes his conversion and entry into the monastery, brought Gethsemani international fame and a wave of new vocations.

Life and products

The monks follow the Rule of Saint Benedict, dividing their day between prayer, reading, and manual labour. The abbey supports itself partly through sales of fruitcakes, fudge, and cheese made by the monks.
