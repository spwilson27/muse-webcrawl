Kutta–Joukowski theorem

The Kutta–Joukowski theorem is a fundamental theorem in aerodynamics, named after the German Martin Kutta and the Russian Nikolai Joukowski (Zhukovsky), who developed its key ideas in the early 20th century. It relates the lift generated by a two-dimensional body in a moving fluid to the speed of the body, the density of the fluid, and the circulation around the body.

Statement

For a steady, incompressible, inviscid flow, the lift per unit span L′ equals the fluid density ρ times the flow velocity V times the circulation Γ, with sign convention L′ = −ρVΓ. The theorem underlies the explanation of how an airfoil generates lift through circulation, in combination with the Kutta condition, which fixes the circulation by requiring smooth flow off the trailing edge.

Applications

The theorem is a cornerstone of classical airfoil theory and is used to derive properties of the lift of wings and rotating cylinders (the Magnus effect), though real flows require corrections for viscosity and three-dimensional effects such as induced drag.
