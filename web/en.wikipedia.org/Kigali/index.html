Kigali is the capital and largest city of Rwanda. Situated near the geographic centre of the country among rolling hills, it is Rwanda's political, economic, and transport hub, home to roughly 1.7 million people in the City of Kigali province.

History

Kigali was founded in 1907 by the German colonial administrator Richard Kandt as a small administrative outpost and trading post, chosen for its central location and healthy hilltop setting. It grew slowly under Belgian rule and became the capital when Rwanda gained independence in 1962. In April 1994 the city was the starting point of the Rwandan genocide, in which some 800,000 people, mostly Tutsi, were murdered nationwide in about 100 days; the Kigali Genocide Memorial at Gisozi, burial place of some 250,000 victims, commemorates the atrocity.

Geography and cityscape

The city sprawls across several hills and valleys at an altitude of around 1,500–1,600 metres, giving it a mild climate despite its equatorial location. Districts include the commercial centre around Kigali City Tower, the government quarter of Kacyiru, and suburbs spreading outward as the population grows rapidly.

Economy

Kigali is noted for order, cleanliness — plastic bags are banned nationwide and a monthly community clean-up, Umuganda, is held — and an ambition to become a regional hub for conferences, technology, and finance, symbolised by the Kigali Convention Centre (opened 2016). Kigali International Airport serves the city, with a larger airport under construction at Bugesera.
