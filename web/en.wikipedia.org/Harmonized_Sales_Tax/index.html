Harmonized Sales Tax

The Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) is a consumption tax in Canada that combines the federal Goods and Services Tax (GST) with a provincial sales tax into a single value-added tax administered by the Canada Revenue Agency. It applies in several provinces, with rates varying by province.

Harmonized Sales Tax
Country: Canada
Type: Value-added consumption tax
Administered by: Canada Revenue Agency
Federal component: 5% GST

History

The HST was first adopted in 1997 by New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, and Newfoundland and Labrador. Ontario and British Columbia harmonized in 2010; British Columbia later reverted to a separate provincial sales tax in 2013 following a referendum. Prince Edward Island adopted the HST in 2013.

Rates and operation

Combined rates have ranged from 13% to 15%, depending on the provincial portion. Businesses generally collect the tax on taxable supplies and claim input tax credits for tax paid on their purchases, so the burden falls on the final consumer. Certain goods and services, such as basic groceries and prescription drugs, are zero-rated or exempt.
