South India

South India
States: Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, plus Puducherry
Area: about 635,000 km²
Population: about 250 million
Largest city: Bengaluru / Chennai

South India, also known as Peninsular India, is the southern part of India comprising the states of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Telangana, and the union territories of Puducherry and Lakshadweep. Geographically it is the peninsular plateau south of the Vindhya range and the Narmada River, bounded by the Arabian Sea, the Indian Ocean and the Bay of Bengal.

Geography

The region is dominated by the triangular Deccan Plateau, flanked by the Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats mountain ranges, between which flow eastward rivers — the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri — that sustain intensive rice agriculture. The climate ranges from tropical wet in Kerala and the Konkan-influenced coast to semi-arid in the interior rain shadow.

History

South India has a distinct history shaped by kingdoms such as the Satavahanas, the Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas, and the Tamil dynasties of the Cholas, Cheras and Pandyas; the Chola Empire projected power across the Bay of Bengal to Southeast Asia. The Vijayanagara Empire resisted the Deccan sultanates in the 14th–16th centuries. The region traded with Rome, Arabia and China for millennia in spices, textiles and gems.

Languages and culture

The principal languages belong to the Dravidian family — Telugu, Tamil, Kannada and Malayalam — each with its own script and classical literature; Tamil's Sangam literature dates back over two thousand years. Cultural hallmarks include Carnatic music, Bharatanatyam dance, temple architecture with towering gopurams, banana-leaf meals centred on rice, dosa and sambar, and major film industries in Chennai, Hyderabad, Bengaluru and Kochi.

Economy

South India is among India's most industrialised and urbanised regions, known for information-technology hubs in Bengaluru and Hyderabad, automobile manufacturing around Chennai, textiles in Coimbatore and Tiruppur, and high literacy in Kerala, which also pioneered a distinctive development model based on education, health and remittances from Gulf workers.
