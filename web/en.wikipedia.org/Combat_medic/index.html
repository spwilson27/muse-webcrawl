Combat medic

A combat medic is a military medical specialist responsible for providing emergency medical treatment at the point of injury on the battlefield, primary care, and health protection for deployed forces. In the US Army the role is Military Occupational Specialty 68W; Navy hospital corpsmen serve with Marine units, and Air Force pararescuemen perform combat rescue.

Role

Medics practise Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC): controlling catastrophic bleeding with tourniquets, airway management, treating tension pneumothorax, and preparing casualties for evacuation. Their work under fire, in the 'golden hour', has greatly improved survival from wounds.

History

Organised battlefield medicine developed through the Napoleonic Wars (Larrey's flying ambulances), the American Civil War (Jonathan Letterman), and the world wars, where medics such as Desmond Doss became celebrated. Under the Geneva Conventions, medical personnel displaying the red cross are protected non-combatants, though modern medics often carry weapons for self-defence.

Training

Training combines civilian EMT certification with advanced trauma skills, prolonged field care, and preventive medicine, with continuing specialisation into flight medic, special operations, and practical nursing roles.
