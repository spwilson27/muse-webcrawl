Karl Stein (mathematician)

Karl Stein (1 January 1913 – 19 October 2000) was a German mathematician known for his foundational work in complex analysis of several variables. The class of complex manifolds called Stein manifolds is named after him, and he made important contributions to the theory of automorphic functions and the Cousin problems.

Life

Stein studied mathematics at the University of Münster, where he received his doctorate in 1937 under Heinrich Behnke, with a dissertation on automorphic functions. He habilitated in 1940 and spent much of his career at Münster before moving to the University of Munich, where he held a chair until his retirement. He trained several generations of German complex analysts.

Work

In 1951 Stein introduced the class of complex manifolds now called Stein manifolds, defining them by the properties that holomorphic functions separate points and that the manifold is holomorphically convex; they became the central objects of Stein theory, the higher-dimensional analogue of the theory of open Riemann surfaces. With Henri Cartan he developed the notion of the Stein factorisation of a proper holomorphic map, separating the connected-fibre and finite parts of a mapping. He also worked on the Levi problem, Cousin integrals and the theory of analytic polyhedra in several complex variables.

Recognition

Stein was elected to the Bavarian Academy of Sciences and received honours for his role in rebuilding German mathematics after the Second World War. The term "Stein manifold" is standard in every modern textbook on several complex variables.
