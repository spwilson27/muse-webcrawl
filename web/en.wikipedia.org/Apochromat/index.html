Apochromat

An apochromat, or apochromatic lens, is a photographic or telescopic lens corrected to bring three wavelengths of light (typically red, green, and blue) to a common focus, greatly reducing chromatic aberration compared with a simple achromat, which corrects two wavelengths.

Design

Apochromatic correction requires special optical materials with anomalous partial dispersion, such as fluorite or extra-low-dispersion (ED) glass, combined in doublets or triplets. The term derives from the Greek apo ("away from") plus chroma ("colour"), meaning free of colour error.

Applications

Apochromats are used in high-quality telescopes, microscope objectives, and process (reproduction) lenses where colour fidelity and sharpness are critical. In amateur astronomy, "APO" refractors command premium prices. Note that manufacturers sometimes use the label loosely for lenses with merely good colour correction.
