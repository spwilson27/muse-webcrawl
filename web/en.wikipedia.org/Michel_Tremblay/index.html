Michel Tremblay

Michel Tremblay (born June 25, 1942) is a French-Canadian novelist and playwright, regarded as one of the most significant writers in Quebec literature. His work gave literary voice to working-class Montrealers, notably through his use of joual, the popular Quebec French dialect, on the stage.

Early life

Tremblay was born in Montreal and grew up in the working-class Plateau-Mont-Royal district. He worked as a Linotype operator before winning a competition that launched his writing career.

Theatre

His breakthrough play, Les Belles-sœurs (1968), about fifteen working-class women pasting trading stamps, shocked audiences with its joual dialogue and became a landmark of Quebec theatre. Other plays include À toi, pour toujours, ta Marie-Lou (1971), Hosanna (1973), Albertine en cinq temps (1984) and Encore une fois, si vous le permettez (1998). His recurring characters form an interconnected dramatic universe spanning decades.

Fiction

Tremblay's six-volume Chroniques du Plateau-Mont-Royal (1978–1997), beginning with La grosse femme d'à côté est enceinte, chronicles the Montreal neighborhood of his childhood. He has also written autobiographical works and the later La Diaspora des Desrosiers series.

Recognition

Tremblay has received numerous honors, including the Prix Molson and appointment as a Chevalier of the Ordre national du Québec. His works have been translated into many languages and performed internationally.
