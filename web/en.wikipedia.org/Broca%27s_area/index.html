Broca's area

Part ofFrontal lobe
LocationInferior frontal gyrus (Brodmann areas 44 and 45)
FunctionSpeech production, language processing
Named afterPaul Broca

Broca's area is a region in the frontal lobe of the dominant hemisphere, usually the left, of the brain with functions linked to speech production. It comprises parts of the inferior frontal gyrus known as Brodmann areas 44 (pars opercularis) and 45 (pars triangularis). Language processing has been linked to Broca's area since Paul Broca reported impairments in two patients who had lost the ability to speak after injury to the region in the 1860s.

Discovery

In 1861, the French surgeon Paul Broca examined a patient nicknamed "Tan", who could understand language but could utter little more than the syllable "tan". After the patient's death, Broca found a lesion in the left frontal lobe, and similar findings in further cases led him to conclude that this region was the seat of articulated language.

Function

Broca's area is involved in the production of speech, including the planning of articulatory movements, as well as aspects of language comprehension, particularly the processing of complex syntax. It works in concert with Wernicke's area, to which it is connected by the arcuate fasciculus.

Clinical significance

Damage to Broca's area, typically from stroke, produces Broca's aphasia (expressive aphasia), characterized by slow, halting, effortful speech with relatively preserved comprehension. Modern neuroimaging has shown that language functions are distributed across a broader network than the classical Broca–Wernicke model suggested.
