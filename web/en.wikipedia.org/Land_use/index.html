Land use

Land use
CategoriesAgricultural, residential, commercial, industrial, recreational, transport
Related fieldLand-use planning, zoning

Land use describes the human use of land — the management and modification of the natural environment into built environments such as settlements and semi-natural habitats such as fields, pastures, and managed forests. It is distinguished from land cover, which describes the physical surface (forest, water, pavement) regardless of use.

Regulation

Governments regulate land use through planning systems and zoning ordinances that separate incompatible uses — for example, keeping heavy industry away from housing — and through instruments such as comprehensive plans, subdivision rules, and environmental review. In the United States, municipal zoning was upheld in Village of Euclid v. Ambler Realty (1926); in England, comprehensive planning dates from the Town and Country Planning Act 1947.

Global patterns

Agriculture occupies roughly half of the world's habitable land, while urban areas, though small in extent, house most of humanity. Land-use change — deforestation, urbanisation, and intensification — is a major driver of biodiversity loss and greenhouse-gas emissions, and a central topic in sustainability science.
