William Fox (producer)

William Fox
Born: Wilhelm Fried, 1 January 1879, Tolcsva, Austria-Hungary
Died: 8 May 1952, New York City
Known for: Founder of Fox Film Corporation

William Fox (born Wilhelm Fried; 1 January 1879 – 8 May 1952) was a Hungarian-American film industry executive who founded the Fox Film Corporation in 1915 and the Fox West Coast Theatres chain in the 1920s. His companies' merger with Twentieth Century Pictures in 1935 produced 20th Century Fox, and his name survives in the Fox media empire.

Early life

Born to German-Jewish parents in Tolcsva, Hungary, Fried emigrated with his family to New York as an infant. His father changed the family name to Fox. William worked as a newsboy and in the fur and garment trades before buying a Brooklyn nickelodeon in 1904, the start of a growing chain of theatres.

Fox Film

After successfully fighting Thomas Edison's Motion Picture Patents Company in court — a campaign that helped break the Trust's monopoly — Fox moved into production, founding Fox Film in 1915. The studio built stars such as Theda Bara, promoted the "vamp" persona, and later produced prestige pictures including Sunrise (1927). Fox pioneered sound-on-film technology with the Movietone system, buying up theatre chains and the Loew's theatre interests in an ambitious expansion.

Fall

The 1929 stock market crash, a serious car accident, and antitrust pressure destroyed Fox's finances. He lost control of his companies in 1930 and was declared bankrupt in 1936. During his bankruptcy proceedings he attempted to bribe a judge and obstruct justice, for which he served six months in prison in 1943. He spent his remaining years in obscurity, and none of the Fox companies that bear his name retained any family involvement.
