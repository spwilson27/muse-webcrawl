Yaa Asantewaa

Yaa Asantewaa I (c. 1840 – 17 October 1921) was the queen mother of Ejisu in the Ashanti Empire, in present-day Ghana. In 1900 she led the Ashanti war known as the War of the Golden Stool, the last of the Anglo-Ashanti wars fought to resist British colonial rule.

Background

Yaa Asantewaa was appointed queen mother (Ejisuhemaa) by her brother Nana Akwasi Afrane Okese, chief of Ejisu. The queen mother in Akan matrilineal society is a powerful figure, guardian of genealogy and adviser to the chief, and a kingmaker who nominates candidates for the stool.

After the Fourth Anglo-Ashanti War (1896), the British exiled Asantehene Prempeh I to the Seychelles and demanded the Golden Stool (Sika dwa Kofi), the sacred symbol of the Ashanti nation's soul, which no one — not even the king — sat upon. At a meeting in Kumasi in March 1900, Governor Sir Frederick Hodgson demanded to sit on the stool. Yaa Asantewaa, seeing the chiefs hesitate, made her famous speech: if the men of Ashanti would not fight, the women would — "I shall call upon my fellow women. We will fight till the last of us falls in the battlefields."

War and exile

The war began with an Ashanti blockade of the British fort at Kumasi. Yaa Asantewaa served as commander-in-chief and strategist of the Ejisu forces. After months of fighting, British reinforcements broke the resistance. Yaa Asantewaa was captured and exiled to the Seychelles, where she died in 1921. Her remains were returned to Kumasi in 1924. Prempeh I was allowed to return the same year.

Legacy

Yaa Asantewaa is a national heroine of Ghana, celebrated in song ("Yaa Asantewaa, the woman who fought the white men"), in the naming of schools such as Yaa Asantewaa Girls' Senior High School in Kumasi, and as a symbol of anti-colonial resistance and women's leadership.
