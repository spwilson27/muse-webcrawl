Daytona Prototype International

Daytona Prototype International (DPi) was a class of sports prototype racing cars that served as the top category of the IMSA SportsCar Championship from 2017 to 2022. Developed by IMSA as the successor to the Daytona Prototypes, DPi cars were based on the international LMP2 chassis but carried manufacturer-specific engines and bodywork, allowing marques such as Cadillac, Acura, and Mazda to compete for overall victories in North American endurance racing.

Formula

Under the DPi rules, teams used one of four approved LMP2 chassis (from Dallara, Ligier, Multimatic, and Oreca) fitted with a manufacturer's own engine and stylized bodywork evoking its road cars. Performance between entrants was controlled by IMSA's Balance of Performance adjustments. Engines included Cadillac's pushrod V8, Acura's twin-turbo V6, and Mazda's turbocharged four-cylinder.

History

The class debuted at the 2017 Rolex 24 at Daytona, where Wayne Taylor Racing's Cadillac DPi-V.R won — the Cadillac proved dominant, taking the manufacturers' title in its first seasons. Acura's Team Penske program and Mazda's Joest and Multimatic entries challenged in later years. DPi was replaced in 2023 by the LMDh-based GTP class, part of the convergence between IMSA and the FIA World Endurance Championship.
