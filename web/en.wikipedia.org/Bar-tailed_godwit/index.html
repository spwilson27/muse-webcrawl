Bar-tailed godwit

Bar-tailed godwit (Limosa lapponica)
Family: Scolopacidae
Conservation status: Near Threatened (IUCN)
Known for: longest non-stop migration of any bird

The bar-tailed godwit (Limosa lapponica) is a large and strongly migratory wader in the family Scolopacidae, which breeds on Arctic coasts and tundra from Scandinavia to Alaska and winters on coasts in temperate and tropical regions of the Old World, Australia and New Zealand.

Description

It has a distinctive upturned, bicoloured bill, and in breeding plumage males show rich chestnut underparts; females are larger and duller. Its name refers to the black-and-white barring on the tail, in contrast to the black-tailed godwit's plain black tail.

Migration

The species performs some of the longest recorded non-stop flights of any bird: satellite-tracked individuals have flown more than 13,000 km across the Pacific Ocean from Alaska to New Zealand and (in 2022) a juvenile was recorded flying about 13,560 km to Tasmania without stopping. Birds fatten substantially before departure, shrinking digestive organs to make room for fat stores.
