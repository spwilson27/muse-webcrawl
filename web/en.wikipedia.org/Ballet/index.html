Ballet

Ballet
Type: Performing art / dance
Origin: Italian Renaissance courts, 15th century
Key forms: Classical, neoclassical, contemporary

Ballet is a type of performance dance that originated in the Italian Renaissance courts of the 15th century and was further developed in France and Russia as a concert dance form. It is characterised by a codified vocabulary of steps and positions, precise technique, and expressive, often narrative, choreography performed to orchestral music.

History

Catherine de' Medici brought court ballet to France, where Louis XIV founded the Académie Royale de Danse in 1661 and himself danced as the Sun King. In the 19th century, Romantic ballet produced works such as Giselle and introduced dancing en pointe. Imperial Russia developed classical ballet to its peak with choreographer Marius Petipa and composer Tchaikovsky — creator of Swan Lake, The Nutcracker and The Sleeping Beauty. In the 20th century, Diaghilev's Ballets Russes and later George Balanchine's neoclassical style reshaped the art.

Technique

Ballet technique rests on turnout of the legs, five basic positions of the feet, and movements including plié, arabesque, pirouette and jeté. Dancers train daily in class beginning with barre exercises. Female dancers perform on pointe; male dancers are noted for lifts and powerful jumps.

Companies and repertoire

Major companies include the Paris Opera Ballet, the Bolshoi, the Mariinsky, The Royal Ballet in London and New York City Ballet. Contemporary ballet blends classical technique with modern movement.
