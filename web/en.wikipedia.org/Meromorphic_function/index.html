Meromorphic function

Meromorphic function
FieldComplex analysis
DefinitionHolomorphic on a domain except at isolated poles
ExamplesRational functions, gamma function, elliptic functions

In the mathematical field of complex analysis, a meromorphic function on an open subset D of the complex plane is a function that is holomorphic on all of D except for a set of isolated points, each of which is a pole of the function. The term comes from the Greek meros (μέρος), meaning "part", in contrast to holos, "whole". Every meromorphic function on D can be expressed as the ratio between two holomorphic functions (with the denominator not identically zero) defined on D; the poles occur exactly at the zeros of the denominator.

Properties

Meromorphic functions form a field under addition and multiplication, extending the field of holomorphic functions. On the Riemann sphere, meromorphic functions are exactly the rational functions. A meromorphic function on the whole complex plane that is not a polynomial must have infinitely many poles or an essential singularity at infinity — as with cot z or 1/sin z. Unlike holomorphic functions, meromorphic functions can exhibit poles but never essential singularities in the finite plane by definition.

Examples

All rational functions — such as f(z) = (z³ − 2z + 1)/(z⁵ + 3z − 1) — are meromorphic on the whole complex plane. The gamma function and the Riemann zeta function are meromorphic on the whole plane, the zeta function having a single simple pole at z = 1. Elliptic functions, doubly periodic on a lattice, are meromorphic on the plane and descend to meromorphic functions on a torus. By contrast, e1/z at z = 0 and non-constant entire periodic functions with two incommensurable periods are not meromorphic on the whole sphere in the same way; functions with essential singularities or branch points fall outside the class.

On Riemann surfaces

On every Riemann surface, meromorphic functions can be defined as holomorphic maps to the Riemann sphere that are not identically ∞. On a compact Riemann surface, meromorphic functions form a field that is a finite extension of the complex numbers when the surface is algebraic (Riemann–Roch theory), and their study is central to algebraic geometry. On non-compact surfaces, theorems of Mittag-Leffler and Weierstrass guarantee meromorphic functions with prescribed poles and zeros.
