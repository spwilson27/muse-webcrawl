Halitosis

Halitosis, or bad breath, is a symptom in which a noticeably unpleasant breath odour is present.

Halitosis, also known as bad breath or oral malodour, is a symptom in which a noticeably unpleasant breath odour is present. It can cause significant anxiety among those suffering from it, and is also associated with depression and symptoms of obsessive compulsive disorder. Concerns about halitosis date back thousands of years: the condition is mentioned in ancient Egyptian, Greek, Roman, Persian, and Chinese writings, and the Talmud describes bad breath as a disability that could justify divorce.

Causes

In most cases (roughly 85–90%), bad breath originates within the mouth itself. Anaerobic bacteria living on the back of the tongue and in periodontal pockets break down proteins and release volatile sulfur compounds — principally hydrogen sulfide, methyl mercaptan, and dimethyl sulfide — which produce the characteristic odour. Contributing factors include poor oral hygiene, tongue coating, gum disease (gingivitis and periodontitis), dry mouth (xerostomia), dental plaque and food debris, and poorly cleaned dentures. Intensity typically increases upon waking ("morning breath") because saliva flow falls during sleep.

Extra-oral causes account for the remainder and include chronic sinusitis and post-nasal discharge, tonsillitis and tonsil stones, respiratory infections, gastro-oesophageal reflux, and metabolic conditions: uncontrolled diabetes may produce a fruity, acetone-like odour, kidney failure a fishy or urine-like odour, and liver failure a sweet, musty smell (fetor hepaticus). Certain foods (garlic, onion, spices), alcohol, and tobacco also cause transient bad breath.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis ranges from simple self- and clinician-assessment of breath odour (organoleptic measurement) to instrumental methods such as gas chromatography and sulfide monitors that quantify volatile sulfur compounds. A substantial minority of people who seek help have pseudohalitosis (no objectively detectable odour) or halitophobia (persistent fear of bad breath despite reassurance).

Management addresses the underlying cause: mechanical cleaning including tongue scraping or brushing, professional treatment of gum disease, adequate hydration, and antibacterial mouthwashes containing agents such as chlorhexidine, cetylpyridinium chloride, or zinc salts, which reduce bacterial load or neutralise sulfur compounds. The global market for mouthwash, mints, and breath fresheners is worth billions of dollars annually; the term "halitosis" itself was popularised in the 1920s by advertising for Listerine mouthwash, a frequently cited early example of medicalised marketing.
