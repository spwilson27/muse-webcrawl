Inter-American Commission on Human Rights

The Inter-American Commission on Human Rights (IACHR) is an autonomous organ of the Organization of American States (OAS) whose mission is to promote and protect human rights in the American hemisphere. Created in 1959 and headquartered in Washington, D.C., it is, together with the Inter-American Court of Human Rights, one of the two bodies that make up the inter-American system for the promotion and protection of human rights.

Functions

The Commission examines individual petitions alleging human rights violations, conducts on-site visits and country reports, issues precautionary measures in urgent situations, and operates thematic rapporteurships (for example on freedom of expression, children, and persons deprived of liberty). Cases that are not resolved may be referred to the Inter-American Court, whose judgments are binding on states that have accepted its jurisdiction.

Composition

The IACHR consists of seven members, elected in their personal capacity by the OAS General Assembly for four-year terms. Its work is grounded in the American Declaration of the Rights and Duties of Man (1948) and the American Convention on Human Rights (1969).
