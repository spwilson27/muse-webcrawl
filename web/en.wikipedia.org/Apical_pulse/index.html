Apical pulse

The apical pulse is the heartbeat as heard with a stethoscope placed over the apex of the heart, at the point of maximal impulse — usually in the fifth intercostal space at the left midclavicular line. It is a standard measure of heart rate in clinical practice.

Measurement

The clinician auscultates the apex for a full minute when the rhythm may be irregular, counting the "lub-dub" (S1 and S2 sounds) as one beat. The apical rate is often compared with the radial pulse; a difference between the two is called a pulse deficit and can occur in conditions such as atrial fibrillation, where some contractions are too weak to produce a palpable peripheral pulse.

Clinical use

Apical pulse measurement is preferred in infants and young children, in patients with irregular rhythms, and before administering certain cardiac medications such as digoxin, where administration is typically withheld if the apical rate is below 60 beats per minute.
