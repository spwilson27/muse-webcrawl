Dawson City

Dawson City, officially the City of Dawson, is a town in the Canadian territory of Yukon. It is inseparably linked to the Klondike Gold Rush (1896–1899), when it grew almost overnight into one of the largest cities west of Winnipeg, with a population estimated at 40,000. Today it has roughly 1,600 residents and is a National Historic Site complex.

History

The town was founded in 1897 at the confluence of the Klondike and Yukon rivers, near the Tr'ochëk fishing camp of the Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in First Nation, and named after geologist George M. Dawson. When gold was discovered on Bonanza Creek in August 1896, tens of thousands of stampeders descended; Dawson became a supply and entertainment hub of saloons, theatres, and hotels.

By 1899 the rush had faded as gold was found at Nome, Alaska, and large dredging companies consolidated the creeks. Dawson City was the capital of Yukon until 1953, when Whitehorse took over. Its preserved boardwalks, wooden false-front buildings, and landmarks such as Diamond Tooth Gerties gambling hall and the S.S. Klondike sternwheeler now draw tourists, and in 2023 Tr'ondëk-Klondike was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
