Igor of Kiev, also called Igor the Old (died 945), was a Varangian prince of Kievan Rus' who ruled from 912, succeeding Oleg the Wise. According to the Primary Chronicle, he was the son of Rurik, the founder of the Rurik dynasty, though the chronology is uncertain.

Reign

Igor campaigned repeatedly against the Byzantine Empire. A great Rus' fleet attacked Constantinople in 941 and was destroyed by Greek fire; a second expedition in 944 led to a new trade treaty between the Rus' and Byzantium, the text of which is preserved in the Primary Chronicle. Igor also fought the Pechenegs and collected tribute (polyudie) from subject Slavic tribes.

Death and revenge

In 945, after Igor returned to the Drevlians to demand additional tribute beyond the amount already collected, they killed him — according to Byzantine sources, by a brutal execution. His widow Olga became regent for their young son Sviatoslav and took famous revenge on the Drevlians, a series of episodes among the most celebrated in the chronicle tradition.

Legacy

Igor is the hero of the 12th-century epic The Tale of Igor's Campaign, which describes a later prince, Igor Svyatoslavich of Novgorod-Seversk, and is sometimes confused with Igor of Kiev. His reign marks the consolidation of the early Rus' state between the reigns of Oleg and Sviatoslav I.
