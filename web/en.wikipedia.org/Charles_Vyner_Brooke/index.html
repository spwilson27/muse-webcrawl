Sir Charles Vyner de Windt Brooke (26 September 1874 – 9 May 1963) was the third and last of the White Rajahs who ruled the Raj of Sarawak on the island of Borneo, reigning from 1917 until he ceded the territory to Britain as a Crown Colony in 1946.

Early life and accession

The elder son of Rajah Charles Brooke, Vyner was educated at Winchester and Magdalene College, Cambridge, served in the Sarawak administration from the 1890s — including an apprenticeship in the outstation service and as Resident of Mukah and Oya — and succeeded his father in 1917. He continued the Brookes' style of personal, paternalist rule through a small cadre of British officers and Malay and indigenous chiefs, though he was frequently absent in England and left growing authority to administrative officers.

Reign

Vyner's rule saw modest development — rubber and oil revenue, a new council, and in 1941, for the centenary of Brooke rule, a written constitution that promised eventual self-government through the Council Negri. In December 1941 Japanese forces invaded; Vyner, in Australia at the time, remained abroad through the occupation. After liberation in 1945, facing massive reconstruction needs, he decided to cede Sarawak to the British Crown. The cession, effected on 1 July 1946, was bitterly controversial: it passed the Council Negri by a narrow margin and provoked an anti-cession movement among Malay officials and nationalists that culminated in the assassination of the second British governor, Duncan Stewart, in 1949.

Later life

Vyner, whose designation of his nephew Anthony Brooke as heir had been revoked in a long-running family quarrel, retired to London with a government settlement and died there in 1963. Sarawak went on to join the Federation of Malaysia in 1963.
