Un Chien Andalou

Un Chien Andalou ("An Andalusian Dog") is a 1929 French silent short film directed by Luis Buñuel and co-written with Salvador Dalí. Running about 16 to 21 minutes depending on the version, it is one of the most famous works of surrealist cinema.

Content

The film has no conventional plot. It consists of a sequence of dreamlike, irrational scenes connected by intertitles such as "eight years later" that bear no logical relation to the action. Its most notorious image is the opening sequence in which a man (Buñuel himself) appears to slice a woman's eyeball with a straight razor, intercut with a thin cloud crossing the moon. Other scenes feature ants emerging from a hole in a man's hand, a severed hand on the street, and a man dragging grand pianos laden with donkey carcasses and priests.

Production and legacy

Buñuel and Dalí wrote the scenario from their dreams, deliberately rejecting any image that could be explained rationally. Buñuel financed the film with money from his mother. At the Paris premiere he reportedly carried stones in his pockets expecting outrage, but the film was a success among the surrealist circle. It remains a landmark of avant-garde film.
