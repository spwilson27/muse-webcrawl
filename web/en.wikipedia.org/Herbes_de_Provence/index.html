Herbes de Provence (French: [ɛʁb də pʁɔvɑ̃s]; Provençal: èrbas de Provença) is a mixture of dried herbs considered typical of the Provence region of southeastern France. Formerly simply a descriptive term for herbs gathered in the wild hills of the region, commercial blends began to be sold under this name in the 1970s. These blends often contain savory, marjoram, rosemary, thyme, and oregano. Lavender flowers are also included in some traditional recipes and in most blends sold in North America.

History

Provençal cooking traditionally used each herb separately, seasoning dishes with whatever grew in the garrigue — thyme, rosemary, bay, and wild fennel among them. The standardised blend sold as "herbes de Provence" is largely a creation of French spice companies in the 1970s, which codified a mixture and marketed it internationally. Julia Child helped popularise the blend in the United States.

Composition

There is no legally fixed recipe, but a typical French commercial blend is dominated by thyme, with rosemary, savory, marjoram, and oregano. A reference formulation used by spice makers combines roughly thyme, savory, rosemary, marjoram, oregano, and small amounts of other herbs such as basil, bay, sage, or fennel. Blends sold outside France, especially in the United States, frequently add dried lavender buds, valued for a floral note but regarded by some Provençal cooks as non-traditional.

Uses

The blend is used to season grilled meats and fish, roasted vegetables, stews, and dishes cooked in olive oil. It is a classic seasoning for roast chicken, lamb, ratatouille, grilled tomatoes, and Provençal bean and vegetable soups. Because the dried herbs withstand heat, they are often added before or during cooking, or used in marinades and rubs.
