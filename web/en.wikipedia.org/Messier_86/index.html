Messier 86

Catalogue: M86, NGC 4406
Type: Lenticular/elliptical galaxy (S0/E3)
Constellation: Virgo
Distance: ~52 million light-years
Apparent magnitude: 8.9

Messier 86 (also known as M86 or NGC 4406) is an elliptical or lenticular galaxy in the constellation Virgo. It was discovered by Charles Messier in 1781 and lies in the heart of the Virgo Cluster of galaxies, forming a striking pair with the nearby galaxy Messier 84 within Markarian's Chain.

Properties

M86 is one of the largest and brightest galaxies of the Virgo Cluster, with an extensive system of globular clusters — around 3,800 — and faint stellar streams from disrupted dwarf galaxies. It contains a supermassive black hole of roughly 10⁸ solar masses.

Recession velocity

Unusually, M86 displays a blueshift: it is approaching the Milky Way at about 244 km/s (with some measurements giving the highest blueshift of any Messier object), because it is falling into the Virgo Cluster from behind at high speed. As it plows through the cluster's intracluster medium, ram pressure strips its gas, leaving a long X-ray plume of hot gas trailing behind it, observed by the Chandra X-ray Observatory.
