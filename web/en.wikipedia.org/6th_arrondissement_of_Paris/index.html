6th arrondissement of Paris

The 6th arrondissement of Paris (VIe arrondissement) is one of the 20 arrondissements of the capital of France. Known as the arrondissement du Luxembourg, it lies on the Left Bank (Rive Gauche) of the Seine and covers about 2.15 km². It includes the historic Saint-Germain-des-Prés quarter and the Latin Quarter's western edge.

Landmarks

The arrondissement contains the Luxembourg Palace, seat of the French Senate, and its garden, the Jardin du Luxembourg. Other landmarks include the church of Saint-Sulpice, the Saint-Germain-des-Prés abbey church — the oldest church in Paris — the Odéon theatre, and the École nationale supérieure des Beaux-Arts.

Culture and history

In the twentieth century the district was the heart of Parisian intellectual and artistic life, home to existentialist writers such as Jean-Paul Sartre and Simone de Beauvoir and to famous cafés including Les Deux Magots, Café de Flore, and the Brasserie Lipp. The Institut de France and several major publishing houses are also located here.

Demographics and economy

With a population of roughly 40,000, the 6th is among the least populous arrondissements and one of the most expensive residential districts in Paris, known for antique shops, art galleries, and luxury boutiques.
