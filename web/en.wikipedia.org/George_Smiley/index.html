George Smiley

George Smiley
Created by: John le Carré
First appearance: Call for the Dead (1961)
Occupation: Intelligence officer, later Chief of the Circus
Portrayed by: Alec Guinness, Gary Oldman, others

George Smiley is a fictional character created by John le Carré. Smiley is a career intelligence officer with "The Circus," the British overseas intelligence agency (a fictional counterpart of MI6), and serves as the protagonist of three novels — collectively the Karla trilogy — and a supporting or central character in several more, including Call for the Dead (1961), A Murder of Quality (1962), Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy (1974), The Honourable Schoolboy (1977), and Smiley's People (1979).

Character

Smiley is deliberately the antithesis of James Bond. Le Carré describes him as short, fat, and of undistinguished appearance — "resembling a toad" — with thick spectacles he habitually polishes on the end of his tie. A scholar of seventeenth-century German literature recruited from Oxford before the Second World War, he is quiet, self-effacing, and apparently cuckolded by his beautiful, aristocratic, unfaithful wife Ann. His strengths are a prodigious memory, patience, and a deep understanding of human weakness, which make him a superb interrogator and analyst rather than a man of action.

Role in the novels

In Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy, a forcibly retired Smiley is brought back to identify a Soviet mole at the highest level of the Circus — eventually unmasked as Bill Haydon, his former colleague and Ann's lover. His nemesis across the trilogy is Karla, the spymaster of Moscow Centre, whom Smiley once interrogated in a Delhi prison and who never speaks on the page. In Smiley's People he finally defeats Karla by exploiting the Soviet agent's one vulnerability — his love for his institutionalised daughter — forcing him to defect to the West at a Berlin bridge crossing. The victory leaves Smiley morally troubled rather than triumphant.

Portrayals

Smiley was portrayed most famously by Alec Guinness in the BBC television adaptations of Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy (1979) and Smiley's People (1982), performances le Carré himself praised, remarking that Guinness had effectively inhabited the role. Gary Oldman played Smiley in the 2011 film Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy, receiving an Academy Award nomination; other actors to have played him include Rupert Davies, James Mason, Denholm Elliott, and Simon Russell Beale on radio.
