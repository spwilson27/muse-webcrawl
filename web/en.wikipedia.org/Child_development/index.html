Child development
PeriodBirth to end of adolescence
DomainsPhysical, cognitive, language, social-emotional
Key theoristsPiaget, Vygotsky, Erikson, Bowlby

Child development is the sequence of physical, language, thought and emotional changes that occur from birth to the beginning of adulthood. Development proceeds in broadly predictable stages, though individual timing varies, and is shaped by interacting genetic, nutritional, social and cultural influences.

Stages and milestones

Infants progress from reflexive movements to sitting, crawling and walking, usually by about 12–15 months, while babbling develops into first words and then rapid vocabulary growth around age two. The preschool years bring symbolic play, theory of mind and self-control. Middle childhood is marked by concrete reasoning and peer relationships; adolescence adds puberty, abstract thought and identity formation. Standardised milestone checklists (such as those of the CDC and WHO) help identify developmental delays.

Theories

Jean Piaget described four stages of cognitive development from sensorimotor to formal operations. Lev Vygotsky emphasised social learning and the zone of proximal development. Erik Erikson framed psychosocial crises across the lifespan, and John Bowlby and Mary Ainsworth founded attachment theory, describing how early bonds with caregivers shape later relationships. Modern developmental science integrates these with neuroscience and genetics, studying critical periods and the effects of adversity and enrichment.
