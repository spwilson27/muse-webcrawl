Malt

Malt is germinated cereal grain that has been dried in a process known as malting. The grain — most commonly barley, but also wheat, rye, oats and others — is soaked in water to sprout, then dried with hot air to stop germination.

Production and purpose

Malting develops enzymes, especially amylases, that convert the grain's starches into fermentable sugars such as maltose. Kilning at different temperatures and durations produces malts of different colours and flavours, from pale base malts to dark roasted malts.

Uses

Malt is a principal ingredient in beer and whisky production. Malt extract is used in foods and drinks such as malted milk, malt vinegar, confectionery and breakfast cereals, and as a flavouring and sweetener in baking.

Types

Common types include pale ale malt, lager (pilsner) malt, crystal/caramel malts, chocolate and black malts, and specialty malts such as smoked and peated malt used in some whiskies.
