Pura vida (pronounced [ˈpuɾa ˈβiða]) is a Spanish phrase, literally "pure life", that has become the unofficial motto and defining expression of Costa Rica. Used as a greeting, a farewell, an answer to "how are you?", and a general affirmation that life is good, the phrase embodies a relaxed, optimistic philosophy associated with Costa Rican culture.

Origin

The phrase is often traced to the 1956 Mexican film ¡Pura vida!, in which the main character uses it repeatedly in the face of misfortune. It spread through Costa Rica in the following decades and by the 1990s was firmly established as a national catchphrase, appearing on everything from tourist merchandise to official tourism promotion.

Usage and meaning

Costa Ricans (ticos) use "pura vida" in many contexts: to say hello or goodbye, to agree, to express satisfaction, or to wish someone well. Beyond a verbal tic, the phrase is understood to express an outlook valuing simplicity, gratitude, nature and unhurried contentment — an attitude frequently linked to Costa Rica's lack of a standing army (abolished in 1948), its environmental protection policies and its high rankings in happiness and life-expectancy indices.
