Gadaa system

The Gadaa system (Oromo: Gadaa) is a traditional system of governance of the Oromo people of Ethiopia and northern Kenya. It is an indigenous democratic system in which the community is organised into generation sets (luba) that pass through a series of grades over the course of their lives, with political, military, judicial, and ritual responsibilities rotating among them.

Structure

Males pass through grades at eight-year intervals — ten grades covering eighty years of life in total. The grade in power, whose members are typically in their forties, elects leaders including the Abbaa Gadaa (father of the Gadaa), who serves a single non-renewable eight-year term. Laws are debated and proclaimed at assemblies (caffee) held under a sacred sycamore tree (odaa), and officials who abuse their position can be removed before their term ends.

Recognition

The system regulated political, economic, social, and religious life for centuries and is still practised in parts of Oromia. In 2016 UNESCO inscribed the Gadaa system on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, describing it as an indigenous system of governance that incorporates mechanisms for conflict resolution and the protection of women's rights through the parallel siinqee institution.
