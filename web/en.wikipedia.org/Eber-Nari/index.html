Eber-Nari

Eber-Nari (Akkadian: Ebir-nari; Aramaic: ʿAbar Nahara, "across the river") was a region and administrative province of the Neo-Assyrian, Neo-Babylonian and Achaemenid Persian empires comprising the lands west of the Euphrates River — roughly modern Syria, Lebanon, Israel, Palestine, Jordan and parts of southeastern Turkey and Cyprus.

History

The term originated in Akkadian usage for the territories "across the river" (seen from Mesopotamia). Under the Achaemenids, Eber-Nari became a major satrapy, listed by Herodotus as the fifth tribute district, paying 350 talents of silver. From Babylonian administrative texts the satrap is known to have governed from Damascus or Sidon, overseeing sub-provinces including Judah (Yehud), Samaria, Phoenicia and Cilicia.

Biblical references

The Hebrew form "Beyond the River" (עבר הנהר, Ever HaNahar) appears in the books of Ezra and Nehemiah, whose narratives of the Jewish return from exile are framed by correspondence with Achaemenid officials of the province, such as the governor Tattenai. These texts provide important evidence for Persian-period administration in the Levant.

Administration and culture

Imperial Aramaic served as the administrative language of the satrapy, spread by Persian bureaucracy. Coinage was issued by Phoenician cities and provincial authorities. After Alexander the Great's conquests (330s BC), Eber-Nari was absorbed into the Hellenistic kingdoms and its provincial structure dissolved into the Seleucid and Ptolemaic satrapies of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.
