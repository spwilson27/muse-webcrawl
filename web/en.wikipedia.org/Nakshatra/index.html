Nakshatra

The 27 nakshatras dividing the ecliptic

Nakshatra (Sanskrit: नक्षत्रम्, romanized: Nakṣatram) is the term for a lunar mansion in Hindu astrology and Indian astronomy. A nakshatra is one of 27 (or 28) sectors along the ecliptic, each spanning 13°20′ of arc. The names of the nakshatras reflect the names of prominent stars or asterisms within or near each sector.

The concept is attested from the earliest Vedic texts: a full list of nakshatras with deities appears in the Taittiriya Samhita and the Atharvaveda. The nakshatras are used to track the Moon's nightly position — the Moon takes about 27.3 days to complete its sidereal circuit, spending roughly one day in each — and they underpin the Hindu calendar and muhurta (electional astrology).

The 27 nakshatras

The series begins with Ashvini (in Aries) and proceeds: Ashvini, Bharani, Krittika, Rohini, Mrigashira, Ardra, Punarvasu, Pushya, Ashlesha, Magha, Purva Phalguni, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Chitra, Svati, Vishakha, Anuradha, Jyeshtha, Mula, Purva Ashadha, Uttara Ashadha, Shravana, Dhanishta, Shatabhisha, Purva Bhadrapada, Uttara Bhadrapada and Revati. An intercalary 28th nakshatra, Abhijit, is sometimes counted between Uttara Ashadha and Shravana.

Use in astrology

In Jyotisha (Hindu astrology), the nakshatra occupied by the Moon at birth (janma nakshatra) is considered foundational: it determines the starting period of the Vimshottari dasha system of planetary periods, and is central to horoscope matching (kundali compatibility) for marriage, based on a 36-point scoring system (guna milan). Each nakshatra is associated with a ruling deity, a ruling planet, a symbol, and characteristic qualities, and is divided into four quarters (padas) of 3°20′ each, corresponding to the nine signs' navamsa divisions.
