Dark Night of the Soul

Dark Night of the Soul (Spanish: La noche oscura del alma) is a poem written by the 16th-century Spanish mystic and poet St. John of the Cross, and the name of a treatise he wrote later, commenting on the poem. The phrase has come to describe a spiritual crisis in the journey toward union with God — a period of desolation in which a person feels abandoned by the divine.

The poem and treatise

John of the Cross wrote the poem while imprisoned in Toledo in 1577–79 by his Carmelite superiors. Its eight stanzas describe the soul's joyful escape from the "house" of the senses to union with the Beloved. In the treatise Ascent of Mount Carmel and the Dark Night commentary, he distinguishes the "night of the senses" from the deeper "night of the spirit", purgations through which God purifies the soul.

Broader use

In modern usage the phrase is applied more loosely to periods of depression, loss of meaning, or spiritual emptiness — for example Mother Teresa's decades-long experience of interior darkness, revealed in her posthumous letters. Psychologists and writers sometimes use it for the collapse of a person's perceived meaning in life, followed by transformation.
