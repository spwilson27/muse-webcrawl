Propadiene

Propadiene
Formula: C3H4
Molar mass: 40.065 g/mol
Appearance: colorless gas
Boiling point: −34.5 °C

Propadiene, commonly known as allene, is the organic compound with the formula H2C=C=CH2. It is the simplest allene, a class of compounds featuring two adjacent carbon–carbon double bonds. Propadiene is a colorless, flammable gas that is isomeric with propyne, and the equilibrium mixture of the two is sometimes called MAPD gas in industrial contexts.

Structure and bonding

The central carbon atom of propadiene is sp-hybridized, forming two cumulated double bonds. The two terminal CH2 groups lie in planes perpendicular to one another, giving the molecule D2d symmetry. This orthogonal arrangement of π bonds is characteristic of allenes and underlies their distinctive reactivity and axial chirality when substituted asymmetrically.

Production and uses

Propadiene occurs in equilibrium with propyne in the cracking of hydrocarbons. Industrially, the MAPD mixture is a byproduct of propane dehydrogenation and steam cracking for propylene production, and it must usually be removed or hydrogenated because it poisons polymerization catalysts. Substituted allenes are valuable intermediates in organic synthesis.
