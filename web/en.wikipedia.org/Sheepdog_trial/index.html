Sheepdog trial

A Border Collie fetching sheep toward the handler during a trial.

A sheepdog trial (also called a dog trial or herding trial) is a competitive dog sport in which herding dogs, usually Border Collies, move sheep around a field, through fences, gates, and enclosures under the direction of their handlers. The handler directs the dog by voice commands and whistles, often from a fixed post, while a judge scores the work against an ideal standard.

Elements of a trial

A standard course includes: the outrun, in which the dog runs out in a wide arc behind the sheep; the lift, the dog's first contact to start the sheep moving; the fetch, bringing the sheep in a straight line to the handler; the drive, moving the sheep away from the handler through gates; the shed, separating designated sheep from the group; and the pen, guiding the sheep into a small enclosure. Points are deducted for deviations such as crossing the course, splitting the flock, or rough handling. Trials are run against a time limit.

History and organisation

The first recorded sheepdog trials were held at Bala, Wales, in 1873. The International Sheep Dog Society (ISDS), founded in 1906, organises national and international championships in Britain and Ireland, and the sport is popular in the United States (under the USBCHA), Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa. The television programme One Man and His Dog brought the sport to a wide British audience from 1976.
