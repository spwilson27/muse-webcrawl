Lili Boulanger

Marie-Juliette Olga "Lili" Boulanger (21 August 1893 – 15 March 1918) was a French composer and the first woman to win the Prix de Rome for composition, which she achieved in 1913 with her cantata Faust et Hélène. She was the younger sister of the noted composer and teacher Nadia Boulanger.

Life

Born in Paris into a musical family — her father Ernest Boulanger had also won the Prix de Rome — Lili showed precocious talent, attending classes at the Paris Conservatoire as a small child. Her health was fragile throughout her life, damaged by childhood illness, and she died of intestinal tuberculosis (now thought to have been Crohn's disease) at the age of 24 in Mézy-sur-Seine.

Works

Despite her short life she left works of striking maturity, including the psalm settings Psaume 24, Psaume 129, and Psaume 130 (Du fond de l'abîme), the orchestral pieces D'un matin de printemps and D'un soir triste, and many songs and chamber works. Her music combines late-Romantic harmony with the influence of Fauré, Debussy, and early modernism.
