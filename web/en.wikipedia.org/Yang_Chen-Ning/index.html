Yang Chen-Ning (Chinese: 楊振寧; born 1 October 1922), also known as C. N. Yang or by the English name Frank Yang, is a Chinese theoretical physicist who has made fundamental contributions to statistical mechanics and particle physics. He and Tsung-Dao Lee received the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics for their work on parity nonconservation of the weak interaction.

Work

In 1956, Yang and Lee proposed that parity might not be conserved in weak interactions, a suggestion confirmed experimentally by Chien-Shiung Wu in the Wu experiment. In 1954, Yang and Robert Mills developed the theory of non-abelian gauge fields, known as Yang–Mills theory, which became the mathematical framework underlying the Standard Model of particle physics. His work with James Cronin is separate; with Rodney Baxter he is associated with the Yang–Baxter equation, central to exactly solvable models in statistical mechanics.

Career

Yang studied at the National Southwestern Associated University and Tsinghua University, earned his doctorate at the University of Chicago in 1948 under Edward Teller, and spent most of his career at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton and at Stony Brook University, where he founded the Institute for Theoretical Physics (now the C. N. Yang Institute for Theoretical Physics). He later became an honorary director of the Institute for Advanced Study at Tsinghua University in Beijing.
