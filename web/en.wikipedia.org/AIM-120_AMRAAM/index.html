The AIM-120 AMRAAM (Advanced Medium-Range Air-to-Air Missile) is an American beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile capable of all-weather day-and-night operations. It is the standard medium-range missile of the US Air Force and Navy and of more than thirty allied air forces, carried by aircraft including the F-15, F-16, F/A-18, Eurofighter Typhoon, Gripen, and F-35.

Guidance

The AMRAAM is a "fire-and-forget" weapon. After launch it flies toward the target on inertial guidance, receiving mid-course corrections via a datalink from the launching aircraft; at a computed range its own active radar seeker switches on ("goes pitbull") for terminal homing, freeing the pilot to manoeuvre or engage other targets. Compared with the semi-active AIM-7 Sparrow it replaced, the launching aircraft need not keep its radar locked on the target.

Development and variants

Development began in the late 1970s under a joint USAF–USN program; the missile entered service in 1991 and scored its first combat kills over Iraq in 1992–93. Successive variants — AIM-120B, C (with clipped wings for internal carriage on the F-22), and D (with two-way datalink, GPS-aided navigation, and substantially increased range) — have kept it current, and it is also fired from the ground by the NASAMS air-defence system.
