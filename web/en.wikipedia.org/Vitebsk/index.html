Vitebsk

Country: Belarus
Region: Vitebsk Region
Population: approx. 360,000
First mentioned: 974

Vitebsk (Belarusian: Віцебск; Russian: Витебск) is a city in north-eastern Belarus, near the borders with Russia and Latvia, and the administrative centre of Vitebsk Region. It stands on the Western Dvina (Daugava) River and is one of the oldest cities in the country, traditionally founded in 974.

History

Vitebsk developed as a trading post of Kievan Rus' and later a principality before passing to the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, and (in 1772, with the First Partition of Poland) the Russian Empire. It had a large Jewish community — over half the population in the late 19th century — largely destroyed in the Holocaust. The city suffered severe destruction in the Second World War and was liberated by Soviet forces in June 1944 during the Vitebsk–Orsha Offensive.

Culture

Vitebsk is the birthplace of artist Marc Chagall, whose house museum and art centre commemorate him; the Vitebsk People's Art School, founded after the Revolution, was led by Chagall and taught figures such as Kazimir Malevich and El Lissitzky, central to the Russian avant-garde. Since 1992 the city has hosted the Slavianski Bazaar international arts festival.
