The foot (plural feet; symbol ft or the prime symbol ′) is a unit of length in the British imperial and United States customary systems of measurement. Since the International Yard and Pound Agreement of 1959, one foot has been defined as exactly 0.3048 metres. A foot comprises 12 inches, and three feet compose a yard.

History

Units based on the length of the human foot are ancient: the Greek pous, the Roman pes, and similar measures were used across Europe, but their lengths varied considerably from place to place and trade to trade — historical "feet" ranged roughly from 250 mm to 335 mm. England standardized its foot in the medieval period, and the unit spread globally through the British Empire. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries most countries adopted the metric system, but the foot survived in everyday use in the United States and, for some purposes, in the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth countries.

Usage

The foot remains the standard unit for altitude in international aviation (flight levels are expressed in hundreds of feet) and is widely used in the United States for human height, construction, real estate, and shipping. In the United Kingdom it is still commonly used for height and in some trades. A slightly different "survey foot" was used in US land surveying until its deprecation at the end of 2022; it differed from the international foot by about one part in 500,000.

Relation to other units

One foot equals 12 inches, 1/3 yard, 1/5,280 of a statute mile, and exactly 30.48 centimetres. The square foot and cubic foot are the corresponding units of area and volume, and the foot-pound is a unit of energy and torque in the imperial system.
