Libyan civil war (2011)

The Libyan civil war (2011), also called the First Libyan Civil War, was fought between the government of Muammar Gaddafi and anti-Gaddafi rebels, beginning as protests inspired by the Arab Spring in February 2011 and becoming a full-scale civil war within weeks.

Background

Gaddafi's rule, begun with a 1969 coup, combined oil wealth with a repressive political system officially described as a jamahiriya (state of the masses). Human rights abuses, corruption, and regional grievances — particularly in Cyrenaica in the east — fed long-standing opposition.

Intervention and outcome

Following United Nations Security Council Resolution 1973, a coalition including France, the United Kingdom, and the United States began strikes on 19 March 2011; NATO assumed command of the operation. Rebel forces captured Tripoli in August and killed Gaddafi in Sirte on 20 October 2011. The National Transitional Council declared liberation on 23 October 2011.

The war's legacy includes continuing instability in Libya, regional weapons proliferation, and an international debate over the doctrine of humanitarian intervention and "responsibility to protect".
