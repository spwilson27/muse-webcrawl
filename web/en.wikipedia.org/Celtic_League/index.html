Celtic League

The Celtic League is a pan-Celtic organisation, founded in 1961, that aims to promote modern Celtic identity and culture, particularly the languages and political self-determination of the six Celtic nations: Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Brittany, Cornwall, and the Isle of Man.

History

The League was founded at the National Eisteddfod of Wales at Rhosllannerchrugog in 1961, growing out of earlier inter-Celtic congresses and the Celtic Congress movement. Its first general secretary was the Welsh nationalist Gwynfor Evans, later the first Plaid Cymru MP. The League campaigned for official recognition of the Celtic languages and supported national movements in each country, while formally committing itself to peaceful and democratic methods.

Activities

The League monitors and publicises human-rights and language issues in the Celtic countries, submitting reports to bodies such as the United Nations and the Council of Europe. Its branches exist in each Celtic nation and internationally, including in the Celtic diaspora in North America and Australia. Its journal, Carn, founded in 1973, publishes news and analysis in English and the Celtic languages.

Aims

The League's stated aims include fostering cooperation between the Celtic nations, developing their consciousness of a shared heritage and struggle, and campaigning for the freedom and cultural survival of each nation.
