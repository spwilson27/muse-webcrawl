Sadi Carnot (physicist)

Nicolas Léonard Sadi Carnot (1 June 1796 – 24 August 1832) was a French mechanical engineer, physicist and military officer who is often described as the "father of thermodynamics". The son of mathematician Lazare Carnot, he studied at the École Polytechnique in Paris.

Reflections on the Motive Power of Fire

In his only published work, Réflexions sur la puissance motrice du feu (1824), Carnot analyzed steam engines scientifically and introduced the concept of the Carnot cycle, an idealized heat engine cycle of maximum possible efficiency. He showed that the efficiency of a heat engine depends only on the temperatures of the hot and cold reservoirs, a result now known as Carnot's theorem.

Legacy

Carnot's work received little attention during his lifetime, but was later developed by Émile Clapeyron and became the foundation of the second law of thermodynamics, as formulated by Rudolf Clausius and Lord Kelvin. He died of cholera in Paris at the age of 36.
