Anne Frank House

The Anne Frank House (Dutch: Anne Frank Huis) is a writer's house and biographical museum dedicated to Jewish wartime diarist Anne Frank. It occupies a canal house at Prinsengracht 263 in Amsterdam, the Netherlands, in whose secret annex (the Achterhuis) Anne Frank and seven others hid from Nazi persecution from 1942 to 1944.

The building

The house was built in 1635 by Dirk van Delft and acquired in 1740 by the ancestors of the Frank family's business associates. Otto Frank's companies, Opekta and Pectacon, operated from the premises. Behind a movable bookcase, the annex provided hiding space for the Frank family, the Van Pels family, and Fritz Pfeffer. On 4 August 1944 the hiding place was betrayed and its occupants arrested; only Otto Frank survived the camps.

The museum

After the war the building was threatened with demolition, but campaigners and Otto Frank preserved it. The Anne Frank House opened as a museum on 3 May 1960. The annex rooms are kept empty, at Otto Frank's request, conveying the void left by the deportations; Anne's original diary and other writings are displayed. The museum now receives well over a million visitors annually and has been repeatedly expanded, with a neighbouring museum building opened in 1999.

Mission

The Anne Frank Foundation operates the museum and works internationally to promote the diary's message against antisemitism, racism, and discrimination, including travelling exhibitions shown in dozens of countries.
