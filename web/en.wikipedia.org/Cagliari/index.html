Cagliari (Sardinian: Casteddu) is an Italian municipality and the capital city of the island of Sardinia, an autonomous region of Italy. It is the island's largest city and a major port on the Mediterranean Sea.

History

The area has been inhabited since the Neolithic period. The settlement, known to the Phoenicians and Carthaginians as Karaly, became the Roman municipium of Carales, an important naval base and trading port. After the fall of Rome it passed through Vandal, Byzantine, and Giudicato rule, before being conquered by Pisa in the 13th century, which fortified the Castello hill with walls, towers, and the cathedral. In 1324 the Aragonese took the city after the nearby Battle of Lucocisterna, and Cagliari became a capital of the Kingdom of Sardinia. Under Savoy rule from 1720 it shared in the history of the Kingdom of Sardinia and, from 1861, of unified Italy. The city suffered heavy Allied bombing in 1943.

Geography

Cagliari stands on the south coast of Sardinia at the Gulf of Cagliari (Golfo degli Angeli), around a series of hills including the historic Castello height. To the east lies the long sandy beach of Poetto; to the west and east are the lagoons of Santa Gilla and Molentargius, noted for their colonies of greater flamingos.

Main sights

Landmarks include the Roman amphitheatre carved into the rock, the Pisan towers of San Pancrazio and the Elephant, the Cathedral of Santa Maria, the Bastion of Saint Remy, and the sanctuary and basilica of Our Lady of Bonaria. The National Archaeological Museum houses finds from Nuragic, Punic, and Roman Sardinia, including material related to the Giants of Mont'e Prama.

Economy and transport

The port of Cagliari is one of Italy's principal transshipment and ferry harbours. Cagliari Elmas Airport serves the south of the island, and the city is the hub of Sardinia's railway network.
