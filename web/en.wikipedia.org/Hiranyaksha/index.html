Hiranyaksha (Sanskrit: हिरण्याक्ष, "golden-eyed") is an asura (demon) in Hindu mythology. According to the Puranas, he dragged the earth goddess Bhudevi to the bottom of the cosmic ocean, where he was slain by Varaha, the boar avatar of the god Vishnu, after a battle said to have lasted a thousand years.

Legend

Hiranyaksha and his brother Hiranyakashipu were, in a previous birth, the gatekeepers Jaya and Vijaya of Vishnu's abode Vaikuntha, cursed to be born as demons. Blessed by a boon of near-invincibility from Brahma, Hiranyaksha terrorised the gods and plunged the earth into the ocean depths. Vishnu assumed the form of a gigantic boar, dived into the ocean for a thousand years, slew the demon with his mace, and raised the earth on his tusks.

Texts

The story appears in the Mahabharata and several Puranas, including the Bhagavata Purana and Vishnu Purana. The Varaha legend is a popular subject in Indian temple sculpture, notably at the Udayagiri Caves.
