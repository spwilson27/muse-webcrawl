French Fifth Republic

The Fifth Republic (French: Cinquième République) is France's current republican system of government, established on 4 October 1958 by Charles de Gaulle under the Constitution of the Fifth Republic. It emerged from the collapse of the Fourth Republic amid the May 1958 crisis and the Algerian War, and it replaced a purely parliamentary regime with a hybrid semi-presidential system featuring a powerful presidency.

Origins

The Fourth Republic was crippled by ministerial instability and unable to resolve the Algerian War. In May 1958, a revolt by French settlers and army units in Algiers threatened civil war, and the National Assembly invested Charles de Gaulle — retired since 1946 — as head of government with authority to draft a new constitution. The constitutional text, prepared under Michel Debré, was approved by referendum on 28 September 1958 by roughly four-fifths of voters, and de Gaulle became the first President of the Fifth Republic in January 1959.

Institutions

The Constitution establishes a dual executive. The President of the Republic, elected by direct universal suffrage since 1962 (a reform approved by referendum over parliament's objections) and serving a five-year term since 2000, appoints the Prime Minister, presides over the Council of Ministers, commands the armed forces, and may dissolve the National Assembly and call referendums. The Prime Minister and government direct domestic policy and are responsible to the National Assembly, which can dismiss them by a vote of censure. A Constitutional Council reviews legislation.

Cohabitation

When presidential and parliamentary majorities are opposed, the system produces "cohabitation": the President appoints a Prime Minister from the opposing majority, and effective domestic power shifts to the government while the President retains influence in foreign and defence affairs. Cohabitation occurred in 1986–1988 (Mitterrand–Chirac), 1993–1995 (Mitterrand–Balladur) and 1997–2002 (Chirac–Jospin).

Presidents

Presidents of the Fifth Republic have been Charles de Gaulle (1959–1969), Georges Pompidou (1969–1974), Valéry Giscard d'Estaing (1974–1981), François Mitterrand (1981–1995), Jacques Chirac (1995–2007), Nicolas Sarkozy (2007–2012), François Hollande (2012–2017), and Emmanuel Macron (2017– ). Notable crises of the regime include the generals' putsch of 1961, the events of May 1968, and repeated debates over the breadth of presidential power — critics have called the system a "republican monarchy".
