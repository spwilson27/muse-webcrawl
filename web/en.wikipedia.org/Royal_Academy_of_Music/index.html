Royal Academy of Music
Founded: 1822
Location: Marylebone Road, London
Type: Conservatoire
Affiliation: University of London

The Royal Academy of Music (RAM) in London is the oldest conservatoire in the United Kingdom, founded in 1822 and granted a royal charter in 1830. It provides undergraduate and postgraduate training in performance, composition, conducting, jazz, and musical theatre, and is a member institution of the University of London.

History and buildings

The Academy has occupied its present home on Marylebone Road since 1911. Its facilities include the Duke's Hall concert space, recital rooms, and an extensive museum of historic instruments, including Stradivari violins. Distinguished alumni and teachers have included the composer Ralph Vaughan Williams, the pianist Myra Hess, and the conductor Simon Rattle.

Studies

Students receive one-to-one tuition, orchestral and ensemble training, and regular performance opportunities, with masterclasses from visiting international artists. The Academy's collections include manuscripts and instruments of major historical importance.
