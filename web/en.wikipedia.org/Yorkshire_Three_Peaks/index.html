Yorkshire Three Peaks

The Yorkshire Three Peaks are the three highest and most prominent fells of the Yorkshire Dales in northern England: Whernside (736 m / 2,415 ft), Ingleborough (723 m / 2,372 ft) and Pen-y-ghent (694 m / 2,277 ft). Together they form the goal of the Yorkshire Three Peaks Challenge, a circular walk of about 24 miles (39 km) with roughly 1,585 m (5,200 ft) of ascent, traditionally completed within 12 hours.

The peaks

  • Whernside — the highest point in modern North Yorkshire, a long whaleback ridge above Ribblehead and its famous railway viaduct.
  • Ingleborough — a flat-topped limestone massif crowned by the ramparts of an Iron Age hill fort, riddled below with cave systems including Gaping Gill.
  • Pen-y-ghent — the smallest of the three, recognisable by its stepped "lion's head" profile of gritstone over limestone, near the village of Horton in Ribblesdale.

The challenge

The classic route starts and ends at Horton in Ribblesdale, climbing Pen-y-ghent first, then crossing to Whernside via Ribblehead, and finishing over Ingleborough. The walk was first recorded as a challenge in the 19th century; the first recorded completion is attributed to teachers from Giggleswick School in 1887. Thousands of walkers attempt it each year, many raising money for charity, and organised races are also held over the course — the Three Peaks Race, a fell race first run in 1954, covers the same summits. Heavy footfall has made path erosion a significant concern, and the Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority undertakes continuous path restoration in the area.
