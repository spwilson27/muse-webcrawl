Thamnophis

Thamnophis is a genus of garter snakes, native to North and Central America, ranging from Canada to Costa Rica. The genus comprises roughly three dozen species and many more subspecies, the best known being the common garter snake (Thamnophis sirtalis), one of the most widespread reptiles in North America.

Description

Garter snakes are generally slender, small to medium-sized snakes, most species measuring 45–90 cm (18–35 in) in total length. Most have longitudinal yellow or light-coloured stripes running along a brown, green, or black body, a pattern that inspired the name by resemblance to old-fashioned stocking garters. They bear live young (ovoviviparous) and feed chiefly on amphibians, earthworms, slugs, fish and small rodents, often hunting near water.

Venom

Although long regarded as harmless, garter snakes produce a mild venom in Duvernoy's gland, delivered by enlarged rear teeth. The secretion helps subdue amphibian prey; in humans it typically causes at most slight local swelling or irritation. Some populations sequester toxins from poisonous newts they eat, and common garter snakes are famous for a coevolutionary arms race with rough-skinned newts, in which the snakes have evolved resistance to the newt's tetrodotoxin.

Ecology and behaviour

Many northern populations hibernate communally in underground dens (hibernacula), sometimes in aggregations of thousands of individuals. The red-sided garter snake dens near Narcisse, Manitoba, are among the largest known snake gatherings in the world, drawing dense mating aggregations each spring.
