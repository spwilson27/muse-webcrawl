Infantry tactics

DomainLand warfare
Basic unitsFireteam, squad, platoon
Core conceptsFire and movement, suppression, combined arms

Infantry tactics are the methods by which infantry units fight on the battlefield: how foot soldiers deploy, move, use firepower and terrain, and cooperate with other arms. Tactics have evolved continuously with weapons technology, from massed spear formations to today's dispersed, networked small units.

Historical development

Ancient infantry fought in dense formations such as the Greek phalanx and Roman legion. Gunpowder brought linear tactics of musket volleys, refined in the Napoleonic era with columns, lines and skirmishers. Rifled weapons and machine guns in the 19th and early 20th centuries made massed assaults suicidal, producing trench warfare in the First World War and, in response, infiltration tactics by German stormtroopers and combined-arms methods.

Modern principles

Contemporary infantry tactics rest on fire and movement: one element suppresses the enemy with fire while another manoeuvres. Small units — fireteams and squads — use cover, concealment, bounding overwatch and hand signals or radios. Supporting weapons include machine guns, mortars, grenade launchers and anti-tank missiles, coordinated with artillery, armour, engineers and air support.

Specialised environments

Urban warfare (fighting in built-up areas), jungle, mountain, arctic and trench warfare each demand adapted techniques for movement, clearing buildings, and sustaining troops. Night operations have been transformed by night-vision equipment, and drones now provide squad-level reconnaissance and strike options.
