Pleistocene megafauna

Pleistocene megafauna is the collective term for the large animals that lived during the Pleistocene epoch (about 2.6 million to 11,700 years ago), especially those of the last glacial period. Megafauna are usually defined as animals with an adult body weight over about 44 kilograms (97 lb), though the term often focuses on the very largest species.

Notable examples

The fauna included woolly mammoths and mastodons, woolly rhinoceroses, giant ground sloths such as Megatherium, the giant armadillo relative Glyptodon, cave bears, cave lions, the giant deer or "Irish elk", saber-toothed cats such as Smilodon, and in Australia the giant wombat relative Diprotodon and huge flightless birds.

Extinction

Most of these species vanished in the Quaternary extinction event between roughly 50,000 and 10,000 years ago, a wave of losses that coincided with both the end of the last ice age and the global spread of anatomically modern humans. The relative roles of climate change and human hunting — the "overkill hypothesis" championed by Paul Martin — remain debated, with most researchers favouring a combination of pressures.
