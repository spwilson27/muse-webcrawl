BeiDou, formally the BeiDou Navigation Satellite System (BDS; Chinese: 北斗卫星导航系统), is a Chinese satellite navigation system. Named after the Big Dipper asterism (Beidou in Chinese), it provides positioning, navigation, and timing services worldwide, alongside the American GPS, Russian GLONASS, and European Galileo systems.

Development

The system was built in three phases. BeiDou-1 (2000–2007) was an experimental regional system using geostationary satellites that also offered two-way short messaging. BeiDou-2, providing service over the Asia-Pacific region, became operational in 2012. BeiDou-3 added medium Earth orbit and inclined geosynchronous satellites; its final satellite was launched in June 2020, and the completed global system was formally commissioned on 31 July 2020.

Services

BeiDou provides open civilian positioning and restricted military signals, global and regional short-message communication — a distinctive feature among global navigation systems — and search-and-rescue support. Its hybrid constellation of satellites in geostationary, inclined geosynchronous, and medium Earth orbits is designed to give especially strong coverage in the Asia-Pacific region.

Applications

BeiDou receivers are standard in most smartphones sold in China, and the system is used in transportation, agriculture, disaster relief, surveying, and the Chinese military. China has promoted BeiDou internationally as part of its "space Silk Road", and services are used in more than 100 countries.
