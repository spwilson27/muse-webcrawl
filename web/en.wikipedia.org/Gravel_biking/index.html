Gravel biking

Gravel biking is a cycling discipline that combines elements of road cycling and mountain biking, ridden mostly on unpaved surfaces such as gravel roads, farm tracks, dirt roads and light trails. It grew rapidly in popularity in the United States during the 2010s, particularly in the Midwest, where long rural gravel road networks host large grassroots racing events.

Bicycles

A gravel bike resembles a drop-bar road bicycle but with features adapted to rougher surfaces: clearance for much wider tyres (typically 38–50 mm), more stable and relaxed frame geometry, lower gearing, disc brakes, and often mounts for racks, fenders and extra bottles for long self-supported rides. Some models include flared handlebars for control and short-travel suspension or compliant seatposts for comfort.

Events

Signature events include Unbound Gravel (formerly Dirty Kanza) in Kansas, with distances up to 200 miles, and the Belgian Waffle Ride series. Many events emphasize participation, self-reliance and remote scenery over pure competition, with riders expected to navigate and carry their own supplies. The discipline has also been formalized: the UCI held its first Gravel World Championships in 2022.

Related disciplines

Gravel biking overlaps with cyclo-cross, whose bikes are similar but built for short closed-circuit races, and with bikepacking and adventure cycling, which extend the same style of riding to multi-day journeys.
