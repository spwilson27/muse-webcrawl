Pilgrimage to Cythera

ArtistJean-Antoine Watteau
Year1717
MediumOil on canvas
Dimensions129 cm × 194 cm
LocationLouvre, Paris

Pilgrimage to Cythera (French: Pèlerinage à l'île de Cythère), also known as The Embarkation for Cythera, is a painting of 1717 by the French artist Jean-Antoine Watteau, submitted as his reception piece to the Académie Royale de Peinture et de Sculpture in Paris. It is now in the Musée du Louvre. A second, slightly different version hangs in Charlottenburg Palace, Berlin.

Subject

Cythera, an island off the southern Peloponnese, was sacred to Aphrodite, goddess of love, and in the literature and theatre of Watteau's time stood for an island of love and courtship. The painting shows elegantly dressed couples in an idyllic wooded landscape near a statue of Venus: some move towards a gilded boat at the left while others linger, accompanied by putti. Scholars have long debated whether the figures are arriving at Cythera or, as now more commonly read, departing it—the melancholy mood of the turning, reluctant couples suggests a leave-taking as the day of love ends.

Significance

The Academy, uncertain how to classify the work, received Watteau in a category created for it—painter of fêtes galantes. The painting became the defining image of that genre: scenes of aristocratic leisure and flirtation in dreamlike parkland, painted with feathery brushwork and a bittersweet sense of transience. It is regarded as one of Watteau's masterpieces and a foundational work of the French Rococo.
