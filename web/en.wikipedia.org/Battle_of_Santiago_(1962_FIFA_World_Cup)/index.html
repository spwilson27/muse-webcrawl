Battle of Santiago (1962 FIFA World Cup)

Date2 June 1962
VenueEstadio Nacional, Santiago, Chile
MatchChile 2–0 Italy
RefereeKen Aston (England)

The Battle of Santiago (Spanish: Batalla de Santiago) is the name given to a notoriously violent first-round match of the 1962 FIFA World Cup, played between hosts Chile and Italy on 2 June 1962 at the Estadio Nacional in Santiago. Chile won 2–0, but the match is remembered as one of the most disgraceful in World Cup history.

Background

Relations between the teams were poisoned before kick-off. Italian journalists had published insulting dispatches about Santiago and Chile, describing the capital in terms Chileans took as a slur on their country; the articles were reprinted in the Chilean press, inflaming public opinion. Several of Italy's squad were of South American origin, further charged by the home crowd as traitors, and the atmosphere in the stadium was openly hostile.

The match

The first foul came within seconds, and the first dismissal after twelve minutes, when Italy's Giorgio Ferrini was sent off for a retaliatory kick — and refused to leave, having to be dragged away by police. Thereafter the game dissolved into a sequence of punches, spitting and violent tackles that the English referee Ken Aston struggled to control. Chile's Leonel Sánchez broke the nose of Italian captain Humberto Maschio with a left hook that went unpunished, while Sánchez himself survived two dismissals' worth of offences. Italy's Mario David was sent off for a flying kick at Sánchez's head. Police intervened on the field several times. Late goals from Jaime Ramírez and Jorge Toro gave nine-man Italy's conquerors a 2–0 win.

Aftermath

When British television showed the match, presenter David Coleman introduced it as "the most stupid, appalling, disgusting and disgraceful exhibition of football, possibly in the history of the game". The spectacle helped drive reform: Aston, who had refereed the match, was inspired by it to invent the yellow and red card system, introduced at the 1970 World Cup. Chile went on to finish third at the tournament, their best-ever result; Italy were eliminated in the group stage.
