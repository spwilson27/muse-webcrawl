Jacqueline Cochran

Jacqueline Cochran (1906 – 1980) was an American aviator and racing pilot who pioneered women's aviation. During the Second World War she directed the Women Airforce Service Pilots (WASP), and in 1953 she became the first woman to break the sound barrier.

Born11 May 1906, Pensacola, Florida
Died9 August 1980, Indio, California
Known forDirector of WASP; first woman past Mach 1
AwardsDistinguished Service Medal; Harmon Trophies

Racing career

Cochran learned to fly in the early 1930s and quickly entered air racing, winning the transcontinental Bendix Trophy in 1938 and setting numerous speed and altitude records. She competed against — and frequently beat — the leading male pilots of the day.

War service and records

She ferried bombers to Britain with the Air Transport Auxiliary, then organised and led the WASP programme, in which over a thousand women flew military aircraft within the United States. After the war she set further jet records in an F-86 Sabre, flying as a consultant with test pilot Chuck Yeager's support, and at her death held more distance and speed records than any pilot, male or female.
