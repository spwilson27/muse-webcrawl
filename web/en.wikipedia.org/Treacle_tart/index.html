Treacle tart

A treacle tart with its traditional lattice top, served with custard or clotted cream.

TypeTart
Place of originUnited Kingdom
Main ingredientsShortcrust pastry, golden syrup, breadcrumbs, lemon juice and zest
CourseDessert

Treacle tart is a traditional British dessert. The earliest known recipe dates from the late 19th century, appearing in Mrs Beeton's Book of Household Management era cookery as syrup became widely available. It consists of a shortcrust pastry shell filled with a mixture of golden syrup — sold in Britain since the 1880s under brands such as Lyle's — fresh breadcrumbs, and lemon juice and zest, which cuts the intense sweetness. The top is often finished with a lattice of pastry strips.

Preparation

The pastry case is blind-baked, then filled with warmed golden syrup stirred together with breadcrumbs, lemon zest and juice, and sometimes a little cream or an egg to set the filling. It is baked until the filling is just set but still slightly soft, and served warm with custard, cream, clotted cream or ice cream. Despite the name, modern treacle tart is made with light golden syrup rather than dark treacle (molasses), though older recipes used the darker syrup.

In culture

Treacle tart is famously the favourite food of Harry Potter in J. K. Rowling's novels, frequently served at Hogwarts feasts. It is also a staple of British school dinners and pub menus, and the Cheshire Cat in Alice's Adventures in Wonderland (1865) describes a treacle well — a punning precursor of the dessert's popularity.
