Pantalone

Pantalone (Italian: [pantaˈloːne]; Venetian: Pantalon) is one of the principal stock characters of the Italian commedia dell'arte. He is a wealthy, elderly Venetian merchant — greedy, status-conscious, and often lecherous — who serves as one of the vecchi (old men) whose schemes and opposition drive the plots, typically obstructing the young lovers (innamorati) before being outwitted by the clever servants (zanni).

Character and costume

Pantalone is conventionally played as a gaunt old man with a pointed beard, wearing a long red waistcoat or jacket, red tight-fitting trousers and stockings, slippers — traditionally Turkish-style with upturned toes — a black cape (zimarra), a soft red or black cap, and a hooked-nose leather mask with bushy eyebrows. A money pouch hangs at his belt, and he often carries a dagger or handkerchief. He speaks in the Venetian dialect, walks with a stooped gait and thrust-forward hips, and alternates between miserly caution and sudden lecherous energy when pursuing women far younger than himself.

Origins and influence

The character emerged in the 16th century as Venetian comedy companies codified their stock types; his name has been linked to San Pantaleone, a saint popular in Venice, and to the phrase pianta leone ("plant the lion"), referring to Venetian merchants raising the lion banner of St Mark in conquered trading posts. His trousers gave the English language the word "pantaloons" — the origin of "pants". Pantalone evolved into the Pantaloon of English pantomime and harlequinade, and his type survives in countless stage and screen misers and foolish old men, including forerunners of characters in Molière and Goldoni.
