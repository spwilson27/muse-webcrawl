Middle class

The middle class refers to a class of people in the middle of a social hierarchy, often defined by occupation, income, education, or ownership of assets. In contemporary sociology, the middle class is typically divided into the lower middle class and the upper middle class. The term first appeared in the United Kingdom in the late 18th century, describing the social group between the aristocracy and the working class that grew with industrialisation — merchants, professionals, and business owners.

Definitions

Definitions vary widely between countries and analysts: economists may define middle class by income bands (for example, two-thirds to double the median household income, as in Pew Research Center analyses of the United States), while sociologists emphasise occupation, education, and cultural capital. Marxist analysis treats the middle class, or petty bourgeoisie, as those who own some means of production but also work, distinct from the capitalist bourgeoisie and the proletariat.

Trends

The growth of a global middle class — particularly in China and other emerging economies — has been a major economic story of the 21st century, while in many advanced economies sociologists and journalists describe middle-class incomes as stagnant or squeezed by housing, healthcare, and education costs.
