Chloralkali process
Feedstock: Sodium chloride brine
Products: Chlorine, sodium hydroxide, hydrogen
Methods: Membrane cell, diaphragm cell, mercury cell (phased out)

The chloralkali process is an industrial process for the electrolysis of sodium chloride solution (brine), producing chlorine and sodium hydroxide (caustic soda)—two commodity chemicals essential to industry—along with hydrogen.

Chemistry

At the anode, chloride ions are oxidised to chlorine gas; at the cathode, water is reduced to hydrogen and hydroxide ions, which combine with sodium ions to form sodium hydroxide. Keeping the products separate is essential, since chlorine reacts with hydroxide.

Technologies

Three cell designs have been used: the mercury (Castner–Kellner) cell, now largely phased out for environmental reasons; the diaphragm cell; and the more efficient, cleaner membrane cell, which dominates new capacity.
