Friedrich Karl Rudolf Bergius (11 October 1884 – 30 March 1949) was a German chemist known for the Bergius process for producing synthetic fuel from coal by high-pressure hydrogenation. He shared the 1931 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Carl Bosch for their contributions to the invention and development of chemical high-pressure methods.

Career

Bergius studied chemistry at the University of Breslau and the University of Leipzig, receiving his doctorate in 1907. Working partly in Fritz Haber's orbit, he pioneered experimental work at very high pressures. In 1913 he patented a process for liquefying coal by hydrogenation under high pressure and temperature, converting coal and heavy oils into gasoline-like fuels.

Work and later years

His coal liquefaction technology, industrialized by IG Farben, became strategically important to Germany's synthetic fuel program. Bergius also developed a process for producing sugar by hydrolysing wood with concentrated hydrochloric acid. After the Second World War he emigrated to Argentina, where he worked as an adviser and died in Buenos Aires in 1949.
