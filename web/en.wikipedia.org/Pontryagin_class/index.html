Pontryagin class

In mathematics, the Pontryagin classes, named after Lev Pontryagin, are certain characteristic classes associated with real vector bundles. The Pontryagin classes lie in integral cohomology groups with degrees that are multiples of four, H4k(M; Z).

Definition

Given a real vector bundle E over a space M, its k-th Pontryagin class pk(E) is defined by pk(E) = (−1)k c2k(E ⊗ C), where c2k is the (2k)-th Chern class of the complexification of E. The total Pontryagin class is the product p(E) = 1 + p1(E) + p2(E) + ⋯. The Pontryagin classes are integers (not just mod-2 classes like their relatives the Stiefel–Whitney classes), though they are 2-torsion apart from certain combinations.

Properties and applications

Pontryagin classes are invariant under isomorphism of vector bundles and under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms when applied to the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold; in fact Hirzebruch's signature theorem expresses the signature of a closed oriented 4k-manifold as a specific rational linear combination (an L-genus) of Pontryagin numbers. In 1966 Sergei Novikov proved the topological invariance of the rational Pontryagin classes — a landmark result showing that these smooth invariants depend only on the underlying topological manifold. However, integral Pontryagin classes are not homotopy invariants; exotic spheres can be distinguished using them, and John Milnor's discovery of exotic 7-spheres used Pontryagin classes of their tangent bundles.
