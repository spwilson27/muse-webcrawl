La Scala (officially Teatro alla Scala) is an opera house in Milan, Italy, and one of the world's most famous and prestigious venues for opera and ballet. The theatre was inaugurated on 3 August 1778 with Salieri's L'Europa riconosciuta.

History

The theatre was built on the site of the church of Santa Maria alla Scala, from which it takes its name, after the previous ducal theatre burned down in 1776. Designed by neoclassical architect Giuseppe Piermarini, it quickly became a centre of operatic life: premieres include Rossini's La gazza ladra, Donizetti's works, Bellini's Norma, and many operas of Verdi, including Nabucco, Otello and Falstaff. Puccini's Madama Butterfly and Turandot also premiered there.

The theatre today

The house's season traditionally opens on 7 December, the feast of Saint Ambrose, Milan's patron saint. La Scala is home to an orchestra, chorus and ballet company, and to the Teatro alla Scala Academy. A major renovation completed in 2004 restored the auditorium and modernised the stage machinery.

Museum and academy

The Museo Teatrale alla Scala, adjacent to the theatre, displays costumes, scores and memorabilia from the house's history.
