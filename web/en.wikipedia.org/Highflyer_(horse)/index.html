Highflyer (1774 – 18 October 1793) was an undefeated Thoroughbred racehorse and one of the most successful and influential sires of the 18th century. Bred by Sir Charles Bunbury and sold as a yearling to Richard Tattersall, the founder of the famous bloodstock auction house, Highflyer was named for the highflyer trees at Great Barton, Suffolk, where Tattersall kept him. Unbeaten in his racing career, he became the dominant stallion of his era: he was the leading sire in Great Britain and Ireland a record thirteen times (1785–1796 and 1798), and his descendants shaped the modern Thoroughbred.

Racing career

Highflyer raced between 1777 and 1779, principally at Newmarket, and never lost a race in at least fourteen starts. His wins included the July Stakes as a two-year-old and numerous sweepstakes and match races against the best horses of the day. Contemporary accounts considered him one of the finest racehorses of the century, although his career was relatively short and conducted before the modern system of Classic races was fully established.

Stud career

Retired to stud at Great Barton, Highflyer stood at steadily rising fees—eventually reaching 50 guineas—and was so heavily patronised that Tattersall is said to have made his fortune from him. His progeny won hundreds of races; among the most notable were the Derby winners Noble (1786), Young Eclipse, and Skyscraper (1789), the Oaks winners, and the St Leger winner Pewett. His sons included Sir Peter Teazle, one of the best horses of the era and himself a champion sire, as well as Delpini, Rockingham, and Walnut. Through Sir Peter Teazle and other sons and daughters, Highflyer's blood pervades modern pedigrees; he is also an important damsire, appearing in the pedigrees of many classic winners.

Highflyer died on 18 October 1793 at Great Barton, aged nineteen, reportedly after being cast in his box. He was buried at the farm, and a famous epitaph was composed for him by his owner.

Pedigree

Highflyer was sired by Herod (1758), the great stallion by Tartar out of Cypron, and himself a leading sire eight times. His dam, Rachel (1763), was by Blank (by the Godolphin Arabian) and was bred by the Duke of Ancaster. Highflyer's pedigree thus combines the Herod line with the Godolphin Arabian through his dam.

Legacy

Highflyer is regarded as the most important sire between Herod and the 19th-century greats. His name survives in the Highflyer Stakes at Newmarket's history and in the expression "highflyer" entering racing parlance. The Highflyer production, an annual bloodstock publication, later took his name.
