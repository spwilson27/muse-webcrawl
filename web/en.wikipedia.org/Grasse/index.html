Grasse

The old town of Grasse, in the hills above the French Riviera

Grasse (French pronunciation: [ɡʁas]; Provençal Occitan: Grassa) is a commune in the Alpes-Maritimes department in southeastern France, on the French Riviera. With a population of around 50,000, it is widely regarded as the world's capital of perfume.

History

Grasse grew in the Middle Ages as a centre of tanning; its leather gloves were fashionable but ill-smelling, so tanners began scenting them with local flowers. By the 17th century the glovers' trade gave way to dedicated perfumery, and the surrounding hills proved ideal for growing jasmine, rose, tuberose, lavender, and other fragrant plants. Houses such as Galimard (founded 1747), Molinard (1849), and Fragonard (1926) established the town's reputation, and today dozens of perfume companies operate there, producing natural essences and compositions for the global fragrance industry.

Perfume heritage

The skills related to perfume in the Pays de Grasse — cultivation of perfume plants, knowledge of natural raw materials, and the art of perfume composition — were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2018. The town hosts the International Perfume Museum and an annual jasmine festival each August. Famous creations associated with Grasse include the jasmine and May rose essences used in Chanel No. 5.

Geography

The town sits on a hillside at about 350 metres elevation, some 15 kilometres inland from Cannes and the Mediterranean coast, enjoying a microclimate favorable to flower cultivation.
