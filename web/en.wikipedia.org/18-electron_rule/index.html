18-electron rule

The 18-electron rule is a chemical rule of thumb used primarily for predicting and rationalising the stability of metal complexes, especially organometallic compounds of transition metals. It states that thermodynamically stable complexes tend to have a total of 18 valence electrons around the metal centre.

Basis

The count of 18 corresponds to filling the metal's valence orbitals — one s, three p and five d orbitals, holding two electrons each — giving a closed-shell configuration analogous to a noble gas. The rule is analogous to the octet rule for main-group elements and was formalised in the 1920s–30s in connection with metal carbonyls such as Cr(CO)₆, Fe(CO)₅ and Ni(CO)₄, all of which obey it.

Scope and exceptions

The rule works best for low-spin complexes of middle and late transition metals with strong-field π-acceptor ligands. Early transition metals, high-spin complexes and many square-planar d⁸ complexes (stable at 16 electrons, e.g. Wilkinson's catalyst) are common exceptions. Reactive 16- and 17-electron species are important intermediates in catalysis.
