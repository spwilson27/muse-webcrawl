Preakness Stakes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The Preakness is the middle jewel of the American Triple Crown.

The Preakness Stakes is an American thoroughbred horse race held on the third Saturday in May at Pimlico Race Course in Baltimore, Maryland. It is the second leg of the American Triple Crown, following the Kentucky Derby two weeks earlier and preceding the Belmont Stakes by three weeks. The race is run over 1 3/16 miles (about 1,900 metres) on dirt for three-year-olds, with colts and geldings carrying 126 pounds and fillies 121 pounds.

Preakness Stakes

Location: Pimlico Race Course, Baltimore, Maryland

Inaugurated: 1873

Distance: 1 3/16 miles (9.5 furlongs)

Surface: Dirt

Grade: I

History

The race was first run in 1873 and named by former Maryland governor Oden Bowie after Preakness, a colt who won the Dinner Party Stakes at Pimlico's opening in 1870; the name came from a Native American settlement name meaning "quail woods". The race was not run in 1891–93 and was held at other tracks (including Morris Park and Gravesend in New York) before returning permanently to Pimlico in 1909. There have been 13 Triple Crown winners whose campaigns passed through the Preakness, most recently Justify in 2018.

Traditions

The winner is draped with a blanket of Maryland's state flower, the black-eyed Susan (in practice often chrysanthemums painted to resemble them, since the flowers are not in bloom in May), and receives the Woodlawn Vase — created by Tiffany & Co. in 1860 and regarded as one of the most valuable trophies in American sport; the original is kept at the Baltimore Museum of Art and a replica is presented. The crowd traditionally sings "Maryland, My Maryland". The race-day record attendance ranks among the largest in American racing, and the infield festivities ("InfieldFest") are a famous part of the event.
