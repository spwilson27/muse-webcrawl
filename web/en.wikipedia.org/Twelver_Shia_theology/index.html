Twelver Shia theology
Religion: Islam (Shia)
Scripture: Quran
Key concept: Imamate of the Twelve Imams

Twelver Shia theology is the body of doctrine held by Twelver Shi'a Muslims, the largest branch of Shia Islam. Its defining belief is in twelve divinely appointed Imams, descendants of the Prophet Muhammad through his daughter Fatima and son-in-law Ali, the last of whom, Muhammad al-Mahdi, is believed to be in occultation and will return as the saviour of humanity.

Principles of religion

Twelver theology rests on five principles (usul al-din): Tawhid (the oneness of God), Adl (divine justice), Nubuwwa (prophethood), Imamah (leadership of the community by the Imams) and Ma'ad (resurrection and judgement). A distinctive feature is the emphasis on divine justice and human free will, developed in dialogue with Mu'tazilite rationalism.

The Imamate

The Imams are considered infallible (ma'sum) and possessors of divinely granted knowledge. The twelfth Imam, born in 868 CE, is held to have entered the Major Occultation in 941 CE; during his absence, religious authority is exercised by qualified jurists, a role elaborated in the doctrine of wilayat al-faqih.

Sources

Alongside the Quran, Twelvers rely on hadith transmitted through the Imams, collected in the Four Books, the most famous being al-Kafi by al-Kulayni.
