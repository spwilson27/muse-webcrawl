Tarator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tarator (Bulgarian: таратор) is a cold soup popular in the Balkan region in summer. It is made of yogurt, cucumber, garlic, walnut, dill, vegetable oil (often sunflower oil) and water, and is served chilled or even with ice. Variations in the Balkans include salad versions without water, similar to Greek tzatziki, and Turkish cacık.

Preparation and regional variants

The cucumbers are finely chopped or grated and mixed with thick yogurt, crushed garlic, oil and salt; the mixture is diluted with cold water to a soup consistency and finished with chopped dill and ground walnuts. In North Macedonia the dish is likewise called tarator, while in Serbia a drier salad form prevails. An Albanian version, tarator, uses similar ingredients, and a Levantine sauce of the same name made from tahini and lemon juice accompanies falafel and shawarma — a distinct preparation sharing only the name.

Tarator is commonly served as a first course or as a side dish with grilled meats; in Bulgaria it is considered a national dish and a staple of summer menus, sometimes eaten with rakia.
