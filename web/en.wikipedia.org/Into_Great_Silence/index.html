Into Great Silence

Into Great Silence (German: Die große Stille) is a 2005 documentary film directed by Philip Gröning about the daily life of the Carthusian monks of the Grande Chartreuse monastery in the French Alps. Nearly three hours long, it contains almost no dialogue, no commentary, no artificial lighting and no music beyond the monks' own chanting.

Production

Gröning first proposed the film to the monks in 1984; they replied that they would consider it when more time had passed, and granted permission sixteen years later. He lived in the monastery for months and filmed alone over a period of about six months, using available light. The Carthusians follow a rule of near-total solitude and silence, devoting themselves to prayer, study and manual work in individual cells, gathering for communal worship chiefly at night matins, Mass and vespers.

Style and reception

The film unfolds at the pace of monastic life, interweaving scenes of prayer, work, meals eaten in silence, and the changing alpine seasons, punctuated by short quotations from Scripture. Critics praised it as a meditative experience rather than a conventional documentary, and it became an unexpected art-house success, winning the Grand Jury Prize for documentary at the 2006 Sundance Film Festival and the European Film Award for Best Documentary.
