Appalachian dulcimer

ClassificationString instrument (plucked zither)
OriginAppalachian Mountains, US, early 19th century
StringsTypically 3–5
RelatedHammered dulcimer (unrelated), scheitholt, langeleik

The Appalachian dulcimer (also called the mountain dulcimer or lap dulcimer) is a fretted string instrument of the zither family, originating in the Appalachian region of the United States in the early 19th century. It is typically played on the lap, with the melody string noted while the other strings sound as drones.

Description

The instrument has a long, narrow wooden body — traditionally hourglass, teardrop, or elliptical in shape — with a raised fretboard carrying diatonic frets. Most have three or four strings: one or two melody strings and one or two drones. Players traditionally used a noter (a small stick) and a quill or pick, though fingerpicking and chordal playing are now common.

History

The dulcimer developed among Scots-Irish and German settlers, probably influenced by European fretted zithers such as the Pennsylvania German scheitholt. It remained a local folk instrument until the folk revival of the 1950s, when performers such as Jean Ritchie popularised it; it was later adopted by musicians including Joni Mitchell, who played it on her album Blue (1971).
