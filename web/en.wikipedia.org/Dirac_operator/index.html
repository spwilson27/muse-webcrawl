Dirac operator

In mathematics and quantum mechanics, a Dirac operator is a differential operator that is a formal square root of a second-order operator such as a Laplacian. The original example, due to Paul Dirac, is the operator on Euclidean space whose square gives the Laplacian, constructed using gamma matrices satisfying the relations of a Clifford algebra.

Formal definition

In general, a Dirac operator on a Riemannian manifold is a first-order differential operator D acting on sections of a Clifford module bundle such that D² is the Laplacian associated with a connection on the bundle. Dirac operators were introduced by Dirac in 1928 in his formulation of the relativistic wave equation for the electron, the Dirac equation.

Examples

Examples include the classical Dirac operator on spinor bundles of spin manifolds, the Dolbeault operator on complex manifolds, and operators arising in Seiberg–Witten theory. The spectrum of a Dirac operator is central to index theory: the Atiyah–Singer index theorem computes the analytical index of an elliptic operator in topological terms, and Dirac operators furnish its most important example.

In physics

In high-energy physics, Dirac operators appear in the Lagrangian of quantum field theory, where the kinetic term for fermionic fields is written using the Dirac operator coupling the spinor field to gauge fields, as in quantum chromodynamics.
