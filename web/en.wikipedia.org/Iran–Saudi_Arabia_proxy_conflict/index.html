Iran–Saudi Arabia proxy conflict

The Iran–Saudi Arabia proxy conflict, sometimes referred to as the Middle Eastern Cold War, is the ongoing struggle for regional influence between Iran and Saudi Arabia. The two countries have provided varying degrees of support to opposing sides in conflicts across the Middle East, including in Syria, Yemen, Lebanon, Bahrain, and Iraq, while generally avoiding direct military confrontation.

Background

Rivalry between Tehran and Riyadh long predates the current phase — extending to competing claims of Islamic leadership, the Sunni–Shia divide, and Persian–Arab tensions — but intensified after the Iranian Revolution of 1979, which established a Shia theocratic republic that sought to export its model and denounced the Saudi monarchy. The relationship deteriorated further with the 1987 Mecca incident, the Iran–Iraq War (during which Saudi Arabia backed Iraq), and disputes over oil policy and Gulf security.

Arenas of competition

After the 2003 US invasion of Iraq removed Saddam Hussein's Sunni-led regime, Iran's influence in Baghdad expanded, alarming Riyadh. The Arab Spring of 2011 widened the contest: Saudi Arabia intervened in Bahrain, while both powers backed opposing factions in the Syrian civil war, where Iran supported the Assad government and Saudi Arabia supported rebel groups. In Yemen, a Saudi-led coalition intervened from 2015 against the Houthi movement, which receives Iranian backing. In Lebanon, Iran's support for Hezbollah is countered by Saudi ties to Sunni and Christian parties. Both states also compete diplomatically and economically for influence among the Gulf states and in international energy markets, and tensions have spilled into crises such as the 2016 severing of diplomatic relations after Saudi Arabia executed the Shia cleric Nimr al-Nimr and protesters attacked Saudi missions in Iran, and the 2019 attacks on Saudi oil facilities attributed to Iran by several governments.

Détente

In March 2023, in a deal brokered by China, Iran and Saudi Arabia agreed to restore diplomatic relations and reopen embassies, a rapprochement that survived subsequent regional crises including the Gaza war beginning in October 2023, though the underlying rivalry in influence and security policy continues.
