Mary II of England

Mary II (30 April 1662 – 28 December 1694) was Queen of England, Scotland and Ireland, co-reigning with her husband, King William III, from 1689 until her death in 1694. Mary was the eldest daughter of James, Duke of York (later James II), and his first wife, Anne Hyde. She married her Protestant cousin, William of Orange, in 1677.

Early life and marriage

Raised as a Protestant on the orders of Charles II despite her father's conversion to Catholicism, Mary was married at fifteen to William of Orange, stadtholder of the Dutch Republic. The marriage was politically arranged to strengthen Protestant ties, and Mary moved to the Netherlands.

Glorious Revolution and reign

After the birth of a Catholic heir to James II in 1688, English notables invited William to intervene. In the Glorious Revolution, James fled and Parliament declared the throne vacant, offering the crown jointly to William and Mary. They accepted the Declaration of Right, later the Bill of Rights 1689, establishing constitutional monarchy.

William often campaigned abroad against Louis XIV of France, and during his absences Mary administered the kingdom in her own right, proving an able and popular ruler. She was deeply involved in the reformation of manners, church affairs, and the rebuilding of Greenwich Hospital. Mary died of smallpox at Kensington Palace in 1694, aged 32, without surviving children; William continued to reign alone until 1702.

Legacy

The College of William & Mary in Virginia, founded in 1693, was named for the two monarchs. Mary was mourned widely, and her funeral was among the most elaborate state ceremonies of the era.
