Culture of Pakistan

The culture of Pakistan (Urdu: ثقافتِ پاکستان) is the product of the region's diverse peoples and its long history at the crossroads of South Asia, Central Asia, and the Middle East. Pakistani culture is a blend of the cultures of its major ethnic groups — Punjabis, Sindhis, Pashtuns, Baloch, Kashmiris, and the Urdu-speaking Muhajirs — overlaid with Islamic traditions and the heritage of ancient civilizations including the Indus Valley Civilization, Gandhara, and successive Persian, Turkic, and Mughal empires.

Languages and literature

Urdu is the national language and lingua franca, while English is widely used in government and education; major regional languages include Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi, and Saraiki. Pakistani literature includes the Sufi poetry of figures such as Bulleh Shah and Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, the philosophical poetry of Muhammad Iqbal, Pakistan's national poet, and the revolutionary verse of Faiz Ahmed Faiz.

Music, dress, and food

Music ranges from Sufi devotional forms — qawwali, popularized worldwide by Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, and ghazal — to regional folk and contemporary pop. The shalwar kameez is the national dress for both men and women. Pakistani cuisine is rich and varied, featuring dishes such as biryani, nihari, haleem, kebabs, and karahi, with strong regional variation. Truck art — the elaborate decoration of trucks and buses with floral patterns, calligraphy, and portraits — is a distinctive folk art form.

Society and festivals

Islam shapes social life for the overwhelming Muslim majority; major observances include Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, and Muharram commemorations. Festivals such as Basant (kite flying in Lahore) and Sufi shrine festivals (urs) remain important cultural events. Cricket is the most popular sport, and field hockey, in which Pakistan has won multiple Olympic titles, is the national sport.
