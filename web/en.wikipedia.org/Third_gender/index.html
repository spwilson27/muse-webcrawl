Third gender

Third gender is a category used by some societies to recognise people who are considered neither men nor women, or who combine or move between masculine and feminine roles. Anthropologists and sociologists have applied the term to social categories documented in many cultures, past and present, where gender is not treated as a strict binary.

Examples

In South Asia, hijras — people assigned male at birth or intersex who adopt feminine roles and often live in structured communities — have a recorded history of more than two thousand years and have been legally recognised as a third gender in India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Nepal. In Thailand, kathoey occupy a recognised if informal position. Among many Indigenous peoples of North America, the modern pan-Indigenous term "two-spirit" describes gender-variant people who historically held special social and ceremonial roles. Other examples include the fa'afafine of Samoa, the muxe of Oaxaca, Mexico, the sworn virgins (burrnesha) of Albania, and the five-gender system of the Bugis people of Sulawesi.

Legal recognition

Several countries now permit a non-binary or third-gender marker on identity documents, and courts in India (2014) and elsewhere have affirmed the right of transgender people to self-identify. The concept is distinct from, but related to, modern Western understandings of non-binary gender identity.
