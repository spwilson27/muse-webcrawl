Spawn (biology)

Spawn is the eggs and sperm released or deposited into water by aquatic animals. As a verb, to spawn refers to the process of releasing the eggs and sperm, and the act of both sexes is called spawning. Most aquatic animals, except for aquatic mammals and reptiles, reproduce through the process of spawning.

Overview

Spawn consists of the reproductive cells (gametes) of many aquatic animals, some of which will become fertilised and produce offspring. The process typically involves females releasing ova (unfertilised eggs) into the water, often in large quantities, while males simultaneously or sequentially release spermatozoa (milt) to fertilise them. Most fish reproduce by spawning, as do most other aquatic animals including crustaceans (such as crabs and shrimps), molluscs (such as oysters and squid), echinoderms (such as sea urchins and sea cucumbers), amphibians (such as frogs and toads), and corals.

Strategies

Reproductive strategies vary widely. Broadcast spawners release gametes freely into the water column, relying on current and chance for fertilisation — a strategy used spectacularly by corals in mass spawning events synchronised with lunar cycles. Other species deposit eggs on substrates, build nests (as salmon do with redds), guard eggs, or brood young in the mouth. Many fish migrate long distances to spawning grounds in a behaviour called homing when they return to their natal waters, as salmon famously do.

Ecology and fisheries

Spawning aggregations and seasons are critical to fisheries management: spawning stock biomass is a key measure of fish population health, and closed seasons and protected spawning grounds are used to let stocks replenish. Mass release of eggs also provides food for other marine organisms, and the corpses of spawned-out salmon enrich freshwater and riparian ecosystems with marine-derived nutrients.
