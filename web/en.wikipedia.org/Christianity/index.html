Christianity is an Abrahamic monotheistic religion based on the life and teachings of Jesus of Nazareth. Its adherents, known as Christians, believe that Jesus is the Son of God and the saviour of humanity, whose coming as the Messiah (Christ) was prophesied in the Hebrew Bible. Christianity is the world's largest religion, with approximately 2.4 billion followers, roughly a third of the global population.

Beliefs

The central tenets of Christianity, summarised in creeds such as the Nicene Creed, include belief in one God who is a Trinity of Father, Son and Holy Spirit; the incarnation of God the Son in Jesus Christ; his crucifixion and resurrection; the salvation of humanity from sin through faith in Christ; his ascension into heaven; and his prophesied second coming. Christians regard the Bible — the Old Testament (corresponding broadly to the Hebrew Bible) and the New Testament — as sacred scripture. The life, death and resurrection of Jesus are recorded in the four canonical Gospels of Matthew, Mark, Luke and John.

Core practices include baptism, the Eucharist (also called Communion or the Lord's Supper), prayer, worship in churches, and the observance of festivals such as Christmas (celebrating Jesus's birth) and Easter (celebrating his resurrection).

History

Christianity emerged from Second Temple Judaism in Roman Judaea in the first century AD. Followers of Jesus, initially a Jewish sect, spread the faith through the Roman world after his death (c. 30/33 AD), led by apostles including Peter and Paul of Tarsus, whose missionary journeys opened the movement to Gentiles. Despite periods of persecution, Christianity grew steadily and was legalised by the Edict of Milan (313); in 380 it became the state church of the Roman Empire.

Over the centuries the church divided. The East–West Schism of 1054 separated the Roman Catholic Church in the Latin West from the Eastern Orthodox Church in the Greek East; earlier, the Oriental Orthodox churches had separated after the Council of Chalcedon (451). In the sixteenth century the Protestant Reformation, begun by figures such as Martin Luther and John Calvin, splintered Western Christianity into numerous denominations. From the fifteenth century onward, European colonisation and missionary activity spread Christianity worldwide; today the largest populations of Christians are in the Americas, Europe, sub-Saharan Africa and parts of Asia, and the fastest growth is in the Global South, notably Pentecostalism.

Denominations

The largest single tradition is the Catholic Church, with about 1.3–1.4 billion baptised members, headed by the Pope. Protestantism (including Anglicanism, Lutheranism, Reformed, Methodist, Baptist, Pentecostal and many other traditions) comprises roughly 800–900 million adherents; Pentecostal and charismatic movements are its fastest-growing part. Eastern Orthodoxy has about 220 million adherents, and Oriental Orthodoxy about 60 million. There are tens of thousands of distinct denominations, though many cooperate through ecumenical bodies such as the World Council of Churches.

Culture and influence

Christianity has profoundly shaped Western civilisation — its art, architecture, music, philosophy, ethics, law and calendar — and has had a major global impact through education, medicine and charitable work, as well as through episodes of coercion, persecution and colonialism. Christian theology and ethics have been central topics of philosophy, and the Bible is the best-selling book of all time.
