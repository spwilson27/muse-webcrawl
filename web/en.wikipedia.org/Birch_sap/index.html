Birch sap

Birch sap, also called birch water, is the sap extracted from the trunks of birch trees in early spring before the leaves open. It is traditionally consumed in northern and eastern Europe and parts of Asia as a refreshing drink.

Composition

The sap is a slightly sweet, thin liquid containing sugars (chiefly xylose and fructose), amino acids, minerals such as potassium, calcium and magnesium, and vitamin C.

Harvest and use

A hole is drilled into the trunk and a tube inserted to collect the flowing sap. Fresh sap spoils quickly and is often fermented into birch wine or concentrated into syrup, which requires far more sap per litre than maple syrup.
