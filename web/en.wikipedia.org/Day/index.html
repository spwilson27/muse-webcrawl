A day is the time period of a full rotation of the Earth with respect to the Sun. On average, this is 24 hours (86,400 seconds). As a day passes at a given location it experiences morning, noon, afternoon, evening, and night. The day is also used as a unit of time in the International System of Units, with the symbol d, defined as exactly 86,400 seconds.

Solar and sidereal days

A solar day is the interval between two successive solar noons, when the Sun reaches its highest point. Because Earth orbits the Sun while rotating, a sidereal day—measured against distant stars—is shorter, about 23 hours, 56 minutes, and 4 seconds. The mean solar day smooths out seasonal variations caused by Earth's axial tilt and elliptical orbit.

Calendar day and daylight

Civil time divides the calendar day at midnight. "Day" may also refer only to the hours of daylight, as opposed to night. The distribution of daylight varies with latitude and season, producing phenomena such as the midnight sun and polar night near the poles.

Other planets

Rotation periods differ across the solar system: a day on Mars (a "sol") lasts about 24 hours 39 minutes, while Venus rotates so slowly that its sidereal day lasts 243 Earth days, longer than its year.
