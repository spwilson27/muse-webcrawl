Skolt Sámi

The Skolt Sámi are a Sámi ethnic group traditionally inhabiting the border region of Finland, Norway and Russia around Lake Inari and the former Petsamo area. They speak Skolt Sámi, a severely endangered Uralic language, and are historically distinguished from other Sámi by their Eastern Orthodox Christian faith.

Skolt Sámi

Regions: Inari (Finland), Sør-Varanger (Norway), Kola (Russia)
Language: Skolt Sámi
Religion: Eastern Orthodox Christianity
Population: ~1,000–2,000

History

The Skolts lived in semi-nomadic siida (village) communities organising reindeer herding, fishing and hunting. The border changes of the Second World War — Finland's cession of Petsamo to the Soviet Union — led to the evacuation and resettlement of Finnish Skolts, mainly in Sevettijärvi, Keväjärvi and Nellim in the Inari municipality.

Culture

Skolt culture preserves distinctive handicrafts (duodji), root-basket weaving, beadwork and the leuʹdd vocal tradition. Finland recognises the Skolts in its Sámi legislation, and language-revitalisation efforts include Skolt Sámi media and education.
