Musical development

In music, development is a process by which a musical idea is communicated in the course of a composition. It refers to the varied restatement, transformation and exploration of thematic material — through fragmentation, sequence, modulation, contrapuntal combination and changes of texture — rather than its simple repetition.

Sonata form

The term is most precisely associated with the development section of sonata form, which stands between the exposition (which presents the principal themes) and the recapitulation (which restates them in the tonic). In the development, the composer typically fragments and sequences themes, moves through remote keys, and builds tension, a practice brought to its height by Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven and Brahms. Beethoven in particular made development the dramatic engine of works such as the Eroica Symphony.

Developing variation

The concept broadens in Arnold Schoenberg's notion of "developing variation", which he attributed to Brahms: the continuous transformation of a basic motive so that new material is always derived from what precedes it. Developmental techniques of this kind occur throughout Western art music and, in informal ways, in improvisation and popular music.
