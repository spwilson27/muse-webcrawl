Internal rate of return

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Internal rate of return (IRR) is a method of quantifying the results of an investment or project. It can be defined as the interest rate at which the net present value (NPV) of all the cash flows from a project or investment is equal to zero. IRR is used to evaluate the attractiveness of a project or investment: if the IRR of a new project exceeds a company's required rate of return, that project is desirable.

Definition

Given a series of cash flows CF0, CF1, …, CFn occurring at successive periods, the IRR r satisfies:

NPV = Σ CFt / (1 + r)t = 0

Because this equation is a polynomial in 1/(1+r), it generally cannot be solved analytically and is found numerically, for example by iteration or with spreadsheet functions such as IRR and XIRR.

Uses

IRR is widely used in capital budgeting to rank prospective projects, in private equity and venture capital to report fund performance, and in comparing investments with different cash-flow profiles. It is closely related to the money-weighted rate of return.

Problems with IRR

The IRR rule can mislead when cash flows change sign more than once, producing multiple IRRs or none; when comparing mutually exclusive projects of different scale or duration, where IRR can rank projects differently from NPV; and because the calculation implicitly assumes interim cash flows are reinvested at the IRR itself. Modified internal rate of return (MIRR) was developed to address some of these shortcomings.
