Middelburg, Zeeland

Middelburg (Dutch: [ˈmɪdəlbʏr(ə)x]) is a city and municipality in the south-western Netherlands, serving as the capital of the province of Zeeland. Situated on the central peninsula of the Zeeland delta, it has a population of roughly 48,000. Middelburg was an important trading city during the Dutch Golden Age, home to one of the chambers of the Dutch East India Company (VOC).

History

Middelburg originated around a ninth-century ring fortress built against Viking raids. It received city rights in 1217. During the Dutch Golden Age the city flourished as a centre of trade, lens grinding, and textile manufacture; its VOC chamber was second in importance only to Amsterdam's. The city's monumental centre reflects this wealth, with ornate merchants' houses along its canals.

On 17 May 1940, during the Second World War, much of Middelburg's historic centre was destroyed by bombardment and fire. Post-war reconstruction carefully rebuilt the centre in traditional style. In November 1944 the city was flooded during the liberation of Walcheren island.

Geography and sights

Middelburg is connected to the Veerse Meer and the North Sea by the Kanaal door Walcheren. Major sights include the late-Gothic town hall, one of the finest in the Netherlands; the abbey complex with the 90.5-metre Lange Jan ("Long John") tower; and the Zeeuws Museum. The city hosts University College Roosevelt, an English-language liberal arts college affiliated with Utrecht University, founded in 2004.

Transport

Middelburg railway station lies on the line between Vlissingen and Roosendaal, with onward connections to the Randstad. The A58 motorway connects the city with the rest of Zeeland and North Brabant.
