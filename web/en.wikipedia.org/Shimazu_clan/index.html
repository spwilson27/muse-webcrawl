Shimazu clan


The Shimazu kamon: a cross within a circle.

The Shimazu clan (島津氏, Shimazu-shi) were the daimyō of the Satsuma han, which spread across Satsuma, Ōsumi, and Hyūga provinces in southern Kyushu, Japan. The clan claimed descent from the Seiwa Genji branch of the Minamoto, and its founder, Shimazu Tadahisa, was appointed guardian (shugo) of Satsuma in the late 12th century. Remarkably, the Shimazu held their core territory continuously for roughly seven centuries, until the abolition of the han system in 1871.

Sengoku period

During the Sengoku period, under leaders such as Shimazu Yoshihisa and his brother Yoshihiro, the clan conquered nearly all of Kyushu by 1586, defeating the Ōtomo and Ryūzōji clans. Their advance was halted only by Toyotomi Hideyoshi's invasion of Kyushu in 1587, after which the Shimazu retained Satsuma and Ōsumi. Shimazu forces fought in Hideyoshi's invasions of Korea, winning a notable victory at the Battle of Sacheon in 1598.

Edo period

At the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600 the Shimazu sided with the losing Western Army, yet their remoteness and military strength meant the new Tokugawa shogunate left them in place as tozama daimyō. In 1609 the clan invaded the Ryukyu Kingdom, reducing it to a vassal whose profitable trade with China the Shimazu quietly exploited throughout the Edo period, despite the national policy of seclusion.

Meiji Restoration

Satsuma, together with Chōshū, led the movement to overthrow the shogunate in the Boshin War. Shimazu retainers such as Saigō Takamori and Ōkubo Toshimichi became key figures of the Meiji government — even as Saigō later died leading the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877 against that same government.
