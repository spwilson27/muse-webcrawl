Characteristic impedance

A transmission line modelled as distributed inductance and capacitance
Characteristic impedance
SymbolZ0
UnitOhm (Ω)
Typical values50 Ω, 75 Ω (coaxial cable)

The characteristic impedance or surge impedance (usually written Z0) of a uniform transmission line is the ratio of the amplitudes of voltage and current of a single wave propagating along the line; that is, a wave travelling in one direction in the absence of reflections in the other direction.

For a lossless line with inductance L and capacitance C per unit length, the characteristic impedance is Z0 = √(L/C), a purely real quantity. More generally, Z0 = √((R + jωL)/(G + jωC)), where R and G are the series resistance and shunt conductance per unit length.

Applications

Matching a load to the characteristic impedance of a line prevents reflected waves, maximises power transfer in some contexts, and avoids signal distortion. Standard coaxial cables are manufactured with characteristic impedances of 50 Ω for radio and test equipment and 75 Ω for video and television distribution. The characteristic impedance of free space is approximately 377 Ω.
