Tertiary source

Encyclopedias are prototypical tertiary sources

A tertiary source is an index or consolidation of primary and secondary sources. Tertiary sources compile, digest, and organize information drawn from other sources rather than presenting original research or first-hand accounts.

Examples

Typical tertiary sources include encyclopedias, dictionaries, textbooks, almanacs, bibliographies, indexes, abstracts, and guidebooks. Depending on the discipline and purpose, some works can be classed differently: a textbook is usually tertiary, while a scholarly monograph is secondary. Wikipedia itself is a tertiary source, summarizing published material with citations to the underlying sources.

Use in research

Tertiary sources are valued as starting points—for background, definitions, and pointers to more authoritative primary and secondary literature—but academic conventions generally discourage citing them as evidence in research papers, because they are one step removed from the original analysis and can introduce summarization errors.
