Arleigh Burke

Arleigh Albert Burke (19 October 1901 – 1 January 1996) was an admiral of the United States Navy who distinguished himself during World War II and the Korean War, and who served as Chief of Naval Operations during the administrations of Presidents Dwight D. Eisenhower and John F. Kennedy. He is the namesake of the Arleigh Burke-class guided-missile destroyers.

World War II

Burke commanded Destroyer Squadron 23, the "Little Beavers", in the Solomon Islands campaign of 1943–44, fighting in a remarkable string of night engagements including the Battle of Empress Augusta Bay and the Battle of Cape St. George. His squadron's high-speed runs gave rise to his nickname "31-Knot Burke", after a message reporting that boiler problems limited him to 31 knots. He later served as chief of staff to Admiral Marc Mitscher aboard the fast carriers.

Chief of Naval Operations

Appointed Chief of Naval Operations in 1955, Burke served an unprecedented three terms, until 1961. He championed the development of nuclear-powered submarines and the Polaris submarine-launched ballistic missile programme, central pillars of Cold War deterrence, and presided over the Navy during crises from Suez to Quemoy-Matsu. After retiring, he co-founded the Center for Strategic and International Studies in Washington, D.C.

Legacy

The lead ship of the Arleigh Burke class, USS Arleigh Burke (DDG-51), was commissioned in 1991 with Burke in attendance — a rare honour for a living namesake. Burke died in 1996 at age 94 and is buried at the United States Naval Academy Cemetery.
