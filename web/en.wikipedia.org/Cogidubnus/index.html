Cogidubnus

Reign: 1st century AD

Title: Client king of the Regni (Britain)

Full name: Tiberius Claudius Cogidubnus

Tiberius Claudius Cogidubnus (also written Togidubnus or Cogidumnus) was a British king of the Regni tribe in the first century AD, in the decades following the Roman invasion of Britain in AD 43. He ruled as a client king loyal to Rome, probably from Chichester (Noviomagus Reginorum) in what is now West Sussex.

Sources

Cogidubnus is mentioned by the Roman historian Tacitus in the Agricola, who records that certain states were given to him and that he remained loyal "down to our own times" — an example, Tacitus remarks, of the Roman practice of using kings as instruments of subjugation. His Roman citizenship, shown by the name Tiberius Claudius, suggests it was granted under the emperor Claudius or Nero.

The Chichester inscription

A marble inscription found in Chichester in 1723 records the dedication of a temple to Neptune and Minerva for the welfare of the imperial house, erected with the authority of Cogidubnus, styled "great king of Britain" (or "king and legate"). The grand palace at Fishbourne, near Chichester, with its mosaics and formal gardens, has often been attributed to him, though this remains speculative.
