Phở

Phở is a Vietnamese soup dish consisting of broth, rice noodles (bánh phở), herbs, and meat (usually beef — phở bò — or chicken — phở gà). It is widely considered the national dish of Vietnam and is popular around the world, served in restaurants and street stalls throughout the country, especially as a breakfast dish.

Characteristics

The broth, the defining element, is made by simmering beef (or chicken) bones with charred onion and ginger and spices including star anise, cloves, cinnamon, cardamom, and fennel seed, seasoned with fish sauce and rock sugar. The bowl is garnished with scallions, onions, Thai basil, cilantro, bean sprouts, lime, and chili, with hoisin and sriracha sauces often added at the table.

History

Phở originated in the early 20th century in northern Vietnam, around Hanoi and Nam Định, influenced by both Chinese noodle soups and French colonial demand for beef. After the partition of Vietnam in 1954, northern refugees carried the dish south, where it acquired sweeter broth, more herbs, and greater variety. Following the Vietnam War, the diaspora spread phở globally.

Regional styles

Northern (Hanoi) phở tends to have a clearer, more delicate broth, wider noodles, and fewer garnishes; southern (Saigon) phở is sweeter, with a richer assortment of herbs, sprouts, and cuts of meat. Both styles use flat white rice noodles.
