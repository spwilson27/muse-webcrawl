Cephalonia

Cephalonia (Kefalonia) is the largest of the Ionian Islands in western Greece. Its capital is Argostoli. The island is known for its rugged mountains, including Mount Ainos, its caves such as Melissani and Drogarati, and beaches such as Myrtos.

History

The island was inhabited in antiquity and is sometimes linked in tradition to the Homeric kingdom of Odysseus, though Ithaca is a separate nearby island. It passed through Byzantine, Venetian, and Ottoman periods and was part of the British-protected United States of the Ionian Islands before union with Greece in 1864. During the Second World War, Italian and German occupation ended with the Massacre of the Acqui Division in 1943. A devastating earthquake in 1953 destroyed most settlements.

Geography and economy

Tourism, fishing, and agriculture, including Robola wine, are important to the economy. The island gained wider fame through Louis de Bernières' novel Captain Corelli's Mandolin and its film adaptation.
