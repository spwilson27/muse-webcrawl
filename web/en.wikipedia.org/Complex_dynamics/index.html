Complex dynamics
Branch ofComplex analysis, dynamical systems

Complex dynamics, or holomorphic dynamics, is the study of dynamical systems obtained by iterating a holomorphic function — that is, repeatedly applying a function of a complex variable to its own output. Even for the simple quadratic family f(z) = z2 + c, the long-term behaviour of orbits is extraordinarily rich, giving rise to the fractal geometry of Julia sets and the Mandelbrot set. Complex dynamics is a subfield of complex analysis, distinct from that field's classical function theory: its questions concern iteration, stability, and chaos rather than differentiation and integration alone.

The Fatou–Julia dichotomy

For a rational map of the Riemann sphere, the sphere splits into two completely invariant sets: the Fatou set, where the iterates form a normal family and dynamics is stable and predictable, and its complement, the Julia set, where the dynamics is chaotic and sensitive to initial conditions. The theory was founded in memoirs by Pierre Fatou and Gaston Julia around 1918–1920, lay relatively dormant for decades, and was revived spectacularly in the late 1970s and 1980s with computer graphics and the work of Benoit Mandelbrot, Adrien Douady, John Hubbard, and John Milnor.

Quadratic polynomials and the Mandelbrot set

The Mandelbrot set is the set of parameters c for which the Julia set of z2 + c is connected — equivalently, for which the orbit of the critical point 0 does not escape to infinity. Its intricate boundary encodes the bifurcation structure of the whole family. Major open problems include the density of hyperbolicity conjecture and whether the Mandelbrot set is locally connected (MLC).
