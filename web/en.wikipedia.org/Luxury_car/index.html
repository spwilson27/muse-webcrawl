Luxury car

A flagship full-size luxury sedan

Class: Premium automobile segment

Examples: Mercedes-Benz S-Class, BMW 7 Series, Rolls-Royce Phantom

A luxury car is a passenger automobile marketed as providing increased levels of comfort, equipment, performance, and refinement beyond regular cars, at a correspondingly premium price. The term is largely subjective and marketing-driven: qualities cited include superior materials, quiet and smooth ride, advanced driver-assistance and infotainment technology, spacious rear seating, and brand prestige.

Segments

The luxury market is conventionally divided into compact executive, executive (mid-size), and full-size flagship saloons — exemplified by the Mercedes-Benz C-, E-, and S-Class — together with luxury SUVs, coupés, and ultra-luxury ("über-luxury") marques such as Rolls-Royce, Bentley, and Maybach, whose cars may be largely hand-built and extensively personalised. Japanese manufacturers entered the segment decisively with the launch of Lexus (1989), followed by Acura and Infiniti, while newer brands such as Genesis have followed.

History and trends

Before the Second World War, luxury often meant bespoke coachbuilt bodies on chassis from makers such as Duesenberg, Hispano-Suiza, and Packard. Post-war mass production shifted luxury toward flagship models of volume manufacturers. More recently the segment has been reshaped by electrification (with instant torque and silence suiting luxury positioning), subscription features, and demand for luxury SUVs and crossovers, which now dominate sales in many markets.
