Time (magazine)

Time (stylised TIME) is an American news magazine published in New York City. Founded in 1923 by Briton Hadden and Henry Luce, it was the first weekly news magazine in the United States and for much of the twentieth century one of the most influential, recognised by its red border, its concise narrative style, and its annual Person of the Year selection. At its peak its worldwide circulation was in the millions; it has since shifted towards a digital-first model with reduced print frequency.

History

Hadden and Luce, Yale classmates, launched Time on 3 March 1923 to summarise the week's news for busy readers, rewriting stories from other publications in a brisk, distinctive voice. Under Luce, who led the company after Hadden's early death in 1929, Time Inc. grew to include Fortune, Life and Sports Illustrated. The magazine's annual "Man of the Year" (now Person of the Year) franchise began in 1927 with Charles Lindbergh, and its covers and framing shaped American political discourse for decades. The Time 100 list of influential people began in 1999. After mergers with Warner Communications and the troubled AOL combination, the print magazine declined with the industry; Time Inc. was sold to Meredith Corporation in 2018, and the magazine was promptly resold to Salesforce co-founder Marc Benioff and his wife Lynne.

Style and features

Time pioneered a formula of short, sharply written news summaries organised in departments, cover stories personifying the news, and stylistic tics ("Time-speak") that were widely parodied. Special issues include Person of the Year, the Time 100, and the magazine's reporting of major events. European, Asian and South Pacific editions were published for many years; the brand now operates principally through its website and events.
