Grand Paris Express

Planned network of the Grand Paris Express

The Grand Paris Express is a group of new rapid transit lines under construction in the Île-de-France region of France. The project comprises four new lines of the Paris Métro (lines 15, 16, 17, and 18) and extensions of existing lines 11 and 14. It is one of the largest transport infrastructure projects in Europe.

Project

The network will add about 200 kilometres of mostly underground track and 68 new stations, largely in the suburbs surrounding Paris, enabling suburb-to-suburb travel without passing through the city centre. Line 15, a ring line of about 75 kilometres, is the backbone of the scheme; lines 16 and 17 serve the north and east, including Charles de Gaulle Airport; line 18 serves the southwest, including Orly Airport and the Saclay plateau. The fully automated trains are expected to carry around two million passengers a day at completion.

History

The project originated in the late 2000s under President Nicolas Sarkozy as the "Grand Paris" plan, merging with the regional Arc Express proposal in 2011. The Société du Grand Paris (now Société des grands projets) was created to deliver it. Construction began in 2015; extensions of Line 14 opened for the 2024 Paris Olympics, while the new lines are scheduled to open in phases between the mid-2020s and around 2031, after repeated cost increases and delays pushed the budget to roughly €36–42 billion.

Impact

The project is intended to reduce car dependence, connect deprived suburbs to employment centres, and anchor large-scale urban development around new stations across the metropolis.
