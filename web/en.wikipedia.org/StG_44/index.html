The StG 44, the first successful assault rifle.

The StG 44 (abbreviation of Sturmgewehr 44, "assault rifle 44") is a German assault rifle developed during World War II by Hugo Schmeisser. It is also known by its early designations as the Maschinenpistole 43, MP 43, and MP 44. The StG 44 was the first successful assault rifle, with design features including an intermediate cartridge, controllable automatic fire, a more compact design than a battle rifle with a reduced muzzle velocity, and a larger magazine capacity.

Development

Experience on the Eastern Front showed that most infantry combat occurred within 400 metres, where full-power rifle cartridges were unnecessarily powerful and uncontrollable in automatic fire. German engineers developed the shortened 7.92×33mm Kurz intermediate round, and weapons such as the MKb 42 were tested from 1942. Adolf Hitler initially resisted the new rifle concept, so it was produced under the cover designation of a machine pistol (MP 43/44). Troops' enthusiastic reports led Hitler to approve the program and, in 1944, to bestow the propaganda name Sturmgewehr — "storm rifle", the origin of the term "assault rifle".

Service and legacy

Around 425,000 StG 44s were produced by the end of the war. It was issued especially on the Eastern Front and remained in limited use after 1945 with some East German units, Yugoslav paratroopers (into the 1980s), and various irregular forces; examples have surfaced in conflicts into the 21st century. The StG 44 strongly influenced postwar thinking about infantry weapons: Mikhail Kalashnikov acknowledged the general concept's influence on the environment that produced the AK-47, and the intermediate-cartridge select-fire design became the template for modern assault rifles such as the M16 and AK series.
