Pepper spray

Pepper spray, also known as OC spray (from oleoresin capsicum), is a lachrymator — a chemical compound that irritates the eyes to cause tears, pain, and temporary blindness. It is used in policing, riot control, and personal self-defence, and as a deterrent against aggressive animals such as bears and dogs.

Effects

The active ingredient, capsaicin, is derived from chili peppers. On contact it causes immediate closing of the eyes, difficulty breathing, a burning sensation on the skin, and disorientation, typically lasting 20–45 minutes without permanent harm in most cases.

History and legality

OC sprays were developed in the 1970s and widely adopted by law enforcement in the 1980s and 1990s as a less-lethal alternative. Legality for civilian possession varies widely by country and jurisdiction; it is restricted or prohibited in some places.
