Administration (British company law)

Administration in British company law is an insolvency procedure under the Insolvency Act 1986 in which an administrator is appointed to manage a company's affairs, business and property. Its statutory objectives are, in order, to rescue the company as a going concern, to achieve a better result for creditors than liquidation, or to realise property for secured or preferential creditors.

Procedure

An administrator may be appointed by the court, by a qualifying floating charge holder, or out of court by the company or its directors. Appointment triggers a moratorium that prevents creditors from enforcing claims while the administrator trades or sells the business.

Reform

The modern regime, reshaped by the Enterprise Act 2002, streamlined the older administrative receivership system and emphasised company rescue. "Pre-pack" administrations, where a sale is agreed before appointment, are common but controversial.
