The Caroline Islands (Spanish: Islas Carolinas) are a widely scattered archipelago of about 600 small islands in the western Pacific Ocean, northeast of New Guinea. Politically they are divided between the Federated States of Micronesia (the central and eastern islands) and the Republic of Palau (at the western end).

Geography and peoples

The islands range from mountainous volcanic islands such as Pohnpei and Kosrae to low coral atolls. Their indigenous peoples speak a variety of Micronesian languages and are renowned for long-distance voyaging by outrigger canoe, using traditional navigation by stars, swells, and bird flight. On Yap, huge stone discs (rai stones) were used as a form of money.

History

Named by Spanish explorers for King Charles II of Spain, the Carolines were claimed by Spain but effectively colonised only late in the 19th century. In 1899, after the Spanish–American War, Spain sold the islands to Germany. Japan seized them in 1914 and administered them under a League of Nations mandate; they saw major fighting in the Second World War, notably at Truk (Chuuk) Lagoon. After the war they became part of the US-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, before achieving independence as the Federated States of Micronesia (1986) and Palau (1994).
