Polar motion

PhenomenonQuasi-periodic movement of Earth's rotation axis relative to its crust
ComponentsChandler wobble (~433 days), annual wobble, long-term drift
Monitored byInternational Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service (IERS)

Polar motion is the movement of the Earth's rotation axis relative to its crust, so that the positions of the geographic poles wander over the surface by several metres. It is measured by the coordinates of the Celestial Intermediate Pole with respect to the terrestrial reference frame.

Components

The principal components are the Chandler wobble, a free oscillation with a period of about 433 days named for its discoverer Seth Carlo Chandler, and an annual forced wobble driven by seasonal redistribution of air and water masses. Superimposed is a slow secular drift of the mean pole, historically toward North America and influenced by glacial isostatic adjustment and modern ice-sheet melting.

Causes

Polar motion is excited by changes in the Earth's mass distribution and angular momentum: atmospheric and oceanic circulation, groundwater extraction, earthquakes, and the melting of glaciers and ice sheets.

Measurement

Tracking polar motion precisely is essential for satellite navigation, telescope pointing, and geodesy; it is monitored using VLBI, satellite laser ranging, and GNSS.
