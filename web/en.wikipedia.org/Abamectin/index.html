TypeInsecticide, acaricide, anthelmintic
Chemical classAvermectin (macrocyclic lactone)
Source organismStreptomyces avermitilis

Abamectin is a widely used insecticide and anthelmintic belonging to the avermectin family of macrocyclic lactones. It is a natural fermentation product of the soil bacterium Streptomyces avermitilis and consists mainly of avermectin B1a with a smaller proportion of avermectin B1b.

Mode of action

Like other avermectins, abamectin binds to glutamate-gated chloride channels found in invertebrate nerve and muscle cells, increasing chloride permeability and hyperpolarising the cells. The result is paralysis and death of the pest. These channels are absent in vertebrates, and the compound crosses the mammalian blood–brain barrier poorly, which accounts for its selective toxicity, although it remains hazardous to humans and other animals in sufficient doses.

Uses

Abamectin is used in agriculture to control insect and mite pests of citrus, pome fruit, vegetables, cotton, ornamentals and many other crops; it is especially valued against leafminers, thrips, aphids and spider mites, acting by contact and ingestion with translaminar activity in leaves. Formulations are also used in ant baits and cockroach control products, in veterinary medicine against parasites, and in some countries in public-health pest control. The related compound ivermectin is the principal avermectin used in human and animal medicine.

Environmental and safety profile

Abamectin binds strongly to soil and degrades rapidly in sunlight, but it is highly toxic to bees, aquatic invertebrates and fish, so labels restrict application near water and during crop flowering. Resistance in pest mites and insects has been documented where the compound is used intensively.
