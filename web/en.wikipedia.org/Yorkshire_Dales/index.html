Yorkshire Dales

Valley landscape in the Yorkshire Dales National Park

The Yorkshire Dales are a collection of river valleys and hills in northern England, historically part of the county of Yorkshire. The area is mainly in North Yorkshire, with parts in Cumbria and Lancashire. Most of the area falls within the Yorkshire Dales National Park, created in 1954 and extended in 2016, which covers about 2,178 square kilometres (841 sq mi). The word "dale" comes from the Old Norse dalr, meaning valley.

Geography

Each dale is named after its river: Wensleydale (the only major dale named after a village, Wensley, rather than its river, the Ure), Swaledale, Wharfedale, Nidderdale, Airedale, and Ribblesdale among them. The landscape is built from Carboniferous limestone and gritstone, producing limestone pavements such as those at Malham Cove, scars, gorges, waterfalls like Aysgarth Falls and Hardraw Force, and extensive cave systems including Gaping Gill. The Yorkshire Three Peaks — Whernside, Ingleborough, and Pen-y-ghent — are a popular walking challenge.

History and economy

The Dales were shaped by sheep farming, lead mining (especially in the 18th and 19th centuries), and quarrying. Field barns and drystone walls are characteristic features. Today tourism, agriculture, and cheesemaking (notably Wensleydale cheese) underpin the economy. Market towns include Hawes, Grassington, Pateley Bridge, and Sedbergh. The area inspired James Herriot's veterinary stories and provides the setting for adaptations of All Creatures Great and Small.
