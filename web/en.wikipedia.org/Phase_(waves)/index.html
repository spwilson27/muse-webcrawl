Phase (waves)

In physics and mathematics, the phase of a wave or other periodic function F(t) of some real variable t (such as time) is an angle-like quantity representing the fraction of the cycle covered up to t. It is expressed on a scale such that it varies by one full turn as the variable t passes through each period, and by 2π radians (or 360 degrees) over the full cycle. For a sinusoid, F(t) = A sin(2πft + φ), the quantity φ is the initial phase (or phase offset), and the full argument of the sine is the instantaneous phase.

Phase difference

The phase difference (or phase shift) between two waves of the same frequency is the fraction of a period by which one lags or leads the other. Two waves with zero phase difference are said to be in phase and combine constructively; waves half a cycle apart (π radians) are out of phase and interfere destructively. Phase difference is central to interference, diffraction, and the analysis of alternating-current circuits, where it is measured between voltage and current.

Phase velocity and group velocity

The speed at which a point of constant phase propagates is the phase velocity, which may differ from the group velocity at which energy or information travels in a dispersive medium.
