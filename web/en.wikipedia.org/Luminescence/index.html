Luminescence

Definition: Emission of light not caused by heat
Types: Photoluminescence, chemiluminescence, bioluminescence, electroluminescence, radioluminescence, thermoluminescence

Luminescence is the spontaneous emission of light by a substance not resulting from heat – sometimes called "cold light". It occurs when electrons in excited states return to lower energy states, releasing photons.

Types

Photoluminescence is stimulated by photon absorption, including fluorescence (immediate) and phosphorescence (delayed). Chemiluminescence results from chemical reactions, as in glow sticks; its biological form is bioluminescence. Electroluminescence is produced by electric current, used in LEDs and OLED displays. Other forms include triboluminescence (friction), radioluminescence (ionising radiation) and sonoluminescence (sound in liquid).

Applications

Luminescent materials are used in lighting, displays, safety markings, forensic analysis, medical imaging and scientific assays.
