Catalan's conjecture

Catalan's conjecture, now known as Mihăilescu's theorem, is a theorem in number theory stating that the only solution in positive integers of the equation xa − yb = 1 with exponents greater than 1 is 3² − 2³ = 1. In other words, 8 and 9 are the only consecutive perfect powers.

History

The conjecture was made by the Belgian mathematician Eugène Charles Catalan in 1844. Partial results accumulated over the following century and a half, notably by Robert Tijdeman in 1976, who showed there were only finitely many solutions. In 2002 the Romanian mathematician Preda Mihăilescu gave a complete proof, and the result has since carried his name.

Related problems

The theorem relates to broader questions about exponential Diophantine equations, including Pillai's conjecture, which remains open.
