Yemeni civil war (2014–present)

The Yemeni civil war is an ongoing conflict that began in late 2014, when the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah), allied with forces loyal to former president Ali Abdullah Saleh, seized the capital Sanaa and overthrew the internationally recognised government of President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi. In March 2015 a Saudi-led coalition of Arab states intervened militarily to restore Hadi's government, beginning an air campaign and blockade that, combined with the fighting, produced what the United Nations has repeatedly called one of the world's worst humanitarian crises. The Houthis — whom Saudi Arabia and its allies accuse of being backed by Iran — retain control of Sanaa and Yemen's northwest, while the south is contested between the government and the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council.

Background

Yemen, unified only in 1990, was impoverished and fractured: the Zaidi Shia Houthis of the north had fought six wars against Saleh's government in the 2000s, a southern secessionist movement resented rule from Sanaa, and al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula exploited the chaos. The 2011 Arab Spring uprising forced Saleh out in favour of his deputy Hadi, but the transition faltered amid economic collapse and a disputed national dialogue. Houthi forces exploiting anger over fuel-subsidy cuts took Sanaa in September 2014.

Course of the war

After Hadi fled to Aden and then Riyadh, the Saudi-led coalition launched Operation Decisive Storm on 26 March 2015. The front lines hardened into a stalemate: battles for Taiz, the coalition's 2018 offensive on the port of Hodeidah (halted by the UN-brokered Stockholm Agreement), and fighting over the gas-rich province of Marib. Saleh broke with the Houthis in December 2017 and was killed by them. In 2022 Hadi transferred power to a Presidential Leadership Council, and a UN-brokered truce from April to October 2022 sharply reduced violence; although it formally lapsed, a de facto truce largely held. From late 2023 the Houthis attacked Red Sea shipping in declared solidarity with Palestinians during the Gaza war, drawing US and British airstrikes, even as direct Saudi-Houthi talks made halting progress toward a settlement.

Humanitarian crisis

The war destroyed Yemen's economy and infrastructure. A UNDP assessment estimated 377,000 deaths by the end of 2021 — most from indirect causes such as hunger and preventable disease — and projected the toll rising thereafter. Around 4.5 million people have been internally displaced; millions face acute food insecurity, and the country suffered the largest cholera outbreak in recorded history, with more than two million suspected cases from 2016. Aid agencies have repeatedly warned of famine-like conditions, worsened by restrictions on imports through Hodeidah and the collapse of public salaries and services.
