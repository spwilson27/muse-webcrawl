Zwarte Piet

Zwarte Piet (Dutch for "Black Pete") is the companion of Sinterklaas (Saint Nicholas) in the folklore of the Netherlands and Belgium. In the traditional celebration on the evening of 5 December, Zwarte Piet helps distribute sweets and presents to children. The character is traditionally portrayed with a blackened face, curly hair, colourful Renaissance-style clothing and gold earrings, and is said to be black from soot collected while climbing down chimneys.

Origins

The modern figure was popularised by Jan Schenkman's 1850 book Sint Nikolaas en zijn knecht, which introduced a servant companion. Over time the single helper multiplied into many Pieten with assigned roles.

Controversy

Since the early 21st century the portrayal has been the subject of sustained public debate in the Netherlands, with critics describing the blackface depiction as a racist caricature and defenders regarding it as a harmless children's tradition. Many celebrations have shifted toward "soot Pieten" (Roetveegpieten) with only smudges of soot, and national broadcasters and large cities have adopted the soot version.
