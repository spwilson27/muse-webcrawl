The England national rugby union team represents England in men's international rugby union. It is administered by the Rugby Football Union (RFU) and plays home matches at Twickenham Stadium in London. England competes annually in the Six Nations Championship with France, Ireland, Italy, Scotland, and Wales, and every four years in the Rugby World Cup.

History

England played in the first rugby international, losing to Scotland at Raeburn Place, Edinburgh, in 1871. The side dominated much of the early Home Championship but went through long lean periods in the twentieth century. A resurgence began in the late 1980s under Will Carling, with Grand Slams in 1991 and 1992 and a World Cup final appearance in 1991. Under coach Clive Woodward and captain Martin Johnson, England reached world number one and won the 2003 Rugby World Cup in Sydney, beating hosts Australia 20–17 after extra time with Jonny Wilkinson's last-minute drop goal — still the only World Cup win by a northern-hemisphere team.

Later years

England reached further World Cup finals in 2007 (losing to South Africa) and 2019 (again losing to South Africa), won Six Nations titles including a Grand Slam in 2016 under Eddie Jones, and finished third at the 2023 World Cup in France.

Identity and stadium

England play in white shirts with navy shorts and wear a red rose emblem, adopted from the nineteenth century. Twickenham, owned by the RFU and holding over 82,000 spectators, is the largest dedicated rugby stadium in the world. The anthem "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" is the crowd's signature song.

Records

Jason Leonard was long England's most-capped player, later surpassed by Ben Youngs. Jonny Wilkinson remains England's record points scorer, and Rory Underwood its leading try scorer of the amateur and early professional era; Maro Itoje and Owen Farrell are among the leading figures of recent squads.
