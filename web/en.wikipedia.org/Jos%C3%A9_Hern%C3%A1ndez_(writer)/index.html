José Hernández (writer)

José Rafael Hernández y Pueyrredón (10 November 1834 – 21 October 1886) was an Argentine poet, journalist, and politician, best known as the author of Martín Fierro, the epic poem of gaucho life that is considered the foundational work of Argentine literature.

Life

Hernández was born on a ranch near San Martín, Buenos Aires Province, and spent part of his childhood in the countryside, gaining firsthand knowledge of the gauchos whose world he would later depict. He fought in the Argentine civil wars, worked as a journalist in Paraná and Buenos Aires, and was involved in politics in Corrientes and Entre Ríos, at times in opposition to Domingo Faustino Sarmiento's centralizing policies.

Martín Fierro

El gaucho Martín Fierro (1872) tells of a gaucho conscripted into frontier military service who deserts, becomes an outlaw, and laments the destruction of the traditional gaucho way of life. Its sequel, La vuelta de Martín Fierro (1879), charts his return and reconciliation. Written in the gauchos' own speech and meter, the poem was an immediate popular success and later came to be regarded as Argentina's national epic. Hernández also founded the newspaper El Río de la Plata and served as a legislator in Buenos Aires, where he died in 1886.
