Stephen King

Born: 21 September 1947, Portland, Maine, U.S.
Occupation: Author
Genres: Horror, supernatural fiction, suspense, fantasy
Spouse: Tabitha Spruce

Stephen Edwin King (born September 21, 1947) is an American author. Widely known for his horror novels, he has been crowned the "King of Horror". He has also explored other genres, among them suspense, crime, science-fiction, fantasy, and mystery.

King's books have sold more than 400 million copies, and many have been adapted into films and television series, including The Shawshank Redemption, The Green Mile, It, and The Shining.

Career

King's first published novel, Carrie (1974), was a success that allowed him to write full-time; he had famously retrieved the manuscript from the trash after his wife Tabitha encouraged him to finish it. It was followed by 'Salem's Lot (1975), The Shining (1977), The Stand (1978), and It (1986). The eight-volume Dark Tower series (1982–2012) links much of his fictional universe. Under the pen name Richard Bachman he published several novels, including Rage and The Running Man.

Life and style

King lives in Maine, the setting of many of his stories' fictional towns such as Derry and Castle Rock. In 1999 he was severely injured when struck by a van while walking, an experience later woven into his fiction. His nonfiction book On Writing (2000) is a widely read memoir and craft guide. His style is noted for detailed characterisation, vernacular dialogue, and the intrusion of the supernatural or the horrific into ordinary small-town American life. He received the National Medal of Arts in 2015.
