Zolotoy Rog (Russian: Золотой Рог, lit. "Golden Horn") is a sheltered horn-shaped bay in Vladivostok, Russia, around which much of the city's port and historic centre is built. An inlet of the Peter the Great Gulf in the Sea of Japan, it divides the Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula and is one of the finest natural harbours in the Russian Far East.

Name and history

The bay was named by analogy with the Golden Horn of Constantinople (Istanbul), which it resembles in shape. Vladivostok was founded as a military post on its shore in 1860, and the harbour became the home of the Russian Pacific Fleet and a major commercial port, ice-free in practice thanks to icebreaker support in winter. The bay was shallowed and reshaped by port construction over the decades, and its waters have suffered heavy pollution from naval and industrial activity.

Zolotoy Bridge

The bay is spanned by the Zolotoy Bridge, a cable-stayed bridge opened in August 2012 for the APEC summit held in Vladivostok that year, which connected the city centre directly with the Churkin district across the harbour.
