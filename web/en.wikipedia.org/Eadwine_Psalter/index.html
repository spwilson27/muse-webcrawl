Eadwine Psalter

The Eadwine Psalter is a heavily illuminated 12th-century English psalter, produced around 1155–1160 at Christ Church, Canterbury, probably for use at the cathedral priory. It is named after the scribe-monk Eadwine, whose famous portrait appears in the manuscript. The psalter is now held by the Wren Library of Trinity College, Cambridge (MS R.17.1).

Contents

The manuscript presents the Psalms in three Latin versions side by side (Romanum, Gallicanum and Hebraicum), with interlinear glosses in Old English and Anglo-Norman French — a remarkable witness to trilingual scholarship in post-Conquest England. It also contains a calendar, computistical material, canticles and prayers.

Illumination

Its decoration includes a celebrated cycle of full-page miniatures adapted from the 9th-century Utrecht Psalter, itself present at Canterbury, along with historiated initials and the large author portrait of Eadwine at work, one of the most famous images of a medieval scribe. At the end is a series of coloured drawings of the monastic buildings and waterworks of Christ Church.

Significance

The Eadwine Psalter is regarded as one of the masterpieces of English Romanesque book production and an important source for 12th-century language, monastic life and water engineering.
