Alexander Dubček (27 November 1921 – 7 November 1992) was a Slovak politician who led Czechoslovakia as First Secretary of the Communist Party from January 1968 until April 1969. He is best remembered for the Prague Spring, his attempt to build "socialism with a human face" through political and economic liberalisation, which was crushed by the Warsaw Pact invasion of August 1968.

Early life

Dubček was born in Uhrovec, Slovakia, and spent part of his childhood in the Soviet Union, where his father, a committed communist, helped establish the Interhelpo cooperative in Kyrgyzstan. The family returned to Czechoslovakia in 1938. During the Second World War Dubček joined the resistance and took part in the Slovak National Uprising of 1944, in which his brother Július was killed.

Rise to power

After the war Dubček rose steadily through the Slovak branch of the Communist Party, becoming its First Secretary in 1963. A quiet reformer critical of the conservative Antonín Novotný, he replaced Novotný as national party leader on 5 January 1968 with Moscow's acquiescence.

The Prague Spring

Dubček's Action Programme of April 1968 promised freedom of speech and the press, rehabilitation of purge victims, federalisation of the state between Czechs and Slovaks, and market elements in the economy. Censorship collapsed in practice, and a flowering of public debate followed, including the "Two Thousand Words" manifesto of June 1968. Alarmed, the Soviet leadership under Leonid Brezhnev demanded a halt; when negotiations failed, Warsaw Pact forces invaded on the night of 20–21 August 1968. Dubček was arrested and taken to Moscow, where he signed protocols conceding much of the reform programme.

Downfall and later life

Forced to preside over the dismantling of his own reforms, Dubček was replaced by Gustáv Husák in April 1969, briefly served as ambassador to Turkey, and was expelled from the party in 1970. He spent nearly two decades in obscurity as a forestry official in Slovakia. During the Velvet Revolution of 1989 he returned to public life as a hero of reform, was elected chairman of the Federal Assembly, and supported Václav Havel's presidency. He died in November 1992 from injuries sustained in a car accident, weeks before Czechoslovakia peacefully dissolved.
