Henley Royal Regatta is a rowing event held annually on the River Thames by the town of Henley-on-Thames, England. First staged in 1839, it is one of the most prestigious regattas in the world and a fixture of the English social season. Racing takes place over a straight course of 1 mile 550 yards (2,112 m) between Temple Island and the finish at Henley Bridge, in head-to-head knockout match races between two crews at a time — a format unusual in modern rowing, where multi-lane racing is standard.

History

The regatta grew out of a single race at the Henley fair in 1839, the year of the first Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race at Henley. It expanded rapidly into a multi-day amateur event, receiving the title "Royal" in 1851 when Prince Albert became its patron. Henley's amateur rules and definitions historically shaped the governance of rowing; disputes over amateurism at Henley helped prompt the founding of the Amateur Rowing Association. The regatta was for much of its history restricted to men, but women's events were progressively added — a full programme followed trials in the 1980s and 1990s, and the Princess Royal Challenge Cup for women's single sculls was instituted in 1993.

Events and trophies

The most famous trophies include the Grand Challenge Cup for men's eights, the oldest and most prestigious event, first raced in 1839; the Diamond Challenge Sculls for men's single scullers; the Stewards' Challenge Cup for coxless fours; the Queen Mother Challenge Cup for quadruple sculls; and the Princess Elizabeth Challenge Cup for schoolboy eights. Events are arranged in a hierarchy for elite, club, student and junior crews, attracting entries from universities, clubs and national squads worldwide.

The social event

Henley is as much a social as a sporting occasion. The Stewards' Enclosure enforces a celebrated dress code — blazers, ties and boaters for men, and dresses below the knee for women — while spectators line the towpath and watch from boats and the Leander Club enclosure. The regatta traditionally falls in the week before Wimbledon and, with Royal Ascot and Wimbledon, forms part of the classic English summer sporting season.
