A fire department (American English) or fire brigade (Commonwealth English), also known as a fire authority, fire district, or fire and rescue service, is an organization that provides firefighting services as well as other emergency services. Fire departments are most commonly a public sector organization that operate within a municipality, county, state, nation, or special district. Private and specialist fire protection organizations also exist, such as those providing protection at airports or industrial sites.

Organization

A fire department is typically led by a fire chief and organised into shifts or platoons stationed at fire stations throughout its jurisdiction. Departments may be fully career (staffed by full-time professional firefighters), fully volunteer, or a combination of both. In many jurisdictions, fire departments are also the primary providers of emergency medical services, and most calls for service are medical rather than fire-related.

Functions

Beyond extinguishing fires, modern fire departments provide technical rescue (vehicle extrication, rope, water and confined-space rescue), hazardous materials response, fire prevention and code enforcement, public fire safety education, and arson investigation through a fire marshal's office.

History

Organised firefighting dates to ancient Rome, where the Vigiles patrolled the city. After the Great Fire of London in 1666, insurance companies formed private brigades. The first organised municipal fire services emerged in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries; Edinburgh is credited with establishing the world's first municipal fire brigade in 1824 under James Braidwood.
