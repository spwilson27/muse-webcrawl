Pat Finucane

Born: 1949, Belfast, Northern Ireland
Died: 12 February 1989 (aged 39), Belfast
Occupation: Solicitor

Patrick Finucane (1949 – 12 February 1989) was an Irish solicitor from Belfast who was murdered by loyalist paramilitaries of the Ulster Defence Association (UDA) at his family home. His killing, one of the most controversial of the Troubles, became the focus of decades of investigations into alleged collusion between loyalist gunmen and elements of the British security forces.

Pat Finucane, whose 1989 murder led to repeated inquiries into state collusion.

Career

Finucane studied law at Trinity College Dublin and co-founded the Belfast law firm Madden & Finucane. He became well known for representing republican and nationalist clients, including IRA prisoners during the 1981 hunger strikes and the families of people killed by the security forces, as well as some loyalist clients. Shortly before his death, he had successfully challenged the British government in a case concerning the treatment of prisoners.

Murder and investigations

On 12 February 1989, two masked gunmen broke into the Finucane family home in north Belfast and shot him fourteen times in front of his wife and three children. Inquiries by Sir John Stevens later established that the gunmen had been assisted by agents working for the state: the UDA's intelligence chief Brian Nelson, who supplied targeting information, was a British Army agent, and weapons and information passed through a network involving police and military intelligence. In 2004 the UDA member Ken Barrett pleaded guilty to the murder. A 2012 review by Sir Desmond de Silva concluded there had been "shocking levels of state collusion", and Prime Minister David Cameron acknowledged collusion in Parliament, though the Finucane family has continued to campaign for a full public inquiry.
