Principal areas of Wales

The principal areas of Wales, also known as the principal areas (Welsh: prif ardaloedd), are the unitary authorities of Wales. There are 22 of them: nine counties, three cities and counties, and ten county boroughs. They were established on 1 April 1996 under the Local Government (Wales) Act 1994, which replaced the previous two-tier system of counties and districts with single-tier authorities responsible for all local government services in their areas.

Types

  • Counties — Anglesey (Ynys Môn), Carmarthenshire, Ceredigion, Denbighshire, Flintshire, Gwynedd, Monmouthshire, Pembrokeshire and Powys.
  • Cities and counties — Cardiff, Swansea and Wrexham. (Newport and Bangor are cities within other authorities; St Davids and St Asaph are small cathedral cities.)
  • County boroughs — Blaenau Gwent, Bridgend, Caerphilly, Conwy, Merthyr Tydfil, Neath Port Talbot, Newport, Rhondda Cynon Taf, Torfaen and the Vale of Glamorgan.

Governance

Each principal area is administered by a principal council, whose official name varies by type ("County Council", "County Borough Council", or "City and County Council"). Councillors are elected every five years. For ceremonial purposes Wales is still divided into eight preserved counties, each with a Lord Lieutenant, broadly corresponding to the counties that existed between 1974 and 1996 (such as Clwyd, Dyfed, Gwent and Mid Glamorgan).

History

The 1996 reorganisation followed proposals by the Secretary of State for Wales to simplify local administration. The new map partly revived the historic counties for some areas (such as Anglesey and Monmouthshire) while creating new industrial county boroughs in the South Wales Valleys. Proposals to reduce the number of principal areas by merger have been debated since devolution but not implemented.
