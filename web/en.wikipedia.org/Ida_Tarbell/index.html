Ida Tarbell

Ida Minerva Tarbell (1857–1944).

Ida Minerva Tarbell (November 5, 1857 – January 6, 1944) was an American writer, investigative journalist, biographer, and lecturer. She was one of the leading muckrakers of the Progressive Era and is best known for The History of the Standard Oil Company, a nineteen-part series published in McClure's Magazine from 1902 to 1904 and issued in book form in 1904. The exposé documented the unfair practices by which John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil trust had come to dominate the American oil industry.

Life and career

Tarbell was born in Erie County, Pennsylvania, in the heart of the oil region; her father was an independent oil producer whose livelihood was threatened by Standard Oil's secret railroad rebates during the "oil war" of 1872. She graduated from Allegheny College in 1880, one of the few women in her class, and studied in Paris before joining the staff of McClure's in 1894.

Legacy

Her Standard Oil investigation contributed to the climate of opinion behind the United States Supreme Court's 1911 decision ordering the dissolution of the Standard Oil monopoly into successor companies including ExxonMobil, BP, and Chevron. Tarbell also wrote widely read biographies of Abraham Lincoln and Napoleon Bonaparte. In 1999, The History of the Standard Oil Company was ranked fifth on New York University's list of the top 100 works of twentieth-century American journalism.
