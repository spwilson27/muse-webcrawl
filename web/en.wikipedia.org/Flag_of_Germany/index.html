Flag of Germany

Proportion3:5
Adopted23 May 1949 (West Germany); 3 October 1990 (reunified Germany)
DesignHorizontal tricolour of black, red, and gold

The flag of Germany is a tricolour consisting of three equal horizontal bands displaying the national colours of Germany: black, red, and gold (German: Schwarz-Rot-Gold).

History

The colours are associated with the uniforms of the Lützow Free Corps during the Napoleonic Wars and were displayed at the Hambach Festival of 1832 and by the Frankfurt Parliament of 1848, which first adopted black-red-gold as the flag of a German national state. The Weimar Republic used the tricolour from 1919, but Nazi Germany replaced it, and it was restored by the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) in 1949; East Germany used the same tricolour with its coat of arms added from 1959. Upon reunification in 1990 the plain tricolour became the flag of the whole country.

Variants

The federal service flag (Bundesdienstflagge) bears the federal shield (Bundesschild) and is reserved for official government use. Article 22 of the German Basic Law establishes black, red, and gold as the colours of the federal flag.
