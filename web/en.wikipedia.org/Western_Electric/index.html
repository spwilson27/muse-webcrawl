Western Electric

Western Electric Company
Industry: Telecommunications equipment
Founded: 1869 (as Gray and Barton)
Parent: AT&T (from 1881/1899)
Fate: Absorbed into AT&T Technologies (1984); eventually part of Lucent Technologies

The Western Electric Company was an American electrical engineering and manufacturing company that served as the primary supplier and manufacturing arm of the Bell System for more than a century, from 1881 until 1984. It produced the telephones, switches and cables used throughout the AT&T network.

History

The company grew out of an electrical workshop in Cleveland, Ohio, founded by Enos Barton with telegraph inventor Elisha Gray; it moved to Chicago and was incorporated as the Western Electric Manufacturing Company in 1872. After Alexander Graham Bell's telephone patents were established, AT&T acquired control of Western Electric in 1881 and made it the manufacturing unit of the Bell System, producing equipment designed by Bell Telephone Laboratories.

Western Electric's vast Hawthorne Works in Cicero, Illinois, was the site of the famous Hawthorne studies of the 1920s and 1930s, foundational to industrial psychology and management theory. During the Second World War the company manufactured radar and military communications equipment. The company also played a role in early sound film technology through its Western Electric sound system, and in the development of the transistor, which was manufactured in its plants.

Breakup and legacy

Following the breakup of the Bell System on 1 January 1984, Western Electric was folded into the new AT&T Technologies. It was later spun off with Bell Labs as part of Lucent Technologies in 1996. Classic Western Electric products, such as the Model 302 and Model 500 telephones, are remembered for their durability; the company also made the WE 300B vacuum tube, still prized by audiophiles.
