Invention of the telephone

Key patentUS 174,465 (Alexander Graham Bell, 1876)
First intelligible call10 March 1876, Boston
Other claimantsElisha Gray, Antonio Meucci, Johann Philipp Reis

The invention of the telephone was the culmination of work by several inventors in the 1870s to transmit speech electrically. Alexander Graham Bell received the first United States patent for the device in March 1876 and is generally credited as its inventor, though the priority of the invention has long been disputed.

Background

Early experiments included Johann Philipp Reis's apparatus of 1861, which could transmit musical tones and faint speech, and Antonio Meucci's voice-communication device, for which he filed a patent caveat in 1871 but could not afford to renew it. Telegraph engineer Elisha Gray was working along similar lines with a liquid transmitter.

Bell's patent and first call

Bell's patent application was filed on 14 February 1876, the same day Gray filed a caveat describing a similar design — a coincidence that fuelled decades of litigation. On 10 March 1876 Bell spoke the famous words "Mr. Watson, come here, I want to see you" to his assistant Thomas Watson over a liquid-transmitter apparatus in Boston. The Bell Telephone Company was founded in 1877, and telephony spread rapidly.

Disputes and recognition

Court challenges, most notably the case that reached the US Supreme Court in 1888, upheld Bell's patent. In 2002 the US House of Representatives passed a resolution recognising Meucci's contributions, a symbolic gesture that remains debated by historians. Practical improvements — Edison's carbon microphone, Strowger's automatic exchange, and loading coils — turned the invention into a mass communication system.
