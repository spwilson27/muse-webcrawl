Chiafalo v. Washington

Chiafalo v. Washington, 591 U.S. 578 (2020), was a United States Supreme Court case concerning the authority of states to punish presidential electors who do not vote as pledged — so-called "faithless electors". The Court unanimously held that states may require electors to pledge to vote for the winner of the state's popular vote and may penalise or replace electors who break that pledge.

Chiafalo v. Washington

Court: Supreme Court of the United States

Decided: July 6, 2020

Citation: 591 U.S. 578

Outcome: Affirmed; states may penalise faithless electors

Background

In the 2016 presidential election, three Washington state electors pledged to Hillary Clinton instead voted for Colin Powell, and were fined $1,000 each under state law. One of them, Peter Chiafalo, challenged the fine, arguing that the Twelfth Amendment and the First Amendment give electors free discretion. The Washington Supreme Court upheld the fine, and the case was taken up alongside a companion case from Colorado, decided per curiam the same day.

Decision

Writing for the Court, Justice Elena Kagan held that Article II gives states broad power over the appointment of electors, including the power to condition appointment on a pledge and to sanction faithless voting. The Court reasoned that nothing in the Constitution expressly grants electors independent judgment, and that longstanding practice supports state control.
