Build-operate-transfer

Build-operate-transfer (BOT) is a form of project delivery and financing used mainly for large infrastructure projects, and a common type of public–private partnership. Under a BOT arrangement, a public authority grants a concession to a private company or consortium to finance, design, build and operate a facility — such as a toll road, bridge, power plant, airport or water treatment plant — for a specified period, often 20 to 30 years. During the concession the operator collects the revenue from users and pays off the project debt and returns to investors. At the end of the term, ownership of the facility transfers back to the public authority.

Structure

A BOT project is typically carried out through a special-purpose project company, financed largely by debt secured against the project's future cash flows rather than by the sponsors' balance sheets (project finance). Construction is handled under a fixed-price contract, and operations under a long-term operating agreement. The arrangement allows governments to deliver infrastructure without immediate public borrowing, while transferring construction and operating risks to the private sector; in exchange, the public accepts user charges, long concession periods, and the risk that demand may be lower than forecast.

Variants

Related models include build-own-operate-transfer (BOOT), build-own-operate (BOO), build-transfer-operate (BTO) and design-build-finance-operate (DBFO), differing mainly in the timing of ownership transfer and the allocation of ownership during the concession. BOT schemes have been used extensively for toll motorways in Europe and Asia, power stations in developing economies, and major works such as the Channel Tunnel (under a BOOT-style concession).
