Vedic astrology

Vedic astrology, also known as Jyotiṣa (Sanskrit: ज्योतिष, "science of light") or Hindu astrology, is the traditional Hindu system of astrology. It is one of the six auxiliary disciplines (Vedāṅga) connected with the study of the Vedas. Vedic astrology differs from Western (tropical) astrology chiefly in using the sidereal zodiac, which accounts for the precession of the equinoxes, and in its emphasis on the Moon sign and the lunar mansions (nakṣatra).

Principles

A Vedic chart (kuṇḍalī) is cast for the moment and place of birth. The nine graha ("seizers") — Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, and the lunar nodes Rāhu and Ketu — are placed among the twelve signs (rāśi) and twelve houses (bhāva). The rising sign (lagna) and especially the Moon sign are central, and the 27 nakṣatras subdivide the zodiac for finer interpretation.

Predictive systems

Distinctive techniques include the daśā systems, especially the Viṃśottarī daśā, a 120-year cycle of planetary periods used to time events, and the many divisional charts (varga) examined for specific areas of life. Foundational texts include the Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra, attributed to the sage Parāśara, and the Bṛhat Jātaka of Varāhamihira (6th century CE).

Cultural role

Jyotiṣa remains widely practised in India and Nepal, where astrologers are consulted for marriage compatibility, naming, choosing auspicious times (muhūrta) for ceremonies and ventures, and medical and career questions. Like other forms of astrology it is regarded by the scientific community as a pseudoscience.
