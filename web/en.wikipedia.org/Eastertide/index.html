Eastertide

Eastertide (also known as Eastertime or the Easter season) is the period of fifty days in the Christian liturgical year from Easter Sunday to Pentecost Sunday. It is celebrated as a single joyful feast — sometimes called the "Great Sunday" of the year — commemorating the resurrection of Jesus and, in its final days, his Ascension and the coming of the Holy Spirit.

Observance

In the Roman Catholic Church and many other Western churches, Eastertide comprises the seven weeks (fifty days counting inclusively) following Easter Day. The season includes the Ascension of the Lord, celebrated on the fortieth day or the following Sunday, and concludes with Pentecost. The liturgical colour is white or gold; the paschal candle is lit at services, "Alleluia" returns to the liturgy after its Lenten absence, and in place of the Angelus the Regina caeli is sung or recited.

Structure

The first eight days form the Octave of Easter, each day treated liturgically as Easter Day itself, and traditionally the season of baptismal preparation culminates for neophytes. Sundays of the season are numbered as the Second through Seventh Sundays of Easter. In the Eastern Orthodox Church the corresponding season, Pascha, begins at the Paschal Vigil and Bright Week is marked by open royal doors and processions; the season ends with the leave-taking before Ascension and Pentecost.

Customs

Folk customs of the season in various countries include Easter eggs, lamb dishes, processions, and the blessing of homes. The Easter season also encompasses Rogation days of prayer for the harvest in some traditions (though these fall just before the Ascension).
