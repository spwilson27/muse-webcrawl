Inside Passage
LocationPacific Northwest coast of North America
ExtentFrom Puget Sound, Washington, to Skagway, Alaska
LengthAbout 1,600 km (1,000 mi) of channels

The Inside Passage (French: Passage Intérieur) is a coastal route for ships and boats along a network of passages which weave through the islands on the Pacific coast of the North American Fjordland. The route extends from northwestern Washington state in the United States, through western British Columbia in Canada, to southeastern Alaska.

Geography

The passage is formed by the fjords and channels between the mainland and the archipelagos of the coast, including Vancouver Island, Haida Gwaii, and the Alexander Archipelago. Because it is sheltered from the open Pacific, it offers comparatively protected waters for navigation.

Use

The route is heavily used by cruise ships, freight traffic, tugs with barges, and fishing vessels. The Alaska Marine Highway ferry system and BC Ferries connect the isolated coastal communities along the route. The scenery of mountains, glaciers and rainforest makes it a major tourist destination.
