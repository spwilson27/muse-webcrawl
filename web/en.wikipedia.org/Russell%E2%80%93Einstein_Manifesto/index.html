Russell–Einstein Manifesto

The Russell–Einstein Manifesto was issued in London on 9 July 1955 by the philosopher Bertrand Russell, in the midst of the Cold War. It highlighted the dangers posed by nuclear weapons and called for world leaders to seek peaceful resolutions to international conflict. The manifesto was signed by eleven leading scientists and intellectuals, most prominently Albert Einstein, who signed it days before his death in April 1955 — his last public statement.

Content

The manifesto warned that a war fought with hydrogen bombs might extinguish human life, and urged the public and governments to "remember your humanity, and forget the rest". It asked scientists to assemble in conference to assess the perils of weapons of mass destruction and called on the world's peoples to press their governments to renounce war as an instrument of policy.

Signatories and legacy

Besides Russell and Einstein, the signatories included Max Born, Percy Bridgman, Leopold Infeld, Frédéric Joliot-Curie, Hermann Muller, Linus Pauling, Cecil Powell, Józef Rotblat and Hideki Yukawa. The manifesto led directly to the Pugwash Conferences on Science and World Affairs, first held in 1957 in Pugwash, Nova Scotia, which became an influential channel of scientist-to-scientist diplomacy; Rotblat and the Pugwash Conferences were jointly awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1995.
