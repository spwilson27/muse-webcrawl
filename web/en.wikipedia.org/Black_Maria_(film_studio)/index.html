Black Maria (film studio)

Built1893
LocationWest Orange, New Jersey
OwnerThomas Edison
Closed1901

The Black Maria was Thomas Edison's movie production studio in West Orange, New Jersey, generally regarded as the first American film studio. Built in late 1892 and completed in 1893 at a cost of about $637, the tar-paper-covered building earned its nickname — slang for a police prisoner van — from its dark, cramped appearance.

Design

The studio was mounted on a rotating platform so it could follow the sun, and its roof opened to admit daylight, then the only practical light source for filming. Edison's Kinetograph camera recorded short subjects: vaudeville acts, dancers, boxers, strongmen, and scenes staged by cameraman William K. L. Dickson.

Productions and legacy

Famous films made at the Black Maria include Blacksmith Scene (1893), Fred Ott's Sneeze (1894), and boxing matches with champion James J. Corbett. Production moved to a glass studio in the Bronx in 1901, and the original building was demolished in 1903. A replica stands at the Thomas Edison National Historical Park.
