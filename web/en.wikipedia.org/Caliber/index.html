Caliber

In firearms, caliber (or calibre) is the specified nominal internal diameter of the gun barrel bore — regardless of how or where the bore is measured — and hence the diameter of the projectile intended for it. It is measured in inches or in millimetres. In the United States it is often expressed in hundredths or thousandths of an inch (e.g. .45 caliber, .223 caliber), while in most other countries metric designations are used (e.g. 9 mm, 5.56 mm).

Measurement

Nominal caliber is not always an exact measurement: in a rifled barrel the bore may be specified by the diameter across the lands or across the grooves, and historical naming conventions often depart from the true bullet diameter. For example, the .38 Special actually fires a bullet about 0.357 inches in diameter. Shotguns are described by gauge, a related but different system based on the number of lead balls of bore diameter that make up a pound.

Barrel length

"Caliber" is also used for the length of large gun barrels expressed in multiples of the bore diameter: a 16-inch/50-caliber naval gun has a bore of 16 inches and a barrel 50 × 16 inches = 800 inches long.

Cartridge naming

Caliber forms part of cartridge designations such as .30-06 Springfield (0.30-inch bore, adopted in 1906) and 7.62×51mm NATO (bullet diameter × case length). Two cartridges of similar nominal caliber may be entirely different and non-interchangeable, so full cartridge names are used for safety.
