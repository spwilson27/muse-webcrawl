2001: A Space Odyssey is a 1968 epic science fiction film produced and directed by Stanley Kubrick. The screenplay was written by Kubrick and Arthur C. Clarke, and was inspired by Clarke's 1951 short story "The Sentinel" and other of his stories. The film follows a voyage by astronauts Dave Bowman and Frank Poole, assisted by the sentient supercomputer HAL 9000, to Jupiter after the discovery of an alien monolith.

Plot

The film moves from a prehistoric sequence, "The Dawn of Man", in which a monolith inspires a tribe of apes to discover the use of a bone as a weapon, through a famous match cut to an orbiting spacecraft in 2001, to Dr Heywood Floyd's investigation of a monolith excavated on the Moon, and finally to the Discovery One mission, HAL's breakdown and murder of the crew, and Bowman's journey through the Star Gate, ending in his transformation into the Star Child.

Production and reception

Renowned for its scientifically accurate depiction of space flight, pioneering special effects, and minimal dialogue, the film won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects. Initial reviews were sharply divided, but it became the highest-grossing North American film of 1968 and is now widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential films ever made; it was selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry in 1991.
