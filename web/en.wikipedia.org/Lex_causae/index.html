Lex causae

Lex causae (Latin for "the law of the cause") is a term in conflict of laws (private international law) denoting the law that a court applies to decide the substance of a case once its choice-of-law rules have identified the applicable legal system.

Use

When a dispute has connections to more than one jurisdiction, the forum court first characterises the issue, then applies its choice-of-law rules — referring, for example, to the lex loci contractus for a contract or the lex loci delicti for a tort — to select the lex causae. Procedural matters, by contrast, are governed by the lex fori, the law of the forum. Questions such as renvoi and the incidental question arise in determining the precise content of the lex causae.
