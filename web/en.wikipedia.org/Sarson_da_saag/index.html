Sarson da saag

Sarson da saag is a traditional vegetarian dish from the Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent, made from mustard greens (sarson) and spices such as ginger and garlic. It is a staple winter food in Punjab and is regarded as one of the most iconic dishes of Punjabi cuisine.

Preparation

The dish is prepared by slowly simmering chopped mustard leaves, often together with spinach (palak), bathua (chenopodium), and other greens, until soft. The cooked greens are pounded or blended into a coarse paste, thickened with maize flour, and finished with a tempering (tadka) of butter or ghee, garlic, dried red chillies, and ginger.

Serving

Sarson da saag is traditionally served hot with makki di roti (flatbread made from cornmeal), topped with a knob of white butter and often accompanied by jaggery and a glass of lassi. The combination is celebrated in Punjabi popular culture and is strongly associated with the harvest season and rural life in Punjab.

Etymology

In Punjabi, sarson denotes the mustard plant and saag refers to any dish of cooked leafy greens; hence sarson da saag means "saag of mustard (greens)."
