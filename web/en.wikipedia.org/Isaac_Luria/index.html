Isaac ben Solomon Luria (1534 – July 25, 1572), known as Ha'ARI (the Lion) or Arizal, was a leading rabbi and Jewish mystic in the community of Safed in Ottoman Syria. He is considered the father of contemporary Kabbalah; his teachings, known as Lurianic Kabbalah, reshaped Jewish mysticism and influenced Jewish religious life for centuries.

Life

Luria was born in Jerusalem to an Ashkenazi father and Sephardi mother, and raised in Egypt, where he studied rabbinic law under leading scholars and devoted years to the study of the Zohar. In 1570 he moved to Safed, then the centre of a remarkable circle of kabbalists that included Moses ben Jacob Cordovero. Luria taught there for only about two years before dying in an epidemic at the age of 38, but his impact was immense.

Teachings

Luria wrote very little himself; his doctrines were recorded by his disciple Hayyim Vital, principally in Etz Chaim (Tree of Life). At the heart of his system stand three ideas: tzimtzum, the contraction of the infinite divine light (Ein Sof) to make space for creation; shevirat ha-kelim, the "breaking of the vessels" that once held the divine light, scattering holy sparks through the material world; and tikkun, the restoration of the cosmos that human beings accomplish through prayer, commandments, and ethical action by liberating the sparks.

Influence

Lurianic Kabbalah spread rapidly through the Jewish world, shaping prayer rites (the Nusach HaARI), customs such as Kabbalat Shabbat, and later movements including Sabbatianism and Hasidism. His tomb in Safed remains a place of pilgrimage.
