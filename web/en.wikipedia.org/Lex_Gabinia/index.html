Lex Gabinia

The lex Gabinia ("Gabinian Law") was an ancient Roman law enacted in 67 BC, granting the general Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus (Pompey) extraordinary command — a proconsular imperium over the entire Mediterranean Sea and its coasts — in order to combat the pirates who were disrupting Rome's grain supply.

Provisions

Proposed by the tribune Aulus Gabinius, the law gave Pompey command for three years over the sea and up to 50 miles inland, with authority over 500 ships, 120,000 infantry and 5,000 cavalry, and access to the public treasury. The Senate strongly opposed concentrating such power in one man, but the measure passed the popular assembly with the support of Julius Caesar, among others.

Results

Pompey divided the Mediterranean into sectors, cleared the western basin in about forty days, and defeated the Cilician pirates within three months. The law set a precedent for extraordinary commands, later echoed by the lex Manilia, which transferred the Mithridatic War to Pompey.
