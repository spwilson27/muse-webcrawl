Backsaw

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

TypeHand saw
Used forPrecision woodworking: tenons, dovetails, mitres
Typical length250–400 mm blade
TeethFine, typically 11–20 teeth per inch

A backsaw is a hand saw with a stiffening rib — the "back", traditionally of brass or folded steel — along the edge of the blade opposite the teeth. The back keeps the relatively thin blade rigid during the cut, preventing flexing and allowing thin-kerf, finely toothed blades that cut accurately. Backsaws are used for precision joinery, typically together with a bench hook or mitre box.

Types

The tenon saw is the largest common backsaw, used for cutting tenons and general bench work. Dovetail saws are smaller and finer-toothed for cutting dovetail joints, usually filed for rip cuts. Small gents' saws have a straight turned handle, while the larger mitre saw is used with a mitre box for accurate angle cuts. Japanese dozuki saws share the same stiffened-back principle but cut on the pull stroke.

Characteristics

Backsaw blades are usually 250–400 mm long with 11–20 teeth per inch, crosscut or rip filed depending on the task, and only a slight set to the teeth for a narrow, clean kerf. Quality examples have a heavy brass back that adds useful weight to the cut.
