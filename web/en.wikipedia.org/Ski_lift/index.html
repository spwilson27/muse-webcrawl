Ski lift

A ski lift is a mechanism for transporting skiers up a hill, most commonly at a ski resort. Lifts are essential to modern downhill skiing, replacing hours of climbing with a ride of a few minutes.

Ski lift

Types: Chairlift, gondola, cable car, surface lift
First modern chairlift: Sun Valley, 1936

Types

  • Aerial lifts carry passengers suspended above the ground: chairlifts, gondolas, aerial trams and hybrid lifts.
  • Surface lifts pull riders who remain on the snow: T-bars, button (Poma) lifts, rope tows and magic carpets.

History

The first purpose-built chairlift was installed at Sun Valley, Idaho, in 1936, adapted from a banana-loading conveyor by engineer James Curran. Detachable-grip chairlifts and high-capacity gondolas, developed from the 1980s, greatly increased uphill capacity and ride comfort.

Safety

Lifts are subject to rigorous inspection regimes; safety features include derailment sensors, emergency brakes and restraint bars on chairs.
