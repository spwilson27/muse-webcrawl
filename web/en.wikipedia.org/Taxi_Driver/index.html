Taxi Driver

Directed by: Martin Scorsese
Written by: Paul Schrader
Starring: Robert De Niro, Jodie Foster, Cybill Shepherd, Harvey Keitel
Music by: Bernard Herrmann
Released: February 8, 1976
Running time: 114 minutes

Taxi Driver is a 1976 American neo-noir psychological drama film directed by Martin Scorsese, written by Paul Schrader, and starring Robert De Niro as Travis Bickle, a mentally unstable Vietnam veteran who works as a night-time taxi driver in New York City. The film also features Jodie Foster, Cybill Shepherd, Harvey Keitel, Peter Boyle, Leonard Harris and Albert Brooks in supporting roles, and features the final complete score of composer Bernard Herrmann.

Plot

Travis Bickle, a lonely and insomniac ex-Marine, takes a job driving a cab through New York's seediest districts. Disgusted by the crime and decay he sees, rejected by the campaign worker Betsy whom he courts, and obsessed with rescuing Iris, a twelve-year-old prostitute, Bickle drifts toward violence. After shaving his head into a mohawk and failing to assassinate a presidential candidate, he guns down Iris's pimp and others in a brothel shootout; wounded but surviving, he is improbably hailed by the press as a hero.

Reception and legacy

The film won the Palme d'Or at the 1976 Cannes Film Festival and was nominated for four Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Actor. De Niro's improvised line "You talkin' to me?" became one of the most famous in cinema. Taxi Driver is regularly ranked among the greatest films ever made and was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1994. Its themes of urban alienation and vigilantism have remained influential, and the film has a notorious connection to John Hinckley Jr., whose obsession with Foster motivated his 1981 attempt to assassinate President Ronald Reagan.
