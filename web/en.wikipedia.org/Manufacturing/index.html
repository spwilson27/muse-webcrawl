Manufacturing

Sector: Secondary sector of the economy
Examples: Steel, automobiles, electronics, textiles
Related: Industry, factory system

Manufacturing is the creation or production of goods with the help of equipment, labour, machines, tools and chemical or biological processing or formulation. It is the essence of the secondary sector of the economy, transforming raw materials into finished products on a large scale.

History

For most of history, goods were made by hand in small workshops. The Industrial Revolution of the late 18th and 19th centuries introduced the factory system, powered machinery, interchangeable parts and the division of labour, first in British textiles and then in steel, transport and chemicals. In the 20th century, Henry Ford's assembly line brought mass production, later refined by lean manufacturing methods developed in Japan, and increasingly by industrial automation and robotics.

Processes

Manufacturing processes include casting, moulding, machining, joining, forming, additive manufacturing (3D printing) and assembly. Modern production is organised through supply chains linking suppliers of materials and components to assemblers and distributors, and is measured as a key component of gross domestic product in industrial economies.

Modern trends

Contemporary manufacturing is shaped by globalisation, computer-aided design and manufacturing, smart factories associated with "Industry 4.0", reshoring debates, and pressure to reduce energy use and emissions. China became the world's largest manufacturing nation in the early 21st century.
