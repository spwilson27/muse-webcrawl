Baja California

Capital: Mexicali
Largest city: Tijuana
Country: Mexico
Population: ~3.8 million

Baja California (Spanish: "Lower California") is a state in northwestern Mexico, occupying the northern half of the Baja California Peninsula. It is bordered by the U.S. state of California to the north, Sonora and Arizona to the northeast, the Gulf of California to the east, Baja California Sur to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west. Its capital is Mexicali and its largest city is Tijuana, on the border with San Diego.

Geography

The state is dominated by the Peninsular Ranges, including the Sierra de Juárez and Sierra de San Pedro Mártir, whose Picacho del Diablo (3,096 m) is the peninsula's highest peak. Much of the interior is arid desert, while the northwest enjoys a Mediterranean climate suited to wine production in the Valle de Guadalupe, Mexico's premier wine region.

History

The peninsula was first mapped by Spanish explorers in the 1530s and was long mistaken for an island. Jesuit and later Franciscan and Dominican missions were established from 1697 onward. Baja California became a Mexican territory and then, in 1952, the 29th state of Mexico, the last state admitted in the 20th century until Baja California Sur followed in 1974.

Economy

Manufacturing for export (maquiladoras), especially electronics and medical devices in Tijuana, is central to the economy, alongside tourism, agriculture in the Mexicali Valley, and fishing in the Gulf of California.
