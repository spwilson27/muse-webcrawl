Portsmouth Harbour is a large natural harbour in Hampshire, England, opening into the Solent between the Isle of Wight and the mainland. It is a drowned valley (ria) formed at the end of the last Ice Age. The harbour separates the city of Portsmouth, on Portsea Island to the east, from Gosport to the west.

Geography

The harbour entrance, between Old Portsmouth and Gosport, is narrow — less than 400 metres wide — and was historically defended by forts and towers on both sides. Inside, the harbour opens into a broad tidal basin of mudflats and channels, with arms extending north toward Fareham and the inlets of Portchester and Paulsgrove. The harbour covers roughly 1,700 hectares at high water, much of it intertidal mudflat of ecological importance, designated as a Site of Special Scientific Interest and a Special Protection Area for birds.

Naval base

Portsmouth has been a principal base of the Royal Navy since the Tudor period. Henry VII built the world's first dry dock there in the 1490s, and HM Naval Base Portsmouth remains one of the Navy's three operating bases, home to a large share of the surface fleet, including the Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers. The historic dockyard preserves HMS Victory, HMS Warrior, and the Mary Rose.

Civilian use

Portsmouth International Port operates ferry and freight services to France, Spain, and the Channel Islands, and a cross-harbour ferry links Portsmouth with Gosport. The harbour is also a busy centre for recreational sailing.
