Fox Movietone News

Fox Movietone News was a newsreel series produced by the Fox Film Corporation and later 20th Century Fox, running from 1928 to 1963. It was one of the earliest and most successful sound newsreels in the United States, bringing synchronized sound coverage of current events to cinema audiences before the era of television news.

History

William Fox acquired the rights to the Tri-Ergon sound-on-film process and launched Fox Movietone News in 1928. The first issue appeared on September 28, 1928, and the series soon became famous for its coverage of major events, including Charles Lindbergh's transatlantic flight celebrations, the Wall Street Crash of 1929, and later the Second World War. The newsreel's cameramen traveled the world recording speeches, sports, disasters, and ceremonies with synchronized sound, a novelty that gave Fox a competitive edge over silent rivals such as Pathé News and Hearst Metrotone News.

During the 1930s and 1940s, Fox Movietone News was issued twice weekly in the United States and in numerous international editions. Lowell Thomas served as a prominent commentator from 1935. The Second World War brought extensive combat coverage, and the series won Academy Awards recognition for its wartime documentaries. As television news expanded in the 1950s, theatrical newsreels declined, and Fox Movietone News ceased regular production in 1963.

Legacy

The surviving Fox Movietone News archive, comprising millions of feet of film, is held by the University of South Carolina's Moving Image Research Collections and by Fox's successor companies. It remains a major primary source for historians and documentary filmmakers studying twentieth-century events.
