Diamond (gemstone)

CompositionCarbon (C)
Hardness10 (Mohs scale)
GradingCut, color, clarity, carat weight ("four Cs")
Major sourcesRussia, Botswana, Canada, Australia, South Africa

A diamond is a solid form of the element carbon with its atoms arranged in a crystal structure called diamond cubic. As a gemstone, diamond is prized for its brilliance, fire, and exceptional hardness, and it is the traditional stone of engagement rings in many cultures.

Properties

Diamond is the hardest known natural material, ranking 10 on the Mohs scale. Its high refractive index and dispersion give cut diamonds their sparkle. Gem-quality stones are graded by the "four Cs": cut, color, clarity, and carat weight.

History and trade

Diamonds were first mined in India and traded along ancient routes. Major discoveries in South Africa in the 19th century and later in Russia, Botswana, and Canada reshaped the industry. The Kimberley Process was established to stem the trade in "blood diamonds" that fund conflict.

Synthetic diamonds

Laboratory-grown diamonds, produced by high-pressure high-temperature (HPHT) and chemical vapor deposition (CVD) methods, are chemically identical to mined diamonds and have become a growing segment of the gem market.
