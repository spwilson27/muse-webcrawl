Teresa of Ávila

Born28 March 1515, Ávila, Spain
Died4 October 1582, Alba de Tormes, Spain
Canonized1622, by Pope Gregory XV
Feast day15 October
HonorsDoctor of the Church (1970)

Saint Teresa of Ávila (born Teresa Sánchez de Cepeda y Ahumada; 28 March 1515 – 4 or 15 October 1582), also called Saint Teresa of Jesus, was a Spanish noblewoman who became a Carmelite nun, a prominent Spanish mystic, and a Counter-Reformation religious reformer and author. She founded the reformed Discalced Carmelites.

Life

Teresa entered the Carmelite Convent of the Incarnation in Ávila in 1535. After years of illness and spiritual struggle, she underwent a profound religious awakening around 1554, experiencing visions and mystical prayer that she described with caution and wit. In 1562 she founded the small Convent of St. Joseph in Ávila, inaugurating a stricter, contemplative observance; she went on to found sixteen more convents, aided by St. John of the Cross, who reformed the male branch.

Writings

Her works are classics of spiritual literature: her autobiography, The Life of Teresa of Jesus; The Way of Perfection; and The Interior Castle (1577), which charts the soul's journey through seven mansions toward union with God. She was canonized in 1622 and in 1970 became the first woman named a Doctor of the Church.
