Fare

A fare is the fee paid by a passenger for use of a public transport system: rail, bus, taxi, airplane, ferry, etc. In the case of air transport, the term airfare is often used. Fare structure is the system set up to determine how much travel on the network costs, and may be flat (the same price for all journeys), distance-based, zonal, or time-based (such as peak and off-peak pricing).

Collection

Fares have historically been collected in cash by conductors or drivers, or by the sale of tickets and tokens before travel. Modern systems use proof-of-payment inspection, turnstiles and gates, and electronic fare media such as contactless smart cards (for example London's Oyster card and Hong Kong's Octopus card), mobile ticketing and contactless bank cards. Fare evasion — travelling without a valid ticket — is penalised in most systems by fines or penalty fares.

Types of fares

  • Single and return fares for individual journeys;
  • Period tickets (day passes, weekly and season tickets) allowing unlimited travel within a period;
  • Concessionary fares at reduced prices for children, students, seniors and others;
  • Integrated fares valid across modes or operators within a region;
  • Free fares, where a system is funded so that passengers pay nothing at the point of use.

Fare policy

Fares rarely cover the full cost of public transport; most systems receive public subsidy. Debates over fare levels, fare capping, and free public transport concern the balance between revenue, ridership, equity and environmental goals.
