Bulgaria

Bulgaria, officially the Republic of Bulgaria, is a country in Southeast Europe. It is situated on the eastern portion of the Balkans, bordering Romania to the north, Serbia and North Macedonia to the west, Greece and Turkey to the south, and the Black Sea to the east. The capital and largest city is Sofia. Bulgaria covers a territory of 110,994 square kilometres and has a population of about 6.4 million.

History

The first Bulgarian Empire was founded in 681 AD by Khan Asparuh, and Bulgaria became a centre of Slavic culture during the Middle Ages; the Cyrillic script was developed in the Preslav and Ohrid literary schools of the First Empire. After centuries of Byzantine and then Ottoman rule — Bulgaria was part of the Ottoman Empire for nearly five centuries — the Principality of Bulgaria was established in 1878, and full independence as a kingdom was declared in 1908. Bulgaria fought on the side of the Central Powers in the First World War and of the Axis in the Second World War. In 1946 it became a communist people's republic aligned with the Soviet Union, and in 1990 it transitioned to democracy and a market economy.

Geography

Bulgaria's landscape is dominated by the Balkan Mountains (Stara Planina), which run east–west across the middle of the country, the Danubian Plain to the north, the Thracian Plain to the southeast, and the Rhodope Mountains in the southwest. The highest peak, Musala (2,925 m), is the tallest in the Balkans. The Danube forms most of the northern border. The climate is temperate and transitional between continental and Mediterranean.

Politics and economy

Bulgaria is a parliamentary republic and a member of the European Union (since 2007) and NATO (since 2004). It adopted the euro as its currency in 2026, replacing the lev. The economy is an industrialised, open free-market economy with significant sectors in energy, mining, metallurgy, agriculture, tourism and information technology.

Culture

Bulgaria is known for its folk music, rose oil production (centred on the Rose Valley near Kazanlak), yogurt and its distinctive strain Lactobacillus bulgaricus, and a rich heritage of Orthodox Christian monasteries, including Rila Monastery. The Bulgarian language is a South Slavic language written in Cyrillic.
