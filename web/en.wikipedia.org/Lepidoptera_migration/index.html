Lepidoptera migration

Lepidoptera migration is the seasonal movement of butterflies and moths over distances of hundreds to thousands of kilometres, undertaken to escape unfavourable conditions, exploit seasonal resources and breed. Many species migrate, but the best known is the monarch butterfly (Danaus plexippus), whose multi-generational circuit between Canada, the United States and overwintering sites in Mexico covers up to 4,800 km.

Patterns and mechanisms

Migrating Lepidoptera include the painted lady (Vanessa cardui), which undertakes the longest butterfly migration known — a circuit of some 14,000 km between Europe and tropical Africa — as well as the red admiral, cloudless sulphur, bogong moth and the death's-head hawkmoth. Because individual lifespans are short, most long journeys are completed by a relay of generations. Migrants navigate using a sun compass corrected by an internal circadian clock, and probably magnetic and polarised-light cues; monarchs' time compensation is mediated by clocks in their antennae. Many migrations are wind-assisted, with moths selecting fast-moving airstreams at altitude — a behaviour revealed by radar studies of species such as the silver Y moth.
