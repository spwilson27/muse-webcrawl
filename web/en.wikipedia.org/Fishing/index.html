Fishing

Fishing is the activity of trying to catch fish. Fish are often caught as wildlife from the natural environment (freshwater or marine), but may also be caught from stocked bodies of water such as ponds, canals, park wetlands and reservoirs. Fishing techniques include hand-gathering, spearing, netting, angling, shooting and trapping, as well as more destructive and often illegal techniques such as electrocution, blasting and poisoning.

History

Fishing is an ancient practice dating back at least to the Upper Paleolithic, some 40,000 years ago. Archaeological evidence — fish bones, bone hooks and cave paintings — shows that early hunter-gatherer societies relied on fish as an important source of protein. In ancient Egypt, the Nile provided abundant fish, and fishing with nets, spears and rods is depicted in tomb scenes from around 2000 BCE.

Commercial fishing

The commercial fishing industry harvests wild fish for profit, supplying a global food market. Modern industrial fishing uses large vessels with trawls, purse seines and longlines, guided by sonar and satellite data. Overexploitation has depleted many stocks; the Food and Agriculture Organization has reported that a substantial share of assessed fish stocks are fished beyond biologically sustainable levels, prompting quotas, marine protected areas and certification schemes.

Recreational fishing

Recreational fishing, or angling, is practised for pleasure and sport by millions of people. It ranges from fly fishing for trout in rivers to deep-sea game fishing for marlin and tuna. Many jurisdictions require licences and enforce catch limits, closed seasons and catch-and-release rules to conserve stocks.
