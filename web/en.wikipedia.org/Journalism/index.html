Journalism is the production and distribution of reports on the interaction of events, facts, ideas and people that are the "news of the day" and that inform society to at least some degree of accuracy. The word journalism applies to the occupation, the methods of gathering information, and the organising literary styles. Journalists' primary role is to gather, verify and present information about current events, trends and public affairs, traditionally for newspapers and periodicals and, since the twentieth century, also for radio, television and digital media.

History

News dissemination is ancient — from Rome's Acta Diurna to handwritten newsletters for merchants — but journalism as a distinct occupation emerged with the printed newspaper in the early seventeenth century (the first printed newspapers appeared in Strasbourg and Wolfenbüttel around 1605–1609) and spread with the periodical press. The nineteenth-century "penny press", telegraphy and the rotary press created mass-circulation newspapers; the twentieth century added news agencies, photojournalism, radio and television news, and eventually 24-hour cable news and online journalism.

Forms and genres

Journalistic output includes hard news reporting, feature writing, investigative journalism (prolonged inquiry into wrongdoing, as in the Watergate reporting of Bob Woodward and Carl Bernstein), business, sports, science and arts journalism, opinion and editorial writing, and newer forms such as data journalism, solutions journalism and citizen journalism enabled by social media. Genres are governed by conventions: the inverted pyramid lead, attribution of sources, and a norm — historically variable — of separating news from opinion.

Ethics and professional norms

Professional codes of journalism ethics emphasise truthfulness and accuracy, verification, independence from sources and advertisers, minimising harm, and accountability to the public. Freedom of the press is protected in many constitutional orders (for example the First Amendment to the US Constitution) and is monitored internationally by organisations such as Reporters Without Borders, whose Press Freedom Index documents threats to journalists worldwide, from censorship to violence.

Contemporary challenges

The digital transformation of media has undermined advertising-based business models, shrunk local newsrooms (producing "news deserts"), sped the news cycle, and raised concerns about misinformation and "fake news". Journalists increasingly work across multimedia formats, and audience metrics and social platforms shape editorial decisions.
