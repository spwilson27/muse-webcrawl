Miniatur Wunderland

Miniatur Wunderland (German for "miniature wonderland") is a model railway attraction in Hamburg, Germany, and the largest of its kind in the world. It is located in the historic Speicherstadt district and was built by the brothers Frederik and Gerrit Braun together with Stephan Hertz.

History

Construction began in December 2000, and the first three sections — Knuffingen, Central Germany and Austria — opened in August 2001. Further sections followed: Hamburg, the USA, Scandinavia, Switzerland, the Knuffingen Airport (opened in 2011 after six years of construction and a cost of about €3.5 million), Italy, and South America. A bridge to a neighbouring warehouse opened further expansion space, with sections depicting Monaco, Provence and other regions.

The layout

The exhibition includes more than 16 kilometres of track, over 1,100 trains with more than 10,000 wagons, hundreds of thousands of lights and human figurines, and tens of thousands of trees. A sophisticated computer system controls trains, road vehicles that charge themselves at hidden stations, ships, and a working airport where model aircraft taxi, take off and land. Day and night alternate every 15 minutes.

Reception

Miniatur Wunderland is one of Germany's most popular tourist attractions, drawing well over a million visitors annually. In 2016 it was voted the country's most popular attraction for international visitors in a German National Tourist Board poll.
