Density

Density
Symbolρ
SI unitkilogram per cubic metre (kg/m³)
Formulaρ = m / V

Density (volumetric mass density or specific mass) is the ratio of a substance's mass to its volume. The symbol most often used for density is ρ (the Greek letter rho), although the Latin letter D is also used. Mathematically, density is defined as mass divided by volume: ρ = m/V.

Units and values

The SI unit of density is the kilogram per cubic metre (kg/m³); grams per cubic centimetre (g/cm³) are also common. At standard conditions, water has a density of about 1,000 kg/m³, air about 1.2 kg/m³, while osmium and iridium, at roughly 22,600 kg/m³, are the densest naturally occurring elements. The density of a material varies with temperature and pressure, generally decreasing as temperature rises.

Applications

Density determines whether an object floats in a fluid: by Archimedes' principle, an object less dense than the fluid will float. Density measurements are used to identify substances, assess purity, design ships and aircraft, and characterise stars and planets. Densities of mixtures and solutions are routinely measured with hydrometers and pycnometers.
