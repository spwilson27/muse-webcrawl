Frank P. Ramsey

Frank Plumpton Ramsey (1903–1930), philosopher, mathematician and economist.

Frank Plumpton Ramsey (22 February 1903 – 19 January 1930) was a British philosopher, mathematician and economist who made fundamental contributions to all three fields before his death at the age of 26. John Maynard Keynes and Ludwig Wittgenstein both regarded him as a thinker of the first rank.

Life and work

Ramsey was educated at Winchester College and Trinity College, Cambridge. As an undergraduate he translated Wittgenstein's Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus into English and published an influential review of it; his discussions with Wittgenstein contributed to the latter's later philosophy. In 1924 he became a fellow of King's College, Cambridge.

In philosophy, Ramsey's "The Foundations of Mathematics" (1925) defended and refined logicism, and he proposed a pragmatic, redundancy theory of truth and a theory of partial belief that founded modern decision theory: his essay "Truth and Probability" (1926) showed how degrees of belief can be measured through betting behaviour, anticipating Bayesian subjective probability, and sketched what is now called the Ramsey–de Finetti theorem.

In mathematics, his 1930 paper "On a Problem of Formal Logic" gave rise to Ramsey theory, a branch of combinatorics studying the conditions under which order must appear in large structures. In economics, he produced three landmark papers: on optimal taxation (the Ramsey rule), on optimal saving (the Ramsey–Cass–Koopmans growth model), and on the theory of public finance — all written in his early twenties. He died in January 1930 following complications from jaundice and surgery.
