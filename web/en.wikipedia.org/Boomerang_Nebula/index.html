Boomerang Nebula


Illustration: Boomerang Nebula

The Boomerang Nebula is a bipolar outflow of gas from an aging star located about 5,000 light-years away in the constellation Centaurus. It is famous as the coldest place in the universe known to occur naturally, with a measured temperature of about 1 kelvin (−272.15 °C), colder than the cosmic microwave background.

Discovery and structure

The nebula was observed in 1980 by Keith Taylor and Mike Scarrott, who named it for its asymmetric, boomerang-like appearance in ground-based telescopes. Hubble Space Telescope images taken in 1998 revealed a more hourglass- or bow-tie-shaped structure, suggesting it is a preplanetary nebula — a dying star shedding its outer layers.

The coldest place known

Gas expanding from the central star at roughly 164 km/s cools adiabatically as it rushes outward, dropping to about 1 K. Only laboratory experiments on Earth have produced lower temperatures.
