Idiolect

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An idiolect is an individual's distinctive and unique use of language, including their speech habits, vocabulary, pronunciation, and grammar. The term combines the Greek idio (personal, private) with -lect, as in dialect. No two people speak a language in exactly the same way; an idiolect is shaped by region, family, education, profession, and personal history.

In linguistics

The concept is important in theoretical linguistics because language can be studied as an internal, individual system—what Noam Chomsky calls I-language—as well as a social phenomenon. Sociolinguists study how idiolects cluster into dialects, sociolects, and registers, and forensic linguists exploit idiolectal features (characteristic word choices, spellings, and syntactic habits) in authorship attribution and the analysis of disputed documents and messages.

Related terms

A dialect is spoken by a community; an ethnolect by an ethnic group; a sociolect by a social class or group; and a chronolect by a generation. "Idiolect" sits at the finest-grained end of this continuum of lects.
