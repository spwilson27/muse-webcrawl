Pastured poultry

Pastured poultry is a sustainable agriculture technique that calls for raising chickens, ducks, geese, turkeys, and other poultry directly on pasture. Since the technique is often combined with sustainable agriculture and permaculture, pastured poultry is promoted as an environmentally friendly alternative to conventional confined poultry raising.

Method

Birds are typically housed in movable, floorless pens—popularised by farmer and author Joel Salatin as the "chicken tractor"—which are moved daily to fresh ground. The birds graze on grasses and insects, supplementing feed rations, while their manure fertilises the pasture in place. Egg-laying flocks may instead be free-ranged in fenced paddocks with mobile coops.

Benefits and criticisms

Advocates cite improved soil fertility, healthier pastures, better animal welfare, and meat and eggs with favourable fatty-acid profiles. Critics note higher labour and land costs, exposure to predators and weather, and questions about scalability. In the United States, the term "pastured" for poultry has historically lacked a single enforced legal definition, unlike "organic" certification.
