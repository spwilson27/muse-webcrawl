Presidency of Donald Trump

Donald Trump
President: 20 January 2017 – 20 January 2021 (45th); from 20 January 2025 (47th)
Party: Republican
Vice presidents: Mike Pence; JD Vance

Trump served as the 45th president (2017–2021) and returned as the 47th in 2025.

Donald Trump first served as the 45th president of the United States from 2017 to 2021, and — uniquely since Grover Cleveland — returned to the office as the 47th president in January 2025 after winning the 2024 election.

First term (2017–2021)

Trump's first administration enacted the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (2017), pursued deregulation, appointed three Supreme Court justices (Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett), renegotiated NAFTA as the USMCA, imposed tariffs on China and other trading partners, withdrew the U.S. from the Paris climate agreement and the Iran nuclear deal, and restricted immigration, including a travel ban on several Muslim-majority countries and a border wall programme. He was impeached by the House in 2019 over the Ukraine affair and acquitted by the Senate. The final year was dominated by the COVID-19 pandemic, which killed over 400,000 Americans by the term's end and triggered a sharp recession partially offset by the CARES Act. After losing the 2020 election to Joe Biden, Trump refused to concede, claiming fraud; on 6 January 2021 a mob of his supporters attacked the U.S. Capitol during the electoral vote count. He was impeached a second time for incitement of insurrection and again acquitted after leaving office.

Second term (2025– )

Trump began his second, non-consecutive term on 20 January 2025 with JD Vance as vice president, moving quickly on executive orders concerning immigration enforcement, tariffs, federal workforce reductions through the Department of Government Efficiency, and withdrawal from international agreements.
