Sakuma Shōzan (1811–1864) was a Japanese scholar and reformer of the late Edo period, a leading advocate of Western military science and of opening Japan to foreign knowledge.

Work

A samurai of the Matsushiro Domain, Shōzan studied Dutch learning (rangaku), gunnery and Western science, and warned of Japan's vulnerability to Western naval power after the Opium War in China. His slogan "Eastern ethics, Western techniques" summarised his programme of selective modernisation. Among his students were figures such as Yoshida Shōin and Katsu Kaishū.

Death

Imprisoned for several years after an incident involving his student Yoshida Shōin, Shōzan was later sent to Kyoto, where he was assassinated by anti-foreign extremists in 1864.
