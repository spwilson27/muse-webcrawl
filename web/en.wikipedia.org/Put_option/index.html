Put option

TypeDerivative (option)
CounterpartCall option
Key termsStrike price, expiration, premium

In finance, a put or put option is a derivative contract that gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell a specified amount of an underlying security at a specified price (the strike price) within a specified time. The seller (writer) of the put is obliged to buy the underlying at the strike price if the buyer exercises the option.

Payoff

The value of a put increases as the price of the underlying falls below the strike price. At expiration, a put is worth the greater of zero and the strike price minus the underlying's price, less the premium paid. The buyer's maximum loss is limited to the premium, while a seller's potential loss can be substantial.

Uses

Investors buy puts to speculate on falling prices or to hedge: a protective put, for example, insures a stock holding against a decline, functioning like an insurance policy. Puts are also one leg of many option strategies, such as spreads, straddles, and collars. In the Black–Scholes model, put and call prices are linked by put–call parity.
