Kingston upon Hull

Kingston upon Hull, usually shortened to Hull, is a port city and unitary authority in the East Riding of Yorkshire, England, lying where the River Hull joins the Humber Estuary, about 25 miles inland from the North Sea.

History

The port was founded in the 12th century by monks from Meaux Abbey and was acquired by Edward I in 1293, who renamed it "King's town upon Hull". Hull grew as a major trading and whaling port, was the home constituency of William Wilberforce, and suffered heavy bombing in the Second World War. The fishing industry collapsed after the Cod Wars of the 1970s.

Culture

Hull was UK City of Culture in 2017. Landmarks include Hull Minster, the Humber Bridge nearby, The Deep aquarium and the Museums Quarter. Poet Philip Larkin lived and worked in the city at the University of Hull.
