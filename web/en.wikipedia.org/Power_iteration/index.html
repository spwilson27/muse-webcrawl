Power iteration

Power iteration

Also known as: Power method, Von Mises iteration

Field: Numerical linear algebra

Finds: Dominant eigenvalue and eigenvector

Convergence: Geometric, ratio |λ₂/λ₁|

In mathematics, power iteration (also known as the power method) is an eigenvalue algorithm: given a diagonalizable matrix A, the algorithm produces a number λ, the greatest (in absolute value) eigenvalue of A, and a nonzero vector v, its corresponding eigenvector, such that Av = λv.

The method is simple: start with a random vector and repeatedly multiply it by A, normalising at each step. Because the component of the vector along the dominant eigenvector is amplified relative to all others by a factor of |λ₂/λ₁| at each step, the iterates align with the dominant eigenvector, and the Rayleigh quotient gives the eigenvalue.

Convergence and limitations

Convergence is slow when the two largest eigenvalues are close in magnitude. The method fails or behaves poorly when the dominant eigenvalue is not unique in magnitude — for example a pair of complex-conjugate eigenvalues of equal modulus. Shifts, deflation and acceleration techniques (such as the Rayleigh quotient iteration and the Lanczos and Arnoldi methods) extend the basic idea to harder problems.

Applications

Power iteration underlies Google's original PageRank computation, where the dominant eigenvector of a huge, sparse link matrix ranks web pages, and it remains in use for large-scale eigenvalue problems, principal component analysis and spectral clustering.
