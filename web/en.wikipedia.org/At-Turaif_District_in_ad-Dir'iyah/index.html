At-Turaif District in ad-Dir'iyah

The At-Turaif district in ad-Dir'iyah, northwest of Riyadh, is the original seat of the Saudi royal family and the historic capital of the First Saudi State (the Emirate of Diriyah, 1727–1818). Founded in the 15th century on the banks of Wadi Hanifa, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2010 — the first Saudi site to be listed — as an outstanding example of Najdi architecture in mud-brick.

History

Diriyah was founded in 1446–47 by Mani' ibn Rabiah al-Muraidi, an ancestor of the House of Saud. In 1727 Muhammad ibn Saud made it his capital, and in 1744 he formed the alliance with the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab that launched the First Saudi State. From At-Turaif the emirate expanded to control most of the Arabian Peninsula, including Mecca and Medina.

Architecture

The district contains the Salwa Palaces, a complex of royal residences, administrative buildings, treasuries and mosques built of mud-brick in the Najdi style — characterised by triangular window openings, crenellated rooflines and massive walls adapted to the desert climate — overlooking the palmeries of Wadi Hanifa.

Destruction and abandonment

In 1818, after a six-month siege, Ottoman-Egyptian forces under Ibrahim Pasha captured Diriyah and razed it; its last ruler, Abdullah ibn Saud, was executed in Constantinople. The population moved to the new settlement of Diriyah and eventually to Riyadh, leaving At-Turaif in ruins for nearly two centuries.

Restoration

The Arriyadh Development Authority has restored the district as a national heritage and museum quarter, with the palaces of Salwa housing exhibitions on Saudi history. The site anchors the wider Diriyah Gate development planned as a cultural and tourism destination.
