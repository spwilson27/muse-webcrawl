Robert Gould Shaw

Robert Gould Shaw
Born: 10 October 1837, Boston, Massachusetts
Died: 18 July 1863, Morris Island, South Carolina
Unit: 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment

Robert Gould Shaw (1837–1863) was an American officer in the Union Army during the American Civil War. Born into a prominent abolitionist family in Boston, he accepted command of the 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment, one of the first African-American units in the United States Army.

Service

Shaw had served with the 2nd Massachusetts Infantry at Antietam before taking command of the 54th in 1863. He insisted his men receive equal treatment and supported their refusal of unequal pay. On 18 July 1863 Shaw led the regiment in the assault on Fort Wagner on Morris Island, South Carolina, and was killed at the head of his troops; the Confederates buried him in a mass grave with his men, an act intended as an insult that his family embraced as an honour.

Legacy

Shaw and the 54th are commemorated by the Shaw Memorial in Boston by Augustus Saint-Gaudens, and their story was told in the 1989 film Glory.
