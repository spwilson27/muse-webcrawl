Charles Stewart Parnell (1846–1891) was an Irish nationalist politician who served as a Member of Parliament from 1875 and was the leader of the Irish Parliamentary Party from 1882 until his fall in 1890. One of the most powerful figures in 19th-century Irish and British politics, he was dubbed the "uncrowned king of Ireland."

Career

A Protestant landlord from County Wicklow, Parnell rose through parliamentary obstruction tactics and his presidency of the Irish National Land League during the Land War, which sought fair rents and land reform for tenant farmers. He welded the Irish MPs at Westminster into a disciplined party and in 1885–86 held the balance of power, extracting Gladstone's commitment to Irish Home Rule, though the first Home Rule Bill was defeated in 1886.

Downfall and death

In 1889 Parnell was named as co-respondent in the divorce case of Captain William O'Shea, whose wife Katharine had been Parnell's partner for a decade. Gladstone and the Catholic hierarchy in Ireland demanded his removal; the party split in a bitter meeting in Committee Room Fifteen, and Parnell died within a year, aged 45. The "Parnell split" divided Irish nationalism for a decade.
