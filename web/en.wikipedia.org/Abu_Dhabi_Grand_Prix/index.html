Abu Dhabi Grand Prix

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Abu Dhabi Grand Prix (Arabic: جائزة أبوظبي الكبرى) is a Formula One motor race held annually at the Yas Marina Circuit on Yas Island in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates. First held in 2009 as Formula One's first day–night race, it has traditionally served as the final round of the World Championship season.

History

The inaugural Abu Dhabi Grand Prix took place on 1 November 2009 and was won by Sebastian Vettel of Red Bull Racing. The race starts in late afternoon and finishes after sunset under floodlights, a format later copied by other events. The circuit, designed by Hermann Tilke, is notable for its pit-lane exit passing beneath the track and the Yas Viceroy hotel straddling the circuit.

Title deciders

Yas Marina has hosted several championship-deciding finales. In 2010, Vettel won the race and, with Fernando Alonso finishing only seventh, became the youngest World Champion in history at the time. In 2014, under a controversial double-points system, Lewis Hamilton beat team-mate Nico Rosberg to the title; in 2016 Rosberg clinched his only championship with second place. The 2021 race produced one of the most disputed finales in the sport's history: a late safety car restart allowed Max Verstappen to pass Hamilton on the final lap and win his first championship, a decision that led to an FIA inquiry and the removal of race director Michael Masi.

Circuit changes

Ahead of the 2021 event the track layout was modified—removing the slow chicane before the back-straight hairpin and re-profiling the hotel section—to encourage closer racing and overtaking.
