Pope Gregory X

Pope Gregory X (born Teobaldo Visconti; c. 1210 – 10 January 1276) was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 1 September 1271 to his death in 1276. He was elected after the longest papal election in the history of the Catholic Church, the election of 1268–1271, which took two years and nine months. In response he promulgated the constitution Ubi periculum (1274), which established the papal conclave — locking the cardinals in seclusion until they reached a decision.

Path to election

Visconti, a native of Piacenza, served as archdeacon of Liège and accompanied Cardinal Ottobono Fieschi on his legation to England. At the time of his election he was in the Holy Land with the crusade of the future Edward I of England; he was not even a priest and had to be ordained and consecrated after reaching Italy in 1272. The election that chose him had deadlocked so long that the people of Viterbo, where the cardinals met, locked them in the episcopal palace, removed its roof, and restricted their food — measures Gregory later formalised in Ubi periculum.

Pontificate

Gregory's chief aims were the recovery of the Holy Land, the reunion of the Latin and Greek churches, and reform. He summoned the Second Council of Lyon (1274), which secured a temporary union with the Byzantine Church under Michael VIII Palaiologos and approved new crusade taxation; it also ratified the conclave rules. He received envoys from the Mongol Ilkhan Abaqa seeking an alliance against the Mamluks, and the Venetian travellers Niccolò and Maffeo Polo passed through his orbit on their journey to the court of Kublai Khan. Gregory died at Arezzo while returning from Lyon, and was beatified in 1713.
