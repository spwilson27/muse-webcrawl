MessagePad

The MessagePad is a series of personal digital assistant devices developed by Apple Computer, Inc. (now Apple Inc.) and launched in 1993. It was the first series of devices to use the Newton platform, running the Newton OS with handwriting recognition, and the first to use an ARM processor (the ARM 610). Apple CEO John Sculley coined the term "personal digital assistant" (PDA) for the category.

Models

The original MessagePad (H1000) was announced at CES in 1992 and released in August 1993 for $900. Successive models included the MessagePad 100, 110, 120, 130, the eMate 300 (a laptop-form educational model), and finally the MessagePad 2000 and 2100 (1997), which were substantially faster, with backlit screens and much-improved recognition.

Reception and legacy

The early handwriting recognition was famously mocked — notably in the comic strip Doonesbury and in The Simpsons ("Eat up Martha") — although the system in fact learned the user's writing and later versions were praised. Steve Jobs discontinued the Newton line in 1998 shortly after returning to Apple. Despite commercial failure, the Newton pioneered concepts later central to smartphones and tablets, and its ARM architecture lineage led to the chips in modern Apple devices.
