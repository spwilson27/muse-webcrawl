Kundalini yoga

Kuṇḍalinī yoga is a school of yoga influenced by the Shaktism and Tantra schools of Hinduism. It derives its name from the kuṇḍalinī, a dormant energy believed to be coiled like a serpent at the base of the spine, which practice aims to awaken and raise through the chakras to the crown of the head, bringing spiritual awakening. In the modern West, the term is strongly associated with the system taught from 1969 by Yogi Bhajan.

Concept of kundalini

In Tantric physiology, kundalini (Sanskrit: "coiled one") is the divine feminine energy (Shakti) lying dormant in the muladhara chakra at the base of the spine. Through practices such as asana, pranayama, mantra chanting, mudra, and bandha, the energy is awakened and ascends the central channel (sushumna), piercing the chakras until it unites with Shiva at the crown, resulting in liberation and bliss. Classical descriptions appear in texts such as the Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa (1577), translated by John Woodroffe (Arthur Avalon) as The Serpent Power (1919).

Kundalini yoga as taught by Yogi Bhajan

The Sikh teacher Yogi Bhajan (1929–2004) introduced his system to the United States in 1969, founding the 3HO (Healthy, Happy, Holy Organization). Classes combine sequences called kriyas with breathwork (including Breath of Fire), chanting (often the Adi Mantra "Ong Namo Guru Dev Namo"), meditation, and white clothing and turbans for teachers. After Bhajan's death, investigations and a 2020 independent report substantiated allegations of misconduct, prompting reforms and debate within the movement.

Reception and research

Some clinical studies suggest benefits of Kundalini yoga practice for anxiety, obsessive-compulsive symptoms, and cognitive decline, though evidence remains preliminary. Traditional sources also warn that premature or forced awakening can cause distress, a set of symptoms popularly called "kundalini syndrome."
