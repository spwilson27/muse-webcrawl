Irish Confederate Wars

The Irish Confederate Wars, also called the Eleven Years' War, were fought in Ireland from 1641 to 1653. They formed the Irish theatre of the Wars of the Three Kingdoms, which also included the English Civil War and the Scottish Civil Wars. The conflict began with the Irish Rebellion of 1641 and ended with the Cromwellian conquest of Ireland.

Background

Ireland in 1641 was divided among Gaelic Irish Catholics, Old English Catholics descended from medieval settlers, and New English and Scottish Protestant settlers planted in Ulster and Munster. Dispossession, religious discrimination and fears of a Puritan English parliament drove Catholic landowners to launch a rising in October 1641.

The Confederation of Kilkenny

In 1642 Irish Catholics formed the Catholic Confederation, based at Kilkenny, which governed much of Ireland as a de facto state while professing loyalty to Charles I. The Confederates fought a shifting three-sided war against English Parliamentarians, Royalists and Scottish Covenanters in Ulster. Battles such as Benburb (1646), won by Owen Roe O'Neill, were offset by defeats at Dungan's Hill and Knocknanuss in 1647.

Cromwellian conquest

After the execution of Charles I in 1649, Oliver Cromwell landed in Ireland with the New Model Army. Massacres at Drogheda and Wexford were followed by the sieges of Waterford, Limerick and Galway. The war ended with the capitulation of the last Irish forces in 1653.

Aftermath

The war was catastrophic: famine and plague may have killed a large fraction of Ireland's population. The Act for the Settlement of Ireland confiscated most Catholic-owned land, and Catholic landholding fell from about 60 per cent in 1641 to under 10 per cent by the 1670s.
