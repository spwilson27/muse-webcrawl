The 1963 Skopje earthquake (Macedonian: Скопски земјотрес од 1963 година) was a 6.1-magnitude earthquake which occurred in Skopje, SR Macedonia (now North Macedonia), then part of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, on 26 July 1963. Over 1,070 people died, more than 3,000 were injured, and between 120,000 and 200,000 were left homeless. Between 75 and 80 percent of the city was destroyed.

Aftermath

The city was rebuilt with international assistance coordinated by the United Nations, becoming a symbol of international solidarity during the Cold War: aid arrived from both Western and Eastern bloc countries. The reconstruction master plan was designed by the Japanese architect Kenzō Tange, whose team won the international competition organised by the UN.

The ruined old railway station, its clock stopped at 5:17, was preserved as a memorial and now houses the Museum of the City of Skopje. President John F. Kennedy ordered relief supplies to the city, and the earthquake remains one of the defining events in Skopje's modern history.
