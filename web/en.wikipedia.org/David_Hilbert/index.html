David Hilbert

David Hilbert (23 January 1862 – 14 February 1943) was a German mathematician, one of the most influential of the 19th and early 20th centuries. He worked across invariant theory, algebraic number theory, geometry, integral equations, mathematical physics and the foundations of mathematics. His list of 23 problems, presented at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Paris in 1900, set much of the agenda for 20th-century mathematics.

Life and career

Hilbert was born in Königsberg, East Prussia, and took his doctorate there in 1885 under Ferdinand von Lindemann. His early fame came in invariant theory: his 1888 basis theorem proved the existence of finite bases for invariants by a revolutionary non-constructive argument (prompting Paul Gordan's famous remark, "This is not mathematics; this is theology"). His Zahlbericht (1897) unified algebraic number theory. In 1895 he moved to Göttingen, which under Hilbert and Felix Klein became the world's leading mathematics centre. His Grundlagen der Geometrie (1899) gave geometry a rigorous axiomatic foundation. Work on integral equations led to the theory of Hilbert spaces, foundational for functional analysis and quantum mechanics. In 1915, in parallel with Einstein, he derived the field equations of general relativity from a variational principle. In the 1920s he pursued "Hilbert's program" — a complete, consistent formalisation of mathematics — whose ambitions were undercut by Gödel's incompleteness theorems of 1931. He retired in 1930; the Nazi purges soon emptied Göttingen of the school he had built. He died in Göttingen in 1943. His epitaph quotes his 1930 address: Wir müssen wissen. Wir werden wissen. — "We must know. We will know."

Hilbert's problems

The 23 problems ranged over the continuum hypothesis, the consistency of arithmetic, the Riemann hypothesis (Problem 8, still open), the classification of number fields, and the foundations of physics. Many were solved during the 20th century, several negatively; they guided generations of research.
