Rabindranath Tagore

Rabindranath Tagore
7 May 1861 – 7 August 1941
Bengali poet, writer, composer, philosopher and painter
Nobel Prize in Literature, 1913

Rabindranath Tagore FRAS (7 May 1861 – 7 August 1941) was a Bengali polymath who worked as a poet, writer, playwright, composer, philosopher, social reformer and painter. He reshaped Bengali literature and music and Indian art with Contextual Modernism in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1913 he became the first non-European and the first lyricist to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, largely for the English version of his poetry collection Gitanjali (Song Offerings).

Life

Born in Calcutta into the distinguished Tagore family of Jorasanko, he was the youngest son of Debendranath Tagore, a leader of the Brahmo Samaj. Tagore largely avoided classroom schooling and was tutored at home. After a brief stay in England, he managed family estates in East Bengal (now Bangladesh), where his great short stories and poems matured.

Works and legacy

Tagore wrote thousands of songs (Rabindra Sangeet), novels such as Gora and Ghare Baire (The Home and the World), and the short stories of Galpaguchchha. Two of his songs became national anthems: "Jana Gana Mana" for India and "Amar Shonar Bangla" for Bangladesh. He founded Visva-Bharati University at Santiniketan and the experimental school that preceded it, travelled the world lecturing, and denounced British rule while opposing narrow nationalism. He renounced his knighthood in 1919 in protest at the Jallianwala Bagh massacre.
