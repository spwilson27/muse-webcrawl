Archimedean solid

An Archimedean solid is a highly symmetric, semi-regular convex polyhedron composed of two or more types of regular polygons meeting at identical vertices. There are 13 Archimedean solids, plus the infinite families of prisms and antiprisms sometimes counted with them.

Classification

The 13 solids include the truncated tetrahedron, cuboctahedron, truncated cube, truncated octahedron, rhombicuboctahedron, truncated cuboctahedron, snub cube, icosidodecahedron, truncated dodecahedron, truncated icosahedron (the shape of a classic soccer ball), rhombicosidodecahedron, truncated icosidodecahedron, and snub dodecahedron.

History

The solids are named after Archimedes, who discussed them in a now-lost work. They were rediscovered during the Renaissance, notably by Piero della Francesca and later described completely by Johannes Kepler in 1619.
