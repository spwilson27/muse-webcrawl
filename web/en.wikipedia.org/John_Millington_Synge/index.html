John Millington Synge

Born16 April 1871, Rathfarnham, County Dublin, Ireland
Died24 March 1909 (aged 37), Dublin, Ireland
OccupationPlaywright, poet, writer
Notable worksThe Playboy of the Western World; Riders to the Sea

Edmund John Millington Synge (16 April 1871 – 24 March 1909) was an Irish playwright, poet and collector of folklore, and a key figure in the Irish Literary Revival. A co-founder of Dublin's Abbey Theatre with W. B. Yeats and Lady Gregory, he is best known for The Playboy of the Western World (1907), whose premiere provoked riots, and the one-act tragedy Riders to the Sea (1904).

Life

Synge was born in Rathfarnham into a prosperous Anglo-Irish Protestant family. He studied at Trinity College Dublin and the Royal Irish Academy of Music, and after a period in Germany and Paris — where Yeats urged him to visit the Aran Islands — he spent several summers on Inishmaan, learning Irish and recording island life. The experience shaped his plays and his travel book The Aran Islands (1907).

Plays

His plays, written in a stylised version of the English dialect of rural Ireland, include In the Shadow of the Glen (1903), Riders to the Sea, The Well of the Saints (1905), The Playboy of the Western World and The Tinker's Wedding. Audiences hostile to his unsentimental portrayal of Irish peasants rioted at the Playboy's opening in January 1907. His tragedy Deirdre of the Sorrows was left unfinished at his death.

Death and legacy

Synge died of Hodgkin's disease in 1909, aged 37, while engaged to the actress Molly Allgood. His work profoundly influenced later Irish dramatists, and Riders to the Sea is often regarded as one of the finest one-act plays in English.
