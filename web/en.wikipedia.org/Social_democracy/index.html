Social democracy is a social, economic, and political philosophy within socialism that supports political and economic democracy and a gradualist, reformist, and democratic approach toward achieving social equality and, in some interpretations, socialism. In modern practice, social democracy has come to be associated with a mixed economy, a comprehensive welfare state, strong trade unions, and policies aimed at reducing inequality while retaining capitalist markets.

Origins

Social democracy emerged from the nineteenth-century European workers' movement. Thinkers such as Eduard Bernstein argued for "revisionism"—achieving socialist goals through parliamentary reform rather than revolution—which split the movement from revolutionary Marxism after the Russian Revolution of 1917. Parties such as Germany's SPD, Sweden's SAP, and Britain's Labour Party became the principal vehicles of the tradition.

The postwar settlement

After 1945, social democratic governments built extensive welfare states: universal healthcare, public education, social insurance, and full-employment policies. The Nordic model—developed most fully in Sweden, Norway, and Denmark—combined high taxes, generous benefits, and coordinated wage bargaining with competitive market economies, producing some of the world's highest living standards and lowest inequality.

The Third Way and after

In the 1990s, leaders such as Tony Blair and Gerhard Schröder promoted a "Third Way" that accepted market liberalisation while emphasising opportunity and public-service reform. Financial crisis after 2008, austerity politics, and the rise of both left-wing challengers and right-wing populists weakened many traditional social democratic parties, a decline sometimes termed "Pasokification" after the collapse of Greece's PASOK. Nonetheless, social democratic parties continue to govern in several European countries and remain a central tradition of the centre-left.

Criticism

From the left, social democracy is criticised for managing rather than transforming capitalism; from the right, for high taxation and expansive government. Supporters respond that it has delivered historically unmatched combinations of prosperity, security, and freedom.
