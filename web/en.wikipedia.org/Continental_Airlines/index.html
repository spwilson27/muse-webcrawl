Continental Airlines

Continental Airlines was a major American airline that operated from 1934 until it merged with United Airlines in 2012. Founded as Varney Speed Lines, it was headquartered in Houston, Texas, with major hubs at Houston Intercontinental, Newark Liberty and Cleveland Hopkins airports. At its peak it served destinations across the Americas, Europe and Asia, and was among the largest airlines in the United States.

History

The airline began in 1934 as the southwest division of Varney Speed Lines and took the name Continental in 1937 under Robert F. Six, who built it into a transcontinental carrier. Military charter work during the Vietnam War era and Pacific expansion followed. After deregulation, the airline endured two bankruptcies (1983 and 1990) under owner Frank Lorenzo's Texas Air Corporation, then recovered in the 1990s under Gordon Bethune, whose "Go Forward Plan" transformed its service and finances. Continental left the SkyTeam alliance for Star Alliance in 2009.

Merger with United

In May 2010 Continental and United announced a merger creating the world's largest airline at the time; the holding company took the United Continental Holdings name. The carriers received a single operating certificate in November 2011, and on 3 March 2012 Continental's systems were integrated into United, retiring the Continental name while United retained Continental's globe logo and livery elements.

Fleet and services

Continental operated an all-Boeing mainline fleet including the 737, 757, 767 and 777, with regional services under the Continental Express and Continental Connection brands. Its frequent-flyer program was OnePass.
