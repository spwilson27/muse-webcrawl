Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind

Theatrical release poster

Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind is a 2004 American science fiction romantic drama film directed by Michel Gondry from a screenplay by Charlie Kaufman, based on a story by Gondry, Kaufman, and Pierre Bismuth. It stars Jim Carrey as Joel Barish and Kate Winslet as Clementine Kruczynski, two strangers who meet on a train and begin a relationship, unaware that they had previously been a couple whose memories of each other were medically erased.

Plot

After their relationship ends painfully, Clementine hires the firm Lacuna, Inc. to erase all memories of Joel. On learning this, Joel undergoes the same procedure. During the overnight erasure, his mind revisits the memories in reverse order, and as the happier early memories surface he changes his mind, attempting to hide Clementine within unrelated recollections to save her from deletion. The effort fails, and the last memory fades with Clementine whispering "Meet me in Montauk." In a subplot, Lacuna employee Mary reveals that she had her own affair with the firm's founder erased. The film closes with Joel and Clementine, having heard recordings of the bitter things they said about each other during their intake interviews, deciding to try again anyway.

Title and reception

The title quotes Alexander Pope's poem "Eloisa to Abelard" (1717): "Eternal sunshine of the spotless mind! / Each pray'r accepted, and each wish resign'd". The film won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and Winslet was nominated for Best Actress. It is frequently ranked among the greatest films of the twenty-first century and is noted for its non-linear narrative, practical in-camera effects, and exploration of memory and identity.
