The Stockton and Darlington Railway (S&DR) was a railway company that operated in north-east England from 1825 to 1863. It was the world's first public railway to use steam locomotives, opening on 27 September 1825 to carry coal from collieries near Shildon and Darlington to the port of Stockton-on-Tees, with passengers carried from the outset.

Opening

Promoted chiefly by the Quaker businessman Edward Pease and engineered by George Stephenson, the line was authorised by Act of Parliament in 1821; a 1823 amendment permitted steam locomotives and passenger traffic. On opening day Stephenson drove Locomotion No. 1 hauling a train of coal wagons and a purpose-built passenger coach, Experiment, carrying several hundred passengers at speeds up to about 15 mph (24 km/h).

Development

The railway proved that steam haulage was commercially viable for a public carrier, stimulating the "railway mania" that followed. Passenger services were initially run by independent coach operators using horses before the company took them over with locomotives. The S&DR pioneered many practices of later railways and merged into the North Eastern Railway in 1863. Parts of its route remain in use today as the Tees Valley line, and its 1975 sesquicentenary and 2025 bicentenary (Railway 200) were celebrated as the birth of the modern railway.
