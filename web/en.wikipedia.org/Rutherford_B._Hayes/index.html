Rutherford Birchard Hayes (4 October 1822 – 17 January 1893) was the 19th president of the United States, serving from 1877 to 1881. A lawyer and Union brevet major general in the Civil War, he served as governor of Ohio before winning the presidency in the bitterly disputed election of 1876.

The election of 1876 and the Compromise of 1877

Hayes lost the popular vote to Democrat Samuel J. Tilden, and disputed returns from Florida, Louisiana and South Carolina left the electoral count unresolved. A special Electoral Commission awarded all disputed votes to Hayes, and the informal Compromise of 1877 — by which Republicans conceded an end to federal Reconstruction — smoothed his inauguration. As president, Hayes withdrew the last federal troops from the South, ending Reconstruction.

Presidency

Hayes promoted civil-service reform against the spoils system, battling his own party's Stalwart faction; used federal troops cautiously in the Great Railroad Strike of 1877; defended the gold standard against greenback and silver inflationists, vetoing the Bland–Allison Act (which Congress overrode); and began modest efforts toward civil rights and assimilation-era Native American policy reform. True to a campaign pledge, he served a single term and retired to Fremont, Ohio, devoting himself to education and prison reform causes.
