North Atlantic Craton

The North Atlantic Craton (NAC) is an Archaean craton exposed in southern West Greenland, the Nain Province in Labrador, Canada, and northwestern Scotland. It is one of the oldest pieces of continental crust on Earth, containing rocks as old as about 3.9 billion years, including the Acasta Gneiss–related terranes and the Amitsoq gneisses of Greenland. The craton was split apart by the opening of the Labrador Sea and the North Atlantic Ocean in the Mesozoic and Cenozoic.

Geology

The craton is composed mainly of tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite (TTG) gneisses intruded by granites, together with supracrustal belts such as the Isua Greenstone Belt in Greenland, which preserves some of the oldest known evidence bearing on early life and surface conditions on Earth. In Scotland, the corresponding rocks form the Lewisian complex of the Hebrides and the northwest mainland. In Labrador, the Nain Province preserves the eastern portion of the craton, including the Saglek and Nulliak supracrustal assemblages.

History

The craton formed through the accretion of Archaean terranes before about 2.7 billion years ago and was later reworked at its margins during the Palaeoproterozoic, notably in the Nagssugtoqidian orogen of Greenland. It formed part of the supercontinent Columbia and later of the Laurentian core of North America before rifting separated the Greenland and Labrador portions. The craton is also known for its kimberlite fields and occurrences of diamonds and other mineral deposits associated with ancient, thick lithospheric roots.
