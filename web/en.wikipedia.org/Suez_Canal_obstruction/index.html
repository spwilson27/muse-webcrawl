Suez Canal obstruction

The Suez Canal obstruction occurred in March 2021, when the container ship Ever Given ran aground in the Suez Canal in Egypt and blocked it for six days, obstructing one of the world's busiest trade routes.

Grounding

On 23 March 2021, the Ever Given — a 400-metre-long, roughly 224,000-tonne Golden-class container ship operated by Evergreen Marine and travelling from Malaysia to Rotterdam — was passing northbound through the canal in high winds and a dust storm when it lost steerage and its bow became wedged in the canal's eastern bank, leaving the ship diagonal across the waterway. The canal, through which around 12% of global trade passes, was completely blocked.

Refloating and consequences

An international salvage effort using dredgers (including the dredger Mashhour) and tugboats, aided by a spring tide, freed the ship on 29 March. By then more than 400 vessels were queued at both ends of the canal; the backlog took several more days to clear. The blockage held up an estimated US$9–10 billion of goods per day and became a major global news story and internet phenomenon. The ship was subsequently impounded by Egypt during a compensation dispute and released in July 2021 after a settlement.

The Suez Canal had previously been closed for eight years after the Six-Day War of 1967, an episode remembered for the trapped "Yellow Fleet" of ships.
