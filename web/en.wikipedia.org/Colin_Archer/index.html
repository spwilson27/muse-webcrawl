Colin Archer (22 July 1832 – 8 February 1921) was a Norwegian naval architect and shipbuilder known for his rugged, seaworthy pilot boats and rescue cutters, and for designing the polar exploration ship Fram.

Life and work

Archer was born in Larvik to Scottish immigrant parents. He spent time in Australia as a young man, working as a farmer in Queensland, before returning to Norway and establishing his shipyard at Larvik. There he developed the double-ended pilot boats that came to bear his name, hulls characterized by a pointed stern ("double-enders") that proved exceptionally seaworthy in North Sea conditions.

His most famous commission was the Fram, built for Fridtjof Nansen's attempt to drift across the Arctic Ocean frozen into the ice (1893–1896), and later used by Roald Amundsen on his South Pole expedition. Archer also designed the rescue vessels of the Norwegian Society for Sea Rescue; the surviving sailing rescue boat Colin Archer (RS 1), launched in 1893, remains an icon of Norwegian maritime heritage. The "Colin Archer type" became a generic term for heavy-displacement double-ended cruising yachts, influencing yacht designers worldwide.
