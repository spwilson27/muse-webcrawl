An angel is a supernatural spiritual being found in many religions and mythologies. In Abrahamic religions — Judaism, Christianity and Islam — angels are typically depicted as benevolent celestial intermediaries between God (or Heaven) and humanity, acting as messengers, guides and protectors. The word derives from the Greek angelos (ἄγγελος), meaning "messenger", itself a translation of the Hebrew mal'akh.

In Judaism

In the Hebrew Bible, angels appear as messengers of God to the patriarchs, as agents of divine judgement, and as a heavenly host praising God. Named angels such as Michael and Gabriel appear in later books such as Daniel. Jewish tradition developed elaborate angelology, including guardian angels and the archangels Michael, Gabriel, Raphael and Uriel.

In Christianity

Christian tradition inherited Jewish angelology and systematised it: the sixth-century writer Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite arranged angels into nine choirs in three hierarchies — Seraphim, Cherubim and Thrones; Dominions, Virtues and Powers; Principalities, Archangels and Angels. Angels announce the birth of Jesus in the Gospels, minister to him, and are expected to accompany his return. The doctrine of the guardian angel, assigned to each person, became popular devotion in Catholicism. Fallen angels, led by Satan or Lucifer, are held to have rebelled against God and become demons.

In Islam

Belief in angels (malāʾika) is one of the six articles of faith in Islam. Angels are created from light, have no free will, and perfectly obey God. Jibril (Gabriel) conveyed the revelation of the Quran to Muhammad; Mikail (Michael) provides sustenance; Israfil will blow the trumpet at the Day of Judgement; and Azrael is the angel of death. Two angels, Kiraman Katibin, record each person's deeds.

Depiction in art

Early Christian art depicted angels as youthful men; from the late Roman period onward they acquired wings, halos and flowing robes, and in medieval and Renaissance art they became a central subject — often shown playing musical instruments, bearing lilies or trumpets, or as plump infants (putti/cherubs) in Baroque art. Angels remain pervasive in popular culture as symbols of protection, guidance and the afterlife.
