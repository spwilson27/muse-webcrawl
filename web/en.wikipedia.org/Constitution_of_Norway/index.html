Constitution of Norway

The Constitution of Norway (Grunnloven) was adopted on 16 May 1814 by the Norwegian Constituent Assembly at Eidsvoll and signed on 17 May — the date celebrated as Norway's Constitution Day. It is the second-oldest written constitution still in force in the world, after that of the United States.

History

Drafted in the upheaval of the Napoleonic Wars, after the Treaty of Kiel transferred Norway from Denmark to Sweden, the constitution proclaimed Norway an independent kingdom. Sweden accepted the constitution with revisions later in 1814 as the basis of a personal union, which lasted until 1905. Amendments over two centuries have introduced parliamentarism (1884 in practice), universal suffrage, and human-rights provisions, while the document's language was modernized in 2014.

Structure

The constitution divides power among the Storting (legislature), the King and Council of State (executive), and the courts (judiciary headed by the Supreme Court). It guarantees rights including freedom of expression and protection against retroactive laws, and it can be amended by a two-thirds majority of the Storting across an intervening election.
