Scientism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Scientism is a term—usually pejorative—that describes the belief that the methods and findings of the natural sciences are, or should be, the sole or supreme standard for all fields of inquiry, including philosophy, the humanities, and questions of value and meaning. In a stronger form, scientism holds that only what is scientifically measurable or testable is real or knowable.

Uses of the term

The term gained currency in the mid-twentieth century. Friedrich Hayek used it in The Counter-Revolution of Science (1952) for the "slavish imitation" of scientific method in the social sciences, where he thought it produced misleading results. Karl Popper criticized "scientism" in historicist social theory while defending critical rationalism. Later, philosophers and theologians levelled the charge against logical positivism, strong forms of reductionism, and claims by some popular science writers that science can settle moral and existential questions entirely on its own.

Debate

Critics argue that scientism is self-refuting: the claim "only scientifically testable claims are meaningful" is not itself scientifically testable. They also contend that history, ethics, aesthetics, and first-person experience require methods beyond measurement. Defenders of a science-centred worldview—who rarely adopt the label themselves—respond that critics erect a straw man: they argue instead for the general reliability of scientific methods and for applying empirical inquiry wherever questions are empirical, without denying the value of the humanities. Philosophers such as Tom Sorell have offered moderate analyses in which scientism names a spectrum of attitudes, some defensible and some not.

Related views

Scientism is associated with, but distinct from, science itself; it is also distinguished from pseudoscience (claims falsely presented as science) and from anti-science attitudes. The concept is central to debates about the demarcation problem, the scope of naturalism, and the place of science in culture.
