Orb-weaver spider

Araneidae
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Arthropoda
Class: Arachnida
Order: Araneae
Family: Araneidae

Orb-weaver spiders are members of the family Araneidae. They are the most common group of spiders building spiral wheel-shaped webs often found in gardens, fields and forests. The English word "orb" can mean "circular", hence the English name of the group. Araneids have eight eyes, hairy or spiny legs, and no stridulating organs. The family contains over 3,000 species in about 170 genera.

Webs

Orb-weavers build vertical or near-vertical webs consisting of a frame of non-sticky silk, radial threads, and a spiral of sticky capture silk. Many species rebuild their webs every day, eating the old web to recycle its protein. The spider typically waits at the hub or in a nearby retreat connected by a signal thread, responding to vibrations when prey becomes entangled.

Behaviour and diet

The spiders feed mainly on flying insects such as flies, moths and beetles caught in their webs. Prey is often wrapped in silk before being eaten. Most orb-weavers are harmless to humans, though some of the larger tropical species can give a mild bite.

Notable genera

Familiar genera include Araneus (the European garden spider Araneus diadematus), Argiope (signature spiders, which add conspicuous stabilimenta to their webs), Nephila and Trichonephila (golden silk orb-weavers, which spin very large, strong webs), and Gasteracantha (spiny-backed orb-weavers).
