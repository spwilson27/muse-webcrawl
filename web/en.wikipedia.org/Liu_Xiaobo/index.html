Liu Xiaobo (28 December 1955 – 13 July 2017) was a Chinese writer, literary critic, and human rights activist who called for political reforms and an end to one-party rule in China. He was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 2010 while imprisoned, becoming the first Nobel laureate to die in state custody since the German pacifist Carl von Ossietzky in 1938.

Early life and scholarship

Liu was born in Changchun to an intellectual family. During the Cultural Revolution he was sent to the countryside in Inner Mongolia as a "sent-down youth." After universities reopened, he studied Chinese literature at Jilin University and earned a doctorate at Beijing Normal University in 1988, where he became a lecturer known for sharp critiques of Chinese culture and philosophy, notably in his 1988 book Critique of the Choice: Dialogues with Li Zehou.

1989 and its aftermath

While a visiting scholar at Columbia University in 1989, Liu returned to Beijing to join the Tiananmen Square protests. He was one of the "Four Gentlemen" who staged a hunger strike in the square and helped negotiate a peaceful evacuation of students in the early hours of 4 June. He was imprisoned for his role until 1991, and was later detained again in the 1990s, including a three-year term of re-education through labour (1996–1999) for advocating political reform and the release of 1989 prisoners.

Charter 08 and imprisonment

In December 2008 Liu was a principal drafter of Charter 08, a manifesto inspired by Czechoslovakia's Charter 77 that called for constitutional democracy, separation of powers, freedom of expression, and respect for human rights. He was detained the day before its release and in December 2009 was sentenced to eleven years' imprisonment for "inciting subversion of state power."

Nobel Peace Prize and death

On 8 October 2010 the Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded Liu the Nobel Peace Prize "for his long and non-violent struggle for fundamental human rights in China." China condemned the award, censored the news domestically, and pressured other countries to boycott the ceremony, at which Liu was represented by an empty chair. In June 2017 he was granted medical parole after being diagnosed with terminal liver cancer, and he died under guard in a Shenyang hospital on 13 July 2017. His ashes were scattered at sea. His widow, the poet and artist Liu Xia, who had been held under house arrest since 2010, was allowed to leave China for Germany in 2018.
