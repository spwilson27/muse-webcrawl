Achernar

Achernar is the brightest star in the constellation of Eridanus and the ninth-brightest in the night sky. It has the Bayer designation Alpha Eridani (α Eridani, abbreviated Alpha Eri, α Eri). Forming the southern terminus of the "river" of Eridanus, it lies about 139 light-years from the Sun in the far southern sky and is not visible from latitudes north of about 33°N. The name Achernar derives from Arabic, meaning "the End of the River".

Properties

Achernar is a hot blue main-sequence star of spectral type B6 Vep with a mass of about 6–7 solar masses and a luminosity several thousand times that of the Sun. It is a binary system, with a companion of roughly two solar masses in an orbit of about 7 astronomical units and a period of approximately 15 years.

Achernar is famous for its extreme rotation: it spins at at least 250 km/s at its equator, close to the speed at which it would break apart. This rapid rotation makes it strongly oblate — interferometric observations have shown its equatorial diameter is more than 50% greater than its polar diameter, the most extreme flattening measured for any star. It is also a Be star, surrounded by a disc of hot gas thrown off from its equator, and it shows variability associated with this disc.
