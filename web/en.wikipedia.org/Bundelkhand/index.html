Bundelkhand

Bundelkhand
Region of India
Location: Northern Madhya Pradesh and southern Uttar Pradesh
Major cities: Jhansi, Banda, Chhatarpur, Tikamgarh

Bundelkhand is a geographical and cultural region in central India, spanning parts of the modern states of Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. It is named after the Bundela Rajput clan, which ruled the region from the 16th century onwards.

Geography

The region lies south of the Yamuna river and is largely a rocky upland of Vindhyan sandstone, characterised by hills, scrub forest and seasonal rivers. Important rivers include the Ken, Betwa and Dhasan.

History

The area was successively ruled by the Imperial Guptas, the Kalachuris, the Chandelas — who built the famous temples of Khajuraho — and later the Bundelas, whose capital was Orchha. In the 18th century the Marathas under the Peshwa extended influence over the region, and the British gradually annexed its princely states during the 19th century.

Culture

The local dialect, Bundeli, is a Western Hindi language. The region is known for the legend of Alha and Udal, folk epics sung by bards, and for the martial tradition associated with Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi, a heroine of the Indian Rebellion of 1857.
