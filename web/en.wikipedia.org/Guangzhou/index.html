Guangzhou, historically known in the West as Canton, is the capital and largest city of Guangdong province in southern China. Situated on the Pearl River about 120 kilometres northwest of Hong Kong, it is one of China's largest cities and a major port, manufacturing hub and centre of Cantonese culture and cuisine.

History

Founded as Panyu over two thousand years ago, Guangzhou became a terminus of the maritime Silk Road. From 1757 to 1842 it was the only Chinese port open to foreign trade under the Canton System, a position that made it wealthy and cosmopolitan. The First Opium War began after Commissioner Lin Zexu destroyed confiscated opium at nearby Humen in 1839. Guangzhou was also the scene of Sun Yat-sen's early revolutionary activity and the Huanghuagang Uprising of 1911.

Economy and landmarks

The city hosts the Canton Fair, China's oldest and largest trade fair, held twice yearly since 1957. Landmarks include the Canton Tower (604 m), Shamian Island's colonial architecture, the Chen Clan Academy and the Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall. Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport is among China's busiest, and the city hosted the 2010 Asian Games.
