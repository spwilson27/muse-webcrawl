Dot product

In mathematics, the dot product or scalar product is an algebraic operation that takes two equal-length sequences of numbers (usually coordinate vectors) and returns a single number (a scalar). In Euclidean geometry, the dot product of the Cartesian coordinates of two vectors is widely used, and is related to lengths and angles: for vectors a and b it equals |a| |b| cos θ, where θ is the angle between them.

Algebraic definition

Algebraically, the dot product of two vectors a = (a1, …, an) and b = (b1, …, bn) is defined as the sum of the products of corresponding components:

a · b = Σi=1..n ai bi = a1b1 + a2b2 + … + anbn.

Geometric definition

Geometrically, the dot product is the product of the Euclidean magnitudes of the two vectors and the cosine of the angle between them:

a · b = |a| |b| cos θ.

The two definitions are equivalent in Cartesian coordinates. It follows that two nonzero vectors are perpendicular exactly when their dot product is zero, and that the dot product of a vector with itself gives its squared length: a · a = |a|2.

Properties

The dot product is commutative (a · b = b · a), distributive over vector addition, and bilinear: it is linear in each argument. Unlike ordinary multiplication it is not associative, and it is defined only between two vectors, yielding a scalar rather than a vector.

Applications

In physics the dot product gives the work done by a force acting along a displacement (W = F · d) and the power developed by a force on a moving body (F · v). The scalar projection of one vector onto another, used in computer graphics for lighting calculations (Lambert's cosine law) and in signal processing and machine learning as a similarity measure (cosine similarity), is likewise based on the dot product. The generalisation of the dot product to abstract vector spaces is the inner product.
