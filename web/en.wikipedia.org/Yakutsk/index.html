Yakutsk (Russian: Якутск; Yakut: Дьокуускай, Djokuuskay) is the capital city of the Sakha Republic, Russia, located about 450 km south of the Arctic Circle on the western bank of the Lena River. Founded in 1632 as a Cossack ostrog (fort), it is one of the oldest Russian settlements in the Far East and a major centre of the diamond-mining region of Yakutia.

Climate

Yakutsk has an intensely continental subarctic climate. Winter temperatures routinely fall below −40 °C and have reached about −64 °C, making it often cited as the coldest major city in the world; summers, in contrast, can exceed +35 °C, one of the largest seasonal ranges anywhere. Most of the city is built on continuous permafrost, with buildings raised on piles to avoid thawing the ground.

History

From Yakutsk, expeditions such as those of Semyon Dezhnev and Vitus Bering's Kamchatka expeditions set out to explore Siberia, Alaska and the Arctic. The city grew as an administrative and trade hub for furs and, later, gold and diamonds. In the Soviet period it expanded rapidly with the development of the ALROSA diamond industry.

Transport and culture

Yakutsk is served by an airport and by a road across the frozen Lena in winter; a long-planned bridge across the Lena has been under development for years. The city is a centre of Yakut (Sakha) culture, hosting the Yhyakh summer festival, mammoth remains museums, and the Melnikov Permafrost Institute.
