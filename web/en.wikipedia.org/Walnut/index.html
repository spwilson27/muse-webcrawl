Walnut

A walnut is the edible seed of any tree of the genus Juglans, most notably the Persian or English walnut, Juglans regia. The seed is enclosed in a hard, wrinkled shell contained within a green husk, and is widely eaten raw or used in cooking and baking.

Nutrition and uses

Walnuts are rich in polyunsaturated fats, including alpha-linolenic acid (an omega-3), protein, fibre, magnesium and antioxidants. They are used in dishes ranging from baklava and Persian fesenjān to salads and walnut oils. Walnut timber, especially from the black walnut (Juglans nigra) of North America, is highly prized for furniture and gunstocks.

Production

China is the world's largest producer of walnuts, followed by the United States (mainly California), Iran and Turkey. Walnut trees also produce juglone, a compound that can inhibit the growth of some other plants nearby (allelopathy).
