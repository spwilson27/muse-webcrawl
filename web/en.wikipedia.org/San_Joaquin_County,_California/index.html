San Joaquin County is a county in the U.S. state of California, located in the northern San Joaquin Valley at the eastern edge of the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta. Its county seat and largest city is Stockton. One of the original counties created at statehood in 1850, it is a major centre of agriculture, logistics and inland shipping.

Geography

The county covers about 1,426 square miles, mostly flat alluvial valley floor watered by the San Joaquin, Mokelumne, Calaveras and Stanislaus rivers. The Delta's network of channels and leveed islands dominates the county's west, giving Stockton an inland deep-water port connected to San Francisco Bay. The climate is Mediterranean, with hot, dry summers and cool winters with tule fog.

History

The region was home to Yokuts and Miwok peoples before Spanish exploration; Gabriel Moraga's expeditions in the early 1800s named the river for Saint Joachim. Under Mexican rule the area formed part of large ranchos. During the Gold Rush, Stockton — founded by Charles M. Weber — became the supply gateway for the southern mines and grew into a manufacturing and shipping hub. The county later became a centre of mechanised agriculture and food processing.

Economy and communities

Agriculture remains a leading sector, with grapes and wine production around Lodi, cherries, almonds, walnuts, tomatoes and dairy. The Port of Stockton, major rail lines and Interstate 5 and Highway 99 make the county a distribution hub, and its cities — Stockton, Tracy, Lodi, Manteca, Lathrop, Ripon and Escalon — increasingly house commuters to the San Francisco Bay Area and Silicon Valley. The University of the Pacific, founded in 1851, is among the institutions based in Stockton.
