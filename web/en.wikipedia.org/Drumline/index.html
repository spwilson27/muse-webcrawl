Drumline

A drumline is a section of percussion instruments usually played as part of a musical marching ensemble. A drumline can also be a standalone group. The term is most common in the United States, where drumlines are a standard part of high school and university marching bands and of drum and bugle corps.

Sections

The marching drumline, often called the battery, typically includes snare drums, tenor drums (multi-toms or "quads/quints"), tonal bass drums — usually four to six drums of different pitches played as a unit — and crash cymbals. The stationary percussion section, or front ensemble ("pit"), with keyboard percussion and accessories, is sometimes counted as part of the wider percussion section but does not march.

Technique and culture

Drumlines play highly technical, precisely synchronised music based on rudiments — standardised sticking patterns such as paradiddles, flams, and rolls. Players use matched grip in most modern lines and traditional grip in some snare lines, a legacy of military drumming. Before and after shows, drumlines warm up in "the lot," where spectators gather to watch; corps and bands also trade cadences and engage in informal drum battles. Indoor percussion ensembles extend the activity into the winter season with theatrical productions.

In popular culture

The 2002 film Drumline brought the style of historically Black college and university (HBCU) show-style drumlines to wide attention; HBCU bands emphasise high-stepping showmanship alongside drumming virtuosity.
