Brahmo Samaj

Founded1828, Calcutta
FounderRaja Ram Mohan Roy
TypeHindu reform movement

The Brahmo Samaj is a monotheistic reform movement within Hinduism founded in Calcutta in 1828 by Raja Ram Mohan Roy. It sought to purify Hindu practice by rejecting idolatry, priestly authority, and practices such as sati, while affirming belief in one formless God drawn from the Upanishads.

History

Under Roy and his successors — notably Debendranath Tagore, who reorganized the Samaj in the 1840s, and Keshub Chandra Sen, whose activism widened its social-reform agenda — the movement influenced education, women's rights, and opposition to child marriage and caste restrictions. Disagreements over doctrine and reform produced schisms, including the Sadharan Brahmo Samaj of 1878.

Legacy

The Brahmo Samaj was a central force in the Bengal Renaissance and in the emergence of modern Indian reform thought. Its members included leading figures of the Tagore family; Rabindranath Tagore, the Nobel laureate poet, was raised in the Brahmo tradition. Congregations continue to worship in India and Bangladesh today.
