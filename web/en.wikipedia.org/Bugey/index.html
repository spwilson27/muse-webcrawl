The Bugey is a historic region in the Ain department of eastern France, between Lyon and Geneva. It lies in the foothills of the southern Jura mountains, bounded by the Rhône and Ain rivers, and its traditional capital is Belley. The area is divided into the mountainous Haut-Bugey around Nantua and the lower Bas-Bugey along the Rhône.

History

In the Middle Ages the Bugey was contested between the counts of Savoy and the Dauphiné. By the Treaty of Lyon in 1601, Henry IV of France acquired the Bugey, Bresse, and Gex from Savoy in exchange for the Marquisate of Saluzzo, attaching the region definitively to France.

Economy and culture

The Bugey is known for its wines, including the sparkling Bugey-Cerdon made by the méthode ancestrale, and for Comté and Bleu de Gex cheeses produced in its mountain pastures. The gastronomer Jean Anthelme Brillat-Savarin was born in Belley in 1755. The Bugey Nuclear Power Plant, on the Rhône near Saint-Vulbas, is a major regional employer.

Geography

The Grand Colombier, rising to 1,534 metres, is one of the highest summits of the Jura in the region and a noted viewpoint and cycling climb.
