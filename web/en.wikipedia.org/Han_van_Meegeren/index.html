Han van Meegeren

Han van Meegeren (born Henricus Antonius van Meegeren; 10 October 1889 – 30 December 1947) was a Dutch painter and art forger, considered one of the most ingenious art forgers of the 20th century. He created paintings in the style of Johannes Vermeer that were accepted as genuine by leading experts.

Career and forgeries

Van Meegeren's own traditional work was dismissed by critics, which he blamed for his turn to forgery. In 1937 his Supper at Emmaus, presented as a newly discovered early Vermeer, was hailed as a masterpiece by the scholar Abraham Bredius. He sold further fake Vermeers for large sums, perfecting techniques with Bakelite resin to harden paint so it would pass alcohol tests and appear centuries old.

Arrest and trial

After the Second World War, a van Meegeren "Vermeer" was traced to Hermann Göring's collection, and he was arrested for collaborating by selling Dutch cultural property. To prove his innocence of that charge he confessed to forgery and, under guard, painted another "Vermeer" in custody. In 1947 he was convicted of forgery and fraud and sentenced to one year in prison, but died of a heart attack before serving it.

Legacy

His case exposed weaknesses in connoisseurship and remains a classic study in art authentication; his forgeries are now collectible in their own right.
