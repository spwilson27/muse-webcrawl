Looney 11 rule

Sharia-compliant wills must reconcile fixed inheritance shares with testamentary freedom.

The Looney 11 rule is a rule of thumb used in drafting wills that comply with Islamic inheritance law (Sharia) in some jurisdictions, notably discussed in the context of British Muslims making wills under English law. Islamic law of inheritance prescribes fixed shares for certain relatives (the faraid system) and generally permits a person to bequeath by will no more than one-third of their estate to non-heirs or charity.

Background

The "rule" is named after a practitioner who popularised the approach of dividing the estate into elevenths, a common denominator arising when shares such as one-third, one-sixth and other Quranic fractions are combined for a typical family (for example, a spouse, parents and children). Dividing the estate into elevenths allows the prescribed shares of surviving relatives to be expressed as whole numbers of elevenths, simplifying the drafting of a will that mirrors the Sharia distribution while remaining valid under local law.
