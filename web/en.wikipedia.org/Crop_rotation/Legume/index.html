Legume

Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

A legume is a plant in the family Fabaceae (or Leguminosae), or the fruit or seed of such a plant. When used as a dry grain, the seed is also called a pulse. Legumes are grown agriculturally, primarily for human consumption, for livestock forage and silage, and as soil-enhancing green manure. Well-known legumes include beans, soybeans, peas, chickpeas, peanuts, lentils, lupins, alfalfa, and clover.

A variety of pulses (dry legume seeds).

Nitrogen fixation

Most legumes have symbiotic nitrogen-fixing bacteria called rhizobia within nodules in their root systems. These bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia usable by the plant, which is why legumes are central to crop rotation: a legume crop enriches the soil with nitrogen for following crops, reducing the need for synthetic fertiliser.

Uses and nutrition

Legumes are a staple protein source worldwide, rich in protein, fibre, complex carbohydrates, folate, and minerals, and low in fat. Pulses such as lentils and chickpeas form the basis of dishes across South Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America; soybeans are a major global commodity for food, feed, and oil; peanuts are both a food and oil crop. Forage legumes like alfalfa and clover are essential in livestock farming.

Botany

The characteristic fruit of the family is a pod that typically splits along two seams to release the seeds. Fabaceae is one of the largest families of flowering plants, with roughly 19,000–20,000 species, ranging from herbs to large trees such as acacias.
