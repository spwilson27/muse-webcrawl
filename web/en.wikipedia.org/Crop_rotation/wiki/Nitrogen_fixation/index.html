Nitrogen fixation

Nitrogen fixation
Process: N₂ → ammonia (NH₃)
Catalyst: Nitrogenase enzyme
Key organisms: Rhizobium, cyanobacteria

Nitrogen fixation is the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen gas (N₂) into ammonia and related compounds that plants can assimilate. Because the triple bond in N₂ is extremely strong, fixation requires large energy inputs and, in biology, the enzyme nitrogenase.

Biological fixation

Certain bacteria and archaea (diazotrophs) fix nitrogen. Symbiotic rhizobia live in root nodules of legumes such as clover, peas, and soybeans, trading fixed nitrogen for plant carbohydrates — the reason legumes feature in crop rotations. Free-living cyanobacteria fix nitrogen in oceans and soils.

Industrial fixation

The Haber–Bosch process synthesizes ammonia from N₂ and hydrogen at high temperature and pressure over an iron catalyst. It underpins modern fertilizer production and supports a large fraction of the world's population, though it is energy-intensive.
