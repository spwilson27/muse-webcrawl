British Agricultural Revolution
Period: c. 1700–1850
Key innovations: Four-field rotation, seed drill, selective breeding, enclosure

The British Agricultural Revolution was the unprecedented increase in agricultural production in Britain between the early 18th and mid-19th centuries arising from new farming techniques and land reforms. It supported rapid population growth and freed labor for the Industrial Revolution.

Innovations

The Norfolk four-course rotation cycled wheat, turnips, barley, and clover, eliminating fallow while restoring soil nitrogen. Jethro Tull promoted the seed drill; Robert Bakewell pioneered systematic livestock selective breeding; and Charles "Turnip" Townshend popularized turnips and rotation on his estates.

Enclosure

Parliamentary enclosure consolidated open fields and commons into hedged private farms. It raised efficiency but displaced many smallholders and commoners, driving migration to industrial towns.
