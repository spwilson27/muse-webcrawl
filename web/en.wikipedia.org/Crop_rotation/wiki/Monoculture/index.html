Monoculture
Practice: Growing a single crop species
Contrast: Polyculture, crop rotation
Risks: Pest outbreaks, disease, soil depletion

Monoculture is the agricultural practice of producing a single crop, plant, or livestock species over a wide area and for many consecutive years. It is widely used in industrial farming because it simplifies planting, mechanization, and harvesting.

Advantages and disadvantages

Uniform crops allow specialized machinery and economies of scale. However, genetic uniformity makes fields vulnerable: pests and pathogens adapted to the crop can spread explosively, as in the Irish Potato Famine (a blight on the genetically narrow Lumper potato) and the loss of the Gros Michel banana to Panama disease. Monocultures also deplete specific soil nutrients and often depend on heavy fertilizer and pesticide inputs.

Alternatives

Crop rotation, intercropping, and polyculture restore soil health and break pest cycles, and are promoted by sustainable agriculture.
