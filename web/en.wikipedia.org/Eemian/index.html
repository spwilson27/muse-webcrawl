Eemian
Agec. 130,000 – 115,000 years ago
EquivalentMarine Isotope Stage 5e; Last Interglacial
Named afterEem river, Netherlands

The Eemian is the interglacial period that began about 130,000 years ago and ended about 115,000 years ago, preceding the Last Glacial Period. It corresponds to Marine Isotope Stage 5e and is the European regional name for the Last Interglacial; its equivalents include the Sangamonian of North America and the Ipswichian of Britain.

Climate

During the Eemian optimum, global temperatures were roughly 1–2 °C warmer than pre-industrial levels and sea level was some 6–9 metres higher than today, reflecting reduced Greenland and Antarctic ice. In northern Europe the climate was warm enough for hippopotamuses in the Thames and for temperate forests to grow far north; the name derives from deposits near the river Eem in the Netherlands, recognised by Pieter Harting in 1874.

Significance

The Eemian is widely studied as an analogue for future warming. Neanderthals occupied Europe throughout the period; anatomically modern humans were present in the Levant but had not yet permanently colonised Europe.
