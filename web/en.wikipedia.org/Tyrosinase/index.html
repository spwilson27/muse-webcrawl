Tyrosinase

Tyrosinase
EC number1.14.18.1
ClassOxidoreductase (monophenol monooxygenase)
CofactorCopper (binuclear Cu center)
SubstratesL-tyrosine, L-DOPA
ProductDopaquinone → melanin
Gene (human)TYR, chromosome 11q14–q21

Tyrosinase is an oxidase enzyme that is the rate-limiting enzyme for controlling the production of melanin. It catalyzes the oxidation of phenols such as the amino acid tyrosine — first to L-DOPA and then to dopaquinone — using molecular oxygen; dopaquinone subsequently polymerizes through a series of reactions into melanin pigments. The enzyme is a binuclear copper-containing metalloprotein found across animals, plants, fungi, and bacteria.

Function

In animals, tyrosinase is located in melanosomes within melanocytes and drives skin, hair, and eye pigmentation; the resulting melanin protects tissue against ultraviolet radiation. In plants and fungi, tyrosinase (often called polyphenol oxidase) catalyzes the enzymatic browning seen in cut fruit and is involved in wound response. In arthropods the enzyme participates in sclerotization (hardening) of the cuticle.

Genetics and disease

In humans, tyrosinase is encoded by the TYR gene. Mutations that inactivate the enzyme cause oculocutaneous albinism type 1 (OCA1), characterized by absent or greatly reduced melanin, white or light hair and skin, and visual problems such as nystagmus and photophobia. Partial-activity variants produce milder phenotypes, and normal polymorphisms of TYR contribute to ordinary variation in pigmentation.

Applications and inhibition

Tyrosinase inhibitors such as kojic acid, arbutin, and hydroquinone are used in cosmetics for skin lightening and in food science to prevent browning. The enzyme is also the target of research into treatments for hyperpigmentation disorders and a model for studying copper enzyme catalysis.
