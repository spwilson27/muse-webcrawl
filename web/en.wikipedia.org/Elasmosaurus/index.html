Elasmosaurus

Life reconstruction of Elasmosaurus, showing its extremely long neck

Elasmosaurus is a genus of plesiosaur that lived in North America during the Campanian stage of the Late Cretaceous period, about 80.5 million years ago. It is known from a single fossil specimen found in the Pierre Shale of Kansas in 1867 and described by Edward Drinker Cope in 1868.

Description

Elasmosaurus was a large marine reptile, about 10.3 metres (34 feet) long, of which more than half was its remarkably elongated neck, which bore around 72 vertebrae — more than any other known animal. Its small head had sharp teeth suited to catching fish and cephalopods, and its four flippers propelled it through the Western Interior Seaway. Despite popular depictions, its neck was relatively stiff and could not be raised out of the water in a swan-like pose.

History of discovery

The original restoration by Cope mistakenly placed the skull at the end of the tail — an error pointed out by Othniel Charles Marsh and often cited as a trigger of the "Bone Wars" rivalry between the two palaeontologists. Elasmosaurus gives its name to the family Elasmosauridae, the long-necked plesiosaurs of the Cretaceous seas.
