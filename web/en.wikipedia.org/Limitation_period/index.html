Limitation period

A limitation period is a time limit set by law — a statute of limitations — within which legal proceedings must be commenced. After the period expires, a claim may be barred, even if it would otherwise be meritorious. Periods differ by jurisdiction and by type of claim: contracts, personal injury, property damage, and criminal offences each have their own rules, and the most serious crimes often have no limitation period at all.

Rationale

Limitation periods promote diligence by claimants, protect defendants from stale claims when evidence has decayed and witnesses' memories have faded, and provide legal certainty and repose.

Accrual and tolling

A period usually begins when the cause of action accrues — in some systems, when the claimant discovers or should have discovered the injury. Limitation may be tolled (paused) for minors, for incapacity, or while a defendant is absent from the jurisdiction, and acknowledging a debt may restart the clock in some legal systems.
