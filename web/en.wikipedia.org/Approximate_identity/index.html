Approximate identity

Approximate identity
FieldFunctional analysis, harmonic analysis
Related conceptsConvolution, Dirac delta function, mollifier

In mathematics, an approximate identity is a net or sequence of functions that converges, in an appropriate sense, to the identity element for an operation such as convolution, even when the algebra in question has no true identity element. Approximate identities play a central role in harmonic analysis and the theory of Banach algebras.

Definition

In a Banach algebra A, a net (eλ) is a left approximate identity if eλa converges to a for every element a of A; two-sided approximate identities are defined analogously. For convolution on the group algebra L1(G) of a locally compact group, an approximate identity is a net of integrable functions concentrating near the identity of the group, with integral one, so that convolution with them approaches the identity operation.

Examples

Standard examples include scaled bump functions concentrating at the origin, the Fejér and Poisson kernels on the circle, and mollifiers used to approximate functions by smooth functions. The Dirac delta function can be viewed as the idealised limit of an approximate identity, though it is a distribution rather than a function.
