Girl group

A girl group is a music act featuring several female singers who generally harmonise together. The term "girl group" is also used in a narrower sense in the United States to denote the wave of American female pop singing groups, many of whom were influenced by doo-wop, which flourished in the late 1950s and early 1960s. Classic girl groups of that era include the Shirelles, the Supremes, the Ronettes, the Marvelettes, Martha and the Vandellas, and the Shangri-Las.

History

Early years and the 1960s boom

Female vocal harmony groups date to the 1930s and 1940s, with acts such as the Andrews Sisters and the Boswell Sisters. The girl group sound crystallised in the late 1950s, blending doo-wop, rhythm and blues, and teen pop. The Shirelles' "Will You Love Me Tomorrow" (1960) became the first number-one hit by an African-American girl group. Producers and songwriters such as Phil Spector, Berry Gordy, and the Brill Building teams turned the format into a dominant force in pop music, characterised by lush orchestration (Spector's "Wall of Sound"), call-and-response vocals, and songs addressed to teenage listeners.

Later revivals

The format repeatedly returned to prominence: in the 1980s with the Bangles and Bananarama; in the 1990s with American R&B groups En Vogue, TLC, and Destiny's Child, and the British Spice Girls, whose "girl power" slogan made them the best-selling girl group of all time; and in the 2000s and 2010s with the Pussycat Dolls, Girls Aloud, Fifth Harmony, and Little Mix. In South Korea and Japan, girl groups are a central pillar of the K-pop and J-pop idol industries, with acts such as Girls' Generation, Twice, Blackpink, and AKB48 achieving global success.

Characteristics

Girl group music typically features close vocal harmonies, a lead singer supported by backing vocalists, coordinated choreography and styling, and themes of romance and friendship. In the idol systems of East Asia, members are often selected through auditions and trainee programmes and marketed with distinct individual personas within the group.
