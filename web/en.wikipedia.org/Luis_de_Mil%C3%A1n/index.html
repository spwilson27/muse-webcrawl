Luís de Milán

Luís de Milán
Born: c. 1500
Died: c. 1561
Occupation: composer, vihuelist, writer

Luís de Milán (also Luis Milán; c. 1500 – c. 1561) was a Spanish Renaissance composer and the first composer in history to publish music for the vihuela de mano, as well as one of the first musicians to specify tempo indications in his music.

El Maestro

Milán served at the court of Germaine de Foix in Valencia. His Libro de música de vihuela de mano intitulado El Maestro, printed in Valencia in 1536 and dedicated to King John III of Portugal, is the earliest surviving collection of vihuela music. It contains fantasias, pavanes, and villancicos and romances for voice and vihuela, arranged progressively from simple to virtuosic as a teaching method — hence the title "The Teacher". Milán's prefaces include unusually detailed instructions on tempo and expression, marking passages to be played fast or slow.

Other works

He also published El cortesano (1561), a witty account of courtly life at Valencia modelled on Castiglione's Book of the Courtier. His fantasias remain central to the early guitar and vihuela repertoire, and his six pavanes were long misattributed in part to other composers.
