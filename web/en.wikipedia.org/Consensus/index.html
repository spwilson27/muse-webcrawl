Consensus

Consensus is a general agreement among the members of a group. Consensus decision-making is a process that seeks the consent of all participants, aiming to resolve or mitigate the objections of the minority in order to achieve a decision acceptable to everyone, rather than simply outvoting them.

Consensus decision-making

Consensus decision-making has roots in Quaker practice, Indigenous governance traditions, and Japanese ringi processes, and is used by cooperatives, activist networks, and some legislatures and standards bodies. Participants may support a proposal, stand aside (register disagreement without blocking), or block (veto) in formal models. Critics note that consensus can be slow and can privilege the status quo, while supporters argue it produces more durable and inclusive outcomes than majority voting.

Related senses

In science, a scientific consensus is the collective position of the community of scientists in a field, such as the consensus on human-caused climate change. In distributed computing, consensus is the problem of getting multiple processes to agree on a single data value, addressed by algorithms such as Paxos and Raft.
