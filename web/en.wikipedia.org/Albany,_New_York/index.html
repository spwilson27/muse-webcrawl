Albany, New York

Country: United States
State: New York
Population: approx. 99,000 (2020)
Role: Capital of New York State

Albany is the capital city of the U.S. state of New York and the seat of Albany County. It lies on the west bank of the Hudson River about 150 miles (240 km) north of New York City. Albany is known for its rich colonial history, stately architecture, and as a centre of state government and higher education; it forms the heart of New York's Capital District.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Mohican people. Dutch fur traders established Fort Orange in 1624, the first permanent European settlement in the region, and the village of Beverwijck grew around it. The English took control in 1664 and renamed it Albany in honour of the Duke of Albany, the future James II. Albany was chartered as a city in 1686 — making it one of the oldest continuously chartered cities in the United States — and became the state capital in 1797. The opening of the Erie Canal in 1825 cemented its role as a transport hub.

Geography and economy

Albany sits in the Hudson Valley, with rolling terrain rising westward toward the Helderberg Escarpment. Government is the dominant employer, alongside education, healthcare, and a growing nanotechnology sector anchored by Albany NanoTech and SUNY Polytechnic Institute. The Empire State Plaza, built in the 1960s and 1970s under Governor Nelson Rockefeller, dominates the downtown skyline.

Culture

The city hosts the New York State Museum, The Egg performing arts centre, and the Albany Institute of History & Art, founded in 1791. Annual events include the Tulip Festival, celebrating Albany's Dutch heritage.
