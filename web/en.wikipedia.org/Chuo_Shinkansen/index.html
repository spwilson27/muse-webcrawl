Chuo Shinkansen

The Chuo Shinkansen (中央新幹線, Chūō Shinkansen) is a Japanese maglev railway line under construction between Tokyo and Osaka, with an intermediate stop at Nagoya. Operated by the Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central), it will use the SCMaglev superconducting magnetic levitation technology, allowing trains to travel at a maximum operating speed of 500 km/h (311 mph), cutting the Tokyo–Nagoya journey to about 40 minutes and Tokyo–Osaka to about 67 minutes.

History

Maglev research in Japan began in the 1960s, and a dedicated test track was built in Yamanashi Prefecture in the 1990s. In 2003 a three-car train set a world rail speed record of 581 km/h, and in 2015 the L0 Series reached 603 km/h, the fastest speed ever attained by a land vehicle of its kind. The government formally approved the Linear Chuo Shinkansen project in 2011, and JR Central began construction in December 2014.

Route

The first section, from Shinagawa Station in Tokyo to Nagoya, runs some 286 km, roughly 86 percent of it in tunnels bored deep beneath the Southern Japanese Alps and other mountain ranges. A long-running dispute with Shizuoka Prefecture over water flow in the Oi River tunnel section delayed the planned 2027 opening; JR Central has since revised its schedule. The line is planned ultimately to extend from Nagoya to Osaka.

Technology

The SCMaglev system uses superconducting magnets mounted on the train bogies, which interact with coils in the guideway walls for levitation, guidance, and propulsion. The train floats about 10 cm above the guideway at speed, with rubber-tyred wheels deployed at low speeds.
