Battle of Zhovti Vody

Date29 April – 16 May 1648
LocationZhovti Vody ("Yellow Waters"), Ukraine
ResultCossack–Tatar victory
ContextOpening of the Khmelnytsky Uprising

The Battle of Zhovti Vody was the first major battle of the Khmelnytsky Uprising against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. Cossack forces under Bohdan Khmelnytsky, allied with Crimean Tatars under Tugay Bey, destroyed a Commonwealth advance force near the stream called Zhovti Vody, the "Yellow Waters."

Course of the battle

Registered Cossacks sent against the rebels defected to Khmelnytsky, and the Polish camp, besieged for over two weeks, was overrun in May 1648 when its defenders attempted to retreat. The Polish commander Stefan Potocki was mortally wounded.

Significance

Followed days later by the victory at Korsun, Zhovti Vody shattered Commonwealth authority in Ukraine and opened a decade of war that reshaped Eastern Europe.
