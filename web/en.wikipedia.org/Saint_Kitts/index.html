Saint Kitts, officially Saint Christopher, is an island in the West Indies and the larger of the two islands that constitute the Federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis. Located in the Leeward Islands chain of the Lesser Antilles, it lies about 2 km (1.25 mi) from its sister island of Nevis across the channel known as The Narrows. The island's capital and main port is Basseterre.

Geography

Saint Kitts is roughly oval in shape, some 29 km long and 8 km wide, with a narrow southeastern peninsula. Its interior is dominated by a volcanic mountain range whose highest peak, Mount Liamuiga (formerly Mount Misery), rises to 1,156 m and holds a crater lake. Fertile lowlands around the coast were long devoted to sugar cane cultivation.

History

The island was inhabited by the Kalinago people before European arrival. Christopher Columbus sighted it in 1493. In 1623 the English established their first successful Caribbean colony here under Thomas Warner, and in 1625 French settlers arrived, making Saint Kitts the oldest English and French colony in the Caribbean — the "Mother Colony of the West Indies". Control alternated between Britain and France until the Treaty of Paris (1783) confirmed British possession. The fortress of Brimstone Hill, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, guarded the island during this era. Sugar, worked by enslaved Africans, drove the economy until the industry's decline in the late 20th century; the last sugar harvest was in 2005.

Government and economy

Saint Kitts forms part of the independent federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis, which gained independence from the United Kingdom in 1983. Tourism, offshore financial services and citizenship-by-investment programmes have replaced sugar as the mainstays of the economy. The island is served by Robert L. Bradshaw International Airport and a scenic railway that tours the old sugar estates.
