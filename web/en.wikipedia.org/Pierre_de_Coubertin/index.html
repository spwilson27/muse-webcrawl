Born
1 January 1863, Paris, France
Died

Pierre de Frédy, Baron de Coubertin (1863–1937) was a French educator and historian, founder of the International Olympic Committee (IOC) and the father of the modern Olympic Games.

Revival of the Olympics

Inspired by the ancient Greek games and by the role of sport in British education, Coubertin proposed reviving the Olympics at a congress at the Sorbonne in 1894, where the IOC was founded. The first modern Games took place in Athens in 1896. Coubertin served as the IOC's second president from 1896 to 1925 and designed the Olympic rings symbol in 1913.

Ideas and legacy

Coubertin believed sport promoted international understanding, amateurism, and moral as well as physical education; his motto, drawn from Bishop Ethelbert Talbot, held that "the important thing in the Olympic Games is not to win, but to take part." His views on women's participation reflected the prejudices of his era. Under the pseudonym Georges Hohrod, he himself won a gold medal in literature at the 1912 Stockholm Games. The Pierre de Coubertin Medal honours athletes exemplifying sportsmanship.
