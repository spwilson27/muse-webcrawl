Central Tibetan Administration

The Central Tibetan Administration (Tibetan: བོད་མིའི་སྒྲིག་འཛུགས་, Wylie: Bod mi'i sgrig 'dzugs, THL: Bömi Drikdzuk, lit. 'Tibetan People's Organisation'), often referred to as the Tibetan Government in Exile, is the central government of Tibet, based in Dharamshala, India. It is composed of a judicial branch, a legislative branch and an executive branch, and claims to be the legitimate government of Tibet while not being officially recognised by any sovereign state.

It was established in 1959 after the 14th Dalai Lama fled Tibet following the failed uprising against Chinese rule. The CTA administers Tibetan refugee settlements and schools in India, Nepal and Bhutan, and advocates the "Middle Way" policy of genuine autonomy for Tibet within the People's Republic of China.

Structure

The executive is the Kashag (cabinet), headed by the Sikyong (political leader), an office directly elected by the Tibetan diaspora since the Dalai Lama devolved his political authority in 2011; Penpa Tsering has served as Sikyong since 2021. The legislature is the Tibetan Parliament-in-Exile, with 45 members elected from the three traditional provinces of Tibet, religious schools, and diaspora constituencies in Europe, North America and Australasia. The judiciary is headed by the Tibetan Supreme Justice Commission.

Activities

Through the Green Book (Chatrel), Tibetans in exile make voluntary contributions that fund the administration. The CTA runs departments of religion and culture, home, education, health, security, information and international relations, and finance, and maintains Offices of Tibet in a dozen cities worldwide.
