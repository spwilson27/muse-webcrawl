Carol Susan Dweck (born October 17, 1946) is an American psychologist. She holds the Lewis and Virginia Eaton Professorship of Psychology at Stanford University. Dweck is known for her work on the mindset psychological trait — the idea that people's beliefs about whether ability is fixed or malleable shape achievement and motivation.

Mindset theory

Dweck distinguishes between a fixed mindset, in which intelligence is seen as a static trait, and a growth mindset, in which intelligence is seen as something that can be developed through effort and learning. Her research, conducted with colleagues over several decades, suggests that students praised for process rather than innate ability tend to embrace challenges and persist through difficulty.

Career

Dweck received her PhD from Yale University in 1972. She taught at the University of Illinois, Harvard University, and Columbia University before joining Stanford in 2004. She is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and a fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

Selected works

Her 2006 book Mindset: The New Psychology of Success popularised the fixed-versus-growth mindset distinction for a general audience. She also wrote Self-Theories: Their Role in Motivation, Personality, and Development (1999).
