Visigoths

The Visigoths (Latin: Visigothi) were a Germanic people united under the rule of a king and living within the Roman Empire during late antiquity. Together with the Ostrogoths, they were one of two major political entities formed from the Goths.

[Image: A Visigothic votive crown of gold and gems from the Treasure of Guarrazar.]

History

The Visigoths, streaming across the Danube in 376 to escape the Huns, defeated the Romans at the Battle of Adrianople in 378 and under Alaric I famously sacked Rome in 410. Granted land in Gaul, they later established the Visigothic Kingdom centred on Toledo in Hispania, which lasted until the Muslim conquest of 711–718.

Culture and legacy

Originally Arian Christians, the Visigoths converted to Nicene Catholicism under Reccared I in 589. Their legal code, the Liber Iudiciorum, influenced medieval Spanish law, and their gold votive crowns survive as masterpieces of early medieval art.
