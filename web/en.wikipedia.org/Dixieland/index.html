Dixieland

Dixieland, sometimes referred to as traditional jazz, is a style of jazz based on the music that developed in New Orleans at the start of the 20th century. The genre combined brass band marches, French quadrilles, ragtime, and blues with collective, polyphonic improvisation.

Dixieland
Stylistic originsRagtime, blues, brass band
OriginsEarly 1900s, New Orleans
Typical instrumentsTrumpet, clarinet, trombone, banjo, tuba, drums, piano

Characteristics

A standard Dixieland front line features trumpet (or cornet), clarinet, and trombone, supported by a rhythm section of piano, banjo or guitar, tuba or double bass, and drums. The trumpet plays the melody, the clarinet improvises ornamentation above it, and the trombone provides countermelodies and rhythmic "tailgate" slides below.

History

The Original Dixieland Jass Band made the first jazz recordings in 1917, and musicians such as King Oliver, Jelly Roll Morton, and Louis Armstrong carried the style to Chicago and New York. A Dixieland revival flourished in the 1940s and 1950s, and the style remains a staple of traditional jazz festivals.
