Laramie River

The Laramie River is a 280-mile (450 km) long tributary of the North Platte River in the U.S. states of Colorado and Wyoming. Rising in the Rawah Wilderness of northern Colorado, it flows north through Laramie, Wyoming, and joins the North Platte near Fort Laramie.

Course and use

The river passes through the Laramie Plains and Wheatland Reservoir. Its water is used for irrigation and municipal supply, and trans-basin diversions move Laramie water toward the Cache la Poudre basin.

History

The river, city, county and Fort Laramie all take their name from Jacques La Ramee, a French-Canadian fur trapper active in the region in the early 19th century.
