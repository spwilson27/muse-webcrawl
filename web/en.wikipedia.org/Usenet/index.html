Usenet
Created: 1980
Creators: Tom Truscott, Jim Ellis
Protocol: NNTP
Structure: Newsgroups in hierarchies

Usenet is a worldwide distributed discussion system developed in 1980 by Tom Truscott and Jim Ellis at Duke University. Users read and post messages (articles) to topical categories called newsgroups, which servers exchange with one another in a flooding pattern.

Organization

Newsgroups are arranged in hierarchies such as comp.* (computing), sci.* (science), rec.* (recreation), and alt.* (alternative topics). The system predates the World Wide Web and helped shape internet culture—concepts such as FAQs, flaming, and spam emerged there. The annual "September" influx of new students became notorious, and 1993's arrival of AOL users was dubbed "Eternal September."

Decline

Web forums, social media, and the withdrawal of ISP-run news servers reduced Usenet's role, though it persists for text discussion and binary file distribution.
