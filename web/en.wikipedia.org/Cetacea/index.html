Cetacea

Cetacea is an infraorder of aquatic mammals belonging to the order Artiodactyla that includes whales, dolphins and porpoises. Key characteristics are their fully aquatic lifestyle, streamlined body shape, often large size and an entirely carnivorous diet. They are famed for their high intelligence and complex social organisation. The infraorder is divided into two parvorders: Mysticeti (baleen whales, filter feeders with keratinous baleen plates) and Odontoceti (toothed whales, which echolocate). There are around 90 living species, ranging from the vaquita (~1.4 m) to the blue whale, the largest animal known ever to have existed. Molecular and fossil evidence shows cetaceans evolved from land-dwelling even-toed ungulates about 50 million years ago, and are the closest living relatives of hippopotamuses.

Evolution

The earliest cetaceans, the archaeocetes, appeared in the early Eocene of Indo-Pakistan. Pakicetus was a wolf-sized land mammal identified as a cetacean by features of its ear bone (the involucrum); Ambulocetus, the "walking whale", swam like an otter; later protocetids spread along coasts, and the fully aquatic basilosaurids of the late Eocene had tiny hind limbs. Modern mysticetes and odontocetes diverged roughly 34 million years ago as ocean circulation changed with the opening of the Southern Ocean. Echolocation in toothed whales and baleen filter-feeding in mysticetes enabled exploitation of abundant small prey.

Anatomy and physiology

Cetaceans have fusiform bodies, forelimbs modified into flippers, no external hind limbs, and a tail ending in horizontal flukes that provide propulsion by vertical undulation. The blowhole(s) on top of the head connect to a respiratory system permitting rapid breathing at the surface; a thick blubber layer insulates and stores energy. Odontocetes produce high-frequency clicks in nasal passages and receive echoes through fat bodies in the jaw, building acoustic images; the melon focuses sound. Mysticetes instead produce low-frequency song and calls — humpback song is among the most complex animal vocalisations. Many species undertake the longest migrations of any mammal, between polar feeding grounds and warm breeding lagoons.

Intelligence and society

Cetaceans have large brains — the sperm whale's is the heaviest of any animal — with complex social structures, dialects, cooperative hunting (such as bubble-net feeding and orca wave-washing), play, and evidence of cultural transmission. Orcas live in matrilineal pods; bottlenose dolphins form fission–fusion societies with individual signature whistles.

Conservation

Industrial whaling in the 19th–20th centuries killed millions of cetaceans, driving blue, right and other whales to near-extinction before the International Whaling Commission's 1986 moratorium on commercial whaling. Present threats include bycatch in fishing gear (critically endangering the vaquita and North Atlantic right whale), ship strikes, ocean noise, plastic pollution and climate-driven prey shifts. The baiji (Yangtze river dolphin) was declared functionally extinct in 2006, the first cetacean extinction attributed to human activity.
