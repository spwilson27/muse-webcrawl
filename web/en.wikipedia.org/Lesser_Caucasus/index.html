Lesser Caucasus

The Lesser Caucasus is the second of the two main mountain ranges of the Caucasus, extending for about 600 km across the highlands of Armenia, Georgia and Azerbaijan, south of the Greater Caucasus range. It runs roughly parallel to the Greater Caucasus, from which it is separated by the Colchis lowland in the west and the Kura-Aras lowland in the east, and is joined to it by the Likhi (Surami) Range.

Geography

Unlike the Greater Caucasus, the Lesser Caucasus forms not a single continuous ridge but a complex of intersecting volcanic highlands and ranges, including the Javakheti Range, the Trialeti Range and the mountains of Nagorno-Karabakh. Its highest peak is Mount Aragats (4,090 m), an extinct volcano and the highest mountain in Armenia. The range forms part of the Alpide belt and is geologically younger and more volcanic in character than its northern counterpart. The Armenian Highlands extend southward from the Lesser Caucasus into eastern Turkey and north-western Iran.
