Moselle (wine)

[Image: Steep vineyards along the Moselle river]
RegionMoselle valley, Germany (with Luxembourg and France)
Main grapeRiesling
SoilsDevonian slate
StyleLight, low-alcohol, high-acid white wines

Moselle wine is produced in the valley of the river Moselle, principally in Germany's Mosel wine region — the country's third largest — with additional production in Luxembourg and in the Moselle appellation of north-eastern France. The German Moselle is famed for delicate, aromatic Riesling wines grown on some of the steepest vineyards in the world.

Geography and viticulture

Vines are planted on steep slate slopes rising from the winding river between Trier and Koblenz, along the lower Saar and Ruwer tributaries. The blue and red Devonian slate retains heat and imparts a characteristic mineral note, while the river moderates the cool northern climate, allowing long, slow ripening. Many sites, such as the Bremmer Calmont, have gradients of up to 65 degrees and must be worked entirely by hand.

Wine styles

Moselle Rieslings are typically low in alcohol, high in acidity, and range from bone-dry (trocken) to lusciously sweet, including late-harvest Prädikat levels up to Trockenbeerenauslese and Eiswein. Other grapes include Müller-Thurgau, Elbling and Pinot noir (Spätburgunder) for still and sparkling wines.

History

Viticulture in the valley dates to Roman times; the Romans introduced vines in the 2nd century AD. Monasteries expanded plantings in the Middle Ages, and in the 19th century Moselle Rieslings ranked among the world's most expensive wines.
