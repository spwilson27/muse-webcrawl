Cerro de la Silla

The Cerro de la Silla ("Saddle Hill") is a mountain and designated natural monument located within the metropolitan area of Monterrey, in the Mexican state of Nuevo León. Its distinctive saddle-shaped silhouette is the most widely recognized symbol of the city of Monterrey.

Name and history

The mountain was named by Alberto del Canto, the Portuguese founder of Monterrey, in the 16th century, because its two peaks resemble a horse saddle. In 1991 the Mexican federal government declared it a natural monument, protecting its slopes and ecosystems. The two principal summits are the Pico Antena, which carries telecommunications antennas, and the higher Pico Norte.

Geology and ecology

The hill is formed of folded sedimentary rock of the Sierra Madre Oriental system. Its slopes host thorn scrub, oak and mixed forest communities typical of northeastern Mexico, and it is a popular destination for hiking, with routes ascending from the suburb of Guadalupe.
