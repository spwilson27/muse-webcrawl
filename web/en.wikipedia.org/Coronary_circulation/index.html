Coronary circulation

Coronary circulation is the circulation of blood in the blood vessels that supply the heart muscle (myocardium). The coronary arteries deliver oxygenated blood to the heart, and the cardiac veins remove deoxygenated blood.

Anatomy

The left and right coronary arteries arise from the aorta just above the aortic valve. The left coronary artery divides into the left anterior descending artery, which runs down the front of the heart, and the circumflex artery, which wraps around the left side. The right coronary artery supplies the right ventricle and, in most people, gives off the posterior descending artery — a pattern called right dominance.

Physiology

Unlike most organs, the heart receives most of its blood flow during diastole, when the relaxed myocardium no longer compresses its own vessels. Flow is tightly autoregulated to match oxygen demand, and the heart extracts a high fraction of the oxygen from the blood passing through it.

Disease

Narrowing of the coronary arteries by atherosclerosis causes coronary artery disease, leading to angina when flow is limited and to myocardial infarction (heart attack) when a vessel is suddenly blocked.
