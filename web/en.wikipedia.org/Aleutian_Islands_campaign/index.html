Aleutian Islands campaign

The Aleutian Islands campaign was a military campaign conducted by the United States and Japan in the Aleutian Islands, part of the Territory of Alaska, during World War II, from 3 June 1942 to 15 August 1943. It was the only campaign of the war fought on incorporated American territory.

Date3 June 1942 – 15 August 1943
LocationAleutian Islands, Alaska
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsUnited States, Canada / Empire of Japan

Japanese occupation

Japanese forces bombed Dutch Harbor in June 1942 and occupied the islands of Attu and Kiska, partly as a diversion for the Midway operation and partly to extend Japan's defensive perimeter. The occupation alarmed the American public, and Unangax (Aleut) residents of Attu were taken to Japan as prisoners.

Allied counteroffensive

American bombers harassed the Japanese garrisons through brutal weather. In May 1943, US troops retook Attu in one of the war's costliest battles relative to numbers engaged, ending with a desperate Japanese banzai charge. Anticipating a similar fight for Kiska, a large Allied force landed there in August 1943 — only to find the Japanese had evacuated under cover of fog weeks earlier.

Legacy

The campaign is sometimes called the "Forgotten Battle." Conditions were dominated by the notorious Aleutian weather — fog, wind and cold caused heavy casualties on both sides. Attu and Kiska are now protected as part of the Alaska Maritime National Wildlife Refuge, and the battlefield sites are National Historic Landmarks.
