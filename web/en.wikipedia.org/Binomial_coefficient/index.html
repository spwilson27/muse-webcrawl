Binomial coefficient

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the binomial coefficients are the positive integers that occur as coefficients in the binomial theorem. The binomial coefficient indexed by integers n ≥ k ≥ 0 is written

( n choose k ) = n! / ( k! (n − k)! )

and counts the number of ways to choose an unordered subset of k elements from a fixed set of n elements. It is read "n choose k".

History and notation

The coefficients appear in Pascal's triangle, known to mathematicians in India, Persia, and China centuries before Blaise Pascal. The modern notation was introduced by Andreas von Ettingshausen in 1826.

Properties

Binomial coefficients satisfy the symmetry (n choose k) = (n choose n − k) and Pascal's recurrence: (n choose k) = (n − 1 choose k − 1) + (n − 1 choose k). The sum of the coefficients in row n is 2n. They are central to combinatorics, probability (the binomial distribution), and algebra.

Generalisations

The definition extends to real and complex upper indices via the gamma function, giving generalised binomial coefficients used in Newton's binomial series. Multinomial coefficients generalise the idea to partitions into more than two parts.
