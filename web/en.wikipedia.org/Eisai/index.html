Eisai Co., Ltd. (エーザイ株式会社) is a Japanese pharmaceutical company headquartered in Bunkyo, Tokyo. It is one of Japan's largest research-based drugmakers, with a particular focus on neurology and oncology, and employs around 10,000 people worldwide.

History

The company traces its origins to 1936, when Toyotaro Tsukamoto founded a laboratory in Tokyo; Nihon Eisai was formally established in 1941 and renamed Eisai in 1955. The name combines the Japanese words for hygiene ("eisei") and materials/production.

Major products

Eisai's best-known product is Aricept (donepezil), an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor for Alzheimer's disease developed with Pfizer and approved in the United States in 1996. In oncology, its products include Halaven (eribulin) and Lenvima (lenvatinib, developed with Merck). With Biogen it developed the amyloid-targeting Alzheimer's antibodies aducanumab and lecanemab (Leqembi), the latter approved in the United States in 2023.

Philosophy

Eisai promotes a corporate philosophy it calls "human health care" (hhc), under which employees spend part of their working time in direct contact with patients, and the company's articles of incorporation formally commit it to increasing benefits to patients and their families.
