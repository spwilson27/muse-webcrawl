Cervarix

Vaccine description
TargetHuman papillomavirus types 16 and 18
TypeRecombinant (virus-like particle)
ManufacturerGlaxoSmithKline (GSK)
First licensed2007 (European Union); 2009 (United States)

Cervarix is a vaccine against certain types of cancer-causing human papillomavirus (HPV), developed by the pharmaceutical company GlaxoSmithKline. It is a bivalent vaccine protecting against HPV types 16 and 18, which together are responsible for roughly 70% of cervical cancers worldwide. Cervarix is made from virus-like particles (VLPs) of the L1 capsid protein produced in insect cells using a baculovirus expression system, together with GSK's AS04 adjuvant system.

Use

The vaccine is given by intramuscular injection, typically in a two- or three-dose series to adolescents and young adults before likely exposure to the virus. It was first licensed in the European Union in 2007 and approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2009 for females aged 10 through 25. Clinical trials showed very high efficacy against persistent infection and precancerous cervical lesions (CIN2+) caused by HPV 16 and 18, as well as partial cross-protection against some related types such as 31, 33, and 45.

GSK voluntarily withdrew Cervarix from the United States market in 2016 citing low demand, as the competing broader-coverage vaccine Gardasil became dominant; Cervarix remains in use in vaccination programs in many other countries.
