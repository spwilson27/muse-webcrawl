Mermaid

A mermaid is an aquatic creature with the head and upper body of a female human and the tail of a fish. Mermaids appear in the folklore of many cultures worldwide, including Europe, Asia, and Africa. Their male counterparts are called mermen. In some traditions, mermaids eavesdropped on sailors and lured them to their doom; in others they were benevolent, granting wishes or falling in love with humans.

Origins

The earliest known mermaid-like figure is the ancient Assyrian goddess Atargatis, worshipped around 1000 BC, who was said to have transformed into a half-fish form. Similar figures include the Greek Tritons and sirens (originally bird-women, later conflated with fish-tailed beings), the Japanese ningyo, and the West African water spirit Mami Wata.

Folklore and sightings

Medieval bestiaries described mermaids as symbols of vanity and dangerous temptation. Sightings were reported by sailors for centuries; Christopher Columbus recorded seeing "female forms" off the coast of Hispaniola in 1493, now thought to have been manatees. In British folklore, mermaids could foretell disaster, and some, like the Mermaid of Zennor in Cornwall, were linked to church legends.

In popular culture

Hans Christian Andersen's 1837 fairy tale "The Little Mermaid" reshaped the figure for modern audiences, followed by the Copenhagen statue (1913) and Disney's 1989 animated film. Mermaids remain staples of fantasy literature, film, and art.
