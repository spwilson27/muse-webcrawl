United States Coast Guard Cutter

A United States Coast Guard cutter is a commissioned vessel of the United States Coast Guard, generally 65 feet (about 20 metres) or more in length, bearing the prefix USCGC. Smaller vessels are classed as boats. Cutters carry out the Coast Guard's missions of maritime law enforcement, search and rescue, defense readiness, and polar icebreaking.

Terminology

The term "cutter" descends from the sailing revenue cutters of the 1790s. The Coast Guard retained the designation for its larger vessels regardless of type, and hull classification symbols (such as WMSL, WMEC, WPB and WAGB) indicate mission and class.

Modern fleet

The modern fleet includes the Legend-class National Security Cutters, the Heritage-class Offshore Patrol Cutters entering service, Famous- and Reliance-class medium endurance cutters, Sentinel-class Fast Response Cutters, Island-class patrol boats being retired, and the heavy icebreaker Polar Star. The Polar Security Cutter program is intended to recapitalize the polar fleet.

History

Cutters have served in every U.S. conflict since the Revenue-Marine's founding in 1790, from anti-slave-trade patrols and Prohibition rum-running interdiction to convoy escort in the Second World War and counter-narcotics operations today.
