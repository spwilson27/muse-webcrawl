Molecular modelling

Molecular modelling encompasses the theoretical methods and computational techniques used to model or mimic the behaviour of molecules, from small chemical systems to large biological macromolecules and materials. The methods range from quantum-mechanical calculations of electronic structure to classical molecular mechanics and dynamics simulations.

Methods

Quantum chemistry methods, including density functional theory, compute properties from the electronic wavefunction or density but are expensive for large systems. Molecular mechanics represents molecules with empirical force fields — springs for bonds and angles and simple terms for non-bonded forces — enabling molecular dynamics simulations of thousands to millions of atoms over nanosecond-to-microsecond timescales. Intermediate schemes such as QM/MM combine both levels.

Applications

Molecular modelling is a standard tool in drug discovery (docking, virtual screening, and free-energy calculations), protein structure prediction and design, catalysis, polymer and materials science, and the study of enzyme mechanisms. Visualisation software renders structures in styles from stick models to electrostatic surfaces, aiding interpretation.
