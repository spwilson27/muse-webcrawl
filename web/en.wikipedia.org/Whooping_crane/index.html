Whooping crane

Whooping crane (Grus americana)
Height: about 1.5 m (5 ft)
Wingspan: up to 2.3 m
Status: Endangered
Breeding: Wood Buffalo National Park, Canada
Wintering: Texas Gulf Coast

The whooping crane (Grus americana) is an endangered crane native to North America, named for its loud, trumpeting "whooping" call. It is the tallest North American bird and one of the rarest, brought back from the brink of extinction by intensive conservation.

Population recovery

Hunting and habitat loss reduced the species to about 21 wild birds by 1941. The last naturally migrating flock breeds in and around Wood Buffalo National Park in Canada and winters at Aransas National Wildlife Refuge on the Texas coast; it has grown to several hundred birds. Reintroduction programs have established additional flocks, notably a migratory flock in the eastern United States once taught migration routes by ultralight aircraft under Operation Migration, and a non-migratory flock in Louisiana.

Description and habitat

Adults are white with black primary wing feathers and a bare red face, and stand about 1.5 metres tall. They inhabit shallow wetlands, feeding on blue crabs, crayfish, frogs, plants and grains. Threats include habitat loss, collisions with power lines, drought on the wintering grounds and shooting.
