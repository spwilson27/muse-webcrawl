Zone of Avoidance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Zone of Avoidance (ZOA, ZoA), or Zone of Galactic Obscuration (ZGO), is an area on the sky in which extragalactic astronomy is obscured by the Milky Way. The zone was originally called the Zone of Few Nebulae in a 1878 paper by English astronomer Richard Proctor.

Characteristics

The Zone of Avoidance forms a band roughly following the galactic plane; depending on the wavelength of observation, it may hide about 20% of the extragalactic sky at optical wavelengths. The obscuration is caused by interstellar dust absorbing visible light, by the sheer density of Milky Way stars confusing source detection, and — at radio wavelengths — by neutral hydrogen emission and foreground contamination within the Galaxy itself.

Observations

Because dust extinction falls dramatically at infrared wavelengths, surveys such as 2MASS, and radio surveys of the 21-cm line of neutral hydrogen, have allowed astronomers to map galaxies through much of the zone. Notable structures revealed behind the Milky Way include the Great Attractor, the Norma Cluster (ACO 3627), the Vela Supercluster and the galaxy Maffei 1 and Maffei 2, among the nearest large galaxies to the Milky Way. Studying the zone is important for understanding the local cosmic flow and the distribution of nearby large-scale structure.
