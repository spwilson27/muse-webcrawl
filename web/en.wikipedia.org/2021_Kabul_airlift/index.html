The 2021 Kabul airlift was a large-scale evacuation operation conducted at Hamid Karzai International Airport in Kabul, Afghanistan, in August 2021, following the Taliban's capture of the Afghan capital during the final phase of the War in Afghanistan. Between August 15 and August 30, the United States and its allies evacuated more than 124,000 people, making it one of the largest airlifts in history.

Background

Under a 2020 agreement between the United States and the Taliban, U.S. forces were to withdraw from Afghanistan. In the summer of 2021, Taliban forces swept across the country as Afghan government forces collapsed. President Ashraf Ghani fled the country on August 15, 2021, and the Taliban entered Kabul the same day, triggering a rush of Afghans and foreign nationals to the airport.

The evacuation

Chaotic scenes unfolded as thousands of Afghans crowded the runways; several people died clinging to departing aircraft. U.S. and coalition forces secured the airfield while transport aircraft, including C-17 Globemasters, flew continuous sorties. On August 26, a suicide bombing by ISIS-K at the airport's Abbey Gate killed 13 U.S. service members and at least 169 Afghans. The last U.S. military aircraft departed Kabul shortly before midnight on August 30, 2021, ending the nearly 20-year American military presence in Afghanistan.
