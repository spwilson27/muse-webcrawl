Soybean oil


Refined soybean oil

Soybean oil (UK: soya bean oil) is a vegetable oil extracted from the seeds of the soybean (Glycine max). It is one of the most widely consumed cooking oils and the second most consumed vegetable oil worldwide, after palm oil. As a drying oil, processed soybean oil is also used as a base for printing inks and oil paints.

Production

Soybeans contain about 18–20 percent oil by weight. In commercial processing, beans are cracked, conditioned, rolled into flakes, and the oil is extracted with hexane solvent; the crude oil is then degummed, refined, bleached, and deodorized to produce a bland, pale oil. The remaining defatted meal is a major source of protein for animal feed. The United States, Brazil, Argentina, and China are among the largest producers.

Composition and properties

Soybean oil is composed mainly of triglycerides of polyunsaturated fatty acids — roughly 51 percent linoleic acid and 7–10 percent alpha-linolenic acid — along with monounsaturated oleic acid (about 23 percent) and saturated palmitic and stearic acids. Its relatively high polyunsaturated content makes it prone to oxidation; partial hydrogenation was historically used to improve stability, though this produced trans fats and has been phased out in many markets. The smoke point of refined soybean oil is about 234 °C (453 °F).

Uses

Because of its neutral flavour and low cost, soybean oil is widely used for frying and baking and is a principal ingredient in margarine, shortening, mayonnaise, and salad dressings. A growing share of production is used for biodiesel and renewable diesel fuels.
