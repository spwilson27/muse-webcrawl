Zeno's paradoxes

Zeno's paradoxes are a set of philosophical problems devised by the ancient Greek philosopher Zeno of Elea (c. 490–430 BC) to support the doctrine of his teacher Parmenides: that contrary to the evidence of the senses, motion and plurality are illusions, and reality is a single, unchanging whole. The paradoxes — the Dichotomy, Achilles and the tortoise, the Arrow, and the Stadium — have challenged philosophers and mathematicians for over two millennia and remain central to discussions of infinity, continuity, and the nature of space and time.

The paradoxes of motion

The Dichotomy: Before an object can travel a given distance, it must first travel half that distance, and before that half, and so on infinitely; hence it must complete infinitely many sub-journeys and can never even begin, or never finish.

Achilles and the tortoise: If swift Achilles gives a tortoise a head start in a race, then by the time Achilles reaches where the tortoise was, the tortoise has advanced a little further; repeating this argument indefinitely, Achilles can approach ever closer but never overtake the tortoise.

The Arrow: At any single instant, a flying arrow occupies a space exactly equal to itself and is therefore at rest at that instant; since time is composed of instants, the arrow is at rest at every instant and so never moves.

The Stadium: Three rows of bodies, one at rest and two moving in opposite directions, generate contradictions about relative motion and the divisibility of time, suggesting that half a time can equal the whole.

Responses

Aristotle discussed the paradoxes in his Physics, distinguishing potential from actual infinity. Diogenes the Cynic is said to have refuted them by wordlessly walking across the room. The modern mathematical resolution of the Dichotomy and Achilles appeals to the calculus and the convergence of infinite series: an infinite number of ever-smaller intervals can sum to a finite distance and be traversed in finite time (for example, 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + … = 1). Philosophers continue to debate whether this fully answers Zeno — supertasks, measure theory, quantum limits on the divisibility of space and time, and the analysis of instants all remain active topics.
