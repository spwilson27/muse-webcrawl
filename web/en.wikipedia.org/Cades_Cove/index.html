Cades Cove is an isolated valley located in the Tennessee section of Great Smoky Mountains National Park. Surrounded by mountains, the cove is one of the most popular destinations in the park, known for its preserved historic buildings, abundant wildlife and scenic 11-mile (18 km) loop road.

History

For centuries the Cherokee used the valley as hunting grounds; a trail over the mountains connected it to Cherokee settlements in North Carolina. The cove is named after a Cherokee chief called Kade. European-American settlers arrived beginning in 1819–1821, when John Oliver and his wife Lucretia became the first permanent settlers. The community grew to include grist mills, churches, schools and several hundred residents by 1850.

When Tennessee and North Carolina created the national park in the 1920s and 1930s, cove residents were bought out or given lifetime leases; the last school closed in 1944. The National Park Service preserved a collection of log cabins, barns, a working grist mill and three churches as an open-air museum of pioneer life in the Smokies.

Wildlife and visiting

The open fields of the cove make it one of the best places in the park to see white-tailed deer, black bears, coyotes, groundhogs, turkeys and other animals. The loop road is closed to motor vehicles on certain mornings in season so that cyclists and pedestrians can enjoy it. A visitor center and the Cable Mill area stand near the midpoint of the loop.
