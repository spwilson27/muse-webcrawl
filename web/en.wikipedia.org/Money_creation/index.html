Money creation

Money creation, or money issuance, is the process by which the money supply of an economy is increased. In modern economies most money is deposit money created by commercial banks when they make loans, alongside physical currency and central bank reserves created by the central bank.

Bank lending

When a commercial bank makes a loan, it credits the borrower's deposit account, expanding broad money; the deposit is destroyed as the loan is repaid. Bank lending is constrained by capital and liquidity regulation, the demand for loans, and the cost of funding set largely by the central bank's policy rate.

Central bank operations

Central banks create base money — banknotes and commercial banks' reserve balances — through open market operations, lending facilities, and large-scale asset purchases known as quantitative easing.

The money multiplier debate

Textbook accounts long described lending as a multiple of reserves under the money multiplier model. Many central banks, including the Bank of England, now describe the causality as running the other way: loans create deposits, and reserves are supplied elastically afterward.
