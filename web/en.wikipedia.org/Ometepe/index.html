Ometepe is an island formed by two volcanoes, Concepción and Maderas, rising from Lake Nicaragua (Lake Cocibolca) in the Rivas Department of Nicaragua. Its name derives from the Nahuatl words ome (two) and tepetl (hill) — "two hills." With an area of 276 square kilometres, it is the largest island in a freshwater lake of volcanic origin.

Geography

The two cones are joined by a low isthmus, giving the island an hourglass shape. Concepción, on the northwest, is an active, nearly perfect cone (last significant eruptions in the 20th and early 21st centuries, with ash emissions in 2005–2010), while Maderas, to the southeast, is dormant and cloaked in cloud forest, with a crater lake near its summit. The main towns are Moyogalpa and Altagracia, both served by ferries from San Jorge on the mainland.

History and archaeology

Ometepe has been inhabited for at least 3,000 years. It is noted for its pre-Columbian petroglyphs and large stone statues, carved in basalt, many now displayed at the El Ceibo museum and church grounds in Altagracia. The island was settled in the colonial period and suffered raids by pirates who sailed up the San Juan River in the 17th century.

Ecology and tourism

Designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve in 2010, Ometepe protects dry forest, cloud forest, and wetland habitats, with howler monkeys, migratory birds, and the freshwater fauna of the lake. Agriculture — plantains, coffee, tobacco, and beans — dominates the economy, increasingly supplemented by ecotourism, hiking, and visits to the San Ramón waterfall and the Ojo de Agua spring.
