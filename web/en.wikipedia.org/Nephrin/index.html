Nephrin

Nephrin
GeneNPHS1 (chromosome 19q13.12 in humans)
TypeTransmembrane cell-adhesion protein (immunoglobulin superfamily)

Nephrin is a transmembrane protein that is the principal structural component of the slit diaphragm, the filter junction between the foot processes of podocytes in the kidney glomerulus. Encoded by the NPHS1 gene, nephrin is essential for the normal function of the glomerular filtration barrier; without it, plasma proteins leak massively into the urine.

Discovery and structure

Nephrin was identified in 1998 by Karl Tryggvason's group through positional cloning of the gene responsible for congenital nephrotic syndrome of the Finnish type (CNF), a disease long known to cause severe proteinuria from birth. The protein is a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily of cell-adhesion molecules: its extracellular portion contains eight Ig-like domains followed by a fibronectin type III domain, and spans the ~40 nm-wide filtration slit, interacting with nephrin molecules from the adjacent foot process and with partners such as Neph1 and podocin.

Function

Beyond forming a physical sieve, nephrin acts as a signaling hub. Its cytoplasmic tail is phosphorylated by the Src-family kinase Fyn and recruits adaptor proteins (including Nck) that organize the podocyte's actin cytoskeleton, maintaining foot-process architecture. Interactions with podocin, CD2AP, and the actin machinery integrate the slit diaphragm into the cell's mechanical and signaling systems; disruption of this complex underlies several inherited and acquired proteinuric kidney diseases.

Clinical significance

Mutations in NPHS1 cause CNF (NPHS1-type nephrotic syndrome), an autosomal recessive disorder with massive proteinuria in utero and at birth, historically lethal in infancy and now managed with albumin infusions, nephrectomy, dialysis, and transplantation. The two classic Finnish founder mutations are Fin-major (an early frameshift) and Fin-minor (a nonsense mutation). Anti-nephrin autoantibodies have also been reported in some patients with acquired nephrotic syndrome, and reduced nephrin expression occurs in diabetic and other glomerular diseases.
