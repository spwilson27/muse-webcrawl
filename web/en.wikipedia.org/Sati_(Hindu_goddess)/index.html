Sati (Hindu goddess)

Sati (Sanskrit: सती, "the faithful one"), also known as Dakshayani, is a Hindu goddess of marital devotion and longevity. She is the daughter of the patriarch Daksha and the first wife of the god Shiva, who, in the theology of the tradition, later reincarnates as the goddess Parvati. Sati's story, told in the Mahabharata and the Puranas, is one of the foundational myths of goddess worship in Hinduism.

Sati, daughter of Daksha and first consort of Shiva.

Legend

Against her father's wishes, Sati married the ascetic Shiva, whom Daksha despised. When Daksha organised a great fire sacrifice (yajna), he invited all the gods except Shiva. Sati attended uninvited and, publicly insulted by her father, immolated herself in the sacrificial fire — by her yogic power in most versions of the tale. Enraged, Shiva destroyed the sacrifice and, in grief, wandered the universe carrying Sati's body; to restore cosmic order, Vishnu dismembered the corpse, and the places where its parts fell became the Shakti Pithas, the principal shrines of the goddess. Sati was subsequently reborn as Parvati, daughter of the Himalaya, who won Shiva again through ascetic devotion.

Name and practice

The goddess's name, meaning a virtuous and devoted wife, became the source of the term sati (suttee) for the historical rite in which a widow immolated herself on her husband's funeral pyre — a practice distinct from the myth, abolished under British rule in 1829 and criminalised in independent India.
