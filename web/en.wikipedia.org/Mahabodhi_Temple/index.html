Mahabodhi Temple

The Mahabodhi Temple ("Great Awakening Temple") is an ancient Buddhist temple complex at Bodh Gaya, Bihar, India, marking the location where Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, is said to have attained enlightenment (bodhi) while meditating beneath the Bodhi Tree. It is one of the holiest sites in Buddhism and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2002.

History

The emperor Ashoka is traditionally credited with building the first temple on the site in the 3rd century BCE and with erecting the Vajrasana ("Diamond Throne") marking the exact spot of the Buddha's seat. The present pyramidal stone temple dates mainly to the 5th–6th century CE Gupta period, making it one of the earliest Buddhist temples built entirely of brick and a landmark of Indian architecture; it was extensively restored in the 19th century under Burmese missions and the Archaeological Survey of India led by Alexander Cunningham. For centuries the site had fallen under the control of a Hindu mahant; the Bodh Gaya Temple Act of 1949 established shared management, and campaigners such as Anagarika Dharmapala had earlier pressed for Buddhist administration.

Architecture and site

The central tower rises about 55 metres (180 ft) in the nagara style, surrounded by four smaller towers. Inside is a large gilded statue of the Buddha in the earth-touching gesture (bhumisparsha). Behind the temple stands the sacred Bodhi Tree, a descendant of the original tree propagated through Sri Lanka, and around the complex are the seven spots where the Buddha is said to have spent the seven weeks after his enlightenment, the carved railings of the Ashokan period, and monasteries built by Buddhist communities from around the world.
