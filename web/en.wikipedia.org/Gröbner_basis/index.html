Gröbner basis

A Gröbner basis is a particular kind of generating set of an ideal in a polynomial ring k[x1, …, xn] over a field k. The concept was introduced by Bruno Buchberger in his 1965 PhD thesis and named after his supervisor, Wolfgang Gröbner. The Latin-alphabet transliteration Groebner basis is used where the umlaut cannot be typed.

Definition

Fix a monomial order on the polynomial ring. The leading term of a polynomial is its largest monomial. A finite set G contained in an ideal I is a Gröbner basis of I with respect to that order if the ideal generated by the leading terms of the elements of G equals the ideal generated by the leading terms of all elements of I.

Computation

Buchberger's algorithm constructs a Gröbner basis by repeatedly computing S-polynomials — combinations designed to cancel leading terms — and reducing them modulo the current basis. Modern implementations use refined criteria, signature-based methods and F4/F5-style linear algebra to handle large systems.

Applications

Division by a Gröbner basis gives a unique remainder, solving the ideal membership problem. Gröbner bases underpin computational algebraic geometry: they solve polynomial systems, compute dimensions and Hilbert functions, eliminate variables, and are applied in robotics, cryptography, coding theory and systems biology.
