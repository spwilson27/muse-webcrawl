General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon

The General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon is an American single-engine supersonic multirole fighter aircraft originally developed by General Dynamics for the United States Air Force (USAF). Designed as an air superiority day fighter, it evolved into a successful all-weather multirole aircraft with over 4,600 built since 1976. Although no longer purchased by the USAF, improved versions are built for export; production shifted to Lockheed Martin after 1993 and continues for foreign customers. The F-16 is in service with more than 25 air forces worldwide and is often called the "Viper" by its crews.

Design

The F-16 pioneered several innovations in production fighters: a frameless bubble canopy for visibility, a side-mounted control stick, a seat reclined 30 degrees to improve g tolerance, and a relaxed-static-stability airframe controlled by a fly-by-wire system, making it the first production aircraft of its kind. It is powered by a single Pratt & Whitney F100 or General Electric F110 turbofan, with a distinctive chin-mounted intake. The aircraft can sustain 9-g manoeuvres and has a top speed above Mach 2. Armament includes a 20 mm M61 Vulcan cannon and up to eleven hardpoints for missiles, bombs and pods.

Development

The F-16 grew out of the Lightweight Fighter program championed by the informal "Fighter Mafia", which emphasised energy-manoeuvrability theory. The General Dynamics YF-16 won the 1975 fly-off against the Northrop YF-17 and was selected simultaneously by the USAF and four European NATO partners — a deal dubbed at the time "the arms deal of the century".

Operational history

F-16s have flown combat missions in numerous conflicts: Israeli F-16s destroyed Iraq's Osirak reactor in 1981 and fought over Lebanon in 1982; USAF F-16s were heavily engaged in the 1991 Gulf War, the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq; and export users have flown the type in conflicts from the Aegean to South Asia. Upgraded variants such as the F-16C/D Block 50/52 and Block 70/72 "Viper" incorporate AESA radars, conformal fuel tanks and modern avionics. The USAF plans to keep hundreds of F-16s in service into the 2040s alongside the F-35.
