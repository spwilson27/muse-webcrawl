Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis

HLH

Specialty: Haematology, immunology

Symptoms: Fever, enlarged spleen, cytopenias, very high ferritin

Treatment: Immunosuppression (dexamethasone, etoposide), haematopoietic stem cell transplantation for familial forms

Hemophagocytosis in bone marrow

Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), also known as haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis and hemophagocytic syndrome, is an uncommon, life-threatening hyperinflammatory syndrome caused by a severely disturbed immune response in which activated T cells and macrophages proliferate and release large quantities of cytokines, producing fever, cytopenias, liver dysfunction and, histologically, hemophagocytosis — the engulfing of blood cells by macrophages.

Classification

Primary (familial) HLH is caused by inherited defects in cytotoxic lymphocyte function, with mutations in genes such as PRF1 (perforin), UNC13D, STX11 and STXBP2; it typically presents in infancy or early childhood. Secondary HLH occurs in older children and adults in association with infections (especially Epstein–Barr virus), malignancies, autoimmune disease (where it overlaps with macrophage activation syndrome), and immunodeficiency.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis uses the HLH-2004 criteria: five of eight findings — fever, splenomegaly, cytopenias of at least two lineages, hypertriglyceridaemia or hypofibrinogenaemia, hemophagocytosis in marrow, spleen or nodes, low or absent natural killer cell activity, ferritin ≥ 500 µg/L, and elevated soluble CD25. The HScore is used in adults.

Treatment

Treatment aims to suppress the hyperinflammation and remove the trigger: protocols based on dexamethasone and etoposide (HLH-94/2004), treatment of the underlying infection or malignancy, and, for familial and refractory cases, haematopoietic stem cell transplantation. Emapalumab, an anti–interferon-gamma antibody, is approved for refractory primary HLH.
