Benzodiazepine

Benzodiazepines (BZDs, "benzos") are a class of depressant drugs whose core structure is the fusion of a benzene ring and a diazepine ring. They are prescribed for anxiety, insomnia, seizures, muscle spasm and alcohol withdrawal.

Mechanism

Benzodiazepines enhance the effect of the neurotransmitter GABA at the GABAA receptor, increasing chloride ion influx and reducing neuronal excitability, producing sedative, anxiolytic, anticonvulsant and muscle-relaxant effects.

History

The first benzodiazepine, chlordiazepoxide (Librium), was discovered by Leo Sternbach at Hoffmann-La Roche and marketed in 1960, followed by diazepam (Valium) in 1963. They largely replaced the more dangerous barbiturates.

Risks

Long-term use can cause tolerance, dependence and withdrawal symptoms; combined with opioids or alcohol they increase the risk of fatal respiratory depression. Examples include alprazolam, clonazepam, lorazepam and temazepam.
