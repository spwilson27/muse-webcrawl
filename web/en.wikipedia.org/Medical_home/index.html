Medical home

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The medical home, also known as the patient-centred medical home (PCMH), is a team-based health care delivery model that aims to provide comprehensive and continuous medical care to patients with the goal of obtaining maximised health outcomes. It is characterised by a personal physician leading a team that coordinates care across the health system.

Principles

The joint principles of the patient-centred medical home, agreed by major American primary-care organisations in 2007, include a personal physician for each patient, physician-directed team practice, whole-person orientation, coordinated and integrated care, quality and safety, enhanced access, and payment that recognises the added value of the model.

History

The term "medical home" was introduced by the American Academy of Pediatrics in 1967 in the context of care for children with special health-care needs, and was later broadened into a general model for primary care reform.

Evidence

Evaluations suggest medical homes may improve patient experience, preventive care and some measures of quality, with mixed evidence on cost savings.
