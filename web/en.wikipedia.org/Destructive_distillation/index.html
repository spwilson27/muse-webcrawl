Destructive distillation

Destructive distillation is a chemical process in which decomposition of unprocessed material is achieved by heating it to a high temperature in the absence of oxygen; the term is usually applied to the processing of organic material.

Products

Destructive distillation of wood yields charcoal, methanol ("wood alcohol"), acetic acid and tar. Applied to coal, it produces coke, coal tar and coal gas — historically foundational to the chemical industry.

Relation to pyrolysis

The process is a form of pyrolysis; unlike ordinary distillation, the starting material is chemically broken down, so the products differ fundamentally from the feedstock.
