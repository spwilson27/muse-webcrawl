Leuprorelin

Leuprorelin, also known as leuprolide, is a synthetic analogue of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) used as a medication to treat hormone-responsive conditions. It is sold under brand names such as Lupron, Eligard and Prostap. Given by injection or implant, it initially stimulates, then profoundly suppresses, the pituitary release of luteinising hormone and follicle-stimulating hormone, reducing testosterone in men and oestrogen in women to castrate levels.

Leuprorelin is usually given as a long-acting depot injection.

Medical uses

Leuprorelin is a mainstay of androgen deprivation therapy for advanced prostate cancer. In women it is used for endometriosis and to shrink uterine fibroids before surgery. In children it treats central precocious puberty by halting premature pubertal development, and it is also used as a puberty blocker for adolescents with gender dysphoria. It also forms part of some controlled ovarian stimulation regimens in assisted reproduction.

Mechanism

Natural GnRH is released in pulses; continuous exposure to a potent agonist such as leuprorelin first causes a transient surge ("flare") of gonadotropins and sex steroids, then desensitises pituitary GnRH receptors, so hormone output collapses within two to four weeks. In prostate cancer the initial flare may require temporary anti-androgen cover.

Side effects

Hot flushes, sweating, reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, mood changes, injection-site reactions and, with long-term use, loss of bone mineral density and metabolic changes are common consequences of sex-hormone suppression.
