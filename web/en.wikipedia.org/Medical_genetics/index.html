Medical genetics

SpecialtyMedical genetics
FocusDiagnosis and management of hereditary disorders
Key toolsKaryotyping, DNA sequencing, genetic counselling

Medical genetics is the branch of medicine that involves the diagnosis and management of hereditary disorders. Medical genetics is different from human genetics: human genetics is a field of scientific research that may or may not apply to medicine, while medical genetics refers to the application of genetics to medical care. Research on the causes and inheritance of genetic disorders, the diagnosis of rare and common conditions, genetic counselling, and prenatal diagnosis all fall within medical genetics.

The field encompasses disorders caused by single-gene (Mendelian) mutations — such as cystic fibrosis, Huntington's disease and sickle cell disease — chromosomal abnormalities such as Down syndrome, and multifactorial conditions with substantial hereditary components, including many cancers and birth defects. Clinical geneticists work with laboratory specialists using cytogenetics, molecular testing, and whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing; genetic counsellors help patients and families understand risks and make informed decisions.

Subspecialties

Recognised areas include clinical genetics, biochemical genetics (inborn errors of metabolism), cytogenetics, molecular genetics, cancer genetics, and reproductive/prenatal genetics. Pharmacogenomics — tailoring drug therapy to a patient's genotype — is an increasingly important application.
