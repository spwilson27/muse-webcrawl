Intelligent transportation system

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An intelligent transportation system (ITS) is an advanced application which aims to provide innovative services relating to different modes of transport and traffic management, enabling users to be better informed and make safer, more coordinated, and "smarter" use of transport networks. ITS technologies include sensing, communications, and computing applied to vehicles and infrastructure.

Technologies

Common components include traffic signal control systems that adapt timings to measured flows; electronic toll collection, such as transponder-based systems; automatic number-plate recognition for enforcement and congestion charging; variable-message signs; and traveller information delivered by navigation apps and broadcast media. Sensors such as inductive loops, cameras, radar, and GPS probes supply the data on which these systems operate.

Vehicle systems

In-vehicle applications range from satellite navigation and advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) — adaptive cruise control, lane-keeping, and automatic emergency braking — to emergency call systems such as eCall. Vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication aims to let vehicles exchange safety messages with each other and with roadside units using dedicated short-range communications or cellular networks.

Applications and benefits

Public authorities deploy ITS for incident detection and management, ramp metering, integrated public-transport ticketing and real-time passenger information, freight and fleet management, and road-user charging (as in London and Singapore). Evaluations generally find reductions in delays and crashes, though benefits depend on design and uptake. ITS is also central to the development of connected and automated vehicles.

Standards and governance

The United States, the European Union, and Japan maintain ITS architecture frameworks and research programmes; the EU's ITS Directive (2010/40/EU) provides a legal basis for coordinated deployment across member states.
