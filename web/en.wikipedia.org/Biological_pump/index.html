Biological pump

The biological pump is the ocean's biologically driven sequestration of carbon from the atmosphere and surface waters to the deep ocean interior and seafloor sediments. It transfers carbon fixed by photosynthesis into sinking particles, removing it from contact with the atmosphere for centuries to millennia.

Mechanism

Phytoplankton in the sunlit euphotic zone fix CO2. Some of this production is exported as dead cells, aggregates ("marine snow"), and fecal pellets produced by zooplankton, which sink under gravity. Microbes remineralize much of the material at mid-depths; a small fraction reaches sediments and is buried.

Related pumps

The solubility pump (physical dissolution of CO2 in cold water) and carbonate pump (formation and dissolution of calcium carbonate shells) interact with the biological pump. Daily vertical migration of animals actively transports carbon downward.

Climate significance

Without the biological pump, atmospheric CO2 would be far higher. Changes in nutrient supply, warming and ocean acidification may alter pump efficiency, with feedbacks on climate. Proposed ocean iron fertilization seeks to enhance the pump but raises ecological and governance concerns.
