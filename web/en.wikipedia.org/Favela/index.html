Favela

A favela in Rio de Janeiro, with densely built houses covering a hillside.

Favela is a term for a type of low-income informal settlement or slum neighbourhood in Brazil, typically built on the outskirts of large cities or on steep hillsides within them. Favelas are characterised by dense, self-built housing, insecure land tenure, and historically inadequate access to public services such as sanitation, piped water, and electricity. Brazil's statistics institute, the IBGE, officially classifies them as aglomerados subnormais ("subnormal agglomerations"). According to recent census figures, more than 16 million Brazilians live in favelas.

History

The name derives from the favela tree (a spiny shrub, Cnidoscolus quercifolius) native to the sertão of Bahia. Soldiers returning to Rio de Janeiro after the War of Canudos (1896–97), where they had camped on a hill covered in favela trees, settled on a hill near the centre of the city in 1897 while awaiting promised housing and pay; the settlement became known as Morro da Favela. The settlement that grew on Providência Hill is often considered the first favela. Favelas expanded rapidly in the twentieth century as rural migrants moved to industrialising cities such as Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo in search of work.

Characteristics

Houses in favelas are typically built incrementally by residents from brick, concrete, wood, and scrap materials, and neighbourhoods develop dense networks of narrow alleys. Favelas vary greatly: some are small and precarious, while older settlements such as Rocinha in Rio — one of the largest favelas in Brazil — have banks, shops, schools, and media outlets, though residents continue to face violence associated with drug trafficking organisations and militias, and heavy-handed policing.

Government policies

Policy toward favelas has shifted over time between forced removal and upgrading. From 2008, Rio de Janeiro implemented "Pacifying Police Units" (UPPs) intended to retake territory from armed groups before the 2014 World Cup and 2016 Olympics. Federal programmes have invested in paving, drainage, and sanitation in some settlements, and residents' movements advocate for tenure regularisation and integration of favelas into the formal city.

Culture

Favelas have produced distinctive and influential cultural forms, including funk carioca music, passinho dance, and a notable share of Brazil's footballers, musicians, and writers, and they are increasingly depicted in Brazilian cinema and literature, for example in the film City of God (2002).
