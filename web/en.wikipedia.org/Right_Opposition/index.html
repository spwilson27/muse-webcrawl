The Right Opposition was a faction within the Communist Party of the Soviet Union in the late 1920s, led by Nikolai Bukharin, Alexei Rykov and Mikhail Tomsky. It defended the market-oriented New Economic Policy (NEP) and opposed Joseph Stalin's turn to forced collectivisation and breakneck industrialisation.

Background

After Lenin's death in 1924, Bukharin allied with Stalin to defeat first Leon Trotsky's Left Opposition and then the United Opposition of Trotsky, Zinoviev and Kamenev. Bukharin's slogan "Enrich yourselves!", addressed to the peasantry, and his theory of growing into socialism through the market made him the leading theorist of the NEP.

Break with Stalin

The grain procurement crisis of 1927–28 led Stalin to adopt emergency requisitioning and then full collectivisation — policies the Right Opposition condemned as "military-feudal exploitation of the peasantry" that would destroy the worker–peasant alliance (smychka). In private, Bukharin called Stalin "Genghis Khan" and sought, in vain, an alliance with the defeated Kamenev.

Defeat

Stalin, controlling the party apparatus, denounced the group as a "right deviation" and kulak agency. At the April 1929 Central Committee plenum the Bukharin group was condemned; Tomsky lost the trade unions, Rykov the premiership (1930), and Bukharin the editorship of Pravda and the Comintern leadership. The leaders recanted repeatedly but were never restored to power.

Fate

During the Great Terror the former Right Oppositionists became principal targets: Bukharin and Rykov were defendants in the Trial of the Twenty-One in March 1938 and were executed; Tomsky shot himself in 1936 when implicated. Bukharin was rehabilitated in the USSR only in 1988, during perestroika.
