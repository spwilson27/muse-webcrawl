John C. Bogle

[Image placeholder: portrait of John C. Bogle]

John Clifton "Jack" Bogle (May 8, 1929 – January 16, 2019) was an American investor, business magnate, and philanthropist. He was the founder of The Vanguard Group and is credited with creating the first index fund available to individual investors and with being a driving force in the growth of low-cost mutual funds. An assembly of followers is self-identified as "Bogleheads" in his honour.

Bogle graduated from Princeton University in 1951; his senior thesis argued that mutual funds should be run in "the most efficient, honest, and economical way possible". After a career at Wellington Management, a merger dispute led to his dismissal in 1974, and he founded Vanguard the following year. In 1976 Vanguard launched the First Index Investment Trust (now the Vanguard 500 Index Fund), the first stock index fund marketed to the public; initially mocked as "Bogle's folly", index investing eventually grew into the dominant approach in fund management.

Philosophy

Bogle championed low costs, broad diversification, and long-term buy-and-hold investing, arguing that most active managers cannot beat the market after fees. His books include Bogle on Mutual Funds (1993) and Common Sense on Mutual Funds (1999), along with the later The Little Book of Common Sense Investing. Vanguard's unusual mutual ownership structure — the funds own the management company — was central to his model of aligning the firm with its investors.

Bogle retired as Vanguard's CEO in 1996 and continued writing and speaking on corporate governance and investor stewardship until his death in 2019 at age 89.
