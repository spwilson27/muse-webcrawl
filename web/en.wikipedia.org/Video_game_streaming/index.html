Video game streaming

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Video game streaming, in the context of online media, refers to the activity of broadcasting oneself playing video games live over the internet, typically with commentary, a webcam view of the player and a live chat through which viewers interact. Streamers broadcast on platforms such as Twitch, YouTube, Facebook Gaming and Kick, and audiences watch for entertainment, community, and to learn game strategies.

The term is also used for cloud gaming services — sometimes called gaming on demand — in which games are run on remote servers and the video is streamed to the player, as with GeForce Now and Xbox Cloud Gaming. This article principally concerns live broadcasting by players.

History

Live game broadcasting grew out of "Let's Play" videos and speedrunning communities in the late 2000s. Justin.tv, launched in 2007, spun off its gaming section as Twitch in 2011; Twitch's rapid growth led to its acquisition by Amazon in 2014 for about $970 million. Esports tournaments, charity marathons such as Games Done Quick, and star players like Ninja — who famously streamed with rapper Drake in 2018 — pushed streaming into mainstream culture.

Profession and culture

Successful streamers earn income from subscriptions, advertising, sponsorships and viewer donations, and the biggest channels attract millions of followers. Streaming has become a major marketing channel for game publishers, a career path, and a spectator medium rivalling traditional sports broadcasts in younger demographics. It has also raised issues including copyright claims over in-game music, moderation of harassment, gambling-related content, and streamer burnout.
