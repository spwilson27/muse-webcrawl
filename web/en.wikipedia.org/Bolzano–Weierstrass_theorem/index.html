Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem

In mathematics, specifically in real analysis, the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, named after Bernard Bolzano and Karl Weierstrass, is a fundamental result about convergence in finite-dimensional Euclidean space. The theorem states that each infinite bounded sequence in Rn has a convergent subsequence. An equivalent formulation is that a subset of Rn is sequentially compact if and only if it is closed and bounded. The theorem is therefore sometimes called the sequential compactness theorem.

The classical proof repeatedly halves an interval to isolate a limit point.

History

Bolzano first proved a related result in 1817—the existence of a least upper bound for bounded sets, using a bisection argument—but his work was largely overlooked. Karl Weierstrass, in his Berlin lectures of the 1860s and 1870s, proved that every bounded infinite set in Rn has an accumulation point, establishing the theorem in the form now taught. The result became a cornerstone of the rigorous reformulation of analysis in the 19th century.

Proof sketch

For a bounded sequence in R, one repeatedly bisects a bounding interval, keeping a half that contains infinitely many terms of the sequence; the nested intervals shrink to a single point, and choosing one term from each successive interval yields a subsequence converging to that point (using the nested interval property, which rests on the completeness of the real numbers). For Rn, the one-dimensional result is applied successively to each coordinate, extracting nested subsequences finitely many times.

Bounded sequence (xk) in Rn ⇒ there exists a subsequence (xkj) with limj→∞ xkj = x.

Applications and generalisations

The theorem underlies proofs of the extreme value theorem, the Heine–Borel theorem, and existence results throughout analysis and economics—for example, in demonstrating that continuous functions on compact sets attain their maxima and in existence proofs for equilibria and optima. In general metric spaces, the analogous statement characterises compactness: a metric space is compact if and only if every sequence has a convergent subsequence (sequential compactness), though closed and bounded no longer suffices outside finite dimensions, as shown by the Riesz lemma for infinite-dimensional normed spaces.
