Tuber

TypePlant storage organ
StructureModified stem or root
FunctionNutrient storage, perennation, asexual reproduction
ExamplesPotato, sweet potato, yam

A tuber is a modified plant structure enlarged to store nutrients in a stem or root. Tubers are a means plants use to perennate (survive an annual cycle, from one growing season to the next, especially through winter or dry months), to store energy in a dormant season, and to provide asexual reproduction.

Stem tubers

Stem tubers form from thickened rhizomes (stems growing horizontally below the soil) or from stolons (horizontal connectors). The classic example is the potato (Solanum tuberosum). The tuber bears buds in the axils of reduced leaves — the familiar "eyes" of a potato — each of which can grow into a new plant. Other stem tubers include the tuberous begonia, the cyclamen, the Jerusalem artichoke, and the oca.

Root tubers

Root tubers are modified lateral roots that have thickened and withdrawn the nutrients of the plant. Examples include the sweet potato (Ipomoea batatas), cassava, the Dahlia root, and yams (Dioscorea species). True root tubers form at the base of the original stem and possess no nodes or internodes; buds are found only on the proximal end, the part closest to the crown.

Agricultural significance

Tubers are among the world's most important staple crops. The potato is the world's fourth-largest food crop after maize, wheat, and rice, and is a key source of carbohydrate, potassium, and vitamin C. Sweet potatoes, cassava, and yams are vital food security crops across tropical Africa, Asia, and Latin America.
