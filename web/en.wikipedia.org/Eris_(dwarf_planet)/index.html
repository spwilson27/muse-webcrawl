Eris (dwarf planet)

Eris (minor-planet designation 136199 Eris) is a dwarf planet and the most massive object in the scattered disc of the outer Solar System, slightly more massive than Pluto. With a diameter of about 2,326 kilometres, it is marginally smaller than Pluto but roughly 27 per cent more massive. Eris has one known moon, Dysnomia. As of the mid-2020s it lies about 96 astronomical units from the Sun, near its aphelion, and takes 558 years to complete one orbit.

Discovery

Eris was discovered by a Palomar Observatory team led by Mike Brown, with Chad Trujillo and David Rabinowitz, from images taken in October 2003; the discovery was announced in July 2005. Nicknamed "Xena" by the team, it was initially reported as possibly larger than Pluto and hailed in the press as a "tenth planet". Its discovery forced the International Astronomical Union to define the term "planet" in 2006, a process that created the dwarf planet category and reclassified Pluto — a decision Brown later embraced in his memoir How I Killed Pluto and Why It Had It Coming.

Characteristics

Eris has a highly eccentric and steeply inclined orbit (inclination about 44 degrees). Its surface, studied through spectroscopy, is covered with bright methane ice, giving it one of the highest albedos of any Solar System body (about 0.96), comparable to Saturn's moon Enceladus. Its high density, about 2.43 g/cm³, implies a largely rocky interior with a mantle of water ice. The moon Dysnomia allowed astronomers to determine Eris's mass through Kepler's third law.
