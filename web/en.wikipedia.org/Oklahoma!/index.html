Oklahoma! is the first musical written by the duo of composer Richard Rodgers and lyricist-librettist Oscar Hammerstein II. The musical is based on Lynn Riggs' 1931 play Green Grow the Lilacs. Set in farm country outside the town of Claremore, Indian Territory, in 1906, it tells the story of farm girl Laurey Williams and her courtship by two rival suitors, cowboy Curly McLain and the sinister and frightening farmhand Jud Fry.

Background

Rodgers and Hammerstein began working together after Rodgers's earlier collaborator, Lorenz Hart, declined into illness and alcoholism. Oklahoma! was a box-office smash and ran for an then-unprecedented 2,212 performances on Broadway. It is credited with ushering in the "golden age" of the American musical, integrating songs, dances, and plot into a unified whole.

Plot

Curly visits Laurey's farm and asks her to the box social. Laurey, fearing the brooding farmhand Jud Fry, has already promised to go with Jud. At the social, a bidding war for Laurey's picnic basket ends when Curly sells his saddle, horse, and gun to outbid Jud. Laurey fires Jud, who threatens her; later Curly and Laurey marry, and a drunken Jud attacks Curly with a knife but falls on his own blade. The local community quickly acquits Curly so the couple can leave on their honeymoon.

Notable songs

  • "Oh, What a Beautiful Mornin'"
  • "The Surrey with the Fringe on Top"
  • "People Will Say We're in Love"
  • "Oklahoma" (later the state song of Oklahoma)

Film adaptation

A film version was released in 1955, directed by Fred Zinnemann and starring Gordon MacRae and Shirley Jones. The musical has enjoyed many revivals, including a notable 2019 Broadway production by Daniel Fish that reimagined the show in a darker, more intimate staging.
