Shenzhen is a major sub-provincial city in Guangdong province, China, on the eastern bank of the Pearl River estuary, directly north of Hong Kong. Once a market town and fishing community, it became in 1980 the first and most successful of China's special economic zones (SEZs) under Deng Xiaoping's reforms, growing within four decades into a global centre for technology, manufacturing, finance and logistics, often described as China's Silicon Valley.

History

The area was part of Bao'an County for centuries. Following the signing of treaties in the 19th century, the southern part, Hong Kong, came under British control, divided from Shenzhen by the Sham Chun River. In May 1980 Shenzhen was designated a special economic zone as part of the Chinese economic reform, attracting foreign investment with tax incentives and looser regulation. Its population exploded through internal migration, and in 2011 it became the first major Chinese city composed entirely of migrants with no registered native population majority in the traditional sense. It hosted the 2011 Summer Universiade.

Economy

Shenzhen is home to the Shenzhen Stock Exchange and headquarters of technology firms including Huawei, Tencent, DJI, BYD and ZTE. The city is a leading centre for electronics manufacturing, centred on Huaqiangbei, and for hardware prototyping. Its container port is among the world's busiest. The model of rapid, export-led growth in Shenzhen is often discussed alongside state capitalism and market socialism with Chinese characteristics.

Geography and climate

Shenzhen has a humid subtropical climate influenced by monsoons, with hot humid summers and mild winters. It borders Dongguan and Huizhou to the north and Hong Kong to the south. Dapeng Peninsula extends into Mirs Bay and Daya Bay.

Transport and culture

The Shenzhen Metro, opened in 2004, is one of China's largest. Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport serves the city. Landmarks include Ping An Finance Centre — among the world's tallest buildings — Window of the World, Splendid China, and the contemporary art and design scene of OCT Loft.
