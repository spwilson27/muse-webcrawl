Adenocarcinoma
SpecialtyOncology, pathology
DefinitionMalignant epithelial tumour with glandular differentiation
Common sitesLung, colon, prostate, pancreas, breast, stomach, oesophagus, cervix
DiagnosisHistopathology of biopsy; immunohistochemistry
TreatmentSurgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted and immunotherapy

Adenocarcinoma (from Greek adēn, "gland", and karkinōma, "cancerous tumour") is a type of cancer that forms in mucus-secreting glandular cells. It is a malignant epithelial tumour characterised by gland formation and/or mucin production, and it can arise wherever glands or gland-like epithelia exist in the body.

Sites and epidemiology

Adenocarcinomas account for the great majority of cancers of the colon, prostate, pancreas and oesophagus, most cancers of the stomach and cervix, about 40% of lung cancers (the commonest type in non-smokers and women), and a large share of breast cancers (invasive ductal carcinoma of no special type being of glandular origin). Incidence of oesophageal adenocarcinoma has risen sharply in Western countries, associated with gastro-oesophageal reflux, Barrett's oesophagus and obesity.

Pathology

Under the microscope, tumour cells form glands of variable differentiation; poorly differentiated tumours may contain signet-ring cells filled with mucin. Grading and staging (TNM) guide prognosis and treatment. Immunostains and molecular profiling (e.g. KRAS, EGFR, ALK in lung cancer; HER2 in gastric and breast cancer) increasingly direct targeted therapy.

Treatment

Management depends on site and stage: surgical resection with curative intent for localised disease, combined with adjuvant or neoadjuvant chemotherapy and radiotherapy; for advanced disease, systemic chemotherapy, targeted agents (such as EGFR or HER2 inhibitors), and immune checkpoint inhibitors have improved survival in selected patients.
