Madurai is a major city in the Indian state of Tamil Nadu, situated on the banks of the Vaigai River. One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with a recorded history stretching back some 2,500 years, it was the capital of the Pandya kingdom and a famed centre of Tamil learning under the Sangam academies.

History

Madurai appears in Greek and Roman accounts of trade with India and in early Tamil Sangam literature. It was ruled successively by the Pandyas, Cholas, the Madurai Sultanate, Vijayanagara and the Madurai Nayaks, under whom many of its monuments were built. The Meenakshi Amman Temple — dedicated to goddess Meenakshi (a form of Parvati) and her consort Sundareswarar (Shiva) — dominates the city centre with its fourteen towering gopurams covered in thousands of painted stone figures. The annual Meenakshi Tirukalyanam festival draws huge crowds.

Culture and economy

Madurai is a pilgrimage and tourism hub, an educational centre (Madurai Kamaraj University was founded in 1966), and a commercial centre for textiles, jasmine flowers and granite. The Thirumalai Nayakkar Mahal palace (1636) and Gandhi Memorial Museum are other landmarks. The city is known for its street food, its 24-hour bustle, and its association with classical Tamil literature.

Vaigai and festivals

The Chithirai Festival, among the largest in India, re-enacts the celestial wedding of Meenakshi and Sundareswarar and the journey of Lord Kallazhagar into the Vaigai River. Madurai serves as a gateway to hill stations such as Kodaikanal and to the temple towns of southern Tamil Nadu.
