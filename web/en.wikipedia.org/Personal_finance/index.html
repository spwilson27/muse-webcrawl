Personal finance

Personal finance is the financial management which an individual or a family performs to budget, save and spend monetary resources in a manner that considers future financial risks and life events. It encompasses how people earn, save, invest and protect money, and includes banking, budgeting, mortgages, investments, insurance, retirement planning and tax planning.

Financial planning

A personal financial plan typically assesses net worth (assets minus liabilities) and cash flow, then sets goals across several areas:

  • Budgeting — tracking income and expenditure so spending stays within earnings;
  • Protection — insurance against illness, disability, death and property loss, and an emergency fund of liquid savings;
  • Debt management — repaying high-interest debt and using credit prudently;
  • Investment — accumulating assets such as shares, bonds, funds and property matched to goals, horizon and risk tolerance;
  • Retirement planning — pension contributions and other long-term saving;
  • Estate and tax planning — wills, powers of attorney and tax-efficient arrangements.

Behavioural aspects

Behavioural finance shows that personal financial outcomes are strongly influenced by habits and biases — present bias, loss aversion and herd behaviour can undermine saving and investing. Rules of thumb, automation of saving, and financial education are commonly proposed remedies. Financial literacy is increasingly taught in schools and is the subject of public policy in many countries.

Advice

Personal finance advice is provided by regulated financial advisers, accountants, books, media and online tools. Do-it-yourself investing through low-cost index funds and robo-advisers has grown since the 2000s, alongside budgeting apps and comparison services.
