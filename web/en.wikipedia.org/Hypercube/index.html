In geometry, a hypercube is an n-dimensional analogue of a square (n = 2) and a cube (n = 3). It is a closed, compact, convex figure whose 1-skeleton consists of groups of opposite parallel line segments aligned in each of the space's dimensions, perpendicular to each other, and of the same length. An n-dimensional hypercube is also called an n-cube.

Construction

A hypercube can be built inductively: a 0-cube is a point; moving the point sweeps out a segment (1-cube); sweeping a segment perpendicular to itself gives a square (2-cube); sweeping the square gives a cube (3-cube); and sweeping the cube into a fourth dimension gives a tesseract (4-cube). The coordinates of the vertices of a unit n-cube can be taken as all points whose coordinates are each 0 or 1.

Elements

An n-cube has 2n vertices and 2n faces of dimension n−1. The number of m-dimensional elements is given by 2n−m × C(n, m). Hypercubes appear outside geometry as well: the graph formed by vertices and edges of an n-cube, the hypercube graph Qn, is used in parallel computing interconnection networks, and binary strings of length n correspond to its vertices.

In culture

The tesseract has become a cultural icon of the fourth dimension, appearing in Edwin Abbott's Flatland tradition, Robert A. Heinlein's "—And He Built a Crooked House—", Salvador Dalí's painting Crucifixion (Corpus Hypercubus), and numerous films.
