Erwin Schrödinger

Erwin Rudolf Josef Alexander Schrödinger (12 August 1887 – 4 January 1961) was an Austrian physicist who developed the fundamental results underlying quantum theory. He formulated the Schrödinger equation, which describes how the quantum state of a physical system changes in time and earned him the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1933, shared with Paul Dirac. He is also known to the wider public for the "Schrödinger's cat" thought experiment concerning the interpretation of quantum mechanics.

Life

Schrödinger was born in Vienna, the son of a botanist and factory owner, and studied theoretical physics at the University of Vienna under Friedrich Hasenöhrl. After wartime service as an artillery officer, he held positions at Jena, Stuttgart, Breslau and Zurich. In late 1925, while at Zurich, he produced his wave mechanics papers (1926), providing a wave equation for non-relativistic quantum mechanics equivalent to the matrix mechanics of Heisenberg's school. In 1927 he succeeded Max Planck in Berlin. Opposed to the Nazi regime, he left Germany in 1933 for Oxford, then — after a brief return to Graz, from which he fled after the Anschluss — settled in Dublin at Eamon de Valera's invitation in 1939, working at the Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies until his retirement in 1956. He returned to Vienna, where he died in 1961.

Schrödinger's cat and What Is Life?

In 1935 Schrödinger devised his famous thought experiment: a cat sealed in a box with a radioactive source and a poison mechanism would, under the Copenhagen interpretation as he caricatured it, be in a superposition of alive and dead until observed — an absurdity meant to expose the problem of applying quantum superposition to macroscopic objects. His short book What Is Life? (1944), based on Dublin lectures, argued that the genetic material must be an "aperiodic crystal" and speculated on its physical basis; it influenced James Watson, Francis Crick, and other pioneers of molecular biology. He also wrote extensively on the philosophy of science, consciousness, and his Vedantic interests.
