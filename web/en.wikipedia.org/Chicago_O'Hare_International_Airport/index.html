Chicago O'Hare International Airport (IATA: ORD) is the primary international airport serving Chicago, Illinois, located about 17 miles (27 km) north-west of the Loop. One of the world's busiest airports, it is a major hub for both United Airlines and American Airlines and a founding connector of the global hub-and-spoke system.

History

The site began as Orchard Field, an airfield beside a Douglas aircraft plant built during the Second World War. In 1949 it was renamed for Edward "Butch" O'Hare, a naval aviator and Medal of Honor recipient killed in 1943. Scheduled jet services began in the late 1950s, and O'Hare surpassed Midway as Chicago's main airport, becoming the world's busiest for much of the late 20th century. Its IATA code, ORD, preserves the old "Orchard" name.

Facilities and expansion

The airport has four numbered passenger terminals and eight runways following a long modernisation programme that realigned its diagonal runways into parallel east–west strips. The underground Airport Transit System links terminals, and the Blue Line connects O'Hare to downtown Chicago. A new global terminal designed by Studio Gang is under construction to replace Terminal 2. O'Hare competes with Atlanta and Dallas/Fort Worth for the title of busiest US airport by movements and passengers.
