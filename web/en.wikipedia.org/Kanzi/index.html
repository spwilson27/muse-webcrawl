Kanzi (28 October 1980 – 18 March 2025) was a male bonobo known for his abilities in language and cognition research. Raised in a language-rich environment at the Yerkes National Primate Research Center and later at Georgia State University's Language Research Center and the Ape Initiative in Des Moines, Iowa, Kanzi learned to communicate with humans using a keyboard of lexigrams (arbitrary symbols) and to understand spoken English.

Language abilities

Kanzi acquired his lexigram vocabulary not through direct training but by observing the attempts to teach his adoptive mother, Matata, who proved a poor learner. In controlled tests he responded correctly to novel spoken sentences — for example distinguishing "put the raisins in the shoe" from similar requests — at levels comparable to a two-year-old child. He used several hundred lexigrams, combined them in short proto-grammatical sequences, and displayed gestural and vocal communication.

Other skills

Kanzi learned to knap stone tools, producing flakes and sharp edges to cut open containers, and taught himself to build and light fires with matches and lighters to cook food. His abilities have featured in debates about ape cognition, the uniqueness of human language and the ethics of great ape research. His sister Panbanisha, also a notable research subject, died in 2012.
