Nowruz

Nowruz (Persian: نوروز, "new day") is the Persian New Year, marking the first day of spring and the beginning of the year in the Solar Hijri calendar. It is celebrated on or around the March equinox (usually 20 or 21 March) by more than 300 million people across Iran, Afghanistan, Central Asia, the Caucasus, Kurdistan, parts of the Balkans and the worldwide Iranian and Turkic diasporas.

Origins and customs

Nowruz has Zoroastrian roots and has been celebrated in Greater Iran for over 3,000 years. Preparations include spring cleaning (khaneh tekani), and on the eve of the last Wednesday before the New Year, the fire festival of Chaharshanbe Suri, in which people jump over bonfires. The centrepiece of the celebration is the Haft-Sin table, set with seven items beginning with the Persian letter sin — including sprouted greens (sabzeh), sweet pudding (samanu), oleaster (senjed), garlic (sir), apples (sib), sumac (somaq) and vinegar (serkeh) — together with a mirror, candles, painted eggs, goldfish and a book of wisdom.

Festivities last about two weeks, including visits to elders and the exchanging of gifts, and conclude on the thirteenth day, Sizdah Bedar, when families picnic outdoors to ward off ill luck.

Recognition

In 2009 UNESCO inscribed Nowruz on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and the United Nations proclaimed 21 March the International Day of Nowruz.
