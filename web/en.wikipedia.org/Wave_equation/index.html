Wave equation

The wave equation is a second-order linear partial differential equation that describes the propagation of waves — such as sound waves, light waves, and water waves — through a medium. In one spatial dimension it takes the form ∂²u/∂t² = c² ∂²u/∂x², where u is the wave's displacement, t is time, x is position, and c is the wave speed.

History

The one-dimensional equation was derived by Jean le Rond d'Alembert in 1746, who found its general solution as a sum of two travelling waves. Leonhard Euler and Daniel Bernoulli developed the theory further, and the equation became a cornerstone of mathematical physics.

Solutions and applications

General solutions can be built from plane waves, standing waves, and spherical waves, often found by separation of variables or Fourier analysis. The wave equation underlies acoustics, electromagnetism (where Maxwell's equations imply it for electric and magnetic fields), seismology, and fluid dynamics. Variants include inhomogeneous equations for driven systems and nonlinear equations describing large-amplitude waves.
