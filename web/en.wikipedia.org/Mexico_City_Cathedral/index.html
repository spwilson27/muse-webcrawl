Mexico City Metropolitan Cathedral

LocationZócalo, Mexico City
Construction1573–1813
ArchitectManuel Tolsá (completed)
DenominationRoman Catholic

The Mexico City Metropolitan Cathedral (Spanish: Catedral Metropolitana de la Asunción de la Santísima Virgen María a los cielos) is the seat of the Archdiocese of Mexico and stands atop the former Aztec Templo Mayor site on the north side of the Zócalo (Plaza de la Constitución) in downtown Mexico City.

Architecture

Built in stages between 1573 and 1813 using stones from the destroyed Templo Mayor, the cathedral combines Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, Churrigueresque and Neoclassical elements. Its interior contains sixteen chapels, the ornate Altar of the Kings and the Altar of Forgiveness, and two of the largest 18th-century organs in the Americas. The adjoining Metropolitan Sagrario houses the parish archives.

Preservation

The cathedral has sunk unevenly into the soft lakebed clays of the former Lake Texcoco; major engineering works in the 1990s and 2000s corrected its tilt and stabilised the structure.
