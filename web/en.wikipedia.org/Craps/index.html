Craps

Type: Dice game / casino game
Players: 1–20 around a table
Key bets: Pass line, don't pass, come, odds

Craps is a dice game in which players bet on the outcome of the roll of a pair of dice. Players may wager against the casino ("bank craps", the standard casino form) or against each other in informal "street craps". The game is known for its fast pace, noisy atmosphere, and one of the lowest house edges in the casino on its basic bets.

Gameplay

The shooter rolls the dice for all players. On the come-out roll, a total of 7 or 11 ("natural") wins for pass-line bettors, 2, 3, or 12 ("craps") loses, and any other total (4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10) becomes the "point". The shooter then keeps rolling until the point repeats (pass wins) or a 7 appears ("seven out", pass loses). Behind the pass line, players may place an "odds" bet paid at true odds with no house edge — a feature unique among common casino games.

History

Craps descends from the English game of hazard, popular for centuries and mentioned by Chaucer. Bernard Xavier Philippe de Marigny is credited with bringing the game to New Orleans around 1805, and the simplified American version spread through riverboats and army camps. John H. Winn introduced the modern bank craps layout and the "don't pass" bet in the early 20th century, making the game viable for casinos to bank fairly.

Bets and odds

Beyond the pass line, players can make come bets, place bets on numbers, hardways, and one-roll proposition bets in the table's centre, which carry much higher house edges. The pass line with maximum odds is regarded as among the best bets available in a casino.
