Taira no Kiyomori (1118–1181) was a Japanese military leader of the late Heian period who established the first samurai-dominated administrative government in the history of Japan.

Rise to power

Kiyomori led the Taira to victory in the Hōgen Rebellion (1156) and Heiji Rebellion (1160), becoming the de facto ruler of the imperial court. He attained the highest court rank and the office of Daijō-daijin (Chancellor of the Realm) in 1167, married his daughters into the imperial line, and placed his grandson Antoku on the throne.

Rule and fall

His wealth rested partly on trade with Song China through the port of Fukuhara (modern Kobe). His high-handed rule provoked the Genpei War (1180–1185); he died of illness in 1181, and the Taira were destroyed at the Battle of Dan-no-ura four years later. His life is chronicled in The Tale of the Heike.
