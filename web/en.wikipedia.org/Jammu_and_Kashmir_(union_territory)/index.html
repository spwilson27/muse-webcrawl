Jammu and Kashmir is a region administered by India as a union territory, located in the northern part of the Indian subcontinent. It consists of two divisions, Jammu and Kashmir, divided by the Pir Panjal range. The territory was created on 31 October 2019 following the reorganisation of the former state of Jammu and Kashmir under the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, which also carved out Ladakh as a separate union territory.

History

The former princely state of Jammu and Kashmir acceded to India in 1947 under Maharaja Hari Singh. The region has been the subject of a territorial dispute between India, Pakistan and China. In August 2019 the Government of India revoked the special status granted under Article 370 of the Indian constitution, and parliament passed legislation bifurcating the state into two union territories.

Geography

The Kashmir Valley is known for its lakes, including Dal Lake, and houseboats, while the Jammu division is largely foothill and plain terrain along the Chenab and Tawi rivers. The territory shares borders with Himachal Pradesh and Punjab to the south, Ladakh to the east, and Pakistan-administered territories to the west and north.

Government

As a union territory with a legislature, Jammu and Kashmir is administered by a Lieutenant Governor appointed by the President of India, alongside an elected Legislative Assembly and Council of Ministers headed by a Chief Minister. Elections to the assembly were held in 2024, the first since reorganisation.

Tourism

Srinagar, Gulmarg, Pahalgam and Sonamarg in Kashmir, and Vaishno Devi and Patnitop in Jammu, are major tourist destinations.
