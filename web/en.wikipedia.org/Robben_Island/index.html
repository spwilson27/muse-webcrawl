Robben Island

Robben Island in Table Bay, Cape Town

Robben Island (Afrikaans: Robbeneiland) is an island in Table Bay, 6.9 kilometres (4.3 mi) west of the coast of Bloubergstrand, north of Cape Town, South Africa. It takes its name from the Dutch word for seals (robben), hence the literal translation "Seal Island".

History

Since the end of the 17th century the island has been used to isolate political prisoners, lepers and the mentally ill. The Dutch East India Company used it as a refreshment and penal station. In the 19th century it held Xhosa and Khoikhoi leaders who resisted colonial expansion.

Maximum security prison

Under apartheid the island became a maximum-security prison for political prisoners. Nelson Mandela was imprisoned there for 18 of the 27 years he served behind bars before the fall of apartheid, much of it doing hard labour in a limestone quarry. Fellow prisoners included Jacob Zuma, Ahmed Kathrada, Walter Sisulu and Robert Sobukwe.

Museum and heritage site

The last prisoners left in 1991 and the prison closed in 1996. Robben Island was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999. The Robben Island Museum runs ferry tours from the Victoria & Alfred Waterfront, guided in part by former political prisoners.
