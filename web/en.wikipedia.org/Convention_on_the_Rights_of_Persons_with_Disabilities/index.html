Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities

The Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) is an international human rights treaty of the United Nations intended to protect the rights and dignity of persons with disabilities. Parties to the convention are required to promote, protect, and ensure the full enjoyment of human rights by persons with disabilities and ensure that persons with disabilities enjoy full equality under the law. The Convention was adopted by the United Nations General Assembly on 13 December 2006 and opened for signature on 30 March 2007. It entered into force on 3 May 2008, following ratification by the 20th party.

History

The Convention was negotiated during eight sessions of an Ad Hoc Committee of the General Assembly between 2002 and 2006, making it the fastest negotiated human rights treaty. It followed decades of work by the United Nations and disability rights movements to change attitudes and approaches to persons with disabilities, moving from viewing them as objects of charity and medical treatment to viewing them as subjects of rights. The motto of the negotiations was "Nothing about us without us", reflecting the active participation of disabled people's organisations.

Summary of provisions

The Convention's guiding principles include respect for inherent dignity and individual autonomy, non-discrimination, full and effective participation and inclusion in society, accessibility, and equality of opportunity. Its fifty articles address civil and political rights as well as economic, social and cultural rights, covering areas such as accessibility, personal mobility, health, education, employment, habilitation and rehabilitation, participation in political life, and equality and non-discrimination. The text includes specific articles on women with disabilities and children with disabilities.

Optional Protocol

The Optional Protocol to the Convention allows the Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities to receive and consider communications from individuals claiming to be victims of a violation of the Convention by a state party, and to initiate inquiries into grave or systematic violations.

Monitoring

Implementation is monitored by the Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, a body of human rights experts to which states parties must regularly report. The Conference of States Parties meets regularly to consider matters relating to implementation.
