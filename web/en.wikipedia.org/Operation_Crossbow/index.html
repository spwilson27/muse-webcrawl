Operation Crossbow

Operation Crossbow
DateAugust 1943 – March 1945
LocationFrance, Belgium, Netherlands, Germany
Conducted byRoyal Air Force, US Eighth Air Force
TargetsV-1 and V-2 development, production, and launch sites

Operation Crossbow was the code name—later officially the "Counter Measures against the German Long-Range Weapon Programme"—for the Anglo-American campaign during the Second World War against Germany's V-weapons: the V-1 flying bomb and the V-2 rocket. It encompassed intelligence gathering, aerial reconnaissance, and bombing of research, production, storage, and launch facilities in occupied Europe.

Intelligence

British scientific intelligence, led by R. V. Jones, pieced together evidence of the rocket programme from agent reports, decrypted signals, the 1939 Oslo Report, and reconnaissance photographs of the Peenemünde Army Research Center on the Baltic coast. Analysts such as Constance Babington Smith identified the V-1 on its launch ramp in aerial photographs.

Bombing campaign

The campaign opened with Operation Hydra, the RAF Bomber Command raid on Peenemünde on the night of 17–18 August 1943, which killed hundreds—many of them forced labourers and prisoners—and delayed the V-2 programme. Thereafter Allied bombers attacked the large concrete "heavy" sites in northern France, including Watten, Wizernes, and Mimoyecques, as well as dozens of prefabricated V-1 "ski sites" (named for their ski-shaped storage buildings) and later modified launch sites.

Impact

The V-1 offensive against London began on 13 June 1944, a week after D-Day; the first V-2 fell in September 1944. Countermeasures—bombing, anti-aircraft guns, barrage balloons, and fighter interceptions—reduced the weapons' effectiveness, and the launch sites were progressively overrun by Allied ground forces. Historians debate the cost-effectiveness of the huge bombing effort diverted to Crossbow targets, but the campaign is generally credited with delaying the V-weapon offensive and reducing its scale.
