Ramblers

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ramblers (formally the Ramblers' Association) is a registered charity in Great Britain that promotes walking and works to protect the places people walk. Founded in 1935, it is Britain's largest walking organisation, with a membership of over 100,000 people and a network of hundreds of local groups. The Ramblers campaign for public access to land, maintain and defend the network of public rights of way, and organise thousands of group walks each year across England, Scotland and Wales.

History

The origins of the Ramblers lie in the nineteenth-century movement for public access to the countryside. In 1865 the Commons Preservation Society was founded, and in the 1920s and 1930s a series of federations of rambling clubs were formed across the industrial towns of northern England. The Kinder Scout mass trespass of 1932, in which hundreds of walkers walked onto privately owned moorland in the Peak District to protest the lack of access, became a defining event in the movement.

On 1 January 1935 the National Council of Ramblers' Federations was formed, marking the formal founding of the association, and in 1936 it adopted the name Ramblers' Association. The association played a central role in campaigning for the National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949, which created National Parks, National Trails, and the system of definitive maps of public rights of way in England and Wales.

Access campaigning

The Ramblers campaigned for a statutory right to roam, which was substantially achieved by the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000, giving the public a right of access on foot to mapped areas of mountain, moor, heath, down and registered common land in England and Wales. In Scotland, the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 established a more general statutory right of responsible access. The organisation also campaigns to protect and extend the England Coast Path and to prevent the loss of historic footpaths, most notably through its "Don't Lose Your Way" project, which seeks to identify and save paths omitted from the definitive map before the deadline for registering historic rights of way.

Activities

The Ramblers organises group walks led by volunteers, maintains a library of walking routes, and works with local authorities and volunteers to keep paths clear and waymarked. Local Ramblers groups check paths, report obstructions and carry out practical maintenance work such as installing stiles and footbridges.
