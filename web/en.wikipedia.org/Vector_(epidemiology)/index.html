Vector (epidemiology)

In epidemiology, a disease vector is any living agent that carries and transmits an infectious pathogen such that it causes disease in another living organism. Arthropods — especially mosquitoes, ticks, triatomine bugs, sandflies, fleas and lice — form a major group of disease vectors; snails and rodents also act as vectors or intermediate hosts for some pathogens.

Transmission

In mechanical transmission the vector merely carries the pathogen on its body or mouthparts without the pathogen developing in it. In biological transmission the pathogen develops or multiplies within the vector before being transmitted, as when Plasmodium parasites mature in an Anopheles mosquito before causing malaria, or Yersinia pestis in fleas transmitting plague.

Control

Vector control — insecticide-treated bed nets, indoor residual spraying, environmental management, sterile insect releases and protective clothing — is a principal strategy against vector-borne diseases, which the World Health Organization estimates account for more than 17% of all infectious diseases, including malaria, dengue, Zika, Lyme disease and Chagas disease.
