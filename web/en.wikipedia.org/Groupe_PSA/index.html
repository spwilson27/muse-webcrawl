Groupe PSA

IndustryAutomotive

Groupe PSA was a French multinational automotive manufacturer, parent of the Peugeot, Citroën, DS Automobiles, Opel and Vauxhall brands. At its peak it was the second-largest carmaker in Europe. In January 2021 it merged with Fiat Chrysler Automobiles to form Stellantis.

Formation and growth

The group originated in 1976, when Peugeot S.A. took over the financially troubled Citroën from Michelin, creating PSA Peugeot Citroën. PSA bought Chrysler's European operations (Simca, Rootes) in 1978, reviving Talbot, but overextended itself and posted heavy losses in the early 1980s before recovering on the success of the Peugeot 205 and Citroën BX.

Under Jean-Martin Folz and later CEOs the group expanded joint ventures, notably in China with Dongfeng and in Iran with Iran Khodro (suspended under sanctions). In 2012, near-bankrupt after the European debt crisis, PSA formed an alliance with General Motors; in 2014 Dongfeng and the French state each took 14% stakes in a recapitalisation.

Opel acquisition and turnaround

Under Carlos Tavares, appointed in 2014, the "Back in the Race" and "Push to Pass" plans restored profitability through cost discipline and pricing power. In 2017 PSA acquired Opel and Vauxhall from GM for about €2.2 billion, swiftly returning Opel to profit after two decades of losses — a turnaround widely studied in the industry.

Merger into Stellantis

In December 2019 PSA and Fiat Chrysler agreed a 50/50 merger, completed on 16 January 2021. The combined company, Stellantis, headquartered in the Netherlands, unites fourteen brands and became one of the world's largest automakers by volume, with Tavares as its first CEO.
