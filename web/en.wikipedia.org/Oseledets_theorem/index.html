Oseledets theorem

The Oseledets theorem, also called the multiplicative ergodic theorem, is a fundamental result in dynamical systems theory proved by Valery Oseledets in 1965. It establishes the existence of Lyapunov exponents for almost every trajectory of a measure-preserving dynamical system.

Statement

For a cocycle of matrices over an ergodic transformation satisfying an integrability condition, the theorem states that space splits into a flag of subspaces (the Oseledets filtration), on each of which vectors grow or shrink at a definite exponential rate given by the Lyapunov exponents.

Importance

The theorem underlies the rigorous theory of chaos: a positive Lyapunov exponent indicates sensitive dependence on initial conditions. It is applied in fluid dynamics, statistical mechanics, and the study of random matrix products.
