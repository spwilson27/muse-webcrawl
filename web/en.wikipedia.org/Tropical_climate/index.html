Tropical climate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A tropical climate is the first of the five major climate groups in the Köppen climate classification, designated by the letter A. It is defined as a climate in which the average temperature of every month of the year is 18 °C (64.4 °F) or higher, with hot temperatures and high humidity throughout the year. Tropical climates are found in the equatorial belt roughly between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn.

Subtypes

Köppen divides tropical climates by their rainfall pattern. Tropical rainforest (Af) climates receive heavy rain in all months and support evergreen rainforest. Tropical monsoon (Am) climates have a short dry season offset by very heavy wet-season rainfall. Tropical savanna (Aw and As) climates have a pronounced dry season, with the wettest month receiving far more rain than the driest, and support grassland with scattered trees.

Characteristics

Because the sun is nearly overhead year-round, seasonal temperature variation is small — often less than the difference between day and night. Rainfall is driven by the migration of the Intertropical Convergence Zone. Much of the world's biodiversity, and diseases such as malaria and dengue, are concentrated in the tropical zone, and many staple crops — bananas, coffee, cacao, and oil palm — depend on tropical conditions.
