Marin Marais

Marin Marais (31 May 1656 – 15 August 1728) was a French composer and viol player of the Baroque period. A pupil of Monsieur de Sainte-Colombe and of Jean-Baptiste Lully, he served as a musician of the royal chamber at the court of Louis XIV at Versailles and is regarded as one of the greatest masters of the bass viol.

Life

Marais was born in Paris, the son of a shoemaker. He sang as a choirboy at the Sainte-Chapelle, where he learned the viol from Sainte-Colombe. In 1676 he joined Lully's orchestra at the Paris Opéra and in 1679 was appointed ordinaire de la chambre du roi pour la viole, a post he held for decades. He married Catherine d'Amicourt in 1676 and had nineteen children, several of whom became musicians.

Works

Marais published five books of Pièces de viole (1686–1725) containing more than 550 pieces for viol and continuo, including character pieces with evocative titles; his Sonnerie de Sainte-Geneviève du Mont de Paris imitates church bells, and Le Tableau de l'Opération de la Taille famously depicts a bladder-stone operation. He also composed the operas Alcide (with Louis Lully), Ariane et Bacchus, Alcione (1706, whose storm music was famous) and Sémélé (1709), as well as trio sonatas and the Pièces en trio of 1692.

Legacy

His contemporary Titon du Tillet called him unrivalled on the viol. Interest in Marais revived with the early-music movement; the 1991 film Tous les matins du monde, with Gérard Depardieu as Marais and Jordi Savall performing the music, brought his viol works to a wide modern audience.
