Neurokinin B

Neurokinin B (NKB) is a ten-amino-acid neuropeptide of the tachykinin family, encoded by the TAC3 gene in humans. Like the related peptides substance P and neurokinin A, it acts on G protein-coupled tachykinin receptors, with a preference for the NK3 receptor. Neurokinin B is found in neurons of the central and peripheral nervous systems and is involved in smooth-muscle contraction, inflammation and the regulation of hormone secretion.

Role in reproduction

NKB is best known for its role in the control of reproduction. In the arcuate nucleus of the hypothalamus, NKB is co-expressed with kisspeptin and dynorphin in so-called KNDy neurons, which are central to the pulsatile release of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) and hence of LH and FSH. Loss-of-function mutations in TAC3 or its receptor gene TACR3 cause hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and failure of puberty, demonstrating that NKB signalling is essential for normal reproductive development.

Menopause and therapeutics

After menopause, hypertrophy of KNDy neurons and increased NKB signalling to thermoregulatory centres in the hypothalamus are implicated in hot flushes (vasomotor symptoms). This insight led to the development of NK3 receptor antagonists such as fezolinetant, approved in 2023 as a non-hormonal treatment for menopausal hot flushes. Elevated NKB has also been reported in pre-eclampsia, where it may contribute to hypertension.
