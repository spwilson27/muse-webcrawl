Battle of the Netherlands

The Battle of the Netherlands (Dutch: Slag om Nederland) was part of Operation Fall Gelb, the German invasion of the Low Countries and France during the Second World War. Fought from 10 to 14 May 1940, it saw German forces overrun the Netherlands in five days. The campaign ended after the devastating bombing of Rotterdam on 14 May and the Dutch capitulation the following day, although Dutch forces in Zeeland fought on briefly alongside the French. Queen Wilhelmina and the Dutch government escaped to London, and the Netherlands remained under German occupation until 1945.

Background

The Netherlands had remained neutral in the First World War and hoped to do so again, mobilising its army in 1939. Dutch strategy relied on the inundations of the Dutch Waterline and the "Fortress Holland" redoubt behind the Grebbe Line. Germany, however, needed Dutch airfields and territory for the campaign against France, and planned a rapid seizure using airborne troops against The Hague and the great river bridges.

The invasion

10 May

In the early hours of 10 May, German paratroopers and air-landing troops descended on The Hague, Rotterdam and the Moerdijk, Dordrecht and Waal bridges, securing crossings into Fortress Holland. The attempt to capture the Dutch government at The Hague failed with heavy losses, but the river bridges were taken intact. Along the eastern frontier, German ground forces of Army Group B pushed the Dutch back from the IJssel and Maas lines.

The Grebbeberg and Rotterdam

Dutch resistance was fiercest at the Grebbeberg, where Dutch troops held for three days, and at the Afsluitdijk, where the Kornwerderzand position repelled German attacks. On 14 May, with negotiations under way, German bombers devastated the centre of Rotterdam, killing around 800–900 civilians. Threatened with a similar attack on Utrecht, Commander-in-Chief General Henri Winkelman signed the capitulation on 15 May.

Aftermath

The Netherlands was placed under a German civilian administration (Reichskommissariat Niederlande) headed by Arthur Seyss-Inquart. The Dutch government-in-exile and merchant navy joined the Allied cause. Remembrance of the dead is held nationally each year on 4 May, and liberation celebrated on 5 May.
