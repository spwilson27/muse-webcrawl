Cologne Cathedral

Cologne Cathedral (German: Kölner Dom) is a Catholic cathedral in Cologne, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It is the seat of the Archbishop of Cologne and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Construction began in 1248 to house the Shrine of the Three Kings, believed to hold the relics of the Biblical Magi. Work halted in the 16th century with the south tower unfinished; following the medieval plans, the cathedral was completed in 1880, when its 157-metre twin spires made it the world's tallest building. It survived Allied bombing in the Second World War despite fourteen bomb hits.

Architecture

The cathedral is Germany's largest Gothic church, with one of the largest facades ever built and a choir modelled on Amiens Cathedral.
