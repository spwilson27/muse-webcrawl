Akamai Technologies, Inc. is an American technology company headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts, best known for pioneering the content delivery network (CDN). Its globally distributed platform of servers delivers a substantial share of the world's web traffic from locations close to end users, improving speed and reliability and absorbing traffic surges and denial-of-service attacks.

History

Akamai (Hawaiian for "smart" or "cool") was founded in 1998 by MIT professor Tom Leighton and graduate student Daniel Lewin, applying algorithms for distributing content across networks of servers; the work grew out of an MIT challenge to solve web congestion ("slashdotting"). The company went public in 1999. Lewin was killed aboard American Airlines Flight 11 in the September 11 attacks; the Danny Lewin Student Center at MIT commemorates him. Akamai's network grew to hundreds of thousands of servers in over 130 countries.

Business

Beyond media and software delivery, Akamai expanded into cloud security (web application firewalls, bot management, and DDoS mitigation through its 2014 acquisition of Prolexic), and into cloud computing, acquiring Linode in 2022 for about $900 million to build a distributed core-and-edge cloud platform. Customers include many of the world's largest media, retail, financial, and government organizations.
