Balinese (Balinese: Basa Bali) is a Malayo-Polynesian language of the Austronesian language family, spoken by about 3.3 million people, mainly on the Indonesian island of Bali and by Balinese communities on Lombok, in East Java and elsewhere. It is closely related to Sasak and Sumbawa, and more distantly to Javanese and Malay/Indonesian — the national language, with which it coexists in a diglossic situation.

Speech levels

A well-known feature of Balinese is its system of speech levels tied to the traditional caste and politeness hierarchy, comparable to that of Javanese. Speakers choose among registers such as low/common speech (basa ketah), middle speech (basa madia) and refined high speech (basa singgih), with distinct vocabularies for everyday words depending on the relative status of speaker and listener.

Writing

Balinese was traditionally written in the Balinese script, an abugida derived from the Old Kawi script of Java, used for religious manuscripts written on lontar palm leaves, including Hindu liturgical texts. Today the Latin alphabet is used for most everyday writing, and there are ongoing efforts to preserve the traditional script, which is taught in Balinese schools and supported in Unicode.

Status

Although Balinese has several million speakers and strong cultural prestige through Bali's Hindu traditions and literature, it faces pressure from Indonesian in education, media and urban life, and the high registers are declining among younger speakers. Provincial regulations in Bali promote the language's use, including Balinese script on public signage.
