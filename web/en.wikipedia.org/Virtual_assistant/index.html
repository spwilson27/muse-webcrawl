Virtual assistant

A virtual assistant responding to a voice query

Type: Software agent

First released: 2011 (Siri)

Examples: Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant

A virtual assistant (also called an intelligent personal assistant) is a software agent that can perform tasks or services for an individual based on commands or questions. Virtual assistants use natural language processing (NLP) to interpret voice commands and text input, and can answer questions, make recommendations, set reminders, control smart home devices, and perform actions such as playing music or managing calendars.

The term is also used for remote workers who provide administrative, technical, or creative assistance to clients from a home office, though in technology contexts it usually refers to the software systems popularized by smartphones and smart speakers.

History

Early precursors included voice recognition software of the 1990s and projects such as IBM's ViaVoice. The modern era began in 2011 with Apple's Siri, introduced with the iPhone 4S. Amazon followed with Alexa in 2014 alongside the Echo smart speaker, and Google launched Google Assistant in 2016. Microsoft's Cortana and Samsung's Bixby also competed in the space.

Interaction

Users interact with virtual assistants primarily through voice, typically after a "wake word" such as "Hey Siri" or "Alexa". Assistants increasingly support typed input, on-screen suggestions, and multi-turn conversations. Modern systems are built on large language models and speech recognition, allowing more flexible dialogue and task automation.

Privacy concerns

Because assistants listen for wake words and send recordings to cloud servers, they have raised concerns about eavesdropping, data retention, and human review of voice recordings. Several providers were criticized in 2019 after reports that contractors listened to user recordings.
