Kurma

Kurma (Sanskrit: कूर्म, "tortoise") is the second avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu, in the Dashavatara list. In this form Vishnu took the shape of a giant tortoise to support Mount Mandara on his back during the churning of the ocean of milk (Samudra Manthana).

Mythology

In the legend, the devas (gods) and asuras (demons) churned the ocean of milk using the serpent Vasuki as a rope and Mount Mandara as the churning rod, seeking the nectar of immortality (amrita). The mountain began to sink, so Vishnu incarnated as a great tortoise and bore its weight on his shell, allowing the churning to continue and the treasures of the ocean to emerge.

Worship

Kurma is venerated in Vaishnavism, and temples dedicated to this avatar include the Sri Kurmanatha temple at Srikurmam in Andhra Pradesh, one of the few temples dedicated to Kurma.
