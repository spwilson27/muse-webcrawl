Transformation matrix

In linear algebra, a linear map can be represented by a matrix. A transformation matrix is the matrix that, when multiplied by a vector, applies the corresponding geometric transformation — such as rotation, scaling, reflection, shearing or translation (in homogeneous coordinates) — to that vector.

Examples

A two-dimensional rotation through angle θ is given by the matrix with entries cos θ, −sin θ, sin θ and cos θ. Scaling uses a diagonal matrix; reflections negate selected coordinates; shears add a multiple of one coordinate to another. In computer graphics, 4×4 matrices in homogeneous coordinates combine rotation, scaling, translation and perspective projection for three-dimensional rendering.

Composition

Successive transformations correspond to matrix multiplication, and applying several transformations at once amounts to multiplying their matrices together — the key fact behind transformation pipelines in graphics and robotics.
