Stanley Ho

Stanley Ho Hung-sun (Chinese: 何鴻燊; 25 November 1921 – 26 May 2020) was a Hong Kong–Macau billionaire businessman. His original name is Ho Hung-sun in Cantonese. He was the founder and chairman of SJM Holdings, which owns nineteen casinos in Macau including the Grand Lisboa. Ho was known as the "King of Gambling", reflecting the government-granted monopoly he held in the Macau gambling industry for 40 years.

Early life and career

Ho was born in Hong Kong into the influential Hotung family of Eurasian descent, but his family lost its fortune in the stock market crash of the 1930s. He studied at the University of Hong Kong before fleeing to Macau during the Second World War, where he made his first fortune in trading and smuggling luxury goods. In 1961, Ho and partners including Henry Fok and Yip Hon won the Macau gaming monopoly concession, founding the Sociedade de Turismo e Diversões de Macau (STDM).

Business empire

STDM transformed Macau with ferry services from Hong Kong, hotels and casinos such as the Casino Lisboa and the pyramid-crowned Grand Lisboa. Ho's wider interests through Shun Tak Holdings spanned shipping, real estate, hospitality and air transport. When Macau liberalized gaming in 2002, Ho's SJM retained the largest market share among six concessionaires for years. His wealth made him one of Asia's richest men, and members of his large family remain central to the region's casino industry.

Later life

Ho retired from his business roles in 2018. He died in Hong Kong on 26 May 2020, aged 98. He was accorded recognition in both Hong Kong and Macau for his contributions to tourism and charity, though his legacy remains debated because of the social costs of gambling and alleged triad links in the industry—allegations he denied.
