Glam metal

Glam metal (also known pejoratively as hair metal, and sometimes as pop metal) is a subgenre of heavy metal that features pop-influenced hooks and guitar riffs, upbeat rock anthems, and slow power ballads. It borrows heavily from the fashion and image of 1970s glam rock — teased hair, makeup, tight and colourful clothing — combined with hard rock musicianship.

History

The style emerged in the late 1970s and early 1980s on Los Angeles's Sunset Strip, with bands such as Mötley Crüe, Quiet Riot, Ratt, and Twisted Sister. Van Halen's virtuoso guitar work and party image were a major influence, while British acts such as Def Leppard helped carry the sound to a mass audience. In the mid-to-late 1980s glam metal dominated rock radio and MTV: Bon Jovi's Slippery When Wet (1986), Guns N' Roses' Appetite for Destruction (1987), and Poison's Open Up and Say... Ahh! (1988) were among the era's biggest albums, and power ballads by bands such as Warrant, Skid Row, and Whitesnake became crossover hits.

Decline and revival

The rise of grunge and alternative rock in the early 1990s abruptly displaced glam metal from mainstream popularity, and many bands lost their record deals or disbanded. From the 2000s onward, nostalgia tours, reunion albums, and parody acts such as Steel Panther sustained a revival, and veteran bands continued to draw large live audiences.

Characteristics

Musically, glam metal combines virtuosic lead guitar (especially shredding solos), anthemic choruses designed for audience participation, and polished production. Its visual presentation — big hair, spandex, leather, and stage spectacle — is as defining as the music itself, which is why critics who disliked its commercialism coined the dismissive label "hair metal".
