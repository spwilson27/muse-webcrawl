Eurostar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eurostar is an international high-speed rail service connecting the United Kingdom with France, Belgium, and the Netherlands through the Channel Tunnel. Services began on 14 November 1994 between London, Paris, and Brussels. Since 2007 the London terminus has been St Pancras International, reached over the High Speed 1 line.

Services

Eurostar links London St Pancras with Paris Gare du Nord, Brussels Midi/Zuid, and — since 2018 and 2020 respectively — Amsterdam Centraal and Rotterdam Centraal, with intermediate stops including Lille-Europe, Ebbsfleet, and Ashford (the latter two suspended since 2020). The fastest London–Paris journey time is about 2 hours 15 minutes, with trains running at up to 300 km/h (186 mph) on high-speed lines and 160 km/h through the Channel Tunnel.

Rolling stock

The original fleet was the Class 373 (TMST), 400-metre trains built in the 1990s; they were joined from 2015–2016 by the Class 374 "e320" trains built by Siemens. In 2023 Eurostar merged with the Franco-Belgian high-speed operator Thalys, whose services were rebranded as Eurostar in October 2023, creating a single brand covering the former Thalys network to Germany and across France and Belgium.

Operations

Because the United Kingdom is outside the Schengen Area, passengers undergo passport and security checks before boarding at dedicated terminal facilities, an arrangement governed by the juxtaposed-controls treaties between the UK, France, and Belgium. Eurostar carried about 11 million passengers in 2019 and recovered to roughly 18.6 million (including former Thalys services) in 2023.
