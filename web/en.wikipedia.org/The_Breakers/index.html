The Breakers

The Breakers is a Gilded Age mansion located at 44 Ochre Point Avenue in Newport, Rhode Island, United States. It was built between 1893 and 1895 as the summer home of Cornelius Vanderbilt II, a member of the wealthy Vanderbilt family. Designed by architect Richard Morris Hunt in the Italian Renaissance Revival style, the 70-room mansion is the largest of the Newport summer "cottages" and a symbol of the Vanderbilt family's social and financial pre-eminence.

Architecture

The five-storey limestone mansion contains about 62,482 square feet (5,805 m²) of living space on five floors, set in grounds of roughly 13 acres overlooking the Atlantic Ocean, from whose waves the house takes its name. Hunt modelled the villa on the palaces of Genoa and Turin. The interiors, executed by Hunt's team with the Paris firm of Jules Allard and Sons, include a two-storey Great Hall, rooms panelled in rare marbles and woods, and a loggia opening onto the gardens. The house was built to be fireproof, with a steel frame and no wooden structural parts; its boiler was buried underground away from the main building.

History

A previous house on the site, owned by tobacco merchant Pierre Lorillard IV, burned down in 1892 while Cornelius Vanderbilt II was renting it; Vanderbilt bought the estate and commissioned Hunt to build a grander replacement. The family used the house for only a few seasons after Cornelius's death in 1899. His youngest daughter, Countess Gladys Széchenyi, inherited it and leased it to the Preservation Society of Newport County in 1948; the Society bought the mansion and most of its furnishings in 1972 for $365,000.

Public access

The Breakers is now operated as a museum by the Preservation Society of Newport County and is the most visited attraction in Rhode Island, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors annually. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1994.
