Usumacinta River

Usumacinta River
Length: ~1,000 km (with tributaries)
Countries: Guatemala, Mexico
Mouth: Gulf of Mexico (via Grijalva delta)

The Usumacinta River is the largest river of Mesoamerica by discharge, flowing through Guatemala and the Mexican states of Chiapas and Tabasco before joining the Grijalva system and emptying into the Gulf of Mexico.

Course

It begins in the highlands of Guatemala where the Chixoy and Pasión rivers converge, then forms part of the Guatemala–Mexico border, cutting spectacular canyons through the rainforest of the Lacandon Jungle region.

Archaeology

The river was a major trade artery of the Classic Maya civilization; important ruins on its banks include Yaxchilán and Piedras Negras, whose carved lintels and stelae record dynastic wars fought along the river.
