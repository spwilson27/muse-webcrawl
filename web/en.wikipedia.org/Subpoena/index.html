Subpoena

A subpoena (also subpœna; from Latin sub poena, "under penalty") is a written order issued by a government agency, most often a court, compelling a witness to testify, or compelling a person or organisation to produce evidence such as documents or records. Failure to comply may be punished as contempt of court.

Types

A subpoena ad testificandum orders a person to appear and give testimony. A subpoena duces tecum ("bring with you under penalty") orders the recipient to produce documents, electronically stored information, or tangible things for inspection or use as evidence. Subpoenas are used in civil and criminal proceedings, by grand juries, and by legislatures and administrative agencies with subpoena power.

Procedure

Subpoenas may be issued by judges, clerks of court, or in many jurisdictions by attorneys as officers of the court. They must be properly served on the recipient and generally allow a reasonable time for compliance. A recipient who believes a subpoena is improper may move to quash or modify it — for example, on grounds of privilege, undue burden, or lack of jurisdiction. Witnesses are usually entitled to fees and mileage for attendance.

History

The writ originated in English common law under King John and the courts of equity, and the name derives from its opening Latin words threatening a penalty for disobedience.
