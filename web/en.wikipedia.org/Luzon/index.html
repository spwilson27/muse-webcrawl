Luzon (Tagalog: Luson) is the largest and most populous island in the Philippines, and the name of one of the country's three major island groups (with the Visayas and Mindanao). Located in the northern part of the archipelago, it covers 109,965 km² — making it the 15th-largest island in the world — and is home to more than half of the Philippine population, including the capital city, Manila, and the country's largest city, Quezon City. Luzon accounts for the majority of the nation's industry, agriculture and government.

Geography

Luzon is irregularly shaped, with a long north–south spine and the Bicol Peninsula extending southeast. Its terrain is dominated by mountain systems: the Sierra Madre, the Philippines' longest range, along the east coast; the Cordillera Central in the north, containing Mount Pulag (2,928 m), the island's highest peak; and the Zambales Mountains in the west. Between them lies the Central Luzon plain, the country's "rice granary", drained by the Pampanga and Agno rivers; the Cagayan River in the northeast is the Philippines' longest. Luzon contains the country's largest lake, Laguna de Bay, and the remarkable Taal Lake, a lake within a volcanic island within a lake. The island lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire: Mount Pinatubo's 1991 eruption was one of the 20th century's largest, and Mayon Volcano in Bicol is famed for its near-perfect cone. Typhoons strike the island regularly, especially the eastern seaboard.

History

Luzon has been inhabited for tens of thousands of years; the Callao Man fossils from Cagayan date to about 67,000 years ago, among the oldest human remains in Southeast Asia. Pre-colonial Luzon hosted trading polities such as Tondo and Namayan, linked by commerce to China, Japan and the wider region; Chinese and Arab traders were long established at Manila Bay. Spain colonised the island from 1571, when Miguel López de Legazpi founded Manila, and ruled for over 300 years. The Philippine Revolution began in Luzon in 1896 under the Katipunan; Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed independence at Kawit, Cavite, in 1898 and established the First Philippine Republic at Malolos, Bulacan. After the Spanish–American War the United States governed the island, developed Baguio as a hill station, and fought the Philippine–American War largely on Luzon.

In the Second World War, Japanese forces invaded at Lingayen Gulf in December 1941; the island saw the Bataan Death March, the liberation landings of January 1945, and the Battle of Manila, one of the war's most destructive urban battles. Post-war Luzon remained the centre of political upheavals including the People Power Revolution of 1986 on Manila's EDSA avenue.

Economy and demographics

Metro Manila is the Philippines' political and economic heart, concentrating finance, manufacturing, ports and services. Central Luzon and Cagayan Valley produce much of the nation's rice and corn; the island leads in industry, with economic zones such as Clark and Subic (former US bases). The population is ethnically diverse: Tagalogs in the south and centre, Ilocanos in the northwest, Kapampangans and Pangasinenses in the central plains, Bikolanos in the southeast, and Igorot peoples in the Cordillera highlands. Languages include Tagalog (the basis of Filipino), Ilocano, Kapampangan, Pangasinan and Bikol, alongside English.
