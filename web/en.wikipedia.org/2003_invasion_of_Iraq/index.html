The 2003 invasion of Iraq was the first stage of the Iraq War. The invasion phase began on 20 March 2003 and lasted just over one month, during which a combined force of troops from the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia and Poland invaded Iraq and toppled the government of Saddam Hussein. US President George W. Bush and UK Prime Minister Tony Blair argued that Iraq possessed weapons of mass destruction that posed a threat; no stockpiles were subsequently found.

Course of the invasion

The invasion began with an air campaign and a "decapitation strike" on a Baghdad compound where Saddam Hussein was believed to be. Ground forces advanced rapidly from Kuwait: US Marines and Army divisions reached Baghdad within three weeks, and the capital fell on 9 April, symbolised by the toppling of Saddam's statue in Firdos Square. On 1 May, President Bush declared the end of major combat operations in his "Mission Accomplished" speech.

Aftermath

The Coalition Provisional Authority governed Iraq while an insurgency developed against coalition forces and the new Iraqi government. Saddam Hussein was captured in December 2003, tried by an Iraqi tribunal and executed in 2006. The war's justification became the subject of major inquiries, including the Iraq Survey Group, which concluded Iraq had no active WMD production programmes, and the UK's Chilcot Inquiry of 2016.
