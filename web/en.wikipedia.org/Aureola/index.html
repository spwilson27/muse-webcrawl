Aureola

An aureola or aureole is a radiant cloud or halo of light depicted surrounding the whole body of a sacred figure in religious art. The word comes from the Latin aureolus, "golden", the diminutive of aureus. Where a halo encircles only the head, the aureola envelops the entire figure, often in an almond-shaped form known as a mandorla (from the Italian for almond).

In art

Aureolae appear in early Christian, Byzantine and medieval art around Christ in Majesty, the Virgin Mary (especially in scenes of the Assumption), and occasionally angels and saints. They are also found in Buddhist and Hindu iconography, where radiant body halos signify enlightenment or divine energy, and in depictions of deities and emperors in earlier traditions. Gold leaf was frequently used to render the effect in panel painting and mosaic, so that the light appeared to radiate from the figure itself.
