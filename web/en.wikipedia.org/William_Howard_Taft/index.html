William Howard Taft

William Howard Taft (September 15, 1857 – March 8, 1930) was the 27th president of the United States, serving from 1909 to 1913, and the tenth chief justice of the United States, serving from 1921 to 1930, the only person to have held both offices.

Early career

Born in Cincinnati, Ohio, Taft graduated from Yale and Cincinnati Law School. He served as a judge, solicitor general of the United States, and, after the Spanish–American War, as the first civilian governor-general of the Philippines, then as Theodore Roosevelt's secretary of war.

Presidency

Chosen by Roosevelt as his successor, Taft won the 1908 election. His administration pursued antitrust prosecutions (bringing more cases than Roosevelt), tariff revision through the Payne–Aldrich Tariff, and "dollar diplomacy" abroad. But disputes over conservation and tariffs split the Republican Party; in 1912 Roosevelt ran against him on the Progressive ticket, and both lost to Woodrow Wilson.

Chief Justice

Taft achieved his lifelong ambition in 1921 when President Harding appointed him chief justice. He led the Supreme Court for nine years, working to improve its administration and securing the construction of the Supreme Court Building. He resigned in February 1930 due to ill health and died a month later.
