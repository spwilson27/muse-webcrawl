Apple A7

Designed by: Apple Inc.
Launched: September 2013
Process: 28 nm (Samsung)
Cores: 2 (Cyclone)

The Apple A7 is a 64-bit ARM-based system on a chip (SoC) designed by Apple Inc. and manufactured by Samsung on a 28 nm process. It debuted in the iPhone 5S in September 2013 and was later used in the iPad Air and iPad mini 2.

Significance

The A7 was the first 64-bit processor to ship in a consumer smartphone, implementing the ARMv8-A architecture with Apple's dual-core "Cyclone" CPU. Its announcement surprised the chip industry and pushed competitors toward 64-bit designs. Apple claimed roughly twice the CPU and graphics performance of the preceding A6.

Features

The chip pairs with the M7 motion coprocessor, which gathers data from the accelerometer, gyroscope and compass to reduce power consumption. The A7 contains just over one billion transistors on a die of about 102 mm², and its graphics are provided by a PowerVR G6430 GPU.
