Baggy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Baggy was a British alternative dance genre that emerged in the late 1980s and early 1990s, centred on Manchester and the surrounding North West of England. It blended indie rock with the rhythms of acid house, funk, and psychedelia, and is closely associated with the wider Madchester scene. The name came from the loose, flared jeans and baggy tops worn by the bands' followers.

Characteristics

Baggy music typically combined jangling or wah-wah guitars with dance-floor drum loops, heavy basslines and a laid-back, euphoric vocal delivery. Key records included the Happy Mondays' Pills 'n' Thrills and Bellyaches (1990), the Stone Roses' debut album (1989), Inspiral Carpets, the Charlatans, Northside, and the Farm's "All Together Now". Producers such as Paul Oakenfold, who worked with the Happy Mondays, helped fuse the rock and rave worlds.

Scene and legacy

The genre flourished around clubs like the Haçienda and venues across Manchester, amid the tail of the acid house explosion. By 1992 the scene had fragmented — the Stone Roses became embroiled in legal disputes, the Happy Mondays collapsed financially, and guitar music moved first toward grunge and then Britpop, which inherited baggy's laddish swagger and dance-groove rhythm sections. Baggy fashion — bucket hats, flared jeans and oversized tops — was revived in later decades along with reunion tours by its principal bands.
