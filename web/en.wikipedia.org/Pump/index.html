Pump

A pump is a device that moves fluids or slurries by mechanical action, converting electrical or other energy into hydraulic energy.

A pump is a device that moves fluids (liquids or gases), or sometimes slurries, by mechanical action, typically converted from electrical energy into hydraulic energy. Pumps are used for purposes as varied as lifting water from wells, circulating coolant in engines and heating systems, transferring petroleum, dosing chemicals, and inflating tyres.

Types

Pumps fall into two broad classes. Positive displacement pumps move a fixed volume with each cycle, using reciprocating pistons, diaphragms, gears, vanes, screws, or peristaltic action; they can generate high pressures and handle viscous fluids. Dynamic pumps, principally centrifugal pumps, impart velocity to the fluid with a rotating impeller and convert it to pressure; they dominate water supply and process industries because they provide smooth, high-rate flow. Other specialised types include jet (ejector) pumps, airlift pumps, and electromagnetic pumps for liquid metals.

Characteristics

Pump performance is described by flow rate, head (the height to which fluid can be raised), pressure, power, and efficiency, and is plotted on characteristic curves. Practical operation must account for net positive suction head (NPSH) to avoid cavitation — the formation and collapse of vapour bubbles that erodes impellers — as well as priming, sealing, and materials compatibility.

History

Early devices included the shadoof and the Archimedes screw of antiquity and the force pump described by Ctesibius of Alexandria. Steam-driven pumps powered the Industrial Revolution's mines and waterworks, and modern designs span microscopic lab-on-a-chip devices to the enormous pumps of pipelines and power stations.
