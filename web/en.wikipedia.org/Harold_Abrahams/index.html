Harold Maurice Abrahams CBE (15 December 1899 – 14 January 1978) was an English track and field athlete who won the gold medal in the 100 metres at the 1924 Paris Olympic Games. His quest for the title, alongside that of Eric Liddell, was dramatised in the Academy Award-winning film Chariots of Fire (1981), in which he was played by Ben Cross.

Life and career

Born in Bedford to a Lithuanian Jewish immigrant father, Abrahams was educated at Repton School and Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, where he was a leading sprinter and long jumper and competed at the 1920 Antwerp Olympics. Determined to win in Paris, he employed professional coach Sam Mussabini — a controversial step in the amateur ethos of the time — and refined his start, stride and finish. On 7 July 1924 he won the Olympic 100 metres final in 10.6 seconds, beating the favoured Americans, and also earned a silver medal in the 4×100 metres relay. An injury ended his competitive career in 1925.

Later life

Abrahams became a barrister, athletics administrator and one of Britain's most influential athletics journalists and historians. He served as president of the Amateur Athletic Association, chaired the British Amateur Athletic Board, reported on numerous Olympic Games for the BBC and Sunday Times, and co-authored Olympic histories. He was appointed CBE in 1957. He remained a central figure in British athletics until his death in 1978.
