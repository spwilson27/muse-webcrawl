Standard German is pluricentric, with national standard varieties in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland.

Standard German (German: Standarddeutsch, or Hochdeutsch, "High German") is the standardised variety of the German language used in formal contexts and for communication across regions and dialect areas. German is a pluricentric language: there are three fully standardised national varieties — German Standard German, Austrian Standard German, and Swiss Standard German — which differ modestly in vocabulary, pronunciation, and some conventions (for example, Swiss Standard German does not use the letter ß, writing ss instead).

History

Standard German developed from the written chancery languages of the late Middle Ages, harmonising East Central German and Upper German usage. Martin Luther's Bible translation (New Testament 1522; full Bible 1534) was immensely influential, as were the grammars of the Enlightenment and the stage pronunciation codified by Theodor Siebs in 1898. Konrad Duden's orthographic dictionary of 1880 led to a unified spelling adopted across the German Empire in 1901–02, replaced by reforms culminating in the 1996 spelling reform and the creation of the Council for German Orthography in 2004.

Relationship to dialects

"High German" in the standard's name refers to the highland (southern) dialects' participation in the High German consonant shift, not to prestige — though the term is widely understood in the latter sense colloquially. Spoken everyday German in most regions is a continuum between dialect and the standard ("regional standards"), while in Switzerland diglossia prevails: Swiss German dialects are spoken and Swiss Standard German is reserved mainly for writing and formal speech.

Features

Standard German retains three grammatical genders, four cases, strong and weak adjective declension, and verb-second word order in main clauses with verb-final order in subordinate clauses. Nouns are capitalised — a distinctive orthographic feature. Pronunciation standards are described in reference works such as the Duden pronouncing dictionary and the Deutsches Aussprachewörterbuch.
