National Scenic Routes in Norway

The National Scenic Routes (Norwegian: Nasjonale turistveger) are 18 selected drives through Norway's most striking landscapes, developed by the Norwegian Public Roads Administration. Together they cover about 2,150 kilometres of road on the coast, fjords and mountains.

The project

Begun as a pilot in 1994 and expanded in 2004, the programme combines road improvements with bold contemporary architecture: specially designed viewpoints, rest areas, bridges and toilets by architects such as Snøhetta, Reiulf Ramstad and Jensen & Skodvin, plus commissioned artworks. The aim is to enhance the experience of the landscape and promote tourism in rural districts.

Notable routes

Well-known sections include the Atlantic Road (Atlanterhavsvegen), the Trollstigen mountain road in the Geiranger–Trollstigen route, the Stegastein viewpoint above Aurlandsfjord on the Aurlandsfjellet route, the Sognefjellet — Northern Europe's highest mountain pass — and routes in Varanger, Senja, Andøya and the Lofoten Islands.
