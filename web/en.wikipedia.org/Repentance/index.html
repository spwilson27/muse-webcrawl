Repentance

Repentance is the activity of reviewing one's actions and feeling contrition or regret for past wrongs, accompanied by a commitment to change. It is a central concept in many religions, where it typically involves recognition of sin, remorse, confession, and a turning away from wrongdoing toward a renewed life.

In Judaism

The Hebrew concept of teshuvah (תשובה, "return") is elaborated especially around Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Maimonides codified its steps: recognition of the sin, remorse, confession, and resolution not to repeat the act — with complete repentance demonstrated when, given the same opportunity, one refrains. Wrongs against other people require restitution and seeking the injured party's forgiveness.

In Christianity

The New Testament term metanoia (Greek: μετάνοια) means a change of mind and heart. Repentance is paired with faith in the preaching of Jesus and John the Baptist ("Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand"). In Catholic teaching, repentance is expressed through the sacrament of Penance (confession, absolution, and satisfaction). Protestant traditions emphasise repentance as a lifelong posture of turning from sin, itself a gift of grace.

In Islam

Tawba is the Islamic concept of repenting to God for sins. The Qur'an describes God as accepting the repentance of those who believe, cease the wrongdoing, feel remorse, and resolve not to return to it; where the rights of others are involved, restitution is required. Repentance is open until death or the end of the world approaches.

Other contexts

Analogous practices exist in Buddhism (acknowledgement and confession of harmful actions as part of ethical purification) and in secular restorative-justice and addiction-recovery programmes, such as the amends steps of twelve-step fellowships.
