Shawnee County, Kansas


County seat: Topeka
Founded: 25 August 1855
Named for: Shawnee people
Area: ~556 sq mi
Population (2020): ~178,831

Shawnee County is a county in the U.S. state of Kansas. Its county seat and most populous city is Topeka, which is also the state capital. As of the 2020 census, the county population was about 178,831. The county was named for the Shawnee tribe of Native Americans, who were relocated to the region in the 19th century.

History

Shawnee County was one of the original 33 counties created by the first territorial legislature in 1855. The Kansas River flows through the county, and the area was traversed by the Oregon Trail. Topeka was chosen as the state capital when Kansas entered the Union in 1861. The landmark school desegregation case Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka (1954) originated in the city.

Geography and communities

The county covers about 556 square miles in northeastern Kansas. Besides Topeka, communities include Silver Lake, Rossville, Auburn, and part of Willard. The county is the core of the Topeka metropolitan statistical area.
