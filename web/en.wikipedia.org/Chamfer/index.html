Chamfer

Chamfer
TypeTransitional edge
Typical angle45°
FieldsMachining, woodworking, architecture

A chamfer is a transitional edge between two faces of an object, typically a flat surface cut at a 45-degree angle that removes a sharp corner. Chamfers are used in machining, woodworking, and design to ease assembly, remove burrs, reduce stress concentration, and improve safety and appearance.

Uses

In machining, chamfers are cut on the ends of shafts, holes, and threaded fasteners to guide mating parts together and to protect edges from damage. In printed circuit boards and glass, chamfered edges prevent chipping. In architecture, chamfered corners appear on columns, beams, and masonry.

Chamfer versus bevel and fillet

A bevel is any angled edge, whereas a chamfer conventionally spans only part of the edge and is often at 45 degrees. A fillet, by contrast, rounds the corner rather than cutting it flat. In engineering drawings, chamfers are commonly specified by leg length and angle, e.g. 2 mm × 45°.
