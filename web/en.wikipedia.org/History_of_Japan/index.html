History of Japan

The history of Japan extends from the prehistoric Jōmon period to the modern era. Written records begin with Chinese chronicles of the 1st century CE describing the islands' many small polities; a unified imperial state gradually emerged in the Yamato period, adopting Chinese writing, Buddhism, and bureaucratic models.

Classical and feudal Japan

The Nara (710–794) and Heian (794–1185) periods saw a court-centred culture at Kyoto that produced classics such as The Tale of Genji. From 1185 power shifted to military governments (bakufu) headed by shoguns, first at Kamakura and later under the Ashikaga and Tokugawa. Samurai warriors repelled Mongol invasions in 1274 and 1281, endured the civil wars of the Sengoku period, and were finally unified by Oda Nobunaga, Toyotomi Hideyoshi, and Tokugawa Ieyasu. The Tokugawa shogunate (1603–1868) governed from Edo (Tokyo) in relative peace, restricting foreign contact under the sakoku policy.

Modern Japan

The arrival of Commodore Perry's American squadron in 1853 forced Japan open; the Meiji Restoration of 1868 launched rapid industrialisation and modernisation. Imperial Japan won wars against China (1895) and Russia (1905), annexed Korea, and expanded across Asia and the Pacific before its defeat in the Second World War in 1945. Under Allied occupation Japan adopted a pacifist constitution, then achieved decades of rapid economic growth — the "Japanese miracle" — becoming one of the world's largest economies.
