Wedderburn's little theorem

Wedderburn's little theorem is a result in abstract algebra stating that every finite division ring is a field. In other words, for a finite ring, the properties of being a division ring and of being a field coincide. The theorem is named after Joseph Wedderburn, who first proved it in 1905.

Statement

Let R be a finite division ring. Then R is commutative, and therefore a field. A division ring is a ring in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse; the theorem says finiteness forces the multiplication to be commutative.

History

Wedderburn published the first proof in 1905, although Leonard Eugene Dickson had an independent proof around the same time. Wedderburn's original proof contained a gap that was later repaired. Several more elementary proofs have since appeared, including one by Ernst Witt in 1931 using cyclotomic polynomials and one by Hans Zassenhaus. A well-known proof uses the class equation applied to the multiplicative group of the ring together with estimates on cyclotomic polynomial values.

Proof sketch

Let the centre of the division ring D be a finite field F with q elements, and suppose D has dimension n over F. The multiplicative group of D is analysed via its conjugacy classes; the class equation forces n = 1 unless a contradiction arises, showing D = F and hence that D is commutative.
