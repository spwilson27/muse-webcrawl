Laban (Bible)

Laban (Hebrew: לָבָן, meaning "white") is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, described in the Book of Genesis as the brother of Rebekah and the father of Leah and Rachel. He was the son of Bethuel and grandson of Nahor, Abraham's brother, and lived in Paddan Aram (Aram-Naharaim) in Mesopotamia.

Laban welcomed his nephew Jacob, who fled from his brother Esau, and agreed that Jacob would work seven years for the hand of Rachel. At the wedding Laban substituted his elder daughter Leah, arguing that it was not customary to marry off the younger before the elder; Jacob then worked another seven years for Rachel. Jacob's flocks prospered despite Laban repeatedly changing his wages, and after twenty years Jacob departed with his wives, children and livestock. Laban pursued him but, warned in a dream, made a covenant with Jacob at Gilead, marked by a pillar of stones called Jegar-sahadutha / Galeed.

Laban also appears in the Passover Haggadah's retelling, where the phrase "an Aramean sought to destroy my father" has traditionally been interpreted as referring to Laban's dealings with Jacob.
