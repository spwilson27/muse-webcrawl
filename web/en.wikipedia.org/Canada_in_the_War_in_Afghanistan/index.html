Canada in the War in Afghanistan

Canada in the War in Afghanistan refers to the role of the Canadian Armed Forces in the Afghanistan conflict, which lasted from 2001 to 2014 and was Canada's largest military deployment since the Korean War. Canada contributed combat forces, special operations personnel, trainers, and development workers to the NATO-led International Security Assistance Force (ISAF) and to the United States-led Operation Enduring Freedom.

Early involvement

In the aftermath of the September 11 attacks, Canada invoked its support for the United States and deployed naval vessels to the Arabian Sea, air transport and surveillance aircraft, and special forces of Joint Task Force 2 to Afghanistan in late 2001. Canadian regular forces, beginning with a battle group from the Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry, arrived in Kandahar in early 2002 and operated alongside American units.

Kabul and ISAF command

From 2003 to 2005 Canada played a leading role in Kabul, contributing a brigade headquarters and a battle group to ISAF. Canadian Lieutenant-General Rick Hillier commanded ISAF in 2004. During this period Canada also deployed a Provincial Reconstruction Team model later adapted for Kandahar.

Kandahar combat mission, 2006–2011

In 2006 Canada assumed responsibility for Kandahar Province in southern Afghanistan, the heartland of the Taliban insurgency. Canadian troops fought intense battles, including Operation Medusa in September 2006, one of the largest NATO ground operations of the war. The combat mission ended in 2011 after parliamentary debate and a 2008 resolution extending the deployment on conditions including additional allied support.

Training mission and withdrawal

From 2011 to March 2014, Canadian personnel shifted to a training role in Kabul, mentoring the Afghan National Army and police as part of the NATO Training Mission. The last Canadian troops left Afghanistan in March 2014.

Casualties and legacy

A total of 158 Canadian Armed Forces members died during the mission, along with Canadian diplomats, aid workers, and a journalist. The war prompted debate in Canada over military policy, veterans' care, and the treatment of Afghan detainees transferred to Afghan authorities.
