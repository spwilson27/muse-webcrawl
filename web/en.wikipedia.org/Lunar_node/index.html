Lunar node

The Moon's orbit and its two nodes

Types: Ascending node (☊), descending node (☋)

Nodal period: ~27.21 days (draconic month)

Precession period: ~18.6 years

A lunar node is either of the two orbital nodes of the Moon — the points where the Moon's orbit crosses the plane of the ecliptic. The ascending node (north node, symbol ☊) is where the Moon crosses from south to north of the ecliptic; the descending node (south node, symbol ☋) is where it crosses from north to south. The line joining the nodes is called the line of nodes.

Eclipses

Solar and lunar eclipses can occur only when the Moon is near a node at new or full moon, during so-called eclipse seasons that recur roughly every 173 days. The time between successive passages of the Moon through the same node is the draconic (or nodical) month of about 27.21 days, central to eclipse cycles such as the saros.

Precession

Gravitational perturbation by the Sun causes the lunar nodes to precess westward around the ecliptic, completing a full regression in about 18.6 years (6,798 days). This nodal precession combines with the precession of the lunar apsides in eclipse prediction, and the cycle is known in Indian astronomy through the mythic nodes Rahu and Ketu. The 18.6-year cycle also produces the lunar standstill phenomenon, in which the Moon's extreme rising and setting points reach their widest separation.
