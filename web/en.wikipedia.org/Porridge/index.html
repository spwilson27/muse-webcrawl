Porridge

Porridge is a food commonly eaten as a breakfast cereal dish, made by heating or boiling ground, crushed, or chopped starchy plants — typically grain — in milk or water. It is often cooked or served with added flavourings such as sugar, honey, fruit, or syrup to make a sweet cereal, or it can be mixed with spices, meat, or vegetables to make a savoury dish.

Varieties

The most familiar porridge in the English-speaking world is made from oats. Other porridges include rice porridge — congee in China, jōk and related dishes across Asia — maize porridge such as polenta, grits, and the many African staples known as ugali, sadza, nshima, and pap; wheat porridges such as semolina and farina; and millet, sorghum, buckwheat (kasha), and barley porridges.

History

Porridges are among the oldest prepared foods, dating to the early farming cultures of the Neolithic. Grinding grain and boiling it into a soft, digestible paste long predates the leavened bread oven. Oat porridge became a dietary staple in Scotland, where it was traditionally cooked thick enough to be cooled and sliced, stored in a "porridge drawer," and eaten through the week; it is stirred with a wooden spurtle and traditionally eaten standing up with the milk served separately, according to custom.

Nutrition

Whole-grain porridges are rich in soluble fibre (notably oat beta-glucan), complex carbohydrates, and micronutrients, and are associated with reduced cholesterol and steady blood-glucose response.
