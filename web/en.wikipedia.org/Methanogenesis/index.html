Methanogenesis
OrganismsMethanogens (Archaea)
ConditionsStrictly anaerobic
Main productMethane (CH4)
Key coenzymeCoenzyme M, methanofuran, F420

Methanogenesis or biomethanation is the formation of methane coupled to energy conservation by microbes known as methanogens. It is the final step in the decay of organic matter under anaerobic conditions and is carried out exclusively by members of the domain Archaea, principally within the phylum Euryarchaeota. Methanogens are obligate anaerobes, poisoned by oxygen.

Pathways

Most methanogenesis proceeds by the reduction of carbon dioxide with hydrogen (CO2 + 4 H2 → CH4 + 2 H2O), through a distinctive pathway using unusual cofactors such as coenzyme M, methanofuran, and methanopterin, and the nickel-containing enzyme methyl-coenzyme M reductase. Other methanogens cleave acetate (acetoclastic methanogenesis) or use methylated compounds such as methanol and methylamines.

Ecological and practical importance

Methanogenesis occurs in wetlands, rice paddies, sediments, landfills, and the digestive tracts of ruminants and termites, and is a major natural source of atmospheric methane, a potent greenhouse gas. In technology, it is the basis of anaerobic digestion, producing biogas from sewage and agricultural waste.
