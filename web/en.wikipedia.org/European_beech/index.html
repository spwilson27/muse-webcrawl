The European beech or common beech (Fagus sylvatica) is a deciduous tree belonging to the beech family Fagaceae. It is one of the most important forest trees of temperate Europe, dominating many woodlands on well-drained soils.

Description

European beech is a large tree, capable of reaching heights of up to 50 metres and a trunk diameter of 3 metres. It has a typical lifespan of 150 to 200 years, though specimens may live considerably longer. The bark is smooth and grey, the leaves are entire or slightly crenate, and the fruit is a small three-sided nut known as beechmast, borne in soft-spined husks.

Distribution and habitat

The natural range extends from southern Sweden to central Italy, and from northern Spain eastwards to northwest Turkey. Beech is a shade-tolerant climax species that casts dense shade and sheds nutrient-poor litter, strongly influencing the composition of the forests it dominates.

Uses

Beech timber is hard and strong and is used for furniture, flooring, tools and firewood. The nuts are eaten by birds and mammals and were historically used as fodder for pigs. Ornamental cultivars, including copper beech and fern-leaved beech, are widely planted in parks and gardens.
