Kevala jnana

A Jain tirthankara in meditation, representing perfect knowledge.

Kevala jnana (Sanskrit: केवल ज्ञान, "absolute knowledge") is the highest form of knowledge in Jainism — the omniscience attained by a soul that has shed all karmic obscurations. A being who possesses it is called a kevalin and knows all substances in all their modes, across past, present, and future, simultaneously.

Doctrine

Jain philosophy classifies knowledge into five types, from sense-based knowledge (mati) and scriptural knowledge (shruta) through clairvoyance (avadhi) and telepathy (manahparyaya), culminating in kevala jnana. It arises when the four destructive (ghati) karmas — knowledge-obscuring, perception-obscuring, deluding, and obstructing karmas — are destroyed. The liberated soul's knowledge is then unobstructed and infinite.

Kevalins and tirthankaras

Those who attain kevala jnana while still embodied are revered as omniscient teachers; among them, the tirthankaras are those who re-establish the Jain community in each age. Mahavira, the 24th tirthankara, is said to have attained kevala jnana under a sal tree after more than twelve years of ascetic practice. At death, the kevalin's soul is liberated entirely (moksha).

Debates

The possibility of omniscience was debated with other Indian schools, including Buddhist and Hindu philosophers, who questioned whether a finite mind could know all things. Jain logicians such as Samantabhadra and Akalanka wrote defences of the doctrine, arguing that omniscience is the soul's natural state once karmic veils are removed.
