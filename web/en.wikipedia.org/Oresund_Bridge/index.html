The Øresund Bridge (Danish: Øresundsbroen; Swedish: Öresundsbron) is a combined railway and motorway cable-stayed bridge across the Øresund strait between Denmark and Sweden. It is the longest combined road-and-rail bridge in Europe and connects the Danish capital Copenhagen with the Swedish city of Malmö.

Description

The crossing is roughly 16 kilometres long and consists of three parts: the bridge itself, an artificial island called Peberholm built from dredged material, and the 4-kilometre Drogden Tunnel on the Danish side — the tunnel being necessary to avoid interfering with Copenhagen Airport and shipping lanes. The bridge's main span of 490 metres is supported by pylons 204 metres tall, among the highest of their kind.

History and impact

Construction began in 1995 and the link was inaugurated on 1 July 2000 by King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden and Queen Margrethe II of Denmark. Paid for by tolls, the crossing created a single Øresund Region labour market: tens of thousands of people commute between the two countries, and the bridge has become a symbol of European integration — and the setting of the Scandinavian crime drama The Bridge.
