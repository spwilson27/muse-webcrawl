Green Monster

LocationLeft field, Fenway Park, Boston
Height37 ft 2 in (11.3 m)
Distance from home plate310–315 ft (94.5–96 m)
Built1912 (rebuilt in concrete 1934)
TeamBoston Red Sox

The Green Monster is a popular nickname for the high left field wall at Fenway Park, home of the Boston Red Sox in Boston, Massachusetts. At 37 feet 2 inches (11.3 metres) tall, it is among the most famous and distinctive features of any baseball stadium, and it profoundly shapes the way games are played in the park.

History

A high wall has stood in left field since Fenway opened in 1912, originally wooden and fronted by a ten-foot earthen embankment known as Duffy's Cliff. After a fire in 1933, the wall was rebuilt in concrete and tin for the 1934 season. For decades it was covered in advertisements; in 1947 the ads were removed and it was painted green, the origin of its nickname. A manually operated scoreboard remains embedded in the wall, tended by scoreboard operators inside it, complete with the initials of former owners Tom and Jean Yawkey rendered in Morse code on its vertical white lines.

Seats and mascot

In 2003, seats were added atop the wall — the "Monster Seats" — which quickly became some of the most coveted tickets in baseball. The Red Sox mascot, Wally the Green Monster, introduced in 1997, takes his identity from the wall and is said to live inside it.

Effect on play

Because left field is so short — the foul pole is only about 310 feet from home plate — the wall's height compensates, turning would-be home runs into doubles off the wall ("wall-ball doubles") and short fly outs in other parks into Green Monster home runs. Left fielders in Fenway must learn to play caroms off the wall and its scoreboard, a specialised skill associated with players such as Ted Williams, Carl Yastrzemski and Jim Rice.

Cultural legacy

The Green Monster has inspired replicas in minor-league parks and the name has been borrowed for other left field walls. It is a registered icon of Boston sport and an essential stop on tours of Fenway Park.
