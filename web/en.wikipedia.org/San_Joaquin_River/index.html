San Joaquin River

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

San Joaquin River

Source: Sierra Nevada, near Mammoth Lakes, California

Mouth: Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta (Suisun Bay)

Length: about 366 miles (589 km)

Major dam: Friant Dam (Millerton Lake)

Cities on its banks: Fresno (nearby), Stockton

The San Joaquin River (Spanish: Río San Joaquín) is the longest river of Central California. It rises high in the Sierra Nevada, in the Ansel Adams Wilderness of Madera County, and flows west and then north through the flat agricultural heart of the San Joaquin Valley for about 366 miles (589 km), before joining the Sacramento River in the Delta and flowing out through Suisun Bay and San Francisco Bay to the Pacific Ocean.

Course

From its headwaters above 10,000 feet, the river plunges through hydroelectric reservoirs in the Sierra foothills to Friant Dam (completed 1942), which impounds Millerton Lake and diverts most of the river’s flow into the Friant-Kern and Madera canals for irrigation. Below Friant, the river crosses the valley floor past the edge of Fresno, receiving (in wet years) the Kings River floodwaters and the tributaries of the eastside — the Fresno, Chowchilla, Merced, Tuolumne, Stanislaus and Calaveras rivers — before joining the Sacramento at the Delta near Antioch.

Use and environmental issues

The river’s water sustains a large share of California’s irrigated agriculture, but the diversions are among the most contested in the state: for decades some 60 miles of the river below Friant Dam ran dry in most years, extirpating its once-large spring-run Chinook salmon fishery. A landmark 1988 lawsuit by environmental groups and an 18-year legal battle ended in a 2006 settlement, and the San Joaquin River Restoration Program has since released flows to reconnect the river to the Delta and reintroduce salmon. Salinity, agricultural drainage and selenium contamination (dramatised by the Kesterson Reservoir episode of the 1980s) remain persistent problems in the lower river.
