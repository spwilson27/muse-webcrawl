Jack Kirby (born Jacob Kurtzberg; August 28, 1917 – February 6, 1994) was an American comic book artist, writer, and editor widely regarded as one of the medium's major innovators. With Joe Simon he created Captain America in 1941; with writer Stan Lee in the 1960s he co-created much of the Marvel Comics universe, including the Fantastic Four, the X-Men, the Hulk, Thor, Iron Man, Black Panther, and the Silver Surfer.

Career

Raised on New York's Lower East Side, Kirby broke into comics in the late 1930s. His kinetic layouts, foreshortened figures, and "Kirby Krackle" rendering of cosmic energy defined the look of the superhero genre. After wartime service as an infantryman in Europe, he returned to a prolific career across superhero, romance, war, and science-fiction titles.

At Marvel in the 1960s, working under the "Marvel Method," Kirby plotted and drew the flagship titles that launched the Marvel Age of Comics. Feeling under-credited and under-rewarded, he moved to DC Comics in 1970, where he created the epic Fourth World saga and the character Darkseid, and later returned to Marvel, where he created the Eternals.

Legacy

Kirby's influence on comics, film, and popular culture is immense. The Jack Kirby Awards and the Kirby Hall of Fame honour his name, and campaigns by his family and admirers have sought fuller recognition and compensation for his role in creating characters worth billions in media franchises.
