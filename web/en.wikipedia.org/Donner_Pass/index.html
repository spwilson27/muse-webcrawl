Donner Pass

Elevation: 7,056 ft (2,151 m)
Location: Nevada County / Placer County, California, United States
Range: Sierra Nevada

Donner Pass is a 7,056-foot-high (2,151 m) mountain pass in the northern Sierra Nevada, between Truckee and Kingvale in California, United States. Like the emigrant trails that preceded it, the pass is a route through the mountains along which the first transcontinental railroad, the Lincoln Highway, U.S. Route 40, and Interstate 80 cross the Sierra Crest.

History

The pass takes its name from the ill-fated Donner Party, a group of American pioneers who attempted to cross the Sierra Nevada in the winter of 1846–47 and became snowbound near Donner Lake, just east of the summit. Many members of the party perished, and some resorted to cannibalism to survive.

Native Americans had used routes through the area for thousands of years. The Stephens–Townsend–Murphy Party of 1844 was the first wagon train to cross the Sierra via what became known as Donner Pass. The Central Pacific Railroad later built its Sierra crossing here, completing the first transcontinental railroad in 1869; the line included long snow sheds to protect trains from the region's heavy snowfall.

Climate

Donner Pass is one of the snowiest places in the United States, with average annual snowfall exceeding 400 inches (10 m). The winter of 1952 saw a passenger train, the City of San Francisco, stranded for several days by a blizzard near the pass.
