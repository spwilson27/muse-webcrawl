The Monza Circuit, officially the Autodromo Nazionale Monza, is a motor racing circuit near the city of Monza, north of Milan, Italy. Built in 1922 in the royal park of Monza, it is the third purpose-built motor racing circuit in the world and the home of the Italian Grand Prix, which it has hosted in nearly every year of the Formula One World Championship since 1950.

Monza, the "Temple of Speed".

Layout and character

Nicknamed the "Temple of Speed", the circuit is famous for its long straights and fast corners — Curva Grande, the Lesmo corners, the Ascari chicane and the sweeping Parabolica (Curva Alboreto) — with cars spending most of the lap at full throttle. Top speeds of around 350 km/h make it the fastest circuit on the Formula One calendar, and it produced the fastest race in F1 history in 2003. An original banked oval, combined with the road course for a 10 km layout, fell into disuse after fatal accidents and survives as a relic within the park.

History

Monza's history includes triumphs and tragedies: the 1961 crash that killed Wolfgang von Trips and spectators, and the deaths of Jochen Rindt (1970) and Ronnie Peterson (after a 1978 start-line crash) colour its past. Passionate Ferrari supporters, the tifosi, famously invade the track after home victories, such as Charles Leclerc's win in 2019.
