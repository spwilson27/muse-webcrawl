Pomerania

Pomerania (Polish: Pomorze; German: Pommern; Kashubian: Pòmòrskô) is a historical region on the southern shore of the Baltic Sea in Central Europe, today divided between Poland and Germany. Its name derives from the Slavic po more, "by the sea." The region stretches roughly from the Recknitz River in the west, past the Oder estuary and the Vistula Lagoon, to the Vistula delta in the east.

Geography

Pomerania is a lowland of lakes, river valleys, and coastal lagoons (Bodden on the German side), shaped by the last glaciation. Major cities include Szczecin (Stettin), Gdańsk (Danzig), Greifswald, Stralsund, and Koszalin. The Oder River divides the region roughly into Western (Farther) Pomerania in the west and Eastern (Further) Pomerania toward Gdańsk.

History

In the early Middle Ages the region was inhabited by West Slavic Pomeranian tribes. From the 12th century, the Duchy of Pomerania, ruled by the Griffin dynasty, became part of the Holy Roman Empire while undergoing German settlement (the Ostsiedlung). Pomerania passed under Brandenburg-Prussian rule in the 17th and 18th centuries, with parts held by Sweden for a time. After 1945, the Potsdam Agreement placed Pomerania east of the Oder–Neisse line under Polish administration, and the German population was expelled westward. Western Pomerania today forms much of the German state of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, while the Polish portion spans the West Pomeranian, Pomeranian, and parts of other voivodeships.

Culture

The Kashubians, a West Slavic ethnic group around Gdańsk, preserve a distinct language and culture. The region is known for amber, Hanseatic brick Gothic architecture, and seaside resorts such as Binz, Heringsdorf, and Sopot.
