Eisteddfod

The National Eisteddfod is Europe's largest festival of competitive music and poetry

An eisteddfod (plural: eisteddfodau) is a competitive festival of literature, music, and performing arts in Wales. The tradition dates back to at least the 12th century; one of the earliest recorded gatherings was held in 1176 at Cardigan Castle under Lord Rhys, where prizes were awarded to poets and musicians.

The National Eisteddfod

The National Eisteddfod of Wales is held annually in the first week of August, alternating between north and south Wales and between different host communities. It is the largest of several eisteddfodau and is conducted primarily in the Welsh language. Its major honours include the Chair, awarded for an ode (awdl), the Crown, awarded for a poem in free metre, and the Prose Medal. Ceremonies are led by the Gorsedd of the Bards, an association founded in 1792 by Iolo Morganwg as part of the Welsh cultural revival.

Other eisteddfodau

The Urdd National Eisteddfod, organised by the Urdd youth movement, is one of Europe's largest youth arts festivals. The Llangollen International Musical Eisteddfod, founded in 1947, hosts choirs and folk dancers from around the world. Local eisteddfodau are held throughout Wales, and the tradition was carried by Welsh emigrants to communities in Argentina's Chubut Province and elsewhere.
