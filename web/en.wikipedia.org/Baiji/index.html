Baiji

Baiji
Lipotes vexillifer
Family: Lipotidae
Status: Critically endangered, possibly extinct

The baiji (Lipotes vexillifer) is a freshwater dolphin formerly found only in the Yangtze River system of China. Revered in Chinese folklore as the "Goddess of the Yangtze", it is believed to be the first dolphin species driven to extinction by human activity.

Description

Baiji were pale blue-grey in colour, up to about 2.5 metres long, with a long, narrow, slightly upturned beak and small eyes. Because the Yangtze is turbid, the species relied heavily on echolocation. Females were larger than males and gave birth to a single calf every two years.

Decline

For centuries the population numbered in the thousands, but industrialisation of the Yangtze — heavy boat traffic, fishing nets and rolling hooks, dam construction (including the Gezhouba and Three Gorges dams), pollution and habitat loss — caused a catastrophic collapse. Estimates fell to a few hundred in the 1980s and perhaps a dozen by the late 1990s.

Extinction

A comprehensive six-week expedition in late 2006 found no baiji and the species was declared functionally extinct. The last captive individual, Qiqi, died in 2002. Occasional unconfirmed sightings have kept a sliver of hope alive, and the baiji is formally listed as critically endangered, possibly extinct. Its disappearance is a landmark in conservation history, showing how quickly a river system can be transformed beyond the survival of its apex species.
