Indiana University (IU) is a public university system in the U.S. state of Indiana, founded in 1820. Its flagship campus is Indiana University Bloomington, a major research university, and its system includes campuses in Indianapolis (IU Indianapolis), and regional campuses across the state.

History

IU began as the State Seminary School in Bloomington in 1820, became Indiana College in 1828 and Indiana University in 1838. It was among the first public universities to admit women (1867). In the 20th century it grew into a leading research institution, home to figures such as sex researcher Alfred Kinsey and geneticist Hermann Joseph Muller.

Campuses and academics

The Bloomington campus enrols over 40,000 students and is known for the Kelley School of Business, the Jacobs School of Music, the Maurer School of Law and strong programmes in languages, folklore and informatics. IU's health sciences are centred in Indianapolis, where the IU School of Medicine is one of the largest in the United States.

Athletics and culture

The Indiana Hoosiers compete in the Big Ten Conference; the men's basketball programme, shaped by coach Bob Knight, won three NCAA championships under him. The Bloomington campus is noted for its limestone architecture and wooded grounds.
