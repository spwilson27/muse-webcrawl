Raja Ampat Islands

The Raja Ampat Islands are an archipelago off the northwest tip of the Bird's Head Peninsula in West Papua, Indonesia. The name means "Four Kings", referring to the four main islands: Waigeo, Batanta, Salawati and Misool, around which lie more than a thousand smaller islands and reefs. The administrative centre of Raja Ampat Regency is Waisai on Waigeo.

Biodiversity

Raja Ampat lies at the heart of the Coral Triangle and is regarded as one of the most biodiverse marine regions on Earth. Surveys have recorded more than 1,500 fish species and around three-quarters of the world's known hard coral species in its waters. Its reefs support manta rays, reef sharks, sea turtles, dugongs and the endemic walking (epaulette) sharks. On land, the islands hold rainforest with birds of paradise, including the red and Wilson's birds-of-paradise.

People and history

The islands have long been inhabited by Papuan peoples, historically influenced by the sultanates of Tidore and Ternate. Local communities traditionally manage marine resources through practices such as sasi, periodic closures of fishing areas. The name "Four Kings" comes from a local legend of four kings descended from eggs found by a woman on Waigeo.

Tourism and conservation

Since the 2000s, Raja Ampat has become a celebrated destination for scuba diving and ecotourism, managed partly through a network of marine protected areas and an entrance-fee system funding conservation. Threats have included destructive fishing, mining proposals and tourism pressure, prompting strong community and NGO conservation efforts.
