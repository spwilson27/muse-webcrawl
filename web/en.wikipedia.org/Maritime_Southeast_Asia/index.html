Maritime Southeast Asia

Map of the Maritime Southeast Asian archipelagos (image placeholder).

Maritime Southeast Asia is the maritime, island region of Southeast Asia, conventionally comprising Indonesia, Malaysia (including East Malaysia on Borneo), the Philippines, Singapore, Brunei, and Timor-Leste, in contrast to Mainland Southeast Asia. It is sometimes also called the Malay Archipelago or the East Indies.

Geography and peoples

The region is a vast archipelago straddling the equator between the Indian and Pacific oceans, including the Greater and Lesser Sunda Islands, the Malay Peninsula's maritime fringe, the Moluccas and New Guinea's western portion. Its peoples are predominantly Austronesian-speaking, and the seas — including the Strait of Malacca, among the world's busiest shipping lanes — have historically linked the region's cultures and trade as much as its landmasses.

History

Maritime empires such as Srivijaya and Majapahit dominated regional trade before the arrival of Portuguese, Spanish, Dutch and British colonial powers, whose rule reshaped the region's modern states.
