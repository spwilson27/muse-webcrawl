Chylothorax

Specialty: Pulmonology, thoracic surgery

Cause: Leakage of chyle into the pleural space

A chylothorax is an accumulation of chyle, a milky lymphatic fluid rich in triglycerides, in the pleural cavity around a lung. It usually results from injury or obstruction of the thoracic duct.

Causes and symptoms

Causes include thoracic surgery, trauma, malignancy (especially lymphoma), infection, and congenital lymphatic abnormalities. A large collection can cause breathlessness, cough, and chest discomfort; prolonged loss of chyle may cause malnutrition and immune deficiency.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is confirmed by finding triglycerides and chylomicrons in pleural fluid. Treatment ranges from drainage, dietary fat restriction or parenteral nutrition, and medicines such as octreotide to thoracic duct embolization or surgical ligation.
