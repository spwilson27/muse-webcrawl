The Mystery of the Yellow Room

The Mystery of the Yellow Room (French: Le mystère de la chambre jaune, 1907) is a detective novel by French writer Gaston Leroux. It is one of the earliest and most famous "locked-room mystery" novels.

Plot

Mathilde Stangerson is found brutally attacked inside a room locked from the inside with no possible exit, at the Château du Glandier. Eighteen-year-old reporter Joseph Rouletabille — Leroux's series detective — investigates alongside police detective Frédéric Larsan. The solution turns on the timing of the attack and a disguised identity: the assailant is Larsan himself, the criminal Ballmeyer.

Legacy

The novel established a benchmark for impossible-crime fiction, praised by John Dickson Carr; Agatha Christie and others worked in its shadow. Leroux later wrote The Phantom of the Opera.
