LGBT culture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

LGBT culture is a culture shared by lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer communities. It encompasses distinctive art, literature, film, music, slang, social spaces and symbols — most prominently the rainbow flag designed by Gilbert Baker in 1978 — as well as shared history centred on the struggle for civil rights.

Elements

Key elements include Pride parades and festivals, held in many cities around June to commemorate the 1969 Stonewall riots in New York; drag performance traditions; ball culture originating among Black and Latino communities in New York; gay villages or neighbourhoods such as Castro Street in San Francisco and Canal Street in Manchester; and a large body of literature, cinema and music addressing LGBT experience. Historically, coded languages such as Polari in Britain and shared symbols allowed communities to recognise one another under criminalisation.

Diversity and debate

LGBT culture is not monolithic: it varies by country, generation, race and sub-community, and scholars debate the extent to which a single "LGBT culture" exists as distinct from the separate cultures of its constituent groups. Commercialisation of Pride and questions of inclusion are ongoing topics of discussion within the communities themselves.
