Rahman Baba

Rahman Baba
Abd al-Raḥmān Bābā
c. 1632 – 1706 CE
Pashto Sufi poet of the Mughal era

Abd al-Raḥmān Bābā (1632–1706, Pashto: عبدالرحمان بابا), popularly known as Rahman Baba, was a renowned Pashtun Sufi saint, member of the Sufi Dervish orders and poet from Peshawar during the Mughal era. Along with his contemporary Khushal Khan Khattak, he is considered one of the most popular poets among the Pashtuns in Pakistan and Afghanistan.

Life

Rahman was born into a Momand family of the Ghoryakhel tribe in Bahadur Killi, near Peshawar. He received religious education, reportedly in Kohat and from teachers in Peshawar, and devoted himself to prayer, asceticism and poetry rather than worldly office, earning the honorific Baba ("father/elder").

Poetry

His Diwan (collection) contains some 350 poems, written in an accessible Pashto filled with Sufi themes of divine love, humility, the vanity of worldly power and tolerance. His verses remain widely sung and quoted today. His tomb and shrine near Peshawar is a place of pilgrimage and an annual Urs festival; it was damaged by militants in 2009 and subsequently restored.
