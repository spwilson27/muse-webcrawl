Autarky

Autarky
TypeEconomic policy / condition
DefinitionSelf-sufficiency; absence of trade with other economies

Autarky is the condition of a country, region or entity that is economically self-sufficient and conducts little or no trade with the outside world. The term derives from the Greek autárkeia, meaning "self-sufficiency".

History

Autarkic policies have been pursued at various times for strategic, ideological or developmental reasons. Examples include Spain under Franco in the 1940s, Nazi Germany's attempts to reduce dependence on imports, Albania under Enver Hoxha, and North Korea under the Juche ideology. Tokugawa Japan's sakoku policy restricted most foreign contact for over two centuries, though it was not complete autarky.

Economics

Economists generally argue that autarky forgoes the gains of specialisation and comparative advantage, raising prices and limiting choice, although supporters cite national security, infant-industry protection and resilience against external shocks. In price theory, the "autarky price" of a good — its price in the absence of trade — is a standard benchmark in trade models.
