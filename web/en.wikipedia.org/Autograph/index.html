Autograph

An autograph is a person's own handwriting or signature. The term most often refers to the signature of a celebrity collected by a fan as a souvenir, but it can also denote a manuscript written in the author's own hand, as opposed to a copy made by a scribe or printer.

Collecting

Autograph collecting is a long-established hobby. Collectors seek signatures on photographs, letters, documents, sports equipment, and musical instruments. The value of an autograph depends on the fame of the signer, rarity, condition, and authenticity. Professional authentication services examine handwriting, ink, and provenance to distinguish genuine signatures from forgeries and from "autopen" machine reproductions.

History

Collecting signatures became popular in Europe in the sixteenth century, when scholars kept alba amicorum (friendship albums). In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries the hobby expanded to actors, athletes, musicians, and political figures. Some historical autographs, such as those of William Shakespeare, of whom only a handful of signatures survive, are exceptionally valuable.

Legal aspects

Signed documents have legal significance as evidence of agreement or authorship; see signature.
