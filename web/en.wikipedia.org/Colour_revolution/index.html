Colour revolution

Colour revolution (sometimes color revolution) is a term used since the early 2000s to describe a series of largely nonviolent protest movements in post-communist countries of Eastern Europe and Central Asia that succeeded in toppling authoritarian or semi-authoritarian governments. The movements typically adopted a specific colour or flower as their symbol and demanded free elections and democratic reform.

Examples

The wave is generally traced from the Bulldozer Revolution that overthrew Slobodan Milošević in Serbia in 2000, through Georgia's Rose Revolution (2003), Ukraine's Orange Revolution (2004) and Kyrgyzstan's Tulip Revolution (2005). Student movements such as Serbia's Otpor! pioneered tactics — branding, humour, election monitoring and disciplined nonviolence — that were exported to other countries.

Interpretations

Supporters describe the colour revolutions as indigenous democratic movements driven by election fraud and corruption. Critics, including the governments of Russia and China, contend they were orchestrated by Western governments and NGOs. Ukraine's Euromaidan protests of 2013–14 and later upheavals are sometimes discussed as successors to the original colour revolutions.
