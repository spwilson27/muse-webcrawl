Linear map

Also called: Linear transformation, linear operator
Field: Linear algebra
Key properties: Additivity and homogeneity

In mathematics, and more specifically in linear algebra, a linear map (also called a linear mapping, linear transformation, vector space homomorphism, or in some contexts linear function) is a mapping between two vector spaces that preserves the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication.

Definition

If V and W are vector spaces over the same field K, a function f : V → W is linear if for all vectors u, v ∈ V and all scalars c ∈ K:

  • f(u + v) = f(u) + f(v) (additivity)
  • f(cu) = cf(u) (homogeneity of degree 1)

Consequences include f(0) = 0 and preservation of linear combinations. Geometrically, linear maps send lines to lines and fix the origin: rotations, scalings, reflections, shears, and projections of Euclidean space are familiar examples, as is the derivative operator on spaces of functions.

Matrices and structure

Once bases are chosen for finite-dimensional spaces, every linear map is represented by a matrix, and composition of maps corresponds to matrix multiplication. Fundamental constructions include the kernel (null space) and image (range), related by the rank–nullity theorem. Linear maps that are bijective are isomorphisms; those from a space to itself are endomorphisms, and linear functionals map into the scalar field, forming the dual space.

Applications

Linear maps are central to differential equations, quantum mechanics (where observables are linear operators), computer graphics, signal processing, and much of modern mathematics and engineering.
