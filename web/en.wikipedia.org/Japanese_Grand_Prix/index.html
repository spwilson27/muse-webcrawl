The Japanese Grand Prix is a motor racing event in the Formula One World Championship, first held as a championship race in 1976. Since 1987 it has been held almost continuously at the Suzuka Circuit, a demanding figure-of-eight track beloved by drivers, except in 2007 and 2008, when it returned to Fuji Speedway.

History

The first two championship Japanese Grands Prix were held at Fuji Speedway in 1976 and 1977. The 1976 race, run in torrential rain, decided the famous championship duel between Niki Lauda — who withdrew, judging conditions too dangerous — and James Hunt, who finished third to take the title. After a fatal accident in 1977 the race disappeared from the calendar for a decade.

Formula One returned to Japan in 1987 at Suzuka, owned by Honda. Positioned late in the season, the race decided many championships. The rivalry between Ayrton Senna and Alain Prost produced the sport's most infamous collisions there in 1989 and 1990, each settling the world title. Michael Schumacher won the race six times, a record, while Suzuka's 130R corner and the "S" curves are regarded as a supreme test of car and driver.

Recent years

The race was cancelled in 2020 and 2021 because of the COVID-19 pandemic, then returned in 2022, when Max Verstappen clinched his second world championship in a rain-shortened event. In 2024 the Grand Prix moved to a spring date, in April, when Verstappen won again. Suzuka is confirmed on the calendar into the 2030s.

Notable winners

  • Michael Schumacher — 6 wins (1995, 1997, 2000, 2001, 2002, 2004)
  • Lewis Hamilton — 5 wins
  • Sebastian Vettel — 4 wins
  • Ayrton Senna — 3 wins
