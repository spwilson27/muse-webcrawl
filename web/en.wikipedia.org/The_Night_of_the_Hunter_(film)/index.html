The Night of the Hunter is a 1955 American film noir thriller directed by Charles Laughton — his only film as director — and starring Robert Mitchum, Shelley Winters, and Lillian Gish. Based on Davis Grubb's 1953 novel, itself inspired by the real-life serial killer Harry Powers, it tells of a corrupt, murderous self-styled preacher who marries and murders a widow in pursuit of the $10,000 her executed husband hid, and of her two children who flee him down the Ohio River.

Plot

Reverend Harry Powell, who has the words LOVE and HATE tattooed across his knuckles and delivers sermons on their eternal struggle, learns in prison of Ben Harper's hidden bank robbery money. Released, Powell courts Harper's widow Willa, murders her when she discovers his nature, and terrorizes her children John and Pearl, who escape by boat. Downriver they are sheltered by Rachel Cooper (Gish), a tough elderly woman who cares for abandoned children; in the climactic confrontation she defends them with a shotgun, and Powell is arrested and tried.

Style and production

The film's dreamlike, expressionist look — shot in black and white by Stanley Cortez, with stylized sets, underwater photography, and lyrical river sequences accompanied by hymns — drew on silent cinema and German Expressionism. James Agee wrote the screenplay, though Laughton extensively reshaped it.

Reception and legacy

Ignored or dismissed on release, the film's failure discouraged Laughton from directing again. It was rediscovered by critics from the 1970s onward and is now considered a masterpiece: Mitchum's Powell is routinely ranked among cinema's greatest villains, and in 1992 the film was added to the United States National Film Registry. Its influence can be seen in the work of directors from Martin Scorsese to the Coen brothers and Spike Lee, whose Do the Right Thing directly quotes Powell's love/hate speech.
