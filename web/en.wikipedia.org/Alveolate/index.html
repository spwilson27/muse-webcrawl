Alveolate

The alveolates (from Latin alveolatus, "hollowed") are a superphylum of protists within the SAR supergroup. They are united by the presence of cortical alveoli — flattened vesicles (sacs) packed into a continuous layer just beneath the cell membrane, supporting it — though this feature has been secondarily lost in some lineages. Alveolates include free-living photosynthetic and predatory forms as well as important parasites.

Major groups

The three best-known alveolate phyla are:

  • Ciliates (Ciliophora) — mostly free-living single-celled organisms covered in cilia, such as Paramecium.
  • Dinoflagellates (Dinoflagellata) — many are photosynthetic plankton; some cause harmful algal blooms ("red tides") and others live symbiotically in corals.
  • Apicomplexans (Apicomplexa) — obligate intracellular parasites including Plasmodium, which causes malaria, and Toxoplasma.

Other lineages include the Colpodellida, Chromerida and Perkinsida. Molecular phylogenetics places alveolates together with stramenopiles and rhizarians in the SAR clade.

Characteristics

Most alveolates have mitochondria with tubular cristae. Many dinoflagellates and apicomplexans possess plastids derived from secondary endosymbiosis of a red alga; in apicomplexans the reduced plastid, the apicoplast, is essential for survival and is a target for antiparasitic drugs.
