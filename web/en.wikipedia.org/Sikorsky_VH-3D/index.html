Sikorsky VH-3D

Sikorsky VH-3D Sea King
National origin: United States
Manufacturer: Sikorsky Aircraft
Introduction: 1974 (VH-3D)
Status: In service (being replaced by VH-92A)
Primary user: United States Marine Corps (HMX-1)
Role: Presidential transport ("Marine One")

The Sikorsky VH-3D is a variant of the SH-3 Sea King helicopter operated by the United States Marine Corps squadron HMX-1 ("The Nighthawks") as the principal short-distance transport for the President of the United States. When the President is aboard, the aircraft uses the call sign Marine One.

History

Presidential helicopter service began in 1957 with a Bell UH-13J, and the Sea King entered the role in 1961 with the VH-3A. The improved VH-3D variant entered service in 1974. The fleet habitually operates in identical groups with decoy aircraft, departing from the White House South Lawn, Joint Base Anacostia–Bolling and Andrews Air Force Base, often connecting the President with Air Force One.

Features

The VH-3D is fitted with defensive systems, secure communications and a VIP interior seating up to about 14 passengers. The white-and-green livery, kept immaculate by HMX-1, is among the most recognisable in aviation.

Replacement

After the cancellation of the VH-71 Kestrel programme in 2009, the VH-92A Patriot — based on the Sikorsky S-92 — was selected in 2014 to replace the ageing VH-3D and VH-60N fleet, with presidential service beginning in the 2020s.
