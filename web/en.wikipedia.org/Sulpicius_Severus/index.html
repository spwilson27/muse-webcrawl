Sulpicius Severus

Sulpicius Severus (c. 363 – c. 425) was a Christian writer and native of Aquitania in Western Roman Gaul. He is best known for his Life of Saint Martin of Tours, one of the most influential works of early Christian hagiography, and for his Chronicle, a history of the world from creation to about 400 AD.

Born into a senatorial family, Sulpicius was educated as a lawyer and practised in the courts before renouncing his career around 394 under the influence of Martin of Tours and his friend Paulinus of Nola. He withdrew to an estate at Primuliacum in southern Gaul, where he lived a semi-monastic life and wrote his works.

Works

The Vita Martini, begun during Martin's lifetime, modelled Christian sainthood on classical biography and helped spread Martin's cult across Europe. His Dialogues continued Martin's miracles in dialogue form, and his three letters concerning Martin's death circulated widely. The Chronica is valued for its account of the Priscillianist controversy in Gaul and Spain. His polished Latin style earned him comparison with Sallust.
