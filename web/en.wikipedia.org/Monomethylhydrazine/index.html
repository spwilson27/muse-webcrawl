Monomethylhydrazine

Monomethylhydrazine (MMH), CH3NHNH2

Monomethylhydrazine (MMH) is a highly toxic, volatile hydrazine with the chemical formula CH3NHNH2. It is a colourless liquid with an ammonia- or fish-like odour, miscible with water, and flammable in air. Despite its dangers, MMH is widely used as a rocket propellant because it ignites spontaneously (hypergolically) on contact with oxidisers such as nitrogen tetroxide, giving engines that are reliable and restartable.

Uses

MMH paired with mixed oxides of nitrogen (MON) powered the Space Shuttle's orbital manoeuvring and reaction control systems, the Apollo lunar module's heritage designs, and many satellites and upper stages, including Ariane and numerous spacecraft thrusters, where storability at room temperature for years is essential. It is also an intermediate in some chemical syntheses. In nature, MMH is the toxic breakdown product responsible for the poisoning caused by false morel mushrooms (Gyromitra), via the compound gyromitrin.

Toxicity

MMH is absorbed through the skin and lungs, attacks the central nervous system (causing convulsions, by antagonising vitamin B6 and glutamate decarboxylase), damages the liver and blood, and is a suspected carcinogen. Propellant handlers wear full protective suits (SCAPE suits) during loading operations, and pyridoxine (vitamin B6) is the specific antidote for acute poisoning.
