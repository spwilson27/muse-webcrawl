Nebula Award for Best Novel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nebula Award for Best Novel is given each year by the Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association (SFWA) to the best science fiction or fantasy novel published during the previous year. It is one of the most prestigious awards in the field; winners are nominated and voted on by SFWA's professional membership.

History

The first Nebula Awards were presented in 1966, when Frank Herbert's Dune won for Best Novel. Eligibility was later standardised to novels of 40,000 words or more published in the eligibility year. Multiple winners include Ursula K. Le Guin, William Gibson, Kim Stanley Robinson, and N. K. Jemisin; notable honorees include Larry Niven's Ringworld, Isaac Asimov's The Gods Themselves, Orson Scott Card's Ender's Game and Speaker for the Dead (which won the award in consecutive years, alongside their Hugo Awards), and Neil Gaiman's American Gods.

Significance

Together with the Hugo Award for Best Novel, voted by fans, the Nebula marks the field's twin peaks; novels that win both are considered canonical classics of science fiction and fantasy. See also Nebula Award and Hugo Award for Best Novel.
