The Govan Stones are an internationally important collection of early medieval carved stones displayed at Govan Old Parish Church in Glasgow, Scotland. The collection of 31 stones dates from the 9th to 11th centuries, when Govan was a major royal and ecclesiastical centre of the Brittonic Kingdom of Strathclyde.

The collection

The centrepiece is the Govan Sarcophagus, a richly carved stone coffin thought to have held the relics of Saint Constantine, decorated with interlace, hunting scenes, and a mounted warrior. The collection also includes five massive "hogback" stones — recumbent grave markers shaped like Viking houses, found almost exclusively in northern Britain — cross shafts, and the tall Jordanhill cross.

Rediscovery and significance

Many stones had lain in the churchyard for centuries and some were reused in walls or lost; 16 carved stones once in the graveyard are now missing. Archaeological work and community campaigns in the 21st century brought the collection wider recognition, and it is now presented as one of the finest survivals of Strathclyde's sculpture, comparable to the better-known monuments of Iona and Pictland.
