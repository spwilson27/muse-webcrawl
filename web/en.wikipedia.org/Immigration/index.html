Immigration

Immigration is the international movement of people to a destination country of which they are not usual residents or where they do not possess nationality, in order to settle as permanent residents. Commuters, tourists, and short-term visitors are not considered immigrants. The movement out of a country is emigration.

Causes

Migration is commonly explained by push factors (conflict, persecution, poverty, disasters) and pull factors (employment, safety, education, family reunification). Refugees and asylum seekers move under the protection of international law, including the 1951 Refugee Convention.

Scale

The United Nations estimated about 281 million international migrants in 2020, roughly 3.6% of the world population. Major destination countries include the United States, Germany, Saudi Arabia, and Russia; major sources include India, Mexico, and China.

Policy and debate

States regulate immigration through visas, quotas, points systems, and border enforcement. Debates concern economic effects on wages and public services, cultural integration, security, and the rights of migrants; remittances sent home are a major source of income for many origin countries.
