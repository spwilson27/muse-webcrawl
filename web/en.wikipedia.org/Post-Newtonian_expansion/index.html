Post-Newtonian expansion

In general relativity, post-Newtonian expansions are mathematical series expansions of the Einstein field equations used to find approximate solutions in situations where gravity is weak and velocities are small compared with the speed of light, but where Newtonian gravity alone is not sufficiently accurate. The expansions are organised in powers of a small parameter, typically v/c or the ratio of the gravitational potential to c².

Applications

Post-Newtonian theory provides the framework for precision tests of general relativity in the Solar System, including the perihelion precession of Mercury, light deflection, and the Shapiro time delay. It is also essential for computing the gravitational waveforms of inspiralling binary systems observed by detectors such as LIGO and Virgo: the phase evolution of binary black hole and neutron star mergers is calculated to high post-Newtonian orders and combined with numerical relativity.

Parameterized post-Newtonian formalism

The parameterized post-Newtonian (PPN) formalism generalises the expansion by introducing parameters (such as γ and β) that measure how much a given metric theory of gravity departs from general relativity, allowing experiments to constrain whole families of alternative theories at once. Measurements, for example from the Cassini spacecraft, constrain γ to agree with general relativity to within about one part in 100,000.
