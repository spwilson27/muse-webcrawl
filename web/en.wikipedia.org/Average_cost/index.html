Average cost

In economics, average cost (AC) or unit cost is equal to total cost (TC) divided by the number of units of a good produced:

AC = TC / Q

Average cost is an important factor in determining how businesses will choose to price their products, and it plays a key role in decisions about production levels and market supply.

Components

Average total cost (ATC) can be decomposed into average fixed cost (AFC) plus average variable cost (AVC). Average fixed cost declines continuously as output rises because fixed costs are spread over more units, while average variable cost typically first falls and then rises, reflecting diminishing marginal returns.

Short run and long run

In the short run, the average cost curve is typically U-shaped: costs per unit fall as fixed costs are spread and specialization improves efficiency, then rise as diminishing returns set in. In the long run, when all inputs are variable, the shape of the average cost curve reflects economies and diseconomies of scale.

Relationship with marginal cost

The marginal cost curve intersects the average cost curve at its minimum point. When marginal cost is below average cost, average cost is falling; when marginal cost is above average cost, average cost is rising. In long-run competitive equilibrium, firms produce at the minimum of the average cost curve.
