Dasein

Dasein (German: [ˈdaːzaɪn]) is a German word meaning "being there" or "presence", formed from da (there) and sein (being). In ordinary German it can mean existence in general, but it became a central technical term in the philosophy of Martin Heidegger, above all in Being and Time (1927).

Heidegger's use

Heidegger uses Dasein to refer to the kind of being that human beings have — the entity whose own being is an issue for it. Rather than treating the human as a subject or a rational animal, he analyses Dasein through structures such as being-in-the-world (In-der-Welt-sein), care (Sorge), being-toward-death (Sein-zum-Tode), and temporality. Dasein is always "mine" (Jemeinigkeit) and exists either authentically, owning its finite possibilities, or inauthentically, absorbed in the anonymous "they" (das Man).

Earlier and later usage

The word had been used by earlier German philosophers, including Hegel and Jaspers; Karl Jaspers made Dasein a term of his Existenz philosophy. Heidegger's usage, influential on existentialism, hermeneutics and deconstruction, is frequently left untranslated in English-language scholarship.
