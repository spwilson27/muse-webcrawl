Daydream Nation is the fifth studio album by American rock band Sonic Youth, released on 18 October 1988. The double album was recorded at Greene Street Recording in New York City and was the group's last release on an independent label before signing with Geffen Records.

The album features an unlit candle photograph by Gerhard Richter, Kerze ("Candle", 1982), on its cover, and Richter's Zwei Kerzen (1982) on the back. Widely regarded as one of the greatest albums of the 1980s, it was selected by the Library of Congress in 2005 for preservation in the National Recording Registry as a work that is "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant."

Background and recording

After the critical success of Sister (1987), Sonic Youth sought a recording environment that would let them develop longer, more structured compositions while retaining their signature use of alternate guitar tunings and extended instrumental passages. The band booked Greene Street Recording, a studio previously used by hip hop acts, and recorded with engineer Nick Sansano. The sessions cost roughly $30,000, a large sum for the band at the time.

Music and themes

Daydream Nation balances pop-inflected songs such as "Teen Age Riot" and "Silver Rocket" with extended noise-oriented pieces, including the three-part "Trilogy" that closes the album. Lyrical themes draw on William Gibson's cyberpunk novel Neuromancer, the American counterculture, and the media landscape of the late Reagan era. The opening track, "Teen Age Riot," is often read as a fantasy of an alternative America led by figures from underground culture.

Reception and legacy

Upon release, the album topped the UK Indie Chart and was named album of the year in several critics' polls, including The Village Voice's Pazz & Jop survey of 1988. Retrospectively it is frequently placed among the most influential alternative rock records, praised for bridging underground noise rock and the mainstream alternative boom of the early 1990s. In 2007 the band performed the album in its entirety as part of the Don't Look Back concert series.
