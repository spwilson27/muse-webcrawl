Monkey Mia

Monkey Mia is a popular tourist destination located about 25 kilometres (16 miles) northeast of Denham in the Shark Bay region of Western Australia. It lies within the Shark Bay Marine Park and the surrounding Shark Bay UNESCO World Heritage Site. Monkey Mia is best known for its wild bottlenose dolphins, which swim to shore every morning to be hand-fed by rangers and visitors — an interaction that has taken place since the 1960s and has made the beach one of Australia's most famous wildlife attractions.

The dolphins

Dolphins began visiting the beach in the 1960s after a local fisherman's wife started feeding a dolphin that followed their boat. By the 1980s a small group of dolphins visited daily, and a researcher camp was established in 1984; long-term studies of the Shark Bay dolphins have since made important contributions to knowledge of dolphin society, tool use (sponging) and maternal behaviour. Because of concerns about overfeeding, the feeding programme is now strictly controlled by the Department of Biodiversity, Conservation and Attractions: only a few adult females are fed small amounts of fish, and feeding occurs only in the mornings under ranger supervision. The Monkey Mia dolphins are part of the large Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphin population of Shark Bay.

Setting and name

The area features red sand dunes, white beaches and clear turquoise water, with dugongs, turtles, rays and sharks also common in the bay. The origin of the name "Monkey Mia" is uncertain: "Mia" is believed to derive from an Aboriginal word for "home" or "shelter", while "Monkey" may refer to a schooner called Monkey that visited the area in the 19th century, or to pearl lugger crews. The site includes a visitor centre, a resort and camping facilities.
