The Scream

The Scream
Artist: Edvard Munch
Year: 1893
Medium: Oil, tempera, pastel and crayon on cardboard
Dimensions: 91 cm × 73.5 cm
Location: National Gallery, Oslo

The Scream is a composition created by Norwegian artist Edvard Munch in 1893. The Norwegian name is Skrik (Shriek), and the German title Munch gave it was Der Schrei der Natur (The Scream of Nature). The agonised face in the painting has become one of the most iconic images in art, symbolising the anxiety of the human condition.

Description and inspiration

Munch recalled walking at sunset when clouds turned blood red and he sensed an infinite scream passing through nature. The androgynous figure on a bridge, hands to its ears and mouth open, is set against a swirling fjord landscape under an orange sky.

Versions

Munch created four versions in paint and pastels between 1893 and 1910, held in Oslo museums and private collections. One pastel version sold at auction in 2012 for nearly US$120 million, then a record.

Thefts

Versions were stolen in 1994 and 2004 and recovered; the incidents increased the work's fame.

Legacy

The image has been extensively reproduced, parodied, and used as an emoji (😱), in films such as Home Alone and Scream.
