Prayer in Christianity

Prayer in Christianity is the practice of communicating with God — addressing God the Father, in the name of Jesus Christ, through the Holy Spirit — and is a central element of Christian life and worship. Christians understand prayer through the teaching and example of Jesus and the practice of the early church described in the New Testament.

Biblical foundations

Jesus taught his disciples to pray with the Lord's Prayer ("Our Father"), recorded in the Gospels of Matthew and Luke, which remains the most widely used Christian prayer. He modelled prayer in solitude and at key moments of his ministry, and parables such as the persistent widow and the Pharisee and the tax collector teach humility and perseverance. Types of prayer commonly distinguished from Scripture include adoration, confession, thanksgiving, and supplication (often abbreviated ACTS), together with intercession for others.

Forms and practices

Christian prayer may be vocal or mental, spontaneous or liturgical, individual or corporate. Liturgical traditions use set forms: the Liturgy of the Hours (Divine Office) in Catholicism, daily offices in Anglicanism, and the prayer cycles of Eastern Orthodoxy, which also cherishes the Jesus Prayer and the hesychast tradition. Catholic and Orthodox Christians pray with the intercession of Mary and the saints (a practice rejected in most Protestant traditions, which address God directly) and use aids such as rosaries. Pentecostal and charismatic Christians emphasise spontaneous prayer, prayer in tongues, and prayer for healing; contemplative traditions practise centring prayer and lectio divina.
