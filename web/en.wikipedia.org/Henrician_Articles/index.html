Henrician Articles

The Henrician Articles (Polish: Artykuły henrykowskie) were a permanent contract between the szlachta of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and each newly elected king, in force from 1573 to 1795. Named for Henry of Valois, the first king elected under them, they formed, together with the pacta conventa, the constitutional foundations of the Commonwealth.

Henrician Articles
Adopted1573
Named forHenry of Valois (Henri III of France)
In force until1795; superseded in part 1791

Provisions

The Articles guaranteed: free election of the king; biennial sessions of the Sejm; religious tolerance as affirmed by the Warsaw Confederation; no taxes or levies without Sejm consent; no war or peace without consultation; a council of resident senators to advise the king; and — crucially — if the king transgressed the law, the nobility were released from obedience (the rokosz right of resistance, de non praestanda oboedientia).

Significance

They made the Commonwealth a contractual, elective monarchy limited by law, the core of Golden Liberty, and were sworn by every elected king from Henry (1573) to Stanisław August Poniatowski (1764), until the Constitution of 3 May 1791 introduced hereditary succession (never implemented).
