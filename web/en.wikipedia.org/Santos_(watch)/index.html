Santos (watch)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

ManufacturerCartier
Introduced1904 (created); 1911 (sold publicly)
Named forAlberto Santos-Dumont

The Cartier Santos is a wristwatch created by Louis Cartier in 1904 for his friend, the Brazilian aviator Alberto Santos-Dumont. It is widely regarded as one of the first purpose-built men's wristwatches and the first pilot's watch. At a time when men carried pocket watches, Santos-Dumont complained that he could not check the time while flying without taking his hands off the controls; Cartier's solution mounted a small square-cased watch on a leather strap.

Design

The Santos is distinguished by its square case, Roman numeral dial, blued sword-shaped hands, and exposed screws on the bezel — a deliberately industrial detail inspired by the rivets and bolts of early aviation and the Eiffel Tower era. Cartier began selling the model to the public in 1911. It has remained in continuous production in evolving forms, including the Santos Galbée and the modern Santos de Cartier collection, with steel, gold, and skeletonised versions.

Significance

The Santos helped establish the wristwatch as a practical and fashionable object for men, a transition accelerated by the First World War, and it remains one of Cartier's flagship lines. Its design language — visible screws, geometric case, integrated bracelet options — has made it an enduring icon of watch design.
