Reed–Solomon error correction

Reed–Solomon codes are a group of error-correcting codes introduced by Irving S. Reed and Gustave Solomon in 1960. They are a non-binary cyclic special case of BCH codes, constructed by evaluating a polynomial over a finite field at multiple points.

Properties

An RS code with n symbols and k data symbols can correct up to (n−k)/2 symbol errors, or twice as many erasures at known positions. Because errors in data storage and transmission tend to occur in bursts, Reed–Solomon codes are especially effective at correcting burst errors.

Applications

They are used in CDs, DVDs and Blu-ray discs (in cross-interleaved form), QR codes, DSL and WiMAX links, satellite communications, and deep-space probes — the Voyager spacecraft used Reed–Solomon coding to transmit images across the solar system. They also underpin distributed storage systems such as RAID 6.
