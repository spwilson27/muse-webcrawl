Franz Ferdinand are a Scottish rock band formed in Glasgow in 2002. The band's original line-up was composed of Alex Kapranos (lead vocals, lead guitar, keyboards), Nick McCarthy (rhythm guitar, keyboards, vocals), Bob Hardy (bass guitar), and Paul Thomson (drums, percussion, backing vocals). Julian Corrie (keyboards, guitar, backing vocals) and Dino Bardot (guitar, backing vocals) joined in 2017 after McCarthy left, and Audrey Tait (drums) joined in 2021 after Thomson left.

The band came to prominence in the mid-2000s as part of the post-punk revival. Their debut album, Franz Ferdinand (2004), entered the UK Albums Chart at number three and won the 2004 Mercury Prize. The single "Take Me Out" became their signature hit, winning a Grammy nomination and wide international success.

History

The band took their name from Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria, whose assassination in 1914 helped precipitate the First World War. They built a following through performances at the Chateau, a derelict warehouse in Glasgow's Gorbals district that served as rehearsal space and art-party venue. Signed to Domino Records, they released the "Darts of Pleasure" EP in 2003.

Their second album, You Could Have It So Much Better (2005), topped the UK chart; Tonight: Franz Ferdinand (2009) adopted a more dance-oriented sound, and Right Thoughts, Right Words, Right Action followed in 2013. In 2015 they released FFS, a collaborative album with the American band Sparks. Always Ascending appeared in 2018, and the greatest-hits collection Hits to the Head in 2022. Their sixth studio album, The Human Fear, was released in January 2025.

Style

Franz Ferdinand's music is characterised by angular guitar riffs, danceable rhythms, and a fusion of indie rock with disco and funk influences, often described as dance-punk or art rock. Kapranos has said the band aimed to make music "for girls to dance to".
