Romuald Traugutt

Romuald Traugutt
Born: 16 January 1826, Shastakova, Russian Empire (now Belarus)
Died: 5 August 1864 (aged 38), Warsaw, Congress Poland
Known for: Dictator of the January Uprising (1863–1864)

Romuald Traugutt (16 January 1826 – 5 August 1864) was a Polish general and war hero, best known as the last dictator of the January Uprising against the Russian Empire in 1863–1864. A former officer in the Imperial Russian Army, he took command of insurgent forces and, from October 1863, led the Polish National Government with dictatorial powers, attempting to reorganise the uprising and win foreign support.

Early career

Traugutt was born in Shastakova in the Grodno Governorate. He joined the Russian army as an engineer officer, served in the Danubian Principalities during the Crimean War, and later in the Caucasus and Hungary. He retired in 1862 with the rank of lieutenant colonel and settled on his estate.

January Uprising

When the uprising broke out in January 1863, Traugutt initially hesitated, then took command of a partisan unit in the Polesie region, winning local engagements before his force was dispersed. In October 1863 the National Government appointed him dictator. Working underground in Warsaw under an assumed identity, he reformed the government into ministerial departments, sought to turn the guerrilla effort into a regular war, decreed measures to implement peasant enfranchisement, and sent emissaries to Western capitals to gain diplomatic backing, which never materialised.

Arrest and death

Russian police arrested Traugutt in April 1864. He was tried by a military court and hanged, together with four associates, at the Warsaw Citadel on 5 August 1864. He became a national martyr in Poland; numerous schools, streets, and military units bear his name, and he appeared on the 20-złoty banknote of the People's Republic of Poland.
