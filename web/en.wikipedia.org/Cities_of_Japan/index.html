Cities of Japan

A city (shi, 市) in Japan is a unit of local government under the Local Autonomy Law. To become a city, a municipality generally must have a population of at least 50,000, with a proportion of residents engaged in urban occupations, though mergers have produced exceptions. Japan has nearly 800 cities, in addition to towns, villages and the special wards of Tokyo.

Designations

Certain large cities receive expanded powers. Designated cities (seirei shitei toshi) have populations over 500,000 and are subdivided into wards (ku); there are twenty of them, including Osaka, Nagoya, Sapporo, Fukuoka and Kobe. Core cities (chūkaku-shi) have at least 200,000 inhabitants and receive delegated prefectural functions.
