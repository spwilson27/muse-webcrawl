Nuclear fallout

[Image placeholder: Nuclear fallout]

Nuclear fallout is the residual radioactive material propelled into the upper atmosphere following a nuclear blast. It is so called because it "falls out" of the sky after the explosion and shock wave have passed. The radioactive material, often consisting of fission products mixed with vaporised soil and weapon debris, can spread over large areas and contaminate land, water and food chains.

Types and effects

Local fallout, deposited within hours near the burst, consists of larger particles and is intensely radioactive; global fallout, from small particles carried into the stratosphere, returns to earth over months or years at diluted levels. Exposure causes acute radiation sickness and increases long-term cancer risk. Contamination of pasture by iodine-131 and of the wider environment by caesium-137 and strontium-90 are major concerns.

Protection

Civil defence guidance emphasises distance, shielding by mass, and time, since fallout radiation decays rapidly — roughly following the "seven-ten rule", in which for every sevenfold increase in time after detonation there is a tenfold decrease in dose rate.
