Paphos

Paphos (Greek: Πάφος) is a coastal city in southwest Cyprus and the capital of the Paphos District. In classical antiquity, two locations were called Paphos: Old Paphos (Palaipaphos), at modern Kouklia, and New Paphos, about 12 km west, founded around 320 BC. Paphos is intimately associated with the goddess Aphrodite, whose legendary birthplace — Petra tou Romiou ("Aphrodite's Rock") — lies nearby, and whose great sanctuary at Old Paphos was famous throughout the ancient world.

History

Paphos appears in inscriptions from the 12th century BC and was inhabited in Mycenaean times. New Paphos became the island's capital under the Roman administration; the Apostle Paul visited the city (Acts 13), where the proconsul Sergius Paulus converted and the magician Elymas was struck blind. The city declined after earthquakes in late antiquity, was raided in the Arab-Byzantine wars, and passed through Byzantine, Lusignan, Venetian, and Ottoman hands before becoming part of modern Cyprus.

Archaeology and heritage

The whole of Paphos has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1980. Its treasures include the Tombs of the Kings (a large Hellenistic necropolis carved from rock), the Paphos Archaeological Park with Roman villas (Houses of Dionysos, Theseus, Aion, and Orpheus) celebrated for their mosaic floors depicting mythological scenes, the Odeon, the Byzantine castle at the harbour (rebuilt by the Ottomans), and the Chrysopolitissa church complex with St Paul's Pillar.

Modern city

Today Paphos is a major tourist resort, served by Paphos International Airport, with a permanent population of roughly 36,000 in the town and around 90,000 in its urban area. It shared the title of European Capital of Culture in 2017.
