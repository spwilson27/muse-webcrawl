Endowment

An endowment is a donation of money or property to a nonprofit organisation which uses the resulting investment income for a specific purpose. In the United States and elsewhere, the term most commonly refers to a permanent fund held by institutions such as universities, hospitals, museums, religious bodies, and charitable foundations, where the principal is kept intact and only income — or a prudent portion of returns — is spent.

Types

  • True (permanent) endowments are donor-restricted: the principal must be held indefinitely, and spending is limited to income or an approved spending rate.
  • Term endowments may be spent after a period of time or an event specified by the donor.
  • Quasi-endowments are funds an institution's board designates to function like endowments; because they were not donor-restricted, the board may undesignate them.

Management and spending rules

Endowments are typically invested in diversified portfolios of equities, bonds, real estate, and alternative assets, aiming for long-run growth that preserves purchasing power after spending and inflation. Many institutions adopt spending rules of roughly 4–5 percent of a moving average of market value. In the US, the Uniform Prudent Management of Institutional Funds Act (UPMIFA) guides prudent spending from donor-restricted funds.

University endowments

The largest endowments belong to wealthy universities: Harvard University's endowment exceeded fifty billion US dollars in the 2020s, followed by other major American research universities and by Oxford and Cambridge colleges in the United Kingdom. Endowment income funds professorships (endowed chairs), scholarships, libraries, and research, giving institutions a measure of independence from tuition and annual fundraising. Critics sometimes question accumulation by very wealthy institutions and have proposed minimum payout requirements or taxes on large endowments.

Historical and religious forms

Endowed benefactions are ancient: the Islamic waqf dedicated assets in perpetuity for charitable or religious purposes, and medieval European colleges and chantries were supported by landed endowments.
