Alexandru Ioan Cuza

Alexandru Ioan Cuza (20 March 1820 – 15 May 1873) was the first domnitor (ruler) of the United Principalities of Moldavia and Wallachia, the state that became modern Romania. His double election in 1859 united the two principalities, and his reign brought sweeping reforms.

Reign1859–1866
Born20 March 1820, Bârlad, Moldavia
Died15 May 1873, Heidelberg, Germany

Double election and unification

In January 1859, assemblies in both Moldavia and Wallachia elected Cuza as their prince, exploiting a loophole in the Paris Convention of 1858. The Great Powers eventually recognized the union, and in 1862 the two principalities formally merged into a single state, Romania, with Bucharest as its capital.

Reforms

With his prime minister Mihail Kogălniceanu, Cuza enacted major reforms: secularization of monastic estates (1863), a land reform freeing peasants from feudal dues (1864), a new Civil Code and Penal Code based on French models, compulsory free primary education, and the founding of the universities of Iași (1860) and Bucharest (1864). His increasingly authoritarian methods — including a 1864 coup that expanded his powers — alienated conservatives and liberals alike.

Abdication and death

In February 1866 a coalition of opponents forced Cuza to abdicate. He spent the rest of his life in exile in Austria, Italy and Germany, dying in Heidelberg in 1873.
