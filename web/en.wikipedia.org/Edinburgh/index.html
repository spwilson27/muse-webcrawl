Edinburgh (Scots: Edinburgh; Scottish Gaelic: Dùn Èideann) is the capital city of Scotland and one of its 32 council areas. The city is located in southeast Scotland and is bounded to the north by the Firth of Forth, an estuary of the North Sea. It is the second-most populous city in Scotland, after Glasgow, and the seventh-most populous city in the United Kingdom.

Recognised as the capital of Scotland since at least the 15th century, Edinburgh is the seat of the Scottish Government, the Scottish Parliament, and the highest courts in Scotland. The Palace of Holyroodhouse is the official residence of the monarch in Scotland. The city has long been a centre of education, particularly in medicine, Scots law, literature, philosophy, the sciences and engineering. The University of Edinburgh, founded in 1582, is one of Scotland's ancient universities.

History

The area around Castle Rock has been inhabited since at least the Bronze Age. Edinburgh became the royal capital under the early Scottish kings, and the Old Town grew along the Royal Mile between Edinburgh Castle and Holyrood Abbey. The city suffered in the wars with England — it was burned in 1544 during the "Rough Wooing" — but flourished after the Union of the Crowns (1603) and retained its legal and religious institutions after the Union of Parliaments in 1707. In the 18th century it was a powerhouse of the Scottish Enlightenment, home to David Hume and Adam Smith, and the elegant Georgian New Town was built north of the old ridge. The Old and New Towns together were listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995.

Culture

Edinburgh is famous for its festivals, which together represent the largest annual performing-arts gathering in the world: the Edinburgh International Festival and the Fringe (begun 1947), the Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo, and festivals of film, books, jazz and science. Landmarks include Edinburgh Castle, the Royal Mile, St Giles' Cathedral, Arthur's Seat — the extinct volcano that rises above Holyrood Park — Calton Hill, and the Scott Monument. Literary associations span Walter Scott, Robert Louis Stevenson, Arthur Conan Doyle and J. K. Rowling.

The city is also a major financial centre — the second largest in the UK — and a hub for tourism, technology and higher education.
