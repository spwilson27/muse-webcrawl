James Marcus Schuyler (November 9, 1923 – April 12, 1991) was an American poet, a central figure of the New York School of poets. He received the Pulitzer Prize for Poetry in 1981 for his book The Morning of the Poem.

Life

Schuyler was born in Chicago and grew up in East Aurora, New York. He attended Bethany College in West Virginia, served in the US Navy during the Second World War, and lived for two years in Italy on Ischia, where he worked for a time as W. H. Auden's secretary. In 1947 he moved to New York, taking a job at the Museum of Modern Art and becoming close to the painters Jane Freilicher and Fairfield Porter and to poets John Ashbery, Frank O'Hara, Kenneth Koch and Barbara Guest — the circle that formed the New York School.

Work

Schuyler wrote art criticism for Art News and served as a curator at the Museum of Modern Art. With John Ashbery he wrote the comic novel A Nest of Ninnies (published 1969). His poetry books include Freely Espousing (1969), The Crystal Lithium (1972), Hymn to Life (1974) and The Morning of the Poem (1980), whose long title poem is regarded as his masterpiece. Schuyler's poems are conversational, diaristic and attentive to weather, light, gardens and the passing of days, often beginning "Today" or with a precise observation of the scene outside a window.

Later life and legacy

Schuyler struggled for much of his life with bipolar disorder and periods of hospitalization and poverty, supported by friends and grants. He died of a stroke in Manhattan in 1991. His Collected Poems (1993) and the Selected Prose confirmed his place among the major American poets of the second half of the twentieth century.
