Maotai

Type: Baijiu (sorghum spirit)
Origin: Maotai town, Guizhou, China
Alcohol: ~53% ABV (classic bottling)
Producer: Kweichow Moutai Co. Ltd.

Maotai, or Moutai, is a style of baijiu — a strong distilled spirit made from fermented sorghum — produced in the town of Maotai in Guizhou province, China. The best-known producer, Kweichow Moutai, is one of the most valuable liquor companies in the world, and its spirit is traditionally served at Chinese state banquets.

Production

Maotai is the archetype of "sauce-aroma" (jiangxiang) baijiu. Local red sorghum and wheat-based starter are fermented in stone pits and distilled repeatedly over a year-long cycle of steaming, stacking and fermentation, then blended and aged for several years before bottling. The microclimate and river water of the Chishui valley in Maotai town are said to make the style impossible to reproduce identically elsewhere.

History

Distilling in the area is centuries old; the spirit gained international exposure at the 1915 Panama-Pacific Exposition in San Francisco. After 1949 local distilleries were consolidated into the state-owned Moutai distillery. It famously featured in diplomacy — served to visiting leaders including Richard Nixon in 1972 — and bottles, especially aged ones, are prized as gifts and investments in China.

Characteristics

Classic maotai is bottled at 53% alcohol by volume, with a complex savoury, fermented aroma often compared to soy sauce, and a long, warm finish.
