Pagani Zonda

The Pagani Zonda is a mid-engine sports car produced by the Italian manufacturer Pagani in San Cesario sul Panaro, near Modena. It debuted at the 1999 Geneva Motor Show, and was the first production car of the company founded by Horacio Pagani. The car was named after the zonda wind, a dry, warm föhn wind that blows over the Andes in Argentina, Pagani's country of birth.

Design and construction

The Zonda's chassis is built around a central carbon-fibre monocoque with chrome-molybdenum subframes, with bodywork largely in carbon fibre. All production Zondas were powered by naturally aspirated Mercedes-AMG V12 engines, displacing between 6.0 and 7.3 litres. The quad-exhaust, centre-mounted circular tailpipes and twin-canopy roofline became the model's signature.

Models

The original Zonda C12 (1999) was followed by the C12-S, the Zonda F (2005, named in honour of Juan Manuel Fangio), the track-only Zonda R (2009), and the Zonda Cinque (2009), limited to five coupés and five roadsters. Production of standard models ended in the early 2010s as the Huayra replaced the Zonda, but numerous one-off and "760-series" bespoke cars continued to be built for individual customers for years afterwards.

Performance

Later variants produced up to 760 PS (559 kW; 750 hp), with 0–100 km/h times around 2.7–3.4 seconds and top speeds above 350 km/h (217 mph). The Zonda R lapped the Nürburgring Nordschleife in 6:47 in 2010, then a record for production-derived cars.
