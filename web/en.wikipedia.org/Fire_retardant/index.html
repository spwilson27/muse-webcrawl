Fire retardant

A fire retardant is a substance that is used to slow down or stop the spread of fire or reduce its intensity. This is commonly accomplished by chemical reactions that reduce the flammability of fuels or delay their combustion. Fire retardants may also cool the fuel through physical action or endothermic chemical reactions. They are available as powder, to be mixed with water, as fire-fighting foams, fire-retardant gels, coatings and sprays, and as additives incorporated into manufactured materials such as plastics, textiles and timber.

Wildland firefighting

Long-term retardants dropped from air tankers — most recognisably the red-orange slurries typified by Phos-Chek — consist mainly of water mixed with ammonium phosphate fertiliser salts, a colouring agent such as iron oxide, and thickeners. Unlike water alone, which evaporates quickly, the retardant remains effective after the water has gone: the phosphate salts alter the way vegetation decomposes when heated, promoting the formation of char instead of flammable gases and thereby slowing a fire's advance. Retardant is typically dropped ahead of an approaching wildfire rather than directly onto flames, to create a treated fuel break.

Flame retardants in materials

Flame retardants added to furnishings, electronics and building materials work by several mechanisms: endothermic decomposition that absorbs heat, formation of a protective char layer, dilution of flammable gases, and gas-phase radical quenching by halogenated compounds. Some older brominated flame retardants have been restricted because of persistence and toxicity concerns, driving a shift toward phosphorus-based and mineral alternatives such as aluminium hydroxide.

Environmental concerns

Because aerial retardants are fertiliser-based, their use near waterways is restricted: the ammonia and phosphate load can harm aquatic life, and retardant exclusion zones are normally observed around lakes and streams. Fire agencies argue the ecological cost of an uncontrolled wildfire generally exceeds that of retardant use.
