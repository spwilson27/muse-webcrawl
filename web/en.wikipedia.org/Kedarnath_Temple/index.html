Kedarnath Temple

Kedarnath Temple is a Hindu temple dedicated to Shiva, located in the town of Kedarnath in the Rudraprayag district of Uttarakhand, India, at an altitude of 3,583 metres in the Garhwal Himalayas near the Mandakini River. It is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and one of the four sites of the Char Dham pilgrimage circuit.

Legend and history

Tradition associates the temple with the Pandavas of the Mahabharata, who are said to have sought Shiva's forgiveness here after the Kurukshetra war; Shiva, evading them in the form of a buffalo, is worshipped in the temple as a conical rock formation representing the buffalo's hump. The temple is traditionally said to have been built by the philosopher-saint Adi Shankara in the 8th century CE; his samadhi is located behind the temple. Built of massive interlocking grey stone slabs without mortar, the temple stands on a high platform and remains open only from late April to October because of snow.

2013 floods

In June 2013, catastrophic floods and landslides devastated the Kedarnath valley, killing thousands in the region. The temple itself survived almost intact, protected in part by a huge boulder that came to rest behind it — now called the Bhim Shila — although the surrounding town was destroyed. Pilgrimage resumed the following year after major reconstruction of paths and facilities.
