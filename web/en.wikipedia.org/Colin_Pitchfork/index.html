Colin Pitchfork

Colin Pitchfork (born 23 March 1960) is a British double murderer and rapist. He was the first person convicted of murder based on DNA fingerprinting evidence, and the first to be caught as a result of mass DNA screening. He raped and murdered two schoolgirls, Lynda Mann in 1983 and Dawn Ashworth in 1986, in Leicestershire, England.

The investigation

After the second murder, police arrested a local teenager, Richard Buckland, who confessed to one of the killings. Geneticist Alec Jeffreys had recently developed DNA profiling at the University of Leicester; testing showed Buckland was innocent — the first person exonerated by DNA evidence — and that both murders were committed by the same man. Police then undertook a mass screening ("the blooding") of some 5,000 local men. Pitchfork persuaded a colleague, Ian Kelly, to give a sample in his place, but the deception was overheard in a pub and reported. Pitchfork was arrested in September 1987, confessed, and in January 1988 was sentenced to life imprisonment with a minimum term of 30 years, later reduced to 28.

Release and recall

Pitchfork was released on licence in September 2021 after the Parole Board judged him suitable for release, a decision that drew public and political criticism. He was recalled to prison in November 2021 for breaching licence conditions by approaching young women. His later bids for release were rejected, with a 2024 rehearing ordered after the Parole Board's earlier decision was found to require reconsideration; he remained in custody pending the outcome.
