Publishing is the activity of making information, literature, music, software and other content available to the public for sale or for free. Traditionally, the term refers to the creation and distribution of printed works, such as books, newspapers, and magazines; with the advent of digital information systems, publishing has expanded to include digital works such as ebooks, digital journals and websites.

Stages of publishing

A publisher typically acquires the rights to a work from its author, then manages editing, design, typesetting, production, marketing and distribution. Authors may be paid an advance against royalties. In academic publishing, manuscripts undergo peer review before acceptance.

History

Publishing became possible with the invention of writing and became more widespread with printing. The movable-type press of Johannes Gutenberg in the fifteenth century sharply reduced costs and spread literacy. Copyright law, beginning with the Statute of Anne in 1710, gave authors and publishers defined rights in their works.

Sectors

The industry is usually divided into trade (fiction and general non-fiction), educational, academic and professional publishing, together with newspaper and magazine publishing and music publishing. The largest trade publishers are sometimes called the "Big Five": Penguin Random House, Hachette, HarperCollins, Macmillan and Simon & Schuster.

Digital publishing and self-publishing

Digital printing, print-on-demand and online retail have lowered the barriers to entry, enabling widespread self-publishing and the growth of small independent presses. Open access models have reshaped scholarly publishing, shifting costs from readers to authors or funders.
