Clock

A clock is a device that measures and displays the time. The clock is one of the oldest human inventions, meeting the need to measure intervals of time shorter than the natural units such as the day, the lunar month and the year. Devices operating on several physical processes have been used over the millennia.

History

Early timekeeping devices included sundials, water clocks, candle clocks and hourglasses. The first mechanical clocks, using the verge escapement, appeared in Europe around 1300 and were installed in towers and monasteries. The invention of the mainspring in the 15th century allowed portable clocks and then watches; Christiaan Huygens' pendulum clock of 1656 dramatically improved accuracy, and the balance spring did the same for watches. Electric clocks arrived in the 19th century, quartz clocks in 1927, and atomic clocks — which now define the second — in the 1950s.

Types

Clocks are classified by how they keep time (pendulum, balance wheel, quartz crystal, atomic transitions), how they display it (analogue hands or digital digits), and their purpose, from tower clocks and marine chronometers to alarm clocks, stopwatches and the system clocks inside computers.

Cultural role

Standardised time, driven by railways and telegraphs in the 19th century, made the clock central to industrial and modern life; time zones and Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) are maintained today using ensembles of atomic clocks.
