John Michael "Ozzy" Osbourne (3 December 1948 – 22 July 2025) was an English singer, songwriter, and media personality. He rose to prominence during the 1970s as the lead vocalist of the heavy metal band Black Sabbath, during which period he adopted the nickname "Prince of Darkness".

Career

Osbourne co-founded Black Sabbath in 1968 and sang on landmark albums including Paranoid (1970) and Master of Reality (1971), helping to define heavy metal. Dismissed from the band in 1979, he launched a successful solo career with Blizzard of Ozz (1980) and Diary of a Madman (1981), working with guitarist Randy Rhoads. He later founded the Ozzfest touring festival.

Media fame and later life

In the 2000s, Osbourne became a reality television star through MTV's The Osbournes, featuring his wife and manager Sharon and their children. He reunited with Black Sabbath on several occasions, including a final performance in Birmingham in July 2025, weeks before his death. Osbourne had been diagnosed with Parkinson's disease, which he disclosed publicly in 2020.
