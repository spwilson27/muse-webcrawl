Premier of China

The premier of China, officially the Premier of the State Council of the People's Republic of China, is the head of government of China and leader of the State Council. The premier is nominally the second most powerful position in China's political system after the general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party and president, and is formally appointed by the president upon nomination, subject to approval by the National People's Congress.

Role and powers

The premier directs the work of the State Council, China's chief administrative authority and cabinet, presiding over its executive meetings and overseeing the ministries, the People's Bank of China and the National Audit Office. The premier is chiefly responsible for economic policy and the day-to-day administration of the state, while the paramount leader — conventionally the CCP general secretary — sets overall political direction. The premier serves a five-year term, renewable once.

History

The office was created with the founding of the People's Republic in 1949, with Zhou Enlai as its first holder, serving until his death in 1976. Subsequent premiers were Hua Guofeng, Zhao Ziyang, Li Peng, Zhu Rongji, Wen Jiabao and Li Keqiang. Li Qiang became premier in March 2023. Historically the premier has been one of the top two figures in the party leadership, ranked second in the Politburo Standing Committee.

Residence and workplace

The premier works at Zhongnanhai in Beijing, the headquarters of the party and State Council, adjacent to the Forbidden City.
