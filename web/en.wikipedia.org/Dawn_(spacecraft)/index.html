Dawn (spacecraft)

Dawn was a space probe launched by NASA in September 2007 with the mission of studying two large bodies in the asteroid belt: Vesta and Ceres. It was the first spacecraft to orbit two extraterrestrial bodies, the first to visit either Vesta or Ceres, and the first to orbit a dwarf planet.

Managed by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, Dawn used three xenon ion thrusters, whose gentle but persistent thrust allowed it to match velocities with its targets over an eleven-year journey of some 6.9 billion kilometres. It entered orbit around Vesta on 16 July 2011, surveyed the giant asteroid for fourteen months, then departed for Ceres, arriving on 6 March 2015.

Discoveries

At Vesta, Dawn confirmed it as a differentiated protoplanet with a basaltic surface and a huge impact basin at its south pole. At Ceres, it found bright salt deposits in Occator Crater, evidence of past cryovolcanic activity at Ahuna Mons, and widespread water ice and organic material, reshaping understanding of the largest object in the asteroid belt.

The mission ended on 1 November 2018 when the spacecraft exhausted its hydrazine fuel and lost the ability to point its antenna at Earth; it remains in a stable orbit around Ceres.
