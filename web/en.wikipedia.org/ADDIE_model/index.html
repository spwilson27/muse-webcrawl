ADDIE model

The ADDIE model is a framework used by instructional designers and training developers for building educational and training programs. Its name is an acronym for the five phases it defines: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation. It is one of the best-known instructional systems design (ISD) models.

History

The framework was developed in the 1970s at Florida State University's Center for Educational Technology under a contract with the United States Army, as part of work on interservice procedures for instructional systems development. The original formulation contained many more steps organized within the five phases; the simplified five-phase version became widely adopted in corporate training and education.

Phases

  • Analysis – identify the learning problem, the audience, goals, and constraints.
  • Design – specify learning objectives, assessment instruments, exercises, and content sequencing.
  • Development – create and assemble the instructional materials and media.
  • Implementation – deliver the course or training to learners.
  • Evaluation – formative evaluation at each phase and summative evaluation of the finished product.

Criticism

Critics have described ADDIE as overly linear and waterfall-like, arguing that real instructional design is iterative. Defenders respond that ADDIE is intended as a flexible guideline rather than a rigid sequence.
