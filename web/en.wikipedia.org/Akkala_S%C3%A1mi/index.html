Akkala Sámi
RegionKola Peninsula, Russia

FamilyUralic > Sámi > Eastern Sámi

Akkala Sámi was a Sámi language spoken in the Sámi villages of Aʼkel, Čuʼkksuâl and Sââʼrvesjäuʼrr, in the inland parts of the Kola Peninsula in Russia. It was the easternmost of the Sámi languages and was closely related to Skolt Sámi, though once thought to be a dialect of Kildin Sámi.

Extinction

The last known native speaker, Marja Sergina, died on 29 December 2003, and Akkala Sámi is regarded as extinct. However, a small number of people retain some knowledge of the language, and there is documentary material: a chapter of a Russian translation of the Gospel of Matthew was published in Akkala Sámi in 1897, and linguists recorded vocabulary and texts during the twentieth century. The language was written in the Cyrillic script.
