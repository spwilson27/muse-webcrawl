The Bombardier BiLevel Coach is a bilevel (double-deck) commuter rail passenger car originally designed by Hawker Siddeley Canada for Ontario's GO Transit and built from 1978 onward by Hawker Siddeley, then the Urban Transportation Development Corporation, and later Bombardier Transportation. Recognizable by its distinctive octagonal cross-section, it became one of the most widely used commuter rail cars in North America.

Design

The BiLevel has two full passenger decks connected by short staircases at the ends, with a mezzanine level at the doors. Its aluminum body and tapered profile were designed to fit restricted clearances in the Toronto area while nearly doubling capacity over a single-level car; later generations added crash energy management features, accessible seating, and updated interiors. Cars are built as coaches, cab cars with an operating cab for push-pull service, and accessible cars.

Operators

More than a thousand BiLevel cars have been built for agencies including GO Transit (the largest fleet), Metrolink and Coaster in California, Sounder in Seattle, West Coast Express in Vancouver, Tri-Rail in Florida, FrontRunner in Utah, ACE and other U.S. and Canadian commuter railroads, and Montreal's Exo. Following Alstom's acquisition of Bombardier Transportation in 2021, production and support of the design continue under Alstom.
