Gregory Peck

Eldred Gregory Peck (April 5, 1916 – June 12, 2003) was an American actor. One of the most popular film stars from the 1940s to the 1970s, he starred in numerous major films including Alfred Hitchcock's Spellbound (1945), The Yearling (1946), Gentleman's Agreement (1947), Twelve O'Clock High (1949), Roman Holiday (1953) opposite Audrey Hepburn, The Guns of Navarone (1961) and Cape Fear (1962).

Peck in his Academy Award-winning role as Atticus Finch

Career and honours

Peck won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his portrayal of the principled lawyer Atticus Finch in To Kill a Mockingbird (1962), a role the American Film Institute later named the greatest hero in American film. Known for playing characters of moral integrity, he was also active off screen: he served as president of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, helped found the American Film Institute, and supported liberal political causes. President Lyndon B. Johnson awarded him the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1969. Peck continued acting into the 1990s and died in Los Angeles in 2003 at the age of 87.
