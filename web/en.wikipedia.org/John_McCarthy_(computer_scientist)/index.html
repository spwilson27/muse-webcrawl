John McCarthy (September 4, 1927 – October 24, 2011) was an American computer scientist and cognitive scientist, one of the founders of the discipline of artificial intelligence. He co-authored the proposal for the 1956 Dartmouth workshop that launched the field, coined the term "artificial intelligence", and invented the Lisp programming language.

Career

McCarthy studied mathematics at Caltech and Princeton, then worked at Dartmouth, MIT and, from 1962, Stanford University, where he founded the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (SAIL) and remained a professor until 2000.

Contributions

He developed Lisp (1958–60), still influential today, introduced garbage collection and time-sharing concepts, advocated of mathematical logic in AI, and developed the situation calculus and circumscription for reasoning about actions and common sense.

Honours

McCarthy received the Turing Award in 1971 for his contributions to artificial intelligence, as well as the Kyoto Prize and the National Medal of Science.
