Thomas W. Lawson

BornFebruary 26, 1857, Charlestown, Massachusetts, United States
DiedFebruary 7, 1925 (aged 67), Boston, Massachusetts, United States
OccupationFinancier, author, stock promoter
Known forFrenzied Finance (1905); the schooner Thomas W. Lawson

Thomas William Lawson (February 26, 1857 – February 7, 1925) was an American businessman and writer, a flamboyant Boston financier and stock promoter who became one of the most notorious figures of early twentieth-century American high finance. Having made and lost several fortunes in copper stocks, he turned muckraker with his best-selling expose Frenzied Finance (1905), which described the manipulations of the Amalgamated Copper Company and the "System" of interlocking financial interests, and was serialised in Everybody's Magazine.

Career

Lawson began work as an office boy at 12 and speculated in stocks as a teenager. In the 1890s he became associated with Henry H. Rogers of Standard Oil in the promotion of copper properties, including the Butte & Boston and Amalgamated Copper ventures, whose collapse cost small investors millions and turned Lawson against his former partners. He built the vast Cape Cod estate "Dreamwold", bred horses and dogs, wrote poetry and novels such as Friday, the Thirteenth (1907), credited with popularising the superstition, and ran for the United States Senate.

The schooner

Lawson is also remembered as the namesake of the Thomas W. Lawson (1902), the only seven-masted schooner ever built and one of the largest sailing vessels in history. The steel-hulled ship proved unwieldy and was wrecked off the Isles of Scilly in December 1907 with heavy loss of life, her cargo of oil causing an early major oil spill.
