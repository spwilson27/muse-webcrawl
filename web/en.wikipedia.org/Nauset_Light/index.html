Nauset Light

Nauset Light

Location: Eastham, Massachusetts

First lit: 1877 (current tower 1923)

Tower height: 48 feet (15 m)

Nauset Light, officially Nauset Beach Light, is a lighthouse in Eastham, Massachusetts, within the Cape Cod National Seashore. The red-and-white tower is one of the most recognizable landmarks on Cape Cod and appears as the emblem on Massachusetts' Cape Cod and Islands license plate.

History

The station dates to 1838, when three brick towers — the "Three Sisters" — were built to distinguish the light from others on the coast. Coastal erosion repeatedly threatened the station; the original towers were replaced and relocated over the years. The present cast-iron tower was moved to Nauset in 1923 from Chatham, where it had served as one of a twin pair. In 1996, with the tower standing only about 35 feet from the edge of the eroding cliff, it was moved back roughly 300 feet in a widely watched operation.

Today

The lighthouse remains an active aid to navigation maintained by the U.S. Coast Guard, while the Nauset Light Preservation Society cares for the tower and offers seasonal tours. The nearby Three Sisters towers are preserved in a wooded area a short walk away.
