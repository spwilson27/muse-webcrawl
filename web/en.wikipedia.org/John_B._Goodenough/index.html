John Bannister Goodenough (25 July 1922 – 25 June 2023) was an American materials scientist and solid-state physicist. He is credited with key breakthroughs in the development of the lithium-ion battery and shared the 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with M. Stanley Whittingham and Akira Yoshino. At 97, he was the oldest person to receive a Nobel Prize.

Early life and career

Born in Jena, Germany, to American parents, Goodenough grew up in New England, attended Phillips Exeter Academy and Yale University, served as a meteorologist in the U.S. Army Air Forces during the Second World War, and earned a PhD in physics from the University of Chicago in 1952. At MIT's Lincoln Laboratory he worked on magnetic memory, contributing to the development of RAM, and formulated the Goodenough–Kanamori rules of superexchange in magnetic oxides.

Lithium-ion battery work

At the University of Oxford from 1976, his group — building on Whittingham's lithium intercalation concept — demonstrated in 1980 that lithium cobalt oxide (LiCoO2) could serve as a high-voltage, stable cathode material, roughly doubling the voltage of Whittingham's titanium disulfide design and making practical rechargeable lithium-ion batteries possible. His group later developed manganese spinel and lithium iron phosphate cathodes, the latter central to many modern electric-vehicle batteries. Sony commercialised the lithium-ion battery in 1991.

Later years

Goodenough moved to the University of Texas at Austin in 1986, where he remained an active professor and researcher into his late nineties, working on solid-state and glass batteries. He received the National Medal of Science, the Enrico Fermi Award and the Copley Medal (2019). He died in Austin in 2023, aged 100.
