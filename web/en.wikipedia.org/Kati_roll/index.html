Kati roll

A kati roll (also spelled kathi roll) is a street-food dish originating from Kolkata, West Bengal, India. In its original form, it consists of skewer-roasted kebab meat wrapped in a paratha bread and served with onions, chillies, and sauces.

History

The kati roll is generally credited to Nizam's restaurant in Kolkata, founded in 1932, which served kebabs to British colonial customers. According to popular account, busy office workers and colonial patrons who were reluctant to handle kebabs with their hands prompted the wrapping of the meat in paratha; the name kati derives from the Bengali word for the bamboo skewers (kathi) on which the meat was grilled — later replaced by iron skewers.

Preparation

The paratha is fried, often with a coating of beaten egg, then filled with grilled chicken, mutton, paneer, or other fillings, along with sliced onions, green chillies, lime juice, and sauces, and rolled in paper for eating by hand. Variations include double-egg and double-meat rolls, and vegetarian versions with potato or paneer tikka.

Spread

Kati rolls spread across India and to the Indian diaspora worldwide, becoming a staple of Indian fast food in cities such as New York, London, and Dubai, where they are sometimes marketed simply as "frankies" (the Mumbai variant created in the 1960s) or wraps.
