Battle of Posada

Battle of Posada
Date9–12 November 1330
LocationPosada, in the Carpathian foothills, present-day Romania
ResultWallachian victory
BelligerentsVoivodeship of Wallachia; Kingdom of Hungary
CommandersBasarab I; Charles I of Hungary

The Battle of Posada was fought between 9 and 12 November 1330 between the forces of Basarab I, voivode of Wallachia, and King Charles I of Hungary. It is one of the most celebrated victories in Romanian history and confirmed Wallachia's independence from Hungarian overlordship.

Charles, refusing Basarab's offers of submission and tribute, invaded Wallachia and captured Severin, but failed to bring the Wallachians to battle and began a retreat through the mountains of the southern Carpathians. In a narrow defile — traditionally identified with Posada — Basarab's men blocked the road and attacked the trapped Hungarian column from the heights with rocks, logs and arrows for three days. King Charles escaped only by exchanging his royal insignia with a loyal knight, who was killed in his place.

Significance

The disaster ended Hungarian attempts to reduce Wallachia to vassalage for a generation. Basarab, known as "the Founder" (Întemeietorul), consolidated the first independent Romanian principality, and the battle is famously depicted in the fourteenth-century Illuminated Chronicle.
