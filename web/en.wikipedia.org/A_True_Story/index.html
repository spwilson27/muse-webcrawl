A True Story

AuthorLucian of Samosata
LanguageAncient Greek
GenreSatire, proto-science fiction
Date2nd century AD

A True Story (Ancient Greek: Ἀληθῆ διηγήματα, Alēthē diēgēmata) is a prose work by the Syrian-Greek writer Lucian of Samosata, composed in the second century AD. It is a parody of the fantastic travel tales then popular in Greek literature, in which narrators claimed to report journeys to distant and marvellous lands. Lucian opens by openly announcing that everything he is about to relate is false, and that the only true statement in the work is that he is lying.

Plot

The narrator and a company of sailors set out from the Pillars of Heracles into the Atlantic Ocean. Their ship is caught by a whirlwind and carried up to the Moon, where they become involved in a war between the King of the Moon and the King of the Sun over the colonisation of Venus. The armies include fantastic creatures such as horse-vultures, flea-archers and giant spiders that weave webs across the sky.

After further adventures — being swallowed by a gigantic whale in whose belly whole peoples live, visiting the Island of the Blessed where they meet Homer and other heroes, and encountering the damned on the Isle of Wicked Men — the travellers eventually return to Earth. Lucian ends by promising a sequel that was never written.

Significance

Because of its voyages to the Moon and Venus, battles between alien peoples and artificial creatures, A True Story is frequently described as one of the earliest works of science fiction, as well as a foundational example of the parody and the tall tale. It has influenced later fantastic voyages including Jonathan Swift's Gulliver's Travels and the adventures of Baron Munchausen.
