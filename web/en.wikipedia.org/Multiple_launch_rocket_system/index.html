Multiple launch rocket system
TypeSelf-propelled rocket artillery
Notable examplesBM-21 Grad, M270 MLRS, HIMARS, BM-30 Smerch

A multiple launch rocket system (MLRS) is a type of rocket artillery system that contains multiple launchers which are fixed to a single platform, used to deliver a large volume of unguided or guided rockets simultaneously to saturate an area target. Rockets offer a higher payload-to-area effect than conventional tube artillery, though (for unguided rockets) with lower precision.

The first widely used modern MLRS was the Soviet BM-13 Katyusha of the Second World War, truck-mounted rails launching 132 mm rockets, feared by German troops for its shrieking salvoes. Cold War descendants include the ubiquitous BM-21 Grad (122 mm, 40 tubes) and the heavier BM-27 Uragan and BM-30 Smerch. In the West, the tracked M270 MLRS entered service in 1983 and introduced containerised rocket pods; its wheeled derivative, the M142 HIMARS, can fire the same family of GPS-guided GMLRS rockets, giving MLRS-class systems precision-strike capability at ranges of 70 km and beyond. Precision-guided rockets, and tactical ballistic missiles such as ATACMS launched from the same platforms, have blurred the line between rocket artillery and missile forces.

Characteristics

MLRS platforms emphasise mobility — "shoot and scoot" — to avoid counter-battery fire, since firing produces a large smoke and dust signature. Reloading is typically by crane with pre-packaged pods. Cluster-munition warheads were common during the Cold War but have been restricted by the 2008 Convention on Cluster Munitions for many states, pushing development toward unitary and alternative warheads.
