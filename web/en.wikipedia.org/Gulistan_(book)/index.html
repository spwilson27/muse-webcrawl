Gulistan (book)

The Gulistan (Persian: Golestān, "The Rose Garden") is a landmark of Persian literature written in 1258 by the poet Saadi Shirazi. It is one of the most widely read books in the Persian-speaking world and, through translation, in the West.

Contents

The book consists of stories and anecdotes in prose, interspersed with short poems, organised into eight chapters on subjects including the manners of kings, the character of dervishes, the excellence of contentment, the benefits of silence, love and youth, weakness and old age, education, and the rules of conduct in life. Saadi drew on three decades of travel across the Middle East, Central Asia and India following the Mongol invasions, and the tales mix practical wisdom, humour and moral reflection. Its companion work, the Bustan ("The Orchard"), was completed a year earlier and is written entirely in verse.

Influence

The Gulistan was the first Persian work translated into a Western language (Latin, 1651), and a line from it — "Human beings are members of a whole…" — adorns the entrance of the United Nations headquarters in New York. For centuries it served as a standard textbook for learners of Persian from India to the Ottoman Empire.
