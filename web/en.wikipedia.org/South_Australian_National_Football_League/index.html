South Australian National Football League

The South Australian National Football League (SANFL) is an Australian rules football league based in Adelaide, South Australia. Founded in 1877 as the South Australian Football Association, it is the oldest surviving football league of any code in Australia and one of the oldest in the world. Before the Australian Football League (AFL) became a national competition, the SANFL was regarded as level with Victoria's VFL in standard.

History

The league's foundation clubs included South Adelaide and Port Adelaide; Port Adelaide went on to become its most successful club, winning 36 premierships before joining the AFL in 1997, while continuing to field a team in the SANFL for a period. Norwood, North Adelaide, Sturt, Glenelg, and West Torrens (later merged into Woodville-West Torrens) are among its storied clubs. In 2014 the league's headquarters moved to Adelaide Oval, where its grand finals are now played.

Competition today

The SANFL now functions as the premier state league in South Australia, with ten clubs including reserve teams of the AFL's Adelaide Crows and (until recently) Port Adelaide. It also administers junior and women's competitions, including the SANFL Women's League established in 2017. The league's best player each season receives the Magarey Medal, first awarded in 1898.
