Tablet computer

A tablet computer, commonly shortened to tablet, is a mobile device, typically with a mobile operating system and touchscreen display processing circuitry, and a rechargeable battery in a single, thin, flat package. Tablets, being computers, can do what other personal computers can do, but lack some input/output abilities they have in common with them; they generally feature a large touchscreen display and are operable without a keyboard or mouse.

History

The concept was anticipated by devices such as the GRiDPad (1989) and the pen-based systems of the 1990s, including Apple's Newton MessagePad. Microsoft promoted Windows-based "Tablet PCs" in the 2000s, but the category took off with the launch of Apple's iPad in 2010, followed by Android tablets from Samsung and others. E-readers such as the Amazon Kindle form a related class of reading-oriented tablets.

Types

Modern tablets include slate tablets, convertible and hybrid 2-in-1 devices with detachable keyboards, rugged tablets for field work, and dedicated graphics tablets used for digital drawing. Sizes generally range from about 7 to 13 inches, with larger professional models available.

Use

Tablets are used for media consumption, education, point-of-sale and healthcare documentation, aviation electronic flight bags, and creative work with stylus input.
