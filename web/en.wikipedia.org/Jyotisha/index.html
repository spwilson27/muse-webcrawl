TraditionHindu astrology and astronomy
ZodiacSidereal
Key textVedanga Jyotisha; Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra

Jyotiṣa (Sanskrit: ज्योतिष, "science of heavenly bodies") is the traditional Hindu system of astrology and astronomy. One of the six Vedangas, the auxiliary disciplines connected with the study of the Vedas, it was originally concerned with fixing the dates of sacrifices and calendrical rites by observing the Sun, Moon, and stars, and developed into a comprehensive system of horoscopic astrology.

Branches

Classical Jyotisha is divided into three branches (skandhas): siddhanta, mathematical astronomy; samhita, mundane astrology concerning weather, omens, and collective events; and hora, horoscopic astrology concerning individuals, which includes natal charts (jataka), electional astrology (muhurta) for choosing auspicious times, and interrogational astrology (prashna).

Elements of the system

Jyotisha uses the sidereal zodiac of twelve signs (rashi), adjusted for the precession of the equinoxes by the ayanamsa, in contrast to the tropical zodiac of Western astrology. Its distinctive features include the twenty-seven lunar mansions (nakshatra), the nine "planets" or navagraha (including the lunar nodes Rahu and Ketu), and the system of planetary periods (dashas), most famously the Vimshottari dasha of 120 years, used to time predicted life events. A birth chart, or kundali, maps the positions of these bodies at the moment and place of birth.

History

The earliest text is the Vedanga Jyotisha, dating to the late Vedic period. From the early centuries CE, Hellenistic astrological concepts were absorbed and naturalized in works such as the Yavanajataka, and the tradition reached classical form in the astronomical treatises of Aryabhata, Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita and Brihat Jataka, and the Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra attributed to the sage Parashara. Regional schools, notably in Kerala, developed distinctive techniques such as Jaimini astrology and prashna.

Practice today

Jyotisha remains widely practised in South Asia for marriage matching, naming children, setting dates for ceremonies and ventures, and personal consultation. Its astronomical component contributed significantly to the history of Indian mathematics and astronomy, while its astrological claims are regarded by scientists as pseudoscientific.
