Zygosity

Zygosity describes the degree of similarity of the alleles for a trait in an organism. An organism is homozygous for a gene when it has two identical alleles on the corresponding chromosomes, and heterozygous when the two alleles differ. The terms derive from the zygote formed at fertilisation.

Types

Homozygosity may be homozygous dominant (two copies of the dominant allele) or homozygous recessive. Females with two X chromosomes can be heterozygous or homozygous for X-linked genes, while males are hemizygous for most X-linked genes because they have only one copy. "Nullizygous" describes an organism missing both copies of a gene.

Significance

Zygosity determines how traits are expressed and inherited: recessive disorders such as cystic fibrosis manifest in homozygotes, while heterozygotes may be unaffected carriers. In twin studies, zygosity distinguishes monozygotic (identical) twins, who develop from one zygote, from dizygotic (fraternal) twins.
