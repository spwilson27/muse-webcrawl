Ross Bagdasarian
Born: January 27, 1919, Fresno, California
Died: January 16, 1972, Beverly Hills, California
Also known as: David Seville
Known for: Creating Alvin and the Chipmunks

Ross Bagdasarian (1919–1972), known by the stage name David Seville, was an American singer, songwriter, and record producer of Armenian descent. He created the fictional musical group Alvin and the Chipmunks.

Career

Bagdasarian served in the US Army during World War II and appeared in small film roles, including a part as a songwriter in Alfred Hitchcock's Rear Window (1954). He wrote "Come On-a My House" with his cousin, the author William Saroyan, which became a hit for Rosemary Clooney in 1951.

The Chipmunks

Experimenting with variable-speed tape recording, Bagdasarian had a 1958 novelty hit with "Witch Doctor," followed that year by "The Chipmunk Song (Christmas Don't Be Late)," which introduced Alvin, Simon, and Theodore — sped-up voices Bagdasarian performed himself. The song topped the American charts and won three Grammy Awards. The characters were developed into the animated series The Alvin Show (1961) and, after his death, into a long-running franchise managed by his son, Ross Bagdasarian Jr.
