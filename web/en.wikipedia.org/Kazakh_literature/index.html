Kazakh literature

Kazakh literature encompasses the oral and written literary traditions of the Kazakh people. For centuries it was dominated by oral epic poetry performed by bards known as zhyrau and akyns, while written literature in the modern sense developed from the nineteenth century.

Oral tradition

Kazakh oral literature includes heroic epics such as Koblandy Batyr, Er Targyn and the love epic Kozy Korpesh – Bayan Sulu, as well as the improvisational song contests called aitys. The wider Turkic epic cycle of Alpamysh is also part of the tradition.

Modern literature

Abai Kunanbaev (1845–1904), poet, composer and philosopher, is regarded as the founder of modern Kazakh written literature. In the twentieth century, novelists such as Mukhtar Auezov, author of the epic novel Abai Zholy (The Path of Abai), brought Kazakh prose to international attention.
