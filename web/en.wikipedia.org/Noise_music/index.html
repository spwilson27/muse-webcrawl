Noise music

Noise music is a genre of music that is characterised by the expressive use of noise. This type of music tends to challenge the distinction that is made in conventional musical practices between musical and non-musical sounds. Noise music includes a wide range of musical styles and sound-based creative practices that feature noise as a primary aspect.

History

Roots trace to Luigi Russolo's 1913 Futurist manifesto The Art of Noises and his intonarumori noise instruments, through musique concrète, John Cage's experiments, and the tape and feedback work of the 1960s avant-garde. In the late 1970s and 1980s, industrial music and the Japanese noise (Japanoise) scene — artists such as Merzbow, Hijokaidan and Incapacitants — established harsh noise as a distinct underground genre, often built from distortion, feedback, metal percussion and extreme volume.

Styles and practice

Subgenres include power electronics, harsh noise wall, noise rock and glitch. Performers frequently use contact microphones, circuit-bent electronics, prepared instruments and pedals rather than conventional composition, and releases have historically circulated through cassette culture and small independent labels.
