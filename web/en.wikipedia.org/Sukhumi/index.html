Sukhumi

Sukhumi (Georgian: სოხუმი, Sokhumi; Abkhaz: Аҟәа, Aqwa; Russian: Сухум, Sukhum) is the capital and largest city of Abkhazia, a disputed region on the eastern coast of the Black Sea. The city, which has a population of about 65,000, is recognised by most of the international community as part of Georgia, though it has been controlled by the separatist Republic of Abkhazia since 1993.

History

The site's history goes back to the 6th century BC, when Greek settlers from Miletus founded the colony of Dioscurias, an important trading port. Under Rome and then Byzantium the city was known as Sebastopolis. In the Ottoman period it was called Sukhum-Kale. The city became a popular resort in the Russian Empire and the Soviet Union, famed for its subtropical climate, beaches, and sanatoria.

War and aftermath

During the War in Abkhazia (1992–1993), Sukhumi was heavily damaged and its large Georgian population was expelled. The city has slowly recovered since, though many buildings remain ruined or abandoned. Russia recognised Abkhazia's independence in 2008, a move condemned by Georgia and most other states.

Geography and climate

Sukhumi lies in a wide bay on the Black Sea, backed by the Caucasus foothills. It has a humid subtropical climate with mild winters, and its botanical garden, founded in 1840, is one of the oldest in the Caucasus. The city is a rail terminus and home to Abkhazian State University and several research institutes, including the former primate research centre.
