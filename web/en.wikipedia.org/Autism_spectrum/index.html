The autism spectrum, often called autism spectrum disorder (ASD), is a group of neurodevelopmental conditions characterised by differences in social communication and interaction, alongside restricted or repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests or activities, and differences in sensory processing. The term "spectrum" reflects the wide variation in the type and severity of traits among autistic people.

History

The word "autism" was coined by the psychiatrist Eugen Bleuler in 1911. In 1943 Leo Kanner described "early infantile autism", and in 1944 Hans Asperger independently described a similar profile. The DSM-5, published in 2013, merged earlier separate diagnoses — autistic disorder, Asperger syndrome, childhood disintegrative disorder and PDD-NOS — into the single category of autism spectrum disorder, graded by levels of support needed.

Characteristics

Core characteristics include differences in social-emotional reciprocity, nonverbal communication and relationship development, together with repetitive motor movements, insistence on sameness, highly focused interests and hyper- or hypo-reactivity to sensory input. Traits typically appear in the first two years of life, although diagnosis may come later, particularly in girls and women.

Causes

ASD is highly heritable, with twin studies estimating heritability between roughly 60 and 90 per cent. Hundreds of genes have been associated with increased likelihood, and prenatal environmental factors may also contribute. There is no scientific evidence linking autism to vaccines.

Support and perspectives

There is no cure for autism, and many advocates in the neurodiversity movement regard it as a difference rather than a disorder requiring elimination. Supports focus on communication, education, occupational therapy and accommodations suited to the individual, with early intervention associated with better outcomes in adaptive skills.
