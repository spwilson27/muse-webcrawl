Artocarpus

Family: Moraceae

Genus: Artocarpus J.R.Forst. & G.Forst.

Species: About 60

Range: Southeast Asia, Pacific, South Asia

Artocarpus is a genus of approximately 60 species of trees and shrubs in the mulberry family, Moraceae, native to Asia and the Pacific. The name derives from the Greek words artos ("bread") and karpos ("fruit"). Several species bear edible fruit and are widely cultivated throughout the tropics.

Notable species

The best-known species is the breadfruit (Artocarpus altilis), a staple food across Oceania famously carried on HMS Bounty. The jackfruit (A. heterophyllus) produces the largest tree-borne fruit in the world, sometimes exceeding 50 kg, and is the national fruit of Bangladesh. Other cultivated species include the seeded breadnut (A. camansi), cempedak (A. integer), marang (A. odoratissimus) and lakoocha (A. lacucha).

Description

All Artocarpus species are laticiferous trees or shrubs whose damaged tissues exude milky latex. Leaves are often large and, in breadfruit, deeply lobed. The plants are typically monoecious, with separate male and female inflorescences; the female heads develop into large syncarpous multiple fruits.

Uses

Beyond food, Artocarpus species provide timber, traditional medicines, dyes and fibre. Because many species support wildlife and yield large harvests with little input, breadfruit in particular has been promoted for food security in tropical regions facing climate change.
