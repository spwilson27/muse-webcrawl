David Lance Arneson (/ˈɑːrnɪsən/; 1 October 1947 – 7 April 2009) was an American game designer best known for co-developing Dungeons & Dragons with Gary Gygax. Arneson's inventive play style, developed in his Blackmoor campaign in the Twin Cities, Minnesota, in the early 1970s, introduced the core concepts of role-playing: players each controlling a single character, a referee narrating an open-ended fantasy world, dungeon exploration, experience points and character advancement.

Blackmoor and the birth of role-playing

A miniature wargamer associated with the Midwest Military Simulation Association, Arneson experimented with David Wesely's Braunstein games and with fantasy wargaming using Chainmail. In 1971 he began the Blackmoor campaign, in which players explored a monster-filled castle dungeon as individual heroes rather than armies. He demonstrated the game to Gary Gygax in Lake Geneva, Wisconsin, and the two collaborated to turn Arneson's notes and house rules into a publishable product: Dungeons & Dragons, released by TSR in 1974.

Later career

Arneson worked briefly at TSR before leaving amid disputes over credit and royalties; lawsuits between Arneson and Gygax/TSR were settled out of court, and Arneson retained co-creator credit. He founded Adventure Games, published the Blackmoor supplements, wrote the role-playing game Adventures in Fantasy (1979), and later taught computer game design at Full Sail University in Florida. He was inducted into the Academy of Adventure Gaming Arts and Design Hall of Fame in 1984. The documentary Secrets of Blackmoor (2019) and Jon Peterson's The Elusive Shift (2020) reassessed his foundational role in the hobby.
