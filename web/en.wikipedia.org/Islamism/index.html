Islamism

Islamism, also often called political Islam, is a set of political ideologies holding that Islam should guide social and political life as well as personal life. Islamists generally seek the implementation of sharia, though movements differ widely — from participation in democratic elections to revolutionary violence.

History

Modern Islamism emerged in the early twentieth century in response to colonialism and the decline of Muslim political power. Hassan al-Banna founded the Muslim Brotherhood in Egypt in 1928; Abul A'la Maududi developed a parallel movement in British India and founded Jamaat-e-Islami; and Sayyid Qutb's writings later inspired more militant strands. The Iranian Revolution of 1979 established a Shia Islamist state under Ayatollah Khomeini.

Varieties

Mainstream Islamist parties have contested elections in countries including Turkey, Egypt, Tunisia, Morocco and Jordan. Salafi movements emphasise emulation of the earliest Muslims, while jihadist organisations such as al-Qaeda and the Islamic State (ISIS) have pursued global violence. Scholars debate the definition and boundaries of the term, and some Islamists prefer labels such as "Muslim democrats".
