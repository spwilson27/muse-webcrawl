Sedimentary basin

A sedimentary basin is a region of Earth's crust that has subsided over a long period, allowing thick accumulations of sediment to be deposited and preserved. Basin fills commonly reach several kilometres in thickness and record tens to hundreds of millions of years of Earth history.

Formation

Subsidence is driven by tectonic processes including lithospheric stretching (rifting), flexure of the lithosphere under loads such as mountain belts and sediment piles, and thermal contraction of formerly heated crust. Major basin types include rift basins, passive margin basins, foreland basins, forearc and back-arc basins, intracratonic basins, and strike-slip (pull-apart) basins.

Basin analysis

The study of sedimentary basins combines stratigraphy, structural geology, geophysics, and geochemistry. Basin analysis is central to petroleum geology, since most hydrocarbon accumulations occur in sedimentary basin fills where source rocks, reservoirs, seals, and traps coincide.

Examples

Major examples include the Permian Basin of Texas, the North Sea Basin, the Persian Gulf Basin, the Siberian basins, and the Great Artesian Basin of Australia.
