Video game remaster

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A video game remaster is an updated re-release of an existing video game that improves its presentation — typically graphics, resolution, frame rate, audio and performance — while keeping the underlying game design, content and code substantially the same. Remasters are usually produced for newer hardware than the original release.

Characteristics

Common improvements in a remaster include higher-resolution textures and models, modern rendering features, support for widescreen and 4K displays, faster loading, remastered soundtracks, quality-of-life fixes and bundled downloadable content. Examples include The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker HD, the Mass Effect Legendary Edition, Crash Bandicoot N. Sane Trilogy (marketed as a remaster-plus), and numerous "HD remaster" collections of PlayStation 2-era games for later consoles.

Remaster versus remake

A remaster is distinct from a video game remake, in which a game is rebuilt from scratch with new assets and often redesigned gameplay, as in Resident Evil 2 (2019) or Final Fantasy VII Remake. The boundary can blur: some releases marketed as remasters contain extensive new art, while others labelled remakes stay extremely faithful. A simple port, by contrast, adapts a game to new hardware with few enhancements, and enhanced ports sit between ports and remasters.

Industry role

Remasters are comparatively inexpensive to produce and let publishers monetize back catalogues, keep franchises visible between new entries, and introduce classic games to new audiences. Specialists such as Bluepoint Games and Nightdive Studios built reputations on high-quality remasters, and subscription and storefront services have made remastered classics a staple of digital catalogues.
