China and the World Trade Organization


China's accession to the WTO, 2001

China became a member of the World Trade Organization (WTO) on 11 December 2001, following a long process of negotiations that began in 1986 when China sought to resume its status as a contracting party to the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT). Accession required China to undertake extensive market-opening commitments, and it is generally regarded as a landmark in the country's integration into the global economy.

Accession negotiations

China's bid took over 15 years, spanning its economic reform programme and lengthy bilateral negotiations with the United States, the European Union and others. A pivotal US–China bilateral agreement was reached in November 1999, and the United States granted China permanent normal trade relations in 2000. The accession package was approved at the WTO's Doha ministerial conference in November 2001. China accepted a number of special provisions, including a transitional product-specific safeguard mechanism and a special review process, and agreed to significant liberalisation of trade in goods, agriculture and services, and to strengthen protection of intellectual property.

Economic impact

After accession, China's exports and foreign direct investment grew dramatically, and it became the world's largest exporter of goods by 2009 and its second-largest economy by 2010. Economists have studied the "China shock" — the rapid growth of imports from China — and its concentrated effects on manufacturing employment in the United States and other countries. Within China, accession is credited with anchoring domestic reforms, while debates continue over state subsidies, state-owned enterprises, currency practices and compliance with the spirit of China's commitments.

China in the WTO system

China has been an active participant in WTO dispute settlement, as both complainant and respondent, in cases concerning matters such as raw materials export restrictions, rare earths, and measures affecting intellectual property rights. Its treatment as a "non-market economy" in other members' anti-dumping investigations remained contentious after the 15th anniversary of accession in 2016.
