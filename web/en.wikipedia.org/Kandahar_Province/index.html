Kandahar (Pashto: کندهار) is one of the 34 provinces of Afghanistan, located in the southern part of the country. It borders Helmand to the west, Uruzgan to the north, Zabul to the east and Pakistan's Balochistan province to the south. Its capital is the city of Kandahar, the second-largest city in Afghanistan and the spiritual birthplace of the Taliban movement.

Geography

The province is largely arid plain and desert, crossed by the Arghandab River, whose valley around the capital supports orchards famous for pomegranates, grapes and melons. Agriculture and trade through the Spin Boldak border crossing with Pakistan are the mainstays of the provincial economy.

History

The Kandahar region, part of ancient Arachosia, was conquered by Alexander the Great, ruled by Indo-Greek, Kushan, Arab, Mongol and Persian empires, and became the first capital of Ahmad Shah Durrani, founder of modern Afghanistan, in 1747. The city resisted British forces in the Anglo-Afghan wars. During the Soviet–Afghan War the province was a centre of mujahideen resistance; it was here that Mullah Omar founded the Taliban in 1994. After 2001 Kandahar became the focus of some of the heaviest fighting of the NATO-led war, including major Canadian and American operations, before the Taliban retook the province in August 2021.

Demographics

The population is overwhelmingly ethnic Pashtun, principally of the Durrani and Ghilzai tribal confederations, with small Tajik, Hazara and Baloch minorities. Pashto is the dominant language and Sunni Islam the predominant religion.
