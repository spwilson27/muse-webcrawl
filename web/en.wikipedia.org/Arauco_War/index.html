Arauco War

Arauco War
Date1536/1550 – 1883 (intermittent)
LocationSouthern Chile (Biobío region and Araucanía)
BelligerentsSpanish Empire / Chile vs. Mapuche peoples
ResultMapuche autonomy south of the Biobío for centuries; eventual Chilean occupation of Araucanía (1861–1883)

The Arauco War was a long-running conflict between colonial Spaniards—and later the independent Chilean state—and the Mapuche peoples of south-central Chile. Beginning with the Spanish conquest in the mid-16th century, the war continued intermittently for more than three centuries, making it one of the longest conflicts in the Americas.

Course of the war

Early campaigns led by Pedro de Valdivia met fierce resistance; Valdivia himself was killed after the Battle of Tucapel in 1553 by forces associated with the toqui Lautaro, and his successor García Hurtado de Mendoza faced the warrior Caupolicán. The Mapuche victory at Curalaba in 1598 destroyed the Spanish cities south of the Biobío River, establishing a frontier that endured for centuries. The Parliament of Quilín in 1641 formally recognised Mapuche autonomy south of the river, and a system of frontier trade and periodic parlamentos coexisted with raids (malones) and punitive expeditions.

Conclusion

After Chilean independence, the state pursued the "Pacification of Araucanía" between 1861 and 1883, occupying Mapuche territory and confining communities to reservations. The war's history became central to Chilean national identity and literature, notably through Alonso de Ercilla's epic poem La Araucana.
