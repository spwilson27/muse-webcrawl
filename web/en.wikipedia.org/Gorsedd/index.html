A gorsedd (plural gorseddau) is a community or meeting of modern-day bards. The word is Welsh for "throne". The best-known is the Gorsedd of the Bards of the Isle of Britain (Gorsedd Beirdd Ynys Prydain), an association of poets, writers, musicians, artists, and others who have contributed to Welsh culture and language.

History

The Gorsedd was founded in 1792 by Edward Williams, better known as Iolo Morganwg, who claimed it revived the ceremonies of the ancient Celtic druids; modern scholarship regards much of this as his invention. The Gorsedd was first linked to the eisteddfod in 1819, and its colourful ceremonies — conducted in Welsh, with members wearing white (bards), blue (ovates), or green (druids) robes — are now a central feature of the National Eisteddfod of Wales.

Ceremonies

The Gorsedd presides over ceremonies such as the Crowning and Chairing of the winning poets, led by the Archdruid, who is elected for a three-year term. New members are admitted by examination in Welsh or by honour for services to Wales. Similar gorseddau exist in Cornwall and Brittany as part of the Celtic revival.
