Vostok (rocket family)

The Vostok (Russian: Восток, "East") family of rockets was a series of Soviet expendable launch vehicles derived from the R-7 Semyorka ICBM and the earlier Luna launchers. Members of the family launched the first artificial satellite's successors, the first human being into space, and hundreds of reconnaissance and scientific satellites between 1958 and 1991.

Variants

  • Vostok-L (8K72) — the Luna variant that launched the first probes toward the Moon in 1958–1960, including Luna 3, which photographed the Moon's far side.
  • Vostok-K (8K72K) — used for the six crewed Vostok missions (1961–1963), beginning with Yuri Gagarin's flight on 12 April 1961, and the first Zenit reconnaissance satellites.
  • Vostok-2 (8A92) and Vostok-2M (8A92M) — satellite launchers that flew Zenit, Meteor weather and other payloads; the 2M remained in service until 1991, the last R-7 derivative of its generation.

All Vostok rockets shared the R-7 core: four tapered strap-on boosters around a central sustainer, with an added upper stage. The boosters' separation in flight produced the famous "Korolev cross" pattern in the sky. The family was succeeded in its crewed role by the Voskhod and Soyuz rockets.
