Social planning is the process of analysing social conditions, setting goals, and designing policies and programmes to improve the well-being of a community. Practised by governments, social planning councils, and non-profit organisations, it applies research and community consultation to decisions about social services, income supports, housing, health, and inclusion—typically at the local or regional level, where it complements economic and land-use planning.

Approaches

Theorists of community practice, notably Jack Rothman, distinguish social planning from locality development and social action. In the social planning model, an expert-driven, task-oriented process gathers data, assesses needs, and proposes rational solutions to problems such as homelessness or service gaps; community participation may be limited to consultation. Critics argue this technocratic style can overlook the knowledge and priorities of affected residents, and contemporary practice increasingly blends planning with participatory and asset-based methods.

Practice

Social planners produce community needs assessments, demographic and poverty profiles, and social plans that set priorities for funding and services. In Canada, independent social planning councils—some dating to the early twentieth century welfare councils—conduct research on poverty, childcare, and housing and advocate for policy change. Municipalities also employ social planners to coordinate responses to issues such as opioid crises, newcomer settlement, and ageing populations. Indicators projects track progress using measures of health, education, income, and belonging.

History

The roots of social planning lie in charity organisation societies, settlement houses, and the social surveys of Booth and Rowntree, and it expanded with the growth of welfare states after 1945. From the 1960s, community development corporations and anti-poverty programmes broadened the field; later decades brought both managerial, evidence-based approaches and renewed emphasis on social justice and equity frameworks.
