McCulloch–Pitts neuron

The McCulloch–Pitts neuron is an early mathematical model of a biological neuron, proposed by Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts in their 1943 paper "A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity". It is widely regarded as the first formal model of an artificial neural network and a founding work of both neural network theory and theoretical neuroscience.

The model

In the model, a neuron receives binary inputs from other neurons. It fires (outputs 1) if the sum of excitatory inputs minus inhibitory inputs reaches a threshold; any active inhibitory input can veto firing altogether. Neurons operate in discrete, synchronous time steps, and signals take one time step to propagate. McCulloch and Pitts showed that networks of such neurons, with feedback loops, could compute any logical function and were equivalent in power to a Turing machine (in a later formalisation by Kleene).

Influence

The paper influenced cybernetics, John von Neumann's design of self-reproducing automata, and later perceptron and connectionist research. Although biologically simplified — real neurons use graded potentials and spikes with rich temporal dynamics — the model established the idea that cognition might be explained by networks of simple logical units.
