Tycho's Supernova

Tycho's Supernova (SN 1572)
Constellation: Cassiopeia
Observed: November 1572
Type: Type Ia supernova
Remnant: Tycho's SNR (3C 10)

Tycho's Supernova (SN 1572), also known as Tycho's Nova, was a supernova of Type Ia in the constellation Cassiopeia, one of the few naked-eye supernovae recorded in history. It appeared in November 1572 and was observed in detail by the Danish astronomer Tycho Brahe.

Observations

Brahe's careful measurements showed the new star had no detectable diurnal parallax, proving it lay far beyond the Moon — a result that challenged the Aristotelian doctrine of an unchanging celestial realm. At its peak the star rivalled Venus in brightness and remained visible for about sixteen months.

Remnant

The expanding remnant, now a prominent radio and X-ray source, lies roughly 8,000–9,800 light-years away. Light echoes and spectroscopy confirm the Type Ia classification, the thermonuclear explosion of a white dwarf.
