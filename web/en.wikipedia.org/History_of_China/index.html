The history of China spans several millennia across a wide geographical area. Chinese civilisation originated along the Yellow River with Neolithic cultures such as Yangshao and Longshan, and the earliest historically attested dynasty, the Shang (c. 1600–1046 BC), left the earliest Chinese writing in oracle bone inscriptions. The traditional narrative of dynastic cycles — founding vigour, flourishing, decline, and replacement under the "Mandate of Heaven" — structures much of China's recorded history.

Ancient and imperial China

The Zhou dynasty (1046–256 BC) introduced the Mandate of Heaven; its later centuries (Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods) saw the flourishing of Confucianism, Daoism and Legalism. In 221 BC Qin Shi Huang unified China, standardising script, weights and measures; his short-lived Qin gave way to the Han dynasty (206 BC – AD 220), which consolidated the imperial system, expanded along the Silk Road, and gave its name to China's majority ethnic group. After centuries of division, the Sui (581–618) reunified China and built the Grand Canal; the Tang (618–907) were a cosmopolitan golden age, and the Song (960–1279) saw commercial revolution, printing, paper money and gunpowder weapons.

Mongol, Ming and Qing

The Mongol Yuan dynasty (1271–1368) completed the conquest of all China under Kublai Khan. The Ming (1368–1644) restored native rule, rebuilt the Great Wall largely as seen today, and launched Zheng He's treasure voyages. The Manchu Qing dynasty (1636/1644–1912) expanded China to its greatest territorial extent, but in the 19th century suffered the Opium Wars, unequal treaties and the catastrophic Taiping Rebellion.

Republic and People's Republic

The 1911 Revolution ended two millennia of imperial rule; the Republic of China (1912) was followed by warlord fragmentation, the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945) and civil war, ending in the Communist victory and the proclamation of the People's Republic of China by Mao Zedong on 1 October 1949, with the Republic retreating to Taiwan. The PRC underwent the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution before Deng Xiaoping's reforms from 1978 unleashed decades of rapid economic growth, making China the world's second-largest economy.
