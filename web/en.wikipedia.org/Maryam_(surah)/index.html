Maryam (Arabic: مريم) is the 19th chapter (surah) of the Qur'an, containing 98 verses (ayat). The chapter is named after Maryam (Mary), the mother of Isa (Jesus), who appears prominently in it; it is the only chapter of the Qur'an named after a woman, and Mary is the only woman named in the Qur'an.

Contents

The surah opens with the story of Zakariya (Zechariah) and the miraculous birth of his son Yahya (John the Baptist), followed by its longest section: the account of Mary, the annunciation by the angel, and the virgin birth of Jesus. In the narrative, the infant Jesus speaks from the cradle to defend his mother's honor, an episode unique to the Qur'anic telling. The chapter goes on to recount, in compressed form, the stories of Abraham, Moses, Ishmael, Idris, and other prophets, and closes with reflections on the resurrection, judgment, and the mercy of God.

Themes and classification

Classified as a Meccan surah, Maryam emphasizes God's mercy (raḥma) — the divine name al-Raḥmān ("the Most Merciful") recurs throughout — and defends the doctrines of monotheism and bodily resurrection against skepticism. According to tradition, verses from this chapter were recited by Ja'far ibn Abi Talib before the Negus of Abyssinia during the first Muslim migration (hijra) to Ethiopia, moving the Christian king to shelter the refugees.
