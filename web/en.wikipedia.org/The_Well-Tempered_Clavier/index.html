The Well-Tempered Clavier (German: Das Wohltemperierte Klavier), BWV 846–893, is two sets of preludes and fugues in all 24 major and minor keys for keyboard by Johann Sebastian Bach. In Bach's time clavier was a generic name indicating a keyboard instrument, most typically the harpsichord or clavichord — but not excluding the organ.

Contents

Each set contains 24 pairs of pieces, one pair for each major and minor key, arranged in rising chromatic order from C major to B minor. Book I (BWV 846–869) was compiled in 1722 while Bach was Kapellmeister at Köthen; its title page states it was written "for the profit and use of musical youth desirous of learning, and especially for the pastime of those already skilled in this study". Book II (BWV 870–893) was compiled around 1739–1742 in Leipzig from pieces written over many years.

"Well-tempered" tuning

The title refers to a tuning system — a "well temperament" — in which all keys are usable, as opposed to meantone systems that made remote keys unusable. Although sometimes described as demonstrating equal temperament, scholars generally hold that Bach had in mind one of the circulating well temperaments of his day, which give each key a slightly different character.

Influence

The collection is among the most important works in Western classical music; Hans von Bülow called it the "Old Testament" of piano literature (Beethoven's 32 piano sonatas being the "New Testament"). It has been a cornerstone of keyboard pedagogy and inspired cycles in all keys by later composers, including Chopin's 24 Preludes and Shostakovich's 24 Preludes and Fugues.
