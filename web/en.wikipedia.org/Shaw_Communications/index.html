Shaw Communications Inc. was a Canadian telecommunications company headquartered in Calgary, Alberta, which provided telephone, Internet, television, and mobile services principally in Western Canada. Founded in 1966 by JR Shaw as Capital Cable Television in Edmonton, it grew into Canada's second-largest cable operator, serving more than three million customers across British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, and northern Ontario. In April 2023, Shaw was acquired by Rogers Communications in a C$26 billion transaction, one of the largest in Canadian corporate history; its wireless arm, Freedom Mobile, was divested to Quebecor's Vidéotron as a condition of approval.

History

JR Shaw began the company with a small Edmonton cable system; by the 1970s and 1980s it had expanded through aggressive acquisition of cable systems across western and central Canada. The company went public in 1971 and adopted the Shaw Communications name in 1993. It launched digital cable and high-speed internet in the late 1990s, home phone service over its cable network in 2005, and bought back the Canwest television assets (through Shaw Media) in 2010, which it sold to Corus Entertainment in 2016 in order to concentrate on connectivity.

Shaw entered the wireless market by purchasing spectrum in the 2008 AWS auction; after years of delay it acquired the upstart carrier Wind Mobile in 2016 and rebranded it Freedom Mobile in 2017, positioning it as a low-cost fourth carrier in Ontario, Alberta, and British Columbia.

Rogers acquisition

Rogers announced its agreement to acquire Shaw in March 2021 for C$40.50 per share. The deal faced intense scrutiny from the Competition Bureau, which sought to block it over concerns about wireless prices; following a Competition Tribunal decision in Rogers' favour (upheld by the Federal Court of Appeal in January 2023) and the agreed sale of Freedom Mobile to Quebecor for C$2.85 billion, the federal government approved the transaction with conditions, and it closed on 3 April 2023. The Shaw brand was progressively retired as services were migrated to Rogers, while Shaw's satellite TV business (Shaw Direct) continued under Rogers for a time under its own name.

Operations and legacy

At its peak Shaw employed around 9,600 people and reported annual revenues of roughly C$5.5 billion. The Shaw family retained significant interests in Corus Entertainment after the sale. The company left a substantial philanthropic footprint, including the Shaw Charity Classic golf tournament and support for children's hospitals in Western Canada. Its founder, JR Shaw, died in 2020; his son Jim Shaw served as CEO until 2011, followed by Brad Shaw, who became executive chair and led the company into the Rogers transaction.
