Florida State Seminoles

The Florida State Seminoles are the athletic teams representing Florida State University (FSU) located in Tallahassee, Florida. They compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC) in NCAA Division I, primarily in the FBS for football. The Seminoles field 18 varsity teams in 11 sports, and their colours are garnet and gold.

Football

The football program is the school's most prominent. Under head coach Bobby Bowden (1976–2009) Florida State became a national power, winning national championships in 1993 and 1999 and producing an unmatched run of 14 consecutive top-five AP Poll finishes (1987–2000). A third national championship followed in 2013 under Jimbo Fisher, behind Heisman Trophy winner Jameis Winston. Heisman winners Charlie Ward (1993) and Chris Weinke (2000) also played for the Seminoles. The team plays at Doak Campbell Stadium, one of the largest college stadiums in the United States, and its traditions include Osceola and Renegade — a depiction of the Seminole leader Osceola riding an Appaloosa horse and planting a flaming spear at midfield before home games, performed with the approval of the Seminole Tribe of Florida.

Other sports

Florida State has won national championships in women's soccer (2014, 2018, 2021, 2023), softball (2018), women's basketball tournament appearances including a Final Four, and track and field (men's outdoor titles in 2006 and 2008). The baseball team, long coached by Mike Martin, has made many College World Series appearances.

Name and imagery

The "Seminoles" name was adopted in 1947 and is used with the explicit support of the Seminole Tribe of Florida, which has a formal relationship with the university; the NCAA exempted FSU from its 2005 policy on Native American mascots on that basis.
