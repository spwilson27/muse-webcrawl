The Singapore Prison Service (SPS) is a government agency of Singapore under the Ministry of Home Affairs. It administers the country's prison institutions, including the Changi Prison Complex, and is responsible for the custody, rehabilitation, and reintegration of offenders.

History

Singapore's prison system traces its origins to the early colonial period. Changi Prison, built in 1936, became notorious during the Second World War when it held Allied prisoners of war and civilian internees under Japanese occupation. After the war the site continued as the nation's principal prison, and a modern Changi Prison Complex was progressively developed to consolidate older institutions.

Rehabilitation and reintegration

The SPS brands its officers as "Captains of Lives", emphasising rehabilitation alongside secure custody. It works with the Singapore Corporation of Rehabilitative Enterprises (SCORE) to provide vocational training and employment support, and supports the Yellow Ribbon Project, a community initiative launched in 2004 to promote acceptance of ex-offenders and second chances.

Facilities

Principal institutions include the Changi Prison Complex, Admiralty West Prison, and the Tanah Merah Centre. Capital and corporal punishment sentences in Singapore are carried out within the prison system under judicial order.
