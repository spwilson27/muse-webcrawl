La Araucana (1569, 1578 and 1589) is a Renaissance epic poem in Spanish by Alonso de Ercilla y Zúñiga, narrating the first phase of the Arauco War in Chile, in which Spanish conquistadors fought the Mapuche (then called Araucanians). It is regarded as the founding literary work of Chile and one of the great epic poems of the Spanish Golden Age, notable for treating the indigenous Mapuche heroes with admiration and dignity.

Composition

Ercilla, a Spanish soldier and courtier, served in Chile from 1557 and, according to tradition, began drafting verses on scraps of paper during the campaign. The poem is written in 37 cantos of ottava rima across three parts published in Madrid. It blends eyewitness battle narrative with classical epic machinery, geography and ethnography of Chile, and episodes such as the story of the Mapuche leaders Lautaro and Caupolicán and the warrior woman Guacolda.

Themes and significance

Unusually for a colonial epic, La Araucana praises the valour of the Mapuche, whom Ercilla portrays as noble defenders of their land, while criticising Spanish cruelty and greed; the poem's famous opening declares that he sings not of ladies and courtly love but of the deeds of Spaniards and of the indomitable Araucanians. Voltaire praised it, and it influenced later American literature. In Chile it became a national epic, and figures such as Lautaro became enduring symbols in both Chilean and Mapuche identity.
