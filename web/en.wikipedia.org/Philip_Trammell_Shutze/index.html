Philip Trammell Shutze

Philip Trammell Shutze (1890–1982) was an American architect known for his work in the Classical Revival style, primarily in Atlanta, Georgia. Trained at the Georgia Institute of Technology and the American Academy in Rome, Shutze became one of the leading practitioners of traditional architecture in the southeastern United States during the twentieth century.

Life and career

Shutze studied architecture at Georgia Tech and in 1912 won the Rome Prize, which allowed him to study at the American Academy in Rome. On returning to Atlanta he joined the firm of Hentz, Reid and Adler, eventually becoming a partner in Hentz, Adler & Shutze. His deep knowledge of Italian Renaissance and English Georgian precedents shaped a body of work noted for scholarship and refined detail.

Notable works

Among his best-known buildings are the Swan House (1928) in Atlanta, built for Edward and Emily Inman and now part of the Atlanta History Center, and the Academy of Medicine building. Shutze also designed numerous private residences, churches and institutional buildings throughout Georgia.

Legacy

Shutze's work is regarded as a high point of American classical design, and the Swan House is frequently cited as one of the finest classical houses built in the United States in the twentieth century.
