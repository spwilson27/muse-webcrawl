The World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) is one of the 15 specialized agencies of the United Nations, headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland. It administers the global system of intellectual property — patents, trademarks, industrial designs, and (in part) copyright — and promotes international cooperation in IP policy.

History

WIPO's roots lie in the Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property (1883) and the Berne Convention for copyright (1886), whose bureaus were merged in 1893 into an organisation known as BIRPI. The WIPO Convention was signed in Stockholm in 1967 and took effect in 1970; WIPO became a UN specialized agency in 1974.

Functions

WIPO administers more than two dozen treaties and runs global registration services that generate most of its (largely self-funded) budget: the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) for international patent applications, the Madrid System for trademarks, and the Hague System for designs. It also provides arbitration and mediation, including the Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy (UDRP) for internet domain names, publishes the Global Innovation Index with partners, and runs development and capacity-building programmes for developing countries.

Governance and debates

Member states govern WIPO through its General Assembly and committees. Recurrent debates concern the balance between IP protection and public health or access to knowledge, the stalled treaty work on traditional knowledge and genetic resources (which produced a treaty in 2024), and the organisation's development agenda adopted in 2007.
