The Chapel of Bones (Portuguese: Capela dos Ossos) is one of the best-known monuments in Évora, Portugal. It is a small interior chapel adjoining the Church of St. Francis, famous because its interior walls are covered with human skulls and bones.

History

The chapel was built in the sixteenth century by Franciscan monks. At the time, Évora's cemeteries were overcrowded, and the monks exhumed the remains of some 5,000 individuals from dozens of burial grounds. Rather than discard them, the friars arranged the bones decoratively within the chapel as a memento mori — a meditation on the transience of earthly life in keeping with the Counter-Reformation spirit of the age.

Above the entrance is inscribed the celebrated warning: "Nós ossos que aqui estamos pelos vossos esperamos" — "We bones that here are, for yours await." Inside, the ceiling is painted with frescoes of biblical scenes, and two desiccated corpses — one of them a child — hang among the displays. A poem by Father António da Ascenção, displayed in the chapel, meditates on the fragility of existence and the approach of death.

Description

The chapel is entered through a broad arch and consists of three naves about 18.7 metres long and 11 metres wide. Daylight enters through a few small openings, so the ossuary is dimly lit. It remains one of the most visited sites in Évora, comparable to the Sedlec Ossuary in the Czech Republic and the Capela dos Ossos in Faro.
