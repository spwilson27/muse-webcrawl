Évora (Portuguese pronunciation: [ˈɛvuɾɐ]) is a city and municipality in south-central Portugal, the capital of the Alentejo region and of the Évora District. Its well-preserved historic centre, still partially enclosed by medieval walls, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.

History

Known to the Romans as Ebora Liberalitas Julia, the city preserves a Corinthian Roman temple from the 1st century AD, often (probably incorrectly) called the Temple of Diana. After periods of Visigothic and Moorish rule, Geraldo Geraldes ("the Fearless") retook Évora for the Portuguese crown in 1165. The city flourished in the 15th and 16th centuries as a residence of the Avis dynasty; the University of Évora was founded by Jesuits in 1559.

Sights

The Gothic cathedral (Sé de Évora), begun in 1186, is the largest medieval cathedral in Portugal. The Chapel of Bones (Capela dos Ossos), lined with the skulls and bones of some 5,000 monks, bears the famous inscription "Nós ossos que aqui estamos pelos vossos esperamos" ("We bones that here are, for yours await"). Other monuments include the Água de Prata aqueduct, built into rows of houses, the Royal Palace, and the Praça do Giraldo, the main square.

Economy

Évora is a market and service centre for the surrounding agricultural plains of the Alentejo — cork, wheat, olives, and wine — and a growing hub for aeronautics manufacturing and tourism.
