MIT Sloan School of Management

The MIT Sloan School of Management (branded as MIT Sloan) is the business school of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Founded in 1914 as the engineering administration curriculum, it is named after Alfred P. Sloan, the former chairman of General Motors and a graduate of MIT, whose foundation funded its development into an independent school in the 1950s.

Programs

MIT Sloan offers undergraduate, MBA, Executive MBA, Master of Finance, Master of Business Analytics, Master of Science in Management Studies, and doctoral programs, as well as executive education. Its teaching emphasises quantitative analysis, innovation, and the management of technology, and it is closely integrated with MIT's engineering and science departments.

Research and culture

The school is home to research centres in areas including system dynamics (founded by Jay Forrester), finance, digital business, and sustainability. Faculty have included Nobel laureates such as Robert Merton, Myron Scholes, and Bengt Holmström. The school's main complex includes the Tang Center and the E62 building, completed in 2010.
