Ferdinand de Saussure

Ferdinand de Saussure (1857–1913)

Ferdinand de Saussure (26 November 1857 – 22 February 1913) was a Swiss linguist, semiotician and philosopher. His ideas laid a foundation for many significant developments in both linguistics and semiotics in the 20th century. He is widely considered one of the founders of 20th-century linguistics and of semiotics.

Course in General Linguistics

Saussure published only limited work during his lifetime. His most influential ideas come from the Course in General Linguistics (1916), compiled posthumously by his students Charles Bally and Albert Sechehaye from lecture notes. The Course introduced key concepts: the distinction between langue (the abstract system of a language) and parole (individual speech acts); the linguistic sign as a union of signifier and signified; the arbitrariness of the sign; and the contrast between synchronic and diachronic study of language.

Legacy

Saussure's structuralist approach influenced anthropology, literary theory and philosophy, notably through Claude Lévi-Strauss and the Prague school of linguistics. His youthful work on Indo-European vowels, proposing "laryngeal" consonants, was later vindicated by the decipherment of Hittite.
