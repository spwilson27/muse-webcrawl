Pine Ridge Indian Reservation

The Pine Ridge Indian Reservation (Lakota: Wazí Aháŋhaŋ Oyáŋke), also called Pine Ridge Agency, is an Oglala Lakota Indian reservation in southwestern South Dakota, United States. It is one of the largest reservations in the country, covering about 2.1 million acres (3,469 square miles), including all of Oglala Lakota County and parts of surrounding counties, with a small portion extending into Nebraska.

History

The reservation was established in 1889 from part of the Great Sioux Reservation created by the 1868 Treaty of Fort Laramie. In December 1890 the Wounded Knee Massacre, the last armed conflict of the Indian Wars, occurred on the reservation, where U.S. cavalry killed some 250–300 Lakota, many of them women and children. In 1973, activists of the American Indian Movement occupied Wounded Knee for 71 days to protest tribal government corruption and federal treaty violations, drawing national attention.

Present day

Pine Ridge is governed by the Oglala Sioux Tribe under a constitution adopted under the Indian Reorganization Act. The reservation faces severe poverty — per-capita incomes have long been among the lowest in the United States — alongside challenges in housing, health, and alcohol-related harm, the latter fuelled historically by liquor sales in the border town of Whiteclay, Nebraska. It is also a centre of Lakota language and cultural revitalization, home to Oglala Lakota College and the Red Cloud Indian School.
