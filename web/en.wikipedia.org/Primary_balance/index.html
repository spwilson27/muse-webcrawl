Primary balance

The primary balance is the fiscal balance of a government excluding interest payments on its debt. It is calculated as government revenue minus government expenditure other than interest. A positive value is a primary surplus; a negative value is a primary deficit.

Use in debt analysis

The primary balance is a key measure of fiscal sustainability: a government that runs a primary surplus is covering all its current spending from revenue and paying down the real burden of its debt, whereas persistent primary deficits cause debt to grow even before interest costs. Analysts compare the primary balance with the interest rate–growth differential to assess whether a country's debt-to-GDP ratio will stabilize.

Related measures

The overall fiscal balance includes interest payments, while the cyclically adjusted primary balance removes the estimated effects of the business cycle to reveal underlying fiscal policy.
