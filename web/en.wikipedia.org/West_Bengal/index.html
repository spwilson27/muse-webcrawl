West Bengal (Bengali: Paścim Baṅga) is a state in the eastern portion of India. It is situated along the Bay of Bengal, along with a population of over 91 million inhabitants within an area of 88,752 km² (34,267 sq mi) as of 2011. The population estimate as of 2023 is around 102,552,787. West Bengal is the fourth-most populous and thirteenth-largest state by area in India, as well as the eighth-most populous country subdivision of the world.

Geography

The state stretches from the Himalayan foothills around Darjeeling in the north — famous for tea gardens and views of Kangchenjunga — through the fertile Gangetic plain to the mangrove deltas of the Sundarbans in the south, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and habitat of the Bengal tiger. It borders Bangladesh to the east, Nepal and Bhutan to the north, and the Indian states of Assam, Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, and Sikkim.

History

Bengal was partitioned in 1947 along religious lines: the Hindu-majority west joined India as West Bengal, while the Muslim-majority east became East Pakistan (later Bangladesh). Kolkata (formerly Calcutta), capital of British India until 1911, remained the state capital. The state was governed by the Left Front, led by the Communist Party of India (Marxist), from 1977 to 2011 — the world's longest-serving democratically elected communist government — and since 2011 by the All India Trinamool Congress under Mamata Banerjee.

Culture

West Bengal is a center of Bengali culture: the literature of Rabindranath Tagore (Asia's first Nobel laureate), the films of Satyajit Ray, the Durga Puja festival in Kolkata (inscribed by UNESCO in 2021), and Baul folk music. The state is also known for its sweets such as rasgulla and sandesh, the Darjeeling tea industry, and a strong tradition in football and the arts.
