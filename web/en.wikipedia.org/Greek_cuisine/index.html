Greek cuisine

Greek cuisine is the cuisine of Greece and the Greek diaspora, sharing many characteristics with other cuisines of the eastern Mediterranean and the Balkans. Contemporary Greek cookery makes wide use of fresh vegetables, olive oil, grains, fish, wine and meat (white and red, including lamb, poultry and pork). Other important ingredients include olives, yogurt, cheese, pasta, lemon juice, herbs such as oregano and mint, bread and aubergines (eggplants).

Overview

Greek cooking has a recorded history stretching back to antiquity; Archestratus wrote an early gastronomic poem in the 4th century BC, and the Greek culinary tradition influenced Roman, Byzantine, Ottoman and modern European cuisines. The triad of wheat, olive oil and wine underlies the diet, together with vegetables and herbs. Meals are often communal, beginning with meze — small shared dishes that may include tzatziki, taramasalata, dolmades, grilled octopus and feta cheese.

Characteristic dishes

  • Moussaka — a baked casserole of aubergine, spiced minced meat and béchamel sauce.
  • Souvlaki and gyros — skewered or spit-roasted meat, often served in pita bread with tomato, onion and tzatziki.
  • Horiatiki (Greek salad) — tomato, cucumber, onion, olives and a slab of feta, dressed with olive oil and oregano.
  • Spanakopita and tiropita — filo pastries filled with spinach and feta, or cheese.
  • Avgolemono — egg-lemon soup or sauce.
  • Fasolada — bean soup, often called the national dish.
  • Baklava, galaktoboureko and spoon sweets — syrup-soaked desserts of filo, nuts and honey.

Cheeses and drinks

Feta, protected by EU designation of origin, is the best-known Greek cheese; others include graviera, kasseri, kefalotyri and the soft myzithra. Greece has one of the world's oldest wine traditions, with indigenous varieties such as Assyrtiko, Xinomavro and Agiorgitiko. Ouzo (anise-flavoured spirit), tsipouro and retsina wine are characteristic drinks, and strong Greek coffee is a social staple.

Regional cuisines

Regional traditions vary widely: Crete is noted for olive oil, wild greens and dakos; the Ionian islands show Venetian influence; Macedonia and Thrace carry Anatolian and refugee Greek influences; and the Aegean islands emphasise seafood, capers and fava.
