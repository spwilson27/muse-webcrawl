Blaschke product

The open unit disk, on which Blaschke products are defined.

In complex analysis, a Blaschke product is a bounded analytic function in the open unit disk that is constructed from Möbius transformations mapping the disk to itself. Named after Wilhelm Blaschke, who introduced them in 1915, these functions play a central role in Hardy space theory.

Definition

A single Blaschke factor for a point a in the unit disk is the function φa(z) = (|a|/a) · (a − z)/(1 − āz) (with the appropriate convention when a = 0), which has one zero at a and modulus one on the unit circle. A finite Blaschke product is a product of finitely many such factors times a unimodular constant. An infinite Blaschke product, formed from a sequence of points {an}, converges exactly when the Blaschke condition Σ (1 − |an|) < ∞ holds; the product then has zeros precisely at those points, counting multiplicity.

Properties and applications

Blaschke products are exactly the proper holomorphic maps of the disk to itself (in the finite case) and are the inner functions whose boundary values have modulus one almost everywhere. Every bounded analytic function on the disk can be factored into a Blaschke product, a singular inner function, and an outer function — the canonical factorization in H∞ theory. Blaschke products also arise in signal processing and control theory as all-pass filters, and finite Blaschke products give rise to model spaces and Takenaka–Malmquist systems used in approximation theory.
