ICD-11

The ICD-11 is the eleventh revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD). Developed and maintained by the World Health Organization (WHO), it replaces ICD-10 as the global standard for recording and reporting diseases, disorders, injuries and other related health conditions. ICD-11 was adopted by the 72nd World Health Assembly in May 2019 and came into effect on January 1, 2022.

Development

Work on the revision began in 2007 and involved contributions from over 300 institutions in 55 countries, with the content developed through a collaborative, web-based process. Unlike earlier revisions, ICD-11 was designed from the outset for use in electronic health records and digital information systems: every entity has a unique identifier (URI), and the classification is delivered through application programming interfaces and a multilingual coding tool.

The classification contains roughly 17,000 unique categories and more than 120,000 codable terms in its Mortality and Morbidity Statistics (MMS) linearization. A system of "postcoordination" allows coders to combine stem codes with extension codes to express additional detail, such as laterality, severity and anatomical site.

Notable changes from ICD-10

  • Gaming disorder was added as a diagnosable condition under disorders due to addictive behaviours.
  • Conditions related to sexual health were placed in a new chapter, and gender incongruence was moved out of the mental disorders chapter.
  • Stroke was reclassified as a disease of the nervous system rather than the circulatory system.
  • Traditional medicine conditions were given a supplementary chapter, and antimicrobial resistance coding was expanded.
  • Chronic primary pain was given its own classification within a reorganised pain framework.

Structure

ICD-11 is organised into 26 chapters, plus supplementary sections for functioning assessment, extension codes and traditional medicine. Codes are alphanumeric, beginning with a letter (with the letters I and O avoided to prevent confusion with the digits 1 and 0).
