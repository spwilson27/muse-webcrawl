West Ham United Football Club is a professional football club based in Stratford, East London, England. Founded in 1895 as Thames Ironworks and reformed in 1900 as West Ham United, the club competes in the Premier League and plays home matches at the London Stadium, its home since 2016 after 112 years at the Boleyn Ground (Upton Park).

History

The club joined the Football League in 1919 and reached the first Wembley FA Cup final in 1923. West Ham has won the FA Cup three times (1964, 1975, 1980) and the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1965. Three West Ham players — captain Bobby Moore, Geoff Hurst and Martin Peters — were key members of England's 1966 World Cup-winning team. In 2023 the club won the UEFA Europa Conference League, its first major trophy in 43 years.

Identity

The club's nicknames, "the Hammers" and "the Irons", and its crossed-hammers crest derive from its shipbuilding origins at the Thames Ironworks. Its supporters sing "I'm Forever Blowing Bubbles". West Ham's academy has produced notable players including Rio Ferdinand, Frank Lampard and Declan Rice, and the club's main rivals include Millwall and Tottenham Hotspur.
