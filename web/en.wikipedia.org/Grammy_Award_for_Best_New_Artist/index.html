Grammy Award for Best New Artist

The Grammy Award trophy

The Grammy Award for Best New Artist has been awarded since the 2nd Annual Grammy Awards in 1960. It is one of the four "General Field" categories of the Grammy Awards, alongside Record of the Year, Album of the Year, and Song of the Year. The award honors an artist who releases, during the eligibility year, the first recording that establishes their public identity.

Criteria

Eligibility rules have been revised several times. Historically, an artist was disqualified if they had released more than three albums or 30 tracks; the rules were changed in 2017 and again in subsequent years to accommodate artists who first gain attention through features or singles. An artist may not be entered for Best New Artist more than three times, and previous winners in certain categories face additional restrictions.

Notable winners

Early winners include Bobby Darin (1960), Peter Nero (1962), and the Beatles (1965). Later recipients include Cyndi Lauper (1985), Mariah Carey (1991), Sheryl Crow (1995), Lauryn Hill (1999), Norah Jones (2003), Amy Winehouse (2008), Adele (2009), Sam Smith (2015), Dua Lipa (2019), Billie Eilish (2020), Olivia Rodrigo (2022), and Victoria Monét (2024).

The "Best New Artist curse"

Music commentary has long noted a perceived "Best New Artist curse," under which some winners fail to sustain their early success. Counterexamples such as the Beatles, Mariah Carey, and Adele demonstrate that the award has no consistent effect, and the notion is generally treated as superstition amplified by memorable failures, including the 1990 win by Milli Vanilli, whose award was revoked after it emerged that the duo had not sung on their album.
