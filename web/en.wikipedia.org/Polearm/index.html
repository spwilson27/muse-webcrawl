Polearm

A polearm or pole weapon is a close combat weapon in which the main fighting part of the weapon is fitted to the end of a long shaft, typically of wood, extending the user's effective range and striking power. Polearms are predominantly melee weapons, with a subclass of spears which could also be thrown. Because many polearms were adapted from agricultural implements or other fairly abundant tools, and contained relatively little metal, they were cheap to make and readily available. When belligerents in warfare had a poorer access to resources, or there was a lack of structured formation to support heavy infantry or cavalry, many would outfit themselves with inexpensive and readily available pole weapons.

On the battlefield, polearms were the standard weapons for infantry to engage armoured cavalry and infantry, because polearms combine a spear's reach with the ability to cut, hook or bludgeon. The pike, halberd, glaive and bill are among the best-known European types.

History

The earliest polearms were simple spears: a stone, and later metal, point mounted on a shaft. As warfare developed, heads became larger and more varied, combining spear points with axe blades, hammers, hooks and spikes to deal with increasingly effective armour. In medieval Europe, polearms saw widespread use from the 13th to the 16th centuries; Swiss and German mercenary infantry built their battlefield reputations on dense pike formations. Similar weapons developed independently elsewhere: the Japanese naginata and yari, the Chinese ji and guandao, and many others.

The development of firearms, especially the combination of pike and shot and later the bayonet, gradually made dedicated polearms obsolete on the battlefield, though they survived as ceremonial weapons and in the hands of guards and watchmen.

Classification

  • Spears and lances: primarily thrusting points on a shaft.
  • Pikes: extra-long spears used in formation.
  • Axe-headed polearms: halberd, poleaxe, bill, glaive.
  • Hammer- and pick-headed polearms: lucerne hammer, bec de corbin.
  • Blunt and flailed types: goad, military fork, partisan.
