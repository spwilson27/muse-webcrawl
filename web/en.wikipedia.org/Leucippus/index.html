Leucippus

Leucippus (Greek: Λεύκιππος; fl. 5th century BC) was a Greek philosopher credited with founding atomism, the theory that everything is composed of indivisible particles — atoms — moving in a void. He is traditionally regarded as the teacher of Democritus, who systematised and extended his doctrines; so intertwined are their views in later sources that scholars often speak of the joint system of "Leucippus and Democritus".

Life and work

Almost nothing certain is known of Leucippus's life; even his birthplace is disputed between Elea, Miletus and Abdera, and his historicity itself was doubted in antiquity (Epicurus reportedly denied he ever existed). Diogenes Laërtius attributes to him a work called The Great World-System (Megas Diakosmos), though the surviving fragment on determinism — "Nothing happens in vain, but everything from reason and by necessity" — may come from Democritus's On Mind. Aristotle and Theophrastus consistently treat Leucippus as the originator of the atomic theory, devised as a response to the Eleatic arguments of Parmenides and Zeno: atoms preserve the unchanging being of Parmenides' One, multiplied into infinitely many beings moving in empty space.
