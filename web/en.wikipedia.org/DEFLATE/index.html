Deflate

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Deflate (stylised DEFLATE) is a lossless data compression file format that uses a combination of LZ77 and Huffman coding. It was designed by Phil Katz, for version 2 of his PKZIP archiving tool, and was later specified in RFC 1951 (1996).

Format

A deflate stream consists of a series of blocks. Each block begins with a 3-bit header indicating whether it is the final block and how its data is encoded: stored (uncompressed), compressed with fixed Huffman codes, or compressed with dynamic Huffman codes transmitted with the block. Compressed data encodes literal bytes and length–distance pairs that copy earlier decompressed data, as in LZ77, allowing back-references of length 3–258 bytes within a 32 KiB sliding window.

Uses

Deflate is the compression at the heart of the ZIP archive format, gzip, zlib, and the PNG image format, and is widely used in HTTP content encoding. Because the format was kept patent-free, it became the standard general-purpose compression of the 1990s and remains ubiquitous, although newer formats such as zstd and Brotli offer better ratios or speed.
