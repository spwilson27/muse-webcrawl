Matrix congruence

In mathematics, two square matrices A and B over a field are called congruent if there exists an invertible matrix P over the same field such that PTAP = B, where PT denotes the transpose of P.

Relation to quadratic forms

Congruence arises naturally from a change of basis for a bilinear form or quadratic form: if A is the matrix of a bilinear form in one basis, then the matrix in a new basis is congruent to A. Unlike matrix similarity, congruence preserves symmetry and, over the real numbers, the signature of a symmetric form.

Classification

Sylvester's law of inertia states that two real symmetric matrices are congruent if and only if they have the same number of positive, negative and zero eigenvalues (their inertia). Over the complex numbers, symmetric matrices are classified under congruence by rank alone. Congruence of Hermitian matrices is defined using the conjugate transpose and likewise preserves inertia.
