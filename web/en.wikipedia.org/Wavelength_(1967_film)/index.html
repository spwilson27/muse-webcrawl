Wavelength (1967 film)

Director: Michael Snow
Released: 1967
Running time: 45 minutes
Country: Canada

Wavelength is a 1967 experimental film by Canadian artist Michael Snow, regarded as a landmark of structural film and avant-garde cinema. It won the Grand Prix at the Experimental Film Competition in Knokke-le-Zoute, Belgium, in 1967.

Description

The film consists of what appears to be a single continuous 45-minute zoom across a New York loft room, moving from a wide view of the interior toward a photograph of ocean waves pinned to the far wall — the literal "wavelength" of the title. The zoom was in fact assembled from shots taken over a week, using different film stocks, filters, and exposures, so the image shifts in color and light as it narrows. Within the room, four brief human incidents occur: people move a bookcase, a woman listens to the radio, a man collapses and apparently dies, and a woman telephones to report the body. The soundtrack is built around a slowly rising sine wave glissando, ascending from about 50 Hz to 12,000 Hz over the film's duration.

Reception and legacy

Critic P. Adams Sitney identified Wavelength as a defining work of structural film, in which a film's shape organizes its content. It is regularly placed among the most important experimental films ever made and is widely taught in film studies.
