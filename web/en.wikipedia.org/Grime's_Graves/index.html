Grime's Graves

LocationWeeting, Norfolk, England
PeriodNeolithic, c. 2650–2300 BC
TypeFlint mine
AreaApprox. 37 acres (15 ha)
ManagementEnglish Heritage; scheduled monument

Grime's Graves is a large Neolithic flint mining complex near Weeting in Norfolk, England, close to the edge of Thetford Forest in the Breckland. It is the largest of the known prehistoric flint mines in England and the only one where visitors can descend into an excavated shaft. The strange name derives from the Anglo-Saxon period: the pits were attributed to the god Grim (a byname of Woden/Odin), and "Graves" comes from an Old English word for pits or hollows.

The mines

The site covers about 37 acres pockmarked with more than 400 filled shafts and pits. Neolithic miners dug shafts up to 13 metres deep through chalk to reach seams of high-quality black flint — especially the prized "floorstone" layer — and then dug radiating galleries along the seam. Using red deer antler picks and shoulder-blade shovels, they extracted flint for axes and other tools, which were roughly shaped on site and traded widely across Britain. Radiocarbon dating places the main mining activity around 2650–2300 BC, contemporary with the later phases of Stonehenge.

Archaeology and ritual

The mines were first recognised as prehistoric in 1868–70, when Canon William Greenwell excavated a shaft and proved the pits were dug with antler tools. Later excavations found evidence of deliberate deposits, including a chalk carving and an arrangement of objects at the base of one shaft interpreted as a shrine, possibly intended to placate the powers of the earth for the flint removed. One shaft, unusually, was left largely unworked after the flint proved poor; the apparent shrine there suggests the miners made offerings when a pit failed.

Visiting

The site is managed by English Heritage and is a scheduled monument and Site of Special Scientific Interest, its chalk grassland supporting rare Breckland plants. Visitors can descend by ladder 9 metres into one excavated pit and view the galleries.
