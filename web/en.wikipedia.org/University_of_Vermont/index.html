The University of Vermont (UVM), officially the University of Vermont and State Agricultural College, is a public land-grant research university in Burlington, Vermont, United States. Founded in 1791 — the same year Vermont joined the Union — it is the fifth-oldest university in New England and one of the eight "Public Ivy" institutions. The Latin name Universitas Viridis Montis ("University of the Green Mountains") gives the initials UVM.

Academics

UVM enrolls about 14,000 students in programs across eight colleges and schools, including the Robert Larner College of Medicine (founded 1822), the Rubenstein School of Environment and Natural Resources, the Grossman School of Business, and the College of Engineering and Mathematical Sciences. The university is known for environmental studies, health sciences, and its Gund Institute for Environment. Its medical centre, UVM Medical Center, is Vermont's largest hospital and teaching centre.

Campus and traditions

The campus sits on a hill above Lake Champlain with views to the Adirondacks. Landmarks include the Billings Memorial Library, designed by H.H. Richardson, and the Fleming Museum of Art. Students celebrate the "Naked Bike Ride" at the end of each semester, and winter athletics benefit from nearby ski areas; alumnus Billy Kidd and Barbara Ann Cochran are among its Olympic skiers.

Athletics

The Catamounts compete in the America East Conference and Hockey East. The men's ice hockey team has reached the NCAA Frozen Four, and the men's soccer team won the NCAA national championship in 2024.
