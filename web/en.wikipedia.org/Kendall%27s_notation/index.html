Kendall's notation

In queueing theory, Kendall's notation (proposed by David George Kendall in 1953) is the standard system for classifying a queueing model. A queue is described as A/B/C, extended when needed to A/B/C/K/N/D, where A describes the arrival process, B the service-time distribution, C the number of servers, K the capacity of the system, N the size of the calling population and D the queueing discipline.

Common symbols

Common letters include M (Markovian: Poisson arrivals or exponential service), D (deterministic), G (general), Ek (Erlang) and Hk (hyperexponential). Thus the classic single-server queue is written M/M/1, and M/M/c denotes c parallel servers with Poisson arrivals and exponential service.
