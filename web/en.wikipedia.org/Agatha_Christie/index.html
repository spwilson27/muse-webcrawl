Dame Agatha Mary Clarissa Christie, Lady Mallowan (née Miller; 15 September 1890 – 12 January 1976) was an English author known for her 66 detective novels and 14 short story collections, particularly those revolving around fictional detectives Hercule Poirot and Miss Marple. She also wrote the world's longest-running play, The Mousetrap, and under the pen name Mary Westmacott, six romance novels. Guinness World Records lists her as the best-selling fiction author of all time, with her novels having sold more than two billion copies.

Life

Christie was born in Torquay into a wealthy upper-middle-class family. She served as a nurse and apothecary's assistant during the First World War, acquiring the knowledge of poisons that permeates her fiction. Her first novel, The Mysterious Affair at Styles (1920), introduced Poirot. In 1926 she disappeared for eleven days after her husband Archie Christie asked for a divorce — a famous episode never fully explained. She later married archaeologist Max Mallowan and accompanied him on digs in the Middle East, which inspired novels such as Murder in Mesopotamia and Death on the Nile.

Works

Her most celebrated novels include The Murder of Roger Ackroyd (1926), Murder on the Orient Express (1934), And Then There Were None (1939) — the world's best-selling mystery — The ABC Murders, and A Murder Is Announced. The Mousetrap opened in London's West End in 1952 and ran continuously until the COVID-19 pandemic, the longest initial run of any play in history.

Legacy

Christie is often called the "Queen of Crime". Her plots, fair-play clues, and surprise resolutions codified the conventions of the Golden Age detective story, and her works have been adapted endlessly for film, television, radio, and stage. She was appointed a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1971.
