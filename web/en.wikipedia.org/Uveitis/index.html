Uveitis

Ciliary injection around the cornea in anterior uveitis.

Uveitis is inflammation of the uvea, the pigmented layer of the eye between the inner retina and the outer fibrous layer of sclera and cornea. The uvea consists of the iris, ciliary body, and choroid. Uveitis is classified anatomically as anterior, intermediate, posterior, or panuveitis, depending on the part of the uveal tract primarily affected; anterior uveitis (iritis) is the most common form.

Causes and symptoms

Causes include autoimmune and autoinflammatory disorders (such as ankylosing spondylitis, sarcoidosis, and Behçet's disease), infections (herpesviruses, toxoplasmosis, tuberculosis, syphilis), trauma, and masquerade conditions including intraocular lymphoma; in many cases no cause is found. Symptoms of anterior uveitis include eye pain, redness, photophobia, and blurred vision, with inflammatory cells and protein flare visible in the anterior chamber.

Treatment

Treatment aims to suppress inflammation and prevent complications such as synechiae, glaucoma, cataract, and macular oedema, which can cause permanent vision loss. First-line therapy is usually corticosteroids (topical, injected, or systemic), with dilating drops for pain and adhesion prevention; steroid-sparing immunosuppressive drugs and biologics such as adalimumab are used for chronic or severe disease.
