Lesbian

A lesbian is a homosexual woman, or more broadly a non-man, who is romantically or sexually attracted to women. The term is also used as an adjective describing female homosexuality or same-sex attraction between women. The word derives from the name of the Greek island of Lesbos, home of the 6th-century BCE poet Sappho, whose verses expressing love for women gave rise to the association.

History

Relationships between women are documented throughout history, though the modern concept of lesbian identity emerged only in the late nineteenth century, when sexologists such as Richard von Krafft-Ebing and Havelock Ellis classified female same-sex attraction as a distinct orientation. In the twentieth century, lesbian communities and culture developed in cities in Europe and North America, and lesbian feminism of the 1970s treated lesbianism as both a sexual identity and a political stance. Legal and social recognition has varied widely across countries and eras, from criminalisation and persecution to marriage equality in many jurisdictions in the twenty-first century.

Culture

Lesbian culture encompasses literature, film, art and community institutions, from the novels of Radclyffe Hall and the pulp fiction of the 1950s to contemporary media. Symbols associated with lesbian identity include the labrys, the black triangle, and various pride flags, notably the sunset flag of pink, orange and white stripes.
