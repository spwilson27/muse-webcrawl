Adrenergic receptor

The adrenergic receptors (adrenoceptors) are a class of G protein-coupled receptors targeted by the catecholamines noradrenaline (norepinephrine) and adrenaline (epinephrine). They mediate much of the sympathetic nervous system's "fight or flight" response.

Types

There are two main groups: alpha receptors (α1, α2) and beta receptors (β1, β2, β3), each with distinct tissue distributions and effects — for example β1 receptors increase heart rate and contractility, β2 receptors relax bronchial smooth muscle, and α1 receptors constrict blood vessels.

Clinical significance

Drugs acting on these receptors are widely used: beta blockers for hypertension and heart disease, β2 agonists such as salbutamol for asthma, and α-blockers for hypertension and prostate symptoms.
