Nouvelle-Aquitaine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nouvelle-Aquitaine (French for "New Aquitaine") is the largest administrative region in France, spanning the south-west of the country. It was created by the territorial reform of 2014–2016 through the merger of the former regions of Aquitaine, Poitou-Charentes, and Limousin, and took effect on 1 January 2016. Its capital and largest city is Bordeaux.

Geography

The region stretches from the foothills of the Massif Central in the east — the high plateaux of Limousin — to the Pyrenees on the Spanish border in the south, and along the Atlantic coast, the longest coastline of any French region. The Landes forest, one of the largest maritime pine forests in Europe, dominates the south-west, while the coast features famous resorts such as Biarritz, Arcachon, La Rochelle, and the Île de Ré, as well as the Dune of Pilat, the highest dune in Europe. The Gironde estuary, formed by the Garonne and Dordogne rivers, is the largest estuary in western Europe.

Economy and culture

Nouvelle-Aquitaine is France's leading agricultural region, renowned for the wines of Bordeaux, Cognac brandy, foie gras, oysters from Marennes-Oléron, and Agen prunes. Bordeaux's vineyards and historic port are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, as are the prehistoric decorated caves of the Vézère valley in Dordogne, including Lascaux. The region includes part of French Basque Country around Bayonne and the Béarn of Pau, and strong regional identities persist in Poitou, Limousin, Périgord, and Saintonge.

Tourism, aeronautics (around Bordeaux and the Basque coast), higher education, and research are major economic sectors; the region's universities in Bordeaux, Poitiers, Limoges, La Rochelle, and Pau serve around 180,000 students.

History

The region's name deliberately echoes the historic province of Aquitaine, a duchy once ruled by Eleanor of Aquitaine and, through her marriage to Henry II, attached to the English crown for three centuries before returning to France at the end of the Hundred Years' War. The provisional name "Aquitaine-Limousin-Poitou-Charentes" was replaced by "Nouvelle-Aquitaine" in 2016 after a public consultation.
