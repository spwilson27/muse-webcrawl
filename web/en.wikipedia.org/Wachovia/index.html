Wachovia

Wachovia Corporation was a diversified financial services company based in Charlotte, North Carolina, and for a time the fourth-largest bank holding company in the United States. Its name derived from "Wachau", the Latin form of the Wachau region of Austria, the homeland of the Moravian settlers who founded the North Carolina settlement where the bank originated.

History

Wachovia National Bank was founded in 1879 in Winston (later Winston-Salem), North Carolina. Through a long series of mergers — including the 2001 combination of the original Wachovia with First Union, which took the Wachovia name — the company grew into a national retail and commercial banking, wealth management, and brokerage business.

Collapse and acquisition

Wachovia was badly weakened by its 2006 acquisition of Golden West Financial, a California mortgage lender holding a large portfolio of adjustable-rate "option ARM" loans, just before the housing market collapsed. In September 2008, facing a quiet run on its deposits amid the financial crisis, regulators brokered an emergency sale; Wells Fargo ultimately acquired Wachovia whole in a deal valued at about $15.1 billion, completed on 31 December 2008. Wachovia branches were converted to the Wells Fargo brand by 2011.
