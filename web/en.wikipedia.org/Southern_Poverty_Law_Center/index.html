Southern Poverty Law Center (SPLC)
Founded: 1971
Founders: Morris Dees, Joseph J. Levin Jr., Julian Bond
Headquarters: Montgomery, Alabama
Type: nonprofit civil-rights legal advocacy organisation

The Southern Poverty Law Center (SPLC) is an American nonprofit legal advocacy organisation based in Montgomery, Alabama, specialising in civil rights litigation and public education. Founded in 1971 by attorneys Morris Dees and Joseph J. Levin Jr., with civil-rights leader Julian Bond as its first president, the SPLC became nationally known for lawsuits that bankrupted Ku Klux Klan organisations and for its monitoring of extremist and hate groups in the United States.

Work

The Center's litigation has included the 1987 judgment in Donald v. United Klans of America, which held the Klan liable for the lynching of Michael Donald, and cases on behalf of Vietnamese fishermen terrorised by the Texas Klan. Its Intelligence Project publishes the Intelligence Report and an annual census of hate groups; its Teaching Tolerance programme (now Learning for Justice) supplies anti-bias materials to schools. The SPLC also maintains the Civil Rights Memorial, designed by Maya Lin, honouring those killed in the civil rights movement.

Criticism and controversies

The SPLC's hate-group designations and its large endowment have drawn criticism from across the political spectrum, including defamation litigation it has settled. In 2019 co-founder Morris Dees was fired amid internal complaints about workplace culture, and senior leadership resigned; the organisation subsequently restructured and unionised.
