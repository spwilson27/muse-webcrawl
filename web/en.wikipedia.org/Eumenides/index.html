Eumenides

Eumenides (Greek: Εὐμενίδες, "the gracious ones") is the third and final play of the Oresteia trilogy by the ancient Greek playwright Aeschylus, following Agamemnon and The Libation Bearers. It was first performed in 458 BC, when the trilogy won first prize at the Dionysia festival in Athens.

Plot

Orestes, who killed his mother Clytemnestra to avenge his father Agamemnon, is pursued by the Erinyes (Furies), ancient goddesses of vengeance. Having been purified at Delphi under Apollo's protection, he flees to Athens, where the goddess Athena establishes a homicide court — the Areopagus — to try his case. Apollo argues in his defence, the Erinyes prosecute, and when the jury's vote is tied, Athena casts the deciding vote for acquittal.

Themes

Athena then persuades the Furies to accept a new role as benevolent guardians of Athens, renamed the Eumenides ("Kindly Ones") and given a sanctuary in the city. The play dramatizes the transition from blood vengeance to civic justice administered by law courts, and is thought to reflect contemporary Athenian legal reforms, including the recent curtailing of the Areopagus's powers by Ephialtes.

Legacy

The Oresteia is the only surviving trilogy of Greek tragedies, and Eumenides remains a foundational text in discussions of law, justice, and the origins of the jury trial.
