Chemical nomenclature

Chemical nomenclature is a set of rules to generate systematic names for chemical compounds, allowing each name to unambiguously correspond to one structure. The most widely used system is maintained by the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC).

IUPAC nomenclature covers organic compounds (based on carbon chains, functional groups, prefixes and suffixes, e.g. "-ol" for alcohols) and inorganic compounds (based on composition and oxidation states). Official recommendations appear in the "Blue Book" (organic) and "Red Book" (inorganic).

Alongside systematic names, many compounds retain traditional or trivial names, such as water, ammonia and acetic acid. Other systems include CAS index names and pharmaceutical INN names.
