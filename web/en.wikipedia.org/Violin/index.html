Violin

The violin, sometimes known as a fiddle, is a wooden chordophone with four strings tuned in fifths (G3, D4, A4, E5), played with a bow. It is the smallest and thus highest-pitched instrument in the violin family of modern orchestras.

History

The violin emerged in 16th-century northern Italy, with makers such as Andrea Amati, and reached near-modern form in the workshop of Antonio Stradivari in Cremona. Violins became central to Western classical music and are also prominent in folk traditions from Irish fiddle to Indian classical music.

Construction

A violin has a hollow body of spruce and maple, a neck and ebony fingerboard, four strings running over a bridge, and is sounded by drawing a horsehair bow across the strings or by plucking (pizzicato).
