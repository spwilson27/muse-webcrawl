Ari Þorgilsson (1067/68 – 9 November 1148), known as Ari the Learned (Icelandic: Ari fróði), was Iceland's earliest known historian and the author of the Íslendingabók (Book of the Icelanders), the oldest written account of Iceland's settlement and early history. A priest and chieftain (goði), he belonged to one of the leading families of the Icelandic Commonwealth.

Life

Ari was born at Helgafell in western Iceland; after his father's early death he was raised at Haukadalur by Hallur Þórarinsson and educated by Teitur Ísleifsson, son of Iceland's first bishop. He was ordained a priest and became a respected chieftain and lawspeaker-level authority, though by his time he was celebrated above all for learning — the later historian Snorri Sturluson praised his accuracy and named him among his sources.

The Íslendingabók

Written between about 1122 and 1133, the Íslendingabók is a concise vernacular history covering the Norse settlement of Iceland from about 870, the founding of the Althing in 930, the conversion to Christianity in 999/1000, the lives of the first bishops and a chronology of lawspeakers. Unusually for its era, Ari named his informants and distinguished reliable testimony from conjecture; he says he revised the work after criticism from bishops Þorlákur and Ketill and the priest Sæmundur Sigfússon. A longer work by Ari on the kings of Norway is lost.

Legacy

Ari's chronology underpins the dating of Icelandic settlement (the "landnám tephra" from the eruption of about 871±2 has broadly confirmed it) and his writings shaped all later Icelandic historiography, including Landnámabók and the sagas. He is regarded as the founder of Icelandic historical writing.
