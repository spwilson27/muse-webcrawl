Ride cymbal

The ride cymbal is a standard cymbal in most drum kits, usually the largest cymbal in the setup, commonly 20 inches (51 cm) in diameter. It maintains a steady rhythmic pattern rather than the accenting role of a crash cymbal, and the term "ride" refers to the ability to keep "riding" on it for an extended period.

Sound and playing

The ride cymbal produces a shimmering, sustained "ping" when struck with the tip of the drumstick on its bow. Its raised centre cup, the bell, gives a bright, cutting tone favoured in Latin and heavy rock playing. Striking the edge produces a crash-like wash.

History and use

The ride pattern became central in jazz, where drummers such as Jo Jones and Kenny Clarke shifted timekeeping from the bass drum and hi-hat to the ride cymbal, freeing the other limbs for comping. It remains fundamental in jazz, rock, funk, and metal.

Variants

Variants include sizzle cymbals, fitted with rivets; flat rides, without a bell, popularised by Roy Haynes; swish and pang cymbals; and crash/ride hybrids.
