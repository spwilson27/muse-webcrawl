Eyeglass prescription

An eyeglass prescription is an order written by an eyewear prescriber, such as an optometrist or ophthalmologist, that specifies the value of all parameters the prescriber has deemed necessary to construct or dispense corrective lenses appropriate for a patient. If an examination indicates that corrective lenses are appropriate, the prescriber generally provides the patient with an eyewear prescription at the conclusion of the exam.

A typical spectacle prescription

Components

Prescriptions are written for the right eye (OD, oculus dexter) and the left eye (OS, oculus sinister) separately. The principal values are:

AbbreviationMeaning
SPHSphere power in dioptres, correcting myopia (negative) or hyperopia (positive)
CYLCylinder power, correcting astigmatism
AXISThe orientation of the cylinder, from 0 to 180 degrees
ADDAdditional magnifying power for reading or bifocal lenses, used in presbyopia
PDPupillary distance, the distance between the centres of the pupils

Legal status

In many jurisdictions, patients are entitled to receive a copy of their prescription after an eye examination, allowing them to purchase lenses from any supplier. Prescriptions usually have an expiry date, commonly one or two years.
