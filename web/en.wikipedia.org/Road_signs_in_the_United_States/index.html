Road signs in the United States

Road signs in the United States are standardised by the Federal Highway Administration's Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD), first published in 1935, with state supplements permitted. Unlike most countries, the United States relies heavily on text legends rather than pictorial symbols, though symbol signs have been increasingly adopted.

Categories

The MUTCD classifies signs as regulatory (such as the red octagonal STOP sign and rectangular white speed limit signs), warning (usually yellow diamonds; orange in work zones), and guide signs (green for highways, blue for services, brown for recreation). The stop sign's distinctive octagonal shape, adopted in the 1920s, allows it to be recognised from behind or when obscured.

Typography and route markers

Highway guide signs use the FHWA Series fonts (Highway Gothic), with the mixed-case Clearview typeface approved for optional use since 2004. Route markers include the black-and-white Interstate shield, the US Highway shield, and a wide variety of state and county markers. The current MUTCD edition is the eleventh, published in December 2023.
