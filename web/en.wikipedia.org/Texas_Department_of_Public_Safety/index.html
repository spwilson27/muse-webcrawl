Texas Department of Public Safety

The Texas Department of Public Safety (Texas DPS) is a department of the state government of Texas. The DPS is responsible for statewide law enforcement and driver license administration, and is headquartered in Austin. Its best-known division is the Texas Highway Patrol, whose troopers patrol the state's highways; the department also includes the storied Texas Rangers Division, one of the oldest law-enforcement organisations in North America.

History

The department was created in 1935, when the Texas Legislature consolidated the Texas Highway Patrol and the Texas Rangers under a single agency during the governorship of James V. Allred. The Rangers trace their own origins to 1823, when Stephen F. Austin organised a small group of men to protect settlers in Mexican Texas.

Divisions

  • Texas Highway Patrol — traffic enforcement and criminal interdiction on rural highways.
  • Texas Rangers — major-crimes investigation, public corruption, and cold cases.
  • Driver License Division — issues driver licences and identification cards.
  • Texas Division of Emergency Management — coordinated the state's disaster response (transferred to Texas A&M University System in 2019).
  • Intelligence and Counter Terrorism Division — operates fusion centres and threat assessment.
  • Criminal Investigations Division and the Texas State Crime Laboratory.

Structure

The department is governed by the five-member Public Safety Commission, appointed by the governor. Its director holds the rank of colonel. DPS operates regional offices across Texas and employs several thousand commissioned officers and civilian staff.
