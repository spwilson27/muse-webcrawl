Language interpretation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Interpreters at work in a simultaneous interpretation booth]

Language interpretation is the facilitation of oral or sign-language communication between users of different languages, performed by an interpreter. It is distinct from translation, which deals with written text: the interpreter renders a message from a source language into a target language in real time or near-real time, conveying meaning, register, and tone rather than word-for-word equivalence.

Modes

Simultaneous interpretation requires the interpreter to render the message while the speaker continues talking, typically from a soundproof booth with listeners receiving the output through headphones. The technique was developed for large multilingual conferences in the twentieth century and was famously used at the Nuremberg trials; it is the standard mode at the United Nations and the European Union. Consecutive interpretation involves the interpreter speaking after the source speaker has finished a segment, often aided by a specialized note-taking system; it is common in diplomatic, legal, and business settings. Other modes include whispered interpretation (chuchotage) for one or two listeners, liaison interpreting for small groups, and signed language interpretation.

Settings

Interpreters work in conference, court, medical, military, and community settings, each with its own demands and codes of ethics emphasizing accuracy, impartiality, and confidentiality. Community and medical interpreting have increasingly become professionalized, with certification requirements in many jurisdictions.

Technology

Remote simultaneous interpretation expanded rapidly with videoconferencing, while machine interpreting systems based on automatic speech recognition and machine translation have entered limited use, though professional interpreters remain standard for high-stakes communication.
