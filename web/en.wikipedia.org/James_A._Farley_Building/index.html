The James A. Farley Building is a monumental Beaux-Arts building in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, occupying the two full city blocks between Eighth and Ninth Avenues and 31st and 33rd Streets, across Eighth Avenue from Pennsylvania Station and Madison Square Garden. Completed in stages between 1914 and the 1930s to designs by McKim, Mead & White, it served for a century as New York's main United States Postal Service facility.

Architecture and inscription

The granite building, raised over the tracks of Pennsylvania Station, is famed for its Corinthian colonnade along Eighth Avenue — the longest colonnade of its kind in the city — and for the inscription spanning its frieze: "Neither snow nor rain nor heat nor gloom of night stays these couriers from the swift completion of their appointed rounds." Adapted from Herodotus's praise of the Persian mounted messengers, the sentence (selected by architect William Mitchell Kendall) became the unofficial motto of the US Postal Service. The building was renamed in 1982 for James A. Farley, Postmaster General under Franklin D. Roosevelt and a powerful Democratic Party figure.

Moynihan Train Hall

As mail operations declined and moved elsewhere, the building's vast interior was repurposed. On 1 January 2021 the Moynihan Train Hall opened within its western half, converting the former mail-sorting hall into a soaring Amtrak and Long Island Rail Road concourse with a glass skylight, realising a plan championed by Senator Daniel Patrick Moynihan to relieve the cramped subterranean Penn Station opposite. The redevelopment also includes Meta (formerly Facebook) as a major office tenant, retail space, and a smaller retail post office continuing postal operations on Eighth Avenue.
