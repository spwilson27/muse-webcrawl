The Kwai Tsing Container Terminals are the principal container port facilities of Hong Kong, located in the Kwai Chung and Tsing Yi districts of the New Territories, in the upper reaches of Victoria Harbour near Rambler Channel. The complex comprises nine container terminals with 24 berths, and for decades made Hong Kong one of the busiest container ports in the world.

Development

The first terminal at Kwai Chung opened in 1972 as Hong Kong's trade boomed, and successive terminals were built through the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s, with Terminal 9 on Tsing Yi Island completed in the early 2000s. The port grew with China's export manufacturing, handling over 20 million TEUs annually at its peak in the 2000s, when Hong Kong repeatedly ranked as the world's busiest container port.

Operations and decline in ranking

Terminals are operated by private companies including Hongkong International Terminals (Hutchison Port Holdings), Modern Terminals, COSCO-HIT and Asia Container Terminals. Since the 2010s, Hong Kong's ranking has slipped as mainland ports such as Shanghai, Shenzhen and Guangzhou expanded and direct calls replaced transshipment through Hong Kong; throughput fell to roughly 14–17 million TEU in the early 2020s. The port remains a major transshipment hub with extensive mid-stream operations, in which smaller vessels transfer containers to anchored ships.

Transport links

The terminals are linked to the urban road network and the airport via the Tsing Yi bridges and the Stonecutters Bridge, once among the world's longest cable-stayed bridges. Most cargo moves by truck and barge, as the port has no direct rail freight connection.
