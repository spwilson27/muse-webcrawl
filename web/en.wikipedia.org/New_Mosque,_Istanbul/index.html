New Mosque (Yeni Cami)
LocationEminönü, Fatih, Istanbul, Turkey
Construction1597–1665 (with long interruption)
PatronsSafiye Sultan; completed under Turhan Hatice Sultan

The New Mosque (Turkish: Yeni Cami), originally the Valide Sultan Mosque, is an Ottoman imperial mosque located in the Eminönü quarter of Istanbul, Turkey, on the southern shore of the Golden Horn near the Galata Bridge and the Spice Bazaar. Completed in 1665 after a construction history of nearly seventy years, it is one of the most prominent landmarks of the Istanbul skyline and among the largest mosques in the city.

History

Construction began in 1597 under Safiye Sultan, wife of Murad III and mother of Mehmed III, to a design by the royal architect Davud Ağa, a successor of Mimar Sinan. Work stopped after Safiye's fall from power in 1603 and the site languished for more than half a century, deteriorating and suffering fire damage in 1660. The queen mother Turhan Hatice Sultan, mother of Mehmed IV, revived the project, and the mosque was completed and inaugurated in 1665.

Architecture

The mosque follows the classical Ottoman scheme of a central dome supported by four half-domes and four smaller corner domes, with cascades of subsidiary domes over the courtyard and side galleries — an arrangement recalling the Şehzade and Sultan Ahmed mosques. The interior is richly decorated with İznik tiles, carved marble, and a gilded, tile-covered royal loge connected to the royal pavilion (Hünkâr Kasrı). Two slender pencil minarets rise from the courtyard corners. The complex originally included a hospital, primary school, public baths, a türbe (tomb), fountains and shops whose rents supported the foundation; the adjacent Egyptian (Spice) Bazaar formed part of its endowment.
