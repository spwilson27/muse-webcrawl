Prambanan

Prambanan is a 9th-century Hindu temple compound in Central Java, Indonesia, dedicated to the Trimurti — Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver and Shiva the destroyer. It is the largest Hindu temple complex in Indonesia and one of the largest in Southeast Asia, and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1991.

History

Built around 850 by the Mataram Kingdom — under Rakai Pikatan and his successors — near the Buddhist Borobudur built by their rivals, Prambanan marked Hindu resurgence in Java. It was abandoned within a century as the court moved east, and collapsed in earthquakes; its ruins were rediscovered in the Dutch colonial era and reconstruction began in the 1930s. The main Shiva temple was restored by 1953; the 2006 Yogyakarta earthquake damaged the complex, which was repaired and remains an active Hindu worship site, notably for Nyepi-related and other ceremonies.

Architecture

The compound originally held about 240 temples in concentric squares. The inner zone's three great temples face statues of their gods, with the 47-metre Shiva temple at the centre flanked by Vishnu and Brahma temples and three smaller temples for their animal vehicles. Relief panels narrate the Ramayana (and, on the Brahma temple, the Krishnayana). Local legend attributes the temples to the mythical Bandung Bondowoso building a thousand temples in a night for Princess Rara Jonggrang, whose statue is identified with the Durga image in the Shiva temple.
