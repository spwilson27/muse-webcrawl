Linear differential equation

A linear differential equation is a differential equation that is defined by a linear polynomial in the unknown function and its derivatives. Linearity means that the equation involves the unknown function and its derivatives only to the first power and not multiplied together. Such equations contrast with nonlinear differential equations, and they have a well-developed theory: the general solution of a homogeneous linear equation is a superposition of particular solutions.

Ordinary linear equations

A linear ordinary differential equation of order n has the form an(x)y⁽ⁿ⁾ + … + a1(x)y′ + a0(x)y = g(x). If g(x) = 0 the equation is homogeneous, and its solutions form a vector space of dimension n. Equations with constant coefficients can be solved via the characteristic polynomial; repeated roots introduce polynomial factors times exponentials. Non-homogeneous equations are solved by methods such as variation of parameters or undetermined coefficients.

Partial differential equations

Linear partial differential equations, such as the heat equation, wave equation, and Laplace's equation, admit solution techniques including separation of variables, Fourier and Laplace transforms, eigenfunction expansions, and Green's functions. The linearity of these equations underlies much of classical mathematical physics.
