Orval Eugene Faubus (January 7, 1910 – December 14, 1994) was an American politician who served as the 36th governor of Arkansas from 1955 to 1967, winning election to six consecutive two-year terms. He is best remembered for his role in the Little Rock Crisis of 1957, when he ordered the Arkansas National Guard to block the entry of nine African American students — the "Little Rock Nine" — into Little Rock Central High School, in defiance of the United States Supreme Court's desegregation ruling in Brown v. Board of Education.

Little Rock Crisis

Faubus initially presented himself as a racial moderate, but facing a segregationist challenger in the 1956 primary he embraced the defense of segregation. On September 4, 1957, National Guardsmen turned away the Little Rock Nine. After a federal court order, Faubus withdrew the Guard, and when the students entered amid mob unrest on September 23, President Dwight D. Eisenhower federalized the Arkansas National Guard and dispatched soldiers of the 101st Airborne Division to enforce the court order and protect the students. In 1958 Faubus closed Little Rock's public high schools for a year rather than permit integration — the "Lost Year."

Later career

Faubus remained popular with Arkansas voters for a time and won re-election through 1964. He left office in 1967, later made unsuccessful comeback attempts, and supported subsequent governors. Historians generally rank him among the emblematic segregationist officials of the Massive Resistance era, though his earlier career included progressive positions on taxation and public works.
