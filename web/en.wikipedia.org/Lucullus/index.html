Lucullus

Lucius Licinius Lucullus
Bornc. 118 BC
Diedc. 57/56 BC
OccupationGeneral, consul (74 BC)
Known forThird Mithridatic War; proverbial luxury

Lucius Licinius Lucullus (c. 118 – c. 57/56 BC) was a Roman general and statesman, closely connected with Lucius Cornelius Sulla. He won fame as commander in the Third Mithridatic War against Mithridates VI of Pontus, and became proverbial for the wealth and luxury of his retirement — the English word "lucullan" derives from his name, and he is credited with introducing the cherry to Europe.

Early career

Lucullus served under Sulla in the Social War and the First Mithridatic War, commanding the fleet that won the battles of Tenedos and the Neoptolemus. As quaestor and later proquaestor in Asia he reformed the province's crushing finances, earning the hostility of the Roman tax farmers (publicani) but the gratitude of the cities.

Third Mithridatic War

As consul in 74 BC, Lucullus obtained command against Mithridates VI. Over the following years he relieved Cyzicus, won victories at Cabira and elsewhere, invaded Pontus and then Armenia, defeating Tigranes the Great at Tigranocerta (69 BC) and Artaxata. Mutinies among his long-serving troops and political intrigue at Rome undermined his position, and in 66 BC the command passed to Pompey under the Lex Manilia.

Retirement and luxury

Blocked for years from his triumph (finally celebrated in 63 BC), Lucullus withdrew into private life at his great estates, including the Gardens of Lucullus on Rome's Pincian Hill and villas near Tusculum and Naples. His banquets became legendary — Plutarch records his retort to a servant that "Lucullus dines with Lucullus tonight" — and his library was one of the finest of the ancient world. He is credited with introducing the cherry and the apricot to Italy from Asia Minor.
