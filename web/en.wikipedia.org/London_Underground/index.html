The London Underground — commonly known as the Tube — is a rapid transit system serving Greater London and parts of the adjacent counties of Buckinghamshire, Essex and Hertfordshire in England. It is the world's oldest underground railway, having opened in 1863 with the Metropolitan Railway between Paddington and Farringdon.

Network

The system has 11 lines and 272 stations, with about 402 km of track, of which roughly 45% is actually underground. The lines include the deep-level "tube" lines — Piccadilly, Northern, Central, Bakerloo, Victoria, Jubilee and Waterloo & City — and the sub-surface lines Metropolitan, District, Circle and Hammersmith & City. The iconic Tube map, designed by Harry Beck in 1931, pioneered the schematic diagram style used by transit systems worldwide.

History

Early lines were built by competing private companies using cut-and-cover tunnels and steam trains. Electrification and the deep-level tubes followed from 1890, when the City and South London Railway opened. The lines were unified under the London Passenger Transport Board in 1933, passed to public ownership in 1948, and since 2000 have been operated by Transport for London. The Elizabeth line (Crossrail) opened in 2022 as a separate but connected service.
