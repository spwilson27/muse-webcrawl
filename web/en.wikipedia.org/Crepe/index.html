Crepe

Type: Very thin pancake
Origin: Brittany, France
Main ingredients: Wheat flour, eggs, milk, butter

A crêpe or crepe is a very thin type of pancake, originating in Brittany, France, made from a wheat-based batter cooked on a hot griddle or pan. Crêpes are served with sweet or savoury fillings and have become a popular street food and dessert across the world.

Types

Sweet crêpes (crêpes sucrées) use wheat flour and are filled with chocolate-hazelnut spread, jam, fruit, whipped cream, or simply sugar and lemon; the famous crêpe Suzette is flambéed with orange butter and liqueur. Savoury crêpes (crêpes salées), called galettes in Brittany, use buckwheat flour and take fillings such as ham, egg, and cheese — the classic galette complète. Buckwheat galettes are traditionally paired with Breton cider.

Preparation

The batter — flour, eggs, milk, melted butter, sugar, and salt — is rested, then ladled in a thin layer onto a large flat griddle (a billig) and spread with a rozell, a T-shaped wooden spreader. The crêpe cooks in under a minute per side. Candlemas (February 2), known in France as La Chandeleur, is traditionally crêpe day, when diners flip a crêpe one-handed while holding a coin for luck and prosperity.

Related dishes

Thin pancakes of similar kind exist in many cuisines, including Russian blini, Jewish blintzes, Hungarian palacsinta, Ethiopian injera (a fermented flatbread), and the dosa of South India.
