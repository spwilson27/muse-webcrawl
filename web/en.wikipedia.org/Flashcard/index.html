Flashcard

A flashcard or flash card is a card bearing information on both sides, which is intended to be used as an aid in memorisation. Each flashcard typically bears a question or prompt on one side and its answer on the other. Flashcards are widely used for learning vocabulary, historical dates, formulae and any subject matter that can be learned through question-and-answer format, by students, language learners and professionals.

Learning method

Flashcard study exploits the testing effect: actively recalling an answer strengthens memory more than re-reading it. Used with spaced repetition — reviewing cards at expanding intervals, with difficult cards shown more often — flashcards become a highly efficient long-term memorisation technique. The Leitner system, proposed by Sebastian Leitner in the 1970s, organises physical cards into boxes sorted by how well the learner knows each item; cards answered correctly move to a less frequently reviewed box, and mistakes return to the first box.

Digital flashcards

Software implementations automate scheduling using algorithms derived from the SuperMemo research of Piotr Woźniak. Popular applications include Anki (free and open source, with a large library of shared decks), Quizlet, Memrise and Duolingo-style course platforms. Digital decks can embed images, audio and cloze deletions, and synchronise across devices. Medical, law and language students are heavy users; large shared Anki decks are standard study tools in medical education.

History

Paper flashcards have been used since at least the 19th century; Favell Lee Mortimer's Reading Without Tears (1857) taught reading with flash cards. In the 20th century they became staples of classrooms and self-instruction, sold commercially in boxed sets covering subjects from arithmetic to anatomy. The Leitner box and computer-based spaced repetition transformed the simple card into a systematic learning technology.
