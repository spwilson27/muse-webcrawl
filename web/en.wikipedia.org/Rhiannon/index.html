Rhiannon

Rhiannon is a major figure in Welsh mythology, appearing in the prose tales of the Mabinogion. She is an otherworldly woman — associated with horses, birds with magical song, and a magical bag that can never be filled — who chooses Pwyll, prince of Dyfed, as her husband against her family's wishes, and later suffers false accusation and penance before her name is cleared. Her name derives from the reconstructed Brittonic *Rīgantonā, "great queen," and she is widely thought to preserve the memory of a Celtic goddess, possibly related to the Gaulish horse goddess Epona.

First Branch: Pwyll

In the First Branch of the Mabinogi (Pwyll Pendefig Dyfed), Pwyll encounters Rhiannon riding a large white horse on the mound of Gorsedd Arberth; his riders cannot catch her horse, though it never seems to gallop, until Pwyll calls out and asks her to stop. Rhiannon explains she is being forced to marry Gwawl ap Clud against her will and prefers Pwyll. At the wedding feast a year later, Pwyll rashly promises a disguised Gwawl whatever he asks; Rhiannon devises a stratagem — a small magical bag that can never be filled — by which Gwawl is trapped inside the bag and beaten until he surrenders his claim.

Rhiannon bears Pwyll a son, but the child vanishes on the night of his birth while her attendants sleep; fearing blame, the women smear puppy blood on the sleeping Rhiannon and accuse her of infanticide. As penance she must sit at the horse-block by the gate for seven years, telling her story to travelers and offering to carry them on her back. The child, meanwhile, is raised by Teyrnon Twrf Liant, who found him after driving off the monstrous claw (later identified with Gwawl's vengeance) that stole his foals; because of the boy's resemblance to Pwyll and his affinity with horses, Teyrnon returns him, and he is named Pryderi — "care" — for the anxiety his disappearance caused.

Third Branch: Manawydan

In the Third Branch (Manawydan fab Llŷr), Pryderi is grown, Pwyll is dead, and Rhiannon marries Manawydan, one of the tale's protagonists. When a magical mist empties Dyfed of its people and Pryderi and Rhiannon are trapped by an enchanted fortress (Rhiannon stuck fast holding a golden bowl), Manawydan eventually captures the mouse that is the transformed wife of the enchanter Llwyd ap Cil Coed and bargains for their release and the lifting of the curse — vengeance by Gwawl's friend for Gwawl's humiliation in the bag. Rhiannon's birds (Adar Rhiannon), mentioned in the Second Branch, sing at Harlech to Bran's company for seven years; their song was famed to wake the dead and lull the living to sleep.

Interpretation

Scholars associate Rhiannon with Epona, the widely worshipped Gaulish goddess of horses, on the strength of her horse imagery, her penance at the horse-block, and the horse-theft motif surrounding Pryderi's birth. Her role as a wronged and patient queen who remains dignified under humiliation also makes her a notable example of the "calumniated wife" folktale type in medieval literature.

In popular culture

Rhiannon is the subject of the Fleetwood Mac song "Rhiannon" (1975), written by Stevie Nicks, who encountered the name in a novel and later learned of the Welsh figure. She features prominently in Evangeline Walton's Mabinogion retellings and in other modern fantasy, and is venerated by some contemporary Pagans as a goddess.
