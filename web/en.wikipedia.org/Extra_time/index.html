Extra time

Extra time is an additional period of play used in association football and some other sports to determine a winner when a knockout match ends level after regulation time, when a replay is impractical. In football the standard format is two periods of 15 minutes; if the scores remain level, the match is usually decided by a penalty shoot-out.

Football

Extra time is used in knockout competitions such as the FIFA World Cup knockout stage, the UEFA Champions League and domestic cup competitions. The teams change ends after 15 minutes with only a brief pause. Extra time must be distinguished from stoppage (injury) time, which is added by the referee at the end of each half to compensate for interruptions.

Abolished variants

To reduce the defensive play extra time often produced, FIFA and UEFA experimented with the golden goal — the first goal in extra time wins immediately — at the 1998 and 2002 World Cups, and the silver goal, in which a team leading at half-time of extra time wins, in the early 2000s. Both were abandoned in 2004, and the laws returned to playing the full 30 minutes. The away goals rule, once used to avoid extra time in two-legged ties, was abolished by UEFA in 2021, making extra time more frequent in European competition.

Other sports

Rugby, field hockey, ice hockey (overtime), and Gaelic games also use extra time or overtime formats, with rules varying from sudden death to fixed periods followed by shoot-outs.
