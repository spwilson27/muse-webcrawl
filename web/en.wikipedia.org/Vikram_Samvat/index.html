Vikram Samvat

Vikram Samvat (abbreviated VS), also known as the Vikrami calendar, is a historical Hindu lunisolar calendar whose epoch begins in 57 BCE. Tradition attributes its founding to the legendary king Vikramaditya of Ujjain, said to have established the era to commemorate a victory over the Sakas. The Vikram Samvat year is roughly 57 years ahead of the Gregorian year: for example, VS 2081 began in 2024 CE.

Structure

The calendar counts lunar months named Chaitra, Vaishakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadrapada, Ashwin, Kartika, Margashirsha, Pausha, Magha, and Phalguna. Because twelve lunar months fall about eleven days short of the solar year, an intercalary month (adhika masa) is inserted roughly every 32–33 months to keep the calendar aligned with the seasons. In most of northern India the year begins with Chaitra (March–April); in Gujarat the year begins with Kartika, the day after Diwali. The era likely began as the Krita or Malava era of western India before becoming associated with Vikramaditya in inscriptions from about the 9th century CE.

Use

Vikram Samvat remains the official calendar of Nepal (where it is called Bikram Sambat), used for civil purposes with the new year in mid-April based on the solar reckoning of the Indian national tradition, and it is used throughout India for religious festivals and traditional dating. It stands alongside the other major Indian era, the Shaka era (beginning 78 CE), which underlies the Indian national calendar.
