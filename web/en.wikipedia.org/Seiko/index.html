Seiko

Seiko Group Corporation, commonly known as Seiko, is a Japanese manufacturer of watches, clocks, electronic devices, semiconductors, jewellery and optical products. Founded in 1881 by Kintarō Hattori in Tokyo's Ginza district, it is one of the world's best-known watch brands and a pioneer of quartz timekeeping.

History

Hattori opened a shop selling and repairing clocks in 1881; in 1892 he established the Seikosha factory to manufacture wall clocks. The company produced Japan's first wristwatch, the Laurel, in 1913, and adopted the Seiko brand name in 1924. Postwar Seiko competed fiercely in chronometer trials at Neuchâtel and Geneva, dominating the observatory competitions of the late 1960s.

On 25 December 1969 Seiko introduced the Astron 35SQ, the world's first commercially available quartz wristwatch. Its accuracy and falling cost triggered the "quartz crisis" that upended the Swiss watch industry. Seiko went on to develop the Spring Drive movement, combining mechanical mainspring power with electronic regulation, and in 2012 released the Astron GPS Solar, which sets itself using satellite signals.

Brands and products

The company sells watches across a wide range, from the affordable Seiko 5 line to the luxury Grand Seiko brand (relaunched internationally as an independent marque in 2017) and the haute-horlogerie Credor line. The wider Seiko group historically included Seiko Epson, known for printers and the invention of the quartz watch's mass-production techniques, and Seiko Instruments. Seiko has served as official timer at numerous Olympic Games and World Athletics championships, and its clocks are a familiar feature of Japanese railway stations.
