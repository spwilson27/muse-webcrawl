Ahmad ibn Hanbal

Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Hanbal
Born: November 780, Baghdad
Died: 2 August 855, Baghdad
Occupation: jurist, theologian, hadith scholar
Known for: founder of the Hanbali school; the Musnad

Ahmad ibn Hanbal (Arabic: أحمد بن حنبل‎; November 780 – 2 August 855) was an Arab Muslim jurist, theologian, traditionist and ascetic from Baghdad. He is the eponymous founder of the Hanbali school (madhhab) of Islamic jurisprudence, one of the four major schools of Sunni law, and is regarded by Sunnis as one of the foremost authorities on the hadith (reports of the Prophet Muhammad's words and deeds).

Life

Orphaned young and raised in modest circumstances, Ahmad studied under many teachers, including al-Shafi'i, and travelled widely in search of hadith. His reputation rests on his uncompromising insistence that law and doctrine be derived directly from the Qur'an and the Sunna, with minimal role for personal reasoning (ra'y) or speculative theology.

The Mihna

During the mihna, the inquisition instituted by the caliph al-Ma'mun (833–848) to enforce the doctrine that the Qur'an was created, Ahmad refused to assent and was imprisoned and flogged under al-Ma'mun's successor al-Mu'tasim. His steadfastness made him a popular hero in Baghdad, and the eventual reversal of the policy under al-Mutawakkil marked the ascendancy of traditionalist Sunnism. His great compilation, the Musnad, arranged by narrator, contains some 27,000 hadith reports. Ahmad died in Baghdad in 855; his funeral reportedly drew enormous crowds.
