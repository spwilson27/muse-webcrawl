Incitement

Incitement is the encouragement of another person to commit a crime or unlawful act. In many legal systems it is an inchoate offence: the inciter may be criminally liable even if the encouraged act is never carried out. Closely related concepts include solicitation, counselling, aiding and abetting, and conspiracy.

Common law

At common law, incitement consisted of persuading, encouraging or coercing another to commit an offence. In England and Wales the common-law offence was abolished by the Serious Crime Act 2007, which replaced it with statutory offences of intentionally encouraging or assisting crime and encouraging or assisting crime believing it will be committed.

United States

In United States constitutional law, speech that incites unlawful conduct is protected by the First Amendment unless it satisfies the test in Brandenburg v. Ohio (1969): advocacy may be punished only where it is directed to inciting or producing imminent lawless action and is likely to incite or produce such action. Separately, federal and state statutes punish solicitation to commit crimes.

International law

International law prohibits direct and public incitement to commit genocide, an offence under the Genocide Convention and the Rome Statute, and various instruments restrict incitement to discrimination, hostility or violence against protected groups.
