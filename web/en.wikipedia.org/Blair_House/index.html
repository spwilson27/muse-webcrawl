Blair House

Blair House, the President's Official Guest House, Washington, D.C.

Blair House, also known as The President's Official Guest House, is an official residence in Washington, D.C., used primarily to host visiting heads of state and other distinguished guests of the President of the United States. It is located at 1651 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, directly across from the White House and beside the Eisenhower Executive Office Building.

History

The house was built in 1824 for Joseph Lovett and was purchased in 1836 by Francis Preston Blair, a newspaper editor and political adviser, whose family owned it for a century. The United States government leased and then purchased the property in the 1940s, during the Second World War, when the volume of official visitors made a dedicated guest house necessary. The Blair House complex today comprises four interconnected townhouses with more than 120 rooms, larger in floor area than the White House itself.

Notable events

In 1950, while the White House was under renovation, President Harry S. Truman lived at Blair House, where on November 1, 1950, two Puerto Rican nationalists attempted to assassinate him; White House police officer Leslie Coffelt was killed defending the president. Incoming presidents traditionally stay at Blair House before their inauguration, and visiting leaders sign its guest book and often hold meetings and press events there. The residence is administered by the Department of State's Office of the Chief of Protocol with support from the Blair House Restoration Fund.
