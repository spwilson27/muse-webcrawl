Taksim (politics)

Taksim (Turkish for "division" / "partition") — the Turkish Cypriot political goal of partitioning the island of Cyprus. Advocated from the 1950s by Fazıl Küçük and Rauf Denktaş as the counterpart to Greek Cypriot enosis.

Taksim (Turkish: "division" or "partition" — from Arabic taqsīm, "distribution") was the Turkish Cypriot political objective of partitioning Cyprus into Turkish and Greek sectors, advanced during the Cyprus dispute of the mid-20th century. It arose as a counter-slogan to the Greek Cypriot aim of enosis (union with Greece).

Origins

In the 1950s, as the EOKA campaign pursued enosis, Turkish Cypriot leaders — above all Dr Fazıl Küçük and later Rauf Denktaş — argued that if the island's status were to change, it should be divided, with a Turkish Cypriot zone joining Turkey. The slogan "Taksim ya ölüm" ("Partition or death") was chanted at mass rallies in 1957–58 amid intercommunal violence.

Development

The 1960 Republic of Cyprus was founded instead on a bi-communal partnership rejecting both enosis and taksim. After the intercommunal clashes of 1963–64 and the Greek junta-backed coup of 1974, Turkey intervened militarily, and the island was effectively divided along the Attila Line — a de facto partition that many observers regard as taksim realized in practice, followed by the Turkish Cypriot unilateral declaration of the "Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus" in 1983, recognized only by Turkey. Official Turkish Cypriot policy later shifted to support for a two-state solution or confederation.
