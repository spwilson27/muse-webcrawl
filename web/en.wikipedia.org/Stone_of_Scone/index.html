Stone of Scone

The Stone of Scone (Scottish Gaelic: An Lia Fàil), also known as the Stone of Destiny, is an oblong block of red sandstone used for centuries in the coronation of Scottish monarchs. It was later used in the coronations of English and British monarchs as part of the Coronation Chair at Westminster Abbey.

History

Scottish kings were inaugurated sitting on the stone at Scone Abbey in Perthshire from at least the ninth century. In 1296, Edward I of England seized it as a spoil of war and had it fitted into a wooden chair in Westminster Abbey, where every subsequent English and British monarch (with few exceptions) has been crowned above it. Legends connect it with the biblical Jacob's Pillow and the Irish Lia Fáil at Tara.

Theft and return

On Christmas Day 1950, four Scottish students removed the stone from Westminster Abbey; it broke in two during the theft and was later repaired and recovered at Arbroath Abbey. In 1996, the British government returned the stone to Scotland, where it is displayed at Perth Museum (having previously been at Edinburgh Castle), on condition that it be transported to London for future coronations — as it was for the coronation of Charles III in 2023.
