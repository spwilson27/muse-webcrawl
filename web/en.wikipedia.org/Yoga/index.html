Yoga (Sanskrit: योग, "yoke" or "union") is a group of physical, mental, and spiritual practices or disciplines that originated in ancient India. Yoga aims to still the mind and unite the individual self with a higher reality, and its practices include meditation, breath control (pranayama), ethical disciplines, and physical postures (asanas).

History

The roots of yoga reach back to the Vedic period; seals from the Indus Valley Civilisation depict figures in seated postures sometimes identified as yogic. Philosophical yoga developed in the Upanishads and in the traditions of Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. Around the early centuries CE, Patanjali compiled the Yoga Sutras, the foundational text of classical (Raja) yoga, describing an eight-limbed path (ashtanga). Medieval texts such as the Hatha Yoga Pradipika systematised physical techniques intended to prepare the body for meditation.

Modern yoga

In the late 19th and 20th centuries, teachers such as Swami Vivekananda, Tirumalai Krishnamacharya, B. K. S. Iyengar, and Pattabhi Jois brought yoga to the West and developed posture-based forms. Modern yoga as exercise emphasises asanas, sequences such as the Sun Salutation, and breathing, and is practised worldwide for fitness, flexibility, and stress relief. In 2014 the United Nations proclaimed 21 June as the International Day of Yoga, and in 2016 yoga was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Goals and philosophy

Traditional goals of yoga include moksha (liberation from the cycle of rebirth) and, in some schools, union with the divine. Classical yoga distinguishes the pure consciousness (purusha) from nature (prakriti) and seeks kaivalya, the isolation and freedom of the seer. Other traditions frame yoga as devotion (bhakti), knowledge (jnana), or selfless action (karma).
