Imola Circuit

The Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari at Imola.

The Imola Circuit, officially the Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari, is a 4.909 km (3.050 mi) motor racing circuit in the town of Imola, in the Emilia-Romagna region of Italy, about 40 km east of Bologna. The circuit has an FIA Grade One licence and runs anti-clockwise. It is named after Ferrari founder Enzo Ferrari and his son Alfredo ("Dino").

History

Racing began at Imola on public roads before the permanent circuit opened in 1953, initially for motorcycle events. The track hosted the Italian Grand Prix in 1980 and then, from 1981 to 2006, the San Marino Grand Prix of Formula One. The circuit is forever associated with the black weekend of the 1994 San Marino Grand Prix, when Roland Ratzenberger and three-time world champion Ayrton Senna were killed in separate crashes, prompting sweeping safety reforms throughout Formula One and extensive modifications to the track, including chicanes at Tamburello and Villeneuve.

Return to Formula One

After renovations, Formula One returned to Imola in 2020 with the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix, initially during the COVID-19-affected calendar, and the event has remained on the schedule in subsequent seasons. The circuit also hosts rounds of the Superbike World Championship, endurance racing, and historic events, and lies close to Ferrari's heartland in Maranello and Modena.
