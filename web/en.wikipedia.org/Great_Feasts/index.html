Great Feasts

A Great Feast being celebrated in an Orthodox church.

The Great Feasts (Greek: Μεγάλες Ἑορτές) are the twelve most important feast days in the liturgical year of the Eastern Orthodox Church and those Eastern Catholic Churches that follow the Byzantine Rite. They rank below only Pascha (Easter) in significance and are divided into feasts of the Lord (Despotike) and feasts of the Theotokos (Theometorike).

The twelve Great Feasts

The twelve Great Feasts are:

  1. The Nativity of the Theotokos (8 September)
  2. The Exaltation of the Holy Cross (14 September)
  3. The Presentation of the Theotokos (21 November)
  4. The Nativity of Christ (25 December)
  5. The Theophany (Epiphany) of Christ (6 January)
  6. The Presentation of Christ in the Temple (2 February)
  7. The Annunciation (25 March)
  8. The Entry of the Lord into Jerusalem (Palm Sunday)
  9. The Ascension of Christ
  10. Pentecost
  11. The Transfiguration of Christ (6 August)
  12. The Dormition of the Theotokos (15 August)

Liturgical observance

Each Great Feast is celebrated with an all-night vigil on the eve of the feast, a festal Divine Liturgy, and a forefeast and afterfeast extending the celebration over several days. Feasts falling during fasting periods, such as the Annunciation during Great Lent, modify the fasting rules. Those Great Feasts that fall on fixed dates are reckoned from the church calendar, while Palm Sunday, Ascension, and Pentecost depend on the movable date of Pascha.
