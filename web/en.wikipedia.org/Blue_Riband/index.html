Blue Riband

The Blue Riband is an unofficial accolade given to the passenger liner crossing the Atlantic Ocean in regular service with the record highest average speed. The term was borrowed from horse racing and came into use during the era of competitive transatlantic steamships in the late nineteenth century.

History

From the 1830s onward, British, German, and American lines competed for the fastest crossings, which brought prestige, passengers, and mail contracts. Famed holders included Brunel's Great Western, Cunard's Lusitania and Mauretania (which held the record for over two decades), the French Normandie, and Cunard's Queen Mary. In 1935 shipping magnate Harold Hales formalised the competition by commissioning the Hales Trophy for the fastest Atlantic crossing.

The final and fastest conventional holder was the American liner United States, which set eastbound and westbound records on her maiden voyage in 1952 that have never been beaten by a passenger liner in service. With the decline of liner travel in the jet age, the contest effectively ended, although fast catamaran ferries have since claimed the Hales Trophy.
