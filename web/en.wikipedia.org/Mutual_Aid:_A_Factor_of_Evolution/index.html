Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution is a 1902 collection of essays and a book by the Russian naturalist and anarchist philosopher Peter Kropotkin. Drawing on his zoological expeditions in Siberia and Manchuria and on historical research, Kropotkin argued that cooperation within species, not only competition, is a major driver of evolution, and that mutual aid is a central feature of successful animal societies and human communities.

Contents

The book began as a series of articles written for the journal The Nineteenth Century between 1890 and 1896, partly in response to T. H. Huxley's essay "The Struggle for Existence", which portrayed nature as a gladiatorial contest. Kropotkin surveyed mutual aid among animals, among Indigenous peoples he called "savages", among "barbarians", in the medieval city, and in modern society, arguing that cooperative species and communities tend to thrive.

Influence

Mutual Aid became a foundational text of anarchist communism and an early contribution to the scientific study of cooperation, anticipating later research on altruism, symbiosis and group selection. The book remains in print and is frequently cited in evolutionary biology, anthropology and political theory.
