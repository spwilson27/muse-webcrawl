Arco

Arco is an Italian musical term meaning "bow" or "with the bow". It is used as a direction in string music to indicate that the player should return to bowing the strings after a passage played pizzicato, in which the strings are plucked. The instruction appears in scores for instruments of the violin family, as well as for the double bass.

Usage

Composers and editors write arco above the staff at the point where normal bowing resumes; it cancels the most recent playing technique, such as pizz. (pizzicato) or col legno (striking the string with the wood of the bow). Because arco is the default technique for bowed strings, the marking is needed only after a contrasting instruction.

Related terms

Related bowing directions include détaché, legato, martelé, spiccato, sautillé, and sul ponticello (bowing near the bridge). Instructions concerning which part of the bow to use include au talon (at the frog) and a punta d'arco (at the tip).

Other meanings

The word "arco" also appears in place names, such as Arco, Trentino in Italy and Arco, Idaho in the United States, and formed part of the name of the Atlantic Richfield Company (ARCO), the American oil company.
