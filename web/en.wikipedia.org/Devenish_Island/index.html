Devenish Island

Devenish Island (from Irish Daimhinis, "ox island") is an island in Lower Lough Erne, County Fermanagh, Northern Ireland. It is famous for its early Christian monastic remains, including one of the finest round towers in Ireland.

History

The monastery on Devenish was founded in the 6th century by Saint Molaise. It became an important religious centre on the pilgrimage route through Lough Erne. The site was raided by Vikings in the 9th and 10th centuries and burned in the 12th century, but was rebuilt and continued as the site of a parish church and an Augustinian priory, St Mary's Abbey, into the early modern period.

Remains

The principal monuments include the 12th-century round tower, about 25 metres high, which visitors can climb; the lower church (Teampull Mór); St Molaise's House, a small stone oratory; and the ruins of the Augustinian priory with its carved Romanesque doorway. The island also has a carved high cross and a small museum displaying finds from the site.

Access

Devenish Island is reached by a passenger ferry from Trory Point near Enniskillen during the visitor season. The site is in state care and managed by the Historic Environment Division of Northern Ireland.
