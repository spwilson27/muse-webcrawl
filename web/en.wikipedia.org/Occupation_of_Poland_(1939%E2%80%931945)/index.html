Occupation of Poland (1939–1945)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Period1939 – 1945
Occupying powersNazi Germany and the Soviet Union
OutcomePoland lost about a fifth of its pre-war population

The occupation of Poland by Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union during World War II began with the German–Soviet invasion of Poland in September 1939 under the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact. Poland's territory was divided between the two powers; after Germany attacked the USSR in June 1941, the whole country fell under German occupation until the Red Army drove the Germans out in 1944–45.

Terror and genocide

Both occupiers waged campaigns against the Polish population. The Soviet NKVD murdered some 22,000 Polish officers and intelligentsia in the Katyn massacre and deported hundreds of thousands eastward. German rule was genocidal: Poland's Jewish population of over three million was almost entirely annihilated in the Holocaust, with the major extermination camps — Auschwitz, Treblinka, Belzec, Sobibor, Chelmno and Majdanek — built on occupied Polish soil. Ethnic Poles were subjected to mass executions, expulsions, forced labour and the suppression of education and culture.

Resistance

Poland maintained a government-in-exile and the largest resistance movement in occupied Europe, the Home Army (Armia Krajowa), answerable to the Polish Underground State. Its uprisings included the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising of 1943 and the Warsaw Uprising of 1944, crushed by the Germans with enormous civilian loss. By war's end Poland had suffered proportionally the highest population losses of any country.
