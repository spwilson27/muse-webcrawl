Marcello Gandini

Gandini with the Lamborghini Countach
Born:26 August 1938, Turin, Italy
Died:13 March 2024, Rivoli, Italy
Occupation:Automobile designer
Known for:Lamborghini Miura, Countach; Lancia Stratos

Marcello Gandini (26 August 1938 – 13 March 2024) was an Italian automobile designer, widely regarded as one of the most influential car designers of the twentieth century. Working principally for the coachbuilder Bertone, he created the archetypal "wedge" supercars of the 1960s and 1970s.

Career at Bertone

Gandini joined Gruppo Bertone in 1965, succeeding Giorgetto Giugiaro as chief stylist at just 27 years old. His first production design there, the Lamborghini Miura (1966), revolutionized sports-car design with its transverse mid-engine layout and sensuous styling. He followed it with the Alfa Romeo Montreal, the Lamborghini Espada and Jarama, and the concepts that defined the wedge era: the Alfa Romeo Carabo (1968), the Lancia Stratos Zero (1970), and the Lamborghini Countach (1974), whose scissor doors he pioneered on the Carabo and which became a Lamborghini signature.

Other designs

His portfolio ranges from supercars to mass-market cars: the Lancia Stratos rally car, the Ferrari 308 GT4, the Lamborghini Diablo (later revised by Chrysler), the Bugatti EB 110, the Citroën BX, the Renault 5 Turbo and Supercinq, the Fiat X1/9, and the first-generation BMW 5 Series (E12) under his early direction. After leaving Bertone in 1980, he worked as an independent designer on cars, trucks, helicopters, and industrial projects.

Legacy

Gandini's low, sharp-edged wedge silhouettes set the visual language for generations of supercars. He received an honorary degree in mechanical engineering from the Polytechnic University of Turin in 2024, shortly before his death at the age of 85.
