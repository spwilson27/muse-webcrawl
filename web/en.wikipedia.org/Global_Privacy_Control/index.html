Global Privacy Control


The GPC signal sent by a browser.

Global Privacy Control (GPC) is a technical specification that allows internet users to signal to websites their preference not to have their personal information sold or shared, through an HTTP header or the DOM property navigator.globalPrivacyControl.

Background

GPC was proposed in 2020 by privacy researchers and organisations including the Electronic Frontier Foundation and teams behind browsers such as Brave, DuckDuckGo and Firefox. It was designed as a successor to the failed Do Not Track header, differing by being tied to legal obligations rather than voluntary compliance.

Legal status

Under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act, businesses must treat GPC as a valid opt-out request; the California Attorney General's 2022 settlement with Sephora cited failure to honour GPC signals. Colorado, Connecticut and other US states similarly recognise universal opt-out mechanisms under their privacy laws. The specification has been developed through the W3C Privacy Community Group.

Adoption

GPC is supported natively by Firefox, Brave and DuckDuckGo browsers, and was formerly sent by the DuckDuckGo and other extensions in Chrome. Major publishers including the New York Times and the Washington Post have committed to honouring the signal.
