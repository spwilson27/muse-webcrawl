Seroprevalence

A seroprevalence map summarising antibody test results across regions.

Seroprevalence is the proportion of individuals in a population who test positive for a specific disease or pathogen based on serology — the detection of antibodies (or sometimes antigens) in blood serum. It is usually expressed as a percentage of the total number of samples tested, often broken down by age, sex, region or other characteristics.

Measurement

Seroprevalence is estimated from serosurveys, in which blood samples from a representative sample of the population are tested, commonly with enzyme immunoassays such as ELISA. Because no test is perfect, estimates may be adjusted for the sensitivity and specificity of the assay; at low prevalence, even a small false-positive rate can substantially inflate the raw figure.

Uses

Because antibodies persist after an infection resolves, seroprevalence captures past as well as current infections, including asymptomatic cases that clinical surveillance misses. Public-health agencies use it to estimate the true cumulative incidence of infections such as HIV, hepatitis, measles and SARS-CoV-2, to measure population immunity and vaccine coverage, to identify groups at elevated risk, and to calibrate epidemiological models. A rising age-specific seroprevalence curve can also be used to estimate the force of infection — the rate at which susceptible individuals acquire infection.

Limitations

Antibodies may wane over time, cross-react with related pathogens, or fail to indicate protective immunity, so seroprevalence does not map one-to-one onto either past infection or protection. Survey design, participation bias and changing test performance must all be considered when comparing estimates between places and times.
