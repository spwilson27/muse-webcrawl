The Klaipėda Region (Lithuanian: Klaipėdos kraštas; German: Memelland) is a historical region on the Baltic coast around the city of Klaipėda, in present-day western Lithuania. For centuries it was the northernmost part of East Prussia; its detachment after the First World War made it a recurring flashpoint between Germany and Lithuania.

History to 1919

The port of Memel was founded by Teutonic Knights in 1252 and remained part of Prussia and then the German Empire. The surrounding countryside was largely Lithuanian-speaking, while the city was predominantly German.

Interwar period

The Treaty of Versailles (1919) detached the territory north of the Neman River from Germany and placed it under Allied administration, guarded by French troops. In January 1923 Lithuanian volunteers staged the Klaipėda Revolt and seized the region; the League of Nations accepted the fait accompli, and the 1924 Klaipėda Convention granted the region autonomy within Lithuania.

Return to Germany and aftermath

Local Nazi movements grew in the 1930s. On 20 March 1939 Germany issued an ultimatum demanding the region's return, which Lithuania accepted; Adolf Hitler arrived by warship to proclaim the annexation. After the Second World War the region became part of the Lithuanian SSR in the Soviet Union, its German population expelled, and it remains part of independent Lithuania today.
