Saint-Nazaire

Saint-Nazaire (French: [sɛ̃ nazɛʁ]; Breton: Sant-Nazer) is a commune in the Loire-Atlantique department in western France, at the mouth of the Loire on the Atlantic coast. A major port and shipbuilding centre, it is famous for the Chantiers de l'Atlantique shipyard, which has built some of the world's largest ocean liners and cruise ships, and for the Normandie dry dock, target of the celebrated British commando raid of 1942.

History

The site has been inhabited since prehistoric times; a Neolithic dolmen stands in the town. The modern town grew rapidly after 1856, when basins were dug to serve as the outport of Nantes and a terminus for transatlantic liners. Shipbuilding began in the 1860s and expanded with the Penhoët yards, builders of liners such as the Normandie (1935) and the France (1962).

During the Second World War, German forces built a vast submarine base at Saint-Nazaire, protected by a roof of reinforced concrete up to 9 metres thick. On 28 March 1942, British Commandos launched Operation Chariot, ramming the explosive-laden destroyer HMS Campbeltown into the gates of the Normandie dry dock — the only dock on the Atlantic coast able to service the battleship Tirpitz — and putting it out of action for the rest of the war. The town itself was heavily bombed and largely rebuilt after 1945; German forces in the "Saint-Nazaire pocket" held out until 11 May 1945, among the last in Europe to surrender.

Economy

Saint-Nazaire remains one of France's great industrial ports. Chantiers de l'Atlantique has built record-breaking cruise ships including Queen Mary 2 and the Oasis-class vessels. Airbus operates a major plant assembling fuselage sections, and the port handles bulk cargoes for the Loire estuary industries. The Saint-Nazaire bridge, opened in 1975, links the town to the south bank of the Loire.

Sights

The submarine base has been converted into cultural spaces, including the Escal'Atlantic museum of ocean-liner history, installed partly inside the base, and a heritage museum in the fortified lock. The Espadon submarine is preserved as a museum ship.
