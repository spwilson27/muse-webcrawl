Yueyang Tower is an ancient Chinese tower in Yueyang, Hunan Province, standing on the city wall by the shore of Dongting Lake. It is one of the Four Great Towers of China and is celebrated in Chinese literature above all through Fan Zhongyan's essay Memorial to Yueyang Tower.

History

A tower on the site is traditionally traced to the Three Kingdoms period, when the Wu general Lu Su is said to have built a reviewing platform for naval forces on Dongting Lake. The tower was repeatedly destroyed and rebuilt through the Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties; the present three-storey wooden structure, notable for its helmet-like roof and for being built without nails in its main frame, dates from a Qing rebuilding in 1867.

Literary fame

In 1046 the statesman and writer Fan Zhongyan, at the request of his friend Teng Zijing, who had rebuilt the tower, wrote his famous prose piece without ever having visited it. Its closing ideal — "Be the first to bear the world's cares, and the last to enjoy its pleasures" (xian tianxia zhi you er you, hou tianxia zhi le er le) — became one of the most quoted lines in Chinese political ethics, memorised by generations of schoolchildren. The tower also inspired poems by Du Fu, Li Bai, and many others.

Today

The tower and its surrounding park are a major tourist attraction and protected heritage site, offering views across Dongting Lake toward Junshan Island.
