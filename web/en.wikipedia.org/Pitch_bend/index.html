Pitch bend

Pitch bend is a musical performance technique and control that smoothly raises or lowers the pitch of a note. On string instruments it is achieved by pushing or pulling a string across the fretboard; on electronic instruments it is controlled by a dedicated wheel, lever or joystick, and in MIDI it is a standard message type with its own high-resolution data value.

Electronic instruments and MIDI

Most synthesizers and MIDI keyboards have a spring-loaded pitch bend wheel beside the modulation wheel. Moving it sends MIDI pitch bend messages — 14-bit values centered on 8192 — which the sound generator translates into a frequency shift. The default bend range is usually two semitones up and down, but it can be configured per instrument or channel, commonly to an octave or more; MIDI Polyphonic Expression (MPE) allows independent bends for each note. Pitch bend enables expressive effects such as guitar-like bends, slides, vibrato and falls that would be impossible with discrete note steps alone.

On other instruments

Guitarists bend strings physically or with a vibrato arm ("whammy bar"); blues and rock styles are unimaginable without the technique. Trombonists can bend notes with the slide and embouchure, harmonica players bend reeds by changing their oral cavity, and singers use slides and scoops between pitches.
