Redox

Redox (a contraction of reduction–oxidation) is a family of chemical reactions in which the oxidation states of atoms are changed. Oxidation is the loss of electrons, or an increase in oxidation state, by a molecule, atom, or ion; reduction is the gain of electrons, or a decrease in oxidation state. The two processes always occur together: the substance that is oxidised is the reducing agent, and the substance that is reduced is the oxidising agent.

Concepts

Historically, oxidation meant reaction with oxygen, as in combustion and the rusting of iron, and reduction meant the removal of oxygen from a compound, as in the smelting of metal ores. The modern electron-transfer definition generalises both. Redox reactions are analysed by splitting them into half-reactions for oxidation and reduction, and their tendency to proceed is measured by standard electrode potentials. Oxidation numbers provide a bookkeeping system for tracking electron transfer.

Examples and importance

Redox processes are fundamental throughout chemistry and biology: combustion, corrosion, electrolysis and batteries, the extraction of metals, photosynthesis, and cellular respiration all proceed by electron transfer. In respiration, glucose is oxidised to carbon dioxide while oxygen is reduced to water, releasing energy captured in ATP; photosynthesis runs the opposite transformation using light energy.
