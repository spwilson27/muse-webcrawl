Isambard Kingdom Brunel

Born9 April 1806, Portsmouth, England
Died15 September 1859 (aged 53), London
OccupationCivil and mechanical engineer
Known forGreat Western Railway, Clifton Suspension Bridge, SS Great Eastern

Isambard Kingdom Brunel (9 April 1806 – 15 September 1859) was a British civil engineer widely regarded as one of the most ingenious figures of the Industrial Revolution. His works include the Great Western Railway, ground-breaking steamships, pioneering bridges and tunnels, and dock systems.

Early work

The son of French engineer Marc Isambard Brunel, he assisted his father on the Thames Tunnel between Rotherhithe and Wapping — the first tunnel under a navigable river — and was seriously injured in a flooding accident in 1828. In 1831 his design won the competition for a bridge across the Avon Gorge at Clifton, Bristol; the Clifton Suspension Bridge was completed after his death in 1864.

Railways and ships

Appointed engineer of the Great Western Railway in 1833, Brunel built the London-to-Bristol line to a broad gauge of 7 ft ¼ in, with remarkably gentle gradients through Box Tunnel and the Maidenhead and Royal Albert bridges. He extended the system through Devon and Cornwall and designed Paddington station.

His three great steamships each pioneered new technology: the wooden paddle steamer SS Great Western (1838), first purpose-built transatlantic liner; the iron screw-propelled SS Great Britain (1843), now preserved in Bristol; and the enormous SS Great Eastern (1858), the largest ship in the world for decades, later used to lay the first lasting transatlantic telegraph cable. During the Crimean War he designed the prefabricated Renkioi Hospital in Turkey.

Death and legacy

Overwork and heavy cigar smoking ruined Brunel's health; he suffered a stroke in September 1859 while the Great Eastern was on trials and died days later. In a 2002 BBC poll he was voted the second-greatest Briton of all time, behind Winston Churchill.
