Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast

The Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast (NKAO) was an autonomous oblast created within the Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic on 7 July 1923, encompassing the predominantly ethnic-Armenian mountainous part of the Karabakh region in the South Caucasus. Its capital was Stepanakert.

History

Following the Russian Revolution and the sovietisation of the Caucasus, the disputed Karabakh region was assigned to Soviet Azerbaijan, with the Armenian-majority highlands granted autonomy as the NKAO. The oblast covered about 4,400 square kilometres; by the late Soviet period roughly three-quarters of its population was Armenian.

Dissolution and conflict

In February 1988 the oblast's regional soviet voted to request transfer to the Armenian SSR, an act that ignited the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict. As the USSR collapsed, local Armenians proclaimed the Nagorno-Karabakh Republic; the Azerbaijani parliament formally abolished the oblast on 26 November 1991. The ensuing First Nagorno-Karabakh War (1988–1994) left the territory under Armenian control as the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh until Azerbaijan regained control in 2023.
