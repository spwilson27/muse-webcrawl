Energy in India

Energy in India describes energy production, consumption and policy in the world's most populous country and third-largest energy consumer. India relies heavily on coal, which supplies the majority of its electricity and a large share of primary energy, while importing most of its crude oil and significant liquefied natural gas. Rapid growth in demand accompanies expanding electricity access and one of the world's fastest-growing renewable energy programmes.

Electricity

Installed generating capacity exceeds 400 gigawatts. Coal-fired plants provide roughly 70 per cent of generation, with growing contributions from solar, wind, hydroelectricity and nuclear power. State and central utilities operate the grid through the Power Grid Corporation of India, and near-universal household electrification was declared in the late 2010s, though supply quality and distribution company finances remain challenges.

Renewables and policy

India has set targets of 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030 and net-zero emissions by 2070. Solar parks, rooftop solar schemes, green hydrogen plans and the International Solar Alliance, headquartered in India, are central to this strategy. In transport and households, policy promotes LPG in place of solid fuels, ethanol blending in petrol, and electric vehicles.
