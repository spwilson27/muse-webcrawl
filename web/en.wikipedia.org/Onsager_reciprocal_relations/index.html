Onsager reciprocal relations

The Onsager reciprocal relations are a set of equations in thermodynamics and statistical mechanics, published by the Norwegian-American chemist Lars Onsager in 1931, that describe the symmetry of coupling between simultaneous transport processes in systems near equilibrium. They are regarded as a foundational result of non-equilibrium thermodynamics, sometimes called its "fourth law".

Statement

In linear irreversible thermodynamics, fluxes Ji (of heat, matter, electric charge and so on) are taken to depend linearly on the thermodynamic forces Xj (such as temperature and chemical-potential gradients):

Ji = Σj Lij Xj

where the Lij are phenomenological coefficients. Onsager showed from the microscopic reversibility of molecular dynamics, together with the regression of spontaneous fluctuations, that the matrix of coefficients is symmetric:

Lij = Lji

(with sign modifications in the presence of magnetic fields or rotation, the Onsager–Casimir relations).

Examples

The relations imply, for instance, that the coefficient coupling a temperature gradient to an electric current (the Seebeck effect) equals the coefficient coupling a voltage gradient to a heat current (the Peltier effect) — unifying these thermoelectric phenomena. Similar relations connect thermodiffusion and the Dufour effect, electro-osmosis and streaming potential, and other cross-effects in electrolytes and membranes.

Recognition

Onsager received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1968 "for the discovery of the reciprocal relations bearing his name, which are fundamental for the thermodynamics of irreversible processes."
