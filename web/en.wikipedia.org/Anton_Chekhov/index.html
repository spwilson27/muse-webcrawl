Anton Chekhov

Anton Pavlovich Chekhov (29 January 1860 – 15 July 1904) was a Russian playwright and short-story writer who is considered to be one of the greatest writers in the world. His career as a playwright produced four classics, and his best short stories are held in high esteem by writers and critics.

Life

Chekhov was born in Taganrog, the son of a grocer and grandson of a serf who had bought his family's freedom. He studied medicine at Moscow University and practiced as a doctor while writing hundreds of short stories, often under the pen name Antosha Chekhonte. In 1890 he travelled to the penal colony on Sakhalin Island and wrote a documentary study of conditions there. He suffered from tuberculosis for much of his adult life and died in the German spa town of Badenweiler in 1904.

Works

His classic plays are The Seagull (1896), Uncle Vanya (1897), Three Sisters (1901), and The Cherry Orchard (1904), the latter three premiered by the Moscow Art Theatre under Konstantin Stanislavski. Among his roughly 500 short stories, celebrated examples include "The Lady with the Dog", "Ward No. 6", "The Darling", and "The Steppe". Chekhov's writing is noted for its economy, psychological depth, and the dramatic principle known as "Chekhov's gun" — the idea that every element in a story should be necessary.
