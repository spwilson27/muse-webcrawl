Hong Kong action cinema

Hong Kong action cinema is the principal source of the Hong Kong film industry's global fame. Combining martial arts choreography, acrobatic stunt work, and inventive camerawork, it achieved worldwide influence from the 1970s onward through stars such as Bruce Lee, Jackie Chan, and Jet Li, and directors including John Woo and Tsui Hark.

History

The genre grew out of wuxia swordplay films and Peking opera traditions. Shaw Brothers and Golden Harvest studios industrialised production in the 1960s and 1970s. Bruce Lee's films in the early 1970s sparked a global kung fu craze. In the 1980s Jackie Chan and Sammo Hung developed the comic kung fu and modern stunt-action styles, while John Woo created "heroic bloodshed" gun-fu films such as A Better Tomorrow and The Killer.

Choreography tradition

Action choreographers — notably Yuen Woo-ping — function as co-directors of fight scenes, designing sequences around performers' genuine physical skills rather than editing tricks. This stunt tradition, with its emphasis on long takes and real danger, strongly influenced Hollywood, most visibly in The Matrix.

Decline and legacy

Output declined in the 1990s amid piracy, the Asian financial crisis, and the 1997 handover, and many talents moved to Hollywood. The genre's grammar — wire work, gun fu, and operatic stunt choreography — remains embedded in global action filmmaking.
