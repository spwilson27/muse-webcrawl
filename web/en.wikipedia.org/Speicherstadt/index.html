Speicherstadt

Speicherstadt
LocationHafenCity, Hamburg, Germany
Built1883 – 1927 (three construction phases)
StyleRed-brick Gothic Revival warehouse architecture
StatusLargest warehouse district in the world; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2015, with the Kontorhaus District)

The Speicherstadt (literally "City of Warehouses") in Hamburg, Germany, is the largest warehouse district in the world built on timber-pile foundations. Its red-brick Gothic Revival blocks, standing on thousands of oak piles driven into the Elbe mud, were constructed between 1883 and 1927 as part of Hamburg's free port. Reached by canal on one side and street on the other, the warehouses stored high-value goods — coffee, tea, cocoa, spices, tobacco, carpets and electronics — under customs-free conditions.

History

When Hamburg joined the German customs union in 1888, it negotiated a free-port zone where goods could be stored and processed duty-free; the Speicherstadt was built for that purpose, displacing some 20,000 residents of the Kehrwieder and Wandrahm islands. Hamburg's coffee trade, then the largest in Europe, centred here. The district was heavily bombed in the Second World War and faithfully rebuilt. As container shipping moved trade downstream, traditional warehousing declined, though carpet dealers and logistics firms remain.

Today

The Speicherstadt now lies within the HafenCity redevelopment and mixes warehousing with museums (the Speicherstadtmuseum, the German Customs Museum, Miniatur Wunderland — the world's largest model railway) and offices. In 2015 UNESCO inscribed it, together with the Kontorhaus District and Chilehaus, on the World Heritage List. The nearby Elbphilharmonie concert hall rises just west of the district.
