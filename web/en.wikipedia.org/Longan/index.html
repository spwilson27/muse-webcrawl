The longan (Dimocarpus longan), from the Cantonese for "dragon's eye", is a tropical evergreen tree in the soapberry family Sapindaceae, native to tropical Asia. It produces edible fruit resembling the related lychee and rambutan, with translucent flesh surrounding a single shiny black seed that gives the peeled fruit the appearance of an eyeball.

Cultivation and uses

Longans are grown commercially in China, Thailand, Vietnam, India and elsewhere in subtropical Asia. The fruit is eaten fresh, canned in syrup, or dried; dried longan is used in Chinese cooking, dessert soups and traditional herbal medicine, where it is believed to promote relaxation. The tree can reach 12–20 m in height and is less cold-tolerant than the lychee.
