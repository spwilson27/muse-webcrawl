Oreo

Oreo
Introduced: 1912
Manufacturer: Mondelēz International (Nabisco)
Type: Sandwich cookie
Tagline: "Milk's favorite cookie"

Oreo is a brand of sandwich cookie consisting of two cocoa biscuits or wafers with a sweet crème filling. Introduced in 1912 by the National Biscuit Company (Nabisco), Oreo is the best-selling cookie brand in the world, sold in more than 100 countries. The modern Oreo is produced by Mondelēz International.

History

The first Oreos were made at Nabisco's Chelsea, Manhattan bakery and sold in Hoboken, New Jersey, in 1912, competing with the similar Hydrox cookie. The name's origin is uncertain; theories derive it from the French or ("gold", the colour of early packaging) or the Greek oreo ("mountain" or "beautiful"). The characteristic embossed design dates largely from a 1952 redesign.

Varieties and use

Beyond the original, Oreos are sold in numerous flavours and formats — Double Stuf, Golden (vanilla wafers), mint, peanut butter, and many limited editions — varying by market. The cookie's ritual of "twist, lick, dunk" in milk has been a centrepiece of its advertising for decades. Oreo crumbs and pieces are widely used in desserts, milkshakes and ice cream, and many rival brands sell imitations.
