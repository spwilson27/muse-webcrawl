Cycle track

A cycle track or cycleway is a bikeway separated from motor traffic and dedicated to cycling or shared with pedestrians. Cycle tracks are distinct from cycle lanes, which are marked lanes within the carriageway, because they provide physical separation — typically a kerb, verge, bollards, or a change in level — between cyclists and motor vehicles.

SynonymsSeparated bike lane, protected bike lane, cycleway
SeparationKerb, verge, barrier, or level difference
Typical usersCyclists; sometimes shared with pedestrians

Design

Cycle tracks may be one-way or two-way, and are usually built at road level, at pavement level, or at an intermediate level. Design guidance addresses width, junction treatment, surface material, drainage, and the management of conflicts at side roads and bus stops. At junctions, techniques such as setback crossings and protected signal phases are used to maintain separation through the intersection.

History

Segregated cycle provision dates to the late nineteenth century, and the Netherlands and Denmark built extensive track networks through the twentieth century, correlating with high cycling mode share. Interest in protected tracks grew in North America and the United Kingdom from the 2000s onward as cities sought to attract less confident riders.

Safety and debate

Studies generally find that well-designed cycle tracks reduce injury risk compared with cycling in mixed traffic, while poorly designed tracks that re-merge abruptly at junctions can create conflict points. Provision for cycling is debated in terms of road space allocation, cost, and effects on motor traffic flow.
