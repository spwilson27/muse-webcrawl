Alpine orogeny

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Alpine orogeny is the mountain-building episode that formed the great east–west ranges of southern Europe, North Africa, and western and southern Asia, including the Alps, Pyrenees, Carpathians, Atlas Mountains, Zagros, and in its broader continuation the Himalayas. It resulted from the northward movement of the African and Indian plates and their collision with Eurasia as the Tethys Ocean closed.

Timing

Deformation began in the Late Cretaceous and continued through the Cenozoic, with major phases in the Eocene and Miocene. Mountain building continues today, as convergence persists across the Mediterranean and western Asia.

Ranges formed

Ranges attributed to the Alpine orogeny include the Atlas, Pyrenees, Alps, Apennines, Dinarides, Carpathians, Balkans, Taurus, Caucasus, Zagros, Hindu Kush, Karakoram, and Himalayas.
