Battle of Arracourt

The Battle of Arracourt was a major clash between U.S. and German armoured forces during World War II, fought from 18 to 29 September 1944 near Arracourt in the Moselle department of northeastern France. It was one of the largest tank battles involving U.S. forces of the war, pitting units of the U.S. Third Army under General George S. Patton against German panzer formations attempting to blunt the American advance toward the Saar.

Background

Following the rapid Allied advance across France in the summer of 1944, Patton's Third Army drove toward Lorraine and the German border. Adolf Hitler ordered a counteroffensive, the Lorraine Panzer Offensive, aimed at destroying the American spearheads. The German Fifth Panzer Army, commanded by Hasso von Manteuffel, fielded two panzer brigades and elements of panzer divisions equipped with Panther tanks.

The battle

Combat Command A of the U.S. 4th Armored Division, under Colonel Bruce C. Clarke, was attacked near Arracourt beginning on 18 September. In a series of engagements fought in fog and broken terrain, American Sherman tanks and M18 Hellcat tank destroyers used superior coordination, artillery support, air support from P-47 Thunderbolts, and better-trained crews to defeat the German attacks. Despite the Panther's superiority in armour and gun power, the Germans lost heavily.

Aftermath

By the end of September the German counteroffensive had failed, with the loss of roughly 200 tanks and assault guns against far lighter American losses. The battle demonstrated the effectiveness of American combined-arms tactics and effectively ended Germany's ability to mount a major offensive in Lorraine, though the Third Army's advance soon stalled for want of fuel and supplies.
