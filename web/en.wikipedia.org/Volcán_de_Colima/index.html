The Volcán de Colima, also known as the Colima Volcanic Complex, is a volcanic complex in western Mexico, on the border of the states of Colima and Jalisco. It consists of the older, extinct Nevado de Colima (4,339 m) and the younger, highly active Volcán de Fuego de Colima (about 3,820 m), one of the most active volcanoes in North America.

Activity

The Volcán de Fuego has erupted frequently since the 16th century, with major eruptions in 1606, 1690, 1818, 1869 and 1913, the last forming a crater and generating pyroclastic flows. Renewed activity since the 1990s has featured lava-dome growth and collapse, pyroclastic flows, ashfalls on nearby towns and periodic evacuations. The complex lies within the Colima National Park and is monitored by the University of Colima's observatory. The complex forms part of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt.
