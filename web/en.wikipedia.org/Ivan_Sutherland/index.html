Ivan Sutherland

Born: 16 May 1938, Hastings, Nebraska, U.S.
Known for: Sketchpad; computer graphics; head-mounted display
Awards: Turing Award (1988), Kyoto Prize (2012)

Ivan Edward Sutherland (born 1938) is an American computer scientist widely regarded as the father of computer graphics. His 1963 doctoral creation, Sketchpad, pioneered interactive computing, graphical user interfaces and object-oriented programming concepts, and he went on to found companies and research programmes that shaped the field.

Sketchpad

Sutherland's MIT PhD thesis, Sketchpad: A Man-Machine Graphical Communication System (1963), described a program running on the TX-2 computer that let a user draw directly on a CRT display with a light pen. Sketchpad introduced direct manipulation, geometric constraints that drawings maintained automatically, hierarchical "master" and "instance" objects, and dynamic rubber-band drawing — ideas foundational to CAD, GUIs and object-oriented programming. He earned degrees in electrical engineering from the Carnegie Institute of Technology and Caltech before his MIT doctorate.

Career

After brief service directing ARPA's Information Processing Techniques Office — where he succeeded J. C. R. Licklider and funded early work in time-sharing, networking and graphics — Sutherland taught at Harvard and then co-founded the computer science programme at the University of Utah, which became the world's leading graphics school; his students there included Ed Catmull, Alan Kay, Jim Clark and John Warnock. In 1968 he co-founded Evans and Sutherland, a pioneering maker of flight simulators and graphics systems. He later worked at Caltech on VLSI design with Carver Mead, at Carnegie Mellon, and as a vice-president and fellow at Sun Microsystems, and afterwards at Portland State University.

Virtual reality and other work

With his student Bob Sproull, Sutherland built the first head-mounted display system in 1968 — the heavy, ceiling-suspended device nicknamed the "Sword of Damocles" — which displayed wireframe graphics tracked to the wearer's head, making him a founder of virtual and augmented reality. His other contributions include the Sutherland–Hodgman polygon clipping algorithm (with Gary Hodgman) and early work on hidden-surface removal and incremental computing.

Honours

Sutherland received the ACM Turing Award in 1988 "for his pioneering and visionary contributions to computer graphics", was elected to the National Academies of Sciences and Engineering, and received the Kyoto Prize in 2012. His older brother, Bert Sutherland, was also a noted computer research manager.
