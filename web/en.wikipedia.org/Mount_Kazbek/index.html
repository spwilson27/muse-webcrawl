Mount Kazbek

Mount Kazbek
Elevation: 5,054 m
Location: Georgia–Russia border, Greater Caucasus
Type: dormant stratovolcano
First ascent: 1868 (Douglas Freshfield party)

Mount Kazbek (Georgian: მყინვარწვერი, Mqinvartsveri, "ice peak"; Ossetian: Урсхох) is a dormant stratovolcano and one of the major mountains of the Caucasus range, lying on the border between Georgia's Mtskheta-Mtianeti region and Russia's North Ossetia. At about 5,054 metres it is the third-highest peak in Georgia and one of the most popular mountaineering objectives in the Caucasus.

Geography and climbing

Kazbek rises above the town of Stepantsminda (Kazbegi), reached from Tbilisi by the Georgian Military Highway. The standard route from Georgia climbs past the Gergeti Glacier — via the Bethlemi Hut at 3,670 m — and involves glacier travel but little technical difficulty in good conditions. Below the summit, the fourteenth-century Gergeti Trinity Church, silhouetted against the mountain, is one of Georgia's most iconic sights.

Mythology

In Georgian folklore the mountain is associated with Amirani, the Georgian counterpart of Prometheus, said to have been chained on its slopes for stealing fire; in Greek tradition Prometheus himself was chained in the Caucasus. The mountain also features in local legends of hidden treasure in a cave monastery. Kazbek was first climbed in 1868 by an English party led by Douglas Freshfield, with the Swiss guide François Devouassoud.
