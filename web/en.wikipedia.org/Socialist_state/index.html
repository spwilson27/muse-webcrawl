Socialist state
DefinitionSovereign state constitutionally dedicated to socialism
Examples (current)China, Cuba, Laos, Vietnam, North Korea
Historical exampleUnion of Soviet Socialist Republics

A socialist state, or socialist republic, is a sovereign state constitutionally dedicated to the establishment of socialism. The term is used particularly for states governed by a communist party that claims to be building socialism — typically through a transitional period of state ownership of the means of production and a planned or mixed economy.

Marxist–Leninist states

After the Russian Revolution of 1917, the Soviet Union became the prototype of the Marxist–Leninist state: a one-party system led by a vanguard party, democratic centralism, and state ownership. Following the Second World War this model spread across Eastern Europe and Asia. States still describing themselves as socialist on this model include China, Cuba, Laos, and Vietnam, though several have adopted extensive market reforms ("socialist market economy").

Other usages

Some constitutions declare socialism as a goal without Marxist–Leninist institutions: India, Portugal, Tanzania (under Julius Nyerere's ujamaa), and others have used the term in a democratic socialist or national-developmental sense. Political scientists distinguish these nominal declarations from party-states in which socialism structures the political system.

Debates

Discussion centres on whether historical socialist states achieved genuine social ownership or degenerated into bureaucratic systems, the relationship between one-party rule and socialist democracy, and the role of markets within socialism after the reforms of Deng Xiaoping and Vietnam's Đổi Mới.
