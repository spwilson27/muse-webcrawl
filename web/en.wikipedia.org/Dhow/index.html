Dhow

Dhow (Arabic: داو, dāw) is the generic name for a number of traditional sailing vessels with one or more masts, characterised by lateen sails, used in the western Indian Ocean — along the coasts of the Arabian Peninsula, East Africa, and South Asia.

Types and construction

Dhows range from small fishing and pearling boats (such as the jalibut and shu'ai) to large ocean-going cargo vessels such as the baghlah, boom, and sambuk. Traditionally their hulls were built of teak planks sewn together with coir fibre rather than nailed, and their triangular lateen sails allowed them to sail close to the wind.

History

Dhows carried the monsoon trade between Arabia, Persia, India, and East Africa for many centuries, transporting dates, spices, timber, incense, and ivory. Arab navigators used instruments such as the kamal to steer by the stars. Although motorised vessels have largely replaced them in commerce, dhows remain in use for fishing and coastal trade, and dhow racing is a popular sport in the Gulf states.
