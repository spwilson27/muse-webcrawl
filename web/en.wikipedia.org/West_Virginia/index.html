West Virginia is a landlocked state in the Southern and Mid-Atlantic regions of the United States. It is bordered by Pennsylvania and Maryland to the northeast, Virginia to the southeast, Kentucky to the southwest, and Ohio to the northwest. The state is noted for its rugged mountainous terrain — it is the only state entirely within the Appalachian Mountain range — and for its historically significant coal mining and logging industries. Its capital and most populous city is Charleston.

History

The western counties of Virginia broke away from the Commonwealth of Virginia during the American Civil War. After Virginia seceded from the Union in 1861, Unionist delegates formed a loyal "Restored Government of Virginia" and authorised the creation of a new state, which was admitted to the Union on June 20, 1863 — the only state formed by separating from a Confederate state.

Coal mining transformed the state in the late 19th and 20th centuries, producing bitter labour conflicts including the West Virginia coal wars, which culminated in the Battle of Blair Mountain in 1921, the largest labour uprising in American history. The state's chemical, glass and steel industries grew along its rivers, while mechanisation and competition later drove steep population decline from a 1950 peak.

Geography

West Virginia has the highest average elevation of any state east of the Mississippi River. The Allegheny Mountains run down its eastern side; Spruce Knob, at 1,482 m (4,863 ft), is the highest point. Major rivers include the Ohio, which forms the western border, the Kanawha, the Monongahela and the New River, one of the oldest rivers in North America. About 78 percent of the state is forested.

Economy

Coal remains a defining industry — West Virginia is among the leading coal-producing states — alongside natural gas from the Marcellus Shale, chemicals, tourism and outdoor recreation. The state government is its largest employer. The state song best known to outsiders, John Denver's "Take Me Home, Country Roads" (1971), was adopted as an official state song in 2014.
