Blue Lagoon (geothermal spa)

The Blue Lagoon near Grindavík, Iceland, surrounded by moss-covered lava fields.

The Blue Lagoon (Icelandic: Bláa lónið) is a geothermal spa in southwestern Iceland, located in a lava field on the Reykjanes Peninsula near the town of Grindavík and in front of Mount Þorbjörn. It is one of Iceland's most popular tourist attractions, known for its milky turquoise water, rich in minerals such as silica and sulfur, which is supplied by the nearby Svartsengi geothermal power station.

History

The lagoon formed in 1976 from the wastewater of the Svartsengi geothermal power plant, where superheated water from volcanic fissures is used to generate electricity and heat. In 1981, people began bathing in the pond after its supposed healing powers for skin diseases such as psoriasis were noticed; a bathing facility opened in 1987, and the operating company Blue Lagoon hf. was founded in 1992. A dedicated psoriasis treatment clinic was established in 1994, and the facilities have since expanded into a large resort including restaurants, a hotel and a research and development centre for silica-based skin care products.

Description

The water's temperature averages 37–39 °C (99–102 °F). Its milky blue shade is caused by its high silica content, which reflects sunlight; white silica mud deposits on the lagoon floor and is applied by bathers as a face mask. The water renews itself roughly every 40 hours and is a mixture of about 70 per cent seawater and 30 per cent freshwater, containing silica, algae and minerals. Strict hygiene rules require visitors to shower without swimwear before entering.

Volcanic activity

The spa sits within the active Reykjanes volcanic zone. Following renewed eruptions on the peninsula beginning in 2023, the Blue Lagoon and Grindavík were repeatedly evacuated and the spa temporarily closed at times due to nearby volcanic fissures and lava flows, with protective berms built to divert lava away from the power station and spa infrastructure.
