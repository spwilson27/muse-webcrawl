The Port of Le Havre (French: Port du Havre) is the port and harbour of the city of Le Havre, in Normandy, northern France. Situated at the mouth of the river Seine on the English Channel, it is France's largest container port and its second-largest commercial port overall after Marseille, handling a large share of the country's containerised imports and crude oil traffic.

History

The port was founded in 1517 by King Francis I, who sought a new harbour on the Channel coast to replace the silting ports of Harfleur and Honfleur. Named Franciscopolis and later Le Havre de Grâce, the port grew as a base for cod fishing, colonial trade and transatlantic passenger liners. The great ocean liner era saw the Normandie and other French Line ships sail from Le Havre to New York.

The port and city were heavily damaged during the Second World War, particularly in September 1944, and were rebuilt after the war. From the 1960s onward the port expanded eastward with new docks and, in the 2000s, the Port 2000 project added deep-water container berths capable of receiving the largest container ships afloat.

Facilities and traffic

The port complex extends over some 25 kilometres along the Seine estuary. Its facilities include container terminals (notably the Terminal de France and Terminal Porte Océane at Port 2000), oil terminals at Le Havre and Antifer — the latter built to receive very large crude carriers — ferry berths serving Portsmouth in England, roll-on/roll-off berths, and bulk and cruise terminals.

Since 2021 the port has been administered as part of HAROPA Port, the merged port authority uniting Le Havre with Rouen and Paris along the Seine axis. The port serves the Paris region, France's largest consumer market, and is connected to it by motorway, rail and the Seine river and canal network.
