Poxviridae is a family of large, complex double-stranded DNA viruses that infect vertebrates and invertebrates. Poxviruses are among the largest and most complex viruses known, with brick-shaped or ovoid virions roughly 200–400 nanometres long. Unlike most DNA viruses, they replicate entirely in the cytoplasm of the host cell, encoding their own transcription machinery.

Structure and genome

The poxvirus genome is a single linear molecule of double-stranded DNA with covalently closed hairpin ends, ranging from about 130 to 375 kilobase pairs depending on the genus. The virion contains a dumbbell-shaped core housing the genome and enzymes needed to begin early gene expression immediately after entry.

Classification

The family is divided into two subfamilies: Chordopoxvirinae, which infect vertebrates, and Entomopoxvirinae, which infect insects. Genera of medical importance include Orthopoxvirus, Parapoxvirus, Molluscipoxvirus and Yatapoxvirus.

Diseases

The best-known poxvirus disease is smallpox, caused by variola virus, which was declared eradicated in 1980 following a global vaccination campaign using the related vaccinia virus. Other human infections include mpox, cowpox, molluscum contagiosum and orf.

Research use

Vaccinia virus is widely used as a laboratory model and as a vector for recombinant vaccines and oncolytic virus therapy, owing to its large genome capacity and cytoplasmic replication.
