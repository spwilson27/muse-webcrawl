Hans Frank

Born: 23 May 1900, Karlsruhe, German Empire

Died: 16 October 1946 (aged 46), Nuremberg, Germany

Office: Governor-General of the General Government, 1939–1945

Conviction: War crimes and crimes against humanity; executed

Hans Michael Frank (1900–1946) was a German lawyer and high-ranking Nazi official who served as head of the General Government, the civil administration of German-occupied Poland during the Second World War. Under his rule, Poland's Jewish population was confined to ghettos and deported to extermination camps, and millions of Poles were subjected to terror, forced labour, and murder.

Early career

Frank joined the Nazi Party in the 1920s and became Adolf Hitler's personal legal adviser, defending him in numerous court cases. After the Nazis seized power in 1933 he held senior legal offices in Bavaria and the Reich, including Reich Minister without Portfolio, and founded the Academy for German Law.

General Government

Appointed Governor-General in October 1939, Frank governed from Wawel Castle in Kraków. His administration oversaw the establishment of concentration and extermination camps including Majdanek and the Operation Reinhard camps, the suppression of Polish culture and education, and the deportation of Poles for forced labour. His voluminous official diary, which he surrendered to American troops, became key evidence of his crimes.

Trial and execution

Captured in 1945, Frank was tried at Nuremberg, where he expressed remorse and returned to Catholicism. He was convicted of war crimes and crimes against humanity and hanged on 16 October 1946.
