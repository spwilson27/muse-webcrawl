Per-Ingvar Branemark

Per-Ingvar Brånemark (3 May 1929 – 20 December 2014) was a Swedish physician and research professor, widely regarded as the father of modern dental implantology. He discovered the phenomenon of osseointegration — the direct structural and functional connection between living bone and the surface of a load-bearing implant — and developed the first clinically successful dental implant system.

Per-Ingvar Branemark
Born: 3 May 1929, Karlshamn, Sweden
Died: 20 December 2014, Gothenburg, Sweden
Known for: Osseointegration; dental implants

Discovery of osseointegration

In 1952, while studying blood flow in rabbit bone at the University of Lund using titanium optical chambers, Brånemark found that the titanium had fused firmly to the bone and could not be removed. He recognised the significance of the observation and coined the term osseointegration. Moving to the University of Gothenburg, he pursued the phenomenon despite considerable scepticism from the medical and dental establishment.

Dental implants

In 1965 his team placed the first titanium dental implants in a patient, Gösta Larsson, who had severe jaw deformities; the implants functioned for the rest of Larsson's life, over 40 years. After long-term clinical studies, the results were presented to wide acceptance at a 1982 conference in Toronto, and Brånemark's system was commercialised with Nobelpharma (later Nobel Biocare). Osseointegration also found application in bone-anchored hearing aids and in prosthetic limbs and facial reconstructions attached directly to the skeleton.

Legacy

Brånemark received numerous honours, including the Swedish Engineering Academy's gold medal and the European Inventor Award. Institutes bearing his name were established in several countries, and his work transformed the treatment of edentulous patients worldwide, making implant-supported teeth a standard of care.
