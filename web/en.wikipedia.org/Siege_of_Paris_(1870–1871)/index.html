The Siege of Paris took place from 19 September 1870 to 28 January 1871 during the Franco-Prussian War. German forces completely encircled the French capital, defended by the Government of National Defense, and starved it into surrender.

The siege

After the catastrophe of Sedan, German armies advanced on Paris and closed the ring around the city in September 1870. Within the walls, General Trochu commanded a huge but poorly trained force of regulars, mobiles, and National Guards. French sorties — at Châtillon, Le Bourget, Champigny, and finally Buzenval on 19 January 1871 — all failed to break the encirclement. Provincial armies raised by Léon Gambetta, who had escaped the city by balloon, likewise failed to relieve it.

Life in the besieged city

Parisians communicated with the outside world only by balloon and carrier pigeon microfilm. As food ran out, horses, dogs, cats, rats, and even the animals of the Jardin des Plantes zoo were eaten. From early January 1871 German heavy artillery bombarded the southern districts.

Armistice and aftermath

With starvation imminent, Jules Favre negotiated an armistice on 28 January 1871. The Germans staged a brief victory parade on 1 March. War indemnities, the annexation of Alsace-Lorraine, and the humiliation of the siege fuelled the insurrection of the Paris Commune in March–May 1871. The German Empire had been proclaimed at Versailles during the siege, on 18 January 1871.
