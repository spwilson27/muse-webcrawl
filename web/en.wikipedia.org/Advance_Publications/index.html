Advance Publications, Inc. is a privately held American media company owned by the descendants of Samuel Irving Newhouse Sr. Founded in 1922, the company is headquartered at One World Trade Center in New York City and operates a diverse portfolio of media, entertainment, technology and education assets.

Holdings

Through Condé Nast, Advance publishes magazines including Vogue, The New Yorker, GQ, Vanity Fair, Wired, Architectural Digest and Bon Appétit. Its Advance Local group operates local news organizations and newspapers across the United States, including The Star-Ledger of Newark, The Plain Dealer of Cleveland, The Oregonian of Portland, The Times-Picayune of New Orleans and the Staten Island Advance, the original newspaper purchased by Newhouse in 1922.

Beyond publishing, Advance holds significant investments in other companies. It has been a major shareholder of Discovery, Inc. (now part of Warner Bros. Discovery), holds a stake in Charter Communications, owns the social news aggregation company Reddit, and owns 1010data, Turnitin and the Stage Entertainment theatre company, among other assets.

History

Samuel I. Newhouse Sr. built the company from a single Staten Island newspaper into one of the largest privately held media empires in the United States, acquiring newspapers, magazines, radio and television stations over the middle decades of the twentieth century. In 1959 he acquired Condé Nast Publications, reportedly after his wife Mitzi expressed a wish to own Vogue. After his death in 1979, control passed to his sons Samuel Jr. and Donald, and later to the next generation of the Newhouse family.

In the twenty-first century, Advance has shifted from print ownership toward digital media, data and investment holdings, reducing print publication frequency at many of its newspapers while expanding its portfolio of technology and education companies.
