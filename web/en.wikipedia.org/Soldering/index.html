Soldering

Soldering is a process in which two or more metal items are joined by melting a filler metal (solder) into the joint. Unlike welding, the workpieces themselves are not melted; unlike brazing, soldering uses filler alloys that melt below 450 °C.

Applications

Soldering is fundamental to electronics, where it connects components to printed circuit boards by hand with a soldering iron, or industrially by wave soldering and reflow soldering. Plumbers use it to join copper pipe, and it is also used in stained glass, jewellery, and metalwork.

Process

The joint surfaces are cleaned, flux is applied to prevent oxidation, the parts are heated, and solder is fed into the joint, where capillary action draws it in. A good electronics joint is shiny and concave; dull, grainy "cold joints" result from insufficient heat or movement during solidification and cause intermittent connections.
