Hydrofluoric acid is a solution of hydrogen fluoride (HF) in water. It is a colorless, highly corrosive liquid. Solutions of HF are colourless, acidic and highly corrosive. HF is unusual among acids: although it is a weak acid in the sense of incomplete dissociation, it dissolves glass and many silicate materials, and it is uniquely dangerous because fluoride ions penetrate tissue and bind calcium and magnesium.

Production

Hydrofluoric acid is produced industrially by treating the mineral fluorite (calcium fluoride) with concentrated sulfuric acid, generating hydrogen fluoride gas, which is absorbed into water.

Uses

HF is used to etch and frost glass, clean and polish silicon wafers in semiconductor manufacturing, alkylate petroleum in refineries, produce fluorocarbons and fluoropolymers such as PTFE, and refine metals. Because it attacks glass, it is stored in plastic containers.

Hazards

Contact with hydrofluoric acid can cause deep, initially painless burns; absorbed fluoride can produce hypocalcemia, cardiac arrhythmia, and death. First aid involves immediate washing and application of calcium gluconate gel, and serious exposures require urgent medical care.
