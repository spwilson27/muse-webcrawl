Legislative intent

In law, legislative intent is the purpose or aims that a legislature had in mind when it enacted a statute, used by courts as an aid to interpreting ambiguous statutory language.

Approaches

Intentionalist judges examine legislative history — committee reports, debates and statements of sponsors — to reconstruct that purpose. Textualists object that a legislature is a collective body with no single intent and that only the enacted text is law, favouring plain meaning; purposivism, by contrast, asks what problem the statute was meant to solve.
