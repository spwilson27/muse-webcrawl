Guantanamo Bay detention camp

The Guantanamo Bay detention camp is a United States military detention facility located within Naval Station Guantanamo Bay on the coast of Guantánamo Bay in Cuba. It was established in January 2002 by the administration of President George W. Bush to hold terrorism suspects captured in the "war on terror", principally in Afghanistan.

History

The first detainees arrived on 11 January 2002 and were held at the temporary Camp X-Ray before permanent facilities such as Camp Delta were built. In total about 780 men from some 50 countries have been held at the camp; most were eventually transferred or released without charge. Detainees were designated "enemy combatants" and initially denied access to courts, a position challenged in a series of Supreme Court rulings — including Rasul v. Bush (2004), Hamdan v. Rumsfeld (2006) and Boumediene v. Bush (2008), which affirmed detainees' habeas corpus rights.

Controversy

The camp has been persistently criticised by human rights organisations, including Amnesty International and the International Committee of the Red Cross, over indefinite detention without trial, interrogation techniques described as torture, and hunger strikes by detainees. Successive US presidents have pledged to close the facility without fully doing so, and a small population of detainees, including men accused in connection with the September 11 attacks, has remained there into the 2020s, many held in prolonged pre-trial proceedings before military commissions.
