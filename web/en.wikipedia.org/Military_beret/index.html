Military beret

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The military beret is a soft, round, flat-crowned cap, usually of wool felt, adopted as headgear by armed forces around the world during the twentieth century. Berets are worn as part of dress and service uniforms by many armies, navies, air forces, and police units, with colours and badges distinguishing branches, regiments, and specializations.

History

The beret's military use began with French mountain troops, the Chasseurs Alpins, who adopted a large soft beret in the late nineteenth century. During the First World War and interwar period, tighter-fitting berets were taken up by British tank crews, who needed headgear that could be worn inside armoured vehicles and with headsets. In the Second World War berets spread widely through British and Commonwealth forces, and coloured berets became strongly associated with elite units: the maroon beret of airborne forces, the green beret of commandos (later also of United States Army Special Forces), and the black beret of armoured units.

Colours and symbolism

Beret colours are often prescribed in detail: for example, rifle green, navy blue, scarlet, khaki, and sand or tan for special forces such as the Special Air Service. United Nations peacekeepers wear a distinctive light blue beret. In the United States Army, the black beret became standard headgear in 2001 before being replaced for most soldiers by the patrol cap in 2011, while distinctive colours remained for airborne (maroon), Ranger (tan), and Special Forces (rifle green) troops.
