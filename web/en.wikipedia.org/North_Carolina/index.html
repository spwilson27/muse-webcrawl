North Carolina is a state in the Southeastern United States. It borders Virginia to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the east, South Carolina and Georgia to the south, and Tennessee to the west. Its capital is Raleigh and its largest city is Charlotte. With more than 10.4 million residents in the 2020 census, it is the ninth-most populous state.

Geography

North Carolina has three main geographic regions: the coastal plain in the east, the rolling Piedmont in the center, and the Appalachian Mountains in the west, which include Mount Mitchell, at 6,684 feet (2,037 m) the highest peak in eastern North America. The state's barrier-island coast, the Outer Banks, was the site of the Wright brothers' first powered flight at Kitty Hawk in 1903.

History

Carolina was an English proprietary colony chartered in the 17th century and split into northern and southern provinces in 1712. North Carolina was one of the original Thirteen Colonies; its Provincial Congress issued the Halifax Resolves in April 1776, the first official call for independence. It was the 12th state to ratify the United States Constitution, on November 21, 1789. The state seceded during the Civil War in 1861 and was readmitted in 1868.

Economy

Traditionally known for tobacco, textiles, and furniture, North Carolina's economy has diversified into banking (Charlotte is a major financial center), biotechnology and research around the Research Triangle of Raleigh, Durham, and Chapel Hill, energy, and film production.
