The Padma Shri is the fourth-highest civilian award in the Republic of India, after the Bharat Ratna, the Padma Vibhushan, and the Padma Bhushan. Instituted on 2 January 1954, the award is conferred by the Government of India to recognize distinguished contributions in various fields, including arts, education, industry, literature, science, social service, medicine, sports, and public affairs. The awards are announced every year on the occasion of Republic Day (26 January) and are presented by the President of India at a ceremonial function held at Rashtrapati Bhavan, usually in March or April.

The Padma Shri medal, suspended from a pink ribbon with white stripes.

Design

The decoration is a circular bronze-toned medallion bearing a lotus flower in the center, with the words "Padma" and "Shri" in Devanagari script above and below the lotus; the reverse carries the national emblem and the motto Satyameva Jayate. Recipients also receive a certificate signed by the President.

Selection

Nominations are invited from the public as well as from state governments, ministries, and previous awardees. A Padma Awards Committee constituted by the Prime Minister recommends names, which are approved by the Prime Minister and the President. Any person without distinction of race, occupation, position, or sex is eligible, though government servants are generally excluded except doctors and scientists. More than 3,000 people have received the award since its inception; a number of recipients have later declined or returned the honor for reasons of conscience or protest.
