The Bureau of Justice Statistics (BJS) is the primary statistical agency of the United States Department of Justice. A component of the Office of Justice Programs, it collects, analyses and publishes data on crime, victims, criminals, and the operation of justice systems at all levels of government in the United States.

History and role

The bureau was established on December 27, 1979, under the Justice System Improvement Act, taking over statistical functions previously carried out by the Law Enforcement Assistance Administration. It is one of the principal US federal statistical agencies and is headed by a director appointed by the President.

Data programmes

BJS's best-known programme is the National Crime Victimization Survey (NCVS), which surveys tens of thousands of households about experiences of crime, including crimes not reported to police, supplementing police-recorded statistics. Other major collections cover prison and jail populations, probation and parole, deaths in custody, recidivism, the federal justice system, law enforcement agencies, prosecutors and courts, and capital punishment. Its annual Prisoners and Jail Inmates reports are the standard sources for US incarceration figures.
