Wiener filter

In signal processing, the Wiener filter is a filter used to produce an estimate of a desired or target random process by linear time-invariant (LTI) filtering of an observed noisy process, assuming known stationary signal and noise spectra, and additive noise. The filter minimizes the mean square error between the estimated random process and the desired process. It is named after Norbert Wiener, who developed the theory in the 1940s.

Theory

The Wiener filter is the optimal linear estimator in the mean-square sense. In the frequency domain its transfer function is H(f) = Sxy(f) / Sxx(f), the ratio of the cross-power spectral density of the input and the desired signal to the power spectral density of the input. Solutions exist for causal and non-causal filters, and for finite impulse response implementations the normal equations form the Wiener–Hopf equations.

Applications

Wiener filtering is used in noise reduction, image deblurring and restoration, echo cancellation, channel equalization and deconvolution. The Kalman filter generalizes the same principle to non-stationary processes.
