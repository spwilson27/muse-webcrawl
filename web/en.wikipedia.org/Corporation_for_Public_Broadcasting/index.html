Corporation for Public Broadcasting

The Corporation for Public Broadcasting (CPB) is an American publicly funded non-profit corporation, created in 1967 to promote and help support public broadcasting in the United States. It was established under the Public Broadcasting Act of 1967, signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson, following the recommendations of the Carnegie Commission on Educational Television.

Role and funding

CPB receives an annual appropriation from the United States Congress and distributes the funds to locally owned and operated public television and radio stations, and to producers of programming. It helped found the Public Broadcasting Service (PBS) in 1969 and National Public Radio (NPR) in 1970, though it is separate from both organisations and does not itself produce or broadcast programmes.

Governance

CPB is governed by a board of directors appointed by the President of the United States and confirmed by the Senate, with statutory limits intended to keep the board bipartisan. Its stated mission is to ensure universal access to non-commercial, high-quality content and telecommunications services.

Programming supported

Over the decades CPB funding has supported well-known programmes including Sesame Street, Mister Rogers' Neighborhood, Nova, Frontline and the documentaries of Ken Burns. The corporation also funds community service grants that form the core federal support for local public media stations.
