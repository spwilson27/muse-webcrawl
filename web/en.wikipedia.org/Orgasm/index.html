An orgasm (from Greek orgasmos, "excitement or swelling") is the sudden discharge of accumulated sexual excitement during the sexual response cycle, resulting in rhythmic, involuntary muscular contractions in the pelvic region and intense pleasure. Orgasms are controlled by the autonomic nervous system and are associated in males with ejaculation and in females with contractions of the vaginal wall and uterus.

Physiology

In the model of Masters and Johnson, orgasm is the third of four phases (excitement, plateau, orgasm, resolution). Heart rate, blood pressure and breathing peak, and the hormone oxytocin is released. Neuroimaging studies show deactivation of parts of the prefrontal cortex and activation of reward circuits during orgasm.

Health aspects

Orgasm is a normal part of human sexuality. Difficulties reaching orgasm (anorgasmia) can have physiological or psychological causes and are a recognised clinical concern. Research has explored associations between sexual activity and health outcomes such as stress reduction and sleep quality, though claims of specific benefits remain debated.

Cultural aspects

Attitudes toward orgasm, especially female orgasm, have varied widely across cultures and historical periods, from celebration to taboo, and it has been a subject of scientific study since the work of Alfred Kinsey in the mid-20th century.
