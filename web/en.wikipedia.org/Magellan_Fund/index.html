The Fidelity Magellan Fund (Mutual fund ticker FMAGX) is a United States mutual fund from the Fidelity family of funds. It is perhaps the world's best-known actively managed mutual fund, because of the record-breaking tenure of its manager Peter Lynch, under whom it became for a time the largest mutual fund in the world.

History

The fund was established in 1963 as the Fidelity International Fund and took the Magellan name later in the decade. When Peter Lynch became manager in 1977 the fund held about $18 million in assets; when he retired in 1990 it held more than $14 billion. Lynch's average annual return of 29.2 percent over thirteen years made Magellan the best-performing mutual fund of its era and a byword for growth investing, a record popularised by his books One Up on Wall Street and Beating the Street.

Successors included Morris Smith, Jeffrey Vinik, Robert Stansky, Harry Lange, Steven Kaye, and Jeffrey Feingold; under Stansky the fund's assets peaked at about $110 billion around 2000, and it was closed to most new investors between 1997 and 2008 to manage its size. Subsequent performance roughly tracking or trailing the S&P 500, together with the industry-wide shift to index funds, reduced Magellan's prominence, though it remains one of Fidelity's flagship large-cap growth funds.

Investment approach

Magellan is a diversified large-cap equity fund that invests primarily in growth stocks and value stocks of companies Fidelity's research associates with above-average earnings potential, historically unconstrained by sector or, at times, by geography.
