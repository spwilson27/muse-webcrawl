Castries

Castries is the capital and largest city of Saint Lucia, an island country in the eastern Caribbean Sea. Founded by the French in 1650 as Carénage and renamed in 1785 after Charles Eugène Gabriel de La Croix, marquis de Castries, it is home to roughly a third of the country's population and is its principal port and commercial centre.

Geography and layout

Castries sits on a sheltered natural harbour on the north-west coast of the island, surrounded by hills including Morne Fortune, the historic ridge where the French and British fought over the island. The city centre, rebuilt after destructive fires in 1796, 1813, 1927 and 1948, follows a grid plan around Derek Walcott Square, named for the Nobel Prize-winning poet born in the city. Landmarks include the Cathedral Basilica of the Immaculate Conception, one of the largest churches in the Caribbean, the Central Library and the bustling Castries Market.

Economy and transport

The port handles cargo and a large volume of cruise-ship calls, making tourism central to the city's economy. George F. L. Charles Airport, on the Vigie Peninsula just north of the city, handles regional flights, while most international arrivals use Hewanorra Airport in the south. Government House, the official residence of the Governor General, stands on Morne Fortune.
