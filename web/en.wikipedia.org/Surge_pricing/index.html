Surge pricing

Surge pricing is a form of dynamic pricing in which a company raises prices during periods of high demand, in order to balance supply and demand. It is most associated with ride-hailing services such as Uber, which automatically multiply fares in busy areas or times, but similar mechanisms are used in hospitality, food delivery, and ticketing.

Rationale and effects

Supporters argue that higher prices attract more drivers or sellers into the market and ration scarce supply to those who value it most. Critics counter that surge pricing can look exploitative, particularly during emergencies or disasters, and several services have faced legal and public pressure to cap increases in such situations.

Related concepts

Surge pricing is one instance of dynamic pricing, alongside airline yield management and time-of-day electricity tariffs.
