Abandoned village

An abandoned village is a village that has been deserted by its inhabitants. Villages may be abandoned because of natural disasters, economic changes, disease, war, or deliberate clearance by landowners or governments. In historical archaeology, the sites of such settlements are often called deserted medieval villages (DMVs), especially in Britain, where thousands of settlements were abandoned between the 14th and 16th centuries.

Causes

The causes of village abandonment are varied. In England, the conversion of arable land to sheep pasture during enclosure, combined with the depopulation caused by the Black Death, emptied many settlements. Elsewhere, villages were abandoned when mines or local industries closed, when rivers changed course or wells ran dry, when coastlines eroded, or when governments relocated populations for security or development reasons. Ghost towns in mining regions are a related phenomenon.

Deserted medieval villages

More than 3,000 deserted medieval villages are known in England alone. The best-studied example is Wharram Percy in Yorkshire, excavated over several decades, which revealed the layout of manor house, church, peasant houses, and crofts. Similar deserted settlements are documented in Germany (where they are called Wüstungen), France, Italy, and Spain, where rural depopulation in the 20th century produced the phenomenon of the España vaciada ("emptied Spain").

Modern abandonment

Village abandonment continues today. Settlements have been evacuated because of industrial accidents, such as the town of Pripyat after the Chernobyl disaster, or because of flooding caused by dam construction. In parts of southern Europe, Japan, and China, demographic decline and urban migration have left large numbers of rural villages with few or no permanent residents.
