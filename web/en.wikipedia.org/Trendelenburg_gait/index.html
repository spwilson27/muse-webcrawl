Trendelenburg gait

Trendelenburg gait is an abnormal gait pattern caused by weakness of the hip abductor muscles, principally the gluteus medius and gluteus minimus, which normally stabilise the pelvis when standing on one leg. It is named after the German surgeon Friedrich Trendelenburg, who described the associated Trendelenburg sign in 1895.

Mechanism and appearance

During the stance phase of gait, the abductors of the weight-bearing hip contract to keep the pelvis level. If they are weak — or their mechanics are disrupted — the pelvis drops on the unsupported (swinging) side instead of rising slightly as it normally does. The person compensates by leaning the trunk toward the weak side, producing a characteristic lurching or waddling walk; when both sides are affected, the alternating lean gives a pronounced "waddling" gait.

Causes

Causes include damage to the superior gluteal nerve (which supplies gluteus medius and minimus), poliomyelitis, muscular dystrophy, congenital dislocation or dysplasia of the hip, coxa vara, Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease, hip osteoarthritis, and complications of hip replacement surgery in which the abductor mechanism is injured or the geometry of the joint is altered. Diagnosis rests on observation of gait and the Trendelenburg test: standing on the affected leg for up to 30 seconds causes the opposite side of the pelvis to drop.

Management

Treatment addresses the underlying cause, with physiotherapy to strengthen the abductors, use of a cane in the opposite hand to reduce the abductor force required, and surgical correction of structural hip abnormalities where appropriate.
