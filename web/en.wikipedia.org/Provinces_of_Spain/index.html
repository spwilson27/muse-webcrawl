Provinces of Spain

Spain is divided into 50 provinces (provincias), which form the second level of its territorial organisation below the 17 autonomous communities and the two autonomous cities of Ceuta and Melilla. Most provinces are named after their capital city.

History

The provincial division was established in 1833 by the minister Javier de Burgos, drawing on earlier Enlightenment-era and Napoleonic schemes. Although the 1978 Constitution made the autonomous communities the principal tier of devolved government, provinces remain electoral constituencies for the Congress of Deputies and, in most of the country, retain provincial councils (diputaciones) responsible for supporting smaller municipalities.

Organisation

Some autonomous communities coincide with a single province (Asturias, Cantabria, La Rioja, Madrid, Murcia, Navarre, and the Balearic Islands). In the Basque Country the provinces are officially called "historical territories" (Álava, Biscay, and Gipuzkoa) and have powerful foral deputations.
