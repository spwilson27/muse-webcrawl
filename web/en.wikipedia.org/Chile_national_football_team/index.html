The Chile national football team represents Chile in men's international football and is controlled by the Asociación Nacional de Fútbol Profesional (ANFP). Nicknamed La Roja, the team is one of the four founding members of CONMEBOL and competed in the first World Cup in 1930.

History

Chile hosted the 1962 World Cup and achieved its best finish, third place, led by Leonel Sánchez. After decades of near-misses — including four Copa América runner-up finishes — the "golden generation" of Claudio Bravo, Gary Medel, Arturo Vidal and Alexis Sánchez, coached first by Marcelo Bielsa and later Jorge Sampaoli and Juan Antonio Pizzi, won back-to-back Copa América titles in 2015 (on home soil) and the 2016 Copa América Centenario, both times defeating Argentina in finals decided by penalty shoot-outs. Chile also finished runner-up at the 2017 FIFA Confederations Cup.

Style and rivalries

Under Bielsa the team became known for intense pressing and attacking play, a style maintained by his successors. Chile's principal rivalries are with Peru (the Clásico del Pacífico) and Argentina. The team plays home matches at the Estadio Nacional in Santiago, and its all-time top scorer is Alexis Sánchez.
