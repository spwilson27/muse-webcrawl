Istanbul is the largest city in Turkey, straddling the Bosphorus strait between Europe and Asia. With a population of over 16 million, it is Turkey's economic, cultural, and historic centre and one of the world's largest cities. Founded as Byzantion by Greek colonists in the 7th century BCE, it was refounded by the Roman emperor Constantine the Great in 330 CE as Constantinople, serving as the capital of the Roman/Byzantine Empire for over a thousand years and then of the Ottoman Empire from 1453 until 1922.

History

Constantine dedicated his "New Rome" in 330; the city grew into the largest and wealthiest in Europe, defended by the Theodosian Walls. Under Justinian I it saw the construction of Hagia Sophia (537), still one of the world's great buildings. Constantinople withstood Arab and Bulgar sieges but was sacked by the Fourth Crusade in 1204 and ruled by a Latin empire until its recovery by Michael VIII Palaiologos in 1261. On 29 May 1453, Sultan Mehmed II ("the Conqueror") captured the city, making it the Ottoman capital. Under Suleiman the Magnificent and his architect Mimar Sinan, it gained monuments such as the Süleymaniye Mosque; Topkapı Palace and the Grand Bazaar also date from the Ottoman centuries. After the First World War, the Turkish War of Independence and the founding of the Republic of Turkey in 1923 moved the capital to Ankara; the name "Istanbul" was officially adopted in 1930.

Geography

The city lies on both sides of the Bosphorus, which connects the Black Sea to the Sea of Marmara; the Golden Horn inlet divides the European side. Three bridges and two tunnels (including the Marmaray rail tunnel) cross the strait. The historic areas of Istanbul were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985.

Economy and culture

Istanbul generates a major share of Turkey's GDP and hosts the Borsa Istanbul stock exchange, major ports, and Istanbul Airport, one of the world's busiest. Landmarks include Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque), Topkapı Palace, the Basilica Cistern, Galata Tower, and Dolmabahçe Palace. The city is famous for Turkish cuisine, hammams, the Grand Bazaar and Spice Bazaar, and football clubs Galatasaray, Fenerbahçe, and Beşiktaş.
