Battle of Mount Harriet

Mount Harriet, south-west of Port Stanley, scene of a night attack by 42 Commando on 11–12 June 1982.

Date11–12 June 1982
LocationMount Harriet, East Falkland
ResultBritish victory
BelligerentsUnited Kingdom vs. Argentina

The Battle of Mount Harriet was an engagement of the Falklands War, fought on the night of 11–12 June 1982. British forces of 42 Commando, Royal Marines, attacked and captured the Argentine-held hill of Mount Harriet, one of the ring of high ground defending Port Stanley.

Background

After the landings at San Carlos and the advance across East Falkland, British brigades closed in on Stanley in early June 1982. Mount Harriet, held by the Argentine 4th Infantry Regiment (RI 4), formed part of the outer defensive line alongside Two Sisters and Mount Longdon, attacked the same night.

The attack

Instead of assaulting frontally from the west as the Argentines expected, 42 Commando made a long flanking march south through a minefield, guided by patrols, and attacked from the south-east — into the rear of the Argentine positions. Supported by artillery and naval gunfire, the Marines achieved surprise; Argentine resistance, though fierce in places, collapsed as the attack rolled along the summit. By dawn Mount Harriet was in British hands.

Outcome

British losses were remarkably light — one Royal Marine killed — while Argentine casualties were around 18 killed and some 300 taken prisoner. The fall of Mount Harriet, Two Sisters and Mount Longdon in a single night broke the outer defences of Stanley and set up the final battles for Tumbledown, Wireless Ridge and Sapper Hill, after which Argentine forces surrendered on 14 June.
