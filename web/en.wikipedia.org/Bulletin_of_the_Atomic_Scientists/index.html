The Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists is a nonprofit organization and publication concerning global security and public policy issues, especially those related to the dangers posed by nuclear weapons, climate change, and disruptive technologies. It was founded in 1945 by scientists who had worked on the Manhattan Project, including Eugene Rabinowitch and Hyman Goldsmith, shortly after the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, to inform the public and policymakers about the risks of nuclear weapons.

Doomsday Clock

The Bulletin is best known for the Doomsday Clock, created in 1947 with cover art by Martyl Langsdorf. The clock's time — set annually by the Bulletin's Science and Security Board in consultation with its Board of Sponsors — symbolizes how close humanity is to catastrophe, with midnight representing global disaster. Originally set at seven minutes to midnight, it has ranged from 17 minutes (1991, after the Cold War) to the closest settings in the 2020s, when it was advanced to 90 seconds (2023) and then 89 seconds to midnight (2025).

Publication

Founded as a print newsletter, the Bulletin became a magazine and moved to an online-first format in the 21st century; its scholarly journal content is published in partnership with Taylor & Francis. Contributors have included Albert Einstein, J. Robert Oppenheimer, Bertrand Russell, and many Nobel laureates.
