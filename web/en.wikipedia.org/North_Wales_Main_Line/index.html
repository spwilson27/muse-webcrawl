The North Wales Main Line (Welsh: Prif Linell Gogledd Cymru), also known as the North Wales Coast Line, is a major railway line in the United Kingdom running from Crewe in Cheshire, England, along the north coast of Wales to the port of Holyhead on Anglesey. It forms the trunk route for services between London Euston and Holyhead, where trains connect with ferries to Ireland.

Route

From Crewe the line passes through Chester, then crosses into Wales along the Dee estuary through Flint, Prestatyn, Rhyl, Colwyn Bay, Llandudno Junction and Bangor. West of Bangor, trains cross the Britannia Bridge over the Menai Strait to Anglesey, continuing through Llanfairpwllgwyngyll to Holyhead. Branch lines diverge to Llandudno and to the Conwy Valley line at Llandudno Junction, and to Wrexham via the Borderlands line at Chester.

History

The line was built by the Chester and Holyhead Railway and opened in stages between 1848 and 1850. Its most famous structure is Robert Stephenson's Britannia Tubular Bridge (1850) across the Menai Strait; after a fire in 1970 the bridge was rebuilt, and it now carries a road deck above the railway. The line was engineered for Irish Mail trains linking London with the Dublin ferry, and boat trains remained a feature for over a century.

Services

Today long-distance services are operated by Avanti West Coast between Holyhead/Bangor and London Euston, while Transport for Wales Rail provides local and regional services, including through trains to Cardiff, Manchester Airport and Birmingham. The line is not electrified; resignalling with the European Train Control System on the Cambrian lines has made North Wales a candidate for future digital signalling deployment.
