Structural dynamics

Structural dynamics is a sub-type of structural analysis which covers the behavior of structures subjected to dynamic loading, that is, loads which change with time: actions with high acceleration, such as those from people, traffic, gusts, waves, traffic, explosions, and earthquakes. Any structure can be subject to dynamic loading. Dynamic analysis can be used to find dynamic displacements, time history, and modal analysis.

The governing equations balance applied forces against inertia forces (from accelerations, via Newton's second law), damping forces, and stiffness forces: M·ü + C·u̇ + K·u = F(t). A full solution requires knowing the loading, mass, stiffness, and damping characteristics of the structure. Analysis ranges from the single-degree-of-freedom oscillator to modal superposition of many modes and direct numerical time integration (e.g., Newmark methods).

Types of analysis

Modal analysis finds natural frequencies and mode shapes, which reveal how a structure prefers to vibrate. Response spectrum and time-history analyses are standard in earthquake engineering; harmonic and random vibration analyses serve machinery and wind engineering. Phenomena studied include resonance, fatigue, flutter, and human-induced vibration of floors and footbridges. Tools for mitigating dynamic response include base isolation and tuned mass dampers.

Applications

Structural dynamics underlies the design of towers, bridges, offshore platforms, vehicles, and aircraft, and the qualification of buildings and equipment for seismic and blast loads. Experimental methods, including shake tables and operational modal analysis, validate analytical models.
