Jacopo Carucci (May 24, 1494 – January 2, 1557), usually known as Jacopo da Pontormo, was an Italian Mannerist painter and portraitist from the Florentine School. His work represents a profound stylistic shift from the calm perspectival regularity of the Renaissance.

Works

Pontormo's most famous works include The Deposition from the Cross (c. 1525–1528) in the Capponi Chapel of Santa Felicita, Florence, with its swirling figures, absence of a cross, and luminous, weightless composition; the Visitation in Carmignano; and frescoes at the Medici villa at Poggio a Caiano.

Life

Apprenticed under Leonardo da Vinci's circle and then Andrea del Sarto, Pontormo was admired early by Michelangelo. His later years were marked by reclusiveness, recorded in his surviving diary of 1554–1556. His pupil Bronzino became the leading portraitist of the Medici court.
