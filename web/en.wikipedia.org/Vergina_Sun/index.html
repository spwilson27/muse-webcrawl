Vergina Sun

The Vergina Sun (also known as the Star of Vergina) is a rayed solar symbol appearing in ancient Greek art of the 6th to 2nd centuries BC. It consists of a central disc with sixteen rays — eight straight triangular rays and eight wavy rays — arranged around it. Its name comes from the town of Vergina in Greek Macedonia.

Archaeological finds

The symbol became internationally known after excavations in 1977–78 by Manolis Andronikos at Vergina (ancient Aigai), where it was found in gold on the larnax containing the remains attributed to Philip II of Macedon in the royal tombs. The star also appears on coins, shields, pottery, and other artefacts from the Macedonian kingdom and elsewhere in the Greek world, and has been interpreted as a royal emblem of the Argead dynasty, though scholars debate whether it was an exclusive dynastic badge or a more general decorative and religious motif.

Modern use and dispute

The Vergina Sun was adopted as a national symbol and appeared on the flag of the Republic of Macedonia (now North Macedonia) adopted in 1992. Greece objected, regarding the symbol as an exclusively Greek heritage emblem, and the dispute — part of the wider Macedonia naming dispute — drew international attention. Under Greek pressure, including a trade embargo in 1994–95, the republic replaced its flag in 1995 with a stylised sun design. The Vergina Sun remains the emblem of the Greek region of Macedonia and appears on Greek coins and municipal arms; the 2018 Prespa Agreement further regulated its public use by North Macedonia's institutions.
