Bikram Sambat

Bikram Sambat (abbreviated BS) is a Hindu calendar used in Nepal and parts of India. It is the official calendar of Nepal. The calendar is a tropical solar calendar based on ancient Hindu tradition, and it is approximately 56.7 years ahead of the Gregorian calendar: the BS new year falls in mid-April.

Structure

The Bikram Sambat year consists of twelve months — Baisakh, Jestha, Ashadh, Shrawan, Bhadra, Ashwin, Kartik, Mangsir, Poush, Magh, Falgun, and Chaitra — whose lengths vary between 29 and 32 days and are not fixed by rule but published annually. The era is traditionally dated to 57 BCE and associated with the legendary king Vikramaditya of Ujjain; the related Vikram Samvat era is used in much of India with different new-year conventions.

Use

In Nepal, Bikram Sambat is used for all official purposes, holidays, and daily life, with dates in documents expressed in BS. A BS date can be converted by subtracting about 56.7 years, adjusting for the mid-April new year. The calendar was the official calendar of the Rana regime's administration and remains central to Nepali identity, although the Gregorian calendar is also used in business and international contexts.
