Y Combinator

Y Combinator (YC) is an American technology startup accelerator launched in March 2005 by Paul Graham, Jessica Livingston, Robert Morris, and Trevor Blackwell. It has been used to launch more than 5,000 companies, including Airbnb, DoorDash, Coinbase, Dropbox, Stripe, Reddit, Instacart, and Twitch. The combined valuation of YC companies has exceeded several hundred billion dollars.

Program

YC runs batches of startups (originally summer and winter, later four per year) through a roughly three-month program. Each accepted company receives a standard investment — since 2022, $500,000 total: $125,000 on a post-money SAFE for 7% of the company, plus $375,000 on an uncapped SAFE with a most-favoured-nation clause. Founders receive mentorship from partners, weekly talks from successful founders, and access to the Bookface alumni network. Each batch culminates in Demo Day, where startups pitch to investors.

The program was based in Mountain View and later San Francisco, California. During the COVID-19 pandemic batches went fully remote before returning to in-person operation in San Francisco.

History and leadership

Paul Graham led YC until 2014, when Sam Altman became president; Altman expanded the program's scale and launched YC Continuity, a growth-stage fund, and the online Startup School. Michael Seibel and later Garry Tan (from 2023) succeeded as president and CEO. YC also publishes influential essays and resources on startups, and runs the Hacker News forum.

The accelerator's name comes from the Y combinator, a fixed-point combinator in lambda calculus — a nod to Graham's background in Lisp.
