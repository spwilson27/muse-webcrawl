A Moveable Feast

AuthorErnest Hemingway
Published1964 (posthumously)
GenreMemoir

A Moveable Feast is a memoir by American author Ernest Hemingway about his years as a young, struggling expatriate journalist and writer in Paris in the 1920s. The book was published posthumously in 1964 by his widow Mary Hemingway, assembled from manuscripts and notes Hemingway had worked on in the last years of his life.

Content

The sketches describe Hemingway's apprenticeship as a writer, his first marriage to Hadley Richardson, and his encounters with members of the expatriate "Lost Generation", including Gertrude Stein, Ezra Pound, James Joyce, F. Scott Fitzgerald, and Ford Madox Ford. Famous passages recount writing in Paris cafés, hunger as a discipline, horse racing and skiing trips, and the painful dissolution of his friendships and marriage. The title comes from a remark Hemingway made to his friend A. E. Hotchner: "If you are lucky enough to have lived in Paris as a young man, then wherever you go for the rest of your life, it stays with you, for Paris is a moveable feast."

Editions

A "restored edition" edited by Hemingway's grandson Seán Hemingway appeared in 2009, rearranging chapters and adding material, and provoking debate over which version better represents Hemingway's intentions. The memoir became a bestseller again in France after the November 2015 Paris attacks, when it was adopted as a symbol of the city's resilience.
