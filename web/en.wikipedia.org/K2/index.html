K2

K2 (Mount Godwin-Austen; Chogori)
Elevation: 8,611 m (28,251 ft)
Range: Karakoram
Location: Pakistan–China border
First ascent: July 31, 1954 (Lacedelli & Compagnoni)

K2, at 8,611 metres (28,251 ft), is the second-highest mountain on Earth, after Mount Everest. It lies in the Karakoram range, partly in Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan, and partly in the Trans-Karakoram Tract administered by China. K2 is widely considered the most difficult and dangerous of the fourteen eight-thousanders and is known as the "Savage Mountain."

Climbing history

The mountain was surveyed in 1856 by the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India, which designated it "K2" as the second peak measured in the Karakoram. An Italian expedition led by Ardito Desio achieved the first ascent on July 31, 1954, when Achille Compagnoni and Lino Lacedelli reached the summit via the Abruzzi Spur, the route still most commonly used. K2 remained the only eight-thousander never climbed in winter until a Nepali team summited on January 16, 2021.

Danger

K2 combines extreme altitude, steep and exposed terrain such as the Bottleneck couloir, unpredictable weather, and serac fall. Historically, roughly one climber died for every four who summited; the mountain's worst disaster occurred in August 2008, when eleven climbers were killed in two days. Although its fatality rate has improved with better forecasting and fixed lines, K2 remains far more dangerous than Everest, and has never been climbed from the east face.
