Zion Mule Corps

ActiveApril 1915 – May 1916
CountryBritish Empire
RoleTransport (mule train)
SizeAbout 650 men, 750 mules
EngagementsGallipoli campaign
CommandersJohn Henry Patterson; Joseph Trumpeldor (second-in-command)

The Zion Mule Corps was a transport unit of the British Army in the First World War, raised in 1915 largely from Jewish volunteers — many of them Russian Jews expelled from Ottoman Palestine who had gathered in Egypt. It is regarded as the first Jewish military unit to serve in modern times and a forerunner of the Jewish Legion and ultimately the Israel Defense Forces.

Formation

The corps was proposed by the Zionist activist Ze'ev Jabotinsky and by Joseph Trumpeldor, a one-armed veteran of the Russo-Japanese War. Because regulations restricted foreign volunteers from combat roles, the unit was formed as a mule transport corps. Command was given to Lieutenant Colonel John Henry Patterson, with Trumpeldor as his second-in-command.

Service at Gallipoli

The corps landed at Gallipoli in late April 1915 and carried ammunition, food and water to the front lines under heavy shellfire, suffering casualties and earning commendations for gallantry. It served until the evacuation of the peninsula and was disbanded in mid-1916, though many of its men later joined the Jewish Legion battalions of the Royal Fusiliers.
