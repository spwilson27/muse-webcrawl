Battle of Salga Bay

Salga Bay, Terceira, where Azorean defenders drove a Spanish landing force into the sea on 25 July 1581.

Date25 July 1581
LocationSalga Bay, Terceira Island, Azores
ResultPortuguese (Antonist) victory
BelligerentsSupporters of António of Crato vs. Philip II of Spain

The Battle of Salga Bay was fought on 25 July 1581 at Salga Bay on Terceira Island in the Azores, during the War of the Portuguese Succession. Forces loyal to the Portuguese claimant António, Prior of Crato, defeated a Spanish landing party, preserving Terceira as the last Portuguese territory holding out against Philip II of Spain.

Background

After the death of King Henry of Portugal in 1580, Philip II of Spain claimed the Portuguese crown and sent the Duke of Alba to conquer the mainland, defeating António at the Battle of Alcântara. The Azores, however, rejected Spanish rule; Terceira in particular declared for António and prepared to resist, with French encouragement.

The battle

On 25 July 1581 a Spanish squadron under Pedro Valdés put about a thousand soldiers ashore at Salga Bay, on Terceira's southern coast, hoping to establish a beachhead. The island's governor, Ciprião de Figueiredo, mobilised the local militia, and in the ensuing fight the islanders famously drove a stampede of cattle into the Spanish ranks, breaking their formation. Pressed between the defenders and the shore, the landing force was routed; hundreds of Spaniards were killed or drowned, and only a handful regained their ships.

Aftermath

Salga was one of the few Portuguese successes of the succession war and made Terceira a rallying point for resistance. The respite lasted two years: after the French fleet supporting António was beaten at the Battle of Ponta Delgada in 1582, a large Spanish expedition under Álvaro de Bazán conquered Terceira in 1583, completing Philip's acquisition of the Portuguese empire.
