Adhesion

Adhesion is the tendency of dissimilar particles or surfaces to cling to one another; cohesion refers to the clinging of similar particles or surfaces. Adhesion underlies glues, paints, laminates, and the ability of geckos to climb walls, and it plays roles in biology, such as cell adhesion.

Mechanisms

Several mechanisms contribute: mechanical adhesion (interlocking into pores), chemical adhesion (formation of chemical bonds across an interface), dispersive adhesion (van der Waals forces), electrostatic adhesion, and diffusive adhesion (interdiffusion of polymer chains). Surface energy and wetting determine how well a liquid adhesive spreads on a substrate.

Measurement and failure

Adhesive strength is measured by peel, lap-shear, and pull-off tests. Joints can fail adhesively (at the interface) or cohesively (within the adhesive or substrate). Surface preparation—cleaning, abrasion, plasma or corona treatment—is often decisive for bond quality.
