Gambling in Macau

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gambling in Macau has been legal since the 1850s, when the Portuguese government legalized the activity in its colony. Since then, Macau has become known worldwide as the "Gambling capital of the world" or the "Monte Carlo of the East." Gambling tourism is Macau's biggest source of revenue, comprising a large share of the economy, and gaming taxes supply the majority of government income.

History

For four decades from 1962, casino gambling operated under a monopoly concession held by Stanley Ho's Sociedade de Turismo e Diversões de Macau (STDM). After the handover of Macau to China in 1999, the new Special Administrative Region ended the monopoly in 2002 and awarded concessions to international operators including Sands, Wynn, and MGM, alongside local firms SJM, Galaxy, and Melco. Foreign investment produced mega-resorts on the reclaimed Cotai Strip, including The Venetian Macao, one of the largest casino properties in the world.

The industry

Macau's gross gaming revenue surpassed that of the Las Vegas Strip in 2006 and at its peak was several times larger, driven by visitors from mainland China and Hong Kong, where casino gambling is illegal. Baccarat accounts for the overwhelming majority of revenue. The high-roller VIP segment historically depended on junket promoters who extended credit to wealthy gamblers; an anti-corruption campaign in mainland China from 2014 sharply reduced VIP play, and new gaming legislation in 2022 effectively ended the junket model and imposed ten-year concession terms with greater government oversight.

Regulation and social issues

Casinos are regulated by the Gaming Inspection and Coordination Bureau (DICJ). The legal gambling age is 21. Macau's dependence on gaming has prompted government policies promoting economic diversification into tourism, conventions, and entertainment, as well as measures addressing problem gambling and money laundering.
