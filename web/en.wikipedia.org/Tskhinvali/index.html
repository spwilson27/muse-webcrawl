Tskhinvali

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tskhinvali (Georgian: ცხინვალი; Ossetian: Цхинвал, Tskhinval) is a city in the de facto independent Republic of South Ossetia, recognised internationally as part of Georgia. It serves as the capital of South Ossetia and lies on the Greater Liakhvi River in the southern foothills of the Greater Caucasus, about 100 km northwest of Tbilisi. Its population is roughly 30,000.

History

First mentioned in Georgian chronicles in 1398, the town grew as a trading post where Georgians, Ossetians, Armenians, and Jews traded. In the Soviet period it was the capital of the South Ossetian Autonomous Oblast within the Georgian SSR and was renamed Staliniri from 1934 to 1961. The city was heavily damaged during the 1991–92 South Ossetia War and again in the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, when the Battle of Tskhinvali in August 2008 — beginning with a Georgian artillery assault on the night of 7–8 August, followed by a Russian ground offensive — left much of the city in ruins and displaced most of its Georgian population.

Status

Since 2008, the city has been administered by the Russian-backed South Ossetian authorities and hosts a Russian military base. Georgia regards it as the administrative centre of its Provisional Administrative Entity of South Ossetia and continues to claim it under the laws on occupied territories.
