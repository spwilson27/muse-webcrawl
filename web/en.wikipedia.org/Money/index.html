Money

Money

Functions: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value
Forms: commodity money, fiat money, bank deposits, digital money

Money is any item or verifiable record that is generally accepted as payment for goods and services and for the repayment of debts, such as taxes, in a particular country or socio-economic context. Money was historically an emergent market phenomenon, established by people as a medium of exchange for goods and services; today, most money takes the form of fiat currency issued by governments and bank deposits created by the banking system.

Functions

Economists conventionally attribute three primary functions to money: it is a medium of exchange, allowing trade without the double coincidence of wants required by barter; a unit of account, providing a common measure of value; and a store of value, allowing purchasing power to be saved and retrieved over time. Some authors add a fourth function, a standard of deferred payment.

History

Early forms of money included commodity money such as cattle, grain, shells, and precious metals. Metal coins were standardized in Lydia in the 7th century BCE, and paper money developed in China during the Tang and Song dynasties before spreading to Europe in the early modern period. The gold standard dominated the 19th and early 20th centuries; after the collapse of the Bretton Woods system in 1971, major currencies became fiat money, backed by government decree rather than convertibility into a commodity.

Types and supply

Modern money consists mainly of currency in circulation and deposits at commercial banks. Central banks conduct monetary policy to influence the money supply and interest rates. Measures of the money supply (such as M0, M1, M2, and M3) aggregate money by liquidity. Digital forms, including electronic payments, mobile money, and cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin (introduced in 2009), have expanded the concept of money in the 21st century.
