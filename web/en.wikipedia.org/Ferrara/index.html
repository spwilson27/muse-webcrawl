Ferrara

Ferrara is a city and comune in Emilia-Romagna, northern Italy, capital of the province of Ferrara. Situated about 44 kilometres northeast of Bologna, it is known for its Renaissance-era streets and palaces.

History

Ferrara rose to prominence under the House of Este, who ruled from the 13th century until 1598 and made it a flourishing court city under patrons such as Ercole I d'Este. The Addizione Erculea, a pioneering Renaissance urban expansion designed by Biagio Rossetti, doubled the city's size. After 1598 Ferrara passed to the Papal States and declined politically, though its cultural heritage endured.

Sights

Landmarks include the Castello Estense, a moated medieval castle; the Romanesque-Gothic Ferrara Cathedral; and the Palazzo dei Diamanti, faced with diamond-pointed stone blocks. The historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognised as a remarkable example of Renaissance town planning.
