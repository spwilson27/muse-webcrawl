The National Football League (NFL) is a professional American football league consisting of 32 teams, divided equally between the American Football Conference (AFC) and the National Football Conference (NFC). The NFL is one of the major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada and the highest professional level of American football in the world. Each season begins with a three-week preseason in August, followed by an 18-week regular season from September to January in which each team plays 17 games, concluding with the playoffs and the Super Bowl, the league's championship game.

The shield of the National Football League.

History

The league was formed in 1920 in Canton, Ohio, as the American Professional Football Association and adopted its current name in 1922. It grew slowly in the shadow of college football before expanding in popularity in the 1950s and 1960s. The NFL's great rival, the American Football League (AFL), was founded in 1960; the two leagues agreed to merge in 1966, with the merger completed in 1970, and the championship game between their champions became the Super Bowl.

Structure

The 32 teams are divided into two conferences of 16 teams, each subdivided into four divisions of four teams. The regular season is followed by a single-elimination playoff tournament involving 14 teams, culminating in the Super Bowl between the AFC and NFC champions. The NFL operates under a salary cap and shares national television revenue among its teams, arrangements credited with maintaining competitive balance. Its annual player draft, the NFL Draft, distributes incoming college players among the teams.

Popularity and economics

The NFL is the most popular spectator sport in the United States by television viewership, and the Super Bowl is consistently among the most-watched television broadcasts of the year. The league generates the highest revenue of any sports league in the world, and its franchises are among the most valuable in professional sports.
