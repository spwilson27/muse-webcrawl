Battle of Bataan

Date7 January – 9 April 1942
LocationBataan Peninsula, Luzon, Philippines
ResultJapanese victory
BelligerentsUnited States and Commonwealth of the Philippines vs. Empire of Japan
CommandersDouglas MacArthur, Jonathan Wainwright, Edward P. King vs. Masaharu Homma

The Battle of Bataan (Tagalog: Labanan sa Bataan) was fought by the United States and the Philippine Commonwealth against Japan during the Second World War, from 7 January to 9 April 1942. The battle represented the most intense phase of the Japanese invasion of the Philippines and ended in the largest surrender in American military history, followed by the notorious Bataan Death March.

Background

After the Japanese landings on Luzon in December 1941, General Douglas MacArthur executed War Plan Orange, withdrawing his United States Army Forces in the Far East (USAFFE) into the Bataan Peninsula while the island fortress of Corregidor guarded the entrance to Manila Bay. Some 80,000 American and Filipino troops, along with tens of thousands of civilian refugees, crowded into Bataan with inadequate food and medical supplies; rations were cut to half, then quarter, and disease — malaria, dysentery and dengue — ravaged the defenders.

Battle

The defenders held a line across the peninsula at Abucay, then fell back to a second line along the Orion–Bagac road. Japanese commander Masaharu Homma, having underestimated the strength of the defence, was forced to pause and receive reinforcements. MacArthur was ordered to Australia in March, leaving Jonathan Wainwright in command. The final Japanese offensive opened on 3 April 1942 with heavy artillery and air bombardment; the exhausted, starving II Corps on the eastern side of the peninsula collapsed. On 9 April, Major General Edward P. King surrendered the Bataan force — some 75,000 men — against orders, judging further resistance futile.

Aftermath

The prisoners were forced on the Bataan Death March to Camp O'Donnell, during which thousands of Filipino and American prisoners died from abuse, starvation and murder by their captors. Corregidor held out until 6 May 1942. Homma was later tried and executed for war crimes connected with the march. The defenders' stand delayed the Japanese timetable and became an enduring symbol of resistance in both the United States and the Philippines, commemorated each 9 April as Araw ng Kagitingan (Day of Valor).
