Tax withholding in the United States

Tax withholding in the United States is a system under which employers withhold federal, state and local taxes from employees' wages and remit them directly to the government, crediting the amounts against the employee's eventual tax liability.

System

Federal income tax withholding was introduced in its modern form by the Current Tax Payment Act of 1943, partly at the urging of economist Milton Friedman, who worked on the measure at the Treasury. Employees file Form W-4 to indicate withholding adjustments; employers report wages and withholding annually on Form W-2. Separate withholding applies to Social Security and Medicare payroll taxes under FICA, as well as backup withholding on certain other payments.
