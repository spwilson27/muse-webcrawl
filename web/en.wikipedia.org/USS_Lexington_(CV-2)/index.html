USS Lexington (CV-2)

USS Lexington (CV-2), nicknamed "Lady Lex," was the lead ship of the Lexington-class aircraft carriers of the United States Navy. Originally laid down as a battlecruiser, she was converted during construction under the terms of the Washington Naval Treaty and commissioned in 1927, becoming one of the US Navy's first fleet carriers.

Together with her sister ship Saratoga, Lexington helped develop American carrier doctrine in the interwar Fleet Problems. In 1929 she famously supplied emergency electrical power to the city of Tacoma, Washington, during a drought-induced shortage.

After the outbreak of the Pacific War, Lexington took part in early raids on Japanese positions and in operations in the Coral Sea. At the Battle of the Coral Sea in May 1942, her aircraft helped sink the Japanese light carrier Shōhō and damage Shōkaku, but Lexington was struck by torpedoes and bombs; aviation-fuel explosions later in the day forced her abandonment, and she was scuttled by the destroyer Phelps. Her wreck was located in 2018 by the research vessel Petrel.
