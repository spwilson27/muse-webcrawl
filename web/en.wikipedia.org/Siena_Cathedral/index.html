Siena Cathedral (Italian: Duomo di Siena) is a medieval church in Siena, Italy, dedicated from its earliest days as a Roman Catholic Marian church, and now dedicated to the Assumption of Mary. It was the episcopal seat of the Diocese of Siena, and from the 15th century that of the Archdiocese of Siena-Colle di Val d'Elsa-Montalcino.

The cathedral was designed and completed between 1215 and 1263 on the site of an earlier structure. It has the form of a Latin cross with a slightly projecting transept, a dome and a bell tower. The dome rises from a hexagonal base with supporting columns. The lantern atop it was added by Gian Lorenzo Bernini. The nave is separated from the two aisles by semicircular arches.

Exterior

The exterior and interior are constructed of white and greenish-black marble in alternating stripes, black and white being the symbolic colours of Siena, whose legendary founders were led by a black horse and a white horse. The lower part of the west façade, in Romanesque style, was built by Giovanni Pisano between 1284 and 1299, with sculptures by his workshop.

Interior

The interior is famous for its inlaid marble mosaic pavement, one of the most ornate of its kind in Italy, depicting biblical scenes and figures across 56 panels. The cathedral also contains works by Donatello, Michelangelo, Pinturicchio — whose frescoes decorate the Piccolomini Library — and the pulpit carved by Nicola Pisano between 1265 and 1268.

The unfinished nave

In 1339 Siena planned a massive expansion that would have made the existing building merely the transept of a far larger church. The Black Death of 1348 and structural problems halted the project; the unfinished walls of the new nave, the Facciatone, still stand beside the cathedral.
