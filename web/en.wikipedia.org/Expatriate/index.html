Expatriate

An expatriate ("expat") is a person residing in a country other than their native country.

An expatriate (often shortened to expat) is a person who resides outside their native country. The word comes from the Latin ex ("out of") and patria ("native country, fatherland"). In common usage, the term often refers to educated professionals, skilled workers, or artists taking positions outside their home country, either independently or sent abroad by their employers.

Historically, expatriation also carried the sense of exile or banishment — voluntarily or forcibly removing oneself from one's homeland. In law, expatriation can refer to the renunciation of one's citizenship.

Background

During the 19th and 20th centuries, many Americans and Europeans — including writers and artists of the "Lost Generation" such as Ernest Hemingway and F. Scott Fitzgerald — lived as expatriates in Paris and other European cities.

Modern expatriates

Since the late 20th century the number of expatriates has grown with globalisation. Multinational corporations send employees on international assignments, and destination hubs such as Dubai, Singapore and Hong Kong host large expatriate communities. Assignees may receive expatriate benefits packages including housing, schooling and tax equalisation.

Distinctions

The term is often contrasted with immigrant and migrant worker; usage varies, and critics note the term is frequently applied to Western professionals whereas other mobile people are described differently. Unlike immigrants, expatriates are often understood to intend a temporary stay, though many settle permanently.
