Namazga-Tepe

LocationTurkmenistan, near Kaakhka
RegionKopet Dag foothills
TypeSettlement mound (tepe)
Occupiedc. 5300–1400 BCE

Namazga-Tepe (also Namazga Depe) is a major archaeological site in southern Turkmenistan, near the foothills of the Kopet Dag mountains not far from Ashgabat. It is the type site of the Namazga culture, a sequence of Chalcolithic and Bronze Age farming settlements that provides the standard chronological framework for prehistoric southern Central Asia.

Excavations and sequence

The mound was excavated by Soviet archaeologists from the 1940s onward, including work directed by Vadim Masson. Six successive occupation levels, designated Namazga I through VI, span roughly from the late Neolithic/Chalcolithic transition to the late Bronze Age. The sequence documents the growth of irrigation agriculture, craft specialisation, painted pottery traditions and increasingly complex settlement organisation.

Culture

Namazga III (late Chalcolithic) settlements show fine painted ceramics with geometric and zoomorphic designs, while Namazga IV–V (Early and Middle Bronze Age) mark the emergence of large proto-urban centres such as Altyn Depe and Namazga itself, with monumental architecture, metalwork in copper, bronze and precious metals, and terracotta figurines. The Namazga V period is often associated with the wider Bactria–Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC) horizon. The site declined in the Namazga VI phase during the later second millennium BCE.

Significance

Because of its long, well-stratified sequence, Namazga-Tepe serves as the reference point against which other sites in Turkmenistan, northeastern Iran and the region are dated.
