Phylogenetic tree

A phylogenetic tree, or evolutionary tree, is a diagram that represents the evolutionary relationships among organisms or other entities believed to have a common ancestor. Branching points (nodes) represent inferred ancestors, and branch lengths may be scaled to evolutionary time or amount of change.

History

Charles Darwin included a branching diagram in On the Origin of Species (1859). Modern trees are inferred computationally from morphological data and, since the late 20th century, from DNA and protein sequences using methods such as maximum parsimony, maximum likelihood and Bayesian inference.

Types

Cladograms show branching order only; phylograms scale branches by change; chronograms scale by time. Trees may be rooted (with an explicit common ancestor) or unrooted. Because of horizontal gene transfer, some relationships are better modelled by phylogenetic networks than by trees.
