United States Federal Witness Protection Program

Formed1970
AgencyUnited States Marshals Service
ParticipantsMore than 19,000 witnesses and family members (since 1971)

The United States Federal Witness Protection Program (WPP), also known as the Witness Security Program or WITSEC, is a witness protection program administered by the United States Marshals Service. It was created under the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 and is credited largely to former Department of Justice official Gerald Shur.

Operation

Witnesses — often former members of criminal organisations testifying for the prosecution — receive new identities, new documents (such as birth certificates and Social Security cards), housing, subsistence payments, and relocation to an undisclosed city, in exchange for their testimony and compliance with program rules. Participants must commit no crimes and must not return to their former areas without permission.

Record

According to the Marshals Service, no WITSEC participant who followed program guidelines has ever been harmed or killed while under active protection. The program has protected more than 19,000 witnesses and their family members since it began operations in 1971.
