Badakhshan

Badakhshan is a historical region in the Pamir Mountains of Central Asia, straddling northeastern Afghanistan and southeastern Tajikistan. Today it is divided between Badakhshan Province of Afghanistan, whose capital is Fayzabad, and the Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous Region of Tajikistan, whose capital is Khorog. The Panj River forms much of the border between the two parts.

Geography

The region is dominated by the high ranges of the Hindu Kush and the Pamirs, with deeply cut river valleys, including that of the Kokcha. The Wakhan Corridor, a narrow strip of Afghan territory, extends eastward between Tajikistan and Pakistan toward China.

History

Badakhshan was famed in the medieval world for its lapis lazuli mines at Sar-e-Sang, worked for thousands of years and a principal source of the stone for ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, and for the "Balas rubies" (spinels) of its mines. The region lay on routes of the Silk Road and was described by Marco Polo, who praised its gemstones and mountain horses. Over the centuries it was ruled by local mirs and contested by neighbouring empires; in the 19th century it became a buffer in the "Great Game" between Britain and Russia, and its present division dates from Anglo-Russian boundary agreements of the 1890s.

People

The inhabitants include Tajiks and Pamiri peoples speaking Eastern Iranian languages such as Shughni and Wakhi. Many Pamiris are followers of Ismaili Islam, and the region is noted for its relative religious distinctiveness within Afghanistan and Tajikistan.
