Battle of Gaugamela

The Battle of Gaugamela (Greek: Γαυγάμηλα), also called the Battle of Arbela, took place on 1 October 331 BC between the forces of Alexander the Great of Macedon and the Persian Empire of Darius III. Fought on a plain near modern Erbil in Iraqi Kurdistan, it was the decisive battle of Alexander's conquest of Persia, shattering Achaemenid power and opening the road to Babylon and Persepolis.

Background

After his victories at Issus (333 BC) and the sieges of Tyre and Gaza, Alexander marched into Mesopotamia. Darius assembled a vast army—including scythed chariots, fifteen Indian elephants, and cavalry from across the empire—on a broad plain he had levelled to suit his chariots and horsemen. Ancient sources put Persian numbers in the hundreds of thousands; modern historians generally estimate 50,000–120,000 against Alexander's roughly 47,000 men.

The battle

Alexander advanced obliquely to the right, drawing the Persian left-wing cavalry under Bessus away from the centre. When a gap opened in the Persian line, Alexander led the Companion cavalry in a wedge formation straight at Darius. The Persian king, as at Issus, fled the field, and his army collapsed around him. On the Macedonian left, Parmenion's wing was hard pressed by Persian and Indian cavalry under Mazaeus, some of whom broke through to loot Alexander's camp, but the Persian right also gave way when news of Darius's flight spread. Alexander pursued Darius but failed to capture him; the Persian king was murdered the following year by Bessus.

Aftermath

Gaugamela destroyed the Achaemenid Empire as a military power. Alexander was proclaimed King of Asia, occupied Babylon and Susa, and seized the imperial treasury at Persepolis, which he later burned. The battle is studied as a classic example of how a smaller, well-trained and flexibly commanded army can defeat a much larger force through manoeuvre and decisive concentration at the critical point.
