Charles Goodnight

Charles Goodnight (March 5, 1836 – December 12, 1929) was an American rancher in the Texas Panhandle, celebrated as one of the most famous cattlemen of the American West. With Oliver Loving he blazed the Goodnight–Loving Trail, and he is credited with inventing the chuckwagon.

Early life and trails

Born in Illinois, Goodnight moved to Texas as a boy and worked as a cowboy and scout, serving with the Texas Rangers during the Civil War. In 1866 he and Oliver Loving drove a herd of cattle west from Texas to New Mexico and on to Colorado, establishing the Goodnight–Loving Trail. After Loving was killed by Comanches in 1867, Goodnight carried his body back to Texas for burial, an episode later fictionalised in Larry McMurtry's novel Lonesome Dove, in which the character Woodrow Call is partly based on Goodnight.

Ranching

In 1877 Goodnight founded a ranch in Palo Duro Canyon with the English investor John Adair, creating the JA Ranch, one of the great cattle empires of the Plains, eventually covering more than a million acres. To supply his cowboys he converted a wagon into the first chuckwagon in 1866, with a slanted meal box and compartments that became standard equipment on trail drives.

Later life

Goodnight also worked to preserve the American bison, keeping a herd on his ranch whose descendants survive today as the Texas state bison herd. He married Mary Ann Dyer, and spent his last years at his home in Goodnight, Texas, where he died aged 93.
