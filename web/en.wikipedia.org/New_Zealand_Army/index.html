New Zealand Army

The New Zealand Army (Maori: Ngati Tumatauenga, "Tribe of the God of War") is the land component of the New Zealand Defence Force. It traces its origins to colonial militia forces of the 1840s and the New Zealand Military Forces that served in the South African War and both world wars.

History

New Zealand forces fought in the New Zealand Wars of the 19th century, the Boer War, and as expeditionary forces in the First and Second World Wars, including at Gallipoli, the Somme, Greece, Crete, North Africa and Italy. After 1945 the army contributed to conflicts and peacekeeping in Korea, Malaya, Borneo, Vietnam, East Timor, the Solomon Islands and Afghanistan.

Structure today

The modern army is a small professional force centred on 1 (New Zealand) Brigade at Linton Military Camp, with camps at Waiouru, Burnham, Papakura and Trentham. Core units include the Royal New Zealand Infantry Regiment, the Queen Alexandra's Mounted Rifles (armoured), artillery, engineers, signals and logistics corps, plus the New Zealand Special Air Service. Its standard light armoured vehicle is the NZLAV.
