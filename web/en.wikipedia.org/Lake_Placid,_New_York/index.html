Lake Placid, New York

Lake Placid is a village in the Adirondack Mountains in Essex County, New York, United States. As of the 2020 census its population was 2,205. The village is internationally known as the host of the 1932 and 1980 Winter Olympics and remains a major centre for winter sports training and tourism.

History

The area was first settled in the early 19th century around an iron forge, and the village developed as a resort destination in the later 1800s. Melvil Dewey founded the Lake Placid Club in 1895, promoting winter recreation at a time when most resorts closed in winter. The club's influence brought the 1932 Winter Olympics to the village. In 1980 Lake Placid hosted the Winter Games again; the event is remembered above all for the "Miracle on Ice", in which the young United States hockey team defeated the heavily favored Soviet Union squad 4–3 before going on to win the gold medal.

Geography

Despite its name, the village is situated on the shore of Mirror Lake, just south of the larger Lake Placid. It lies within the town of North Elba, inside the Adirondack Park, at an elevation of about 549 m (1,801 ft), surrounded by High Peaks including Whiteface Mountain.

Sport

Olympic venues — the Olympic Center arena, the ski jumps at the MacKenzie Intervale complex, the Mount Van Hoevenberg sliding track and cross-country trails, and Whiteface ski area — remain in use for World Cup events and as a United States Olympic training site. The village also hosts an annual Ironman triathlon and the Lake Placid horse shows.
