Comanche

The Comanche (Comanche: Nʉmʉnʉʉ, 'the People') are a Native American tribe from the Southern Plains of the present-day United States. In the 18th and 19th centuries they dominated a vast territory called Comancheria — spanning present-day Texas, New Mexico, Oklahoma, Kansas, and Colorado — as master horsemen and formidable raiders and traders.

History

The Comanche separated from the Shoshone in Wyoming in the late 17th century and, after acquiring horses from Spanish New Mexico, migrated south. Their power checked Spanish, Mexican, and later American expansion for over a century. Disease, the destruction of the bison herds, and US campaigns — ending with Quanah Parker's surrender in 1875 — forced them onto reservations in Indian Territory.

Culture

Comanche society was organised in autonomous bands with elected peace chiefs and war leaders. They were renowned for horsemanship, bison hunting, and a trade and raiding network reaching deep into Mexico. The Comanche language is a Numic language of the Uto-Aztecan family.

Today

The Comanche Nation, headquartered in Lawton, Oklahoma, has about 17,000 enrolled members. Comanche Code Talkers served in the Second World War.
