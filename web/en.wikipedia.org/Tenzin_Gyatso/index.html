Tenzin Gyatso

Born6 July 1935, Taktser, Amdo, Tibet
Reign14th Dalai Lama (recognized 1937; enthroned 1940)
ResidenceDharamshala, India (in exile since 1959)
AwardsNobel Peace Prize (1989)

Tenzin Gyatso (born Lhamo Dhondup, 6 July 1935) is the 14th and current Dalai Lama, the spiritual leader of Tibetan Buddhism and the Gelug school. Born in the village of Taktser in Amdo, northeastern Tibet, he was recognized as the reincarnation of the 13th Dalai Lama at the age of two and enthroned in Lhasa in 1940.

Exile

Following the incorporation of Tibet into the People's Republic of China, the Dalai Lama assumed political power in 1950. After the failed Tibetan uprising of 1959, he fled to India, where he established the Central Tibetan Administration in Dharamshala. Tens of thousands of Tibetan refugees followed him into exile.

Advocacy and teachings

The Dalai Lama advocates the "Middle Way Approach": genuine autonomy for Tibet within China, pursued nonviolently. He received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1989 for his peaceful resistance. In 2011 he devolved his political authority to an elected leadership. Renowned for teachings on compassion, interfaith dialogue, and the dialogue between Buddhism and modern science, he has authored many books, including The Art of Happiness.
