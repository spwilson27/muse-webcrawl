Railcar


Placeholder image: Railcar

A railcar is a self-propelled railway vehicle designed to transport passengers. The term usually refers to a train consisting of a single coach (carriage) powered by a diesel, petrol or electric motor, though multiple-unit trains are sometimes described as railcars in North American usage.

Types

Diesel railcars and diesel multiple units are common on lightly used rural lines where a full locomotive-hauled train would be uneconomical. Historically, steam railmotors, petrol railcars and battery railcars were also built. Famous examples include the Budd Rail Diesel Car in North America and British Rail's first-generation DMUs.

Usage

Railcars offer low operating costs and quick acceleration for branch-line and regional services. Modern lightweight railcars have been revived for regional transit in Europe and North America.
