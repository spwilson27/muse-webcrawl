Basel I

Basel I is the first of the Basel Accords, a set of banking regulations issued by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS). Formally titled the International Convergence of Capital Measurement and Capital Standards and published in July 1988, it created a credit risk measurement framework with a minimum capital standard of 8% of risk-weighted assets.

Framework

Under Basel I, a bank's assets were classified into five categories according to credit risk, carrying risk weights of 0% (for example home-country government bonds), 10%, 20%, 50% and 100% (for example most corporate debt). Banks with international operations were required to hold capital — divided into Tier 1 (equity and disclosed reserves) and Tier 2 (supplementary items) — equal to at least 8% of their risk-weighted assets. The accord was implemented in the G-10 countries by the end of 1992 and was subsequently adopted in over 100 countries.

Amendments and successor

A 1996 amendment added requirements for market risk. Basel I was criticised for its crude risk buckets, which treated all corporate borrowers alike regardless of creditworthiness, and it was superseded by the more risk-sensitive Basel II framework, though it remained in use in some jurisdictions for smaller banks.
