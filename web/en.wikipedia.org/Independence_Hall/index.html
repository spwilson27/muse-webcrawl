Independence Hall

Independence Hall is a historic building in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, where both the United States Declaration of Independence (1776) and the United States Constitution (1787) were debated and adopted. Located on Chestnut Street between 5th and 6th Streets, it is the centerpiece of Independence National Historical Park and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979.

History

Designed in the Georgian style by Edmund Woolley and Andrew Hamilton, the building was constructed between 1732 and 1753 as the Pennsylvania State House. Its bell tower originally housed the bell now known as the Liberty Bell. The Second Continental Congress met in the Assembly Room from 1775 to 1783; there George Washington was appointed commander of the Continental Army, and the Declaration of Independence was adopted on July 4, 1776. In the summer of 1787 the Constitutional Convention drafted the U.S. Constitution in the same room. The building also appears on the reverse of the U.S. $100 bill.

Preservation

The state sold the building to the City of Philadelphia in 1816. After alterations and restorations, it was meticulously returned to its 18th-century appearance in the mid-20th century under the National Park Service, which has administered it since the park's creation in 1948. The Assembly Room preserves period furnishings, including the "Rising Sun" chair used by Washington at the Constitutional Convention.
