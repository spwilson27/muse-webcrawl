Multiplayer online battle arena

Multiplayer online battle arena (MOBA) is a subgenre of strategy video games in which two teams of players compete against each other on a predefined battlefield. Each player controls a single character with a set of distinctive abilities that improve over the course of a game and which contribute to the team's overall strategy. The typical ultimate objective is for each team to destroy their opponents' main structure, located at the opposite corner of the battlefield.

Gameplay

Players' characters, often called heroes or champions, gain experience and gold by defeating computer-controlled "creeps" (minions) that advance along lanes, enemy heroes, and neutral monsters. Gold buys items that strengthen the character. Matches commonly last between 20 and 60 minutes, and teamwork, map control, and timing of team fights are decisive.

History

The genre traces its roots to the StarCraft custom map Aeon of Strife and crystallized with Defense of the Ancients (DotA), a 2003 custom map for Warcraft III. Standalone successors — League of Legends (2009) and Dota 2 (2013) — became among the most played PC games in the world and anchor major esports scenes, with The International offering record-breaking prize pools. Other notable titles include Heroes of the Storm, Smite, and mobile MOBAs such as Honor of Kings and Mobile Legends: Bang Bang.
