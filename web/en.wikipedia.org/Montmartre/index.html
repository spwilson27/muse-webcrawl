Montmartre

Montmartre
Location: 18th arrondissement, Paris, France
Elevation: 130 m (highest natural point in Paris)
Landmark: Basilica of the Sacré-Cœur

Montmartre is a large hill and neighbourhood in the 18th arrondissement of Paris, France. It is best known for the white-domed Basilica of the Sacré-Cœur on its summit, for its history as a centre of artistic life in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and for the artists who lived and worked there, including Picasso, Modigliani, Monet, Renoir, Degas, Toulouse-Lautrec and Van Gogh.

History

The name is traditionally linked to the martyrdom of Saint Denis, the first bishop of Paris, said to have been beheaded on the hill in the third century. Long a village outside the city, with vineyards, gypsum quarries and windmills, Montmartre was annexed to Paris in 1860 and was a stronghold of the Paris Commune in 1871. The Sacré-Cœur, built as a national vow after the Franco-Prussian War, was completed in 1914 and consecrated in 1919.

Artistic life

Cheap rents and an exemption from Paris wine taxes made Montmartre a centre of bohemian nightlife: cabarets such as the Moulin Rouge (opened 1889) and Le Chat Noir, dance halls, and studios like the Bateau-Lavoir, where Picasso painted Les Demoiselles d'Avignon. The Place du Tertre still hosts portrait artists, and the Clos Montmartre vineyard survives. The district borders Pigalle and the red-light area at its southern foot.
