Geoffrey A. Landis

Geoffrey Alan Landis (born 28 May 1955) is an American scientist and science fiction writer. He works at the NASA Glenn Research Center in Cleveland, Ohio, on advanced propulsion and power systems, and is known in fiction for rigorously hard science fiction.

Science career

Landis holds a PhD in solid-state physics from Brown University. His research has included photovoltaic power systems, laser propulsion and interstellar flight studies, and work connected with the Mars Exploration Rovers; he was a member of the science team for the Mars Pathfinder and Mars Exploration Rover missions. He has proposed and studied concepts such as Venus cloud colonisation and sulphur-based rocket propellants.

Writing

Landis began publishing fiction in the 1980s. His short story "A Walk in the Sun" won the Hugo Award in 1992, and "Falling Onto Mars" won the Hugo Award for Best Short Story in 2003; he has also won a Nebula Award (for "Ripples in the Dirac Sea", 1989) and the Locus Award. His novel Mars Crossing (2000) won the Locus Award for Best First Novel. His stories are collected in Impact Parameter and Other Quantum Realities (2001). He has also published poetry, winning the Rhysling Award.
