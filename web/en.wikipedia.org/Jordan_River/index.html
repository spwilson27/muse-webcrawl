Jordan River

The Jordan River, also known as Nahr al-Sharieat, is a 251-kilometre-long (156 mi) river in Southwest Asia flowing through the Great Rift Valley into the Dead Sea. It is the river where, according to the Bible, the Israelites crossed into the Promised Land and where Jesus of Nazareth was baptized by John the Baptist. The river holds major significance in Judaism and Christianity.

Course

The Jordan's sources are springs and streams at the foot of Mount Hermon — principally the Hasbani, Banias, and Dan rivers — which unite in the Hula Valley. From there it flows south through the Sea of Galilee and along the Jordan Valley, forming the boundary between Israel and the West Bank to the west and Jordan to the east, before emptying into the Dead Sea at the lowest land elevation on Earth.

Modern issues

Since the 1960s, large-scale diversion of the Jordan's waters by Israel, Jordan, Syria, and Lebanon for irrigation and drinking water has reduced its lower flow drastically, contributing to the shrinking of the Dead Sea. The river's water sharing is a significant political issue in the region, addressed in part by the 1994 Israel–Jordan peace treaty. Baptismal sites on both banks, including Al-Maghtas in Jordan, draw Christian pilgrims.
