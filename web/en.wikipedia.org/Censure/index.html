Censure

Censure
Type: Parliamentary discipline
Severity: Below expulsion
Effect: Formal public reprimand

Censure is a formal, public reprimand of an individual, typically a public official or legislator, by a governing body. It expresses strong disapproval without removing the person from office, and is less severe than expulsion but more severe than a private rebuke.

Legislative use

In the United States Congress, members may be censured by a majority vote of their chamber; the censured member traditionally stands while the resolution is read. Presidents have faced censure resolutions, though none has been adopted by both houses. Similar procedures exist in other parliaments and in professional and religious bodies.

Distinctions

Censure differs from a vote of no confidence, which can force a government from power, and from impeachment, which brings formal charges. See Impeachment and Motion of no confidence.
