Prosecutor

A prosecutor is the legal representative of the state (or other prosecuting authority) who brings and conducts criminal cases against accused persons. Prosecutors decide whether to charge, negotiate pleas, present evidence at trial, and recommend sentences.

Roles by system

In the United States, district attorneys and U.S. Attorneys are typically elected or appointed executives overseeing offices of trial attorneys. In England and Wales, the Crown Prosecution Service (est. 1986) separates prosecution from police investigation. In civil-law systems, public prosecutors (e.g., France's procureur, Germany's Staatsanwalt) may direct investigations, working alongside investigating judges in some jurisdictions. Prosecutors bear special ethical duties, including disclosure of exculpatory evidence (Brady v. Maryland).
