Camping


Placeholder image: Tents pitched at a lakeside campsite

Camping is a form of outdoor recreation or outdoor education involving overnight stays with a basic temporary shelter such as a tent. Camping can also include a recreational vehicle, sheltered cabins, a permanent tent, a shelter, a bivouac, or no shelter at all.

History

Recreational camping became popular in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as industrial workers sought access to nature; Thomas Hiram Holding, author of the Campers Handbook (1908), is often credited as the father of modern recreational camping in the United Kingdom. Organisations such as the Boy Scouts and Girl Guides popularised camping as a means of youth education, and national park systems created managed campgrounds.

Forms

Forms of camping range from primitive backcountry and backpacking camps with minimal equipment, through car camping at developed campgrounds with amenities, to "glamping" in furnished luxury tents. Winter camping, canoe camping and bicycle camping are specialist variants. Campers typically follow Leave No Trace principles to minimise their impact.
