Retailing

Retailing is the sale of goods and services to consumers, in contrast to wholesaling, which is the sale to business or institutional customers. A retailer purchases goods in large quantities from manufacturers, directly or through a wholesaler, and then sells them in smaller quantities to consumers for a profit. Retailers are the final link in the supply chain from producers to consumers.

Shoppers on a retail high street

Retail markets and shops have a long history, dating back to antiquity. Some of the earliest retailers were itinerant peddlers. Over the centuries, retail shops evolved from rudimentary stalls to the sophisticated shopping malls of the modern era. In the digital age, the introduction of electronic commerce has transformed retailing, allowing consumers to purchase goods online and have them delivered directly to their homes.

Definition

Retail refers to the activity of selling goods or services directly to consumers or end users. Some retailers may sell to business customers, and such sales are termed non-retail activity. In some jurisdictions or contexts, legal definitions of retail specify that at least 80 percent of sales activity must be to end users.

Retailing occurs in retail stores, which are located on high streets, in shopping malls, or in other commercial premises. Retailers may also sell through non-store channels, including mail order, catalogues, door-to-door sales, vending machines, and electronic commerce.

Types of retail outlets

Retail stores come in many shapes and sizes. Common types include:

  • Department stores – large stores offering a wide range of goods under one roof, organised into departments.
  • Supermarkets and hypermarkets – large self-service stores selling mainly food and household goods.
  • Discount stores – stores selling goods at lower prices by accepting lower margins and selling higher volumes.
  • Convenience stores – small stores stocking everyday items, often with extended opening hours.
  • Specialty stores – stores concentrating on a specific product category, such as clothing, electronics or books.
  • Warehouse clubs – membership-based stores selling bulk goods at discounted prices.
  • E-commerce – online retailers selling through websites and mobile applications.

Retail strategy

A retail strategy is the plan a retailer uses to attract customers and meet their expectations. The elements of the retail marketing mix are often summarised as the "four Ps": product, price, place and promotion. Retailers decide on the assortment of merchandise, pricing policy, store location and layout, and promotional activities such as advertising and sales promotions.

History

Markets of goods have existed since ancient times. Archaeological evidence suggests that trading posts and shops existed in the ancient world, including in Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece and Rome. In medieval Europe, markets and fairs were the principal means of retail trade, regulated by local authorities. The general store emerged in rural communities, while in cities, shops clustered along streets that often gave their names to the trades practised there.

The modern department store emerged in the mid-19th century, with establishments such as Le Bon Marché in Paris (founded 1838) and later stores in London and New York introducing fixed prices, open display of goods and a wide assortment. Self-service grocery retailing began in the early 20th century, exemplified by Piggly Wiggly (1916) in the United States. The enclosed shopping mall was pioneered in the 1950s, notably by Victor Gruen's Southdale Center (1956).

Online retailing

Electronic commerce began to grow rapidly in the 1990s. Online retailers such as Amazon and eBay, both founded in 1995, demonstrated that large-scale retail could be conducted without physical stores. Online retailing offers consumers convenience, a wider selection and price comparison, while presenting retailers with challenges in logistics, returns and competition. Many traditional retailers adopted an "omnichannel" model integrating physical stores, websites and mobile applications.
