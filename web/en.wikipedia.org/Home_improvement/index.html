Home improvement

Home improvement, also known as home renovation or remodelling, is the process of renovating, making additions to, or otherwise improving one's home. Home improvement projects range from routine maintenance and repairs — painting, plumbing, electrical work, and flooring — to major undertakings such as kitchen and bathroom remodels, room additions, basement conversions and structural alterations. The activity supports a large retail and contracting industry, and a substantial do-it-yourself (DIY) culture.

Types of projects

Common home improvement projects include kitchen remodelling (cabinets, countertops, appliances), bathroom renovation, repainting, replacing roofs and windows, installing central heating and air conditioning, insulation and other energy-efficiency upgrades, extensions and loft or garage conversions, decking and landscaping, and "smart home" automation. Surveys consistently find kitchens and bathrooms offer among the best returns on investment at resale, though returns vary by market. Renovation may also address safety and accessibility, for example by adapting homes for ageing occupants.

The industry

The home improvement market in the United States alone is worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually. Large "big-box" retailers such as Home Depot and Lowe's in North America, and Kingfisher's B&Q and Screwfix in the United Kingdom, dominate the supply of materials and tools. Work is performed both by homeowners and by a fragmented contracting sector of builders, electricians, plumbers and specialist trades, much of it small businesses. The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020–21 produced a surge in spending as households adapted homes for remote work.

DIY culture

Do-it-yourself home improvement grew after the Second World War with suburban homeownership and the spread of power tools. Books, magazines, television programmes and now online video tutorials teach skills from tiling to plastering. DIY reduces labour costs and gives personal satisfaction, but carries risks with gas, electrical and structural work, which in many jurisdictions must be performed or certified by licensed professionals.

Regulation and permits

Most jurisdictions require building permits for structural changes, additions and major mechanical work, and inspections to ensure compliance with building codes covering structure, fire safety, electrical and plumbing standards. Historic districts and listed buildings impose additional controls. Unpermitted work can complicate resale and insurance.

Media

Home improvement is a staple of lifestyle media: British programmes such as Changing Rooms, long-running series like This Old House (from 1979) in the United States, and renovation formats worldwide (including property "flipping" shows) have popularised design trends. The American sitcom Home Improvement (1991–1999), starring Tim Allen as the host of a fictional tool show, took its title from the activity.
