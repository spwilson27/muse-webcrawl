The River Ouse is a river in Yorkshire, northern England. It is formed by the confluence of the River Swale and the River Ure east of Boroughbridge and flows generally south-east through York, Selby, and Goole, joining the River Trent at Trent Falls to form the Humber Estuary. Together with the Ure it forms one of the longest river systems wholly within England, draining some 10,700 square kilometres.

Course and navigation

Principal tributaries include the Nidd, Wharfe, Aire, Calder, Don, and Derwent. The river is tidal below Naburn, south of York, and navigable by commercial and leisure craft; the inland port of Goole, linked to the Aire and Calder Navigation, remains one of Britain's busiest inland ports. York's prosperity in the medieval period rested on Ouse-borne trade, and its bridges — Ouse Bridge, Lendal Bridge, Skeldergate Bridge, and the Millennium Bridge — remain landmarks of the city.

Flooding

The Vale of York is low-lying, and the Ouse has a long history of flooding, notably in 1947, 1982, 2000, and December 2015, when flood defences in York were overwhelmed after the Foss Barrier pump station failed. Extensive barrier banks, flood walls, and washlands now protect York, Selby, and the villages of the lower Ouse.
