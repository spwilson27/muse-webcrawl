Campbell Soup Company

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Campbell Soup Company, doing business as Campbell's, is an American food company headquartered in Camden, New Jersey. Founded in 1869, it is best known for its flagship canned soup products sold under the Campbell's brand, distinguished by their red-and-white labels. The company also owns brands in snacks and other packaged foods, including Pepperidge Farm, Goldfish crackers, Snyder's of Hanover, Lance, Kettle Brand, V8, Prego, and Rao's Homemade.

History

The firm began in 1869 as a partnership between fruit merchant Joseph A. Campbell and icebox manufacturer Abraham Anderson in Camden, New Jersey, canning tomatoes, vegetables, jellies, and soups. In 1897 chemist John T. Dorrance invented condensed soup, halving the water content and drastically cutting packaging and shipping costs; he became company president in 1914. The red-and-white label design, adopted in 1898, was inspired by the Cornell University football uniforms. Campbell's tomato, cream of mushroom, and chicken noodle soups became staples of American kitchens.

Cultural presence

Campbell's soup cans achieved pop-culture immortality through Andy Warhol's 1962 silkscreen series Campbell's Soup Cans, which reproduced 32 varieties of the soup and helped launch the Pop Art movement. The company's "M'm! M'm! Good!" slogan, introduced in the 1930s, remains one of the most recognised in American advertising.

The company today

In the 21st century Campbell's diversified into fresh and organic foods and snacks, acquiring Bolthouse Farms, Plum Organics, Pacific Foods, and Sovos Brands (maker of Rao's). In 2024 the company announced it would drop "Soup" from its corporate name, becoming The Campbell's Company, reflecting that snacks now rival soup in revenue.
