Hangzhou

Hangzhou (Chinese: 杭州)
Capital of Zhejiang province, China
Population: approx. 12 million (metro)

Hangzhou is the capital and most populous city of Zhejiang province in eastern China, situated at the head of Hangzhou Bay on the Qiantang River, about 180 km southwest of Shanghai. It is one of China's most celebrated scenic cities, famous for West Lake, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Hangzhou was the capital of the Wuyue Kingdom (907–978) and, as Lin'an, became the capital of the Southern Song dynasty after 1127, growing into one of the largest and most prosperous cities in the medieval world. Marco Polo described it (as "Kinsay") in lavish terms. The Grand Canal, whose southern terminus is at Hangzhou, linked the city to Beijing for over a millennium.

Modern city

Today Hangzhou is a major technology hub, headquarters of the Alibaba Group, and a centre of China's e-commerce and tea industries — the surrounding hills produce the prized Longjing (Dragon Well) green tea. The city hosted the G20 summit in 2016 and the Asian Games in 2023. Its cultural landscape around West Lake, with pagodas such as Leifeng Pagoda and temples such as Lingyin, draws tens of millions of visitors each year.
