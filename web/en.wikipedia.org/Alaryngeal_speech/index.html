Alaryngeal speech

Alaryngeal speech is speech produced without the use of the larynx, typically by people who have undergone a laryngectomy. It encompasses several methods of voice restoration that substitute another sound source for the vocal folds.

Methods

Esophageal speech involves swallowing or injecting air into the esophagus and releasing it in a controlled manner, causing the pharyngoesophageal segment to vibrate. Tracheoesophageal speech uses a surgically created puncture and a one-way voice prosthesis that shunts pulmonary air into the esophagus. An electrolarynx is an external battery-powered device held against the neck that generates vibrations mechanically.

Characteristics

Alaryngeal speech tends to be lower in pitch and rougher in quality than laryngeal speech, and esophageal speech is usually produced in short phrases because of the limited air supply. With training, many speakers achieve good intelligibility.
