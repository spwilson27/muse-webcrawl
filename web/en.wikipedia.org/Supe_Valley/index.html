Supe Valley

The Supe Valley is a river valley in the Barranca Province of the Lima Region of Peru, formed by the Supe River as it flows from the Andes to the Pacific Ocean. The valley is famous in archaeology as the location of Caral, the main site of the Caral-Supe civilization (also called the Norte Chico civilization), the oldest known civilization in the Americas.

Geography

The Supe River runs roughly 100 kilometres from the Andean foothills to the coast near the town of Supe. The middle valley, around 23 kilometres inland, contains a dense cluster of monumental ceremonial centres dating to the Late Archaic period, around 3000–1800 BCE.

Archaeology

The most prominent site is Caral, a large settlement with platform mounds, sunken circular plazas, and residential architecture, identified by archaeologist Ruth Shady and her team as the capital of an early complex society. Other valley sites include Áspero, a coastal fishing settlement, as well as Chupacigarro, Miraya, Lurihuasi, and Era de Pando. The civilization that built them developed agriculture based on cotton and squash, supplemented by marine resources traded from the coast, and appears to have had no ceramics, writing, or evidence of warfare.

In 2009 the Sacred City of Caral-Supe was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
