DuPont de Nemours, Inc., commonly known as DuPont, is an American multinational chemical and materials company. Founded in 1802 by French immigrant Éleuthère Irénée du Pont as a gunpowder mill on Brandywine Creek, Delaware, it grew into one of the world's largest chemical companies, supplying explosives for much of the Allied effort in both world wars before diversifying into polymers, fibres and agriculture.

Inventions

DuPont's research laboratories produced many landmark materials: neoprene synthetic rubber, nylon (1935, by Wallace Carothers' team), Teflon (PTFE, discovered 1938 by Roy Plunkett), Mylar, Lycra spandex, Kevlar (by Stephanie Kwolek), Tyvek, and Corian. Its Freon refrigerants were later phased out under the Montreal Protocol as ozone-depleting substances, and the company became embroiled in long-running litigation over PFOA ("C8") contamination near its Parkersburg, West Virginia plant, depicted in the 2019 film Dark Waters.

Recent history

In 2015 DuPont announced a merger with Dow Chemical; DowDuPont was formed in 2017 and then split in 2019 into three companies: Dow Inc., Corteva (agriculture) and the new DuPont, focused on specialty products, electronics, water solutions and industrial materials.
