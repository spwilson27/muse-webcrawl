Judith Herman

Judith Lewis Herman (born 1942) is an American psychiatrist and professor of psychiatry at Harvard Medical School, best known for her foundational work on psychological trauma. Her 1992 book Trauma and Recovery is regarded as a landmark in trauma studies and proposed the diagnosis of complex post-traumatic stress disorder (C-PTSD).

Work

Herman's early research with Lisa Hirschman examined incest and the sexual abuse of children, published as Father–Daughter Incest (1981). With psychiatrist Bessel van der Kolk and others she helped found the Victims of Violence Program at Cambridge Hospital. In Trauma and Recovery (1992) she drew parallels between the experiences of combat veterans, survivors of political terror, and survivors of domestic and sexual violence, arguing that recovery proceeds in stages — safety, remembrance and mourning, and reconnection — and must be understood in a social and political context.

Influence

Herman's proposal of complex PTSD influenced later classifications: the ICD-11 adopted C-PTSD as a distinct diagnosis in 2018. Her later book Truth and Repair (2023) examines justice from survivors' perspectives. She has received numerous awards for her contributions to psychiatry and human rights.
