Liturgical movement

The Liturgical movement was a 19th- and 20th-century movement of scholarship and reform that sought the renewal of Christian worship through a return to historical sources and fuller participation of the congregation. It began in Roman Catholic monastic and scholarly circles and influenced most Western churches.

History

Important early figures included Prosper Guéranger, who restored Benedictine life at Solesmes and promoted the Roman liturgy and Gregorian chant. In the early 20th century Pope Pius X encouraged frequent communion and "active participation" of the faithful. Scholars such as Lambert Beauduin in Belgium and Romano Guardini in Germany spread the movement's ideas through journals, congresses and vernacular translations of liturgical texts.

Second Vatican Council

The movement culminated in the Catholic Church in the constitution Sacrosanctum Concilium (1963) of the Second Vatican Council, which authorised extensive reform of the Mass and other rites, including wider use of vernacular languages. Parallel reforms reshaped worship in Anglican, Lutheran and other Protestant churches.
