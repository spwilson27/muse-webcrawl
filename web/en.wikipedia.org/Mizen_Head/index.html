Mizen Head (Irish: Carn Uí Néid) is one of the extreme points of the island of Ireland, at the tip of the Mizen Peninsula in County Cork. It is Ireland's most southwesterly mainland point and a well-known tourist attraction, famous for its dramatic cliff scenery and Atlantic views.

Signal station

Because of the many ships wrecked on the treacherous rocks offshore, a fog signal station was built on Cloghane Island at the very end of the headland in 1909–1910. Keepers reached the station by a path and bridge over a deep chasm; the original concrete arch bridge was replaced by a new steel-and-concrete footbridge opened in 2011. The station was automated in 1993, and the former keepers' houses and station buildings now form a visitor centre with exhibitions on the Fastnet Lighthouse, local shipwrecks and the wildlife of the headland.

Geography

The headland overlooks the Atlantic approaches to the Irish Sea and the English Channel. On clear days the Fastnet Rock lighthouse — the "Teardrop of Ireland", the last sight of home for many emigrants — is visible offshore. The surrounding waters are rich in marine life, with seals, dolphins, whales and basking sharks regularly sighted from the cliffs.
