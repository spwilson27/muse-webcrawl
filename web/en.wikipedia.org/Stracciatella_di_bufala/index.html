Stracciatella di bufala

Stracciatella di bufala is a fresh Italian cheese from Apulia, made from buffalo milk (or, in some versions, cow's milk) using the pasta filata technique. It consists of fine shreds of mozzarella — the name comes from stracciare, "to tear" — mixed with fresh cream. Soft, spoonable and mildly sweet, it is best known as the luscious filling of burrata.

Production

Stracciatella is produced by stretching buffalo-milk curd in hot water into elastic sheets, tearing them into thin strands (straccetti) and soaking the shreds in cream, sometimes lightly salted. The cheese is highly perishable and traditionally eaten within a day or two of production.

Use

In Andria and across Apulia the cheese is enclosed in a pouch of mozzarella to make burrata, created in the early twentieth century as a way of using up mozzarella scraps. Served on its own with bread, olive oil and tomatoes, or spooned over pasta, pizza and bruschetta, stracciatella has spread from a regional speciality to delicatessens and restaurants worldwide. It should not be confused with stracciatella gelato or the Roman egg-drop soup of the same name.
