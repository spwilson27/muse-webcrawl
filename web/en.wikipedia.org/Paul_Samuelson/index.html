Paul Samuelson

Paul Anthony Samuelson (15 May 1915 – 13 December 2009) was an American economist who was the first American to win the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences. His extensive career at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) shaped modern economics across many fields.

His 1947 book Foundations of Economic Analysis recast economics using mathematical methods drawn from thermodynamics, while his textbook Economics (first published 1948, later co-authored with William Nordhaus) became the best-selling economics textbook of all time and a vehicle of the "neoclassical synthesis" combining Keynesian macroeconomics with neoclassical microeconomics.

Contributions

Samuelson made foundational contributions to consumer theory (revealed preference), welfare economics (the Lindahl–Bowen–Samuelson conditions), international trade (the Stolper–Samuelson theorem and factor price equalisation), public finance, capital theory, and financial economics. His 1958 overlapping generations model remains a standard tool. He was an adviser to Presidents Kennedy and Johnson and a long-time columnist for Newsweek.
