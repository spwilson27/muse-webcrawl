Beurling algebra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Beurling algebra is a Banach algebra constructed from a locally compact group together with a weight function, generalising the group algebra L¹(G). The concept is named after Arne Beurling.

Definition

Given a locally compact abelian group G and a weight ω — a positive submultiplicative function satisfying ω(xy) ≤ ω(x)ω(y) — the Beurling algebra L¹(G, ω) consists of functions f for which the integral of |f|ω over G is finite, with convolution as multiplication. On the integers and reals, polynomial and exponential weights give standard examples.

Properties

The spectral properties of Beurling algebras depend sensitively on the growth of the weight: for subexponential weights the algebra is regular and symmetric, while rapidly growing weights can produce non-regular algebras with exotic maximal ideal spaces. The theory connects harmonic analysis with the study of weighted convolution algebras and has applications to Tauberian theorems.
