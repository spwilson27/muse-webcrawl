Adam Ludwig Lewenhaupt

Adam Ludwig Lewenhaupt
Born: 15 April 1659, Copenhagen
Died: 12 February 1719, Moscow
Allegiance: Swedish Empire
Rank: General of the infantry
Battles: Great Northern War

Adam Ludwig Lewenhaupt (15 April 1659 – 12 February 1719) was a Swedish general of the infantry during the Great Northern War. He commanded the Swedish supply corps marching to join Charles XII in Ukraine in 1708, and after defeat at the Battle of Lesnaya and the surrender of his remaining forces following the Battle of Poltava, he spent the rest of his life as a prisoner in Russia.

Early career

Lewenhaupt studied at Lund and Uppsala and initially pursued a diplomatic career before entering military service. He campaigned in Hungary against the Ottomans and in the Netherlands, and at the outbreak of the Great Northern War he was appointed to command troops in the Baltic provinces, where he won victories over Russian forces at Gemauerthof (1705) and was promoted to general and governor of Riga.

The march to Ukraine and Lesnaya

In 1708 Lewenhaupt was ordered to march from Livonia with some 12,500 men and a large, slow supply train to join Charles XII's main army in Ukraine. Intercepted by a Russian corps under Peter the Great at Lesnaya on 9 October 1708, he fought a hard defensive battle, abandoned his wagons and artillery, and reached the king with only about half his force and almost no supplies — a catastrophe that contributed directly to the Swedish starvation before Poltava.

Poltava and captivity

At the Battle of Poltava (8 July 1709) Lewenhaupt commanded the infantry. After the defeat he led the army's retreat to Perevolochna on the Dnieper, where, controversially and without the presence of the king (who had crossed into Ottoman territory), he surrendered the remaining ~14,000–16,000 men to the Russians on 11 July 1709. He died in captivity in Moscow in 1719, never returning to Sweden.
