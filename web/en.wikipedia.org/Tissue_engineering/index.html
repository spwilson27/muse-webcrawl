Tissue engineering

Tissue engineering is a biomedical engineering discipline that uses a combination of cells, engineering, materials methods, and suitable biochemical and physicochemical factors to restore, maintain, improve, or replace different types of biological tissues. It is closely associated with regenerative medicine.

Approach

The classic strategy seeds living cells onto a three-dimensional scaffold made of natural or synthetic biodegradable materials. The scaffold provides mechanical support and guides the cells as they proliferate and produce extracellular matrix, eventually forming functional tissue that can be implanted into a patient. Growth factors and bioreactors that mimic physiological conditions are often used to promote development.

Applications

Tissue-engineered products in clinical use include artificial skin for burn and ulcer treatment, cartilage repair constructs, and tissue-engineered bladders and tracheas in experimental settings. Research continues on more complex vascularised organs such as the heart, liver and kidney, and on enabling technologies including 3D bioprinting and decellularised organ scaffolds.
