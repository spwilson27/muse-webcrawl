ACPI (Advanced Configuration and Power Interface) is an open standard that operating systems use to discover and configure computer hardware components and to perform power management, including turning off unused devices and managing sleep states. First released in December 1996 by Hewlett-Packard, Intel, Microsoft, Phoenix Technologies and Toshiba, it brought power management under the control of the operating system, superseding APM and the Plug and Play BIOS. Since 2013 the standard has been maintained by the UEFI Forum.

Architecture

Firmware provides the OS with ACPI tables (such as the DSDT and SSDTs) written partly in ACPI Machine Language, which describe devices, power states and control methods. The specification defines global system states (G0 working through G3 mechanical off), sleep states S1–S5, device power states D0–D3, and processor performance and idle states (P-states and C-states) used for dynamic voltage and frequency scaling and CPU idling.

Adoption

ACPI is near-universal on x86 personal computers and servers and is also specified for ARM64 systems in server contexts. Operating systems including Windows, Linux, macOS and the BSDs implement ACPI subsystems to enumerate hardware and coordinate power policy.
