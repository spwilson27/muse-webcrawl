Baking

Baking is a method of preparing food that uses prolonged dry heat, normally in an oven, but also in hot ashes or on hot stones. It is most closely associated with bread, but a wide range of foods are baked, including cakes, pastries, pies, biscuits, and also savoury dishes such as casseroles and baked potatoes.

Process

During baking, heat is transferred into the food by radiation, conduction and convection, driving chemical and physical changes: yeast and chemical leavening agents generate gas that raises doughs and batters; starches gelatinise; proteins set; and sugars caramelise and undergo Maillard reactions that produce the browned crusts and characteristic flavours of baked goods. Baking differs from roasting mainly in emphasis — the term is usually applied to foods based on flour or batter, and to dishes cooked without initial structure.

History

Baking is one of the oldest cooking methods: evidence of bread-making extends back tens of thousands of years, and the ancient Egyptians developed leavened bread and purpose-built ovens. Baking became an important trade in Roman and medieval towns, regulated by guilds, and was industrialised in the 19th and 20th centuries with commercial yeast, refined flour and mass-produced ovens. Home baking remains a popular activity worldwide.
