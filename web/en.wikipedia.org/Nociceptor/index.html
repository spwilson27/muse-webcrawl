Nociceptor

A nociceptor ("pain receptor") is a sensory neuron that responds to damaging or potentially damaging stimuli by sending "possible threat" signals to the spinal cord and the brain, which usually results in the perception of pain.

Types and function

Nociceptors have free nerve endings and high activation thresholds. Mechanical nociceptors respond to sharp pressure, thermal nociceptors to temperatures above about 43 °C or extreme cold, chemical nociceptors to irritants and inflammation products, and polymodal nociceptors to combinations of these. Signals travel via fast, myelinated Aδ fibres (sharp "first pain") and slow, unmyelinated C fibres (dull "second pain"). Ion channels such as TRPV1 (the capsaicin and heat receptor) transduce the stimuli. Nociceptors were identified in the early 20th century following Charles Sherrington's work on reflexes.
