Trail of Broken Treaties

Demonstrators during the 1972 caravan to Washington, D.C.

The Trail of Broken Treaties (also known as the Trail of Broken Treaties Caravan and the Pan American Native Quest for Justice) was a cross-country protest caravan in the autumn of 1972, organised by Native American activists of the American Indian Movement (AIM) and allied groups, which travelled from the West Coast of the United States to Washington, D.C., arriving in the week before the presidential election.

The Twenty-Point Position Paper

The caravan carried a "Twenty-Point Position Paper," principally drafted by activist and writer Hank Adams, setting out demands for the restoration of treaty-making, the abolition of the Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA) in its existing form, the restoration of a 110-million-acre land base, the recognition of Native sovereignty and self-government, and federal protection of Indian religions, cultures and resources. The name evoked both the Trail of Tears and the hundreds of treaties between the United States and Native nations that the paper argued had been broken.

Occupation of the BIA headquarters

Finding the housing and reception arrangements in Washington inadequate, and after confrontations with officials, several hundred protesters occupied the headquarters of the Bureau of Indian Affairs on 3 November 1972, renaming it the "Native American Embassy." The occupation lasted until 8 November, ending with a negotiated settlement that included amnesty, travel money home and a commitment that the Twenty Points would be reviewed, though the administration later declined to adopt most of the proposals. The episode was a landmark of the Red Power movement and helped set the stage for the Wounded Knee occupation of 1973.
