Spirometry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A flow–volume loop produced by spirometry testing

Spirometry is the most common of the pulmonary function tests, measuring lung function — specifically the volume and speed of air that can be inhaled and exhaled. The patient breathes into a device called a spirometer, which records flow and volume over time, producing graphs such as the flow–volume loop.

Measurements

Key values include forced vital capacity (FVC), the total volume forcibly exhaled after maximal inhalation; forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1); and their ratio FEV1/FVC. Reduced ratios indicate obstructive defects such as asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), while reduced volumes with a preserved ratio suggest restrictive defects such as pulmonary fibrosis.

Use

Spirometry is used to diagnose and monitor asthma, COPD, cystic fibrosis and other respiratory conditions, to assess fitness for surgery, and in occupational health screening. Results are interpreted against predicted values for the patient's age, sex, height and ethnicity; correct technique and coaching are essential for reliable measurements.
