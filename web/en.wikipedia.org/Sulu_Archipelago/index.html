Sulu Archipelago

The Sulu Archipelago (Tausug: Sūg) is a chain of islands in the Pacific Ocean, in the southwestern Philippines. The archipelago forms the northern limit of the Celebes Sea and the southern limit of the Sulu Sea. It stretches from the Zamboanga Peninsula of Mindanao in the northeast to the coast of Borneo in the southwest.

Geography and people

The major island groups are Basilan, Jolo (Sulu), and Tawi-Tawi, along with hundreds of smaller coral islands. The population consists predominantly of Muslim Moro peoples — principally the Tausug, Sama-Bajau, and Yakan — and the area was the heartland of the Sultanate of Sulu, founded in the fifteenth century, which controlled sea trade between Borneo, Mindanao, and the wider Malay world and maintained treaty relations with China and later with Spain, Britain, and the United States.

History

Spain fought the Sulu Sultanate for centuries (the Moro Wars) without fully subduing it; sovereignty passed to the United States in 1898–99 and then to the Philippines in 1946. The islands today are divided among the provinces of Basilan, Sulu, and Tawi-Tawi, most within the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao. The region remains known for its pearl fisheries — the "Pearl of the Orient" fisheries of Jolo — and for maritime traditions of the Sama-Bajau sea nomads.
