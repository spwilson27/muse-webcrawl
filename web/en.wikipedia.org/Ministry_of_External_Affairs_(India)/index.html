The Ministry of External Affairs (Hindi: विदेश मंत्रालय) is the Indian government ministry charged with conducting India's foreign relations. It is responsible for the country's representation at the United Nations and other international organizations, and advises other ministries on foreign governments and nationals.

History

The ministry traces its origins to the Department of External Affairs established under British rule; Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first prime minister, held the external affairs portfolio himself for many years. Its headquarters are in South Block on Raisina Hill, New Delhi, with additional offices at Jawaharlal Nehru Bhawan.

Structure

The ministry is headed by the Minister of External Affairs, supported by ministers of state, and administratively led by the Foreign Secretary. Officers of the Indian Foreign Service staff India's roughly 190 embassies, high commissions, consulates and permanent missions abroad. Territorial divisions handle bilateral relations, while functional divisions cover areas such as disarmament, economic relations, development partnership and consular services.
