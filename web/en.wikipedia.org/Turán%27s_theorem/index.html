Turán's theorem

Turán's theorem is a foundational result in extremal graph theory, proved by Pál Turán in 1941. It gives the largest possible number of edges in a graph on n vertices containing no (r+1)-clique.

Statement

The unique extremal graph is the Turán graph T(n, r): the complete r-partite graph with parts differing in size by at most one. Hence any Kr+1-free graph has no more edges than T(n, r).

Related results

Mantel's theorem is the case r = 2. Turán's theorem led to Turán-type problems throughout combinatorics, including the (p, q)-theorem and density results for hypergraphs, where Turán's original problem about tetrahedron-free 3-uniform hypergraphs remains open.
