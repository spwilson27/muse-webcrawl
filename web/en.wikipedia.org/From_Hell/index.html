From Hell is a graphic novel by writer Alan Moore and artist Eddie Campbell, originally serialised from 1989 to 1996 and collected in 1999. Set in Victorian London, it presents a sprawling fictional account of the Jack the Ripper murders, built on the royal conspiracy theory advanced by Stephen Knight.

Plot and themes

The narrative identifies the royal physician Sir William Gull as the murderer, committing the Whitechapel killings to conceal the existence of an illegitimate royal child. Beyond its murder mystery, the work is an anatomy of Victorian society — its science, religion, architecture, and misogyny — and a meditation on violence, vision, and the birth of the twentieth century; its title comes from the return address on the "From Hell" letter attributed to the Ripper.

Research and reception

Moore's extensive appendices document the research behind every scene and openly acknowledge where the story departs from fact. From Hell won several industry awards, including an Eisner Award, and is frequently ranked among the greatest graphic novels. A loose film adaptation directed by the Hughes brothers was released in 2001, starring Johnny Depp.
