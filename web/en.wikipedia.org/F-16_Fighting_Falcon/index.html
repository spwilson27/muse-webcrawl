General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon

The General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon is an American single-engine supersonic multirole fighter aircraft developed by General Dynamics for the United States Air Force. Designed as an air superiority day fighter, it evolved into a successful all-weather multirole aircraft. Although the official name is "Fighting Falcon", the aircraft — now built by Lockheed Martin — is commonly known to its pilots and crews as the "Viper".

An F-16 Fighting Falcon of the United States Air Force

Design

The F-16 was the first production fighter deliberately designed to be slightly aerodynamically unstable (relaxed static stability), using a fly-by-wire flight control system to maintain control and achieve exceptional agility. Other innovations include a frameless bubble canopy for visibility, a side-mounted control stick, a seat reclined 30 degrees to improve g tolerance, and a blended wing-body shape. It is armed with an internal M61 Vulcan cannon and can carry a wide range of missiles and bombs on eleven hardpoints.

Service

Introduced in 1978, the F-16 has become the most numerous fixed-wing fighter in military service worldwide, with more than 4,600 built and service in over 25 air forces. It has seen extensive combat with the United States and its allies in the Middle East and the Balkans, and with Israel, Pakistan, and other operators. Later variants such as the Block 70/72 feature an AESA radar and advanced avionics, keeping the type in production and service into the twenty-first century.
