Vitamin D deficiency

Rickets, a consequence of severe deficiency in childhood

Symptoms: Bone pain, muscle weakness, often none

Complications: Rickets, osteomalacia, osteoporosis

Diagnosis: Blood calcifediol level

Vitamin D deficiency is a condition of inadequate levels of vitamin D in the body. It is most commonly caused by insufficient exposure to sunlight, inadequate dietary intake, or impaired absorption or metabolism. Because vitamin D is required for intestinal absorption of calcium, deficiency leads to defective bone mineralization.

Signs and consequences

Many people with mild deficiency have no symptoms. Severe or prolonged deficiency causes rickets in children — soft, weak bones leading to bowed legs and stunted growth — and osteomalacia in adults, with bone pain and muscle weakness. Secondary hyperparathyroidism develops as the body compensates for low calcium, drawing mineral from bone and increasing long-term osteoporosis risk. Deficiency has been associated in observational studies with many other conditions, though causal relationships are often uncertain.

Causes and risk factors

Risk factors include living at high latitudes, winter season, dark skin pigmentation, covering clothing, sunscreen use, older age, obesity, malabsorption disorders such as coeliac disease, and chronic kidney disease, which impairs activation of the vitamin.

Diagnosis and treatment

Status is assessed by measuring serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcifediol); thresholds for deficiency are debated, with many guidelines defining deficiency below 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L). Treatment consists of vitamin D supplementation, typically cholecalciferol, along with adequate calcium intake and sensible sun exposure. Food fortification programs, such as adding vitamin D to milk, have greatly reduced rickets in many countries.
