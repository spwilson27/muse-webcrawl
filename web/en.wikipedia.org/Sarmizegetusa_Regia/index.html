Sarmizegetusa Regia was the capital and the most important military, religious and political center of the Dacian kingdom before the wars with the Roman Empire. Perched at about 1,200 metres in the Orăștie Mountains of present-day Romania, it was the largest of the Dacian fortresses and the residence of kings including Burebista and Decebalus. It should not be confused with Ulpia Traiana Sarmizegetusa, the Roman city founded nearby after the conquest.

The fortress and sanctuaries

The stronghold combined a citadel built with massive walls in the murus dacicus technique — stone faces with an earth-and-timber core — with a sacred precinct of temples and circular and rectangular sanctuaries. The most famous structure is the great circular sanctuary, whose arrangement of andesite blocks has been interpreted as a calendar. Terraces carved into the mountainside supported a settlement estimated to have housed thousands, fed by aqueducts of ceramic pipes bringing water from mountain springs.

Destruction and legacy

After a long siege at the end of Trajan's Second Dacian War, Roman forces destroyed the capital in AD 106; its sanctuaries were demolished and the Dacian kingdom annexed as a Roman province. The ruins, rediscovered and excavated from the 19th century onward, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1999 as part of the Dacian Fortresses of the Orăștie Mountains. The site has also suffered from treasure hunting targeting Dacian gold artifacts.
