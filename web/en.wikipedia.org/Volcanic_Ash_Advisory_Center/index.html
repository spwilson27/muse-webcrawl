Volcanic Ash Advisory Center

A Volcanic Ash Advisory Center (VAAC) is a centre designated by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) to monitor volcanic ash plumes and issue advisories to the aviation community. There are nine VAACs worldwide, each responsible for a defined region, operated by national meteorological or volcanological agencies.

Role and operation

Volcanic ash is a serious hazard to aircraft: ingested ash can melt inside jet engines and solidify on turbine blades, causing engine failure, as in the 1982 British Airways Flight 9 incident and the 1989 KLM Flight 867 incident. VAACs use satellite imagery, volcano observatory reports, pilot reports, and dispersion models to track ash clouds and issue Volcanic Ash Advisories (VAAs) and graphical forecasts at regular intervals during eruptions.

The nine centres

The nine VAACs are located in Anchorage, Buenos Aires, Darwin, London, Montreal, Tokyo, Toulouse, Washington, and Wellington. Their work came to wide attention during the 2010 eruptions of Eyjafjallajökull in Iceland, when the London VAAC's advisories underpinned the closure of much of European airspace.
