Fiji, officially the Republic of Fiji, is an island country in Melanesia, part of Oceania in the South Pacific Ocean, lying about 2,000 kilometres north-northeast of New Zealand. It consists of an archipelago of more than 330 islands — of which about 110 are permanently inhabited — and more than 500 islets. The two largest islands, Viti Levu and Vanua Levu, account for most of the land area and population. The capital and largest city is Suva, on Viti Levu.

History

Fiji was settled by Austronesian peoples (the Lapita culture) roughly 3,500 years ago, later joined by Melanesian peoples; a stratified chiefly society developed, with frequent warfare between confederacies. Dutch explorer Abel Tasman sighted the islands in 1643, and Captain William Bligh sailed through them in 1789. In 1874 the chiefs ceded Fiji to Britain. Colonial rule brought Indian indentured labourers to work sugar plantations, founding the Indo-Fijian community. Fiji became independent in 1970. Its post-independence politics were marked by coups in 1987, 2000 and 2006, before elections under a new constitution in 2014. The 1987 coups led to Fiji's departure from the Commonwealth as a republic; it later rejoined.

Geography and climate

The main islands are mountainous and volcanic in origin, ringed by coral reefs, with a tropical maritime climate tempered by trade winds. The wet season (November to April) brings tropical cyclones; Cyclone Winston in 2016 was among the strongest ever recorded in the Southern Hemisphere. Fiji's reefs and rainforests host significant biodiversity, and the country has been a prominent voice on climate change, presiding over the COP23 UN climate conference in 2017.

Demographics and culture

The population is roughly 56% indigenous iTaukei Fijians and 37% Indo-Fijians, with Rotuman, Chinese, European and other Pacific Islander minorities. English is widely used, alongside Fijian and Fiji Hindi. Christianity predominates among iTaukei, while most Indo-Fijians are Hindu or Muslim. Rugby union is the national passion: Fiji's rugby sevens team won Olympic gold at Rio 2016 and Tokyo 2020. Tourism, sugar, bottled water, garments and remittances drive the economy; Fiji Water is a well-known export brand.

Government

Fiji is a parliamentary republic with a unicameral Parliament; the President is head of state and the Prime Minister head of government. Sitiveni Rabuka became prime minister after the 2022 general election. Fiji is a member of the United Nations, the Commonwealth, and the Pacific Islands Forum.
