Earthdog trial

An earthdog trial (or earthdog test) is a dog sport that tests the working ability and instinct of small terriers and dachshunds — breeds historically used to hunt rodents and other quarry that go to ground. Dogs navigate underground tunnels to locate caged rats protected behind a barrier, demonstrating the hunting drive the breeds were developed for without harming the quarry.

Format

Trials are held under organizations such as the American Kennel Club (AKC), which introduced its Earthdog program in 1994, and the American Working Terrier Association (AWTA), which has run den trials since 1971. The dog is released near a tunnel entrance, must enter and traverse a scent-lined wooden tunnel (typically with turns), and reach the quarry within a set time. At the quarry — rats safely caged and shielded — the dog must "work" by barking, scratching or otherwise indicating for a required period.

Levels and titles

In the AKC program, classes progress from Introduction to Quarry and Junior Earthdog, through Senior Earthdog — which adds a longer, more complex tunnel, a false exit and recall requirements — to Master Earthdog, which includes working in a brace (pair) and honoring another dog's work. Qualifying performances earn titles such as JE, SE and ME.

Purpose

Earthdog tests are non-competitive pass/fail evaluations intended to preserve the hunting instincts of go-to-ground breeds in an urban era, and to give owners a structured outlet for their dogs' natural behavior.
