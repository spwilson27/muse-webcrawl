Ridesharing

Ridesharing, in contemporary usage, refers to transportation services in which passengers book trips, typically through a smartphone app, in vehicles driven by independent drivers. Companies such as Uber, Lyft, DiDi and Bolt operate these ride-hailing networks. The term originally described carpooling, the sharing of a journey between travellers with a common destination.

History

App-based ridesharing began with the founding of Uber in 2009 (as UberCab) in San Francisco, followed by Lyft in 2012. The model spread worldwide, displacing some taxi business and prompting regulatory battles over licensing, insurance, and drivers' employment status.

Operation

Riders request a trip, are matched with a nearby driver, and pay automatically through the app. Prices are set by the platform and may rise with demand under surge pricing. Platforms take a commission on each fare and rate both drivers and passengers.

Impacts and debate

Studies debate ridesharing's effects on traffic congestion, public transit use, road safety and emissions, and its labour model is a central case in arguments about the gig economy. Many jurisdictions have created new regulatory categories for "transportation network companies".
