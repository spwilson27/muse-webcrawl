Kakiage

Kakiage (かき揚げ) is a popular Japanese dish made by mixing small pieces of seafood and vegetables together in tempura batter and deep-frying the mixture as a single round fritter or patty. Unlike ordinary tempura, where each ingredient is fried individually, kakiage binds many bite-sized ingredients — such as shrimp, onions, carrots, burdock root (gobō), sweet potato and mitsuba — into one crisp cluster.

Preparation

The ingredients are cut into thin strips or small pieces, dusted with flour, and coated with a light batter of flour, egg and ice water. Spoonfuls of the mixture are slid into hot oil and pressed flat, frying until golden and crisp. A common regional speciality is sakura-ebi kakiage, made with tiny sakura shrimp, a speciality of Shizuoka Prefecture.

Serving

Kakiage is eaten in several ways: seasoned with salt or dipped in tentsuyu (tempura dipping sauce); placed on top of soba or udon noodles (kakiage soba); or served over a bowl of steamed rice with a sweet soy-based sauce as kakiage donburi (kakiage-don). It is a staple of tempura restaurants, noodle stands and home cooking alike.
