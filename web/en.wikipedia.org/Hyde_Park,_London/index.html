Hyde Park is a Grade I-listed major park in Westminster, Greater London, in the United Kingdom. A Royal Park, it is the largest of the parks and green spaces that form a chain from Kensington Palace through Kensington Gardens and Hyde Park, via Hyde Park Corner and Green Park, past Buckingham Palace to St James's Park. The park is divided by the Serpentine and the Long Water lakes.

The park was established by Henry VIII in 1536 when he took the land from Westminster Abbey and used it as a hunting ground. It opened to the public in 1637 and quickly became popular, particularly for May Day parades. Major improvements occurred in the early 18th century under the direction of Queen Caroline. The park also became a place for duels during this time, often involving members of the nobility.

Geography

Hyde Park covers 350 acres (140 ha) and Kensington Gardens covers 275 acres (111 ha), giving an overall area of 625 acres (253 ha), making the combined area larger than the Principality of Monaco, though smaller than New York City's Central Park. To the southeast is Hyde Park Corner. Beyond this, green routes continue through Green Park and St James's Park.

Features

Popular landmarks include Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Fountain, the Serpentine Gallery, Speaker's Corner, the Diana Memorial Playground, and the 7 July Memorial. Speakers' Corner, in the northeast corner near Marble Arch, has been a traditional site for public debate since the 19th century; notable speakers have included Karl Marx, Vladimir Lenin, and George Orwell.

Events

The park hosted the Great Exhibition of 1851 in the Crystal Palace. In recent decades it has become a major venue for large concerts, including performances by the Rolling Stones in 1969, Pink Floyd, Queen, and Live 8 in 2005. The annual Winter Wonderland festival is also held in the park.
