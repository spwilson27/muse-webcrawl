Enlisted rank

An enlisted rank (also known as an enlisted grade or enlisted rate) is, in most militaries, any rank below a commissioned officer. Enlisted personnel are the largest component of most armed forces and perform the hands-on work of the military, supervised by non-commissioned officers (NCOs) who themselves hold enlisted ranks.

Structure

Enlisted ranks typically progress from junior grades (such as private, seaman, or airman) through NCO grades (corporal, sergeant, petty officer) to senior NCOs (sergeant major, master chief petty officer, chief master sergeant). The NATO rank scale designates enlisted personnel as "other ranks" (OR-1 to OR-9).

Distinction from officers

Commissioned officers receive authority from a commission issued by the head of state, while warrant officers occupy an intermediate category in some forces. Enlisted members generally enter service without a university degree requirement and advance through promotion based on experience, examinations, and vacancies.
