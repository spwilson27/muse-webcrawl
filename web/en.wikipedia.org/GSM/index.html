GSM

The Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM) is a standard developed by the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) to describe protocols for second-generation (2G) digital cellular networks used by mobile devices such as mobile phones and tablets. First deployed in Finland in 1991, GSM became the world's most widely used mobile technology, at its peak carrying the large majority of global mobile connections.

History

Work began in 1982 with the formation of the Groupe Spécial Mobile (from which "GSM" took its name) to design a pan-European digital mobile system to replace incompatible analogue networks. The first GSM network was launched by Radiolinja in Finland in July 1991, and the first roaming agreement followed in 1992. GSM introduced the SIM (Subscriber Identity Module) card, separating subscriber identity from the handset, and standardised services including voice calls, the Short Message Service (SMS texting, originally a by-product of the signalling channel), and international roaming.

Technical features

GSM is a circuit-switched network using TDMA (time-division multiple access) over paired frequency bands — principally 900 MHz and 1800 MHz in most of the world, and 850/1900 MHz in the Americas. Channels of 200 kHz are divided into eight time slots. Data services were later extended by packet-switched GPRS ("2.5G") and by EDGE ("2.75G"), which raised data rates before the arrival of 3G UMTS. GSM security relied on the A5 family of ciphers, since shown to be weak, and authentication is one-way (the network authenticates the handset).

Legacy

GSM networks have been progressively shut down in some countries as operators refarm spectrum for 4G and 5G, but GSM remains in extensive use worldwide, particularly for legacy devices and machine-to-machine communication. The GSM Association (GSMA), representing mobile operators, promotes the industry's interests and organises the Mobile World Congress.
