2016 Nice truck attack

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 2016 Nice truck attack occurred on the evening of 14 July 2016, Bastille Day, in Nice, France. A 19-tonne cargo truck was deliberately driven into crowds celebrating on the Promenade des Anglais, killing 86 people and injuring more than 450 others. The driver, Mohamed Lahouaiej-Bouhlel, a Tunisian living in France, was shot dead by police.

Attack

Following the Bastille Day fireworks display, Lahouaiej-Bouhlel drove a rented Renault Midlum truck onto the Promenade des Anglais, which was closed to traffic and crowded with spectators. He zigzagged through the crowd for about 2 kilometres (1.2 miles), striking hundreds of people. Police and a motorcyclist attempted to stop the truck; officers eventually shot the driver dead near the Palais de la Méditerranée hotel.

Perpetrator

Lahouaiej-Bouhlel, born in Tunisia in 1985, had a French residency permit and a record of petty crime and domestic violence, but was not on a terror watchlist for radicalisation. The Islamic State's Amaq news agency claimed he had acted in response to its calls to attack citizens of coalition countries, though investigators found no evidence of direct organisational direction. Several associates were later tried; in 2022 a Paris court convicted eight defendants of offences including criminal terrorist association.

Reactions

President François Hollande extended the state of emergency that had been in force since the November 2015 Paris attacks and announced increased French military operations against the Islamic State. Three days of national mourning were declared. The attack prompted debate about security arrangements for large public events and the use of vehicles as weapons, a tactic seen in subsequent attacks in Berlin, London, Stockholm, Barcelona and New York.
