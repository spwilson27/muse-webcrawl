Greenbelt (Golden Horseshoe)

CountryCanada
ProvinceOntario
Established2005
AreaApprox. 2 million acres (810,000 ha)
Governing lawGreenbelt Act, 2005

The Greenbelt is a protected area of green space, farmland, forests, wetlands and watersheds in southern Ontario, Canada. It surrounds the largely urbanized Golden Horseshoe region, which includes the Greater Toronto Area and Hamilton, and is one of the largest permanently protected greenbelts in the world.

The Greenbelt was created by legislation passed by the Government of Ontario in February 2005, and the Greenbelt Plan came into effect that year. It consolidated and expanded earlier protected areas, including the Niagara Escarpment Plan (1985) and the Oak Ridges Moraine Conservation Plan (2002), under a single framework of land-use policies.

Geography

The Greenbelt covers roughly 2 million acres (about 810,000 hectares) stretching from the Niagara Peninsula in the southwest, around the western end of Lake Ontario, and east to the Oak Ridges Moraine and the Kawarthas. It includes prime agricultural land in the Niagara Peninsula and Holland Marsh, extensive forests, the Carolinian forest zone, and the headwaters of more than 60 river systems flowing into Lake Ontario and Lake Simcoe.

Purpose and policies

The Greenbelt Plan is intended to permanently protect agricultural land and natural heritage systems from urban sprawl, to support farming and rural economies, and to safeguard water resources. Most forms of urban development are prohibited on designated specialty crop lands, prime agricultural areas and key natural heritage features, although existing towns and villages within the belt are allowed limited growth, and infrastructure such as highways and aggregate extraction may be permitted.

Controversy and revisions

The Greenbelt has been praised by environmental groups for limiting sprawl and preserving farmland close to Canada's largest urban region. It has also been criticized by developers and some municipalities, who argue it constrains housing supply and drives up land prices inside settlement boundaries. In 2022 the Ontario government removed about 7,400 acres from the Greenbelt for housing development while adding other lands; following public controversy and investigations, the removals were reversed in 2023 and the boundaries were restored by legislation.
