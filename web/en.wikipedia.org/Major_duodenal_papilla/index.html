Major duodenal papilla

The major duodenal papilla (also known as the papilla of Vater) is a rounded projection in the descending (second) part of the duodenum where the common bile duct and pancreatic duct open into the intestinal lumen. Through it, bile and pancreatic juice enter the digestive tract to aid digestion.

Anatomy

The papilla lies on the posteromedial wall of the second part of the duodenum, roughly 7–10 cm distal to the pylorus, marked on the mucosal surface by a longitudinal fold. In most individuals the common bile duct and the main pancreatic duct unite just before the papilla to form a short common channel, the hepatopancreatic ampulla (ampulla of Vater), surrounded by the sphincter of Oddi, a ring of smooth muscle that regulates flow and prevents reflux of duodenal contents. In some individuals the two ducts open separately at the papilla. The accessory pancreatic duct drains at the separate, smaller minor duodenal papilla about 2 cm above.

Clinical significance

Gallstones may lodge at the ampulla, obstructing both ducts and causing obstructive jaundice or acute pancreatitis. Tumours arising here (ampullary carcinoma) often present early with jaundice because of their strategic position. The papilla is accessed during endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), in which cannulation and sphincterotomy are performed through it to extract stones or place stents.
