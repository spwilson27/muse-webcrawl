Flow diagram

A flow diagram is a diagram that represents a flow or a set of dynamic relationships in a system. The term is used generically for diagrams showing the movement of material, energy, information, or activity through stages, of which the best known is the flowchart, representing a workflow or process with boxes, diamonds, and arrows. Flow diagrams are used in engineering, chemistry (process flow diagrams), computer science, business process modelling, and quality management.

Types

  • Flowchart — a diagram of an algorithm or process using standardised symbols: ovals for start/end, rectangles for processes, diamonds for decisions.
  • Process flow diagram — used in chemical engineering to indicate the general flow of plant processes and equipment.
  • Data flow diagram — shows the flow of data through an information system, with processes, data stores, and external entities.
  • Sankey diagram — a flow diagram in which the width of arrows is proportional to the quantity flowing, used for energy and material balances.

History

Industrial engineers Frank and Lillian Gilbreth introduced "flow process charts" in 1921. Flowcharts became a standard tool for documenting computer programs in the 1940s–1960s, with symbols standardised by ANSI and ISO, before largely yielding to pseudocode and structured programming notations such as UML activity diagrams. They remain common in process documentation and education.
