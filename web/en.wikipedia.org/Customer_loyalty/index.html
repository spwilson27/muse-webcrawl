Customer loyalty

Customer loyalty is a customer's willingness to repeatedly return to a company to conduct business, typically because of positive experiences, satisfaction with products or services, and the perceived value of the relationship. Loyal customers buy repeatedly, recommend the business to others, and are less sensitive to price changes or competitors' offers.

Importance

Retaining existing customers is generally cheaper than acquiring new ones, and long-term customers tend to spend more over time. Research in marketing commonly cites the finding that a small increase in customer retention can produce a disproportionately large increase in profits, because loyal customers also generate referrals and positive word of mouth.

Loyalty programmes

Firms encourage loyalty through loyalty programmes that reward repeat purchases with points, discounts, or perks. Airline frequent-flyer programmes, hotel reward schemes, and supermarket club cards are prominent examples. Such programmes also generate data that firms use for targeted marketing and personalisation.

Measurement

Loyalty is measured through repeat-purchase rates, customer retention and churn rates, share of wallet, and attitudinal surveys such as the Net Promoter Score, which asks how likely customers are to recommend a business. Analysts distinguish behavioural loyalty (repeat buying) from attitudinal loyalty (genuine preference), noting that repeat buying driven only by convenience or switching costs may vanish when alternatives appear.
