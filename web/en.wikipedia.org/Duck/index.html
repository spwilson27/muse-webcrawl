Duck

Duck is the common name for numerous species of waterfowl in the family Anatidae. Ducks are generally smaller than swans and geese and are found in both fresh and salt water around the world.

Description

Ducks have elongated, broad bodies, relatively long necks, and flattened bills adapted for feeding in water. Many species show strong sexual dimorphism: males (drakes) have bright plumage while females are mottled brown for camouflage. They feed on grasses, aquatic plants, fish, insects, and small molluscs, dabbling at the surface or diving, depending on the species.

Relationship with humans

Ducks have been domesticated for thousands of years for eggs, meat, and feathers (down). The domestic duck descends mainly from the mallard, and the Muscovy duck is a separate domesticated species. Ducks also feature widely in folklore and popular culture.
