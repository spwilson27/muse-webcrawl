Local thermodynamic equilibrium

Local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) is a simplifying assumption used in thermodynamics and radiative transfer. In LTE, each small volume element of a system is treated as being in thermodynamic equilibrium at a well-defined local temperature, even though the system as a whole is not in equilibrium and its temperature varies from place to place.

Conditions

LTE holds when collisions between particles are frequent enough to maintain Maxwell–Boltzmann velocity distributions and Boltzmann population distributions of energy levels locally, compared with the rates at which conditions change. In such conditions, quantities such as temperature, pressure, and chemical potential can be defined at each point, and radiation emission can be described by Kirchhoff's law with a local Planck source function.

Applications

LTE models are widely used in stellar atmosphere calculations, plasma physics, combustion, and atmospheric science. Where collisions are too infrequent, for example in the low-density outer layers of stars, non-LTE (NLTE) modelling is required.
