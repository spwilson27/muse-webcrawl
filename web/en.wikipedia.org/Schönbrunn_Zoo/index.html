Schönbrunn Zoo

The imperial pavilion at the centre of Schönbrunn Zoo.

Schönbrunn Zoo (German: Tiergarten Schönbrunn) is a 17-hectare zoo in the city of Vienna, Austria. Established as an imperial menagerie in 1752, it is the oldest continuously operating zoo in the world. It lies in the grounds of Schönbrunn Palace, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and is one of the most visited attractions in Austria, drawing more than two million visitors a year.

History

The zoo was founded by Francis I, Holy Roman Emperor, husband of Maria Theresa, who had a private menagerie built around a central pavilion in 1752. Initially reserved for the imperial family, it was opened to the public in 1779. Animals were housed in enclosures radiating from the central pavilion, in which the imperial family could take breakfast while watching the animals.

Animals and conservation

The zoo is home to more than 700 species, including giant pandas, Siberian tigers, orangutans, elephants, and polar bears. Schönbrunn achieved international attention with the birth of the giant panda cub Fu Long in 2007, the first panda born in Europe by natural mating in a quarter-century. The zoo is active in European breeding programmes for endangered species and is consistently ranked among the best zoos in Europe.

Architecture

Several historic structures survive, including the baroque central pavilion and the oldest animal houses. Modern additions include a rainforest house, an aquarium and terrarium complex, and a polar bear enclosure opened in 2014.
