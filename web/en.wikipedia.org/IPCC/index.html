Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) is an intergovernmental body of the United Nations established in 1988 by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) and the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP). Its role is to assess the scientific literature on climate change and provide policymakers with regular, objective assessments of the causes, impacts, and future risks of climate change, together with options for adaptation and mitigation. The IPCC does not conduct original research; thousands of volunteer scientists and experts author and review its reports.

Assessment Reports

The IPCC has published six comprehensive Assessment Reports (1990, 1995, 2001, 2007, 2014, and 2021–2023), prepared by three Working Groups: Physical Science Basis; Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability; and Mitigation of Climate Change. Each report includes a Summary for Policymakers approved line by line by member governments. The Sixth Assessment Report concluded that human influence has unequivocally warmed the atmosphere, ocean, and land, and that limiting warming to 1.5 °C requires deep, rapid emissions reductions.

Special reports and recognition

Special reports have addressed topics including 1.5 °C of global warming (2018), the ocean and cryosphere, and land use. In 2007 the IPCC shared the Nobel Peace Prize with Al Gore. The IPCC has 195 member states and is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland.
