Jamshedpur

Jamshedpur is a city in the Indian state of Jharkhand, in the East Singhbhum district, and the largest urban agglomeration in the state. It was founded by the industrialist Jamsetji Tata and is named after him; it is home to Tata Steel, the first steel plant in India, and is often called the "Steel City of India."

History

Jamsetji Tata had long sought a site combining iron ore, coal, water and rail access. The village of Sakchi, at the confluence of the Subarnarekha and Kharkai rivers, was chosen in 1907; the steel works produced its first ingot in 1912. The town was renamed Jamshedpur in 1919 by Lord Chelmsford in Jamsetji's honour. Unusually for an Indian city of its size, much of it was a planned company town administered by Tata's utilities arm rather than a municipal corporation.

Industry and life

Besides Tata Steel, the city hosts Tata Motors' major truck plant, Tata Power, and numerous ancillary industries, making it one of India's principal industrial centres. Jubilee Park, modelled on Mysuru's Brindavan Gardens, and the Dimna Lake are landmarks. Institutions include XLRI (founded 1949, among India's oldest business schools) and the National Institute of Technology Jamshedpur. The population of the urban agglomeration is about 1.3 million.
