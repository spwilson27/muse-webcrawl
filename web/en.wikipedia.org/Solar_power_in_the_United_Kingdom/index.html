Solar power in the United Kingdom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Solar panels in the English countryside

Solar power in the United Kingdom grew rapidly in the 2010s, despite the country's relatively low sunshine hours, as panel costs fell and subsidy schemes (the Feed-in Tariff from 2010 and later Renewables Obligation and Contracts for Difference) encouraged deployment. Installed capacity passed 13 gigawatts in the early 2020s, split between rooftop systems on homes and businesses and ground-mounted solar farms.

Characteristics

Generation peaks in summer and in the middle of the day, complementing wind power. Solar farms have attracted debate over land use and landscape impact, and increasingly are combined with battery storage and, in some cases, sheep grazing (agrivoltaics). Policy after the closure of the feed-in tariff has centred on smart export payments and planning rules for larger sites.
