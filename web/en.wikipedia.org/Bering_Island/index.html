Bering Island

Bering Island (Russian: ostrov Beringa) is the westernmost and largest of the Commander Islands in the Russian Far East, in the Bering Sea. It lies about 175 kilometres east of the Kamchatka Peninsula, separated from it by the Kamchatka Strait. The island is roughly 95 kilometres long and between 15 and 40 kilometres wide, with an area of about 1,660 square kilometres, making it the largest island of the archipelago.

History

The island is named for the Danish explorer in Russian service Vitus Bering, commander of the Second Kamchatka Expedition. In November 1741, returning from the coast of North America, Bering's ship the St. Peter was wrecked on the island's shore. Bering and many of his crew died there of scurvy and exposure during the winter; the survivors, led by Sven Waxell and including the naturalist Georg Wilhelm Steller, built a new vessel from the wreckage and returned to Kamchatka in 1742.

The castaways encountered the huge, slow-moving sirenian that became known as Steller's sea cow; hunted by later fur traders, the species was extinct by about 1768. The island later became a base for the Russian-American Company and for sea-otter and fur-seal hunting.

Geography and settlement

The island's terrain is largely treeless, mountainous tundra, with a cool, humid maritime climate. Its only settlement is the village of Nikolskoye, whose population numbers a few hundred, including a notable Aleut (Unangan) community descended from people brought to the islands in the nineteenth century. The island lies within the Komandorsky Zapovednik, a large nature reserve protecting marine mammals, seabird colonies, and tundra ecosystems.
