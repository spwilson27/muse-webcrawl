Quebec

Quebec City, the provincial capital

Quebec is one of the ten provinces of Canada. It is the largest province by area and the second-largest by population, with about 8.8 million inhabitants (Quebecers or Québécois). It is the only Canadian province whose official language at the provincial level is French, and the vast majority of its population speaks French as a first language. Its capital is Quebec City, and its largest city is Montreal.

The name "Quebec" comes from an Algonquin word meaning "where the river narrows," referring to the narrowing of the St. Lawrence River near Quebec City. The province is bounded by Ontario to the west, Hudson Bay and the Hudson Strait to the north, Newfoundland and Labrador to the northeast, and the Gulf of St. Lawrence and the Maritime provinces and the United States to the southeast.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Iroquoian-speaking St. Lawrence Iroquoians, Algonquian nations, and Inuit in the far north, inhabited the region for millennia. Jacques Cartier explored the St. Lawrence in 1534–1535, and Samuel de Champlain founded Quebec City in 1608, making it the heart of New France. After the Seven Years' War, France ceded Canada to Britain by the Treaty of Paris (1763); the Quebec Act of 1774 preserved French civil law and Catholic rights. Quebec became one of the four founding provinces of Confederation in 1867. The Quiet Revolution of the 1960s rapidly modernized and secularized the province, while the sovereignty movement twice put independence to a referendum—in 1980 and in 1995, the latter failing by less than one percentage point.

Economy and culture

Quebec's economy is driven by abundant natural resources and cheap hydroelectricity from the state utility Hydro-Québec, together with aerospace, information technology, biotechnology, aluminum, forestry, and tourism. It is the world's largest producer of maple syrup. French language and culture are protected and promoted by laws such as the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101, 1977). The province is known for its festivals—including the Montreal International Jazz Festival and the Quebec Winter Carnival—its cuisine (such as poutine and tourtière), and its distinct cinema, literature, and music.
