Grünes Gewölbe

The Grünes Gewölbe holds the treasury of the Wettin electors of Saxony.

The Grünes Gewölbe ("Green Vault") is a museum in Dresden, Germany, containing the largest treasure collection in Europe. Founded by Augustus the Strong, Elector of Saxony and King of Poland, in the 1720s, it occupies vaulted rooms in the western part of Dresden Castle (the Residenzschloss) — the name derives from the malachite-green painted columns and capitals of its original treasury rooms.

The collection

The vaults display the accumulated treasures of the Wettin dynasty, amassed between the 16th and 18th centuries: works in gold, silver, enamel, ivory, amber, bronze and rock crystal, together with curiosities of the Kunstkammer tradition. Masterpieces include the court of the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb, a miniature gold-and-enamel tableau by the court jeweller Johann Melchior Dinglinger, the Dresden Green Diamond — a famous natural green diamond of about 41 carats, set in a hat ornament — the cherry stone carved with 185 faces, and the Golden Coffee Service. Augustus opened parts of the collection to the public as early as 1724, making it one of the world's oldest museums.

War and theft

Most of the treasure was stored in Königstein Fortress during the Second World War; the vaults themselves were damaged in the bombing of Dresden in February 1945. After the war the collection was taken to the Soviet Union and returned to Dresden in 1958. Restored rooms reopened in stages — the New Green Vault in 2004 and the Historic Green Vault in 2006. In the early hours of 25 November 2019, thieves broke into the Historic Green Vault and stole jewellery sets of immense value, including pieces set with the Saxon White Diamond; most of the loot was recovered in December 2022, and the perpetrators were convicted in 2023, though some items remain missing.
