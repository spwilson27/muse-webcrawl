The Thompson River is the largest tributary of the Fraser River in the Canadian province of British Columbia. It begins at the confluence of the North Thompson and South Thompson Rivers at Kamloops and flows generally westward and southwestward for about 489 kilometres to join the Fraser at Lytton. It is named for David Thompson, the great fur trader and mapmaker of the North West Company, who explored the region in the early nineteenth century — although Thompson himself never travelled the river that bears his name.

Course

From Kamloops the river flows west through Kamloops Lake, then past the towns of Savona, Cache Creek and Ashcroft. Below Ashcroft it enters a deep, arid canyon — the Thompson Canyon — carved through the Interior Plateau, where the river runs through semi-desert benchlands of sagebrush and ponderosa pine, among the driest and hottest landscapes in Canada. It passes Spences Bridge before reaching its confluence with the Fraser at Lytton. The two main arms are the clear-flowing North Thompson, which rises near the Columbia Icefield region, and the South Thompson, which drains the Shuswap Lake system.

Ecology and uses

The Thompson is one of the great salmon rivers of North America, supporting runs of Chinook, coho, sockeye and pink salmon, as well as the famous Thompson River steelhead. Its tributary the Adams River hosts one of the largest sockeye salmon runs on the continent. The canyon corridor carries both of Canada's transcontinental railways — Canadian Pacific on one bank and Canadian National on the other — as well as the Trans-Canada Highway, and the river's rapids, including the Jaws of Death and the Frog, are popular with whitewater rafters.
