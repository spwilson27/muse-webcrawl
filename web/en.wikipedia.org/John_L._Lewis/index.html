John L. Lewis

Born: John Llewellyn Lewis, 12 February 1880, Cleveland, Iowa, US
Died: 11 June 1969 (aged 89), Alexandria, Virginia, US
Occupation: Labour leader
Known for: President of the United Mine Workers (1920–1960); founder of the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO)

John Llewellyn Lewis (February 12, 1880 – June 11, 1969) was an American leader of organized labor who served as president of the United Mine Workers of America (UMW) from 1920 to 1960. A major player in the history of coal mining, he was the principal founder of the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO), which established the United Steel Workers of America and helped organize millions of industrial workers in the 1930s.

Early life

Lewis was born in Cleveland, Iowa, to Welsh immigrant parents who had settled in the coal-mining country of Lucas County. He went into the mines as a teenager and rose through the ranks of the UMW in Illinois, becoming a full-time union official and, in 1920, president of the international union, a post he held with an iron grip for forty years.

Leadership of the miners and the CIO

Under Lewis the UMW weathered the collapse of union membership in the 1920s, then surged during the New Deal. After breaking with the American Federation of Labor over the organization of industrial workers, Lewis led the Committee (later Congress) of Industrial Organizations from 1935, bankrolling organizing drives in steel, automobiles and rubber. His fiery oratory and mastery of collective bargaining made him one of the most powerful men in America; coal strikes during and after the Second World War made him equally one of the most controversial. He secured the landmark welfare and retirement fund for miners financed by a royalty on coal tonnage, a model later adopted in other industries.

Later years

Lewis retired as UMW president in 1960 and died in Alexandria, Virginia, in 1969. Historians credit him with transforming the American labour movement by organizing mass-production industries, while noting his authoritarian control of his own union.
