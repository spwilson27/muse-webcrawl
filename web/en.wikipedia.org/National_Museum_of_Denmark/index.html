National Museum of Denmark
Danish: Nationalmuseet
Location: Prince's Palace, Copenhagen
Type: National museum of cultural history
Collections: Danish prehistory to the present; ethnography

The National Museum of Denmark (Danish: Nationalmuseet) in Copenhagen is Denmark's largest museum of cultural history, comprising the histories of Danish and foreign cultures alike. The museum's main building is located a short distance from Strøget at the centre of the city, in the Prince's Palace, a Rococo mansion built for the Danish crown prince in the 1740s.

Collections

The museum's exhibitions cover Danish prehistory — including the iconic Trundholm Sun Chariot, the Gundestrup cauldron, runestones, and Viking treasures — through the Middle Ages and Renaissance to the present day. Its ethnographic collections are among the world's largest, with objects from cultures across the globe, and it also holds classical antiquities and one of Europe's finest coin and medal collections.

The museum system also manages other sites, including the Viking Ship Museum's former home institution for prehistory, the open-air museum Frilandsmuseet, and royal collections such as at Rosenborg Castle's associated institutions.

History

The collections trace their origin to the Royal Danish Cabinet of Curiosities (Kunstkammer), founded around 1650 by Frederick III. In the early 19th century the curator Christian Jürgensen Thomsen organised the antiquities using his famous "three-age system" — Stone Age, Bronze Age, Iron Age — a framework that shaped archaeology worldwide. The museum took up residence in the Prince's Palace in 1849.

Activities

The National Museum conducts archaeological research, conservation, and oversees the danefæ system, under which treasure trove found in Denmark must be offered to the museum.
