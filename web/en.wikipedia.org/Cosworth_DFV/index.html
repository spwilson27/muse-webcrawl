The Cosworth DFV (Double Four Valve) is a 3.0-litre V8 racing engine designed by Keith Duckworth of Cosworth and funded by Ford, introduced for the 1967 Formula One season. It became the most successful engine in Formula One history, winning 155 Grands Prix between 1967 and 1983, and powered twelve Drivers' Championship winners and ten Constructors' Championship-winning teams.

History

Lotus founder Colin Chapman persuaded Ford to fund a new engine for the 3-litre formula introduced in 1966. Duckworth combined two of Cosworth's four-cylinder FVA units into a V8 with four valves per cylinder and gear-driven double overhead camshafts, designed from the outset to serve as a stressed member of the chassis. The DFV won on its debut in the Lotus 49 at the 1967 Dutch Grand Prix, driven by Jim Clark.

Dominance and legacy

From 1968 Ford made the DFV available for purchase by any team, and it effectively became Formula One's standard engine: every Grand Prix from 1969 to 1973 except one was won by a DFV-powered car, and drivers including Graham Hill, Jackie Stewart, Emerson Fittipaldi, James Hunt, Mario Andretti, Alan Jones, Nelson Piquet and Keke Rosberg won championships with it. Its last victory came at Detroit in 1983 with Michele Alboreto's Tyrrell.

Derivatives served for decades elsewhere: the turbocharged DFX dominated American Indy car racing, the DFY and DFZ/DFR served late-1980s F1, and DFV variants raced at Le Mans and in Formula 3000. The engine's longevity and availability are credited with sustaining the era of the independent British "garagiste" teams.
