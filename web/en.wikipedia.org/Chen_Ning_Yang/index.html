Chen Ning Yang

Chen Ning Yang (born 1 October 1922) is a Chinese theoretical physicist known for his work in statistical mechanics and particle physics. He is often known by the name Yang Chen-Ning or Frank Yang.

Life

Born in Hefei, Anhui, Yang studied at National Southwestern Associated University during the Second Sino-Japanese War and earned his doctorate at the University of Chicago in 1948 under Edward Teller. He worked at the Institute for Advanced Study and later at Stony Brook University, and after his retirement divided his time between the United States and China, joining Tsinghua University.

Work

With Tsung-Dao Lee he proposed in 1956 that parity is not conserved in weak interactions, a prediction quickly confirmed experimentally; they shared the 1957 Nobel Prize in Physics. With Robert Mills he developed Yang–Mills theory (1954), a cornerstone of the Standard Model, and with Baxter he is associated with the Yang–Baxter equation.
