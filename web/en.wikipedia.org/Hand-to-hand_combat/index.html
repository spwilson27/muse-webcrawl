Hand-to-hand combat

FocusUnarmed and close-quarters fighting
PractitionersMilitary and law-enforcement personnel
RelatedMartial arts, combatives, melee

Hand-to-hand combat is a physical confrontation between two or more persons at very short range without the use of ranged weapons, or with weapons employed at grappling distance. It includes unarmed striking and grappling as well as fighting with close-combat weapons such as knives and bayonets. The term is closely related to melee combat and to military combatives training.

Military training

Armed forces have long trained soldiers in close combat, from ancient wrestling and pankration to bayonet drills. Modern programmes combine techniques from boxing, wrestling, judo, and other martial arts into systems such as the U.S. Marine Corps Martial Arts Program (MCMAP), the U.S. Army Combatives Program (based largely on Brazilian jiu-jitsu), the Israeli Krav Maga, and the Russian Systema and sambo traditions.

Components

Hand-to-hand fighting encompasses strikes (punches, kicks, elbows, knees), clinching, throws, joint locks, chokes, ground fighting, and defences against weapons. Training emphasises aggression, situational awareness, and the transition to weapons, since actual battlefield use is rare in modern warfare but remains relevant in confined environments, peacekeeping, and law enforcement.
