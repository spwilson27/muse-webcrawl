Meaning"Infinite Light"
SectMahāyāna Buddhism
Pure LandSukhāvatī (Western Paradise)

Amitābha (Sanskrit: अमिताभ, "Infinite Light"; also Amitāyus, "Infinite Life") is a celestial Buddha described in the scriptures of Mahāyāna Buddhism. He is the principal Buddha of Pure Land Buddhism, a branch of East Asian Buddhism, and is also found in Vajrayāna Buddhism, where he is known as one of the Five Tathāgatas.

Doctrine

According to the Larger Sūtra of Immeasurable Life, Amitābha was once a king who renounced his throne and became the monk Dharmākara. Over many aeons he made forty-eight vows, the fulfilment of which would establish a Buddha-land (Sukhāvatī, the "Land of Bliss") in the western direction, free of suffering, into which beings could be reborn simply by calling his name to mind with sincere faith. The eighteenth vow, known as the Primal Vow, promises rebirth in Sukhāvatī to those who sincerely entrust themselves to Amitābha and recite his name even ten times.

Practice

Devotion to Amitābha is centred on the practice of nianfo (Japanese: nembutsu) — the recitation of the phrase "Namo Amitābha Buddha" (Chinese: 南無阿彌陀佛, Nāmó Ēmítuófó; Japanese: Namu Amida Butsu). Schools such as Chinese Pure Land, Japanese Jōdo-shū and Jōdo Shinshū place this practice at the heart of their teaching.

Iconography

Amitābha is usually depicted as a monk in red robes — red being the colour associated with him in the system of the Five Tathāgatas — seated in meditation with hands in the dhyāna mudrā holding an alms bowl. He is often flanked by the bodhisattvas Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta, forming the "Three Saints of the West".
