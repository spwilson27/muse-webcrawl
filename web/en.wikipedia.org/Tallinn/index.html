Tallinn

Tallinn is the capital and most populous city of Estonia. Situated on the southern shore of the Gulf of Finland, about 80 kilometres south of Helsinki, it is the country's political, financial and cultural centre, with a population of roughly 450,000. Tallinn's medieval Old Town, one of the best-preserved in northern Europe, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The site was a trading place from early times; in 1219 the Danish king Valdemar II built a fortress on Toompea hill after conquering the area, and the city — long known by its German name Reval — joined the Hanseatic League in 1285 and prospered as a port trading salt, grain and timber between Russia and western Europe. Tallinn passed to Sweden in 1561 and to the Russian Empire in 1710 after the Great Northern War. It became the capital of independent Estonia in 1918, was occupied by the Soviet Union and Nazi Germany during the Second World War, and regained its role as capital when Estonia restored independence in 1991.

Geography

The city occupies a limestone coast with a harbour sheltered by the Viimsi peninsula and the islands of Aegna and Naissaar. The Old Town is divided between the upper town of Toompea, seat of the Estonian parliament and government, and the lower town of merchants' houses, churches and guild halls. Lake Ülemiste, south of the centre, supplies the city's drinking water.

Economy and technology

Tallinn is Estonia's main port and a significant ferry hub linking Estonia with Finland and Sweden. The city is noted for its technology sector: Skype was developed here, and Estonia's e-government and start-up scene — sometimes called "Silicon Valley on the Baltic" — is concentrated in Tallinn. Tourism, drawn by the Old Town and cruise traffic, is also important.

Culture

The city hosts the Estonian Song Festival tradition, museums such as the Kumu art museum and the Seaplane Harbour, and a medieval festival in the Old Town each summer. Tallinn was a European Capital of Culture in 2011.
