Martial arts film

Martial arts films are a subgenre of action films that feature numerous martial arts combat scenes between characters. These combats are usually the film's primary appeal and entertainment value, and often are a method of storytelling and character expression and development. Martial arts are frequently featured in training scenes and other sequences in addition to fights.

History

The genre developed principally in China, in wuxia films based on popular novels about swordsmen and knight-errants. Hong Kong cinema industrialised martial arts film-making, and the films of Bruce Lee — The Big Boss (1971), Fist of Fury (1972), Enter the Dragon (1973) — ignited worldwide interest in the early 1970s. Jackie Chan developed a distinctive style combining kung fu with comedy and dangerous stunt work, while Jet Li became the emblematic wushu star.

In the 1990s and 2000s, fight choreographers such as Yuen Woo-ping brought martial arts choreography to Hollywood (The Matrix, Kill Bill), and Ang Lee's Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (2000) won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. Later landmarks include Ip Man (2008) and Indonesia's The Raid (2011), showcasing the silat style.

Subgenres

Important subgenres include kung fu films, wuxia (period swordplay), samurai and chanbara films from Japan, ninja films, and contemporary "gun fu" blends mixing martial arts with gunplay. Common conventions include the master-student relationship, revenge plots, tournament structures, and wire-assisted "wire fu" acrobatics.
