Samoyedic languages

The Samoyedic (or Samoyed) languages are a branch of the Uralic language family spoken by roughly 25,000 people in the far north of Eurasia, across the tundra and taiga of northern Russia from the White Sea to the Taymyr Peninsula. Together with the Finno-Ugric languages they form the traditional two-way division of the Uralic family.

Languages

The branch is divided into a northern and a southern group. The northern languages comprise Tundra and Forest Nenets, the most widely spoken Samoyedic languages; Enets (Tundra and Forest); Nganasan, the northernmost Uralic language, spoken on the Taymyr Peninsula; and the extinct Yurats. The southern group includes Selkup, the only southern language with a significant speaker community, and the extinct Kamas (Kamassian) and Mator. Speakers traditionally practised reindeer herding, hunting and fishing.

Status

Nearly all Samoyedic languages are endangered, with intergenerational transmission broken or weakening under pressure from Russian; Nganasan and Enets have only a few hundred or fewer speakers, most of them elderly. Nenets has official status in the Nenets and Yamalo-Nenets autonomous okrugs and is used in regional media and schooling. Linguistically, the Samoyedic languages are notable for features such as extensive consonant gradation-like alternations and a three-way number distinction including a productive dual.
