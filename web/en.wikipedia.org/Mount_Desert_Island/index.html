Mount Desert Island (often abbreviated MDI) is the largest island off the coast of the U.S. state of Maine, with an area of about 280 km² (108 sq mi). It is the sixth-largest island in the contiguous United States. The island is home to most of Acadia National Park and the towns of Bar Harbor, Mount Desert, Southwest Harbor and Tremont.

History

The Wabanaki peoples inhabited the island for thousands of years before European contact. French explorer Samuel de Champlain named it Île des Monts Déserts — "island of the bare mountains" — in 1604, after its treeless summits. In the 19th century the island became a summer retreat for wealthy families ("rusticators") including the Rockefellers, Morgans and Vanderbilts. John D. Rockefeller Jr. financed the network of carriage roads and stone bridges that still crosses the island. In 1947 a great fire burned much of the eastern side of the island and destroyed many grand summer cottages.

Geography

Cadillac Mountain, at 466 m (1,530 ft), is the highest point on the U.S. Atlantic coast north of Brazil and one of the first places in the United States to see the sunrise. The island is split by Somes Sound, a fjard that reaches almost across it. Granite mountains, spruce-fir forest, lakes and a rugged pink-granite coastline characterize the landscape.
