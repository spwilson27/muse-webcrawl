Compulsory purchase order

Compulsory purchase enables land assembly for public projects

A compulsory purchase order (CPO) is a legal function in the United Kingdom and Ireland that allows certain bodies, such as local authorities and infrastructure agencies, to obtain land or property without the consent of the owner, in return for compensation. It is the equivalent of eminent domain in the United States and expropriation in other jurisdictions.

Procedure

An acquiring authority resolves to make a CPO under an enabling Act of Parliament, serves notice on affected owners and occupiers, and submits the order for confirmation by the relevant Secretary of State or devolved minister. Objectors are entitled to be heard at a public inquiry or hearing conducted by an inspector before the order is confirmed, modified, or rejected. Once confirmed, the authority may take possession, typically through a notice to treat and notice of entry, or a general vesting declaration.

Compensation

Owners are entitled to compensation based on the open market value of the land under the "no-scheme principle" — disregarding any increase or decrease in value caused by the scheme itself — together with disturbance payments and, in many cases, statutory loss payments. Disputes over compensation are determined by the Upper Tribunal (Lands Chamber) in England and Wales.

Uses

CPOs are used for road and rail schemes, regeneration projects, housing, utilities, and major events infrastructure, such as land assembly for the London 2012 Olympic Park. Human rights considerations under Article 1 of Protocol 1 of the European Convention on Human Rights require that compulsory acquisition be proportionate and in the public interest.
