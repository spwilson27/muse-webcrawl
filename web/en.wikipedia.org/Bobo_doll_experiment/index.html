Bobo doll experiment

The Bobo doll experiment (or experiments) is the collective name for a series of experiments performed by psychologist Albert Bandura in 1961 and 1963 to study children's behaviour after watching an adult model act aggressively towards a Bobo doll, a toy doll with a rounded bottom that returns to an upright position when knocked down. The studies are often cited as early evidence for observational learning and were a cornerstone of Bandura's social learning theory.

The 1961 experiment

Bandura and his colleagues Dorothea Ross and Sheila Ross tested 36 boys and 36 girls between the ages of three and six from the Stanford University nursery school. Children were assigned to one of three conditions: an aggressive model condition, a non-aggressive model condition, or a control condition with no model.

In the aggressive condition, an adult model punched, kicked, struck with a mallet, and verbally abused the inflatable doll. In the non-aggressive condition, the model quietly assembled tinker toys and ignored the doll. Afterwards, each child was placed in a room with toys including a Bobo doll and was observed behind a one-way mirror.

Findings

Children who had observed the aggressive model imitated many of the model's novel aggressive acts, both physical and verbal, far more than children in the other conditions. Boys imitated physical aggression more than girls, and children were more likely to imitate a same-sex model. The 1963 follow-up studies showed that filmed and cartoon models could produce similar effects, and that seeing the model rewarded or punished (vicarious reinforcement) influenced whether children performed the learned behaviour.

Significance and criticism

The experiments helped shift psychology away from strict behaviorism toward cognitive accounts of learning. Critics have raised ethical concerns about encouraging aggression in children, questioned whether hitting a doll designed to be knocked over measures true aggression, and noted methodological issues such as demand characteristics and the short-term nature of the observations.
