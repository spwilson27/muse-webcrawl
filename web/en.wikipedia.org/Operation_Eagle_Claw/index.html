Operation Eagle Claw was a failed United States military operation ordered by President Jimmy Carter on 24 April 1980 to rescue 52 American diplomats and citizens held hostage at the U.S. embassy in Tehran, Iran. The complex, multi-service mission collapsed at its first staging area in the Iranian desert, and eight American servicemen were killed. The failure humiliated the Carter administration and led to major reforms in U.S. special operations.

Background

On 4 November 1979, Iranian students seized the American embassy in Tehran, beginning the 444-day Iran hostage crisis. After months of fruitless diplomacy, Carter authorised a rescue mission planned over the winter by a joint task force under Major General James Vaught, with Delta Force under Colonel Charles Beckwith assigned to assault the embassy compound.

The mission

The plan required eight Navy RH-53D helicopters, launched from the carrier USS Nimitz, to rendezvous at a remote desert strip code-named Desert One with Air Force transports carrying Delta Force and fuel. A minimum of six helicopters was required to proceed. En route, the formation flew into an unexpected dust storm (a haboob); two helicopters were lost to mechanical problems and disorientation, and a third developed a hydraulic failure at Desert One, leaving only five operational. The mission was aborted.

During the withdrawal, one helicopter repositioning in the darkness collided with a fuel-laden EC-130 transport. The resulting fire killed five airmen and three Marines and destroyed both aircraft. The remaining force evacuated on the other transports, abandoning the helicopters—along with classified documents that Iran later recovered and publicised.

Aftermath

The Holloway Commission investigation criticised planning, compartmentalisation, and the ad hoc joint command structure. The disaster directly spurred the creation of the U.S. Special Operations Command (USSOCOM) in 1987 and the dedicated special-operations aviation unit that became the 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment. The hostages were ultimately released on 20 January 1981, minutes after Ronald Reagan's inauguration, under the Algiers Accords.
