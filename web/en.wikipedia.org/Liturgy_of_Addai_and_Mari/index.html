Liturgy of Addai and Mari

The Liturgy of Addai and Mari is an ancient East Syriac anaphora (eucharistic prayer) used in the Church of the East tradition, principally by the Assyrian Church of the East and the Chaldean Catholic Church. It is attributed to Addai, a disciple of the apostle Thomas, and Mari, both regarded as founders of the church in Mesopotamia.

Antiquity

Scholars consider the core of the anaphora to be very old, possibly dating to the 3rd century, making it one of the oldest eucharistic prayers still in use. It is normally celebrated in Syriac.

Distinctive feature

The anaphora is famous because, in its traditional form, it does not contain a coherent recitation of the Words of Institution ("This is my body…"). In 2001 the Holy See formally recognised the validity of the anaphora as used by the Assyrian Church of the East, noting that the words of consecration are present in a dispersed, euchological form — a landmark in Catholic–Assyrian ecumenical relations.
