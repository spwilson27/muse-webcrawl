Northeast China

Northeast China (Chinese: 东北; pinyin: Dōngběi) is a geographical region of China, usually defined as the three provinces of Liaoning, Jilin, and Heilongjiang, corresponding broadly to the historical region of Manchuria. It borders Russia, North Korea, and Mongolia.

Ice sculptures at the Harbin Ice and Snow Festival

History

The region was home to peoples including the Jurchen, whose descendants, the Manchus, founded the Qing dynasty that ruled China from 1644 to 1912. In the early 20th century Northeast China was contested by Russia and Japan; Japan's puppet state of Manchukuo (1932-1945) industrialized the region. After 1949 it became the People's Republic's first heavy-industry base, built around state-owned steel, coal, oil (Daqing field), and machinery enterprises.

Economy and challenges

Market reforms in the 1990s hit the region's state sector hard, producing the "rust belt" decline sometimes called the "Northeast phenomenon". The fertile black-soil plains nevertheless make it China's grain basket, producing much of the country's corn and soybeans. Major cities include Shenyang, Dalian, Changchun, and Harbin, the last famous for its winter ice festival. The region has experienced population decline in recent censuses.
