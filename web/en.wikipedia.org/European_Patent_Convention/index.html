European Patent Convention

Signed5 October 1973, Munich
Effective7 October 1977
Parties39 contracting states
Short nameEPC

The European Patent Convention (EPC), also known as the Convention on the Grant of European Patents, is a multilateral treaty instituting the European Patent Organisation and providing an autonomous legal system under which European patents are granted. It was signed in Munich on 5 October 1973 and entered into force on 7 October 1977.

System

The EPC establishes a single, centralised examination procedure: an applicant files one application with the European Patent Office (EPO), and if granted, obtains a bundle of national patents in the contracting states designated. The Convention harmonises substantive patent law — patentability requirements (novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability), exclusions (such as discoveries, scientific theories, and methods of medical treatment), and the 20-year patent term — while enforcement and revocation largely remain matters for national courts, subject to central opposition at the EPO within nine months of grant.

Revision

A major revision, known as EPC 2000, entered into force on 13 December 2007, updating the Convention on matters including patentability of medical uses and the hierarchy of the Convention's texts. The EPC is distinct from European Union law, and the European Patent Organisation includes non-EU states such as Switzerland, Norway, Turkey, and the United Kingdom.
