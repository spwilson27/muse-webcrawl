Peace symbols


The peace symbol designed for the British nuclear disarmament movement in 1958.

A number of peace symbols have been used in many cultures and contexts. The best known is the circle-and-lines symbol designed in 1958 by Gerald Holtom for the British Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament (CND), combining the semaphore signals for the letters N and D within a circle. It spread worldwide during the 1960s, especially in connection with the counterculture and anti–Vietnam War movements.

Other symbols

The dove, often shown carrying an olive branch, has ancient associations with peace and was popularised in the 20th century by Pablo Picasso's lithograph La Colombe (1949). The olive branch itself signified peace in ancient Greece and Rome. The rainbow flag, used by the Italian peace movement from 1961, bears the word pace. The broken rifle is the emblem of War Resisters' International, and the white poppy, introduced in Britain in 1933, is worn as a pacifist alternative or supplement to the red remembrance poppy.

The V sign

The palm-outward V sign came to signify peace during the Vietnam War era; with the palm inward it carries a different, insulting meaning in some Commonwealth countries.
