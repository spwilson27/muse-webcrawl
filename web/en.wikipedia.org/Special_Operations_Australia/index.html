Special Operations Australia

Special Operations Australia was the name adopted during World War II by the Australian Services Reconnaissance Department (SRD), a joint Allied special operations unit modelled on Britain's Special Operations Executive (SOE). It conducted clandestine reconnaissance, raiding and guerrilla operations behind Japanese lines in the South West Pacific, including in Borneo, Timor, New Guinea, the Netherlands East Indies and Malaya. It also operated under the cover names "Z Special Unit" for many of its operations.

Origins

The unit was created in 1942 after the fall of Singapore, initially as the Inter-Allied Services Department, and renamed the Services Reconnaissance Department under Allied Intelligence Bureau control. The British SOE's Australian mission, led by officers including G. Egerton Mott, helped establish its training schools at locations such as Z Experimental Station at Cairns and Camp Z at Zareba in Queensland. Ivan Lyon, who led the daring Operation Jaywick raid on Singapore Harbour in 1943, was one of its best-known officers.

Operations

Parties were inserted by submarine, aircraft, parachute or small craft ("folboats" and country craft disguised as local vessels, such as the famous Krait). Operations included Jaywick (1943) and Rimau (1944) against Singapore shipping — Rimau ended with all its members killed or executed — and extensive reconnaissance and coastwatching work ahead of Allied landings, including in support of the Borneo campaign in 1945 (Operations Agas and Semut in North Borneo, which armed and organised local guerrillas against the Japanese).

Legacy

Many operations remained secret for decades; the unit's records were partly destroyed after the war and its members were bound by secrecy agreements. The SRD is a forerunner of modern Australian special forces, and its history has been progressively documented by historians and the Australian War Memorial.
