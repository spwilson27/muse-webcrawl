Air raid shelter

An air raid shelter, or air-raid shelter, is a structure for the protection of non-combatants as well as combatants against enemy attacks from the air. They are similar to bunkers in many regards, although they are not designed to defend against ground attack (but many have been used as defensive structures in such situations).

World War II

During the Second World War, Britain distributed Anderson shelters — corrugated steel structures partly buried in gardens — and Morrison shelters used indoors as reinforced tables. London Underground stations served as deep shelters during the Blitz, despite initial official reluctance. In Germany, large concrete flak towers and public bunkers protected city populations. Communal and street shelters were built in many countries.

Cold War and later

With the advent of nuclear weapons, shelters evolved into fallout shelters designed to protect against radioactive debris. Switzerland famously maintained shelter places for its entire population. Air raid shelters remain in use in some conflict regions, and modern building codes in some countries require protected spaces in new construction.
