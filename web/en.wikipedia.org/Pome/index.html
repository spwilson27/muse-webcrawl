Pome

In botany, a pome is a type of accessory fruit produced by flowering plants in the subtribe Malinae of the rose family (Rosaceae). The edible flesh of a pome is derived not from the ovary wall but mainly from the hypanthium — the floral tube formed by the fused bases of the sepals, petals, and stamens — which swells as the fruit matures. The true fruit is the papery or cartilaginous core containing the seeds.

Examples

The best-known pomes are the apple and the pear. Other examples include quince, loquat, medlar, hawthorn, rowan (mountain ash), serviceberry, and the fruits of cotoneaster and pyracantha. Because the bulk of the fruit is accessory tissue, the apple "core" — a leathery endocarp enclosing the seeds — represents the botanical fruit proper, derived from the inferior ovary typical of the group.

Terminology

The word comes from the Latin pomum, "fruit" or "apple," which also underlies the French pomme. In everyday usage "pome fruit" is a horticultural and trade term covering apples, pears, and their close relatives grown commercially.
