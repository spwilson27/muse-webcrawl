Bindusara

Reignc. 297 – c. 273 BCE
PredecessorChandragupta Maurya
SuccessorAshoka
DynastyMaurya

Bindusara (c. 320 BCE – c. 273 BCE) was the second emperor of the Mauryan Empire of ancient India. The son of Chandragupta Maurya, the empire's founder, Bindusara consolidated and maintained the vast territory he inherited, stretching from Bengal to the Deccan.

Reign

Bindusara is known to Greek historians as Amitraghata ("slayer of enemies"). He maintained diplomatic relations with the Hellenistic world; the Seleucid king Antiochus I sent an ambassador, Deimachus, to his court at Pataliputra. According to tradition, Bindusara asked Antiochus for sweet wine, dried figs, and a Greek sophist.

Succession

Bindusara was succeeded by his son Ashoka, who became one of India's most celebrated rulers. The minister Chanakya, who had served Chandragupta, is also said to have served Bindusara in the early part of his reign.
