Lorenzo Valla

Lorenzo Valla (c. 1407 – 1457), Italian Renaissance humanist.

Lorenzo Valla (Italian: [loˈrɛntso ˈvalla]; c. 1407 – 1 August 1457) was an Italian Renaissance humanist, rhetorician, educator and scholar. He is best known for his textual analysis that proved that the Donation of Constantine was a forgery.

Life and works

Valla was born in Rome to a family from Piacenza. He taught rhetoric at the University of Pavia, travelled among Italian courts, and from 1435 served King Alfonso V of Aragon in Naples. In 1448 he returned to Rome and became a papal secretary under Pope Nicholas V.

His most famous work, written in 1440 while in Alfonso's service, is On the Donation of Constantine (De falso credita et ementita Constantini Donatione declamatio), which demonstrated through linguistic anachronisms and historical errors that the document by which the Emperor Constantine supposedly granted the Papacy temporal power over the western Roman Empire could not have been written in the fourth century. His other works include Elegantiae linguae Latinae (Elegances of the Latin Language), which promoted classical Latin usage; De voluptate (On Pleasure), a dialogue on ethics; and critical annotations on the New Testament that anticipated Erasmus.

Legacy

Valla's philological method — criticizing texts by their language and historical context — is regarded as a foundation of modern textual criticism and historical scholarship.
