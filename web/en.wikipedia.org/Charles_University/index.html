Charles University (Czech: Univerzita Karlova; Latin: Universitas Carolina) is a public research university in Prague, Czech Republic. Founded in 1348 by Emperor Charles IV, it is the oldest university in Central Europe and one of the oldest universities in the world in continuous operation.

History

Modelled on the universities of Paris and Bologna, Charles University opened with four faculties — theology, law, medicine, and the arts. In 1409 the Decree of Kutná Hora shifted voting power to the Czech "nation," prompting German masters and students to leave and found Leipzig University; Jan Hus served as rector in this reforming era. The university's Carolinum building has been its seat since the 1380s. After 1620 the university was merged with the Jesuit Clementinum academy; in 1882 it split into Czech and German universities, and the German university was closed in 1945.

The university today

Charles University now comprises 17 faculties, mostly in Prague with some in Hradec Králové and Plzeň, educating around 50,000 students. Notable alumni include Jan Hus, Franz Kafka, Nikola Tesla (who studied briefly), and several Czech presidents. It is generally ranked the leading university in the Czech Republic.
