Proxemics

Proxemics is the study of human use of space and the effects that population density has on behaviour, communication, and social interaction. The term was introduced by anthropologist Edward T. Hall in 1963, and developed in his 1966 book The Hidden Dimension.

Interpersonal distances

Hall identified four zones of interpersonal distance in Western cultures: intimate distance (up to about 0.45 m), personal distance (0.45–1.2 m), social distance (1.2–3.6 m), and public distance (beyond 3.6 m). Each zone is associated with different kinds of interaction, from close personal contact to formal public speaking.

Cultural variation

Preferred distances vary significantly between cultures: people in many Latin American, Middle Eastern, and Mediterranean cultures habitually stand closer during conversation than people in Northern European and East Asian cultures. Violating expected distances can produce discomfort or be interpreted as intimacy, aggression, or coldness.

Applications

Proxemics informs architecture, office and urban design, human–computer interaction, and virtual reality, where avatars' spatial behaviour affects users' sense of presence and comfort.
