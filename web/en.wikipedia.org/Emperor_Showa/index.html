Hirohito (29 April 1901 – 7 January 1989), posthumously known as Emperor Shōwa, was the 124th emperor of Japan, reigning from 1926 until his death in 1989 — the longest documented reign of any Japanese emperor. His reign spanned Japan's militarist expansion, the Second World War, the Allied occupation, and the postwar economic miracle.

Emperor Hirohito (the Shōwa Emperor)

Early life and accession

Hirohito was the eldest son of Crown Prince Yoshihito (later Emperor Taishō). He was the first Japanese crown prince to travel abroad, touring Europe in 1921, and served as regent for his ailing father from 1921 until succeeding him on 25 December 1926, taking the era name Shōwa ("enlightened peace").

War years

During the 1930s Japan expanded into Manchuria (1931) and China (1937), and in 1941 attacked Pearl Harbor, bringing the Pacific War. The extent of Hirohito's personal responsibility for wartime decisions remains debated among historians; as head of state and supreme commander he sanctioned military operations, while defenders note the constraints of the Meiji constitutional system. In August 1945, after the atomic bombings and Soviet entry into the war, Hirohito broke a deadlocked cabinet to accept the Potsdam Declaration, announcing the surrender in his first radio broadcast on 15 August 1945.

Postwar reign

Unlike other wartime leaders, Hirohito was not tried for war crimes; General MacArthur retained him as a symbol of continuity during the occupation. In the 1946 "Humanity Declaration" he renounced the notion of the emperor as a living god. Under the 1947 constitution he became "the symbol of the State and of the unity of the People", with no political power. He toured the country, pursued marine biology — publishing research on hydrozoans — and in 1975 made a state visit to the United States.

Death

Hirohito died of duodenal cancer on 7 January 1989. His funeral drew representatives from around the world, and he was succeeded by his son Akihito. The question of his wartime role continues to be discussed in Japan and abroad.
