Photojournalism

Photojournalism is journalism that uses images to tell a news story. Practised by photojournalists for newspapers, magazines and agencies, it aims to present honest, impartial and timely visual reporting, governed by ethical standards that prohibit staging or manipulating news images.

History

Printed news photographs began with halftone reproduction in the late 19th century. The genre matured with illustrated magazines such as Life and the work of agencies like Magnum Photos (founded 1947). Figures including Robert Capa, Dorothea Lange and Henri Cartier-Bresson defined its visual language; Capa's dictum "If your pictures aren't good enough, you're not close enough" remains famous.

Practice and ethics

Photojournalists must be technically skilled, fast and observant, and are expected to add nothing to and remove nothing from a news scene. Organisations such as the National Press Photographers Association maintain codes of ethics. Competitions including World Press Photo recognise excellence in the field.
