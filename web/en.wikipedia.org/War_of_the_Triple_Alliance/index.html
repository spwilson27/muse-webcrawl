The War of the Triple Alliance, also known as the Paraguayan War, was a South American war fought from 1864 to 1870, in which Paraguay under President Francisco Solano López fought against an alliance of Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay. It is the deadliest and bloodiest inter-state war in Latin American history, and it devastated Paraguay, which lost a large portion of its prewar male population and substantial territory to Brazil and Argentina.

Causes and course

The war grew out of regional rivalries in the Río de la Plata basin: Paraguay intervened in Uruguay's civil war, seized the Brazilian steamer Marquês de Olinda, and invaded Brazil's Mato Grosso in December 1864, then attacked Argentina's Corrientes province in 1865 after being refused passage. Brazil, Argentina, and the new Uruguayan government signed the Treaty of the Triple Alliance in May 1865. Allied forces won the naval Battle of Riachuelo (1865), repelled the Paraguayan offensives, and advanced into Paraguay after the bloody victories at Tuyutí (1866) and the fall of the fortress of Humaitá (1868). Asunción was occupied in January 1869, but López refused to surrender and waged a guerrilla campaign until he was killed at the Battle of Cerro Corá on 1 March 1870.

Consequences

Estimates of Paraguayan deaths vary widely, but the country may have lost well over half its prewar population, including the great majority of adult males, as well as about a quarter of its territory. Paraguay was occupied by Brazil until 1876. The war consolidated the nation-states of the region and remains a defining trauma in Paraguayan national memory.
