Amazing Stories

Amazing Stories is an American science fiction magazine launched in April 1926 by Hugo Gernsback's Experimenter Publishing. It was the first magazine devoted solely to science fiction. Before Amazing, science fiction stories had appeared in other magazines, including some published by Gernsback, but Amazing helped define and launch a new genre of pulp fiction.

History

Gernsback called the genre "scientifiction" and filled early issues with reprints of Jules Verne, H. G. Wells and Edgar Allan Poe alongside new work. The magazine published early stories by Jack Williamson, John W. Campbell and E. E. "Doc" Smith, and printed the first appearances of Buck Rogers and the Skylark of Space series. Gernsback lost control of the magazine in 1929; it passed through several publishers, including Ziff-Davis, and enjoyed a respected period under editor Cele Goldsmith in the early 1960s. The Hugo Award for Best Professional Magazine went to Amazing's rivals, but the awards themselves were named after Gernsback. After decades of intermittent publication, the title was revived as an online and print magazine in the 2010s.

Influence

Amazing Stories is credited with creating science fiction fandom: Gernsback printed readers' addresses in the letters column, enabling fans to correspond and organise. The magazine's covers, many painted by Frank R. Paul, established much of the visual iconography of early science fiction. The name was also used for the 1985 television anthology series created by Steven Spielberg, and for a 2020 Apple TV+ revival.
