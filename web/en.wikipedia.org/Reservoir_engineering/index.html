Reservoir engineering

Reservoir engineering is a branch of petroleum engineering that applies scientific principles to the drainage problems arising during the development and production of oil and gas reservoirs, combined with economics to maximise the hydrocarbon recovery of a field.

Branch ofPetroleum engineering
Key tasksReserves estimation, production forecasting, enhanced recovery

Overview

Reservoir engineers work with geologists, geophysicists and production engineers to build models of how fluids (oil, gas and water) flow through porous rock. They estimate the volume of hydrocarbons originally in place, predict production rates over the life of the field, and design development plans — well locations, injection schemes and artificial lift — that recover as much of the resource as is economically feasible.

Methods

Core methods include material-balance calculations, decline-curve analysis, well-test analysis, reservoir simulation (numerical models divided into grid cells), and history matching, in which a model is adjusted until it reproduces observed production history. Modern practice relies heavily on three-dimensional geological models and stochastic (probabilistic) estimates of reserves.

Enhanced oil recovery

When primary depletion and waterflooding leave oil behind, reservoir engineers evaluate enhanced oil recovery (EOR) techniques such as gas injection, chemical flooding, and thermal methods like steam injection.
