Anterior pituitary

The anterior pituitary (also called the adenohypophysis or pars anterior) is a major organ of the endocrine system, the anterior lobe of the pituitary gland. Together with the posterior pituitary it forms the pituitary gland (hypophysis), which in humans sits in the sella turcica at the base of the brain, below the hypothalamus.

Hormones

The anterior pituitary synthesizes and secretes several key hormones under the control of hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting hormones delivered by the hypophyseal portal system: growth hormone (GH), prolactin (PRL), adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH).

Development and disorders

Embryologically, the anterior pituitary develops from Rathke's pouch, an invagination of the oral ectoderm, unlike the posterior pituitary which derives from neural tissue. Disorders include pituitary adenomas, which may over-secrete hormones (as in acromegaly, caused by excess GH, or Cushing's disease, caused by excess ACTH), and hypopituitarism, in which hormone production is deficient.
