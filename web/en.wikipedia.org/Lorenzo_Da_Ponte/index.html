Lorenzo Da Ponte

Lorenzo Da Ponte (1749–1838)

Lorenzo Da Ponte (born Emanuele Conegliano; 10 March 1749 – 17 August 1838) was an Italian, later American, opera librettist, poet and Roman Catholic priest. He wrote the libretti for 28 operas by 11 composers, including three of Mozart's most celebrated works: The Marriage of Figaro (1786), Don Giovanni (1787) and Così fan tutte (1790).

Life in Europe

Born into a Jewish family in Ceneda (now Vittorio Veneto), he took the name Lorenzo Da Ponte on baptism, was ordained a priest, and taught literature while writing poetry. Banished from Venice for his writings and lifestyle, he moved to Vienna, where Emperor Joseph II appointed him poet to the Italian theatre. There he collaborated with Salieri and Martín y Soler as well as Mozart. After Joseph's death he lost his post, worked in London as a librettist and bookseller, and accumulated heavy debts.

America

In 1805 Da Ponte emigrated to the United States to escape his creditors. He ran a grocery and book business, gave Italian lessons, and in 1825 became the first professor of Italian literature at Columbia College (now Columbia University) in New York. He championed Italian opera in America, helped stage the first American performance of Don Giovanni (1826), and raised funds for an opera house. He published a celebrated memoir and died in New York in 1838.
