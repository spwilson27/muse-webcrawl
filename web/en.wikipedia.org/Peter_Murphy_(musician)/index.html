Peter John Joseph Murphy (born 11 July 1957) is an English singer and songwriter, best known as the lead vocalist of the post-punk band Bauhaus and for a long solo career. With his deep baritone voice, gaunt appearance and theatrical stagecraft, he became an iconic figure of the gothic rock movement and is often dubbed the "Godfather of Goth".

Bauhaus

Murphy formed Bauhaus in Northampton in 1978 with Daniel Ash, David J and Kevin Haskins. Their 1979 debut single "Bela Lugosi's Dead", a nine-minute dub-influenced track, became a defining anthem of gothic rock. The band released four albums — In the Flat Field (1980), Mask (1981), The Sky's Gone Out (1982) and Burning from the Inside (1983) — and scored a UK top-20 hit with a cover of David Bowie's "Ziggy Stardust" before splitting in 1983. Bauhaus reunited for tours in 1998 and 2005–2008, releasing the album Go Away White (2008), and again in 2019–2022.

Solo career

After a brief collaboration with Mick Karn in Dalis Car (The Waking Hour, 1984), Murphy launched a solo career with Should the World Fail to Fall Apart (1986). Deep (1989) produced his biggest US hit, "Cuts You Up", which topped Billboard's Modern Rock chart. Later albums include Holy Smoke (1992), Cascade (1995), Dust (2002), influenced by Turkish music following his move to Istanbul, Ninth (2011) and Lion (2014).

Personal life

Murphy has lived in Istanbul, Turkey, for many years and converted to Islam (Sufism). In August 2019 he suffered a heart attack before a New York residency but recovered and resumed performing. He has continued to tour Bauhaus material and his solo catalogue extensively.
