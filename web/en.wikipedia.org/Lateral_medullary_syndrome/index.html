Lateral medullary syndrome

Lateral medullary syndrome, also called Wallenberg syndrome or posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA) syndrome, is a neurological condition caused by a stroke in the lateral part of the medulla oblongata, usually from occlusion of the vertebral artery or PICA.

Features

The classic picture is 'crossed': loss of pain and temperature sensation on the ipsilateral face and contralateral body, with ipsilateral Horner syndrome, ataxia, vertigo and nystagmus, dysphagia and hoarseness from nucleus ambiguus involvement, and hiccups. Motor power is characteristically spared.

History

Adolf Wallenberg described the clinical syndrome in 1895 and confirmed the lesion at autopsy in 1901.
