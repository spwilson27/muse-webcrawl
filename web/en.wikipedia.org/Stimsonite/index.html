Stimsonite

Stimsonite is a registered name for a type of road reflector — a retroreflective pavement marker stud — developed in the United States in the 20th century. Stimsonite markers were among the earliest widely installed raised pavement markers used to improve night-time visibility of lane lines.

Background

Raised pavement markers supplement painted markings, which are easily obscured by rain: protruding above the water film, their lenses reflect vehicle headlights back to drivers. Stimsonite-branded markers, produced by the Stimsonite Corporation (later part of Amerace and subsequently other owners), used glass or plastic prismatic reflectors set in metal or plastic housings anchored to the pavement.

Design issues

Non-depressible markers can be damaged by snowploughs in cold climates, leading to recessed and depressible (spring-loaded) designs. Modern standards for colour (white, yellow, red, blue for fire hydrants, green for emergency access) govern successors of the Stimsonite markers, which are also known generically as Botts' dots in parts of the United States.
