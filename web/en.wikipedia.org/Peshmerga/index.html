Peshmerga

The Peshmerga (Kurdish: پێشمەرگە, Pêşmerge, "those who face death") is the official military force of the Kurdistan Region, an autonomous region of Iraq. The name has been applied to Kurdish fighters since the early 20th century and carries deep symbolic weight in Kurdish national identity.

History

Kurdish armed resistance to successive states ruling Kurdistan long predates the modern force. The modern Peshmerga developed through the revolts led by Mustafa Barzani and the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) from the 1940s onward, fighting the Iraqi government in a series of wars and uprisings. After the 1975 Algiers Agreement collapsed external support, the movement fragmented, and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK) formed its own forces. During the Iran–Iraq War and the Anfal campaign of the late 1980s, Peshmerga units operated as guerrillas in the mountains.

Following the 1991 Gulf War and the establishment of a no-fly zone, the Kurds gained de facto autonomy; a civil war between KDP and PUK forces in the mid-1990s, however, split the Peshmerga into rival commands — a division whose legacy persists, with many brigades still answerable to party rather than the unified ministry.

War against the Islamic State

When the Islamic State (ISIS) swept across northern Iraq in 2014 and the Iraqi army collapsed in Mosul, Peshmerga forces defended a front of roughly a thousand kilometres, protecting Erbil, Kirkuk and millions of displaced people. Supported by coalition airpower, they retook territory including Sinjar and parts of the Nineveh plain, and played a supporting role in the 2017 battle for Mosul. Female Peshmerga units received particular international attention.

Organisation and reform

Estimates of strength vary widely, commonly between 150,000 and 200,000 including reserves. Since 2017, the United States and coalition partners have pressed for reform: unifying party-affiliated brigades under the Ministry of Peshmerga Affairs, standardising pay and training, and professionalising command.
