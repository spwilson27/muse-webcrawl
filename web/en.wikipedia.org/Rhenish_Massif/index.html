Rhenish Massif

The Rhenish Massif (German: Rheinisches Schiefergebirge, "Rhenish Slate Mountains") is a low mountain region in western Germany and parts of Belgium, Luxembourg and France. It consists of ancient uplifted Palaeozoic rocks dissected by the Rhine, Moselle, Lahn and Ruhr valleys.

Geography

The massif is divided by the Rhine gorge. West of the river lie the Eifel and the Ardennes (extending into Belgium and France) and the Hunsrück south of the Moselle. East of the Rhine are the Westerwald, the Taunus, the Siegerland, the Bergisches Land and the Sauerland, whose highest point, the Langenberg, reaches 843 metres.

Geology

The rocks — largely Devonian slates, greywackes and quartzites — were folded during the Variscan orogeny in the Carboniferous. Tertiary volcanism shaped parts of the Eifel, whose maar lakes are geologically young. The region is known for slate mining, mineral springs and wine-growing on the steep slate slopes of the Moselle and Middle Rhine.
