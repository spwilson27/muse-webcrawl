Philadelphia Eagles

The Philadelphia Eagles are a professional American football team based in Philadelphia. The Eagles compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the National Football Conference (NFC) East division. The team plays its home games at Lincoln Financial Field and is owned by Jeffrey Lurie.

History

The franchise was founded in 1933, taking its name from the blue eagle emblem of the National Recovery Administration. The Eagles won consecutive NFL championships in 1948 and 1949 under coach Greasy Neale, led by running back Steve Van Buren, and a third in 1960 under Buck Shaw, the only team to defeat Vince Lombardi's Packers in a championship game. Merged briefly with the Pittsburgh Steelers as the "Steagles" during World War II manpower shortages, the club later reached the Super Bowl in the 1980 season (losing Super Bowl XV to the Oakland Raiders) and again in 2004 under coach Andy Reid (losing Super Bowl XXXIX to the New England Patriots).

Super Bowl victories

On 4 February 2018, the Eagles won Super Bowl LII, defeating the Patriots 41–33 behind backup quarterback and game MVP Nick Foles and the famous "Philly Special" trick play. In February 2025 the Eagles won Super Bowl LIX, beating the Kansas City Chiefs 40–22, with quarterback Jalen Hurts named MVP.

Rivalries and culture

The Eagles' fiercest rivalries are with the Dallas Cowboys, New York Giants, and Washington Commanders. The fan base is noted for its intensity, and the fight song "Fly, Eagles Fly" is a staple of home games.
