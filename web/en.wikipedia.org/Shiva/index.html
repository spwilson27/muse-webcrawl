Shiva

Shiva is one of the principal deities of Hinduism and a member of the Trimurti, the triple deity of supreme divinity, alongside Brahma the creator and Vishnu the preserver. Within the Trimurti, Shiva is known as "the Destroyer" — the god who destroys in order to recreate. For his devotees, the Shaivites, Shiva is the Supreme Being who creates, protects, and transforms the universe. He is worshipped across India, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and the Hindu diaspora, and is one of the most complex and paradoxical figures in world religion: both ascetic and householder, benevolent and terrifying.

Iconography

Shiva is typically depicted with a third eye on his forehead — which, when opened, burns things to ashes — a crescent moon in his matted hair, the sacred river Ganga flowing from his locks, a serpent coiled around his neck, and the trident (trishula) and hourglass drum (damaru) in his hands. His body is often smeared with ash, and he wears a tiger skin. Most temples worship him aniconically as the lingam, a rounded stone representing his generative power.

Mythology

Shiva dwells as a meditating ascetic on Mount Kailash in the Himalayas. His first wife, Sati, immolated herself after her father insulted Shiva; she was reborn as Parvati, who won Shiva through devotion and austerity. Their sons are the elephant-headed Ganesha and the war god Kartikeya. Shiva drank the world-destroying poison halahala churned from the cosmic ocean, which turned his throat blue and earned him the name Neelkanth. As Nataraja, the Lord of Dance, he performs the cosmic dance of creation and destruction within a ring of fire — an image famously cast in Chola bronzes.

Worship and traditions

Shaivism is one of the largest Hindu denominations. Devotees observe Maha Shivaratri, the "Great Night of Shiva", with fasting and all-night vigils. The twelve Jyotirlinga shrines and the Char Dham and Kailash pilgrimages draw millions. In Kashmir Shaivism and Tamil Shaiva Siddhanta, philosophers developed sophisticated non-dualist theologies of Shiva, while in Java and Bali, Hinduism — including Shiva worship — spread in the first millennium CE alongside temple-building kingdoms such as Mataram.

Origins

Elements of Shiva's character descend from Rudra, a Vedic storm god, and possibly from a figure depicted on an Indus Valley seal (the so-called "Pashupati seal"), though that identification is debated. By the early centuries CE, the Puranas had assembled the mythology recognisable today.
