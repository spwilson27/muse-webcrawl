The Upper Silesian Industrial Region (Polish: Górnośląski Okręg Przemysłowy) is a large industrial region in southern Poland, centred on the city of Katowice in the historic region of Upper Silesia. It is the largest industrial district in Poland and one of the most heavily industrialized areas of Europe.

Geography and cities

The region forms a polycentric conurbation of some twenty cities and towns, including Katowice, Sosnowiec, Gliwice, Zabrze, Bytom, Ruda Śląska, Tychy, Dąbrowa Górnicza, Chorzów, and Jaworzno. Since 2018 the core cities have been organized as the Metropolis GZM (Górnośląsko-Zagłębiowska Metropolia), Poland's first statutory metropolitan union, of 41 municipalities and over two million inhabitants.

History

Industrialization began under Prussian rule in the late 18th and 19th centuries, exploiting the region's rich bituminous coal seams and deposits of zinc, lead, and silver. Coal mining and steelmaking made Upper Silesia one of the German Empire's industrial heartlands. After the First World War, a 1921 plebiscite and League of Nations settlement divided the industrial district between Germany and Poland; after 1945 the whole region was assigned to Poland and expanded under communist-era heavy industry programmes.

Economy and environment

The region historically depended on hard-coal mining, coking, and steel, producing severe air and land pollution. Since 1989 it has undergone restructuring: many mines have closed while automotive plants, logistics, and services have grown. Upper Silesia remains Poland's principal coal-mining and electricity-generating region, central to debates over the European energy transition.
