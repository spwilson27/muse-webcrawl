United States Department of Housing and Urban Development

The United States Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) is a Cabinet department of the U.S. federal government responsible for national policy and programs that address housing needs, improve and develop communities, and enforce fair housing laws. Created by President Lyndon B. Johnson in 1965, HUD administers public housing, rental assistance vouchers, Community Development Block Grants, homeless assistance programmes, and mortgage insurance through the Federal Housing Administration.

History

HUD's predecessor, the Housing and Home Finance Agency (1947), consolidated federal housing programs. President Johnson signed the HUD Act on 9 September 1965 as part of his Great Society programme, and Robert C. Weaver became the department's first Secretary — and the first African American Cabinet secretary. The Fair Housing Act of 1968, passed in the wake of Martin Luther King Jr.'s assassination, gave HUD responsibility for combating housing discrimination. Later milestones included the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974 (creating the Section 8 voucher programme and Community Development Block Grants), the replacement of distressed public housing under HOPE VI in the 1990s, and HUD's role in responding to the 2008 foreclosure crisis.

Programmes

HUD's major components include the Federal Housing Administration (FHA), which insures mortgages for first-time and low-income buyers; the Office of Public and Indian Housing, which funds about 3,300 public housing authorities serving millions of households, including the Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) programme, the largest single rental assistance programme in the country; Ginnie Mae, which guarantees mortgage-backed securities; the Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity; and programmes combating homelessness through Continuum of Care grants. HUD provides housing to more than 4.3 million low-income families.
