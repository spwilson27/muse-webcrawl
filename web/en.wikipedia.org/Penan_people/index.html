Penan people

The Penan are an indigenous people of Borneo, living principally in the interior rainforests of the Malaysian state of Sarawak and, in smaller numbers, in Brunei. Traditionally nomadic hunter-gatherers, the Penan became internationally known from the 1980s for their resistance to logging of their ancestral forests.

Traditional life

Penan groups traditionally roamed the headwaters of rivers such as the Baram and Limbang, hunting wild pig and deer with blowpipes, gathering rattan and wild sago, which provides their staple starch. Their ethic of molong—"never take more than necessary"—governed sustainable harvesting of forest resources. Most Penan have now settled in permanent longhouse villages, though many retain strong ties to the forest.

Logging resistance

From 1987, Penan communities blockaded logging roads to protest the destruction of their forests, attracting global attention, particularly through the Swiss activist Bruno Manser, who lived with the Penan in the 1980s. Land rights remain contested: Sarawak law recognises only limited native customary rights, and Penan claims to ancestral territory have been the subject of long-running disputes and litigation.
