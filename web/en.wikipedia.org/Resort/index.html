Resort

A resort is a place or commercial establishment used for recreation, relaxation, tourism, or health. The word can refer to a single self-contained hotel property, a complex of lodging and leisure facilities, or an entire town whose economy is organised around visitors.

Types

Beach resorts centre on swimming, water sports, and coastal scenery. Ski resorts provide lifts, pistes, instruction, and winter accommodation. Spa and health resorts developed around mineral springs, baths, climate, or medical treatment. Island, lake, mountain, golf, casino, and theme-park resorts organise travel around particular activities. A destination resort attempts to supply lodging, food, entertainment, and recreation on site so guests need not leave.

Facilities and business models

Resorts may include hotels, villas, restaurants, marinas, pools, conference centres, entertainment venues, and retail. Some operate on an all-inclusive price covering meals and activities; others use timeshare, condominium, membership, or conventional room charges. Seasonal demand strongly influences employment and pricing.

History

Ancient Roman baths and spas were early leisure destinations. European spa towns became fashionable in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, followed by seaside resorts as railways made coastal travel accessible. In the twentieth century, package holidays, commercial aviation, and large hotel groups produced integrated resorts in the Mediterranean, Caribbean, Southeast Asia, and elsewhere.

Impacts

Tourism can bring income, infrastructure, and employment, but resorts may strain water supplies, displace residents, privatise access to landscapes, generate waste, and expose local economies to external shocks. Contemporary planning therefore often considers environmental carrying capacity, labour conditions, cultural heritage, and benefits to surrounding communities.
