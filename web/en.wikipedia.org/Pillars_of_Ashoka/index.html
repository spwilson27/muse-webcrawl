Pillars of Ashoka

The Pillars of Ashoka are a series of monolithic columns erected across the Indian subcontinent by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka during his reign (c. 268–232 BCE). Standing up to 15 metres high, they are among the earliest stone sculptures of India and many bear inscriptions — the Edicts of Ashoka — in Brahmi script.

Description

Each pillar consists of a single shaft of polished sandstone crowned by a capital carved with animals such as lions, bulls and elephants, resting on an inverted lotus or bell-shaped base. The most famous is the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath, whose four lions were adopted as the national emblem of India in 1950, and whose abacus bears the Ashoka Chakra that appears on the Indian flag.

Locations

Surviving pillars stand at sites including Sarnath, Sanchi, Vaishali, Lauriya Nandangarh, Rampurva and Allahabad (Prayagraj). Several were moved in later centuries by rulers such as Firoz Shah Tughlaq, who transported two pillars to Delhi.
