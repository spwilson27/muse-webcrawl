Fennec fox

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Family: Canidae
Genus: Vulpes
Species: V. zerda
Conservation status: Least Concern

The fennec fox (Vulpes zerda) is a small crepuscular fox native to the deserts of North Africa, ranging from Western Sahara and Mauritania to northern Sinai. Its most distinctive feature is its unusually large ears, the largest of any fox relative to body size, which serve to dissipate heat and to hear prey moving beneath the sand. The fennec is the smallest species of fox and is well adapted to life in arid environments.

Description

The fennec weighs about 0.7–1.6 kg (1.5–3.5 lb), measures 24–41 cm in head-and-body length, and has ears 10–15 cm long. Its cream-coloured coat reflects sunlight during the day and conserves heat at night; even its paws are furred, protecting them from hot sand and giving traction. Its kidneys are adapted to restrict water loss, and it can go for long periods without free water.

Behaviour and diet

Fennecs live in small family groups of up to ten individuals, sheltering in burrows dug in sand dunes. They are omnivorous, feeding on small rodents, birds and their eggs, reptiles, insects, and fruit. Hunting is done largely by ear: the fox listens for prey underground and digs rapidly to catch it.

Conservation and human relations

The species is classified as Least Concern by the IUCN, though it is trapped for exhibition and for sale in the exotic pet trade, and its fur is used by indigenous peoples of North Africa. The fennec is the national animal of Algeria, and the Algerian national football team is nicknamed "Les Fennecs".
