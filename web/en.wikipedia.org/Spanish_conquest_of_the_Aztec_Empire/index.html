The Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire was the invasion and annexation of the Mexica (Aztec) state in central Mexico by Spanish conquistadors under Hernán Cortés, acting with tens of thousands of Indigenous allies, between 1519 and 1521. It was one of the most consequential episodes of the European colonisation of the Americas, ending with the fall of Tenochtitlan and the creation of the Spanish colony of New Spain.

Background

The Aztec Empire, ruled from the island city of Tenochtitlan in Lake Texcoco, extracted tribute from much of Mesoamerica but left subject peoples resentful — a weakness Cortés exploited. Spanish expeditions from Cuba had reached the Mexican coast in 1517–18, reporting wealthy cities inland. Cortés sailed in February 1519 with about 500 men, horses, cannon and war dogs, against the orders of Cuba's governor.

The expedition

Landing at Veracruz, Cortés founded a town and scuttled his ships to forestall retreat. His crucial assets were Indigenous allies: first the Totonacs, then the Tlaxcalans, the Aztecs' great rivals, who provided thousands of warriors, porters and supplies. Communication ran through the interpreters Jerónimo de Aguilar and La Malinche (Doña Marina), whose translation and diplomacy were central to the campaign. In November 1519 the Spanish entered Tenochtitlan as guests of the emperor Moctezuma II, whom they soon seized as a hostage.

In 1520, during Cortés's absence to defeat a rival Spanish force under Pánfilo de Narváez, his lieutenant Pedro de Alvarado massacred Aztec nobles during a festival. The city rose; Moctezuma died, and the Spanish were driven out on the disastrous retreat of La Noche Triste (30 June 1520). Smallpox, introduced by the Narváez expedition, then ravaged Tenochtitlan, killing the new emperor Cuitláhuac.

Fall of Tenochtitlan

Cortés regrouped in Tlaxcala, built thirteen brigantines to control Lake Texcoco, and laid siege to the capital in May 1521, cutting its aqueduct and food supply. After roughly three months of brutal fighting through the city's causeways and canals, the last emperor, Cuauhtémoc, was captured on 13 August 1521. Tenochtitlan was razed and rebuilt as Mexico City, capital of New Spain.

Legacy

The conquest opened central Mexico to Spanish rule; disease and exploitation then caused one of history's greatest demographic collapses. It also set patterns — alliances with Indigenous powers, encomienda grants, Catholic evangelisation — repeated across the Americas, and remains a foundational and contested event in Mexican national memory.
