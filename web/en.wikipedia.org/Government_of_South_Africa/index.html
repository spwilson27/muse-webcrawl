Government of South Africa

CountrySouth Africa
ConstitutionConstitution of South Africa (1996)
Head of state and governmentPresident
LegislatureParliament (National Assembly and National Council of Provinces)
CapitalsPretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative), Bloemfontein (judicial)

The Government of South Africa is the national government of the Republic of South Africa, structured under the Constitution of 1996 as a constitutional republic with three spheres of government—national, provincial, and local. Unusually, the functions of government are spread across three capital cities: Pretoria hosts the executive, Cape Town the legislature, and Bloemfontein the judiciary.

Executive

The President is both head of state and head of government, elected by the National Assembly from among its members for a five-year term, renewable once. The President appoints the Deputy President and Cabinet ministers, who head national departments. Since the first democratic elections in 1994, the presidency has been held by Nelson Mandela, Thabo Mbeki, Jacob Zuma, and Cyril Ramaphosa.

Legislature

Parliament consists of the National Assembly, elected by proportional representation, and the National Council of Provinces, representing the nine provinces. South Africa's nine provinces—including Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal, and the Western Cape—each have their own legislature and premier.

Judiciary

The judiciary is independent, headed by the Constitutional Court in Johannesburg, established after the end of apartheid to uphold the Bill of Rights in the 1996 Constitution. Below it sit the Supreme Court of Appeal in Bloemfontein and the High Courts.
