Path of least resistance


Water flowing downhill follows the path of least resistance

The path of least resistance is the route that offers the smallest opposition or difficulty for something moving between two points. The phrase is used both in physics and as a common metaphor in everyday language, where it describes choosing the easiest available option.

In physics

In physical systems, objects and flows tend to follow routes that minimize some quantity. Electric current divides among parallel branches in inverse proportion to their resistance, so more current flows through the branch of lower resistance. Water flows downhill along the steepest descent available. In optics, Fermat's principle states that light travels along the path requiring the least time. More generally, the principle of least action in classical mechanics describes motion as following a path for which the action is stationary.

Figurative use

As an idiom, "taking the path of least resistance" means avoiding confrontation or effort and accepting the easiest course, often with a mildly critical implication that the person is avoiding a more difficult but better course of action. The phrase is also used neutrally in fields such as hydrology, animal behavior, and technology adoption to describe how systems naturally evolve toward configurations of lower energy or effort.
