Spanish Renaissance

The Spanish Renaissance was a movement in Spanish art, literature, music and architecture that spread from Italy into Spain in the late fifteenth century and flourished during the sixteenth century, coinciding with the rise of the Spanish Empire and the beginnings of the Spanish Golden Age. Spain's political union under the Catholic Monarchs, Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile, and the wealth flowing from the Americas provided the conditions for its development.

Architecture

Spanish Renaissance architecture developed distinctive native styles. The Plateresque style, named for its silversmith-like ornament, combined Italian Renaissance motifs with Gothic and Mudéjar elements, as in the facade of the University of Salamanca. The later Purism phase favoured simpler classical forms, exemplified by the Palace of Charles V in the Alhambra at Granada, begun by Pedro Machuca. Under Philip II the austere Herrerian style prevailed, above all in the vast monastery-palace of El Escorial by Juan de Herrera.

Literature and thought

The humanist Antonio de Nebrija published the first grammar of a modern European language, the Gramática Castellana, in 1492. The dialogue novel La Celestina (1499) bridged the medieval and Renaissance worlds, while poets such as Garcilaso de la Vega naturalised Italian metres in Spanish. Erasmian humanism strongly influenced Spanish intellectual life in the early sixteenth century before the Counter-Reformation narrowed its scope. In painting, the period culminated in the work of El Greco, whose elongated, spiritual style bridged Renaissance and Mannerism.

Music

Composers such as Cristóbal de Morales, Francisco Guerrero and Tomás Luis de Victoria made Spain a leading centre of Renaissance polyphony, and the vihuela repertoire flourished at court.
