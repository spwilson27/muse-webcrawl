Origin of life

Abiogenesis
Question: How natural processes produced life from non-living matter
When: On Earth, at least 3.8 billion years ago
Key concepts: Primordial soup, RNA world, hydrothermal vents

The origin of life, or abiogenesis, is the natural process by which life arose from non-living matter, such as simple organic compounds. While the details remain uncertain, the scientific consensus is that the transition from non-living to living entities on Earth was not a single event but a process of increasing complexity involving the formation of a habitable planet, the prebiotic synthesis of organic molecules, molecular self-replication, self-assembly, autocatalysis and the emergence of cell membranes.

Timing and setting

Earth formed about 4.54 billion years ago. Evidence of life appears by at least 3.8 billion years ago, and possibly earlier. Early conditions included a reducing or neutral atmosphere, liquid water oceans, and energy sources such as lightning, ultraviolet light and volcanism. Candidate settings include shallow surface pools, the deep sea around alkaline hydrothermal vents, and volcanic hot springs.

Approaches

The 1952 Miller–Urey experiment showed that amino acids form readily when energy is applied to a mixture of simple gases and water. The RNA world hypothesis proposes that self-replicating RNA preceded DNA and proteins, since RNA can both store information and catalyse reactions. Other models emphasise metabolism first, proposing that networks of small-molecule reactions on mineral surfaces or in vent micropores preceded genetic polymers. Research continues on the synthesis of nucleotides and lipids and on assembling them into self-sustaining protocells.
