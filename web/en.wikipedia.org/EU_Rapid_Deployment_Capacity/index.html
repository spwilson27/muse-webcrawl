The EU Rapid Deployment Capacity (EU RDC) is a planned military rapid reaction force of the European Union under the Common Security and Defence Policy. It was established by the Strategic Compass, the EU's defence strategy document adopted in March 2022, and is intended to allow the Union to deploy up to 5,000 troops quickly in response to crises outside its borders.

Background

The EU had earlier created the EU Battlegroups, battalion-sized packages on standby since 2007, but these were never deployed, largely because of political and funding disagreements among member states. The chaotic evacuation from Kabul in 2021 and Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 revived the push for a more credible, modular force that could act within days.

Structure

The RDC is not a standing army: it draws on substantial forces and strategic enablers — airlift, logistics, medical support and command and control — contributed by member states, with the EU's Military Planning and Conduct Capability (MPCC) in Brussels designated as its operational headquarters. Scenarios for its use include rescue and evacuation operations, initial stabilisation, peacekeeping and support against hybrid threats. Live exercises under the MILEX series began in 2023 to build towards full operational capability.
