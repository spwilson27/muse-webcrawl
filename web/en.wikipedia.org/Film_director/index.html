A film director is a person who controls a film's artistic and dramatic aspects and visualises the screenplay while guiding the cast and crew to fulfil that vision. The director is the central creative figure in filmmaking: selecting and coaching actors, deciding camera placement and movement with the cinematographer, shaping performances, and typically supervising the edit. The extent of a director's authority varies — in auteur-driven cinema the director is considered the work's primary author, while in studio and franchise productions producers may hold greater control, including "final cut".

Role and process

In pre-production the director works on script development, casting, location scouting, storyboarding and design. During principal photography the director stages scenes, directs actors' performances and approves each take. In post-production the director collaborates with the editor on the assembly and director's cut, and oversees sound design, music and visual effects. Directors communicate a unified tone and style across departments — production design, costume, makeup and cinematography — so that thousands of individual decisions cohere into a single work.

History and authorship

Early cinema treated the director as a technician staging scenes; figures such as D. W. Griffith, Sergei Eisenstein and John Ford expanded the role into that of visual author. In the 1950s French critics of Cahiers du Cinéma developed the politique des auteurs (auteur theory), arguing that directors such as Alfred Hitchcock and Howard Hawks imprinted a personal style on studio films; the theory reshaped film criticism and the director's prestige, though it has been criticised for downplaying collaboration.

Training and representation

Directors reach the role through film school, assisting, music videos, commercials, television or independent features. In the United States the Directors Guild of America represents most working directors. Women and minority directors have historically been under-represented, a disparity documented in industry studies and the subject of continuing reform efforts.
