Animal hospital

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An animal hospital is a facility providing veterinary medicine, surgery and inpatient care for animals. Unlike a small veterinary clinic, which typically offers outpatient consultations and minor procedures, an animal hospital is equipped for hospitalisation, emergency and critical care, diagnostic imaging, laboratory testing and major surgery, often on a 24-hour basis.

Services

Typical departments include emergency and critical care, internal medicine, surgery (including orthopaedics), dentistry, dermatology, cardiology, oncology and diagnostic imaging such as radiography, ultrasound and CT scanning. Hospitals employ veterinarians, veterinary nurses or technicians, and support staff, and larger referral hospitals accept complex cases sent by primary-care practices.

Regulation and accreditation

In many countries veterinary premises are regulated by professional bodies, and voluntary accreditation schemes set standards for facilities and care — for example, the American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA) in North America and the Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons' Practice Standards Scheme in the United Kingdom. Teaching hospitals attached to veterinary schools combine treatment with training and research.
