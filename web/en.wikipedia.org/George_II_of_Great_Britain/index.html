George II (10 November 1683 – 25 October 1760) was King of Great Britain and Ireland, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg, and Elector of Hanover from 1727 until his death. He was the second British monarch of the House of Hanover and the last British king to lead an army in battle, at Dettingen in 1743.

Early life

Born in Hanover, George was the only son of the future George I. He became Prince of Wales in 1714 but quarrelled bitterly with his father, and for a time the two courts were rivals. He married Caroline of Ansbach in 1705; she became a powerful intellectual influence on him.

Reign

George II's reign was dominated by his reliance on ministers such as Robert Walpole and later Henry Pelham. Britain fought the War of the Austrian Succession and the Seven Years' War, during which it won major victories in Canada and India in the "Year of Victories" of 1759. The Jacobite rising of 1745, led by Charles Edward Stuart, was crushed at Culloden in 1746.

Death and succession

George II died suddenly at Kensington Palace in 1760 and was succeeded by his grandson George III, his eldest son Frederick, Prince of Wales, having predeceased him.
