Sense and Sensibility

AuthorJane Austen
Published1811
PublisherThomas Egerton
GenreNovel of manners

Sense and Sensibility is a novel by the English author Jane Austen, published in 1811. It was published anonymously; the title page simply stated that the work was "By a Lady". The novel follows the three Dashwood sisters — Elinor, Marianne, and Margaret — and their widowed mother, who are forced to leave their estate at Norland Park after the death of Mr Dashwood and move to a modest cottage on the property of Barton Park.

Plot

Elinor, the eldest sister, represents "sense" — reason, restraint, and social responsibility. Marianne, the middle sister, embodies "sensibility" — emotion, spontaneity, and romantic idealism. After moving to Barton, Marianne falls for the charming but unreliable John Willoughby, whose sudden departure and later marriage to a wealthy heiress leaves her heartbroken. Elinor, meanwhile, quietly suffers her own disappointment over Edward Ferrars, who is secretly engaged to another woman. Marianne eventually learns moderation through illness and reflection and accepts the steady Colonel Brandon, while Elinor is freed to marry Edward when his engagement ends.

Themes

The novel explores the tension between reason and emotion in love and life, the economic precariousness of genteel women dependent on marriage, and the social conventions of Regency England. Austen is often read as neither wholly endorsing Elinor's restraint nor condemning Marianne's feeling, but as dramatizing the costs of each extreme.

Adaptations

The novel has been adapted many times, most notably the 1995 film directed by Ang Lee from a screenplay by Emma Thompson, who also starred as Elinor, with Kate Winslet as Marianne.
