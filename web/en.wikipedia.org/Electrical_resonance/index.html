Electrical resonance

Electrical resonance occurs in an electric circuit at a particular resonant frequency when the impedances or admittances of circuit elements cancel each other. In circuits containing capacitors and inductors, resonance involves the transfer of energy between the magnetic field of the inductor and the electric field of the capacitor.

Series resonance

In a series RLC circuit, resonance occurs when the inductive reactance equals the capacitive reactance: XL = XC. The resonant frequency is f = 1/(2π√(LC)). At resonance the impedance is purely resistive and at its minimum, so the current reaches its maximum.

Parallel resonance

In a parallel resonant circuit, the impedance reaches its maximum at the resonant frequency, and the circuit is sometimes called a tank circuit. Such circuits are widely used in radio receivers to select a narrow band of frequencies.

Quality factor

The sharpness of resonance is described by the quality factor Q, the ratio of the resonant frequency to the bandwidth. High-Q circuits resonate strongly over a narrow frequency range; low-Q circuits respond over a broader range.

Applications

Resonant circuits are fundamental to radio and television tuners, filters, oscillators, and wireless power transfer systems.
