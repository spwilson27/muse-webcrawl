Tōkaidō (road)

The Tōkaidō was the most important of the Five Routes of the Edo period in Japan, connecting the shogunal capital of Edo (modern Tokyo) with Kyoto, the imperial capital, along the Pacific coast of Honshu.

Route and stations

The road ran for roughly 500 kilometres and was served by 53 post stations (shukuba), immortalised in Hiroshige's woodblock series The Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō. Travellers crossed rivers largely by ferry, as bridges were deliberately few for defensive reasons.

History

Formalised under Tokugawa Ieyasu in the early 17th century, the route carried daimyō processions travelling to Edo under the sankin-kōtai system, as well as merchants and pilgrims.

Modern legacy

The name survives in the Tōkaidō Main Line railway and the Tōkaidō Shinkansen, which follow much the same corridor.
