Christchurch

Christchurch (Māori: Ōtautahi) is the largest city in the South Island of New Zealand and the seat of the Canterbury Region. Lying on the Canterbury Plains beside the Pacific coast and the Port Hills, it is New Zealand's third-most populous city, known historically as the "Garden City" for its parks and tree-lined Avon River (Ōtākaro).

History

The area was settled by Māori iwi including Waitaha, Ngāti Māmoe, and Ngāi Tahu; Ōtautahi was an important mahinga kai (food-gathering) site. European settlement was organised by the Canterbury Association, whose first ships arrived in 1850 at Lyttelton; the city was named after Christ Church, Oxford. Christchurch became a city in 1856 and grew as the service centre for Canterbury's agricultural hinterland.

Earthquakes and rebuild

A magnitude 7.1 earthquake struck the region in September 2010, and on 22 February 2011 a magnitude 6.3 aftershock centred near the city killed 185 people and destroyed much of the central city, including the spire of ChristChurch Cathedral. A vast rebuild followed, producing landmarks such as the Transitional "Cardboard" Cathedral by Shigeru Ban, the Tūranga central library, and the Riverside Market, while large residential districts were red-zoned and demolished because of liquefaction.

City life

Hagley Park and the Botanic Gardens anchor the central city; the Arts Centre occupies the former University of Canterbury Gothic Revival buildings. Christchurch is a gateway for Antarctic programmes, hosting logistics bases for several national Antarctic expeditions at its international airport.
