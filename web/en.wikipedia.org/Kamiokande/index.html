Kamiokande

Kamiokande (Kamioka Neutrino Detection Experiment) was a pioneering neutrino observatory located 1,000 metres underground in the Mozumi Mine near Kamioka, Gifu Prefecture, Japan. Built in 1983 as a ring-imaging water Cherenkov detector — a tank of 3,000 tonnes of pure water surrounded by photomultiplier tubes — it was designed to search for proton decay but became famous for neutrino astronomy.

Scientific achievements

In February 1987 Kamiokande detected a burst of neutrinos from supernova SN 1987A in the Large Magellanic Cloud — the first neutrinos ever observed from beyond the solar system, confirming theories of stellar collapse. It also measured neutrinos from the Sun and detected the atmospheric neutrino anomaly, evidence that muon neutrinos oscillate into other flavours, implying neutrinos have mass.

Successors

Kamiokande was led by Masatoshi Koshiba, who shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Physics for the detection of cosmic neutrinos. The experiment was succeeded by the much larger Super-Kamiokande (50,000 tonnes), which began operation in 1996 and confirmed neutrino oscillation, and by KamLAND nearby; the next-generation Hyper-Kamiokande is under construction.
