Edward Theodore Riley (born October 8, 1967) is an American singer, songwriter, and record producer credited with the creation of the new jack swing genre. Riley is best known for his work with the groups Guy and Blackstreet, and for his production for artists including Keith Sweat, Bobby Brown, Michael Jackson, and Lady Gaga.

Career

Raised in Harlem, Riley was a child prodigy musician. In 1987 he co-produced Keith Sweat's debut album Make It Last Forever and, with Aaron Hall and Timmy Gatling, formed the group Guy, whose self-titled 1988 debut established new jack swing — a fusion of R&B melodies with hip hop rhythms and production. Riley produced Bobby Brown's Don't Be Cruel (1988) and co-wrote and produced Michael Jackson's Dangerous (1991), including the hit "Remember the Time".

In 1991 he founded the group Blackstreet, whose 1996 single "No Diggity" (with Dr. Dre and Queen Pen) topped the Billboard Hot 100 and won a Grammy Award. Riley also worked in K-pop in the 2010s, writing and producing for artists such as Girls' Generation, EXO, and f(x) through his work with SM Entertainment. He was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2021.
