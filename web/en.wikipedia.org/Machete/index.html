Machete

A machete is a broad blade used either as an agricultural tool similar to an axe, or in combat like a long-bladed knife. The blade is typically 30 to 45 centimetres long and usually under 3 millimetres thick. In the Spanish language, the word is possibly a diminutive form of the word macho, which was used to refer to sledgehammers.

Agricultural use

The machete is a common tool throughout the tropics and subtropics, used to cut vegetation, harvest sugarcane, open coconuts, and clear brush. Variants include the Philippine bolo, the Malay and Indonesian parang and golok, and the Caribbean cutlass. In agriculture it is often the primary tool of smallholder farmers, doubling as a general-purpose implement for farm and household.

As a weapon

Because of its ubiquity, the machete has frequently been used as an improvised weapon in conflicts and uprisings. It featured in slave revolts and independence struggles in the Caribbean and Latin America, and in modern times machetes have been notoriously used in the Rwandan genocide of 1994. The tool remains regulated as a weapon in some jurisdictions while remaining an everyday implement in many others.

Cultural significance

The machete is a symbol of agricultural labour and resistance in many cultures. In some Afro-Caribbean martial traditions, machete fencing — such as Haitian tire machèt and the Colombian esgrima criolla — developed into formal arts.
