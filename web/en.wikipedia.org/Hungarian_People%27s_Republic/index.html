Hungarian People's Republic

Summary

Existed: 1949–1989

Capital: Budapest

Government: One-party socialist republic

Ruling party: Hungarian Socialist Workers' Party (from 1956)

Key leaders: Mátyás Rákosi, János Kádár

The Hungarian People's Republic was the name of Hungary's communist state from 1949 to 1989. A satellite of the Soviet Union and a member of the Warsaw Pact, it was governed as a one-party socialist republic under the leadership of the Hungarian Working People's Party and, after 1956, the Hungarian Socialist Workers' Party.

Stalinist era and the 1956 revolution

Under Mátyás Rákosi, Hungary underwent forced collectivisation, rapid industrialisation, and political terror by the ÁVH secret police. In October 1956 a popular uprising led by reformist prime minister Imre Nagy demanded free elections and withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact. The Soviet Union crushed the revolution with military force in November 1956; Nagy was later executed, and thousands were imprisoned or fled abroad.

Kádár era and Goulash Communism

János Kádár, installed by Moscow, gradually consolidated power and introduced limited economic reforms known abroad as "Goulash Communism." The New Economic Mechanism of 1968 introduced market elements, and Hungary enjoyed the highest standard of living in the Eastern Bloc, though political freedoms remained restricted and the country accumulated heavy foreign debt in the 1980s.

Transition

In 1988–89 reform communists and opposition movements negotiated a peaceful transition. Imre Nagy was ceremonially reburied in June 1989, and on 23 October 1989 the Hungarian People's Republic was replaced by the democratic Republic of Hungary, ending four decades of communist rule.
