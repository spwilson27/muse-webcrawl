Unisphere

The Unisphere is a spherical stainless steel representation of the Earth located in Flushing Meadows Corona Park in the New York City borough of Queens. Commissioned as part of the 1964–1965 New York World's Fair, the Unisphere was donated by the United States Steel Corporation and constructed by the American Bridge Company. It was designed by landscape architect Gilmore David Clarke.

The Unisphere is one of the park's most prominent features and has become a widely recognized symbol of Queens and of New York City. It is 140 feet (43 m) high and 120 feet (37 m) in diameter, and rises on a 20-foot-high (6.1 m) tripod base. The continents are made of stainless steel, and the globe is surrounded by a circular reflecting pool with fountain jets.

History

The concept for a giant globe at the fair was developed in the early 1960s. Clarke, who had designed the layout of the earlier 1939 World's Fair at the same site, proposed a monumental sphere representing the fair's theme, "Peace Through Understanding." Construction took just over three months in 1963. During the fair, the Unisphere was lit at night and circled by a display representing the orbits of satellites.

Restoration and landmark status

The Unisphere underwent a major restoration from 1993 to 1994, during which the fountains were rebuilt and long-inoperable water jets were returned to service. It was designated a New York City landmark in 1995. The structure appears frequently in film and television productions set in New York.
