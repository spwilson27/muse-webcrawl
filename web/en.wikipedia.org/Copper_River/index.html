The Copper River or Ahtna River (Ahtna: 'Atna'tuu) is a 290-mile (470 km) river in south-central Alaska in the United States. It drains a large region of the Wrangell Mountains and Chugach Mountains into the Gulf of Alaska. It is known for its extensive delta ecosystem, as well as for its prolific runs of wild salmon, which are among the most highly prized stocks in the world.

Course

The river rises from the Copper Glacier on Mount Wrangell in Wrangell–St. Elias National Park, flows generally south through the Chugach Mountains, passing Chitina and the Miles Glacier, and empties into the Gulf of Alaska east of Cordova. The former copper-mining railroad from Cordova followed its lower course; the Million Dollar Bridge crosses the river between Miles and Childs glaciers.

Salmon

Copper River salmon — king (chinook), sockeye, and coho — are prized for their high fat content, developed during the long, cold migration up the swift river. The Copper River salmon fishery near Cordova marks the celebrated opening of Alaska's salmon season each spring.

Delta

The Copper River Delta, covering some 700,000 acres (2,800 km²), is the largest contiguous wetlands complex on North America's Pacific coast and a critical habitat for migrating shorebirds; the annual Copper River Delta Shorebird Festival is held in Cordova. The region was affected by the 1989 Exxon Valdez oil spill.
