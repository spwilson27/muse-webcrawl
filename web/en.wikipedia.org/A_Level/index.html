A-level

The A-level (General Certificate of Education Advanced Level) is a subject-based qualification conferred as part of the General Certificate of Education, as well as a school leaving qualification offered by the educational bodies in the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth countries to students completing secondary or pre-university education.

Introduced in England and Wales in 1951, A-levels are typically taken over two years of sixth-form study and are widely used for university admission, with offers usually expressed as grades (A* to E) in three or four subjects. Reforms in England in the 2010s made the qualification largely linear, with AS-levels decoupled from the full A-level. Several countries offer equivalent A-level qualifications through international examination boards such as Cambridge International and Edexcel.
