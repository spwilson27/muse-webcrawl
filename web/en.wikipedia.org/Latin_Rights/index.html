Latin Rights

Latin Rights (Latin: ius Latii or ius latinum) were a set of legal rights in ancient Rome held by the Latins and later granted to favoured communities, standing between full Roman citizenship and foreigner (peregrinus) status.

Content of the rights

Holders typically enjoyed commercium (the right to trade and hold property under Roman law), connubium (legal intermarriage with Romans), and in some periods ius migrationis and voting rights if resident in Rome. Magistrates of Latin colonies could often acquire full citizenship through office (per magistratum).

History

After the Latin War (340–338 BC) Rome settled Italy through Latin colonies. Grants of Latin rights became a tool of imperial integration in Spain, Gaul and elsewhere — Vespasian famously granted Latin rights to all of Spain (c. AD 74) — until Caracalla's edict of AD 212 extended citizenship empire-wide.
