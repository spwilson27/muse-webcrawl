Hastati

Hastati (singular: hastatus) were a class of infantry in the armies of the early Roman Republic who originally fought as spearmen, and later as swordsmen. They were originally some of the poorest men in the legion and could afford only modest equipment — light armor and a large shield (the scutum). The Roman military historian Polybius differentiated them from the other heavy infantry of the manipular legion: the principes, who were wealthier and older, and the triarii, the veteran third line.

History and role

The hastati formed the first line of heavy infantry in the manipular legion that emerged by the time of the Samnite Wars. In earlier usage the name derived from the hasta, a thrusting spear, but by the mid-Republican period the hastati had exchanged the spear for the sword-based fighting style of the legion: each man carried two pila (heavy throwing spears), a short sword (the gladius), and the large curved scutum. According to Polybius's ordering, a legion contained 1,200 hastati, drawn from the youngest and least experienced of the heavy infantry.

In battle, the hastati engaged first. After throwing their pila to disrupt the enemy formation, they closed with sword and shield. If they failed to break the enemy, they retired through the gaps in the second line of principes, who took up the fight; behind them stood the triarii, whose deployment signaled a desperate situation — the origin of the Latin phrase res ad triarios venit ("it has come to the triarii").

Equipment

The typical hastatus wore a bronze helmet, often with feathers or a crest to increase apparent height, a small square breastplate (pectorale) or, for those who could afford it, a mail shirt, and carried the scutum, pilum and gladius. Hastati, being the youngest men, served as the front line, and as a soldier aged and gained wealth and experience he would progress into the ranks of the principes and ultimately the triarii. The class distinctions of the manipular legion were abolished by the Marian reforms of the late 2nd century BCE, after which all legionaries were equipped uniformly at state expense.
