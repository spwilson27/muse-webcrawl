Singing

Singing is the act of creating musical sounds with the voice. A person whose profession is singing is called a singer, artist, or vocalist. Singers perform music (arias, recitatives, songs, and so on) that can be sung with or without accompaniment by musical instruments.

Technique

Singing involves controlled use of the lungs as an air supply, the larynx as a vibrator, the chest and head cavities as resonators, and the tongue, palate, and lips as articulators. Vocal training develops breath management, posture, tone production, and diction. Singing voices are classified by range and timbre — soprano, mezzo-soprano, contralto, tenor, baritone, and bass in the classical tradition.

Styles and settings

Singing occurs in religious ritual, formal performance (opera, art song, choral music), popular genres, and informal settings. Group singing in choirs has been studied for social and health benefits. Overtone singing and throat singing, as practised in parts of Central Asia, allow a singer to produce more than one pitch simultaneously.

Biological aspects

Fundamental frequency in singing typically ranges from about 60 to over 1,000 Hz depending on voice type. Training can expand a singer's range and dynamic control, while vocal strain can cause injury such as nodules on the vocal folds.
