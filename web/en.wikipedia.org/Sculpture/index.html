Sculpture

Sculpture is the branch of the visual arts that operates in three dimensions, creating forms by carving, modelling, casting, assembling or constructing materials such as stone, metal, clay, wood, glass and plastic. One of the plastic arts, sculpture is among the oldest forms of human expression: small carved figures such as the Venus of Hohle Fels date back some 35,000 years. Works may be free-standing "in the round" or in relief, where figures project from a background surface.

Techniques

Traditional processes are classed as subtractive — carving stone or wood removes material — or additive, as when clay is built up or bronze cast by the lost-wax method, in which a wax model is encased in mould material, melted out and replaced by molten metal. Modern sculptors also weld, bolt, fold and assemble found objects, and contemporary practice extends to installation, kinetic and environmental art and works in light and sound.

History

Ancient Egyptian and Mesopotamian sculpture served temples, tombs and royal display; Greek sculptors of the classical period, such as Phidias and Praxiteles, developed naturalistic anatomy and the contrapposto pose. Roman art excelled in portraiture. Medieval sculpture adorned cathedrals in the Romanesque and Gothic styles. The Renaissance revived classical forms in the work of Donatello and Michelangelo, whose David (1501–1504) remains an emblem of the art. Baroque dynamism (Bernini), nineteenth-century expressiveness (Rodin), and twentieth-century revolutions — Cubism, Constructivism, Dada readymades, Minimalism and land art — successively redefined what a sculpture could be. Non-Western traditions include the bronzes of Benin, Chinese tomb figures and Buddhist statuary, Mesoamerican stone carving and the monumental heads of Easter Island.

Materials and purposes

Durable materials — stone and bronze above all — have carried sculpture across millennia, and most ancient sculpture was originally brightly painted. Sculptures have served religion, commemoration, propaganda and pure decoration, from temple guardians and equestrian monuments to modern abstract works in public squares.
