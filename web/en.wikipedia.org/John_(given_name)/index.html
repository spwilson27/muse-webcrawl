John (given name)

John ( JON) is a common male name in the English language of Hebrew origin. The name is derived from the Latin Ioannes and Iohannes, which are forms of the Greek name Iōánnēs (Ἰωάννης), originally borne by Hellenized Jews, deriving in turn from the Hebrew name Yochanan (יוֹחָנָן), meaning "God is gracious".

Usage

The name became extremely popular in the Christian world because of its bearers in the New Testament — John the Baptist and the apostle John — and was borne by numerous kings, popes, and saints. It was among the most common male names in England and the United States for centuries; in the US it ranked among the top few boys' names from the 1880s until the mid-20th century, before declining, though it remains widely used.

Variants

Forms in other languages include Sean and Eoin (Irish), Ian (Scottish), Johan and Hans (German and Scandinavian), Jean (French), Juan (Spanish), Giovanni (Italian), Ivan (Slavic languages), João (Portuguese), and Jan (Dutch, Polish, and others). Common English diminutives include Jack, Johnny, and Jon.
