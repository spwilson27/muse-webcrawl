G.711

G.711 is a narrowband audio codec originally designed for use in telephony that provides toll-quality audio at 64 kbit/s. It is an ITU-T standard (also known as Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) of voice frequencies) released for use in 1972. It is a required standard in many Voice over IP (VoIP) services and is used in various applications, including H.320 and H.323 specifications.

Operation

G.711 passes audio signals in the range of 300–3400 Hz and samples them at the rate of 8,000 samples per second, with the tolerance on that rate of 50 parts per million (ppm). It uses one of two different logarithmic companding algorithms: μ-law, used primarily in North America and Japan, and A-law, used in most other countries. Each sample is encoded as an 8-bit value, giving a bit rate of 64 kbit/s.

Appendices and extensions

Two appendices were released by ITU-T: Appendix I (1999) defines a packet loss concealment (PLC) algorithm; Appendix II (2000) defines a discontinuous transmission (DTX) algorithm for silence suppression using comfort noise. The wideband extension G.711.1, approved in 2008, provides higher audio quality at 64–96 kbit/s using layered coding.

Licensing

G.711 was released in 1972 and its patents have long expired, so it is freely usable. It remains the baseline codec of the public switched telephone network and the default interoperability codec in VoIP.
