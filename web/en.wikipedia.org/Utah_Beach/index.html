Utah Beach was the code name for the westernmost of the five Allied landing beaches in Normandy, France, during the D-Day landings of Operation Overlord on 6 June 1944. Situated on the Cotentin Peninsula between Pouppeville and La Madeleine, it was assaulted by the US 4th Infantry Division of VII Corps and achieved its objectives with the lightest casualties of any of the five beaches.

Planning

Utah was added to the invasion plan relatively late, to position Allied forces to capture the deep-water port of Cherbourg. Because the Germans had flooded the low-lying land behind the beach, the exits across the inundated area were few and vital. US airborne forces — the 82nd and 101st Airborne Divisions, dropped inland around Sainte-Mère-Église and Carentan during the night — were tasked with seizing the causeways and preventing counterattacks.

The landings

The first wave landed at 06:30. Strong currents had pushed the landing craft about 1,800 metres south of the intended beach — fortuitously, to a less heavily defended sector. Brigadier General Theodore Roosevelt Jr., the 4th Division's assistant commander and the only general to land with the first wave, recognized the error and directed follow-on waves to the new spot with the words, "We'll start the war from right here!" He was later awarded the Medal of Honor. Obstacles were cleared by naval combat demolition units, follow-on waves and armour built up rapidly, and by nightfall some 23,000 troops and 1,700 vehicles were ashore at a cost of fewer than 200 casualties.

Aftermath

Forces from Utah linked with the airborne bridgehead, pushed up the Cotentin, cut the peninsula at Barneville on 18 June, and captured Cherbourg on 26 June, though the Germans had demolished its port facilities. Utah remained an important supply beach for months afterward.
