Amsterdam Airport Schiphol (IATA: AMS, ICAO: EHAM), known informally as Schiphol Airport, is the main international airport of the Netherlands. It is located 9 kilometres (5.6 mi) southwest of Amsterdam, in the municipality of Haarlemmermeer in North Holland.

History

Schiphol opened in 1916 as a military airfield and became a civil airport after the First World War. KLM, founded in 1919, made it its home base. The airport was rebuilt after the Second World War and expanded repeatedly, adopting a single-terminal concept with three large departure halls.

Facilities

The airport has six runways, including the Polderbaan, located far from the terminal to reduce noise. It lies about three metres below sea level on reclaimed polder land — the name Schiphol is often said to derive from a "ship hole" or ships' graveyard in the former Haarlemmermeer lake.

Traffic

Schiphol is consistently among Europe's busiest airports by passengers and air cargo, and is the main hub of KLM and a major base for the SkyTeam alliance.
