Chief of Defence Staff (India)

The Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) is the principal military adviser to the Government of India and the head of the Indian Armed Forces' Department of Military Affairs. The post was created in 2019 to promote jointmanship among the Army, Navy, and Air Force, and its holder is a four-star officer who serves as the permanent chairman of the Chiefs of Staff Committee.

Creation

The creation of a CDS had been recommended after the 1999 Kargil War and by the Group of Ministers review in 2001, but the decision was taken only in August 2019 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. The cabinet approved the post on 24 December 2019, and General Bipin Rawat, then Chief of the Army Staff, became the first CDS on 1 January 2020.

Role and responsibilities

The CDS heads the Department of Military Affairs in the Ministry of Defence, advises the defence minister, promotes joint planning, logistics, and training, and is charged with driving the creation of integrated theatre commands. The CDS does not exercise direct operational command over the individual services; the service chiefs retain command of their forces, while the CDS coordinates them.

Incumbents

General Bipin Rawat served until his death in a helicopter crash on 8 December 2021. After a vacancy of several months, Lieutenant General Anil Chauhan (retired) was appointed the second CDS in September 2022.
