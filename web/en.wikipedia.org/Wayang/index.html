Wayang

Wayang kulit puppets, carved from water buffalo hide and mounted on horn rods.

Wayang (Javanese: ꦮꦪꦁ, romanized: wayang), also known as wajang, is a traditional form of puppet theatre play originating from the Indonesian island of Java. Wayang refers to the entire dramatic show. Sometimes the leather puppet itself is referred to as wayang.

Performances of wayang puppet theatre are accompanied by a gamelan orchestra in Java, and by gender wayang in Bali. The dramatic stories depict mythologies, such as episodes from the Hindu epics the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, as well as local adaptations of cultural legends.

Forms

The best-known form is wayang kulit, shadow puppetry using flat puppets carved from buffalo hide, perforated and painted, whose shadows are cast by lamplight onto a cotton screen. Other forms include wayang golek (three-dimensional wooden rod puppets of West Java), wayang orang (performed by human actors in dance-drama), and wayang beber, in which stories are unrolled from painted scrolls.

The dalang

A single puppeteer, the dalang, manipulates the puppets, voices all the characters, sings, and cues the gamelan ensemble, often performing through the entire night. The dalang is traditionally a revered figure, part artist and part ritual specialist.

Recognition

UNESCO designated wayang a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity on 7 November 2003, and it was inscribed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008.
