Devlet I Giray

Devlet I Giray, Khan of Crimea (1551–1577).

Devlet I Giray (1512–1577), also spelled Devlet Geray, was Khan of the Crimean Khanate from 1551 to 1577. A member of the Giray dynasty, descendants of Genghis Khan, he is best remembered for his long struggle against the expanding Tsardom of Russia, culminating in the burning of Moscow in 1571.

Accession

Devlet came to power in 1551 with the support of the Ottoman Empire, deposing his uncle Sahib I Giray. The Crimean Khanate was an Ottoman vassal state, and the sultan's backing was decisive in Giray succession disputes throughout the period.

Wars with Russia

Devlet's reign was dominated by conflict with Ivan IV of Russia. In 1552 Ivan conquered the Khanate of Kazan, and in 1556 the Astrakhan Khanate, destroying the other successor states of the Golden Horde and threatening Crimea's position on the steppe. Devlet repeatedly raided Russian territory to restore the balance, demanding the return of Kazan and Astrakhan.

Burning of Moscow, 1571

In May 1571 Devlet led a large Crimean army north, evaded the Russian border defences, and reached Moscow. The city was set ablaze; the fire destroyed most of Moscow outside the Kremlin walls, and tens of thousands of captives were carried off to be sold in the slave markets of Caffa. The raid was one of the greatest disasters of Ivan IV's reign.

Battle of Molodi, 1572

Devlet returned the following year intending to complete the subjugation of Russia, but at the Battle of Molodi in the summer of 1572 his army was decisively defeated by Russian forces under Prince Mikhail Vorotynsky. The defeat ended serious Crimean ambitions to reconquer the Volga khanates.

Death and legacy

Devlet I died in 1577 and was succeeded by his son Mehmed II Giray. His reign, though marked by the Molodi defeat, preserved the Crimean Khanate as a major Black Sea power for another two centuries.
