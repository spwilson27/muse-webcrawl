Coset
FieldGroup theory (abstract algebra)
NotationgH (left coset), Hg (right coset)
Key resultLagrange's theorem

The cosets of a subgroup partition the group into equal-sized sets.

In group theory, a coset is a subset of a group formed by multiplying all elements of a subgroup by a fixed element of the group. Cosets are fundamental to the structure theory of groups: they partition the group into blocks of equal size and underlie Lagrange's theorem and the construction of quotient groups.

Definition

Let G be a group and H a subgroup of G. For an element g of G, the left coset of H with representative g is the set gH = {gh : h ∈ H}, and the right coset is Hg = {hg : h ∈ H}. The subgroup itself is the coset with representative the identity. In abelian groups left and right cosets coincide; in general they need not.

Properties

Two left cosets of H are either identical or disjoint, and every element of G belongs to exactly one left coset; thus the left cosets partition G. Each coset has the same number of elements as H, which yields Lagrange's theorem: the order (number of elements) of any subgroup of a finite group divides the order of the group. The number of left cosets, called the index of H in G, equals the number of right cosets.

Quotient groups

When H is a normal subgroup, the set of cosets can itself be made into a group — the quotient group G/H — under the operation inherited from G. Cosets also arise throughout mathematics wherever equivalence relations from group actions appear, for instance in the coset spaces of geometry and in coding theory, where cosets of a linear code organise syndrome decoding of error-correcting codes.
