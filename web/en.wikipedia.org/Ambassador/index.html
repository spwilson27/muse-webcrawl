Ambassador

An ambassador is an official envoy, especially a high-ranking diplomat who represents a state and is usually accredited to another sovereign state or to an international organisation as the resident representative of their government. Ambassadors are the highest-ranking diplomatic agents under the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) and normally head an embassy.

Role and appointment

An ambassador represents their head of state and government abroad: negotiating agreements, reporting political developments, protecting the interests of their country's nationals, and promoting trade and cultural links. Appointment typically requires the host state's prior agreement (agrément), and the ambassador formally takes up duties by presenting letters of credence to the host head of state. Ambassadors enjoy diplomatic immunity and are addressed with the style "His/Her Excellency".

Related terms

Within the Commonwealth of Nations, ambassadors exchanged between member states are called high commissioners. The Holy See's ambassadors are apostolic nuncios. The term is also used figuratively — "brand ambassadors", goodwill ambassadors of UN agencies, and "ambassador-at-large" for roving envoys without a fixed post. Historically, below ambassadors stood envoys and ministers resident; the modern convention, codified at the Congress of Vienna (1815) and updated in 1961, standardised the ranks.
