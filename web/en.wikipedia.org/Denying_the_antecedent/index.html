Denying the antecedent

Denying the antecedent, sometimes called the inverse error, is a formal fallacy of inferring the inverse from an original conditional statement: from "if P then Q" and "not P", wrongly concluding "not Q".

Example

"If it is raining, the grass is wet. It is not raining. Therefore the grass is not wet." This is invalid: the grass could be wet from a sprinkler. The valid counterpart is modus tollens, which denies the consequent.
