Ford Model T

The Ford Model T is an automobile that was produced by the Ford Motor Company from October 1, 1908, to May 26, 1927. It is generally regarded as the first mass-affordable automobile, which made car travel available to middle-class Americans. The relatively low price was partly the result of Ford's efficient fabrication, including assembly line production instead of individual handcrafting. The savings from mass production allowed the price to decline from $780 in 1910 (equivalent to about $26,000 today) to $290 in 1924.

Design and production

Designed by Childe Harold Wills, Joseph A. Galamb, and Eugene Farkas, the Model T had a front-mounted 2.9-litre four-cylinder engine producing 20 horsepower, a planetary transmission operated by pedals (two forward speeds and reverse), magneto ignition, and a hand crank for starting. In 1913 Ford introduced the moving assembly line at Highland Park, cutting chassis assembly time from 12½ hours to about 93 minutes. In 1914 Ford doubled workers' pay to $5 a day, reducing turnover and creating customers of its own workers.

Impact

More than 15 million Model Ts were built; it was the best-selling car in the world until surpassed by the Volkswagen Beetle in 1972. The Model T put America — and, through overseas plants, much of the world — on wheels, stimulating road building, suburbs, and the petroleum industry, and it was named the most influential car of the 20th century in the 1999 Car of the Century competition. Ford replaced it in 1927 with the Model A as competitors, above all General Motors, offered more comfortable and varied cars.
