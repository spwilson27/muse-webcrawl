Kimberlite

Kimberlite is an igneous rock, best known as the principal source of diamonds. It is named after the town of Kimberley in South Africa, where the discovery of an 83.5-carat diamond in 1871 triggered a diamond rush and the excavation of the Big Hole.

Formation

Kimberlites originate deep in Earth's mantle — 150 km or more down, within the diamond stability field — and erupt rapidly in carrot-shaped volcanic pipes, carrying diamond and mantle fragments (xenoliths) to the surface. They occur in ancient cratons; most kimberlites contain no economic diamonds, making exploration a search for the rare diamondiferous pipes.
