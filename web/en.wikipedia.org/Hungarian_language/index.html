Hungarian (magyar nyelv) is a Uralic language of the Ugric branch, spoken mainly in Hungary and in parts of neighbouring countries in the Carpathian Basin. It is the official language of Hungary and one of the 24 official languages of the European Union. With roughly 13 million native speakers, it is by far the most widely spoken Uralic language, and notably it is not an Indo-European language, unlike almost all other languages of Europe.

History

Hungarian diverged from its closest relatives, the Ob-Ugric languages Khanty and Mansi of western Siberia, as the Magyar tribes migrated westward across the Eurasian steppe. The Hungarians settled in the Carpathian Basin in 895–896 AD. The earliest written record of the language is found in the founding charter of the abbey of Tihany (1055), which contains Hungarian words embedded in a Latin text. The first complete text in Hungarian is the Funeral Sermon and Prayer (Halotti beszéd és könyörgés), dating from around 1192–1195.

Over the centuries Hungarian absorbed loanwords from Turkic, Slavic, German, Latin, and other languages, reflecting the changing contacts of the Hungarian people. In the 19th century, a deliberate language reform (nyelvújítás) modernised and enriched the vocabulary.

Grammar

Hungarian is an agglutinative language: grammatical relations are expressed mainly by suffixes attached to word stems. It has no grammatical gender. The case system is extensive, with counts varying by analysis but often given as 18 or more cases. Word order is relatively free and is driven largely by topic and focus rather than by fixed syntactic positions. Vowel harmony governs the choice of suffix vowels: suffixes typically have front- and back-vowel variants that must harmonise with the stem. Hungarian also uses definite and indefinite verb conjugations depending on whether the direct object is definite.

Phonology and alphabet

Hungarian has 14 vowel phonemes, distinguished by quality and length, and about 25 consonants. The Hungarian alphabet uses the Latin script extended with digraphs (such as sz, zs, cs, gy, ly, ny, ty) and accented vowels (á, é, í, ó, ö, ő, ú, ü, ű). Stress normally falls on the first syllable of a word.

Geographic distribution

Besides Hungary, significant Hungarian-speaking communities live in Romania (especially Transylvania), Slovakia, Serbia (Vojvodina), Ukraine (Transcarpathia), Croatia, Slovenia, and Austria, as well as in a worldwide diaspora.
