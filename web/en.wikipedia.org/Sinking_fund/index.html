Sinking fund

A sinking fund is a fund established by an economic entity by setting aside revenue over a period of time to fund a future capital expense, or repayment of a long-term debt. Periodic deposits, accumulated with interest, grow to the amount needed at the maturity date.

In bond finance

Many corporate and municipal bond issues include sinking fund provisions requiring the issuer to retire a specified portion of the issue periodically before maturity, either by purchasing bonds in the open market or by calling them by lot at par. Such provisions reduce default risk and can support the issue's credit rating, though a call at par can disadvantage investors when market prices exceed par.

History

Sinking funds were used by governments from the eighteenth century; Britain's sinking fund, championed by Robert Walpole and later William Pitt the Younger, aimed at redeeming the national debt. In modern accounting the term is also used for reserves set aside to replace a depreciating asset.
