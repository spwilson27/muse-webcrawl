Acts of Union 1707
Union with England Act 1707 (Scotland)
Union with Scotland Act 1706 (England)
Effective: 1 May 1707
Result: creation of the Kingdom of Great Britain

The Acts of Union were two Acts of Parliament, passed in 1706 by the Parliament of England and in 1707 by the Parliament of Scotland, which put into effect the Treaty of Union agreed on 22 July 1706. The Acts united the Kingdom of England and the Kingdom of Scotland — previously separate states with separate legislatures but the same monarch — into a single Kingdom of Great Britain, effective from 1 May 1707.

Background

England and Scotland had shared a monarch since the Union of the Crowns in 1603, but remained legally distinct. English governments sought fuller union to secure the Protestant succession and Scotland's alignment in war and trade, while Scotland, economically weakened by the failure of the Darien scheme and excluded from English colonial trade, negotiated terms including access to English markets, preservation of Scots law and the Presbyterian Church of Scotland, and a payment known as the Equivalent to compensate investors in the Company of Scotland.

Terms and legacy

The combined Parliament of Great Britain sat at Westminster. Scotland sent 45 members to the House of Commons and 16 representative peers to the House of Lords. Scotland retained its own legal system, courts and established church. The Union remains politically contentious: it was opposed by popular riots at the time, was lamented by Robert Burns as a nation "bought and sold for English gold", and is still debated in modern Scottish independence politics.
