The Forty Days of Musa Dagh (German: Die vierzig Tage des Musa Dagh) is a 1933 novel by the Austrian-Bohemian author Franz Werfel. Based on the events of 1915 at Musa Dagh (Moses Mountain) in the Ottoman Empire, it tells the story of a group of Armenians who defied deportation orders during the Armenian genocide, holding out on the mountain for weeks before being rescued by French warships.

Plot

The protagonist, Gabriel Bagradian, is a wealthy, Westernised Armenian who returns to his native village near Antioch and, as deportations begin, organises the defence of several thousand Armenians on Musa Dagh. The novel interweaves the siege with Bagradian's personal story and the wider machinery of the genocide.

Background and reception

Werfel researched the novel after encountering Armenian refugee children working in a carpet factory in Damascus in 1929. The book was a bestseller and brought international attention to the Armenian genocide; it was banned in Nazi Germany and its planned MGM film adaptation was repeatedly blocked under Turkish diplomatic pressure. The novel remains a central literary treatment of the genocide and is widely commemorated by Armenian communities.
