Rock dove

The rock dove (Columba livia), also called the rock pigeon or common pigeon, is a member of the bird family Columbidae. It is the wild ancestor of the domestic pigeon and of the feral pigeons found in cities worldwide.

Description

Wild rock doves are pale grey with two black wing bars, a white rump, and iridescent green and purple neck feathers. They measure about 32–37 cm in length. Domestic and feral populations vary enormously in plumage because of selective breeding and mixing.

Distribution and habits

Native to western and southern Europe, North Africa, and western and central Asia, the species nests on cliff faces and in caves — a habit that transferred readily to buildings in cities. Introduced domesticated stock has established feral populations on every continent except Antarctica. Rock doves feed mainly on seeds and grains, and domesticated descendants have been used as homing pigeons, in racing, and historically as message carriers.
