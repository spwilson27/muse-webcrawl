Augsburg is a city in the southwestern part of the German state of Bavaria, and the capital of the administrative region of Swabia. With about 300,000 inhabitants it is the third-largest city in Bavaria after Munich and Nuremberg. Founded in 15 BC by the Romans as Augusta Vindelicum, named after the emperor Augustus, it is one of Germany's oldest cities.

History

Augsburg grew at the confluence of the Lech and Wertach rivers on the Roman road Via Claudia Augusta. In the late Middle Ages it became a free imperial city and one of Europe's great commercial centres, the seat of the Fugger and Welser banking families — the Fuggerei, founded in 1516 by Jakob Fugger the Rich, is the world's oldest social housing complex still in use. The city gave its name to the Augsburg Confession of 1530, a foundational document of Lutheranism, and to the Peace of Augsburg of 1555, which recognised Lutheranism within the Holy Roman Empire. The Renaissance town hall, built by Elias Holl in 1615–1624, is a landmark of German civic architecture.

Geography

Augsburg lies about 50 km west-northwest of Munich, on the Lechfeld plain. Its historic canal network, part of the Augsburg water management system inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019, powered mills and crafts for centuries.

Economy and culture

Modern Augsburg is a centre for aerospace, robotics and mechanical engineering. It is the birthplace of Leopold Mozart, father of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, and of the playwright Bertolt Brecht, whose birthplace is now a museum. The local football club, FC Augsburg, plays in the Bundesliga.
