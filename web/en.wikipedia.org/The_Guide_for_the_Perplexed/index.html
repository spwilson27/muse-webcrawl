The Guide for the Perplexed

The Guide for the Perplexed (Arabic: Dalālat al-ḥā'irīn; Hebrew: Moreh Nevukhim) is a work of Jewish theology by Moses Maimonides, written in Judeo-Arabic around 1190. It is considered one of the most influential works of medieval Jewish philosophy.

Contents

Addressed to Maimonides' student Joseph ben Judah, the Guide seeks to reconcile Aristotelian philosophy with Jewish theology. It treats biblical anthropomorphisms as allegory, develops a negative theology — God can only be described by what He is not — and discusses divine providence, the problem of evil, creation, and prophecy.

Influence

The work profoundly influenced Jewish thought and was read by Christian scholastics including Thomas Aquinas. Controversy over its rationalism split Jewish communities in the Maimonidean controversies of the 13th century. See also The Interior Castle for another classic of religious literature.
