Chakravala method

Overview
TypeAlgorithm for indeterminate quadratic equations
SolvesPell's equation: x² − Ny² = 1
OriginIndia; described by Jayadeva (11th c.) and Bhāskara II (1150)
NameFrom Sanskrit chakra, "wheel" or "cycle"

The chakravala method (Sanskrit: चक्रवाल विधि) is a cyclic algorithm to solve indeterminate quadratic equations, including Pell's equation:

x² − Ny² = 1

It is commonly attributed to Bhāskara II, whose Bījagaṇita of 1150 gives the method in full, though it was described earlier by Jayadeva (c. 11th century) and builds on the composition (bhāvanā) principle of Brahmagupta (628 CE). The word chakravala derives from chakra, a wheel, reflecting the cyclic nature of the algorithm.

The method

Starting from a near-solution triple (a, b, k) with a² − Nb² = k, the method repeatedly applies Brahmagupta's composition with a trivial solution to generate new triples, choosing at each step an integer that minimizes the next value of |k|, until k becomes ±1, ±2, or ±4, from which a solution of Pell's equation follows directly. The algorithm always terminates with the minimal solution.

Famous example

Bhāskara II used the method to solve x² − 61y² = 1, giving the enormous minimal solution x = 1,766,319,049, y = 226,153,980. The same equation was posed by Fermat as a challenge in 1657, centuries later; its difficulty illustrates the power of the Indian cyclic method, which anticipates aspects of Lagrange's continued-fraction theory of the 18th century.
