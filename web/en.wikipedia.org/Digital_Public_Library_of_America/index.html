Digital Public Library of America

The Digital Public Library of America (DPLA) is an American online library founded by historians and librarians in 2012 to provide free public access to digitised holdings from libraries, archives and museums across the United States. Rather than hosting most content itself, the DPLA aggregates metadata from partner institutions and provides a single discovery portal, along with an application programming interface (API) that lets developers build new tools on the aggregated data.

History

The project grew out of discussions at Harvard's Berkman Center for Internet and Society in 2010 and was developed with support from foundations including the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation and the Arcadia Fund. The DPLA launched publicly on 18 April 2013, offering access to several million items from partners such as the Smithsonian Institution, the National Archives and Records Administration, the New York Public Library and the Digital Commonwealth of Massachusetts.

Network model

The DPLA works through a hub-and-spoke model. Content hubs are large institutions that contribute their own metadata; service hubs are state or regional aggregators that gather metadata from smaller libraries, historical societies and museums in their area. All metadata contributed to the DPLA is made freely available under a CC0 waiver, while the digital objects themselves remain on the contributing institutions' websites with their own rights statements.

Programmes

Beyond the discovery portal, the DPLA has run exhibitions curated from partner collections, community digitisation events, and the Palace Project and other initiatives supporting library e-book lending. It publishes its code as open source and hosts hackathons and an annual conference, DPLAfest.
