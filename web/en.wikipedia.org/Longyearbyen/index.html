Country: Norway (Svalbard)

Population: about 2,400
Coordinates: 78°13′N 15°33′E — world's northernmost settlement of its size

Longyearbyen is the largest settlement and the administrative centre of Svalbard, a Norwegian archipelago in the Arctic Ocean. Located on the shore of Adventfjorden on the island of Spitsbergen, it is one of the world's northernmost inhabited places, with a population of roughly 2,400 from more than 50 nationalities.

History

The town was founded in 1906 by American entrepreneur John Munro Longyear, whose Arctic Coal Company began mining operations; it was named Longyear City, later Norwegianised to Longyearbyen. Store Norske Spitsbergen Kulkompani took over in 1916. The town was evacuated and largely destroyed in the Second World War and rebuilt afterwards.

Life and climate

Longyearbyen experiences polar night from late October to mid-February and midnight sun from April to August. Originally a company coal-mining town, its economy now centres on tourism, research and education: the University Centre in Svalbard (UNIS) and the Svalbard Global Seed Vault (opened 2008) are nearby. Because of permafrost, buildings stand on piles, burials in the local cemetery were halted decades ago, and residents are required to carry firearms outside the settlement as protection against polar bears.
