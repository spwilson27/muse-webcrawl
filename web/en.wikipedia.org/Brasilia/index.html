Brasilia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Monumental Axis, Brasilia

Brasilia (Portuguese: Brasília) is the federal capital of Brazil and the seat of government of the Federal District. The city is located in the Brazilian highlands in the country's Central-West region. It was founded on 21 April 1960 to replace Rio de Janeiro as the national capital. Brasilia is the third-most populous city in Brazil after São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, and its metropolitan area is among the largest in the country.

The city was planned and developed by Lúcio Costa, Oscar Niemeyer and Joaquim Cardozo in 1956 in a plan to move the capital from Rio de Janeiro to a more central location. The landscape architect was Roberto Burle Marx. The city's design divides it into numbered blocks as well as sectors for specified activities, such as the Hotel Sector, the Banking Sector and the Embassy Sector. Brasilia was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987 due to its modernist architecture and uniquely artistic urban planning.

History

The idea of moving Brazil's capital to the interior dated to the colonial period and was written into the first republican constitution of 1891. In 1956, President Juscelino Kubitschek ordered the construction of a new capital in the country's central plateau. The urban plan by Lúcio Costa, known as the Plano Piloto, was selected through a national competition, and Oscar Niemeyer served as the principal architect of the public buildings. Construction took 41 months, and the city was inaugurated on 21 April 1960.

Geography

Brasilia lies on the Planalto Central at an elevation of about 1,100 metres (3,600 feet). It has a tropical savanna climate with a pronounced dry season from May to September. Lake Paranoá, a large artificial lake created by damming the Paranoá River, lies on the eastern side of the city and was built in part to moderate the region's low humidity.

Architecture

The city's most famous structures include the Cathedral of Brasilia, the National Congress building with its twin towers flanked by a bowl and a dome, the Palácio da Alvorada (the presidential residence), the Palácio do Planalto and the Itamaraty Palace. Seen from above, the planned city is often said to resemble an aeroplane, with residential wings extending from the central Monumental Axis.
