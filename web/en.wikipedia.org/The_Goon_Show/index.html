The Goon Show

GenreComedy radio programme
CountryUnited Kingdom
NetworkBBC Home Service

StarringSpike Milligan, Peter Sellers, Harry Secombe

The Goon Show was a British radio comedy programme produced and broadcast by the BBC Home Service from 1951 to 1960. Written chiefly by Spike Milligan, it starred Milligan, Peter Sellers, and Harry Secombe (with Michael Bentine in the earliest series), and is considered a pioneering work of surreal and absurdist comedy.

Style and content

The shows followed characters such as Neddie Seagoon, Eccles, Bluebottle, Major Bloodnok, Moriarty, and Grytpype-Thynne through illogical plots punctuated by elaborate sound effects, musical interludes by Ray Ellington and harmonica player Max Geldray, and announcements by Wallace Greenslade. The programme's anarchic humour satirised postwar British institutions, the military, and the Empire.

Influence

The Goon Show was hugely popular in Britain and the Commonwealth and profoundly influenced later British comedy, including Monty Python, as well as the Beatles. Surviving episodes are still rebroadcast and widely available.
