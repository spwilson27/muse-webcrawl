Foo Fighters

Foo Fighters is an American rock band formed in Seattle in 1994. Initially founded as a one-man project by former Nirvana drummer Dave Grohl, the lineup now consists of Grohl (lead vocals, guitar), Nate Mendel (bass), Chris Shiflett (guitar), Pat Smear (guitar), Rami Jaffee (keyboards) and Josh Freese (drums). Drummer Taylor Hawkins performed with the band from 1997 until his death in 2022. Over their career, Foo Fighters have won 15 Grammy Awards, including Best Rock Album five times, and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2021, their first year of eligibility.

History

Grohl recorded the debut album Foo Fighters (1995) almost entirely alone in the aftermath of Kurt Cobain's death and the end of Nirvana, recruiting a touring band that included bassist Nate Mendel and drummer William Goldsmith. The name came from "foo fighter", the Second World War term for unexplained aerial phenomena. The Colour and the Shape (1997) produced the hits "Monkey Wrench" and "Everlong" and established the band as a major act, though Goldsmith left during recording and was replaced by Taylor Hawkins.

Mainstream success

Subsequent albums — There Is Nothing Left to Lose (1999), One by One (2002), the double album In Your Honor (2005), Wasting Light (2011), recorded in Grohl's garage on analogue tape, and Concrete and Gold (2017) — kept the band among the world's biggest stadium rock acts. Hawkins died suddenly in Bogotá, Colombia, in March 2022 while on tour; tribute concerts were held in London and Los Angeles, and Josh Freese was announced as the band's new drummer in 2023, when the album But Here We Are was released.
