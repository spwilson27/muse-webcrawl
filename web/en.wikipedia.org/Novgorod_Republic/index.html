Novgorod Republic

Novgorod Republic
Existed: 1136–1478
Capital: Novgorod
Government: Republic (veche assembly)
Preceded by: Kievan Rus'
Succeeded by: Grand Duchy of Moscow

The Novgorod Republic was a medieval state that existed from the 12th to the 15th century in the lands around Novgorod in what is now northwestern Russia. Expanding trade networks across northern Europe made it one of the largest states in medieval eastern Europe, stretching at its height from the Baltic Sea to the Ural Mountains.

Government

Novgorod was governed through the veche, a popular assembly that elected officials including the posadnik (mayor) and invited and expelled princes, who served mainly as military leaders. In practice, power was shared with a council of boyar elites. The archbishop of Novgorod also held significant authority.

Economy and culture

The republic grew wealthy on the fur trade and commerce with the Hanseatic League, maintaining a kontor (trading post) in the city. Novgorod produced distinctive architecture, icon painting, and literature, including the birch-bark letters that document everyday literacy. Alexander Nevsky defended the republic against Swedish and German crusaders at the Battle of the Neva (1240) and the Battle on the Ice (1242).

Decline and annexation

Weakened by conflict with Moscow and internal divisions, Novgorod was defeated at the Battle of Shelon in 1471 and annexed by Ivan III in 1478, ending the republic.
