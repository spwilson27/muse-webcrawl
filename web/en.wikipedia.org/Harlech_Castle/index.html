Harlech Castle

Harlech Castle is a medieval fortification in Harlech, Gwynedd, Wales, built by Edward I between 1282 and 1289 during his invasion of Wales. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd.

History

Designed by Master James of St George, the concentric castle stands on a rocky crag overlooking the Irish Sea, originally reachable by sea via a fortified stairway. It withstood sieges during the revolt of Madog ap Llywelyn and Owain Glyndŵr's rebellion, and was the last royalist stronghold to surrender in the English Civil War in 1647.

Architecture

The castle features a massive twin-towered gatehouse and concentric walls exploiting the natural rock. The Welsh song "Men of Harlech" commemorates its defenders. See also Edward I.
