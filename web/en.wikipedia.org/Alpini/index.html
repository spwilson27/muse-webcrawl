Alpini

Alpini are recognised by the black raven feather on their hat, the capello alpino.

The Alpini are the Italian Army's specialist mountain infantry. Founded in 1872, they are the oldest active mountain infantry corps in the world, originally recruited from the Alpine valleys to defend Italy's northern mountain frontier. Their symbol is the feathered hat, the capello alpino, and their motto reflects their mountain origins.

History

The first Alpini companies were raised by Captain Giuseppe Perrucchetti to garrison the Alpine passes. During the First World War the Alpini fought a gruelling high-altitude campaign against Austria-Hungary in the Dolomites and on the Isonzo front, suffering enormous losses. In the Second World War Alpini divisions served in France, Greece and, most disastrously, on the Eastern Front in the Soviet Union, where the ARMIR was destroyed in the winter of 1942–43 — an episode remembered in Italy as a national tragedy.

Modern organisation

Today the Alpini are organised principally in two brigades, the Taurinense and the Julia, under the Alpine Troops Command in Bolzano, together with a training centre and specialist support units. They include mountain artillery, engineers, signals and the famous Alpini paratroopers. Alpini units have served in Afghanistan and other international missions, and their mountain and disaster-relief skills are regularly used at home.

Traditions

The annual national gathering (adunata) of the Associazione Nazionale Alpini draws hundreds of thousands of veterans and families. The corps' slow march, "Trentatré", and its songs such as "Sul cappello" are fixtures of Italian military folklore.
