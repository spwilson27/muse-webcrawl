Affinity maturation

Affinity maturation is the process by which the immune system produces antibodies with increasingly strong binding (affinity) to an antigen during the course of an immune response.

Mechanism

It takes place in germinal centres of lymphoid follicles. Activated B cells undergo rapid proliferation while the enzyme AID introduces point mutations into their immunoglobulin variable-region genes — somatic hypermutation. B cells whose mutated receptors bind antigen most strongly receive survival signals from follicular helper T cells; weaker binders die. Repeated cycles yield high-affinity memory B cells and plasma cells.

Significance

Affinity maturation explains why secondary immune responses are faster and stronger, and it is exploited in vaccine design and in laboratory antibody engineering.
