Public finance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Public finance is the field of economics that studies the role of the government in the economy. It is the branch of economics that assesses the government revenue and government expenditure of the public authorities and the adjustment of one or the other to achieve desirable effects and avoid undesirable ones. The purview of public finance is considered to be threefold, consisting of governmental effects on efficient allocation of resources, distribution of income, and macroeconomic stabilization.

Overview

The economist Richard Musgrave's framework, set out in The Theory of Public Finance (1959), assigns government three functions: allocation (providing public goods and correcting market failures), distribution (adjusting the distribution of income and wealth), and stabilization (maintaining employment and price stability). Public finance studies how governments raise revenue — mainly through taxation, but also fees, fines, and borrowing — and how they spend it on public services, infrastructure, social welfare, and debt service.

Topics

  • Taxation — the design and incidence of taxes on income, consumption, property, and wealth, and the trade-offs between efficiency and equity.
  • Public goods and externalities — goods that are non-rival and non-excludable, and the market failures that justify collective provision or regulation.
  • Government expenditure — spending on health, education, defence, transfers, and infrastructure.
  • Budgeting and deficits — the government budget process, fiscal policy, and the sustainability of public debt.
  • Fiscal federalism — the division of revenue and expenditure responsibilities among levels of government.
  • Public choice — applying economic analysis to political decision-making, bureaucracy, and rent-seeking.

Public finance by government level

In federal systems, public finance operates at national, state or regional, and local levels, each with distinct revenue sources (for example, local property taxes versus national income taxes) and expenditure responsibilities. International public finance addresses issues such as tax competition, cross-border externalities, and the finances of international organizations.
