Grand Bazaar, Istanbul

The Grand Bazaar (Turkish: Kapalıçarşı, meaning "Covered Market") in Istanbul is one of the largest and oldest covered markets in the world. Located inside the walled city in the Fatih district, it covers some 30,700 square metres with 61 covered streets, over 4,000 shops, and 18 gates, attracting tens of thousands of visitors daily — often cited among the world's most-visited tourist attractions.

History

Construction began in the winter of 1455/56, shortly after the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople, when Sultan Mehmed II commissioned a stone bedesten (warehouse-market) for the trade in textiles and jewels. The complex grew over the following centuries as markets around it were roofed over and incorporated. Earthquakes (notably in 1894) and fires repeatedly damaged the bazaar, and it was rebuilt each time; a major restoration followed the 1894 quake under Sultan Abdul Hamid II.

Layout and trade

Historically, each street was devoted to a particular trade — goldsmiths, carpet sellers, leather workers, ceramists, spice and slipper merchants — regulated by the guild system. The two original bedestens, the İç (Inner) Bedesten and the Sandal Bedesten, form the nucleus of the complex. Today the bazaar's shops sell jewellery, carpets, ceramics, lanterns, textiles, antiques, and souvenirs, and the market remains a centre of Istanbul's gold and foreign-exchange trade. Haggling over prices is traditional.

In culture

The Grand Bazaar has appeared in numerous films, including the James Bond movies From Russia with Love (1963) and Skyfall (2012), whose motorcycle chase was filmed across its rooftops.
