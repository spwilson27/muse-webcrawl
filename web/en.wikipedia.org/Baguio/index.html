Baguio, officially the City of Baguio, is a highly urbanised city in the Cordillera Administrative Region of northern Luzon, Philippines. Because of its cool climate at about 1,540 metres (5,050 ft) above sea level, it is known as the "Summer Capital of the Philippines" and the "City of Pines."

History

The area was originally an Ibaloi settlement called Kafagway. During the American colonial period, the United States government selected the site as a hill station and summer capital; architect Daniel Burnham prepared a city plan in 1905, and Baguio was chartered as a city in 1909. The city was heavily damaged in the Second World War and again by the 1990 Luzon earthquake, after which it was rebuilt. Camp John Hay, the Philippine Military Academy, and the presidential Mansion House are landmark institutions.

Climate and tourism

Baguio's average temperatures run roughly 8–10 °C cooler than the lowland Philippines, making it a favoured domestic holiday destination. Attractions include Burnham Park with its man-made lake, Session Road, Mines View Park, strawberry farms in neighbouring La Trinidad, and the annual Panagbenga flower festival held in February. The city is also a major education centre, hosting several universities, and was inscribed as a UNESCO Creative City of Crafts and Folk Art in 2017.
