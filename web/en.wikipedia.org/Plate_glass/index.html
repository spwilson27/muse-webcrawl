Plate glass

Plate glass is a type of flat glass of high optical quality, historically made by casting molten glass onto a flat table, rolling it into a sheet, and then grinding and polishing both surfaces to remove distortion. It was used for mirrors, shopfronts and large windows.

History

The casting process was developed in France in the late 17th century at Saint-Gobain, enabling the large mirrors of the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles. Polished plate remained expensive until mechanised grinding in the 19th century brought it into wider architectural use.

Replacement

From the 1950s the float glass process, developed by Pilkington, produced flat glass with fire-polished surfaces directly on a bath of molten tin, making ground plate glass obsolete for most purposes.
