The 2012 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXX Olympiad, were held in London from 27 July to 12 August 2012. London became the first city to host the modern Olympic Games three times, having previously staged them in 1908 and 1948. London won the right to host in 2005, narrowly defeating Paris in the final round of IOC voting.

Organisation

The Games were centred on the new Olympic Park in Stratford, East London, built on regenerated industrial land. The organising committee, LOCOG, was chaired by former athlete Sebastian Coe, with Danny Boyle directing an opening ceremony that celebrated British history, literature and the National Health Service, and featured the appearance of Queen Elizabeth II alongside Daniel Craig as James Bond.

Sporting highlights

Jamaican sprinter Usain Bolt retained his 100 m, 200 m and 4×100 m relay titles, the last in a world record. American swimmer Michael Phelps won four golds and two silvers to become the most decorated Olympian of all time with 22 medals. Host nation Britain enjoyed its most successful Games in over a century, finishing third in the medal table with 29 golds; "Super Saturday" on 4 August saw Jessica Ennis, Mo Farah and Greg Rutherford win athletics golds on the same night. The 2012 Games were also the first at which every participating nation sent at least one female athlete, and women's boxing made its Olympic debut.

Medal table and legacy

The United States topped the medal table with 47 golds, ahead of China (39) and Great Britain (29). The Olympic Park was redeveloped as the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park, and the stadium later became the home of West Ham United. London 2012 is widely regarded as one of the best-organised and most atmospheric modern Games.
