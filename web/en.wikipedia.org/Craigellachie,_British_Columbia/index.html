Craigellachie, British Columbia

Craigellachie
Unincorporated community
Province: British Columbia
Regional district: Columbia Shuswap
Known for: Driving of the Last Spike, 7 November 1885

Craigellachie is a small unincorporated community in British Columbia, Canada, located at Eagle Pass in the Monashee Mountains between Revelstoke and Sicamous, on the Trans-Canada Highway and the Canadian Pacific Railway main line. It is famous as the site where the Last Spike of Canada's first transcontinental railway was driven on 7 November 1885, completing the Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR).

The Last Spike

The ceremony was deliberately modest: the railway was nearly bankrupt, and the spike itself was an ordinary iron spike, not gold or silver. Donald Smith (later Lord Strathcona), a CPR director, drove it — reportedly bending his first spike and having to try again. The name Craigellachie, chosen by CPR director George Stephen, recalls a village in Moray, Scotland, and the clan Grant motto "Stand fast, Craigellachie!" — a rallying cry used to encourage the railway's backers during its financial crises. The driving of the spike fulfilled the promise of a transcontinental railway that had induced British Columbia to join Confederation in 1871.

Today

A stone cairn and interpretive display mark the site beside the highway, one of British Columbia's most visited roadside historic stops; a commemorative ceremony in 1985 marked the centenary. Passenger trains no longer stop, but the location remains a named point on the CPR line and a symbol of Canadian national unity.
