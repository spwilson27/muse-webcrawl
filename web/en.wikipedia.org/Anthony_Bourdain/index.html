Anthony Michael Bourdain (25 June 1956 – 8 June 2018) was an American celebrity chef, author and travel documentary presenter. He became famous with his bestselling memoir Kitchen Confidential (2000), an unsparing account of life in professional kitchens, and went on to host the travel and food programmes A Cook's Tour, No Reservations, The Layover and Parts Unknown.

Career

Raised in New Jersey, Bourdain graduated from the Culinary Institute of America in 1978 and worked his way through New York kitchens, becoming executive chef at Brasserie Les Halles in 1998. A 1999 New Yorker article, "Don't Eat Before Reading This", led to Kitchen Confidential and a television career built around curiosity about other cultures and their food, from street stalls to war zones.

Death and legacy

Bourdain died by suicide in France in June 2018 while filming Parts Unknown. He won multiple Emmy Awards and a Peabody Award, and is credited with reshaping food and travel television through his empathy, candour and willingness to eat — and listen — almost anywhere. His books include Medium Raw and World Travel: An Irreverent Guide (published posthumously). See also Kitchen Confidential.
