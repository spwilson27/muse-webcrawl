Aliya Rama Raya

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Died: 23 January 1565, Battle of Talikota

Role: Regent and de facto ruler of the Vijayanagara Empire

Dynasty: Aravidu (by marriage into Tuluva line)

Aliya Rama Raya (c. 1484 – 23 January 1565) was the regent and effective ruler of the Vijayanagara Empire of South India in the mid-sixteenth century. Son-in-law (aliya in Kannada and Telugu) of the great emperor Krishnadevaraya, he governed in the name of the nominal Tuluva emperor Sadasiva Raya and directed the empire's affairs for more than two decades.

Rise to power

Rama Raya rose through service under Krishnadevaraya and, after the accession of the weak Achyuta Deva Raya and then Sadasiva Raya in 1542, consolidated control with his brothers Tirumala Deva Raya and Venkatadri. He placed loyal commanders in key provinces, intervened aggressively in the politics of the Deccan sultanates — alternately allying with Bijapur, Ahmadnagar and Golconda against one another — and maintained a splendid court at Vijayanagara described by Portuguese visitors.

Battle of Talikota and death

His shifting diplomacy eventually united the Deccan sultanates against him. At the Battle of Talikota on 23 January 1565, the combined armies of Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda and Bidar met the Vijayanagara host on the banks of the Krishna River. Rama Raya, by then an elderly man, directed the battle from a litter; he was captured and beheaded during the fighting, and his army collapsed. The victorious sultanates sacked the capital city of Vijayanagara, which was largely abandoned — a catastrophe from which the empire never recovered, though Rama Raya's brother Tirumala later refounded it in reduced form as the Aravidu dynasty.
