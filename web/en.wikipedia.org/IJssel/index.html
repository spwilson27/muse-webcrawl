IJssel

The IJssel (Dutch: [ˈɛisəl]; sometimes spelled Yssel in older sources) is a Dutch distributary of the river Rhine that flows northward and ultimately discharges into the IJsselmeer, the lake formerly known as the Zuiderzee. It is roughly 123 kilometres (76 mi) long and is sometimes considered the northernmost branch of the Rhine delta.

Course

The IJssel branches off the Nederrijn at Westervoort, east of the city of Arnhem, where the river's flow is regulated by weirs. From there it flows generally north-east and then north, passing the towns of Doesburg, Zutphen, Deventer, Zwolle and Kampen, before emptying into the IJsselmeer near the Ketelmeer. The river is navigable along its entire length and forms part of an important inland shipping route.

History

The IJssel's channel is partly artificial in origin. In Roman times the general Drusus is traditionally credited with having a canal dug — the Fossa Drusiana — connecting the Rhine to the IJssel valley around 12 BC, partly to facilitate military operations against Germanic tribes. Through the Middle Ages the river became a major trade artery: Zutphen, Deventer and Kampen grew wealthy as members of the Hanseatic League, trading with ports around the North Sea and Baltic.

Name

The name IJssel is thought to derive from the Germanic root isala, meaning "salmon stream". The unusual spelling with the digraph IJ is a characteristic of Dutch orthography.

Tributaries and surroundings

Important tributaries include the Oude IJssel ("Old IJssel"), the Berkel and the Schipbeek. The river valley is bordered by the Veluwe uplands to the west and the Achterhoek and Salland regions to the east.
