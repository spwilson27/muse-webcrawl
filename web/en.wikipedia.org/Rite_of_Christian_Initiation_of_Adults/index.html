Rite of Christian Initiation of Adults

The Rite of Christian Initiation of Adults (RCIA), in recent official translations also rendered as the Order of Christian Initiation of Adults (OCIA), is the process developed by the Catholic Church for prospective converts to Catholicism who are of the age of reason and above. It was restored and promulgated in 1972 following the Second Vatican Council, reviving the ancient catechumenate.

Stages

The process proceeds through several periods: evangelisation and precatechumenate (inquiry); the catechumenate, a longer period of formation entered through the Rite of Acceptance; purification and enlightenment during Lent, entered through the Rite of Election; and mystagogy after initiation. Unbaptised candidates (catechumens) receive baptism, confirmation and first Eucharist, usually at the Easter Vigil; baptised candidates from other traditions (candidates) make a profession of faith.

Rites

Public rites marking the journey include the Rite of Acceptance, the Rite of Election celebrated by the bishop, the scrutinies on the third, fourth and fifth Sundays of Lent, and presentations of the Creed and the Lord's Prayer. Sponsors and godparents accompany the catechumens throughout.
