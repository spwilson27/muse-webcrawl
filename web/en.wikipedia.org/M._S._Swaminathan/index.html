M. S. Swaminathan

Mankombu Sambasivan Swaminathan
Born 7 August 1925, Kumbakonam, British India
Died 28 September 2023, Chennai, India
Known for: Green Revolution in India
Awards: World Food Prize (1987), Padma Vibhushan (1989), Bharat Ratna (2024, posthumous)

Mankombu Sambasivan Swaminathan (7 August 1925 – 28 September 2023) was an Indian agronomist, agricultural scientist, plant geneticist, and administrator, widely regarded as the father of the Green Revolution in India. Working with Norman Borlaug and others, he developed high-yielding varieties of wheat and rice that helped India move from food scarcity to self-sufficiency in staple grains in the 1960s and 1970s.

Career

Swaminathan studied zoology and then agriculture, earning his doctorate in genetics from the University of Cambridge in 1952. At the Indian Agricultural Research Institute in New Delhi he worked on potato, wheat, rice, and jute genetics. In the 1960s he collaborated in introducing Mexican dwarf wheat varieties developed by Borlaug's programme, demonstrating them on farmers' fields and helping adapt them to Indian conditions — a campaign that transformed Indian food production.

He served as Director General of the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (1972–1979) and of the International Rice Research Institute in the Philippines (1982–1988), and later chaired the National Commission on Farmers (2004–2006), whose recommendations included setting minimum support prices at production cost plus 50 percent.

Later work and honours

In 1988 he founded the M. S. Swaminathan Research Foundation in Chennai, promoting sustainable agriculture and rural development. He was the inaugural recipient of the World Food Prize in 1987 and was named one of the 20 most influential Asians of the 20th century by Time magazine. In 2024 he was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.
