History of books

The history of books begins with the invention of writing and the earliest durable media — clay tablets, papyrus scrolls and parchment codices — and runs through the printing press, the paperback, and the modern e-book.

Origins

The earliest books were written on clay tablets in Mesopotamia, on papyrus in Egypt, and on bamboo and silk in China. The scroll was the standard format of the ancient world; great collections such as the Library of Alexandria held hundreds of thousands of rolls.

The codex

Between the 1st and 4th centuries AD the codex — folded sheets bound between covers, usually of parchment — replaced the scroll. The codex allowed random access, used both sides of the writing surface, and was favoured by early Christians. Medieval monasteries preserved and copied manuscripts, and illuminated manuscripts became major works of art.

Printing

Woodblock printing developed in Tang-dynasty China produced the Diamond Sutra (868), the earliest dated printed book; movable type was invented by Bi Sheng around 1040 and later developed in Korea. In Europe, Johannes Gutenberg's system of movable metal type, oil-based ink and the screw press (c. 1450) cut the cost of books dramatically, fuelling the Renaissance, Reformation and Scientific Revolution.

The modern book

Steam-powered presses, cheap paper from wood pulp and rising literacy made books mass media in the 19th century. The 20th century added the mass-market paperback, and the 21st brought e-books and print-on-demand, though the printed codex remains the dominant form.
