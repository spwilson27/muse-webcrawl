Pancetta

Pancetta (Italian pronunciation: [panˈtʃetta]) is a salumi made of pork belly meat that is salt-cured and spiced with black pepper, and sometimes other spices and herbs. It is a staple of Italian cooking, valued for the rich, deep flavour it lends to sauces and other dishes.

Production

Pork belly is trimmed, salted, and seasoned — often with pepper, cloves, nutmeg, fennel or juniper — then cured for at least ten to fifteen days. In its arrotolata (rolled) form it is tied into a tight cylinder and aged for weeks to months; the tesa (flat) form is cured flat. Unlike American bacon, pancetta is not smoked.

Culinary use

Pancetta is frequently diced and rendered as the fatty base of pasta sauces such as spaghetti alla carbonara and amatriciana, and is used to wrap roasts, flavour soups and stews, and top pizzas. Rolled pancetta is also eaten thinly sliced as an antipasto. Several regional varieties, such as Pancetta Piacentina, hold protected designation of origin (PDO) status in the European Union.
