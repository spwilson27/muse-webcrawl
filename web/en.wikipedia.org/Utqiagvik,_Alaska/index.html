Utqiagvik, Alaska

Utqiagvik, Alaska
State: Alaska (North Slope Borough)
Former name: Barrow (until 2016)
Location: Northernmost city in the United States

Utqiagvik (Inupiaq: Utqiaġvik), formerly known as Barrow, is the borough seat and largest city of the North Slope Borough in the U.S. state of Alaska. Located north of the Arctic Circle on the Chukchi Sea coast, it is the northernmost city in the United States and one of the northernmost settlements in the world, with a population of roughly 4,900. In 2016 voters approved renaming the city from Barrow to its traditional Iñupiaq name.

Geography and climate

The city sits near Point Barrow, about 1,300 miles (2,100 km) north of Anchorage. It has a cold desert (tundra) climate: the sun does not set for about 80 days in summer and does not rise for roughly 65 days in winter. There are no roads connecting Utqiagvik to the rest of Alaska; access is by air and seasonal sea lift.

History and culture

The area has been inhabited by Iñupiaq people for over 1,500 years and remains a centre of subsistence whaling culture; the city hosts the Nalukataq whaling festival. It was named Barrow for Sir John Barrow of the British Admiralty. The Wiley Post–Will Rogers Memorial marks the site of their fatal 1935 plane crash nearby.
