Bombardment of Ellwood

The Bombardment of Ellwood was a naval attack by a Japanese submarine against United States coastal targets near Santa Barbara, California, on 23 February 1942 during the Second World War. The Imperial Japanese Navy submarine I-17, commanded by Commander Kozo Nishino, surfaced offshore and fired shells at the Ellwood Oil Field near Goleta. It was the first naval bombardment of the continental United States mainland by a foreign power since the War of 1812, and it stoked fears of a Japanese invasion of the West Coast.

Background

Following the attack on Pearl Harbor in December 1941, Japanese submarines patrolled the North American west coast, sinking merchant ships. I-17's commander, Kozo Nishino, had visited Ellwood before the war as captain of an oil tanker that loaded crude there; legend holds that he resolved to shell the field after slipping on a cactus during a ceremonial reception—an anecdote treated cautiously by historians.

The attack

At about 7:15 p.m. on 23 February 1942—while President Franklin D. Roosevelt was delivering a fireside chat—I-17 surfaced about half a mile offshore and opened fire with its 5.5-inch deck gun, firing 16 to 25 shells over about twenty minutes at the Richfield aviation fuel storage and oil facilities. Shells fell mostly in fields and the surf; damage was slight (a derrick and a pumphouse were hit, with losses of a few hundred dollars), and no one was injured. The submarine then departed westward unharmed; US planes sent to search arrived after it had gone.

Aftermath

News of the shelling, coming on top of Pearl Harbor, intensified invasion fears and West Coast blackout measures. The very next night, the "Battle of Los Angeles"—a prolonged anti-aircraft barrage at imagined enemy aircraft—took place, illustrating the level of public anxiety. The Ellwood attack contributed to the climate that culminated in the internment of Japanese Americans under Executive Order 9066, signed days earlier. I-17 went on to conduct further operations before being sunk in August 1943 near New Caledonia.
