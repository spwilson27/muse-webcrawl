Legal name

Passports and birth certificates record a person's legal name.

A legal name is the name that identifies a person for legal, administrative and other official purposes. A person's legal name typically is their given name and surname as recorded at birth, though the precise rules for determining and changing a legal name vary widely between jurisdictions.

Identification

In most countries, a person's first legal name is assigned shortly after birth and recorded on a birth certificate. The legal name is then used on passports, driving licences, bank accounts, contracts and court documents. Names used in everyday life — nicknames, pseudonyms, stage names and assumed names — may differ from the legal name without necessarily having legal effect.

By jurisdiction

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom there is no single concept of a "legal name" in statute; a person's name is a matter of fact and usage. An adult may change their name simply by using a new one, though a deed poll or statutory declaration is commonly used as documentary evidence of the change.

United States

In the United States, name law is largely a matter of state law and common law tradition. A person may generally adopt a new name by consistent usage, provided the change is not made for a fraudulent purpose, but a court order is often obtained to update official records. Marriage and divorce are common occasions for legal name changes.

Civil law countries

Many civil law jurisdictions regulate names more strictly. In France, given names were historically restricted by law, and changes require official approval. Germany regulates names through its civil code and registry offices, and several countries maintain lists of permitted given names, as in Iceland, where the Icelandic Naming Committee approves names for newborns.

Name change

A legal change of name may be sought for many reasons: marriage, divorce, adoption, personal preference, religious conversion, gender transition, or to escape stigma or danger. Procedures range from simple administrative filings to formal court petitions, and some jurisdictions restrict changes intended to deceive creditors or evade law enforcement.

Legal persons

Companies and other legal entities also have legal names, registered at incorporation, which may differ from their trading names ("doing business as" names). The legal name of a business appears on contracts, tax filings and regulatory documents.
