Treaty of London (1913)

The Treaty of London was signed on 30 May 1913, at the close of the First Balkan War, between the Ottoman Empire and the Balkan League (Bulgaria, Serbia, Greece, and Montenegro). Negotiated under the supervision of the Great Powers at the Conference of London, it ended hostilities and redrew the map of the Balkans, reducing Ottoman territory in Europe to a small area around Istanbul.

Terms

The Ottomans ceded all territory west of the Enez–Kıyıköy (Midye) line to the Balkan League, including Adrianople (Edirne) and most of Macedonia and Thrace, and recognized the independence of Albania, created under Great Power pressure to block Serbia's access to the Adriatic. Crete went to Greece, while the Aegean islands' fate was left to the Powers. Because the treaty left the division of Macedonia among the victors unresolved, Bulgaria attacked Serbia and Greece within weeks, launching the Second Balkan War, whose settlement at Bucharest superseded much of London's allocation.
