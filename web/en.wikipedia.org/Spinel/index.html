Spinel is the magnesium/aluminium member of the larger spinel group of minerals, with the formula MgAl2O4. It crystallises in the isometric system, commonly as octahedra. Its name comes from the Latin spina ("thorn"), after its pointed crystals. Spinel gives its name to the spinel structure, a widespread crystal structure type (AB2O4) adopted by many oxides, including magnetite.

As a gemstone

Transparent spinel in rich reds and pinks was historically mined with ruby in Badakhshan (Afghanistan/Tajikistan), Burma's Mogok valley and Sri Lanka, and for centuries red spinels were classified as rubies ("balas rubies"). Several of the world's most famous "rubies" are in fact spinels: the Black Prince's Ruby in the British Imperial State Crown, the Timur Ruby necklace, and the great spinel atop the Russian imperial crown. Spinel was only recognised as a distinct mineral species in the late 18th century. Fine red, cobalt-blue, and neon pink ("Jedi") spinels are now highly prized in their own right. In 2016 spinel was added as an alternative birthstone for August.

Occurrence

Spinel forms in metamorphosed limestones and dolomites and in aluminium-rich igneous rocks; gem gravels in Myanmar, Vietnam, Tanzania and Sri Lanka yield much of the gem material. Synthetic spinel, grown since the early 20th century, is used in jewellery and for technical applications, and spinel ceramics serve as transparent armour and refractory materials.
