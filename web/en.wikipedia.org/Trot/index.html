Trot (gait)
TypeTwo-beat diagonal gait
FootfallsDiagonal pairs move together
BetweenWalk and canter/gallop

The trot is a two-beat diagonal gait of horses and other four-legged animals in which the diagonal pairs of legs move forward at the same time with a moment of suspension between beats. It is faster than a walk but slower than a canter or gallop.

In equestrianism

Riders may ride the trot either "sitting" (remaining in the saddle through the suspension) or "posting" (rising and sitting in rhythm with the gait, known as the rising trot). Variants include the working, collected, medium and extended trot in dressage, and the faster road trot and racing trot used in harness racing, where Standardbred "trotters" race at the gait.

Other animals

Dogs and many other quadrupeds trot as their default travelling gait; some horse breeds instead perform ambling gaits such as the pace, in which lateral pairs move together.
