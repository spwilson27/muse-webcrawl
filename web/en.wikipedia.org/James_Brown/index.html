James Joseph Brown (May 3, 1933 – December 25, 2006) was an American singer, dancer and musician. The central progenitor of funk music and a major figure of 20th-century popular music, he is referred to by various nicknames, among them the "Godfather of Soul", "Mr. Dynamite" and "Soul Brother No. 1". In a career spanning over 50 years, he influenced the development of several music genres and is one of the most sampled artists in popular music.

Early life and career beginnings

Brown was born in Barnwell, South Carolina, and grew up in poverty in Augusta, Georgia. He began singing in gospel groups, then joined the vocal group the Avons, which became the Flames and later the Famous Flames. With them he recorded "Please, Please, Please" (1956), his first hit, and the landmark Live at the Apollo (1963), which is regarded as one of the greatest live albums ever made.

Funk and fame

In the mid-1960s Brown developed his signature sound — emphasizing the first beat of the bar ("the One") and treating every instrument as percussion. Songs such as "Papa's Got a Brand New Bag" (1965), "I Got You (I Feel Good)" (1965) and "Cold Sweat" (1967) effectively created funk. In 1968 he released "Say It Loud – I'm Black and I'm Proud", which became an anthem of the civil rights and Black pride movements. His 1970s hits with his new band, the J.B.'s, included "Get Up (I Feel Like Being a) Sex Machine" and "The Payback".

Brown's demanding rehearsal discipline — he famously fined band members for mistakes on stage — and his electrifying live act, featuring cape routines, dance steps and splits, made him "the hardest working man in show business". His grooves later became foundational samples for hip hop.

Later life and death

Brown's career declined in the late 1970s amid disco, tax problems and the death of his son Teddy in 1973. He enjoyed a resurgence in the 1980s with "Living in America" (1985). Legal troubles, including a widely publicized 1988 police chase, marred his later years. He died of congestive heart failure complicated by pneumonia on Christmas Day 2006 in Atlanta.

Legacy

Brown was among the first inductees into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1986 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 1992. Rolling Stone has ranked him among the greatest artists of all time, and his influence extends across soul, funk, hip hop, and pop — from Michael Jackson and Prince to countless hip hop producers.
