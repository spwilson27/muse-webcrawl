Svante Pääbo

Born20 April 1955, Stockholm, Sweden
FieldsGenetics, evolutionary biology
AwardsNobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (2022)

Svante Pääbo (born 20 April 1955) is a Swedish geneticist specialising in evolutionary genetics. He is one of the founders of paleogenetics and is best known for sequencing the Neanderthal genome and discovering the Denisovans.

Career

Pääbo founded and directed the Department of Genetics at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig. His team published the draft Neanderthal genome in 2010, showing that Neanderthals interbred with ancestors of modern non-African humans, and in the same year identified the previously unknown Denisovan lineage from a Siberian finger bone.

Honours

In 2022 he received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine "for his discoveries concerning the genomes of extinct hominins and human evolution". His memoir Neanderthal Man: In Search of Lost Genomes appeared in 2014. His mother was the chemist Karin Pääbo; his father, Sune Bergström, also won a Nobel Prize.
