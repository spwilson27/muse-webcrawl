The Russian Olympic Committee (ROC; Russian: Олимпийский комитет России) is the National Olympic Committee representing Russia. It was created in 1911 and recognised by the International Olympic Committee in 1912; dissolved after the Russian Revolution, it was re-established in the final years of the Soviet Union and confirmed as the successor committee of the Russian Federation after 1992.

Doping sanctions and neutral participation

Following revelations of a state-sponsored doping programme — detailed in the McLaren reports of 2016, including manipulation of samples at the Sochi 2014 Winter Olympics — the IOC suspended the ROC in December 2017. Russian athletes competed as "Olympic Athletes from Russia" at PyeongChang 2018, and, after a further CAS ruling reduced a WADA ban, as the team of the "Russian Olympic Committee" (using the acronym ROC) at Tokyo 2020 and Beijing 2022, without flag or anthem.

2023 suspension

In October 2023 the IOC suspended the ROC again, after it incorporated sports organisations from Ukrainian regions annexed by Russia. Russian and Belarusian athletes were later cleared to compete at the Paris 2024 Games only as Individual Neutral Athletes.
