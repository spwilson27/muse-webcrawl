Music education

Music education is a field of practice and research concerned with the teaching and learning of music. It includes instruction in performing instruments and singing, music theory, composition, music history and appreciation, and is delivered in schools, conservatories, community programmes and private lessons.

History

Music held a central place in education from antiquity — it was one of the seven liberal arts of the medieval curriculum, part of the quadrivium alongside arithmetic, geometry and astronomy. In the 19th century, figures such as Lowell Mason introduced music into American public schools, while in Europe national conservatoire systems professionalized training.

Methods

Influential approaches include the Kodály method of Zoltán Kodály, which builds musicianship through singing and folk song; the Orff Schulwerk of Carl Orff, emphasizing improvisation and elemental music-making with percussion; the Dalcroze method, which teaches rhythm through movement; and the Suzuki method, which models instrumental learning on first-language acquisition, with children learning by ear from an early age.

In schools

School music typically spans general music classes, choirs, bands and orchestras. Advocates cite links between music study and language, mathematics and social development, though music's place in the curriculum is frequently contested when education budgets are cut.
