Francisco José de Goya y Lucientes (30 March 1746 – 16 April 1828) was a Spanish romantic painter and printmaker. He is considered the most important Spanish artist of the late 18th and early 19th centuries, and throughout his long career he was a commentator and chronicler of his era. Immensely successful in his lifetime, Goya is often referred to as both the last of the Old Masters and the first of the moderns.

Life and career

Goya was born in Fuendetodos, Aragon, and studied painting in Zaragoza and Madrid, travelling to Italy in 1771. He rose through the court of Madrid: he designed tapestries for the Royal Tapestry Factory, was appointed painter to the king in 1786, and became First Court Painter in 1799 under Charles IV. His portraits of the royal family, most famously Charles IV of Spain and His Family (1800–1801), combine official grandeur with unsparing psychological candour.

A serious illness in 1792–93 left Goya completely deaf, and his art darkened. In 1799 he published Los Caprichos, a series of 80 etchings satirising the follies, superstition, and corruption of Spanish society, including the famous plate "The Sleep of Reason Produces Monsters". During the Peninsular War against Napoleonic France he painted his masterpieces The Second of May 1808 and The Third of May 1808 (1814), unflinching depictions of war and execution, and produced the etching series The Disasters of War.

Late work

In his final years at the Quinta del Sordo ("Villa of the Deaf Man") outside Madrid, Goya painted directly onto the walls the fourteen so-called Black Paintings (c. 1819–1823) — among them Saturn Devouring His Son — works of nightmarish intensity never intended for public view. Disillusioned with the restored absolutist regime of Ferdinand VII, he went into self-imposed exile in Bordeaux in 1824, where he died in 1828. His remains were later transferred to Madrid.

Legacy

Goya's work spans court portraiture, religious painting, tapestry cartoons, satirical prints, and private visions, and he is regarded as a precursor of modern art, influencing artists from Manet and Picasso to the expressionists. The Museo del Prado in Madrid holds the world's largest collection of his paintings and prints.
