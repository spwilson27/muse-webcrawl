Chen-style tai chi

Chen-style tai chi (Chen-style t'ai chi ch'uan) is the oldest of the five traditional family styles of tai chi, originating in Chen Village (Chenjiagou), Wen County, Henan, China, in the 17th century. It is traditionally credited to Chen Wangting, a Ming dynasty general.

Chen style is characterized by alternating slow and explosive movements (fajin), low stances, silk-reeling (chansigong) spiral energy training, and a combination of softness and hardness. Its principal forms include Laojia (Old Frame) First and Second Routines, Xinjia (New Frame), cannon fist, and weapons forms.

All other major styles — Yang, Wu, Wu (Hao), and Sun — derive directly or indirectly from Chen style, principally through Yang Luchan, who learned in Chen Village in the 19th century.
