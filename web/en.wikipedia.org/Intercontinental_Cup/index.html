The Intercontinental Cup, officially the European/South American Cup and later the Toyota Cup, was an international football competition contested between the winners of the European Cup (later UEFA Champions League) and the Copa Libertadores, effectively deciding the world club championship from 1960 to 2004.

History

The competition was first held in 1960, when Real Madrid defeated Peñarol of Uruguay. For its first two decades the tie was played over two legs, home and away, producing famous — and sometimes violent — encounters such as Celtic versus Racing Club (1967) and AC Milan versus Estudiantes (1969). Several European champions declined to participate in the 1970s, and runners-up sometimes took their place. From 1980, under Toyota's sponsorship, the Cup became a single match held in Japan, first at the National Stadium in Tokyo and later in Yokohama.

Final years and successors

The last edition was played in 2004, when Porto defeated Once Caldas on penalties. FIFA's expanded Club World Cup superseded the competition from 2005; in 2017 FIFA officially recognized all Intercontinental Cup winners as club world champions. In 2024 FIFA launched a new annual competition also named the Intercontinental Cup, featuring the champions of all six confederations.

Notable winners

Five clubs won the trophy three times: AC Milan, Real Madrid, Boca Juniors, Nacional, and Peñarol. Pelé's Santos won twice in 1962 and 1963, and Ajax, Bayern Munich, and Juventus each won twice. AC Milan's 1989–90 back-to-back victories under Arrigo Sacchi marked the European high point of the one-off era in Japan.
