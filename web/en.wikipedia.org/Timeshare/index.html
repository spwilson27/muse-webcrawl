Timeshare

A timeshare (sometimes called a vacation ownership or vacation club) is a property with a divided form of ownership or use rights. These properties are typically resort condominium units, in which multiple parties hold rights to use the property, and each owner of the same accommodation is allotted their period of time. Units may be sold as a partial ownership, lease, or "right to use", in which case the latter holds no claim to ownership of the property. The ownership of timeshare programs is varied, and has been changing over the decades.

History

The concept originated in Europe in the 1960s. One of the first timeshares was at a ski resort in the French Alps in 1963, where the developer sold shares with the slogan "No need to rent the room; buy the hotel, it's cheaper!" The model spread to the United Kingdom and then, in the 1970s, to the United States, where developers used it to sell condominium inventory after a real-estate downturn. Major hospitality companies such as Marriott, Hilton, and Disney later entered the business, and exchange networks such as RCI (founded 1974) and Interval International allowed owners to swap weeks at different resorts.

Types

  • Fixed week – the owner uses the same unit during the same week every year.
  • Floating week – the owner may reserve any week within a season.
  • Rotating/flex – weeks rotate among owners.
  • Points-based clubs – owners hold points spendable on reservations at many resorts, of varying sizes and seasons.
  • Fractional ownership – larger shares (e.g., a month or more), usually in higher-end properties.

Criticism

Timeshares are frequently criticized for aggressive, high-pressure sales presentations; annual maintenance fees that rise over time; difficulty reselling (interests often fetch far less than their purchase price); and the difficulty of exiting contracts. Regulators in many jurisdictions impose cooling-off periods during which buyers may rescind a purchase. Consumer advocates generally advise against viewing timeshares as financial investments.
