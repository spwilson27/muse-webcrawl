Ghanaian cuisine

Ghanaian cuisine is the national cuisine of Ghana, centred on starchy staples such as cassava, yam, plantain, maize, and rice, typically served with richly flavoured soups and stews made from tomatoes, peppers, onions, palm nut, groundnuts (peanuts), okra, and leafy greens, together with fish, chicken, goat, or beef. Meals are often eaten communally and traditionally with the right hand.

Staples and dishes

The best-known dish is fufu, a smooth dough of pounded cassava and plantain (or yam), served with soups such as light soup, palm nut soup, or groundnut soup. Other staples include banku and kenkey, fermented maize and cassava doughs usually served with fried fish and hot pepper sauce; waakye, rice and beans cooked with millet leaves; red red, black-eyed peas in palm oil served with fried plantain; and Ghanaian jollof rice, a tomato-based one-pot rice dish whose rivalry with Nigerian jollof is a matter of national pride. Tuo zaafi is common in the north, while kelewele (spiced fried plantain), chichinga (kebabs), and grilled tilapia with shito hot pepper sauce are popular street foods.

Soups and stews

Groundnut (peanut) stew, palm nut soup, okra stew, garden egg stew, and kontomire stew made from cocoyam leaves are widely eaten, often with smoked or dried fish providing much of the flavouring. Ginger, grains of paradise, and hot chillies are characteristic seasonings.
