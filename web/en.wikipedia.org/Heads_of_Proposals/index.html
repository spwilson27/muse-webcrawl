Heads of Proposals

The Heads of the Proposals were the New Model Army's constitutional settlement offer of 1647.

The Heads of the Proposals was a set of constitutional proposals drawn up in 1647 by officers of the New Model Army — principally Henry Ireton with input from John Lambert and the Army's agitators — and presented to the captive King Charles I as a basis for settling the English Civil War. They are often described as the most generous terms offered to the king during the conflict and an important early statement of constitutional ideas.

Background

After Parliament's victory in the First English Civil War, conflict erupted between Parliament and its own army over arrears of pay, indemnity, and the political settlement. The army occupied London's outskirts in the summer of 1647, seized the king from Holmby House, and sought terms directly with him. The proposals were drafted at the army's headquarters and read to Charles at Woburn in July 1647.

Contents

The Heads proposed: biennial parliaments with fixed durations and redistribution of seats more closely in proportion to population (an early step toward electoral reform); parliamentary control of the militia for a term of years and of appointments to great offices of state; reform of the law and the exchequer; religious toleration for most Protestants with no compulsion in worship and repeal of penalties for non-attendance, while retaining bishops shorn of coercive power; an act of oblivion with limited exceptions for leading royalists; and restoration of the king, queen, and royal family to their rights and revenues. Charles would not be required to accept the Presbyterian church settlement Parliament had voted.

Outcome

Charles temporized and secretly negotiated with the Scots, leading to the Engagement and the Second Civil War of 1648. Within the army, the Levellers attacked the Heads as too favourable to the king and advanced their own Agreement of the People; the confrontation between the officers ("Grandees") and the agitators culminated in the Putney Debates of October–November 1647. The Heads of the Proposals were never enacted, but historians regard them as a significant milestone in the development of written constitutionalism and limited monarchy in England.
