Group B

Group B was a set of regulations for grand touring (GT) cars used in sports car racing and rallying, introduced by the FIA in 1982. In rallying, Group B produced some of the most powerful and fastest rally cars ever built, and the category is remembered both for spectacular competition and for the fatal accidents that led to its abolition after 1986.

Regulations

The rules required only 200 road-going examples of a car to be built for homologation, and permitted extensive modification, turbocharging and lightweight construction. With few restrictions on engine power, outputs rose beyond 500 horsepower in cars weighing around 900 kilograms.

Rallying era

Famous Group B cars include the Audi Quattro, Peugeot 205 Turbo 16, Lancia 037 and Delta S4, Ford RS200 and MG Metro 6R4. The all-wheel-drive Audi Quattro revolutionised rallying, and the era drew enormous crowds. A series of deadly accidents in 1985 and 1986, culminating in the deaths of Henri Toivonen and Sergio Cresto in Corsica, prompted the FIA to cancel Group B at the end of 1986.

Legacy

Group B cars remain icons of motorsport, celebrated at historic events and commanding high prices at auction, while the period is often described as the golden age — and the cautionary tale — of rallying.
