Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem

The Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem is a far-reaching generalisation of the classical Riemann–Roch theorem for complex manifolds. Proved by Friedrich Hirzebruch in his 1954 doctoral work, it computes the holomorphic Euler characteristic of a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact complex manifold as an integral of characteristic classes: χ(X, E) = ∫X ch(E) · td(X), where ch is the Chern character and td is the Todd class.

Background

The classical Riemann–Roch theorem relates the dimension of the space of meromorphic functions on a Riemann surface to its genus. Riemann's inequality and Roch's correction term were recast in the 20th century using sheaf cohomology, allowing Hirzebruch to apply his theory of multiplicative sequences and genera to obtain a formula in all dimensions.

Significance

The theorem was a milestone in modern algebraic geometry and a direct precursor of Alexander Grothendieck's Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem and of the Atiyah–Singer index theorem. The special case for the tangent bundle yields the Hirzebruch signature theorem and relations involving Pontryagin and Chern numbers.
