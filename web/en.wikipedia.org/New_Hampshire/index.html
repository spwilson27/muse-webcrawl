New Hampshire

New Hampshire is a state in the New England region of the northeastern United States. It borders Maine to the east, Massachusetts to the south, Vermont to the west, and the Canadian province of Quebec to the north, with a short Atlantic coastline of about 29 km (18 mi) — the smallest of any US coastal state. With an area of 24,214 km² and a population of about 1.4 million, it is the tenth-least populous state. Its capital is Concord and its largest city is Manchester. The state motto, "Live Free or Die", reflects its tradition of individualism and appears on its license plates.

New Hampshire takes its name from the English county of Hampshire. It was one of the original Thirteen Colonies and, in January 1776, became the first to establish an independent government and constitution; it ratified the US Constitution in June 1788 as the ninth state, putting the document into effect. The state is famous in American politics for holding the first primary in the US presidential election cycle, a position protected by state law.

Geography and economy

The state is dominated by the White Mountains, part of the Appalachians, including Mount Washington (1,917 m), whose summit recorded some of the strongest surface winds ever measured. Tourism — skiing, hiking, lakes in the Lakes Region around Lake Winnipesaukee, and autumn foliage — is a leading industry, along with manufacturing, health care and high technology. New Hampshire has neither a general sales tax nor an income tax on wages, relying heavily on property taxes, and consistently ranks among the states with the highest median household income and lowest poverty rates. Its 400-member House of Representatives is one of the largest legislative bodies in the English-speaking world.
