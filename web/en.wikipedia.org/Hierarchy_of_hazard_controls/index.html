Hierarchy of hazard controls

Hierarchy of hazard control is a system used in industry to minimize or eliminate exposure to hazards. It is a widely accepted system promoted by numerous safety organizations. This concept is taught to managers in industry, to be promoted as standard practice in the workplace. It has also been used to inform public policy, in fields such as road safety. It is often represented as an inverted pyramid or triangle, with the most effective controls at the top.

Levels of the hierarchy
1 (most effective)Elimination
2Substitution
3Engineering controls
4Administrative controls
5 (least effective)Personal protective equipment

The levels

Elimination physically removes the hazard and is the most effective control. Substitution replaces the hazard with a safer alternative. Engineering controls isolate people from the hazard, for example through ventilation or machine guarding. Administrative controls change the way people work, through procedures, training and signage. Personal protective equipment (PPE) — gloves, respirators, eye protection — is the least effective because it depends on correct human use and does not remove the hazard.

Application

The hierarchy is embedded in occupational safety and health practice, including guidance from the US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) and the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA). Controls higher in the hierarchy are preferred because they do not rely on human behaviour to be effective.
