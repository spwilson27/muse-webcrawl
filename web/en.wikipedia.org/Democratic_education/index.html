Democratic education

Democratic education is an educational ideal in which democracy is both a goal and a method of instruction. It brings democratic values to education and includes self-determination within a community of equals, as well as democratic decision-making in the governance of schools, with students and staff sharing power over learning, teaching and discipline.

History

The ideas of democratic education trace back to thinkers such as John Dewey, whose Democracy and Education (1916) argued that schools should model democratic life. Practical experiments followed, most famously A. S. Neill's Summerhill School in England (founded 1921) and the Sudbury Valley School in Massachusetts (founded 1968), where students govern school rules through school meetings in which every member, regardless of age, has one vote.

Principles

Democratic schools typically feature: a school meeting with authority over rules and budgets; self-directed learning, with students free to choose their activities; age mixing; and judicial or peer processes for resolving conflicts. Critics question the quality of academic outcomes and preparation for conventional examinations, while supporters argue the model develops autonomy, responsibility and civic skills.
