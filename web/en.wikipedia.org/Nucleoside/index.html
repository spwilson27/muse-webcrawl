Nucleoside

A nucleoside is a molecule consisting of a nitrogenous base (a purine or pyrimidine) joined to a five-carbon sugar, either ribose or deoxyribose, by a glycosidic bond. When one or more phosphate groups are attached, the molecule becomes a nucleotide, the building block of DNA and RNA.

Types

Ribonucleosides include adenosine, guanosine, cytidine and uridine; deoxyribonucleosides include deoxyadenosine, deoxyguanosine, deoxycytidine and thymidine. In nucleoside nomenclature the base attaches at the 1′ carbon of the sugar.

Function and medicine

Beyond their role in nucleic acids, nucleosides and their analogues are important in medicine. Modified nucleosides such as zidovudine (AZT) and aciclovir act as antiviral drugs by interfering with viral replication, and nucleoside analogues are also used in cancer chemotherapy.
