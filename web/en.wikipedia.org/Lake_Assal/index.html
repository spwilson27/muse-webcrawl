Lake Assal

Location: Djibouti
Type: crater lake, endorheic
Surface area: 54 km²
Max. depth: 40 m
Surface elevation: −155 m (lowest point in Africa)
Salinity: approx. 348 g/L

Lake Assal (Arabic: بحيرة عسل) is a crater lake in central Djibouti, in the Afar Depression at the western end of the Gulf of Tadjoura.

At 155 metres below sea level, its shore is the lowest point of land in Africa and the third-lowest on Earth, after the shores of the Dead Sea and the Sea of Galilee. The lake has no outflow, and with high evaporation its salinity of about 34.8% makes it one of the saltiest bodies of water in the world.

Geology and climate

The lake lies within the Afar Triangle, where the African continent is slowly rifting apart. The area is among the hottest inhabited places on Earth, with summer temperatures frequently exceeding 50 °C in the surrounding Danakil Depression.

Salt industry

Salt has been extracted from the lake's shores for centuries by Afar caravans and is still mined commercially; thick salt banks line the western shore, and salt production remains an important local industry.
