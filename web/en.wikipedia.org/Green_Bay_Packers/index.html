Green Bay Packers

The Green Bay Packers are a professional American football team based in Green Bay, Wisconsin. Founded in 1919 by Earl "Curly" Lambeau and George Calhoun, the Packers joined the National Football League (NFL) in 1921 and are the third-oldest franchise in the league. They compete in the NFL's National Football Conference North division and play their home games at Lambeau Field. Green Bay is by far the smallest major-league professional sports market in North America, yet the team is uniquely owned by its fans as a publicly held nonprofit corporation.

Lambeau Field, home of the Packers since 1957

History

Under Lambeau and later coach Vince Lombardi, the Packers became one of the most successful franchises in NFL history. They have won 13 league championships, the most of any team, including nine NFL championships before the Super Bowl era and four Super Bowls (I, II, XXXI and XLV). Lombardi's teams won the first two Super Bowls, and the Super Bowl trophy is named in his honour. Legendary players include Bart Starr, Brett Favre and Aaron Rodgers.

Fan ownership and culture

The Packers are the only community-owned major-league team in the United States; shares of stock pay no dividends and cannot appreciate in value. The waiting list for season tickets numbers in the tens of thousands, and every home game has sold out since 1960. The team's green and gold colours and its loyal "Cheesehead" fanbase are iconic in American sport.
