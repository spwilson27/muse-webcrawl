War of Canudos

The War of Canudos (1896–1897) was a conflict between the Brazilian Republic and the inhabitants of Canudos, a settlement in the arid backlands (sertão) of Bahia founded by the itinerant preacher Antônio Conselheiro. It ended with the complete destruction of the settlement and the deaths of most of its inhabitants.

Background

Canudos attracted thousands of poor sertanejos drawn by Conselheiro's religious teaching and his denunciation of the new republic proclaimed in 1889. Viewing the settlement as a monarchist threat, Bahia and federal authorities sent four successive military expeditions. The first three were defeated by the jagunços, embarrassing the young republic.

Destruction and legacy

A fourth expedition of some 8,000–10,000 troops with artillery finally took Canudos in October 1897; Conselheiro had died of illness shortly before, and the settlement was burned and demolished. The journalist Euclides da Cunha, who covered the campaign, wrote the classic account Os Sertões (1902; Rebellion in the Backlands), one of the greatest works of Brazilian literature.
