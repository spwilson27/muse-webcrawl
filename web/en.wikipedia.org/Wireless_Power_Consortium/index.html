Wireless Power Consortium

The Wireless Power Consortium (WPC) is a multinational technology consortium formed in 2008 that develops and promotes the Qi standard for inductive and resonant wireless charging of portable devices. Its members include major electronics and semiconductor companies, and Qi certification has become a common feature of smartphones and accessories.

Qi standard

Qi (pronounced "chee") transfers power over distances of a few millimetres between a transmitter coil in a charging pad or stand and a receiver coil in the device, using magnetic induction. The original standard delivered up to 5 watts; the Extended Power Profile raised this to 15 watts for faster phone charging. In 2023 the consortium announced Qi2, which adds a Magnetic Power Profile based on magnetic alignment — influenced by Apple's MagSafe — intended to improve efficiency and alignment and to unify magnetic wireless charging across brands.

Other work

Beyond Qi, the consortium has worked on the Ki standard for cordless kitchen appliances, intended to deliver much higher power through countertops, and on specifications for medium-power applications. The WPC runs certification testing through authorised laboratories so that products bearing the Qi logo interoperate safely.
