Vernal, Utah

Vernal is a city in and the county seat of Uintah County, Utah, United States. It is located in northeastern Utah in the Uintah Basin, west of the Colorado border, and had a population of roughly 10,000 as of the 2020 census.

Vernal was settled in 1876 and was unusual among Utah settlements of the era in that it was founded by a captain of the Mormon militia rather than by direct direction of Brigham Young. The town was first called "the Bench" and was renamed Vernal in 1893, from the Latin word for "spring-like."

Economy and attractions

Vernal's economy is based on the extraction of natural resources, including petroleum, natural gas, phosphate, and gilsonite, as well as on agriculture and tourism. The area is famous for its dinosaur fossils: nearby Dinosaur National Monument and the Utah Field House of Natural History State Park Museum draw visitors, and the town is known for dinosaur-themed signage and the pink fiberglass dinosaur "Dinah" that greets travelers. Unlike most of Utah, the Vernal area was not settled by Mormon pioneers as part of a church-directed colonization effort, giving the town a somewhat distinct history.
