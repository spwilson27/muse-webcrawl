The Nansen Refugee Award is an annual humanitarian award presented by the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) to an individual, group, or organisation in recognition of outstanding service to the cause of refugees, displaced, or stateless people.

History

Established in 1954, the award honours the legacy of Fridtjof Nansen, the Norwegian explorer and first League of Nations High Commissioner for Refugees, whose "Nansen passports" gave stateless people identity documents after the First World War. The first recipient was Eleanor Roosevelt. Laureates receive a medal and a monetary prize funded by the governments of Norway and Switzerland, used to support a refugee project.

Laureates

Recipients have included individuals such as Graça Machel, Luciano Pavarotti (through his charitable work), the humanitarian organisation Médecins Sans Frontières (1988? — organisational laureates include the International Council of Voluntary Agencies), and community-level heroes honoured through regional awards introduced in recent years. Since 2017, regional winners from Africa, the Americas, Asia-Pacific, Europe, and the Middle East have also been celebrated alongside the global laureate.
