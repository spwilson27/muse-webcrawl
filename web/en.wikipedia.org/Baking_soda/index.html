Baking soda is the common name for sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3), a white crystalline chemical compound widely used as a leavening agent in baking, as well as in cooking, cleaning, medicine, and fire suppression. In baking, it reacts with acidic ingredients — such as buttermilk, yogurt, lemon juice, vinegar, or cream of tartar — releasing carbon dioxide gas, whose bubbles expand in heat and cause doughs and batters to rise. It is the principal active ingredient of baking powder, which combines it with a dry acid and a starch.

Chemistry

Sodium bicarbonate is amphoteric: it neutralizes acids, forming a salt and water while evolving CO2, and it reacts with bases as well. When heated above about 80–100 °C it decomposes into sodium carbonate, water, and carbon dioxide, which is why recipes relying on baking soda alone need some acidity for full effect and to avoid a soapy, alkaline taste. Industrially it is produced mainly by the Solvay process, and it also occurs naturally as the mineral nahcolite, mined notably in Colorado.

Culinary uses

Beyond baking, baking soda is used to tenderize, to neutralize excess acidity in tomato sauces, to give pretzels and bagel-like products part of their character when used in a boiling bath (usually in stronger alkaline form), in some Asian noodle preparations, and as an ingredient in homemade effervescent drinks.

Other uses

Its mild alkalinity, gentle abrasiveness, and deodorizing ability make sodium bicarbonate a popular household cleaner and refrigerator deodorizer. Medically it is used as an antacid and, intravenously, to treat certain cases of metabolic acidosis and drug toxicity. It is also a component of some dry-chemical fire extinguishers, toothpastes, and bath products.

History

Ancient Egyptians used natron, a natural mixture containing sodium bicarbonate, for cleaning and mummification. Commercial production was pioneered in the 1840s by John Dwight and Austin Church in the United States, whose Arm & Hammer brand (from Church & Dwight) became synonymous with baking soda.
