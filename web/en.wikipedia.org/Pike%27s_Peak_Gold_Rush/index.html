Pike's Peak Gold Rush

Dates
1858–1861
Location
South Platte River and Front Range, present-day Colorado
Slogan
"Pike's Peak or Bust"

The Pike's Peak Gold Rush (later known as the Colorado Gold Rush) was the boom in gold prospecting and mining in the Pike's Peak Country of western Kansas Territory and southwestern Nebraska Territory from July 1858 to February 1861, when Colorado Territory was created. It followed modest gold discoveries along the South Platte River near present-day Denver.

Course of the rush

News of gold found by the Russell party in 1858 drew an estimated 100,000 fortune seekers — the "Fifty-Niners" — westward in 1859, many painting "Pike's Peak or Bust" on their wagons. Rich strikes at Gregory Gulch (Central City), Idaho Springs, and Breckenridge made the region a major producer, though many disappointed prospectors returned east as "go-backs." Boomtown Denver City and Auraria merged into Denver, which survived as the region's supply hub.

Consequences

The rush accelerated the dispossession of the Cheyenne and Arapaho, to whom the land had been guaranteed by treaty, and contributed to the tensions that culminated in the Colorado War of 1864. Placer gold soon gave way to hard-rock mining and smelting, laying the economic foundation of Colorado.
