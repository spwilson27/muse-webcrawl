Ishmael

Ishmael (Hebrew: Yišmāʿēʾl, "God hears"; Arabic: Ismāʿīl) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible and the Quran, the first son of Abraham, born to Hagar, the Egyptian maidservant of Abraham's wife Sarah. In Genesis he is the ancestor of the Ishmaelites, traditionally associated with the peoples of northern Arabia.

In Genesis

When Sarah remained childless, she gave Hagar to Abraham as a concubine; Hagar's pregnancy led to conflict, and she fled, returning after an angelic encounter. After the birth of Isaac, Sarah demanded that Hagar and Ishmael be sent away. In the wilderness of Beersheba, God heard the boy's cry and promised to make of him a great nation. Genesis lists twelve sons of Ishmael, described as princes of twelve tribes, and records that he died aged 137.

In Islam

In Islamic tradition Ishmael (Ismail) is a prophet who, with his father Abraham (Ibrahim), raised the foundations of the Kaaba in Mecca. The festival of Eid al-Adha commemorates Abraham's willingness to sacrifice his son — identified in the Quran's commentary tradition with Ishmael.

In literature

The name is famously borne by the narrator of Herman Melville's Moby-Dick, who opens with the line "Call me Ishmael." See also Isaiah.
