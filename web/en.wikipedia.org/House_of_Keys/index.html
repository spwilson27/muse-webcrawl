The House of Keys (Manx: Yn Kiare as Feed) is the directly elected lower house of the Tynwald, the parliament of the Isle of Man. Its 24 members, known as Members of the House of Keys (MHKs), are elected from 12 two-member constituencies.

History

The House of Keys traces its origins to the 14th century and is part of one of the oldest continuous parliamentary bodies in the world. The origin of the name is uncertain; one theory derives "Keys" from the Manx ceathair as feed ("four and twenty"), referring to its 24 members. Before 1866 the Keys were a self-electing body; the House of Keys Election Act 1866 introduced popular elections. Women were enfranchised in 1881 — the Isle of Man was the first place in the world to give women the vote in a national election — and Emmeline Pankhurst's mother was among those on the register.

Functions

Most legislation originates in the House of Keys, and it elects the Chief Minister of the Isle of Man. The upper house is the Legislative Council. Together with the Legislative Council, the Keys sit as the Tynwald Court to pass legislation, which requires Royal Assent.
