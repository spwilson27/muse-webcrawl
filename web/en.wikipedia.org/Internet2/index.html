Internet2 is an American not-for-profit consortium led by members from the research and education communities, industry and government. Founded in 1996, it operates the Internet2 Network, a high-capacity advanced technology network linking universities, research laboratories, schools, libraries and other institutions across the United States, with connections to peer research networks worldwide.

History

Internet2 was created by 34 university researchers in 1996, growing out of concern that the commercialization of the Internet after the decommissioning of the NSFNET backbone in 1995 would leave research needs unmet. Its early backbone was known as the very-high-speed Backbone Network Service (vBNS) and later as Abilene. The network has been repeatedly upgraded, reaching backbone capacities of 100 Gbit/s and beyond.

Services

Beyond connectivity, Internet2 provides services in areas such as federated identity management (including the InCommon federation), cloud services for campuses, security, and performance measurement. It is not a separate physical "internet" for the public, and it is not a successor to the public Internet; rather, technologies developed and tested on it — such as IPv6 deployment and multicast — often migrate to commercial networks.

Membership

Membership includes several hundred U.S. universities, dozens of regional and state education networks, affiliate nonprofits, industry partners and government research agencies.
