Tennessee State Capitol

Location600 Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd., Nashville, Tennessee
Built1845–1859
ArchitectWilliam Strickland
StyleGreek Revival

The Tennessee State Capitol, located in Nashville, Tennessee, is the seat of government for the U.S. state of Tennessee. It serves as the home of the Tennessee General Assembly and houses the governor's office. Designed by architect William Strickland in the Greek Revival style, it is one of the oldest working state capitols in the United States and a National Historic Landmark.

Construction

The cornerstone was laid on July 4, 1845, and the building was completed in 1859. It was built of Tennessee limestone by a workforce that included enslaved people and convict laborers, a history now acknowledged in interpretation on the site. Strickland, who died during construction in 1854, is entombed in the building's north wall, as is his son Francis, who supervised completion.

Features

Unlike most state capitols, it has no dome; instead a lantern-like tower modeled on the Choragic Monument of Lysicrates crowns the roof. The grounds contain the tombs of President James K. Polk and his wife Sarah, an equestrian statue of Andrew Jackson, and statues of Andrew Johnson and Alvin C. York.
