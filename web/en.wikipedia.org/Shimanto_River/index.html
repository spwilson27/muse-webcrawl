Shimanto River

The Shimanto River (四万十川, Shimanto-gawa) is a 196-kilometre river in Kōchi Prefecture on the island of Shikoku, Japan. It is celebrated as one of Japan's clearest and least developed major rivers, often called the country's "last pure stream" (saigo no seiryū).

Course and features

Rising on Mount Irazu, the river winds through mountain valleys and the Nakamura plain to the Pacific Ocean at Tosa Bay. Unusually for a major Japanese river, its main stem has no large dams. It is famous for its chinkabashi — low-water "sinking bridges" without railings, designed to submerge during floods rather than be washed away — some 47 of which cross the river and its tributaries.

Ecology and culture

The river supports traditional fisheries for ayu (sweetfish), eel, and freshwater shrimp, and riverside communities have strong conservation traditions. Cycling, canoeing, and tourism along its banks are popular, and the Shimanto is grouped with the Kakita and Nagara among Japan's renowned clear streams.
