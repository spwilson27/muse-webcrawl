ʻOro

ʻOro is a god in Polynesian mythology, particularly in the Society Islands. Regarded as a god of war and a son of the supreme god Ta'aroa, ʻOro became the principal deity of the Arioi cult and was worshipped at the great marae of Taputapuātea on Raiatea.

Cult

The cult of ʻOro spread from Raiatea throughout the Society Islands and beyond, carried by voyaging priests. His worship involved elaborate feathered images (toʻo) wrapped in sennit, and, at the height of the cult, human sacrifice. The Arioi, a society of travelling performers and priests, were dedicated to him.

Decline

The cult declined with the arrival of European contact and Christian missions in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, and the great images of ʻOro were destroyed or carried off to museums.
