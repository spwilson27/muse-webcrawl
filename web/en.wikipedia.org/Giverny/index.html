Giverny

Giverny (French pronunciation: [ʒivɛʁni]) is a commune in the Eure department in northern France. It is best known as the location of Claude Monet's garden and home, where the Impressionist painter lived from 1883 until his death in 1926. The village lies on the right bank of the River Seine, about 75 km (47 miles) west of Paris, near Vernon.

Monet's house and gardens

Monet rented, and later bought, a house in Giverny in 1883. He transformed its grounds into two celebrated gardens: the flower garden (the Clos Normand) in front of the house, and the water garden across the road, where he diverted a stream to create a pond planted with water lilies and crossed by a Japanese-style bridge. These gardens became his principal subject for the last thirty years of his life, most famously in the Water Lilies series.

Tourism

After decades of neglect, the property was restored and opened to the public in 1980 under the Fondation Claude Monet; it now receives several hundred thousand visitors each year. The village also hosts the Musée des Impressionnismes Giverny, and many American artists formed a colony in Giverny in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, drawn by Monet's presence.
