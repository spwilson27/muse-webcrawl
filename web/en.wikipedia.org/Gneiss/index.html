TypeMetamorphic rock (high grade)
TextureFoliated; gneissic banding

Gneiss (pronounced "nice") is a common, widely distributed type of high-grade metamorphic rock formed under high temperature and pressure from pre-existing igneous or sedimentary rocks. Its defining feature is gneissic banding: alternating layers of light-coloured minerals (quartz and feldspar) and dark minerals (mica, amphibole and hornblende), a foliation coarser than that of schist.

Formation

Gneiss forms by regional metamorphism deep in the crust, typically during mountain-building episodes where continental plates collide. The intense heat and directed pressure cause minerals to recrystallise and segregate into bands. Gneiss derived from granite is termed orthogneiss; that from sedimentary rock, paragneiss.

Occurrence

Gneisses dominate the ancient cores of continents (cratons). The Acasta Gneiss of Canada's Northwest Territories, dated to about 4 billion years, is among the oldest known rock on Earth. The Lewisian gneiss of north-west Scotland is another famous Precambrian complex.

Uses

Gneiss is quarried for dimension stone, paving and flooring; its banded appearance makes polished gneiss popular for worktops and cladding, sometimes marketed as granite.
