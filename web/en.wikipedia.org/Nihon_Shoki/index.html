Nihon Shoki

The Nihon Shoki (日本書紀, "The Chronicles of Japan"), sometimes translated as the Chronicles of Japan, is the second-oldest book of classical Japanese history. Completed in 720 CE under the editorship of Prince Toneri and Ō no Yasumaro, it is more elaborate and detailed than the earlier Kojiki (712 CE), and has proven to be an important tool for historians and archaeologists of early Japan.

Contents

Written in classical Chinese, the work comprises thirty volumes. It begins with the Japanese creation myth, recounting the Age of the Gods — the descent of the heavenly grandson Ninigi, grandfather of the legendary first emperor Jimmu — and continues through imperial reigns down to Empress Jitō (reigned 686–697). Unlike the Kojiki, it frequently presents variant accounts side by side, introduced by phrases such as "one book says".

Historical value

The Nihon Shoki supplies the chronology long used to date early Japanese events, though modern scholars regard its early dates as artificially extended to lend antiquity to the imperial line. It is a fundamental source for Shinto mythology, the reigns of the early emperors, and Japan's early relations with Korea and China. Together with the Kojiki and the provincial Fudoki, it forms one of the principal primary sources for ancient Japan.
