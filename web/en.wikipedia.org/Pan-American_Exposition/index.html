Pan-American Exposition

The Pan-American Exposition was a world's fair held in Buffalo, New York, United States, from May 1 through November 2, 1901. The fair occupied 350 acres of Delaware Park and surrounding land. It is best remembered as the site of the assassination of United States President William McKinley, who was shot by anarchist Leon Czolgosz on September 6, 1901, and died eight days later.

Planning and theme

Buffalo, then the eighth-largest city in the United States and a major rail and industrial hub, promoted the fair to celebrate commercial and cultural ties among the nations of the Americas following the Spanish–American War. Its theme was Pan-American unity, and its board of architects adopted a Spanish Renaissance ("Free Renaissance") style with buildings painted in a "Rainbow City" colour scheme, dramatically lit at night by electricity generated at Niagara Falls — a showcase of hydroelectric power and incandescent lighting.

Attractions

The 409-foot Electric Tower, covered with hundreds of thousands of bulbs, dominated the grounds. Exhibits included the Temple of Music, the Machinery and Transportation Building, ethnological villages, and early motion picture presentations. Buffalo native and inventor Thomas Edison's company filmed many scenes of the fair. About eight million people attended.

Assassination of McKinley

President McKinley delivered his final speech at the fair on September 5, advocating reciprocal trade agreements. The next day, during a public reception at the Temple of Music, Czolgosz shot him twice with a concealed revolver. McKinley died on September 14, and Vice President Theodore Roosevelt was sworn in as president in Buffalo. Ironically, an early X-ray machine was exhibited at the fair but was not used to locate the fatal bullet.

Legacy

Most fair buildings, built of staff (a plaster-and-fibre material), were demolished afterwards. The New York State Building, built of marble, survives as the Buffalo History Museum. The site is marked by a plaque and a boulder at the spot of the shooting, now a residential neighbourhood.
