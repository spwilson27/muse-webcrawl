The University of Bologna (Italian: Università di Bologna) is a public research university in Bologna, Italy. Founded in 1088 by an organised guild of students (universitas scholarium), it is the oldest university in the Western world in continuous operation and is widely regarded as the first university in the sense of a higher-learning and degree-awarding institute. Its motto, Alma mater studiorum ("nourishing mother of studies"), reflects this status.

History

The university arose from informal schools of Roman law in the late 11th century, where scholars such as Irnerius revived study of the Corpus Juris Civilis. Unlike later universities founded by masters, Bologna was organized by its students, who hired and paid the professors and fined them for absences or dull lectures. In 1158, Frederick Barbarossa issued the Authentica habita, granting scholars legal protections and freedom of movement.

Medieval Bologna became famous for law, but also developed medicine, astronomy, and the humanities; notable figures include the anatomist Mondino de' Liuzzi and, later, Luigi Galvani and Laura Bassi, among the first women to hold a university chair in Europe. Alumni include Dante Alighieri, Petrarch, Nicolaus Copernicus, and Guglielmo Marconi.

Organization and the Bologna Process

Today the university enrols around 90,000 students across its campuses in Bologna, Cesena, Forlì, Ravenna, and Rimini, with a branch in Buenos Aires. In 1999, ministers from 29 European countries signed the Bologna Declaration at the university, launching the Bologna Process to harmonize European higher education degree structures.
