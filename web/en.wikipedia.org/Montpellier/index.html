Montpellier is a city in southern France, prefecture of the Hérault department in the Occitanie region, located near the Mediterranean coast between Marseille and the Spanish border. One of France's fastest-growing and youngest cities, it is renowned for its medieval university and medical school.

The Place de la Comédie, the city's central square.

History

Montpellier first appears in records in 985. Ruled by the Guilhem dynasty and later the Crown of Aragon, it became a prosperous trading city and a centre of scholarship: its law school and school of medicine date to the 12th century, and the University of Montpellier, founded by papal bull in 1220, is among the world's oldest universities; its medical faculty is the oldest in continuous operation, where Rabelais and Nostradamus studied. The city suffered during the Wars of Religion — it was a Huguenot stronghold besieged by Louis XIII in 1622 — and later grew as an administrative capital and wine-trading centre.

The modern city

Under long-serving mayor Georges Frêche and successors, Montpellier expanded with bold contemporary architecture, including the Antigone district designed by Ricardo Bofill in a neoclassical style, the Odysseum leisure area and striking buildings such as the white "tree" tower in Port Marianne. An extensive tram network, with lines designed by artists including Christian Lacroix, is a hallmark of the city. A large student population gives Montpellier a youthful profile, and research, medicine and technology anchor its economy.
