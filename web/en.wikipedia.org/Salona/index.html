Salona (Ancient Greek: Σάλωνα) was an ancient city on the Dalmatian coast, near modern Solin in Croatia. Capital of the Roman province of Dalmatia, it grew into one of the largest cities of the Adriatic world, with a population that may have reached 60,000, and was the birthplace of the emperor Diocletian.

History

Originally a settlement of the Illyrian Delmatae with Greek trading contacts, Salona came under Roman control in the 1st century BC and became a Roman colony, Colonia Martia Iulia Salona. Its sheltered harbour and position at the crossing of inland routes made it the administrative and commercial centre of the eastern Adriatic. Diocletian, born nearby, built his vast retirement palace a few kilometres away at Split — which grew from the palace after Salona's fall.

Christian Salona

Salona became an early centre of Christianity in the Balkans, associated with martyrs such as Saint Domnius, the city's patron. Its bishops presided over councils of Dalmatian churches, and excavations have uncovered numerous basilicas, baptisteries and Christian cemeteries, including the great necropolis at Manastirine.

Decline and excavation

Attacks by Avars and Slavs in the early 7th century (conventionally dated c. 614) destroyed the city; its inhabitants fled to Diocletian's Palace and the islands, founding medieval Split. The ruins — city walls, amphitheatre, theatre, aqueduct, baths and early Christian churches — are among the most important Roman sites in Croatia, excavated systematically since the 19th century by figures such as Frane Bulić.
