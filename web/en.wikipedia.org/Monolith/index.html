Monolith

A monolith is a geological feature consisting of a single massive stone or rock, either occurring naturally — such as a mountain — or carved and moved by humans as a monument or part of a building. The word derives from the Greek monolithos, "one stone."

Natural monoliths

Examples include Uluru in Australia, the Rock of Gibraltar, and El Capitan in Yosemite. Such formations typically arise where resistant rock survives erosion of surrounding material.

Man-made monoliths

Ancient cultures quarried and transported enormous single stones: Egyptian obelisks, the moai of Easter Island, and the largest stones at Baalbek. The largest known ancient monoliths weigh well over a thousand tonnes, and their transport remains a subject of archaeological study.

Figurative use

By extension, "monolithic" describes organisations or systems perceived as unified, rigid, and indivisible, and in software engineering a monolithic application is built as a single unit.
