Houthis

The Houthis (Arabic: الحوثيون, al-Ḥūthiyyūn), officially known as Ansar Allah ("Partisans of God"), are a Zaydi Shia Islamist political and military organization that emerged from the Saada Governorate of northern Yemen in the 1990s. They take their name from their founding leader, Hussein al-Houthi. Since 2014 they have controlled Yemen's capital, Sanaa, and much of the country's populous northwest, and they form one of the principal parties to the Yemeni civil war.

Origins

The movement began as the "Believing Youth" (al-Shabab al-Mu'min), a Zaydi revivalist educational movement founded in 1994 to counter the spread of Salafism in the Zaydi heartland of Saada and to resist the political and economic marginalization of the north. Radicalized by the 2003 US invasion of Iraq, Hussein al-Houthi's followers clashed with the government of President Ali Abdullah Saleh. Hussein was killed by Yemeni forces in 2004, and six rounds of war (the Saada wars) followed until a ceasefire in 2010. Leadership passed to his brother, Abdul-Malik al-Houthi.

Takeover and civil war

Exploiting unrest after the 2011 Arab Spring uprising, the Houthis expanded southward and seized Sanaa in September 2014, later dissolving parliament and forming their own governing bodies; President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi fled to Aden and then Riyadh. In March 2015 a Saudi-led coalition, backed by the United States and others, intervened militarily to restore Hadi's government, beginning a devastating war and humanitarian crisis. The Houthis later allied with — and then fought — former president Saleh, killing him in December 2017. Despite years of coalition airstrikes, the Houthis retained control of the north, and a UN-brokered truce in 2022 substantially reduced fighting.

Red Sea attacks and regional role

Following the outbreak of the Gaza war in October 2023, the Houthis began attacking commercial shipping in the Red Sea and the Bab el-Mandeb strait with missiles and drones, saying they acted in solidarity with Palestinians; the attacks disrupted a major global trade route and drew US- and UK-led airstrikes on Houthi targets from January 2024. The Houthis are widely reported to receive support from Iran, a charge both sides have characterized in varying terms, and they are considered part of Iran's regional "Axis of Resistance."

Ideology and governance

The movement's slogan (the sarkha) — "God is the Greatest; Death to America; Death to Israel; A curse upon the Jews; Victory to Islam" — expresses its anti-imperialist and anti-Zionist posture. In areas they govern, the Houthis run a parallel administration (the Supreme Political Council in Sanaa), tax trade through Hodeidah port, and have been accused by the UN and rights groups of serious abuses, including child soldier recruitment and suppression of dissent.
