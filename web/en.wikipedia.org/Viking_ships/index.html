Viking ships

Viking ships — see also the main article Viking ship — were the vessels built and used by Scandinavians during the Viking Age (c. 793–1066). This article surveys the principal surviving ships and modern replicas.

Surviving ships

The best-preserved originals were buried in ship burials: the Oseberg ship (buried 834), Gokstad ship (c. 890) and Tune ship in Norway, and the five Skuldelev ships scuttled near Roskilde, Denmark, in the 11th century, including the 30-metre longship Skuldelev 2, built in Dublin around 1042.

Replicas

Modern full-scale replicas, such as Draken Harald Hårfagre, the largest Viking ship replica, and the Sea Stallion from Glendalough (a reconstruction of Skuldelev 2), have sailed the North Atlantic to test Viking seamanship and navigation.
