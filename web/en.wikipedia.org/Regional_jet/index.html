Regional jet

A regional jet (RJ) is a short- to medium-haul jet airliner typically seating fewer than 100 passengers, used by regional airlines to feed passengers into major carriers' hub airports and to serve thinner routes that cannot support larger aircraft.

History

The category took off in the 1990s with the Bombardier CRJ series and Embraer ERJ family, which replaced turboprops on many routes with faster, higher-flying jets. Later models such as the Embraer E-Jet series blurred the line with small mainline aircraft. Scope clauses in major-airline pilot contracts have strongly shaped the size and deployment of regional jets in the United States, typically limiting them to about 76 seats.

Characteristics

Regional jets offer jet speed and passenger appeal but higher per-seat costs than larger aircraft. Their role has shifted as airlines have upgauged to larger types and as manufacturers exited the segment — Bombardier sold the CRJ program to Mitsubishi in 2020.
