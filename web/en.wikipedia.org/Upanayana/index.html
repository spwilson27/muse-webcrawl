Upanayana

Upanayana (Sanskrit: उपनयनम्) is a Hindu educational sacrament, one of the traditional samskaras (rites of passage), that marked the acceptance of a student by a preceptor (guru or acharya) and an individual's initiation into a school in Hinduism. The ceremony traditionally included the bestowal of the sacred thread, the yajnopavita, worn over the left shoulder.

Significance

The concept of upanayana is that it is a second, spiritual birth for the child: the initiated are called dvija, "twice-born". Traditionally, the ceremony was performed for boys of the Brahmin, Kshatriya, and Vaishya varnas at ages prescribed in the Grihya Sutras and Dharmashastra texts (traditionally eight, eleven, and twelve years respectively, counted from conception in some texts). The initiated student then entered the brahmacharya stage of life, beginning Vedic study under a guru.

The ceremony

Rites vary by region and community but typically include a ritual bath, the shaving of the head (in some traditions), investiture with the sacred thread of three strands, the whispering of the Gayatri Mantra into the student's ear by the father or guru, and the giving of a staff, girdle, and deerskin as symbols of student life. Festivities and feasting for relatives usually follow.

In modern practice

Today the upanayana is most commonly performed for Brahmin boys shortly before marriage or during adolescence, and the educational function has been largely replaced by formal schooling. Related thread ceremonies exist in other traditions; in Jainism and Sikhism analogues are observed, and in Zoroastrianism the navjote ceremony is a comparable investiture. Some modern communities also perform upanayana for girls, reviving an ancient minority practice mentioned in some texts.
