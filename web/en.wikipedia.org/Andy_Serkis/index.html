Andy Serkis

Andrew Clement Serkis
Born: 20 April 1964, Ruislip Manor, London, England
Occupations: Actor, director, producer
Known for: Gollum; Caesar; performance capture

Andrew Clement Serkis (born 20 April 1964) is an English actor, director, and producer renowned as the leading pioneer of performance-capture acting. His portrayal of Gollum in The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit trilogies, and of the chimpanzee Caesar in the Planet of the Apes reboot series, transformed how digital characters are created and recognised.

Early life and stage work

Serkis studied visual arts at Lancaster University and acted in student theatre before working extensively on stage and in British television and film through the 1980s and 1990s, including the title role in television dramas and an acclaimed turn as Bill Sikes. He was preparing to climb in the Himalayas when cast by Peter Jackson.

Performance capture

As Gollum, Serkis performed on set in a motion-capture suit, voicing the character's split-personality dialogue himself; the performance was so integral that it provoked continuing debate about awards eligibility for digital roles. He repeated the feat as the giant ape in Jackson's King Kong (2005) and reached new acclaim as Caesar, whose arc across Rise, Dawn, and War for the Planet of the Apes (2011–2017) critics praised as the emotional core of the films. In 2011 he co-founded The Imaginarium, a London studio devoted to performance capture.

Other work

Serkis's conventional roles include Alfred Pennyworth in The Batman (2022), Ulysses Klaue in the Marvel films, and Supreme Leader Snoke in the Star Wars sequel trilogy. As director he made Breathe (2017), Mowgli: Legend of the Jungle (2018), and Venom: Let There Be Carnage (2021).
