Bear Dance (Native American)

Bear Dance
Also calledMamaqv (Ute)
Practised byUte people; versions among other western tribes
SeasonSpring

The Bear Dance is a traditional dance of the Ute people of the Great Basin and Colorado Plateau regions of the western United States, and one of the oldest dances continuously practised by a Native American tribe. Variants of bear dances occur among several other peoples of western North America.

Origins

Ute tradition relates that the dance was taught to two brothers by a bear: on returning from a hunt, one brother heard music coming from a cave where a bear was dancing and shuffling back and forth, and he learned the steps and songs so they could be taught to the people. The dance is held in spring, when bears emerge from hibernation, and honours the bear as a source of strength and wisdom.

The ceremony

The dance takes place in a circular brush enclosure (or, today, a corral or dance ground) with an opening to the east. Men and women line up facing each other; women select partners by a movement of the shawl or a step forward. The characteristic music is provided by singers and by the morache, a rasp of notched wood resonated on a hollow drum or tin, whose growling sound imitates a bear. Couples shuffle toward and away from each other in imitation of bears mating or fighting.

Modern observance

The Bear Dance remains a major annual social and ceremonial gathering for the Southern Ute, Ute Mountain Ute and Northern Ute tribes, held over several days in late spring, welcoming visitors and marking the renewal of the year.
