Captain (nautical)

The captain, or master, of a merchant vessel is a licensed mariner who holds ultimate command and responsibility for the ship, its crew, cargo, and passengers. The captain must hold a master's license issued by the flag state and is legally responsible for safe navigation, compliance with maritime law, and the welfare of everyone on board. In port, the captain represents the shipowner and signs documents such as bills of lading.

Duties

The master's authority at sea is nearly absolute in matters of safety: they may deviate from a voyage, jettison cargo, or confine dangerous persons when necessary. Duties include navigation oversight, cargo care, crew discipline, record keeping (the official log), and communication with owners and authorities. By long tradition the captain is expected to be the last to leave a sinking ship.

Terminology

In naval service, "captain" is also a commissioned rank; the officer commanding a warship is addressed as captain regardless of rank. On merchant vessels, the master is often styled captain as a courtesy. The captain's second in command is the chief mate or chief officer.
