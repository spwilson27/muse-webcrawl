Southampton is a port city in Hampshire, on the south coast of England. Situated at the head of Southampton Water, where the rivers Test and Itchen meet, it is one of Britain's principal ports and the country's cruise capital. The city is famous as the home port from which RMS Titanic sailed on her maiden voyage in 1912, and its medieval walls and Bargate survive from its days as a walled trading town.

History

The Romans established a settlement at Clausentum (Bitterne Manor), and the Anglo-Saxon port of Hamwic became a major trading centre. After the Norman Conquest, Southampton grew as the main port for links with Normandy; Henry V's army embarked here for Agincourt in 1415, and the Pilgrim Fathers sailed from Southampton in 1620. The Victorian era transformed it into a great liner port, and in the twentieth century it was the embarkation point for troops in both world wars. Heavy bombing in the Southampton Blitz of 1940–41 devastated the city centre, which was rebuilt in the post-war decades.

The port

The Port of Southampton is one of the UK's busiest: it is a leading port for cruise ships, vehicle exports and container traffic (at its DP World terminal), benefiting from an unusual double high tide that extends the window for large vessels. Famous liners associated with the port include the Queen Mary, Queen Elizabeth and Queen Mary 2.

Education and culture

The University of Southampton is a research-intensive university known for engineering, oceanography and computer science — Tim Berners-Lee held a chair there — and the National Oceanography Centre is based at the waterfront. Solent University serves vocational and maritime training. Cultural institutions include the SeaCity Museum, which tells the Titanic story, the John Hansard Gallery, and the Mayflower Theatre, one of the largest regional theatres in England.

Sport

Southampton Football Club, nicknamed "the Saints", play at St Mary's Stadium and won the FA Cup in 1976. Hampshire County Cricket Club play at the Ageas Bowl in nearby West End, which hosts international cricket. The city is also a centre for sailing, facing the Solent and the Isle of Wight.

Transport

Southampton Central station connects the city to London Waterloo, and the M27 and M3 motorways link it to the national network. Southampton Airport serves domestic and European flights, and ferries run from the city to the Isle of Wight.
