Lorne Michaels

Lorne Michaels (born Lorne David Lipowitz; 17 November 1944) is a Canadian-American television producer, writer, and comedian best known as the creator and executive producer of Saturday Night Live (SNL), the long-running NBC sketch comedy show that premiered in 1975.

Career

Born in Toronto, Michaels began his career in Canadian radio and television before moving to Los Angeles, where he wrote for Rowan & Martin's Laugh-In and other programs. In 1975 he created SNL for NBC, producing its first five seasons before leaving in 1980; he returned in 1985 and has remained its executive producer and showrunner ever since. Under Michaels, SNL launched the careers of generations of comedians, from John Belushi, Dan Aykroyd, and Gilda Radner to Eddie Murphy, Tina Fey, and many others.

Other work

Through his company Broadway Video, Michaels has produced films including Wayne's World, Tommy Boy, and Mean Girls, and late-night television programs hosted by Conan O'Brien, Jimmy Fallon, and Seth Meyers. He has won numerous Emmy Awards, received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, and was appointed a Companion of the Order of Canada.
