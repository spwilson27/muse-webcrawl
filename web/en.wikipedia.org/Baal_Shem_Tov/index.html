Baal Shem Tov

Rabbi Israel ben Eliezer (c. 1698/1700 – 1760), known as the Baal Shem Tov ("Master of the Good Name") or by the acronym Besht, was a Jewish mystic and healer from Poland who is regarded as the founder of Hasidic Judaism.

Life

Born in Okopy in Podolia (now Ukraine), he worked as a teacher's assistant, clay-digger and innkeeper before revealing himself as a religious leader in the 1730s, settling in Medzhybizh. His teachings emphasised joy in worship, prayer with devotion (devekut), and the spiritual value of ordinary people.

Legacy

The Baal Shem Tov's disciples, including the Maggid of Mezritch, spread Hasidism across Eastern Europe. Legends of his miracles and sayings were collected in the volume Shivhei ha-Besht (1814), a foundational text of Hasidic tradition.
