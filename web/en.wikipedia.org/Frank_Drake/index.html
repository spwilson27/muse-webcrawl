Frank Drake

Frank Drake (1930–2022), pioneer of the search for extraterrestrial intelligence.

Frank Donald Drake (28 May 1930 – 2 September 2022) was an American astrophysicist and astrobiologist best known for founding the scientific search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI) and for the Drake equation, which estimates the number of communicative extraterrestrial civilisations in the Milky Way.

Career

Drake studied engineering physics at Cornell and astronomy at Harvard. In 1960, at the National Radio Astronomy Observatory in Green Bank, West Virginia, he conducted Project Ozma, the first modern SETI experiment, listening for radio signals from the nearby stars Tau Ceti and Epsilon Eridani. In 1961 he convened the first SETI conference at Green Bank, for which he devised his famous equation as an agenda: N = R* × fp × ne × fl × fi × fc × L.

Drake made important contributions to the study of pulsars and to planetary radio astronomy, directed the Arecibo Observatory in Puerto Rico, and later taught at Cornell and the University of California, Santa Cruz. He helped design the Pioneer plaque with Carl Sagan and composed, with Sagan and others, the 1974 Arecibo message, a radio transmission beamed toward the globular cluster M13. He was chairman of the SETI Institute's board and a member of the National Academy of Sciences.
