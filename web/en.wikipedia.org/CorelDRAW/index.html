CorelDRAW

CorelDRAW is a vector graphics editor and design platform developed by the Canadian software company Corel (now part of Alludo). First released in 1989 for Windows, it became one of the earliest and most enduring PC-based illustration programs, and for many years was the principal competitor to Adobe Illustrator.

History

CorelDRAW 1.0 shipped in January 1989, written by engineers Michel Bouillon and Pat Beirne under the direction of Corel founder Michael Cowpland. It was among the first desktop publishing and illustration programs for Microsoft Windows, and its early success was driven by bundling with Windows PCs and by its inclusion of a large library of fonts and clip art. Version 3 (1992) added envelope tools and was the first release on CD-ROM. Through the 1990s the package grew into the CorelDRAW Graphics Suite, bundling the PHOTO-PAINT bitmap editor, font management tools and utilities such as Corel Capture and Trace.

Corel acquired the product line's rivals over time, and after a period of decline against Adobe's Creative Suite, CorelDRAW settled into a loyal user base in sign-making, engraving, embroidery, screen printing and technical illustration, where its output tools for vinyl cutters and plotters are prized.

Features

Core tools include Bézier curve editing, node manipulation, text on a path, blends, contours, envelopes, transparency, and multi-page layout. The suite supports import and export of formats such as AI, PDF, EPS, SVG, DXF and DWG. Later versions added non-destructive effects, symmetry drawing, block shadow, and AI-assisted bitmap-to-vector tracing via PowerTRACE. A macOS version, absent for many years, returned in 2019, and CorelDRAW.app provides a web-based companion.

File format

The native file format is CDR. Its structure has changed across versions, which historically made backward and forward compatibility a recurring concern for print shops archiving customer artwork.
