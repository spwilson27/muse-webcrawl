Golestan Palace

LocationTehran, Iran
BuiltSafavid era origins; largely rebuilt under the Qajar dynasty (19th century)
StatusMuseum; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2013)

Golestan Palace (Persian: Kākh-e Golestān, "Palace of Flowers") is a royal Qajar palace complex in Tehran, Iran. Formerly the citadel and seat of government of the Qajar dynasty, it is now a museum and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013.

History

The site originated as the citadel (Arg) of Tehran in the Safavid period and was embellished under Karim Khan Zand in the 18th century. When Agha Mohammad Khan made Tehran his capital in the 1790s, the citadel became the seat of the Qajar kings. Most of the surviving buildings date from the long reign of Naser al-Din Shah in the 19th century, whose travels to Europe influenced their fusion of Persian craftsmanship with European architectural and decorative styles.

Buildings

The complex includes the Marble Throne terrace (Takht-e Marmar), the glittering Mirror Hall (Talar-e Ayeneh), the Brilliant Hall, the Edifice of the Sun (Shams-ol Emareh) — once Tehran's tallest building — and the Ethnography Museum, across landscaped gardens with pools and plane trees. The coronations of the Pahlavi shahs took place here, as did the coronation of Mohammad Reza Shah in 1967.

Collections

The palace museums hold royal regalia, Qajar paintings, photographs — Naser al-Din Shah was an enthusiastic early photographer — manuscripts, and gifts presented by foreign courts.
