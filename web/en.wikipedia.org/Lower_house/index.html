Lower house

TypeLegislative chamber
SystemBicameral legislature

A lower house is one of two chambers of a bicameral legislature, the other chamber being the upper house. Despite its official position "below" the upper house, in many legislatures worldwide the lower house has come to wield more power or otherwise exert significant political influence.

Characteristics

Lower houses are typically directly elected by the population, serve shorter terms than upper houses, and are often the chamber in which revenue and budget bills originate. In parliamentary systems the government is usually formed from, and accountable to, the lower house. Examples include the House of Commons in the United Kingdom and Canada, the House of Representatives in the United States and Australia, and the Lok Sabha in India.

Comparison with upper houses

Upper houses frequently represent regions or states, have longer terms, and may be appointed or indirectly elected. In many countries the lower house can override the upper house on ordinary legislation after a delay or by a special majority.
