Paleis Het Loo, Apeldoorn

Het Loo Palace (Dutch: Paleis Het Loo) is a palace in Apeldoorn, Netherlands, built between 1684 and 1686 for stadtholder William III and his wife Mary II of England. It is a prime example of Dutch Baroque architecture and was the summer residence of the Dutch royal family, the House of Orange-Nassau, for almost three centuries.

History

William III bought the older hunting lodge Het Oude Loo in 1684 and built a new palace beside it as a hunting seat in the forests of the Veluwe. After he and Mary became king and queen of England in 1689, the palace was enlarged and its formal gardens, designed with the help of Daniel Marot, were expanded in imitation of Versailles, with parterres, fountains, cascades and a radiating avenue layout.

Museum

Following the departure of Queen Wilhelmina in 1962 and her death the same year, the palace passed to the state. After a major restoration (1977–1984) that returned much of the interior and gardens to their 17th-century appearance, it opened as the national Museum Paleis Het Loo, presenting the history of the House of Orange-Nassau. The stables house royal carriages and automobiles, and the palace park covers some 650 hectares. A major underground expansion of the museum, designed by KAAN Architecten, opened in 2023.
