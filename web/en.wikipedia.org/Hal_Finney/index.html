Harold Thomas Finney II (May 4, 1956 – August 28, 2014) was an American software developer and cryptographer. He was the lead developer on several console games, and was an early contributor to the Bitcoin project: in January 2009 he received the first bitcoin transaction, sent by Bitcoin's creator, Satoshi Nakamoto. Finney was a noted cypherpunk and an early employee of the PGP Corporation.

Career in cryptography

After graduating from Caltech, Finney worked in video-game development before joining Phil Zimmermann's PGP Corporation, where he was one of the principal programmers of Pretty Good Privacy, the pioneering email-encryption software. He participated in the cypherpunks mailing list, ran a remailer, and in 2004 created reusable proof of work (RPOW) — a system implementing a reusable token based on Adam Back's Hashcash proof-of-work scheme, an important precursor to Bitcoin's mining mechanism. He also confirmed weaknesses in early hash functions and contributed to discussions of digital cash systems by David Chaum and others.

Bitcoin

Finney was among the first to respond to Nakamoto's October 2008 whitepaper with interest rather than scepticism. He downloaded the Bitcoin software on its release, mined some of the earliest blocks, and on January 12, 2009 received 10 bitcoins from Nakamoto in the first transaction on the network, announcing simply: "Running bitcoin." His measured, constructive email exchanges with Nakamoto are among the few surviving records of Bitcoin's earliest days. Because of his expertise and early involvement, Finney was sometimes speculated to be Nakamoto himself, a claim he denied.

Illness and death

In 2009 Finney was diagnosed with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). He continued programming with eye-tracking equipment and wrote about his experience in a widely read 2013 post, "Bitcoin and me." He died in August 2014 and his body was cryopreserved by the Alcor Life Extension Foundation.
