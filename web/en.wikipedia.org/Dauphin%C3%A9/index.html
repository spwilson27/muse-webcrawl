Dauphiné
Former province of France
Capital: Grenoble
Modern departments: Isère, Drôme, Hautes-Alpes

The Dauphiné is a former province of southeastern France, roughly corresponding to the modern departments of Isère, Drôme and Hautes-Alpes. Its capital was Grenoble. The region stretches from the Rhône valley to the high Alps along the Italian border.

History

The province took its name from the title of its rulers, the counts of Albon, who bore the nickname Dauphin ("dolphin"). In 1349 the last independent dauphin, Humbert II, sold the territory to King Philip VI of France on condition that the heir to the French throne bear the title Dauphin de France — a title retained until 1830. The Dauphiné kept its own estates and privileges for centuries and played a part in the opening of the French Revolution with the 1788 Day of the Tiles in Grenoble and the Assembly of Vizille.

Geography and culture

The province includes part of the French Alps, with ranges such as the Écrins and the Vercors, and is known for walnuts from Grenoble, Chartreuse liqueur, and the potato dish gratin dauphinois. Its name is preserved in the Critérium du Dauphiné cycling race.
