Nihil obstat

Nihil obstat (Latin for "nothing hinders" or "nothing stands in the way") is a declaration by an official censor of the Roman Catholic Church that a book or pamphlet contains nothing damaging to faith or morals. It is granted by a censor librorum appointed by a bishop, and normally precedes the imprimatur ("let it be printed"), the bishop's own authorization to publish.

Process

Catholic authors — especially clergy and religious writing on faith, morals, scripture, or theology — submit works for review. The censor's nihil obstat certifies the work is free of doctrinal error; it does not constitute endorsement of the author's opinions, nor was it ever a guarantee of quality. The system of pre-publication censorship was reshaped after the Second Vatican Council, which abolished the old Index of Forbidden Books in 1966, but canon law (canons 822–832) still requires permission for certain categories of publication.
