Tahltan language

Native to: British Columbia, Canada
Speakers: A few dozen fluent (endangered)
Family: Na-Dene → Athabaskan → Northern Athabaskan
Communities: Telegraph Creek, Dease Lake, Iskut

Tahltan is a Northern Athabaskan language spoken by the Tahltan people in northwestern British Columbia, Canada, in the communities of Telegraph Creek, Dease Lake and Iskut along the Stikine River watershed. It is closely related to neighbouring Kaska and Tagish languages.

Status

Like many Indigenous languages of Canada, Tahltan was heavily impacted by the residential school system and is now critically endangered, with most fluent speakers elderly. Community-led revitalisation efforts include language classes, immersion nests, documentation projects and digital learning resources supported by the Tahltan Central Government.

Linguistic features

Tahltan shares the characteristics of Athabaskan languages: a complex verb morphology in which prefixes encode much of the grammar, a tonal contrast, and a classification of nouns by shape and consistency reflected in verb stems for handling and motion. Its sound system includes a rich set of affricates and ejectives typical of the family.
