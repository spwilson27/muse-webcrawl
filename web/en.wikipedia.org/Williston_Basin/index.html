Williston Basin

LocationNorth Dakota, South Dakota, Montana (US); Saskatchewan, Manitoba (Canada)
TypeIntracratonic sedimentary basin
Key formationsBakken, Three Forks, Madison
Known forOil and gas production

The Williston Basin is a large intracratonic sedimentary basin in eastern Montana, western North and South Dakota, southern Saskatchewan and southwestern Manitoba, known for its rich deposits of petroleum, potash and lignite coal.

Geology

The basin began subsiding in the Ordovician period and accumulated a thick sequence of sedimentary rocks, nearly 4,900 m (16,000 ft) at its deepest point near Williston, North Dakota. It is a simple, roughly circular structural depression overlying rocks of the Trans-Hudson orogen and the Superior craton.

Petroleum

Oil was first produced in 1951. Conventional fields such as Beaver Lodge and Antelope produced from the Madison Group. From the 2000s, horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing of the Devonian–Mississippian Bakken Formation and underlying Three Forks Formation triggered the North Dakota oil boom, making North Dakota one of the top oil-producing US states.
