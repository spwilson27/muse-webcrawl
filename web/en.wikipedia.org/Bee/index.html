Bee

CladeAnthophila (within Apoidea)
Known speciesOver 16,000 described
RangeEvery continent except Antarctica
DietNectar and pollen

Bees are winged insects closely related to wasps and ants, known for their roles as pollinators and, in the case of the western honey bee, for producing honey. More than 16,000 known species occur in seven recognised families, ranging from highly social colonies of tens of thousands of individuals to species in which most bees are solitary.

Biology

Bees feed on nectar for energy and pollen for protein. Their branched body hairs trap pollen grains, making them exceptionally effective pollinators; many flowering plants have coevolved with bees, and bee pollination supports a large share of global food crops. Honey bees communicate the location of forage through the "waggle dance". Social species are organised into castes — queens, workers and drones — while bumblebees form smaller annual colonies.

Declines

Many bee populations are in decline due to habitat loss, pesticides (notably neonicotinoids), parasites such as the Varroa mite, and diseases. Colony collapse disorder in managed honey bees drew wide attention in the 2000s. Conservation responses include wildflower planting, reduced pesticide use and protection of nesting habitat.
