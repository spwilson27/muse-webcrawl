Haifa

Haifa (Hebrew: חֵיפָה; Arabic: حيفا) is the third-largest city in Israel, with a population of around 285,000, and the centre of the Haifa metropolitan area, the third-most populous metropolitan area in the country. The city lies on the Mediterranean coast at the foot of Mount Carmel, about 90 kilometres north of Tel Aviv.

Haifa is Israel's chief port and a major industrial centre, home to the country's largest oil refinery, the petrochemical plants of the Kishon River valley, and the Matam high-tech park, where research and development centres of Intel, Microsoft, Google, Apple and other companies are located. The city is also a major educational centre, home to the University of Haifa and the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology, both situated on the slopes of Mount Carmel.

History

The area around Haifa has been inhabited since the Late Bronze Age. A port settlement known in antiquity stood at Tell Abu Hawam, and Haifa is mentioned in Talmudic literature from the 3rd century CE. The Crusaders captured the town in 1100, and it changed hands repeatedly between Christian and Muslim rulers before being razed by the Mamluk sultan Baibars in 1265.

Modern Haifa grew from a small village refounded in 1761 by Daher el-Omar, the Ottoman ruler of Galilee. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries the town developed rapidly: the Hejaz railway reached it in 1905, and under the British Mandate the deep-water port was built (opened 1933) along with oil pipelines from Iraq and large refineries. During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Jewish forces captured the city in April 1948, and most of the Arab population departed or was expelled.

Geography and neighbourhoods

Haifa is built in tiers on Mount Carmel: the Downtown (HaIr HaTachtit) near the port, Hadar HaCarmel midway up the slope, and the Carmel neighbourhoods (the French Carmel, Central Carmel and Ahuza) along the ridge. The Kishon River flows into Haifa Bay east of the port.

Religious significance

Mount Carmel is associated with the prophet Elijah, whose cave beneath the Stella Maris Monastery is venerated by Jews, Christians and Muslims. Haifa is also the world centre of the Baháʼí Faith: the Shrine of the Báb and the Baháʼí World Centre buildings, with their terraced gardens climbing the mountain, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2008. The Ahmadiyya Muslim community is centred in the Kababir neighbourhood.

Transport

The city is served by the Carmelit, a funicular subway that is Israel's only underground railway, by the Metronit bus rapid transit system, coastal railway stations, and a small international airport. The Carmel Tunnels, opened in 2010, carry traffic beneath Mount Carmel.
