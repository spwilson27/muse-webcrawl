Radon

Radon, element 86
SymbolRn
Atomic number86
GroupNoble gases

Radon is a chemical element with the symbol Rn and atomic number 86. It is a radioactive, colourless, odourless and tasteless noble gas produced by the decay of radium in the uranium-238 decay chain. Radon is one of the densest gases at room temperature and is a significant health hazard because of its radioactivity.

Health effects

Radon gas seeps from soil and rock into buildings, where it can accumulate in basements and ground floors. Inhalation of radon and its decay products ("radon progeny") is the second-leading cause of lung cancer after smoking in many countries; health agencies recommend testing homes and installing mitigation systems, typically sub-slab depressurisation fans, where levels exceed reference values such as the US EPA action level of 4 pCi/L (148 Bq/m³).

Occurrence

Because its parent radium-226 is widespread in granitic soils and bedrock, radon levels vary strongly by geology. Historically, high radon exposure in uranium mines (for example at Schneeberg and Jáchymov) caused the lung disease once called "mountain sickness" among miners.
