The Netherlands is a country in northwestern Europe, bordered by Germany to the east, Belgium to the south, and the North Sea to the north and west. Famous for its flat landscape of polders, canals, windmills, and tulip fields, it is one of the world's most densely populated countries and a major trading and agricultural exporter. Its name means "lower countries", reflecting that roughly a quarter of its land lies below sea level.

History

The northern Netherlands threw off Spanish Habsburg rule in the Dutch Revolt (1568–1648), emerging as the Dutch Republic — a maritime and commercial superpower of the 17th-century "Golden Age", when Amsterdam was Europe's richest port, the Dutch East India Company (VOC) spanned the globe, and Rembrandt and Vermeer painted. After Napoleonic occupation, the Kingdom of the Netherlands was established in 1815. The country stayed neutral in the First World War but was occupied by Nazi Germany in 1940–45, suffering the Hunger Winter of 1944–45. After the war it decolonised (Indonesia, Suriname), helped found the European Communities and NATO, and became a prosperous social democracy. The catastrophic North Sea flood of 1953 prompted the Delta Works, among the world's most ambitious flood-defence systems.

Geography and water

Much of the country is reclaimed polder land protected by dikes, dunes, and storm-surge barriers; the highest point, Vaalserberg, is only 322 m. The Rhine, Meuse, and Scheldt deltas converge here, and the port of Rotterdam is Europe's largest. The Caribbean islands of Bonaire, Sint Eustatius, and Saba are special municipalities, and Aruba, Curaçao, and Sint Maarten are constituent countries within the Kingdom.

Society

The Netherlands is known for pragmatic, tolerant social policies, cycling culture (more bicycles than people), and world-class agriculture — it is among the world's largest exporters of food by value. Dutch is the official language, with Frisian co-official in Friesland. The monarch is Willem-Alexander (since 2013).
