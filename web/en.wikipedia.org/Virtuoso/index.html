Virtuoso

A virtuoso (from Italian virtuoso, "virtuous", from Latin virtus) is an individual who possesses outstanding technical ability in a particular art or field, such as fine arts, music, singing, playing a musical instrument, or composition. The term is also used more loosely for a person highly skilled in any pursuit.

In music

In music, a virtuoso is a performing musician of exceptional technique and artistry, often able to execute passages of extreme speed, difficulty and brilliance. The archetype of the virtuoso soloist emerged in the Baroque and Classical eras and reached a cultural peak in the Romantic period with figures such as Niccolò Paganini on the violin and Franz Liszt on the piano, whose performances inspired both admiration and controversy over whether mere display displaced musical depth. Composers wrote concertos and showpieces — cadenzas, études and paraphrases — expressly to display virtuosity.

Wider usage

By extension, "virtuoso" describes mastery in other domains: a virtuoso performance in dance, surgery, cooking or computing. The word derives from the same root as "virtue", and in Renaissance Italy virtuoso first denoted a person of learning and distinction before narrowing to the musical sense.
