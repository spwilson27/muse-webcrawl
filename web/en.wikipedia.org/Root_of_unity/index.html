Root of unity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, a root of unity is any complex number that yields 1 when raised to some positive integer power n. That is, ζ is an nth root of unity if ζn = 1. Geometrically, the nth roots of unity are equally spaced points on the unit circle in the complex plane, at angles that are multiples of 2π/n. They are given by the formula ζ = e2πik/n for k = 0, 1, …, n−1.

Primitive roots

An nth root of unity is primitive if it is not an mth root of unity for any smaller m < n; equivalently its powers generate all n roots. There are φ(n) primitive nth roots, where φ is Euler's totient function. The primitive roots are exactly e2πik/n with gcd(k, n) = 1. The minimal polynomial of a primitive root over the rationals is the nth cyclotomic polynomial Φn(x), of degree φ(n).

Properties and applications

The nth roots of unity form a cyclic group of order n under multiplication. Their sum (for n > 1) is zero, and the orthogonality relations they satisfy underlie the discrete Fourier transform and the fast Fourier transform algorithm. Fields obtained by adjoining roots of unity — cyclotomic fields — are central in algebraic number theory, including Kummer's work on Fermat's Last Theorem and the theory of class numbers.
