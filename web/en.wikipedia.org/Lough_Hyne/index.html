Lough Hyne (Irish: Loch Oighin, "lake of the cauldron") is a fully marine saltwater lough (lake) in West Cork, County Cork, Ireland, about 5 km southwest of Skibbereen. It is Ireland's first marine nature reserve, designated in 1981, and one of the most intensively studied marine environments in Europe, with research extending back to the mid-19th century.

Description

The lough measures roughly one kilometre square and reaches depths of about 52 metres. It is connected to the Atlantic Ocean at Barloge Creek by a narrow channel known as the Rapids. Because the channel is so restricted, the tide inside the lough lags behind the sea outside, creating a distinctive asymmetric tidal regime: the flood rushes in quickly through the Rapids, while the ebb drains slowly.

Ecology

The sheltered, warm waters of Lough Hyne support an unusually rich assemblage of marine life for its latitude, including species more typical of the Mediterranean alongside northern species. Habitats range from rocky shores and sponge-covered underwater cliffs to mudflats and maerl beds. Over 1,000 marine species have been recorded. Castle Island stands in the centre of the lough.

Scientific research

Marine biological research at Lough Hyne began in the 1880s and intensified in the 20th century through expeditions from University College Cork and visiting teams from British universities. Classic studies of the ecology of organisms such as the purple sea urchin Paracentrotus lividus and various sponges were carried out here, making the lough a landmark site in the history of marine ecology.
