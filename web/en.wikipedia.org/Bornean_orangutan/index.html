The Bornean orangutan (Pongo pygmaeus) is a species of orangutan endemic to the island of Borneo. Together with the Sumatran and Tapanuli orangutans, it belongs to the only genus of great apes native to Asia. It is listed as critically endangered, with populations declining due to habitat loss and hunting.

Description

Bornean orangutans have long, shaggy reddish-brown hair and the longest arm span of any primate—adult males may reach 1.5 m in height and weigh 50–100 kg, with arms up to 2 m across. Mature males develop large cheek pads (flanges) and throat sacs used for long-call vocalizations. The species is highly arboreal and mostly solitary; females raise a single infant for up to eight years, the longest interbirth interval of any land mammal. Diet is dominated by fruit, supplemented by leaves, bark, insects, and occasionally eggs.

Subspecies and distribution

Three subspecies are recognized: P. p. pygmaeus (northwest), P. p. wurmbii (southwest/central), and P. p. morio (northeast). Surveys in 2016 estimated around 104,700 individuals remaining in the wild, roughly half the numbers of the early 1970s.

Conservation

The main threats are deforestation for oil palm plantations and timber, forest fires, and poaching for the illegal pet trade. Conservation measures include protected areas such as Tanjung Puting and Danum Valley, rehabilitation centres, and moratoria on clearing primary forest.
