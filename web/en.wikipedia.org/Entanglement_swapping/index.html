Entanglement swapping

Entanglement swapping is a quantum information technique in which a Bell-state measurement on particles from two independent entangled pairs creates entanglement between particles that never interacted directly.

Process

Given pairs A–B and C–D, measuring B and C jointly in the Bell basis projects A and D into an entangled state, up to a correction communicated classically.

Applications

Swapping is the core operation of quantum repeaters, extending entanglement over long distances for quantum communication networks, and has been demonstrated with photons, atoms and solid-state systems, including satellite experiments.
