Split, Croatia

Country: Croatia
County: Split-Dalmatia
Population: approx. 160,000 (city)
Known for: Diocletian's Palace

Split (Croatian pronunciation: [splît]) is the second-largest city of Croatia, the largest city in Dalmatia, and the largest city on the Croatian coast of the Adriatic Sea. It lies on the eastern shore of the Adriatic, on a small peninsula extending into the sea.

The city was founded around the palace built by the Roman emperor Diocletian at the turn of the fourth century AD. Diocletian's Palace, one of the best-preserved monuments of Roman architecture in the world, forms the core of the modern city and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The area was originally the site of the Greek colony of Aspálathos. Diocletian built his retirement palace nearby between 293 and 305 AD, and after his death the palace became an imperial property and later a refuge for inhabitants of the destroyed Roman city of Salona. Split subsequently passed through Byzantine, Croatian, Hungarian, and Venetian rule; Venice governed it from 1420 until 1797, followed by Austrian, French, and Yugoslav periods before Croatian independence in 1991.

Sights and culture

Within the palace walls stand the Cathedral of Saint Domnius — originally Diocletian's mausoleum — the Peristyle, and the Temple of Jupiter. The city's waterfront promenade, the Riva, is its social centre. Split is home to the football club Hajduk Split and hosts the Ultra Europe music festival. It serves as a major ferry port for the central Dalmatian islands such as Hvar, Brač, and Vis.
