Battle of Tolbiac

Battle of Tolbiac
DateTraditionally 496 (possibly 506)
LocationTolbiac (Zülpich), near Cologne, present-day Germany
ResultFrankish victory
BelligerentsFranks under Clovis I vs. Alamanni

The Battle of Tolbiac was fought between the Franks, who were fighting under Clovis I, and the Alamanni, whose leader was killed during the battle. The name of the battle derives from Tolbiac, modern Zülpich near Cologne. The battle is traditionally dated to 496, though some historians favour 506.

Background

Clovis, king of the Salian Franks, campaigned against the Alamanni, a confederation of Germanic tribes pressing into the Rhineland. According to Gregory of Tours, the Franks were faring badly in the battle when Clovis prayed to the Christian God of his wife Clotilde, vowing to be baptised if granted victory.

The battle

The Franks rallied, the Alamannic king fell, and the Alamanni submitted. Gregory's account presents the victory as the moment of Clovis's conversion, a Frankish parallel to Constantine at the Milvian Bridge.

Aftermath

Clovis was baptised at Reims by Saint Remigius, traditionally on Christmas Day 496, along with thousands of his warriors. His conversion to Nicene Christianity — rather than the Arianism of the other Germanic kingdoms — won him the support of the Gallo-Roman church and population, and much of the Alamannic territory was incorporated into the Frankish kingdom.
