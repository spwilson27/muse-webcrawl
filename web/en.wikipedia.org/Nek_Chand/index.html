Nek Chand

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nek Chand Saini (15 December 1924 – 12 June 2015) was an Indian self-taught artist, known for building the Rock Garden of Chandigarh, an immense sculpture garden of figures and landscapes constructed from recycled industrial and domestic waste. One of the great works of outsider art, the garden grew in secret for some 18 years before authorities discovered it in 1975.

Life

Nek Chand was born in the village of Berian Kalan (now in Pakistan) and moved to India at Partition in 1947. Working as a roads inspector for the Public Works Department in Chandigarh, the new capital designed under Le Corbusier, he began around 1957 to clear a small gorge in a protected forest area near Sukhna Lake, building huts and populating them with sculptures made from stones, broken ceramics, glass bangles, electrical waste, and other discarded materials.

The Rock Garden

When the illegal garden—then about 5 hectares—was discovered, it faced demolition, but public support led the city to protect and complete it; it was inaugurated in 1976, and Nek Chand was given a salary, staff, and the title "Sub-Divisional Engineer, Rock Garden". The completed garden spans about 16 hectares (40 acres) of interlinked courtyards, waterfalls and paths lined with thousands of mosaic figures of dancers, musicians, animals and courtiers.

Recognition

Nek Chand received the Padma Shri in 1984. His work has been exhibited internationally, including at the Magiciens de la Terre exhibition in Paris (1989), and a Nek Chand Foundation promotes his art abroad; he died in Chandigarh in 2015, aged 90.
