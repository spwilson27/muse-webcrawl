Golden Horseshoe

The Golden Horseshoe is a densely populated and industrialised region of Ontario, Canada, curving around the western end of Lake Ontario from Niagara Falls through Hamilton and Toronto to Oshawa. With a population of roughly 8 million in its core definition (over 9.7 million in the wider Greater Golden Horseshoe), it is home to about a quarter of Canada's population and is the country's economic heartland.

Geography and definition

The name was popularised by Westinghouse president Herbert H. Rogge in the 1950s, and the horseshoe shape follows the shoreline from the Niagara Peninsula eastward and northward along the lake. Ontario's Places to Grow Act (2005) defined the larger Greater Golden Horseshoe planning region, including outlying cities such as Peterborough, Barrie, Guelph, Kitchener-Waterloo, and Brantford.

Economy

The region concentrates Canada's financial services (centred on Toronto's Financial District), automotive and steel manufacturing (Hamilton, Oshawa), technology, higher education, and transportation infrastructure, including Toronto Pearson International Airport and the Queen Elizabeth Way. The Greenbelt legislation of 2005 protects a band of agricultural and natural land around the urbanised core.
