Prince Henry of Prussia (born 1726)

Prince Henry of Prussia (German: Friedrich Heinrich Ludwig; 18 January 1726 – 3 August 1802) was a Prussian prince and general. He was the thirteenth child of King Frederick William I of Prussia and Princess Sophia Dorothea of Hanover, and a younger brother of Frederick the Great, under whom he served with distinction in the Silesian Wars and the Seven Years' War.

Military career

Henry began his military career at fourteen, serving in the First Silesian War. In the Second Silesian War he distinguished himself at the Battle of Hohenfriedberg (1745). During the Seven Years' War he commanded armies in Saxony and Bohemia, winning the Battle of Freiberg in 1762 — the last major battle of the war — while protecting his brother's flank. Frederick praised his brother as the only general who never made a mistake during the war.

Diplomacy and later life

After the war Henry undertook diplomatic missions, travelling twice to Saint Petersburg, where Catherine the Great proposed him for a throne in the First Partition of Poland arrangements; he played a part in the negotiations that led to the partition. In 1786, after Frederick's death, he hoped for influence under Frederick William II but was sidelined. American envoys reportedly sounded him out in the 1780s about becoming a constitutional monarch of the United States — an episode historians treat cautiously.

Rheinsberg

Henry made his seat at Rheinsberg Palace, where he maintained a court devoted to music, theatre, and French culture, and assembled an important collection. He had married Wilhelmina of Hesse-Kassel in 1752; the marriage was childless and the couple lived largely apart. He is buried at Rheinsberg.
