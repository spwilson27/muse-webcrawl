Cell biology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cell biology (also cellular biology or cytology) is the branch of biology that studies the structure, function and behaviour of cells — the fundamental units of life. Cell biologists investigate organelles and macromolecular machinery, the cell cycle and division, metabolism, signalling, movement, and how cells interact to form tissues. The discipline sits at the intersection of genetics, biochemistry and molecular biology, and its methods underpin modern medicine, biotechnology and drug discovery.

History

The field began with 17th-century microscopy: Hooke's description of cells in cork (1665) and Leeuwenhoek's observations of bacteria and protozoa. The 19th century brought cell theory (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow), the description of chromosomes and mitosis, and the first tissue culture experiments (Ross Harrison, 1907). In the 20th century, electron microscopy, ultracentrifugation, radioactive tracers and fluorescent proteins revealed organelles and let researchers follow molecules in living cells. The 1953 structure of DNA fused cell biology with molecular genetics.

Major topics

  • Structure: membranes, cytoskeleton, nucleus, mitochondria, endomembrane system, ribosomes.
  • Function: protein synthesis and trafficking, energy metabolism, transport, signalling cascades.
  • Growth and division: cell cycle checkpoints, mitosis, meiosis, apoptosis, stem cells and differentiation.
  • Interaction: adhesion, junctions, extracellular matrix, cell migration, immune recognition.

Methods

Key techniques include light and electron microscopy, fluorescence imaging of tagged proteins, cell culture, flow cytometry, subcellular fractionation, and modern omics approaches such as single-cell RNA sequencing, which maps gene expression cell by cell. Landmark journals include Cell, the Journal of Cell Biology and Nature Cell Biology.

Applications

Cell biology explains diseases at their root — cancer as dysregulated growth, lysosomal storage diseases as organelle failure — and enables cell therapies, monoclonal antibodies, vaccines and engineered tissues. It is taught as a core subject in every life-science curriculum.
