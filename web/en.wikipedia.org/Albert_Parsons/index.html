Albert Parsons

Albert Richard Parsons (June 20, 1848 – November 11, 1887) was an American socialist activist and newspaper editor. He was one of the five men executed after the Haymarket affair of 1886 in Chicago and is remembered as one of the Haymarket martyrs.

Early life

Born in Montgomery, Alabama, Parsons served as a young teenager in Confederate forces during the American Civil War, then moved to Texas, where he became a Republican activist advocating civil rights for freedmen during Reconstruction — a stance that made him enemies. Threatened for his politics and his marriage to Lucy Parsons, a woman of mixed African, Native American, and Mexican ancestry, he moved to Chicago in 1873.

Chicago activism

In Chicago Parsons worked as a printer and became a leading figure in the labour movement, editing the anarchist newspaper The Alarm and organising for the eight-hour day. After a bomb was thrown at police during a rally at Haymarket Square on May 4, 1886, Parsons — who had left the rally before the bombing — was arrested and tried for conspiracy to murder. Despite evidence that he was not present when the bomb exploded, he was convicted and hanged on November 11, 1887.

Legacy

The Haymarket defendants were posthumously pardoned by Governor John Peter Altgeld in 1893, who concluded the trial had been unjust. Parsons's execution is commemorated internationally on May Day.
