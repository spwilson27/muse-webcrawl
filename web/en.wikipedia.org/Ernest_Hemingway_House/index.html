Ernest Hemingway House

Ernest Hemingway House
Location: 907 Whitehead Street, Key West, Florida
Built: 1851
Designation: U.S. National Historic Landmark

The Ernest Hemingway House, officially the Ernest Hemingway Home and Museum, is a residence at 907 Whitehead Street in Key West, Florida, where the writer Ernest Hemingway lived from 1931 to 1939. It is a National Historic Landmark and one of Key West's most popular tourist attractions.

History

The two-storey French Colonial house was built in 1851 of native limestone by marine architect and salvage wrecker Asa Tift. Hemingway and his second wife Pauline bought it in 1931 for $8,000 in back taxes. There he wrote or worked on Death in the Afternoon, Green Hills of Africa, the short stories "The Snows of Kilimanjaro" and "The Short Happy Life of Francis Macomber", and the novel To Have and Have Not.

Features

The property contains Key West's first swimming pool, built in 1937–38 for the then-enormous sum of $20,000, and a writing studio in a converted carriage house connected by a walkway. The grounds are famous for their population of polydactyl (six-toed) cats, descended, according to legend, from a ship captain's cat given to Hemingway; several dozen cats live there today.
