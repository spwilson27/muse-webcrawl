Author: Stephen King

It is a 1986 horror novel by American author Stephen King. It was King's 22nd book and the 17th novel published under his own name. The story follows the experiences of seven children — the self-styled "Losers' Club" — in the fictional town of Derry, Maine, as they are terrorized by an ancient evil entity that exploits their fears, most often in the form of Pennywise the Dancing Clown.

Plot

The narrative alternates between two time periods: 1957–58, when the Losers first confront It as children, and 1984–85, when they return as adults after the creature resumes killing. It awakens roughly every 27 years to feed. As children the Losers wound It and force it into hibernation; as adults they must finish the confrontation in the sewers beneath Derry, while their memories and bonds reshape the final battle.

Themes

The novel explores the power of memory, the traumas of childhood, the loss of innocence, and the idea that belief and unity can give children power that adults lose. At over 1,100 pages it is one of King's longest works and weaves in connections to his wider fictional universe, including the Dark Tower series.

Adaptations

It was adapted as a 1990 television miniseries starring Tim Curry as Pennywise, and as two theatrical films — It (2017) and It Chapter Two (2019) — directed by Andy Muschietti with Bill Skarsgård as Pennywise; the films became the highest-grossing horror releases of their time.
