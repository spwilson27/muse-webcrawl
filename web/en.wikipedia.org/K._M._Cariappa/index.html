Field Marshal Kodandera Madappa Cariappa (28 January 1899 – 15 May 1993) was an Indian military officer who was the first Indian Commander-in-Chief of the Indian Army. Affectionately known as "Kipper", he led Indian forces during the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947 and set enduring standards for the apolitical character of the Indian armed forces. He and Sam Manekshaw are the only Indian officers to have held the rank of field marshal.

Early life and service

Cariappa was born in Coorg (Kodagu) in present-day Karnataka and educated at Presidency College, Madras. He was among the first Indians to receive a King's Commission in the British Indian Army, joining the 2/88 Carnatic Infantry in 1919. He served in Mesopotamia, on the North-West Frontier, and in Burma during the Second World War, became the first Indian to command a battalion, and attended the Imperial Defence College in 1946.

Commander-in-Chief

At independence in 1947 Cariappa commanded the Eastern Command during the Partition disturbances and then supervised operations in Kashmir. On 15 January 1949 he succeeded General Sir Roy Bucher as the first Indian Commander-in-Chief; the date is marked as Army Day in India. He retired in 1953 and served as India's High Commissioner to Australia and New Zealand. He was conferred the rank of Field Marshal in 1986. His son, K. C. Cariappa, an Indian Air Force officer, was taken prisoner in the 1965 war; Cariappa famously declined the Pakistani president Ayub Khan's offer to release him out of turn, insisting he be treated like every other prisoner.
