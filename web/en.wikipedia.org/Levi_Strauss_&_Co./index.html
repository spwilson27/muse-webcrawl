Levi Strauss & Co.

Levi Strauss & Co. is an American clothing company known worldwide for its Levi's brand of denim jeans. It was founded in May 1853 when German immigrant Levi Strauss came from Buttenheim, Bavaria, to San Francisco, California, to open a west coast branch of his brothers' New York dry goods business.

Founded: 1853, San Francisco
Founder: Levi Strauss
Headquarters: San Francisco, California
Products: Jeans, clothing
Brands: Levi's, Dockers, Denizen, Signature

History

In 1873, Strauss and tailor Jacob Davis received a patent for rivet-reinforced denim work pants — the first blue jeans — sold initially to miners and labourers of the California Gold Rush era. The company's two-horse trademark, showing horses failing to pull a pair of jeans apart, dates from 1886.

Growth

Levi's became a global cultural icon in the 20th century, associated with cowboys, rebels and youth culture, and staple styles include the 501 original fit. The company went public in 1971, private again in 1985 in a leveraged buyout, and public again in 2019. It also markets the Dockers khakis brand and has faced ongoing debates about labour practices, water use in denim production and sustainability, responding with initiatives such as Water<Less finishing.
