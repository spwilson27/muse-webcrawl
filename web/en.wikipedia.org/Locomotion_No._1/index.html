Locomotion No. 1

Locomotion No. 1 on display

Locomotion No. 1 (originally named Active) is an early steam locomotive built by George and Robert Stephenson at their Forth Street Works in Newcastle upon Tyne in 1825. It hauled the first train on the Stockton and Darlington Railway, the first public railway in the world to use steam locomotives, on the line's opening day, 27 September 1825.

Design

The locomotive is of 0-4-0 wheel arrangement, with vertical cylinders driving the wheels through connecting rods — a design derived from the Stephenson's earlier Killingworth engines but incorporating a multi-tubular boiler arrangement influenced by developments on the Hetton colliery railway. It weighed about 6.5 tons and was capable of hauling trains of coal wagons and a passenger coach at speeds up to about 24 km/h (15 mph).

Service and preservation

Locomotion worked on the Stockton and Darlington Railway for several years, hauling coal trains; a boiler explosion in 1828 killed its driver, John Gillespie. After rebuilding, it continued in service until 1850. Recognised as an historic artefact, it was preserved and displayed at Darlington's North Road station from 1857, becoming one of the first locomotives in the national collection. It is now displayed at the Head of Steam museum (Hopetown Darlington). A working replica was built in 1975 for the 150th anniversary of the railway and is based at Beamish Museum.
