Carlo Petrini (born 22 June 1949) is an Italian activist, journalist and author, best known as the founder of the international Slow Food movement, which promotes local food traditions, biodiversity and sustainable agriculture as an alternative to fast food and industrial food production.

Biography

Petrini was born in Bra, in the province of Cuneo, Piedmont. He studied sociology at the University of Trento and became involved in left-wing politics in the 1970s, writing about food and wine for Italian newspapers including La Stampa and La Repubblica. He was a leading figure in Arcigola, an association linked to the Italian recreation movement ARCI devoted to gastronomy and conviviality.

Slow Food

In 1986, Petrini protested against the planned opening of a McDonald's restaurant near the Spanish Steps in Rome. Out of that protest grew the Slow Food movement, formally founded in Piedmont in 1986 and launched internationally in Paris in 1989 with the signing of the Slow Food Manifesto. The movement expanded to more than 100,000 members in over 150 countries, organized through local chapters called convivia.

Under Petrini, Slow Food developed the Ark of Taste, a catalogue of endangered heritage foods, and the Presidia (Presìdi) projects that support small-scale producers. In 2004 he founded the University of Gastronomic Sciences in Pollenzo, the first university dedicated to gastronomy, and co-created Terra Madre, a global network of food communities launched the same year.

Ideas and recognition

Petrini's philosophy is summarized in the slogan "good, clean and fair": food should taste good, be produced without harming the environment, and provide fair compensation and conditions for producers and workers. His books include Slow Food Nation (2007) and Terra Madre: Forging a New Global Network of Sustainable Food Communities (2009). In 2004, Time magazine named him one of its European heroes, and in 2016 he was appointed Special Ambassador of the UN Food and Agriculture Organization in Europe.
