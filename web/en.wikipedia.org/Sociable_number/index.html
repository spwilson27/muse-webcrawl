Sociable number

Sociable number
FieldNumber theory
DefinitionNumbers forming a cycle under the aliquot sum function
Smallest cycle5-cycle beginning at 12496

Sociable numbers are numbers that form a cyclic sequence under the aliquot function: starting from a number, repeatedly replacing it by the sum of its proper divisors eventually returns to the starting number after more than two steps. Sociable numbers generalise the ideas of perfect numbers (cycles of length 1) and amicable pairs (cycles of length 2).

Example

The best-known example is the cycle of length 5 discovered by Paul Poulet in 1918: 12496 → 14288 → 15472 → 14536 → 14264 → 12496. The smallest member of this cycle is 12496.

Long cycles

A famous cycle of length 28 begins at 14316. Many cycles of length 4 are known, but no cycle of length 3 has ever been found, and it is an open problem whether any exists. The search for aliquot cycles is related to the unresolved Catalan–Dickson conjecture about the behaviour of aliquot sequences.

History

The concept and the term "sociable numbers" were introduced by the Belgian mathematician Paul Poulet in 1918. Large-scale computer searches since the 1970s have catalogued thousands of cycles; as of recent counts, cycles of many lengths are known, though lengths such as 3 remain unattested.
