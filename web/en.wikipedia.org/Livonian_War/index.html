Livonian War

The Livonian War (1558–1583) was a long conflict for control of Old Livonia — present-day Estonia and Latvia — fought by Russia against Denmark-Norway, Sweden, and the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.

Background

The Livonian Confederation, ruled by the Livonian Order and prince-bishops, was weak and divided, while its neighbours coveted its Baltic ports and trade.

Course

Tsar Ivan IV invaded in 1558, capturing Narva and Dorpat. The Livonian Order dissolved in 1561, its lands partitioned among Poland-Lithuania, Sweden and Denmark. Russia later warred with Sweden and with Stephen Báthory of Poland, whose campaigns forced Ivan to sue for peace.

Settlement

The Truce of Jam Zapolski (1582) with Poland and the Treaty of Plussa (1583) with Sweden stripped Russia of its Livonian gains and Baltic coastline.
