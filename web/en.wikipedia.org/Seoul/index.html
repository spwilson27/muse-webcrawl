Seoul, officially the Seoul Special Metropolitan City, is the capital and largest city of South Korea. With a population of about 9.4 million and more than 26 million in its metropolitan area — roughly half the country's population — it is one of the world's largest urban agglomerations and a leading global centre of technology, commerce and popular culture.

History

The area has been settled since prehistoric times. It was the capital of Baekje as Wiryeseong from 18 BC, later held by Goguryeo and Silla. In 1394 Yi Seong-gye, founder of the Joseon dynasty, made Hanseong (Seoul) his capital, and the city remained Korea's capital through the Japanese colonial period (1910–1945), when it was known as Keijō. Devastated during the Korean War (1950–1953), when it changed hands four times, Seoul rebuilt rapidly and hosted the 1988 Summer Olympics and, jointly with Japan, the 2002 FIFA World Cup.

Geography and districts

Seoul lies in north-western South Korea on the Han River, surrounded by mountains including Bukhansan and Namsan. The city is divided into 25 districts (gu), including Gangnam south of the river, the historic centre of Jongno and Jung, and the commercial hub of Myeong-dong.

Economy and culture

Seoul is home to the headquarters of major conglomerates such as Samsung, Hyundai and LG, and is a hub of the Korean Wave (Hallyu) in music, film and television. Landmarks include Gyeongbokgung Palace, Changdeokgung, the N Seoul Tower and the Lotte World Tower, South Korea's tallest building at 555 metres.
