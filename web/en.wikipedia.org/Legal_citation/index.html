Legal citation

Legal citation
US standard: The Bluebook (21st ed., 2020)
UK standard: OSCOLA
Elements: Case name, reporter volume, page, court, year

Legal citation is the practice of crediting and referring to legal authorities such as statutes, cases, regulations and law review articles in legal writing. A case citation typically identifies the parties, the volume and page of the reporter where the decision is published, the court and the year — for example, Marbury v. Madison, 5 U.S. (1 Cranch) 137 (1803).

Style systems

In the United States, The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation, published by the law reviews of Harvard, Columbia, Yale and the University of Pennsylvania, is the dominant style guide; the ALWD Guide is an alternative. The United Kingdom uses OSCOLA (Oxford Standard for Citation of Legal Authorities). Many jurisdictions have adopted neutral, vendor-independent citations using court, year and decision numbers, especially for electronic publication.

Purpose

Standardised citations allow readers to locate authorities precisely and signal the weight and type of authority being relied upon.
