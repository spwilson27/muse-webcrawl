OriginAnatolia / Persia (uncertain)
Main producing regionLorraine, France

The mirabelle plum (Prunus domestica subsp. syriaca) is a small, sweet, yellow plum cultivar group, prized especially in the Lorraine region of northeastern France, where mirabelle de Lorraine holds protected geographical status.

Description

Mirabelles are small, round to oval fruits, about 2–3 cm across, with smooth golden-yellow skin often speckled with red. The flesh is sweet, aromatic and freestone. The trees are also grown elsewhere in France, Germany and central Europe, where the fruit ripens in late summer.

Uses

Mirabelles are eaten fresh and used in tarts (notably tarte aux mirabelles), jams, compotes and a well-known eau-de-vie distilled from the fermented fruit. Lorraine celebrates the harvest with mirabelle festivals, including in Metz. Most French production comes from Lorraine, which accounts for the large majority of world supply.
