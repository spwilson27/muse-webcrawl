Bande dessinée

Bande dessinée (literally "drawn strip") is the French-language tradition of comics, particularly those from France and Belgium (Franco-Belgian comics). Long respected as the "ninth art", bande dessinée is typically published in large-format hardcover albums.

History

The form traces roots to Rodolphe Töpffer in the 19th century and flourished in magazines such as Spirou and Tintin (founded 1946). Hergé's The Adventures of Tintin and Goscinny and Uderzo's Asterix became worldwide successes.

Format and industry

The standard album is an A4 hardcover of 48 or 64 pages in full colour — markedly different from the American comic book or Japanese tankōbon. France hosts the Angoulême International Comics Festival, one of the world's largest comics events.

Notable series

Notable works include Tintin, Asterix, Lucky Luke, Spirou and Fantasio, The Smurfs, Blueberry, and the works of Moebius (Jean Giraud).
