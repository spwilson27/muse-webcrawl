PAX3

GenePAX3 (paired box 3)
SpeciesHuman

ProteinPaired box protein Pax-3
FunctionTranscription factor in embryonic development

PAX3 (paired box 3) is a gene belonging to the PAX family of transcription factors, characterised by the presence of a paired box DNA-binding domain. In humans the gene is located on chromosome 2q36.1. PAX3 plays a critical role in embryonic development, particularly in the formation of the neural crest, the development of melanocytes, and the specification of skeletal muscle progenitor cells.

During embryogenesis, PAX3 is expressed in the dorsal neural tube and in migrating neural crest cells, as well as in the dermomyotome of the somites, where it helps regulate the programme that gives rise to skeletal muscle of the trunk and limbs. Together with the closely related gene PAX7, it maintains muscle stem (satellite) cell populations.

Clinical significance

Mutations in PAX3 cause forms of Waardenburg syndrome, an auditory-pigmentary disorder characterised by hearing loss and changes in the pigmentation of the hair, skin and eyes; types 1 and 3 of the syndrome are associated with PAX3. Waardenburg syndrome type 3 (Klein–Waardenburg syndrome) additionally involves limb abnormalities.

A chromosomal translocation fusing PAX3 with the FOXO1 gene, t(2;13)(q35;q14), is a hallmark of alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, an aggressive soft-tissue cancer of childhood. The resulting PAX3–FOXO1 fusion protein acts as an aberrant transcription factor driving tumour growth. PAX3 has also been studied in melanoma, where it contributes to melanocyte survival and proliferation.
