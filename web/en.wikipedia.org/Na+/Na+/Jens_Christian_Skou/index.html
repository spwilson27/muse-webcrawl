Jens Christian Skou
Born: 8 October 1918, Lemvig, Denmark
Died: 28 May 2018 (aged 99), Aarhus, Denmark
Known for: Discovery of Na+/K+-ATPase
Awards: Nobel Prize in Chemistry (1997)

Jens Christian Skou (8 October 1918 – 28 May 2018) was a Danish biochemist and Nobel laureate. He is best known for his discovery of the sodium–potassium ATPase (Na+/K+-ATPase), the ion pump present in the plasma membrane of virtually all animal cells, which maintains the electrochemical gradients essential for nerve conduction, muscle contraction and nutrient transport.

Life and career

Skou was born in Lemvig, Denmark, into a wealthy family; his father was a timber and coal merchant. He studied medicine at the University of Copenhagen, graduating in 1944, and after working as a surgeon he turned to research on local anaesthetics. In 1947 he joined Aarhus University, where he earned his doctorate in 1954 and spent the rest of his career, becoming professor of physiology and later of biophysics.

Discovery of the sodium–potassium pump

While studying the action of local anaesthetics on nerve membranes, Skou hypothesised that an enzyme in the membrane might use ATP to transport ions. Working with membrane preparations from crab nerves, in 1957 he identified an ATPase whose activity required both sodium and potassium ions and was inhibited by ouabain — the first ion-transporting enzyme to be characterised. The pump exchanges three sodium ions out of the cell for two potassium ions in, consuming a substantial fraction of the body's ATP.

Nobel Prize

In 1997 Skou shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Paul D. Boyer and John E. Walker "for their elucidation of the enzymatic mechanism underlying the synthesis of adenosine triphosphate (ATP)"; Skou received his half "for the first discovery of an ion-transporting enzyme, Na+, K+-ATPase". He continued working at Aarhus into his late nineties and died in 2018, a few months short of his hundredth birthday.
