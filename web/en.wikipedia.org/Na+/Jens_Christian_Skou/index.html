Jens Christian Skou (8 October 1918 – 28 May 2018) was a Danish biochemist and physician. In 1997 he shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the first discovery of an ion-transporting enzyme, Na+/K+-ATPase — the molecular basis of the sodium–potassium pump present in all animal cells.

Life and career

Skou studied medicine at the University of Copenhagen, graduating in 1944, and initially trained as a surgeon while researching local anaesthetics. The observation that anaesthetics interact with cell membranes led him to study membrane enzymes at Aarhus University, where he spent most of his career and became professor of physiology and later biophysics.

The sodium–potassium pump

In 1957, working with membrane preparations from crab nerves, Skou demonstrated an ATPase activity that was stimulated only when both sodium and potassium ions were present and was inhibited by ouabain. He proposed that this enzyme was the long-sought pump that maintains cells' sodium and potassium gradients. The finding founded the field of active ion transport and was recognised with the Nobel Prize forty years later, shared with Paul Boyer and John Walker for their work on ATP synthesis.

Later life

Skou remained associated with Aarhus University into old age and died in 2018, a few months short of his hundredth birthday.
