Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes

LocationLourdes, Hautes-Pyrénées, France
DenominationRoman Catholic
Founded1858 (apparitions); basilica consecrated 1876
Area52 hectares
Annual visitorsapprox. 3 million pilgrims

The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes (French: Sanctuaire de Notre-Dame de Lourdes) is a Roman Catholic pilgrimage complex in Lourdes, in the foothills of the French Pyrenees. It centres on the Grotto of Massabielle, where in 1858 the 14-year-old Bernadette Soubirous reported eighteen apparitions of the Virgin Mary, and has become one of the most visited Catholic shrines in the world, receiving around three million pilgrims annually, many of them sick or disabled people seeking healing.

The shrine

The sanctuary grounds cover 52 hectares along the Gave de Pau river. Above the grotto stands the Upper Basilica, a neo-Gothic church consecrated in 1876, connected by ramps to the Rosary Basilica (1901). Across the river lies the vast underground Basilica of St Pius X (1958), which can hold some 25,000 worshippers. A crowned statue of the Virgin occupies a niche in the grotto, above the altar where Mass is celebrated daily.

Practices

Key devotions include bathing in or drinking water from the spring Bernadette uncovered at the grotto, the nightly torchlight Marian procession (held since 1872), the Eucharistic procession with blessing of the sick, and the Way of the Cross on the hillside above the basilicas. The Lourdes Medical Bureau examines claims of miraculous cures; of thousands of claims, the Church has recognised around seventy as miraculous.

Organisation

The sanctuary is administered by the Diocese of Tarbes-et-Lourdes. Hospitality organisations, notably the Hospitalité Notre-Dame de Lourdes, coordinate the volunteers who assist sick pilgrims, and the town of Lourdes, with only about 13,000 residents, has one of the largest hotel capacities in France.
