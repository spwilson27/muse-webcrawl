Ralph H. Fowler

Born1889, Roydon, Essex, England
Died1944, Cambridge, England
FieldsMathematical physics, astrophysics
InstitutionsUniversity of Cambridge

Sir Ralph Howard Fowler FRS (1889–1944) was a British physicist and astronomer. He is known for applying quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics to astrophysics, most famously in his 1926 explanation of white dwarf stars using Fermi–Dirac statistics.

Work

Fowler supervised a remarkable generation of physicists at Cambridge, including Paul Dirac, and was the doctoral adviser or mentor of several future Nobel laureates. With E. A. Milne he worked on stellar atmospheres. The Fowler–Nordheim theory of field electron emission and the Darwin–Fowler method in statistical mechanics bear his name.

Honours

He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1925, awarded the Adams Prize, and knighted in 1942. The lunar crater Fowler is named in part after him.
