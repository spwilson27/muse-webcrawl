Nuevo León

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nuevo León (Spanish: [ˈnweβo leˈon], "New León") is a state in north-eastern Mexico, bordered by the states of Tamaulipas to the north and east, Coahuila to the west, and San Luis Potosí and Zacatecas to the south, with a short 15-kilometre border with the US state of Texas. Its capital and largest city is Monterrey, one of Mexico's most important industrial and commercial centres.

History

The territory was originally inhabited by nomadic indigenous groups such as the Coahuiltecan peoples. Spanish settlement began in the late 16th century under Luis Carvajal y de la Cueva, who founded the New Kingdom of León (Nuevo Reino de León) in 1582; Monterrey was founded in 1596 by Diego de Montemayor. Development was slow for centuries because of the arid climate and indigenous resistance.

After Mexican independence, Nuevo León became a state of the federation in 1824. In the mid-19th century the state, under figures such as Santiago Vidaurri, at times acted almost independently and was briefly merged with Coahuila. From the late 19th century, Monterrey industrialised rapidly — steel (the Fundidora, 1900), glass, beer, and cement — making Nuevo León the industrial heartland of Mexico and home to many of its largest corporations and its most prominent private university, the Tecnológico de Monterrey.

Geography and economy

The Sierra Madre Oriental crosses the state, and the iconic Cerro de la Silla ("Saddle Mountain") dominates Monterrey's skyline. The climate ranges from semi-arid plains in the north to temperate mountains in the south. The economy is among the strongest of any Mexican state, with high levels of foreign investment, manufacturing (including automotive and appliance plants), steel, and services; the state consistently records one of Mexico's highest human development indices. Popular natural attractions include the Cola de Caballo waterfall, the García Caves (Grutas de García), and the Chipinque ecological park.
