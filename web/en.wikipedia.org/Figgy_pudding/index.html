Figgy pudding

Figgy pudding is a traditional British Christmas dessert closely related to Christmas pudding (plum pudding). Despite its name, historical recipes did not necessarily contain figs; in medieval English, "figgy" could refer to a dish containing raisins or other dried fruit — the word "fig" was at times used loosely for raisins. The pudding is made with suet, breadcrumbs, flour, eggs, sugar, spices and dried fruits, and is traditionally steamed for several hours.

History

Early predecessors of figgy pudding date to the fourteenth century, when "fygey" was a spiced, thickened dish of ground almonds, wine and figs or raisins served during Lent. By the seventeenth century it had evolved into a sweet pudding associated with festive occasions. Figgy pudding is immortalised in the Christmas carol "We Wish You a Merry Christmas", in which carolers demand "Oh, bring us some figgy pudding" and refuse to leave until they receive some — a reflection of the old custom of rewarding Christmas "mummers" and wassailers with food and drink.

Preparation

Modern versions often feature figs prominently, along with dates, raisins, brandy and warm spices such as cinnamon and nutmeg. The pudding is steamed in a basin, unmoulded, and served with custard, cream or hard sauce; it may be flambéed with brandy at the table.
