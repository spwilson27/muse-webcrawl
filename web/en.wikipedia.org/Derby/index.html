Derby is a city and unitary authority area in Derbyshire, England. It lies on the banks of the River Derwent in the south of Derbyshire, of which it was traditionally the county town. Derby gained city status in 1977, and by the 2011 census its population stood at about 249,000. The city gives its name to "derby" fixtures in sport, a term associated with local rivalries, and is renowned internationally for its industrial heritage and its role in the birth of the factory system during the Industrial Revolution.

History

The area of modern Derby was settled by the Romans, who established the fort of Derventio on the east bank of the Derwent near Little Chester. After the Roman withdrawal, the site became an Anglo-Saxon settlement, and its Old Norse name, Djúrabý — "village of the deer" — gave the modern name Derby. The Danes made it one of the Five Boroughs of the Danelaw, together with Leicester, Lincoln, Nottingham and Stamford.

During the English Civil War, Derby was garrisoned by Parliamentary troops commanded by Sir John Gell. In 1745, Bonnie Prince Charlie's Jacobite army reached Derby on its march south — the furthest point of the rising — before the fateful decision to retreat was taken at Exeter House on 4 December 1745, remembered as "Swarkestone Bridge" being the army's southernmost point and the council of war at Derby turning the campaign back.

Industrial Revolution

Derby was a centre of the British Industrial Revolution. In 1721, John Lombe built the Silk Mill on an island in the Derwent — often regarded as the world's first factory — after allegedly acquiring Italian silk-throwing technology by industrial espionage. The Derby Works of the Midland Railway later made the city a major railway centre; Derby station opened in 1840, and the city remains associated with train building through the former Bombardier (now Alstom) works at Litchurch Lane, as well as Rolls-Royce, whose aero-engine division has been based at Sinfin since the early twentieth century.

Economy

Derby's economy is strongly associated with advanced transport manufacturing: Rolls-Royce (aero engines), Alstom (trains, at the historic Derby Litchurch Lane Works) and Toyota (whose UK car plant is at Burnaston, just outside the city). The city has a significant rail cluster and is home to the University of Derby.

Sport

Derby County Football Club, nicknamed the Rams, was founded in 1884 and plays at Pride Park Stadium. The club won the First Division championship in 1971–72 and 1974–75 under Brian Clough and Dave Mackay. In horse racing, "The Derby" at Epsom Downs, first run in 1780, took its name from the 12th Earl of Derby; races named "derby" are now run worldwide, and the word has come to denote any keenly contested local sporting fixture.

Landmarks

  • Derby Cathedral (Cathedral of All Saints), with its Perpendicular Gothic tower and a screen by Robert Bakewell
  • The Silk Mill, now the Museum of Making, a UNESCO Derwent Valley Mills World Heritage Site component
  • Pickford's House Museum and Derby Museum and Art Gallery, noted for its Joseph Wright of Derby paintings
  • Markeaton Park and Darley Park
