The University of California, Santa Barbara (UC Santa Barbara or UCSB) is a public land-grant research university in Santa Barbara County, California, United States. It is part of the University of California system and traces its roots to the Anna Blake School, founded in 1891. The campus joined the UC system in 1944 and moved to its present seaside site — a former Marine air station on a bluff above the Pacific Ocean — in the 1950s.

Academics and research

UCSB comprises five colleges and schools, including the College of Engineering, the College of Letters and Science, and the College of Creative Studies, an undergraduate program modelled on graduate study. The university is classified as an R1 research university and is a member of the Association of American Universities. Its faculty have included several Nobel laureates; the Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics, based on campus, is a leading center for physics research, and the National Center for Ecological Analysis and Synthesis has been influential in environmental science.

Campus and student life

The 1,127-acre campus borders the Pacific Ocean and includes its own beach and lagoon. Most undergraduates live in or near Isla Vista, the densely populated student community adjacent to campus. UCSB's athletic teams, the Gauchos, compete in NCAA Division I, and the men's soccer team famously won the 2006 national championship before record crowds.
