Cabinet Secretary (United Kingdom)

The Cabinet Secretary is the most senior civil servant in the United Kingdom and the head of the Cabinet Office. The post holder serves as the prime minister's principal adviser on the running of government, supports the Cabinet and its committees, and is formally Head of the Civil Service, providing leadership to the several hundred thousand civil servants across departments. The Cabinet Secretary is based at 70 Whitehall, adjacent to 10 Downing Street.

Role

The Cabinet Secretary organises the business of the Cabinet: preparing agendas, commissioning papers and recording decisions, and ensuring that collective decisions are implemented across government. The role also includes responsibility for the honours system, the machinery of intelligence coordination (working through the Joint Intelligence Committee structure), and propriety and ethics in public life, including advice on the Ministerial Code.

History

The office was created in December 1916, when Maurice Hankey became the first secretary to the Cabinet under David Lloyd George, formalising cabinet minutes and collective decision-making for the first time. Successive holders — Hankey, Edward Bridges, Norman Brook and Burke Trend among them — shaped the modern office. From 1983 the post was combined with Head of the Home Civil Service; between 2012 and 2014 the two functions were briefly split before being reunited. Cabinet Secretaries hold the rank of permanent secretary and are typically appointed for the duration of a government's tenure, though they formally serve across changes of administration.
