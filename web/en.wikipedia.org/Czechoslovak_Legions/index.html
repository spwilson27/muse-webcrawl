Czechoslovak Legions

The Czechoslovak Legions were volunteer military formations composed chiefly of Czechs and Slovaks during the First World War. Raised in Russia, France and Italy, they fought for the Entente with the goal of winning Allied support for an independent Czechoslovak state free of Austria-Hungary.

The Russian Legion

The largest legion formed in Russia from Czech and Slovak expatriates and prisoners of war. It distinguished itself at the Battle of Zboriv in 1917 during the Kerensky Offensive. After the Bolsheviks seized power and signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, the legion sought evacuation via the Trans-Siberian Railway to Vladivostok, from where it would sail to France.

Revolt and the Siberian anabasis

In May 1918 clashes with Bolshevik authorities escalated into open revolt. The legion, numbering some 40,000–60,000 men, seized much of the Trans-Siberian Railway and several Siberian cities, becoming a significant factor in the Russian Civil War. Between 1918 and 1920 the legionnaires were evacuated from Vladivostok by sea, completing a journey around the world back to the newly proclaimed Czechoslovakia.

Legions in France and Italy

Smaller Czechoslovak units fought on the Western Front in France and on the Italian front, including at the Piave. Their service helped persuade the Allies to recognise the Czechoslovak National Council, led by Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, as a government-in-waiting.

Legacy

The legions became a founding legend of the First Czechoslovak Republic, and many legionnaires went on to form the officer corps of its army. Their Siberian odyssey inspired novels, films and the term "anabasis", after Xenophon's account of a Greek army's march home.
