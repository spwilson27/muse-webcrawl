Sabaeans

The Sabaeans were an ancient South Arabian people who established the kingdom of Saba, centred in what is now Yemen, with its capital at Marib. Flourishing from roughly the 8th century BCE to the 3rd century CE, Saba grew rich on the incense trade in frankincense and myrrh.

History

Sabaean kings, known by the title mukarrib and later malik, built temples, irrigation works including the great Marib Dam, and caravan cities along the incense route to the Mediterranean. The kingdom is often associated with the biblical Queen of Sheba, called Bilqis in Islamic tradition. Saba was eventually absorbed by the neighbouring kingdom of Himyar.

Language and religion

The Sabaeans spoke Sabaic, an Old South Arabian language written in the Ancient South Arabian script. Their religion was polytheistic, with the moon god Almaqah as a chief deity, worshipped at temples such as Awwam and Barran.
