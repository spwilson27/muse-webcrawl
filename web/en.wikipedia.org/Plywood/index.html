Plywood

Plywood is a composite material manufactured from thin layers, or "plies", of wood veneer that are bonded together with adjacent layers having their wood grain rotated up to 90 degrees to one another. It is an engineered wood from the family of manufactured boards, which include medium-density fibreboard (MDF), oriented strand board (OSB), and particle board (chipboard).

All plywoods bind resin and wood fibre sheets (cellulose cells are long, strong and thin) to form a composite material. This alternation of the grain is called cross-graining and has several important benefits: it reduces the tendency of wood to split when nailed at the edges; it reduces expansion and shrinkage, providing improved dimensional stability; and it makes the strength of the panel consistent across all directions. There is usually an odd number of plies, so that the sheet is balanced — this reduces warping.

History

Laminated wood was used in ancient Egypt and Greece. Modern plywood production began in the early 20th century; the first commercial production in the United States began in Portland, Oregon, in 1905. The material became widely used in construction, furniture, boat building, and aircraft — most famously in the de Havilland Mosquito bomber of the Second World War.

Types

  • Softwood plywood, made from fir, pine, or spruce, used mainly in construction.
  • Hardwood plywood, made from birch, oak, or maple, used in furniture and cabinetry.
  • Marine plywood, made with waterproof glue and durable face veneers for use in moist environments and boat building.
  • Aircraft plywood, made from mahogany or birch with high-strength adhesives.
  • Flexible plywood, designed to bend for curved structures.

Manufacturing

Logs are softened with heat and steam, then peeled on a rotary lathe into continuous sheets of veneer. The veneers are dried, graded, coated with adhesive, laid up in cross-grained stacks, and pressed under high pressure and heat. The finished panels are trimmed, sanded, and graded by the quality of their faces.

Applications

Plywood is used in construction (roofing, flooring, formwork), furniture, cabinetry, packaging, signage, vehicle interiors, and marine construction. Its high strength-to-weight ratio and resistance to splitting make it one of the most versatile engineered wood products.
