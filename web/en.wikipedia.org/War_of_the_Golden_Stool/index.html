The War of the Golden Stool, also known as the Yaa Asantewaa War, was the fifth and final Anglo-Ashanti war, fought in 1900 between the British Empire and the Ashanti Empire in present-day Ghana. The conflict was triggered when the British governor of the Gold Coast, Sir Frederick Hodgson, demanded to sit upon the Golden Stool (Sika Dwa Kofi), the sacred throne and embodiment of the soul of the Ashanti nation — an act considered deeply sacrilegious.

Course of the war

After Hodgson's speech at Kumasi in March 1900 demanding the Stool, the Ashanti rose under the leadership of Yaa Asantewaa, the queen mother of Edweso (Ejisu), who rallied the remaining chiefs to resist. Ashanti forces besieged the British in the fort at Kumasi; Hodgson and a party broke out and reached the coast, while the garrison was relieved in July. British-led columns, many of them Hausa and Yoruba troops, eventually overcame the resistance by September 1900, though the Golden Stool itself was hidden and never surrendered.

Aftermath

Yaa Asantewaa and other leaders were exiled to the Seychelles. Ashanti was formally annexed and incorporated into the Gold Coast colony on 1 January 1902. The Golden Stool resurfaced in the 1920s and remains the most sacred symbol of Ashanti unity; Yaa Asantewaa is celebrated in Ghana as a national heroine.
