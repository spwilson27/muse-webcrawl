Bone tumor

SpecialtyOrthopedic oncology
TypesBenign (e.g. osteochondroma); malignant (e.g. osteosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma)
SymptomsBone pain, swelling, pathological fracture
DiagnosisX-ray, MRI, CT, biopsy

A bone tumor is an abnormal growth of tissue within bone. Bone tumors may be benign (non-cancerous) or malignant (cancerous). Primary malignant bone tumors — arising in bone itself — are rare, accounting for less than one percent of all cancers; much more common are secondary tumors, where cancers from elsewhere in the body metastasise to bone.

Classification

Benign bone tumors include osteochondroma (the most common), enchondroma, osteoid osteoma, and giant cell tumor of bone. The main primary malignancies are osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, and Ewing sarcoma. Osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma occur most often in children and adolescents, frequently around the knee.

Signs and diagnosis

Symptoms include persistent bone pain (often worse at night), a palpable mass, swelling, and fractures after minimal trauma. Imaging with X-ray, MRI, and CT characterises the lesion, but definitive diagnosis requires biopsy interpreted by specialist pathologists.

Treatment

Management depends on type and stage: benign lesions may need only observation or curettage, while malignant tumors typically combine chemotherapy with limb-sparing surgical resection; amputation is now rarely required. Metastatic bone disease is treated with systemic therapy, radiation, and bone-modifying agents.
