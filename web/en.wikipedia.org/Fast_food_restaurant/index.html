Fast food restaurant

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fast food restaurant, also known as a quick service restaurant (QSR), is a specific type of restaurant that serves fast food cuisine and has minimal table service. The food is usually served quickly from a limited menu, cooked in bulk in advance and kept hot, finished and packaged to order, and commonly available for take-out, delivery or drive-through service. The concept depends on speed, standardisation and low cost: meals are prepared following strict procedures so that the product is identical from one outlet to the next.

History

Although cooked food sold from stalls is ancient, the modern fast food restaurant emerged in the United States in the early 20th century. White Castle, founded in 1921, is often credited as the first fast food chain, standardising the hamburger and the assembly-line kitchen. After the Second World War, car culture and suburbanisation fuelled explosive growth: McDonald's — revolutionised by the McDonald brothers' "Speedee Service System" of 1948 and expanded by Ray Kroc from 1955 — became the archetype, followed by Burger King, KFC, Pizza Hut, Subway and many others, most expanding through franchising.

Business model and criticism

Chains typically operate through franchising, in which independent operators pay fees and royalties to use the brand, menu and supply chain. Menus centre on items such as hamburgers, fried chicken, pizza, sandwiches, tacos and fries, adapted to local tastes in international markets. The industry is among the world's largest employers of low-wage labour and has been criticised for its effects on public health (links between fast food consumption and obesity), labour practices, marketing to children, and environmental impact. In response, chains have added salads, grilled options and nutritional labelling, while "fast casual" competitors have blurred the category's boundaries.
