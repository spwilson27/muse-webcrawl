Cod

Cod is the common name for demersal fish of the genus Gadus, belonging to the family Gadidae. The two most commercially important species are the Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua), found in the colder waters of the North Atlantic, and the Pacific cod (Gadus macrocephalus) of the North Pacific.

Characteristics

Cod are bottom-dwelling predators with a distinctive barbel on the chin, three dorsal fins and two anal fins. Atlantic cod can grow to about 2 metres and over 90 kilograms, though typical market fish are far smaller. They feed on smaller fish, crustaceans and invertebrates.

Fisheries

Cod has been one of the most important food fish in the North Atlantic for centuries; dried and salted cod (bacalao) was a staple of trade and a driver of exploration and colonisation. Heavy overfishing led to the collapse of the northern cod stock off Newfoundland in 1992, prompting a moratorium, and many stocks remain depleted despite management measures. Cod is eaten fresh, frozen, and as fish and chips; its liver yields cod liver oil, rich in vitamins A and D.
