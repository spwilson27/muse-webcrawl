Hot hatch

A hot hatch is a high-performance version of a hatchback car, typically a front-wheel-drive compact or small family car fitted with a more powerful engine, sportier suspension and brakes, and distinctive styling. The term, shortened from "hot hatchback", came into common use in the 1980s, and the cars are prized for combining everyday practicality with sports-car performance at a relatively modest price.

History

Early examples include the 1973 Simca 1100 Ti and the 1975 Autobianchi A112 Abarth, but the template was set by the Volkswagen Golf GTI of 1976, which paired a fuel-injected 1.6-litre engine with a practical five-door (later) hatchback body and became an enduring icon. Rivals such as the Peugeot 205 GTi and Renault 5 GT Turbo defined the class in the 1980s, when hot hatches became hugely popular in Europe.

Modern hot hatches

Power outputs have risen steadily; modern hot hatches such as the Volkswagen Golf R, Honda Civic Type R, Ford Focus RS, and Hyundai i30 N deliver 300 horsepower or more, often with turbocharged engines, all-wheel drive or sophisticated limited-slip differentials, and performance rivalling dedicated sports cars. The format remains especially popular in Europe, though SUVs and electrification have reshaped the segment in the 2020s.
