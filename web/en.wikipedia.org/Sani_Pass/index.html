Sani Pass

Elevation2,876 m (9,436 ft)
RangeDrakensberg
LocationBorder of KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, and Mokhotlong District, Lesotho
RouteFrom Himeville/Underberg (South Africa) to Mokhotlong (Lesotho)
SurfaceGravel (South African side); tarred on the Lesotho side

Sani Pass is a mountain pass in the Drakensberg range, linking the town of Himeville in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, with Mokhotlong in Lesotho. At 2,876 metres above sea level, it is the highest road pass in Southern Africa and one of the most dramatic mountain roads on the continent, famed for its steep gravel switchbacks up the Great Escarpment.

Route and history

The road climbs roughly 1,300 metres in its final eight kilometres from the South African border post, zig-zagging in hairpin bends beneath basalt cliffs to the Lesotho border post at the summit. The pass began as a bridle path for Basotho traders and migrant labourers; a rough track was pioneered by 4x4 in 1948–49 by Godfrey Edmonds and others associated with the Mokhotlong Mountain Transport Company, which thereafter ran supplies into eastern Lesotho by jeep and mule where no other road existed.

For decades the unsurfaced South African section demanded high-clearance four-wheel-drive vehicles and was frequently closed by snow, ice and rockfalls in winter. Lesotho tarred its side of the road in the 2010s as part of the A14 route. Long-running plans to tar the South African section have been repeatedly debated and phased, balancing access and trade against the pass's value as an adventure-tourism attraction.

Tourism

Sani Pass is a major draw for 4x4 tours, motorcyclists and cyclists, typically departing from Underberg and Himeville. At the summit stands the Sani Mountain Lodge, which markets its pub as the "highest pub in Africa". The surrounding uKhahlamba Drakensberg Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, offers hiking, San rock art and endemic high-altitude wildlife, while descending travellers enter Lesotho's remote eastern highlands.
