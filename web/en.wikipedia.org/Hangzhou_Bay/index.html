Hangzhou Bay

Hangzhou Bay is a funnel-shaped inlet of the East China Sea, bordered by the province of Zhejiang to the south and the municipality of Shanghai to the north, in eastern China. The Qiantang River flows into the bay.

Geography and tides

The bay narrows from about 100 kilometres wide at its mouth to about 20 kilometres at its head. Its shape amplifies tides dramatically: the Qiantang tidal bore, one of the largest in the world, can reach heights of up to about 9 metres and has been watched by spectators for centuries, especially around the Mid-Autumn Festival. Zhoushan Archipelago lies at the bay's mouth, and major ports include Ningbo-Zhoushan, among the world's busiest by cargo tonnage.

Crossings and development

The Hangzhou Bay Bridge, opened in 2008, links Ningbo with Jiaxing and Shanghai, greatly shortening road journeys around the bay. The surrounding region is one of China's most industrialised and densely populated coasts.
