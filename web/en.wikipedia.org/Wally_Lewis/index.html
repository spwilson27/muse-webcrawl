Walter James Lewis AM (born 1 December 1959), nicknamed "The King" and also known as "the Emperor of Lang Park", is an Australian former professional rugby league footballer who played in the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s, and coached in the 1980s and 1990s. He became a commentator for television coverage of the sport. Lewis is widely regarded as one of the greatest rugby league players of all time, and is one of the original rugby league "Immortals".

Playing career

Lewis played club football in the Brisbane Rugby League premiership with Fortitude Valley and Wynnum Manly, and had a short stint with Wakefield Trinity in England. He was the inaugural captain of the Brisbane Broncos in their first seasons in the NSWRL premiership from 1988, and finished his career with the Gold Coast Seagulls in 1992.

For Queensland, Lewis is synonymous with State of Origin football: he captained the Maroons in 30 of his 31 Origin appearances between 1980 and 1991, won a record eight man-of-the-match awards, and produced legendary performances — none more famous than Game 2 of 1989, when he led an injury-ravaged Queensland side to victory — that established the Origin mythology. He captained Australia in 24 Tests and toured with the undefeated 1982 Kangaroos ("the Invincibles") and the 1986 Kangaroos.

Honours and later life

Lewis was inducted into the Sport Australia Hall of Fame and the Australian Rugby League Hall of Fame, named in the Australian Rugby League's Team of the Century (2008) at five-eighth, and awarded the Medal of the Order of Australia. A bronze statue of him stands outside Lang Park (Suncorp Stadium) in Brisbane. After retiring he coached the Gold Coast Seagulls and Queensland, and became a long-serving sports presenter and commentator for Nine News Queensland; he has spoken publicly about living with epilepsy following brain surgery.
