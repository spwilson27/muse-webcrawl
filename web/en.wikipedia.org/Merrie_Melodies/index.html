Merrie Melodies

Merrie Melodies
Produced: 1931–1969
Studio: Warner Bros. Cartoons
Companion series: Looney Tunes

Merrie Melodies is an American animated comedy short-film series distributed by Warner Bros. from 1931 to 1969, during the golden age of American animation. Together with its sister series Looney Tunes, it introduced such stars as Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, Porky Pig, Elmer Fudd, Tweety, Sylvester, Yosemite Sam, Foghorn Leghorn, Marvin the Martian, Pepé Le Pew, Speedy Gonzales, Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner, and Tasmanian Devil.

Origins

Producer Leon Schlesinger created Looney Tunes in 1930, inspired by Disney's Silly Symphonies, and Merrie Melodies in 1931 as one-shot cartoons built around songs from Warner's music catalogue — a way to promote the studio's songs through animation. Early entries were musical fantasies; characters such as Foxy and Piggy came and went. From 1934 Merrie Melodies moved to colour (initially two-strip Technicolor, then three-strip) while Looney Tunes remained black-and-white until 1943.

Golden age

Directors Tex Avery, Bob Clampett, Friz Freleng, Chuck Jones and Robert McKimson, with voice artist Mel Blanc and composer Carl Stalling, turned the series into a byword for fast, anarchic cartoon comedy. Famous shorts include What's Opera, Doc? (1957) and One Froggy Evening (1955). By the 1940s the practical distinction from Looney Tunes had vanished; both series used the same characters and staff, with Merrie Melodies' rings and theme — "Merrily We Roll Along" — distinguishing the branding. Production ended in 1969 with the closing of the Warner animation studio, though the name has been revived in later television projects.
