Canyon Diablo refers to the many fragments of the asteroid that created Meteor Crater (also known as Barringer Crater) in Arizona, United States. The meteorites have been found around the crater rim and are named for nearby Canyon Diablo, a dry wash west of the crater. The impact occurred roughly 49,000 years ago, when an iron-nickel asteroid estimated at about 50 metres in diameter struck the Colorado Plateau at several kilometres per second, excavating a crater about 1.2 kilometres across and 170 metres deep.

Composition and discovery

Canyon Diablo is a coarse octahedrite iron meteorite, notable for containing graphite nodules with tiny diamonds formed by shock pressures during the impact. The largest recovered fragment, the Holsinger meteorite, weighs about 639 kg and is displayed at the Meteor Crater visitor centre. Fragments were historically collected and studied by geologist Daniel Barringer, who argued—against early skepticism—that the crater was meteoritic in origin, a conclusion later confirmed by Eugene Shoemaker's discovery of the high-pressure silica minerals coesite and stishovite in the crater.

Canyon Diablo is the type material for the obsolete mineral name "cliftonite" (a form of graphite pseudomorph after diamond) and is among the most extensively studied iron meteorites in science.
