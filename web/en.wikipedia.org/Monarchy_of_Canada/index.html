The monarchy of Canada is the form of government embodied by the Canadian sovereign and head of state. It is one of the key components of Canadian parliamentary democracy and is at the core of the country's constitutional federal structure. The reigning monarch is Charles III, who has reigned since 8 September 2022. The monarch is represented in Canada by the governor general, and in each of the ten provinces by a lieutenant governor.

Canada is one of the oldest continuous monarchies in the world, with origins in the French and British colonial periods. The institution was confirmed at Confederation in 1867 and was given a distinctly Canadian character by the Statute of Westminster 1931 and the patriation of the constitution in 1982. The sovereign's Canadian royal style includes the title "King of Canada" and, since 1953, recognition as "Defender of the Faith" in the Canadian context.

Constitutional role

Under the Constitution Act, 1867, executive government and authority in Canada is vested in the Crown. In practice, the sovereign and the governor general act almost always on the advice of the prime minister and Cabinet — the principle of responsible government. The Crown is nevertheless the formal source of authority for the executive, legislative, and judicial branches, and all acts of state are issued in the monarch's name.

Federal and provincial Crowns

Because Canada is a federation, the Crown is understood to operate both federally and within each province — sometimes described as a "divided Crown". Lieutenant governors represent the sovereign in the provinces and, like the governor general, perform ceremonial and constitutional duties including granting royal assent to provincial legislation.

History

The French Crown ruled colonies in what is now Canada from the 16th century until 1763, when New France was ceded to Britain. British monarchs then ruled until Confederation created the Dominion of Canada in 1867. Through the 20th century the Canadian Crown became legally distinct from the British Crown, a process completed with the Canada Act 1982.
