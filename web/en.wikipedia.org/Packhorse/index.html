Packhorse

A packhorse or pack horse is a horse, mule, donkey or pony used to carry goods on its back, usually in sidebags or panniers slung across a packsaddle. Before the development of good roads and wheeled transport, trains of pack animals were the principal means of moving freight overland across much of the world.

History

Packhorses were used from antiquity: caravans of pack camels, mules and horses carried trade goods along routes such as the Silk Road. In Britain, packhorse trains carrying wool, lead, salt and coal remained common into the 18th and 19th centuries, travelling narrow packhorse routes marked by low, single-arched packhorse bridges. In western North America, pack mules supplied mining camps and remain in use for backcountry work.

Equipment and practice

Loads are balanced on either side of the animal and typically limited to around 70–90 kg (150–200 lb) for a horse. Specialised knots such as the diamond hitch secure cargo under a tarpaulin. Pack animals are led in strings by a packer or muleteer, with bells traditionally fitted to warn other traffic on narrow trails.
