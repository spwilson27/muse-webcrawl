Aspen

GenusPopulus (section Populus)
FamilySalicaceae
SpeciesP. tremula, P. tremuloides, P. grandidentata, others

Aspens are deciduous trees of the genus Populus, native to cold regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They are known for their smooth pale bark, rounded leaves that flutter in light wind, and brilliant yellow autumn foliage.

Species and distribution

The Eurasian aspen (Populus tremula) ranges across Europe and northern Asia, while North America has the quaking aspen (P. tremuloides) and bigtooth aspen (P. grandidentata). Quaking aspen is the most widely distributed tree species in North America.

Reproduction and clonal colonies

Aspens reproduce both by seed and by root suckers, forming clonal colonies in which many trunks share a single root system and genetic identity. The famous Pando colony in Utah, composed of tens of thousands of quaking aspen stems, is among the largest and oldest known living organisms by mass. The flattened leaf stalks cause the leaves to tremble or "quake" in the slightest breeze.

Ecology and uses

Aspens are pioneer species that colonise disturbed ground, including areas recovering from wildfire, and their stands support diverse wildlife. The light, soft wood is used for pulp, matches, and oriented strand board.
