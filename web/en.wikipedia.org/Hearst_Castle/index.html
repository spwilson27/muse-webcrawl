Hearst Castle, known formally as La Cuesta Encantada ("The Enchanted Hill"), is a historic estate in San Simeon, located on the Central Coast of California. Conceived by William Randolph Hearst, the publishing tycoon, and his architect Julia Morgan, the castle was built between 1919 and 1947. Since 1958 it has been operated as a California State Park and is open to the public for tours.

History

The land was originally purchased by George Hearst, a mining magnate and United States senator, in 1865. After the death of William Randolph Hearst's mother Phoebe in 1919, Hearst inherited the ranch and soon commissioned Julia Morgan to design a retreat on the hill known as Camp Hill. Construction continued for nearly three decades, with Hearst's changing ideas constantly reshaping the plans.

During the 1920s and 1930s the castle became famous for the lavish parties attended by Hollywood stars, politicians and other celebrities, who arrived by train at San Luis Obispo or by private plane at the estate's airstrip. The estate also maintained one of the world's largest private zoos, with animals such as zebras still roaming the surrounding hills.

Architecture

The main house, Casa Grande, contains 115 rooms including 38 bedrooms, 42 bathrooms and a large assembly room. Its twin towers were inspired by the cathedral of Santa Maria la Mayor in Ronda, Spain. The estate also includes three guest houses — Casa del Mar, Casa del Monte and Casa del Sol — and two spectacular swimming pools: the outdoor Neptune Pool, framed by Roman temple facades, and the indoor Roman Pool, decorated with blue and gold mosaic tiles.

Hearst filled the buildings with an enormous collection of art and antiques drawn from across Europe, including entire ceilings, fireplaces and doorways imported from monasteries and castles.
