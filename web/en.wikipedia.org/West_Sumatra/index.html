West Sumatra (Indonesian: Sumatera Barat) is a province of Indonesia on the west coast of the island of Sumatra. It faces the Indian Ocean and includes the offshore Mentawai Islands. The provincial capital and largest city is Padang. The province covers about 42,000 square kilometres and had a population of roughly 5.5 million at the 2020 census.

Geography

The terrain rises from a narrow coastal plain to the Barisan Mountains, which run the length of the province and include active volcanoes such as Mount Marapi, Mount Talang and Mount Kerinci (the highest peak in Sumatra, on the border with Jambi). Inland lakes include Lake Singkarak and Lake Maninjau, the latter filling a volcanic caldera. The province lies on the seismically active Sunda megathrust and has suffered major earthquakes, notably in 2009, when an earthquake of magnitude 7.9 devastated Padang.

People and culture

The Minangkabau, the province's dominant ethnic group, form the world's largest matrilineal society: ancestral property and the family longhouse, the rumah gadang, pass through the female line. Minangkabau culture also features the tradition of merantau — young men leaving home to seek experience and fortune — which has spread Minangkabau restaurants, famous for dishes such as rendang, nasi Padang and sate Padang, across Indonesia and beyond. The province is a stronghold of Islam, blended historically with customary law (adat).

History

The highlands were the heartland of the Pagaruyung Kingdom. In the early 19th century the region was convulsed by the Padri War (1803–1837), in which reformist Muslims fought customary chiefs; the Dutch intervened and extended colonial control over the region. During the Indonesian Revolution and the 1950s, West Sumatra was a centre of opposition to Jakarta — figures such as Mohammad Hatta, Indonesia's first vice-president, came from the region, and the short-lived Revolutionary Government of the Republic of Indonesia (PRRI) was proclaimed here in 1958.

Administration and economy

The province is divided into regencies and cities, including Bukittinggi, a major tourist centre known for the Jam Gadang clock tower, and the old port of Padang. Agriculture — rice, cinnamon, coffee and palm oil — fishing, tourism (including surfing in the Mentawai Islands) and small industry form the basis of the economy.
