Living National Treasure (Japan)

Living National Treasure is the popular name for a person certified by the Japanese government, under the 1950 Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties, as a Holder of an Important Intangible Cultural Property — a master of a traditional performing art or craft whose skills should be preserved.

System

Holders are designated in fields such as kabuki, bunraku, noh, traditional dance and music, pottery, swordsmithing, lacquerware, dyeing and weaving, papermaking and doll-making. Designation carries an annual grant to support training of successors and documentation of technique. At any time there have been only a little over a hundred living holders, and certification is considered one of Japan's highest cultural honours; the system has been imitated by South Korea and other countries.

Notable holders have included kabuki actors, the potter Shoji Hamada among the first designees of 1955, swordsmiths, and masters of dyeing such as the kimono artist Itchiku Kubota.
