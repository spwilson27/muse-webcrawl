HMOX1

Heme oxygenase 1 (HO-1) is an enzyme that in humans is encoded by the HMOX1 gene, located on chromosome 22q12. HO-1 catalyses the first and rate-limiting step in the degradation of heme, cleaving the heme ring to produce biliverdin, free iron (Fe2+), and carbon monoxide (CO). Biliverdin is subsequently reduced to bilirubin by biliverdin reductase.

Function

Unlike the constitutively expressed heme oxygenase 2 (HO-2), HO-1 is highly inducible: its expression is upregulated by heme itself, oxidative stress, heavy metals, cytokines, endotoxin, and other cellular stressors, largely through the Nrf2 transcription factor pathway. The reaction products are biologically active — carbon monoxide acts as a signalling molecule with vasodilatory and anti-inflammatory effects, biliverdin and bilirubin are antioxidants, and the released iron is sequestered by ferritin, whose synthesis HO-1 activity also promotes.

Clinical significance

HMOX1 deficiency in humans is rare and characterised by haemolysis, inflammation, iron deposition, and endothelial damage. A (GT)n repeat polymorphism in the gene's promoter affects inducibility and has been associated with susceptibility to cardiovascular disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and transplant outcomes. Because of its cytoprotective, anti-inflammatory, and anti-apoptotic effects, induction of HO-1 is studied as a therapeutic strategy in vascular injury, organ transplantation, and inflammatory disorders.
