Women's Health Initiative

Duration1991–2005 (clinical trials); follow-up ongoing
SponsorNational Institutes of Health (NHLBI)
ParticipantsMore than 161,000 postmenopausal women

The Women's Health Initiative (WHI) was a series of clinical studies initiated by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH) in 1991, to address major health issues causing death and disability in postmenopausal women. The studies involved more than 161,000 women aged 50–79.

Components

The WHI included a randomised clinical trial testing hormone therapy, dietary modification, and calcium/vitamin D supplementation, plus a large observational study. The hormone therapy trial of combined estrogen plus progestin was stopped early in 2002 because of increased risks of breast cancer, coronary heart disease, stroke, and pulmonary embolism.

Impact

The 2002 results dramatically changed prescribing practice: use of menopausal hormone therapy fell sharply worldwide, and subsequent analyses explored how risks vary by age and time since menopause. The estrogen-alone trial in women with prior hysterectomy was stopped in 2004.
