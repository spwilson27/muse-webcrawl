Roger Trinquier

Born20 March 1908, La Beaume, France
Died11 January 1986 (aged 77), Vence, France
AllegianceFrance
ServiceFrench Army; colonial infantry, paratroops
WarsSecond World War; First Indochina War; Algerian War

Roger Trinquier (20 March 1908 – 11 January 1986) was a French Army officer and theorist of counter-insurgency warfare. A veteran of French Indochina and Algeria, he is best known for his 1961 book Modern Warfare: A French View of Counterinsurgency (La Guerre moderne).

Career

Trinquier served in China and Indochina before the Second World War, fought with airborne and commando units in Indochina, and commanded parachute forces during the Algerian War, including in the Battle of Algiers. His writings argued that revolutionary warfare required political organisation, population control and intelligence, and controversially defended harsh interrogation methods, making his work both influential and contentious among later counter-insurgency theorists.
