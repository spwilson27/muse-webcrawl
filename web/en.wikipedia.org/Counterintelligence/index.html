Counterintelligence
PurposeProtect against espionage and foreign intelligence operations
MethodsSecurity vetting, surveillance, double agents, deception
Example agenciesFBI (US), MI5 (UK)

Counterintelligence (also spelled counter-intelligence) is the activity of protecting an agency's or state's intelligence programme against an adversary's intelligence service. It includes gathering information and conducting activities to prevent espionage, sabotage, assassinations, and other intelligence activities conducted on behalf of foreign powers.

Activities

Counterintelligence work ranges from defensive measures — security clearances, compartmentation, and protection of classified information — to offensive operations such as penetrating hostile services, running double agents, and feeding deception material. During the Second World War, Britain's Double-Cross System turned every German agent in the country into a controlled channel for deception, including the cover plan for the Normandy landings.

Organisation

States organise counterintelligence differently: in the United States it is shared among the FBI and agency security offices under a national strategy; in the United Kingdom, MI5 handles domestic security intelligence. Notable historical counterintelligence figures include James Jesus Angleton of the CIA. Famous failures include the penetration of Western services by moles such as Kim Philby and Aldrich Ames.
