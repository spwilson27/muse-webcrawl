Wedderburn–Artin theorem

The Wedderburn–Artin theorem is a fundamental result in abstract algebra classifying semisimple rings. In its common modern form, it states that a ring is semisimple (as a module over itself, equivalently right Artinian with zero Jacobson radical) if and only if it is isomorphic to a finite direct product of matrix rings over division rings.

Statement

Precisely: a ring R is semisimple if and only if there exist a positive integer n, division rings D1, …, Dn, and positive integers n1, …, nn such that

R ≅ Mn1(D1) × ⋯ × Mnn(Dn),

where Mm(D) denotes the ring of m × m matrices over D. The factors are unique up to permutation. For a semisimple algebra over a field, the division rings are finite-dimensional division algebras over that field.

History

Joseph Wedderburn proved the theorem for finite-dimensional algebras in 1907, building on his 1905 result that every finite division ring is a field (Wedderburn's little theorem). Emil Artin later generalized the classification to rings satisfying the descending chain condition, giving the form used today. The theorem reduces the study of semisimple rings to that of division rings and underlies the representation theory of finite groups: by Maschke's theorem, a group algebra over a field of characteristic not dividing the group order is semisimple, hence a product of matrix rings, whose sizes correspond to the group's irreducible representations.
