Rani Durgavati (1524–1564) was the ruling queen of Gondwana in central India, celebrated for her administration of the Gond kingdom and her resistance to Mughal expansion. Born into the Chandel dynasty of Mahoba, she married Dalpat Shah, the adopted son of the Gond king Sangram Shah, uniting the two houses.

Regency

After Dalpat Shah's death around 1550, Durgavati ruled as regent for her young son Vir Narayan. Contemporary accounts describe a prosperous and well-administered realm of some 70,000 villages, with its capital at Chauragarh. She is credited with promoting trade, building reservoirs and temples, patronising scholars, and shifting the capital, and she defeated an invasion by Baz Bahadur of Malwa.

War with the Mughals

In 1564 Asaf Khan, the Mughal governor of Kara, invaded Gondwana with Akbar's sanction, attracted by its wealth. Durgavati chose to resist despite being advised to negotiate. After an initial success, her forces were overwhelmed in a narrow valley near Narrai between Jabalpur's hills; wounded and facing capture, she took her own life on 24 June 1564.

Legacy

Durgavati is honoured in Madhya Pradesh with statues, a university in Jabalpur named after her, memorials at her death site, and an express train bearing her name. She is remembered as one of India's notable warrior queens.
