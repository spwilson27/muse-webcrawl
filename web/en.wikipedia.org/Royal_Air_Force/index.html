The Royal Air Force (RAF) is the air and space force of the United Kingdom. Formed towards the end of the First World War on 1 April 1918 by merging the Royal Flying Corps and the Royal Naval Air Service, it was the first independent air force in the world — an air arm not subordinate to either the army or the navy.

History

The RAF played a decisive role in the Second World War, most famously during the Battle of Britain (1940), when Fighter Command under Hugh Dowding repelled the Luftwaffe's attempt to gain air superiority ahead of a planned German invasion. Bomber Command conducted the strategic bombing campaign against Germany, while Coastal Command fought the Battle of the Atlantic. During the Cold War the RAF operated the V bomber force carrying Britain's nuclear deterrent until that role passed to the Royal Navy's submarines in 1969.

Structure and role

The RAF's mission is to provide an agile, adaptable and capable air force. Its principal front-line aircraft have included the Eurofighter Typhoon multi-role fighter, the F-35B Lightning II, the A400M Atlas transport, and the Voyager tanker. Air Command, headquartered at RAF High Wycombe, controls the service's operations. The professional head of the RAF is the Chief of the Air Staff.
