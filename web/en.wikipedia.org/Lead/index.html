Lead

Symbol: Pb
Atomic number: 82
Atomic mass: 207.2
Melting point: 327.5 °C

Lead is a chemical element with the symbol Pb (from Latin plumbum) and atomic number 82. It is a heavy metal that is denser than most common materials, soft and malleable, with a low melting point. Freshly cut lead has a bright silvery appearance that quickly tarnishes to a dull grey.

Lead has been used since antiquity — in plumbing (hence the word), pewter, paint pigments, and later in batteries, ammunition, cable sheathing and radiation shielding. Its principal ore is galena (lead sulfide). Because lead is highly toxic, accumulating in the body and damaging the nervous system, many uses have been phased out, notably tetraethyllead in petrol and lead-based paints, though lead-acid batteries remain its largest application and are widely recycled.

Lead has the highest atomic number of any stable element; its isotopes are the end products of several radioactive decay chains.
