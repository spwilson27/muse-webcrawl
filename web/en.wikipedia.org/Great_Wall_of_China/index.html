Great Wall of China

Great Wall of China
Chinese: 萬里長城 / 万里长城
Location: Northern China
Total length: 21,196 km (all branches, 2012 survey)
Built: 7th century BC – 1644 AD
UNESCO: World Heritage Site (1987)

The Great Wall of China is a series of fortifications made of stone, brick, tamped earth and other materials, generally built along an east-to-west line across the historical northern borders of China to protect Chinese states and empires against raids and invasions from the Eurasian Steppe.

History

Warring States built early walls; Qin Shi Huang connected many after unifying China in 220 BC. The best-known sections were built by the Ming dynasty (1368–1644) against Mongol and Manchu incursions — though the Manchu Qing ultimately crossed it in 1644.

Construction

Ming walls are brick-faced with watchtowers, passes and beacon towers for signalling. Earlier walls were often rammed earth and survive in eroded form in deserts.

Visibility myth

The claim that the Wall is visible from the Moon with the naked eye is a myth; it is difficult even from low Earth orbit without aid.

Tourism

Popular sections near Beijing include Badaling, Mutianyu and Jinshanling. See Hotan for Silk Road context.
