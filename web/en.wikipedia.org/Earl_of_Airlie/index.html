Creation date: 1639
Created by: Charles I

Earl of Airlie is a title in the Peerage of Scotland, created in 1639 for James Ogilvy, 2nd Lord Ogilvy of Airlie. The Ogilvys of Airlie, in Angus, had been created Lords Ogilvy of Airlie in 1491, and the earldom has remained in the Ogilvy family ever since.

Cortachy Castle, Angus, a seat of the Ogilvy Earls of Airlie.

History

The Ogilvys were prominent Angus landowners and hereditary sheriffs. The family suffered forfeiture after the Jacobite risings — the 5th Earl's line was attainted for supporting the 1715 rising and David Ogilvy, styled Lord Ogilvy, commanded a regiment in the 1745 rising before escaping to France; the earldom was restored in 1826. Later earls included David Ogilvy, 11th Earl of Airlie, who was killed at the Battle of Diamond Hill in the Boer War in 1900, and David Ogilvy, 12th Earl, a long-serving Lord Chamberlain.

Recent holders

David Ogilvy, 13th Earl of Airlie (1926–2023), served as Lord Chamberlain from 1985 to 1997, was Chancellor of the Order of the Thistle, and was the first — and as of his time the only — person to hold the post of Lord Chamberlain while being a hereditary peer after reforms of the royal household. He was succeeded by his son David Ogilvy, 14th Earl of Airlie.
