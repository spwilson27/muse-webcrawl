Juan Pujol García
Born: 14 February 1912, Barcelona
Died: 10 October 1988, Caracas
Codename: Garbo (British), Arabel (German)

Juan Pujol García (14 February 1912 – 10 October 1988), also known by his codename Garbo, was a Spanish double agent during World War II who fed false information to Nazi Germany on behalf of British intelligence.

Double agent

Motivated by opposition to fascism after the Spanish Civil War, Pujol offered his services to Britain, was initially rejected, and instead became a German agent — while inventing an entirely fictitious spy network in Britain from Lisbon using guidebooks and reference books. The British then recruited him, moved him to London, and with his case officers built his imaginary network into a key channel of the Double-Cross System.

D-Day deception

Garbo's reporting was central to Operation Fortitude, convincing German commanders that the 1944 Normandy landings were a diversion from a main attack at the Pas-de-Calais, helping hold German forces away from Normandy. He is believed to be the only person decorated by both sides in the war, receiving an MBE and the Iron Cross.

Later life

After the war Pujol faked his death and lived quietly in Venezuela, where he was rediscovered in the 1980s and told his story publicly.
