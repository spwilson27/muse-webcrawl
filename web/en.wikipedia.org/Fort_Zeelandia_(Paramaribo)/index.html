Fort Zeelandia (Paramaribo)

Fort Zeelandia is a fortress in Paramaribo, Suriname, on the left bank of the Suriname River. Built by the English in 1651 as Fort Willoughby, it was captured by the Dutch in 1667 and renamed for the Dutch province of Zeeland. The five-sided brick fort is the centrepiece of the historic inner city of Paramaribo, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and houses the Surinaams Museum.

History

English settlers from Barbados under Francis Willoughby established a trading post and wooden fort at the Suriname River in 1651. In 1667, during the Second Anglo-Dutch War, a Dutch squadron from Zeeland under Abraham Crijnssen captured the colony and the fort; under the Treaty of Breda later that year, Suriname remained Dutch while England kept New Netherland (New York). The Dutch rebuilt the fort in brick as a pentagonal fortress with five bastions, and it served as the colony's government seat and principal defence for two centuries. The square beside it, later called Independence Square, hosts the Presidential Palace.

Military regime and December murders

After Suriname's independence from the Netherlands in 1975, the fort became a military barracks. Following the 1980 coup by Dési Bouterse, it served as the headquarters of the military regime. On the night of 7–8 December 1982, fifteen prominent critics of the regime — journalists, lawyers, union leaders, academics, and businessmen — were taken to the fort and murdered, an event known in Suriname as the Decembermoorden (December murders). The fort remained closed for many years as a result; the trial of those accused, including Bouterse, began only in 2007, and Bouterse was convicted in 2019.

Museum

Restored, the fort now houses the Surinaams Museum, founded in 1947, whose collections document Suriname's colonial history, its Indigenous, African, Javanese, Hindustani, Chinese, and European communities, and the natural history of the country. The fort and the surrounding historic wooden city centre form the core of Paramaribo's UNESCO World Heritage listing, granted in 2002.
