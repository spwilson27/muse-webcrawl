Drahomíra

Drahomíra (died after 935) was a Bohemian duchess, wife of Vratislaus I, Duke of Bohemia, and mother of Saint Wenceslaus and Boleslaus I. After her husband's death in 921, she served as regent of Bohemia during the minority of her son Wenceslaus, though real power was contested with her mother-in-law, Ludmila of Bohemia.

Life

Drahomíra came from the Slavic Havolane tribe of the region around present-day Brandenburg. She married Vratislaus I around 906 and bore at least two sons, Wenceslaus and Boleslaus, and possibly daughters.

After Vratislaus died in 921, Wenceslaus, still a minor, succeeded him. Drahomíra assumed the regency, but Ludmila, Wenceslaus's grandmother and a devout Christian, took charge of the boy's education. The two women fell into conflict, reportedly over Ludmila's influence and the direction of Bohemian policy. According to tradition, Drahomíra had Ludmila murdered at Tetín on 15 September 921. Ludmila was later canonised, and the murder became a defining episode in Bohemian hagiography.

Wenceslaus took power around 925 and sent his mother into exile. She returned after his murder in 935, when Boleslaus I seized the throne. Her subsequent fate is unknown; she is last recorded alive after 935.

Legacy

Drahomíra is remembered chiefly in the legends of Saint Wenceslaus and Saint Ludmila, where she is cast as the pagan-leaning antagonist. Modern historians note that she was likely a competent ruler whose reputation suffered from the hagiographic tradition written by her opponents' partisans.
