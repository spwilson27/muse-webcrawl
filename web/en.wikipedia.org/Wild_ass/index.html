Wild ass

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
ClassMammalia
OrderPerissodactyla
FamilyEquidae
GenusEquus

The term wild ass refers to several species of the genus Equus that live wild in arid regions of Africa and Asia. Unlike the domestic donkey, which descends from the African wild ass, wild asses have never been successfully domesticated, and many of their populations are threatened or critically endangered.

Species

The best-known wild asses include:

  • African wild ass (Equus africanus) — native to the Horn of Africa; the ancestor of the domestic donkey. Critically endangered.
  • Asiatic wild ass or onager (Equus hemionus) — ranging across Central Asia, with subspecies including the Mongolian wild ass (khulan), the Turkmenian kulan, the Persian onager, and the Indian wild ass (khur).
  • Kiang (Equus kiang) — the largest wild ass, native to the Tibetan Plateau.

Characteristics

Wild asses are adapted to desert and steppe environments. They can survive on coarse, sparse vegetation and very little water, and can run at speeds of up to 70 km/h (43 mph) over short distances. They typically live in small herds or solitarily, and males may defend territories around water sources during the breeding season.

Conservation

Habitat loss, competition with livestock, and hunting have reduced wild ass populations across much of their range. Conservation programs, including reintroduction efforts in Israel's Negev desert and in Central Asian reserves, aim to protect remaining populations.
