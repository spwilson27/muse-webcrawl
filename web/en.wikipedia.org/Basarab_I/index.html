Basarab I (died 1352), known as Basarab the Founder, was the voivode who united and established the Principality of Wallachia, laying the foundations of the medieval Romanian state south of the Carpathians. He is the eponymous founder of the House of Basarab, which ruled Wallachia for centuries.

Rise to power

Basarab emerged in the early 14th century as ruler of a territory between the Carpathians and the Danube, initially as a vassal of the Kingdom of Hungary. Around 1324 he is recorded as the Hungarian king's voivode of Transylvania's neighboring lands; in the following years he consolidated control over the region stretching from Severin to the Black Sea approaches, defied Hungarian authority, and ceased paying tribute.

Battle of Posada

In 1330 King Charles I of Hungary invaded Wallachia to restore his suzerainty. After occupying Severin, the Hungarian army was ambushed and decisively defeated by Basarab's forces in a mountain pass at the Battle of Posada (9–12 November 1330), one of the most celebrated victories in Romanian history. The victory secured Wallachia's de facto independence.

Legacy

Basarab founded the princely court at Câmpulung and later Curtea de Argeș, endowed churches, and organized the administration of the new state. His name gave the region of Bessarabia — at times ruled by his successors — its name. He was succeeded by his son Nicholas Alexander.
