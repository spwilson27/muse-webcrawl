FIRE movement

The FIRE movement (Financial Independence, Retire Early) is a lifestyle and personal-finance movement whose goal is gaining financial independence and retiring much earlier than traditional retirement ages. Adherents save and invest a large share of their income — often 50–70 per cent — so that investment returns can eventually cover their living expenses, freeing them from the need to work for money.

Principles

The movement draws on ideas popularised by the 1992 book Your Money or Your Life by Vicki Robin and Joe Dominguez, and by the blog of Mr. Money Mustache (Peter Adeney) from 2011. A common rule of thumb holds that financial independence is reached when savings equal about 25 times annual expenses, allowing withdrawals of roughly 4 per cent per year, following the "safe withdrawal rate" research of the Trinity study. Strategies emphasise low-cost index fund investing, frugality, and tax-advantaged accounts.

Variants

Variants include Lean FIRE (independence on a minimalist budget), Fat FIRE (a more affluent retirement lifestyle), Barista FIRE (part-time work covering some expenses while investments grow) and Coast FIRE (saving enough early that compound growth alone reaches the target by conventional retirement age).

Criticism

Critics note that FIRE is most accessible to high earners, that the 4 per cent rule may not hold over very long retirements or poor market sequences, and that extreme frugality can carry psychological and social costs. Others argue its core message — spending deliberately and valuing time over consumption — is valuable regardless of retirement date.
