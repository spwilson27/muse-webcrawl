Bryggen

Bryggen, also known as Tyskebryggen, is a series of Hanseatic heritage commercial buildings lining the eastern side of the Vågen harbour in Bergen, Norway. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979.

After a great fire in 1702, the wooden gabled warehouses were rebuilt in their traditional form. For centuries Bryggen was the centre of the Hanseatic League's trading empire in Norway, dominated by German merchants exporting stockfish and importing grain.

Today the quarter houses shops, restaurants, artists' studios, and the Bryggens Museum. Fires remain a constant threat, and ongoing restoration works to preserve the leaning timber structures and the archaeological deposits beneath them.
