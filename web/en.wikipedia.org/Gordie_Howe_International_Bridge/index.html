Gordie Howe International Bridge

Crosses: Detroit River

Locale: Detroit, Michigan, United States – Windsor, Ontario, Canada

Design: Cable-stayed bridge

Total length: 2.5 km (1.6 mi)

Named after: Gordie Howe

The Gordie Howe International Bridge, known during development as the Detroit River International Crossing and the New International Trade Crossing, is a cable-stayed international bridge across the Detroit River, connecting Detroit, Michigan, United States, with Windsor, Ontario, Canada. The bridge will provide a new crossing for the busiest trade corridor between the two countries, supplementing the privately owned Ambassador Bridge.

Construction

The project is overseen by the Windsor–Detroit Bridge Authority, a Canadian Crown corporation, and the Government of Canada agreed to fund the construction, including the Michigan interchange and customs plazas. Construction officially began in 2018. The bridge has a main span of 853 metres (2,799 ft), making it one of the longest cable-stayed bridges in North America, supported by two A-frame towers. It carries six lanes of traffic plus a multi-use path for pedestrians and cyclists.

Name

The bridge is named after Canadian ice hockey player Gordie Howe, whose career included 25 seasons with the Detroit Red Wings. The name was announced jointly by the Canadian and Michigan governments in May 2015.
