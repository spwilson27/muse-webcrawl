The Boxing Day Test is a cricket Test match held in Melbourne, Victoria, Australia, involving the Australian national cricket team and an opposing national team that is touring Australia during the southern summer. It begins annually on Boxing Day (26 December) and is played at the Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG).


The MCG on Boxing Day, one of the best-attended days in world cricket.

By long tradition, it is one of the most popular sporting events on the Australian calendar. Day one crowds regularly exceed 60,000, and the record attendance of 91,112 was set on the opening day of the 2013–14 Ashes Test against England.

History

A Sheffield Shield match between Victoria and New South Wales had traditionally been held at the MCG over the Christmas period, and touring teams often played a match against Victoria around the same time. In 1968–69 the West Indies played a Test in Melbourne starting on 26 December, but the Boxing Day Test as an annual fixture of the touring schedule dates from 1980–81, when Australia played New Zealand. From 1990–91 it became an established, near-annual tradition, with Melbourne holding long-term rights to a Test beginning on Boxing Day.

Notable matches

  • 1982–83 — England beat Australia by three runs in one of the closest Ashes finishes, after Allan Border and Jeff Thomson added 70 for the last wicket.
  • 1995–96 — Sri Lankan spinner Muttiah Muralitharan was controversially no-balled for throwing by umpire Darrell Hair.
  • 1998–99 — Dean Headley's bowling inspired an England win by 12 runs, their first victory in the fixture in 12 years.
  • 2010–11 — Australia were bowled out for 98 on day one in front of 84,345 spectators as England retained the Ashes.
  • 2020–21 — India won by eight wickets days after being dismissed for 36 in Adelaide, a celebrated comeback in a series they went on to win.
  • 2021–22 — Scott Boland took 6 for 7 on debut as Australia dismissed England for 68 to retain the Ashes.

Cultural significance

The match is a fixture of the Australian summer, broadcast nationally and watched by large television audiences. The opening day is known for its festive atmosphere, with many spectators attending in fancy dress. The opposing team rotates with the international touring schedule; the fixture forms part of long-running series such as the Ashes (against England) and the Border–Gavaskar Trophy (against India).
