Mare milk

Mare milk is milk secreted by female horses (mares) to feed their foals. It has been consumed by humans for thousands of years, particularly by the nomadic peoples of the Central Asian steppe, and remains the basis of the fermented drink kumis.

Composition

Compared with cow's milk, mare milk is lower in fat and casein and higher in lactose and whey proteins, making its composition closer to human milk. It is rich in vitamin C and in lysozyme and lactoferrin, proteins with antimicrobial properties. Because of its low casein content it does not form a firm curd and is not used for ordinary cheese-making.

Uses

In Mongolia, Kazakhstan, and Kyrgyzstan, mare milk is fermented into kumis (airag in Mongolian), a mildly alcoholic drink central to steppe hospitality and summer festivals. In Europe, powdered mare milk is marketed in health foods and cosmetics, and horse dairies operate in countries such as Germany, Belgium, and France, where the milk is promoted for people with cow's-milk protein allergy.
