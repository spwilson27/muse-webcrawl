Constitution of 1824

The Constitution of 1824 may refer to several constitutions promulgated in that year, most notably the first constitution of the Empire of Brazil and the first federal constitution of Mexico.

Brazil

The Brazilian Constitution of 1824, granted by Emperor Pedro I on 25 March 1824 after he dissolved the constituent assembly, was a liberal document for its time that nevertheless created a distinctive fourth branch of government, the Moderating Power, vested in the emperor. It established Catholicism as the state religion, a bicameral legislature, and a unitarian state, and it remained in force — with amendments such as the Additional Act of 1834 — until the fall of the monarchy in 1889, making it one of the longest-lived constitutions of the era.

Mexico

The Mexican Constitution of 1824, adopted on 4 October 1824, established the United Mexican States as a federal republic after the fall of Agustín de Iturbide's empire. It divided sovereignty among the federal government and 19 states, created a bicameral Congress and a presidency, and made Roman Catholicism the sole permitted religion. It was later displaced by the centralist Siete Leyes of 1836, briefly restored in 1847, and finally superseded by the liberal Constitution of 1857.
