The Curragh

The Curragh (Irish: An Currach) is a flat open plain of almost 5,000 acres (2,000 hectares) of common land in County Kildare, Ireland, between the towns of Kildare, Newbridge and Kilcullen. Formed from glacial deposits of sand and gravel, it is Ireland's most extensive semi-natural grassland and a landscape of great archaeological, military and sporting significance.

History

The plain is rich in prehistoric monuments — ringforts, barrows and standing stones — and in Irish mythology was associated with the goddess Brigid and the assembly of the kings of Leinster. Its turf traditionally supported vast flocks of sheep, and its sheep-grazing rights are still governed by the Curragh of Kildare Acts.

Military camp

A permanent British military camp was established in 1855 during the Crimean War, and the Curragh Camp later became a major centre of the Irish Defence Forces. During the Second World War ("the Emergency") the Curragh interned both Allied and Axis airmen and IRA internees. The "Curragh incident" of March 1914, when British officers indicated they would resign rather than enforce Irish Home Rule against Ulster unionists, was a landmark in the road to Irish independence.

Racing

The Curragh Racecourse, on the plain's edge, is the home of Irish flat racing and hosts all five Irish Classics, including the Irish Derby. The area around it is a centre of thoroughbred training and breeding.
