Diltiazem

ClassCalcium channel blocker (benzothiazepine)
UsesHypertension, angina, arrhythmias
RoutesOral, intravenous, topical

Diltiazem, sold under the brand name Cardizem among others, is a nondihydropyridine calcium channel blocker medication used in the treatment of high blood pressure, angina pectoris, and certain heart arrhythmias including atrial fibrillation.

Medical uses

Diltiazem relaxes vascular smooth muscle, reducing blood pressure, and slows conduction through the atrioventricular node, controlling heart rate. Topical preparations are used for anal fissures. Off-label uses include migraine prophylaxis and Raynaud's phenomenon.

Side effects

Common side effects include swelling of the legs, headache, dizziness, constipation and slow heart rate. It should generally be avoided in people with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and in combination with beta blockers in some settings.

History

Diltiazem was developed in Japan by Tanabe Seiyaku and approved for medical use in the United States in 1982. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
