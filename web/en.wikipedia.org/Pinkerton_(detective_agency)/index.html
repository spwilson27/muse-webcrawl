Pinkerton is an American private investigation and security company founded in 1850 by Allan Pinkerton, a Scottish-American detective and former Chicago police officer. Originally the Pinkerton National Detective Agency, it became famous in the nineteenth century for its work pursuing outlaws, guarding railroads and mail, and providing intelligence services. Its logo — an open eye above the motto "We Never Sleep" — gave rise to the popular term "private eye" for a private detective.

History

Allan Pinkerton, a barrel maker by trade, founded the agency in Chicago after helping to solve a series of counterfeiting and train robbery cases in the 1840s. The agency rose to national prominence when it uncovered a plot to assassinate President-elect Abraham Lincoln in Baltimore in 1861, after which Pinkerton organized a secret service for the Union Army during the American Civil War.

After the war the agency expanded rapidly, opening offices across the United States. Pinkerton operatives pursued notorious outlaws including Jesse James and the Wild Bunch, and the firm became the largest private law-enforcement organization in the country. It also became controversial for its role as strikebreakers and industrial guards, most infamously in the 1892 Homestead Strike, where a gun battle between Pinkerton agents and steelworkers left several dead and badly damaged the agency's reputation.

Twentieth century and beyond

Through the twentieth century the firm shifted toward corporate investigations, security services and risk management. In 1999 Pinkerton was acquired by the Swedish security group Securitas AB. Today Pinkerton operates as a global risk management and corporate investigations firm headquartered in Ann Arbor, Michigan.

Legacy

The agency left a lasting mark on American popular culture. Its operatives featured in dime novels and later in films and television, and the Pinkerton name remains synonymous with private detective work. The company's historical role in labor disputes also made it a symbol, in labor history, of corporate power deployed against unions.
