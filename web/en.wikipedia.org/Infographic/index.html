Infographic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Infographics (a portmanteau of information and graphics) are graphic visual representations of information, data, or knowledge intended to present information quickly and clearly. They combine charts, diagrams, icons, maps, and minimal text to improve cognition by exploiting the human visual system's ability to detect patterns and trends at a glance.

History

Visual explanation is ancient — from cave paintings and Egyptian hieroglyphs to Leonardo da Vinci's annotated drawings. William Playfair invented the bar and pie charts in the 1780s; Florence Nightingale's polar-area diagrams of Crimean War mortality (1858) demonstrated the persuasive power of statistics in pictures; Charles Joseph Minard's 1869 flow map of Napoleon's Russian campaign is often called the greatest statistical graphic ever made. In the 20th century, Otto Neurath's Isotype pictograms and newspaper graphics departments professionalised the form, and USA Today's colourful "Snapshots" popularised infographics for mass audiences in the 1980s.

Modern practice

Digital publishing and social media made infographics a staple of journalism, marketing, education, and government communication in the 2000s and 2010s; long-form vertical infographics designed for scrolling became a recognisable web genre. Common types include statistical infographics, timelines, process diagrams, comparisons, anatomical cutaways, and annotated maps.

Design and criticism

Effective infographics prioritise accuracy, clear hierarchy, and honest scales; misleading axes, distorted pictograms, and decoration that obscures data ("chartjunk", in Edward Tufte's term) draw criticism. Accessibility — colour-blind-safe palettes and text alternatives — is an increasing concern.
