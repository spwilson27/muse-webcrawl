Sushi

Sushi (Japanese: すし, 寿司, 鮨) is a traditional Japanese dish made with vinegared rice (sushi-meshi), typically seasoned with sugar and salt, combined with ingredients such as seafood, vegetables and occasionally tropical fruits. Although strongly associated with raw fish, the defining element of sushi is the seasoned rice; toppings and fillings may be raw, cooked, blanched or marinated.

History

Sushi originated in the paddy fields of Southeast Asia as narezushi — fish preserved by fermentation with rice, a method that reached Japan by the Yayoi period. In the Edo period, vinegar was added to rice to shorten preparation, and in the 1820s Hanaya Yohei of Edo (Tokyo) popularised nigirizushi, hand-pressed rice topped with a slice of fish, sold from street stalls as fast food. After the Second World War, sushi restaurants formalised around counters, and from the 1970s–80s sushi spread worldwide, generating new forms such as the California roll.

Types

Principal types include nigirizushi (hand-pressed), makizushi (rolled in nori seaweed), uramaki (inside-out rolls), temaki (hand rolls), chirashizushi (scattered over a bowl of rice), inarizushi (in seasoned tofu pouches), and oshizushi (pressed sushi of Osaka). Sushi is typically served with soy sauce, wasabi and pickled ginger (gari), and accompanied by green tea.

Nutrition and safety

Sushi is generally low in fat and a source of protein and omega-3 fatty acids, though nutritional profiles vary with ingredients. Raw fish carries some risk of parasites such as Anisakis and of bacterial contamination, mitigated by freezing requirements in many jurisdictions and by using sushi-grade fish. Mercury content in large predatory fish such as some tunas is a consideration for frequent consumers.
