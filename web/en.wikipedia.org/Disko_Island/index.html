Disko Island

Disko Island (Greenlandic: Qeqertarsuaq) is a large island in Disko Bay, off the west coast of Greenland. Covering about 8,578 square kilometres (3,312 sq mi), it is the second-largest island of Greenland proper, after the mainland itself. The island's name in Greenlandic means "the large island".

Geography

Disko Island lies at the mouth of Disko Bay, separated from the Nuussuaq Peninsula to the north by the Sullorsuaq Strait (Vaigat). The island is largely mountainous, formed of basaltic lava flows from Tertiary volcanism connected with the opening of the North Atlantic. Flat-topped mountains, scree slopes and hanging valleys characterise much of the landscape. To the south lies the smaller island of Hareø.

Settlements

The town of Qeqertarsuaq (formerly Godhavn) on the south coast is the island's main settlement, with roughly 850 inhabitants. The small settlement of Kangerluk lies in the Diskofjord on the west coast. Qeqertarsuaq was founded as a whaling station in 1773 and later became an administrative centre for northern Greenland.

Geology and fossils

The island's basalt plateau contains rich fossil deposits from the Paleocene and Eocene epochs, including the remains of tropical plants that grew in Greenland about 55 million years ago. The area also contains deposits of native iron found in basalt, notably at Ovifak (Uivfaq), where large masses of telluric iron were described in the 19th century. Coal seams in the island's sediments were mined at Qullissat, a mining town founded in 1924 and abandoned in 1972.

Flora and fauna

Disko Island is one of the most botanically rich areas of Greenland, with hot springs that keep parts of the coast ice-free and support species found nowhere else in the country. The surrounding waters are important for narwhals, beluga whales and several species of seal, and the island hosts breeding colonies of fulmars, kittiwakes and other seabirds.
