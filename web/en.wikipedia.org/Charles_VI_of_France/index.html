Charles VI of France

Charles VI (3 December 1368 – 21 October 1422), nicknamed the Beloved and later the Mad, was King of France from 1380 until his death in 1422. He is known for his mental illness and the resulting political chaos during the later phase of the Hundred Years' War.

Early reign

Charles succeeded his father Charles V at age eleven, with his uncles acting as regents. He assumed personal rule in 1388 and governed with the Marmousets, a group of reforming counsellors.

Mental illness

In 1392, while leading an expedition, Charles suffered a sudden psychotic episode and attacked his own men. Recurrent bouts of illness, during which at times he believed he was made of glass, left him unable to govern and triggered a struggle between the Armagnac and Burgundian factions.

Later reign

After the English victory at Agincourt in 1415, Charles signed the Treaty of Troyes (1420), disinheriting his son the Dauphin in favour of Henry V of England. He died in 1422 and was succeeded in the south by Charles VII.
