Minimalism (music)

Minimal music (also called minimalism) is a form of art music or other compositional practice that employs limited or minimal musical materials. Prominent features of minimalist music include repetitive patterns or pulses, steady drones, consonant harmony, and reiteration of musical phrases or smaller units such as figures, motifs, and cells. It may include features such as additive process and phase shifting which lead to what has been termed phase music. Minimal compositions that rely heavily on process techniques that follow strict rules are usually described using the term process music.

The approach is marked by a non-narrative, non-teleological, and non-representational approach, often foregrounding the audible processes themselves. The movement originally involved dozens of composers, although only four — La Monte Young, Terry Riley, Steve Reich, and Philip Glass — achieved lasting renown.

History

The word "minimalism" in music was largely popularised by the critic Michael Nyman, who applied it to the work of composers emerging in the 1960s. La Monte Young's static drone pieces of the late 1950s, such as the Trio for Strings (1958), are often cited as precursors. Terry Riley's In C (1964), built from 53 short melodic patterns repeated at the performers' discretion over a steady pulse, became a landmark of the style.

Steve Reich developed techniques of phasing in works such as Piano Phase (1967) and Drumming (1971), while Philip Glass developed additive processes in pieces such as Music in Fifths (1969) and the opera Einstein on the Beach (1976), composed with Robert Wilson. By the late 1970s and 1980s, composers such as John Adams brought minimalist techniques into large-scale orchestral and operatic works, a development sometimes labelled postminimalism.

Characteristics

Minimalist music typically features:

  • Repetition of short melodic or rhythmic cells
  • Slow rates of harmonic change and frequent use of drones or pedal points
  • A steady, clearly articulated pulse
  • Gradual transformation processes, such as phasing and additive/subtractive cycles
  • Consonant harmony, often diatonic or modal

Influence

Minimalism has influenced genres far beyond classical music, including ambient music, krautrock, electronic dance music, and film scoring. Composers such as Arvo Pärt developed a related style sometimes called holy minimalism. The music of minimalist composers has been widely recorded and performed, entering the standard repertory of ensembles such as the Kronos Quartet and Bang on a Can.
