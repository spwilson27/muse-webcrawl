Shavkat Miromonovich Mirziyoyev (born 24 July 1957) is an Uzbek politician who has served as the second president of Uzbekistan since December 2016. Previously prime minister under President Islam Karimov from 2003, he became acting president on Karimov's death in September 2016 and was elected in his own right in December 2016, then re-elected in 2021 and again in 2023 following a constitutional referendum that reset term limits. His presidency has been marked by economic liberalisation and a thaw in relations with neighbours, alongside continuing criticism of political constraints.

Early life and career

Mirziyoyev was born in the Zaamin district of Jizzakh Region and trained as an irrigation engineer at the Tashkent Institute of Irrigation and Melioration. He worked in academia and Komsomol administration in the late Soviet period, then entered politics after independence, serving as governor (hokim) of Jizzakh Region (1996–2001) and of Samarkand Region (2001–2003) before Karimov appointed him prime minister in December 2003.

Path to the presidency

Following Karimov's death, announced on 2 September 2016, the parliament appointed Mirziyoyev acting president on 8 September, bypassing the constitutional successor, the Senate chairman. He won the 4 December 2016 presidential election with a reported 88.6% of the vote, in an election the OSCE said lacked genuine competition but noted some improvements in administration.

Presidency

Mirziyoyev launched a reform programme often described as an "Uzbek thaw": currency convertibility for the som (2017), easing of exit visas, release of political prisoners, efforts to end systematic forced labour in the cotton harvest (the international cotton boycott was lifted in 2022), improved ties with Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Afghanistan, and courting of foreign investment. Uzbekistan's international standing improved and growth accelerated, though power remains highly centralised in the presidency and independent media and opposition face restrictions. In July 2022, protests over proposed constitutional changes to Karakalpakstan's status were suppressed with loss of life. A 2023 referendum adopted a revised constitution extending the presidential term to seven years; Mirziyoyev won the ensuing snap election in July 2023 with about 87% of the vote.
