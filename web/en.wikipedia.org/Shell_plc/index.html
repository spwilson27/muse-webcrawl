Shell plc

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A Shell service station

Shell plc is a British multinational oil and gas company headquartered in London, England. Shell is a public limited company with a primary listing on the London Stock Exchange and secondary listings on Euronext Amsterdam and the New York Stock Exchange. It is one of the oil and gas "supermajors" and, by revenues, one of the largest companies in the world.

Shell was formed in 1907 through the merger of the Royal Dutch Petroleum Company of the Netherlands and the Shell Transport and Trading Company of the United Kingdom, becoming the Royal Dutch Shell Group. The group unified under a single parent company, Royal Dutch Shell plc, in 2005; in 2022 it dropped "Royal Dutch" from its name, moved its headquarters and tax residence to the United Kingdom, and became Shell plc.

Operations

Shell is vertically integrated and active in every area of the oil and gas industry, including exploration and production, refining, distribution and marketing, petrochemicals, power generation and trading. It has a major liquefied natural gas (LNG) business and operates tens of thousands of service stations worldwide under its distinctive red-and-yellow shell logo, the "pecten". Shell also has growing activities in low-carbon energy, including biofuels, hydrogen, electric vehicle charging and renewables.

History

The Shell Transport and Trading Company grew from Marcus Samuel's London trading business, which in the 1890s pioneered bulk oil tanker shipments through the Suez Canal. Royal Dutch, founded in 1890, developed oil fields in the Dutch East Indies. Their 1907 alliance created a group that rivalled Standard Oil, and Shell expanded globally through the twentieth century, playing a central role in events from the Mexican oil industry to post-war Middle East concessions.

The company has faced significant controversies, including its operations in Nigeria's Niger Delta and the 1995 Brent Spar dispute and the execution of Ken Saro-Wiwa, as well as criticism over climate change and a 2021 Dutch court ruling ordering it to reduce emissions — a ruling partly overturned on appeal in 2024.
