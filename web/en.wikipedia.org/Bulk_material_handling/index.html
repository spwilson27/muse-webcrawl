Bulk material handling

Bulk material handling is an engineering field concerned with the design of equipment used for the transport, storage and control of materials in loose bulk form, such as powders, granules, grain, coal, ore, sand, aggregates and other dry solids. It is a core part of mining, agriculture, ports, power generation, cement and chemical processing, wherever large tonnages must be moved efficiently and safely.

Equipment

The workhorse of the field is the belt conveyor, which moves material continuously over distances from a few metres to many kilometres in a single flight. Other equipment includes bucket elevators for vertical lifting, screw conveyors and pneumatic conveying systems for enclosed or dusty materials, hoppers, silos and stockpiles for storage, and reclaimers, stackers, shiploaders and ship unloaders at terminals. Key design concerns are the flow properties of the material — its angle of repose, bulk density, moisture and abrasiveness — and the avoidance of blockages, dust emissions, spillage and degradation.

Engineering considerations

Because many bulk solids do not flow like liquids, silos and hoppers must be designed for mass flow to prevent rat-holing and bridging, and transfer chutes are engineered to control speed and impact. Dust control and explosion protection are critical for combustible materials such as coal and grain. See also: Bulk carrier, Conveyor belt, Silo.
