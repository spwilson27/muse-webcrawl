Commander-in-Chief's Trophy

SportAmerican college football
TeamsArmy, Navy, Air Force
First awarded1972
Named afterThe President of the United States as commander-in-chief

The Commander-in-Chief's Trophy is awarded annually to the winner of the triangular American college football series among the three United States service academies that field Division I FBS teams: Army (West Point), Navy (Annapolis), and Air Force (Colorado Springs).

Competition

The three academies play one another each season in round-robin fashion, including the famous Army–Navy Game that traditionally closes the regular season. The academy with the best record in these head-to-head games wins the trophy; if the series is tied, the previous year's winner retains it. The winning team is traditionally invited to the White House to be presented with the trophy by the President.

History

The trophy was first awarded in 1972, at the suggestion of Air Force general George B. Simler, in part because the Air Force Academy was too young to have established rivalries of comparable antiquity. The trophy itself stands 2.5 feet tall and weighs about 170 pounds, depicting the mascots of the three academies. Air Force has been the most successful of the three programs in the series, followed by Navy and Army.
