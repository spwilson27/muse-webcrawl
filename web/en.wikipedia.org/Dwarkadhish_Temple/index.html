Dwarkadhish Temple

The Dwarkadhish temple, also known as the Jagat Mandir and occasionally spelled Dwarakadheesh, is a Hindu temple dedicated to Krishna, who is worshiped here by the name Dwarkadhish, or 'King of Dwarka'. The temple is located at Dwarka city of Gujarat, India, which is one of the destinations of Char Dham, a Hindu pilgrimage circuit. The temple is one of the Sapta Puri, the seven holy cities of Hinduism.

Description

The main shrine of the five-storied building, supported by 72 pillars, is known as Jagat Mandir or Nija Mandir. Archaeological findings suggest the original temple was built around 200 BCE by Vajranabh, the great-grandson of Krishna. The temple was enlarged in the 15th–16th century. The temple is made of limestone and has intricate carving. The flag atop the temple's shikhara, depicting the sun and moon, is changed five times a day.

Significance

Dwarka is believed to have been the capital of Krishna's kingdom, as described in the Mahabharata and the Harivamsa. The temple is an important pilgrimage site for Vaishnavites and is associated with the saint-poet Mirabai and the philosopher Adi Shankaracharya, who is said to have established one of his four Shankaracharya Peethams at Dwarka.

The temple is located on the banks of the Gomti River, near where it meets the Arabian Sea. Devotees traditionally take a holy dip in the Gomti before entering the temple.
