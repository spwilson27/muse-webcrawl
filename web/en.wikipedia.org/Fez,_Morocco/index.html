Fez (Arabic: فاس, Fās; French: Fès) is a city in northern inland Morocco and the capital of the Fès-Meknès region. It is the second-largest city in Morocco and is regarded as the country's spiritual and cultural capital, home to the University of al-Qarawiyyin, founded in 859 and considered among the oldest continuously operating universities in the world.

History

Fez was founded in 789 by Idris I, founder of the Idrisid dynasty, and expanded by his son Idris II, who settled thousands of Arab families from Córdoba and Kairouan on opposite banks of the river — origins of the Andalusian and Kairouan quarters. Under the Almoravids the two settlements were unified within one wall. The city flourished under the Marinids in the 13th–15th centuries, when Fes el-Jdid (New Fez) was built and many madrasas, including the Bou Inania Madrasa, were erected. Fez served repeatedly as capital of Morocco until the French protectorate moved the administrative capital to Rabat in 1912.

The medina

Fes el-Bali, the old medina, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the world's largest pedestrian urban areas, with some 9,400 alleys. It contains the al-Qarawiyyin mosque-university, the Chouara Tannery (famous for its stone dyeing vats), and dense souks organised by craft. The city gave its name to the fez hat, once dyed crimson in the city using local berries.

Modern city

Beyond the medina, the Ville Nouvelle built under the French protectorate and modern districts house most of the population, universities, and industry, including leatherwork, textiles, and food processing. Fez is also a pilgrimage destination thanks to the tomb of Moulay Idriss II, the city's patron saint.
