Varangians

Varangians
Period: 9th–13th centuries
Origin: Scandinavia (chiefly Sweden)
Regions: Kievan Rus', Byzantium
Famous for: Varangian Guard, founding of Kievan Rus'

The Varangians (Old Norse: Væringjar; Greek: Varangoi) were Viking-age Scandinavian — mainly Swedish — warriors, traders, and mercenaries who travelled east and south along the rivers of Eastern Europe from the 9th to 13th centuries.

Varangians travelled the river routes from the Baltic to the Black and Caspian Seas.

The Rus'

According to the Primary Chronicle, Slavic and Finnic tribes invited the Varangian Rurik and his brothers to rule them in 862; Rurik's successors founded the state that became Kievan Rus'. The Varangians controlled trade routes "from the Varangians to the Greeks" (Dnieper) and the Volga route to the Caspian.

Varangian Guard

The most famous Varangians served as the Varangian Guard, the elite bodyguard of the Byzantine emperors from the late 10th century. Members included the future Norwegian king Harald Hardrada. Runic inscriptions on the Piraeus Lion and in Hagia Sophia attest to their service.

Legacy

The origin of the name "Rus'" and the role of Scandinavians in early East Slavic statehood remain debated in the long-running "Normanist controversy".
