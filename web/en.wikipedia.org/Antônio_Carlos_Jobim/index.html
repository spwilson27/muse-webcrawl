Antônio Carlos Brasileiro de Almeida Jobim (25 January 1927 – 8 December 1994), also known as Tom Jobim, was a Brazilian composer, pianist, guitarist, songwriter, arranger, and singer. He is widely regarded as the father of bossa nova and one of the most important songwriters of the 20th century.

Career

Jobim emerged in the late 1950s in Rio de Janeiro, working with lyricist Vinícius de Moraes on the play and film Orfeu da Conceição (Black Orpheus) and composing the songs for João Gilberto's landmark album Chega de Saudade (1959). His compositions, often with complex harmonies influenced by classical music and jazz and melodies inspired by Brazilian nature, became standards.

Notable songs

His best-known works include "Garota de Ipanema" ("The Girl from Ipanema"), "Chega de Saudade" ("No More Blues"), "Desafinado", "Corcovado" ("Quiet Nights of Quiet Stars"), "Insensatez" ("How Insensitive"), "Águas de Março" ("Waters of March"), and "Wave". He recorded with Stan Getz, Charlie Byrd, Frank Sinatra, and Elis Regina, among many others.

Legacy

Rio de Janeiro's international airport is named after Jobim, and his songs remain among the most recorded in popular music history.
