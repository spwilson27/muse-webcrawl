Instructional scaffolding

Instructional scaffolding is a learning process designed to promote a deeper level of learning. Scaffolding is the support given to students so they can accomplish tasks they would not otherwise be able to complete on their own; the support is gradually withdrawn as learners gain independence, much as scaffolding is removed from a building once construction is complete.

Theory

The concept is closely associated with Lev Vygotsky's zone of proximal development — the gap between what a learner can do unaided and what they can do with guidance. The term "scaffolding" in this educational sense was introduced by Wood, Bruner, and Ross in 1976. Scaffolding strategies include modeling, worked examples, graphic organizers, questioning, think-alouds, and feedback, with support "faded" over time.

Applications

Scaffolding is used across subjects and media, including in online learning environments where digital tools, prompts, and adaptive software can provide contingent support. Effective scaffolding is temporary, calibrated to the learner's current level, and aimed at eventual self-regulation.
