Paul Sills (November 18, 1927 – June 2, 2008) was an American theatre director and improvisation pioneer. The son of Viola Spolin, creator of the Theatre Games method of actor training, Sills built on his mother's games to found the Compass Players and co-found The Second City in Chicago, the seminal improvisational comedy troupe whose format influenced generations of performers. He later created Story Theatre, in which ensembles improvise stage adaptations of folk tales and literature.

Early life

Sills was born in Chicago, the son of Viola Spolin, who developed theatre games while working at Hull House and with the Works Progress Administration. Sills studied at the University of Chicago, where, with fellow students and faculty including David Shepherd and Mike Nichols, he helped form the Playwrights Theatre Club in 1953 and then the Compass Players in 1955—America's first modern improvisational theatre company, performing improvised scenes and cabaret-style satire based on audience suggestions.

The Second City

In December 1959, Sills, with Howard Alk and Bernie Sahlins, opened The Second City in Chicago's Old Town. Sills directed its early revues, developing the Spolin games into a performance method in which actors created characters and satirical sketches through improvisation. Performers from this period included Alan Arkin, Barbara Harris, Severn Darden, and the team of Mike Nichols and Elaine May, who formed their act in the earlier Compass company. The Second City became the training ground for much of American comedy, from Saturday Night Live to modern sketch and improv troupes.

Story Theatre and later work

Leaving Second City, Sills developed Story Theatre, which opened on Broadway in 1970 adapting tales from the Brothers Grimm and Ovid's Metamorphoses; it was nominated for Tony Awards and toured widely. He later founded the Sills/Spolin Theater Works and taught his mother's theatre games around the world, editing and promoting her book Improvisation for the Theater. Sills spent his later years in Door County, Wisconsin, continuing to teach and direct until his death from pneumonia in 2008.
