Crater Lake National Park

Location: Southern Oregon, United States
Established: May 22, 1902
Area: 183,224 acres
Governing body: National Park Service

Crater Lake National Park is a United States national park in southern Oregon, centred on Crater Lake, a deep blue caldera lake formed by the collapse of Mount Mazama after a cataclysmic eruption about 7,700 years ago. At 1,949 feet (594 m), Crater Lake is the deepest lake in the United States and among the deepest in the world, famed for its remarkable clarity and intense blue colour.

Geology

Mount Mazama, once a stratovolcano roughly 12,000 feet high, erupted violently around 5700 BCE, ejecting some 42 times the volume of material released by Mount St. Helens in 1980. The summit collapsed into the emptied magma chamber, forming a caldera that gradually filled with rain and snowmelt — the lake has no inlets or outlets. Wizard Island, a cinder cone rising from the western shore, is a later volcanic feature.

History

The Klamath people, for whom the lake is a sacred place (giiwas), witnessed the eruption and preserved it in oral tradition. Prospectors "rediscovered" the lake in 1853. William Gladstone Steel campaigned for nearly two decades for its protection, and President Theodore Roosevelt signed the park into law in 1902, making it the fifth-oldest U.S. national park.

Visiting

The 33-mile Rim Drive circles the caldera, and Crater Lake Lodge (1915) overlooks the water from the south rim. Heavy snow — averaging over 40 feet annually — closes most roads for much of the year. Boat tours reach Wizard Island in summer, and the lake's floating log, the "Old Man of the Lake", has drifted upright for more than a century.
