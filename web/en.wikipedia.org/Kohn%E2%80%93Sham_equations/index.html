Kohn–Sham equations

The Kohn–Sham equations are a set of one-electron Schrödinger-like equations introduced by Walter Kohn and Lu Jeu Sham in 1965. They are the practical basis of density functional theory (DFT), replacing the interacting many-electron problem with an auxiliary system of non-interacting electrons that yields the same ground-state density.

Formulation

Each Kohn–Sham orbital satisfies an equation with an effective potential that includes the external potential, the Hartree (Coulomb) potential of the electron density, and the exchange–correlation potential, the functional derivative of the exchange–correlation energy — the one term that must be approximated in practice.

Applications

Solving the equations self-consistently is the standard method for computing electronic structure in condensed matter physics, quantum chemistry and materials science, work for which Kohn received the 1998 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
