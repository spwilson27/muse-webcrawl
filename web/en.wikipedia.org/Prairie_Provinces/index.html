The Prairie Provinces (or simply the Prairies) are the three Canadian provinces of Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba, occupying the southern portion of the Canadian Prairies in Western Canada. Together they form a distinct region defined by grassland plains, a continental climate, and an economy historically based on grain farming and resource extraction.

Geography

The region spans the Interior Plains, rising gradually from Manitoba's lowlands westward to the foothills of the Rocky Mountains in Alberta. Much of the south is flat or gently rolling prairie — the northern extension of the North American Great Plains — while the north transitions to boreal forest. The climate is continental, with cold winters, warm summers, and modest precipitation; chinook winds in Alberta can raise winter temperatures abruptly.

History

Indigenous peoples, including Cree, Assiniboine, Blackfoot, and Métis communities, have inhabited the region for millennia. The Hudson's Bay Company long controlled the territory as Rupert's Land, transferred to Canada in 1870. Manitoba became a province in 1870; Alberta and Saskatchewan followed in 1905, carved from the North-West Territories. Completion of the Canadian Pacific Railway in 1885 opened the plains to mass settlement, and homesteaders from Europe and eastern Canada converted the grasslands into wheat farms. The drought and Dust Bowl of the 1930s hit the region severely, spurring cooperative and social-democratic political movements.

Economy

The Prairie Provinces produce much of Canada's wheat, canola, and barley, and Saskatchewan is a world-leading source of potash and uranium. Alberta's oil sands and conventional fields make it the centre of Canada's petroleum industry. Major cities include Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg, Saskatoon, and Regina.

Demographics

The region's population of roughly seven million includes large communities of Ukrainian, German, Filipino, South Asian, and Indigenous heritage, reflecting successive waves of immigration. Most residents live in the southern corridor along the main rail and highway routes.
