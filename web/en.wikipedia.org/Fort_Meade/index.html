Fort George G. Meade is a United States Army installation located in Anne Arundel County, Maryland, between Baltimore and Washington, D.C. It is named for George Gordon Meade, the Union general who won the Battle of Gettysburg. The post is best known as the headquarters of the National Security Agency (NSA).

History

Fort Meade was established in 1917 as Camp Meade, a training camp for World War I soldiers. It became a permanent post after the war and served as a training and reception centre in World War II. The NSA moved its headquarters to Fort Meade in the 1950s, and the installation grew into a major centre for signals intelligence and cyber operations.

Tenants

In addition to the NSA, Fort Meade hosts the headquarters of U.S. Cyber Command, the Defense Information Systems Agency (DISA), the Defense Media Activity, and numerous other defence organizations. It is one of the largest employers in Maryland, with tens of thousands of military, civilian, and contractor personnel, and it functions as a small city with its own schools, housing, and services.
