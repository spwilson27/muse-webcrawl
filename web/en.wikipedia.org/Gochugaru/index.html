Gochugaru

Gochugaru, Korean red chili flakes, is essential to kimchi and many other dishes.

Gochugaru
Korean고춧가루
Literal meaning"chili powder"
Main ingredientSun-dried red chili peppers, seeds removed
RegionKorea

Gochugaru (Korean: 고춧가루), also known as Korean chili flakes or Korean chili powder, is a staple ingredient in Korean cuisine. It is made from sun-dried red chili peppers (typically the taeyangcho variety) that have been deseeded and ground into flakes or powder.

Characteristics and grades

Gochugaru is coarsely ground compared to many chili powders, with a vibrant red colour, a fruity and slightly smoky flavour, and moderate heat. It is graded by texture — coarse flakes (geun-gochugaru), used in kimchi, and fine powder, used in soups, stews and the fermented chili paste gochujang. Heat levels vary by cultivar and growing region; famous producing areas include Yeongyang County, long renowned for its peppers.

Uses

Beyond kimchi and gochujang, gochugaru seasons dishes such as tteokbokki, kimchi-jjigae, yukgaejang, banchan side dishes and marinades for grilled meats. It provides the characteristic red colour of much modern Korean cooking; chili peppers themselves reached Korea only after the Columbian exchange, in the late sixteenth century.
