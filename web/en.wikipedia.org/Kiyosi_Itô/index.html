Kiyosi Itô

Kiyosi Itô (1915–2008) was a Japanese mathematician who founded the modern theory of stochastic calculus. His Itô calculus, including Itô's lemma and the Itô integral, is fundamental to probability theory and mathematical finance.

Career

Itô studied mathematics at the Imperial University of Tokyo and worked at the Statistics Bureau before joining Kyoto University, where he spent most of his career. His breakthrough papers in the 1940s and 1950s developed stochastic differential equations driven by Brownian motion.

Recognition

He received the Kyoto Prize and the inaugural Gauss Prize (2006) for applications of mathematics. Itô's lemma underlies the Black–Scholes option pricing framework, making his work central to modern finance as well as pure mathematics.
