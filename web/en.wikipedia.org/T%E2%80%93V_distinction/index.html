T–V distinction

The T–V distinction is the use of different second-person pronouns depending on the social relationship between speaker and addressee, named from the Latin pronouns tu (singular, familiar) and vos (plural, polite).

Examples

French contrasts tu and vous, Spanish tú and usted, German du and Sie, and Russian ty and vy. The choice encodes familiarity, respect, hierarchy, and social distance.

History

The polite use of the plural pronoun is traced to late Roman imperial usage, spreading through European languages in the Middle Ages. English once had the distinction (thou/you) but largely lost it, retaining "you" for both registers.

Sociolinguistics

Brown and Gilman's classic 1960 study described the pronouns as governed by "power" and "solidarity" semantics, with usage shifting over time toward reciprocal familiar forms in many societies.
