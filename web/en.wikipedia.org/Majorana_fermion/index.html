Majorana fermion

A Majorana fermion, also called a Majorana particle, is a fermion that is its own antiparticle. The concept was proposed in 1937 by Ettore Majorana, who showed that the Dirac equation admits real-valued solutions describing a neutral particle identical to its antiparticle. All known elementary fermions except possibly the neutrino are Dirac fermions, each distinct from its antiparticle.

Neutrinos

Whether neutrinos are Majorana or Dirac particles is an open question in particle physics. If neutrinos are Majorana particles, then neutrinoless double beta decay should occur, and many searches for this process are under way. The seesaw mechanism, a popular explanation for the smallness of neutrino masses, naturally invokes Majorana mass terms.

Condensed matter

In condensed-matter physics, Majorana zero modes — quasiparticle excitations that behave as Majorana fermions — are predicted to occur in topological superconductors and semiconductor nanowires. Because such modes are expected to obey non-Abelian exchange statistics, they are intensively studied as a possible basis for fault-tolerant topological quantum computing. Reports of their observation since 2012 have been influential but remain debated.
