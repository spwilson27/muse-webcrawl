Wascana Centre

Wascana Centre is a large urban park located in the heart of Regina, Saskatchewan, Canada. Built around the shores of Wascana Lake, it is one of the largest urban parks in North America, covering approximately 930 hectares (2,300 acres) — an area larger than New York City's Central Park and Vancouver's Stanley Park combined.

History

Wascana Lake was originally created in 1883 by damming Wascana Creek to provide a water supply for the new territorial capital. The name "Wascana" derives from the Cree word oskana kâ-asastêki, meaning "pile of bones," a reference to the buffalo bones once found along the creek.

In 1908, landscape architect Frederick Todd prepared early plans for the park and the legislative grounds. The lake was dramatically enlarged in the 1930s as a Depression-era relief project, when hundreds of workers deepened and widened it using hand tools and horse-drawn scrapers. In 1962 the Wascana Centre Authority was established by provincial legislation to govern the park lands, bringing together the Government of Saskatchewan, the City of Regina, and the University of Regina.

In 2004, the "Big Dig" project deepened Wascana Lake by about two metres to improve water quality and aquatic habitat.

Features

The park contains the Saskatchewan Legislative Building (completed in 1912), the Royal Saskatchewan Museum, the Saskatchewan Science Centre, the MacKenzie Art Gallery, the Conexus Arts Centre, and the campus of the University of Regina. Recreational facilities include walking and cycling paths, the Wascana Pool, boat rentals, and the Wascana Waterfowl Park.

Governance

The park is managed by the Wascana Centre Authority under Saskatchewan's Wascana Centre Act, with a board drawn from its three funding partners: the province, the city, and the university.
