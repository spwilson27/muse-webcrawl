Sub Pop

Sub Pop Records, the Seattle label closely associated with grunge.

Sub Pop is an American record label founded in 1986 by Bruce Pavitt and Jonathan Poneman in Seattle, Washington. It achieved fame in the late 1980s and early 1990s for signing Seattle bands such as Nirvana, Soundgarden and Mudhoney, central players in the grunge movement.

History

Sub Pop began as a fanzine, Subterranean Pop, started by Pavitt in Olympia, Washington, in 1979, and evolved into a record label; its first full release as Sub Pop was the compilation Sub Pop 100 (1986). The label cultivated a recognisable "Seattle sound" through producer Jack Endino and promoted it aggressively, notably flying British journalists to Seattle in 1989, which helped spark international attention. Nirvana's debut album Bleach was released on Sub Pop in 1989 before the band moved to Geffen.

Near bankruptcy in the early 1990s, the label survived by selling a 49 percent stake to Warner Bros. Records in 1995. In the 2000s Sub Pop diversified successfully into indie rock and beyond, releasing successful albums by The Shins, Fleet Foxes, The Postal Service, Beach House and Father John Misty, while maintaining its Sub Pop Singles Club and the annual SP20-style anniversary celebrations.
