Time on the Cross

AuthorsRobert William Fogel, Stanley L. Engerman
Published1974
SubjectEconomic history of American slavery

Time on the Cross: The Economics of American Negro Slavery (1974) is a history book by Robert William Fogel and Stanley L. Engerman. A landmark of cliometrics, the quantitative study of history, it argued controversially that slavery in the American South was profitable, that slave agriculture was efficient, and that material conditions of enslaved people compared better with those of free industrial workers than was commonly assumed.

Reception

The book won the Bancroft Prize but provoked intense controversy over its methods, data and moral framing. Critics challenged its statistical techniques and its treatment of whipping records, slave diets and family separation, and a large literature of rebuttal followed, including Reckoning with Slavery (1976). Fogel later developed his views further in Without Consent or Contract (1989), which emphasised the evil of slavery.
