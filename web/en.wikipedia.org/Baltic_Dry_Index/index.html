The Baltic Dry Index (BDI) is a shipping and trade index issued daily by the London-based Baltic Exchange. It measures the cost of moving major raw materials — iron ore, coal, grain, and other dry bulk commodities — by sea, averaging timecharter rates across a set of global routes for three classes of dry bulk carriers: Capesize, Panamax, and (since 2005) Supramax vessels. The BDI is widely watched as a leading economic indicator, because dry bulk demand tracks industrial production and cannot readily be manipulated or speculated upon.

History

The index began in 1985 as the Baltic Freight Index (BFI), and the BDI replaced it as the headline measure in 1999. During the mid-2000s commodity supercycle it soared to an all-time high of 11,793 points on 20 May 2008, then collapsed by roughly 94% within months as the global financial crisis destroyed trade demand. It set an all-time low of 290 points in February 2016 amid fleet overcapacity and slowing Chinese demand, then spiked again during the post-pandemic supply-chain disruption, exceeding 5,600 in October 2021.

Methodology

Brokers on a panel assess rates for representative routes and vessel sizes; the BDI combines the Capesize (40%), Panamax (30%), and Supramax (30%) timecharter averages (weights revised over time) converted into a single index value. Unlike freight derivatives, the index itself is not tradeable, though forward freight agreements (FFAs) settle against its component indices.

Interpretation

Because ship supply responds to demand only with long construction lags, changes in the BDI primarily reflect shifts in global demand for raw materials, making it a coincident-to-leading barometer of world industrial activity — volatile, but unusually free of speculative distortion.
