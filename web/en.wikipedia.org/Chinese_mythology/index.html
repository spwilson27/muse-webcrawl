Chinese mythology

Chinese mythology is mythology that has been passed down in oral form or recorded in literature from the geographic area now known as Greater China. It draws on traditions associated with China's ethnic groups, Taoism, Confucian ancestor traditions, Buddhism, and Chinese folk religion, and includes creation myths, culture heroes, deities, and legendary creatures such as dragons.

Creation myths

In the best-known creation story, the giant Pangu emerged from a cosmic egg and separated heaven and earth, holding them apart until his body became the features of the world. Another major figure, the goddess Nüwa, created humanity from yellow clay and repaired the broken sky with five coloured stones after a cosmic disaster.

Culture heroes

The Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors are legendary rulers credited with foundational inventions: Fuxi with the trigrams and fishing, Shennong (the Divine Farmer) with agriculture and herbal medicine, and the Yellow Emperor (Huangdi) with writing, the compass cart, and the Chinese calendar. Yu the Great is remembered for controlling the great floods and founding the Xia dynasty.

Key texts and creatures

The Classic of Mountains and Seas (Shanhaijing) preserves geography, gods, and monsters from early China. Mythic beings include the Chinese dragon (long), the qilin, the fenghuang (phoenix), and the Four Auspicious Beasts. Later classics such as Journey to the West and Investiture of the Gods reshaped older myths for popular audiences.
