Software bug

A software bug is a defect, error, or flaw in a computer program that causes it to produce an incorrect or unexpected result, or to behave in unintended ways. Bugs range from minor cosmetic glitches to system crashes, data corruption, and security vulnerabilities.

Etymology

The term long predates computing for engineering defects. Its popularisation in computing is associated with a 1947 incident at Harvard, when operators of the Mark II found a moth trapped in a relay; Grace Hopper's team taped it into the logbook. Hopper popularised "debugging", though she did not coin "bug".

Causes and costs

Bugs arise from mistakes in requirements, design, coding, and interaction between components. Studies commonly cited by the industry estimate that defects cost the global economy trillions of dollars annually. Famous costly bugs include the Ariane 5 flight 501 failure (1996), caused by a numeric conversion overflow, and the Year 2000 problem.

Management

Defects are tracked in bug-tracking systems, prioritised by severity and priority, and addressed through debugging, testing, static analysis, and code review.
