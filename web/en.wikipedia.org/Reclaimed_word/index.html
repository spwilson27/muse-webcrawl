Reclaimed word

A reclaimed or reappropriated word is a word that was at one time pejorative but has been brought back into acceptable usage, usually starting within its original target group. Reclamation is a form of semantic change in which a slur or insult is adopted by the people it was used against, defusing its power and turning it into a badge of identity or solidarity.

Examples

Widely cited examples include "queer", reclaimed by LGBT activists and academics from the late 1980s onward (as in Queer Nation and queer theory); "geek" and "nerd", once insults for socially awkward people, now often worn proudly; and terms such as "suffragette", originally coined dismissively by a newspaper and embraced by the women's suffrage movement. Some reclamations remain contested: the acceptability of a reclaimed term often depends on who uses it, with in-group use welcomed and out-group use still offensive.

Mechanism and debate

Linguists note that reclamation typically proceeds through ironic or defiant in-group use, media visibility, and gradual neutralisation. Critics within target communities sometimes oppose reclamation, arguing that a word's hurtful history cannot be undone or that mainstream adoption dilutes its political edge. The process is related to, but distinct from, the "euphemism treadmill", in which neutral terms acquire negative connotations over time.
