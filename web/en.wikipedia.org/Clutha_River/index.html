Clutha River

Clutha River / Mata-Au

Location: Otago, South Island, New Zealand

Length: about 338 km (210 mi)

Source: Lake Wānaka

Mouth: Pacific Ocean, near Balclutha

The Clutha River (Māori: Mata-Au) is the second longest river in New Zealand and the longest in the South Island. It flows south-southeast for about 338 kilometres (210 mi) through Central and South Otago, from Lake Wānaka in the Southern Alps to its mouth on the Pacific Ocean near Balclutha. It has the largest catchment and the highest average discharge of any New Zealand river.

Course

The river begins at the head of Lake Wānaka and flows through the Upper Clutha valley past Albert Town and Cromwell, where it is joined by the Kawarau River. Below Cromwell it passes through the Cromwell Gorge and the Roxburgh Gorge — both now partly impounded for hydroelectric generation — then through Roxburgh, Ettrick, and Beaumont before crossing the fertile Clutha plains past Balclutha to the sea.

Hydroelectric power

The river hosts two major hydroelectric dams: the Clyde Dam, New Zealand's third-largest hydroelectric station, completed in the early 1990s, and the older Roxburgh Dam, commissioned in 1956. The dams created Lake Dunstan and Lake Roxburgh respectively.

History

The river valley was a focus of the Otago gold rush of the 1860s, when gold was dredged from the river gravels on an industrial scale. The river is notable for its swift current, its turquoise colour, and severe historical floods, notably in 1878 and 1999.
