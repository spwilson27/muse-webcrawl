The Staatskapelle Dresden (officially the Sächsische Staatskapelle Dresden) is a German symphony orchestra based in Dresden, the capital of Saxony. Founded in 1548 by Elector Moritz of Saxony, it is one of the oldest orchestras in the world and is regarded as one of the world's leading orchestras. Its home is the Semperoper, where it serves as the opera orchestra of the Saxon State Opera as well as giving symphonic concerts.

History

Originally the court orchestra of the Electors of Saxony, the Kapelle employed many eminent musicians, including Heinrich Schütz in the seventeenth century. In the nineteenth century Carl Maria von Weber and Richard Wagner served as its Kapellmeister; Wagner called it his "miraculous harp". Richard Strauss had a particularly close association with the orchestra, which premiered several of his operas, including Feuersnot, Salome, Elektra, and Der Rosenkavalier.

Chief conductors

Chief conductors have included Ernst von Schuch, Fritz Reiner, Fritz Busch, Karl Böhm, Joseph Keilberth, Rudolf Kempe, Otmar Suitner, Herbert Blomstedt, Giuseppe Sinopoli, and Bernard Haitink. Christian Thielemann served as chief conductor from 2012 to 2024; Daniele Gatti took up the post in 2024.

Recordings

The orchestra has an extensive discography, notably recordings of the German Romantic repertoire under Kempe and Blomstedt, and Strauss operas and tone poems. It tours internationally and was the first recipient of the European Prize for the Preservation of the World's Musical Heritage.
