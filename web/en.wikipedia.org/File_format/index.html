File format

A file format is a standard way that information is encoded for storage in a computer file. It specifies how bits are used to encode information in a digital storage medium, and may be proprietary or open, documented or undocumented.

Identifying formats

Formats are commonly indicated by the filename extension (such as .txt, .jpg, or .pdf) and, on the Internet, by MIME types. Many formats begin with characteristic "magic numbers", such as the bytes ÿØÿà marking a JPEG image. Operating systems variously rely on extensions, metadata, or these signatures to associate files with applications.

Categories

Major categories include text formats (plain text, HTML, XML), image formats (JPEG, PNG), audio formats (MP3, FLAC), video formats (often container formats such as MP4), document formats (PDF, Office Open XML), and archive and compression formats (ZIP, tar).

Open and proprietary formats

Open formats are published and free of legal restrictions on use, favouring interoperability and long-term archiving; proprietary formats are controlled by a single vendor and may require licences to implement.
