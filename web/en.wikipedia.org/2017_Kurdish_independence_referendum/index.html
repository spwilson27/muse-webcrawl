The 2017 Kurdish independence referendum was held on 25 September 2017 in northern Iraq in the Kurdistan Region and in Kurdish-held disputed territories, including Kirkuk. Announced by President Masoud Barzani, the vote asked whether voters wanted the region to become an independent state.

Background

The Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) had enjoyed autonomy since 1991 and was recognised in Iraq's 2005 constitution. The rise of the Islamic State in 2014 saw Kurdish peshmerga forces expand into disputed areas, including the oil-rich city of Kirkuk, that Baghdad also claimed. An earlier referendum planned for 2014 was postponed because of the war against ISIL.

Result and aftermath

Official results showed 92.73 percent voting in favour of independence, on turnout of 72.16 percent. The Iraqi federal government, Iran and Turkey condemned the vote; Iraq's Supreme Court ruled it unconstitutional. In October 2017, Iraqi forces retook Kirkuk and most disputed territories in a swift operation, airports in the region were closed to international flights, and Barzani announced he would step down as president. The independence bid was effectively halted, though the referendum remains a landmark in Kurdish national aspirations.
