Drum machine

The Roland TR-808 (1980), an iconic drum machine

A drum machine is an electronic musical instrument that creates percussion sounds, drum beats, and patterns. Drum machines may imitate drum kits or other percussion instruments, or produce unique electronic sounds. Most modern drum machines are sequencers with a sample playback or synthesized component, allowing musicians to program rhythms rather than play them live.

History

Early rhythm machines, such as the Wurlitzer Side Man (1959) and Ace Tone and Korg preset rhythm boxes, accompanied organists and lounge performers. Programmable units arrived in the 1970s, notably the Roland CompuRhythm CR-78 (1978). The Linn LM-1 (1980) introduced sampled acoustic drums, while the Roland TR-808 (1980) and TR-909 (1983), initially commercial disappointments, became foundational to hip hop, house, techno, and electro music.

Operation

Users program patterns on a step sequencer grid, typically of 16 steps per bar, assigning sounds to steps and setting tempo, swing, and accents. Patterns can be chained into songs, saved, and synchronized with other instruments via MIDI or control voltages.

Impact

Drum machines reshaped popular music production, enabling a single producer to create complete rhythm tracks and giving rise to signature machine-driven grooves across electronic and urban genres. Software drum machines are now standard in digital audio workstations.
