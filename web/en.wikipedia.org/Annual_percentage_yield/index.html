Annual percentage yield

The annual percentage yield (APY) is a normalized representation of an interest rate, based on a compounding period of one year. APY figures allow a reasonable, single-point comparison of different offerings with varying compounding schedules. However, as compared to the stated (nominal) rate, and similar to the effective annual rate, APY does not account for the possibility that the account holder will withdraw the deposit before maturity.

Formula

APY is calculated as APY = (1 + r/n)n − 1, where r is the stated annual interest rate and n is the number of compounding periods per year. For example, an account with a stated rate of 5% compounded monthly has an APY of about 5.116%.

United States

In the United States, the calculation and disclosure of APY is governed by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation and the Truth in Savings Act (Regulation DD). Banks and credit unions must disclose the APY to consumers for deposit accounts, allowing savers to compare products on an equal footing regardless of how frequently interest compounds.
