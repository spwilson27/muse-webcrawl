Restriction factor

A restriction factor is a bacterial protein or system that inhibits the replication of foreign DNA, most notably bacteriophage genomes and other mobile genetic elements, as part of the cell's defence against viral infection.

Mechanisms

The classic restriction factors are the restriction endonucleases of restriction–modification systems, which cut invading DNA at specific sequences while the host's own DNA is protected by methylation. More broadly, the term includes proteins such as CRISPR–Cas effectors and, in animals, antiviral restriction factors like APOBEC3G, TRIM5α, tetherin and SAMHD1, which block specific stages of viral replication.

Significance

Restriction–modification systems shaped molecular biology: their enzymes became the foundational tools of recombinant DNA technology. Restriction factors also drive an evolutionary arms race, with viruses evolving countermeasures such as anti-restriction proteins and viral antagonists of host factors.
