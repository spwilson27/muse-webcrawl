Palomar Observatory is an astronomical observatory on Palomar Mountain in San Diego County, California, owned and operated by the California Institute of Technology (Caltech). It is best known for the Hale Telescope, a 200-inch (5.1-metre) reflecting telescope that was the largest effective telescope in the world from its completion in 1949 until 1976.

History

The observatory was the vision of astronomer George Ellery Hale, who secured a $6 million grant from the Rockefeller Foundation in 1928 to build a 200-inch telescope. The site on Palomar Mountain, at 1,712 m elevation, was chosen for its clear, dark skies away from city lights. The telescope's giant Pyrex mirror blank was cast by Corning Glass Works in 1934 and transported across the United States; delays caused by the Depression and the Second World War meant first light did not come until 26 January 1949. The telescope was named in Hale's honour.

Research

With the Hale Telescope, astronomers such as Edwin Hubble's successors extended the cosmic distance scale, and in the 1960s Maarten Schmidt's Palomar observations of quasars revealed them to be extremely distant, luminous objects. The observatory's 48-inch Samuel Oschin Schmidt telescope carried out the landmark Palomar Observatory Sky Survey (POSS) of the 1950s, for decades the standard photographic atlas of the northern sky, and today supports transient surveys such as the Zwicky Transient Facility, which searches for supernovae, asteroids and other changing objects. The Hale Telescope has been modernised with adaptive optics and continues in nightly use.

The observatory is open to visitors, with a small museum and public gallery in the Hale dome.
