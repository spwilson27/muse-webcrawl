Multiple sequence alignment


Field: Bioinformatics, computational biology
Inputs: Three or more nucleotide or amino-acid sequences
Common tools: Clustal, MUSCLE, MAFFT, T-Coffee

A multiple sequence alignment (MSA) is a sequence alignment of three or more biological sequences, generally proteins, DNA, or RNA. By inserting gaps so that homologous residues line up in columns, an MSA displays regions of similarity that may indicate structural, functional, or evolutionary relationships among the sequences.

Methods

Exact alignment by dynamic programming becomes computationally infeasible as the number of sequences grows, so practical tools use heuristics. Progressive alignment (used by Clustal) builds a guide tree and aligns the closest sequences first; iterative methods (MUSCLE, MAFFT) repeatedly refine the result; consistency-based methods (T-Coffee) incorporate pairwise information from multiple sources; and probabilistic methods use hidden Markov models. Substitution matrices such as BLOSUM and PAM and gap penalties score candidate alignments.

Uses

MSAs underpin phylogenetic tree construction, identification of conserved motifs and active sites, protein structure prediction, primer design, and genome annotation. Alignment quality strongly affects downstream analyses, and benchmarking suites such as BAliBASE are used to compare methods.

Visualization and formats

Alignments are stored in formats such as FASTA, Clustal, and Stockholm, and are often coloured by residue conservation and chemistry. Manual editing tools like Jalview allow inspection and curation.
