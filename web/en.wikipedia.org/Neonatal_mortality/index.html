Neonatal mortality

Neonatal mortality
Definition: Death of a live-born infant within the first 28 days of life
Measure: Neonatal mortality rate (NMR) — deaths per 1,000 live births
Global deaths: ~2.3–2.4 million per year (recent UN estimates)

Neonatal mortality is the death of a live-born infant within the first 28 completed days of life. The neonatal mortality rate (NMR) — deaths per 1,000 live births — is a key indicator of child health and of the quality of maternal and newborn care. Deaths in the first week (early neonatal deaths) account for roughly three-quarters of neonatal deaths, and the first day of life is the single riskiest. Neonatal deaths make up nearly half of all deaths of children under five worldwide.

Causes

The leading causes globally are complications of preterm birth, intrapartum-related events (including birth asphyxia), and infections such as sepsis, pneumonia, and meningitis; congenital anomalies account for a growing share as other causes decline. Most neonatal deaths occur in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, where rates exceed 25 per 1,000 live births in the highest-burden countries, compared with under 3 per 1,000 in much of Europe and East Asia.

Trends and interventions

The global NMR fell from about 37 per 1,000 live births in 1990 to around 17–18 per 1,000 by the early 2020s — a slower decline than for older children, which is why Sustainable Development Goal 3.2 targets an NMR of at most 12 per 1,000 in every country by 2030. Effective interventions include skilled birth attendance, emergency obstetric care, antenatal corticosteroids, newborn resuscitation, kangaroo mother care for preterm infants, early and exclusive breastfeeding, hygienic cord care, and prompt antibiotic treatment of infections.
