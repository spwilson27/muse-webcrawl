Clayton Christensen

Clayton Christensen
Born: 6 April 1952, Salt Lake City, Utah
Died: 23 January 2020, Boston, Massachusetts
Occupation: Academic, author, consultant
Known for: Disruptive innovation

Clayton Magleby Christensen (April 6, 1952 – January 23, 2020) was an American academic and business consultant who developed the theory of "disruptive innovation," first introduced in his 1997 book The Innovator's Dilemma. He was a professor at Harvard Business School.

Early life and education

Christensen was born in Salt Lake City, Utah, and grew up in a family of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. He studied economics at Brigham Young University, applied econometrics and economics as a Rhodes Scholar at the University of Oxford, and later earned an MBA and a doctorate in business administration from Harvard Business School.

Career

Before joining Harvard's faculty in 1992, Christensen worked as a consultant at Boston Consulting Group and co-founded CPS Technologies, a ceramics materials company. At Harvard he taught the popular course "Building and Sustaining a Successful Enterprise."

Disruptive innovation

His theory holds that successful firms can fail by doing everything "right" — listening to their best customers and investing in high-margin products — while entrants with simpler, cheaper products take root in overlooked markets and move upmarket. Christensen later applied the framework to healthcare, education, and national prosperity in books such as The Innovator's Health Care and Disrupting Class. He also authored the personal-strategy bestseller How Will You Measure Your Life? (2012).
