Fender Musical Instruments Corporation
Founded: 1946, Fullerton, California, U.S.
Founder: Leo Fender
Headquarters: Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Products: Electric guitars, bass guitars, amplifiers

The Fender Musical Instruments Corporation is an American manufacturer of musical instruments and amplifiers. Fender produces acoustic guitars, bass amplifiers and public address equipment; however, it is best known for its solid-body electric guitars and bass guitars, particularly the Stratocaster, Telecaster, Jaguar, Jazzmaster, Precision Bass, and Jazz Bass.

History

The company was founded by Leo Fender in Fullerton, California, in 1946 as Fender's Radio Service, evolving from radio and amplifier repair into instrument manufacture. In 1950 Fender introduced the Telecaster, the first mass-produced solid-body electric guitar, followed by the Precision Bass (1951) and the Stratocaster (1954) — instruments that revolutionised popular music.

Leo Fender sold the company to CBS in 1965. Quality concerns during the CBS era led a group of employees and investors, led by William C. Schultz, to buy Fender back in 1985, forming the modern Fender Musical Instruments Corporation. Production is centred in Corona, California, and Ensenada, Mexico, with additional manufacturing in Asia for the Squier line.

Brands and products

Besides the Fender brand, the company owns or licenses Squier, Gretsch, Jackson, Charvel and EVH. Fender amplifiers such as the Twin Reverb, Bassman and Deluxe Reverb are staples of rock, country and blues.

Cultural impact

Fender instruments have been played by many of the most influential guitarists in popular music, including Buddy Holly, Jimi Hendrix, Eric Clapton, David Gilmour, Stevie Ray Vaughan and Kurt Cobain. The Stratocaster is among the most recognisable and imitated guitar designs in the world.
