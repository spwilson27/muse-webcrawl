Heart

The heart is a muscular organ found in most animals. This organ pumps blood through the blood vessels of the circulatory system. The pumped blood carries oxygen and nutrients to the tissues, while carrying metabolic waste such as carbon dioxide to the lungs. In humans, the heart is approximately the size of a closed fist and is located between the lungs, in the middle compartment of the chest, called the mediastinum.

In humans, other mammals, and birds, the heart is divided into four chambers: upper left and right atria and lower left and right ventricles. The heart pumps blood in two circuits: the pulmonary circulation, in which deoxygenated blood travels to the lungs and back, and the systemic circulation, which delivers oxygenated blood to the rest of the body.

Structure

The heart wall consists of three layers: the inner endocardium, the middle myocardium — the thick layer of cardiac muscle — and the outer epicardium, all enclosed in a double-walled protective sac, the pericardium. The right side of the heart receives deoxygenated blood from the body via the superior and inferior vena cava and pumps it through the pulmonary arteries to the lungs. The left side receives oxygenated blood from the pulmonary veins and pumps it through the aorta to the body; its wall is much thicker, since it must generate much higher pressure.

Four valves prevent backflow: the tricuspid valve between the right atrium and ventricle, the pulmonary valve, the mitral valve between the left atrium and ventricle, and the aortic valve. The "lub-dub" sounds of the heartbeat are produced by their closing.

Electrical conduction

The heartbeat is initiated by pacemaker cells in the sinoatrial node, located in the right atrium. The electrical signal spreads across the atria and, after a delay at the atrioventricular node, travels rapidly through the bundle of His and Purkinje fibers to trigger ventricular contraction. A resting adult heart beats about 72 times per minute — more than 100,000 times a day and roughly 3 billion times in an average lifetime.

Circulation

Each minute the heart pumps about 5 liters of blood at rest. The heart's own muscle is supplied by the coronary arteries; blockage of one of these causes a heart attack (myocardial infarction), in which part of the myocardium dies.

Development

The heart is the first functional organ to develop in the embryo, beginning to beat and pump blood around the third week of gestation in humans. It forms from cardiogenic mesoderm that fuses into a primitive heart tube, which then loops and septates into the four-chambered organ.

Diseases

Cardiovascular diseases are the leading cause of death globally. They include coronary artery disease, heart failure, cardiomyopathies, valvular disease, congenital heart defects and arrhythmias such as atrial fibrillation. The medical specialty devoted to the heart is cardiology.
