Operation Torch was the Allied invasion of French North Africa during the Second World War, carried out between 8 and 16 November 1942. It was the first major Allied amphibious operation of the war in the European–African theatre and the first large-scale deployment of American ground forces against the Axis. The landings took place simultaneously in French Morocco and Algeria, territories controlled by Vichy France.

Background

American planners had initially favoured an early cross-Channel invasion of occupied France, but British leaders argued that Allied forces were not yet ready and pressed instead for an attack on the "soft underbelly" of Axis-held territory. North Africa offered a way to clear the southern shore of the Mediterranean, secure the Suez Canal supply route, and give American troops combat experience before the invasion of Europe proper.

The landings

Three task forces landed on 8 November 1942. The Western Task Force under Major General George S. Patton sailed directly from the United States and landed near Casablanca in Morocco; the Center Task Force landed at Oran and the Eastern Task Force at Algiers in Algeria. Resistance from Vichy French forces varied: fighting was heavy around Oran and Casablanca, while at Algiers a coup by the French Resistance helped secure the city quickly.

Aftermath

Admiral François Darlan, the senior Vichy official present in Algiers, ordered a ceasefire on 10 November in exchange for being recognised as head of the French administration in North Africa — an arrangement that caused controversy among the Allies. The operation nevertheless succeeded: Allied forces pushed east into Tunisia, where they met the German and Italian forces retreating westward after the Second Battle of El Alamein, setting the stage for the Tunisia Campaign.
