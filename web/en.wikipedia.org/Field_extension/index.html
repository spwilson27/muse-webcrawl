In mathematics, particularly abstract algebra, a field extension is a pair of fields L ⊇ K such that the operations of K are those of L restricted to K; K is called the base field (or subfield) and L the extension field. The extension is denoted L/K (read "L over K"). Field extensions are the central object of study in field theory, and their symmetries are the subject of Galois theory. A familiar example is the extension of the rational numbers ℚ by the real and complex numbers: ℂ/ℝ is an extension of degree 2, formed by adjoining i = √−1.

Degree and algebraic elements

An extension field L is a vector space over K; its dimension is the degree [L : K] of the extension, which may be finite or infinite. Degrees multiply in towers: [M : K] = [M : L]·[L : K]. An element of L is algebraic over K if it is a root of a nonzero polynomial with coefficients in K, and transcendental otherwise. Adjoining a single algebraic element α gives the simple extension K(α), whose degree equals the degree of α's minimal polynomial; adjoining a transcendental element gives a field isomorphic to the rational function field K(t).

Important classes of extensions

An algebraic extension is normal if it contains all roots of the minimal polynomial of each of its elements, and separable if those minimal polynomials have no repeated roots. Extensions that are both normal and separable are Galois extensions, whose automorphism groups correspond bijectively to their intermediate fields by the fundamental theorem of Galois theory. Splitting fields and algebraic closures are key constructions; Kronecker's theorem shows any polynomial can be given a root in some extension field.

Applications

Field extensions underlie the classical impossibility proofs of Greek geometry (doubling the cube and trisecting the angle require elements of degree 3, unreachable by quadratic towers), the Abel–Ruffini theorem on the insolubility of the general quintic by radicals, algebraic number theory (number fields are finite extensions of ℚ), and the theory of finite fields used in coding theory and cryptography.
