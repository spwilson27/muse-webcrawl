Sterile services department

A sterile services department (SSD), also known as a central sterile services department (CSSD) or sterile processing department (SPD), is a facility within or serving a hospital where reusable medical devices — principally surgical instruments — are cleaned, disinfected, inspected, assembled, packaged and sterilized for safe reuse on patients. The department is a critical link in infection prevention and control.

Function

After use in an operating theatre or ward, contaminated instruments are transported to the SSD in closed containers. Staff working in the "dirty" wash area receive the items, sort them, and clean them using washer-disinfectors, ultrasonic baths and manual cleaning. Clean instruments pass to the inspection, assembly and packaging (IAP) area, where each tray is checked against a checklist, reassembled, wrapped or sealed in pouches, and labelled. Items are then sterilized, most commonly in steam autoclaves, with low-temperature methods such as hydrogen peroxide gas plasma used for heat-sensitive devices. Sterile packs are stored in controlled conditions and distributed back to theatres and departments.

Design and workflow

Modern departments are arranged as a one-way flow from dirty to clean to sterile zones, separated by physical barriers and pass-through washers and sterilizers, to prevent cross-contamination. Environmental controls cover air quality, temperature, humidity and lighting, and water quality is monitored because it affects both cleaning efficacy and instrument condition.

Quality and regulation

Sterile services operate under national and international standards covering decontamination and sterilization, and each sterilization cycle is monitored with physical, chemical and biological indicators. Traceability systems record which instruments were processed in which load, allowing packs to be tracked to individual patients — an important capability if a sterilization failure or product recall occurs.
