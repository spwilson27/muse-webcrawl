Breaking news

Breaking news, also called a news flash or special report, is a current issue that broadcasters feel warrants interrupting scheduled programming to report its details. The term is also used in online and print journalism for rapidly developing stories.

History

Radio and later television transformed breaking news coverage: bulletins interrupted programmes for events such as the assassination of John F. Kennedy in 1963. The launch of CNN in 1980 created the 24-hour news cycle, in which channels cover developing stories continuously.

Usage and criticism

Networks use on-screen banners, alerts, and special graphics to flag breaking items. Critics argue that the label has been devalued by overuse for minor or routine stories, and that speed pressures can lead to reporting errors during unfolding events.
