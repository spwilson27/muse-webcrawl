Russo-Circassian War

Date1763 – 21 May 1864
LocationCircassia, Northwest Caucasus
ResultRussian victory; annexation of Circassia
ConsequencesCircassian genocide and mass expulsion to the Ottoman Empire

The Russo-Circassian War (1763–1864) was the century-long military conflict between the Russian Empire and the Circassians (Adyghe) of the northwestern Caucasus. It formed part of Russia's conquest of the Caucasus. The conflict began with Russian fort-building in Kabarda in 1763 — notably the fortress of Mozdok — and resistance from Circassian princes, and it ended on 21 May 1864 with the surrender of the last Circassian forces at Qbaade (now Krasnaya Polyana).

Course of the war

Circassia, a confederation of tribal principalities stretching along the Black Sea coast and the western Caucasus, controlled territory Russia coveted to secure its southern frontier and a warm-water coastline. Cossack lines of stanitsas steadily advanced, and Circassian resistance combined guerrilla warfare in the mountains with attempts at centralised leadership and appeals for British and Ottoman support. Figures such as Sefer Bey Zanuqo and, in later years, Muhammad Amin (naib of Imam Shamil in Circassia) tried to unify the tribes. In the war's final phase after 1859, General Nikolai Yevdokimov applied a systematic strategy of clearing the population, colony by colony, burning villages and crops.

Circassian genocide and exile

The war ended with the mass expulsion of the Circassian population: the great majority of Circassians — estimates commonly range from around 800,000 to 1.5 million people — were deported to the Ottoman Empire in 1864 and the following years, and very large numbers died from disease, starvation and shipwreck during the exodus. Only a small minority remained in the Caucasus, resettled on the left bank of the Kuban. The Circassian diaspora today lives mainly in Turkey, Jordan, Syria, Israel and elsewhere. Georgia's parliament recognised the events as genocide in 2011, and Circassians worldwide commemorate 21 May as a day of mourning; Russia rejects the genocide characterisation.
