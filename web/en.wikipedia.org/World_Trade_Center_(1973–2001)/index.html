The original World Trade Center (WTC) was a large complex of seven buildings in the Financial District of Lower Manhattan in New York City. It opened on April 4, 1973, and was destroyed during the September 11 attacks of 2001.

The Twin Towers in 2001, viewed from New York Harbor.

History

The complex was conceived to revitalize Lower Manhattan and to concentrate firms engaged in international trade. Designed by architect Minoru Yamasaki with Emery Roth & Sons, its centerpiece was the pair of 110-story Twin Towers — for a time the tallest buildings in the world. Construction used an innovative tube-frame structural design with prefabricated modules.

Attacks

The complex was bombed on February 26, 1993, when a truck bomb exploded in the North Tower's parking garage, killing six people. On September 11, 2001, al-Qaeda hijackers flew American Airlines Flight 11 into the North Tower and United Airlines Flight 175 into the South Tower. Both towers collapsed within less than two hours; the other buildings in the complex were destroyed or damaged beyond repair, and nearly 3,000 people died in the attacks overall.

Aftermath

The site, known as Ground Zero, was cleared and rebuilt as the new World Trade Center, centered on One World Trade Center and the National September 11 Memorial & Museum.
