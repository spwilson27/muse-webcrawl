By-election

By-election
Election type
Also called: Special election (US)
Trigger: Vacancy between general elections

A by-election (also spelled bye-election) is an election used to fill an office that has become vacant between general elections, for example upon the death, resignation or disqualification of a sitting legislator.

Practice

By-elections are common in parliamentary systems using single-member districts, such as the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and India. In the UK, a writ is moved in the House of Commons — usually by the party that held the seat — and the vote typically follows within a few weeks or months. In the United States, the equivalent contest for Congress or state legislatures is called a special election.

Significance

Turnout is usually lower than at general elections, and results are often treated as mid-term verdicts on the government; by-elections can produce large swings and occasional surprise results. Until 1918 in Britain, MPs appointed to ministerial office were required to seek re-election in a ministerial by-election.

Exceptions

Many proportional-representation systems fill vacancies from party lists instead, and some offices are filled by appointment or succession rather than by-election.
