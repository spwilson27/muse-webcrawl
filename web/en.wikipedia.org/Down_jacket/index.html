Down jacket

A typical quilted down jacket

A down jacket, known more commonly in the fashion industry as a puffer jacket or simply puffer, is a quilted coat which is insulated with the soft feathers of duck or goose. The air pockets created by the bulk of the feathers allow for the retention of warm air. Down jackets are valued for an exceptional warmth-to-weight ratio and compressibility, and are widely used in outdoor recreation, mountaineering and everyday winter wear.

History

Down-filled clothing has long been used in cold climates, but the modern down jacket is credited to American outdoorsman Eddie Bauer, who in 1936, after nearly dying of hypothermia on a winter fishing trip, patented a down-filled jacket — the "Skyliner" — in 1940. George Finch, a chemist and mountaineer, wore an early eiderdown-filled jacket (the "Finch coat") on the 1922 British Mount Everest expedition. Moncler, founded in France in 1952, supplied down jackets to expedition and ski teams, and in the 1980s and 1990s brightly coloured puffers became mainstream fashion items.

Construction and fill power

The insulation quality of down is measured by fill power — the volume in cubic inches that one ounce of down occupies when lofted — with higher figures (600–900) indicating warmer, lighter, more compressible down. Duck down is generally cheaper; goose down tends to achieve higher fill powers. Stitched-through or box-wall baffles hold the down in place and prevent cold spots. Shell fabrics are typically lightweight nylon or polyester, sometimes with a durable water repellent (DWR) finish.

Issues and alternatives

Down loses most of its insulating ability when wet, which has driven demand for hydrophobic treated down and synthetic alternatives such as PrimaLoft. Animal-welfare concerns about live-plucking and force-feeding led to standards such as the Responsible Down Standard (RDS). Down is a by-product of the food industry; recycled down is also used.
