Particle board

Particle board, also known as particleboard, chipboard, or low-density fibreboard, is an engineered wood product manufactured from wood chips and a synthetic resin or other suitable binder, which is pressed and extruded. Particle board is often confused with oriented strand board (OSB), a different type of fibreboard that uses machined wood flakes and offers more strength.

Characteristics

Particle board is cheaper, denser and more uniform than conventional wood and plywood and is substituted for them when cost is more important than strength and appearance. Particleboard can be made more attractive by painting or the use of wood veneers onto surfaces that will be visible. Though it is denser than conventional wood, it is the lightest and weakest type of fibreboard, except for insulation board. Medium-density fibreboard and hardboard, also called high-density fibreboard, are stronger and denser than particleboard.

Manufacturing

Particle board is manufactured by bonding wood particles such as sawdust, shavings and flakes with an adhesive, typically urea-formaldehyde resin, under heat and pressure. The particles are sorted by size, with finer particles used for the outer layers to give a smoother surface. Additives such as wax and water repellents may be included.

Uses

Particle board is widely used in flat-pack furniture, kitchen cabinets, worktops, flooring underlayment, and speaker boxes. It is commonly covered with melamine laminate, giving melamine-faced chipboard used for shelving and furniture.

Environmental and health concerns

Formaldehyde resins can release formaldehyde gas, particularly when the board is cut. Modern standards limit emissions, and alternative binders are increasingly used.
