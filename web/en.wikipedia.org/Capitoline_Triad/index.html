The Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus, seat of the Capitoline Triad.

The Capitoline Triad was a group of three deities worshipped in ancient Rome at the Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus on the Capitoline Hill: Jupiter, his wife Juno, and his daughter Minerva. This grouping, of Etruscan origin — corresponding to the Etruscan gods Tinia, Uni, and Menrva — replaced an earlier, archaic triad of Jupiter, Mars, and Quirinus.

The temple

The Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus, the largest temple of the Roman Republic, was traditionally vowed by Tarquinius Priscus, built under Tarquinius Superbus, and dedicated in 509 BC, the first year of the Republic. Within its single podium stood three parallel cellae, with Jupiter's in the centre, Juno's on the left (viewer's right in some reconstructions), and Minerva's on the right. The cult statue of Jupiter, of terracotta by the Veientine sculptor Vulca in the original temple, later replaced in gold and ivory, showed the god holding a thunderbolt and sceptre, dressed as a triumphing general.

The Capitoline temple was the focal point of Roman state religion. Roman triumphs ended there with sacrifices to Jupiter Optimus Maximus; the consuls took their inaugural vows in the temple; and the standards of the state, treaties, and the Sibylline Books were kept there. The temple burned and was rebuilt several times, in 83 BC, AD 69, and AD 80, each time on the same massive podium, whose remains survive beneath the Palazzo Caffarelli.

Spread of the cult

Capitoline temples modelled on the Roman original were erected in cities throughout Italy and the provinces — a marker of Roman civic identity — with examples at Pompeii, Cosa, Sufetula (Sbeitla), and many other sites. Typically each replicated the triple-cella arrangement and elevated podium approached by a frontal staircase.

The second triad

Scholars sometimes distinguish a second, "plebeian" or Aventine Triad — Ceres, Liber, and Libera — worshipped on the Aventine Hill from 493 BC, which formed a focus for the religious and political identity of the plebeian order, in deliberate parallel to the patrician Capitoline cult.
