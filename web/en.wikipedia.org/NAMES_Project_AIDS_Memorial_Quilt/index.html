The NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt, often called simply the AIDS Memorial Quilt, is an enormous community folk-art project and memorial commemorating people who have died of AIDS-related causes. Each panel, measuring three feet by six feet — roughly the size of a grave — is made by friends, partners or family members with the name and often personal mementos of the person commemorated.

History

Activist Cleve Jones conceived the quilt in San Francisco in 1985, after a candlelight march in which placards bearing the names of the dead were taped to the old Federal Building, resembling a patchwork quilt. The first public display, on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., on 11 October 1987 during the Second National March on Washington for Lesbian and Gay Rights, comprised 1,920 panels and drew half a million visitors. The quilt then toured nationally, raising money for AIDS service organisations.

The quilt today

By the time of its largest full display on the Mall in 1996, the quilt covered the entire grounds. It grew to more than 50,000 panels commemorating over 110,000 people and weighing some 54 tons, making it the largest community art project in the world. Since 2020 the quilt has been cared for by the National AIDS Memorial in San Francisco; panels are displayed at events around the United States, and a digital archive allows visitors to search and view individual panels online.
