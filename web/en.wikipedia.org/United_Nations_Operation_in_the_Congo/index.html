United Nations Operation in the Congo

The United Nations Operation in the Congo (French: Opération des Nations Unies au Congo, abbreviated ONUC) was a peacekeeping force established by the United Nations in July 1960, during the Congo Crisis, in response to a request for military aid from the newly independent Republic of the Congo. It remained in the country until 30 June 1964. At its peak ONUC numbered almost 20,000 troops, and it was one of the largest and most complex operations of the early Cold War.

Background

The Congo gained independence from Belgium on 30 June 1960. Within days the Congolese army mutinied, Belgium intervened militarily without Congolese consent, and the mineral-rich province of Katanga, led by Moïse Tshombe and backed by Belgian interests, declared secession; South Kasai soon followed. Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba and President Joseph Kasa-Vubu appealed to the UN, and the Security Council authorized a force to assist until Congolese forces could maintain order and to secure Belgian withdrawal.

Course of the operation

Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld initially insisted ONUC could not intervene in internal conflicts, frustrating Lumumba, who was dismissed by Kasa-Vubu in September 1960, arrested, and murdered in Katanga in January 1961. After Lumumba's death the Security Council authorized ONUC to use force to prevent civil war. Through operations including Rumpunch, Morthor, and Grandslam, UN forces fought Katangese troops and foreign mercenaries, ending the Katanga secession in January 1963. Hammarskjöld himself died in a plane crash near Ndola, Northern Rhodesia, in September 1961 while travelling to negotiate a ceasefire — circumstances that remain disputed.

Legacy

ONUC nearly bankrupted the UN, triggering a financing crisis and the 1962 Certain Expenses advisory opinion of the International Court of Justice. It stretched peacekeeping from observation into enforcement, shaped debate about intervention for decades, and remains one of the UN's most consequential — and controversial — operations; the UN returned to the Congo with MONUC/MONUSCO in 1999.
