Ensemble forecasting

Ensemble forecasting is a method in numerical weather prediction and other predictive modeling that runs many forecasts from slightly different initial conditions or model formulations. The collection of outcomes estimates uncertainty and the probability of different scenarios.

Rationale

Because the atmosphere is chaotic, small errors in observations can grow rapidly. A single deterministic forecast hides that sensitivity, while an ensemble shows whether members agree on a stable pattern or diverge into competing possibilities.

Methods

Forecast centers perturb initial conditions using techniques such as singular vectors or bred vectors, and may vary model physics or combine models. Products include ensemble means, spread maps, probability forecasts for rain or temperature thresholds, and spaghetti diagrams for cyclone tracks.

Applications

Ensembles are used for severe weather outlooks, tropical cyclone forecasting, river flooding, aviation, and energy planning. Their value is greatest when users interpret probabilities rather than treating the mean as a certain outcome. See also numerical weather prediction and chaos theory.
