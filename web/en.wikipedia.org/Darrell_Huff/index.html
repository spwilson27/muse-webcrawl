Darrell Huff

Born: 15 July 1913, Gowrie, Iowa, US
Died: 27 June 2001, Carmel, California
Notable work: How to Lie with Statistics (1954)

Darrell Huff (15 July 1913 – 27 June 2001) was an American writer, best known as the author of How to Lie with Statistics (1954), one of the best-selling statistics books of the second half of the twentieth century.

Career

Huff was born in Gowrie, Iowa, and graduated from the University of Iowa, where he also earned a master's degree in psychology. He worked as an editor at Better Homes and Gardens and Liberty magazine before becoming a full-time freelance writer. He wrote numerous "how-to" articles and books on topics from home improvement to mathematics.

How to Lie with Statistics

Illustrated by Irving Geis, the short, humorous book explains how statistics can mislead through biased samples, misleading averages, truncated graphs, and spurious precision. It became a standard text in introductory statistics courses and was praised by readers such as Bill Gates. Huff later testified as a witness for the tobacco industry, and his unpublished How to Lie with Smoking Statistics became a point of controversy among historians of statistics.
