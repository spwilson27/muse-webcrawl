Encomienda

The encomienda was a Spanish labor and tribute system in the Americas in which the Crown granted a colonist (encomendero) the right to tribute and labor from a group of Indigenous people, nominally in exchange for protection and Christian instruction.

History

Adapted from institutions of the Reconquista and first applied in the Caribbean, the encomienda spread through Mexico and Peru after the conquests of the Aztec and Inca empires. In practice it often involved forced labor and abuse, contributing to catastrophic Indigenous population decline alongside disease. Critics such as Bartolomé de las Casas prompted the New Laws of 1542, which sought to limit inheritance of encomiendas.

Decline

The system gradually gave way to repartimiento, haciendas, and wage labor, and was formally abolished in most areas during the 18th century.
