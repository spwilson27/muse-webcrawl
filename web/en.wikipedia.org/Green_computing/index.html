Green computing

Energy-efficient data centre design is a central concern of green computing.

Green computing, also called sustainable computing or green IT, is the study and practice of designing, manufacturing, using, and disposing of computers, servers, and associated systems efficiently and with minimal impact on the environment. Its goals include reducing energy consumption and carbon emissions, minimising hazardous materials, extending product lifetimes, and maximising the recyclability of equipment and electronic waste.

Practices

Green computing encompasses power management features such as sleep modes and processor throttling; server virtualisation and consolidation, which raise utilisation of data-centre hardware; efficient cooling and power usage effectiveness (PUE) metrics for facilities; and cloud computing architectures that shift workloads to highly efficient hyperscale data centres. On the consumer side it includes product certifications and regulations such as Energy Star, the EU's Ecodesign rules, RoHS restrictions on hazardous substances, and WEEE rules on electronic waste recycling.

Origins

The term gained currency in the early 1990s, when the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency launched the Energy Star programme in 1992 and sleep modes became common in personal computers. Research under the banner of green computing now spans low-power chip design, algorithmic energy efficiency, carbon-aware scheduling of computation, and the lifecycle assessment of information technology.
