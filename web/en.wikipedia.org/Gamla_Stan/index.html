Gamla Stan (Swedish: [ˈɡâmːla ˈstɑːn], "The Old Town"), until 1980 officially Staden mellan broarna ("The Town between the Bridges"), is the old town of Stockholm, Sweden. Gamla Stan consists primarily of the island Stadsholmen, but also incorporates the surrounding islets of Riddarholmen, Helgeandsholmen, and Strömsborg.

Overview

The old town dates from the 13th century and consists of medieval alleyways, cobbled streets, and archaic architecture influenced by North German Gothic. Around 3,000 people live in Gamla Stan, which is one of the best-preserved medieval city centres in Europe and one of Stockholm's principal tourist attractions.

Notable sites

Gamla Stan is home to Stockholm Palace, the official residence of the Swedish monarch; Stockholm Cathedral (Storkyrkan); the Nobel Prize Museum; and Stortorget, the oldest square in Stockholm, site of the Stockholm Bloodbath of 1520. Other landmarks include the Riddarholmen Church, the burial place of Swedish kings; the House of Nobility (Riddarhuset); and Mårten Trotzigs Gränd, the narrowest street in Stockholm, only 90 centimetres (35 inches) wide at its narrowest point.

History

Stockholm was founded around 1252, traditionally by Birger Jarl, and grew around the castle Tre Kronor on Stadsholmen. The medieval street plan survives in the lanes radiating from Stortorget. Although fires and redevelopment replaced many wooden buildings with stone ones, the old town retained its dense medieval character, and in the twentieth century it was protected as a cultural heritage environment.
