Olbia (locally [ˈɔlbja]; Sardinian: Terranoa) is a city and comune of about 60,000 inhabitants in north-eastern Sardinia, Italy, in the historical region of Gallura. It is the economic centre of this part of the island and is very close to the famous Costa Smeralda tourist area.

History

Olbia is one of the oldest cities in Sardinia, with evidence of Nuragic settlement, and was later a Greek and then Phoenician trading post before becoming a Roman port known as Olbia. In the Middle Ages it was the capital of the Giudicato of Gallura under the name Terranova. The historic centre preserves the Romanesque church of San Simplicio, built in the 11th–12th centuries.

Geography and transport

The city sits at the head of a wide gulf. Olbia Costa Smeralda Airport and a busy ferry port connect it to mainland Italy, making it one of the principal gateways for tourists visiting Sardinia's north-east coast.

Economy

Tourism, maritime trade, and food processing (notably mussels from the gulf) underpin the local economy. The nearby beaches of Pittulongu and Porto Istana attract summer visitors.
