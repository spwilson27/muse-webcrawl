Escape velocity

Trajectories of projectiles launched at increasing speed from a planet

In celestial mechanics, escape velocity or escape speed is the minimum speed needed for an object to escape from contact with or orbit of a primary body, assuming no other forces (such as thrust or aerodynamics) act on it. For a spherically symmetric, massive body, the escape velocity at a given distance is calculated by the formula:

ve = √(2GM / r)

where G is the universal gravitational constant, M the mass of the body, and r the distance from the centre of mass of the body to the object. For Earth, the escape velocity at the surface is about 11.2 km/s (about 40,320 km/h), ignoring atmospheric resistance.

Overview

The existence of escape velocity is a consequence of the conservation of energy and the existence of a gravitational potential well of finite depth. An object given kinetic energy equal to the magnitude of its gravitational potential energy can reach an infinite distance with zero residual speed. The escape velocity from a surface is independent of the mass of the escaping object and of the direction in which it is launched (provided the trajectory does not intersect the body).

Escape velocity and orbits

Escape velocity should not be confused with the speed required to enter orbit. The circular orbital velocity at a given altitude is lower, by a factor of √2, than the escape velocity at the same altitude. An object in a closed orbit never reaches escape velocity; escape occurs only when the trajectory becomes parabolic (at exactly escape velocity) or hyperbolic (above it).

Notable values

  • From the Sun's surface: about 617.5 km/s
  • From Earth's surface: about 11.2 km/s
  • From the Moon's surface: about 2.4 km/s
  • From Mars's surface: about 5.0 km/s
  • From the Solar System, starting at Earth's orbit: about 42.1 km/s relative to the Sun

Isaac Newton illustrated the concept with his "cannonball" thought experiment, in which a projectile fired from a mountaintop with ever greater speed eventually orbits or escapes the planet entirely.
