The Transfăgărășan (trans + Făgăraș), officially numbered DN7C, is a paved mountain road crossing the southern section of the Carpathian Mountains in Romania. It runs through the Făgăraș Mountains, the highest mountains in the country, connecting Transylvania with Wallachia between the towns of Cârțișoara (near Sibiu) and Bascov (near Pitești).

Built as a strategic military route under Nicolae Ceaușescu, the road is now one of Romania's most famous tourist attractions, celebrated for its hairpin bends, long S-curves, and dramatic alpine scenery. The television programme Top Gear famously called it "the best driving road in the world" in 2009.

History

The road was constructed between 1970 and 1974, largely by the Romanian military, in response to the 1968 invasion of Czechoslovakia: Ceaușescu wanted a rapid military route across the mountains in case of a Soviet invasion. The construction used some 6,000 tonnes of dynamite and cost the lives of dozens of soldiers and workers. The road passes the Poenari Castle, associated with Vlad the Impaler, and climbs to Bâlea Lake, a glacier lake at 2,034 m, reached through the 887-metre Bâlea Tunnel—the longest road tunnel in Romania.

Route and conditions

From the north, the road climbs from about 500 m through dense forest to the treeline and the tundra around Bâlea Lake, then descends south past the Vidraru Dam on the Argeș River. Because of heavy snow, the high section is closed for most of the year, typically opening from late June until late October.
