Arithmetic

Arithmetic is the branch of mathematics dealing with the properties and manipulation of numbers, particularly the traditional operations of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. It is the oldest and most elementary part of mathematics, used in tasks ranging from daily counting to advanced science and commerce.

History

Prehistoric tally marks and counting boards precede written numerals. The Egyptians and Babylonians developed practical arithmetic by about 2000 BC; Babylonian scribes computed with a sexagesimal (base-60) positional system and solved problems now classed as elementary algebra. Greek mathematicians such as Euclid and Diophantus raised arithmetic to a theoretical study of number properties — Euclid's Elements books VII–IX develop divisibility and prime numbers, and prove the infinitude of the primes.

The Hindu–Arabic numeral system, with its positional notation and zero, developed in India and reached Europe through Arabic texts such as al-Khwarizmi's treatise On the Calculation with Hindu Numerals (c. 825), transforming practical computation. Decimal fractions were popularized by Simon Stevin in 1585.

Operations and concepts

Arithmetic begins with the natural numbers and the four operations, governed by properties such as commutativity, associativity, and distributivity, and an order of operations. It extends to integers, rational numbers (fractions), and real numbers, and includes exponentiation, roots, ratios, percentages, and logarithms. Number theory, sometimes called "higher arithmetic", studies the deeper properties of the integers.

Foundations and education

In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries arithmetic was given axiomatic foundations, notably the Peano axioms for the natural numbers, and Gödel's incompleteness theorems showed limits to such formal systems. Arithmetic remains a foundation of primary education worldwide and of all computation: digital computers perform arithmetic in binary, as formalized in arithmetic logic units.
