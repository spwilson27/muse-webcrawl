Puebla

Country: Mexico
State: Puebla
Founded: 16 April 1531
Population (metro): about 3.2 million

Puebla (Spanish: [ˈpweβla]; Nahuatl languages: Cuetlaxcoapan), officially Heroica Puebla de Zaragoza, is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Puebla, and the fourth largest city in Mexico. It lies in the Valley of Puebla about 110 km southeast of Mexico City, at an altitude of roughly 2,135 m, in the shadow of the volcanoes Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl. The historic centre, with its grid plan, cathedral and hundreds of colonial buildings faced with colourful talavera tiles, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Puebla was founded in 1531 as a planned Spanish settlement on the route between Mexico City and the port of Veracruz — unusual in being built on a site without a major pre-existing indigenous city, though the great pyramid of nearby Cholula attests to the valley's ancient importance. It became a wealthy centre of agriculture, textiles and pottery. The city is famous for the Battle of Puebla on 5 May 1862, when General Ignacio Zaragoza's outnumbered Mexican forces defeated a French expeditionary army — an event celebrated annually as Cinco de Mayo. Puebla was renamed in Zaragoza's honour.

Culture and cuisine

Puebla is renowned as a gastronomic capital. It is the birthplace of mole poblano, the rich chocolate-and-chilli sauce legendarily created by nuns of the Santa Rosa convent, and of chiles en nogada, poblano chillies stuffed and covered with walnut sauce and pomegranate seeds, whose green, white and red colours echo the Mexican flag. The city is also known for talavera ceramics, ornate baroque churches such as the Rosary Chapel of Santo Domingo, and its large university population.

Economy

Modern Puebla is a major industrial hub: a Volkswagen plant, among the company's largest outside Germany, anchors an automotive cluster, complemented by food processing, textiles and education.
