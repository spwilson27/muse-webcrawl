K Records is an independent record label based in Olympia, Washington, founded in 1982 by Calvin Johnson. Emerging from the cassette culture of the early 1980s, the label became a central institution of American indie rock and the do-it-yourself ethos, championing lo-fi production, amateurism as an aesthetic principle, and a childlike, playful style its founder called "love rock."

History

Johnson, who had worked at KAOS, the community radio station of The Evergreen State College, began K as a cassette-only label distributing tapes by local acts including his own band Beat Happening. The label's distinctive hand-drawn shield logo became a widely recognised symbol of independent music. K expanded into vinyl and CDs while remaining committed to low-cost production and mail-order distribution.

International Pop Underground

In 1991 K organised the International Pop Underground Convention in Olympia, a festival that became a landmark of indie and riot grrrl culture, featuring an opening "Girl Night" and performances by numerous underground acts. Acts associated with the label include Beat Happening, Bikini Kill, Built to Spill (early singles), Modest Mouse (early releases), The Microphones/Mount Eerie, Mirah, Beck (the album One Foot in the Grave), and Kimya Dawson.

Philosophy and influence

K Records promoted the idea that anyone could make music and distribute it outside the corporate industry, an ethos summarised in punk's DIY tradition. Its influence spread through fanzines, cassette trading, and touring networks, helping to define the sound and institutions of indie rock in the Pacific Northwest and beyond.
