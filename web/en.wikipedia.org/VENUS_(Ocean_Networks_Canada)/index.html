VENUS, the Victoria Experimental Network Under the Sea, is a cabled ocean observatory operated by Ocean Networks Canada at the University of Victoria. It consists of instrumented seafloor networks in Saanich Inlet and the Strait of Georgia off the coast of British Columbia, connected by electro-optical cables that supply power and carry data to shore in real time.

Description

The first VENUS node was installed in Saanich Inlet in February 2006 at about 100 metres depth; a second array followed in the Strait of Georgia in 2008, in roughly 170–300 metres of water near the Fraser River delta. Instruments measure temperature, salinity, currents, oxygen, turbidity and pressure, and include hydrophones for marine-mammal and vessel sounds and cameras observing seafloor life. All data are openly available through Ocean Networks Canada's Oceans 3.0 data portal.

Research

VENUS supports studies of estuarine circulation and hypoxia in Saanich Inlet, sediment and turbidity flows at the Fraser delta front (including monitoring relevant to slope stability and the nearby port and ferry infrastructure), ocean acoustics, and the effects of climate change and human activity on coastal ecosystems. Together with the deep-ocean NEPTUNE observatory off Vancouver Island, it forms one of the world's largest cabled ocean observing systems.
