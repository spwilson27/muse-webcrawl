Sikh Dharma

Sikh Dharma, also known historically as Sikh Dharma of the Western Hemisphere and, from 2013, Sikh Dharma International, is a religious organisation that emerged in the United States in the 1970s from the movement founded by Harbhajan Singh Khalsa, better known as Yogi Bhajan. It teaches a form of Sikhism combined with Kundalini Yoga as taught by Yogi Bhajan, and its members — many of them converts of European ancestry — are often called "gora Sikhs" (white Sikhs).

History

Yogi Bhajan arrived in North America in 1968 and founded the 3HO (Healthy, Happy, Holy Organization) in 1969 to teach Kundalini Yoga. Many of his students adopted the Sikh faith, and in 1971 he was given the title Siri Singh Sahib and recognised by the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee (SGPC) as a missionary authority for the Western Hemisphere. The organisation was incorporated as Sikh Dharma in 1973. Headquarters were established in Los Angeles and at Hacienda de Guru Ram Das in Española, New Mexico.

Beliefs and practices

Sikh Dharma followers observe the central practices of Sikhism: belief in one God (Ik Onkar), the authority of the Guru Granth Sahib, daily recitation of banis, keeping kesh (uncut hair) under a turban, wearing white clothing, and participating in sangat and langar. To these, the movement adds Kundalini Yoga, meditation, mantra and a yogic lifestyle drawn from Yogi Bhajan's teachings — a combination that distinguishes it from mainstream Punjabi Sikh practice and has at times been controversial within the wider Sikh community.

Organisation

Sikh Dharma International is based in Española, New Mexico, and is affiliated with other organisations in the wider "family" founded by Yogi Bhajan, including 3HO and the Kundalini Research Institute. It runs ministries, trains Sikh ministers, organises the annual Summer Solstice Sadhana and Winter Solstice gatherings, and is connected with businesses such as Yogi Tea and Akal Security founded by community members.
