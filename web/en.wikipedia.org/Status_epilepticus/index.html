Status epilepticus

Specialty: Neurology, emergency medicine
Symptoms: Prolonged or repeated seizures without recovery of consciousness
Treatment: Benzodiazepines, then levetiracetam, fosphenytoin or phenobarbital

Status epilepticus (SE) is a single epileptic seizure lasting more than five minutes, or two or more seizures within a five-minute period without the person returning to normal between them. It is a life-threatening medical emergency, particularly if treatment is delayed.

Classification

Status epilepticus may be convulsive — involving rhythmic jerking of the limbs and loss of consciousness — or non-convulsive, in which seizure activity is detectable on an electroencephalogram (EEG) without prominent motor signs. Definitions historically used a 30-minute threshold, but the operational definition of five minutes for convulsive seizures is now widely used to guide urgent treatment.

Causes

Causes include withdrawal from or non-compliance with antiseizure medication, stroke, traumatic brain injury, metabolic disturbances, alcohol withdrawal, infections of the central nervous system, and brain tumours.

Treatment

First-line treatment is a benzodiazepine such as lorazepam, diazepam, or midazolam. If seizures continue, second-line agents include intravenous levetiracetam, fosphenytoin, or valproate. Refractory status epilepticus may require anaesthetic infusions and intensive care. Prolonged seizures can cause neuronal injury, so rapid treatment is essential.
