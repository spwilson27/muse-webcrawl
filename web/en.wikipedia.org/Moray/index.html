Moray (Scottish Gaelic: Moireibh or Moireabh) is one of the 32 local government council areas of Scotland. It lies in the north-east of the country, with a coastline on the Moray Firth, and borders Highland Council Area and Aberdeenshire. Its administrative centre is Elgin. The modern council area corresponds broadly to the historic county of Morayshire (Elginshire) together with most of the historic county of Banffshire.

History

The name Moray is first recorded for a province and early medieval kingdom (Gaelic Moireb) whose rulers, including Macbeth (Mac Bethad mac Findláich), sometimes held the kingship of Alba in the 11th century. The region became a sheriffdom and later a county, and was part of the Grampian region from 1975 to 1996 before becoming a unitary council area.

Geography and economy

Moray's landscape ranges from the fertile coastal plain of the Laich o' Moray to the uplands of the Cairngorms fringe. The River Spey flows through the area, and Speyside is world-famous for Scotch whisky distilling, hosting around half of Scotland's malt whisky distilleries, including Glenfiddich and Macallan. Other major industries include food processing (Baxters of Fochabers, Walkers shortbread of Aberlour), fishing at Buckie and Lossiemouth, and the large Royal Air Force station at RAF Lossiemouth and army barracks at Kinloss.

Towns and villages

Principal settlements include Elgin, Forres, Buckie, Lossiemouth, Keith, Aberlour, Dufftown, Rothes, and Fochabers.
