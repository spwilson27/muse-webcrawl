Land of Israel

The Land of Israel (Hebrew: אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל, Eretz Yisra'el) is the traditional Jewish name for the area of the southern Levant. Related biblical, religious and historical English terms include the Holy Land, the Promised Land and Palestine. The term's precise geographical extent varies in biblical and later sources.

Biblical definitions

The Hebrew Bible gives several descriptions of the land promised to the descendants of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Genesis 15 describes a span "from the river of Egypt to the great river, the Euphrates", while Numbers 34 and Ezekiel 47 give more specific boundaries roughly matching the area between the Mediterranean Sea and the Jordan River and beyond. In Jewish tradition the land is central to religious law: many commandments (mitzvot) apply only, or especially, within it.

History

The region was home to the ancient kingdoms of Israel and Judah, and subsequently passed under Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman and Byzantine rule, later becoming part of successive Islamic caliphates, the Crusader states, the Ayyubid and Mamluk sultanates, the Ottoman Empire and the British Mandate for Palestine. The modern State of Israel was established in 1948 in part of this territory.

Usage

"Eretz Yisrael" was used in the British Mandate period as the Hebrew name of Mandatory Palestine (with the initials א״י appended to "Palestine" in official documents), and since 1948 it has continued to carry religious, cultural and political significance.
