AT&T Stadium

AT&T Stadium
Location: Arlington, Texas, United States
Opened: May 27, 2009
Capacity: 80,000 (expandable to ~105,000)
Tenants: Dallas Cowboys (NFL)

AT&T Stadium is a retractable-roof stadium in Arlington, Texas, United States. It serves as the home of the Dallas Cowboys of the National Football League (NFL) and was completed on May 27, 2009. Nicknamed "Jerry World" after Cowboys owner Jerry Jones, it is one of the most expensive sports venues ever built, at a cost of about $1.3 billion, and has the world's largest column-free interior.

Features

The stadium's most famous feature is its enormous center-hung high-definition video board, which stretches from one 20-yard line to the other. The retractable roof and huge glass end-zone doors are inspired by the Cotton Bowl's design, and a pair of 300-foot arches span the length of the structure. With standing-room areas, capacity can exceed 100,000; it set an NFL regular-season attendance record of 105,121 in 2009.

Events

AT&T Stadium hosted Super Bowl XLV in 2011, the 2014 NCAA Final Four, WrestleMania 32 (2016, drawing a claimed 101,763 fans) and WrestleMania 38 (2022), the annual Cotton Bowl Classic, and major boxing and soccer matches. It is scheduled to host matches of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, during which it will temporarily be called "Dallas Stadium."
