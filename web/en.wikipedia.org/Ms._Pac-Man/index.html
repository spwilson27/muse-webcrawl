Ms. Pac-Man is a 1982 maze arcade video game developed by General Computer Corporation and published by Midway in North America. It is the first sequel to Pac-Man (1980) and the first entry in the series not made in-house by Namco.

Gameplay

Gameplay builds on the original: the player guides Ms. Pac-Man through mazes, eating pellets and avoiding the four ghosts — Blinky, Pinky, Inky and Sue (who replaces Clyde). Improvements over Pac-Man include four different mazes, moving bonus fruit, less predictable ghost behaviour, and animated intermissions telling the story of Pac-Man and Ms. Pac-Man's romance.

Development and reception

The game began as an unauthorized enhancement kit called Crazy Otto, created by GCC programmers; Midway, seeking a Pac-Man follow-up, bought the project and, with Namco's approval, reshaped it into Ms. Pac-Man. It became one of the best-selling and most critically acclaimed arcade games of all time, outselling the original in North America, and is frequently cited among the greatest video games ever made.
