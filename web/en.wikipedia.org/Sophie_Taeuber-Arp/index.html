Sophie Henriette Gertrude Taeuber-Arp (19 January 1889 – 13 January 1943) was a Swiss artist, painter, sculptor, textile designer, furniture and interior designer, architect, and dancer, and a central figure of Dada and of geometric abstraction. Working across media with unusual freedom, she is now regarded as one of the most important artists of the European avant-garde.

Life and career

Taeuber studied textile design in St. Gallen and applied arts in Munich and Hamburg. In Zurich during the First World War she taught textile design at the School of Arts and Crafts, took part in the Cabaret Voltaire from 1916, danced in the circle of Rudolf Laban and Mary Wigman, and designed marionettes for a 1918 production of King Stag. She married the Alsatian artist Jean (Hans) Arp in 1922.

In the later 1920s the couple moved to France, where Taeuber-Arp designed the interiors of the Café de l'Aubette in Strasbourg (with Arp and Theo van Doesburg) — a landmark of abstract interior architecture — and built their studio-house in Clamart. She became a leading figure of Concrete art in Paris, editing the journal Plastique and exhibiting with the Cercle et Carré and Abstraction-Création groups.

Work

Her work — from embroidered and woven abstractions, beadwork, and turned-wood "Dada heads" to the playful reliefs and linear drawings of her last years — applies the same rigorous geometric vocabulary across fine and applied art, dissolving the hierarchy between them. Financially, her design and teaching long supported the couple.

Death and legacy

She died accidentally of carbon monoxide poisoning from a faulty stove in Zurich in 1943, while the couple had taken refuge in Switzerland. Long overshadowed by her husband's reputation, she has been re-established by major retrospectives; her portrait appears on the Swiss 50-franc banknote (1995 series).
