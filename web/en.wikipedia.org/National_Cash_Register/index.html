The National Cash Register Company — later NCR Corporation — was an American technology company founded in Dayton, Ohio, in 1884 to manufacture and sell the first mechanical cash registers. Under its longtime president John Henry Patterson, it became one of the dominant business-machine companies of the twentieth century.

History

Saloonkeeper James Ritty patented the "Incorruptible Cashier" in 1879 to stop employees pilfering receipts. Patterson bought the patents and the small company in 1884, renamed it National Cash Register, and built it through aggressive, pioneering sales methods: he is credited with creating the modern sales organisation, complete with training schools, quotas, territories and guaranteed leads. NCR's ornate brass registers became fixtures of shops worldwide.

From registers to computers

NCR expanded into accounting machines, and in the 1950s into electronic computers (the NCR 304, built with General Electric) and business forms, later becoming a major maker of automated teller machines (ATMs) and retail point-of-sale systems. AT&T acquired NCR in 1991 and spun it off again in 1997. In 2007 the company moved its headquarters from Dayton to Georgia. In October 2023 NCR split into two public companies: NCR Voyix (digital commerce) and NCR Atleos (ATMs).
