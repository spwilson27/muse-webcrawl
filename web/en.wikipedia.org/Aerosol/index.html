An aerosol is a suspension of fine solid particles or liquid droplets in air or another gas. Aerosols may be natural — such as fog, clouds, dust and sea spray — or artificial, such as smoke, smog, haze and particulate air pollutants. The term, modelled on "hydrosol", was coined in the 1920s. In everyday language "aerosol" often refers to a pressurised spray can product that delivers its contents as a fine mist.

Atmospheric aerosols scattering sunlight, visible as haze.

Properties

Aerosol particles typically range from a few nanometres to over 100 micrometres in diameter. Their behaviour depends on size: ultrafine particles behave almost like gas molecules, while larger particles settle under gravity. Particle size distributions, concentration and chemical composition determine how aerosols scatter and absorb light, how long they remain airborne, and where they deposit in the human respiratory tract. Liquid droplets in an aerosol are usually spherical; solid particles may be irregular.

Atmospheric aerosols

Natural atmospheric aerosols include sea salt, mineral dust, volcanic sulfate and smoke from wildfires. Human activities add sulfate, nitrate, black carbon and organic particles from fossil-fuel combustion and agriculture. Aerosols influence Earth's climate directly by scattering and absorbing solar radiation and indirectly by acting as cloud condensation nuclei that alter cloud brightness and lifetime. Sulfate aerosols exert a cooling effect that partly offsets greenhouse-gas warming, while black carbon absorbs sunlight and warms the atmosphere. Airborne particles are also a major health concern: fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is linked to cardiovascular and respiratory disease.

Generation and applications

Aerosols are generated deliberately in medicine (inhalers and nebulisers delivering drugs to the lungs), agriculture (pesticide spraying), industry (spray painting and coating) and household products (deodorants, air fresheners, cleaning sprays). The aerosol spray can, developed in the 1940s, uses a liquefied or compressed propellant; chlorofluorocarbon propellants were phased out after they were found to deplete stratospheric ozone. Aerosols also play a central role in disease transmission, as respiratory droplets and droplet nuclei can carry viruses and bacteria through the air.
