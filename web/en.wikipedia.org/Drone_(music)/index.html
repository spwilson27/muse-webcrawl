Drone (music)

In music, a drone is a harmonic or monophonic effect or accompaniment where a note or chord is continuously sounded throughout most or all of a piece. A drone is also the part of certain instruments that plays such a note — for example, the unkeyed pipes of a bagpipe, or the sympathetic and plucked bass strings of instruments such as the tanpura, sitar and hurdy-gurdy.

History and distribution

Drone accompaniment is among the oldest features of music worldwide. In Indian classical music, a continuous drone — usually the tonic and fifth — is supplied by the tanpura or electronic shruti box and forms the tonal foundation over which melody unfolds. In European folk traditions, bagpipes of many regions maintain a constant drone beneath a chanted melody. Medieval organum frequently sustained a long-held tenor note beneath florid upper voices.

Drone as a musical element

Simple melodies played against a fixed drone foreground the changing intervals between melody notes and the drone, giving rise to characteristic harmonic colour without functional chord progression. The drone may be a single note, a fifth, or a fuller chord; it may be emphasised at the start and fade into the background, or remain prominent throughout. In the 20th century, composers such as La Monte Young made the sustained drone itself the substance of entire works.
