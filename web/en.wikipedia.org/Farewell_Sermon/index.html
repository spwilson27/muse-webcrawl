Farewell Sermon

The Farewell Sermon (Arabic: خطبة الوداع, Khutbat al-Wada'), also known as Muhammad's Final Sermon, was delivered by the Islamic prophet Muhammad on the 9th of Dhu al-Hijjah, 10 AH (6 March 632 CE), in the Uranah valley of Mount Arafat near Mecca, during his Farewell Pilgrimage, shortly before his death. It is preserved in several hadith collections and early biographies and is regarded as a summary of the ethical and social teachings of Islam.

Contents

In the sermon Muhammad emphasised the sanctity of life and property, the abolition of usury (riba) and of blood feuds from the pre-Islamic era, fair treatment of women, and the fulfilment of trusts and debts. He declared that no Arab has superiority over a non-Arab, nor a white person over a black person, except by piety — a passage frequently cited as a charter of human equality. He told the assembled believers that he was leaving behind the Qur'an and, in some versions, his Sunnah (example) as guidance, and asked those present to convey his words to those who were absent.

Transmission and significance

Versions of the sermon appear in Sahih Muslim, in the works of Ibn Hisham and al-Tabari, and elsewhere, with some variation in wording and detail. Muslims commemorate its setting annually during the Hajj, when the Day of Arafat rites re-enact the gathering. The Farewell Sermon is widely quoted in discussions of human rights, equality and social justice in Islam.
