Awadhi cuisine

Region: Awadh, Uttar Pradesh, India

Centre: Lucknow

Known for: Dum pukht cooking, kebabs, biryani, kormas

Awadhi cuisine is the cuisine of the Awadh region of northern India, centred on the city of Lucknow in present-day Uttar Pradesh. Developed in the kitchens of the Nawabs of Awadh, it blends Central Asian and Persian influences with Indian techniques, and is closely related to, but distinct from, Mughlai cuisine.

Techniques

The style is famous for dum pukht — slow cooking in a sealed vessel, which allows food to cook in its own steam and juices. Other hallmarks include delicate spicing, the extensive use of saffron, rose water, and kewra (screwpine) essence, and finely minced meat pastes for kebabs.

Signature dishes

  • Galouti kebab — exceptionally soft minced-meat kebabs, legendarily created for an ageing, toothless nawab.
  • Tunday kebab and kakori kebab — celebrated spiced kebabs named for their places or makers.
  • Lucknowi biryani — a fragrant, comparatively mild biryani cooked by the dum method.
  • Shami kebab, kormas, nihari, and rich breads such as sheermal and the layered Lucknowi paratha.
  • Desserts including shahi tukda and kulfi.

Legacy

Lucknow's reputation for refined food is proverbial in South Asia, and Awadhi restaurants and the dum biryani technique have spread across India and the diaspora. The professional cooks of the nawabi courts, the bawarchis and rakabdars, are credited with systematising many of its recipes.
