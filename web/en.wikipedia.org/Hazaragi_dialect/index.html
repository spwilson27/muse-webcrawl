Hazaragi dialect

Hazaragi
Native name: هزارگی (Āzaragī)
Region: Hazarajat, central Afghanistan; diaspora in Pakistan and Iran
Family: Indo-European → Indo-Iranian → Iranian → Western Iranian → Persian → Dari → Hazaragi

Hazaragi (Persian: هزارگی, Āzaragī) is an eastern dialect of Persian spoken by the Hazara people of central Afghanistan (the Hazarajat) and by Hazara communities in Pakistan (notably Quetta) and Iran. It is closely related to Dari, the standard Persian of Afghanistan, but is distinguished by a large stock of loanwords from Mongolic and Turkic languages, reflecting the Hazara people's partial Mongol ancestry.

Features

Hazaragi retains vocabulary and some phonetic features lost in standard Dari, and it includes many words from Mongolian — for example terms for household items, animals, and kinship — along with Turkic borrowings. Estimates suggest a significant fraction of its distinctive vocabulary is of Mongolic or Turkic origin. Speakers and linguists debate whether Hazaragi should be classed as a dialect of Persian/Dari or as a separate language; it is largely mutually intelligible with Dari, and Hazara speakers typically shift toward the standard in formal settings.

Status

Hazaragi is primarily an oral language; written Hazara literature is usually produced in standard Dari or in Urdu among Pakistani Hazaras. In Quetta, Pakistan, Hazaragi has a stronger literary and media presence, with poetry, radio, and publications in the dialect written in the Perso-Arabic script.
