Lisieux

Country: France
Region: Normandy
Department: Calvados

Lisieux is a commune in the Calvados department in the Normandy region in northwestern France, and the capital of the Pays d'Auge. It is one of the oldest dioceses in France and a major pilgrimage centre.

History

Lisieux was the capital of the Lexovii, a Gallic tribe, and an important Roman town known as Noviomagus Lexoviorum. Saint Thérèse of Lisieux, canonised in 1925, lived in the town's Carmelite convent; the vast Basilica of Saint Thérèse, completed in 1954, draws hundreds of thousands of pilgrims each year.

Landmarks

The Cathedral of Saint-Pierre, begun in the 12th century, is an early example of Norman Gothic architecture. The town also preserves half-timbered houses in its historic centre, much of which was rebuilt after heavy bombing in 1944.
