21-gun salute

A 21-gun salute is the most commonly recognised of the customary gun salutes performed by the firing of cannons or artillery as a military honour. The custom originates in naval tradition, in which a warship fired its guns harmlessly out to sea to demonstrate peaceful intent; shore batteries, able to reload faster, could answer with three shots for every one fired by the ship, giving rise to the multiple of seven and, ultimately, twenty-one.

Usage

By the nineteenth century the 21-gun salute had become the standard honour for heads of state, royal occasions and national celebrations. Lower-ranking officials traditionally receive fewer guns, in odd-numbered increments. In the United States, a 21-gun salute is fired for the President, former presidents and visiting heads of state, and at noon on Independence Day and other national observances. Similar salutes mark royal birthdays, coronations and state funerals in the United Kingdom and many other countries.

Related customs

The 21-gun salute is distinct from the three-volley salute fired by a rifle party at military funerals, with which it is often confused. Gun salutes also feature in the ceremonial of many armed forces for occasions such as the opening of legislatures and the arrival of distinguished visitors.
