Miter saw

A miter saw (also mitre saw) is a power saw used to make accurate crosscuts and miter cuts in a workpiece by pulling a mounted circular saw blade down onto the material in a short, controlled motion. Miter saws are widely used in woodworking, carpentry, and framing for cutting molding, trim, and lumber at precise angles.

Types

  • Standard miter saw — the blade pivots left and right to set the miter angle.
  • Compound miter saw — adds blade tilt, so the saw can cut bevels as well as miters; dual-bevel models tilt in both directions.
  • Sliding miter saw — the blade assembly slides on rails, increasing the width of board that can be cut.
  • Laser and LED guide models — project a line showing where the blade will cut.

History

The hand miter box guided hand saws at preset angles long before powered versions. The power miter saw was popularized in the 1970s, notably by manufacturers such as Rockwell/Delta, and compound and sliding variants followed as blade guards, electric brakes, and dust collection improved safety and convenience.

Operation

The workpiece rests against a fence on the saw's table; the operator sets the miter angle (commonly indicated by stops at 0°, 15°, 22.5°, 30°, and 45°) and any bevel, locks the setting, and lowers the spinning blade through the material. Because the blade enters from above and the cut is short, miter saws make cleaner, more repeatable crosscuts than most other portable saws, but they cannot rip boards along the grain.
