Ventral tegmental area

Part ofMidbrain (tegmentum)
NeurotransmitterDopamine (also GABA, glutamate)
Key projectionsNucleus accumbens, prefrontal cortex
FunctionReward, motivation, cognition

The ventral tegmental area (VTA) is a group of neurons in the midbrain tegmentum, close to the substantia nigra. It is the origin of the dopaminergic mesolimbic and mesocortical pathways and plays a central role in reward processing, motivation, and the neural effects of addictive drugs.

Structure and connections

VTA dopamine neurons project principally to the nucleus accumbens and ventral striatum (the mesolimbic pathway) and to the prefrontal cortex (the mesocortical pathway). The area also contains GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons and receives inputs from the lateral habenula, prefrontal cortex, and brainstem nuclei.

Function

VTA neurons fire in response to unexpected rewards and reward-predicting cues, encoding reward prediction error — a signal central to reinforcement learning. Anticipation and consumption of food, social interaction, and other natural rewards engage the VTA, as do drugs of abuse.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of VTA dopamine signalling is implicated in addiction, schizophrenia, depression, and Parkinson's disease. Nearly all addictive substances increase dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens by acting on the VTA, which underlies their reinforcing properties.
