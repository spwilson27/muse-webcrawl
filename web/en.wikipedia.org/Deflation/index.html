Deflation

Deflation
DefinitionGeneral decline in prices for goods and services
OppositeInflation
Measured byConsumer price index (CPI)
Common causesReduced money supply, falling demand, technological progress

Deflation is a decrease in the general price level of goods and services. Deflation occurs when the inflation rate falls below 0% (a negative inflation rate). Inflation reduces the value of currency over time, but deflation increases it, allowing more goods and services to be bought with the same amount of money.

Causes

Deflation can arise from a reduction in the supply of money or credit, an increase in the supply of goods relative to demand, or a fall in aggregate demand. When consumers and businesses expect prices to fall, they may delay purchases, which further depresses demand — a self-reinforcing process sometimes called a deflationary spiral.

Effects

While falling prices benefit consumers in the short term, sustained deflation raises the real value of debt, discourages spending and investment, and can depress wages and employment. Central banks generally seek to avoid deflation, often targeting low positive inflation instead. Economists distinguish harmful deflation associated with economic contraction from "good deflation" driven by productivity growth.

History

Notable deflationary episodes include the Great Depression of the 1930s, when prices in the United States fell by roughly a quarter, and Japan's "Lost Decades" beginning in the 1990s. The economist Irving Fisher developed the debt-deflation theory to explain how falling prices can deepen a downturn.
