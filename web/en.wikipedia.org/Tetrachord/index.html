Tetrachord

[Placeholder image: Tetrachord.png]

In music theory, a tetrachord (Greek: τετράχορδον, 'four strings') is a series of four notes separated by three intervals. In traditional music theory a tetrachord always spanned the interval of a perfect fourth. Ancient Greek music theory divided the octave into two tetrachords joined by a tone; the three genera — diatonic, chromatic, and enharmonic — were defined by the internal divisions of the tetrachord.

Modern usage

Tetrachords remain a tool for analysing scales: a major scale can be seen as two identical tetrachords separated by a whole tone.
