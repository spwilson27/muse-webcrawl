Army of the Andes

The Army of the Andes (Spanish: Ejército de los Andes) was a military force created by the patriot government of the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata and commanded by General José de San Martín. In 1817 it carried out the Crossing of the Andes — one of the most celebrated feats of the Spanish American wars of independence — to liberate Chile from Spanish rule, and afterwards formed the core of the expedition that liberated Peru.

Army of the Andes Active: 1816–1824 (approx.)
Allegiance: United Provinces of the Río de la Plata
Commander: José de San Martín
Key engagements: Crossing of the Andes; Battle of Chacabuco; Battle of Maipú

Formation

San Martín, appointed governor of Cuyo in 1814, built the army at Mendoza from scratch: recruits included Argentine volunteers, Chilean exiles and freed slaves, supported by an extensive logistical organisation producing uniforms, weapons and provisions locally. At its departure in January 1817 the army numbered roughly 4,000–5,000 troops with artillery, engineers and some 10,000 mules and horses.

The crossing and liberation of Chile

The army crossed the Andes by several passes simultaneously, enduring extreme altitude and cold, and descended into Chile in February 1817. It defeated the royalists at the Battle of Chacabuco on 12 February 1817, entering Santiago shortly after. After a setback at Cancha Rayada in March 1818, San Martín's victory at the Battle of Maipú on 5 April 1818 secured Chilean independence. In 1820 the army sailed north as the Liberating Expedition of Peru, proclaiming Peruvian independence in Lima in July 1821.
