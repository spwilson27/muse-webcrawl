Jedermann (play)

Jedermann. Das Spiel vom Sterben des reichen Mannes (Everyman. The play of the rich man's dying) is a play by the Austrian playwright Hugo von Hofmannsthal, first performed in 1911. It is based on several medieval morality plays, including the English Everyman (c. 1495) and the Dutch Elckerlijc.

Plot

God, dissatisfied with mankind's selfishness, sends Death to summon the wealthy Everyman (Jedermann) to judgment. Jedermann, in the midst of a feast with his mistress and companions, begs for a reprieve and seeks companions for the journey. His friends, his servant Mammon, and his wealth all desert him; only his neglected Good Deeds, strengthened by Faith, accompany him to the grave, where he repents and is saved.

Reception and the Salzburg Festival

The play is best known for the open-air production founded by Max Reinhardt on the square in front of Salzburg Cathedral in 1920, which became the signature event of the Salzburg Festival, founded by Hofmannsthal, Reinhardt, and Richard Strauss. The production has been revived almost every year since, interrupted only by the Second World War, and the role of Jedermann is one of the most prestigious in German-language theatre.
