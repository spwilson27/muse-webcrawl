Chengdu J-20

The Chengdu J-20, also known as the Mighty Dragon, is a twin-engine, all-weather stealth fifth-generation fighter aircraft developed by China's Chengdu Aircraft Corporation for the People's Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF).

Development

The J-20 emerged from the J-XX programme of the 1990s. Its first flight took place on 11 January 2011, during a visit to China by the U.S. Secretary of Defense. The aircraft was officially revealed in 2016 and entered service in 2017, making China the second country to field an operational stealth fighter.

Design

The J-20 features a long fuselage with chined forebody, canard foreplanes, diverterless supersonic inlets, and internal weapons bays carrying PL-15 long-range air-to-air missiles. Later variants use the indigenous WS-15 engine, and a two-seat variant, the J-20S, has also flown.

Role

Analysts assess the J-20 as optimised for long-range interception and strikes against high-value airborne assets in the Western Pacific.
