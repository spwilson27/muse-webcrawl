Died3 December 1888, Jena, German Empire

Carl Zeiss (11 September 1816 – 3 December 1888) was a German scientific instrument maker, optician, and businessman who founded the workshop that became Carl Zeiss AG. He is regarded as a pioneering manufacturer of optical instruments, particularly microscopes, and helped transform their production from craft trial-and-error into applied science.

Life and work

Zeiss opened a workshop in Jena in 1846, initially repairing instruments and building simple microscopes. Seeking consistent quality, he began systematic research into lens design and in 1866 took on physicist Ernst Abbe as a research partner. Their collaboration—and Otto Schott's new optical glasses from 1884—made possible scientifically designed microscope objectives, photographic lenses, and other precision optics.

Legacy

After Zeiss's death, Abbe transferred the firm to the Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung (Carl Zeiss Foundation), an early model of employee welfare and corporate social responsibility. The Zeiss name became synonymous with high-quality lenses for cameras, microscopes, binoculars, and planetariums.
