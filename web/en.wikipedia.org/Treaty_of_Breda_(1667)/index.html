Treaty of Breda (1667)

Signed31 July 1667
LocationBreda, Dutch Republic
PartiesEngland; Dutch Republic; France; Denmark–Norway

The Treaty of Breda was signed in the Dutch city of Breda on 31 July 1667 by England, the Dutch Republic, France, and Denmark–Norway. It brought a hasty end to the Second Anglo-Dutch War (1665–1667), negotiations having been hastened by the Dutch Raid on the Medway in June 1667.

Terms

The treaty was largely based on uti possidetis, each side keeping what it held. England retained New Netherland in North America, including New Amsterdam (renamed New York), while the Dutch kept Suriname in South America and the nutmeg-producing island of Run in the East Indies. The English Navigation Acts were softened to allow Dutch ships to carry goods from Germany and the Spanish Netherlands. France returned Acadia to England, while England returned French possessions in the Caribbean.

Significance

The settlement confirmed the Dutch as England's chief commercial rival for another generation and reshaped the colonial map of North and South America, giving England a continuous stretch of territory along the American east coast.
