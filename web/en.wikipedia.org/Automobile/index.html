An automobile (or car) is a motor vehicle with wheels. Most definitions of cars state that they run primarily on roads, seat one to eight people, have four wheels, and mainly transport people rather than cargo. There are around one and a half billion automobiles in use worldwide.

History

The year 1886 is regarded as the birth year of the automobile, when German inventor Karl Benz patented his Benz Patent-Motorwagen. Cars became widely available during the 20th century: the Ford Model T, introduced in 1908 and built on the first moving assembly line for cars from 1913, made motoring affordable to the middle class. Control systems evolved from tiller steering to steering wheels, and starters, brakes, and transmissions grew steadily more sophisticated. The internal combustion engine dominated for a century, but the 21st century has seen rapid growth in hybrid and battery-electric vehicles.

Design and technology

A modern car typically comprises an engine or electric motor, a transmission, suspension, steering, brakes, and an increasingly electronic architecture controlling safety systems, driver assistance, and infotainment. Design balances aerodynamics, crash safety, comfort, cost, and emissions. Fuel sources include petrol (gasoline), diesel, electricity, hydrogen, and biofuels.

Impacts

The automobile reshaped cities, suburbs, and economies, enabling mass mobility and sprawling development while generating road casualties (about 1.2 million deaths per year globally), air pollution, and a major share of transport greenhouse-gas emissions. Governments regulate vehicles through licensing, safety standards, and emissions rules, and many cities now promote public transport, cycling, and electrification as alternatives.
