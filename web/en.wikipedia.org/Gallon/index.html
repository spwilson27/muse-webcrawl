Gallon

The gallon is a unit of volume in the British imperial and United States customary systems of measurement. Two primary definitions are in current use: the imperial gallon, defined as exactly 4.54609 litres, and the US gallon, defined as exactly 3.785411784 litres (231 cubic inches).

History

The gallon originated in England as a measure for wine and ale, with several different gallons in use before standardization. The imperial gallon was defined in 1824 based on the volume of 10 pounds of water at 62°F. The United States retained the older Queen Anne wine gallon of 231 cubic inches.

Usage

The imperial gallon remains official in a few countries but was replaced by the litre in the United Kingdom and Canada for most purposes, though fuel economy is still often quoted in miles per gallon. The US gallon is used for fuel, milk, and other liquids in the United States and some Latin American countries.
