Computer graphics

Computer graphics deals with generating images and art with the aid of computers. It is a core technology in digital photography, film, video games, digital art, cell phone and computer displays, and many specialised applications.

The field divides broadly into two-dimensional graphics, based on raster images and vector shapes, and three-dimensional graphics, in which scenes are modelled as collections of surfaces in virtual space and rendered into images.

Rendering

Rendering converts a scene model into a two-dimensional image. Techniques range from rasterisation, which projects geometric primitives onto the image plane and is used for real-time graphics, to ray tracing and path tracing, which simulate the transport of light for photorealistic results. Graphics processing units (GPUs) accelerate both approaches.

History

The field began in the 1950s and 1960s with systems such as Sketchpad (1963), developed by Ivan Sutherland. Key institutions included the University of Utah, and companies such as Pixar carried 3D graphics into feature film with Toy Story (1995), the first fully computer-animated feature.

Applications

Computer graphics are used in graphical user interfaces, data visualisation, computer-aided design, video games, simulation, medical imaging, and virtual and augmented reality.
