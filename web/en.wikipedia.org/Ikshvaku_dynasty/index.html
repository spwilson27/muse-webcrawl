Ikshvaku dynasty

The Ikshvaku dynasty (Sanskrit: Ikṣvāku vaṃśa) is a legendary dynasty of ancient India, also called the Sūryavaṁśa or Solar dynasty because its kings claimed descent from the sun god Surya through his descendant Ikshvaku. The dynasty is said to have ruled the kingdom of Kosala with its capital at Ayodhya.

Origins

According to Hindu tradition, the dynasty was founded by Ikshvaku, a son of Vaivasvata Manu, the progenitor of mankind. Lists of Ikshvaku kings appear in the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Puranas, and Buddhist and Jain texts. The most celebrated king of the line is Rama, the hero of the Ramayana.

Notable kings

Puranic lists name more than a hundred kings of the dynasty. Among the most prominent are:

  • Ikshvaku — the founder of the dynasty.
  • Sagara — associated with the story of the descent of the Ganges.
  • Bhagiratha — said to have brought the river Ganga to earth.
  • Raghu — ancestor of Rama, from whom the line is also called Raghuvamsha.
  • Dasharatha — king of Ayodhya and father of Rama.
  • Rama — the seventh avatar of Vishnu and hero of the Ramayana.

Historical traditions

Later dynasties in Indian history claimed Ikshvaku descent to legitimize their rule. A historical Ikshvaku dynasty (the Andhra Ikshvakus) ruled in the lower Krishna valley in the 3rd–4th centuries CE, with its capital at Nagarjunakonda. Jain tradition holds that the first Tirthankara, Rishabhanatha, belonged to this lineage, and Buddhist texts connect the Buddha's Shakya clan with the Ikshvaku line through King Okkaka.
