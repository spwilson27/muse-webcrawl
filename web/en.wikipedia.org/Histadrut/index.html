The Histadrut (Hebrew: ההסתדרות, "the Federation"), formally the General Federation of Labour in Israel, is Israel's national trade union centre and the largest voluntary organisation in the country. Founded in December 1920 by 87 delegates in Haifa, during the British Mandate, it united Jewish workers in Palestine and quickly became far more than a union.

Role in the pre-state period

Before 1948 the Histadrut functioned as a quasi-state for the Jewish Yishuv. Through its holding company Hevrat HaOvdim it ran construction firms, agricultural settlements support, a bank (Bank Hapoalim), the Kupat Holim health service, newspapers, theatres and the workers' sports movement. Its defence arm was the Haganah. David Ben-Gurion, its general secretary from 1921, used it as a springboard to national leadership.

After statehood

Within the State of Israel the Histadrut remained a central economic and social power, at its height organising the large majority of workers and operating much of the economy. Reforms from the 1980s onward stripped away most of its non-union functions: the 1995 National Health Insurance Law ended its role running health care, and its industrial and banking holdings were sold or spun off.

Today

The modern Histadrut concentrates on collective bargaining, labour law enforcement and organising previously unorganised sectors, including migrant and hi-tech workers. It remains a major actor in Israeli labour relations and wage negotiations with the government and employers.
