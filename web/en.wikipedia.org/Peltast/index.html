Peltast

A peltast (Greek: πελταστής, peltastēs) was a type of light infantry in ancient Greece, typically armed with several javelins and a light shield called a peltē (πέλτη), from which the troop type took its name. Peltasts originated in Thrace and Paeonia and became increasingly important in Greek warfare during the 5th and 4th centuries BC.

Equipment and tactics

The pelte was a crescent-shaped wicker shield covered with goat or sheep skin, light enough to allow rapid movement. Peltasts fought as skirmishers: they harassed enemy formations with javelin volleys, avoided close combat with heavy hoplites, and exploited rough terrain where phalanxes could not operate effectively. Many also carried a short sword for close fighting when necessary.

Rise to prominence

Peltasts demonstrated their value in the Peloponnesian War, notably at the Battle of Sphacteria (425 BC), where Athenian light troops forced the surrender of Spartan hoplites. In the 4th century BC the Athenian general Iphicrates reformed his peltasts with longer spears and swords and lighter equipment, winning notable victories. Thracian and mercenary peltasts became a standard component of Greek and later Hellenistic armies.
