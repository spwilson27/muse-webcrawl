Mazurka

The mazurka (Polish: mazurek) is a Polish musical form based on folk dances in triple meter, usually at a lively tempo, with the accent characteristically falling on the second or third beat. It originated in the Mazovia region of Poland.

Characteristics

Mazurkas feature dotted rhythms, frequent repetition of phrases, and a tendency to linger on weak beats, giving the dance a proud, somewhat improvisatory character distinct from the waltz, which accents the first beat.

In classical music

Frédéric Chopin composed nearly sixty mazurkas for solo piano, transforming the folk dance into a refined art form that expressed Polish national identity during a period when Poland was partitioned among neighbouring empires. Later composers including Karol Szymanowski also wrote mazurkas. Outside classical music, mazurka dances spread across Europe and the Americas, influencing folk traditions from Ireland to Brazil and the Philippines.
