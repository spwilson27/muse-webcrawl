United Technologies

United Technologies Corporation (UTC) was an American multinational conglomerate headquartered in Farmington, Connecticut. It researched, developed, and manufactured products in numerous areas, including aircraft engines, aerospace systems, HVAC, elevators and escalators, fire and security, building automation, and industrial products. In April 2020, UTC merged with the Raytheon Company to form Raytheon Technologies (now RTX), after spinning off Carrier and Otis as independent companies.

United Technologies was one of the largest American industrial conglomerates.

History

The company's roots lie in the United Aircraft and Transport Corporation, formed in 1929 by William Boeing and Frederick Rentschler, which combined aircraft manufacturing, engines, and airlines. Antitrust legislation in 1934 forced the breakup of the aviation holding companies, producing United Airlines, Boeing, and United Aircraft, the engine and components maker that became the core of UTC. Renamed United Technologies in 1975 under president Harry Gray, it diversified aggressively, acquiring Otis Elevator in 1976 and Carrier in 1979.

Businesses

At its peak, UTC's principal divisions were Pratt & Whitney (aircraft engines), Collins Aerospace (avionics and aerospace systems, enlarged by the 2018 acquisition of Rockwell Collins), Otis (elevators and escalators), and Carrier (heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration). UTC was a major defense contractor and a component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average.
