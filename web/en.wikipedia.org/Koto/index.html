Koto

OriginJapan (derived from Chinese zheng)

RelatedZheng, geomungo, đàn tranh

The koto (Japanese: 箏) is a Japanese plucked half-tube zither and the national instrument of Japan. It is derived from the Chinese zheng and similar to the Korean geomungo and Vietnamese đàn tranh.

Description

The koto is about 180 cm (71 in) long and made from paulownia wood. Its thirteen strings are stretched over movable bridges (ji) along the length of the hollow body, allowing the tuning to be adjusted for each piece. Players use picks (tsume) on the thumb and first two fingers of the right hand, while the left hand presses strings to alter pitch and add ornamentation.

History

Introduced from China in the early Nara period (710–784), the koto was first played as part of the court music ensemble gagaku. In the Edo period, blind musician Yatsuhashi Kengyō (1614–1685) established the foundations of solo koto music (sōkyoku). Twentieth-century innovator Michio Miyagi composed extensively for the instrument and developed the 17-string bass koto.
