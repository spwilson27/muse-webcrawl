Edward Osborne Wilson (10 June 1929 – 26 December 2021) was an American biologist, naturalist, ecologist and entomologist, recognised as a leading authority on ants (myrmecology) and as the founder of sociobiology.

Work

A professor at Harvard University for over forty years, Wilson pioneered the study of chemical communication in ants, island biogeography (with Robert MacArthur, The Theory of Island Biogeography, 1967), and the character displacement and taxon cycle concepts. His book Sociobiology: The New Synthesis (1975) applied evolutionary reasoning to social behaviour across species, including humans, provoking intense controversy. On Human Nature (1978) and The Ants (1990, with Bert Hölldobler) each won the Pulitzer Prize.

Conservation

In later life Wilson became a prominent advocate for biodiversity conservation, popularising the term "biodiversity" and proposing, in Half-Earth (2016), that half the planet's surface be reserved for nature. He founded the E. O. Wilson Biodiversity Foundation.
