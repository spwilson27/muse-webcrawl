Nikola Tesla

Born: 10 July 1856, Smiljan, Austrian Empire
Died: 7 January 1943, New York City
Known for: AC electricity, induction motor, Tesla coil

Nikola Tesla (10 July 1856 – 7 January 1943) was a Serbian-American inventor and electrical engineer best known for his contributions to the design of the modern alternating current (AC) electricity supply system.

Career

Born in the village of Smiljan in present-day Croatia, Tesla emigrated to the United States in 1884 and briefly worked for Thomas Edison before striking out on his own. His patents for the AC induction motor and polyphase system, licensed by Westinghouse, proved decisive in the "war of the currents" against Edison's direct current; AC powered the 1893 Chicago World's Fair and the Niagara Falls hydroelectric plant. He also developed the Tesla coil, experimented with wireless lighting, X-rays, and remote control, and pursued wireless power transmission at his Wardenclyffe Tower, which was never completed.

Later life and legacy

Tesla died alone and in debt in a New York hotel room, but his reputation soared posthumously. The SI unit of magnetic flux density, the tesla, is named for him, as is the electric-car company Tesla, Inc.
