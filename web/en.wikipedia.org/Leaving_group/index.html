Leaving group

In chemistry, a leaving group (LG) is a molecular fragment that departs with a pair of electrons in heterolytic bond cleavage. Leaving groups can be anions, cations, or neutral molecules. Common anionic leaving groups are the halide ions chloride (Cl−), bromide (Br−), and iodide (I−), as well as sulfonate esters such as tosylate (TsO−). The ability of a leaving group to depart is called lability.

Leaving group ability

For a leaving group to leave, it must be able to stabilize the extra electron density that results from bond heterolysis. In general, the weaker the base, the better the leaving group: good leaving groups are typically the conjugate bases of strong acids. Thus halide leaving-group ability increases in the order F− < Cl− < Br− < I−, tracking decreasing basicity.

Common leaving groups

Leaving groupNotes
I−, Br−, Cl−Halides; good leaving groups

Hydroxide (OH−) and alkoxide ions are poor leaving groups because they are strong bases; alcohols are therefore usually converted to alkyl halides or sulfonates before substitution.
