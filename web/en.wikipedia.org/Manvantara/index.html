Manvantara

The manvantaras within one kalpa
Duration:71 chaturyugas = 306,720,000 years
Per kalpa:14 manvantaras
Current:Vaivasvata (7th)

A manvantara, in Hindu cosmology, is a period of time equal to the reign of one Manu — the progenitor of humanity in each cycle of creation. The word is a compound of Manu and antara ("interval" or "period"). According to the Puranas, a manvantara lasts for 71 chaturyugas (great ages), or 306,720,000 solar years, and each is ruled by a different Manu, accompanied by his own Indra, gods, and seven sages (saptarishis).

Structure of cosmic time

Fourteen manvantaras make up one kalpa, a single day of the creator god Brahma (about 4.32 billion years). Between successive manvantaras are transitional periods called sandhya, each lasting as long as a Satya Yuga, during which the earth is said to be submerged in water. At the end of each kalpa comes a night of Brahma of equal length, followed by a new day of creation.

The fourteen Manus

The Manus of the current kalpa (the Śveta-Vārāha Kalpa) include Svayambhuva, Svarocisha, Uttama, Tapasa/Tamasa, Raivata, and Cakshusha as the first six, followed by the present seventh, Vaivasvata Manu — the Manu of the current age and the father of Ikshvaku and of Ila. Seven more Manus (Savarna and others) are yet to come. The story of Vaivasvata and the great flood, in which he is warned by the fish Matsya, is one of the oldest flood narratives in Indian literature.

Sources

The scheme is described in texts such as the Mahabharata, the Manusmriti, and especially the Puranas, including the Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana, which enumerate the Manus, their Indras, and their sages in detail.
