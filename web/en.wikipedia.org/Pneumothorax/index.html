Pneumothorax

Medical condition
Specialty: Pulmonology, thoracic surgery, emergency medicine
Symptoms: Sudden chest pain, shortness of breath
Causes: Spontaneous (ruptured bleb), trauma, underlying lung disease
Treatment: Observation, needle aspiration, chest tube

A pneumothorax is an abnormal collection of air in the pleural space between the lung and the chest wall. Symptoms typically include sudden onset of sharp, one-sided chest pain and shortness of breath. In a minority of cases, a one-way valve is formed by an area of damaged tissue, and the amount of air in the space increases; this is a tension pneumothorax, a medical emergency that can cause dangerously low blood pressure and death if untreated.

Classification

Pneumothoraces are divided into primary spontaneous (occurring without underlying lung disease, often in tall, thin young men, from rupture of small subpleural blebs), secondary spontaneous (in people with lung diseases such as COPD or cystic fibrosis), traumatic, and iatrogenic (following a medical procedure).

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually by chest X-ray, CT scan, or ultrasound; physical examination findings alone can be unreliable. On an upright chest radiograph, the edge of the collapsed lung and absence of lung markings beyond it are the classic findings.

Treatment

Small pneumothoraces may resolve with observation and supplemental oxygen. Larger or symptomatic ones are treated by needle aspiration or a chest tube (thoracostomy) connected to a drainage system. Recurrent cases may require pleurodesis or surgery. Tension pneumothorax requires immediate needle decompression followed by a chest tube.
