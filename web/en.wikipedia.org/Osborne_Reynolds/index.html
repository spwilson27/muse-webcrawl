Osborne Reynolds FRS (23 August 1842 – 21 February 1912) was an Irish-born British engineer and physicist who made foundational contributions to fluid dynamics. He was professor of engineering at Owens College, Manchester (later the University of Manchester), from 1868 to 1905.

Work

Reynolds's most famous experiment, reported in 1883, injected dye into water flowing through glass pipes and showed that flow passes from laminar ("direct") to turbulent ("sinuous") motion above a critical value of a dimensionless parameter — now the Reynolds number, Re = ρvL/μ. His 1895 paper decomposed turbulent flow into mean and fluctuating parts, introducing the Reynolds stresses and the Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) equations still central to engineering turbulence modelling. He also studied lubrication, explaining the pressure generation in journal bearings (1886), the dilatancy of granular materials, and tidal and river modelling with scaled experiments.

Honours

Reynolds was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1877 and awarded its Royal Medal in 1888. The Reynolds number, the Reynolds stress, and a unit of kinematic viscosity once called the "reyn" bear his name.
