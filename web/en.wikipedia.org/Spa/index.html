Spa

A thermal pool at a spa resort

A spa is a location where mineral-rich spring water (and sometimes seawater) is used to give medicinal baths. Spa towns or spa resorts (including hot springs resorts) typically offer various health treatments, which are also known as balneotherapy. The belief in the curative powers of mineral waters goes back to prehistoric times, and such practices have been popular worldwide, but notably in Europe and Japan.

Etymology

The term is derived from the name of the town of Spa, Belgium, whose mineral springs have been known since Roman times. It has sometimes been popularly (but incorrectly) explained as an acronym of the Latin phrase sanitas per aquam, "health through water".

History

Medicinal bathing was practised in ancient Egyptian, Greek and Roman cultures; the Roman baths combined bathing with social, ritual and therapeutic functions. Spa culture revived in early modern Europe at towns such as Spa, Bath, Baden-Baden and Vichy, and reached a height of fashion in the 18th and 19th centuries. In the 20th and 21st centuries, the term expanded to include day spas offering massage, facials and other cosmetic and wellness treatments.

Types

Modern spas include destination spas, resort and hotel spas, day spas, mineral springs spas and medical spas. Treatments commonly offered include hydrotherapy, massage, mud baths, saunas, steam rooms and a range of body and skin treatments.
