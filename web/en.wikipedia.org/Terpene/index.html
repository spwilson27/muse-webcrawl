Terpene

Terpenes are a large and diverse class of natural products consisting of compounds with the formula (C5H8)n, where n is greater than one. They are produced primarily by plants, particularly conifers, and by some insects. Terpenes are often responsible for the aromatic qualities of plants: they are major components of resin, turpentine (from which the name derives), essential oils, and the fragrances of flowers, herbs and cannabis.

Classification

Terpenes are classified by the number of isoprene (C5) units they contain: hemiterpenes (1 unit), monoterpenes (2, e.g. pinene, limonene, menthol), sesquiterpenes (3), diterpenes (4, e.g. the precursors of gibberellins and the anticancer drug paclitaxel's core), sesterterpenes (5), triterpenes (6, e.g. squalene, the precursor of steroids) and tetraterpenes (8, the carotenoids). Modified terpenes containing oxygen or rearranged skeletons are called terpenoids.

Biological role and uses

In plants terpenes serve as defensive compounds against herbivores and pathogens, as attractants for pollinators, and as signalling molecules; the volatile terpenes emitted by forests contribute to the formation of aerosols and the blue haze over mountain ranges. Commercially, terpenes are used as fragrances and flavours, solvents (turpentine), and pharmaceuticals — including the antimalarial artemisinin (a sesquiterpene lactone) and the chemotherapeutic paclitaxel. Rubber is a polyterpene.
