Herbert Giles, sinologist

Herbert Allen Giles (8 December 1845 – 13 February 1935) was a British diplomat and sinologist who was the second professor of Chinese at the University of Cambridge. He modified the romanisation system of Thomas Wade into the Wade–Giles system, which remained the standard transcription of Mandarin Chinese in the English-speaking world for most of the 20th century.

Career

The son of the classical scholar John Allen Giles, Herbert Giles joined the British consular service and served in China from 1867 to 1892, posted to ports including Tianjin, Ningbo and Tamsui in Taiwan. Ill health forced his retirement to England, where he became only the second holder of the Cambridge chair of Chinese (1897–1932), succeeding Wade himself.

Works

Giles produced the Chinese–English Dictionary (1892; revised 1912), for decades the standard work of its kind, and A Chinese Biographical Dictionary (1898). His translations introduced English readers to Chinese literature: Strange Stories from a Chinese Studio (Pu Songling's tales), the Tao Te Ching, the Analects of Confucius, the Zhuangzi, and a widely read History of Chinese Literature (1901). He feuded vigorously with rival translators and was a combative defender of his scholarly positions. His son, Lionel Giles, was also a notable sinologist and translator of Sun Tzu.
