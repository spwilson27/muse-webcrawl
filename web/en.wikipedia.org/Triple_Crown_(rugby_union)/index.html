Triple Crown (rugby union)

Triple Crown
Competition: Six Nations Championship
Contested by: England, Ireland, Scotland, Wales

In rugby union, the Triple Crown is an honour contested within the Six Nations Championship by the four Home Nations — England, Ireland, Scotland and Wales. It is won by a team that defeats all three of the other Home Nations in a single championship season.

History

The term came into use in the late nineteenth century, when the championship involved only the four home teams. For most of its history the Triple Crown was an informal honour with no trophy; a physical trophy, designed by Waterford Crystal, was first awarded in 2006. Ireland, England and Wales have won it most often.

Related honours

A team winning all five of its Six Nations matches achieves a Grand Slam. Matches between individual pairs of nations also carry their own trophies, such as the Calcutta Cup between England and Scotland.
