Epoprostenol

Epoprostenol is a synthetic analogue of prostacyclin (prostaglandin I2, PGI2), a naturally occurring prostaglandin produced by the endothelium. It is a potent vasodilator and inhibitor of platelet aggregation. Sold under brand names including Flolan and Veletri, it is used primarily in the treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH).

Medical use

Epoprostenol was the first therapy shown to improve survival in severe PAH and remains a mainstay for patients with advanced disease. Because it has a very short half-life (about six minutes) and is unstable at room temperature in its original formulation, it must be given by continuous intravenous infusion through a central venous catheter using an ambulatory pump. Abrupt interruption of the infusion can cause life-threatening rebound pulmonary hypertension.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include flushing, headache, jaw pain, diarrhea, nausea, and musculoskeletal aches. Serious risks relate to the delivery system, including catheter-related bloodstream infections and pump malfunction, as well as bleeding due to platelet inhibition.

History

Prostacyclin was discovered in 1976 by a team led by John Vane, work that contributed to the 1982 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Epoprostenol was approved for PAH in the 1990s; more stable formulations and related prostacyclin analogues such as treprostinil and iloprost followed.
