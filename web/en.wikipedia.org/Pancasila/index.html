Pancasila

Pancasila (Indonesian: [pantʃaˈsila]) is the official, foundational philosophical theory of Indonesia. The name derives from the Sanskrit pañca ("five") and śīla ("principles"). It consists of five principles that are held to be interrelated and form the basis of the Indonesian state, intended to unite the country's diverse peoples, religions, and cultures.

The five principles

As stated in the preamble to the 1945 Constitution, the five principles (sila) are: (1) Belief in the One and Only God (Ketuhanan Yang Maha Esa); (2) Just and civilised humanity; (3) The unity of Indonesia; (4) Democracy guided by the inner wisdom in the unanimity arising out of deliberations amongst representatives; and (5) Social justice for all the people of Indonesia. They are symbolised on the national emblem, Garuda Pancasila, by a star, a chain, a banyan tree, a bull's head, and rice and cotton.

History

Sukarno first articulated Pancasila in a speech on 1 June 1945 to the Investigating Committee for Preparatory Work for Independence (BPUPK). The Jakarta Charter of June 1945 included a clause obliging Muslims to follow sharia, which was dropped on 18 August 1945 — the day after independence was proclaimed — to preserve national unity. Under Suharto's New Order, Pancasila was heavily promoted as state ideology, with mandatory indoctrination courses (P4), and was made the sole foundation (asas tunggal) of all organisations. After 1998, its interpretation opened up again, and debates continue over its meaning for pluralism and religious freedom.
