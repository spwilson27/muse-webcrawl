Financial market

A financial market is a market in which people trade financial securities and derivatives at low transaction costs. Securities include stocks and bonds, and precious metals. Financial markets channel funds from savers and investors to businesses, governments, and individuals who need capital, and provide mechanisms for price discovery and risk transfer.

Types of financial markets

Financial markets are commonly divided into:

  • Capital markets, for long-term securities: stock markets, where shares of companies are issued and traded, and bond markets, where debt securities are traded.
  • Money markets, for short-term borrowing and lending, typically under a year, using instruments such as treasury bills and commercial paper.
  • Derivatives markets, for contracts such as futures and options whose value derives from an underlying asset.
  • Foreign exchange markets, where currencies are traded — the largest market in the world by trading volume.
  • Commodity markets, for physical goods such as energy, metals, and agricultural products.

Functions

Beyond raising capital, financial markets allow investors to earn a return, enable risk to be shared and hedged, provide liquidity so assets can be sold quickly, and aggregate information into prices. Primary markets deal in newly issued securities; secondary markets trade existing securities between investors. Markets may be organised exchanges, such as the New York Stock Exchange, or decentralised over-the-counter networks.
