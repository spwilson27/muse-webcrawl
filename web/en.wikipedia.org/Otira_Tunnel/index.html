Otira Tunnel

The Otira Tunnel is a railway tunnel on the Midland Line in the Southern Alps of New Zealand, running for 8.5 kilometres (5.3 miles) between Otira in the west and Arthur's Pass in the east. When it opened on 4 August 1923, it was the longest tunnel in the British Empire.

Construction

Construction began in 1907 and took over 15 years, hampered by wet, unstable rock on the western side and by wartime labour shortages. The tunnel descends on a steep 1-in-33 gradient from Arthur's Pass, which required electrification of the section (at 1,500 V DC) so steam locomotives could avoid suffocating smoke; the electrification was removed in 1997 when more powerful diesel locomotives arrived.

Operation

Coal trains from West Coast mines and the TranzAlpine passenger service pass through the tunnel. Ventilation doors at the Otira portal and extraction fans manage diesel exhaust on the long grade.
