Carbonated water

Carbonated water is water in which carbon dioxide gas has been dissolved under pressure, producing small bubbles and a slightly acidic taste from the carbonic acid formed. It is sold as sparkling water, soda water, club soda, seltzer, and fizzy water, with small differences in mineral content and added salts between products.

History

Naturally carbonated springs were prized for centuries. In the 18th century, Joseph Priestley developed a practical method of infusing water with "fixed air" (carbon dioxide) in 1767, and Johann Jacob Schweppe commercialised carbonated mineral water from 1783, founding the Schweppes company. Artificial carbonation made soda water a staple of pharmacies and bars, and the base for soft drinks.

Types

Seltzer is simply carbonated water with no added minerals. Club soda contains added salts such as sodium bicarbonate or potassium sulfate. Sparkling mineral water is naturally carbonated (or carbonated with gas from the same source) and contains dissolved minerals from its spring. Tonic water is a sweetened, quinine-flavoured variety used in mixed drinks.

Production

Water is chilled and pressurised with CO2 in a carbonator; the gas dissolves, and equilibrium with carbonic acid gives a pH of roughly 3–4. Home carbonation systems allow consumers to carbonate tap water with refillable gas cylinders.
