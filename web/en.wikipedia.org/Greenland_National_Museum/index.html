The Greenland National Museum and Archives (Greenlandic: Nunatta Katersugaasivia Allagaateqarfialu) is the national museum of Greenland, located in the colonial harbour district of Nuuk, the capital. It preserves and exhibits the cultural and natural history of Greenland, from the earliest Palaeo-Inuit cultures to the present day.

History

The museum was founded in the mid-1960s and was one of the first museums in Greenland. It is housed in a complex of historic buildings on the Nuuk waterfront, including a former cooperage and warehouse structures dating to the colonial period. The national archives were formally established in 1982 and later merged administratively with the museum. In 1991 the institution took responsibility for repatriated cultural material returned from Denmark, including a large collection transferred from the National Museum of Denmark under an agreement on the return of Greenlandic cultural heritage.

Collections

The museum's best-known exhibits are the Qilakitsoq mummies, eight remarkably preserved bodies — six women and two children — dating to around 1475, discovered in 1972 in a rock grave near the abandoned settlement of Qilakitsoq in the Uummannaq district. The mummies, preserved by the cold, dry conditions, wear traditional sealskin clothing and provide unique evidence about Inuit life in the fifteenth century.

Other galleries cover the successive waves of Arctic cultures — Independence I, Saqqaq, Dorset, and Thule — as well as Norse settlement, whaling, hunting culture, traditional kayaks and umiaks, dog sledges, and the colonial and modern periods. The museum also administers records of ancient monuments and historic buildings throughout Greenland.
