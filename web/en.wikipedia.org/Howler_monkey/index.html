Howler monkeys (genus Alouatta) are New World monkeys native to the forests of Central and South America. They are famous for their loud howls, produced by an enlarged hyoid bone and modified larynx, which can be heard up to about 5 km away and are among the loudest calls of any land animal.

Description and behaviour

Howlers are stocky, slow-moving canopy dwellers with prehensile tails. Unusually for New World monkeys, they have trichromatic colour vision, an adaptation linked to their heavily folivorous diet: leaves demand long gut retention and much rest, so howlers spend most of the day inactive. Troops of roughly 6–15 animals are led by dominant males; dawn choruses advertise territory and help troops avoid costly confrontations.

Species and conservation

Recognised species include the mantled howler, red howler, black howler and Yucatán black howler. Several are threatened by deforestation and hunting; the brown howler is critically endangered in parts of its range, and yellow fever epizootics have caused severe die-offs.
