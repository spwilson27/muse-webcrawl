2007–2008 financial crisis

Period2007–2008 (effects through 2009 and beyond)
TriggersUS housing bubble; subprime mortgage defaults
Key eventBankruptcy of Lehman Brothers, 15 Sep 2008
ConsequenceGreat Recession

The 2007–2008 financial crisis, or the global financial crisis (GFC), was a severe worldwide economic crisis. It was the most serious financial crisis since the Great Depression. Predatory lending in the form of subprime mortgages to low-income homebuyers and a collapse of the United States housing bubble contributed to the crisis.

Causes

During the early and mid-2000s, US house prices rose rapidly while lending standards fell. Risky mortgage loans were packaged into mortgage-backed securities and collateralised debt obligations sold worldwide. When house prices fell and defaults rose, these securities lost value, exposing banks and investors to enormous losses.

Collapse and response

In 2008 the crisis reached its peak: Bear Stearns was sold under duress, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac were placed in conservatorship, and Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy on 15 September 2008. Governments and central banks responded with bailouts, emergency lending, interest-rate cuts and stimulus measures, including the US Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP).

Aftermath

The crisis triggered the Great Recession, with rising unemployment and foreclosures worldwide, and led to regulatory reforms such as the Dodd–Frank Act in the United States and the Basel III banking standards.
