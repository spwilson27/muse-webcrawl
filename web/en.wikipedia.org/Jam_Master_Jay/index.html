Jam Master Jay

Jason William Mizell
Born: 21 January 1965, Brooklyn, New York
Died: 30 October 2002, Queens, New York
Occupation: DJ, producer

Jam Master Jay (born Jason William Mizell; January 21, 1965 – October 30, 2002) was an American musician and DJ, the turntablist of the influential hip hop group Run-DMC. His scratching, mixing and beat-juggling underpinned the group's stripped-down, rock-inflected sound and helped define the role of the hip hop DJ.

Career

Mizell grew up in Hollis, Queens, and played several instruments before taking up turntables. In 1983 he joined Joseph "Run" Simmons and Darryl "D.M.C." McDaniels to form Run-DMC. The group broke through with singles like "Rock Box" and the Aerosmith collaboration "Walk This Way" (1986), and albums including Raising Hell. Jay also founded the JMJ Records label, which launched the career of Onyx, and mentored a young 50 Cent.

Death and legacy

On 30 October 2002 Mizell was shot and killed in his recording studio in Jamaica, Queens. The murder remained unsolved for nearly two decades; in 2020 two men were indicted, and in 2024 they were convicted of his murder. Run-DMC was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2009, and Jam Master Jay is commemorated by a mural and street naming in Hollis.
