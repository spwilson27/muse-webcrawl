Arusha Declaration

The Arusha Declaration was a statement of Tanzanian socialist policy adopted by the Tanganyika African National Union (TANU) in 1967 under President Julius Nyerere. Announced in the northern town of Arusha on 5 February 1967, it became the defining charter of ujamaa, Nyerere's vision of African socialism.

Contents

The declaration affirmed that Tanzania's development must rely on its own people and resources — agriculture and hard work rather than foreign aid and loans. It laid down a leadership code forbidding party and government leaders from holding shares, directorships, second salaries or houses let for rent, in an effort to prevent the emergence of a privileged political class.

Implementation

Following the declaration, the government nationalised banks, major industries, plantations and import-export trade. The policy was later extended through the compulsory villagisation programme of the 1970s, which moved millions of rural Tanzanians into planned ujamaa villages.

Legacy

Although ujamaa's economic record was widely judged a failure and Tanzania moved toward economic liberalisation in the 1980s, the Arusha Declaration remains a landmark document in the history of African socialism and a symbol of national self-reliance.
