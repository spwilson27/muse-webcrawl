Rivers of Italy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The rivers of Italy are generally shorter and steeper than those of larger European countries, because the peninsula is narrow and divided lengthwise by the Apennine Mountains. Most Italian rivers flow either to the Adriatic Sea or to the Tyrrhenian Sea; a smaller number drain to the Ionian and Ligurian seas.

Principal rivers

The longest Italian river is the Po, 652 kilometres long, which rises in the Cottian Alps and crosses the whole of northern Italy from west to east, fed by Alpine tributaries such as the Ticino, Adda, Oglio, and Mincio, before building a wide delta into the Adriatic. The Adige, at 410 kilometres, is the second longest. The Tiber (405 km), flowing through Rome, is the principal river of central Italy and the longest that flows entirely through the peninsula's Apennine spine to the Tyrrhenian.

Regimes

Alpine rivers have a glacial regime with maximum flow in early summer; Apennine rivers such as the Arno have a rain-fed Mediterranean regime, prone to destructive flooding in autumn — as in the 1966 Arno flood in Florence — and to low summer water. Italian rivers are heavily used for irrigation in the Po Valley, hydroelectricity in the Alps, and water supply; the Po delta and the lagoon rivers of the northeast are of major ecological importance.
