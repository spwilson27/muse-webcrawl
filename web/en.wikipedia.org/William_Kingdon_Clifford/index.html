William Kingdon Clifford

William Kingdon Clifford
Born: 4 May 1845, Exeter, England
Died: 3 March 1879, Madeira
Fields: Mathematics, philosophy
Known for: Clifford algebra; "The Ethics of Belief"

William Kingdon Clifford (4 May 1845 – 3 March 1879) was an English mathematician and philosopher. Building on Hermann Grassmann's work, he introduced what is now termed geometric algebra, a special case of which is known as Clifford algebra, named in his honour. He was also known for his essay "The Ethics of Belief" (1877), a foundational text in the philosophy of evidence.

Mathematics

Clifford studied at King's College London and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he was second wrangler. In 1871 he became professor of applied mathematics at University College London. His work unified quaternions and Grassmann's exterior algebra into the structures now called Clifford algebras, with wide later applications in geometry and physics. He also coined the term "scalar product" and contributed to the theory of biquaternions. Remarkably, Clifford speculated in 1870 that matter might be a manifestation of curvature in space — an idea anticipating aspects of general relativity.

Philosophy

In "The Ethics of Belief," Clifford argued that "it is wrong always, everywhere, and for anyone, to believe anything upon insufficient evidence" — a principle known as Clifford's principle, debated ever since, including in William James's rejoinder "The Will to Believe." Clifford coined the phrase "mind-stuff" for his monist view of consciousness.

Death and legacy

Overwork and illness led Clifford to seek health in the Mediterranean; he died of tuberculosis on Madeira at the age of 33. His posthumous Lectures and Essays and the completion of his Elements of Dynamic by others preserved his influence on British mathematics and philosophy.
