UNCITRAL

The United Nations Commission on International Trade Law (UNCITRAL) is the core legal body of the United Nations system in the field of international trade law. It was established by the UN General Assembly in 1966 and is headquartered in Vienna, Austria, with a regional centre in Seoul, South Korea.

UNCITRAL's mandate is the progressive harmonization and unification of the law of international trade, reducing legal obstacles to international commerce. It prepares conventions, model laws, legislative guides, and rules that states can adopt into their domestic law.

Among its best-known instruments are the United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (CISG, 1980), the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules, the UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration (1985), the Model Law on Electronic Commerce (1996), and the New York Convention's companion texts on recognition of foreign arbitral awards. Its work also covers insolvency, secured transactions, procurement, transport, and online dispute resolution.

The Commission consists of 70 member states elected by the General Assembly, representing the world's principal legal and economic systems, and works through six working groups.
