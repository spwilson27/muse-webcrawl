Flinders Ranges

The Flinders Ranges are the largest mountain range in South Australia, starting about 200 km (125 miles) north of the city of Adelaide. The discontinuous ranges stretch for over 430 km (265 miles) from Port Pirie to Lake Callabonna. The Adnyamathanha people are the Aboriginal custodians of the range; its best-known landmark is Wilpena Pound, a large natural amphitheatre of mountains.

Geology

The ranges consist of sedimentary rocks of the Adelaide Geosyncline, deposited between about 800 and 500 million years ago and folded and uplifted during the Delamerian orogeny. The region is internationally famous for the Ediacara Hills, where some of the earliest known complex multicellular fossils — the Ediacaran biota — were discovered in 1946 by geologist Reg Sprigg.

Flora and fauna

The semi-arid ranges support native cypress pine, mallee, and sugar gum woodland, with wildflower displays after rain. Wildlife includes the yellow-footed rock wallaby, emus, wedge-tailed eagles, and large populations of kangaroos.

Tourism

Much of the central range is protected in the Ikara-Flinders Ranges National Park. The ranges feature in the paintings of Hans Heysen and in Aboriginal Dreaming traditions, and Arkaroola in the north is a noted wilderness sanctuary and astronomy destination.
