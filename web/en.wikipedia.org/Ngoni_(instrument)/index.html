Ngoni (instrument)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ngoni (also written n'goni) is a traditional West African string instrument, a type of spike lute played by griots (in Wolof, jali) among the Bamana, Mandinka, and other peoples of Mali, Guinea, Senegal, and neighbouring countries. It is considered one of the ancestors of the banjo, which developed from similar lutes carried across the Atlantic by enslaved Africans.

Construction

The ngoni's body is carved from a single piece of wood hollowed out like a boat and covered with animal skin, traditionally cow or goat hide. A long neck passes through the body, and strings — historically of gut or hair, today often nylon fishing line — run from tuning pegs on the neck over a bridge on the skin soundboard. Traditional ngonis have four to seven strings. The large donso ngoni, or "hunter's harp", associated with the donso hunter brotherhoods of the Wassoulou region, has six strings and is accompanied by percussion instruments including the karagnan metal scraper.

Modern use

In the late 20th and early 21st centuries, Malian musicians modernised the instrument. Bassekou Kouyaté, leader of the band Ngoni Ba, formed the first ensemble built around ngonis of different sizes — including an electric bass ngoni — bringing the instrument to international audiences and collaborations with artists from other traditions. The smaller, higher-pitched kamale ngoni ("young person's ngoni") became central to Wassoulou popular music. Notable players include Kouyaté, Donso ngoni masters of the hunter tradition, and younger virtuosos who have adapted the instrument to jazz, blues, and fusion contexts.
