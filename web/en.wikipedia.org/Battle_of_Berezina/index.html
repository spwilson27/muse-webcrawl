Battle of Berezina

The Battle of Berezina took place from 26 to 29 November 1812, between Napoleon's Grande Armée, retreating from Moscow after the failed French invasion of Russia, and the Russian armies under Field Marshal Mikhail Kutuzov. The French fought to force a crossing of the Berezina River at Studienka while Russian forces under Chichagov, Wittgenstein and Kutuzov converged to trap them. Although the crossing cost the Grande Armée terrible losses — tens of thousands of combatants and camp followers died or were captured — Napoleon and the core of his army escaped destruction.

Course of the battle

Marshal Oudinot's discovery of a ford at Studienka and a feint towards Borisov misled Chichagov, who moved south and left the crossing weakly guarded. Dutch and French engineers under General Jean Baptiste Eblé built two trestle bridges in freezing water on 26 November. Oudinot and Ney held the west bank against Chichagov while Victor's IX Corps, as rearguard, fought Wittgenstein on the east bank. On the night of 28–29 November Victor crossed and the bridges were burned, stranding thousands of stragglers who were killed or captured by Cossacks. In French, "Bérézina" remains a byword for a disaster.
