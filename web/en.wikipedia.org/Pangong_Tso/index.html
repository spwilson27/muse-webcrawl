Pangong Tso

Pangong Tso or Pangong Lake (Tibetan for "high grassland lake") is an endorheic (landlocked) lake in the Himalayas, situated at an elevation of about 4,225 metres (13,862 ft) on the border between India (Ladakh) and China (Tibet Autonomous Region). It is approximately 134 km long, extending from India into China; roughly one-third of the lake lies in India and two-thirds in China. At its widest point it is about 5 km across, and its waters are saline despite the altitude.

Geography and ecology

The lake is famed for its changing shades of blue and freezes completely in winter despite its salinity. It lies along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) between India and China and forms part of the Indus river basin region. The lake and its surroundings support migratory birds such as bar-headed geese and Brahmini ducks during the summer breeding season, and the area is a proposed Ramsar wetland site. Tourist access from Leh, via the Chang La pass, grew greatly after the lake featured in popular films such as 3 Idiots (2009).

Border disputes

Pangong Tso has been a longstanding friction point in the Sino-Indian border dispute. During the 1962 Sino-Indian War, the area saw fighting around the Sirijap posts. In May–June 2020, clashes between Indian and Chinese troops along the lake's northern bank — around the "Finger" areas — and in the Galwan valley triggered a prolonged military standoff; a disengagement agreement for the Pangong north and south banks was reached in February 2021. The Khurnak Fort area at the lake's eastern end is also disputed.
