3-j symbol

In quantum mechanics, the Wigner 3-j symbol, also called the 3-jm symbol, is a quantity closely related to the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients used in the theory of angular momentum. It was introduced by Eugene Wigner and has a higher degree of symmetry than the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients.

Definition

The 3-j symbol is defined in terms of the Clebsch–Gordan coefficient by a formula involving a phase factor and the square root of 2j3+1. It vanishes unless the magnetic quantum numbers sum to zero and the three angular momenta satisfy the triangle conditions.

Symmetry properties

The 3-j symbol is invariant under even permutations of its columns and acquires a sign depending on the sum of the angular momenta under odd permutations. It also has regge symmetries beyond those of the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, giving it a total of 72 symmetry operations.

Applications

3-j symbols appear in the addition of angular momenta, the Wigner–Eckart theorem, atomic and nuclear spectroscopy, and the evaluation of integrals of spherical harmonics such as the Gaunt coefficients. They are related to the 6-j and 9-j symbols used for coupling more than three angular momenta.
