Beau Sancy

Weight34.98 carats (6.996 g)
CutModified pear double rose cut
OriginIndia (Golconda)
SoldSotheby's Geneva, 2012, for ~$9.7 million

The Beau Sancy is a 34.98-carat modified pear double rose-cut diamond, long one of the most famous gems in European royal collections. It is the smaller companion of the larger Grand Sancy (55.23 carats), now in the Louvre.

History

The diamond was acquired in Constantinople by Nicolas de Harlay, seigneur de Sancy, the French diplomat and gem collector whose name it bears. In 1604 it was sold to Marie de' Medici, queen consort of Henri IV of France, who had it set in her crown for her coronation in 1610. The stone later passed through the hands of the Dutch House of Orange and the Prussian Hohenzollern dynasty, remaining in the Prussian royal family for some 300 years.

2012 sale

In May 2012 the Beau Sancy was auctioned by Sotheby's in Geneva, selling to an anonymous buyer for about $9.7 million (9.57 million Swiss francs with fees reported at the time), roughly double its pre-sale estimate. The sale attracted attention as one of the most important historic diamonds to come to market in decades. See also Sancy and Diamond.
