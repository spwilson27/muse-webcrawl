Sveriges Riksbank, commonly called the Riksbank, is the central bank of Sweden. Founded in 1668, it is the world's oldest central bank and the third oldest bank of any kind still in operation. The Riksbank is an authority under the Riksdag, the Swedish parliament, and is responsible for monetary policy with the objective of maintaining price stability, as well as promoting a safe and efficient payment system.

History

The Riksbank grew out of the failed Stockholms Banco, founded by Johan Palmstruch, which in 1661 issued Europe's first banknotes and collapsed after over-issuing them. In 1668 the estates of the realm took over the bank as the Riksens Ständers Bank, making it accountable to parliament rather than the Crown. It issued the world's first central-bank banknotes and, in 1968, on its tercentenary, endowed the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, commonly known as the Nobel Prize in Economics.

Monetary policy

Since 1993 Sweden has operated with a floating exchange rate and an inflation target of 2 percent, measured by the CPIF. The Executive Board sets the policy rate (repo rate, now called the Riksbank rate). In 2015 the Riksbank became one of the first central banks to cut its policy rate below zero, holding it negative until the end of 2019, and it also undertook quantitative easing through purchases of government bonds. The bank has been a leading investigator of central bank digital currency through its e-krona project.

Governance

The Riksbank is governed by an Executive Board of six members appointed by the General Council, which is in turn appointed by the Riksdag. The Governor chairs the Executive Board; Stefan Ingves served as governor from 2006 to 2022, succeeded by Erik Thedéen in 2023.
