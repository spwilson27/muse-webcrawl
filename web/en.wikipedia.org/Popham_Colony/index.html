Popham Colony

The Popham Colony—also known as the Sagadahoc Colony—was a short-lived English colonial settlement in North America. It was founded in 1607 on the banks of the Kennebec River in what is now Phippsburg, Maine, and abandoned the following year.

History

The colony was sponsored by the Plymouth Company under Chief Justice Sir John Popham. About 120 colonists under George Popham and Raleigh Gilbert built Fort St. George, a storehouse, and dwellings, and constructed the pinnace Virginia, the first English ship built in the Americas. After a harsh winter, the death of George Popham, and news that Gilbert had inherited an estate in England, the surviving colonists returned to England in 1608.

Archaeology

Excavations since the 1990s have uncovered the fort's layout and artifacts, confirming the site and giving the colony, overshadowed by Jamestown, renewed attention.
