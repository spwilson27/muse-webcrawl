Land parcel

A land parcel is a defined area of land that forms the basic unit of ownership recorded in a cadastre or land registry. Parcels are delimited by boundaries, given unique identifiers, and used as the foundation for land administration, taxation, conveyancing, and planning.

Definition and identification

Each parcel is typically described by its surveyed boundaries and assigned an identifier unique within its jurisdiction — an assessor's parcel number (APN) in the United States, a title number in England and Wales, or a cadastral parcel number in continental European systems. Parcels can be created or divided by subdivision, or consolidated by amalgamation, each change recorded in the cadastre. The standard data model for such records is described by the Land Administration Domain Model (ISO 19152).

Uses

Parcels underpin property tax assessment, zoning and land-use planning, agricultural administration (for example, the European Union's Land Parcel Identification System used to administer farm subsidies), utility mapping, and geographic information systems. In many countries the cadastral map is publicly available online, with parcels overlaid on aerial imagery.

Related terms

A "lot" in North American usage usually denotes a parcel within a recorded subdivision plat. In England and Wales the equivalent registered unit is the title, which may comprise one or more parcels. Historical systems of parcel measurement include the hide, the virgate, and the Public Land Survey System's sections and quarter-sections in the United States.
