Thistle is the common name of a group of flowering plants in the family Asteraceae characterized by leaves with sharp prickles on the margins and by flower heads of disk florets, typically purple or pink, surrounded by protective spiny bracts. Well-known thistles belong to genera such as Cirsium, Carduus, and Onopordum.

Ecology

Thistle flowers are rich nectar sources for bees, butterflies, and other pollinators, and their seeds are eaten by birds such as goldfinches. Some species introduced outside their native ranges, such as Canada thistle (Cirsium arvense), have become aggressive agricultural weeds, while others, like milk thistle (Silybum marianum), have a long history of use in herbal medicine.

Symbolism

The thistle is the national emblem of Scotland, traditionally said to have saved a Scottish army when a barefoot invading Norseman stepped on one and cried out in pain. It gives its name to the Order of the Thistle, Scotland's order of chivalry, whose motto is Nemo me impune lacessit — "no one provokes me with impunity."
