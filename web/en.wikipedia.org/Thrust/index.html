Thrust

Thrust is a reaction force described quantitatively by Newton's third law. When a system expels or accelerates mass in one direction, the accelerated mass will cause a force of equal magnitude but opposite direction to be applied to that system. The force applied on a surface in a direction perpendicular to the surface is also called thrust.

Examples

A fixed-wing aircraft generates forward thrust when air is pushed in the direction opposite to flight, by a spinning propeller, a jet engine, or a fan. A rocket is propelled by thrust produced when an enclosed chamber's exhaust gases are expelled through a nozzle; the resulting force propels the rocket in the opposite direction. Similarly, a boat generates thrust when its propeller pushes water backwards.

Formula

For a rocket or jet engine, thrust can be expressed as the product of mass flow rate and exhaust velocity, plus a pressure term: F = ṁve + (pe − p0)Ae, where ṁ is the mass flow rate, ve the exhaust velocity, and the second term accounts for the difference between exhaust and ambient pressure across the nozzle exit area.

Thrust to power

The power needed to generate thrust is proportional to the speed of the vehicle and the thrust produced. For a propeller or jet moving through air, the propulsive power equals thrust multiplied by the true airspeed, which is why jet engines become more efficient at producing power at higher speeds, while propellers deliver more thrust per unit of power at low speeds.

Related concepts

Thrust is measured in newtons in the SI system, or pounds-force in imperial units. Concepts related to thrust include thrust-to-weight ratio, specific impulse, and thrust vectoring, in which the direction of an engine's thrust is deliberately varied to control a vehicle.
