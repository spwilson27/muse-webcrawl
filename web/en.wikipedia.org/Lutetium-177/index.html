Lutetium-177

Lutetium-177 (177Lu)
Half-life: 6.65 days
Decay: beta emission (with gamma photons)
Use: targeted radionuclide therapy

Lutetium-177 (177Lu) is a radioactive isotope of lutetium used in nuclear medicine for targeted radionuclide therapy. Its beta particles travel only a short distance in tissue, delivering radiation to tumours while sparing surrounding healthy tissue, and its gamma emissions allow imaging.

Production

The isotope is produced in nuclear reactors, either by neutron irradiation of enriched ytterbium-176 (the "no-carrier-added" route) or directly of lutetium-176.

Medical uses

Lutetium-177-labelled compounds include 177Lu-dotatate (Lutathera), approved for somatostatin-receptor-positive neuroendocrine tumours, and 177Lu-PSMA-617 (Pluvicto), approved for metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer.
