En-bloc clip

An en-bloc clip is a type of cartridge clip used to load a magazine with a fixed number of cartridges as a single unit. Unlike a stripper clip, which is removed after the cartridges are pushed into the magazine, an en-bloc clip is inserted into the weapon together with its cartridges and remains inside the magazine until the last round is fired, whereupon it is ejected.

Design and use

The en-bloc system was pioneered in the 1880s by Ferdinand Mannlicher for the Austrian Steyr-Mannlicher rifles, which loaded five-round clips. The most famous example is the eight-round en-bloc clip of the American M1 Garand rifle, adopted in the 1930s: the clip is pressed into the rifle's internal magazine from the top, and when the last cartridge is fired the empty clip is automatically ejected with a distinctive metallic "ping". The French Berthier rifles and carbines used three- and later five-round en-bloc clips, and the Italian Carcano used a six-round design.

Advantages and disadvantages

En-bloc clips allow fast reloading and keep cartridges protected and aligned, but a partially spent clip cannot easily be topped up with loose rounds, and the weapon often cannot be loaded at all without its clips. These drawbacks led most post-war designs to favour detachable box magazines or stripper-clip loading instead.
