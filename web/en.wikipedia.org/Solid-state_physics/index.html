Solid-state physics

Solid-state physics
Field of: Condensed matter physics
Key topics: Crystal structure, band theory, semiconductors, superconductivity
Methods: X-ray crystallography, quantum mechanics

Solid-state physics is the study of rigid matter, or solids, through methods such as quantum mechanics, crystallography, electromagnetism and metallurgy. It is the largest branch of condensed matter physics and forms the theoretical basis of materials science and of semiconductor technology.

Crystal structure

Most solids are crystalline: their atoms are arranged in a periodic lattice. X-ray crystallography, pioneered by William Henry Bragg and William Lawrence Bragg, allows the structure of crystals to be determined. Imperfections such as vacancies, dislocations and grain boundaries strongly influence mechanical and electrical properties.

Electronic band theory

Band theory explains why some solids are metals, others semiconductors and others insulators: electrons occupy allowed energy bands separated by forbidden gaps. Doping a semiconductor with small amounts of impurity creates the n-type and p-type materials from which diodes and transistors are built.

Phenomena

The field also covers superconductivity (zero electrical resistance below a critical temperature), magnetism, ferroelectricity, superfluidity in solid helium research, and topological phases of matter.
