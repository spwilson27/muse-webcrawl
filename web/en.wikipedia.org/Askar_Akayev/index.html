Askar Akayevich Akayev (Kyrgyz: Аскар Акаевич Акаев; born 10 November 1944) is a Kyrgyz politician and physicist who served as the first President of Kyrgyzstan from 1990 until his overthrow in the Tulip Revolution of March 2005. He was the only Central Asian leader of his generation to come to power from an academic rather than a Communist Party apparatus background.

Early life and career

Akayev was born in the village of Kyzyl-Bayrak in the Chuy Valley. He studied physics in Leningrad, earning a doctorate in mathematical and theoretical physics, and worked for many years at the Kyrgyz Academy of Sciences, becoming its president in 1989. He specialised in holography and optical information processing and authored numerous scientific works. In 1989 he entered politics as a people's deputy of the USSR, aligning himself with reformist currents around Mikhail Gorbachev.

Presidency

After unrest in 1990 discredited the republic's Communist leadership, the Supreme Soviet of the Kirghiz SSR elected Akayev president in October 1990 as a compromise candidate. When the Soviet Union dissolved, he won Kyrgyzstan's first direct presidential election in October 1991 and was re-elected in 1995 and 2000. Initially praised as Central Asia's most liberal ruler — he promoted market reform, a free press and close ties with the West, and Kyrgyzstan was the first Central Asian state to join the WTO and introduce its own currency, the som, in 1993 — Akayev's rule grew increasingly authoritarian in its later years, marked by constitutional referendums expanding presidential power, pressure on opposition media, and accusations of family corruption and nepotism.

Overthrow and exile

Disputed parliamentary elections in February–March 2005 triggered protests that escalated into the Tulip Revolution; on 24 March 2005 demonstrators stormed the presidential palace in Bishkek and Akayev fled, first to Kazakhstan and then to Moscow, where he formally resigned on 4 April 2005. He has since lived in Russia, returning to academic work as a professor at Moscow State University, researching mathematical modelling of historical and social dynamics (cliodynamics). Kyrgyz authorities have periodically sought to strip him of his former-president immunity and to question him in corruption investigations.
