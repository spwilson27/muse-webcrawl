The Emergency (India)

Period25 June 1975 – 21 March 1977
PresidentFakhruddin Ali Ahmed
Prime MinisterIndira Gandhi
Legal basisArticle 352 of the Constitution of India ("internal disturbance")

In India, "the Emergency" refers to a 21-month period from 1975 to 1977 when Prime Minister Indira Gandhi had a state of emergency declared across the country by President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed under Article 352 of the Constitution, citing "internal disturbance". Officially issued on the night of 25–26 June 1975, the order gave the government sweeping powers: elections were suspended, civil liberties curtailed, the press censored, and most opposition leaders imprisoned. It remains the most controversial episode in the history of independent India.

Background and measures

The proclamation followed a judgment of the Allahabad High Court on 12 June 1975 voiding Gandhi's 1971 election for electoral malpractice, amid mass protest movements led by Jayaprakash Narayan calling for her resignation. Under the Emergency, the Maintenance of Internal Security Act (MISA) was used to detain an estimated 100,000 or more people, including opposition politicians, journalists, and activists. Constitutional amendments, notably the 42nd Amendment (1976), curtailed judicial review. A compulsory mass sterilisation campaign, associated with Sanjay Gandhi, performed millions of vasectomies — many coerced — while slum-clearance drives in Delhi displaced hundreds of thousands.

End and aftermath

Gandhi unexpectedly called elections in January 1977; the opposition Janata Party coalition won a landslide, and the Emergency was formally lifted on 21 March 1977. The subsequent Shah Commission documented extensive abuses. The 44th Amendment (1978) replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion" as the ground for emergency and added safeguards against a recurrence. The episode left a lasting imprint on Indian politics, strengthening civil-liberties jurisprudence and public resistance to authoritarianism.
