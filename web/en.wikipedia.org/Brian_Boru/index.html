Brian Boru (Middle Irish: Brian Bóruma mac Cennétig; c. 941 – 23 April 1014) was an Irish king who ended the centuries-long dominance of the Uí Néill dynasties as High Kings of Ireland. Rising from the small kingdom of Dál gCais in Thomond, he became King of Munster, subdued Leinster, and by about 1002 was recognised as High King, taking the title Imperator Scottorum — "Emperor of the Irish" — in the Book of Armagh.

Rise to power

Brian and his brother Mathgamain campaigned against the Norse of Limerick; after Mathgamain's murder Brian defeated the Limerick Norse and killed their king Ímar in 977. He then overcame the Eóganachta to become King of Munster, and after years of campaigning against Máel Sechnaill mac Domnaill the two agreed to divide Ireland, before Brian's growing power led Máel Sechnaill to acknowledge him as sole High King.

Reign

Brian is credited in tradition with rebuilding churches and monasteries plundered by raiders, restoring roads and bridges, and promoting learning; his epithet Boru (Bóruma) derives from the tribute of cattle exacted from subordinate kingdoms.

Battle of Clontarf and death

In 1013 the King of Leinster and the Norse King of Dublin revolted, drawing Viking allies from abroad. At the Battle of Clontarf on Good Friday, 23 April 1014, Brian's forces won a bloody victory, but Brian — by then in his seventies — was killed in his tent, according to tradition by the fleeing Norseman Brodir. His death, celebrated and embellished in the Cogadh Gaedhel re Gallaibh, became a founding legend of Irish national identity.
