The Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions are a set of necessary conditions for a solution of a nonlinear programming problem to be optimal, generalising the method of Lagrange multipliers to problems with inequality constraints. Given suitable regularity ("constraint qualification") conditions, a local optimum must satisfy: stationarity of the Lagrangian, primal feasibility of the constraints, dual feasibility (non-negative multipliers for inequality constraints), and complementary slackness (each inequality multiplier is zero unless its constraint is active).

History and statement

The conditions appeared first in the 1939 master's thesis of William Karush and were later presented by Harold W. Kuhn and Albert W. Tucker in 1951; they became known as the KKT conditions after Karush's priority was recognised in the 1970s. For a problem of minimising f(x) subject to gi(x) ≤ 0 and hj(x) = 0, one forms the Lagrangian L = f + Σ μi gi + Σ λj hj and requires ∇L = 0, μi ≥ 0, and μi gi(x) = 0 at the optimum.

Sufficient conditions and applications

For convex problems (convex objective and inequality functions, affine equalities), the KKT conditions are also sufficient for a global optimum, making them central to convex optimization, duality theory, and algorithm design. In economics and engineering the multipliers have interpretations as shadow prices, and the conditions underlie the theory of support vector machines in machine learning.
