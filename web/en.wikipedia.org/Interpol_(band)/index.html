Interpol is an American rock band from Manhattan, New York. Formed in 1997, its original line-up consisted of Paul Banks (lead vocals, rhythm guitar), Daniel Kessler (lead guitar, backing vocals), Carlos Dengler (bass guitar, keyboards) and Greg Drudy (drums). Drudy left in 2000 and was replaced by Sam Fogarino; Dengler departed in 2010 after the completion of the band's fourth album.

History

Interpol emerged from the New York scene that also produced the Strokes and Yeah Yeah Yeahs, becoming leading figures of the post-punk revival. Their debut album, Turn On the Bright Lights (2002), released on Matador Records, was critically acclaimed for its atmospheric guitar interplay and Banks's baritone, frequently compared to Joy Division's Ian Curtis. The follow-up Antics (2004) broadened their audience with songs such as "Evil" and "Slow Hands".

Later work

The band moved to Capitol Records for Our Love to Admire (2007), returned to Matador for the self-titled Interpol (2010), and continued as a trio with El Pintor (2014), named an anagram of "Interpol", followed by Marauder (2018) and The Other Side of Make-Believe (2022). Touring bassists and keyboard players, including Brad Truax and Brandon Curtis, have supported the core trio live.

Musical style

Interpol's sound is characterised by staccato, interlocking guitars, Dengler's melodic, driving bass lines, precise drumming and sombre lyrical themes of desire and urban alienation, delivered in a deliberately formal visual style of tailored suits.
