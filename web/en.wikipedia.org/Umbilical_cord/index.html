In placental mammals, the umbilical cord (also called the navel string, birth cord or funiculus umbilicalis) is a conduit between the developing embryo or fetus and the placenta. It carries oxygenated, nutrient-rich blood to the fetus and deoxygenated, nutrient-depleted blood away, and is normally cut after birth, leaving a scar — the navel (belly button).

Structure

The cord normally contains three vessels: two umbilical arteries, which carry deoxygenated blood from the fetus to the placenta, and one umbilical vein, which carries oxygenated blood back to the fetus. The vessels are embedded in a gelatinous connective tissue called Wharton's jelly and covered by a layer of amnion. At full term the cord is typically about 50–60 cm long and 2 cm in diameter, and it often shows a helical twist.

Development

The cord develops from the yolk sac and allantois by about the fifth week of development, replacing the yolk sac as the fetus's nutrient supply. It connects at the fetal abdomen at the point that becomes the navel after birth.

Function and circulation

Unlike the systemic circulation after birth, within the cord the vein carries oxygenated blood and the arteries carry deoxygenated blood. The umbilical vein supplies the fetal liver and, via the ductus venosus, the inferior vena cava; after birth the vessels constrict and the cord is clamped and cut.

Clinical significance

Complications include a single umbilical artery (associated with congenital anomalies), knots, prolapse of the cord during labour, and a nuchal cord wrapped around the fetal neck. Cord blood can be sampled in utero (cordocentesis) and is collected after birth as a source of haematopoietic stem cells for transplantation. Delayed cord clamping of one to three minutes is recommended by many guidelines to improve neonatal iron stores. The dried stump usually falls off within one to three weeks.

In other animals

All placental mammals have an umbilical cord; in many species the mother severs it by chewing after delivery. In marsupials and some other groups the cord is very short and detaches quickly.
