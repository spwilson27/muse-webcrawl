Chole bhature

Chole bhature is a popular dish from the Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent, consisting of chole — a spicy curry of white chickpeas — served with bhature, a deep-fried leavened bread made from refined flour (maida). It is widely eaten as a breakfast or lunch dish across North India and Pakistan and is a staple of roadside eateries (dhabas) and restaurants alike.

Preparation

The chickpeas are soaked overnight and simmered with onions, tomatoes, ginger, garlic, and a spice blend typically including anardana (dried pomegranate seeds), amchur (dried mango powder), garam masala, and tea leaves or amla for a dark colour. The bhature dough, leavened with yeast or fermented with yogurt and baking soda, is rolled out and deep-fried until it puffs into a soft, balloon-like bread.

Serving and variations

The dish is usually accompanied by pickled onions, green chutney, pickles, and sometimes a glass of lassi. Variations include paneer-stuffed bhature and the smaller, crispier kulcha pairing known as chole kulche. In Delhi the dish is an iconic street and restaurant food, and it has spread globally with the Punjabi diaspora.
