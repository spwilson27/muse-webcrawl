Momo (food)

Momo is a type of steamed filled dumpling native to the Himalayan regions of Nepal, Tibet and Bhutan, and popular across South Asia, particularly in Nepal and the Indian states of Sikkim, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Darjeeling and Ladakh. Made from a simple flour dough wrapped around a savoury filling, momos are closely related to Chinese jiaozi, Mongolian buuz and Central Asian manti, and are thought to have spread along Himalayan trade routes.

Preparation

The dough is rolled into small circles and filled with minced meat — traditionally yak or buffalo in Tibet and Nepal, and today commonly chicken, goat or pork — or with vegetables, paneer or cheese. The filling is seasoned with onion, garlic, ginger, cumin and coriander. Momos are folded into half-moon or round purse shapes and steamed in a tiered steamer called a mucktoo. They are typically served with a spicy dipping sauce (achar) made from tomatoes, sesame seeds and chillies, or with a clear soup.

Varieties

Common variations include jhol momo, served swimming in a thin, tangy sesame-tomato soup popular in Kathmandu; kothey, which are pan-fried; fried momo, deep-fried until crisp; C-momo, tossed in a chilli sauce; and tandoori momo, cooked in a clay oven. In Nepal, buffalo ("buff") momo is a staple street food, and momo is often described informally as the country's national dish, although dal bhat holds that title officially for many. The dish has become a fast-food phenomenon in India, where thousands of stalls and chains sell momos with dozens of fillings, including sweet dessert versions.
