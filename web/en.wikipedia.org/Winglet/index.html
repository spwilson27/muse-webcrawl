Winglet

[Image: Winglet]

A winglet is a near-vertical extension at the tip of an aircraft wing that reduces lift-induced drag by weakening the wingtip vortices. Winglets increase the effective aspect ratio of the wing without greatly increasing its span, improving fuel efficiency — commonly by several percent — and are used on airliners, business jets and gliders.

History

The concept dates to early aviation: Frederick Lanchester patented end-plate concepts in 1897, and devices appeared on some interwar aircraft. Modern winglets were developed by NASA engineer Richard T. Whitcomb in the 1970s, whose wind-tunnel and flight tests showed significant drag reductions. They were adopted widely from the 1980s and 1990s onward, appearing on aircraft such as the Boeing 747-400, Airbus A320 family and most subsequent airliners.

Types

Designs include upward-canted winglets, blended winglets that curve smoothly from the wing, wingtip fences extending both above and below the wing, raked wingtips (a planform extension rather than a vertical device), and the split-scimitar and "sharklet" variants marketed by manufacturers. Airbus calls its winglets "sharklets" on the A320neo family, while Boeing's 737 MAX uses a distinctive dual-feather advanced technology winglet.
