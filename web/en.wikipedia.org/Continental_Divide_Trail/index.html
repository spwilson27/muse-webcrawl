The Continental Divide National Scenic Trail (in short the Continental Divide Trail, or CDT) is a United States National Scenic Trail running 3,028 miles (4,873 km) — commonly rounded to about 3,100 miles — between the U.S. borders with Mexico and Canada. It follows the Continental Divide of the Americas along the Rocky Mountains and traverses five U.S. states: Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, Colorado, and New Mexico.

Route

From the Mexican border at the Crazy Cook Monument in New Mexico, the trail crosses desert, the Gila Wilderness, and the San Juan Mountains of Colorado — where it reaches its highest point on Grays Peak — then follows the Wind River Range and Yellowstone country in Wyoming, skirts the Idaho border, and crosses Montana through the Bob Marshall Wilderness to its northern terminus at Waterton Lakes in Glacier National Park. Along the way it passes numerous national parks and wilderness areas.

Thru-hiking

The CDT is regarded as the most rugged of America's three great long-distance trails; together with the Appalachian Trail and Pacific Crest Trail it forms the "Triple Crown of Hiking". Only a few hundred people attempt a thru-hike each year. As of the early 2020s roughly 95% of the trail was complete, with some sections still following roads. The Continental Divide Trail Coalition, founded in 2012, is the principal non-profit advocate for the trail.
