Horizon

The sea horizon at sunset

The horizon is the apparent line that separates the surface of a celestial body (such as the Earth) from its sky. In many locations the true horizon is obscured by trees, buildings, mountains and other terrain, in which case the resulting intersection of Earth and sky is called the visible horizon. When looking at a sea from a shore, the part of the sea closest to the horizon is called the offing.

Distance to the horizon

Because the Earth is curved, the horizon is at a finite distance that depends on the observer's height. For an observer at height h above sea level, the distance to the horizon is approximately 3.57 × √h kilometres, with h in metres. Thus an observer standing at sea level with eyes 1.7 m above the water sees the horizon about 4.7 km away, while from the top of a 100 m tower the horizon is roughly 36 km away. Atmospheric refraction typically extends the optical horizon slightly beyond the geometric value.

Types of horizon

Astronomers distinguish the astronomical horizon, the great circle on the celestial sphere measured from the observer's zenith at 90°, and the apparent or sensible horizon affected by refraction. In art and linear perspective, the horizon line corresponds to the viewer's eye level.
