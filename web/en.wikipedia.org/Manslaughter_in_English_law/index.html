Manslaughter in English law

Jurisdiction: England and Wales
Type: Criminal offence (common law)
Maximum sentence: Life imprisonment (discretionary)

Manslaughter in English law is a common law offence of unlawfully killing another human being without the malice aforethought required for murder. Because it lacks that intent element, manslaughter generally carries a lower degree of culpability than murder, though it remains one of the most serious offences in the criminal law of England and Wales and can attract a discretionary life sentence.

Voluntary manslaughter

Voluntary manslaughter occurs where the defendant kills with the intent required for murder but a partial defence applies which reduces the offence. The recognised partial defences are diminished responsibility, loss of control, and killing in pursuance of a suicide pact, the latter two having been reformed by the Coroners and Justice Act 2009, which replaced the old defence of provocation.

Involuntary manslaughter

Involuntary manslaughter covers killings where the defendant lacked the intent for murder. Its principal forms are unlawful act manslaughter (also called constructive manslaughter), which requires an unlawful and dangerous act causing death; gross negligence manslaughter, based on a duty of care, a breach so serious it amounts to a crime, and a risk of death; and reckless manslaughter. Corporate manslaughter is separately governed by the Corporate Manslaughter and Corporate Homicide Act 2007.

Sentencing

Unlike murder, manslaughter has no mandatory life sentence. Judges have a wide discretion, from suspended sentences in cases of minimal culpability up to life imprisonment for the gravest cases, guided by sentencing guidelines issued by the Sentencing Council.
