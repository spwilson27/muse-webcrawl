BÉPO

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

BÉPO is an ergonomic keyboard layout designed for efficient typing in French. Named after the first four letters on its home letter row, it was developed by a community effort in the 2000s, applying frequency analysis of French text in the same spirit as the Dvorak layout for English.

Design

The layout places the most common French letters (e, a, s, i, n, t, r, l, u, o and accented vowels) on the home row to minimize finger travel, and gives direct or easy access to the accented characters and typographic signs of French — é, è, ê, à, ç, ù, guillemets « », and the like — which are awkward on the traditional French AZERTY layout. It also seeks to keep common programming symbols reasonably accessible.

Adoption

BÉPO drivers are available for major operating systems, and the community maintains layouts and training resources. In 2019, the French national standards body AFNOR published standard NF Z71-300, which defined an improved AZERTY layout and a standardized version of BÉPO, giving the layout official recognition. Its user base remains small compared with AZERTY but is well established among French-speaking ergonomics enthusiasts.

Variants

Several variants exist for different keyboard geometries, including compact and ortholinear keyboards, as well as adaptations for other languages and for one-handed typing.
