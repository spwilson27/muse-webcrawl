Cryptocurrency

A cryptocurrency (colloquially crypto) is a digital currency designed to work through a computer network that is not reliant on any central authority, such as a government or bank, to uphold or maintain it. Individual coin ownership records are stored in a digital ledger — typically a blockchain — which is a computerised database using strong cryptography to secure transaction records, control the creation of additional coins, and verify the transfer of coin ownership.

History

Precursors include David Chaum's eCash (1983) and Wei Dai's b-money proposal (1998). The first decentralised cryptocurrency was Bitcoin, created in 2008 by the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto in a white paper, with the network launching in January 2009. Thousands of alternative coins ("altcoins") followed; Ethereum, launched in 2015, added programmable smart contracts, enabling decentralised finance (DeFi) and non-fungible tokens (NFTs). The total market capitalisation of cryptocurrencies has at times exceeded two trillion US dollars, and in 2021 El Salvador became the first country to adopt Bitcoin as legal tender.

How it works

Transactions are broadcast to a peer-to-peer network and grouped into blocks. Consensus mechanisms order transactions and prevent double-spending: proof of work requires miners to expend computing power solving cryptographic puzzles, while proof of stake selects validators in proportion to coins pledged. Users hold funds in digital wallets controlled by private keys; losing a key generally means losing access permanently. Public keys and addresses provide pseudonymity rather than full anonymity.

Criticism and regulation

Cryptocurrencies are criticised for extreme price volatility, use in fraud and ransomware, speculative bubbles, and — for proof-of-work systems — enormous energy consumption rivalling that of mid-sized countries. Exchanges have failed spectacularly, most notably Mt. Gox (2014) and FTX (2022). Governments have responded with varying mixes of taxation, anti-money-laundering rules, licensing, and outright bans, while central banks explore their own digital currencies (CBDCs) in response.
