Lorentz invariance

Lorentz invariance (or Lorentz symmetry) is the property of physical laws remaining unchanged under Lorentz transformations — the transformations between inertial reference frames in special relativity, comprising rotations and boosts at constant velocity.

It implies that the speed of light is the same for all inertial observers and that experimental results do not depend on the orientation or uniform motion of the laboratory through space. Lorentz invariance is a cornerstone of special and general relativity and of the Standard Model of particle physics. Precision experiments — from Michelson–Morley-type tests to astrophysical observations — have searched for violations motivated by some quantum-gravity proposals, but none have been confirmed.
