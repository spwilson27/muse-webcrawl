Talk box

The talk box (or talkbox) is an effects unit that allows musicians to modify the sound of a musical instrument by shaping the frequency content of the sound and to apply speech sounds (in the same way as singing) onto the sounds of the instrument. Typically, a talk box directs sound from the instrument into the musician's mouth by means of a plastic tube adjacent to a vocal microphone. The musician controls the modification of the instrument's sound by changing the shape of the mouth, "vocalizing" the instrument's output into a microphone.

History

The first known use of a talk box-like device was the "singing guitar" of Alvino Rey, who in 1939 used a throat microphone to modulate his steel guitar. The modern talk box was developed by Doug Forbes and popularized by Bob Heil, whose Heil Talk Box became the standard commercial unit in the 1970s.

Notable recordings

Peter Frampton's use of the talk box on "Show Me the Way" and "Do You Feel Like We Do" (1976) made the effect famous. Joe Walsh used it on "Rocky Mountain Way" (1973), and Roger Troutman of Zapp used a custom-made talk box extensively in funk music.
