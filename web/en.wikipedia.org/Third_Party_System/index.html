Third Party System

The Third Party System is the period of United States political history from about 1854 to the mid-1890s, characterised by intense competition between the Republican and Democratic parties and exceptionally high voter turnout and partisanship.

Features

National issues included the legacy of the Civil War and Reconstruction, tariffs, currency (gold versus free silver), and patronage. Republicans dominated the presidency while Democrats held strength in the South and among urban immigrants. Elections were close; between 1876 and 1892 the popular vote was repeatedly decided by tiny margins.

End

The system collapsed with the election of 1896, when William McKinley's victory over William Jennings Bryan inaugurated the Fourth Party System and a generation of Republican ascendancy.
