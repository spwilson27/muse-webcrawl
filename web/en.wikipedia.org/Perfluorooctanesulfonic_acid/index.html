Perfluorooctanesulfonic acid

Formula: C8HF17O3S
Molar mass: 500.13 g/mol
Appearance: White powder (as salts)
Other names: PFOS, perfluorooctane sulfonate

Perfluorooctanesulfonic acid (PFOS) is an anthropogenic fluorosurfactant and global pollutant. It is a per- and polyfluoroalkyl substance (PFAS) consisting of an eight-carbon perfluorinated chain terminated by a sulfonic acid group. Its salts, particularly the potassium salt, were the key ingredient in the fabric protector Scotchgard and were used in a wide range of industrial and consumer applications, including aqueous film-forming foams.

Properties and uses

The carbon–fluorine bonds in PFOS are among the strongest in organic chemistry, giving the molecule exceptional chemical and thermal stability and both oil- and water-repelling properties. These qualities made it valuable as a surfactant and surface treatment, but also make it extremely resistant to environmental degradation, so it persists in soil and water and bioaccumulates in wildlife and humans.

Health and regulation

PFOS has a long half-life in the human body (several years) and has been associated in epidemiological studies with increased cholesterol, immune suppression, thyroid disruption, and certain cancers. The principal US manufacturer, 3M, voluntarily phased out PFOS production in the United States between 2000 and 2002, and production declined worldwide thereafter. PFOS was added to Annex B of the Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants in 2009, restricting its production and use internationally, and several jurisdictions have set stringent drinking-water limits for PFOS and related compounds.
