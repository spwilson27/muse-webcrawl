Japan Tobacco Inc. (JT) is a Japanese tobacco company headquartered in Tokyo. It is one of the largest tobacco companies in the world, and its international arm, JT International (JTI), based in Geneva, sells brands such as Winston and Camel (outside the United States), Mevius and LD in more than 100 markets. The Japanese government remains its largest shareholder, required by law to hold at least one-third of its shares.

History

Tobacco was a state monopoly in Japan from 1904, administered after 1949 by the Japan Tobacco and Salt Public Corporation. JT was established in 1985 as part of the privatisation of public corporations and listed on the stock exchange in 1994. It expanded internationally by acquiring RJ Reynolds' non-U.S. tobacco business in 1999 and the British company Gallaher Group in 2007, then among the largest overseas acquisitions by a Japanese firm.

Business

Alongside tobacco, JT operates pharmaceutical and processed-food businesses through subsidiaries. Its domestic brands include Mevius (formerly Mild Seven), Seven Stars and Cabin.
