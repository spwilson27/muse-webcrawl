Political Constitution of Mexico

The Constitution of 1917, still in force, emerged from the Mexican Revolution.

The Political Constitution of the United Mexican States (Spanish: Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos) is the current constitution of Mexico. It was drafted in Santiago de Querétaro by a constituent congress during the Mexican Revolution and promulgated on 5 February 1917, replacing the Constitution of 1857.

Key provisions

The constitution was the first in the world to enshrine social rights. Its famous Article 27 vested ownership of land, water, and subsoil resources in the nation, enabling land reform through communal ejidos and the later expropriation of foreign oil companies. Article 123 established labour rights including the eight-hour day, minimum wage, and the right to strike. Article 3 mandated free, secular public education, and Article 130 imposed strict restrictions on the Catholic Church's political and property rights, reflecting the revolutionary state's anticlericalism.

The document establishes a federal presidential republic of 32 federal entities (31 states and Mexico City), with a bicameral Congress and a president elected to a single six-year term (sexenio) with no re-election — a principle summed up in the revolutionary slogan "effective suffrage, no re-election".

Amendments

Although never replaced, the constitution has been amended hundreds of times, with reforms touching electoral institutions, energy policy, education, and human rights, including a major 2011 human-rights reform. Constitution Day is observed in Mexico on 5 February.
