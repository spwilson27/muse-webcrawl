Baum–Welch algorithm

The Baum–Welch algorithm is a method for estimating the unknown parameters of a hidden Markov model (HMM) from observed data. It is a special case of the expectation–maximization (EM) algorithm, and is named after Leonard E. Baum and Lloyd R. Welch, who developed it at the Institute for Defense Analyses in the late 1960s.

Description

Given an observation sequence and a model structure (numbers of hidden states and observable symbols), the algorithm iteratively improves estimates of the initial-state distribution, the state-transition probabilities and the emission probabilities so as to increase the likelihood of the observations.

Forward–backward procedure

Each iteration has two steps. The expectation step uses the forward–backward algorithm to compute, for every time step, the probability of being in each state and of each state-to-state transition, given the current parameters. The maximization step then re-estimates the parameters from these expected counts. Iterations continue until the likelihood converges to a local maximum; different initialisations may lead to different solutions, so multiple restarts are common in practice.

Applications

Baum–Welch training was historically central to speech recognition, and it remains used in bioinformatics (for example profile HMMs for sequence alignment), part-of-speech tagging, cryptanalysis and other sequence-modelling tasks.
