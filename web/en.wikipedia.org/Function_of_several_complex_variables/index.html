Function of several complex variables

The theory of functions of several complex variables is the branch of mathematics dealing with functions f(z₁, z₂, …, zₙ) defined on domains in the n-dimensional complex space ℂⁿ, with n > 1. Such functions are typically studied under the condition of being holomorphic — complex differentiable in each variable separately.

Differences from one variable

Although the theory grows out of classical complex analysis, it differs from it profoundly. By Hartogs's extension theorem, any holomorphic function on a domain in ℂⁿ (n ≥ 2) automatically extends across compact "holes", so isolated singularities of the familiar one-variable type cannot occur. Domains of holomorphy need not be arbitrary open sets, and there is no analogue of the Riemann mapping theorem: most domains in ℂⁿ are not biholomorphically equivalent.

Key concepts

Central notions include holomorphic and meromorphic functions defined by convergent power series in several variables, domains of holomorphy, pseudoconvexity, plurisubharmonic functions, Stein manifolds, and the Cauchy–Riemann equations in several variables. Cauchy's integral formula generalizes using the Bochner–Martinelli kernel.

History

The subject developed through work by Weierstrass, Cousin, Poincaré, Hartogs, and later Cartan, Oka, and Grauert. Kiyoshi Oka's solutions of the Cousin problems and the Levi problem in the 1930s–1940s laid the foundations of the modern theory and led into sheaf theory and complex geometry.

Applications

Several complex variables is used in complex geometry, algebraic geometry, partial differential equations (the ∂̄-problem), and mathematical physics, including quantum field theory.
