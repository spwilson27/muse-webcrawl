Cavalleria rusticana (Italian for "rustic chivalry") is an opera in one act by Pietro Mascagni to an Italian libretto by Giovanni Targioni-Tozzetti and Guido Menasci, adapted from a short story and subsequent play by Giovanni Verga. Premiered in Rome in 1890, it was an immediate sensation and is regarded as the first and defining work of the verismo style in opera.

Synopsis

On Easter morning in a Sicilian village, the young villager Turiddu returns from military service to find his former lover Lola married to the carter Alfio. Turiddu has seduced Santuzza, who pleads with him in vain. Betrayed, Santuzza reveals the affair to Alfio, who challenges Turiddu to a duel according to the Sicilian code of honour. Turiddu bids farewell to his mother and is killed offstage.

Music

The opera is famous for its orchestral Intermezzo, widely used in film and popular culture, including in The Godfather Part III and Raging Bull. Other notable numbers include the Easter Hymn and Turiddu's Siciliana.

Performance tradition

Cavalleria rusticana is almost always performed in a double bill with Ruggero Leoncavallo's Pagliacci, the pairing known as "Cav/Pag".
