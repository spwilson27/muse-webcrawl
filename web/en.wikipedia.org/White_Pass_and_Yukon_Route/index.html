White Pass and Yukon Route
Locale: Alaska, British Columbia, and Yukon
Termini: Skagway, Alaska — Whitehorse, Yukon
Built: 1898–1900
Gauge: 3 ft (914 mm) narrow gauge
Length: 107.5 miles (173 km) as built

The White Pass and Yukon Route (WP&Y, WP&YR) is a Canadian and U.S. Class II narrow-gauge railroad linking the port of Skagway, Alaska, with Whitehorse, the capital of Yukon. Built during the Klondike Gold Rush of 1898–1900, it climbs nearly 2,900 feet in 20 miles over the White Pass summit, with steep grades, cliff-hanging curves, trestles, and tunnels through the Coast Mountains.

History and present day

The railway reached Whitehorse in July 1900, just as the gold rush waned; it survived by carrying freight — including wartime traffic and later lead-zinc ore and container traffic — until it suspended operations in 1982 when metal prices collapsed. It reopened in 1988 as a heritage excursion railway for cruise-ship tourists at Skagway, running summer trains to the summit and into Canada, and became one of Alaska's most popular visitor attractions, carrying several hundred thousand passengers a season. In 2018 Carnival Corporation acquired the railway; the line is designated an International Historic Civil Engineering Landmark.
