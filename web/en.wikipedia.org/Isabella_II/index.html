Isabella II (Spanish: Isabel II; 10 October 1830 – 9 April 1904) was Queen of Spain from 1833 until her deposition in 1868. Her accession as an infant under the Pragmatic Sanction of 1830, which set aside Salic law, was contested by her uncle Don Carlos and triggered the Carlist Wars.

Reign

Isabella succeeded her father Ferdinand VII at the age of two. Her mother, Maria Christina, acted as regent until 1840, followed by General Espartero until Isabella was declared of age in 1843, aged thirteen. In 1846 she was married to her cousin Francisco de Asís, Duke of Cádiz, in a match arranged amid great-power rivalry; the marriage was unhappy and her private life became a source of scandal.

Her reign was marked by chronic political instability, the alternation of Moderate and Progressive governments, repeated military pronunciamientos, and wars including the Hispano-Moroccan War (1859–60) and the Chincha Islands War. Although Spain industrialised and built railways during this period, the monarchy's prestige steadily declined.

Deposition and exile

In September 1868 the "Glorious Revolution" forced Isabella into exile in France. She formally abdicated in 1870 in favour of her son, who was restored to the throne as Alfonso XII in 1874. Isabella lived mostly in Paris, at the Palacio Castilla, made only occasional visits to Spain, and died there in 1904.
