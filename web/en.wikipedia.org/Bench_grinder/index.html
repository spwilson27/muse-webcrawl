TypeStationary power tool
FunctionGrinding, sharpening, shaping metal
AbrasivesAluminium oxide, silicon carbide wheels; wire and buffing wheels

A typical double-ended bench grinder.

A bench grinder is a benchtop machine tool used to drive abrasive wheels for grinding, sharpening, deburring and shaping metal tools and parts. It typically consists of an electric motor with a grinding wheel mounted on each end of its shaft, the whole unit bolted to a workbench.

Description

Standard grinders carry two wheels of different grits—commonly a coarse wheel for rapid material removal and a finer wheel for finishing. Adjustable tool rests support the workpiece at a set angle, and transparent spark guards protect the operator's eyes. Wheels may be replaced with wire brushes for cleaning and rust removal or with buffing wheels for polishing.

Safety

Grinding wheels must be "ring-tested" and rated for the machine's speed, correctly dressed, and used with eye protection; a bursting wheel is a serious hazard. The gap between wheel and tool rest is kept small (about 3 mm) to prevent work being drawn in.
