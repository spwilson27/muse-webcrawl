Konrad Zuse


Konrad Ernst Otto Zuse (22 June 1910 – 18 December 1995), German engineer and computer pioneer.

Konrad Ernst Otto Zuse (22 June 1910 – 18 December 1995) was a German civil engineer, pioneering computer scientist, inventor and businessman. He is widely regarded as the creator of the first programmable, fully automatic digital computer.

Computers

Working largely in isolation in Berlin, Zuse built the mechanical Z1 (1938), the relay-based Z2, and in 1941 the Z3 — the world's first working programmable, fully automatic digital computer, using binary floating-point arithmetic and electromechanical relays, with programs stored on punched film. His Z4, completed in 1945, was delivered to ETH Zurich in 1950 and was for a time the only working digital computer in continental Europe.

Between 1942 and 1946 Zuse also developed the Plankalkül ("plan calculus"), one of the first high-level programming languages, though it remained unpublished for decades. His company, Zuse KG, founded in 1949, produced a series of computers and was eventually absorbed by Siemens.

Later life

In later life Zuse pursued painting and developed ideas about a computing universe in his book Rechnender Raum (1969; "Calculating Space"), an early statement of digital physics. He received numerous honours, and a replica of the Z1 is displayed in the German Museum of Technology in Berlin.
