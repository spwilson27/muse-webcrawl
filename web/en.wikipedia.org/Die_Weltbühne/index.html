Die Weltbühne

Die Weltbühne (The World Stage) was a German weekly magazine for politics, art, and economics, founded in Berlin in 1905 by Siegfried Jacobsohn as a theatre journal (originally Die Schaubühne) and renamed in 1918. It became one of the most important forums of the radical democratic and pacifist left during the Weimar Republic.

History

After Jacobsohn's death in 1926, the magazine was edited by Kurt Tucholsky and then by Carl von Ossietzky. Its contributors included many of Germany's leading left-wing intellectuals and satirists. The journal's fierce criticism of the Reichswehr's secret rearmament led to the famous Weltbühne trial of 1931, in which Ossietzky was convicted of treason.

Suppression and exile

The magazine was banned in March 1933 shortly after the Nazi seizure of power; exile editions appeared in Prague, Vienna, Zurich, and Paris under the title Die neue Weltbühne until 1939. Ossietzky, arrested by the Nazis, was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize for 1935 and died in 1938 from the effects of his imprisonment.
