Protein structure prediction

Protein structure prediction is the inference of the three-dimensional structure of a protein from its amino acid sequence. Because a protein's structure determines its function, predicting structure computationally bridges the gap between the vast number of known sequences and the much smaller number of experimentally determined structures.

Methods

  • Homology (comparative) modelling — builds a model from the known structure of an evolutionarily related protein
  • Threading (fold recognition) — fits a sequence onto known folds
  • Ab initio / de novo methods — assemble structures from fragments and physical principles, e.g. Rosetta
  • Deep learning — neural networks trained on known structures and sequence alignments

CASP and AlphaFold

Progress is benchmarked in the biennial CASP experiments (Critical Assessment of Structure Prediction), begun in 1994. At CASP14 in 2020, DeepMind's AlphaFold 2 achieved accuracy comparable to experimental methods for most targets, widely described as solving the protein folding problem. Predicted structures for over 200 million proteins were subsequently released in the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database.

Applications

Accurate models aid drug discovery, enzyme engineering, and the interpretation of genetic variants. Predictions are less reliable for disordered regions, large complexes, and structures dependent on ligands or membranes.
