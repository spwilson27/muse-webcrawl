Government of China

The government of the People's Republic of China is based on a system of people's congresses within the parameters of a unitary state under the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), in which the National People's Congress (NPC) is formally the highest organ of state power. In practice, authority is concentrated in the CCP's Politburo Standing Committee, and the party's general secretary — who typically also serves as president of the People's Republic and chairman of the Central Military Commission — is the paramount leader.

Constitutional framework

Under the Constitution of 1982 (as amended), state organs include the NPC and its Standing Committee (legislature), the President (head of state), the State Council (the executive, headed by the Premier), the Central Military Commission, the National Supervisory Commission, the Supreme People's Court, and the Supreme People's Procuratorate. The system operates under the principle of democratic centralism, and since 2018 the constitution names CCP leadership as the defining feature of the political system; the same amendment removed presidential term limits.

Territorial administration

China is divided into 23 provinces (including Taiwan, claimed but not administered), five autonomous regions, four direct-controlled municipalities, and two special administrative regions, Hong Kong and Macau, governed under the "one country, two systems" principle. Below the provincial level are prefectures, counties, and townships, each with a people's government and people's congress operating in parallel with party committees.
