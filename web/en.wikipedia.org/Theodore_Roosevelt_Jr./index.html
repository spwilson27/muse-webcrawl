Theodore Roosevelt III (September 13, 1887 – July 12, 1944), known as Theodore Roosevelt Jr., was an American government, business, and military leader. He was the eldest son of President Theodore Roosevelt and First Lady Edith Roosevelt. He served as Assistant Secretary of the Navy, Governor of Puerto Rico, and Governor-General of the Philippines. In World War II he became the only general to land with the first wave on D-Day, at Utah Beach, and was awarded the Medal of Honor for his actions that day.

Early life and World War I

Educated at Groton and Harvard, Roosevelt worked in business before serving with distinction in the First World War as a battalion and regimental commander in the 1st Division; he was gassed and wounded, and received the Distinguished Service Cross. He helped found the American Legion in 1919. As Assistant Secretary of the Navy (1921–1924) he supervised the allocation of petroleum leases that became entangled in the Teapot Dome scandal, though he was cleared of wrongdoing. He served as Governor of Puerto Rico (1929–1932) and Governor-General of the Philippines (1932–1933), and ran unsuccessfully for Governor of New York in 1924 against Al Smith.

World War II

Returning to active duty in 1941, Roosevelt commanded the 26th Infantry Regiment and served as assistant division commander of the 1st Infantry Division in North Africa and Sicily. Transferred to the 4th Infantry Division, he repeatedly petitioned to accompany the first assault wave at Normandy. On 6 June 1944, landing with the first wave at Utah Beach and finding the landing had drifted off course, he coolly directed traffic under fire, walking the beach with a cane and a pistol, and declared, "We'll start the war from right here!" His leadership earned the Medal of Honor. He died of a heart attack in Normandy five weeks later, on 12 July 1944, and is buried at the Normandy American Cemetery beside his brother Quentin, who was killed in World War I.
