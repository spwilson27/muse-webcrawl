The Airbus A380 is a very large wide-body airliner developed and produced by Airbus. It is the world's largest passenger airliner and the only full-length double-deck jet airliner. Studies began in 1988 and the programme was formally launched in 2000 as the A3XX, intended to challenge the Boeing 747's dominance in the high-capacity market.

Development and service

The A380 made its first flight on 27 April 2005 from Toulouse, France, and entered commercial service on 25 October 2007 with Singapore Airlines on the Singapore–Sydney route. Major operators have included Emirates (the largest customer, with 123 ordered), Singapore Airlines, Lufthansa, Qantas, British Airways and Air France (retired).

Design

The A380-800 measures 72.7 m long with a wingspan of 79.8 m and is powered by four Engine Alliance GP7200 or Rolls-Royce Trent 900 engines. Its two full passenger decks allow amenities such as onboard showers and bars in premium configurations on some airlines.

End of production

Airlines increasingly favoured more fuel-efficient twin-engine aircraft for point-to-point routes. After Emirates reduced its orders, Airbus announced in February 2019 that A380 production would end; the final aircraft was delivered to Emirates in December 2021. Many A380s were retired during the COVID-19 pandemic, though several airlines later returned the type to service on high-demand routes.
