Stroke

Stroke
SynonymsCerebrovascular accident
TypesIschemic (87%), hemorrhagic
SymptomsFace drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty, sudden confusion
Risk factorsHypertension, smoking, diabetes, atrial fibrillation

A stroke is a medical condition in which poor blood flow to the brain causes cell death. It is a leading cause of death and disability worldwide. Rapid treatment is critical — summarised by the FAST mnemonic: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services.

Types and Causes

Ischemic strokes result from a blocked artery, usually a blood clot from atherosclerosis or an embolus from the heart in atrial fibrillation. Hemorrhagic strokes result from a ruptured vessel, often due to hypertension or aneurysm. A transient ischemic attack (TIA) causes brief symptoms without permanent infarction but warns of future stroke.

Treatment and Prevention

Acute ischemic stroke may be treated with thrombolytic drugs (such as alteplase) within hours, or mechanical thrombectomy for large-vessel occlusion. Hemorrhagic stroke may require surgery and blood-pressure control. Prevention includes controlling hypertension, anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation, statins, smoking cessation, and antiplatelet therapy.

Rehabilitation

Survivors often need rehabilitation for movement of the skeleton and muscles, speech, and cognition; early multidisciplinary rehab improves outcomes.
