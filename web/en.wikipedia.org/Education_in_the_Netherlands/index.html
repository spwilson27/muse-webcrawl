Education in the Netherlands

Education in the Netherlands is characterized by a division: education is oriented toward the needs and background of the pupil, and the system is divided into schools for different age groups and ability levels. Education is compulsory from ages 5 to 16, with a qualification requirement until age 18. Schools are funded by the government and may be public or "special" (based on a religious or pedagogical philosophy, such as Catholic, Protestant, Montessori, or Steiner schools), all financed on equal terms under the constitutional freedom of education.

Primary education

Primary school (basisschool) runs for eight grades, from age 4 or 5 to about 12. In the final year, pupils take the CITO end-of-primary test, whose result, together with the teacher's advice, determines the recommended level of secondary education.

Secondary education

Secondary education is streamed into three main types: VMBO (four years, pre-vocational, with theoretical and practical pathways), HAVO (five years, senior general secondary, preparing for higher professional education), and VWO (six years, pre-university, including the gymnasium with Latin and Greek). Placement at around age 12 makes the Dutch system one of the earliest-tracking in Europe, though pathways between levels exist and later mobility is possible.

Vocational and higher education

Secondary vocational education (MBO) provides practical training at four levels. Higher education is binary: research universities (WO) — thirteen public institutions including Amsterdam, Utrecht, Leiden (founded 1575), and Groningen — offer academic degrees on the Bologna bachelor's/master's structure, while universities of applied sciences (hogescholen, HBO) provide professionally oriented programmes. Statutory tuition is charged but modest, and students receive loans through DUO.

International standing

Dutch pupils perform above the OECD average in international comparisons such as PISA, and the Netherlands is a leading destination for English-taught degree programmes, with a large share of master's programmes offered in English.
