Injera

Injera (Amharic: እንጀራ) is a sour fermented pancake-like flatbread with a slightly spongy texture, traditionally made of teff flour. It is the national dish of Ethiopia and Eritrea, where it serves both as food and as an eating utensil: stews and salads (called wat) are placed on the injera, and diners tear off pieces of the bread to scoop up the food by hand.

Preparation

Injera is made by mixing teff flour with water and allowing the batter to ferment for several days, developing its characteristic tangy flavour. The liquid batter is then poured in a spiral onto a large heated clay plate (a mitad), often covered with a lid so the top cooks by steam. The finished bread has a smooth underside and a porous top surface full of small holes ("eyes") formed by escaping gases. Where teff is expensive or unavailable, wheat, barley, sorghum, or rice flour may be substituted in whole or in part.

Varieties and serving

In Ethiopia, injera is eaten daily in most households, commonly served with a variety of spicy meat and vegetable stews. The rolled, filled form called injera firfir (injera torn and mixed with stew) is a popular breakfast. In Eritrea the dish is also known as tayta. Because teff is gluten-free, injera has attracted interest abroad among gluten-free consumers, and Ethiopian restaurants worldwide serve it as the foundation of shared platters.
