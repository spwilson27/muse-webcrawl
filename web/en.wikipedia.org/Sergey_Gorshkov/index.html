Sergey Gorshkov

Sergey Georgyevich Gorshkov (Russian: Сергей Георгиевич Горшков; 26 February 1910 – 13 May 1988) was a Soviet naval officer who served as Commander-in-Chief of the Soviet Navy from 1956 to 1985, the longest tenure in that post, and transformed it into a global blue-water fleet.

Career

Born in Kamianets-Podilskyi, Gorshkov joined the navy in 1927 and commanded ships and flotillas in the Black Sea during the Second World War, supporting landings at Kerch and Novorossiysk. He commanded the Black Sea Fleet from 1951 and in January 1956 was appointed Commander-in-Chief by Nikita Khrushchev.

Building the Soviet fleet

Responding to initial cuts under Khrushchev, Gorshkov argued for a balanced fleet in writings culminating in his book The Sea Power of the State (1976). Under his leadership the Soviet Union built a large nuclear submarine force, ballistic missile submarines, naval aviation, and surface forces capable of sustained operations in the Mediterranean, the Indian Ocean, and worldwide — a central feature of the Cold War naval balance with the United States.

Legacy

Gorshkov was twice named Hero of the Soviet Union and promoted to Admiral of the Fleet of the Soviet Union. The Russian Navy's Admiral Gorshkov-class frigates are named in his honour.
