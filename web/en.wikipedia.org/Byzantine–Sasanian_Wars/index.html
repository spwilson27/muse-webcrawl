The Byzantine–Sasanian wars were a series of conflicts between the Byzantine (East Roman) Empire and the Sasanian Empire of Persia from 502 to 628 AD. They continued the centuries-old Roman–Persian wars and were among the most destructive struggles of late antiquity, fought over the contested borderlands of Mesopotamia, Armenia and the Caucasus.

Principal wars

  • Anastasian War (502–506) — Kavad I invaded Roman Mesopotamia; ended in a Byzantine purchase of peace.
  • Iberian War (526–532) — fought over Iberia (Georgia); closed by the "Eternal Peace" between Justinian I and Khosrow I.
  • Lazic War (541–562) — Khosrow I broke the peace, sacked Antioch (540), and campaigned in Lazica on the Black Sea; ended with the Fifty-Year Peace.
  • War of 572–591 — long stalemate ended when Byzantium backed Khosrow II against a usurper and gained territory in Armenia and Iberia.
  • War of 602–628 — the last and greatest war: Persia conquered the Levant, Egypt and much of Anatolia, besieging Constantinople in 626; the emperor Heraclius counter-invaded Persia, won the Battle of Nineveh (627), and Khosrow II was overthrown. Peace restored the pre-war borders.

Consequences

The final war devastated both empires' finances, armies and border defences. Within a decade of 628, Arab Muslim armies of the Rashidun Caliphate destroyed the Sasanian Empire entirely and stripped Byzantium of Syria, Palestine and Egypt — a catastrophe historians link directly to the mutual exhaustion of the two empires in 602–628.
