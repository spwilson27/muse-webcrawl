West Frisian language

West Frisian
Native name: Frysk
Region: Friesland, Netherlands
Native speakers: about 350,000–450,000
Language family: Indo-European > Germanic > West Germanic > Anglo-Frisian > Frisian

West Frisian (Frysk), usually simply called Frisian in the Netherlands, is a West Germanic language spoken mostly in the province of Friesland (Fryslân) in the north of the Netherlands, chiefly by about 350,000 to 450,000 people. It is the closest living relative of English among the continental languages, descending alongside it from Anglo-Frisian; a well-known rhyme compares Frisian "Bûter, brea en griene tsiis" with English "Butter, bread and green cheese."

Status and use

Frisian has official status in Friesland alongside Dutch and is used in provincial administration, primary education, and regional broadcasting (Omrop Fryslân). Most speakers are bilingual in Dutch, and the language is classified as definitely endangered by UNESCO, though transmission to children remains relatively strong in rural areas. The main dialects are Clay Frisian, Wood Frisian, and South-West Frisian, with urban Town Frisian varieties heavily influenced by Dutch.
