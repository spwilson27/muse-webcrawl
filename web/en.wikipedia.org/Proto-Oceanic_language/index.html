Proto-Oceanic language

Proto-Oceanic (abbreviated POc) is the reconstructed ancestor of the Oceanic languages, a subgroup of the Austronesian language family comprising some 450–600 languages spoken across Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia. It is reconstructed by comparing its daughter languages using the comparative method.

Homeland and history

Linguistic and archaeological evidence places the Proto-Oceanic speech community in the Bismarck Archipelago off northeastern New Guinea around 1500–1000 BC, associated with the Lapita culture. From there, seafaring speakers spread eastward through the Pacific, eventually reaching as far as Hawaiʻi, Rapa Nui, and New Zealand.

Features

Reconstructions, notably by linguists such as Otto Dempwolff and Robert Blust, recover aspects of POc phonology, grammar, and vocabulary — including terms for canoes, sailing, horticulture, and reef and sea life that illuminate the culture of its speakers. POc is the parent of subgroups including the Admiralties, Western Oceanic, and Central–Eastern Oceanic languages, the latter including the Polynesian languages.
