Persona non grata

In diplomacy, a persona non grata (Latin for "person not welcome", plural: personae non gratae) is a foreign person whose entering or remaining in a particular country is prohibited by that country's government. The designation is the most serious form of censure a country can apply to foreign diplomats, who are otherwise protected by diplomatic immunity from arrest.

Diplomatic usage

Under Article 9 of the 1961 Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, a receiving state may "at any time and without having to explain its decision" declare a member of a diplomatic mission persona non grata. A person so declared must be recalled by the sending state or have their functions terminated; if the sending state refuses, the receiving state may refuse to recognise the person as a member of the mission.

Non-diplomatic usage

The term is also used more loosely outside diplomacy to describe a person who is unwelcome or ostracised in a group, organisation, or place.
