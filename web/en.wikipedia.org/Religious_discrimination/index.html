Religious discrimination

Campaigners protest restrictions on religious dress and practice.

Religious discrimination is the unequal treatment of a person or group on the basis of their religious beliefs, affiliation or practices. It ranges from social prejudice and employment discrimination to legal disabilities, exclusion from public office, and violent persecution. Freedom from religious discrimination is protected as a human right in Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights.

Forms

Historical forms include the civil disabilities imposed on Catholics and Jews in Britain (largely removed in the nineteenth century), the persecution of religious minorities such as the Baháʼís in Iran, Ahmadis in Pakistan, Rohingya Muslims in Myanmar, Uyghur Muslims in China, and Christians in parts of the Middle East and North Korea, and modern controversies over religious dress — including bans on headscarves and face veils in some European states and on Sikh turbans in certain workplaces and schools. Antisemitism and Islamophobia are widely studied contemporary forms.

Legal protections

In the United States, Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 prohibits employment discrimination based on religion and requires reasonable accommodation of religious practice — a duty reinforced by the Supreme Court's decision in Groff v. DeJoy (2023). The First Amendment bars government establishment of religion and protects its free exercise. In the European Union, the Employment Equality Directive (2000) prohibits religious discrimination in employment, while the European Convention on Human Rights protects freedom of thought, conscience and religion under Article 9.

Debates

Recurring debates concern the boundary between protecting believers and protecting belief from criticism, exemptions for religious employers and service providers, and conflicts between religious-freedom claims and protections against discrimination on other grounds such as sex and sexual orientation.
