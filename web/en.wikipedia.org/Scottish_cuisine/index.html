Scottish cuisine

Scottish cuisine encompasses the cooking styles, traditions, and recipes associated with Scotland. It has distinctive attributes and recipes of its own and shares much with wider British and European cuisine as a result of local, regional, and continental influences, both ancient and modern.

Traditional dishes

The best-known Scottish dish is haggis, a savoury pudding of sheep's offal minced with onion, oatmeal, and spices, traditionally served with "neeps and tatties" (turnip or swede and potatoes) and celebrated in Robert Burns's "Address to a Haggis". Other traditional foods include Cullen skink (a smoked haddock soup), Arbroath smokies, Scotch broth, stovies, porridge, cranachan, and shortbread.

Ingredients

Scottish cooking makes extensive use of oats, barley, potatoes, root vegetables, and dairy produce, together with game such as venison and grouse, and fish and shellfish including Atlantic salmon and the haddock used in the Scottish version of fish and chips. Scotland's larder also supports a notable tradition of cheese-making and of whisky production, with Scotch whisky accompanying or flavouring many dishes.

Modern Scottish cuisine

Contemporary Scottish chefs combine the country's natural produce with modern and international techniques. Deep-fried foods sold in fish and chip shops — famously including the deep-fried Mars bar — have given Scottish takeaway food a notorious, if partly jocular, reputation, while the national soft drink Irn-Bru is a fixture of popular culture.
