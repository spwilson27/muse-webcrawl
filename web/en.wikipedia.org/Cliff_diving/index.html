Cliff diving

Cliff diving
Type: Extreme sport / diving discipline
Typical heights: 20 m (women), 27 m (men) in competition
Sanctioning: World Aquatics (high diving)
Famous venue: La Quebrada, Acapulco

Cliff diving is the sport of diving into water from great heights off cliffs or similarly elevated natural or artificial structures, typically performed without intermediate platforms. Divers enter the water at speeds that can exceed 75–90 km/h, making the impact comparable to hitting a hard surface; entries are therefore made feet-first, unlike platform diving, to reduce the risk of injury.

History and traditions

Leaping from cliffs into the sea is an old Hawaiian tradition: in the 18th century King Kahekili II of Maui was famed for the practice, known as lele kawa, and the cliffs at Kaunolū on Lanai are associated with the custom of demonstrating courage and loyalty. In Acapulco, Mexico, the professional divers of La Quebrada have performed since the 1930s, plunging from 35 m into a narrow inlet, timing their jumps with the incoming swell. Similar traditions exist at sites in Europe and elsewhere.

Modern competition

Since 2009 the Red Bull Cliff Diving World Series has staged international competitions from heights of 27 m for men and 21 m for women at natural and urban venues worldwide. Under World Aquatics (formerly FINA), the related discipline of high diving — with fixed platforms at 27 m (men) and 20 m (women) — debuted at the World Aquatics Championships in 2013. Judges score dives by takeoff, flight manoeuvres such as somersaults and twists, and entry. Safety teams of scuba divers and rescue personnel are stationed in the water at competitions.
