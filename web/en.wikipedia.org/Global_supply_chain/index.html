Global supply chain

A global supply chain is a network of organisations, suppliers, manufacturers, logistics providers and distributors spanning multiple countries, through which raw materials, components and finished products flow from sources of supply to end customers around the world. Global supply chains expanded rapidly from the 1980s onward with containerised shipping, trade liberalisation and advances in information technology.

Structure and management

Modern supply chains often involve specialised suppliers in different countries for each production stage — for example, semiconductors fabricated in East Asia, assembled into devices elsewhere, and sold globally. Supply chain management coordinates procurement, production planning, inventory, transport and warehousing, balancing cost against speed and reliability. Practices such as just-in-time manufacturing minimise inventory but reduce buffers against disruption.

Risks and resilience

Global supply chains are exposed to natural disasters, pandemics, geopolitical conflict, trade restrictions and transport bottlenecks; the COVID-19 pandemic, the 2021 Suez Canal blockage and semiconductor shortages prompted widespread reassessment. Responses include supplier diversification, nearshoring and "friend-shoring", larger safety stocks, and digital tracking for visibility.
