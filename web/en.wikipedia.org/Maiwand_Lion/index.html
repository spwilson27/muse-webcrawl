Maiwand Lion

The Maiwand Lion is a war memorial in Forbury Gardens in Reading, Berkshire, England. It commemorates the officers and men of the 66th (Berkshire) Regiment of Foot who died at the Battle of Maiwand and in other campaigns in Afghanistan during the Second Anglo-Afghan War of 1878–1880.

Description

The memorial is a large statue of a lion, representing the 66th Regiment's fighting spirit, standing on a granite plinth. It was sculpted by George Blackall Simonds and unveiled in 1886. The lion is about 9.4 metres (31 feet) long overall and was cast in iron at Simonds' foundry in Reading.

History

At the Battle of Maiwand on 27 July 1880, the 66th Regiment suffered heavy casualties, with several hundred men killed as they made a last stand against Afghan forces. The memorial became a symbol of Reading and of the regiment; the town's football club, Reading F.C., is nicknamed "the Royals" and its crest features a lion inspired by the statue.

Legacy

The Maiwand Lion is a Grade II listed structure. It remains one of Reading's best-known landmarks and a focus for Remembrance Day commemorations.
