NetBSD is a free and open-source Unix-like operating system derived from Berkeley Software Distribution (BSD). It is famous for its portability: its slogan "Of course it runs NetBSD" reflects ports to dozens of hardware platforms, from servers and desktops to embedded systems and game consoles.

History

NetBSD was founded in 1993 by Chris Demetriou, Theo de Raadt, Adam Glass, and Charles Hannum, based on 386BSD and the 4.4BSD-Lite release. It was one of the earliest open-source BSD systems, alongside FreeBSD.

Features

The project emphasises clean design, well-documented code, and a complete, self-contained source tree. Technologies originating in or refined by NetBSD include the pkgsrc package system, the rump kernel (anykernel) architecture, and widespread IPv6 support via the KAME project.
