Leo Fender

Born: Clarence Leonidas Fender, 10 August 1909, Anaheim, California
Died: 21 March 1991 (aged 81)
Known for: Fender Telecaster, Stratocaster, Precision Bass

Clarence Leonidas Fender (August 10, 1909 – March 21, 1991) was an American inventor and founder of the Fender Musical Instruments Corporation. He designed some of the most influential electric guitars and basses in popular music, including the Telecaster, Stratocaster and Precision Bass.

Career

Fender began by repairing radios in Fullerton, California, before building amplifiers and, in 1951, the first mass-produced solid-body electric guitar, the Telecaster, and the first mass-produced electric bass, the Precision Bass. The Stratocaster followed in 1954.

Later years

Fender sold his company to CBS in 1965 and later co-founded Music Man and G&L Musical Instruments. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992, despite famously never learning to play the guitar himself.
