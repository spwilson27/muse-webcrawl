August Spies

Born: 10 December 1855, Friedewald, Electorate of Hesse

Died: 11 November 1887 (aged 31), Chicago, Illinois, United States

Occupation: Newspaper editor, labour activist

Known for: Haymarket affair

August Vincent Theodore Spies (10 December 1855 – 11 November 1887) was a German-American labour activist and newspaper editor. Spies immigrated to the United States in 1872, settled in Chicago, and became editor of the anarchist newspaper Arbeiter-Zeitung. He was a prominent figure in the movement for the eight-hour working day.

Haymarket affair

On 4 May 1886, Spies spoke at a rally in Chicago's Haymarket Square protesting police violence against strikers. When police moved to disperse the gathering, an unknown person threw a bomb, killing several officers and civilians. Spies was arrested and, with seven co-defendants, convicted of conspiracy to murder in a trial widely criticised as unjust; no evidence linked him directly to the bombing.

Execution and legacy

Spies was hanged on 11 November 1887, alongside Albert Parsons, Adolph Fischer and George Engel. His reported last words — "The time will come when our silence will be more powerful than the voices you strangle today" — are inscribed on the Haymarket Martyrs' Monument in Forest Park, Illinois. In 1893, Illinois governor John Peter Altgeld pardoned the surviving defendants and condemned the trial as a miscarriage of justice. The Haymarket martyrs became enduring symbols of the international labour movement, and International Workers' Day (May Day) commemorates the affair.
