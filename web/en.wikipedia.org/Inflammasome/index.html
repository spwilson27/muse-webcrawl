Inflammasome

Inflammasome assembly activates caspase-1
ComponentsSensor (e.g. NLRP3, AIM2), adaptor ASC, caspase-1
ProductsIL-1β, IL-18; pyroptosis via gasdermin D
DiscoveredTerm coined 2002 (Martinon, Burns & Tschopp)

The inflammasome is a cytosolic multiprotein complex of the innate immune system that, upon sensing microbial products or endogenous danger signals, assembles in macrophages and other cells and activates inflammatory caspases, principally caspase-1. Active caspase-1 processes the pro-inflammatory cytokines interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and interleukin-18 (IL-18) into their mature forms and cleaves gasdermin D, whose fragments perforate the cell membrane and cause a lytic, inflammatory form of cell death called pyroptosis.

Structure and activation

Canonical inflammasomes consist of a sensor protein — often a pattern-recognition receptor such as NLRP3, NLRC4, AIM2 or pyrin — the adaptor protein ASC (which polymerises into a micron-sized "speck"), and pro-caspase-1. NLRP3, the best studied sensor, responds to an unusually broad range of stimuli, including extracellular ATP, crystalline materials (uric acid, cholesterol, silica, asbestos), amyloid aggregates and pore-forming toxins; activation typically requires a priming signal (e.g. via Toll-like receptors) followed by a second, activating signal often involving potassium efflux. Non-canonical inflammasomes sense cytosolic lipopolysaccharide directly through caspase-4/5 (humans) or caspase-11 (mice).

Role in disease

Gain-of-function mutations in NLRP3 cause the cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes (CAPS), rare autoinflammatory diseases treated with IL-1 blockers. Inflammasome activation is implicated in gout (triggered by monosodium urate crystals), atherosclerosis, type 2 diabetes, Alzheimer's disease and silicosis, making NLRP3 inhibitors an active area of drug development. Inflammasomes also contribute to host defence against bacteria, fungi and viruses.

History

The term "inflammasome" was coined in 2002 by Fabio Martinon, Kimberly Burns and Jürg Tschopp, who identified the complex as the platform activating caspase-1.
