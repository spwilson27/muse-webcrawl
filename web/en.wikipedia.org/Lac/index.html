Lac

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lac is the scarlet resinous secretion of a number of species of lac insects, of which the most commonly cultivated is Kerria lacca. Cultivation begins when a farmer gets a stick (broodlac) that contains eggs ready to hatch and ties it to the tree to be infested. Thousands of lac insects colonize the branches of the host trees and secrete the resinous pigment.

Production

The coated branches are harvested and the coating scraped off as sticklac, which is crushed, sieved, and repeatedly washed to remove insect parts, yielding seedlac. Further refining produces shellac, a natural polymer used for centuries as a wood finish, food glaze, and electrical insulator, and once the basis of 78 rpm gramophone records. India and Thailand are the major producers.

Host trees and harvesting

Important host trees include kusum (Schleichera oleosa), palas (Butea monosperma), and ber (Ziziphus mauritiana). The red pigment, lac dye, was historically used as a crimson colorant for textiles and leather.
