Unschooling

Unschooling is an informal learning method and philosophy that advocates learner-chosen activities as a primary means of education. It is a form of homeschooling in which children learn through their natural life experiences — play, household responsibilities, personal interests, work experience, travel, books, family, mentors, and social interaction — rather than through a structured curriculum.

History

The term was coined in the 1970s by educator John Holt, a leading advocate of homeschooling and author of Growing Without Schooling magazine, founded in 1977. Holt argued that children learn best when not coerced, echoing earlier ideas of Ivan Illich's "deschooling" and A. S. Neill's Summerhill School.

Practices and criticism

Unschooling families vary widely; some provide many resources and guidance while following the child's interests, while a small "radical unschooling" minority extends the philosophy to all areas of a child's life. Critics raise concerns about gaps in subjects such as mathematics, socialization, and legal compliance, while supporters point to motivation, depth of interest-based learning, and self-reports of successful outcomes in higher education and employment.
