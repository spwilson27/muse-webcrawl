Scalar (physics)

In physics, a scalar is a physical quantity that can be described by a single numerical value (with units), without any direction. Scalars are contrasted with vectors, which require both magnitude and direction.

Examples

Common scalar quantities include mass, temperature, electric charge, time, speed, energy, electric potential, volume, and density. Examples of vectors, by contrast, include displacement, velocity, force, and electric field. Speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector and is therefore a scalar.

Properties

The value of a true scalar is independent of the coordinate system used to describe it: it is invariant under rotations and (for Lorentz scalars in relativity) under Lorentz transformations. In field theory, a scalar field assigns a number to every point in space; the Higgs field is a notable example, and its quantum excitation, the Higgs boson, is a scalar particle with spin 0. Related notions in mathematics include pseudoscalars, which change sign under improper rotations such as reflections.
