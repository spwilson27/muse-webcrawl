King's Highway 11, commonly referred to as Highway 11, is a provincially maintained highway in the Canadian province of Ontario. At roughly 1,785 kilometres, it is one of the longest provincial highways in Canada. The route forms a great arc from Barrie northward through Muskoka and North Bay, then west across Northern Ontario through Kapuskasing and Thunder Bay to Rainy River on the United States border.

History

The southernmost portion of Highway 11 followed Yonge Street from Toronto, often described as the "longest street in the world" while the two were conflated; the Toronto–Barrie section was downloaded to municipal control in the late 1990s. Yonge Street itself was begun under Lieutenant Governor John Graves Simcoe in the 1790s as a military road. The northern sections were built in stages during the twentieth century as part of the development of Ontario's resource frontier and the Trans-Canada Highway network.

Route

From Barrie the highway passes Orillia, Gravenhurst, Bracebridge, and Huntsville, serving as the main road into cottage country. North of North Bay it continues through Temiskaming Shores, Kirkland Lake, Timmins-area junctions, Cochrane, Kapuskasing, Hearst, and Longlac, then joins Highway 17 near Nipigon for a shared stretch around Lake Superior before separating west of Thunder Bay and running to Atikokan and Fort Frances.
