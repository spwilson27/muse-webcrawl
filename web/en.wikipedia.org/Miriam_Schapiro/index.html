Miriam Schapiro (November 15, 1923 – June 20, 2015) was a Canadian-born American artist associated with the Pattern and Decoration movement and a pioneer of feminist art. She coined the term "femmage" for her collage works that combined paint with fabric and other materials traditionally associated with women's domestic work, such as quilting, embroidery, and appliqué.

Early life and education

Schapiro grew up in Brooklyn, New York, the daughter of Russian-Jewish immigrants. Her father, an industrial designer, encouraged her art. She studied at Hunter College and then at the University of Iowa, where she earned her BFA, MA, and MFA in the 1940s, studying printmaking with Mauricio Lasansky. In 1946 she married the painter Paul Brach.

Career

In the 1950s Schapiro painted in an Abstract Expressionist idiom in New York. After moving to California in 1967, she co-founded, with Judy Chicago, the Feminist Art Program at the California Institute of the Arts in 1971. Together with Chicago, she helped create Womanhouse (1972), a landmark feminist installation in a Los Angeles mansion. In the mid-1970s she became a leading figure of the Pattern and Decoration movement, producing large-scale, exuberantly coloured collages celebrating women's craft traditions. Her works include Wonderland (1983) and a series of fans, hearts, and kimono-shaped canvases.

Legacy

Schapiro's work is held by the Museum of Modern Art, the Whitney Museum, and the National Gallery of Art, among others. She received numerous honours, including a Guggenheim Fellowship and the Women's Caucus for Art Lifetime Achievement Award, and is credited with helping to legitimise decoration, craft, and autobiography as subjects for high art.
