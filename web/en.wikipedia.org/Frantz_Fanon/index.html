Frantz Fanon (1925–1961)
Born20 July 1925, Fort-de-France, Martinique
Died6 December 1961, Bethesda, Maryland, US
OccupationsPsychiatrist, philosopher, writer
Notable worksBlack Skin, White Masks (1952); The Wretched of the Earth (1961)

Frantz Omar Fanon (20 July 1925 – 6 December 1961) was a French West Indian psychiatrist and political philosopher from Martinique whose works became foundational for postcolonial studies, critical theory and liberation movements. His analysis of the psychology of colonisation and his defence of anti-colonial struggle influenced independence movements across Africa and beyond.

Life

Fanon volunteered for the Free French forces during the Second World War and was decorated for his service in France. He studied medicine and psychiatry in Lyon, where he wrote his first book, Black Skin, White Masks (1952), an analysis of the psychological effects of racism on the colonised. In 1953 he became head of the psychiatric department at the Blida-Joinville hospital in Algeria, where he developed methods of social therapy. After the outbreak of the Algerian War in 1954 he secretly aided the National Liberation Front (FLN), resigned his post in 1956, and was expelled from Algeria; he then worked openly for the FLN in Tunis and as an ambassador of the provisional Algerian government.

The Wretched of the Earth

Diagnosed with leukaemia, Fanon dictated his most famous work, The Wretched of the Earth (1961), in a matter of weeks. Published with a preface by Jean-Paul Sartre, the book analysed the dehumanising effects of colonialism and argued that decolonisation is inherently a violent phenomenon. Fanon died in Bethesda, Maryland, in December 1961, aged 36, and was buried in Algeria. His writings later shaped Black consciousness movements, liberation theology and postcolonial thought worldwide.
