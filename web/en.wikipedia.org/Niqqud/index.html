Niqqud

Niqqud (Hebrew: נִקּוּד, "dotting" or "pointing") is a system of diacritical marks used in the Hebrew script to indicate vowels, distinguish consonant pronunciations, and mark cantillation. The marks appear as small dots and dashes placed above, below, or inside the consonantal letters.

History

The system was developed by the Masoretes of Tiberias in the early medieval period, roughly between the 5th and 10th centuries CE, as part of their effort to preserve the pronunciation of the Hebrew Bible. The Tiberian pointing became the standard system and is the one used in modern pointed Hebrew texts.

Use

In modern Hebrew, niqqud is used mainly in poetry, children's books, dictionaries, religious texts, and materials for language learners; everyday writing is normally unpointed, relying on context and on "full spelling" conventions using the letters vav and yod as vowel markers. The principal vowel signs include hiriq, tzere, segol, patah, qamatz, holam, qubutz, and shuruk, along with the dagesh, which marks gemination or a plosive pronunciation of the letters bet, gimel, dalet, kaf, pe, and tav.
