Period (physics)

In physics, the period of a wave or oscillation is the time taken to complete one full cycle. It is conventionally denoted T and measured in seconds. Period is the reciprocal of frequency: T = 1/f.

Oscillations

For a simple pendulum of length L, the period for small swings is approximately T = 2π√(L/g). For a mass on a spring, T = 2π√(m/k), where m is mass and k the spring constant. In uniform circular motion, the period is the time for one complete revolution.

Waves

For a travelling wave, period relates to wavelength λ and wave speed v by v = λ/T = λf. The angular frequency ω = 2π/T = 2πf is widely used in equations of simple harmonic motion.
