Gulf of St. Lawrence

The Gulf of St. Lawrence is the outlet of the Great Lakes into the Atlantic.

The Gulf of St. Lawrence (French: Golfe du Saint-Laurent) in eastern Canada is the world's largest estuary and the outlet of the North American Great Lakes into the Atlantic Ocean via the St. Lawrence River. It covers roughly 226,000 km² and is bordered by Quebec, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, Nova Scotia, and Newfoundland and Labrador.

Geography

The gulf is bounded by the Strait of Belle Isle to the northeast, the Cabot Strait between Newfoundland and Cape Breton Island, and the Strait of Canso. Islands within the gulf include Anticosti Island, Prince Edward Island, the Magdalen Islands and Cape Breton. Deep channels (the Laurentian Channel) cut the otherwise relatively shallow shelf, and a persistent estuarine circulation draws nutrient-rich deep water toward the head of the channel, sustaining rich fisheries.

History and ecology

Basque, Breton and Norman fishers worked these waters for cod and whales from the early 16th century; Jacques Cartier sailed the gulf in 1534. The ecosystem supports cod, redfish, snow crab, seals and the endangered North Atlantic right whale, and in recent decades has experienced warming, oxygen decline and the collapse and partial recovery of groundfish stocks.
