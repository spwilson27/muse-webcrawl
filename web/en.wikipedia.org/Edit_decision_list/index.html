Edit decision list

An edit decision list (EDL) is a representation of all the editing decisions made during the production of a film, television programme or video project. It lists, in a machine-readable or human-readable form, the source material, the in-points and out-points of every edit, and the order in which shots are assembled in the finished programme. EDLs originated in linear videotape editing, where an offline edit performed on low-resolution copies was later conformed on high-quality broadcast equipment using the list of timecodes recorded in the EDL.

History

The concept dates to the era of CMX editing systems in the 1970s, when controllers such as the CMX 600 and later the CMX 3400 stored edit decisions on punched paper tape or floppy disk. Formats such as CMX 3600 became a de facto standard and are still widely supported by non-linear editing software.

Use

In modern workflows, an editor exports an EDL from a non-linear editor such as Avid Media Composer, Adobe Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve. The list is used to conform an online edit, transfer a project between systems, or archive the structure of a programme independently of proprietary project files. Because a basic EDL can describe only a single video track and simple cuts and dissolves, richer formats such as AAF and XML-based interchange formats are often preferred for complex projects.

Format

A typical EDL event line contains an event number, the source reel or clip name, track type (video or audio), the transition type (cut, dissolve, wipe), and four timecodes: source in, source out, record in and record out.
