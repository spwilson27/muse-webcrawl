Tokyo Stock Exchange

Tokyo Stock Exchange
Founded: 1878 (as Tokyo Kabushiki Torihikijo)
Location: Nihonbashi-Kabutocho, Tokyo
Owner: Japan Exchange Group

The Tokyo Stock Exchange (東京証券取引所, Tōkyō Shōken Torihikijo), abbreviated TSE, is the largest stock exchange in Japan and one of the largest in the world by market capitalisation. It is operated by Japan Exchange Group (JPX), formed in 2013 by the merger of the Tokyo and Osaka exchanges.

History

The exchange was founded in 1878 as the Tokyo Kabushiki Torihikijo and began regular trading soon after. It closed during the final phase of the Second World War and reopened in 1949 under the postwar Securities Exchange Law. At the height of the Japanese asset price bubble in December 1989, the TSE accounted for an extraordinary share of world equity market value; the Nikkei 225 index peaked at 38,915 that month, a record it did not surpass until 2024.

Market structure

Trading is fully electronic, conducted through the arrowhead system. A major restructuring in April 2022 reorganised listings into three segments: Prime, Standard, and Growth. Well-known indices based on TSE prices include the Nikkei 225 and the broader TOPIX.
