Bell Rock Lighthouse

The Bell Rock Lighthouse, off the coast of Angus, Scotland, is the world's oldest surviving sea-washed lighthouse. It stands on Bell Rock, a reef in the North Sea about 18 km east of the Firth of Tay.

Construction

Built between 1807 and 1810 by Robert Stevenson, the 35-metre granite tower was constructed on a reef submerged at high tide, requiring workers to live on a temporary beacon and a support ship.

Operation

The light first shone in 1811. The lighthouse was automated in 1988 and is operated by the Northern Lighthouse Board. It is regarded as a masterpiece of engineering.
