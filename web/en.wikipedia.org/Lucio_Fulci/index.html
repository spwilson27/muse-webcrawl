Lucio Fulci (17 June 1927 – 13 March 1996) was an Italian film director, political activist, screenwriter and actor. Although he worked in a wide array of genres through a career spanning nearly five decades, including comedies and spaghetti Westerns, he earned an international cult following for his giallo and horror films.

Career

Fulci studied medicine before turning to cinema, training at the Centro Sperimentale in Rome. He began as a screenwriter and assistant director, notably for Steno, and directed popular comedies in the 1950s and 1960s. His thriller A Lizard in a Woman's Skin (1971) and the giallo Don't Torture a Duckling (1972) marked his turn toward darker material.

Horror films

Fulci's international breakthrough came with Zombi 2 (1979), marketed abroad as a sequel to George A. Romero's Dawn of the Dead. He followed it with the loosely connected "Gates of Hell" trilogy — City of the Living Dead (1980), The Beyond (1981) and The House by the Cemetery (1981) — noted for their dreamlike plots and extreme gore effects. Several of his films were classified as "video nasties" in the United Kingdom. In later years, declining budgets and ill health (he suffered from diabetes) diminished his output, though his reputation was revived by cult-film audiences and retrospective festivals in the 1990s.

Legacy

Often nicknamed the "Godfather of Gore", Fulci is now regarded as one of Italy's most significant horror directors, influencing later filmmakers and inspiring tributes, documentaries and soundtrack reissues of the scores by Fabio Frizzi and others.
