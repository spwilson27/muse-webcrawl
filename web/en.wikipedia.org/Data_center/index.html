Data center

A data center (American English) or data centre (British English) is a building, a dedicated space within a building, or a group of buildings used to house computer systems and associated components, such as telecommunications and storage systems. It generally includes redundant or backup components for power, cooling, networking and physical security.

Design and tiers

Modern data centers are classified by the Uptime Institute's tier system (I–IV) according to redundancy and expected availability, with Tier IV offering fault-tolerant design. Key systems include uninterruptible power supplies and generators, precision air conditioning or liquid cooling, raised floors or containment aisles, fire suppression, and layered physical access control. Efficiency is measured by power usage effectiveness (PUE), the ratio of total facility energy to IT energy.

Types and trends

Facilities range from enterprise data centers operated by a single company, to colocation facilities renting space and power to many tenants, to the hyperscale campuses of cloud providers such as Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud. Growth in cloud computing and artificial intelligence has driven construction of very large sites and concern over electricity and water consumption; operators increasingly buy renewable energy and site facilities near cheap power.
