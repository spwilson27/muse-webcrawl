Context-adaptive binary arithmetic coding (CABAC) is a form of entropy encoding used in video compression. It combines arithmetic coding with adaptive probability models that are selected and updated according to the context of the symbols being coded. CABAC is a key source of the compression efficiency of the H.264/AVC and HEVC video standards.

How it works

CABAC operates in three stages. First, non-binary syntax elements (such as motion vector differences or quantised coefficients) are binarised — converted into a sequence of binary decisions called bins. Second, for each bin a context model is chosen based on neighbouring, previously coded information, and the model's probability estimate is updated as coding proceeds. Third, a binary arithmetic coder encodes each bin using its estimated probabilities, with special fast "bypass" coding for bins assumed to be equally likely.

Performance and cost

In H.264, CABAC typically saves around 5–15% of bit rate compared with the simpler context-adaptive variable-length coding (CAVLC) option, with the largest gains at higher quality levels. The cost is greater computational complexity and serial dependencies that complicate parallel decoding; later standards such as HEVC retained CABAC while redesigning its contexts and throughput, and VVC improved it further.

Applications

Beyond broadcast and streaming video in H.264, HEVC and VVC, CABAC-like schemes are used in other compression standards and image codecs. The technique exemplifies the general principle that accurate, adaptive probability modelling is as important to compression as the coder itself.
