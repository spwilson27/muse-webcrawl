Hirsutism

Hirsutism is excessive body hair on parts of the body where hair is normally absent or minimal. The word is from early 17th century: from Latin hirsutus meaning "hairy". It usually refers to a male-pattern of hair growth in women that may be a sign of a more serious medical condition, especially if it develops well after puberty. The growth of facial hair is often cited as the primary indicator of hirsutism.

Causes

Hirsutism is caused by increased production of, or sensitivity to, androgens. The most common cause is polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Other causes include congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Cushing's syndrome, androgen-secreting tumours, and certain medications such as anabolic steroids and some anticonvulsants. In many cases no underlying cause is found (idiopathic hirsutism).

Diagnosis

Severity is commonly scored with the Ferriman–Gallwey scale, which grades hair growth in nine androgen-sensitive areas. Laboratory evaluation may include testosterone, DHEA-S, and screening for PCOS and other endocrine disorders.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause where possible and may include combined oral contraceptives, anti-androgens such as spironolactone, lifestyle measures for PCOS, and cosmetic hair removal (shaving, waxing, laser hair removal, electrolysis) or topical eflornithine.
