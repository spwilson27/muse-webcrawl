Switchblade

Image placeholder: a side-opening automatic knife (stiletto style).

A switchblade (also known as an automatic knife, pushbutton knife, ejector knife, flick knife or flick blade) is a type of knife with a folding or sliding blade contained in the handle, opened automatically by a spring when a button, lever or switch on the handle is activated.

Types and history

Two basic types predominate: side-opening knives, whose blades pivot out like an ordinary pocket knife — including the classic Italian stiletto — and out-the-front (OTF) knives, whose blades slide out of the front of the handle. Automatic knives date to 18th-century folding designs, and mass production began in the United States and Italy in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Regulation and reputation

In the 1950s American media associated switchblades with juvenile delinquency and gangs, a panic reflected in films such as Rebel Without a Cause and the musical West Side Story. The result was the US Federal Switchblade Act of 1958 and similar laws in many countries, heavily restricting their sale and possession. Since the 2000s, many US states have repealed their bans, and automatic knives are legal in much of the country, though restrictions remain common elsewhere.
