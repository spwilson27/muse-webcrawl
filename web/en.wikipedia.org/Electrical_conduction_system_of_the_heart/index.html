Electrical conduction system of the heart

The electrical conduction system of the heart is a network of specialized cardiac muscle cells that initiates and coordinates the heartbeat.

Components

The sinoatrial (SA) node, in the right atrium, acts as the natural pacemaker, generating impulses that spread across the atria causing them to contract. The impulse is delayed at the atrioventricular (AV) node, allowing the ventricles to fill, then travels rapidly through the bundle of His, the right and left bundle branches, and the Purkinje fibers, triggering coordinated ventricular contraction.

Disorders

Abnormalities of conduction cause arrhythmias, heart block, bundle branch block, and conditions such as Wolff–Parkinson–White syndrome. Conduction is recorded clinically by the electrocardiogram (ECG). Artificial pacemakers and defibrillators can replace or correct faulty conduction.
