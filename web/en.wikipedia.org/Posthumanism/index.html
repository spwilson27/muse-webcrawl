Posthumanism

Posthumanism or post-humanism is a philosophical and critical movement that questions traditional humanism's assumptions about human nature, human exceptionalism and the boundaries between humans, animals, machines and nature. It encompasses several strands, including philosophical posthumanism, cultural posthumanism, and its relation to — and distinction from — transhumanism.

Overview

Thinkers associated with posthumanism include Donna Haraway, whose "A Cyborg Manifesto" (1985) blurred the lines between organism and machine; N. Katherine Hayles, who analysed how information technologies reshape embodiment in How We Became Posthuman (1999); and Cary Wolfe and Francesca Ferrando, who developed critical and philosophical posthumanism. The field draws on poststructuralism, animal studies, environmental philosophy and science and technology studies.

Themes

Recurring themes include decentring the human, the ethical standing of nonhuman animals, ecological interdependence, and the effects of biotechnology and artificial intelligence on identity. Unlike transhumanism, which generally seeks to enhance the human, critical posthumanism interrogates the category of "the human" itself.
