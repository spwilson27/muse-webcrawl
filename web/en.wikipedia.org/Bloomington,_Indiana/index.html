Bloomington, Indiana

Bloomington is a city in and the county seat of Monroe County, Indiana, United States. It is the seventh-largest city in Indiana and the fourth-largest outside the Indianapolis metropolitan area. The population was 79,168 at the 2020 census. The city is best known as the home of Indiana University Bloomington, the flagship campus of the Indiana University system, which dominates the city's economy and culture.

History

The area where Bloomington is situated was previously home to the Delaware, Potawatomi, Miami, and Eel River Wyandot peoples. Bloomington was founded in 1818 by a group of settlers from Kentucky, Tennessee, the Carolinas, and Virginia who were attracted to the area by its rich soil and frontier position. A post office was established in 1825, and the town was incorporated in 1827. Indiana University was founded in 1820 as the State Seminary School and became Indiana College in 1828, growing into one of the largest universities in the United States.

The limestone quarries of the surrounding region, famously celebrated in the 1979 film Breaking Away, which was set and filmed in Bloomington, supplied stone for landmarks including the Empire State Building and the Pentagon.

Geography

Bloomington lies in south-central Indiana, in the rolling hills of the Indiana Uplands near the edge of the glaciated plains. The terrain is marked by limestone karst features, sinkholes, and caves. Nearby attractions include Lake Monroe, the largest lake in Indiana, Hoosier National Forest, and Griffy Lake. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the city has a total area of about 23.36 square miles.

Education

Indiana University Bloomington enrolls more than 40,000 students and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity." It is home to the Jacobs School of Music, the Kelley School of Business, the Maurer School of Law, and the Kinsey Institute, founded by Alfred Kinsey in 1947. The university's basketball team, the Indiana Hoosiers, plays at Simon Skjodt Assembly Hall and is a defining feature of local identity.

Culture

Bloomington has a noted music scene and is associated with artists and the independent label Secretly Canadian. The city hosts the Lotus World Music and Arts Festival each autumn. The 1983 film locations and the annual Little 500 bicycle race—immortalised in Breaking Away—remain part of the city's identity.
