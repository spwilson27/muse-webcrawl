Drivetrain

A drivetrain (also often spelled drive train) or transmission system is the group of components that deliver mechanical power from the prime mover to the driven components. In automotive engineering, the drivetrain includes the engine or motor, transmission, drive shafts, differentials and the final drive components such as drive wheels — in some usages excluding the engine itself, when it is more precisely called the powertrain's mechanical part.

Function

The function of the drivetrain is to couple the engine that produces the power to the driving wheels that use the mechanical power to rotate the axle. This connection involves physically linking the two components, which may be at opposite ends of the vehicle and so require a long propeller shaft or driveshaft. The operating speed of the engine and wheels are also different and must be matched by the correct gear ratio; as the vehicle speed changes, the ideal engine speed must remain approximately constant for efficient operation, so the gearbox ratio must also be changed, manually, automatically or by continuous variation.

Layouts

Common automobile layouts include front-engine front-wheel drive (FF), front-engine rear-wheel drive (FR), mid-engine and rear-engine variants, and all-wheel drive systems that distribute torque to all four wheels, often through a transfer case and additional differentials.

Bicycle drivetrains

On a bicycle, the drivetrain comprises the pedals, cranks, chainrings, chain (or belt), sprockets and derailleurs or hub gears that transmit the rider's pedalling power to the rear wheel.
