Insurgency

Insurgency
TypeIrregular warfare
AimOverthrow or challenge established authority
TacticsGuerrilla warfare, sabotage, subversion, propaganda
ResponseCounter-insurgency (COIN)

An insurgency is a violent, armed rebellion against an established authority — a government, occupying power, or administration — by an irregular force. Insurgents typically lack the resources of a state army and rely on guerrilla tactics, mobility, and support or tolerance from part of the population. The term is often used for movements seeking to overthrow a government or expel a foreign occupier.

Characteristics

Insurgencies generally combine military action with political organisation: building shadow administrations, recruiting, fundraising, and propaganda aimed at winning legitimacy. Theorists from Mao Zedong onwards have described protracted people's war developing through strategic defensive, stalemate, and offensive phases. Insurgencies are usually asymmetric conflicts, in which the weaker side avoids decisive battle.

History

The word gained currency during the colonial wars of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Major insurgencies include the Peninsular War against Napoleonic France, the Malayan Emergency, the Vietnam War, the Soviet–Afghan War, and more recent conflicts in Iraq and Afghanistan. States respond with counter-insurgency, combining military operations with political reform and development to separate insurgents from the population.

Legal and political aspects

Whether a group is labelled insurgent, terrorist, or freedom fighter is politically contested. International humanitarian law treats many insurgencies as non-international armed conflicts governed by Common Article 3 of the Geneva Conventions.
