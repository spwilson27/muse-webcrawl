The Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of Amiens (French: Basilique Cathédrale Notre-Dame d'Amiens) is a Roman Catholic church in Amiens, France. It is the largest cathedral in France by interior volume and a masterpiece of French Gothic architecture.

History

Construction began in 1220 after a fire destroyed the earlier Romanesque cathedral. The nave was completed by about 1266, and the great west facade with its richly sculpted portals became a model of High Gothic design. The cathedral preserves a relic said to be the head of John the Baptist, brought from Constantinople after the Fourth Crusade.

Architecture

The nave reaches a height of 42.3 metres, among the tallest in France, and the interior volume is roughly 200,000 cubic metres. Its plan follows the cruciform layout with a long choir, ambulatory, and radiating chapels. The labyrinth set into the nave floor dates to 1288.

Heritage

Amiens Cathedral was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981. The polychromy of its facade sculptures, rediscovered during laser cleaning in the 1990s, is recreated in a nightly light show.
