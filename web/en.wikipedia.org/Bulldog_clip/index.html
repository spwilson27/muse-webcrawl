Bulldog clip

A bulldog clip is a device for temporarily holding sheets of paper together, consisting of a rectangular sheet of springy steel curved into a cylinder, with two flat steel handles. The clip is opened by squeezing the handles together, the papers are inserted, and the spring pressure holds them firmly. In the United States the similar device is usually called a binder clip, though binder clips are typically black with folding wire handles, whereas the classic bulldog clip is all-metal, often nickel-plated.

History

The bulldog clip was patented in 1920 in the United Kingdom by the firm that developed the "Bulldog" trademark, and the name became generic for the type. It was originally marketed for sealing and fastening as well as for holding papers.

Uses

Bulldog clips hold documents, pin notices to boards, close food packages, and serve in crafts and DIY as inexpensive clamps. Larger versions act as the binding mechanism of clipboards and as stand-alone presentation bindings. Their strong spring allows them to grip substantial stacks of paper, and the flat handles can be used to hang the clip from a nail or hook.

Design

The clip is manufactured in sizes measured by the length of the steel barrel, commonly from about 16 mm to over 100 mm. The handles fold flat so clipped documents can be filed or stacked.
