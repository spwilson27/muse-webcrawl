Tourtière is a French Canadian meat pie dish originating from the province of Quebec, traditionally made with finely diced or ground pork, veal, or beef and potatoes, baked in a pastry shell. It is a traditional part of the Christmas réveillon and New Year's Eve meal in Quebec, and is also popular in the Acadian regions of New Brunswick and among Franco-American communities in New England.

Etymology and history

The dish takes its name from the tourte, the vessel in which it was originally cooked; the word is also linked to the tourte, the passenger pigeon once abundant in eastern Canada and used as a filling. Meat pies of this type arrived with French settlers in the 17th century and adapted to local ingredients and cold-climate keeping.

Regional varieties

Recipes vary by region and family. The tourtière of Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean is a deep-dish pie of cubed meats and potatoes baked slowly in a large vessel, quite distinct from the shallow ground-meat pies of Montreal or Quebec City. Seasonings commonly include onion, garlic, and warm spices such as cinnamon, cloves, allspice, and savory. Tourtière is often served with fruit ketchup, pickled beets, or chutney.
