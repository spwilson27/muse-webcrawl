Schedule 13G


SEC ownership disclosure filing

Schedule 13G is a form filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 to disclose beneficial ownership of more than 5% of a class of equity securities of a public company. It is the short-form counterpart to Schedule 13D and is available to investors who acquire their stake without the purpose or effect of changing or influencing control of the issuer.

Eligibility and deadlines

Rule 13d-1 permits certain investors to file on Schedule 13G instead of Schedule 13D, including qualified institutional investors, passive investors holding less than 20% of the class, and investors who acquired their securities before the class was registered. Filing deadlines vary by category: qualified institutions generally file within 45 days after the end of the calendar year (with monthly amendments in specified circumstances), while passive investors must file promptly after crossing thresholds. Amendments are required when ownership changes materially or a filer becomes ineligible.

Contents

The schedule identifies the issuer, the reporting persons, the number of shares beneficially owned, the percentage of the class represented, and certifications that the securities were not acquired for the purpose of influencing control. If a Schedule 13G filer's intentions change toward control, it must switch to Schedule 13D and becomes subject to restrictions on voting and further purchases during a cooling-off period.
