Humboldt Redwoods State Park

Humboldt Redwoods State Park is a state park in Humboldt County, California, United States. It protects over 51,000 acres (21,000 hectares) of forest dominated by old-growth coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens), including the Rockefeller Forest, the largest remaining contiguous stand of old-growth redwoods in the world. The park is administered by the California Department of Parks and Recreation.

History

The park was established in 1921 following the efforts of the Save the Redwoods League, founded in 1918 to preserve rapidly disappearing old-growth redwood groves from logging. The Rockefeller Forest was acquired in part with a major donation from John D. Rockefeller Jr. Today the park contains some 17,000 acres (6,900 ha) of old-growth forest within its boundaries.

Features

The Avenue of the Giants, a 31-mile (50 km) scenic drive paralleling U.S. Route 101, runs through the park along the South Fork of the Eel River. The Founders Grove honors the founders of the Save the Redwoods League. The park offers over 100 miles (160 km) of hiking trails, campgrounds, and swimming and fishing in the Eel River. The park's visitor center is located near Weott.

The park's redwoods include some of the tallest trees on Earth; the Stratosphere Giant, once believed to be the world's tallest tree, stands within the Rockefeller Forest.

Ecology

Besides redwoods, the park supports Douglas-fir, tanoak, madrone, and California bay laurel, along with wildlife including black-tailed deer, black bear, banana slugs, and northern spotted owls in the surrounding region.
