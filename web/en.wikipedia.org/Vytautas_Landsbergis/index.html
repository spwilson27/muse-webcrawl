Vytautas Landsbergis

Chairman of the Supreme Council of Lithuania
In office: 11 March 1990 – 25 November 1992
Born: 18 October 1932, Kaunas, Lithuania
Occupation: Politician, musicologist
Known for: Leader of Sąjūdis; first head of state of restored Lithuania

Vytautas Landsbergis (born 18 October 1932) is a Lithuanian politician and musicologist who led Lithuania's independence movement at the end of the Soviet era. As chairman of the Supreme Council — effectively the first head of state of the restored Republic of Lithuania — he presided over the declaration of independence of 11 March 1990, the first by any Soviet republic.

Early life and scholarship

Landsbergis was born in Kaunas into a prominent family; his father, Vytautas Landsbergis-Žemkalnis, was an architect, and his mother, Ona Jablonskytė-Landsbergienė, an ophthalmologist honoured as Righteous Among the Nations for sheltering a Jewish child during the war. Trained as a pianist at the Lithuanian Academy of Music, he became a professor of musicology, writing extensively on the composer and painter M. K. Čiurlionis and on Lithuanian folk music.

Sąjūdis and independence

In June 1988 Landsbergis was elected to the council of Sąjūdis, the Lithuanian reform movement that rapidly turned toward full independence. After Sąjūdis candidates swept the February 1990 elections to the Supreme Soviet of the Lithuanian SSR, Landsbergis became its chairman. On 11 March 1990 the council proclaimed the Act of the Re-Establishment of the State of Lithuania. Moscow responded with an economic blockade and, in January 1991, military assaults on the Vilnius TV tower and other sites, which killed fourteen civilians. Iceland became the first country to recognise Lithuania's independence, and after the failed August 1991 coup in Moscow, recognition became general.

Later career

Landsbergis co-founded the Homeland Union (Lithuanian Conservatives) in 1993, served as Speaker of the Seimas from 1996 to 2000, and sat as a Member of the European Parliament from 2004 to 2014. He remains an influential and sometimes polarising commentator on Lithuanian politics and security, noted for his hard line toward Russia. His grandson Gabrielius Landsbergis has served as foreign minister of Lithuania.
