Electronic toll collection


An all-electronic toll gantry; vehicles are charged without stopping

Electronic toll collection (ETC) is a wireless system to automatically collect the usage fee or toll charged to vehicles using toll roads, HOV lanes, toll bridges and toll tunnels. It is a faster alternative to toll booths, where vehicles must stop and the driver manually pays the toll with cash or a card; with ETC, vehicles may pass at near-normal speed, reducing congestion and emissions at toll plazas.

Technology

Most systems use radio-frequency identification (RFID) transponders mounted in or on the vehicle — as windshield tags or licence-plate tags — read by antennas at toll points. Others use dedicated short-range communications (DSRC), satellite positioning (GNSS) with on-board units, or automatic number-plate recognition (ANPR) cameras that bill the registered keeper. A back office matches transactions to accounts, applying charges to prepaid balances or postpaid invoices.

Systems worldwide

  • E-ZPass – the largest system in the United States, used across much of the East and Midwest.
  • 407 ETR in Toronto, Canada – opened 1997 as one of the first all-electronic, barrier-free toll motorways.
  • AutoPASS in Norway, Telepass in Italy, Via Verde in Portugal, and Japan's ETC system for expressways.
  • London congestion charge and Stockholm's congestion tax rely on ANPR enforcement.

Issues

Debates around ETC concern privacy (movement data), billing accuracy and penalties for unregistered users, interoperability between regional systems, and the conversion of cash toll plazas to all-electronic operation, which removes toll-collector jobs but eliminates toll-plaza queues and accidents.
