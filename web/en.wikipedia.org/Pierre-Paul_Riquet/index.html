Pierre-Paul Riquet

Born
1609, Béziers, France
Died
1680, Toulouse, France
Known for
Designing and building the Canal du Midi

Pierre-Paul Riquet (1609–1680) was a French engineer and canal builder responsible for the construction of the Canal du Midi, the great waterway linking the Atlantic to the Mediterranean across southern France.

The Canal du Midi

A wealthy tax farmer in Languedoc, Riquet solved the project's central problem — supplying water to the canal's summit level at the Seuil de Naurouze — by diverting streams from the Montagne Noire into the reservoir of Saint-Ferréol, then the largest dam in Europe. Work began in 1667 with royal approval under Louis XIV and his minister Colbert. Riquet spent much of his own fortune on the enterprise and died in 1680, months before the canal opened in 1681.

Legacy

The 240 km canal, with its locks, aqueducts, and tunnels, was one of the greatest engineering achievements of the 17th century and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Statues of Riquet stand at Béziers and along the canal he created.
