Yoyogi National Gymnasium (国立代々木競技場) is an indoor arena in Yoyogi Park, Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan. Designed by the architect Kenzō Tange and completed in 1964 for the Tokyo Summer Olympics, it is celebrated worldwide for its dramatic suspended roof and is regarded as a masterpiece of modernist architecture.

Design

The First Gymnasium consists of a sweeping roof suspended from two massive steel cables strung between concrete pylons, creating a crescent-shaped arena. The structural design by engineer Yoshikatsu Tsuboi drew on suspension-bridge engineering and produced a column-free interior. A smaller Second Gymnasium, with a single spiral mast, houses a subsidiary arena.

History

At the 1964 Games the First Gymnasium hosted swimming and diving, and the Second hosted basketball. The complex later became a major venue for ice hockey, volleyball, handball and concerts; it hosted handball at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics. It is frequently listed among the most beautiful sports buildings of the twentieth century and influenced later stadium design internationally.
