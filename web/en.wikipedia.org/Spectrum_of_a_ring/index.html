Spectrum of a ring

The spectrum of the integers, Spec(ℤ): a generic point for the zero ideal and closed points for each prime.

In commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, the spectrum of a commutative ring R, denoted by Spec(R), is the set of all prime ideals of R, equipped with the Zariski topology and a sheaf of rings, the structure sheaf. The construction, due to Alexander Grothendieck, turns commutative rings into geometric spaces called affine schemes and is the foundation of modern algebraic geometry.

Zariski topology

For an ideal I of R, let V(I) be the set of prime ideals containing I. The closed sets of the Zariski topology are exactly the sets V(I). The closure of a point corresponding to a prime ideal 𝔭 is the set of prime ideals containing 𝔭, so points are closed only if the ideal is maximal; the zero ideal, when R is a domain, is a generic point whose closure is the whole space.

Structure sheaf

The structure sheaf 𝒪 assigns to each open set U the ring of functions on U that locally arise as fractions a/s with a in R and s outside the relevant prime ideals. The stalk of the sheaf at 𝔭 is the localisation R𝔭, a local ring whose maximal ideal corresponds to the point itself. Distinguished open sets D(f), consisting of primes not containing f, form a basis, and sections over D(f) are the localisation R[1/f].

Examples

Spec(ℂ[x]) — prime ideals (x − a) for each a ∈ ℂ, plus the zero ideal: the affine line.

Spec(ℤ) consists of the zero ideal and the ideals (p) for each prime number p, giving an arithmetic analogue of a curve. For a field k, Spec(k) is a single point. The functor Spec gives a contravariant equivalence between commutative rings and affine schemes, with morphisms Spec(S) → Spec(R) corresponding to ring homomorphisms R → S.
