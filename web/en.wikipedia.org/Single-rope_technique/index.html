Single-rope technique

Single-rope technique (SRT) is a set of methods used to descend and ascend on a single rope. SRT is used in caving, potholing, rock climbing, canyoning, and rope access work.

Techniques

Descent is made with a friction device such as a rack, bobbin descender, or stop descender, which allows controlled speed and stopping. Ascent uses mechanical ascenders — typically a hand ascender and a chest ascender — in systems such as the "frog" sit-stand method common in European caving, or rope-walking systems common in the United States. A safety backup and cowstails are used when passing rebelays and deviations.

History

Mechanical ascending devices were pioneered in the 1930s by Henri Brenot and developed in France and the United States. SRT largely replaced ladders in deep caves during the mid-twentieth century, enabling exploration of very deep systems such as those in the Alps and Mexico.

Rigging

SRT ropes are rigged to avoid rub points, using rebelays, deviations, and rope protectors. European practice commonly uses thinner semi-static rope and places bolts or natural anchors so a single rope suffices for each pitch.
