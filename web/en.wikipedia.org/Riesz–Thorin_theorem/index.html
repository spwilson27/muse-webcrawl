Riesz–Thorin theorem

The Riesz–Thorin theorem, often called the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem or the Riesz–Thorin convexity theorem, is a result in functional analysis, proved by Marcel Riesz in 1927 and reformulated by his student Olof Thorin in 1938. It bounds the norm of a linear operator acting between Lp spaces in terms of its bounds at two endpoint pairs of exponents.

Interpolation between two known bounds yields bounds at every intermediate pair of exponents.

Statement

Suppose T is a linear operator defined on simple functions, bounded from Lp0 to Lq0 with norm M0, and from Lp1 to Lq1 with norm M1. For 0 < θ < 1 define

1/pθ = (1−θ)/p0 + θ/p1,   1/qθ = (1−θ)/q0 + θ/q1.

Then T extends to a bounded operator from Lpθ to Lqθ with norm at most M01−θM1θ.

Proof and applications

Thorin's proof uses complex analysis, in particular the Hadamard three-lines theorem applied to an analytic family of functions. Standard applications include the Hausdorff–Young inequality for the Fourier transform, boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, and Young's inequality for convolution. It is the prototype of the general theory of interpolation spaces, later extended by the real interpolation method of Marcinkiewicz.
