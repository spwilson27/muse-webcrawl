Battle of Camarón

Date30 April 1863
LocationCamarón de Tejeda, Veracruz, Mexico
ResultMexican victory; French convoy reached Puebla
BelligerentsFrance (French Foreign Legion) vs. Mexico
Strength65 legionnaires vs. c. 1,200–2,000 Mexican troops

The Battle of Camarón (Batalla de Camarón) was fought on 30 April 1863 during the French intervention in Mexico, at the village of Camarón de Tejeda in Veracruz. A company of the French Foreign Legion, escorting a convoy carrying supplies, ammunition, and three million francs in gold for the French army besieging Puebla, was attacked by a far larger Mexican force and fought to near-annihilation. The battle became the founding legend of the French Foreign Legion.

The fight

The 3rd Company of the Foreign Regiment, reduced by sickness to 3 officers and 62 men under Captain Jean Danjou, encountered Mexican cavalry and infantry under Colonel Francisco de Paula Milán near Palo Verde. Danjou withdrew into the Hacienda Camarón, an inn surrounded by a courtyard wall, intending to delay the Mexicans so the convoy could escape. Surrounded, the legionnaires refused repeated demands to surrender. Danjou was killed in the early afternoon; his successors, Lieutenant Vilain and then Second Lieutenant Maudet, also fell. By evening the survivors were out of ammunition and water, and the last five able men fixed bayonets and charged, preferring death to surrender. They were spared by the Mexican commander, who is said to have exclaimed that these were not men but demons.

Aftermath and commemoration

French Legion casualties were extreme: dozens killed and the rest wounded, nearly all captured, though prisoner exchanges later freed many. The convoy reached Puebla safely. The Legion commemorates Camarón Day every 30 April as its most sacred anniversary; Captain Danjou's wooden prosthetic hand, recovered from the battlefield, is the Legion's most precious relic, paraded annually at Aubagne. In Mexico, the battle is likewise honoured as an example of Mexican arms and of the honour shown to a brave enemy.
