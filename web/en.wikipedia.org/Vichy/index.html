Vichy
Commune in Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, France
Population: about 25,000
Known for: mineral springs, spa town

Vichy is a commune in the Allier department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of central France. It lies on the banks of the River Allier and is famous as a spa town whose mineral waters have been celebrated since Roman times. During the Second World War it lent its name to the French State of Marshal Philippe Pétain — "Vichy France" — which governed the unoccupied zone from 1940 to 1944.

History

The thermal springs of Vichy were known to the Romans, who called the settlement Aquae Calidae. The town's great age as a fashionable resort came in the 19th century under Napoleon III, who visited repeatedly and commissioned parks, promenades and the grand casino. In July 1940, after France's defeat, the National Assembly voted full powers to Pétain, and Vichy became the seat of the collaborationist regime. After the war the town returned to its role as a spa and holiday resort.

The waters

Vichy's springs, including the celebrated Célestins and Grande Grille sources, produce naturally carbonated mineral water rich in bicarbonate of soda. Bottled Vichy water and the town's pastilles — mint lozenges made with salts from the springs — are known throughout France.
