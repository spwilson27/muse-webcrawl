Quantum system

A quantum system is a physical system — such as an atom, a molecule, or a collection of particles — whose behaviour must be described by quantum mechanics rather than classical physics. Its state is represented by a vector (or, for mixed states, a density matrix) in a Hilbert space, and measurable quantities correspond to Hermitian operators.

Description

An isolated quantum system evolves unitarily under the Schrödinger equation; an open quantum system also interacts with its environment, leading to decoherence and dissipation described by master equations such as the Lindblad equation. Composite systems are described by tensor products of the state spaces of their parts, which permits entanglement.

Examples

Examples range from a single electron in a magnetic field (a qubit) and the hydrogen atom to superconducting circuits, trapped ions and Bose–Einstein condensates engineered as controllable quantum systems.
