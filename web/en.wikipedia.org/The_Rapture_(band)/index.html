The Rapture (band)

OriginNew York City, United States
GenresDance-punk, post-punk revival, indie rock
Years active1998–2014, 2019–present
LabelsDFA, Sub Pop, Vertigo, Modular

The Rapture is an American rock band from New York City, formed in 1998. Along with acts such as LCD Soundsystem, they were central to the dance-punk movement of the early 2000s, fusing post-punk guitar rock with house, disco and funk rhythms.

History

The band was founded by singer-guitarist Luke Jenner and drummer Vito Roccoforte, who had met in San Diego before relocating to New York. Their 2002 single "House of Jealous Lovers," produced with the then-new DFA production team of James Murphy and Tim Goldsworthy, became a club and indie landmark — widely credited with igniting the dance-punk revival — and introduced the cowbell-heavy sound associated with the DFA label.

Their debut album Echoes (2003) was a critical success, praised for tracks such as "Sister Saviour" and "Love Is All." Subsequent albums Pieces of the People We Love (2006) and In the Grace of Your Love (2011) incorporated disco, gospel and soul influences. Bassist and vocalist Gabriel Andruzzi and multi-instrumentalist Matt Safer were key members; Safer departed in 2009. The band quietly ceased activity around 2014 before reuniting for live shows in 2019.

Style and legacy

The Rapture's sound pairs angular guitar, insistent basslines and punk energy with four-on-the-floor dance beats, cowbell, saxophone and Jenner's falsetto vocals, drawing on influences from Gang of Four and Public Image Ltd to Chicago house. Their work helped define the sound of 2000s indie dancefloors and bridged rock and club cultures in the years before EDM's mainstream breakthrough.
