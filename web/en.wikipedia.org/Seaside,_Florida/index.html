Seaside, Florida

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Seaside's pastel cottages and picket fences are hallmarks of New Urbanist design.

Seaside is an unincorporated master-planned community on the Florida Panhandle in Walton County, between Panama City Beach and Destin. Founded in 1981 by developer Robert S. Davis on land inherited from his grandfather, Seaside is one of the first and best-known examples of the New Urbanism movement in the United States.

The town was designed by architects Andrés Duany and Elizabeth Plater-Zyberk of the Miami firm DPZ. Its plan revived traditional town-building principles: a walkable street grid, a central town square, narrow streets, front porches, white picket fences, and a strict design code requiring houses to be built in vernacular Florida styles with pastel colors. Cars are de-emphasized and most daily needs are within walking distance.

History

Robert Davis inherited the 80-acre parcel in 1978 and, inspired by memories of childhood summers spent in traditional Florida beach towns, set out to recreate that atmosphere. Construction began in 1981. Seaside grew gradually, cottage by cottage, and its commercial center developed around a town green and amphitheater. The community gained worldwide attention as a model for traditional neighborhood development and influenced hundreds of later New Urbanist projects.

In popular culture

Seaside was the primary filming location for the 1998 film The Truman Show, starring Jim Carrey, in which it served as the fictional town of Seahaven. The town's picture-perfect appearance made it an ideal stand-in for the artificial world of the film.

Design and architecture

The Seaside code mandates features such as metal roofs, screened porches, and towers, and requires that no two adjacent houses be painted the same color. The plan includes a network of sandy footpaths connecting beach pavilions — one for each street — nine in total, each designed by a different architect. The community contains several hundred homes, along with shops, restaurants, a chapel, and an amphitheater hosting concerts and events.
