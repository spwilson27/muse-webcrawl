eIDAS

eIDAS (electronic IDentification, Authentication and trust Services) is an EU regulation — Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 — that establishes a framework for electronic identification and trust services for electronic transactions in the European single market. It was adopted in July 2014 and replaced the earlier eSignature Directive.

Provisions

eIDAS requires member states to recognise notified national electronic identification schemes from other member states, enabling cross-border access to public services. It defines trust services including electronic signatures, seals, timestamps, registered delivery services and website authentication certificates. A qualified electronic signature has the equivalent legal effect of a handwritten signature throughout the EU.

Trust services and supervision

Qualified trust service providers are supervised by national bodies and included in trusted lists, allowing relying parties to verify the status of certificates and signatures across borders.

eIDAS 2.0

In 2024 the EU adopted an amending regulation establishing the European Digital Identity framework, under which member states must offer a European Digital Identity Wallet allowing citizens to store credentials and prove identity throughout the Union, building on the original eIDAS system.
