The Port of South Louisiana is a river port district on the lower Mississippi River in the U.S. state of Louisiana. Stretching about 54 miles along both banks of the Mississippi River between New Orleans and Baton Rouge, it is one of the largest tonnage ports in the United States and in the Western Hemisphere, frequently ranked first in the nation by total cargo tonnage.

Overview

The port district encompasses facilities in St. Charles, St. John the Baptist, and St. James parishes. Its headquarters are in LaPlace. The port's jurisdiction contains numerous privately owned industrial docks, grain elevators, petrochemical plants, and refineries, as well as public terminals owned by the port commission itself.

Cargo

The port handles enormous volumes of bulk cargo: grain and agricultural products barged down the Mississippi from the Midwest for export, crude oil and petroleum products, chemicals, steel, and fertilizers. Its grain elevators are among the most important export facilities for American corn, soybeans, and wheat. Annual tonnage has exceeded 250 million short tons in strong years, much of it transferred between barges and ocean-going vessels at mid-stream moorings.

History

The Port of South Louisiana was created by the Louisiana legislature in 1960 to promote commerce along the river corridor between the state's two largest cities. Industrial development along the stretch — sometimes called the "Chemical Corridor" or "Cancer Alley" farther north — has made the district a focal point both of economic activity and of environmental debate.
