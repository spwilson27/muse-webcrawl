First Nations in Canada

Dancers at a First Nations powwow.

First Nations (French: Premières Nations) is a term used to identify Indigenous peoples in Canada who are neither Inuit nor Métis. Traditionally, First Nations in Canada were peoples who lived south of the tree line, and mainly south of the Arctic Circle. There are over 630 First Nations communities in Canada, which represent more than 50 nations and 50 Indigenous languages. According to the 2021 census, more than 1 million people in Canada identify as First Nations.

Terminology and legal status

The term "First Nations" came into common use in the 1970s and 1980s, replacing "Indian" in many contexts, though the legal term "Indian" remains in the Indian Act and the Constitution. Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982 recognises and affirms the existing aboriginal and treaty rights of the Aboriginal peoples of Canada. First Nations people may hold legal "status" under the Indian Act, with implications for band membership, reserves, and certain rights and benefits; others are non-status.

History

First Nations peoples have inhabited what is now Canada for many thousands of years, developing diverse societies, languages, and trade networks. Following European contact, relations were shaped by the fur trade, alliances, and treaties, but also by disease, dispossession, and colonisation. Policies of assimilation included the reserve system, the pass system, bans on ceremonies such as the potlatch, and the residential school system, which removed children from their families and caused lasting harm documented by the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (2008–2015).

Contemporary issues

First Nations governments exercise varying degrees of self-government, and modern treaties and land-claims agreements continue to be negotiated. Major public issues include land and resource rights, child welfare, access to clean drinking water on reserves, housing, and the implementation of the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples. The National Day for Truth and Reconciliation is observed on 30 September.
