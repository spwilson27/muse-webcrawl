Philippine Military Academy

Philippine Military Academy
Motto: Courage, Loyalty, Integrity
Established: October 25, 1898 (as Academia Militar)
Location: Fort Gregorio H. del Pilar, Baguio
Type: Military academy

The Philippine Military Academy (PMA) is the premier military training institution of the Philippines, producing the officer corps of the Armed Forces of the Philippines. Located at Fort Gregorio H. del Pilar in Loakan, Baguio City, the academy is sometimes called the "West Point of the Philippines" because of its origins in the American colonial period and its long-course curriculum modelled partly on the United States Military Academy.

History

The academy's lineage traces to the Academia Militar founded in Malolos on October 25, 1898, during the Philippine Revolution, under General Emilio Aguinaldo. The institution was re-established by the Philippine Constabulary on February 17, 1905, in Intramuros, Manila, and was transferred to Baguio in 1908. The National Defense Act of 1935 formally created the Philippine Military Academy, extending the course to four years.

During the Second World War the academy was dissolved and its cadets and officers fought in Bataan and elsewhere; it reopened after the war and moved to its present site at Fort del Pilar in 1947. Women were first admitted to the corps of cadets in 1993.

Academics and training

Cadets — known as "plebes" in their first year — complete a four-year Bachelor of Science program combining academic subjects with military science, physical training and leadership development. Graduates are commissioned as second lieutenants or ensigns in the Army, Air Force, or Navy. Admission is by nationwide competitive examination for Filipino citizens aged 17 to 22.
