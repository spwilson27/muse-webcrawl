Identity theorem

Two holomorphic functions agreeing on a set with a limit point agree everywhere on their domain.

In complex analysis, the identity theorem for holomorphic functions states that if two holomorphic functions f and g defined on a connected open subset D of the complex plane agree on a subset of D that has a limit point inside D, then f and g are identical on all of D. In particular, a holomorphic function is completely determined by its values on any arbitrarily small neighborhood, or even on any convergent sequence of distinct points.

Proof idea

The theorem follows from expanding the difference h = f − g in a power series around the limit point: because the zeros of h accumulate there, every derivative of h at that point must vanish, so the series is identically zero in a disk, and connectedness propagates this equality across the whole domain by an open-and-closed argument.

Consequences

The identity theorem is the foundation of analytic continuation: it guarantees that any continuation of a holomorphic function along different paths to the same point must give the same value, so extensions are unique. It also underlies identity theorems for Riemann surfaces and for holomorphic functions of several complex variables, and it contrasts sharply with smooth real functions, which can agree on an interval yet differ elsewhere.
