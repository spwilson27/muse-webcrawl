Aventine Triad

The Aventine Triad (also known as the plebeian Triad or the agricultural Triad) is a modern term for the joint cult of the Roman deities Ceres, Liber and Libera. The cult was established around 493 BC on or near the Aventine Hill in Rome. It was closely associated with the plebeians — the common citizens of Rome — in contrast to the patrician Capitoline Triad of Jupiter, Juno and Minerva.

Origins

According to tradition, the cult was founded during a famine, following the advice of the Sibylline Books and the secession of the plebs. The temple of Ceres, Liber and Libera was vowed by the dictator Aulus Postumius in 496 BC and dedicated in 493 BC on the slope of the Aventine overlooking the Circus Maximus. The triad reflected Greek influence: Ceres corresponded to Demeter, Liber to Dionysus, and Libera to Persephone (or Ariadne).

Plebeian association

The temple became the headquarters of the plebeian aediles, and its archive stored the plebiscites and, later, senatorial decrees. The cult and temple thus embodied plebeian political identity during the Conflict of the Orders. The grain supply of Rome (the annona) was also tied to Ceres, reinforcing the triad's connection with the common people and their food supply.
