1984 anti-Sikh riots

The 1984 anti-Sikh riots, also known as the 1984 Sikh massacre, were a series of organised pogroms against Sikhs in India following the assassination of Indira Gandhi by her Sikh bodyguards on 31 October 1984. The violence was triggered in response to the assassination, which itself followed Operation Blue Star, the Indian military action at the Golden Temple in Amritsar.

Government estimates released decades later recorded about 2,800 deaths across India, including about 2,100 in Delhi; independent sources estimate the number of deaths at about 8,000 to 17,000. The Central Bureau of Investigation later concluded that the violence was well organised, and several political leaders were accused of involvement, though few were convicted.

Aftermath

Successive commissions of inquiry, most notably the Nanavati Commission, investigated the riots. In 2018, former Congress leader Sajjan Kumar was convicted and sentenced to life imprisonment by the Delhi High Court, one of the most significant convictions arising from the violence.
