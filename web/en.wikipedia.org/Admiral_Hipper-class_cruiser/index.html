The Admiral Hipper class was a group of five heavy cruisers built by Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine in the mid-1930s. The class comprised Admiral Hipper, the lead ship, Blücher, Prinz Eugen, Seydlitz and Lützow. Only the first three ships of the class saw action with the Kriegsmarine during World War II. Work on Seydlitz was halted when she was approximately 95 percent complete; it was decided to convert her into an aircraft carrier, but this was not completed either. Lützow was sold incomplete to the Soviet Union in 1940.

Design

The ships were designed within the nominal 10,000-ton limit of the Anglo-German Naval Agreement, but in practice displaced considerably more. They were armed with eight 20.3 cm (8 in) guns in four twin turrets, a heavy anti-aircraft battery of twelve 10.5 cm guns, and twelve torpedo tubes. Armour protection included a 70–80 mm main belt. Their high-pressure steam propulsion plants proved troublesome in service, and their range was more limited than intended.

Service history

Admiral Hipper participated in the invasion of Norway in April 1940, leading the group that fought its way into Oslofjord, during which Blücher was sunk by Norwegian coastal defences. Admiral Hipper later conducted commerce-raiding sorties into the Atlantic and operations in the Barents Sea, including the Battle of the Barents Sea in December 1942. Prinz Eugen famously accompanied the battleship Bismarck on Operation Rheinübung in May 1941 and survived the war, later being expended in atomic bomb tests at Bikini Atoll. Admiral Hipper was scuttled in May 1945.

Ships in class

  • Admiral Hipper — launched 1937, scuttled 1945.
  • Blücher — launched 1937, sunk 9 April 1940 in Oslofjord.
  • Prinz Eugen — launched 1938, surrendered 1945, sunk after atomic tests 1946.
  • Seydlitz — launched 1939, never completed, scuttled 1945.
  • Lützow — sold to the USSR as Petropavlovsk, later Tallinn.
