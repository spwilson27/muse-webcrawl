Dynamic range

Dynamic range is the ratio between the largest and smallest values a system can represent or reproduce, usually expressed in decibels (dB). It is used in audio, photography, electronics and signal processing generally.

Audio

In audio, dynamic range describes the difference between the loudest undistorted signal and the noise floor. Human hearing spans roughly 120–140 dB; a CD provides about 96 dB. Compression of dynamic range is widely used in recording and broadcasting.

Photography

For cameras, dynamic range is the ratio between the brightest and darkest tones a sensor can capture in a single exposure, often measured in stops. Higher dynamic range allows detail to be retained in both shadows and highlights, and is a basis of high-dynamic-range (HDR) imaging.

Measurement

Dynamic range is computed as 20·log₁₀ of the ratio of amplitudes (or 10·log₁₀ for power ratios) and depends on quantization, noise and distortion characteristics of the device.
