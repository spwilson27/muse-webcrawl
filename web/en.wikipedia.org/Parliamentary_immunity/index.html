Parliamentary immunity
Forms: Non-liability (speech) and inviolability (arrest)
Purpose: Protect legislators' independence

Parliamentary immunity is a legal protection granted to members of a legislature that shields them, in varying degrees, from prosecution or civil suit so they can perform their duties without intimidation.

Forms

Parliamentary non-liability (or privilege of speech) absolutely protects members for votes cast and words spoken in parliament — as in Article 9 of the English Bill of Rights 1689 and the Speech or Debate Clause of the US Constitution. Parliamentary inviolability, common in continental European systems, protects members against arrest or prosecution for acts outside parliament unless the chamber lifts the immunity, and is therefore relative rather than absolute.

Criticism

Broad immunity has sometimes been criticised for sheltering corruption; many countries have narrowed its scope or seen high-profile votes to lift a member's immunity.
