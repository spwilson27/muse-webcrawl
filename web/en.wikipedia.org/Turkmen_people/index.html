The Turkmens are a Turkic ethnic group native to Central Asia, living mainly in Turkmenistan and in adjacent areas of Iran, Afghanistan and Uzbekistan. They speak Turkmen, an Oghuz Turkic language, and are traditionally known for their nomadic and semi-nomadic pastoralism, horsemanship and carpet weaving.

History

Turkmens descend largely from the Oghuz Turks who migrated westward from the Altai region in the early Middle Ages. Turkmen tribes came under Persian, Arab and Mongol influence in turn; in the 19th century the Russian Empire conquered their lands after battles such as Geok Tepe (1881). Under Soviet rule the Turkmen Soviet Socialist Republic was formed in 1925, and Turkmenistan became independent in 1991.

Culture

Traditional society was organised into tribes — including the Teke, Yomut, Ersari, Salyr and Saryk — whose distinctive guls (medallions) appear on hand-knotted Turkmen carpets, prized worldwide. The Akhal-Teke horse, bred in Turkmenistan, is famed for its endurance and metallic sheen. The epic tradition of Dede Korkut and the poetry of Magtymguly Pyragy hold central places in Turkmen identity.

Diaspora

Significant Turkmen communities live in northeastern Iran (Golestan province), northern Afghanistan and the Stavropol region of Russia, with a smaller diaspora in Turkey and the West.
