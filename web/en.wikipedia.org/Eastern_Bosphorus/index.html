Eastern Bosphorus

Eastern Bosphorus
Russian: Босфор Восточный
Location: Vladivostok, Russia
Coordinates: Primorsky Krai, Russian Far East
Connects: Amur Bay and Ussuri Bay

The Eastern Bosphorus (Russian: Босфор Восточный, Bosfor Vostochny) is a strait in the Russian Far East that separates the Muravyov-Amursky Peninsula, on which the city of Vladivostok stands, from Russky Island. It connects Amur Bay to the west with Ussuri Bay to the east, both part of Peter the Great Gulf in the Sea of Japan.

Geography

The strait is about 9 kilometres long and narrows to roughly 800 metres at its narrowest point. It is named after the Bosphorus strait at Istanbul, by analogy with other features in the region named in the 19th century.

Russky Bridge

The strait is spanned by the Russky Bridge, completed in 2012 in time for the APEC summit held on Russky Island. At its opening it was the world's longest cable-stayed bridge, with a central span of 1,104 metres and pylons 321 metres tall.
