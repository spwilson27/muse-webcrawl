Benetton Group

Benetton Group S.r.l. is a global fashion brand based in Ponzano Veneto, Italy, founded in 1965 by the Benetton family. The company operates a network of roughly 4,000 stores worldwide under the brands United Colors of Benetton and Sisley. It became internationally famous for the brightly coloured knitwear that made the "United Colors" name a byword for casual fashion in the 1980s and 1990s, and for its provocative advertising campaigns under creative director Oliviero Toscani, which addressed racism, war, AIDS, and capital punishment. The company is controlled by the Benetton family's holding company Edizione.

History

In 1955 Luciano Benetton, then a 20-year-old salesman, noticed that his sister Giuliana's brightly coloured hand-knitted sweaters attracted attention; the siblings began selling knitwear, pioneering the practice of dyeing garments after knitting to respond quickly to fashion trends. The business expanded through franchising, requiring little capital from the family, and by the 1980s Benetton was one of the world's largest casual-wear companies. It sponsored the Benetton Formula One team, which won constructors' championships in 1995 with Michael Schumacher before being sold to Renault.

Advertising and controversy

From 1982, photographer Oliviero Toscani created campaigns that largely omitted the products themselves, instead showing images such as a newborn baby, a priest and nun kissing, and the deathbed photograph of AIDS activist David Kirby. The campaigns won awards and provoked boycotts and lawsuits; Toscani left in 2000 after a campaign featuring death-row inmates angered victims' groups and retail partners.

Recent years

The group faced declining sales and competition from fast-fashion rivals such as Zara and H&M. It was delisted from the Milan stock exchange in 2012, and the family has repeatedly restructured the business, with Luciano Benetton returning as chairman in 2018.
