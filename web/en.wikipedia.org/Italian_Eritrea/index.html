Italian Eritrea

Asmara, the capital, was remodelled with Italian modernist architecture in the 1930s.

Italian Eritrea (Italian: Colonia Eritrea, "Colony of Eritrea") was a colony of the Kingdom of Italy in the territory of present-day Eritrea. Formed from Italian possessions around the Red Sea port of Massawa, it was officially proclaimed on 1 January 1890 and named after the Greek name for the Red Sea (Erythra Thalassa).

History

Italian expansion began when the Rubattino shipping company bought the bay of Assab in 1869–70, which the Italian state took over in 1882; Italian forces occupied Massawa in 1885 and pushed inland, founding Asmara. Defeat by Ethiopian forces at the Battle of Adwa in 1896 fixed the colony's borders. Under Fascism in the 1930s, Eritrea became the staging base for the invasion of Ethiopia in 1935–36 and was merged into Italian East Africa (Africa Orientale Italiana). Infrastructure, roads, railways and industry were developed, largely to serve Italian settlement and the military.

End of the colony

British forces conquered Eritrea in 1941 during the East African campaign. After a period of British administration, the territory was federated with Ethiopia in 1952 under a UN resolution; Eritrea eventually won independence in 1993 after a thirty-year war. Traces of the colonial era remain visible in Asmara's architecture, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
