Ross and Cromarty is a former county and current lieutenancy area in the Highlands of Scotland. It was formed in 1889 by merging the historic counties of Ross-shire and Cromartyshire — the latter famously consisting of the small town of Cromarty and numerous scattered enclaves within Ross-shire. The county's administrative centre was Dingwall.

Geography

The area stretches from the North Sea coast in the east — including the Black Isle, the Cromarty Firth and towns such as Dingwall, Invergordon and Tain — across mountainous Highland terrain to the Atlantic west coast, including Wester Ross, Applecross and Gairloch, and includes the Isle of Lewis in the Outer Hebrides (historically part of Ross-shire). Much of the landscape is rugged glens, lochs and moorland, with a low population density.

History

Ross was an ancient earldom and province. As a local government county, Ross and Cromarty existed from 1890 until 1975, when it was absorbed into the Highland region (with Lewis going to the Western Isles). The name survives in the lieutenancy area, in constituencies such as Ross, Skye and Lochaber and Caithness, Sutherland and Easter Ross, and in local usage, particularly for Wester Ross and Easter Ross.
