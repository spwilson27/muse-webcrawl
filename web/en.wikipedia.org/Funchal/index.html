Funchal is the capital and largest city of the Portuguese Autonomous Region of Madeira, located on the south coast of Madeira Island in the Atlantic Ocean. Its name derives from funcho, the Portuguese word for fennel, which grew abundantly where Portuguese settlers led by João Gonçalves Zarco established the settlement in the early 15th century.

Geography and climate

The city rises in a natural amphitheatre from its harbour up steep volcanic slopes to about 800 metres, giving many districts sweeping ocean views. Funchal enjoys a mild Mediterranean-influenced subtropical climate, with average temperatures around 20 °C year-round, which made it a favoured winter resort for European elites in the 19th century — visitors included Winston Churchill and Empress Elisabeth ("Sisi") of Austria.

Attractions

Highlights include the Mercado dos Lavradores (farmers' market), the 15th-century cathedral (Sé), the Monte Palace gardens reached by cable car, and the famous carros de cesto wicker toboggans that carry tourists down from Monte. Funchal's New Year's Eve fireworks display, staged around the bay, was once recognised by Guinness World Records as the largest in the world. It is also the birthplace of footballer Cristiano Ronaldo, honoured with a museum and the airport's name.
