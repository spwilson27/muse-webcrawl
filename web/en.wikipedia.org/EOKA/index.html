EOKA

EOKA (Greek: ΕΟΚΑ), from the initials of Ethniki Organosis Kyprion Agoniston (National Organisation of Cypriot Fighters), was a Greek Cypriot nationalist guerrilla organisation that fought a campaign for the end of British rule in Cyprus and for enosis (union with Greece) between 1955 and 1959. It was led by Colonel Georgios Grivas, a Greek Cypriot officer of the Greek Army, under the political leadership of Archbishop Makarios III.

The campaign, 1955–1959

EOKA launched its armed campaign on 1 April 1955 with bomb attacks on government and military targets. The British responded by declaring a state of emergency, deploying large numbers of troops, and exiling Makarios to the Seychelles in 1956. EOKA fighters, based largely in the Troodos Mountains and in urban cells, carried out ambushes, bombings and assassinations of British personnel and Greek Cypriot opponents, while British forces conducted large-scale search operations and imposed curfews and collective punishments. The conflict also deepened tensions between the Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot communities, and the Turkish Cypriot organisation TMT was formed in response.

Outcome

The campaign ended with the London–Zürich Agreements of February 1959, which established Cyprus as an independent republic — falling short of enosis — with Britain retaining sovereign base areas at Akrotiri and Dhekelia. Cyprus became independent on 16 August 1960 with Makarios as its first president. A successor organisation, EOKA B, was formed in 1971 and took part in the 1974 coup against Makarios that precipitated the Turkish invasion of Cyprus.
