Port of San Pedro

The Port of San Pedro (French: Port de San-Pédro) is a seaport in southwestern Côte d'Ivoire, on the Gulf of Guinea about 350 kilometres west of Abidjan. Opened in 1971, it is the country's second port and one of the largest cocoa-exporting ports in the world, serving the agricultural hinterland of western Côte d'Ivoire and landlocked Mali and Guinea.

San Pedro handles much of the world's cocoa exports.

History and role

Developed under President Félix Houphouët-Boigny as part of the ARSO regional development plan to open up the Ivorian southwest, the port was designed to export cocoa, coffee, timber, rubber, and palm oil from the fertile San Pedro region. Côte d'Ivoire produces around 40 percent of the world's cocoa, and San Pedro has at times shipped more cocoa beans than any other port. Facilities include bulk and container terminals, a fishing port, and storage for exports; expansion projects have added capacity to relieve congestion at Abidjan and support mining exports, including iron ore and nickel from the west.

City and region

The port stimulated the growth of the town of San Pedro into a city of several hundred thousand inhabitants, with planned industrial zones and tourism along nearby beaches such as Grand-Béréby. The Port of San Pedro is managed by the Port Autonome de San Pedro (PASP), an Ivorian state enterprise.
