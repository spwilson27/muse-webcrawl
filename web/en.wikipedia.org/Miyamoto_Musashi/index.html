Miyamoto Musashi

Miyamoto Musashi
Born: c. 1584, Japan
Died: 13 June 1645 (aged c. 61)
Occupation: swordsman, strategist, writer, artist
Known for: The Book of Five Rings; undefeated in 61 duels

Miyamoto Musashi (Japanese: 宮本 武蔵; c. 1584 – 13 June 1645), also known as Shinmen Takezō, was a Japanese swordsman, philosopher, strategist, writer, and rōnin, who became renowned through stories of his unique double-bladed swordsmanship and undefeated record in 61 duels. He is considered a kensei (sword-saint) of Japan.

Life and duels

Musashi fought his first duel at thirteen, defeating the samurai Arima Kihei. In 1604 he fought a celebrated series of duels against the Yoshioka school of Kyoto, emerging victorious. His most famous duel took place in 1612 against Sasaki Kojirō on the island of Ganryūjima: arriving deliberately late, Musashi killed Kojirō with a wooden sword he had carved from an oar during the boat ride to the island. He founded the Niten Ichi-ryū school, teaching the use of two swords simultaneously — the long katana and the short wakizashi.

The Book of Five Rings

In his final years Musashi retired to the Reigandō cave in Kumamoto, where he wrote The Book of Five Rings (Go Rin no Sho), a treatise on strategy, tactics, and the philosophy of martial arts organised into five books named for earth, water, fire, wind, and the void. Completed shortly before his death in 1645, it remains widely read, including by business readers as a text on competition and strategy. He also authored the Dokkōdō, a short list of 21 precepts for life.

Legacy

Musashi is a towering figure in Japanese popular culture, the subject of Eiji Yoshikawa's novel Musashi, the manga Vagabond, numerous films (including the Samurai Trilogy starring Toshiro Mifune), and countless other works. He was also an accomplished ink painter and craftsman in his later years.
