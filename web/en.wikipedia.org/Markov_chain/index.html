Markov chain

A Markov chain is a stochastic process describing a sequence of possible events in which the probability of each event depends only on the state attained in the previous event — a property called the Markov property, sometimes summarised as "memorylessness".

Definition

A discrete-time Markov chain is defined by a set of states and a transition matrix giving the probability of moving from each state to each other state. The chain evolves by successive application of the transition probabilities. Andrey Markov introduced the concept in 1906 while studying sequences of dependent random variables.

Properties

Key questions concern irreducibility (whether every state can reach every other), aperiodicity, recurrence and transience of states, and the existence of a stationary distribution — a probability distribution unchanged by the transition matrix. Ergodic chains converge to a unique stationary distribution regardless of the starting state.

Applications

Markov chains model weather patterns, queues, population genetics, board games, speech recognition (via hidden Markov models), search-engine ranking (PageRank is the stationary distribution of a Markov chain over web pages), and statistical physics. Sampling methods based on chains include Markov chain Monte Carlo.
