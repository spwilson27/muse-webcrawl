Traditional medicine

Traditional medicine is the sum total of the knowledge, skills and practices based on the theories, beliefs and experiences indigenous to different cultures, used in the maintenance of health and in the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of illness. The World Health Organization estimates that a large share of the world's population relies on traditional medicine for some part of its primary health care.

Major traditions

The best-known systems include traditional Chinese medicine (with acupuncture, herbal medicine and concepts such as qi and yin and yang), Ayurveda from India, Unani medicine from the Greco-Arab tradition, and numerous African and indigenous American healing practices. Herbal remedies drawn from these traditions have yielded modern drugs such as artemisinin, derived from sweet wormwood.

Regulation and evidence

Traditional practices vary widely in their evidentiary support. Some herbal treatments have been validated in clinical trials, while others are ineffective or unsafe. Health authorities generally urge caution: traditional products can interact with conventional drugs, and quality control is inconsistent in many markets.
