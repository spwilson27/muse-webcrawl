Prodrazvyorstka

Prodrazvyorstka (Russian: продразвёрстка, short for продовольственная развёрстка, "food apportionment levy") was the policy and campaign of confiscating grain and other agricultural production from peasants at nominal fixed prices according to specified quotas, practised in Soviet Russia during the Russian Civil War as part of War Communism.

History

The Imperial Russian government had introduced a limited grain levy in 1916 amid First World War shortages; the Provisional Government continued it, and the Bolsheviks expanded it radically after taking power. From 1919, procurement detachments (prodotryady) travelled to villages to seize "surpluses", which in practice often included seed grain and the peasants' own subsistence. Quotas were set centrally, often without regard to local output.

Consequences

Peasants responded by hiding grain, reducing sown area, or resisting — most dramatically in the Tambov Rebellion of 1920–21 and unrest in western Siberia. Combined with drought, requisitioning contributed to the catastrophic famine of 1921–22, which killed millions. Together with the Kronstadt rebellion, the crisis convinced Lenin to retreat: in March 1921 the Tenth Party Congress replaced prodrazvyorstka with the prodnalog, a fixed tax in kind that left peasants free to sell their surplus — the centrepiece of the New Economic Policy.
