Polyrhythm

Polyrhythm is the simultaneous use of two or more rhythms that are not readily perceived as deriving from one another, or as simple manifestations of the same meter. The rhythmic layers may be distinguished by different divisions of the beat, such as three against two.

Characteristics

The most common polyrhythm is three against two (hemiola-like groupings), where one voice plays three evenly spaced notes in the time another plays two. More complex ratios such as 5:4 or 7:3 occur in contemporary classical and progressive music.

Traditions

Polyrhythm is a foundation of much sub-Saharan African music, where interlocking drum patterns create a composite rhythm. It is also prominent in Indian classical music, jazz (notably in the work of drummers such as Elvin Jones), and in the music of composers including Brahms, Chopin, and Steve Reich, whose phase-shifting pieces explore gradual rhythmic displacement.

Cross rhythm

Polyrhythm is sometimes distinguished from cross rhythm, in which the conflicting patterns are the prevailing meter itself rather than an embellishment within it.
