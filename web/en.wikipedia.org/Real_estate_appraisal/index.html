Real estate appraisal

An appraiser inspects and measures a property as part of developing an opinion of value.

Real estate appraisal, property valuation or land valuation is the process of developing an opinion of value for real property, usually its market value. Real estate transactions often require appraisals because they occur infrequently and every property is unique, so a professional, licensed appraiser's opinion is used as a basis for banks to decide the terms on which to lend against the property, among many other uses.

Types of value

The most commonly sought opinion is market value: the most probable price a property should bring in a competitive and open market under conditions requisite to a fair sale. Other definitions include value in use, investment value, insurable value and liquidation value, each suited to a different purpose such as taxation, insurance or forced sale.

Approaches to value

Appraisers traditionally consider three approaches and reconcile them into a final opinion:

Sales comparison approach — compares the subject property with recent sales of similar properties, adjusting for differences in location, size, condition and features. This is the dominant approach for residential property.

Cost approach — estimates the value of the land plus the depreciated cost of reproducing or replacing the improvements, on the principle that a buyer will pay no more than the cost of acquiring an equivalent substitute.

Income approach — used for income-producing property, it capitalizes the net operating income the property generates, using a capitalization rate, or discounts projected future cash flows to a present value.

Regulation and standards

In the United States, appraisals for federally related transactions must be performed by state-licensed or certified appraisers following the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP), maintained by the Appraisal Foundation; licensing was greatly expanded after the savings and loan crisis under the Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act of 1989. Other countries have analogous professional bodies, such as the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors in the United Kingdom. Automated valuation models (AVMs) increasingly supplement or substitute for traditional appraisals in low-risk lending.
