Fort Tryon Park

Location: Hudson Heights / Inwood, Manhattan, New York City

Area: 67 acres (27 ha)

Opened: 1935

Features: The Cloisters; Heather Garden

Fort Tryon Park is a public park located in the Hudson Heights and Inwood neighbourhoods of the borough of Manhattan in New York City. The 67-acre park sits on a high ridge above the Hudson River and contains The Cloisters, the medieval art branch of the Metropolitan Museum of Art.

History

The park is named for the Revolutionary War Battle of Fort Washington, fought in November 1776; Sir William Tryon was the last British governor of New York. The land was assembled from several estates, including that of Cornelius Vanderbilt II's son-in-law and of C. K. G. Billings, whose driveway and remnant structures survive. Philanthropist John D. Rockefeller Jr. purchased the land and donated it to the city; the park was designed by the Olmsted Brothers firm, principally Frederick Law Olmsted Jr., and opened in 1935.

Features

The park is known for its Heather Garden — one of the largest heath and heather gardens on the U.S. East Coast — its alpine garden, stone terraces, and views over the Hudson to the New Jersey Palisades. The Cloisters, opened in 1938, incorporates architectural elements from five medieval European cloisters and houses the Met's collection of medieval art, including the Unicorn Tapestries. The park also contains Billings Terrace and the remains of the Billings estate, and hosts an annual medieval festival.
