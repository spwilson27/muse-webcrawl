Planck constant

The energy of a photon is proportional to its frequency, with proportionality constant h

Symbolh
Value6.62607015×10−34 J·Hz−1 (exact)
Reduced constantħ = h/2π ≈ 1.054571817×10−34 J·s

The Planck constant, or Planck's constant, denoted by h, is a fundamental physical constant of foundational importance in quantum mechanics: a photon's energy is equal to its frequency multiplied by the Planck constant, and the wavelength of a matter wave equals the Planck constant divided by the associated particle momentum. The constant was introduced by Max Planck in 1900 in his successful theory of blackbody radiation, work that marked the origin of quantum theory.

Value and definition

Since the 2019 revision of the International System of Units (SI), the Planck constant has an exact defined value: h = 6.62607015×10−34 joule-hertz (J·Hz−1), equivalently joule-seconds. Together with the speed of light and the caesium hyperfine frequency, this definition fixes the size of the kilogram. The reduced Planck constant, ħ ("h-bar"), equal to h/2π, appears throughout quantum mechanics, for example in the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.

Significance

Planck proposed that electromagnetic energy could be emitted only in quantised form, in packets (quanta) proportional to the radiation frequency — a radical departure from classical physics introduced as an empirical fit to the spectrum of a blackbody. Einstein extended the idea in 1905 by proposing that light itself consists of quanta (photons), explaining the photoelectric effect. The constant also sets the scale of atomic structure: it enters the Bohr model of the atom, the Schrödinger equation, and the de Broglie wavelength of matter. Planck received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1918 for the discovery of energy quanta.

Planck units

Combining h (or ħ) with the speed of light and the gravitational constant yields the system of natural units known as Planck units, including the Planck length (~1.616×10−35 m) and Planck time, scales at which quantum gravity is expected to become important.
