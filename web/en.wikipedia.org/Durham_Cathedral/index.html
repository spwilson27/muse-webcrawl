Durham Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of Christ, Blessed Mary the Virgin and St Cuthbert of Durham, is a Church of England cathedral in the city of Durham, England. It is regarded as one of the finest examples of Norman architecture in Europe and, together with Durham Castle, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986. The cathedral houses the shrines of St Cuthbert and the Venerable Bede.

History

The present building was begun in 1093 and largely completed within forty years, replacing an earlier Anglo-Saxon church built to house the relics of St Cuthbert, which had been carried to Durham by monks fleeing Viking raids on Lindisfarne. The relics of Bede were also brought to Durham. William of St-Calais founded the Benedictine chapter and the office of Prince-Bishop, whose holders governed the County Palatine of Durham for centuries.

Architecture

The nave vault of Durham (completed around 1130) is among the earliest surviving rib vaults, and its pointed transverse arches anticipate Gothic construction. The massive cylindrical and compound piers carry distinctive incised geometric decoration. Later additions include the Chapel of the Nine Altars at the east end and the great central tower, rebuilt in the 15th century. The Rose Window and the Galilee Chapel, sheltering Bede's tomb, date to the 12th century.

Modern era

The cathedral appeared as a filming location for the Harry Potter films. Its library holds major medieval manuscripts, including the Durham Gospels. In 2016 the central tower's roof and monastic buildings were opened as the Open Treasure exhibition.
