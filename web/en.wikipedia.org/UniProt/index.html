UniProt

UniProt (Universal Protein Resource) is a comprehensive, freely accessible database of protein sequence and functional information, maintained by the UniProt Consortium — the European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI), the Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics (SIB), and the Protein Information Resource (PIR) at Georgetown University. It is the world's leading resource for protein data and is heavily used in bioinformatics research.

Components

UniProt consists of several components. The UniProt Knowledgebase (UniProtKB) contains protein sequences with functional annotation and is divided into Swiss-Prot, the manually reviewed and curated section created by Amos Bairoch in 1986, and TrEMBL, the automatically annotated supplement containing computer-translated sequences from nucleotide databases. The UniProt Reference Clusters (UniRef) merge closely related sequences at 100%, 90% and 50% identity to reduce redundancy. The UniProt Archive (UniParc) stores the complete history of protein sequences, including obsolete ones.

Identifiers and annotation

Each UniProtKB entry has a stable accession (for example P69905 for human haemoglobin subunit alpha) and captures the protein sequence, taxonomy, function, catalytic activity, subcellular location, post-translational modifications, variants and cross-references to more than 100 external databases. Annotation quality ranges from expert manual curation with literature citations in Swiss-Prot to fully automated rule-based annotation in TrEMBL.

History

UniProt was formed in 2002 by merging Swiss-Prot, TrEMBL and PIR-PSD under a single consortium, with funding from the US National Institutes of Health and the European Commission. Swiss-Prot had been distributed since 1986 from Geneva; the collaboration made its curated content freely available worldwide. UniProt releases appear monthly and by the 2020s UniProtKB contained over 200 million sequences, the great majority in TrEMBL.
