The Tiger I was a German heavy tank of World War II that operated beginning in 1942 in Africa and in the Soviet Union, usually in independent heavy tank battalions. Its official designation was Panzerkampfwagen VI Sd.Kfz. 181. The Tiger I gave the German armed forces their first armoured fighting vehicle with the 8.8 cm KwK 36 gun, derived from the famous 8.8 cm Flak anti-aircraft gun.

Design

The tank weighed about 54 tonnes, carried armour up to 100 mm thick on the hull front and turret, and was powered by a Maybach petrol engine. Its interleaved road wheels gave a smooth ride but were prone to clogging with mud, snow and ice. The design was complex and expensive, requiring extensive maintenance.

Combat history

Tigers earned a formidable reputation: their gun and armour outmatched most Allied and Soviet tanks of 1942–43, and a small number of aces achieved very high kill tallies. However, mechanical unreliability, high fuel consumption and the difficulty of recovering a broken-down vehicle limited its strategic impact. Production ended in August 1944 in favour of the Tiger II.
