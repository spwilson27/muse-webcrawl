Home automation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A smartphone app controlling lighting, climate and security.

Home automation or domotics is building automation for a home, called a smart home or smart house. A home automation system monitors and/or controls home attributes such as lighting, climate, entertainment systems and appliances, and may also include home security such as access control and alarm systems.

History

Early home automation began with labour-saving machines — washing machines, refrigerators and dishwashers of the early twentieth century. Computerised automation appeared in the 1970s with the X10 protocol (1975), which sent control signals over household wiring. The term "smart house" was coined in 1984. The spread of broadband internet, Wi-Fi, smartphones and the Internet of Things in the 2000s and 2010s transformed the field: products such as the Nest thermostat (2011), Philips Hue lighting (2012) and voice-controlled speakers such as Amazon Echo (2014) brought automation to mass markets.

Systems and standards

Devices communicate over wired systems (KNX, X10) or wireless protocols including Zigbee, Z-Wave, Bluetooth LE, Wi-Fi and Thread. Interoperability has long been complicated by competing ecosystems (Amazon Alexa, Google Home, Apple HomeKit, Samsung SmartThings); the Matter standard, launched in 2022 by the Connectivity Standards Alliance, was designed to unify smart home devices across platforms.

Applications and issues

Applications include energy management (smart thermostats and meters), lighting and shading control, security cameras, video doorbells and locks, leak and smoke detection, voice control, and assisted living for elderly or disabled people. Benefits claimed include convenience, energy savings and safety. Concerns include privacy (always-listening devices and data collection), security vulnerabilities in connected devices, system complexity, vendor lock-in and the environmental cost of short-lived electronics.
