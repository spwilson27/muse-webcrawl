Talulah Gosh

Talulah Gosh were an English indie pop band from Oxford, formed in 1986. Alongside acts such as the Shop Assistants and the Pastels, they were a leading group in the C86 and "shambling" indie pop scenes, and their deliberately ramshackle, exuberant sound — self-consciously amateurish and dismissively labelled "twee" by critics — became highly influential on later indie pop and riot grrrl musicians.

Career

The band formed around Amelia Fletcher (vocals, guitar), her brother Mathew Fletcher (drums), Peter Momtchiloff (guitar), Rob Pursey (bass), and Elizabeth Price (vocals, guitar). Their first single, "I Told You So", appeared on the 53rd & 3rd label in 1986, and they contributed the song "Five Words" to the NME's C86 cassette. Singles such as "Beatnik Boy", "Bringing Up Baby", and "Testcard Girl" followed. Original bassist Rob Pursey left and was replaced by Chris Scott; Elizabeth Price departed in 1987 and was replaced by Eithne Farry. The band split in 1988, having released no studio album during their lifetime.

Aftermath and legacy

The Fletcher siblings and Momtchiloff went on to form Heavenly, a more accomplished and longer-lived indie pop band, and later Marine Research and Tender Trap; Amelia Fletcher became an influential figure in UK indie pop and was awarded an OBE for services to economics. Talulah Gosh's recordings were collected on the compilation Backwash (1996, K Records; reissued expanded by Damaged Goods in 2013 as Was It Just a Dream?), and the band's handful of singles are now regarded as foundational documents of the twee pop genre.
