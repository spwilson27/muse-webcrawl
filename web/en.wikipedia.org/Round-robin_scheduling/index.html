Round-robin scheduling

Round-robin (RR) scheduling is an algorithm used in operating systems and networks for scheduling processes and data flows. Each process is assigned a fixed time slot in cyclic order, handling all processes without priority.

Description

The scheduler keeps ready processes in a first-in, first-out queue. Each process runs for at most one time quantum; if it does not finish, it is preempted and placed at the back of the queue. The choice of quantum is critical: too large and the algorithm degenerates into first-come, first-served; too small and context-switching overhead dominates.

Applications

Round-robin scheduling is simple, fair, and starvation-free, making it a common default in time-sharing systems. Variants such as weighted round robin are used in network packet scheduling.
