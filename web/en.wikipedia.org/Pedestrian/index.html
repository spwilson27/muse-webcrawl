A pedestrian is a person travelling on foot, whether walking or running. In some communities, those travelling with small wheels such as roller skates, skateboards and push scooters, as well as wheelchair users, are also included as pedestrians. Legal definitions vary by jurisdiction but generally cover anyone on foot within or crossing a roadway.

History

Walking was the universal mode of human transport until the spread of wheeled vehicles and, in the twentieth century, the mass adoption of the automobile. As motor traffic grew, cities introduced sidewalks (footways), marked crosswalks and pedestrian signals, and laws such as jaywalking offences in the United States shifted priority toward vehicles in many road systems. Since the late twentieth century, urban planners have increasingly promoted walkability, pedestrianisation of city centres, and traffic-calming measures.

Safety

Pedestrians are vulnerable road users: they lack any protective structure, so collision outcomes worsen sharply with vehicle speed. The World Health Organization estimates that pedestrians account for roughly 22% of the world's road traffic deaths. Countermeasures include lower speed limits, protected crossings, pedestrian refuges, separated footways and vehicle designs that reduce injury severity.

Rights and infrastructure

Pedestrian infrastructure includes sidewalks, footpaths, crosswalks (such as zebra crossings and signal-controlled crossings), footbridges and underpasses. Many countries recognise a hierarchy of road users placing pedestrians first in street design guidance.
