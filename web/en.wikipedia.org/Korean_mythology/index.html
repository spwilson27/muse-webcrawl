Korean mythology is the body of myths and legends handed down in Korea, encompassing the foundation myths of its kingdoms, shamanic narrative songs recited by mudang (shamans), and a rich body of folktales featuring gods, spirits, ghosts and supernatural creatures.

Foundation myths

The most famous foundation myth is that of Dangun, the legendary founder of Gojoseon in 2333 BCE. According to the myth, Hwanung, the son of the Lord of Heaven, descended to Mount Taebaek; a bear and a tiger prayed to become human, and the bear, transformed into a woman, bore Dangun. Other kingdoms had their own founders: Jumong founded Goguryeo, Park Hyeokgeose founded Silla, and the six chiefs of Gaya feature in the egg myths of King Suro.

Shamanic narratives

In the shamanic tradition, myths survive as sung epics during gut (rituals). The Princess Bari myth tells of an abandoned seventh daughter who journeys to the afterlife to fetch the elixir of life for her dying parents and becomes the goddess who guides the dead. Jeju Island preserves a particularly complete cycle of bon-puri myths about local gods.

Folk creatures

Korean folklore includes the dokkaebi (mischievous goblins), the nine-tailed fox gumiho, the haetae (a fire-eating guardian beast), dragons and mountain spirits (sanshin). Household gods, village guardian posts (jangseung) and shamanic shrines reflect the persistence of these beliefs alongside Buddhism and Confucianism.
