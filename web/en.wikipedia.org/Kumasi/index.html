Kumasi is a city in the Ashanti Region, and the second-largest city in Ghana. It was the capital city of the historical Ashanti Empire, and as such, Kumasi remains of supreme importance in Ashanti culture; the city is the seat of the Asantehene (the king of Ashanti), whose court is at the Manhyia Palace.

History

Tradition holds that Kumasi was founded around 1680 by King Osei Tutu I, who, guided by the priest Okomfo Anokye, planted a kum tree at the site and received the Golden Stool — the sacred symbol of the Ashanti nation — which is said to have descended from the sky. Kumasi grew into a major trading centre of the forest zone. British forces burned the city in 1874 during the Anglo-Ashanti wars, and the Ashanti kingdom was incorporated into the Gold Coast colony in 1901 after the War of the Golden Stool (1900), led by Queen Mother Yaa Asantewaa.

Today

Known as "The Garden City" for its many species of flowers and plants, Kumasi is a commercial hub for cocoa, timber and gold, hosts the Kejetia Market — one of the largest open-air markets in West Africa — and is home to Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) and the football club Asante Kotoko.
