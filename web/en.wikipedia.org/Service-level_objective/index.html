Service-level objective


An SLO defines a target together with an error budget.

A service-level objective (SLO) is a key element of a service-level agreement (SLA) between a service provider and a customer. SLOs are agreed upon as a means of measuring the performance of the service provider and are outlined as a way of avoiding disputes based on misunderstanding. An SLO is a specific, measurable target for a service-level indicator (SLI) — for example, that 99.9% of requests will succeed, or that 95% of requests will complete within 200 milliseconds over a rolling 30-day window.

SLOs, SLIs and SLAs

An SLI is the measured metric (such as availability, latency, throughput or correctness), an SLO is the target value or range for that metric over a period, and an SLA is the wider contract of which SLOs form a part, often with financial consequences for breach. In site reliability engineering practice, SLOs are frequently internal targets set somewhat stricter than any external SLA, so that problems are detected before contractual commitments are broken.

Error budgets

The complement of an SLO is its error budget: an availability SLO of 99.9% permits 0.1% of requests to fail — about 43 minutes of downtime per month. Error budgets, popularised by Google's site reliability engineering organisation, provide a data-driven basis for balancing reliability work against feature development: while budget remains, teams may release faster and accept more risk; if the budget is exhausted, changes are frozen in favour of reliability work.

Choosing SLOs

Good SLOs measure what users actually experience, are achievable but not trivially so, and are few in number for each service. Setting targets too high (e.g. 100% availability) is generally seen as counterproductive, since no system can achieve them and the attempt distorts engineering priorities.
