World's busiest airports

The world's busiest airports are ranked principally by passenger traffic — the total of enplaned and deplaned passengers — as compiled annually by Airports Council International (ACI). Other measures include aircraft movements (take-offs and landings) and cargo tonnage, for which different airports lead.

Passenger traffic

Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport in the United States has been the world's busiest airport by passengers in almost every year since 1998, exceeding 100 million passengers annually before the COVID-19 pandemic and again as traffic recovered. Other perennial leaders include Dallas/Fort Worth, Denver, Chicago O'Hare, Los Angeles, Dubai International, London Heathrow, Istanbul Airport, Delhi and Tokyo Haneda. Beijing Capital led in some Asian rankings before the pandemic, and Chinese airports have climbed steadily with the growth of domestic aviation.

Other measures

Atlanta also usually leads in aircraft movements, while the busiest cargo airport is Hong Kong International, followed by Memphis International — home to FedEx Express's main hub — and Shanghai Pudong. The busiest airport for international passengers alone is Dubai. Rankings collapsed and reordered sharply in 2020–2021 as pandemic restrictions suppressed travel, with Chinese and US domestic hubs temporarily dominant, before international hubs recovered.

Figures change annually; consult ACI's latest release for the current year's table.
