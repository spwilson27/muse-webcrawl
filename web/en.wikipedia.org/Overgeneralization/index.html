Overgeneralization

Overgeneralization is the application of a rule or conclusion beyond the cases it properly covers. In language acquisition, it describes children applying regular grammatical patterns to irregular forms, as in "goed" or "mouses".

In reasoning

As a fallacy, hasty generalisation draws broad conclusions from small or unrepresentative samples. In cognitive therapy, overgeneralisation is a cognitive distortion in which a single negative event is seen as a never-ending pattern of defeat, associated with depression and anxiety.

In learning

Developmental psycholinguists regard children's overregularisation errors as evidence that they learn rules rather than merely imitating, with correct irregular forms gradually replacing errors as vocabulary grows.
