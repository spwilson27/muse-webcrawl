Fruit (plant structure)

In botany, a fruit is the seed-bearing structure in flowering plants (angiosperms) formed from the ovary after flowering. Fruits are the means by which angiosperms disperse their seeds — by wind, water, or animals that eat the fruit and deposit the seeds elsewhere. The wall of the fruit, developed from the ovary wall, is the pericarp, often differentiated into exocarp (skin), mesocarp (flesh), and endocarp (inner layer).

Classification

Botanists classify fruits as simple (from a single ovary, e.g. a peach or a bean pod), aggregate (from one flower with many ovaries, e.g. a raspberry), or multiple (from a cluster of flowers, e.g. a pineapple). Fleshy simple fruits include drupes (stone fruits) and berries; dry fruits may be dehiscent, splitting open to release seeds, or indehiscent, like grains and nuts.

Botanical versus culinary fruit

The botanical definition differs from everyday usage: tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers, squash, and grains are botanically fruits, while some culinary "fruits", such as rhubarb stalks, are not. Many vegetables are fruits in the botanical sense because they develop from a flower's ovary and contain seeds.

Development

After pollination and fertilization, hormones from the developing seeds stimulate the ovary wall to grow, while the petals and other flower parts usually wither. Some fruits develop without fertilization (parthenocarpy), producing seedless varieties such as certain bananas and grapes.
