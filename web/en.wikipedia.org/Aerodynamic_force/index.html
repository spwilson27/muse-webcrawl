Aerodynamic force


Forces on an airfoil section

An aerodynamic force is a force exerted on a body by the air (or other gas) in which the body is immersed, arising from the relative motion between the body and the gas. It is the resultant of two physical mechanisms: the pressure distribution acting normal to the body's surface, and the shear stress (skin friction) acting tangent to the surface.

Components

The aerodynamic force is conventionally resolved into two components relative to the oncoming flow direction:

  • Lift — the component perpendicular to the direction of motion of the surrounding fluid.
  • Drag — the component parallel to, and opposing, the direction of motion.

The magnitude of the aerodynamic force is commonly expressed as F = ½ ρ v² S CF, where ρ is the fluid density, v is the relative speed, S is a reference area, and CF is a dimensionless force coefficient. The resultant force acts through the center of pressure of the body.
