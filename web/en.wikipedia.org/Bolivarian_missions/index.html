Bolivarian missions

The Bolivarian missions are a series of social programmes implemented in Venezuela under President Hugo Chávez from 2003 onward, named for independence hero Simón Bolívar. Funded largely by revenues from the state oil company PDVSA, the missions targeted poverty, illiteracy, healthcare, food security and housing.

Principal missions

Notable missions include Misión Barrio Adentro (free primary healthcare staffed substantially by Cuban medical personnel), Misión Robinson (literacy), Misión Ribas and Misión Sucre (secondary and university education), Misión Mercal (subsidised food), Misión Vivienda (housing) and Misión Milagro (eye care).

Assessment

Supporters credit the missions with sharp reductions in poverty and illiteracy during the oil boom; critics argue they were fiscally unsustainable, politicised in their administration, and unable to survive the subsequent collapse of oil revenues and the Venezuelan economy. Their decline formed part of the broader Venezuelan crisis of the 2010s.
