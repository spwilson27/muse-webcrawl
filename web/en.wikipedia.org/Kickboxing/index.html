Kickboxing is a full-contact hybrid martial art and boxing-type combat sport based on punching and kicking. Kickboxing originated in the 1950s and 1960s and is practised in a boxing ring, normally with boxing gloves, mouth guards, shorts, and bare feet to favour the use of kicks. A kickboxer intentionally hits their opponent with punches and kicks to score points or knock them out.

History

The modern sport emerged in Japan in the 1960s, when boxing promoter Osamu Noguchi and karateka Tatsuo Yamada combined elements of karate and Muay Thai to create a new ring sport, holding the first events in 1966. In the 1970s, American variants developed: "full contact karate" allowed punches and kicks above the waist, while the widely influential K-1 promotion, founded in Japan in 1993, popularised a ruleset featuring knees and low kicks and made kickboxing a global spectator sport. Dutch kickboxing, a blend of Muay Thai, boxing and kyokushin karate, became especially influential in European and world competition.

Styles and rules

Rules vary between organisations and styles. "International" (low-kick) rules permit kicks to the legs; "full contact" rules restrict targets to above the waist; "K-1 rules" allow knees and limited clinching. Bouts are usually three to five rounds of two or three minutes. Scoring is usually by a panel of judges if no knockout occurs. Major promotions have included K-1, Glory, and ONE Championship.

Techniques

Kickboxing employs the punches of Western boxing (jab, cross, hook, uppercut) combined with kicks such as the roundhouse kick, front kick, side kick and spinning back kick; K-1 and Muay Thai-derived styles add knee strikes and, in some rulesets, elbow strikes. Defensive techniques include blocking, parrying, slipping and footwork. Training typically involves heavy bag and pad work, sparring, shadowboxing, skipping and conditioning.
