La Calavera Catrina

La Calavera Catrina is a zinc etching by Mexican printmaker José Guadalupe Posada, depicting a female skeleton wearing only an elegant hat, satirising the Mexican elite's imitation of European fashions.

History

The image was published around 1910–1912 in a penny sheet; Posada's original caption mocked 'those who are today garbanzo bugs' who would end as bare skulls. The name 'La Catrina' was popularised later, notably through Diego Rivera's mural, which gave her a full figure and dress.

Legacy

La Catrina became an iconic image of the Day of the Dead (Día de Muertos), inspiring costumes, figurines and face painting.
