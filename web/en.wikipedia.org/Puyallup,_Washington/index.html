Puyallup is a city in Pierce County, Washington, United States, located about 10 miles southeast of Tacoma and 35 miles south of Seattle. It is home to the Washington State Fair, one of the largest state fairs in the United States.

History

The city and the Puyallup River take their name from the Puyallup Tribe, whose name in Lushootseed means "generous people". The valley was settled by pioneer Ezra Meeker in the 19th century and became a centre for hops farming, and later for raspberries, blackberries, and daffodils. The town was incorporated in 1890.

Events and economy

The Washington State Fair, held each September at the Puyallup fairgrounds, draws more than a million visitors; a smaller Spring Fair takes place in April. The city also hosts a well-known daffodil parade tradition associated with the regional Daffodil Festival. Puyallup has grown into a suburban community within the Seattle–Tacoma metropolitan area, with Good Samaritan Hospital among its major employers.
