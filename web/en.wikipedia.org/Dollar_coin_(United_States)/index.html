Dollar coin (United States)

The dollar coin is a United States coin with a face value of one dollar. Dollar coins have been minted in gold, silver, and base-metal versions since 1794, but have never achieved widespread circulation in modern times, where the one-dollar bill dominates. The current issue is the golden-coloured Native American and American Innovation series.

Early and silver dollars

The first US dollar coins, the Flowing Hair dollars of 1794–95, were modelled on the Spanish dollar. Silver dollars — Draped Bust, Seated Liberty, Trade, and the celebrated Morgan dollar (1878–1921) and Peace dollar (1921–35) — circulated mainly in the West and were struck in large part to satisfy silver-mining interests under legislation such as the Bland–Allison Act.

Modern issues

The large Eisenhower dollar (1971–78) was followed by the smaller Susan B. Anthony dollar (1979–81, 1999), whose similarity in size to the quarter hindered adoption. The golden Sacagawea dollar debuted in 2000, succeeded by the Presidential $1 Coin Program (2007–2016, 2020) and the American Innovation dollars (2018–2032). Since 2012, circulating issues have been struck only for collectors.

Circulation debate

Studies by the Government Accountability Office have repeatedly found that replacing the dollar bill with coins would save money over time, but public preference for notes, vending-industry transitions, and the absence of bill withdrawal have kept coins marginal in the United States — unlike in countries such as Canada and the eurozone, where high-value coins circulate readily.
