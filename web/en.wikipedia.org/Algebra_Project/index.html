Algebra Project

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Founded: 1982

Founder: Bob Moses

Type: Nonprofit educational organisation

Focus: Mathematics literacy for underserved students

The Algebra Project is a national US nonprofit organisation that aims to improve mathematics achievement for students in historically underserved communities. It was founded in 1982 by civil rights activist and educator Bob Moses, who argued that mathematical literacy is a civil right essential to full participation in modern economic life.

Origins

Moses, a veteran of the Mississippi voter registration struggles of the 1960s, began the project after finding that his daughter's middle school in Cambridge, Massachusetts, did not offer algebra — the gateway course for advanced mathematics and, in turn, for college admission. Using a MacArthur "genius" fellowship, he developed a curriculum and community-organising approach that began in public schools in Cambridge and spread to districts in Mississippi and across the United States.

Approach

The project's pedagogy treats learning algebra as a five-step curricular process grounded in students' physical experience: beginning with a shared event or trip, representing it pictorially, describing it in everyday language, translating it into structured language, and finally expressing it in standard mathematical symbols. It also trains young people through the related Math Literacy Workers programme to teach and organise around mathematics in their own communities. Moses set out the philosophy in the book Radical Equations: Civil Rights from Mississippi to the Algebra Project (2001).
