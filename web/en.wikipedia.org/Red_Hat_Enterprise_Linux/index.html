Red Hat Enterprise Linux

DeveloperRed Hat, Inc.
OS familyLinux (Unix-like)
Initial release2000 (as Red Hat Linux Advanced Server)
Package managerRPM, DNF/Yum

Red Hat Enterprise Linux (RHEL) is a commercial open-source Linux distribution developed by Red Hat for the commercial market. It is widely used on servers, mainframes and in high-performance computing, and is sold with subscription-based support and a long, stable release lifecycle — typically ten years of support per major version.

History

RHEL evolved from Red Hat Linux, which Red Hat discontinued as a consumer product in 2003 in favour of the community-driven Fedora project. Enterprise versions are developed in a public development model based largely on Fedora, and RHEL in turn forms the basis of rebuild distributions; CentOS was for many years a free, binary-compatible rebuild before Red Hat shifted it to the rolling-release CentOS Stream model.

Features

RHEL uses the RPM package format and the DNF (formerly Yum) package manager. It includes security features such as SELinux in enforcing mode by default, extensive hardware certification, and support for virtualisation and containers. Major releases include RHEL 7 (2014), RHEL 8 (2019) and RHEL 9 (2022).

Variants

Red Hat offers variants for servers, workstations, high-performance computing clusters, IBM mainframes and Power systems, and edge deployments.
