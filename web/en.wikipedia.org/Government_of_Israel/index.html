Government of Israel

Formation14 May 1948 (state proclaimed)
Head of governmentPrime Minister of Israel
LegislatureKnesset (120 members)
Legal basisBasic Laws of Israel; Basic Law: The Government (2001)
SeatJerusalem

The government of Israel (officially the Cabinet of Israel, Hebrew: Memshelet Yisrael) exercises executive authority in the State of Israel. It consists of ministers, headed by the prime minister, chosen from the members of the Knesset and collectively responsible to it. Israel is a parliamentary republic: the president is head of state with largely ceremonial duties, while executive power rests with the cabinet.

Formation

After a general election, the president consults the Knesset factions and assigns the task of forming a government to the member judged most likely to succeed. That candidate has 28 days (extendable by 14) to assemble a coalition commanding a majority of the Knesset's 120 seats, and the resulting government must win a vote of investiture. Because elections use closed-list proportional representation with a low threshold, no party has ever won an outright majority, and every Israeli government has been a coalition.

The cabinet and ministries

Major ministries include Foreign Affairs, Defense, Finance, Interior, Justice, Education, Health and the Prime Minister's Office. A smaller Security Cabinet (established by law in 2001) handles defence and foreign-policy decisions. The cabinet meets weekly, usually on Sundays. Ministers may appoint deputy ministers, who need not be members of the government.

Direct election experiment

Between 1996 and 2001 Israel experimented with the direct election of the prime minister, separate from the Knesset vote, intending to strengthen the executive. The reform is generally judged to have fragmented the party system further and weakened prime ministers, and it was repealed in 2001, restoring the purely parliamentary system.

Caretaker and transitional governments

Israeli law provides for continuity: when a government falls or the Knesset disperses, the outgoing cabinet continues as a transitional (caretaker) government with restricted powers until a new one is sworn in. Political instability between 2019 and 2022 produced five elections in under four years, with long periods of caretaker rule under Benjamin Netanyahu and then a broad "change government" under Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid before Netanyahu returned to office at the end of 2022.
