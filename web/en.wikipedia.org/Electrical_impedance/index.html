Electrical impedance

Electrical impedance is the measure of the opposition a circuit presents to alternating current. It extends the concept of resistance to AC circuits, accounting for both magnitude and phase.

Impedance Z is a complex quantity measured in ohms: Z = R + jX, where R is resistance and X is reactance. Inductive reactance (from inductors) is positive and capacitive reactance (from capacitors) is negative; both vary with frequency. The magnitude is |Z| = √(R² + X²), and the phase angle gives the shift between voltage and current.

Applications

Impedance matching maximizes power transfer and minimizes reflections in audio, radio-frequency, and transmission-line systems. Impedance spectroscopy is used to study batteries, corrosion, and biological tissue.
