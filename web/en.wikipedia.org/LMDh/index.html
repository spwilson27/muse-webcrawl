LMDh (Le Mans Daytona h) is a sports prototype category for sports car racing jointly created by IMSA, the ACO and the FIA. Cars built to the regulations compete in the top class of the IMSA SportsCar Championship (as GTP) and are eligible for the FIA World Endurance Championship Hypercar class alongside Le Mans Hypercar entries.

Regulations

LMDh cars are built on a spec chassis supplied by one of four approved constructors (Dallara, Ligier, Multimatic and Oreca) and use a standard hybrid system — a Bosch motor generator unit and Williams Advanced Engineering battery — driving the rear axle, combined with a manufacturer-designed internal combustion engine. Combined output is regulated to roughly 500 kW, and bodywork carries manufacturer styling cues.

History

The category was announced in January 2020 as a convergence formula allowing the same car to race at both Daytona and Le Mans. It debuted at the 2023 24 Hours of Daytona with entries from Acura, BMW, Cadillac and Porsche; Lamborghini joined subsequently.
