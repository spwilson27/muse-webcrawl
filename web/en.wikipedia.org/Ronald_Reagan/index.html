Ronald Wilson Reagan (February 6, 1911 – June 5, 2004) was an American politician and actor who served as the 40th president of the United States from 1981 to 1989. A member of the Republican Party, he had previously served as the 33rd governor of California from 1967 to 1975 and as president of the Screen Actors Guild. Reagan was a leading figure of the American conservative movement, and his presidency is often called the Reagan Revolution.

Early life and acting career

Reagan was born in Tampico, Illinois, and raised in Dixon. He graduated from Eureka College in 1932 and worked as a radio sports announcer, famously recreating Chicago Cubs baseball games from wire reports. Signing with Warner Bros. in 1937, he appeared in more than fifty films, including Knute Rockne, All American (1940), in which he played George Gipp and earned the nickname "the Gipper", and Kings Row (1942). During the Second World War he served in the Army Air Forces' First Motion Picture Unit. As president of the Screen Actors Guild he testified before the House Un-American Activities Committee during the blacklist era.

Political rise

Originally a New Deal Democrat, Reagan moved rightward in the 1950s as a spokesman for General Electric, and his 1964 televised speech "A Time for Choosing" in support of Barry Goldwater launched his political career. Elected governor of California in 1966 and re-elected in 1970, he raised taxes while also signing significant legislation, including abortion liberalization and gun control. After a near-miss challenge to Gerald Ford for the 1976 Republican nomination, he won the presidency in 1980, defeating incumbent Jimmy Carter amid high inflation and the Iran hostage crisis.

Presidency

Reagan's first term was marked by "Reaganomics" — large tax cuts (the Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981), deregulation, and increased military spending — an assassination attempt in March 1981 that he survived, and a hard line against labor unions, exemplified by firing striking air traffic controllers in 1981. He won a landslide re-election in 1984, carrying 49 states. His second term saw the Iran–Contra affair, in which officials secretly sold arms to Iran and diverted proceeds to Nicaraguan Contra rebels, seriously damaging his administration; and a dramatic turn in the Cold War, as summits with Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev — at Reykjavík in 1986 and Washington in 1987 — produced the INF Treaty eliminating a class of nuclear missiles. His 1987 speech at the Brandenburg Gate, urging Gorbachev to "tear down this wall", became iconic. When Reagan left office in 1989, the Cold War was nearing its end; the Berlin Wall fell ten months later.

Later life and legacy

In 1994 Reagan announced that he had Alzheimer's disease, and he withdrew from public life; he died in 2004 and received a state funeral. Historians and conservatives credit him with revitalizing the Republican Party, reshaping American politics toward lower taxes and smaller domestic government, and contributing to the end of the Cold War; critics point to rising deficits, inequality, and social-program cuts. He consistently ranks among the most highly regarded presidents in public surveys.
