John Lewis

Born: John Robert Lewis, 21 February 1940, Troy, Alabama, US
Died: 17 July 2020 (aged 80), Atlanta, Georgia, US
Office: U.S. Representative for Georgia's 5th congressional district (1987–2020)
Known for: Civil rights leader; chairman of the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC); "Bloody Sunday" marcher, Selma 1965

John Robert Lewis (February 21, 1940 – July 17, 2020) was an American civil rights leader and politician best known for his chairmanship of the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC) and for leading the march across the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma on "Bloody Sunday" in 1965. He represented Georgia's 5th congressional district in the U.S. House of Representatives from 1987 until his death in 2020, and was widely regarded as the "conscience of the Congress".

Civil rights activism

Born the son of sharecroppers near Troy, Alabama, Lewis was inspired as a teenager by the Montgomery bus boycott and the preaching of Martin Luther King Jr. While a student at Fisk University he organized sit-ins at segregated lunch counters in Nashville, and in 1961 he joined the Freedom Rides, suffering beatings and arrest. As chairman of SNCC from 1963 to 1966 he helped organize voter registration drives during Freedom Summer and spoke — in a speech famously toned down under pressure — at the 1963 March on Washington.

On 7 March 1965 Lewis co-led some 600 marchers across the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma, Alabama, where state troopers attacked them with tear gas and clubs; Lewis's skull was fractured. Televised images of "Bloody Sunday" galvanized national support for the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Congressional career

Lewis served on the Atlanta City Council before being elected to Congress in 1986. A leading liberal voice and senior member of the Congressional Black Caucus, he championed voting rights, healthcare and gun control, and led the 2016 House sit-in over gun legislation. His graphic-novel memoir trilogy March, co-written with Andrew Aydin and illustrated by Nate Powell, won the National Book Award in 2016. Lewis received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2011. He died of pancreatic cancer in July 2020 and lay in state in the U.S. Capitol rotunda, the first Black lawmaker to receive that honour there.
