Greco-Italian War

Date: 28 October 1940 – 23 April 1941
Location: Southern Balkan Peninsula
Result: Greek defensive victory; German intervention follows

The Greco-Italian War (Greek: Ελληνοιταλικός Πόλεμος) took place between the kingdoms of Italy and Greece from 28 October 1940 to 23 April 1941. This local war began the Balkans Campaign of World War II between the Axis powers and the Allies, and eventually turned into the Battle of Greece with British and German involvement.

Background

On 15 August 1940, the Italian submarine Delfino torpedoed the Greek cruiser Elli in Tinos harbour. On the morning of 28 October 1940, the Italian ambassador delivered an ultimatum to Prime Minister Ioannis Metaxas demanding free passage for Italian troops to occupy strategic sites in Greece. Metaxas refused—the famous "Ohi" ("No")—and Italy invaded from Albania within hours.

The Greek counteroffensive

Expecting a swift victory, the Italian forces under General Sebastiano Visconti Prasca advanced into Epirus but were halted at the Battle of Elaia–Kalamas. By mid-November the Greek army had counterattacked along the whole front, capturing Korçë, Sarandë, and Gjirokastër and driving deep into Italian-held Albania. The victory was one of the first Allied successes on land against the Axis.

Italian spring offensive and German intervention

Italy reinforced Albania and launched a major spring offensive in March 1941, personally supervised by Mussolini, which failed with heavy losses. On 6 April 1941 Germany invaded Greece through Yugoslavia and Bulgaria in Operation Marita, outflanking the Greek positions. An armistice with Germany was signed on 23 April 1941, and Greece fell under triple occupation.

Legacy

The Greek resistance delayed the Axis timetable and remains a source of national pride; 28 October is commemorated in Greece as Ohi Day. The campaign forced Germany to divert forces to the Balkans, though historians debate how much it delayed Operation Barbarossa.
