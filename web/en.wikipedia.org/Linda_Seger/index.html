Linda Seger (born April 27, 1945) is an American script consultant, author and lecturer on screenwriting. She is widely credited with helping to create the profession of the script consultant in Hollywood: beginning in 1981, she built a practice analysing and advising on screenplays for studios, producers and writers, and her 1987 book Making a Good Script Great became one of the standard texts on rewriting and script structure.

Career

Seger was raised in Peshtigo, Wisconsin, and studied English and drama, earning a master's degree and later a doctorate with a dissertation relating theatre, film and religion. After teaching, she moved into script analysis, initially working with director Henry Jaglom. Finding that studios and production companies lacked a systematic outside reader for scripts in development, she began consulting professionally — an occupation that barely existed at the time.

Over her career Seger has consulted on more than two thousand scripts and worked with major studios and independent producers, as well as teaching seminars internationally. Her clients have included writers and producers whose films went on to commercial and critical success. She emphasises story structure, theme, character arcs and the rewriting process rather than rigid formulas.

Bibliography

Her books include Making a Good Script Great (1987), Creating Unforgettable Characters (1990), Making a Good Writer Great, Advanced Screenwriting, The Art of Adaptation: Turning Fact and Fiction into Film (1992), When Women Call the Shots, And the Best Screenplay Goes To…, and Writing Subtext: What Lies Beneath (2011). She has also written on spirituality in film, including Jesus Rode a Donkey.

Views

Quaker in faith, Seger has written extensively on the spiritual and moral dimensions of film, and on the representation of women and religion in cinema.
