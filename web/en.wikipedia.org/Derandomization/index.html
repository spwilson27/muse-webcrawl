Derandomization

In theoretical computer science, derandomization is the process of converting a randomized algorithm into an efficient deterministic algorithm achieving similar results, or of proving that such a conversion is possible in general. Because randomness is often a powerful computational resource, understanding whether it adds real power is a central question of complexity theory.

Approaches

Techniques for derandomization include the method of conditional expectations, pairwise-independent and k-wise independent random variables, expander graphs and pseudorandom generators. The celebrated result that primality testing is in P (the AKS primality test) derandomized the earlier Miller–Rabin test.

Complexity-theoretic status

The class BPP captures problems solvable efficiently with randomness; whether BPP = P is open. Results by Nisan and Wigderson show that sufficiently strong circuit lower bounds imply strong pseudorandom generators, and work by Impagliazzo and Wigderson suggests that, under plausible assumptions, BPP = P — i.e., randomness can in principle be eliminated with little cost.
