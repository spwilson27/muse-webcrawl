Nopal

Nopal is a common name in Spanish for Opuntia cacti (commonly referred to in English as prickly pear), as well as for their pads. There are about 114 known species in Mexico, where the plant is a common ingredient in numerous Mexican cuisine dishes. The nopal pads, called nopales or nopalitos, are sold fresh, bottled, canned or dried, and the sweet fruits, called tunas, are eaten as snacks.

Culinary use

Nopales are cleaned of spines, cut into strips and grilled, boiled or pickled; they have a tart, green-bean-like flavour and a slightly mucilaginous texture. They appear in salads, egg dishes, tacos and the dish nopales a la mexicana. The nopal figures on Mexico's coat of arms, where an eagle perches on a cactus growing from an island in a lake.

Cultivation and other uses

Nopals are drought-tolerant and grown for food, fodder, hedges and, historically, as host plants for cochineal insects producing red dye. They are rich in fibre and micronutrients and are studied for effects on blood sugar.
