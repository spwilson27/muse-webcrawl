Human Y-chromosome DNA haplogroup

In human genetics, a human Y-chromosome DNA haplogroup is a haplogroup defined by specific differences in the non-recombining portions of DNA from the male-specific Y chromosome (Y-DNA). Because the Y chromosome passes largely unchanged from father to son, Y-DNA haplogroups trace direct paternal lineages and are used to study the prehistoric migrations of populations.

Naming and tree structure

Major haplogroups are designated by capital letters A through T, with subclades indicated by alternating numbers and lowercase letters (for example R1b1a). The Y Chromosome Consortium published a standard phylogenetic tree in 2002, maintained since by the International Society of Genetic Genealogy (ISOGG). All living men descend patrilineally from "Y-chromosomal Adam," the most recent common ancestor of the tree, who lived in Africa on the order of 200,000–300,000 years ago.

Major haplogroups and distributions

Haplogroup A and B are largely confined to Africa and represent the deepest branches. Haplogroup E, especially E1b1a, predominates in sub-Saharan Africa, while E1b1b is common around the Mediterranean. Haplogroup R, particularly R1b in western Europe and R1a in eastern Europe and South Asia, is the most frequent in Europe. Haplogroup O dominates East and Southeast Asia, Q is characteristic of Indigenous peoples of the Americas and Siberia, J is common in the Middle East, and I in northern and eastern Europe.

Uses

Y-DNA haplogroups are used in population genetics, forensic analysis, and genetic genealogy, where surname projects combine Y-chromosome testing with documentary records to trace paternal ancestry. Their paternal-only inheritance gives a partial view of ancestry, complementing mitochondrial DNA haplogroups, which trace the maternal line.
