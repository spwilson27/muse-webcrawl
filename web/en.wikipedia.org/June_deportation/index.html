The June deportation (Estonian: juuniküüditamine; Latvian: jūnija deportācija; Lithuanian: birželio trėmimai) was a mass deportation by the Soviet Union of tens of thousands of people from the territories occupied in 1939–40: the Baltic states, occupied Poland (mostly present-day western Belarus and western Ukraine) and Moldavia, carried out in accordance with NKVD orders and the "Serov Instructions".

Background

Following the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, the USSR annexed Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania in the summer of 1940, along with Bessarabia and northern Bukovina from Romania and eastern territories of Poland. Sovietisation included the preparation of lists of "anti-Soviet elements" — former officials, landowners, industrialists, police, military officers, teachers and members of civic organisations.

The deportations

In the night of 13–14 June 1941, NKVD units simultaneously arrested families on the prepared lists. Men were generally separated and sent to Gulag labour camps, while women, children and the elderly were exiled to forced settlements in Siberia, Kazakhstan and the far north. Approximately 17,500 people were deported from Lithuania, about 15,400 from Latvia and roughly 10,000 from Estonia in the first wave, with further waves following across the occupied territories.

Consequences

Many deportees died of hunger, cold and disease in transit and in exile. The operation was interrupted by the German invasion of the Soviet Union on 22 June 1941. The June deportation remains a central trauma in the national memory of the Baltic states, commemorated annually on 14 June, and is regarded by the European Court of Human Rights and several national legislatures as a crime against humanity.
