Academy Award for Best Actor

The Academy Award for Best Actor is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS). It is given to an actor who has delivered an outstanding performance in a leading role in a film released that year. The award is traditionally presented by the previous year's Best Actress winner.

The 1st Academy Awards were held in 1929, and the award was given to Emil Jannings for his performances in The Last Command and The Way of All Flesh. Currently, nominees are determined by single transferable vote within the actors branch of AMPAS; winners are selected by a plurality vote from the entire eligible voting members of the Academy.

Records

Daniel Day-Lewis holds the record for the most wins in this category, with three Best Actor awards, for My Left Foot (1989), There Will Be Blood (2007) and Lincoln (2012). Nine actors have won the award twice, including Spencer Tracy, Marlon Brando, Jack Nicholson and Tom Hanks. The youngest winner is Adrien Brody, who won at age 29 for The Pianist (2002); the oldest is Anthony Hopkins, who won at age 83 for The Father (2020). Peter O'Toole holds the record for the most nominations without a win, with eight.

Winners and nominees

The award has been given every year since 1929. Notable repeat nominees include Laurence Olivier, Paul Newman, Al Pacino, and Denzel Washington. Winners are chosen from five nominees (ten in the earliest years), and the category is paired with the Academy Award for Best Actress, as well as the supporting categories including the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress.
