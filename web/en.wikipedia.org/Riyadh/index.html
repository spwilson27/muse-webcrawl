Riyadh

Riyadh (Arabic: الرياض, ar-Riyāḍ, "the Gardens") is the capital and largest city of Saudi Arabia, and the capital of the Riyadh Province. With a population of roughly seven million, it is the political and administrative centre of the kingdom and one of the largest cities on the Arabian Peninsula.

History

Riyadh grew from the historic town of Diriyah's successor settlement on the site of the ancient city of Hajr. It was captured by Abdulaziz Ibn Saud in 1902, becoming the base from which he reunified the peninsula; when the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia was proclaimed in 1932, Riyadh became its capital. Oil wealth transformed the small walled town into a sprawling modern metropolis in the later 20th century.

Geography and economy

The city sits on a plateau in the Najd region at about 600 m elevation, with a hot desert climate. Landmarks include the Kingdom Centre, Al Faisaliah Centre, the historic Masmak Fortress, and Diriyah's At-Turaif district, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Riyadh is the centre of Saudi government, finance, and the ongoing Vision 2030 development programme, and it hosts the Riyadh Metro, which opened in 2024.
