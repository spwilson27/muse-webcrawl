Gaussian quadrature

In numerical analysis, Gaussian quadrature (or Gauss quadrature) is a rule for approximating a definite integral by a weighted sum of function values at specially chosen points, called nodes. An n-point Gaussian rule is exact for all polynomials of degree up to 2n − 1, the highest degree achievable with n points.

Theory

For integrals over [−1, 1] with unit weight, the optimal nodes are the roots of the Legendre polynomial of degree n, and suitable weights can be computed from the polynomial's derivative. The construction generalises to other weight functions and intervals through families of orthogonal polynomials, giving the Gauss–Legendre, Gauss–Chebyshev, Gauss–Laguerre, and Gauss–Hermite rules.

Computation

Nodes and weights are efficiently computed by the Golub–Welsch algorithm, which finds them from the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a symmetric tridiagonal matrix built from the three-term recurrence of the orthogonal polynomials. Error terms involve the (2n)-th derivative of the integrand, so the rules converge rapidly for smooth functions.
