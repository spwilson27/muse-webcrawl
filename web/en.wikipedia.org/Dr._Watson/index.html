Dr. Watson

Dr. John Watson is a fictional character created by Sir Arthur Conan Doyle. He is the companion, friend, and biographer of the detective Sherlock Holmes, and the narrator of most of the Holmes stories. Watson is a former British Army surgeon who served in Afghanistan before meeting Holmes in London in 1881.

Watson first appears in A Study in Scarlet (1887), where he shares rooms with Holmes at 221B Baker Street. He marries Mary Morstan in The Sign of the Four (1890) and later practises medicine in London, while continuing to assist Holmes on cases.

Character

Watson serves as the reader's stand-in: Holmes explains his deductions to Watson, and therefore to the reader. He is brave, loyal, and practical, and his medical training occasionally proves useful in investigations. Conan Doyle deliberately made Watson an ordinary, decent man in contrast to Holmes's extraordinary intellect, a pairing that has become a template for detective fiction — the brilliant detective and his loyal companion.

In adaptations

Watson has been portrayed by many actors, including Nigel Bruce opposite Basil Rathbone, Martin Freeman in the BBC series Sherlock, Jude Law in Guy Ritchie's films, and Lucy Liu as Joan Watson in the American series Elementary.
