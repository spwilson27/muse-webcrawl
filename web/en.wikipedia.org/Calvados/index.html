Calvados is a brandy from Normandy in France, made from apples (and sometimes pears). It is the only French brandy produced under an appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) outside the Cognac and Armagnac regions, and it takes its name from the Calvados department of Normandy.

Production

Calvados is made by first pressing apples to produce cider, which is fermented and then distilled. The resulting eau-de-vie is aged in oak casks for a minimum of two years for the basic appellation; longer ageing produces smoother, more complex spirits. Dozens of apple varieties, both sweet and bitter, are traditionally blended to give the cider its balance.

Appellations

There are three AOCs: AOC Calvados, the broad regional appellation; AOC Calvados Pays d'Auge, from the prized Pays d'Auge district, which requires double pot distillation; and AOC Calvados Domfrontais, which must contain at least 30 percent pears. Age statements include Fine or VS (at least two years), Vieux or Réserve (three years), and VSOP (four years), with XO and hors d'âge blends aged six years or more.

Consumption

Calvados is traditionally drunk as an apéritif or digestif, and features in the Norman custom of the trou normand, a pause of calvados taken between courses of a long meal. It is also used in cooking, in cocktails such as the Jack Rose, and in the traditional coffee-calva.
