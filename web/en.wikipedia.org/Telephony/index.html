Telephony

Telephony is the field of technology involving the development, application, and deployment of telecommunication services for the purpose of electronic transmission of voice, fax, or data, between distant parties. The term is derived from the telephone, invented in the 1870s, and initially referred to the provision and operation of telephone networks. In the modern era, telephony encompasses fixed-line, mobile, and internet-based voice services.

History

Telephony began with the telephone systems of the late 19th century, in which human operators at manual switchboards connected calls. Automatic electromechanical exchanges, beginning with the Strowger switch, progressively replaced operators. Digital switching, fiber-optic transmission, and stored-program control followed in the later 20th century, while signaling systems such as SS7 separated call control from the voice path.

Technology

Traditional telephony used circuit-switched networks—the public switched telephone network (PSTN)—in which a dedicated circuit is established for each call. Modern systems increasingly use packet-switched voice over IP (VoIP), digitizing and compressing voice and sending it over data networks. Telephony endpoints range from plain old telephone service (POTS) handsets to software phones and mobile devices.

Services

Telephony services include local and long-distance voice calling, mobile telephony, toll-free numbers, conference calling, voicemail, call forwarding, and emergency services such as 911 and 112. Businesses use private branch exchanges (PBX) and, more recently, cloud-based unified communications platforms.
