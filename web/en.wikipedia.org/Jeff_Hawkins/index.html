Jeff Hawkins

Jeffrey Hawkins

Born: 1 June 1957, Huntington, New York, U.S.

Alma mater: Cornell University; UC Berkeley

Known for: PalmPilot, Handspring, Numenta

Jeffrey Hawkins (born June 1, 1957) is an American businessman, neuroscientist and inventor, best known as the founder of Palm, Inc. and Handspring and the inventor of the PalmPilot personal digital assistant. He later co-founded the neuroscience company Numenta.

Computing career

After working at Intel and GRiD Systems, Hawkins founded Palm Computing in 1992. Following the disappointing Zoomer, he designed the PalmPilot (1996), a compact, fast and simple handheld that became a mass-market success. In 1998 he left to co-found Handspring, whose Visor handhelds and later Treo smartphones anticipated the converged mobile phone.

Neuroscience

Hawkins has pursued a lifelong interest in how the brain works. He established the Redwood Neuroscience Institute in 2002 and, with Dileep George, founded Numenta in 2005 to develop machine intelligence based on neocortical theory. His books On Intelligence (2004, with Sandra Blakeslee) and A Thousand Brains (2021) set out his theory of intelligence and the neocortex. He was elected to the National Academy of Engineering in 2003.
