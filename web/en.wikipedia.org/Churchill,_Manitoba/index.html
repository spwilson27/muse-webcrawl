Churchill, Manitoba

Churchill is a town on the shore of Hudson Bay in Manitoba, Canada, at the mouth of the Churchill River. It has a population of under 1,000 and is famous for its polar bears, which gather nearby each autumn, earning the town the title "Polar Bear Capital of the World". Churchill is also known for beluga whale watching in summer and for northern lights viewing in winter.

History

The area has been inhabited for thousands of years by ancestors of today's Cree, Dene, and Inuit peoples. The Hudson's Bay Company built Fort Churchill in 1717 after earlier trading posts and explorations in the region. The modern port was developed by the Canadian government from 1929, and the Hudson Bay Railway reached the town the same year. During the Cold War, the Churchill Rocket Research Range hosted sounding rocket launches for upper-atmosphere research.

Transport

Churchill has no road connection to the rest of Manitoba. It is reached by air (Churchill Airport) and by the Hudson Bay Railway from Winnipeg, and its deep-water port is Canada's only Arctic seaport connected to the North American rail network. The port handles grain and other cargo during the short ice-free season.

Wildlife and tourism

Each October and November, polar bears congregate near the town while waiting for Hudson Bay to freeze; specialised tundra vehicles carry tourists to view them. In summer, thousands of beluga whales enter the Churchill River estuary. The surrounding area supports birdwatching, the Itsanitaq Museum's Inuit art collection, and the Prince of Wales Fort National Historic Site across the river.
