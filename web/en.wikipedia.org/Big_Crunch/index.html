Big Crunch

The Big Crunch is a hypothetical scenario for the ultimate fate of the universe, in which the expansion of the universe eventually reverses and the universe recollapses, ultimately ending as a black hole singularity or causing a reformation of the universe starting with another big bang. Suggestive evidence, from observations of the accelerating expansion of the universe around the late 1990s, indicates that a Big Crunch is less likely to happen than other scenarios such as the Big Freeze, though the question remains debated.

In the Big Crunch scenario, cosmic expansion halts and reverses into collapse.

Overview

The Big Crunch theory holds that the average density of the universe is enough to stop its expansion and begin contracting. If gravity ultimately overcomes expansion, galaxies would blueshift, merge, and the temperature of the universe would rise without bound as it contracted. Roughly 100,000 years before a collapse, the temperature of the universe would be so high that stars would be hotter inside than on their surfaces, and a few minutes before the end, atomic nuclei would break apart before the entire universe compressed into a singularity.

Observational status

The scenario depends on the nature of dark energy and the average density of the universe. Most cosmologists agree that observations — including measurements of distant supernovae showing accelerated expansion — suggest the universe will expand forever unless dark energy behaves unexpectedly, for instance if its equation of state changes with time. Some recent studies of dark energy have revived discussion of a possible future collapse, but no consensus exists.

Cyclic models

A related idea is the cyclic model, in which a Big Crunch is followed by a Big Bounce or a new Big Bang, producing an endless sequence of universes. Variants appear in loop quantum cosmology and in older oscillating-universe theories associated with Alexander Friedmann and Richard Tolman.
