Hugo Award for Best Related Work

The Hugo Award for Best Related Work is one of the Hugo Awards given each year for primarily non-fiction works related to science fiction or fantasy, or to the fields' fandom. It is presented by the World Science Fiction Society at the annual Worldcon, and is voted on by members of that year's convention. The category was created in 1980 as the Hugo Award for Best Non-Fiction Book and took its present name in 1999.

Scope

Eligible works include criticism, biography, art books, reference works, and other non-fiction connected to science fiction and fantasy; since the renaming, works of any length or format may qualify, including essays, blog posts, and fan writing projects. The category is broadly defined as covering any work related to the field that is not eligible in another Hugo category.

Notable winners

Winning works have included encyclopedias of science fiction and fantasy, biographies of authors, art collections, and critical studies. Repeat winners in the category include John Clute and Peter Nicholls (for The Encyclopedia of Science Fiction), and works associated with authors and editors such as L. Sprague de Camp and Isaac Asimov have also been honoured. In recent years the award has gone to a wide range of projects, from exhibition catalogues to online essay series and fan-history projects.

Relationship to other awards

The Hugo Awards also include categories for fiction of various lengths, dramatic presentations, and editors and artists; Best Related Work complements these by recognising the literature about the field itself.
