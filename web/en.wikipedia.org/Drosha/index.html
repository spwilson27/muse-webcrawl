Drosha is a Class 2 member of the ribonuclease III enzyme family and the core nuclease that executes the initiation step of microRNA (miRNA) processing in the nucleus of animal cells. It was identified in 2000, and named after Drosophila, the fruit fly in which it was characterized.

Function

Drosha forms the catalytic heart of the Microprocessor complex together with its cofactor DGCR8 (called Pasha in invertebrates). The complex binds a primary miRNA transcript (pri-miRNA) and cleaves it about 11 base pairs from the base of its hairpin stem, releasing a ~65–70-nucleotide precursor miRNA (pre-miRNA) with a characteristic 2-nucleotide 3′ overhang. The pre-miRNA is then exported to the cytoplasm by Exportin-5, where a second RNase III enzyme, Dicer, cuts it into the mature ~22-nucleotide miRNA duplex that is loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). Drosha also has functions beyond miRNA biogenesis, including cleavage of certain messenger RNAs (such as its own partner DGCR8's message, a feedback loop) and roles in ribosomal RNA processing.
