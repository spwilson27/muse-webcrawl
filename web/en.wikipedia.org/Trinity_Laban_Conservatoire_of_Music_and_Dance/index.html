Trinity Laban Conservatoire of Music and Dance is a music and dance conservatoire based in Greenwich and Deptford, in south-east London. It was formed in 2005 by the merger of two institutions with long independent histories: Trinity College of Music and the Laban Dance Centre. It is one of the leading centres for the training of professional musicians and contemporary dancers in the United Kingdom.

History

Trinity College of Music was founded in 1872 by the Reverend Henry George Bonavia Hunt, initially to improve the standard of church music and choral singing. It grew into a full conservatoire offering degrees in performance and composition, and in 2001 it moved from Marylebone to King Charles Court at the Old Royal Naval College in Greenwich, a building designed in part by Sir Christopher Wren.

The Laban Dance Centre grew out of the work of Rudolf Laban, the Hungarian-born dance theorist who developed the system of movement analysis known as Labanotation and Laban Movement Analysis. The centre bearing his name was established in London in the mid-20th century and became a leading institution for contemporary dance training. In 2003 the dance faculty moved into a striking new building in Deptford designed by the Swiss architects Herzog & de Meuron, which won the Stirling Prize for architecture that year.

Faculties and study

The conservatoire has two faculties: the Faculty of Music, based at King Charles Court in Greenwich, and the Faculty of Dance, based at the Laban Building in Deptford Creekside. It offers undergraduate and postgraduate programmes in classical performance, jazz, composition, musical theatre, choreography and dance science. Trinity Laban was the first conservatoire in the UK to offer a master's degree in dance science.

The institution is also known for its extensive work in music and dance education and community engagement across London, including programmes for young musicians and dancers.

Notable alumni

  • Composers and performers associated with Trinity include conductors, jazz musicians and composers across classical and contemporary genres.
  • Dance alumni have gone on to dance and choreograph with companies such as Ballet Rambert and numerous independent contemporary companies.
