Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve is a United States national park and preserve in south-central Alaska. It is the largest national park in the United States, covering 13,175,799 acres (53,321 km²) — larger than Switzerland — and contains nine of the sixteen highest peaks in the country, including Mount St. Elias at 18,008 feet (5,489 m).

Geography

The park protects the convergence of the Wrangell, St. Elias, Chugach and Alaska mountain ranges, vast glaciers such as the Malaspina and Hubbard, and the historic Kennecott copper mines. It was established on December 2, 1980, by the Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act, and forms part of the Kluane/Wrangell–St. Elias/Glacier Bay/Tatshenshini-Alsek UNESCO World Heritage Site.
