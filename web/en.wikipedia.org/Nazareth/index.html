Nazareth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nazareth (Arabic: النَّاصِرَة; Hebrew: נָצְרַת) is the largest city in the Northern District of Israel, with a population largely of Arab citizens, both Muslim and Christian. It is famous as the city described in the New Testament as the childhood home of Jesus and is a centre of Christian pilgrimage, with many churches commemorating biblical events.

History

In the time of Jesus, Nazareth was a small Jewish agricultural village of a few hundred people, overshadowed by nearby Sepphoris. The Gospels record the Annunciation to Mary there and Joseph and Mary's return with the child Jesus ("He shall be called a Nazarene"). After the Byzantine period the city passed through Muslim, Crusader, and Ottoman hands; Tancred made it a Crusader lordship. In the 19th century churches and hospices were rebuilt, and under the British Mandate and then Israel it grew into the largest Arab city in the country.

Landmarks

The great Basilica of the Annunciation, consecrated in 1969, stands over the grotto traditionally identified as Mary's home. Other sites include St. Gabriel's Church over Mary's Well, the Greek Orthodox Church of the Annunciation, and the ancient market of the old city. See also Jesus and Galilee.
