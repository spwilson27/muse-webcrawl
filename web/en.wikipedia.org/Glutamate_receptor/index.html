Glutamate receptor

Glutamate receptors are synaptic and extrasynaptic receptors located primarily on the membranes of neuronal and glial cells. Glutamate is the principal excitatory neurotransmitter of the vertebrate central nervous system and an abundant amino acid; it activates both ionotropic receptors (ligand-gated ion channels) and metabotropic receptors (G protein-coupled receptors). Glutamatergic transmission underlies most fast excitatory signaling in the brain and is central to synaptic plasticity, learning, and memory — and, when excessive, to excitotoxic neuronal injury.

Ionotropic receptors

Three families of ionotropic glutamate receptors are named for the agonists that activate them:

ReceptorSubunitsKey properties
AMPA receptorGluA1–GluA4Fast Na+/K+ currents; mediates baseline fast excitation
NMDA receptorGluN1, GluN2A–D, GluN3Requires glutamate, a co-agonist (glycine/D-serine) and depolarization to relieve Mg2+ block; highly Ca2+-permeable
Kainate receptorGluK1–GluK5Pre- and postsynaptic modulation, slower kinetics

The NMDA receptor acts as a molecular coincidence detector: it opens fully only when presynaptic glutamate release coincides with postsynaptic depolarization, a property that makes it essential to long-term potentiation (LTP) in the hippocampus.

Metabotropic receptors

The eight metabotropic glutamate receptors (mGluR1–8) are class C G protein-coupled receptors grouped by sequence and signaling: Group I (mGluR1, 5) couple to Gq and raise intracellular calcium; Groups II (mGluR2, 3) and III (mGluR4, 6, 7, 8) couple to Gi/o and typically act as presynaptic autoreceptors that suppress transmitter release. mGluR6 is specialized for retinal ON-bipolar cell signaling.

Role in plasticity and disease

Trafficking of AMPA receptors into and out of synapses is a leading mechanism of LTP and LTD. Dysregulated glutamate signaling is implicated in stroke and traumatic excitotoxicity, Alzheimer's disease (the NMDA antagonist memantine is an approved treatment), schizophrenia (the NMDA-hypofunction hypothesis), epilepsy, chronic pain, and anxiety and addiction, in which mGluRs are active drug targets.

Pharmacology

Agonists include glutamate itself, NMDA, AMPA and kainate; notable antagonists and modulators include ketamine and phencyclidine (NMDA channel blockers), memantine, CNQX and NBQX (AMPA/kainate antagonists), perampanel (an approved AMPA antagonist antiepileptic), and allosteric modulators of mGluRs under clinical investigation.
