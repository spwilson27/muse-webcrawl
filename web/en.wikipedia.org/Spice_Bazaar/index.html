The Spice Bazaar (Turkish: Mısır Çarşısı, meaning "Egyptian Bazaar") in Istanbul, Turkey, is one of the largest bazaars in the city. Located in the Eminönü quarter of the Fatih district, it is the second largest covered shopping complex after the Grand Bazaar. It is sometimes translated as the Egyptian Bazaar or Egyptian Market, because it was built with revenues from the Ottoman eyalet of Egypt in 1660, and because spices were imported via Egypt.

History

The building was designed by the court architect Koca Kasım Ağa, but construction works began under the supervision of another court architect, Mustafa Ağa, in the last months of 1660. It formed part of the complex of the New Mosque (Yeni Cami), and the rents from its shops were intended to support the upkeep of the mosque and its charitable works. The bazaar has an L-shaped plan with six gates, and its shops have long been famous for selling spices, Turkish delight, jewellery, dried fruits and nuts, and other goods.

The bazaar today

The Spice Bazaar remains one of Istanbul's most popular attractions, drawing locals and tourists to its vaulted halls filled with pyramids of colourful spices, teas, lokum (Turkish delight), dried fruits, honey, and traditional remedies. The market area around it, including the surrounding streets of Eminönü and the flower and pet markets nearby, forms a dense commercial district by the Golden Horn.
