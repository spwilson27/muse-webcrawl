Otago


Country: New Zealand
Location: South-eastern South Island
Largest city: Dunedin
Population: about 245,000

Otago is a region of New Zealand located in the southern half of the South Island, administered by the Otago Regional Council. It covers roughly 32,000 square kilometres — New Zealand's second-largest local government region — and includes the city of Dunedin, the resort towns of Queenstown and Wānaka, and the dry inland basin of Central Otago.

Geography and history

The landscape ranges from the rugged Southern Alps lakes district in the west to rolling tussock country and the schist ranges of Central Otago, famous for its extremes of temperature and its stone-fruit orchards and pinot noir vineyards. Māori settlement centred on coastal sites such as the Otago Peninsula. The modern province was founded in 1848 by Scottish Free Church settlers, who established Dunedin; the discovery of gold near Lawrence in 1861 triggered the Otago gold rush, briefly making Dunedin New Zealand's largest and richest city and financing its university, founded in 1869.

Economy

Agriculture, tourism, education and hydroelectric generation on the Clutha River (New Zealand's largest river by volume) underpin the regional economy. The University of Otago in Dunedin is the country's oldest university, and Queenstown-Lakes is one of New Zealand's premier international tourist destinations.
