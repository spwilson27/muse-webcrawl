Orthogonal group

In mathematics, the orthogonal group in dimension n, denoted O(n), is the group of all distance-preserving linear transformations of an n-dimensional Euclidean space — rotations and reflections — equivalently the group of n × n orthogonal matrices under multiplication, where a matrix Q is orthogonal if QTQ = QQT = I.

Properties

Orthogonal matrices have determinant ±1. The subgroup of matrices with determinant +1 is the special orthogonal group SO(n), consisting of rotations. O(n) is a compact Lie group of dimension n(n−1)/2 with two connected components; SO(n) is the identity component. Its Lie algebra consists of skew-symmetric matrices. Over general fields, orthogonal groups are defined relative to a quadratic form, giving groups such as the indefinite orthogonal groups O(p,q) important in relativity — the Lorentz group is O(1,3).

Low dimensions

SO(2) is isomorphic to the circle group of rotations of the plane. SO(3), the rotation group of three-dimensional space, is central to mechanics and quantum theory, where it relates to the spin groups: its double cover is SU(2). SO(4) enjoys an exceptional decomposition via quaternions.
