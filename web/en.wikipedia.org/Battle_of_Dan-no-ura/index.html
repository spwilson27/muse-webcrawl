The Battle of Dan-no-ura (Japanese: 壇ノ浦の戦い) was a major naval battle of the Genpei War, fought on April 25, 1185, in the Shimonoseki Straits off the southern tip of Honshu. The fleet of the Minamoto clan, led by Minamoto no Yoshitsune, destroyed that of the Taira clan, ending the war and clearing the way for the establishment of the Kamakura shogunate.

The battle

The Taira, accompanied by the child Emperor Antoku and bearing the imperial regalia, initially benefited from the tides, which were thought to favour their more experienced sailors. But the currents reversed during the day — a shift the Taira had failed to anticipate as well as their opponents — and the defection of Taguchi Shigeyoshi, who revealed the location of the emperor's ship, proved decisive. Minamoto archers reportedly targeted the Taira helmsmen and rowers.

Death of Emperor Antoku

As defeat became certain, many Taira nobles drowned themselves; Antoku's grandmother, Taira no Tokiko, leapt into the sea with the seven-year-old emperor in her arms. The sacred sword Kusanagi was said to have been lost with him — one of the enduring mysteries of Japanese tradition. The battle is vividly retold in The Tale of the Heike, and local folklore holds that the heikegani ("Taira crabs") of the straits bear the faces of the drowned warriors.
