Waffle iron

A waffle iron or waffle maker is a kitchen appliance used to cook waffles. It consists of two hinged metal plates with a grid pattern that molds the batter and cooks it from both sides at once, producing the waffle's characteristic pattern of raised squares and pockets.

History

Early waffle irons were pairs of iron plates on long handles, heated over a fire or on a stove; ornate patterned irons were used in the Low Countries and France from the Middle Ages onward for wafers and waffles. The modern stovetop iron was patented in the United States by Cornelius Swartwout in 1869. Electric models appeared in the early 20th century — General Electric developed an early electric waffle iron around 1911 — and thermostats and nonstick coatings followed, making the appliance a standard countertop device.

Types

Waffle irons vary by the style of waffle they produce: standard American waffles, thick Belgian waffles with deep pockets, thin and crisp waffles, and novelty shapes. Commercial versions used in hotels and restaurants can bake several waffles at once, and rotating "flip" designs help spread batter evenly. A related cultural note: in 1971, Nike co-founder Bill Bowerman famously used a waffle iron to mold the rubber sole of early Nike running shoes, giving rise to the "Waffle Trainer".
