The 1984 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXIII Olympiad, were held from 28 July to 12 August 1984 in Los Angeles, California. They were the second Summer Games hosted by the city after 1932. With a record 140 nations participating, the Games are remembered both for their commercial success and for the absence of fourteen Eastern Bloc nations led by the Soviet Union.

Boycott

The Soviet Union announced its boycott in May 1984, citing security concerns and "chauvinistic sentiments" in the United States, in what was widely understood as retaliation for the American-led boycott of the 1980 Moscow Olympics. Fourteen allied nations joined, though Romania notably broke ranks and competed, receiving a warm welcome. The boycotting nations staged the alternative Friendship Games.

Organisation and finance

Under organising committee president Peter Ueberroth, the Los Angeles Games were almost entirely privately financed, relying on existing venues, corporate sponsorship and television rights rather than new public construction. The Games produced a surplus of more than US$230 million — an unprecedented result that transformed the financial model of the Olympic movement after the debt-laden 1976 Montreal Games.

Sporting highlights

American sprinter and long jumper Carl Lewis matched Jesse Owens' 1936 feat by winning four gold medals. Gymnast Mary Lou Retton became the first non-European to win the women's all-around title. Britain's Daley Thompson retained his decathlon title, and Sebastian Coe repeated his 1500 metres gold from Moscow. The Games featured the first women's Olympic marathon, won by Joan Benoit, and synchronised swimming and rhythmic gymnastics made their Olympic debuts.

Medal table and legacy

The United States topped the medal table with 83 gold medals and 174 medals overall, its largest haul ever, aided by the Soviet absence. The Los Angeles model of private financing influenced every subsequent Olympic bid, and profits funded youth sport through the LA84 Foundation.
