Road rage

Heavy congestion, a common trigger of road rage

Road rage is aggressive or angry behaviour exhibited by motorists. These behaviours include rude and verbal insults, yelling, physical threats or dangerous driving methods targeted at other drivers, pedestrians or cyclists in an effort to intimidate or release frustration.

The term originated in the United States in the late 1980s, popularised by Los Angeles television reports of freeway shootings. Psychologists classify severe road rage as a possible symptom of intermittent explosive disorder.

Causes

Contributing factors include traffic congestion, time pressure, anonymity inside a vehicle, perceived slights such as being cut off, and displacement of stress from other areas of life. Studies associate younger male drivers with higher rates of aggressive driving.

Legal status

Road rage is distinguished from ordinary aggressive driving in many jurisdictions: aggressive driving is a traffic offence, while road rage is a criminal offence involving intent to harm. Penalties range from fines and licence suspension to imprisonment where assault or injury results.

Prevention

Motoring organisations advise drivers to avoid eye contact and retaliation, allow extra journey time, and never exit the vehicle during a confrontation. Several countries run public-awareness campaigns against aggressive driving.
