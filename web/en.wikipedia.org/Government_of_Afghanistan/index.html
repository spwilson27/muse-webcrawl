Government of Afghanistan

The government of Afghanistan is the central administration of the country, centred in the capital, Kabul. Since the Taliban takeover of August 2021, Afghanistan has been ruled as an Islamic Emirate by the Taliban under a supreme leader, without international recognition as the legitimate government for most of that period and without a published constitution in force. Previously, from 2004 to 2021, the country was the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan, a presidential republic under the 2004 constitution.

The Islamic Republic (2004–2021)

Following the fall of the first Taliban regime in 2001, the Bonn Agreement established an interim administration, and the 2004 constitution created a presidential system with a directly elected president as head of state and government, a bicameral National Assembly (the Wolesi Jirga and Meshrano Jirga), and an independent judiciary headed by the Supreme Court. Presidents Hamid Karzai (2004–2014) and Ashraf Ghani (2014–2021) led the republic, which depended heavily on international military and financial support.

The Islamic Emirate (2021–present)

As United States and NATO forces withdrew in 2021, the Taliban captured Kabul on 15 August 2021; President Ghani fled the country. The Taliban re-established the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan, governed by a supreme leader, Hibatullah Akhundzada, based in Kandahar, with a council of ministers in Kabul headed by a prime minister (Hasan Akhund). Laws are based on the group's interpretation of Islamic sharia. The regime has imposed sweeping restrictions on women and girls, including bans on secondary and university education for girls. Its government long went unrecognized by any UN member state; Russia became the first country to formally recognize it in 2025.

Structure and provinces

Afghanistan is divided into 34 provinces (velayat), each headed by a governor. Ministries in Kabul oversee finance, interior, defence, health, and other portfolios, and district and provincial administrations deliver local services.
