Cavia

Cavia is a genus in the subfamily Caviinae that contains the rodents commonly known as guinea pigs or cavies. The best-known species in this genus is the domestic guinea pig, Cavia porcellus, an important food source in South America and a widely kept household pet elsewhere.

Species

The genus includes species such as the Brazilian guinea pig (Cavia aperea), the shiny guinea pig (Cavia fulgida), the Santa Catarina's guinea pig (Cavia intermedia), the greater guinea pig (Cavia magna), the montane guinea pig (Cavia tschudii), and Cavia patzelti. Wild species are found across South America in grasslands, savannas, and rocky areas.

Characteristics

Cavies are stout, tailless rodents with short limbs and continuously growing teeth. They are herbivorous grazers, highly social, and communicate with a variety of vocalisations. Females give birth to well-developed, precocial young after a relatively long gestation for a rodent.

Domestication

Cavia porcellus was domesticated in the Andes several thousand years ago from wild ancestors close to C. tschudii. Guinea pigs became popular pets in Europe after the 16th century and have been widely used as laboratory animals in scientific research.
