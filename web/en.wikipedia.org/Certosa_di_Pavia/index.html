The Certosa di Pavia is a monastery and complex in Lombardy, northern Italy, situated near a small town of the same name in the province of Pavia, about 8 kilometres north of the city of Pavia. Built between the late 14th and 16th centuries, it is one of the largest monasteries in Italy and among the finest examples of Italian Renaissance architecture, renowned for its richly sculpted marble façade.

History

The charterhouse was commissioned by Gian Galeazzo Visconti, first Duke of Milan, who laid the foundation stone on 27 August 1396. He intended it as the dynastic mausoleum of the Visconti family and as the fulfillment of a vow made by his wife Caterina Visconti. Construction, supervised first by Marco Solari and later by architects including Giovanni Antonio Amadeo, proceeded slowly and continued under the Sforza dukes; the church was consecrated in 1497. Work on the decoration of the façade and interiors continued into the 16th and 17th centuries.

The Carthusian monks were expelled when the monastery was suppressed in 1782 under Joseph II; after various occupations the complex returned to religious use, was declared a national monument in 1866, and was given to Cistercian monks in the 20th century (the Carthusians briefly returned in the 20th century before Cistercian administration).

Art and architecture

The church combines a late Gothic plan with Renaissance decoration. The façade, designed by Amadeo and finished in the early 16th century, is covered in marble inlay, medallions, statues and reliefs. Inside are the tombs of Gian Galeazzo Visconti and of Ludovico Sforza and Beatrice d'Este, frescoes by Bergognone (Ambrogio da Fossano), paintings by Perugino and others, and an elaborate carved choir. The complex includes the Great Cloister, the Small Cloister, the monks' cells, and a museum (Museo della Certosa) with plaster casts and exhibits on the monastery's history.
