Chassis

A ladder-frame truck chassis.
FunctionStructural frame supporting body, engine, and running gear
Common typesLadder frame, monocoque/unibody, backbone, space frame
Word originFrench châssis, "frame"

A chassis (from French châssis, pronounced "shass-ee") is the load-bearing framework of a vehicle or other manufactured object, supporting the rest of the structure. In an automobile, the chassis traditionally consists of the frame together with the engine, transmission, suspension, steering, and wheels — the "rolling chassis" to which a body is added. By extension, the term is used for the frame or enclosure of electronic equipment, railway vehicles, artillery, and aircraft landing structures.

Vehicle chassis types

  • Ladder frame — two longitudinal rails joined by crossmembers, resembling a ladder. The classic construction for trucks, SUVs, and early cars: strong in bending and easy to modify for coachbuilt bodies, but heavy and relatively flexible in torsion.
  • Monocoque / unibody — the body shell itself carries the structural loads, with no separate frame. Now standard for passenger cars, it offers better crash performance, stiffness, and weight efficiency.
  • Backbone chassis — a strong central spine connecting front and rear suspension assemblies, used in some sports cars (Lotus) and trucks (Tatra).
  • Space frame — a three-dimensional lattice of tubes or struts, common in racing cars and specialist sports cars; the related tub of carbon-fibre composite is used in Formula One.
  • Platform — modern manufacturers increasingly design shared "platforms" or "skateboard" architectures, especially for electric vehicles, where a flat floor pan carries the battery pack.

Functions

The chassis must support the weight of the vehicle and its loads, resist bending and torsional forces in cornering and over uneven ground, locate the suspension and steering, and contribute to crashworthiness by providing controlled load paths and crumple structures. Stiffness of the chassis strongly affects handling, since suspension geometry can only work as designed on a rigid foundation.

Other uses

In electronics, a chassis is the metal frame or enclosure that holds circuit boards and components — as in a computer case or a radio chassis — providing mounting, shielding, and grounding. Railway rolling stock and horse-drawn vehicles also have chassis in analogous senses.
