Three marks of existence

In Buddhism, the three marks of existence (Pali: tilakkhaṇa; Sanskrit: trilakṣaṇa) are three characteristics shared by all conditioned phenomena — everything that exists in the cycle of rebirth (samsara):

  • Anicca — impermanence: all conditioned things are inconstant and ever-changing.
  • Dukkha — suffering or unsatisfactoriness: conditioned existence cannot provide lasting satisfaction.
  • Anattā — non-self: no phenomenon possesses an unchanging, independent essence or soul.

Realising the three marks is central to liberation

Anicca (impermanence)

Anicca holds that all conditioned phenomena arise and pass away, depending on causes and conditions; nothing remains unchanged even for two consecutive moments. Clinging to what is inherently unstable is a root cause of suffering, and contemplation of impermanence is a major theme of insight meditation (vipassana).

Dukkha (suffering)

Dukkha, the subject of the Buddha's first noble truth, ranges from obvious physical pain to the subtle unsatisfactoriness of pleasant experiences, which are unreliable because they are impermanent. The Buddha taught that "whatever is impermanent is dukkha" (yad aniccaṃ taṃ dukkhaṃ).

Anattā (non-self)

Anattā denies that any phenomenon — including the five aggregates (skandhas) that constitute a person — is a permanent self or soul (attā/ātman). This teaching distinguishes Buddhism from the Hindu traditions of its time, which affirmed an eternal self. The stock formula of the early texts runs: "all dhammas are non-self" (sabbe dhammā anattā), extending the claim even to unconditioned nirvana.

Role in practice

Insight into the three marks, developed through hearing, reflection and meditation, uproots ignorance (avijjā) and craving, leading toward awakening. In the Mahayana tradition the three marks are sometimes supplemented or reformulated — for example, some sutras speak of nirvana itself as characterised by permanence, bliss, self and purity — while Theravada maintains the three marks as universal and definitive.
