Judea

Judea (Hebrew: Yehuda; Greek: Ioudaia; Latin: Iudaea)
Region of the southern Levant
Principal city: Jerusalem
Terrain: Mountainous ridge, descending to the Dead Sea

Judea is a historical and geographical name for the mountainous southern region of the Levant, roughly corresponding to the territory of the ancient Kingdom of Judah and, in modern terms, the southern part of the West Bank and adjacent areas of Israel.

Geography

Judea consists of a central mountain ridge running north–south, rising around Jerusalem, Hebron, and Bethlehem, and falling away eastward to the Judean Desert and the Dead Sea — the lowest land on Earth — and westward toward the coastal plain. The region's rocky terrain, scarcity of water, and defensible highlands shaped its history.

History

In the Iron Age, Judea was the heartland of the Kingdom of Judah, whose capital was Jerusalem and whose Temple stood at its religious center. After the Babylonian exile and Persian, Hellenistic, and Hasmonean periods, Judea came under Roman rule, becoming the province of Judaea in AD 6. It was the setting for much of the life of Jesus and for the Jewish–Roman wars of the 1st and 2nd centuries AD.

Under successive Byzantine, Islamic, Crusader, Mamluk, and Ottoman administrations, the name Judea fell out of official use, though it remained current in religious and scholarly contexts. Today the term is used historically and, in the form "Judea and Samaria," as the Israeli administrative name for the West Bank.
