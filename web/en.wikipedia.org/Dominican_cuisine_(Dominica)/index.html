Dominican cuisine (Dominica)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dominican cuisine is the cuisine of the island nation of Dominica in the Caribbean (not to be confused with the cuisine of the Dominican Republic). It is a Creole cuisine shaped by Kalinago, African, French and British influences, based on the island's abundant tropical produce, root vegetables and seafood.

Staples and dishes

Ground provisions — yam, dasheen (taro), sweet potato, tannia and green banana — form the base of most meals, alongside breadfruit and plantain. Saltfish (salted cod), a legacy of the colonial trade, is a national staple, commonly served with bakes (fried dough) for breakfast. Callaloo, a soup of dasheen leaves cooked with coconut milk, okra and seasonings (sometimes with crab), is often cited as the national dish. Other favourites include goat water (a spiced goat stew), pelau, sancoche, and one-pot broths of fish or chicken with dumplings and provisions.

Ingredients and influences

Fresh fish such as mahi-mahi, tuna and flying fish, plus river crayfish and land crabs, supply much of the protein. The "mountain chicken" — in fact a large frog, the crapaud — was long a celebrated delicacy, though it is now endangered and protected. Seasoning is generous: garlic, onion, celery, thyme and Scotch bonnet peppers, with coconut milk enriching many dishes. Tropical fruits — mango, guava, passion fruit, soursop and citrus — provide juices and desserts, and bay oil and locally made rum punch accompany festive meals.

Culture

Dominica's Creole heritage is celebrated each October during the World Creole Music Festival and Creole Day, when traditional dishes are served in abundance and many Dominicans wear madras dress.
