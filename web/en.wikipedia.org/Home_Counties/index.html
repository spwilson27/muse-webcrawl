Home Counties

The home counties are the counties of England that surround London. The term has no precise legal definition, but it conventionally includes Berkshire, Buckinghamshire, Essex, Hertfordshire, Kent, Surrey, and Sussex (East and West Sussex), with Middlesex — now largely absorbed into Greater London — historically counted among them. Bedfordshire, Hampshire, and Oxfordshire are sometimes included.

Origin and usage

The phrase, in use since the nineteenth century, originally referred to the counties from which the capital drew its "home" services and assizes circuits. Because the home counties contain much of London's commuter belt, the term has acquired social connotations of affluence, suburbia, and political conservatism; "home counties" accents and attitudes feature in British cultural shorthand, and stockbroker-belt towns such as Esher and Gerrards Cross typify the region's image.

Geography

The region includes the chalk hills of the North Downs and Chilterns, the Thames Valley, and the Kent and Sussex coasts within reach of the capital. Green Belt policy since the mid-twentieth century has limited continuous sprawl, preserving a pattern of market towns, villages, and commuter settlements served by dense rail networks into London terminals.
