Drum kit

A drum kit (also called a drum set, trap set, or simply drums) is a collection of drums, cymbals and sometimes other auxiliary percussion instruments set up to be played by one person and usually adapted to the musical style it is being used in. The individual instruments are usually struck with sticks, brushes or hands.

Standard configuration

A typical kit consists of a bass drum (played with a foot pedal), a snare drum on a stand, two or three tom-toms (rack toms and a floor tom), a hi-hat (two cymbals on a pedal-operated stand), a crash cymbal and a ride cymbal, each mounted on stands. The drummer sits on a stool called a throne and coordinates all four limbs: the right foot on the bass-drum pedal, the left foot on the hi-hat pedal, and both hands with sticks.

History

Before the development of the typical drum kit, drums and cymbals used in military and orchestral settings were played by separate percussionists. In the 1840s, drummers began experimenting with foot pedals; in 1909 the Ludwig company patented a workable bass-drum pedal. In the 1910s–20s, New Orleans jazz drummers assembled "trap sets" (from "contraption"), combining drums and effects; the hi-hat became standard in the 1930s, and shell sizes and configurations were standardised in the swing era. Rock drummers of the 1960s and 70s expanded kits with extra toms and cymbals, and double bass-drum pedals became common in heavy metal.

Electronic drums

Electronic drum kits, using rubber or mesh pads triggering sampled or synthesised sounds, became popular from the 1980s onward for practice, recording and live performance at controlled volumes.
