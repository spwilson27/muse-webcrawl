Lo-fi music

Lo-fi music
Stylistic origins: DIY culture, indie rock, garage rock, home recording
Cultural origins: 1950s–1980s, United States
Characteristics: low-fidelity recording, tape hiss, distortion, imperfections retained
Derivative forms: lo-fi hip hop, bedroom pop, chillwave, hypnagogic pop

Lo-fi (short for low fidelity) is a music or production aesthetic in which elements usually treated as flaws in a recording or performance — distorted audio, background noise, tape hiss, off-key or unpolished playing — are deliberately retained, sometimes as an intentional stylistic choice. The term has described home-recorded music since at least the 1950s, and came to denote a recognised genre and aesthetic from the 1980s and 1990s onward, associated with acts such as Daniel Johnston, Guided by Voices, Sebadoh, Beck and the Elephant 6 collective.

A cassette four-track recorder, emblematic of lo-fi home production

History

Cheap recording technology democratised music-making: the Beach Boys' home-recorded albums of the late 1960s, R. Stevie Moore's 1970s output, and the DIY cassette culture of punk and post-punk all anticipated lo-fi. In the 1990s, "lo-fi" became a marketing and critical label for indie rock recorded on inexpensive equipment — Pavement's Slanted and Enchanted (1992), Beck's "Loser" (1993) and Guided by Voices' Bee Thousand (1994) being landmarks — though many artists resisted the term as suggesting mere technical incompetence rather than an aesthetic.

Lo-fi hip hop

In the 2010s, "lo-fi" became most strongly associated with lo-fi hip hop: downtempo instrumental beats built from jazz and soul samples, vinyl crackle, tape saturation and swung drums, influenced by J Dilla and Nujabes. Popularised through YouTube livestream channels — most famously the 24/7 "lofi hip hop radio" stream (originally ChilledCow, later Lofi Girl) — the style became ubiquitous background music for studying and relaxation, spawning a vast internet subculture.

Characteristics

Beyond audio imperfections, lo-fi aesthetics often include nostalgic artwork, analogue media references, and an ethic of authenticity opposed to polished commercial production. In the digital era, the sound is usually simulated with plug-ins that add wow and flutter, hiss and bit-crushing to otherwise pristine recordings.
