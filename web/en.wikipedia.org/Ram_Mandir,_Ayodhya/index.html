Ram Mandir, Ayodhya

DeityRam Lalla (infant Rama)
LocationRam Janmabhoomi, Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh, India
StyleNagara architecture
Groundbreaking5 August 2020
Consecrated22 January 2024
Governing trustShri Ram Janmbhoomi Teerth Kshetra

The Ram Mandir is a Hindu temple complex in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh, India, built at Ram Janmabhoomi, the site Hindus believe to be the birthplace of the deity Rama. It was constructed following the Supreme Court of India's 2019 verdict in the Ayodhya dispute, which awarded the disputed land to a government-formed trust, the Shri Ram Janmbhoomi Teerth Kshetra.

Construction

Prime Minister Narendra Modi laid the foundation stone on 5 August 2020. The temple was designed by the Sompura family of temple architects, following the plans originally drawn up in 1988 by Chandrakant Sompura, in the Nagara style of northern Indian temple architecture. The main structure is built of pink Bansi Paharpur sandstone without structural steel, with carved pillars, five mandapas (halls), and a principal shikhara (spire) rising above the sanctum.

Consecration

The idol of Ram Lalla, depicting Rama as a five-year-old child, was consecrated in the sanctum sanctorum on 22 January 2024 in a pran pratishtha ceremony led by Prime Minister Modi, attended by thousands of invited guests and broadcast nationally. Construction of the remaining parts of the complex continued after the consecration.

Significance

The temple's completion was a defining political and cultural event in modern India, fulfilling a decades-long movement by Hindu organisations and the Bharatiya Janata Party. The surrounding city of Ayodhya underwent extensive redevelopment, including a new airport and railway station, to accommodate pilgrims and tourists.
