Grand Coulee Dam is a concrete gravity dam on the Columbia River in the U.S. state of Washington, built to produce hydroelectric power and provide irrigation water. Constructed between 1933 and 1942 as a New Deal project of the Bureau of Reclamation, it was the largest power station in the United States for decades and remains among the largest concrete structures in the world.

Power and irrigation

With three powerhouses and an installed capacity of about 6,809 megawatts, Grand Coulee is still the largest power-producing facility in the United States by capacity. It also anchors the Columbia Basin Project, pumping water into Grand Coulee's dry channel to form Banks Lake, which feeds irrigation canals watering roughly 2,700 km² of central Washington farmland.

Impacts

The dam blocked salmon migration to the upper Columbia, devastating fisheries and the livelihoods of Indigenous peoples including the Spokane Tribe and the Colville Tribes, whose lands were flooded by the reservoir, Franklin Delano Roosevelt Lake. The dam has no fish passage. Folk singer Woody Guthrie, commissioned by the Bonneville Power Administration in 1941, celebrated the project in songs such as "Grand Coulee Dam".
