Kareem Abdul-Jabbar (born Ferdinand Lewis Alcindor Jr.; April 16, 1947) is an American former professional basketball player who played 20 seasons in the National Basketball Association (NBA) for the Milwaukee Bucks and the Los Angeles Lakers. During his career as a center, Abdul-Jabbar was a record six-time NBA Most Valuable Player (MVP). He was a 19-time NBA All-Star, a 15-time All-NBA selection, and an 11-time NBA All-Defensive Team honoree. He was a member of six NBA championship teams as a player and two more as an assistant coach, and was twice voted NBA Finals MVP.

Abdul-Jabbar is regarded as one of the greatest players of all time. He was the NBA's all-time leading scorer with 38,387 points until LeBron James surpassed the record in 2023. His signature "skyhook" shot is considered one of the most unstoppable moves in basketball history.

College career

Playing under the name Lew Alcindor at UCLA under coach John Wooden, he won three consecutive national championships from 1967 to 1969 and was named the tournament's most outstanding player each year. His dominance led the NCAA to ban the slam dunk in 1967, a rule that remained in place until 1976.

Professional career

Selected first overall by the Milwaukee Bucks in the 1969 NBA draft, Abdul-Jabbar won an NBA championship with the team in 1971. He converted to Islam and changed his name in 1971. In 1975 he was traded to the Los Angeles Lakers, where he won five more championships alongside Magic Johnson during the "Showtime" era of the 1980s. He retired in 1989 at age 42.

Legacy

Abdul-Jabbar is also an actor, basketball coach, and best-selling author. In 2016, President Barack Obama awarded him the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
