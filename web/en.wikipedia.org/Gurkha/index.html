Gurkhas are soldiers native to Nepal recruited into the British Army, the Indian Army and other forces, renowned worldwide for their courage, loyalty and endurance. The name derives from the hill town of Gorkha, from which King Prithvi Narayan Shah unified Nepal in the 18th century.

History

British officers first encountered Gorkha troops during the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–16) and, impressed by their fighting qualities, began recruiting them under the Treaty of Sugauli. Gurkha regiments served in both World Wars — some 200,000 served in the Second World War, in Burma, North Africa and Italy — earning 13 Victoria Crosses across their history. At the Partition of India in 1947, regiments were divided between Britain and India. Gurkhas later fought in Malaya, Borneo, the Falklands War (1982), Iraq and Afghanistan.

Traditions

Gurkha selection in Nepal is famously gruelling, including the doko race carrying 25 kg uphill. Their signature weapon is the curved khukuri knife. The Brigade of Gurkhas remains part of the British Army, and campaigners led by Joanna Lumley won Gurkha veterans the right to settle in the United Kingdom in 2009.
