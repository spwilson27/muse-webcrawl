Local field

In mathematics, a local field is a special type of field that is a locally compact topological field with respect to a non-discrete topology. Local fields are important in algebraic number theory, where they arise as completions of global fields (such as the rational numbers or function fields) at a given place, or prime. The prototype examples are the real numbers ℝ and the complex numbers ℂ (the archimedean local fields), and the fields of p-adic numbers ℚp for a prime number p, together with their finite extensions (the non-archimedean local fields).

Definition and classification

A local field is a field K equipped with a topology making it a locally compact topological field, with a non-discrete topology. Equivalently, K carries an absolute value for which it is complete and whose residue field is finite (in the non-archimedean case) or which is isomorphic to ℝ or ℂ.

By a classical classification theorem, every local field is isomorphic to one of the following:

  • the real numbers ℝ;
  • the complex numbers ℂ;
  • a finite extension of the p-adic numbers ℚp for some prime p (characteristic zero, non-archimedean);
  • the field of formal Laurent series 𝔽q((t)) over a finite field 𝔽q (positive characteristic p, non-archimedean).

Non-archimedean local fields

A non-archimedean local field carries a discrete valuation v with value group ℤ. The set of elements of non-negative valuation forms the ring of integers 𝔬, a discrete valuation ring whose unique maximal ideal 𝔭 is generated by any element of valuation 1, called a uniformizer π. The quotient 𝔬/𝔭 is a finite field, the residue field, with q = pf elements; the integer f is called the residue degree and the degree of an extension decomposes as [L:K] = e·f, where e is the ramification index. An extension is unramified when e = 1 and totally ramified when f = 1.

The multiplicative group decomposes as K× ≅ ℤ × 𝔬×, and the unit group admits a filtration by the higher unit groups U(n) = 1 + 𝔭n, whose structure is studied through the logarithm and exponential maps.

Applications

Local fields are the natural setting for local class field theory, which describes the abelian extensions of K through the reciprocity map from K× to the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension. The Hasse principle connects properties over a global field with properties over all of its completions: for instance, a quadratic form over ℚ represents zero nontrivially if and only if it does so over ℝ and over every ℚp. Local fields also underpin the study of representations of p-adic groups in the Langlands program and harmonic analysis via the additive Haar measure and Fourier transform.
