Distributive property

The distributive property is a property of binary operations that generalizes the distributive law from elementary algebra: a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c. An operation ∗ distributes over another operation + if, for all elements x, y, z, x ∗ (y + z) = (x ∗ y) + (x ∗ z) (left distributivity), with an analogous condition for right distributivity.

Examples

Multiplication of numbers distributes over addition and subtraction; division distributes over addition only from the right. In set theory, union distributes over intersection and vice versa; in propositional logic, conjunction distributes over disjunction and vice versa. Matrix multiplication distributes over matrix addition, and the dot and cross products have their own distributivity relations. Operations that do not distribute include addition over multiplication (in ordinary arithmetic) and exponentiation over addition.

In abstract algebra

Distributivity is one of the defining axioms of rings and fields, where multiplication distributes over addition, and of distributive lattices. It underlies common algebraic manipulations such as expanding brackets and factoring — the FOIL method for binomials is an application of the distributive property.
