Lake Constance (German: Bodensee) is a lake on the River Rhine at the northern foot of the Alps, bordered by Germany to the north, Switzerland to the south and Austria to the east. At 63 km long and 536 km² in area, it is the third-largest lake in Central Europe by surface area and an important source of drinking water for about five million people in Baden-Württemberg and Switzerland.

Geography

The lake consists of two main connected basins: the deep Upper Lake (Obersee), which includes the Überlingen arm, and the shallower Lower Lake (Untersee), joined by the Seerhein at the city of Konstanz. The Rhine enters the Upper Lake near Bregenz in Austria and leaves the Lower Lake at Stein am Rhein, continuing as the High Rhine towards Basel. Notable islands include Reichenau, a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its early medieval monastery; the flower island of Mainau; and the old town of Lindau, built on an island in the Bavarian part of the lake.

History and borders

Unusually, the international borders in the main lake were never formally delimited: the surrounding states hold differing legal views, and the lake is effectively administered as a condominium in its deep-water parts while fisheries and navigation are regulated by treaty commissions. The region was a centre of Alemannic settlement, and the medieval Council of Constance (1414–1418), which ended the Western Schism, met on its shore. Ferry and shipping links have connected the three countries since the nineteenth century; the Count Zeppelin airships were developed at Friedrichshafen on the north shore.

Climate and nature

The lake moderates the local climate, allowing wine growing on its shores — the Müller-Thurgau and Spätburgunder wines of the Bodensee region — and extensive fruit orchards. Its shores and the Rhine delta in Austria form major bird sanctuaries. In the severe winter of 1962–63 the lake froze over completely (a Seegfrörne), an event that has occurred only rarely in recorded history.
