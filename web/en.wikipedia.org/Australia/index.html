Australia

Capital: Canberra

Largest city: Sydney

Official language: English (de facto)

Government: Federal parliamentary constitutional monarchy

Population: ~27 million

Area: 7,692,024 km²

Currency: Australian dollar (AUD)

Australia, officially the Commonwealth of Australia, is a country comprising the mainland of the Australian continent, the island of Tasmania and numerous smaller islands. It is the world's sixth-largest country and the largest in Oceania. Indigenous Australians have inhabited the continent for at least 65,000 years; European colonisation began in 1788 with a British penal settlement at Sydney Cove, and the six colonies federated as a nation on 1 January 1901.

Geography

Australia is the driest inhabited continent, dominated by the Outback's deserts and semi-arid rangelands, with fertile plains in the southeast and tropical rainforests in the northeast. The Great Barrier Reef, the world's largest coral reef system, lies off Queensland. Unique fauna include marsupials such as kangaroos and koalas, monotremes like the platypus, and abundant endemic bird and plant species.

Government and society

Australia is a federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy, with Charles III as king, represented by a governor-general. The prime minister leads the government in Canberra. A highly urbanised, multicultural population of about 27 million — with nearly 30 percent born overseas — is concentrated in the coastal capitals Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide.

Economy

The economy is one of the world's largest and most developed, led by services, mining (iron ore, coal, gold, LNG), agriculture and education exports. Australia recorded nearly three decades of uninterrupted growth before 2020 and maintains close trade ties with China, Japan, the United States and other Asian partners.
