Hysteresis

Hysteresis is the dependence of the state of a system on its history. For example, a magnet may have more than one possible magnetic moment in a given magnetic field, depending on how the field has changed in the past. Plots of a single component of the moment often form a loop or hysteresis curve, where there are different values of one variable depending on the direction of change of another variable. This history dependence is the basis of memory in a hard disk and the remanence that retains a record of the Earth's magnetic field in the past.

Types

Magnetic hysteresis occurs in ferromagnetic materials, where magnetization lags the applied field, producing a loop characterized by coercivity and retentivity; the energy lost per cycle is proportional to the loop area. Elastic hysteresis appears in rubber-like materials, whose stress–strain curves differ on loading and unloading. Hysteresis also appears in economics (e.g. unemployment persistence), biology (e.g. in enzyme and neural systems), and in control engineering, where the Schmitt trigger exploits hysteresis to provide noise immunity in electronic circuits.

Models

Mathematical descriptions include the Preisach model, which represents hysteresis as a superposition of elementary relay operators, and the Jiles–Atherton model of magnetic hysteresis. The term derives from the Greek ὑστέρησις, "deficiency" or "lagging behind", and was introduced by Sir James Alfred Ewing in 1881.
