The White Deer Grotto Academy (Chinese: 白鹿洞書院; pinyin: Báilùdòng Shūyuàn) is a famous historical Confucian academy at the foot of Mount Lu in Jiangxi, China. It is counted among the Four Great Academies of ancient China and takes its name from the recluse Li Bo, who kept a white deer there in the 10th century — "grotto" referring to his cave-like retreat.

History

Li Bo studied and taught at the site around 940, during the Five Dynasties period, and an academy grew up around it under the early Song dynasty before falling into ruin. The great Neo-Confucian philosopher Zhu Xi rebuilt the academy in 1179–1181 while serving as prefect of Nankang, personally lecturing there and drafting its famous Articles of Learning (the "Revelations of the White Deer Grotto Academy"), which became a model charter for academies across East Asia. A celebrated debate between Zhu Xi and his rival Lu Jiuyuan was held at the academy in 1181.

Influence

For centuries the academy was one of China's leading centres of Neo-Confucian scholarship, with hundreds of students at its height. Its regulations influenced academies in China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam. The site, repeatedly rebuilt, survives today as a protected cultural monument within Lushan National Park, with pavilions, lecture halls, a library and shrines to Zhu Xi and earlier teachers.
