California

California is a state in the Western United States, lying on the Pacific Coast. With nearly 39 million residents across an area of 163,696 square miles (423,970 km2), it is the most populous US state, the third-largest by area, and the most populated subnational entity in North America. Its capital is Sacramento; its largest city is Los Angeles.

History

Indigenous peoples inhabited California for millennia before Spanish colonisation began in 1769 with missions and presidios. After Mexican independence it became Alta California, was ceded to the United States in 1848, and the Gold Rush beginning that year brought hundreds of thousands of settlers. California entered the Union as the 31st state in 1850.

Geography

The state spans deserts (Mojave, Death Valley — the lowest and hottest point in North America), the fertile Central Valley, the Sierra Nevada with Mount Whitney, the highest summit in the contiguous US, redwood forests, and 840 miles of coastline. The San Andreas Fault gives it frequent earthquakes.

Economy

California's economy, exceeding $4 trillion gross state product, would rank among the world's five largest if it were a country. It leads in technology (Silicon Valley), entertainment (Hollywood), agriculture, aerospace, and tourism.

Demographics and government

No racial or ethnic group forms a majority. The state government operates under its 1879 constitution with a governor, bicameral legislature, and a prominent ballot-initiative process.
