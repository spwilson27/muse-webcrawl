The Joint European Torus (JET) was a magnetically confined plasma physics experiment — the world's largest operating tokamak for most of its life — located at Culham Centre for Fusion Energy in Oxfordshire, UK. Built by a European collaboration and operated under EUROfusion, JET was designed to study the feasibility of nuclear fusion using deuterium–tritium fuel, the reaction planned for power stations.

History and achievements

Construction began in 1978 and first plasma was achieved in 1983. In 1991 JET performed the world's first controlled release of fusion power using a deuterium–tritium mixture, and in 1997 it set a world record of 16 megawatts of fusion power with a gain (Q) of about 0.67. In its final deuterium–tritium campaigns, JET produced 59 megajoules of fusion energy over five seconds in 2021 and surpassed this with 69 megajoules in 2023.

Role and decommissioning

JET's beryllium and tungsten wall and its tritium handling capabilities made it the principal testbed for ITER, the international tokamak under construction in France. Scientific operations ended in December 2023, after forty years of experiments, and the machine entered decommissioning, which is being used to develop remote-handling techniques for future fusion plants.
