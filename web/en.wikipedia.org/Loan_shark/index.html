Loan shark

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Loan shark
MeaningUnlicensed moneylender charging extremely high interest
Typical ratesOften far above legal caps; equivalent APRs can reach hundreds or thousands of percent
Enforcement methodsIntimidation, threats, violence, property damage
Legal statusIllegal in most jurisdictions
Related termsUsury; payday loan; loansharking

A loan shark is a person or business that lends money at extremely high rates of interest, typically outside the law and often with threats of violence or other illegal means used to collect debts. The term is applied to unlicensed lenders who target borrowers unable to obtain credit from banks—because of poverty, bad credit, or urgency—and who enforce repayment through intimidation rather than courts.

Practices

Loan sharks generally operate cash businesses without paperwork or receipts, lending small sums quickly with interest calculated weekly or even daily. Borrowers who fall behind may find the debt spiralling as interest compounds or is rolled into new loans. Collection tactics can include repeated visits, damage to property, public humiliation, threats against family members, and actual violence. In some countries informal lenders have long-standing local roles—for example the "5-6" lenders in the Philippines (lend five, repay six) or illegal moneylenders in Japan (yami kinyū) and Hong Kong—while in the United States, loansharking was historically a major revenue source for organized crime families, a connection dramatized in popular culture from The Sopranos to countless films.

Law and policy

Because interest rates above statutory ceilings (usury laws) and unlicensed lending are offences in most jurisdictions, loan sharking is policed by specialist units such as England's Illegal Money Lending Team. Enforcement is hampered by victims' fear of reporting. Policy responses include promoting credit unions and community development finance, regulating legal high-cost credit such as payday loans, and financial-inclusion programs intended to give vulnerable borrowers safer alternatives.

Distinction from legal high-cost credit

Licensed payday lenders and other high-cost short-term credit providers also charge very high effective rates, but they are regulated, document their loans, and must use lawful collection methods. Critics argue some legal products replicate the debt-trap economics of loansharking; regulators in the UK and elsewhere have responded with price caps and affordability checks. The defining features of the loan shark proper remain illegality and coercive enforcement.
