Western Front (World War II)

Western Front
Dates: 1939–1945
Location: Western and northern Europe
Theatre: Second World War
Result: Allied victory; occupation of Germany

The Western Front in the Second World War was the theatre of war encompassing Denmark, Norway, Luxembourg, Belgium, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, France, Italy (in the broader Western campaign context) and Germany itself. The term usually refers to the fighting in France and Germany between the Western Allies and Nazi Germany.

1939–1941

After Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, the front in the west settled into the "Phoney War" until May 1940, when Germany launched the Battle of France. Using blitzkrieg tactics through the Ardennes, German forces defeated the French, British, Belgian and Dutch armies in six weeks; the British Expeditionary Force was evacuated from Dunkirk, and France signed an armistice in June 1940. The Battle of Britain that summer prevented a German invasion of the United Kingdom.

1944–1945

The Western Allies returned to mainland France with the Normandy landings (Operation Overlord) on 6 June 1944, the largest seaborne invasion in history. After breaking out of Normandy and liberating Paris, Allied armies advanced to the German frontier. Germany's last offensive, the Battle of the Bulge in December 1944, was defeated, and in early 1945 the Allies crossed the Rhine, encircled the Ruhr industrial region and advanced into the heart of Germany. Western and Soviet forces met at the Elbe on 25 April 1945, and Germany surrendered unconditionally on 8 May 1945.
