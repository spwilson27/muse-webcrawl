Joseph ben Ephraim Karo

Born: 1488, Toledo, Spain
Died: March 24, 1575, Safed, Ottoman Palestine
Occupation: Rabbi, legal codifier, Kabbalist
Known for: Author of the Shulchan Aruch and Beit Yosef

Joseph ben Ephraim Karo (1488 – March 24, 1575), also spelled Caro or Qaro, was a Sephardic rabbi and the author of the last great codification of Jewish law, the Shulchan Aruch, which remains the standard code of Orthodox Jewish practice. He is often referred to simply as "the Mechaber" (the Author).

Life

Karo was born in Toledo, Spain, in 1488. Following the expulsion of the Jews from Spain in 1492, his family fled to Portugal and then to the Ottoman Empire, settling in Nikopol (in modern Bulgaria) and later Adrianople, where he began his great legal work. He eventually settled in Safed in the Galilee, then a flourishing centre of Jewish learning and Kabbalah, where he headed a yeshiva and served as a leading rabbi alongside figures such as Moses Alshech and Solomon Alkabetz. His students included Moses ben Jacob Cordovero and Hayyim Vital.

Works

Karo laboured for more than thirty years on the Beit Yosef ("House of Joseph"), a comprehensive commentary on Jacob ben Asher's Arba'ah Turim that surveyed the full range of earlier authorities before rendering decisions. From this vast commentary he distilled the Shulchan Aruch ("Set Table"), a concise code organized in four sections covering daily life, dietary and ritual law, marriage, and civil law. First printed in Venice in 1565, it was adopted across the Jewish world; the glosses of the Polish rabbi Moses Isserles adapted it for Ashkenazi practice.

Kabbalah

Karo was also a mystic. For some fifty years he kept a diary, the Maggid Mesharim, recording the teachings of a heavenly mentor (a maggid) who spoke to him in nightly visitations, a phenomenon studied by scholars as a landmark in the history of Jewish mysticism. He died in Safed in 1575 and was buried in its old cemetery.
