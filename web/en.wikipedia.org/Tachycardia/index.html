Tachycardia

SpecialtyCardiology
DefinitionResting heart rate > 100 beats per minute
TypesSinus, supraventricular, ventricular

Tachycardia, also called tachyarrhythmia, is a heart rate that exceeds the normal resting rate. In general, a resting heart rate over 100 beats per minute is accepted as tachycardia in adults. Heart rates above the resting rate may be normal (such as with exercise) or abnormal (such as with electrical problems within the heart). The opposite of tachycardia is bradycardia.

An ECG showing sinus tachycardia at about 150 beats per minute

Classification

Tachycardias are commonly classified by the location of their origin. Sinus tachycardia arises from the sinoatrial node and is usually a physiological response to fever, pain, blood loss, anxiety, or exercise. Supraventricular tachycardias (SVT) originate above the ventricles and include atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, and AV nodal reentrant tachycardia. Ventricular tachycardia originates in the ventricles and can be life-threatening, potentially degenerating into ventricular fibrillation.

Causes

Causes of tachycardia include fever, anemia, hyperthyroidism, hypovolemia, pulmonary embolism, sepsis, heart failure, myocardial infarction, stimulants such as caffeine and cocaine, and adverse effects of medications. Sustained tachycardia can itself weaken the heart muscle, a condition known as tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis rests on the electrocardiogram (ECG), which distinguishes narrow-complex from wide-complex tachycardias and helps identify the mechanism. Management depends on the type and on whether the patient is stable: unstable patients require urgent synchronized cardioversion, while stable patients may be treated with vagal maneuvers, adenosine, beta blockers, calcium channel blockers, or catheter ablation for recurrent arrhythmias.
