American Zoetrope (also known as Omni Zoetrope and Zoetrope Studios) is a privately run American film production company, centered in San Francisco, California, founded in 1969 by filmmakers Francis Ford Coppola and George Lucas. Intended as an independent alternative to the Hollywood studio system, it became a hub of the New Hollywood movement.

History

The studio's early productions included Lucas's THX 1138 (1971) and Coppola's The Godfather (1972) and The Conversation (1974). American Zoetrope produced the films of the Coppola family, including Sofia Coppola's work, and has been involved in productions such as Apocalypse Now, The Outsiders, Bram Stoker's Dracula, and Lost in Translation.

The company is headquartered in the historic Sentinel Building (Columbus Tower) in San Francisco's North Beach neighborhood, which Coppola purchased in the 1970s.
