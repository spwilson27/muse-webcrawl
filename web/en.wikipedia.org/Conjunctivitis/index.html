Conjunctivitis

Other namePink eye
SymptomsRedness, irritation, discharge, watering
CausesViruses, bacteria, allergies, irritants

Conjunctivitis, commonly called pink eye, is inflammation of the conjunctiva, the thin transparent tissue covering the white of the eye and lining the inside of the eyelids. It is one of the most common eye conditions worldwide and usually resolves without lasting harm to vision.

Causes

The most frequent cause is viral infection, often adenoviruses associated with the common cold. Bacterial conjunctivitis, commonly from Staphylococcus or Streptococcus species, produces thicker, purulent discharge. Allergic conjunctivitis accompanies hay fever and is marked by intense itching, while chemical splashes, foreign bodies and contact lens wear can also inflame the conjunctiva. Newborns may develop ophthalmia neonatorum from infections acquired during birth.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features include redness of the white of the eye, a gritty or burning sensation, watering or discharge that may stick the eyelids together on waking, and mild sensitivity to light. Vision is usually normal; pain, marked light sensitivity or reduced vision suggest a more serious condition requiring prompt assessment.

Treatment

Most viral cases resolve on their own within one to two weeks and are managed with hygiene and lubricating drops. Topical antibiotics are used for bacterial cases, and antihistamine drops treat allergic disease. Because infectious conjunctivitis spreads readily, hand washing and avoiding shared towels reduce transmission.
