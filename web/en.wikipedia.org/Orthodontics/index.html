Orthodontics

Dental braces — brackets bonded to the teeth connected by an archwire — are the classic orthodontic appliance.

Orthodontics is a dentistry specialty that addresses the diagnosis, prevention, management, and correction of mal-positioned teeth and jaws, and misaligned bite patterns. It may also address the modification of facial growth, known as dentofacial orthopedics. The name derives from Greek orthos ("correct, straight") and odous ("tooth").

Treatment

Orthodontic treatment uses fixed or removable appliances to apply gentle, sustained pressure that gradually moves teeth through the alveolar bone. Fixed braces consist of brackets bonded to the teeth joined by an archwire, often supplemented by elastic bands. Clear aligner systems, such as removable transparent trays changed every one to two weeks, are an increasingly common alternative, especially for adults. Headgear, palatal expanders, and temporary anchorage devices may be used to influence jaw growth or provide anchorage.

Indications

Treatment addresses malocclusions including crowding, spacing, overbite, underbite (prognathism), crossbite, and open bite. Therapy commonly begins in adolescence, though interceptive treatment in younger children and adult orthodontics are both routine. After active treatment, retainers are worn to hold the teeth in position while surrounding tissues stabilise.

Training

Orthodontists are dentists who complete an additional two to three years of accredited specialty training after dental school. Edward Angle, who founded the first school of orthodontics in 1900, is regarded as the founder of the modern specialty.
