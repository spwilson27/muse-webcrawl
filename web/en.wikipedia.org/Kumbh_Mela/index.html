Kumbh Mela

Kumbh Mela
TypeHindu pilgrimage and festival
CycleEvery 12 years at each site; Ardh (half) Kumbh every 6 years; Maha Kumbh after 12 cycles
SitesPrayagraj, Haridwar, Nashik, Ujjain
Central riteRitual bathing (snan) at the river or sangam
RecognitionUNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2017)

The Kumbh Mela is a mass Hindu pilgrimage in which devotees gather to bathe in a sacred river, considered the largest peaceful gathering in the world. It rotates among four sites — Prayagraj (at the confluence of the Ganges, Yamuna and mythical Saraswati), Haridwar, Nashik and Ujjain — on a twelve-year cycle governed by the positions of Jupiter, the Sun and the Moon.

Mythology and history

The festival's mythology derives from the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean of milk: as gods and demons fought over the kumbh (pot) of the nectar of immortality, drops fell at the four festival sites. Pilgrim gatherings at Prayag were described by the Chinese monk Xuanzang in the 7th century CE, and the festival took shape over the following centuries with the patronage of kings and the organization of monastic orders (akharas).

The festival

On auspicious bathing days, processions of naga sadhus and akharas lead millions to the water for the shahi snan (royal bath). A temporary megacity of tents, pontoon bridges, hospitals and sanitation is erected for the event. The 2013 Prayag Kumbh drew an estimated 100–120 million pilgrims over 55 days, and the 2025 Maha Kumbh at Prayagraj was reported by authorities to have received several hundred million visits over its 45 days.

Significance

Bathing at the Kumbh is believed to cleanse sins and aid liberation (moksha). UNESCO inscribed the Kumbh Mela on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017.
