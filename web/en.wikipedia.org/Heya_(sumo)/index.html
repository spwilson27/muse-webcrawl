Heya (sumo)

Morning training (asageiko) at a heya

A heya (Japanese: 部屋, "room"; most often translated as "stable") in sumo wrestling is an organisation of wrestlers where they train and live together in a communal lifestyle. A stable is headed by an oyakata (stablemaster), a retired wrestler who is a member — an "elder" — of the Japan Sumo Association and who owns one of the 105 elder shares (toshiyori kabu).

Organisation

All professional wrestlers (rikishi) must belong to a heya. As of recent years there have been just over forty active stables, grouped into five clans (ichimon): Dewanoumi, Nishonoseki, Tokitsukaze, Takasago and Isegahama. Wrestlers from the same stable never compete against each other in official tournaments except in playoffs for a championship. New recruits typically join in their mid-teens and rise through the divisions; only the top-ranking sekitori receive salaries and private rooms, while lower-ranked wrestlers share dormitories and perform the stable's cooking, cleaning and other duties.

Daily life

Life in a heya is strictly hierarchical. The day begins before dawn with training (asageiko) on an empty stomach, followed by the day's main meal — often chankonabe, the protein-rich hot pot associated with sumo — and an afternoon nap that, together with the enormous calorie intake, helps wrestlers gain weight. The stablemaster's wife, the okamisan, traditionally manages much of the household and supports the wrestlers. After retirement, a stablemaster must hand over the stable by the age of 65.
