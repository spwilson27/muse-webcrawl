Robert Wiene

Robert Wiene
Born: 27 April 1873, Breslau, Germany
Died: 17 July 1938, Paris, France
Known for: The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari

Robert Wiene (1873–1938) was a German film director of the silent era, best known for directing the landmark German Expressionist film The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (1920).

Career

Wiene began as a stage actor and director before moving into film during the First World War. Caligari, with its distorted painted sets and story of a murderous hypnotist, became the defining work of Expressionist cinema and a lasting influence on horror and film noir. His other films include Genuine (1920), Raskolnikow (1923), an adaptation of Crime and Punishment, and The Hands of Orlac (1924).

Later years

After the Nazi rise to power, Wiene, who was of Jewish descent, emigrated, eventually settling in Paris, where he died in 1938 while working on the film Ultimatum.
