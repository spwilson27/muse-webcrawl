Alpha Librae

Constellation: Libra
Components: α1 Librae, α2 Librae (Zubenelgenubi)
Apparent magnitude: 2.75 (α2)
Distance: ≈ 77 light-years

Alpha Librae (α Librae) is a multiple star system in the constellation of Libra. Despite its Bayer designation "alpha", it is only the second-brightest object in the constellation after Beta Librae. Its brighter component, α2 Librae, bears the traditional name Zubenelgenubi, from the Arabic for "the southern claw", reflecting a time when the stars of Libra were considered part of Scorpius's claws.

Components

The system consists of two widely separated stars visible in binoculars. α2 Librae is itself a spectroscopic binary of A-type stars, while α1 Librae is an F-type binary; a fifth component, KU Librae, may be physically associated. α2 Librae lies close to the ecliptic and can be occulted by the Moon and, rarely, by planets.

Observation

Alpha Librae lies about 77 light-years from the Sun. Its position near the ecliptic made it useful in antiquity for tracking the Moon and planets, and the "claws" names of both Alpha and Beta Librae preserve Babylonian and Greek astronomical traditions.
