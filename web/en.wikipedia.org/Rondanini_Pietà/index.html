Rondanini Pietà

Rondanini Pietà
Artist: Michelangelo
Year: 1552–1564
Medium: Marble
Location: Castello Sforzesco, Milan

The Rondanini Pietà is a marble sculpture by the Italian Renaissance artist Michelangelo Buonarroti, worked on from about 1552 until the artist's death in 1564. It was his final sculpture, left unfinished, and depicts the Virgin Mary mourning over the body of the dead Christ.

Description

The group is tall and attenuated: Mary stands upright, supporting the elongated, weightless body of Christ, whose limbs appear almost detached from the torso. The figures merge into a single vertical form, giving the work an ethereal, spiritual quality quite different from the balanced naturalism of Michelangelo's earlier Pietà in St Peter's Basilica (1498–1499). Traces of an earlier composition remain visible, including a detached arm of Christ, since Michelangelo radically re-carved the block in his last years.

History

The sculpture takes its name from the Rondanini (Rondanini/Rondanino) family, in whose Roman palace courtyard it stood for centuries. After passing through several owners it was acquired by the city of Milan in 1952 and placed in the Castello Sforzesco, where it is displayed in its own museum space.
