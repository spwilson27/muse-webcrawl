S.L. Benfica

Sport Lisboa e Benfica (European Portuguese: [sɨˈpɔɾ liʒˈβoɐ i βɐ̃jˈfikɐ]), commonly known as Benfica, is a Portuguese professional football club based in Lisbon. Founded on 28 February 1904 as Sport Lisboa, it merged with Grupo Sport Benfica in 1908. Benfica is one of the "Big Three" clubs of Portugal, alongside Sporting CP and FC Porto, and has never been relegated from the Primeira Liga.

The Estádio da Luz ("Stadium of Light") in Lisbon.

History

Benfica's golden era came in the 1960s under Hungarian coach Béla Guttmann, when the club won the European Cup in 1961 and 1962 — the latter side led by the legendary Eusébio — and reached three further European Cup finals in the decade. After the 1962 victory Guttmann famously declared that Benfica would not be European champions again "in one hundred years"; the club has since lost all eight European finals it has contested, a run supporters call "the curse of Béla Guttmann."

Stadium and support

The club plays at the Estádio da Luz, a 65,000-seat stadium opened in 2003 on the site of the original 1954 ground. Benfica's supporters, known as benfiquistas, make it one of the best-supported clubs in the world; its members (sócios) have numbered among the highest of any football club. The club's motto is E pluribus unum ("Out of many, one"), its colours are red and white, and its symbol is the eagle Vitória, which circles the stadium before home matches.

Honours

Benfica is the most successful club in Portuguese football, with a record 38 Primeira Liga titles, a record 26 Taça de Portugal trophies, and two European Cups. It completed the domestic double numerous times and won the league unbeaten in 1972–73 and 1977–78.
