576p

576p is the shorthand name for a video display resolution with 576 vertical lines and progressive scanning. It is the progressive-scan counterpart of the 576i standard used by PAL and SECAM television systems, and has a standard resolution of 720 × 576 pixels (or 768 × 576 with square pixels), at frame rates typically of 25 or 50 frames per second.

Usage

576p was classified as an enhanced-definition television (EDTV) format. It was the maximum resolution of DVDs in PAL regions when played with a progressive-scan player, and was used by some broadcasters — notably in Australia, where 576p was adopted as an early "high definition" format by the Seven Network — before true HD formats (720p and 1080i) became standard. The format also appears in standard-definition digital television production and in the 625-line digital video ecosystem.

Comparison

Compared with 576i, the progressive version avoids interlace artefacts such as combing on moving objects and flicker on fine horizontal detail, at the cost of double the bandwidth at the same frame rate. Its NTSC-region analogue is 480p.
