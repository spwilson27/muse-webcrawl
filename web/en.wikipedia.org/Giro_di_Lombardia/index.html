Giro di Lombardia

The Giro di Lombardia (Tour of Lombardy), officially Il Lombardia, is a one-day professional cycling race held annually in Lombardy, Italy, traditionally in autumn. First held in 1905, it is one of the five "Monuments" of the professional road cycling calendar and is often called the "Race of the Falling Leaves" (la classica delle foglie morte) because of its late-season date. It is the last Monument of the year and part of the UCI World Tour.

Course

The route has varied over the years, with finishes in Milan, Como, and Bergamo. Its signature climbs include the Madonna del Ghisallo — whose chapel is a shrine to cycling — the steep Muro di Sormano, and the Colma di Sormano. The hilly terrain around Lake Como favours strong climbers and all-rounders rather than sprinters.

History

The race was first organised in 1905 by the newspaper La Gazzetta dello Sport. Fausto Coppi holds the record with five victories (1946–1949 and 1954). Other multiple winners include Alfredo Binda, Costante Girardengo, Gino Bartali, Eddy Merckx, and Damiano Cunego. In recent years Tadej Pogačar has won multiple consecutive editions. Together with Milan–San Remo, the Tour of Flanders, Paris–Roubaix, and Liège–Bastogne–Liège, Il Lombardia completes the set of cycling's Monuments.
