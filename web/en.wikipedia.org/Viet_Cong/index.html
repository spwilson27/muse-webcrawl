The Viet Cong, officially the National Liberation Front of South Vietnam (NLF), was an armed communist organisation in South Vietnam and Cambodia. Its military arm, the People's Liberation Armed Forces, fought against the South Vietnamese and United States governments during the Vietnam War.

Origins

The term “Viet Cong” — short for Việt Cộng sản, “Vietnamese communist” — was used by the South Vietnamese government of Ngo Dinh Diem for its opponents. The NLF was formally founded on 20 December 1960, uniting former Viet Minh cadres who had remained in the south after the 1954 Geneva Accords with other opposition groups. It was directed and supplied by North Vietnam under Ho Chi Minh and commanders such as Vo Nguyen Giap, with supplies moving down the Ho Chi Minh Trail.

War

The Viet Cong waged guerrilla warfare — ambushes, booby traps, assassinations and village-level political organisation — alongside main-force units. Its Cu Chi tunnel networks became famous. In the 1968 Tet Offensive the Viet Cong suffered very heavy losses and thereafter the war was increasingly carried by regular North Vietnamese forces, though the NLF remained the political face of the insurgency and formed a Provisional Revolutionary Government in 1969.

End

After the fall of Saigon in 1975 and reunification, the NLF was dissolved in 1976–1977 and merged into the Vietnamese Fatherland Front.
