Persistent identifier

A persistent identifier (PI or PID) is a long-lasting reference to a document, file, web page, person, or other object. The term is usually used of digital identifiers that are managed so that they remain resolvable even when the location or ownership of the identified object changes, in contrast to ordinary URLs, which frequently break ("link rot").

Characteristics

A persistent identifier system combines a unique, opaque identifier string with a resolution service and a commitment by a registration authority to maintain the mapping between the identifier and the object's current location and metadata. Persistence is therefore as much an organisational promise — succession planning, funding, governance — as a technical property.

Examples

Widely used PID schemes include the Digital Object Identifier (DOI) for publications and datasets; the Handle System on which DOIs are based; Archival Resource Keys (ARKs); Persistent URLs (PURLs); ORCID iDs for researchers; International Standard Book Numbers (ISBNs) and other long-established offline identifiers; and Wikidata identifiers for entities. Uniform Resource Names (URNs) were an early IETF attempt at location-independent naming.

Uses

Persistent identifiers underpin scholarly citation, research-data management under the FAIR principles, cultural-heritage cataloguing, and author disambiguation, because citations to a PID remain actionable even when websites are reorganised.
