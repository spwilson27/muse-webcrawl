The Premier League Golden Boot is an annual association football award presented to the player who scores the most goals in the Premier League during a season. It has been awarded since the league's inaugural 1992–93 season.

History

Teddy Sheringham won the first award with 22 goals in 1992–93. The goals record for a 42-game season is 34, shared by Andy Cole (1993–94) and Alan Shearer (1994–95). After the league settled at 38 games per team, Mohamed Salah set a new mark of 32 in 2017–18, before Erling Haaland scored 36 in 2022–23.

Multiple winners

Thierry Henry won the award a record four times with Arsenal. Alan Shearer and Mohamed Salah have won it three times each; Harry Kane also has three. When players finish level on goals, the award is shared, as occurred in 2018–19 (Salah, Sadio Mané and Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang, 22 each) and 2021–22 (Salah and Son Heung-min, 23 each).

Notable facts

Didier Drogba, Robin van Persie, Jimmy Floyd Hasselbaink and Michael Owen are among the two-time winners. Nicolas Anelka (2008–09) was for a time the lowest-scoring outright winner with 19 goals in the 38-game era, before later shared awards.
