Deputy lieutenant

A deputy lieutenant is a Crown appointment and one of several deputies to the lord-lieutenant of a lieutenancy area (a county or equivalent) in the United Kingdom. Deputy lieutenants support the work of the lord-lieutenant, the sovereign's personal representative in the county, and may deputise for them at ceremonial and civic functions.

Deputy lieutenants are appointed by the lord-lieutenant, subject to the sovereign not disapproving of the appointment. Their commissions are usually gazetted in The London Gazette or The Edinburgh Gazette. They retire at age 75 and are entitled to the post-nominal letters DL.

Role

Duties include representing the lord-lieutenant at local events, presenting honours and awards on behalf of the Crown, supporting citizenship ceremonies, liaising with local units of the armed forces and cadet organisations, and promoting voluntary service within the county. Unlike the lord-lieutenant, deputy lieutenants do not hold office as magistrates by virtue of the appointment, though many are appointed justices of the peace separately.

Vice lord-lieutenant

One deputy lieutenant is commonly appointed as vice lord-lieutenant, who stands in for the lord-lieutenant in their absence or illness and carries the post-nominals without a separate designation in most usage.
