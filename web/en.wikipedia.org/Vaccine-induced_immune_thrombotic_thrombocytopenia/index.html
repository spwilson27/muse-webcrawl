Vaccine-induced immune thrombotic thrombocytopenia

Vaccine-induced immune thrombotic thrombocytopenia (VITT), also called thrombosis with thrombocytopenia syndrome (TTS), is a rare but serious adverse event associated with adenoviral-vector COVID-19 vaccines, particularly the Oxford–AstraZeneca vaccine (ChAdOx1 nCoV-19) and the Janssen (Johnson & Johnson) vaccine.

Clinical features

VITT typically occurs 4 to 42 days after vaccination and combines thrombosis — often at unusual sites such as the cerebral venous sinuses or splanchnic veins — with thrombocytopenia (low platelet count). It is driven by antibodies against platelet factor 4 (PF4) that activate platelets, in a mechanism resembling autoimmune heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, though patients have usually not received heparin.

Management and epidemiology

Diagnosis relies on clinical features, platelet count, D-dimer, imaging, and PF4 antibody testing. Treatment uses non-heparin anticoagulants and intravenous immunoglobulin. Incidence was estimated at roughly one case per 50,000–250,000 doses depending on age group and setting; because of VITT, many countries restricted the AstraZeneca vaccine by age or favoured mRNA vaccines, and Janssen vaccine use was also limited.
