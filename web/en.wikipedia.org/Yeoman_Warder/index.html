Yeoman Warder

The Yeoman Warders of His Majesty's Royal Palace and Fortress the Tower of London, popularly known as Beefeaters, are the ceremonial guardians of the Tower of London. In principle they are responsible for guarding the Tower, its prisoners and the Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom; in practice, the modern Yeoman Warders act primarily as guides and ambassadors, conducting famous free tours of the fortress for its millions of annual visitors.

History

The corps traces its origins to the 15th century under Henry VII, whose personal bodyguard, the Yeomen of the Guard (founded 1485), provided the Tower's warders. The nickname "Beefeater" is popularly said to derive from a historical ration of beef — denoting the warders' favoured status at court — though the etymology is uncertain and may relate to buffetiers, attendants at the royal sideboard. The Tudor state dress uniform of scarlet and gold, still worn on state occasions, dates from the reign of Henry VIII; the everyday uniform is a dark blue "undress" with red trimmings, introduced in the 19th century.

Recruitment and duties

Yeoman Warders are drawn from retired senior non-commissioned officers of the British Armed Forces: candidates must have at least 22 years of service with an exemplary record, the Long Service and Good Conduct Medal, and the rank of warrant officer or senior non-commissioned officer. About 35 warders (including the Chief Yeoman Warder and the Yeoman Gaoler) live with their families within the Tower. Their ceremonial duties include the nightly Ceremony of the Keys, the locking of the Tower performed every evening for centuries. Moira Cameron, appointed in 2007, was the first woman to become a Yeoman Warder. The warders also traditionally keep charge of the Tower's famous ravens.
