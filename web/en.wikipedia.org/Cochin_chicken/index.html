The Cochin is a breed of large domestic chicken. It derives from very large feather-legged chickens brought from the port of Shanghai in China to Europe and North America in the 1840s and 1850s. It is reared principally for exhibition. It was formerly known as the Cochin-China, after the region of Cochinchina loosely associated in the Western imagination with the Far Eastern trade.

History

The arrival of these giant, heavily feathered birds — at first rumoured to have been presented to Queen Victoria — sparked a craze known as "hen fever" in Britain and America, launching the modern fancy-poultry hobby. Although originally promoted as a productive breed, the Cochin's real importance was in stimulating poultry shows and the breeding of many other Asiatic breeds, including the Brahma and Langshan.

Characteristics

Cochins are among the largest chickens: cocks may weigh nearly 6 kg. The most distinctive feature is the extraordinarily abundant, soft plumage that covers the body, legs and feet, giving the bird a rounded, ball-like silhouette. Recognized colour varieties include buff, black, white, blue, partridge, barred and splash. Hens are calm, make reliable brooders and mothers, and lay a moderate number of medium brown eggs, tending to go broody readily. A bantam version, in Britain historically called the Pekin bantam, is also kept worldwide.
