Taiwan–United States relations

Taiwan–United States relations — the informal but robust relationship between Taiwan (the Republic of China) and the United States since 1979, governed by the Taiwan Relations Act and conducted through the American Institute in Taiwan and TECRO.

Taiwan–United States relations are the international relations between Taiwan (officially the Republic of China, ROC) and the United States of America. The United States recognised the ROC as the government of China from 1949 until 1979, when it switched recognition to the People's Republic of China (PRC) as part of its "One China policy". Relations since then have been unofficial but extensive, underpinned by the Taiwan Relations Act of 1979.

Framework

Under the Act, the U.S. maintains cultural, commercial, and other relations with the people of Taiwan through the American Institute in Taiwan (AIT), a nominally private organization staffed by U.S. officials; Taiwan's counterpart is the Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Office (TECRO) in Washington. The United States is committed to providing Taiwan with arms of a defensive character and to maintaining the capacity to resist coercion, while pursuing a policy of "strategic ambiguity" about direct intervention.

Trade and security

Taiwan is one of the United States' largest trading partners, and a key node in semiconductor supply chains. Arms sales — including F-16 fighters, Patriot missiles, and anti-ship weapons — recur as a point of friction with Beijing. Tensions over the Taiwan Strait, including the crises of 1954–55, 1958, and 1995–96, have repeatedly tested the arrangement.

Recent developments

High-level visits, transit stops by Taiwanese leaders, and legislation such as the Taiwan Travel Act (2018) have expanded the relationship without formal diplomatic recognition.
