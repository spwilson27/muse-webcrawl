Pilaf

Pilaf (also pilau, plov, polo) is a rice dish in which the grains are cooked in a seasoned broth, often after being sautéed in oil or butter with onions and spices, so that they remain separate and fluffy. Meat, vegetables, dried fruit and nuts may be added.

History and spread

The method is described in medieval Arabic and Persian cookbooks and spread with Persian and Islamic culinary culture across Central and South Asia, the Middle East, the Caucasus, the Balkans, East Africa and the Caribbean. Uzbek plov, Persian polo, Indian pilau and Turkish pilav are celebrated regional forms; plov is inscribed on UNESCO's list of intangible cultural heritage for Uzbekistan and Tajikistan.

Preparation

Rinsing or soaking the rice, toasting it in fat, adding a measured amount of hot liquid, and steaming it covered without stirring produce the characteristic separate grains. A prized crisp bottom crust (Persian tahdig) may form.
