Flugelhorn

The flugelhorn is a brass instrument that resembles the trumpet and cornet but has a wider, more conical bore. It is a type of valved bugle, developed in Germany in the early 19th century from a traditional English valveless bugle. The German word Flügel means wing or flank, suggesting its use to signal the flank of a military unit in battle.

Characteristics

The flugelhorn is usually pitched in B♭, like most trumpets and cornets, and uses a deeper, conical mouthpiece. Its bore and bell produce a darker, warmer, and mellower tone than the trumpet, blending easily with French horns and the other instruments of the brass band, in which it occupies a standard seat. Most flugelhorns have three piston valves, though rotary-valved models exist in German and Austrian traditions, and some jazz instruments add a fourth valve.

Use in jazz

The flugelhorn became a prominent jazz instrument through players such as Miles Davis, Clark Terry, Art Farmer, and Freddie Hubbard. Chuck Mangione's 1977 recording "Feels So Good" brought it popular fame. Trumpeters commonly double on flugelhorn for ballads and lyrical passages.
