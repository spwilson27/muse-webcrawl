New Zealand Rugby

New Zealand Rugby (NZR), founded in 1892 as the New Zealand Rugby Football Union, is the governing body for rugby union in New Zealand. It administers the national teams, most famously the men's All Blacks and the women's Black Ferns, and organises domestic competitions.

History

Rugby was introduced to New Zealand in 1870 by Charles Monro, and provincial unions formed over the following two decades. The NZRFU was established in 1892; the 1905 "Originals" tour of Britain made the All Blacks internationally famous. The union became professional-era New Zealand Rugby in the 1990s and changed its name from NZRFU in 2006.

Competitions and teams

NZR runs the National Provincial Championship (NPC), the Heartland Championship and the women's Farah Palmer Cup, and co-owns Super Rugby Pacific franchises such as the Crusaders and Blues. The All Blacks have won the Rugby World Cup three times (1987, 2011, 2015) and are noted for the haka performed before matches. NZR is built on 26 affiliated provincial unions.
