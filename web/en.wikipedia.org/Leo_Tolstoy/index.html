Leo Tolstoy

Count Lev Nikolayevich Tolstoy (9 September 1828 – 20 November 1910), usually referred to in English as Leo Tolstoy, was a Russian writer regarded as one of the greatest and most influential authors of all time.

Life and works

Born into an aristocratic family at Yasnaya Polyana, Tolstoy served as an artillery officer in the Crimean War before turning to literature. His masterpieces War and Peace (1865–1869) and Anna Karenina (1875–1877) are monumental works of realist fiction. Other writings include A Confession, The Kingdom of God Is Within You, and the novella The Death of Ivan Ilyich.

Beliefs and legacy

Following a spiritual crisis in the 1870s, Tolstoy adopted a radical form of Christian anarchism and pacifism, advocating nonviolent resistance — ideas that influenced Mahatma Gandhi and Martin Luther King Jr. His moral philosophy, educational experiments and critique of state and church authority made him a global figure in his final decades.
