Mormon Battalion

The Mormon Battalion was the only religiously based unit in United States military history. It served in the United States Army during the Mexican–American War (1846–1847), marching nearly 2,000 miles from Council Bluffs, Iowa, to San Diego, California — one of the longest military marches in US history.

Active1846–1847
CountryUnited States
SizeAbout 500 volunteers
ConflictMexican–American War

Formation

Recruited in July 1846 from members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints who were migrating west after expulsion from Illinois, the battalion's pay and clothing allowances provided crucial support to the Mormon pioneers. The unit fought no major battles but helped secure California and built infrastructure along its route.

Legacy

Veterans of the battalion later participated in the California Gold Rush; some were present at Sutter's Mill during the gold discovery and helped blaze wagon routes, including the Mormon Emigrant Trail over the Sierra Nevada.
