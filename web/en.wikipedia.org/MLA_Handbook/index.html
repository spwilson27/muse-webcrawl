The MLA Handbook is a guide published by the Modern Language Association of America that sets out MLA style, the standard system for documenting sources in academic writing in the humanities, especially language and literary studies. It is used by schools, universities and publishers in over 3,000 institutions.

Content

The handbook covers the mechanics of scholarly prose, plagiarism and research methods, the formatting of papers, and the documentation of sources through in-text parenthetical citations keyed to a works-cited list. The eighth edition (2016) replaced source-specific citation recipes with a universal template of "core elements" — author, title, container, publisher, date, location — adaptable to new media; the ninth edition (2021) refined and expanded this approach.

History

MLA documentation style originated in the association's 1951 style sheet. The first edition of the handbook appeared in 1977, aimed at undergraduate and graduate students, and it became the association's best-selling publication, while the companion MLA Style Manual for scholars was discontinued after its third edition.
