File drawer problem

The file drawer problem is the tendency for studies with statistically significant, positive results to be published while studies with null or negative results remain unpublished — figuratively filed away in researchers' desk drawers. The term was coined by the psychologist Robert Rosenthal in 1979. The resulting distortion, known as publication bias, means that the published literature and meta-analyses based on it can systematically overestimate the true size of an effect.

Consequences

If journals and authors preferentially publish positive findings, meta-analysts pooling only published studies may conclude that a treatment works when the full body of evidence is equivocal. Rosenthal proposed the "fail-safe N" as a diagnostic: the number of unpublished null studies that would have to exist to reduce an observed meta-analytic effect to non-significance. Other detection methods include funnel plot asymmetry and trim-and-fill techniques.

Remedies

Proposed and adopted remedies include prospective registration of clinical trials and study protocols, registered reports (in which journals accept studies before results are known), preprint servers, and journals dedicated to null results. Funders and regulators increasingly require reporting of all trial outcomes regardless of direction.
