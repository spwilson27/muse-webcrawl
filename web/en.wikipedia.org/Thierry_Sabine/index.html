Thierry Sabine (13 June 1949 – 14 January 1986) was a French wrangler, motorcycle racer and founder and main organizer of the Dakar Rally, the world's most famous rally raid.

Origins of the Dakar

In 1977, competing in the Abidjan–Nice rally, Sabine became lost on his motorcycle in the Libyan desert and was rescued after three days close to death. The experience convinced him that the desert could provide a supreme challenge for racers. He resolved to organize a rally beginning in Paris and finishing in Dakar, Senegal, under the motto "A challenge for those who go; a dream for those who stay behind." The first Paris–Dakar Rally set off from the Place du Trocadéro on 26 December 1978, and Sabine founded the Thierry Sabine Organisation (TSO) to run it.

Death

Sabine was killed on 14 January 1986, during the eighth Dakar Rally, when his helicopter crashed into a dune in Mali during a sudden sandstorm. The singer Daniel Balavoine and three others — pilot François-Xavier Bagnoud, journalist Nathalie Odent, and radio technician Jean-Paul Lefur — also died in the crash. A memorial stands to him at the site known as Arbre Thierry Sabine in the Ténéré desert, and his rally continues today as the Dakar Rally.
