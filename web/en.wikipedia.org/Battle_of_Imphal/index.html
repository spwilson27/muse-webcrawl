Battle of Imphal

Battle of Imphal
Date: 8 March – 3 July 1944
Location: Imphal, Manipur, India
Result: Allied victory
Part of the Burma campaign of the Second World War

The Battle of Imphal took place in the region around the city of Imphal, the capital of the state of Manipur in north-east India, from March until July 1944. Together with the simultaneous Battle of Kohima, it marked the turning point of the Burma campaign, when the Japanese Fifteenth Army under Lieutenant-General Renya Mutaguchi was defeated in its offensive (Operation U-Go) to invade India.

Background

Mutaguchi's plan depended on capturing the Allied supply bases at Imphal quickly and living off captured stocks, with support from Subhas Chandra Bose's Indian National Army. Lieutenant-General William Slim, commanding the British Fourteenth Army, anticipated the offensive and withdrew his forward divisions onto the Imphal plain to fight on ground of his choosing, supplied by air.

The battle

Japanese divisions crossed the Chindwin River in March and converged on Imphal from several directions, cutting the main road to Dimapur. The IV Corps held a perimeter around the plain while reinforcements were flown in, including an entire division airlifted from the Arakan. Japanese attacks in April and May failed to break the defences, and their troops, short of food and ammunition in the monsoon, began to starve and sicken. The relief of Kohima allowed the British 2nd Division to drive south and reopen the road to Imphal on 22 June; Mutaguchi ordered a general retreat in early July.

Aftermath

The Japanese lost more than 50,000 men killed, wounded, or dead of disease and starvation — one of the worst defeats ever suffered by the Imperial Japanese Army. The Fourteenth Army pursued into Burma in 1945. In a 2013 poll at the National Army Museum, Imphal–Kohima was voted Britain's greatest battle.
