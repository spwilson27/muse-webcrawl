In linguistics, apophony is any sound change within a word that indicates grammatical information, often a change in a vowel. It is also called vowel alternation or vowel gradation. The best-known type is the Indo-European vowel alternation known as ablaut, from German Ablaut. Apophony contrasts with changes made by affixes: instead of adding a prefix or suffix, the word itself changes internally.

Types

Vowel changes are termed vocalic apophony; changes in consonants, such as alternations in voicing, are consonantal apophony; changes in prosody, such as stress or tone, are prosodic apophony; and nasalisation alternations are also attested. The phenomenon overlaps with the concepts of internal sandhi and morphophonemic alternation.

Indo-European ablaut

In Proto-Indo-European, ablaut was a systematic alternation between the vowels e, o and zero (no vowel) in a root, used to derive related words and mark grammatical forms. It survives vestigially in the Germanic strong verbs of English: sing – sang – sung, drive – drove – driven, bind – bound. German uses ablaut extensively, as in singen – sang – gesungen. Traces also appear in noun pairs such as foot – feet and goose – geese, though those reflect umlaut, a different though related historical process of vowel fronting.

In other languages

Apophony occurs worldwide. In Semitic languages, consonantal roots are inflected by inserting different vowel patterns, as in Arabic kataba "he wrote," kutiba "it was written," and kitāb "book," all from the root k-t-b. In some Athabaskan languages, verb stems vary their vowels to mark aspect. Celtic languages show vowel affection as a grammatical signal, and modern Irish uses initial consonant mutations in a comparable internal-change role.

Terminology

The term apophony was introduced to linguistics in the early twentieth century (notably by the French linguist Antoine Meillet) to provide a language-neutral term covering such alternations, avoiding the specifically Germanic connotations of ablaut and the restricted sense of umlaut.
