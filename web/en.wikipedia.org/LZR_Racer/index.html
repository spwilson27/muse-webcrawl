LZR Racer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The LZR Racer is a line of competition swimsuits manufactured by Speedo using high-tech fabric composed of woven elastane-nylon and polyurethane, launched on 13 February 2008. The suit was developed with help from NASA wind-tunnel testing and computational fluid dynamics, and its welded (rather than stitched) seams and polyurethane panels were designed to reduce drag and compress the swimmer's body.

Record-breaking impact

Swimmers wearing the LZR Racer set an extraordinary number of world records in 2008–2009 — the overwhelming majority of records broken at the 2008 Beijing Olympics, where Michael Phelps won eight gold medals in the suit, were achieved in the LZR or similar full-body polyurethane suits from rival manufacturers. Critics described the effect as "technological doping".

Ban

In response, FINA (now World Aquatics) changed its rules effective 1 January 2010: competition swimsuits must be made of textiles, may not cover the neck or extend past the shoulders and ankles, and are limited in thickness and buoyancy. The LZR Racer era is regarded as a turning point in how sports govern equipment technology.
