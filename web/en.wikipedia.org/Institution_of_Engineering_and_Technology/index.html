The Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET) is a British professional institution for engineers and technicians in electrical, electronic, manufacturing, mechanical, systems and information technology fields. With roughly 154,000 members in some 148 countries, it is among the world's largest engineering institutions. The IET is a licensed body of the Engineering Council and awards Chartered Engineer (CEng), Incorporated Engineer (IEng), Engineering Technician (EngTech) and ICT Technician registration, with membership grades including Fellow (FIET) and Member (MIET).

History

The IET was formed on 31 March 2006 by the merger of two venerable bodies: the Institution of Electrical Engineers (IEE), founded in 1871 as the Society of Telegraph Engineers, and the Institution of Incorporated Engineers (IIE), whose roots went back to 1884. The IEE received its royal charter in 1921 and counted Michael Faraday's experimental successors among its early community; the merged institution inherited the IEE's headquarters at Savoy Place in London (its home since 1909) and its operational base at Michael Faraday House in Stevenage. The IET holds its own royal charter, granted in 2006.

Activities

The IET publishes more than 40 peer-reviewed journals (including Electronics Letters, founded 1965) and thousands of conference papers, and operates the Inspec bibliographic database, which indexes well over 20 million records in physics, computing and engineering. Its magazine for members is E&T (Engineering & Technology). Perhaps its most widely used publication is the IET Wiring Regulations (BS 7671), the standard governing electrical installations in the United Kingdom. The institution also organises the IET Faraday Challenge and other schools outreach, awards the Faraday Medal and the A. F. Harvey Engineering Research Prize, and convenes communities ("networks") across technical sectors from power and transport to cyber security.
