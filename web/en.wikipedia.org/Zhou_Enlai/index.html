Zhou Enlai (Chinese: 周恩来; 5 March 1898 – 8 January 1976) was a Chinese statesman and revolutionary who served as the first Premier of the People's Republic of China from its founding in 1949 until his death. A leading figure of the Chinese Communist Party and a close associate of Mao Zedong, Zhou was renowned as a diplomat and administrator and was instrumental in shaping China's foreign policy.

Early life and revolution

Born in Huai'an and educated in Tianjin and Japan, Zhou joined the May Fourth Movement and studied and organised communist cells in Europe in the early 1920s. He became political commissar of the Whampoa Military Academy, survived the 1927 Shanghai massacre, helped direct the Nanchang Uprising, and endured the Long March, supporting Mao's rise at the Zunyi Conference. During the Second Sino-Japanese War he was the Communist Party's liaison to the Nationalist government in Chongqing.

Premiership

As premier and (until 1958) foreign minister, Zhou ran the state bureaucracy, negotiated at the 1954 Geneva Conference, and presented the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence at Bandung in 1955. During the Cultural Revolution he worked to protect officials, infrastructure, and cultural relics from the worst excesses while remaining publicly loyal to Mao. In the 1970s he, with Mao, engineered the rapprochement with the United States, meeting Henry Kissinger and hosting President Richard Nixon in 1972, and promoted the Four Modernizations.

Death and legacy

Zhou died of cancer in January 1976; public grief at his Qingming Festival memorial that April erupted into the Tiananmen Incident. He remains widely admired in China for his integrity, diligence, and dedication.
