Cosmos: A Personal Voyage is a thirteen-part, 1980–81 television series written by Carl Sagan, Ann Druyan, and Steven Soter, with Sagan as presenter. It was first broadcast by the Public Broadcasting Service in the United States, and became the most widely watched series in the history of American public television, seen by at least 500 million people in 60 countries.

Content

The series covers a wide range of scientific subjects, including the origin of life and a perspective of humans' place in the universe. Episodes include "The Shores of the Cosmic Ocean", "One Voice in the Cosmic Fugue", "Harmony of the Worlds", and "The Backbone of Night". Sagan's narration, the special effects, and the music — including works by Vangelis — made the series a landmark of science communication.

Legacy

The series won an Emmy Award and a Peabody Award. Sagan's companion book Cosmos (1980) spent 70 weeks on the New York Times Best Seller list. A sequel series, Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey, hosted by Neil deGrasse Tyson, aired in 2014, followed by Cosmos: Possible Worlds in 2020.
