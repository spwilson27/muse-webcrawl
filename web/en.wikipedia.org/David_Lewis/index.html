David Lewis

David Kellogg Lewis (28 September 1941 – 14 October 2001) was an American philosopher widely regarded as one of the most important analytic philosophers of the twentieth century. He taught at Princeton University for most of his career and made influential contributions to metaphysics, philosophy of language, philosophy of mind, logic and ethics.

Modal realism

Lewis is best known for modal realism, the view defended in his 1986 book On the Plurality of Worlds that every possible world is a concrete reality just as real as the actual world, and that things are true "possibly" because they are true in some world. The theory was famously met with what Lewis called the "incredulous stare", but he argued that its systematic theoretical benefits outweighed its counterintuitive ontology.

Other work

In Convention (1969) Lewis analysed conventions using game theory, providing an account of how language can be conventional without explicit agreement. His work on counterfactuals gave the standard similarity-based semantics for counterfactual conditionals. He also developed Humean supervenience, counterpart theory, and influential accounts of causation, mental states (a form of functionalism), and persistence through time.

Life

Lewis was born in Oberlin, Ohio, studied at Swarthmore College and Oxford, and took his PhD at Harvard under W. V. O. Quine. He taught briefly at UCLA before moving to Princeton in 1970, where he remained until his death from complications of diabetes in 2001.
