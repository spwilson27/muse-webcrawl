Lowell, Massachusetts

Lowell is a city in Middlesex County, Massachusetts, on the Merrimack River about 25 miles northwest of Boston. Founded in the 1820s as a planned textile manufacturing centre and named for industrialist Francis Cabot Lowell, it became the largest industrial complex in the United States and a cradle of the American Industrial Revolution.

History

Lowell's mills, powered by an extensive canal system, employed thousands, including the "mill girls" and later waves of immigrants. Textile production declined in the 20th century, but the city has been revitalised in part through the Lowell National Historical Park (established 1978), the University of Massachusetts Lowell, and a large Cambodian-American community. Writer Jack Kerouac was born in Lowell.
