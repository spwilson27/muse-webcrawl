Preterm birth

Preterm birth
DefinitionBirth before 37 completed weeks of gestation
Frequencyc. 15 million babies per year worldwide (about 1 in 10)
SpecialtyObstetrics, neonatology

Preterm birth, also known as premature birth, is the birth of a baby at fewer than 37 weeks gestational age, as opposed to full-term delivery at approximately 40 weeks. Extreme preterm birth occurs before 28 weeks, very preterm at 28–32 weeks, and moderate-to-late preterm at 32–37 weeks.

Causes and risk factors

Known risk factors include a previous preterm birth, infection, chronic conditions such as diabetes and high blood pressure, pre-eclampsia, multiple pregnancy, smoking, and extremes of maternal age. In many cases the precise cause is unknown; a large share of preterm births follow spontaneous preterm labour or preterm rupture of membranes, while others are medically induced for maternal or fetal indications.

Outcomes and care

Complications of prematurity — including respiratory distress syndrome, intraventricular haemorrhage, necrotising enterocolitis, and retinopathy of prematurity — are the leading cause of death in children under five. Survival has improved greatly with neonatal intensive care, antenatal corticosteroids, surfactant therapy, and kangaroo mother care. Worldwide, roughly 15 million babies are born preterm each year, with rates of about 5–18% of births depending on the country.
