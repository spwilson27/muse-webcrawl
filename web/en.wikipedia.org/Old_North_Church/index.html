Old North Church

Old North Church, officially Christ Church in the City of Boston, is an Episcopal church located in the North End of Boston, Massachusetts. Built in 1723, it is the oldest standing church building in Boston and a National Historic Landmark. It is famous for its role at the start of the American Revolutionary War, as the place from which the signal lanterns of Paul Revere's ride were shown.

History

The church was constructed in 1723, inspired by the work of Christopher Wren. Its 191-foot (58 m) steeple is the tallest in Boston. The original steeple was destroyed by hurricanes in 1804 and again in 1954, and was rebuilt to the original design.

The lantern signal

On the night of April 18, 1775, on the eve of the Battles of Lexington and Concord, the church sexton Robert Newman, and possibly Captain John Pulling, climbed the steeple at the direction of Paul Revere and displayed two lanterns. The signal — arranged by Revere as "one if by land, two if by sea" — warned Charlestown patriots that British troops were crossing the Charles River by boat to march on Concord. The lanterns burned only briefly, less than a minute, to avoid alerting the British. Henry Wadsworth Longfellow immortalised the episode in his 1861 poem "Paul Revere's Ride."

Today

Old North Church remains an active Episcopal parish. It stands on the Freedom Trail and receives hundreds of thousands of visitors each year. Its interior preserves box pews, and the church houses busts and the oldest set of peal bells in North America, cast in 1744.
