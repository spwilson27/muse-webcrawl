Metabolic network

A metabolic network is the complete set of metabolic and physical processes that determine the physiological and biochemical properties of a cell. It comprises the chemical reactions of metabolism organized as pathways, together with the regulatory interactions that guide them.

Reconstruction and modelling

Genome-scale metabolic network reconstructions, built from annotated genomes and biochemical databases such as KEGG, catalog every known reaction and the genes encoding the enzymes. These reconstructions can be converted into mathematical models used with constraint-based methods such as flux balance analysis to predict growth and reaction fluxes.

Applications

Metabolic network models are used in systems biology, metabolic engineering of microbes for producing fuels and chemicals, drug target discovery, and the study of diseases such as cancer, where metabolism is rewired. Network analysis shows that metabolic networks are highly connected, with a few hub metabolites (such as ATP and NADH) participating in many reactions.
