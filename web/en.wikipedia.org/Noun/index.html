Noun

In grammar, a noun is a word that represents a concrete or abstract thing, such as living creatures, places, actions, qualities, states of existence, and ideas. A noun may serve as the subject or the object of a verb, or the object of a preposition, and nouns constitute one of the main parts of speech (word classes) in most languages.

Definitions and identification

Traditional grammar defines nouns semantically as "a person, place, thing or idea", but modern linguistics prefers distributional and morphological criteria: in English, nouns typically can be preceded by the article the, pluralised with -s, and possessivised with -'s. Nouns head noun phrases, which function as subjects, objects and complements in clauses.

Classification

Nouns are commonly divided into proper nouns, which name specific entities (e.g. Nottingham, Mary) and are capitalised in English, and common nouns. Common nouns may further be countable or uncountable (mass) nouns, concrete or abstract, and collective nouns denoting groups, such as team or flock.

Grammatical categories

In many languages nouns inflect for categories such as number (singular and plural), case (see nominative case), gender, and definiteness. Languages such as the Bantu languages organise nouns into elaborate noun class systems, while many East Asian languages use numeral classifiers when counting nouns.
