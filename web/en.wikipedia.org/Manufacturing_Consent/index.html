Manufacturing Consent

Authors: Edward S. Herman and Noam Chomsky
Published: 1988
Subject: Mass media criticism
Full title: Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media

Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media is a 1988 book by Edward S. Herman and Noam Chomsky. The title borrows a phrase from Walter Lippmann's Public Opinion (1922), which spoke of the "manufacture of consent" as a technique of managing democracy.

The propaganda model

The book proposes a propaganda model of the mass media: news passes through five filters — concentrated ownership, advertising as the primary income source, reliance on official sources, "flak" as a means of disciplining the media, and anti-communism (later generalised by Chomsky to ideologies such as the "war on terror") as a control mechanism. Together, the authors argue, these filters bias coverage in favour of powerful interests without the need for direct censorship.

Case studies

Herman and Chomsky apply the model to comparative coverage — for example, the differing treatment of victims in enemy and client states ("worthy" and "unworthy" victims), elections in El Salvador and Nicaragua, and coverage of the Vietnam War and of the Khmer Rouge in Cambodia.

Reception

The book is a foundational text of media studies and political communication, widely taught and frequently reprinted. Critics have disputed some of its case studies and argued the model understates journalists' independence and audience agency. A 1992 documentary film adapted the book around Chomsky's work.
