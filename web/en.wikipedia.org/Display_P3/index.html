Display P3

Display P3 is a colour space created by Apple Inc. and announced in 2015. It is based on the DCI-P3 colour gamut used in digital cinema projection but uses the D65 white point and the sRGB gamma curve, making it suitable for consumer displays. Display P3 offers a gamut roughly 25 percent larger than sRGB, with noticeably more saturated reds and greens.

History

Apple introduced Display P3 with the late-2015 iMac with Retina 5K display, marketing the feature as "Retina P3". The iPhone 7, released in 2016, and later iPads, MacBooks and Apple external displays adopted the colour space as part of the company's wide-colour initiative. Apple's operating systems perform colour management so that sRGB content continues to render correctly on P3 displays.

Technical characteristics

  • Primaries — the same red, green and blue chromaticities as DCI-P3 (red 630 nm monochromatic, green 532 nm, blue 467 nm approximately).
  • White point — D65 (6504 K), unlike the ~6300 K theatrical white of DCI-P3.
  • Transfer function — the sRGB gamma (~2.2), rather than the 2.6 gamma of cinema projection.
  • Encoding — commonly expressed in 10-bit per channel in professional workflows; ICC profile name "Display P3".

Usage

Display P3 is widely used for photography and video display on Apple devices. Web standards have added support for the colour space: CSS Color Module Level 4 defines the display-p3 colour space, allowing web authors to specify wide-gamut colours with a fallback to sRGB. Android and other platforms likewise use DCI-P3-based wide-gamut modes in premium devices.
