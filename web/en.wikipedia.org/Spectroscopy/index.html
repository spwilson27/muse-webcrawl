Spectroscopy
Study of interaction between matter and electromagnetic radiation
Key tools: spectrometer, spectroscope, prism, diffraction grating
Applications: astronomy, chemistry, physics, materials science

Spectroscopy is the field of study that measures and interprets the electromagnetic spectra that result from the interaction between electromagnetic radiation and matter as a function of wavelength or frequency of the radiation.

History

Isaac Newton used a prism in the 1660s to split sunlight into colours. In the 19th century Joseph von Fraunhofer observed dark absorption lines in the solar spectrum, and Gustav Kirchhoff and Robert Bunsen showed each chemical element produces a unique pattern of spectral lines, founding spectral analysis.

Types

Absorption spectroscopy measures light absorbed by a sample; emission spectroscopy measures light emitted. Other forms include infrared, ultraviolet-visible, NMR, Raman, X-ray and mass spectrometry (often grouped with spectroscopic methods).

Applications

Spectroscopy identifies the composition of stars and distant galaxies, determines chemical structures, monitors pollutants, and underpins medical imaging. See also Great Lakes environmental studies and related physics topics.
