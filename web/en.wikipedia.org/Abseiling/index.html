Abseiling

Abseiling (from German abseilen, "to rope down"), also known as rappelling in North America, is the controlled descent off a vertical drop, such as a rock face, by descending on a fixed rope. This technique is used by climbers, mountaineers, cavers, canyoners, search and rescue and rope access technicians to descend cliffs or slopes that are too steep and/or dangerous to descend without protection.

Technique

The descender is attached to a climbing harness, and the climber controls the speed of descent by varying the friction on the rope, usually by holding the rope with a brake hand below the descender. The rope is anchored at the top of the descent, either around a natural feature such as a tree or rock, or through artificial anchors. After descending, the rope is retrieved by pulling one end down from below.

Common descender devices include the figure-eight, tubular belay devices used in rappel mode, and mechanical descenders used in rope access work. A backup, such as a friction hitch (for example a Prusik knot) on the rope below or above the descender, is often used to guard against loss of control.

History

The origin of the term abseil is attributed to Roger Frison-Roche, and the technique of rappelling itself is generally credited to Jean Charlet-Straton, a Chamonix guide who devised it in the 19th century while descending the Petit Dru. Early climbers used body rappels, such as the Dülfersitz method, wrapping the rope around the body to create friction.

Applications

Abseiling is used in climbing and mountaineering for descending after a climb, in caving and canyoning for negotiating drops, by military personnel for rapid building and cliff descents, and by rope access technicians for building maintenance, inspection, and rescue work.

Safety

Abseiling is a common cause of climbing accidents when done incorrectly, particularly through anchors failing, ropes being cut over sharp edges, or rappelling off the end of the rope. Stopper knots tied in the ends of the rope and careful checking of the system before weighting it are standard precautions.
