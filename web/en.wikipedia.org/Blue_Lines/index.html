Blue Lines

Blue Lines is the debut studio album by the English electronic music group Massive Attack, released on 8 April 1991 by Wild Bunch and Virgin Records. Blending hip hop, reggae dub, soul and electronic production, the album is regarded as the founding document of the trip hop genre, although the group themselves disliked the label.

Background and recording

Massive Attack emerged from the Bristol sound-system collective The Wild Bunch. The album was recorded over several years in Bristol and London with collaborators including vocalists Shara Nelson, Horace Andy and the rapper Tricky. Its construction combined samples from sources as diverse as Mahavishnu Orchestra and Isaac Hayes with live instrumentation and sparse, downtempo beats.

Songs

The album's best-known tracks are “Unfinished Sympathy”, a sweeping orchestral single sung by Nelson that is frequently ranked among the greatest songs of all time, and “Safe from Harm”. Other tracks include “Daydreaming”, “Be Thankful for What You've Got” and “Hymn of the Big Wheel”.

Legacy

Blue Lines was critically acclaimed on release and has appeared in numerous all-time lists, including a place in Rolling Stone's 500 Greatest Albums of All Time. It established the template for trip hop and influenced acts such as Portishead and Radiohead. During the 1991 Gulf War the group briefly dropped “Attack” from its name on single releases.
