Spring Drive

Spring Drive is a name given to a series of watch movements originally conceived by Yoshikazu Akahane at Suwa Seikosha (now Seiko Epson) in 1977 and produced by the Seiko Watch Corporation. The Spring Drive replaces the conventional escapement with a device Seiko calls the Tri-synchro regulator, which uses an electromagnetic brake, regulated by a quartz oscillator, to control the unwinding of a mainspring.

Operation

The mainspring drives the hands directly through a gear train, as in a mechanical watch, giving the seconds hand a perfectly smooth glide motion. A glide wheel generates a small electrical current that powers the quartz circuit and an electromagnetic brake, which regulates the glide wheel to exactly eight revolutions per second. There is no battery.

History

After decades of development, Spring Drive watches were first sold in 1999 in Japan and internationally from 2005, notably in the Grand Seiko line.
