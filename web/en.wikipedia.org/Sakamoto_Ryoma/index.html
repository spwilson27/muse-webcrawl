Sakamoto Ryōma

Sakamoto Ryōma (坂本 龍馬, 3 January 1836 – 10 December 1867) was a Japanese samurai and influential figure of the Bakumatsu period, the turbulent final years of the Tokugawa shogunate. A low-ranking samurai from Tosa Domain, he became a key broker of the alliance that overthrew the shogunate and launched the Meiji Restoration.

Early life

Born in Kōchi, Tosa Domain (now Kōchi Prefecture), to a family of wealthy sake brewers who had purchased the lowest samurai rank of gōshi, Ryōma studied swordsmanship, eventually training at the Hokushin Ittō-ryū school in Edo. Returning to Tosa, he joined the Tosa Loyalist Party, which advocateded sonnō jōi — "revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians."

Kaientai and the Satchō Alliance

Ryōma left Tosa without permission, becoming a rōnin. In Nagasaki he founded the Kameyama Shachū, a private trading and shipping company that doubled as a private navy — later reorganised as the Kaientai. With the mediation of figures such as Katsu Kaishū, he helped forge the secret Satchō Alliance (1866) between the rival domains of Satsuma and Chōshū, the military backbone of the anti-shogunate movement.

Eight-point plan and assassination

Ryōma drafted the Senchū Hassaku ("Eight Proposals While Shipboard"), a programme calling for the restoration of imperial rule, a bicameral legislature, meritocratic appointment of officials and fair diplomacy. These ideas influenced the new Meiji government's Charter Oath. In 1867 he persuaded Tosa's leadership to press the shōgun Tokugawa Yoshinobu to resign peacefully, which he did in November 1867. Weeks later, on 10 December 1867, Ryōma was assassinated at the Ōmiya inn in Kyoto, together with his associate Nakaoka Shintarō; the killers were never conclusively identified, though shogunate loyalist forces such as the Shinsengumi and the pro-shogunate Mimawarigumi have been blamed.

Legacy

Ryōma remains one of Japan's most popular historical figures, celebrated in novels (notably Shiba Ryōtarō's Ryōma Goes His Way), television dramas and museums in Kōchi.
