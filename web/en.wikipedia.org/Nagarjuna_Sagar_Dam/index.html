Nagarjuna Sagar Dam is a masonry dam across the Krishna River at Nagarjuna Sagar, on the border of Nalgonda district in Telangana and Guntur district in Andhra Pradesh, India. Built between 1955 and 1972, it was one of the earliest multipurpose irrigation and hydroelectric projects undertaken after Indian independence, and its reservoir is among the largest in the world by storage capacity.

History

The foundation stone was laid by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru on 10 December 1955. Construction employed tens of thousands of workers, much of it by manual labour, and the dam was formally dedicated in 1967 as the canals were progressively commissioned. The project is named after Nagarjuna, the second-century Buddhist philosopher associated with the ancient site of Nagarjunakonda, which was submerged by the reservoir; its monuments and relics were excavated and relocated to an island museum in the lake before inundation.

Description

The dam is 124 metres high from its deepest foundation and about 1.55 kilometres long, with 26 crest gates. Its reservoir, Nagarjuna Sagar, has a gross storage of about 11.5 billion cubic metres. Two canals — the Jawahar (right) canal and the Lal Bahadur Shastri (left) canal — irrigate well over a million acres across the two states. The powerhouse has an installed capacity of 815.6 MW from conventional and reversible turbine units.

The dam is a major tourist attraction, and the reservoir supports the nearby Nagarjunsagar–Srisailam Tiger Reserve on its banks.
