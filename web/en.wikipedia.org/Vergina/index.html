Vergina

Vergina (Greek: Βεργίνα) is a small town in northern Greece, in the regional unit of Imathia, Macedonia. It is famous as the site of the ancient city of Aigai, the first capital of the Kingdom of Macedon, and for the royal tombs discovered there in the 1970s, one of which is widely attributed to Philip II, father of Alexander the Great.

Ancient Aigai

Aigai was the original capital of Macedon before the court moved to Pella around 400 BC, but it remained the royal burial place and ceremonial centre. It was at Aigai, in 336 BC, that Philip II was assassinated during celebrations for his daughter's wedding, and Alexander was proclaimed king. Excavations have revealed a palace, a theatre (where Philip was killed), temples, and an extensive necropolis.

The royal tombs

In 1977–78, Greek archaeologist Manolis Andronikos excavated the Great Tumulus and found a cluster of unlooted royal tombs. Tomb II contained gold grave goods, weapons, and a gold larnax bearing the Vergina Sun emblem and holding cremated remains — attributed by Andronikos and many scholars to Philip II, though some argue for Philip III Arrhidaeus. The finds, including the king's armour, a ceremonial shield, and the gold wreath of Queen Meda, are displayed in an underground museum built within the tumulus.

Heritage status

The archaeological site of Aigai was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1996 for its testimony to Macedonian civilization and its significance for Greek history and art.
