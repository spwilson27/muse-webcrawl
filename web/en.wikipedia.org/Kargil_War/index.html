Kargil War

The Kargil War, also known as the Kargil conflict, was fought between India and Pakistan from May to July 1999 in the Kargil district of Ladakh (then part of Jammu and Kashmir) along the Line of Control (LoC). Pakistani soldiers and Kashmiri militants infiltrated positions on the Indian side of the LoC, aiming to sever the link between Kashmir and Ladakh and force negotiations on the Kashmir dispute.

Course of the conflict

India discovered the infiltration in May 1999 and launched Operation Vijay to evict the intruders. The fighting took place at extreme altitude — many posts above 5,000 metres — in rugged mountain terrain. Key battles included those for Tololing, Tiger Hill, and Point 4875. The Indian Air Force conducted Operation Safed Sagar in support. India recaptured most positions, and, under international pressure, particularly from US President Bill Clinton, Pakistan withdrew its forces.

Outcome

By 26 July 1999 — celebrated in India as Kargil Vijay Diwas — India had regained control of the heights. India suffered over 500 killed; Pakistani casualties are estimated in the hundreds to over a thousand. The war drew worldwide attention as the first armed conflict between two states that had openly tested nuclear weapons the previous year.

Aftermath

The Kargil Review Committee in India led to defence reforms. In Pakistan, the conflict strained relations between Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif and army chief Pervez Musharraf, who seized power in a coup in October 1999.
