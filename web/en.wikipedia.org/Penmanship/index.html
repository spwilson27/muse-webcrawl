Penmanship
DefinitionThe technique and art of writing by hand
Notable methodsSpencerian script, Palmer Method, Copperplate
Related fieldsCalligraphy, palaeography, graphology

Penmanship is the technique of writing with the hand using a writing instrument, and the art of producing legible, consistent and aesthetically pleasing handwriting. Once a core subject of formal education, it encompassed posture, grip, letter formation and extensive copying drills from copybooks.

History

In the nineteenth century, American writing masters such as Platt Rogers Spencer popularised a flowing cursive style known as Spencerian script, widely used in business correspondence. Austin Norman Palmer's simpler, muscular-movement Palmer Method, introduced in the late nineteenth century, dominated US school instruction for decades and was promoted as a rapid, legible "business hand." In Britain, the Vere Foster copybooks and civil-service hands served a similar role, while Copperplate (English round hand) remained a model of formal elegance.

Decline and revival

The typewriter, ballpoint pen and finally the computer progressively displaced formal penmanship instruction during the twentieth century; many school systems reduced or dropped cursive teaching in the 2000s and 2010s. At the same time, fountain pen collecting, journaling and modern calligraphy have sustained a hobbyist revival, and research continues into the role of handwriting in learning and memory.
