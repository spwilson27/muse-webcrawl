Asian Americans

Total population~24 million (alone or in combination, 2020 census); about 7% of the US population
Largest groupsChinese, Indian, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese
RegionsCalifornia, New York, Texas, and major metropolitan areas

Asian Americans are Americans of Asian ancestry, including naturalized citizens and their descendants. The term, coined in the late 1960s by activists including Yuji Ichioka and Emma Gee, encompasses people with origins in East Asia, Southeast Asia and South Asia. Asian Americans numbered about 20.6 million alone (and over 24 million alone or in combination) in the 2020 United States census, making up roughly 7 percent of the population, and are among the fastest-growing racial groups in the country, largely through immigration.

History

Filipino sailors were present in what is now Louisiana in the 18th century, and large-scale Asian immigration began with Chinese migrants during the California Gold Rush of the 1850s. Chinese workers helped build the transcontinental railroad, but anti-Asian sentiment led to the Chinese Exclusion Act of 1882, the first US law barring immigration by nationality, followed by broader restrictions culminating in the Immigration Act of 1924. During the Second World War, about 120,000 Japanese Americans were forcibly incarcerated in internment camps. Exclusion laws were repealed in stages, and the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965 reopened immigration, transforming the size and diversity of Asian American communities, which grew further with refugees from Vietnam, Cambodia and Laos after 1975.

Demographics and culture

The largest origin groups are Chinese, Indian, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean and Japanese Americans. Asian Americans are highly diverse in language, religion, income and education; as a group they have the highest median household income of any US racial category, alongside significant internal disparities. The group has been discussed through the contested "model minority" stereotype, which scholars note can obscure discrimination — including a rise in anti-Asian incidents during the COVID-19 pandemic — and the experiences of less affluent communities.

Politics and representation

Asian Americans have served in Congress since the 1950s (beginning with Dalip Singh Saund and Hiram Fong) and in cabinet and judicial posts; Kamala Harris, of Indian descent through her mother, became Vice President in 2021. May is celebrated as Asian American and Pacific Islander Heritage Month.
