Atlantic Ocean

The Atlantic Ocean is the second-largest of the world's five oceanic divisions, covering approximately 85.1 million square kilometres (32.9 million sq mi), about one-fifth of Earth's surface. It separates the "Old World" of Africa, Europe, and Asia from the "New World" of the Americas. The Atlantic is bounded by the Americas to the west and Europe and Africa to the east, and is connected to the Arctic Ocean in the north and the Southern Ocean in the south. Its name derives from the Greek mythology of Atlas.

Geography and geology

The Atlantic has an elongated S-shape and is divided at the Equator into the North and South Atlantic. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge, a submarine mountain range running the length of the ocean, marks the boundary between diverging tectonic plates; seafloor spreading widens the ocean by roughly 2–3 cm per year. The Atlantic formed as the supercontinent Pangaea broke up beginning about 200 million years ago. Important marginal seas include the Caribbean Sea, the Mediterranean Sea, the North Sea, the Baltic Sea and Hudson Bay.

Currents and climate

Major currents include the warm Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift, which moderate the climate of western Europe; the cold Canary and Benguela currents along Africa; and the Brazil and Labrador currents in the west. The Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) transports heat northward and is a key element of global climate. The Sargasso Sea, bounded only by currents, is known for its floating sargassum seaweed.

History

The Atlantic became the central arena of the Age of Discovery: Christopher Columbus's 1492 voyage opened sustained transatlantic contact, followed by colonisation, the Columbian exchange, and the transatlantic slave trade, which forcibly transported an estimated 12 million Africans. In the 20th century the ocean was contested in both world wars' Battles of the Atlantic, bridged by Charles Lindbergh's 1927 solo flight, and crossed by the first transatlantic telegraph cable in 1866 (after a short-lived 1858 cable).

Economy and environment

The Atlantic supports major fisheries (though stocks such as cod have been depleted), offshore oil in the North Sea and Gulf of Mexico, and the busiest shipping routes in the world. Pollution, overfishing, and climate-driven changes in circulation and sea level are major concerns.
