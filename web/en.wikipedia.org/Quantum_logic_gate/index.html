Quantum logic gate

In quantum computing, a quantum logic gate is a basic quantum circuit operating on a small number of qubits. Unlike classical logic gates, quantum gates are unitary and therefore reversible; they are represented by unitary matrices acting on the qubits' state space.

Common gates

Single-qubit gates include the Pauli X, Y, and Z gates (analogous to rotations), the Hadamard gate, which creates superposition, and phase gates such as S and T. The most important two-qubit gate is the controlled-NOT (CNOT), which flips the target qubit when the control qubit is |1⟩. Together with single-qubit rotations, CNOT forms a universal gate set: any unitary operation can be approximated from them. Other gates include SWAP, Toffoli (CCNOT), and Fredkin (CSWAP).

Universality

Sets such as {Hadamard, phase, CNOT, π/8} are universal, and the Solovay–Kitaev theorem guarantees efficient approximation of arbitrary gates by such sets.

Physical implementation

In hardware, gates are realized by pulses — microwave pulses for superconducting qubits, laser pulses for trapped ions — and are characterized by their fidelity and duration relative to coherence times.
