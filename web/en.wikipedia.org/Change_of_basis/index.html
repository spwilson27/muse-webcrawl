Change of basis

In mathematics, an ordered basis of a vector space allows one to express every vector as a unique linear combination of basis vectors, and hence to represent vectors by their coordinates. The change of basis is the operation of converting the coordinates of a vector (or the matrix of a linear map or bilinear form) from one basis to another.

Change-of-basis matrix

If B and C are two bases of the same vector space, the change-of-basis matrix P from B to C has as its columns the coordinates of the old basis vectors expressed in the new basis. The coordinates of a vector transform by multiplying by the inverse of P, while the matrix of a linear operator transforms by a similarity transformation, A′ = P−1AP. Bilinear forms and quadratic forms transform by congruence, A′ = PTAP.

Applications

Changing basis is fundamental in diagonalisation, in computer graphics (where it expresses transformations between coordinate systems), and in physics, where tensor components transform according to change-of-basis rules under a change of coordinates.
