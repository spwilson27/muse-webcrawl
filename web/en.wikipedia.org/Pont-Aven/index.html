Pont-Aven is a commune in the Finistère department of Brittany in north-western France. It takes its name from the river Aven, which flows through the town to a small tidal port. Pont-Aven is famous above all as an artists' colony: in the 1880s it became the centre of the Pont-Aven School around Paul Gauguin and Émile Bernard, where the style known as Synthetism was developed.

History

The town grew around its bridge over the Aven and its many watermills — a local saying counted "14 mills, 15 houses". From the 1860s, American and European artists began summering in Pont-Aven, attracted by the low cost of living and Breton scenery. Gauguin first came in 1886 and returned repeatedly; his Vision of the Sermon (1888) was painted here. The Musée de Pont-Aven now documents the colony's history.

Geography and culture

The Aven descends through a chaos of boulders in the Bois d'Amour, a wooded riverside path popular with painters. The town is also known for its butter galettes (Traou Mad biscuits) and for the chapel of Trémalo, whose wooden crucifix inspired Gauguin's Yellow Christ.
