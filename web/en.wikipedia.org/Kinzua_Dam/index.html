Kinzua Dam

The Kinzua Dam and the Allegheny Reservoir behind it.

The Kinzua Dam is a large earth-fill and concrete gravity dam on the Allegheny River in Warren County, Pennsylvania, United States. Completed in 1965 by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, it created the Allegheny Reservoir for flood control, low-flow augmentation for Pittsburgh and the Ohio River, recreation and hydroelectric power. The dam is 1,897 feet (578 m) long and 179 feet (55 m) high.

Displacement of the Seneca Nation

Construction of the dam flooded roughly 10,000 acres of the Allegheny Reservation of the Seneca Nation of Indians in New York, including the Cornplanter Grant, and forced the relocation of about 600 Seneca people, the removal of homes, churches, schools and burial grounds. The Seneca argued that the project violated the Treaty of Canandaigua of 1794, in which the United States had pledged that their lands would remain theirs forever; federal courts allowed the project to proceed, and alternative dam sites proposed by engineer Arthur Morgan were rejected. The flooding became a lasting symbol of broken treaty promises, commemorated in Peter La Farge's song "As Long as the Grass Shall Grow", recorded by Johnny Cash on his 1964 album Bitter Tears.

Operations

The Seneca Pumped Storage Generating Station, operated in connection with the reservoir, uses the elevation difference between the Allegheny Reservoir and a smaller upper reservoir to generate peaking power. The reservoir — known on the New York side as Kinzua Lake — is a popular recreation area within the Allegheny National Forest.
