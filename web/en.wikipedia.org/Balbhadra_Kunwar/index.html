Balbhadra Kunwar

Balbhadra Kunwar (1789 – 1823) was a Nepalese military commander celebrated as a national hero of Nepal for his defense of the Nalapani fort during the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–1816).

Battle of Nalapani

In October–November 1814, Balbhadra held the small hill fort of Khalanga (Nalapani) near Dehradun with about 600 soldiers against a much larger British force. After a month-long siege, out of water and ammunition, he famously refused to surrender and withdrew fighting. The British erected a memorial honoring his bravery — a rare tribute to an enemy commander.

Later life and death

After the war, Balbhadra left Nepal and took service in the Sikh Empire's army in Lahore, where he was killed in 1823 fighting in Afghanistan.

Legacy

He remains one of the most revered figures in Nepali military history, commemorated in song, memorials, and school curricula.
