Flemish dialects

Flemish dialects are the traditional regional varieties of Dutch spoken in Flanders, the northern part of Belgium, and in French Flanders in northern France. They descend from Old and Middle Dutch dialects of the medieval County of Flanders and neighbouring territories. "Flemish" in everyday use can also refer to the standard Dutch spoken in Belgium (Belgian Dutch), but dialectologists use it more narrowly for the traditional local vernaculars.

Main groups

Four principal dialect groups are distinguished. West Flemish, spoken in West Flanders and French Flanders, is the most distinctive, preserving features such as the pronunciation of g as [h] and old diphthongs; it is sometimes treated as a separate language. East Flemish is spoken in East Flanders around Ghent and Aalst. Brabantian, centred on Antwerp, Mechelen and Brussels, has been the most influential group in the formation of Belgian standard Dutch; the Antwerp dialect is prominent in media and popular culture. Limburgish, spoken in Belgian Limburg and transitional toward the dialects of Dutch Limburg and the Rhineland, is tonal and is recognised as a regional language in the Netherlands.

Features and status

Flemish dialects differ from standard Dutch in pronunciation (for example the "soft g" of Brabantian and Limburgish versus the guttural g of the northern Netherlands), vocabulary influenced historically by French, and certain grammatical traits. Between the dialects and the standard language lies tussentaal ("in-between language"), an informal colloquial Belgian Dutch widely used in media and everyday speech.

Traditional local dialects have been in decline since the mid-20th century as standard Dutch, taught in schools and spread by broadcasting, displaced them in many domains; younger speakers often speak regional colloquial varieties rather than the markedly local dialects of their grandparents. Dialect societies, dictionaries such as the Woordenboek van de Vlaamse Dialecten, and popular culture (music, television series and social media in dialect) sustain interest in preserving them.
