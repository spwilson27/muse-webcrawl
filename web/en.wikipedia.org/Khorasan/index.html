Khorasan

Khorasan (also spelled Khurasan or Khorassan, from Middle Persian Khwarāsān, "where the sun arrives") is a historical geographical region in Western and Central Asia, corresponding roughly to northeastern Iran, western and northern Afghanistan, and southern parts of Central Asia including parts of Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan.

History

Under the Sasanian Empire, Khorasan was one of the empire's four frontier provinces. After the Muslim conquest of Persia in the 7th century, it became a major province of the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates, administered from cities such as Merv, Nishapur, Herat, and Balkh. The region was a centre of learning, trade, and Persian culture along the Silk Road.

The Tahirids, Saffarids, Samanids, and Ghaznavids all ruled from Khorasan at various times, and Nishapur became famed for its scholars, ceramic workshops, and madrasas. The Mongol invasions of the 13th century devastated many of its cities. In modern Iran, the historical province was divided in 2004 into three provinces: Razavi Khorasan, North Khorasan, and South Khorasan.

Geography

The region is bounded by the Kopet Dag mountains to the northwest, the Hindu Kush to the east, and the central Iranian desert to the south. Its name survives in the Iranian provinces and in the concept of "Greater Khorasan" used by historians.
