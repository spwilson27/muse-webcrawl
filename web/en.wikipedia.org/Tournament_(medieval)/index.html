Tournament (medieval)

A tournament, or tourney, was a series of chivalric contests held in medieval and Renaissance Europe, in which knights competed in martial sports — principally the melee, the joust, and displays of horsemanship — before an audience of nobles and spectators. Tournaments served as training for war, as theatre celebrating chivalric values, and as occasions for knights to win honor, ransoms, and prizes.

Origins

Tournaments emerged in France in the eleventh and twelfth centuries. The earliest events were melees: two teams of mounted knights fought across open countryside with ordinary weapons, capturing opponents for ransom. The church repeatedly condemned tournaments as dangerous and vain — participants killed in them could be denied Christian burial — and kings periodically banned them, though with little lasting effect.

Forms of contest

From the thirteenth century, the joust — a contest between two knights charging with couched lances — grew in prominence beside the melee. Over time, tournaments became more regulated and theatrical: blunted "arms of courtesy" replaced war weapons, barriers (the tilt) separated the jousting horses, heralds recorded the arms of competitors, and events acquired pageantry, allegorical themes, and prizes awarded by ladies of the court. Foot combats and specialized forms such as the pas d'armes flourished in the fifteenth century.

Decline

Tournaments lost their military function as warfare professionalized and gunpowder weapons spread. The accidental death of King Henry II of France in a joust in 1559 hastened their decline in France. Elaborate court spectacles and carrousels preserved elements of the tradition into the seventeenth century.
