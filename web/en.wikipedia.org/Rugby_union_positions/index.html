Rugby union positions

In rugby union, a team fields fifteen players, each wearing a number corresponding to a standardised position. The team divides into eight forwards (numbers 1–8), who contest set pieces such as scrums and lineouts and do much of the close-quarters ball carrying, and seven backs (numbers 9–15), who generally operate in more space and handle distribution, kicking and finishing.

Forwards

The front row consists of the loosehead prop (1), hooker (2) and tighthead prop (3): in the scrum the props support the hooker, who strikes for the ball and also throws in at lineouts. Behind them, the two locks or second rows (4 and 5) provide scrum power and are the principal lineout jumpers. The back row comprises the blindside flanker (6), openside flanker (7) and number eight (8): flankers link forwards and backs, contest breakdowns and tackle heavily, while the number eight controls the ball at the base of the scrum and carries powerfully.

Backs

The scrum-half (9) feeds the scrum and distributes the ball from rucks and mauls; the fly-half (10) is the chief decision-maker, directing attack and kicking tactically. Centres — inside centre (12) and outside centre (13) — combine running, passing and defensive organisation in midfield. The wings (11 and 14) are usually the fastest players, finishing attacking moves in the wide channels, and the fullback (15) fields kicks, provides defensive cover and counter-attacks from deep. Substitutes (numbers 16–23) cover each position group, with specialist front-row replacements mandatory for safety.
