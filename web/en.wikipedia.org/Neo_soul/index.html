Neo soul

Neo soul is a genre of popular music. The term was coined by Motown executive Kedar Massenburg in the 1990s to market a style of soul and contemporary R&B that blended the earthy sound of 1970s soul with jazz, funk, hip hop, and African influences, in deliberate contrast to the slick, producer-driven R&B mainstream.

Characteristics and artists

Neo soul recordings favour live instrumentation, vintage keyboards, and introspective songwriting. D'Angelo's Brown Sugar (1995) and Erykah Badu's Baduizm (1997) defined the genre's breakthrough; other leading artists include Lauryn Hill, Maxwell, Jill Scott, India.Arie, Angie Stone, Musiq Soulchild, Raphael Saadiq, and the Soulquarians collective. The movement's critical peak is often placed around 2000–2002, after which its commercial momentum faded, though its influence permeates later R&B and artists such as Frank Ocean and SZA.

Reception

Many artists resisted the label as a marketing category; later commentators have debated whether "neo soul" names a coherent genre or a moment. Massenburg himself later suggested the term had run its course.
