Archie Mason Griffin (born August 21, 1954) is an American former football running back who played for the Ohio State Buckeyes in college and the Cincinnati Bengals in the National Football League. He is the only player to win the Heisman Trophy twice, receiving the award in 1974 and 1975.

College career

Playing under coach Woody Hayes at Ohio State, Griffin rushed for 5,589 yards over four seasons, an NCAA record at the time, and ran for 100 or more yards in 31 consecutive regular-season games. He started in four Rose Bowls, a distinction shared by only a few players, and finished his college career with 924 carries, 26 touchdowns and a 6.0-yard average per carry. Ohio State retired his number 45 in 1999.

Professional career and later life

Griffin was a first-round draft pick of the Cincinnati Bengals in 1976 and played seven seasons with the team, including Super Bowl XVI, accumulating 2,808 rushing yards as a professional before injuries shortened his career. After football he worked in administration at Ohio State, serving as assistant athletic director and as president and CEO of the university's alumni association. He was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 1986.
