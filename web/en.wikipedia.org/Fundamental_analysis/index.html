Fundamental analysis

Fundamental analysis, in accounting and finance, is the analysis of a business's financial statements, health, competitors, and markets, used to estimate the intrinsic value of a security. It contrasts with technical analysis, which studies price patterns. Analysts examine revenue, earnings, growth rates, return on equity, debt, and management quality, alongside macroeconomic and industry conditions.

Approach

Analysis may be "top-down" — beginning with the global economy and narrowing to a company — or "bottom-up", starting from a specific firm's prospects. Valuation techniques include discounted cash flow models and multiples such as the price-earnings ratio.

Criticism

Proponents of the efficient-market hypothesis argue that publicly available fundamental information is already reflected in prices, limiting excess returns. Value investors such as Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffett have famously used fundamental analysis to identify undervalued stocks over long horizons.
