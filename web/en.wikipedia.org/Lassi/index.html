Lassi

Lassi (Punjabi: ਲੱਸੀ; Urdu: لسی; Hindi: लस्सी) is a traditional yogurt-based drink from the Punjab region of the Indian subcontinent. It is made by blending yogurt (curd/dahi) with water, and may be flavoured with spices, salt, or sugar and fruit. Lassi is a popular refreshment across India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Nepal, often consumed during or after a meal.

Varieties

Salted lassi (namkeen lassi) is seasoned with salt and roasted cumin, and is similar to doogh or chaas. Sweet lassi is sweetened with sugar and may be flavoured with rosewater, saffron, or cardamom; in Punjab it is traditionally served with a thick layer of malai (clotted cream) on top. Mango lassi, made with mango pulp, is especially popular in restaurants worldwide. Regional variants include the makhaniya lassi of Jodhpur and the sweet lassis of Varanasi.

Preparation and culture

Traditionally lassi was churned in a large earthen pot with a wooden whisk (madhani), producing a frothy drink with a topping of butter. Sweet Punjabi lassi is famously served in tall glasses or earthen kulhars. The drink has ancient roots in the dairy culture of the Indus valley region and is associated with cooling the body in hot weather and aiding digestion after spicy food. A related preparation, bhang lassi, incorporates cannabis and is consumed during festivals such as Holi in some regions.
