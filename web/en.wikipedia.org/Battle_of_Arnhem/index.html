Battle of Arnhem

Battle of Arnhem
Date: 17–26 September 1944
Location: Arnhem, Netherlands
Combatants: United Kingdom, Poland vs Germany
Result: German victory

The Battle of Arnhem was a major battle of the Second World War fought in and around the Dutch city of Arnhem from 17 to 26 September 1944, as the airborne component of Operation Market Garden. The British 1st Airborne Division, with the Polish 1st Independent Parachute Brigade, was dropped to seize bridges over the Lower Rhine at Arnhem — the deepest objective of the operation — while ground forces advanced from Belgium.

Men of the 1st Airborne Division in Oosterbeek, September 1944.

Course of the battle

The plan went wrong from the start: drops were made far from the bridge, radios failed, and two SS Panzer divisions (II SS Panzer Corps) were refitting near Arnhem, unspotted by Allied intelligence. Only Lieutenant Colonel John Frost's 2nd Battalion reached and held the north end of the road bridge — the episode immortalised as "a bridge too far". The rest of the division was compressed into a shrinking perimeter at Oosterbeek, while XXX Corps' ground advance was delayed at Nijmegen. After nine days, the remnants — about 2,000 of more than 10,000 men — were evacuated across the Rhine on the night of 25–26 September (Operation Berlin). The rest were killed or captured. Arnhem remained in German hands until April 1945, and its Dutch population was forcibly evacuated. The battle is commemorated by the Airborne Museum at Oosterbeek and the John Frost Bridge.
