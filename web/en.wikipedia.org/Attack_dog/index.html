Attack dog

An attack dog is a dog trained by a human to attack a target on command, on sight, or by inference. Attack dogs have been used in warfare, policing and private protection for centuries, and are distinct from guard dogs, which primarily deter and alert.

History

Dogs were used in battle by ancient armies including the Greeks, Romans and Persians, sometimes fitted with armour or spiked collars. In the modern era, military and police forces formalised training during the First and Second World Wars.

Training

Training typically builds on prey drive and controlled aggression, teaching the dog to bite and hold a padded sleeve or suit and to release on command. Breeds commonly used include the German Shepherd, Belgian Malinois, Doberman Pinscher and Rottweiler.

Legal status

Many jurisdictions regulate or prohibit the private training and keeping of attack dogs, and owners may face civil and criminal liability for injuries inflicted by them.
