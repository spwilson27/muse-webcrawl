Kitos War

The Kitos War (115–117 AD), also known as the Diaspora Revolt or the Second Jewish–Roman War, was a series of Jewish uprisings against the Roman Empire in Cyrenaica, Egypt, Cyprus, and Mesopotamia during the reign of the emperor Trajan. It takes its name from Lusius Quietus — "Kitos" in Jewish sources — the Moorish general who suppressed the revolt in Mesopotamia and was then appointed governor of Judaea.

Course of the revolt

While Trajan campaigned against Parthia in the east, Jewish communities rose in revolt in 115. In Cyrenaica under a leader named Lukuas (Andreas), rebels destroyed Greek and Roman temples and infrastructure; violence spread to Alexandria and Egypt, where fighting between Jewish and Greek inhabitants devastated the city, and to Cyprus, where the revolt led to the destruction of Salamis and a subsequent Roman ban on Jews living on the island. In newly conquered Mesopotamia, Jewish resistance complicated Trajan's campaign.

Suppression and consequences

Trajan dispatched Marcius Turbo to Egypt and Cyrenaica and Lusius Quietus to Mesopotamia; both suppressed the revolts with extreme severity by 117. The Jewish communities of Egypt and Cyrenaica — among the oldest and largest in the diaspora — were effectively annihilated, and Alexandria's great Jewish population never recovered. The revolt's memory is preserved in rabbinic references to the "war of Quietus" and in papyri and writers such as Cassius Dio and Eusebius. The devastation set the stage for the final, catastrophic Bar Kokhba revolt in Judaea a generation later (132–136 AD).
