Massively multiplayer online role-playing game

A massively multiplayer online role-playing game (MMORPG) is a video game genre combining role-playing games with massively multiplayer online games, in which a very large number of players interact with one another within a persistent virtual world. Players assume the roles of characters, often in a fantasy or science-fiction setting, and control their actions through quests, combat, crafting and social play.

Characteristics

Common features include a persistent world that continues while the player is offline, character progression through experience points and equipment, player-versus-environment content such as dungeons and raids, player-versus-player combat, guilds or clans, in-game economies and auction houses, and regular content updates or expansions. Most MMORPGs historically used monthly subscriptions; many later adopted free-to-play models with in-game purchases.

History

The genre descends from text-based multi-user dungeons (MUDs) of the late 1970s and 1980s. Graphical pioneers included Neverwinter Nights (1991), Meridian 59 (1996), Ultima Online (1997) and EverQuest (1999). World of Warcraft (2004) brought the genre to mass popularity, peaking at over 12 million subscribers in 2010. Other major titles include Final Fantasy XIV, EVE Online, Guild Wars 2, RuneScape, Elder Scrolls Online and Black Desert Online; in Asia, titles such as Lineage were hugely influential.

Culture

MMORPGs support large social communities, virtual economies studied by economists, and phenomena such as gold farming and the 2005 "Corrupted Blood" incident in World of Warcraft, later studied by epidemiologists as a model of disease spread.
