Battle of Jarama

Battle of Jarama
Date: 6–27 February 1937
Location: Jarama valley, near Madrid, Spain
Result: Stalemate (Republican strategic success)
Part of the Spanish Civil War

The Battle of Jarama was fought from 6 to 27 February 1937 southeast of Madrid during the Spanish Civil War. General Francisco Franco's Nationalist forces attempted to cross the Jarama River and cut the Madrid–Valencia road, the capital's lifeline, but were stopped by the Spanish Republican Army, reinforced by the International Brigades.

The battle

Nationalist troops under General José Enrique Varela crossed the Jarama in the opening days and gained a bridgehead on the heights beyond, but Republican counterattacks, directed by General José Miaja and stiffened by Soviet tanks and aircraft, contained the advance. The XV International Brigade — including the British, American (Abraham Lincoln), and Franco-Belgian battalions — suffered very heavy casualties holding positions such as "Suicide Hill". The American battalion lost the bulk of its strength in a disastrous attack on 23 February. By late February both sides were dug in and exhausted.

Aftermath

The Nationalists gained some ground but failed to cut the Valencia road or encircle Madrid, and the front line at Jarama barely moved for the rest of the war. The battle is remembered in the folk song "Jarama Valley" and for the early, costly role of the International Brigades in the defence of Madrid.
