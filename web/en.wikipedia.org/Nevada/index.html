Nevada

Nevada is a state in the Western United States, bordered by Oregon to the northwest, Idaho to the northeast, California to the west, Arizona to the southeast and Utah to the east. It is the seventh-largest state by area (286,380 km²) but one of the most sparsely populated, with about 3.2 million residents — roughly three-quarters of whom live in Clark County, home of the Las Vegas metropolitan area. The capital is Carson City. Nevada's name comes from the Spanish nevada, meaning "snow-covered", a reference to the Sierra Nevada range.

Most of Nevada lies within the Great Basin, a high desert of alternating mountain ranges and valleys; it is the driest state in the US. The state is known for legal gambling, liberal marriage and divorce laws, legal prostitution in some rural counties, and mining — Nevada produces most of the gold mined in the United States. The federal government owns about 80 percent of the state's land, including the Nevada National Security Site and the secretive Area 51 facility.

History

The territory was part of New Spain and then Mexico before being ceded to the US after the Mexican–American War (1848). The discovery of the Comstock Lode silver deposit in 1859 drove a mining boom and population rush; Nevada became the 36th state on 31 October 1864, during the Civil War, earning the nickname "Battle Born". The 20th century brought the Hoover Dam (1935), the legalisation of gambling (1931) and the rise of Las Vegas as an entertainment capital.

Economy

Tourism and gaming dominate the economy of southern Nevada; Las Vegas attracts over 40 million visitors a year. Mining (gold, silver, lithium), ranching, and increasingly logistics, technology and renewable energy are also important. Reno, the state's second urban centre, has grown as a distribution and tech hub. Nevada has no state income tax, funding government substantially through gaming and sales taxes.
