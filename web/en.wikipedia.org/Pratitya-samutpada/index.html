Pratītyasamutpāda (Sanskrit: प्रतीत्यसमुत्पाद), commonly translated as dependent origination or dependent arising, is a key doctrine in Buddhism shared by all schools. It states that all dharmas (phenomena) arise in dependence upon other phenomena: "if this exists, that exists; if this ceases to exist, that also ceases to exist". The basic principle is that all things — including the self, objects, and mental states — arise and pass away in a mutually interdependent web of cause and effect, and therefore have no independent, permanent essence.

The formula

The Buddha's summary of the doctrine is expressed in the formula: "When this is, that is. From the arising of this comes the arising of that. When this isn't, that isn't. From the cessation of this comes the cessation of that." (imasmiṃ sati idaṃ hoti). In the early texts the Buddha is said to have discovered this principle on the night of his awakening, and to have regarded it as profound and difficult to see, "deep in its meaning and appearance".

The twelve nidanas

The most famous application of the doctrine is the chain of twelve nidānas (links) explaining how suffering (dukkha) and rebirth (samsara) perpetuate themselves:

  1. Avidyā — ignorance, especially of the Four Noble Truths
  2. Saṃskāra — volitional formations
  3. Vijñāna — consciousness
  4. Nāmarūpa — name and form (mind and body)
  5. Ṣaḍāyatana — the six sense bases
  6. Sparśa — contact
  7. Vedanā — feeling
  8. Tṛṣṇā — craving
  9. Upādāna — clinging
  10. Bhava — becoming
  11. Jāti — birth
  12. Jarāmaraṇa — ageing and death

By reversing the chain — removing ignorance — the whole cycle of suffering can be brought to an end, yielding liberation (nirvana).

Interpretations

Theravada commentators such as Buddhaghosa explained the twelve links across three lifetimes: ignorance and formations belong to a past life; consciousness through becoming span the present life; birth and ageing-and-death fall in a future life. Mahayana philosophers, above all Nagarjuna (c. 2nd century CE), identified dependent origination with emptiness (śūnyatā): since everything arises dependently, nothing has intrinsic existence (svabhāva). In East Asian Buddhism, Huayan developed the image of Indra's net — an infinite net of jewels each reflecting all the others — to express total interpenetration.
