State Council of China

State Council of the People's Republic of China
Formation27 September 1954
TypeExecutive organ of state power
HeadquartersZhongnanhai, Beijing
LeaderPremier of China

The State Council of the People's Republic of China, also known as the Central People's Government, is the chief administrative authority of China. It is chaired by the premier and includes the vice premiers, state councillors, ministers of ministries and commissions, the auditor-general, and the secretary-general.

Constitutional role

Under the Constitution of China, the State Council is the executive organ of the highest organ of state power, the National People's Congress (NPC), and is responsible and accountable to the NPC and its Standing Committee.

Organisation

The State Council consists of a plenary session and an executive meeting. Beneath it sit constituent departments including the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Ministry of National Defense, the National Development and Reform Commission, and the People's Bank of China. Major reorganisations occurred in 1998, 2018, and 2023, consolidating ministries and creating bodies such as the Ministry of Ecology and Environment.

Functions

The State Council adopts administrative measures, enacts administrative regulations, issues decisions and orders, drafts laws for the NPC, prepares the national economic plan and state budget, and directs the work of local governments at all levels.

Leadership

The premier is nominated by the president and confirmed by the NPC. Recent premiers include Zhou Enlai, Zhu Rongji, Wen Jiabao, and Li Keqiang.
