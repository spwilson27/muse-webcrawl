Aly Bain

Aly Bain MBE
Born: 15 May 1946, Lerwick, Shetland, Scotland
Genres: Scottish traditional, folk
Instrument: Fiddle

Aly Bain MBE (born 15 May 1946) is a Scottish fiddler widely acclaimed as one of the finest exponents of the Shetland fiddle tradition. Born in Lerwick, Shetland, he began playing at the age of eleven and was tutored by the influential Tom Anderson, who gathered him into the Shetland Fiddlers' Society.

Career

Bain co-founded the folk group The Boys of the Lough in the late 1960s, touring internationally and recording many albums of Scottish, Irish and Northumbrian traditional music. He is perhaps best known to television audiences for the BBC series Transatlantic Sessions, in which he served as musical director alongside American dobro player Jerry Douglas, bringing together folk and country musicians from both sides of the Atlantic. He also presented the documentary series Down Home and Aly Bain's Legends of Scottish Music. A long-standing musical partnership with accordionist Phil Cunningham produced numerous duo albums and tours.

Honours

Bain has received several honorary doctorates from Scottish universities and was appointed MBE for services to traditional music. He is credited with bringing Shetland fiddling to a worldwide audience.
