Commissioner for oaths
Occupation: legal official
Functions: administering oaths, taking affidavits and statutory declarations

A commissioner for oaths is a person appointed by a lord chancellor or equivalent authority to administer oaths and take affidavits, affirmations and statutory declarations. The office exists in England and Wales, Ireland, and many Commonwealth jurisdictions.

Functions

Commissioners witness the swearing of affidavits used in court proceedings, administer oaths of office, and take statutory declarations. In England and Wales, every solicitor holding a practising certificate is automatically a commissioner for oaths, though they may not act in proceedings in which they are interested.

Fees

Statutory fees are prescribed for administering an oath and marking exhibits; in England and Wales the standard fee has long been £5 per affidavit with £2 per exhibit.

Related offices

The commissioner for oaths is distinct from a notary public, whose acts carry wider international recognition, and from commissioners for taking affidavits appointed for particular courts.
