Processed food

Processed food is food that has been altered in some way during preparation. Broadly, food processing includes methods as basic as washing, chopping, pasteurising, freezing, fermenting, and packaging, as well as the addition of ingredients such as preservatives, flavours, and nutrients. Convenience food and ultra-processed products are categories of heavily processed foods.

History

Food processing is ancient: drying, salting, smoking, and fermentation preserved food for millennia. Industrial processing expanded in the 19th century with canning (developed by Nicolas Appert for Napoleon's armies), pasteurisation, and milling of white flour, and again after World War II with frozen meals, instant foods, and shelf-stable snacks designed for convenience.

Classification

The NOVA classification, developed by researchers at the University of São Paulo, groups foods into: (1) unprocessed or minimally processed foods; (2) processed culinary ingredients such as oils and sugar; (3) processed foods such as canned vegetables and cheese; and (4) ultra-processed foods — industrial formulations of extracts and additives, such as soft drinks, packaged snacks, and reconstituted meats.

Health debate

Processing itself is not inherently harmful (pasteurisation and fortification improve safety and nutrition), but high consumption of ultra-processed foods — typically energy-dense and high in added sugar, sodium, and unhealthy fats — is associated in epidemiological studies with obesity, cardiovascular disease, and type 2 diabetes. Public-health guidance in several countries now advises limiting ultra-processed products.
