Bosnian War

The Bosnian War was an international armed conflict that took place in Bosnia and Herzegovina from 1992 to 1995, following the breakup of Yugoslavia. It was fought principally between the forces of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina, the self-proclaimed Bosnian Serb Republika Srpska, and the self-proclaimed Croat Herzeg-Bosnia.

Background and course

After Bosnia and Herzegovina declared independence in 1992, Bosnian Serb forces, supported by the Yugoslav People's Army, sought to partition the country along ethnic lines. The war was marked by ethnic cleansing, the siege of Sarajevo — the longest siege of a capital city in modern warfare — and the Srebrenica massacre of July 1995, in which more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys were killed, an act later judged genocide by international courts. NATO air strikes in 1995 helped bring the parties to negotiations.

Outcome

The war ended with the Dayton Agreement in December 1995, which preserved Bosnia and Herzegovina as a single state composed of two entities, the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and Republika Srpska. Around 100,000 people were killed and more than two million displaced. The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia prosecuted numerous war crimes arising from the conflict.
