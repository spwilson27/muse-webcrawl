Ramanuja (c. 1017 – 1137), also known as Ramanujacharya, was an Indian Hindu philosopher, guru and social reformer. He is one of the most important exponents of the Sri Vaishnava tradition within Hinduism, and the foremost proponent of Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism), a sub-school of Vedanta.

Life

Ramanuja was born in the village of Sriperumbudur, near modern Chennai, into a Tamil Brahmin family. He studied under the Advaita scholar Yadavaprakasha at Kanchipuram but came to disagree with his teacher's interpretation of the Upanishads. After a pilgrimage during which he became the disciple of Yamunacharya's lineage, he succeeded as head of the temple community at Srirangam, the principal centre of Sri Vaishnavism. Tradition holds that he lived to the age of 120.

Philosophy

In his principal work, the Sri Bhashya, a commentary on the Brahma Sutras, Ramanuja argued against Shankara's pure monism (Advaita). He taught that the individual soul (chit) and matter (achit) are real and eternally distinct, yet exist as the "body" of God (Vishnu/Narayana), who is their inner self and controller. Liberation is attained through devotion (bhakti) and self-surrender (prapatti) to God's grace.

Social influence

Ramanuja reformed temple administration and worship at Srirangam and Melukote, is credited with opening aspects of temple ritual and teaching beyond a single caste, and organised the Sri Vaishnava community into an enduring institutional structure. His influence extended across South India and later inspired devotional movements throughout the subcontinent.
