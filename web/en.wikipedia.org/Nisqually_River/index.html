The Nisqually River is a river in west-central Washington state in the United States, approximately 130 kilometres (81 mi) long. It originates on the Nisqually Glacier on the southern slopes of Mount Rainier and flows generally northwest, past the towns of Ashford, Elbe, Eatonville, McKenna and Yelm, before emptying into the Nisqually Reach of Puget Sound near DuPont. The river forms part of the boundary between Pierce and Thurston counties and passes through the Nisqually Indian Reservation and Joint Base Lewis–McChord.

History

The river is the traditional home of the Nisqually people, for whom salmon fishing was central; the fishing rights struggle of the 1960s and 1970s, associated with activist Billy Frank Jr., culminated in the 1974 Boldt Decision affirming treaty fishing rights. The 1854 Treaty of Medicine Creek was signed near the river's mouth. The 2001 Nisqually earthquake, one of the largest in Washington's recorded history, took its name from the area, though its epicentre was near the delta.

Dams and habitat

The Nisqually River Project, operated by Tacoma Power, includes the Alder and LaGrande dams, completed in the 1940s, which generate hydroelectricity and created Alder Lake. The estuary at the river's mouth was significantly restored in 2009 when dikes were removed at the Billy Frank Jr. Nisqually National Wildlife Refuge, re-establishing hundreds of acres of tidal marsh important for salmon and migratory birds.
