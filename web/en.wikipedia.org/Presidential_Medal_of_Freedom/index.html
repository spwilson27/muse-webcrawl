Presidential Medal of Freedom

Presidential Medal of Freedom
Awarded by: President of the United States
Established: 1963
Precedence: Alongside the Congressional Gold Medal, the highest civilian award of the United States

The Presidential Medal of Freedom is the highest civilian award of the United States, alongside the Congressional Gold Medal. It was established in 1963 by President John F. Kennedy, superseding the Medal of Freedom established by President Harry S. Truman in 1945 to honour civilian service during World War II.

Description

The medal is awarded by the President of the United States "for especially meritorious contribution to the security or national interests of the United States, world peace, cultural or other significant public or private endeavors." The award is not limited to United States citizens and, while a civilian award, can also be awarded to military personnel, who wear it in uniform.

History

The medal's design features a white star on a blue enamel pentagon, surrounded by gold eagles, suspended from a ribbon. Recipients are typically announced around the Fourth of July or near the end of a president's term. There is also a grade of the award "with distinction", worn with a sash and star on rare occasions.

Recipients

Recipients have included presidents, civil rights leaders, artists, athletes, scientists and philanthropists — among them Martin Luther King Jr. (posthumously), Mother Teresa, Jonas Salk, Maya Angelou, Muhammad Ali, Aretha Franklin, Simone Biles and Denzel Washington. A handful of individuals have received the medal twice, including Colin Powell and Ellsworth Bunker.
