Pulp (paper)

Pulp is a fibrous lignocellulosic material prepared by chemically or mechanically separating cellulose fibres from wood, fibre crops, waste paper or rags. Mixed with water and other additives, pulp is the basic raw material used in the production of paper and paperboard, and of some textiles and chemical products.

Types

Mechanical pulp is produced by grinding or refining wood, retaining most of its lignin; it is used for newsprint and is prone to yellowing. Chemical pulp, mainly from the kraft process, dissolves lignin away, yielding stronger, brighter fibre for printing and packaging papers. Semi-chemical and recycled (deinked) pulps fill other niches.

History

Papermaking from plant fibre pulp began in China by the 2nd century AD; wood pulp processes were developed in the 19th century, with groundwood pulping patented in the 1840s and the kraft process invented by Carl F. Dahl in 1879.
