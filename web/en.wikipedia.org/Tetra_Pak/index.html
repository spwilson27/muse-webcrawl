Tetra Pak is a Swedish-Swiss multinational food packaging and processing company, a subsidiary of the Tetra Laval group. It is best known for inventing the aseptic carton packaging that bears its name — most famously the tetrahedral "Tetra Classic" pack and the brick-shaped "Tetra Brik" — used worldwide for milk, juice, and other liquid foods.

History

The company traces its origins to Åkerlund & Rausing, a packaging firm founded in Lund, Sweden in 1929 by Ruben Rausing and Erik Åkerlund. In 1951 Tetra Pak was founded as a subsidiary to develop a new kind of packaging for liquid foods. Its first product, launched in 1952, was a tetrahedron-shaped carton formed from a continuous tube of plastic-coated paperboard, filled and sealed below the liquid level. The design radically reduced material use compared with glass bottles.

The Tetra Brik, introduced in 1963, became the company's most successful package. Combined with ultra-high-temperature (UHT) treatment, Tetra Pak's aseptic technology allowed milk and juice to be stored for months without refrigeration, transforming food distribution in much of the world.

Products and operations

Tetra Pak supplies packaging material, filling machines, and complete processing lines for dairy products, juices, soups, and prepared foods. Well over a hundred billion Tetra Pak packages are sold each year. The company's packaging material is a laminate of paperboard, polyethylene, and a thin layer of aluminium foil, which together provide strength, a liquid barrier, and protection against oxygen and light.

Environmental issues

The multilayer construction that makes the cartons effective also complicates recycling, since the paper, plastic, and foil layers must be separated. Tetra Pak has promoted carton recycling schemes and investment in recycling capacity, and critics have pressed the company over plastic caps, straws, and the fate of cartons in countries with limited waste infrastructure.
