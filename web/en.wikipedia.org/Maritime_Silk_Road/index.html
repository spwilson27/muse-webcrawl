Maritime Silk Road

The Maritime Silk Road or Maritime Silk Route is the maritime section of the historic Silk Road that connected Southeast Asia to East Asia, the Indian subcontinent, the Arabian peninsula, eastern Africa, and Europe.

It began by the 2nd century BCE and flourished later on, carrying goods such as spices, ceramics, glassware, and textiles between port cities. Chinese junks and Arab dhows plied routes across the South China Sea and Indian Ocean, aided by monsoon winds.

In the 21st century the name has been reused in China's Belt and Road Initiative as the 21st Century Maritime Silk Road.
