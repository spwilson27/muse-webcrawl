The Beijing National Stadium (Chinese: 北京国家体育场), officially the National Stadium and commonly known as the Bird's Nest (鸟巢), is a stadium at the Olympic Green in Chaoyang, Beijing, China. Designed for the 2008 Summer Olympics and Paralympics, it also hosted the opening and closing ceremonies of the 2022 Winter Olympics, making Beijing the first city to stage both Summer and Winter Games and the stadium the first to host both opening ceremonies.

Design

The stadium was designed by the Swiss firm Herzog & de Meuron in collaboration with Arup and the China Architecture Design & Research Group, with the Chinese artist Ai Weiwei as artistic consultant. Its form derives from a "nest" of apparently chaotic steel members: some 42,000 tonnes of unwrapped steel form a lattice shell around a concrete bowl, the structure itself being the architecture, with no applied facade. The roof's saddle-shaped ellipse measures roughly 330 by 220 metres, and the building stands about 69 metres tall. Beneath the stadium a geothermal system of underground pipes helps heat and cool the interior.

Construction

Ground was broken on 24 December 2003. Construction was suspended for several months in 2004–05 while the design was revised — including removal of a planned retractable roof — partly in response to the collapse of a roof at Paris's Charles de Gaulle Airport and partly to cut steel costs. The stadium was completed in 2008 at a reported cost of ¥2.3 billion (about US$428 million).

Notable events

At the 2008 Games the stadium hosted athletics and the football final, and witnessed Usain Bolt's world records in the 100 m and 200 m sprints. The opening ceremony, directed by Zhang Yimou and watched by an estimated global television audience in the billions, is widely regarded as one of the most spectacular in Olympic history. The stadium also hosted the 2015 World Championships in Athletics, and in winter it has hosted snow sports events and a seasonal "Bird's Nest Happy Ice and Snow Season" festival. It is a major tourist attraction in its own right, drawing large numbers of visitors daily.
