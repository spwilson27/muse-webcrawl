Gimbal

A gimbal is a pivoted support that permits rotation of an object about an axis. A set of three gimbals, mounted one inside another with orthogonal pivot axes, can be used to allow an object mounted on the innermost gimbal to remain independent of the rotation of its support — the principle used for ship's compasses, gyrocompasses, and inertial navigation platforms.

History

Gimbal-like devices were described in ancient China and by Philo of Byzantium; the Cardan suspension, named after Gerolamo Cardano (who described it in the 16th century), kept ship's lamps and compasses level at sea. Gimbals later became essential to rocketry and spaceflight, where rocket engines are gimballed — swivelled by actuators — to provide thrust vector control.

Applications

Besides compasses, gyroscopes and inertial measurement units, gimbals are used in camera stabilisation (including modern motorised three-axis camera gimbals for phones and drones), chronometers, and the mounting of rocket engines. A set of gimbals can suffer gimbal lock, in which two axes align and one degree of rotational freedom is lost — a phenomenon that nearly caused problems on early space missions and also arises in the mathematics of Euler angles.
