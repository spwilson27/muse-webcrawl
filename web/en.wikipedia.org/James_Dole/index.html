James Dole

James Drummond Dole (27 September 1877 – 20 May 1958) was an American industrialist known as the "Pineapple King," who founded the Hawaiian Pineapple Company, which became the Dole Food Company, and built the pineapple industry in Hawaii.

Early life and Hawaii

Dole was born in Jamaica Plain, Massachusetts, and graduated from Harvard University in 1899. Inspired by his cousin Sanford B. Dole, the former president of the Republic of Hawaii, he moved to Honolulu with a small amount of savings and bought 61 acres in Wahiawa, Oahu, to experiment with pineapple cultivation.

The pineapple empire

The Hawaiian Pineapple Company was incorporated in 1901. Dole's innovations — efficient canneries, mechanised growing and packing, and aggressive national advertising, partly in partnership with the invention of a high-speed peeling and coring machine by Henry Ginaca — made canned pineapple a mass-market product. In 1922 he bought most of the island of Lanai and developed it into the largest pineapple plantation in the world.

Financial strain during the Great Depression cost Dole control of the company in 1932; Castle & Cooke took over management, though Dole remained a director until 1948. The company was renamed Dole Food Company in 1991.

Dole also helped pioneer commercial air service to Hawaii by sponsoring the 1927 Dole Air Race from California to Honolulu, which ended tragically with several aircraft lost. Dole Middle School in Honolulu is named for him.
