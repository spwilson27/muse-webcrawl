Boyle's law

Boyle's law, also called the Boyle–Mariotte law, is an experimental gas law stating that the pressure of a given amount of gas held at constant temperature is inversely proportional to its volume. In symbols, P ∝ 1/V, or PV = k, where P is pressure, V is volume, and k is a constant for a fixed mass of gas at fixed temperature.

The law is named after Robert Boyle, who published it in 1662 based on experiments with trapped air using a J-shaped tube of mercury; Edme Mariotte independently stated it in 1676. Richard Towneley and Henry Power had earlier noticed the relationship.

Boyle's law, combined with Charles's law and Gay-Lussac's law, yields the combined gas law and is a special case of the ideal gas law. It explains everyday phenomena such as the behaviour of a syringe, breathing, and the expansion of gas bubbles as a diver ascends.
