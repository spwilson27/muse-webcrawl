Léon Foucault

Born: 18 September 1819, Paris, France
Died: 11 February 1868 (aged 48), Paris
Known for: Foucault pendulum, speed of light measurements, gyroscope

Jean Bernard Léon Foucault (18 September 1819 – 11 February 1868) was a French physicist best known for his demonstration of the rotation of the Earth with the device now known as the Foucault pendulum.

Foucault pendulum

In 1851 Foucault suspended a 28-kilogram brass-coated lead bob on a 67-metre wire from the dome of the Panthéon in Paris. The plane of the pendulum's swing appeared to rotate, providing the first direct, simple evidence that the Earth rotates on its axis.

Other work

Working with Armand Fizeau, Foucault measured the speed of light and showed it travels more slowly in water than in air, supporting the wave theory of light. He also invented the gyroscope, discovered eddy currents (Foucault currents), and improved telescope mirrors and photographic techniques.
