Turkmenistan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Turkmenistan is a landlocked country in Central Asia bordered by Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Afghanistan, Iran and the Caspian Sea. Its capital and largest city is Ashgabat. With a population of roughly 6–7 million spread over about 488,000 km², it is one of the world's most sparsely populated countries; some 80% of its territory is covered by the Karakum Desert.

History

The region was the heartland of ancient Parthia and the oasis city of Merv on the Silk Road. Conquered by Russia in the 19th century, it became the Turkmen SSR within the Soviet Union and gained independence in 1991. Saparmurat Niyazov, "Turkmenbashi the Great", ruled with an elaborate personality cult until his death in 2006; he was succeeded by Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow, and in 2022 by the latter's son, Serdar Berdimuhamedow, in a dynastic transfer that kept the family in power. Turkmenistan follows a policy of "permanent neutrality" recognised by the United Nations.

Economy

Turkmenistan holds some of the world's largest natural gas reserves — the Galkynysh field is among the biggest on Earth — and gas exports, principally to China, dominate the economy, alongside oil, cotton and textiles. The Darvaza gas crater, a burning crater in the Karakum Desert, is an unofficial tourist attraction.

Culture

Turkmen culture is famed for its carpets, the Akhal-Teke horse, and traditional dress and music. The official language is Turkmen, a Turkic language written in a Latin-based alphabet since the 1990s.
