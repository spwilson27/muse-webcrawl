Prime Minister of Japan

The Prime Minister of Japan is the head of government of Japan. The prime minister chairs the Cabinet and is vested with executive power under the post-war Constitution of Japan. The prime minister is designated from among the members of the National Diet and formally appointed by the Emperor; if the two houses of the Diet disagree, the choice of the House of Representatives prevails.

History

The office was created in 1885 during the Meiji period, replacing the older Daijō-kan system. Itō Hirobumi, a principal author of the Meiji Constitution, was the first prime minister. Under the pre-war system the prime minister depended on the Emperor's appointment, and military ministers could bring down cabinets; the post-war constitution made the office responsible to the elected Diet.

Post-war Japan has been governed mostly by the Liberal Democratic Party. Notable prime ministers include Shigeru Yoshida, who led the country during the Allied occupation and recovery; Eisaku Satō, the longest-serving of the post-war era before Shinzo Abe; Junichiro Koizumi; and Shinzo Abe, Japan's longest-serving prime minister overall, who served 2006–2007 and again 2012–2020 and was assassinated in 2022. Shigeru Ishiba became prime minister in October 2024.

Role

The prime minister appoints and may dismiss ministers of state, introduces bills on behalf of the cabinet, controls and supervises the administrative branches, and serves as the supreme commander of the Japan Self-Defense Forces. The official office and residence is the Kantei in Nagatachō, Tokyo.
