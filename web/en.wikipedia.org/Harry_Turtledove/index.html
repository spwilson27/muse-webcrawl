Harry Turtledove

Harry Norman Turtledove (born June 14, 1949) is an American author best known for his work in the alternate history genre, a field in which he is widely regarded as its leading contemporary practitioner and sometimes called the "master of alternate history". He has also written science fiction and fantasy. Before turning to fiction full-time, Turtledove earned a PhD in Byzantine history from the University of California, Los Angeles, and worked as a technical writer.

Life and career

Turtledove grew up in Gardena, California. He briefly attended the California Institute of Technology before transferring to UCLA, where he completed his doctorate in Byzantine history in 1977. His scholarly background heavily informs his fiction, particularly his interest in Byzantium, the Balkans, and Mediterranean history.

Under the pen name Eric G. Iverson, he published his first novels, including Wereblood and Werenight (1979). He later wrote works set in the world of the Videssos cycle, a fantasy series inspired by Byzantine history in which a Roman legion is transported to a magical world.

Alternate history works

Turtledove's breakthrough in alternate history came with The Guns of the South (1992), in which time travellers supply the Confederate Army with AK-47 rifles during the American Civil War. His best-known work is the eleven-volume Southern Victory series, beginning with How Few Remain (1997), which imagines a Confederate victory in 1862 and follows the divergent history of North America through two world wars. The Worldwar tetralogy (1994–1996) depicts an alien invasion interrupting the Second World War, continued in the Colonization trilogy and Homeward Bound. Other notable works include Ruled Britannia, In the Presence of Mine Enemies, Joe Steele, and the Days of Infamy duology about a Japanese occupation of Hawaii.

Style and themes

Turtledove's novels are characterised by large ensemble casts, multiple points of view, meticulous attention to historical detail, and an interest in how ordinary people experience great events. He has won numerous awards, including the Sidewise Award for Alternate History.
