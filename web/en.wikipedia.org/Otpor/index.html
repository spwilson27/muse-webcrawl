Otpor

Formation1998, Belgrade
TypeCivic resistance movement
SymbolClenched fist
FateMerged into the Democratic Party (2004)

Otpor (Serbian: Отпор, "Resistance") was a civic youth movement in Serbia that played a central role in the overthrow of President Slobodan Milošević in October 2000. Founded in 1998 by students at the University of Belgrade, it became known for its black clenched-fist symbol, street theatre, humour, and strictly nonviolent discipline.

Origins

Otpor emerged in response to repressive university and media laws. Rejecting a single leader, it organised as a decentralised network that the regime found difficult to decapitate, though thousands of its activists were arrested or beaten during its two years of activity.

Role in 2000

In the 2000 election campaign Otpor ran the "Gotov je" ("He is finished") campaign and worked, alongside the Democratic Opposition of Serbia coalition, on voter turnout and election monitoring. After Milošević refused to accept defeat, mass protests culminating on 5 October 2000 forced his resignation.

Legacy

Otpor veterans later founded the Centre for Applied Nonviolent Action and Strategies (CANVAS), which has trained pro-democracy activists from many countries. Otpor itself transformed into a political party in 2003 and merged into the Democratic Party in 2004.
