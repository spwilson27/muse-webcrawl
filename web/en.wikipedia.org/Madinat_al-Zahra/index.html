Medina Azahara

Madinat al-Zahra (Medina Azahara) was a fortified palace city built in the tenth century by Abd al-Rahman III, the Umayyad caliph of Córdoba, on the western outskirts of Córdoba in present-day Spain. Begun around 936–940, it served as the seat of the Caliphate of Córdoba's government and court, and it lends its name to the modern archaeological site and museum. In 2018 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

Tradition attributes the city's foundation to al-Zahra, a favourite of the caliph, though its purpose was principally political: a new capital demonstrating the power and legitimacy of the newly proclaimed caliphate. Construction continued under al-Hakam II. During the civil wars of the early eleventh century the city was sacked and abandoned, and its ruins were quarried for centuries.

Archaeology

Excavations begun in 1911 have uncovered roughly a tenth of the walled area, including the caliph's reception hall (Salón Rico), richly decorated with carved vegetal ornament, and the House of Ya'far. The site's museum, opened in 2009, won the Aga Khan Award for Architecture.
