Bicycle racing

Bicycle racing is a competitive physical activity using bicycles. There are several categories of bicycle racing including road bicycle racing, cyclo-cross, mountain bike racing, track cycling, BMX, and cycle speedway. Non-racing cycling sports include artistic cycling, cycle polo, freestyle BMX and mountain bike trials.

History

The first bicycle race is popularly held to have been a 1,200 metre race on 31 May 1868 at the Parc de Saint-Cloud, Paris, won by James Moore. Road racing grew rapidly in popularity in France, Italy, Spain and Belgium, giving rise to events that endure today, including Paris–Roubaix (1896), the Tour de France (1903) and the Giro d'Italia (1909). The world governing body, the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI), was founded in 1900. Cycling has been part of the Olympic Games since 1896.

Disciplines

Road racing

Road races may be one-day events or multi-day stage races such as the Grand Tours. Riders compete individually and in teams, using tactics such as drafting in the peloton, breakaways, and lead-out trains for sprinters.

Track cycling

Track races are held on banked velodromes and include sprints, pursuits, points races, the keirin, and the madison.

Other disciplines

Cyclo-cross combines road and off-road riding over obstacles in autumn and winter. Mountain bike racing includes cross-country and downhill events; BMX racing and freestyle are contested on small-wheeled bikes.
