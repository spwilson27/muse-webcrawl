Constitution of 1917

The Political Constitution of the United Mexican States of 1917 (Spanish: Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos de 1917) is the current constitution of Mexico. It was drafted in Santiago de Querétaro by a Constitutional Convention during the Mexican Revolution. It was approved by the Mexican Constitutional Congress on 5 February 1917, and remains, with many amendments, the fundamental law of Mexico.

Background

Following the overthrow of Porfirio Díaz, revolutionary leader Venustiano Carranza called elections for a constituent congress in 1916. Carranza's draft was comparatively conservative, largely updating the liberal Constitution of 1857, but the delegates — influenced by the revolutionary armies' demands and by figures such as the Magonista movement — adopted substantially more radical social provisions.

Key articles

  • Article 3 — guarantees free, secular public education and restricted the role of the Catholic Church in schooling;
  • Article 27 — vested ownership of land and natural resources in the nation, enabled redistribution of large estates (latifundios) into communal ejidos, and restricted foreign ownership of land and subsoil resources;
  • Article 123 — established extensive labour rights, including the eight-hour day, minimum wage, the right to strike, restrictions on child labour, and equal pay provisions — among the most progressive labour provisions in any constitution at the time;
  • Article 130 — imposed strict controls on churches and clergy as part of the constitution's anticlerical measures.

Legacy

The 1917 Constitution was the first in the world to set out social rights, predating the Russian Constitution of 1918 and the Weimar Constitution of 1919. It has been amended hundreds of times but remains in force. Its anniversary, 5 February, is a national holiday in Mexico.
