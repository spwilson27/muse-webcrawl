Vizier

A vizier (also spelled wazir) is a high-ranking political advisor or minister in the lands ruled by Muslim monarchs. The institution originated in the Abbasid Caliphate, where the wazir served as the chief minister of the caliph, and later spread throughout the Islamic world, most famously in the Ottoman Empire, where the Grand Vizier was the de facto head of government, second only to the sultan.

Etymology

The word derives from the Arabic wazir (وزير), meaning "helper" or "burden-bearer" — one who bears the burden of administration on behalf of the ruler. The Qur'an uses the term of Aaron as the wazir of Moses.

History

Under the Abbasids, powerful vizier families such as the Barmakids effectively governed the empire. In the Ottoman Empire, the Grand Vizier presided over the Imperial Council (Divan), held the seal of state, and commanded armies; many grand viziers, such as Sokollu Mehmed Pasha, wielded enormous power. The office also existed in Safavid Persia, Mughal India, Egypt, and al-Andalus.

In ancient Egypt, a similar office existed: the tjaty, often translated "vizier", was the pharaoh's chief administrator and judge, a role held by figures such as Rekhmire and Imhotep.
