Legitimacy (political)

In political science, legitimacy is the right and acceptance of an authority — a government, regime or ruler — to exercise power, recognised by those governed by it.

Sources

Max Weber identified three types of legitimate authority: traditional (custom and heredity), charismatic (the leader's personal qualities) and rational-legal (rules and offices, as in constitutional democracies). Legitimacy lowers the cost of governance because obedience is given voluntarily rather than by coercion alone, and its loss can lead to unrest or revolution.
