Lunar Laser Ranging experiment

Started: 1969, Apollo 11
Method: Timing laser pulses reflected from lunar retroreflectors
Precision: Millimetre-level in modern measurements
Stations: Apache Point (APOLLO), McDonald, Grasse, Wettzell, Matera

The Lunar Laser Ranging experiment measures the distance between Earth and the Moon by timing laser pulses reflected from retroreflector arrays left on the lunar surface by Apollo 11, 14 and 15 and by Soviet Lunokhod rovers.

History

The first successful returns were obtained in August 1969, weeks after Apollo 11 landed. Measurements have continued for over five decades, making it one of the longest-running space experiments.

Results

The Moon recedes from Earth at about 3.8 cm per year due to tidal dissipation. Data test general relativity, including the equivalence principle (Earth and Moon fall toward the Sun equally to high precision), constrain variation of the gravitational constant, and reveal a fluid lunar core and lunar librations.
