Cissbury

Cissbury Ring is an Iron Age hill fort on the South Downs near Findon, Worthing, in West Sussex, England. At about 24 hectares (60 acres), it is the second-largest hill fort in England. The site takes its name from a supposed association with Cissa, an early South Saxon leader, though the fort long predates him.

Neolithic flint mines

Before the hill fort was built, the hill was the site of one of Britain's earliest and most important Neolithic flint mines. From about 4000 BC miners dug shafts and galleries to extract high-quality black flint from the chalk. Over 200 shafts have been identified, making Cissbury comparable in importance to Grime's Graves in Norfolk.

The hill fort

The fort's rampart and ditch were constructed around 400 BC. The single rampart encloses an area containing evidence of later occupation, including a Romano-British settlement and a mint of the Anglo-Saxon period outside the main defences. The site is now owned by the National Trust and is noted for its chalk grassland flora and butterflies.
