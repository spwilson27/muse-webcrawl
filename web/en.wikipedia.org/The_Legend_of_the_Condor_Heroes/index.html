The Legend of the Condor Heroes

The Legend of the Condor Heroes (Chinese: 射鵰英雄傳; pinyin: Shèdiāo Yīngxióng Zhuàn) is a wuxia novel by Chinese writer Jin Yong (Louis Cha). It is the first part of the Condor Trilogy and was serialized from 1 January 1957 to 19 May 1959 in the Hong Kong newspaper Hong Kong Commercial Daily. The novel, one of Jin Yong's most famous works, has been adapted into numerous films and television series and translated into English (published 2018–2021 as Legends of the Condor Heroes).

Plot

Set against the Jin–Song wars and the rise of Genghis Khan in the early 13th century, the story follows Guo Jing, a slow-witted but honest boy raised on the Mongolian steppe, who journeys south into China to fulfill a duel pledged between his teachers, the Seven Freaks of Jiangnan, and the Taoist Qiu Chuji. He meets the brilliant, mischievous Huang Rong and trains under masters including Hong Qigong of the Beggars' Gang, learning the Eighteen Dragon-Subduing Palms. Around them swirl the gentler tragic arc of Guo's sworn brother Yang Kang, who chooses wealth and treason, the romance of Mu Nianci, and the rivalries of the great martial artists of the age — the "Five Greats" — culminating at the Mount Hua tournament.

Themes and legacy

The novel's moral center is Guo Jing's creed that "the true hero serves his country and his people," dramatized in the sequel's defense of Xiangyang. Its blend of history, romance and martial-arts fantasy made it a cornerstone of modern Chinese popular culture; its many television adaptations (notably the 1983 TVB series) are classics of Hong Kong drama.
