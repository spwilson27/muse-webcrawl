Etiquette

Etiquette is the set of conventional rules of personal behaviour in polite society, usually in the form of an ethical code that delineates the expected and accepted social behaviors that accord with the conventions and norms observed by a society, a social class, or a group. Modern etiquette is generally concerned with treating people with respect and consideration.

History

Codified manners have ancient roots, from the instructions of Ptahhotep in ancient Egypt to Confucian ideals of ritual propriety. In Europe, courtly codes of behaviour developed in the Renaissance; Erasmus's De Civilitate (1530) was an influential early manual. The word "etiquette" comes from the French étiquette, a small card or ticket, and is associated with the court of Louis XIV at Versailles, where cards prescribed rules of conduct.

Types

Etiquette covers many domains: dining and table manners, greetings and introductions, dress codes, correspondence, telephone and digital communication ("netiquette"), business conduct, and behaviour at ceremonies such as weddings and funerals. Norms vary considerably across cultures: what is polite in one society may be rude in another.

Etiquette and morality

Etiquette is distinct from ethics or morality: it governs the outward forms of courtesy rather than underlying moral worth, though many writers, including Emily Post, argued that good manners express genuine consideration for others.
