Kyrgyzstan, officially the Kyrgyz Republic, is a landlocked country in Central Asia. Mountainous and rugged, it is bordered by Kazakhstan to the north, Uzbekistan to the west, Tajikistan to the south and China to the east. Its capital and largest city is Bishkek. Kyrgyzstan is nicknamed "the Switzerland of Central Asia" for its alpine scenery, centred on the Tian Shan mountain system and the great high-altitude lake Issyk-Kul.

History

The Kyrgyz, a Turkic people, migrated into the region over many centuries; their epic of Manas — one of the longest epic poems in the world — anchors national identity. The territory was traversed by the Silk Road, passing through the caravanserai of Tash Rabat, and came under Kokand Khanate rule before annexation by the Russian Empire in 1876. As the Kirghiz SSR it was a constituent republic of the Soviet Union from 1936.

Independence in 1991 brought an early experiment with democracy and market reforms under Askar Akayev, but authoritarian drift, corruption and regional divides produced repeated upheavals: the Tulip Revolution of 2005, the April 2010 revolution — followed by deadly interethnic violence in Osh — and the October 2020 protests that annulled disputed election results. A 2021 referendum restored a presidential system under Sadyr Japarov.

Geography

Over 90% of Kyrgyzstan lies above 1,500 m. The Tian Shan and Pamir-Alai ranges hold thousands of glaciers; Jengish Chokusu (formerly Pik Pobedy, 7,439 m) is the country's highest summit, and Khan Tengri (7,010 m) the world's northernmost 7,000-metre peak. Lake Issyk-Kul, the world's second-largest alpine lake, never freezes despite its altitude. The fertile Fergana Valley in the southwest is the agricultural heartland.

Economy and culture

The economy rests on agriculture, gold mining (the Kumtor mine is the largest single contributor), hydropower and remittances from migrant workers in Russia and Kazakhstan. Nomadic traditions remain vivid: horse games such as kok boru (dead goat polo), yurt (boz üy) dwelling, felt crafts (shyrdak carpets) and eagle hunting are celebrated at the World Nomad Games, founded in Kyrgyzstan in 2014. The population is predominantly Muslim, with a significant Russian Orthodox minority, and includes Kyrgyz, Uzbek, Russian, Dungan and Uyghur communities.
