Dominique François Jean Arago (1786–1853) was a French mathematician, physicist, astronomer and statesman. He contributed to optics, magnetism and geodesy, directed the Paris Observatory, and as a minister in 1848 abolished slavery in the French colonies.

Scientific career

Arago completed the meridian arc survey in Spain begun by Delambre and Méchain, surviving imprisonment during the Peninsular War. Elected to the Academy of Sciences at 23, he worked on the polarisation of light with Fresnel, discovered the chromosphere of the Sun and rotary magnetism ("Arago's rotations", 1824), and later supported Foucault's measurement of the speed of light demonstrating that light travels slower in water than in air — a key test of the wave theory of light.

Political career

A liberal deputy from 1830, Arago became Minister of War and the Navy after the February Revolution of 1848 and effectively headed the executive; his government abolished slavery throughout the French empire and introduced universal male suffrage. He refused the oath to Napoleon III after the 1851 coup and withdrew from public life.

Honours

His is one of the 72 names inscribed on the Eiffel Tower. The Paris meridian is marked by the "Arago medallions" set into the city's streets.
