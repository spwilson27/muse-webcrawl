Hydrometallurgy

Hydrometallurgy is a technique within the field of extractive metallurgy, the obtaining of metals from their ores, which involves the use of aqueous solutions for the recovery of metals from ores, concentrates and recycled or residual materials. Processing techniques that complement hydrometallurgy are pyrometallurgy, vapour metallurgy and molten salt electrometallurgy. Hydrometallurgy is typically divided into three general areas: leaching, solution concentration and purification, and metal or metal compound recovery.

Leaching

Leaching involves the use of aqueous solutions to extract metal from metal-bearing materials, leaving the residue behind as gangue. Lixiviants range from dilute sulfuric acid, used for copper oxide ores, to sodium cyanide solutions used for gold, and ammonia-based systems for nickel and cobalt. Leaching may be carried out in heaps, vats or autoclaves under pressure.

Concentration and purification

After leaching, the pregnant leach solution is concentrated and purified, commonly by solvent extraction (liquid–liquid extraction), ion exchange, or precipitation of impurities. In solvent extraction, the metal is transferred into an organic phase and then stripped back into a clean aqueous solution.

Metal recovery

The final step recovers the metal or its compounds by electrowinning, precipitation, cementation or hydrogen reduction. Copper, zinc, nickel, cobalt, uranium and rare earth elements are all produced commercially by hydrometallurgical routes, which can treat lower-grade ores than pyrometallurgy and often operate at lower temperatures, though they generate aqueous waste streams that must be managed.
