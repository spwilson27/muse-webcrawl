Tuna

KingdomAnimalia
PhylumChordata
FamilyScombridae
TribeThunnini
GeneraThunnus, Katsuwonus, Euthynnus, Allothunnus, Auxis

A tuna (also called a tunny) is a saltwater fish that belongs to the tribe Thunnini, a subgrouping of the Scombridae (mackerel) family. The Thunnini comprise 15 species across five genera, the sizes of which vary greatly, ranging from the bullet tuna (maximum length 50 cm, weight 1.8 kg) up to the Atlantic bluefin tuna (maximum length 4.6 m, weight 684 kg). The Atlantic bluefin averages 2 m and is believed to live up to 50 years.

Biology

Tunas are fast swimmers; some species can cruise at 70 km/h (43 mph). Unlike most fish, which have white flesh, the muscle of tuna ranges from pink to dark red, the colour resulting from myoglobin, an oxygen-binding molecule. Tunas are remarkable among fish in being partially warm-blooded (endothermic): they can maintain body temperatures higher than that of the surrounding water, which improves the efficiency of their muscles and allows them to hunt in cold waters.

Tunas are pelagic predators that feed on smaller fish, squid, and crustaceans. They are found in warm seas worldwide and migrate long distances, sometimes crossing entire ocean basins.

Fisheries

Tuna is an important commercial fish. The species most used for canning are skipjack (Katsuwonus pelamis), yellowfin (Thunnus albacares), and albacore (Thunnus alalunga). Bluefin species are highly prized for sushi and sashimi, particularly in Japan, where a single fish has sold for millions of dollars. Intensive fishing has severely depleted several bluefin populations, and the Southern bluefin tuna is listed as endangered. Tuna fisheries are managed by regional fisheries management organisations, though illegal and unreported fishing remains a problem.

As food

Tuna may be served raw as sashimi or sushi, seared, grilled, or canned. Canned tuna is one of the most widely consumed seafoods in the world. Because tuna sit high in the food chain, they accumulate mercury, and health authorities advise limiting consumption, especially for pregnant women and young children.
