The Seine (French: La Seine) is a 777-kilometre-long (483 mi) river in northern France. Its drainage basin lies largely in the Paris Basin and covers some 79,000 square kilometres. Rising at Source-Seine in the Côte-d'Or department, the river flows northwest through Troyes, Paris and Rouen before emptying into the English Channel at Le Havre and Honfleur.

Course

The Seine is France's second-longest river after the Loire. It is navigable by ocean-going vessels as far as Rouen, about 120 km from the sea, and commercial river traffic can reach Burgundy. Within Paris the river is spanned by 37 bridges, and its banks, from the Pont de Sully to the Pont d'Iéna, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Two islands lie in the river within the city: the Île de la Cité, the historic heart of Paris, and the Île Saint-Louis.

History

The name derives from the Latin Sequana, itself from a Celtic root. The river was the scene of major events including the Viking siege of Paris in 885–886 and the D-Day forces' advance after the Normandy landings in 1944. In January 1910 the river rose more than eight metres in the Great Flood of Paris, inundating much of the city. The Seine was famously the venue for the opening ceremony of the 2024 Paris Olympic Games, with athletes parading by boat.

Water quality

Historically heavily polluted, the Seine's water quality has improved greatly since the 1960s through wastewater treatment investment; Atlantic salmon have returned to the river. Swimming events in the river formed part of the Paris 2024 Olympics after a major clean-up programme.
