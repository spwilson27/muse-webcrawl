Port Hedland is a town in the Pilbara region of Western Australia, approximately 1,800 kilometres north of Perth. It is home to the world's largest bulk export port by tonnage, handling iron ore shipped from the mines of the Pilbara by companies including BHP, Rio Tinto, Fortescue, and Roy Hill. The port's annual throughput exceeds 500 million tonnes, the great majority of it iron ore bound for steel mills in China, Japan, and South Korea.

History

The area is the traditional land of the Kariyarra people, who know the place as Marapikurrinya. The harbour was named by Captain Peter Hedland, master of the cutter Mystery, who explored the coast in 1863. A townsite was gazetted in 1896 to serve the pastoral and pearling industries, and grew with the construction of a jetty and a railway to the Marble Bar goldfields in 1911. The modern town's fortunes were transformed from the 1960s by the development of the Pilbara iron ore industry.

Port and economy

The port, managed by Pilbara Ports, consists of berths at Port Hedland's inner harbour on both the town side and Finucane Island, and is accessed by a long dredged channel through one of the world's largest tidal ranges. Trains more than two kilometres long deliver ore from mines hundreds of kilometres inland. The town itself includes the residential areas of Port Hedland and South Hedland, developed in the 1970s, and has an arid tropical climate with very hot summers and exposure to tropical cyclones.
