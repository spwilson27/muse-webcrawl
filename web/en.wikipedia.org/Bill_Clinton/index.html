William Jefferson Clinton (born William Jefferson Blythe III, 19 August 1946) is an American politician and lawyer who served as the 42nd president of the United States from 1993 to 2001. A member of the Democratic Party, he previously served as governor of Arkansas from 1979 to 1981 and again from 1983 to 1992.

Early life and education

Clinton was born in Hope, Arkansas; his father died before his birth, and he took the surname of his stepfather, Roger Clinton. He attended Georgetown University, won a Rhodes Scholarship to Oxford, and earned a law degree at Yale, where he met Hillary Rodham. After a spell teaching law, he entered Arkansas politics, becoming attorney general in 1976 and governor in 1978.

Presidency

Defeating incumbent George H. W. Bush in 1992, Clinton presided over the longest peacetime economic expansion in American history, achieving budget surpluses, low unemployment, and welfare reform, and ratifying NAFTA. His second term was dominated by the Lewinsky scandal, which led to his impeachment by the House of Representatives in 1998 on charges of perjury and obstruction of justice; he was acquitted by the Senate in 1999. Foreign policy included interventions in the Balkans and an unsuccessful final push for Israeli–Palestinian peace at Camp David in 2000.

Post-presidency

After leaving office, Clinton founded the Clinton Foundation, working on global health and development, and has served as an elder statesman and campaigner, including supporting the presidential campaigns of his wife Hillary Clinton, Secretary of State under Barack Obama and the 2016 Democratic nominee.
