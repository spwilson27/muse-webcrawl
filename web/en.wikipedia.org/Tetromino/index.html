Tetromino

A tetromino is a geometric shape composed of four squares, connected orthogonally (that is, at the edges and not the corners). Tetrominoes, like dominoes and pentominoes, are a particular type of polyomino. The corresponding polycube, called a tetracube, is a geometric shape composed of four cubes connected orthogonally.

A popular use of tetrominoes is in the video game Tetris (with the name altered to "Tetriminos"), which refers to them as Tetriminos. The tetrominoes used in the game are specifically the one-sided tetrominoes.

Free tetrominoes

Polyonimoes formed of four squares have seven distinct configurations if rotations and reflections are counted, or five "free" tetrominoes if reflections are considered the same. The five free tetrominoes are conventionally named by the letter of the alphabet they most closely resemble:

  • I — four squares in a straight line.
  • O — a 2×2 block.
  • T — a row of three squares with one attached below the centre.
  • L — a row of three squares with one attached below the left (or, in its mirror, the right) end.
  • S — two offset rows of two squares (and its mirror image, Z).

One-sided and fixed tetrominoes

If reflections are considered distinct but rotations are not, there are seven one-sided tetrominoes: I, O, T, L, J, S and Z. If both rotations and reflections are considered distinct, there are 19 fixed tetrominoes. The one-sided tetrominoes are exactly the set used in Tetris.

Tiling

A single set of free tetrominoes has a total area of 20 squares, yet they cannot tile a 4×5 rectangle: colouring the rectangle like a chessboard gives 10 squares of each colour, while the T tetromino always covers three squares of one colour and one of the other, making the balance impossible. Two sets of one-sided tetrominoes (area 56) likewise cannot fill a rectangle; the smallest rectangle two full one-sided sets can tile is 7×8 with one square's worth of parity tricks — in fact they cannot tile any rectangle at all, a result provable by a modified colouring argument.
