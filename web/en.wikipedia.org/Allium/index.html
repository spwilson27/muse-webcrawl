Allium

Allium is a genus of monocotyledonous flowering plants in the family Amaryllidaceae, subfamily Allioideae. It includes the onion, garlic, scallion, shallot, leek, and chives. The generic name Allium is the Latin name for garlic.

Description

Allium species are perennial bulbous plants that produce chemical compounds, mostly cysteine sulfoxides, which give them their characteristic onion or garlic taste and odor. Many are grown as food crops, while others are cultivated as ornamentals for their showy flower heads (umbels).

Taxonomy and species

The taxonomy of Allium is complicated; the genus contains several hundred species native to the Northern Hemisphere, including A. cepa (onion), A. sativum (garlic), A. ampeloprasum (leek), A. schoenoprasum (chives), and A. fistulosum (Welsh onion).

Uses

Alliums are among the world's most important vegetable crops and flavorings. Some species are reputed to be toxic to dogs and cats. Ornamental alliums are popular in gardens for their spherical purple, white, or pink inflorescences.
