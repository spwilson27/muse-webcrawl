Srebrna Góra Fortress

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The fortress above Srebrna Góra

Srebrna Góra Fortress (Polish: Twierdza Srebrnogórska, German: Festung Silberberg) is a large mountain fortress in Lower Silesia, Poland, built by Prussia between 1765 and 1777 under Frederick the Great to secure Silesia, conquered from Austria. It crowns the pass above the town of Srebrna Góra (Silver Mountain) in the Bardzkie and Sowie mountains.

Design and history

One of the largest mountain fortresses in Europe when built, the complex consisted of bastions and forts on several peaks, including the great Donjon, designed for a garrison of several thousand and constructed to withstand siege artillery. It was never taken in battle; during the Napoleonic Wars it was besieged by French and allied forces in 1807 but held out until the peace settlement. In the later 19th century it served as a prison (holding, among others, the socialist Wilhelm Liebknecht) and then as a tourist attraction. Today it is a historic monument and museum hosting events and reenactments.
