GIFT City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City) is a planned central business district and smart city on the banks of the Sabarmati River in Gandhinagar, Gujarat, India. It hosts India's first operational International Financial Services Centre (IFSC), intended to compete with offshore financial centres such as Dubai, Singapore, and Mauritius-GIFT structures by allowing financial firms to transact in foreign currencies on Indian soil.

Development

Conceived in the late 2000s when Narendra Modi was Chief Minister of Gujarat, the project is developed by Gujarat International Finance Tec-City Company Limited, a joint venture involving the state government. The master plan covers some 886 acres, with a domestic zone and a special economic zone containing the IFSC. Notable buildings include GIFT One and GIFT Two towers and the regional headquarters of major Indian banks.

The IFSC

The GIFT IFSC is regulated by the International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA), established in 2020 as a unified regulator. It hosts offshore banking units of Indian and foreign banks, capital-market intermediaries, insurers, aircraft and ship leasing firms, and two international stock exchanges, India INX and NSE IFSC (later NSE IX), where foreign-currency derivatives trade. The exchanges also host the SGX Nifty-successor product, GIFT Nifty, after trading migrated from Singapore in 2023.

Ambitions and criticism

Supporters see GIFT City as central to India's ambition to onshore financial activity previously routed through foreign centres. Critics have pointed to slower-than-planned occupancy, limited social infrastructure in its early years, and dependence on tax incentives, though activity rose sharply in the early 2020s.
