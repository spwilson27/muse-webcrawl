Giacomo Casanova

Giacomo Girolamo Casanova (2 April 1725 – 4 June 1798) was an Italian adventurer and author from the Republic of Venice. His autobiography, Histoire de ma vie (Story of My Life), is regarded as one of the most authentic and vivid sources on the customs and social life of 18th-century Europe. Casanova travelled widely, working variously as a cleric, soldier, violinist, lottery promoter, spy and librarian, but became so famous for his amorous exploits that his surname is now synonymous with "womaniser".

Early life

Casanova was born in Venice to a family of actors and was raised largely by his grandmother while his parents toured. Educated by the priest Antonio Gozzi, he studied law briefly at the University of Padua and entered the Church, but was expelled from seminary for scandalous conduct. He then drifted through a series of posts as secretary, soldier in the Venetian army, and violinist.

Travels and adventures

In 1755 Casanova was arrested by the Venetian Inquisition for blasphemy and occult practices and imprisoned in the Piombi, the notorious cells under the lead roof of the Doge's Palace. His daring escape in 1756 — recounted in his History of My Flight — made him famous across Europe. In Paris he helped establish a state lottery that made him wealthy; he later travelled to courts across the continent, meeting figures such as Voltaire, Frederick the Great, Catherine the Great and Pope Clement XIII. He also worked as a spy for the Venetian Inquisition after being allowed to return in 1774, before being exiled again in 1783.

Later years and memoirs

Casanova spent his final years as librarian to Count Waldstein at the castle of Dux in Bohemia, where he wrote his memoirs in French. The work, published posthumously in expurgated editions from the 1820s and in full only in 1960, offers an unmatched portrait of 18th-century society and cemented his legend.
