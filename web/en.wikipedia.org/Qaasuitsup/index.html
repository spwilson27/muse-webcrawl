Qaasuitsup

Qaasuitsup (Greenlandic: Qaasuitsup Kommunia) was a municipality in western Greenland, created on 1 January 2009 as part of the Greenlandic municipal reform and dissolved on 1 January 2018. It was, at the time of its dissolution, the world's largest municipality by area, covering some 660,000 km² (255,000 sq mi) — larger than France.

History

Qaasuitsup was formed by merging the eight former municipalities of northwestern Greenland: Aasiaat, Ilulissat, Kangaatsiaq, Qaanaaq, Qasigiannguit, Qeqertarsuaq, Upernavik, and Uummannaq. Its administrative centre was Ilulissat, the largest town in the region and a major tourist destination for the Ilulissat Icefjord, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Dissolution

On 1 January 2018, Qaasuitsup was split into two new municipalities: Avannaata in the north (including Ilulissat, Qaanaaq, Upernavik and Uummannaq) and Qeqertalik in the south (including Aasiaat, Kangaatsiaq, Qasigiannguit and Qeqertarsuaq).

Geography

The municipality stretched along the west coast of Greenland from Disko Bay north to Thule (Qaanaaq), bordering the Northeast Greenland National Park. Settlements were reachable only by sea and air, with no road connections between towns.
