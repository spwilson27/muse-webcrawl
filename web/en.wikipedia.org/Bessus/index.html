Bessus

Bessus (Old Persian: Bayaçā; died 329 BC), also known by his throne name Artaxerxes V, was a Persian nobleman who served as satrap of Bactria under King Darius III of the Achaemenid Empire. He is best known for seizing and murdering Darius during the king's flight from Alexander the Great, and for proclaiming himself king in the eastern provinces.

Career

Bessus commanded the Bactrian cavalry on the Persian left wing at the Battle of Gaugamela in 331 BC. After the Persian defeat he accompanied Darius III in his flight eastward toward Bactria. Near Hecatompylos in the summer of 330 BC, Bessus and fellow conspirators including the chiliarch Nabarzanes and Barsaentes arrested Darius; when Alexander approached, they stabbed the king and fled, leaving him to die as Alexander arrived.

Rule and death

Bessus assumed the upright tiara of Persian kingship and the name Artaxerxes, rallying the eastern satrapies against Alexander. His support soon melted away as Alexander advanced across the Hindu Kush into Bactria and Sogdiana. In 329 BC his own followers, led by Spitamenes, seized him and handed him over to Alexander. Bessus was mutilated — his nose and ears cut off in Persian fashion for a regicide — and executed, at Ecbatana according to some accounts.
