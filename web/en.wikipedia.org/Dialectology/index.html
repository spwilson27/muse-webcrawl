Dialectology

Dialectology is the scientific study of linguistic dialect, the regional and social varieties of a language. It examines how pronunciation, grammar, and vocabulary vary across space and among social groups, and how those varieties change over time.

Methods

Traditional dialectology, established in the 19th century, sent fieldworkers to survey rural speakers — often older, non-mobile men — and plotted the results in linguistic atlases, drawing isoglosses: lines on a map marking the boundary of a particular linguistic feature. Famous projects include Georg Wenker's German dialect atlas and the Survey of English Dialects. In the 20th century, William Labov and others shifted attention to urban speech and social variation, founding modern sociolinguistics; contemporary dialectology also uses corpora and computational "dialectometry" to measure aggregate distances between varieties.

Concepts

Key concepts include the dialect continuum, in which adjacent varieties differ only slightly while distant ones diverge greatly; the distinction between accent (pronunciation only) and dialect (grammar and vocabulary as well); and dialect levelling, the reduction of regional differences under the influence of mobility and standard languages.
