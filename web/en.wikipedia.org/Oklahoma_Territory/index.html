Oklahoma Territory was an organized incorporated territory of the United States that existed from 2 May 1890 to 16 November 1907, when it was joined with the Indian Territory and admitted to the Union as the State of Oklahoma. Its capital was Guthrie.

History

The region had been designated Indian Territory after the forced removal of the "Five Civilized Tribes" in the 1830s. Following the Civil War and subsequent treaties, large areas deemed "Unassigned Lands" lay in the centre. After a decade of agitation by "Boomers" demanding settlement, Congress opened the land, and the Land Run of 22 April 1889 saw some 50,000 settlers claim homesteads in a single day — those who entered early were dubbed "Sooners".

The Organic Act of 2 May 1890 formally created Oklahoma Territory from the western portion of the region, including the Panhandle ("No Man's Land"). Further land runs and allotments — Cheyenne-Arapaho (1892), Cherokee Outlet (1893), Kickapoo (1895), and lotteries for Kiowa-Comanche and Wichita lands (1901) — steadily enlarged it. George W. Steele was the first territorial governor; the last was Frank Frantz.

Statehood

In 1905 leaders of the Five Tribes proposed a separate Native-majority State of Sequoyah from Indian Territory, but Congress instead required the two territories to enter as one state. Under the Enabling Act of 1906 a constitutional convention met at Guthrie, and on 16 November 1907 President Theodore Roosevelt proclaimed Oklahoma the 46th state.
