Oroonoko

AuthorAphra Behn
Published1688
GenreProse fiction; early novel

Oroonoko: or, the Royal Slave is a short work of prose fiction by Aphra Behn (1640–1689), published in 1688 by William Canning. It tells the story of an African prince, Oroonoko, who is tricked into slavery and transported to Surinam, then an English colony in South America, where he leads a revolt and meets a tragic death.

Plot

Oroonoko, grandson of the king of Coramantien in West Africa, falls in love with the beautiful Imoinda. Both are betrayed — Imoinda is taken into the king's harem and later sold into slavery, and Oroonoko himself is kidnapped by a slave-ship captain. Reunited in Surinam under slave names (Caesar and Clemene), Oroonoko organises a slave rebellion. When the revolt fails, and fearing Imoinda's fate, he kills her with her consent and is himself captured and executed by dismemberment.

Significance

Oroonoko is one of the earliest English novels and a landmark in the history of women's writing. It has been read both as an early critique of slavery and colonial cruelty and as a royalist, romanticised portrayal; Thomas Southerne's stage adaptation (1695) kept the story famous through the eighteenth century. Modern scholarship debates Behn's claim that the tale is a true history based on her own stay in Surinam.
