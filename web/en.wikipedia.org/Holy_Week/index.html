Holy Week

Holy Week (Latin: Hebdomada Sancta) is the week preceding Easter in the Christian liturgical year, commemorating the final days of Jesus Christ's life, his crucifixion, and his burial, culminating in the celebration of his resurrection on Easter Sunday. It begins on Palm Sunday and includes Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, and Holy Saturday; its last three days form the Easter Triduum. Holy Week is the most solemn period of the Christian calendar and is marked by distinctive liturgies, processions, and devotions across Catholic, Orthodox, and many Protestant traditions.

The days of Holy Week

Palm Sunday recalls Jesus's triumphal entry into Jerusalem, when crowds spread palm branches before him; liturgies distribute blessed palms and read the Passion narrative. The first three weekdays commemorate events such as the cleansing of the Temple, the anointing at Bethany, and Judas Iscariot's agreement to betray Jesus. Maundy Thursday (from Latin mandatum, the "new commandment") celebrates the Last Supper, the institution of the Eucharist, and Christ's washing of the disciples' feet, which many churches re-enact. Good Friday is a day of fasting and mourning centred on the Passion, the veneration of the Cross, and, in many places, street processions and passion plays. Holy Saturday is a day of quiet waiting, ending after dark with the Easter Vigil — the blessing of the new fire, the Paschal candle, and the first proclamation of the Resurrection.

Observances around the world

Holy Week (Spanish: Semana Santa) is especially famous in Spain, where confraternities in Seville, Málaga, and other cities carry immense floats (pasos) bearing sculptures of Christ and the Virgin through crowded streets, accompanied by penitents in pointed hoods (capirotes). Elaborate processions also mark the week in Guatemala (with dyed-sawdust carpets, alfombras), the Philippines (including the chanting of the Pasyon and, controversially, real crucifixion re-enactments), Mexico, Italy, and Greece. In the Eastern Orthodox Church, Holy Week services are exceptionally long and rich, including the Bridegroom matins, the service of the Twelve Gospels, and the candlelit Epitaphios procession on Good Friday evening.

History

The observance developed in 4th-century Jerusalem, where the pilgrim Egeria described a full week of processions visiting the sites of the Passion, and spread through the Christian world. Over the centuries the liturgy accumulated its characteristic elements — the blessing of palms, the Tenebrae services of responsories in darkness, the washing of feet, and the dramatic readings of the Passion from each Gospel in turn. Reformation traditions varied: Lutherans retained much of Holy Week, while some Reformed churches simplified it; the 20th-century liturgical movement, and reforms such as Pius XII's 1955 restoration of the Triduum services, reshaped modern practice.
