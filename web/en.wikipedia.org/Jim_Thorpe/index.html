Jim Thorpe
Bornc. 1887–1888, near Prague, Oklahoma, U.S.
Died28 March 1953, Lomita, California, U.S.
SportTrack and field, football, baseball

James Francis Thorpe (Sac and Fox name: Wa-Tho-Huk, "Bright Path") was an American athlete widely regarded as one of the most versatile sportsmen of the modern era. A Native American, he won gold medals in the pentathlon and decathlon at the 1912 Stockholm Olympics.

Olympic success and controversy

At Stockholm, Thorpe dominated both multi-event competitions. In 1913 his medals were stripped after it emerged he had played semi-professional baseball, violating strict amateur rules of the time. The International Olympic Committee restored him as sole champion in 2022, following earlier partial reinstatement in 1983.

Professional career

Thorpe played professional baseball, including for the New York Giants, and professional American football, starring for the Canton Bulldogs and serving as the first president of what became the NFL. He was named to the NFL 1920s All-Decade Team and is enshrined in the Pro Football Hall of Fame.

Legacy

Associated Press polls named Thorpe among the greatest athletes of the 20th century. The town of Jim Thorpe, Pennsylvania, is named in his honour.
