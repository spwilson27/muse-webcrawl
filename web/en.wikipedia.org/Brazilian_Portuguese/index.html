Brazilian Portuguese

Portuguese in Brazil

Brazilian Portuguese (Portuguese: português brasileiro) is the variety of the Portuguese language spoken in Brazil, by virtually all of the country's more than 200 million inhabitants. It is the most widely spoken variety of Portuguese worldwide.

Differences from European Portuguese

Brazilian and European Portuguese differ in pronunciation, vocabulary, and some points of grammar and spelling. Brazilian pronunciation is generally more syllable-timed with more open vowels, while European Portuguese reduces unstressed vowels more strongly. Vocabulary diverges for everyday items — for example, ônibus (Brazil) versus autocarro (Portugal) for "bus". Brazil makes wider use of the gerund (estou fazendo) where Portugal uses the infinitive construction (estou a fazer). Spelling was largely unified by the 1990 Portuguese Language Orthographic Agreement, though differences in accentuation remain.

Influences

Brazilian Portuguese absorbed vocabulary from indigenous languages such as Tupi (names of flora, fauna, and places), from African languages brought by enslaved people, and from later waves of European and Japanese immigration.
