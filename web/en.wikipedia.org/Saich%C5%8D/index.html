Saichō

Born15 September 767, Ōmi Province, Japan
Died26 June 822, Mount Hiei, Japan
Known forFounder of Tendai Buddhism in Japan

Saichō (767–822), posthumously known as Dengyō Daishi, was a Japanese Buddhist monk who founded the Tendai school of Buddhism in Japan, based on the Chinese Tiantai tradition centred on the Lotus Sutra.

Life

Ordained at a young age, Saichō withdrew to Mount Hiei near Kyoto to study and meditate. In 804 he sailed to Tang China as part of an official embassy, studying Tiantai, esoteric Buddhism, Zen and the precepts under Chinese masters. Returning in 805, he established Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei as the centre of the new Tendai school.

Legacy

Saichō sought imperial recognition for an independent Mahayana ordination platform on Mount Hiei, granted shortly after his death. Enryaku-ji became one of the most powerful religious institutions in Japan, and many founders of later schools — including Hōnen, Shinran, Dōgen and Nichiren — began their training there.
