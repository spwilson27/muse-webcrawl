Terminal High Altitude Area Defense

THAAD interceptor launch during a test

Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD), formerly Theater High Altitude Area Defense, is an American anti-ballistic missile defense system designed to shoot down short-, medium-, and intermediate-range ballistic missiles in their terminal phase by intercepting with a hit-to-kill approach.

Description

A THAAD battery consists of truck-mounted launchers carrying eight interceptors each, the AN/TPY-2 X-band radar, and a fire control unit. The interceptor carries no warhead; it destroys its target by direct collision at altitudes both inside and outside the atmosphere (endoatmospheric and exoatmospheric interception). THAAD forms the upper tier of a layered defense alongside the lower-tier Patriot system.

History and deployment

Development began in the late 1980s under the Strategic Defense Initiative; Lockheed Martin is the prime contractor. The system was first deployed operationally in 2008 and has been stationed in Guam, the Middle East, Israel, Romania, and South Korea. The 2017 deployment of a battery to Seongju, South Korea, in response to North Korean missile tests, prompted strong diplomatic and economic protests from China, which objected to the radar's reach.
