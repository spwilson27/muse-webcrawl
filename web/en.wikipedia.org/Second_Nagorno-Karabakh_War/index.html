Second Nagorno-Karabakh War

Date27 September – 10 November 2020
LocationNagorno-Karabakh and surrounding districts, South Caucasus
BelligerentsAzerbaijan (supported by Turkey) vs. Armenia and the Republic of Artsakh
ResultAzerbaijani victory; ceasefire agreement signed 9–10 November 2020
Territorial changesAzerbaijan regained the districts surrounding Nagorno-Karabakh and part of the region itself, including Shusha

The Second Nagorno-Karabakh War was an armed conflict fought in the autumn of 2020 between Azerbaijan, supported by Turkey, and the self-proclaimed Republic of Artsakh together with Armenia, in the disputed region of Nagorno-Karabakh and the surrounding territories. It was the latest and largest escalation of the unresolved Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, which had begun with the First Nagorno-Karabakh War of 1988–1994.

Background

Nagorno-Karabakh, an ethnic Armenian-majority enclave internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan, had been controlled since 1994 by Armenian forces and the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh, along with seven surrounding Azerbaijani districts. Decades of mediation by the OSCE Minsk Group failed to produce a settlement, and skirmishes along the line of contact were frequent.

Course of the war

Fighting began on 27 September 2020. Azerbaijan, having invested heavily in modern weaponry including Turkish Bayraktar TB2 and Israeli Harop loitering munitions, made extensive use of unmanned aerial vehicles against Armenian armour and air defences. Azerbaijani forces advanced in the south along the Aras river, captured Jabrayil and Fuzuli, and in early November took the strategically important city of Shusha (Shushi), overlooking Stepanakert.

Ceasefire and aftermath

On the night of 9–10 November 2020, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Russia signed a ceasefire agreement brokered by Russian president Vladimir Putin. Azerbaijan retained the territories it had captured, Armenia returned the remaining occupied districts of Agdam, Kalbajar and Lachin, and Russian peacekeepers were deployed along the Lachin corridor. The conflict reshaped the regional balance of power, strengthening Turkish and Azerbaijani influence in the South Caucasus.
