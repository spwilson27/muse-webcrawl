Saṃsāra (Buddhism)


Image placeholder: Saṃsāra (Buddhism)

Saṃsāra (Sanskrit and Pali: संसार, "wandering") in Buddhism is the beginningless cycle of repeated birth, mundane existence and dying again that all beings pass through. It is characterized by dukkha (suffering, unsatisfactoriness) and is driven by ignorance (avidyā) and craving, perpetuated by karma. Liberation from saṃsāra — nirvana — is the goal of Buddhist practice.

The six realms

Beings are reborn according to their karma into one of six realms (gati): the god realm (deva), the demigod or asura realm, the human realm, the animal realm, the hungry ghost realm (preta), and the hell realms (naraka). None of these states is permanent; even gods eventually die and are reborn elsewhere. The realms are often depicted in the bhavacakra (wheel of life), a symbolic picture held by Yama, the lord of death.

Dependent origination

The mechanism of saṃsāra is explained by pratītyasamutpāda (dependent origination), classically presented as twelve links (nidānas) beginning with ignorance and ending with aging and death. Because the cycle has no discoverable beginning, beings are said to have wandered through it for an inconceivable length of time.

Liberation

Escape from saṃsāra is achieved by uprooting ignorance and craving through the Noble Eightfold Path, culminating in awakening (bodhi) and nirvana. In Mahayana Buddhism, bodhisattvas are said to remain compassionately engaged with the world while no longer bound by involuntary rebirth.
