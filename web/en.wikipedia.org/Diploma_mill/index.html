Diploma mill

Diploma mills sell degrees with little or no academic study.

A diploma mill (also known as a degree mill) is a company or organization that claims to be a higher education institution but provides illegitimate academic degrees and diplomas for a fee, without requiring the academic study normally associated with such credentials. The degrees may be used to fraudulently claim educational qualifications in job applications and resumés.

Characteristics

Diploma mills share several common features: little or no interaction with faculty; degrees awarded based on "life experience" or a flat fee; names deliberately similar to those of legitimate, respected universities; addresses that are post office boxes, suites, or offshore locations; and accreditation by fake or unrecognized accreditation agencies ("accreditation mills") created to lend an appearance of legitimacy.

Legal status and enforcement

In the United States the use of unaccredited degrees is restricted in several states; Oregon, Texas, and others publish lists of institutions whose degrees may not lawfully be used for employment. The Federal Trade Commission and the U.S. Department of Education warn consumers about diploma mills, and the FBI's Operation Diploma Scam investigations in the 1980s led to numerous convictions. Internationally, the spread of online education has made diploma mills harder to police, and large operations — such as the Pakistan-based company Axact, exposed in 2015 — have sold fake degrees worldwide on an industrial scale.

Related practices

Related forms of credential fraud include essay mills and contract cheating services, which complete assignments for enrolled students, and "life experience degrees" offered by otherwise legitimate-appearing unaccredited institutions. Accreditation mills are the parallel phenomenon by which diploma mills obtain bogus endorsements.
