Maxim gun

The Maxim gun is a recoil-operated machine gun invented in 1884 by Hiram Stevens Maxim. It was the first fully automatic machine gun in the world, using the energy of each fired round to eject the spent cartridge and load the next. Water-cooled and belt-fed, it could fire around 600 rounds per minute and was adopted in variants by many armies, including the British Vickers, German MG 08, and Russian PM M1910.

History

Maxim, an American-born inventor working in London, demonstrated his gun to military observers in the mid-1880s. The Maxim played a notorious role in colonial warfare — Hilaire Belloc's couplet "Whatever happens, we have got / The Maxim gun, and they have not" captured its reputation — and at battles such as Omdurman (1898). In the First World War, its descendants on all sides contributed to the defensive dominance of trench warfare.

Design

The design uses a toggle-lock action and a water jacket around the barrel for cooling; it was typically served by a crew and mounted on a tripod or wheeled carriage. The Maxim Gun Company merged into Vickers, and the design's lineage continued in service for decades.
