Peenemunde (German: Peenemünde) is a municipality on the Baltic island of Usedom in northeastern Germany, at the mouth of the Peene river. It is internationally known as the site of the Peenemünde Army Research Center, where Nazi Germany developed the world's first large ballistic missile, the V-2 rocket.

History

The village was a small fishing and harbour settlement on the Peenestrom. In 1936 the German military acquired the northern part of Usedom and built an extensive research establishment: the army's facility under Walter Dornberger and technical director Wernher von Braun developed the A-4 rocket, later renamed the V-2, while the Luftwaffe tested the V-1 flying bomb nearby. The first successful A-4 flight took place on 3 October 1942, when the rocket reached the edge of space.

Production used concentration-camp labour, and thousands of prisoners died building the weapons. On the night of 17–18 August 1943, the Royal Air Force attacked the site in Operation Hydra, killing hundreds, including many forced labourers, and delaying the programme. Production was moved underground to the Mittelwerk near Nordhausen. Soviet forces captured Peenemünde in May 1945.

After the war

The site was largely dismantled by the Soviet Union. During the GDR era it hosted a naval and air base. After reunification, parts of the complex became the Peenemünde Historical Technical Museum, opened in 1991 in the former power station, which documents both the technical achievements and the crimes of the rocket programme.
