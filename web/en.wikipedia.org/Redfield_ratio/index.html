Redfield ratio

The Redfield ratio is the atomic ratio of carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus found in marine phytoplankton and throughout the deep oceans, approximately C : N : P = 106 : 16 : 1. It was described by the American oceanographer Alfred C. Redfield in 1934.

Significance

Redfield observed that the proportions of these elements in deep ocean water closely matched their proportions in plankton, suggesting that the chemistry of the ocean is regulated by the growth and remineralization of marine organisms. The ratio is a cornerstone of ocean biogeochemistry and is widely used in models of nutrient limitation and the biological carbon pump.

Deviations

When the local N:P ratio falls below about 16:1, nitrogen tends to limit growth; above it, phosphorus tends to be limiting. Deviations from the canonical ratio occur in surface waters and in particular species, and are used diagnostically in nutrient studies.
