E. O. Wilson

Edward Osborne Wilson (10 June 1929 – 26 December 2021) was an American biologist, naturalist and writer. A professor at Harvard University for most of his career, he was a leading authority on ants (myrmecology) and a founder of the fields of island biogeography and sociobiology.

Career

Born in Birmingham, Alabama, Wilson was partly blinded in one eye by a childhood fishing accident and turned to the close study of insects. At Harvard he studied ant systematics and pheromone communication, and with Robert MacArthur published The Theory of Island Biogeography (1967), a landmark of ecology. His 1971 book The Insect Societies and 1975's Sociobiology: The New Synthesis extended evolutionary explanations of behaviour, controversially, to humans.

Writing and honours

Wilson won two Pulitzer Prizes for general non-fiction, for On Human Nature (1979) and The Ants (1991, with Bert Hölldobler). His later works, including The Diversity of Life (1992), Consilience (1998) and Half-Earth (2016), championed biodiversity conservation; he helped promote the Encyclopaedia of Life project.
