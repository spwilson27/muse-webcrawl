The Apple Vision Pro is a mixed reality headset developed by Apple Inc. Announced at the company's Worldwide Developers Conference on June 5, 2023, it was released in the United States on February 2, 2024. Apple markets the device as a "spatial computer", blending digital content with the user's physical surroundings through high-resolution cameras and displays, rather than as a conventional virtual reality headset.

Hardware

The Vision Pro features a curved laminated glass front and an aluminium alloy frame. Its dual micro-OLED displays provide a combined total of about 23 million pixels, exceeding a 4K resolution per eye. The headset is powered by an Apple M2 chip for general processing alongside a dedicated Apple R1 chip, which processes input from twelve cameras, five sensors and six microphones to reduce motion-to-photon latency to around 12 milliseconds.

The device has no controllers; users interact by looking, tapping their fingers together, flicking, and dictating. An array of infrared cameras tracks eye movement, while downward-facing cameras track the hands. An external battery pack connects by cable and provides roughly two hours of use. A feature called EyeSight uses an outward-facing OLED display to show a rendered image of the wearer's eyes to people nearby.

Software

Vision Pro runs visionOS, an operating system derived from iOS frameworks, presenting applications as floating windows in three-dimensional space. The headset launched with a library of compatible iPad and iPhone applications, spatial versions of FaceTime, Safari, Photos and other Apple apps, and immersive environments. A digital crown adjusts the level of immersion from augmented reality through to full virtual reality scenes.

Release and reception

Pre-orders opened in the United States on January 19, 2024, with the launch price of US$3,499 for the 256 GB model. Sales expanded to further countries including China, Japan, the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia during mid-2024. Reviewers praised the display quality, pass-through fidelity and eye-and-hand tracking, while criticising the high price, weight, limited battery life and the small library of purpose-built software.
