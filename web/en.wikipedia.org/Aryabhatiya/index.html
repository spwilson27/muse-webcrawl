Aryabhatiya

The Aryabhatiya or Aryabhatiyam, a Sanskrit astronomical treatise, is the magnum opus and only known surviving work of the 5th-century Indian mathematician Aryabhata. Composed around 499 CE, when Aryabhata states he was 23 years old, it summarises Indian mathematics and astronomy in 118 verses.

Structure

The work is divided into four sections: the Gitikapada on cosmology and units of time, the Ganitapada on mathematics, the Kalakriyapada on the reckoning of time and planetary motions, and the Golapada on the celestial sphere.

Contents

The Aryabhatiya gives an approximation of pi as 3.1416, a table of sines, methods for solving linear indeterminate equations (the kuttaka or pulveriser method), and the assertion that the Earth rotates on its axis, explaining the apparent daily motion of the stars. It also presents rules for areas, volumes, arithmetic and geometric progressions, and shadow computations.

Influence

The treatise founded a tradition of commentary, most famously by Bhaskara I in the 7th century. Through translations into Arabic it influenced Islamic astronomy, and its methods underpin the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics.
