Cyberspace

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cyberspace is an interconnected digital environment. It is a type of virtual world popularised in science fiction. A metaphor for the global computer network, cyberspace encompasses the internet, telecommunications networks, computer systems, and the information and interactions they host. The word was coined by science-fiction author William Gibson, who used it in his 1982 short story "Burning Chrome" and popularised it in his 1984 debut novel Neuromancer, where he defined it as "a consensual hallucination experienced daily by billions of legitimate operators".

The term derives from "cybernetics", the study of control and communication in animals and machines founded by Norbert Wiener in the 1940s, itself from the Greek kybernḗtēs, meaning "steersman" or "governor".

Uses of the term

In the 1990s, "cyberspace" was widely adopted by internet users, policymakers, and the media to describe the emerging online world as a space apart from ordinary reality. John Perry Barlow's 1996 "Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace" famously asserted that governments had no sovereignty over this new domain. Scholars debated whether online activity should be governed by the laws of physical jurisdictions or treated as a separate realm — a debate framed by David Johnson and David Post's "Law and Borders" (1996) and Lawrence Lessig's response that cyberspace is regulable through its code and architecture.

Strategic and legal usage

Governments and militaries now define cyberspace operationally as a domain of operations alongside land, sea, air, and space. The US Department of Defense describes it as a global domain within the information environment consisting of interdependent networks of information technology infrastructures and resident data. This framing supports doctrines of cyber warfare, cyber defence, and deterrence, and has led to the creation of dedicated commands. Critics of the metaphor argue that treating the internet as a "place" can mislead law and policy, since every server and user sits in some physical jurisdiction.
