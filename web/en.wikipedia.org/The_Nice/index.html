The Nice

The Nice were an English progressive rock band active in the late 1960s. Centred on keyboardist Keith Emerson, they pioneered the fusion of rock with classical music and jazz, becoming a major influence on the progressive rock movement. Their best-known recording is a flamboyant arrangement of Leonard Bernstein's "America" from West Side Story.

History

The group formed in 1967 as the backing band for soul singer P. P. Arnold before stepping out on their own. Their debut album, The Thoughts of Emerlist Davjack (1967), blended psychedelic rock with Emerson's Hammond organ interpretations of classical themes. Albums Ars Longa Vita Brevis (1968) and Nice (1969) expanded their orchestral ambitions, and the posthumous Five Bridges (1970), featuring a suite performed with orchestra, reached the UK top five.

Legacy

Emerson's theatrical stagecraft — stabbing his Hammond organ with knives and playing it upside down — became legendary. After the band split in 1970, Emerson formed Emerson, Lake & Palmer, while Jackson and Davison continued in Jackson Heights and Refugee. The Nice reunited briefly for concerts in 2002–2003.
