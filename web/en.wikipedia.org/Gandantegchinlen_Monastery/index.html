Gandantegchinlen Monastery (Mongolian: Гандантэгчинлэн хийд) is a Mongolian Buddhist monastery in Ulaanbaatar, the capital of Mongolia. Its name translates roughly as "the great place of complete joy." It is the centre of Buddhism in Mongolia and one of the country's most important religious and cultural sites.

History

The monastery was founded in 1809 under the name Shar Sum ("Yellow Temple"). It was moved several times before settling at its present site on Dalkhyn Hill in 1838, taking the name Gandantegchinlen. In the nineteenth century it grew into one of Mongolia's largest monasteries, home to more than 5,000 monks and housing important colleges of philosophy, medicine, astrology and tantric studies.

During the anti-religious purges of the late 1930s under the Mongolian People's Republic, most of Mongolia's monasteries were destroyed and thousands of monks were executed or imprisoned. Gandantegchinlen was closed in 1938 but, unusually, was allowed to reopen in 1944 — partly as a showcase for foreign visitors — making it the only monastery permitted to function in Mongolia during the socialist period, albeit with a small number of monks under state supervision.

After the democratic revolution of 1990, religious practice revived freely, and the monastery again became the headquarters of Mongolian Buddhism, seat of the Khamba Lama, the country's chief abbot.

The Avalokiteśvara statue

The monastery's best-known feature is the Avalokiteśvara temple, which houses a 26.5-metre gilded statue of Migjid Janraiseg, the bodhisattva of compassion. The original statue, erected in 1911 when Mongolia declared independence from Qing China under the Bogd Khan, was removed by Soviet troops in 1938 and is believed to have been melted down. A new statue, funded by public donations, was completed and consecrated in 1996.

Present day

Today several hundred monks reside at the monastery, which maintains colleges including a Buddhist university. It is a major pilgrimage destination and one of Ulaanbaatar's principal tourist attractions.
