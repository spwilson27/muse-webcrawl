Atlanta Campaign

Date7 May – 2 September 1864
LocationNorthwestern Georgia and the Atlanta area
ResultUnion victory; capture of Atlanta
Union commanderWilliam Tecumseh Sherman
Confederate commandersJoseph E. Johnston; John Bell Hood (from 18 July)
StrengthUnion c. 112,000; Confederate 50,000–65,000

The Atlanta Campaign was a series of battles fought in the Western Theater of the American Civil War throughout northwest Georgia and the area around Atlanta during the summer of 1864. Union Major General William Tecumseh Sherman invaded Georgia from the vicinity of Chattanooga, Tennessee, beginning in May 1864, opposed by the Confederate Army of Tennessee under General Joseph E. Johnston. Atlanta, a vital rail hub and industrial centre of the Confederacy, was Sherman's objective.

Course of the campaign

Sherman's force of about 110,000 men in three armies advanced by repeatedly flanking Johnston out of strong defensive positions, beginning at Dalton and Resaca in May, through the fighting at New Hope Church, Pickett's Mill and Kennesaw Mountain in June (the latter a costly failed frontal assault on 27 June). Johnston conducted a skilful fighting retreat but steadily yielded ground, which exasperated Confederate President Jefferson Davis; on 17 July, with Sherman at Atlanta's gates, Davis replaced Johnston with the aggressive John Bell Hood. Hood launched a series of attacks — at Peachtree Creek (20 July), the Battle of Atlanta (22 July, where Union general James B. McPherson was killed), and Ezra Church (28 July) — all repulsed with heavy Confederate losses. Sherman then extended his lines to cut Atlanta's railroads; after the Battle of Jonesborough (31 August – 1 September) severed the last line, Hood evacuated the city, and Union troops occupied Atlanta on 2 September.

Significance

The capture of Atlanta was a major strategic and political victory for the Union. Coming alongside Farragut's victory at Mobile Bay, it transformed Northern public opinion, which had been gloomy over the stalemate in Virginia, and greatly boosted President Abraham Lincoln's re-election in November 1864 — an election that ensured the war would be prosecuted to a finish. In November, Sherman abandoned Atlanta (after ordering its military infrastructure destroyed) and began his March to the Sea toward Savannah.
