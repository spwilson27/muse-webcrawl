Songhai Empire

The Songhai Empire was a state located in the western part of the Sahel during the 15th and 16th centuries. At its peak, it was one of the largest African empires in history. The state is known by its historiographical name, derived from its largest ethnic group and ruling elite, the Songhai people. Sonni Ali established Gao as the empire's capital, although a Songhai state had existed in and around Gao since the 11th century.

Sonni Ali (r. 1464–1492) expanded the empire through conquest along the Niger River, capturing Timbuktu and Djenné. His successor, Askia Muhammad I (Askia the Great, r. 1493–1528), consolidated the state through administrative reforms, promoted Islam, and encouraged learning and scholarship, making Timbuktu's Sankore University a famed center of learning. At its height the empire controlled the trans-Saharan trade in gold and salt across much of West Africa.

Decline and fall

After Askia Muhammad was deposed by his son in 1528, internal conflicts weakened the empire. It fell in 1591 when a Moroccan expeditionary force under Judar Pasha, equipped with firearms, defeated the Songhai at the Battle of Tondibi. The empire fragmented into smaller kingdoms, of which the Dendi Kingdom survived longest.
