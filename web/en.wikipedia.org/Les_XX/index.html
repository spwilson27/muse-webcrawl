Les XX

Les XX (French for "The Twenty") was a group of twenty Belgian artists founded in Brussels in 1883 by the lawyer and art patron Octave Maus. For ten years its annual exhibitions were among the most important showcases of avant-garde art in Europe, introducing Belgian audiences to Neo-Impressionism, Post-Impressionism and Art Nouveau.

History

The founding members included James Ensor, Théo van Rysselberghe, Fernand Khnopff and Anna Boch — the group's only female member. Each year the twenty members invited twenty additional artists, many from abroad: Georges Seurat's A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte was shown in 1887, and works by Paul Signac, Camille Pissarro, Vincent van Gogh, Paul Gauguin, Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec, Paul Cézanne and Odilon Redon appeared at subsequent salons. At the 1890 exhibition, van Gogh sold The Red Vineyard — the only documented sale of his lifetime — to Anna Boch. In 1893 Maus dissolved Les XX and replaced it with La Libre Esthétique, which continued until 1914.

Legacy

Les XX exemplified the artist-run exhibition society as an alternative to official salons, and its Brussels shows were a key conduit through which French avant-garde movements reached northern Europe.
