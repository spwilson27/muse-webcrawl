The politics of China operate within a one-party system in which the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), founded in 1921 and in power since 1949, holds a constitutionally guaranteed leading role. The People's Republic of China is governed through parallel party and state hierarchies, with the party supreme.

Party and state

The CCP's highest bodies are the National Congress, the Central Committee, the Politburo and its Standing Committee. The state structure comprises the National People's Congress (the formal legislature), the Presidency, the State Council (the government, led by the Premier), and the Central Military Commission, which commands the People's Liberation Army. Since 1993 the same person has usually held the offices of party General Secretary, President, and CMC Chairman.

Political structure and ideology

China is a unitary state divided into provinces, autonomous regions, municipalities, and the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau, run under the "one country, two systems" formula. Official ideology has evolved from Mao Zedong Thought through Deng Xiaoping Theory to Xi Jinping Thought, combining market reforms with tight party control over media, civil society, and the judiciary.

Elections and participation

Direct elections occur only at the village and local-congress level; higher bodies are indirectly elected. Eight minor "democratic parties" participate in the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference under CCP leadership, but no opposition parties are permitted.
