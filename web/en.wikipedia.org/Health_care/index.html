Health care

Health care, or healthcare, is the improvement of health via the prevention, diagnosis, treatment, amelioration or cure of disease, illness, injury, and other physical and mental impairments in people. Health care is delivered by health professionals and allied health fields. Medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, midwifery, nursing, optometry, audiology, psychology, occupational therapy, physical therapy, athletic training, and other health professions all constitute health care.

Delivery

Care is conventionally divided into primary care (the first point of contact, delivered by general practitioners and community services), secondary care (specialist care in hospitals, usually by referral), tertiary care (highly specialised consultative care), and sometimes quaternary care. Access to health care varies across countries and is influenced by social and economic conditions and by health policy. Health care systems are organisations established to meet the health needs of targeted populations, financed through taxation, social or private insurance, or out-of-pocket payment.

Systems and financing

Models include the Beveridge model (tax-funded public provision, as in the United Kingdom's National Health Service), the Bismarck model (social insurance funds, as in Germany), national health insurance (as in Canada), and largely private systems such as that of the United States, which has the world's highest per-capita spending. The World Health Organization advocates universal health coverage — access to needed services without financial hardship — as a central global goal.

Workforce and quality

The global health workforce faces chronic shortages and maldistribution, especially in low-income countries and rural areas. Quality and safety are major concerns: medical error, hospital-acquired infection, and unequal access remain persistent problems, and preventive care and public health measures are often the most cost-effective interventions.
