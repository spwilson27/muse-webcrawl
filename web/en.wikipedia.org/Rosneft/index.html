Rosneft Oil Company (Russian: Роснефть, "Russian Oil") is an integrated energy company headquartered in Moscow and majority-owned by the Russian state through Rosneftegaz. It is Russia's largest oil producer and one of the largest publicly traded oil companies in the world by reserves and production, with operations spanning exploration, production, refining and retail across Russia and abroad.

History

Rosneft was established in 1993 as a state enterprise holding the oil assets retained by the Russian government after the break-up of the Soviet oil industry. Its transformation into a giant came in 2004–2005, when it acquired Yuganskneftegaz, the main production unit of the dismantled Yukos oil company, at a controversial state-run auction following Yukos's tax prosecution and bankruptcy—an episode that became emblematic of the reassertion of state control over Russia's energy sector.

The company floated shares in London and Moscow in 2006 in one of the largest IPOs of its time. In 2013, Rosneft completed the roughly $55 billion acquisition of TNK-BP from BP and the AAR consortium, then the largest deal in Russian corporate history; BP emerged with a stake of about 20 percent in Rosneft. In 2016 the state sold a 19.5 percent stake to a Glencore–Qatar Investment Authority consortium as part of a partial privatisation.

Operations

Rosneft's core production lies in West Siberia, the Volga-Urals region, East Siberia and Sakhalin, with major refining capacity across Russia and a large filling-station network. It has pursued Arctic offshore and tight-oil projects, including the Vostok Oil project in the Russian far north, and held international assets and trading operations, notably in Germany through refineries such as Schwedt.

Sanctions

Following Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014 and especially the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Rosneft was targeted by Western sanctions; BP announced its exit from its shareholding, foreign executives left the board, and European governments moved to curtail its refining and trading presence. The company has since reoriented crude sales toward Asian markets.
