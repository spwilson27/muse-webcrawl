Transmission (medicine)

In medicine, public health, and biology, transmission is the passing of a pathogen causing an infectious disease from an infected host individual or group to a particular individual or group, regardless of whether the other individual was previously infected. The term strictly refers to the transmission of microorganisms directly from one individual to another.

Routes of transmission

Airborne and droplet

Airborne transmission occurs when pathogens are carried on very small particles that remain suspended in air over long distances, as with tuberculosis and measles. Droplet transmission involves larger respiratory droplets produced by coughing, sneezing, or talking, which travel short distances, as with influenza and many common colds.

Contact and fomites

Direct contact includes skin-to-skin transfer and exchange of body fluids. Indirect contact transmission occurs via contaminated surfaces or objects called fomites.

Other routes

Faecal–oral transmission spreads pathogens shed in faeces through contaminated food or water. Vector-borne transmission uses arthropods such as mosquitoes and ticks. Vertical transmission passes from mother to child during pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding. Iatrogenic transmission results from medical procedures.

Transmission dynamics

The basic reproduction number, R₀, describes the average number of secondary cases generated by one case in a fully susceptible population and guides control measures such as vaccination, isolation, ventilation, and hygiene.
