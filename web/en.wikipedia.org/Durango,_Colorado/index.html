Durango is a home rule city and the county seat of La Plata County, Colorado, United States. Situated in the Animas River Valley in the south-western part of the state, surrounded by the San Juan Mountains, it had a population of about 19,000 at the 2020 census. Durango is best known as the eastern terminus of the Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad and as a gateway to Mesa Verde National Park.

History

The city was organised in September 1880 by the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad as a rail and smelting centre serving the mining camps of the San Juan Mountains, including nearby Silverton. It was named after Durango, Mexico, which in turn took its name from Durango in the Basque Country of Spain. The railway reached the town in 1881 and extended to Silverton in 1882. Mining, smelting and ranching sustained the early economy, and the historic downtown along Main Avenue largely dates from the boom of the 1880s and 1890s.

Geography and climate

Durango lies at an elevation of about 6,512 feet (1,985 m) where the Animas River exits the mountains onto high-desert terrain associated with the Colorado Plateau. The climate is semi-arid with cold, snowy winters and warm summers; the nearby Purgatory Resort and the San Juan National Forest support year-round outdoor recreation.

Tourism and economy

The Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad, a surviving 3-foot-gauge heritage line, carries tourists on the 45-mile journey through the Animas Canyon to Silverton and is one of Colorado's best-known attractions. Durango is also a base for visiting Mesa Verde National Park, Chimney Rock National Monument and the Southern Ute Indian Reservation, and hosts Fort Lewis College, a public liberal arts college. Mountain biking, rafting on the Animas River, skiing and the annual Iron Horse Bicycle Classic race against the train are prominent features of local life.
