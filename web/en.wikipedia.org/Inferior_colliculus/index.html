Inferior colliculus

Part ofMidbrain (tectum)
SystemAuditory system
PairCorpora quadrigemina (with superior colliculi)

The inferior colliculus (IC) is a paired structure in the midbrain that serves as the principal midbrain nucleus of the auditory pathway. Together with the superior colliculi above them, the inferior colliculi form the four bumps of the corpora quadrigemina on the dorsal surface of the midbrain.

Anatomy

Each inferior colliculus consists of a central nucleus surrounded by dorsal and external cortices. It receives ascending input from the cochlear nuclei, superior olivary complex and nuclei of the lateral lemniscus of both sides, and projects mainly to the medial geniculate nucleus of the thalamus, which in turn relays to the auditory cortex. Commissural fibres interconnect the two colliculi.

Function

The inferior colliculus integrates and processes auditory information: spectral and temporal features of sounds, sound localisation cues, and combinations of inputs from both ears converge here. It is organised tonotopically, with neurons arranged by characteristic frequency. The IC also participates in multisensory integration and in the acoustic startle and orienting responses, partly through connections with the superior colliculus.

Clinical significance

Lesions of the inferior colliculus can impair sound localisation and auditory processing despite intact hearing thresholds. The nucleus is a target of research in tinnitus and central auditory processing disorders, and auditory midbrain implants have been developed for patients who cannot use cochlear implants.
