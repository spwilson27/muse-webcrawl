Vancouver Island is a large island in southwestern British Columbia, Canada, separated from the mainland by the Strait of Georgia and Johnstone Strait. It is the largest island on the west coast of the Americas, stretching about 456 km (283 miles) from northwest to southeast. The island is named for George Vancouver, who surveyed the region in the 1790s.

The rugged west coast of Vancouver Island near Tofino.

Geography

The island is dominated by the Vancouver Island Ranges, covered in temperate rainforest. Its east coast is sheltered and comparatively dry; the west coast faces the open Pacific with deep fjords such as Clayoquot Sound. Notable towns include Victoria (the provincial capital, at the island's southern tip), Nanaimo, Courtenay, and Tofino.

History

Indigenous peoples — including the Nuu-chah-nulth, Kwakwaka'wakw, and Coast Salish — have lived on the island for millennia. The Colony of Vancouver Island was established in 1849 and merged with the mainland colony in 1866. The island is connected to the mainland only by ferry and air, with no fixed road link.

Economy

Forestry, fishing, mining, agriculture, and tourism drive the economy; Victoria also hosts a large public-sector workforce and a growing technology sector.
