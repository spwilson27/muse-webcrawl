Hot rod

A hot rod is a typically American car that has been rebuilt or modified with a large engine for greater speed and acceleration, or styled to imitate such a car. Hot rods originated in Southern California in the 1930s, when enthusiasts modified cheap older cars — especially Ford Model Ts, Model As, and 1932 Ford V8s — and raced them on dry lake beds and, later, drag strips.

History

The culture flourished after World War II as returning servicemen with mechanical training and surplus income took up the hobby. The term "hot rod" itself is of uncertain origin, possibly a contraction of "hot roadster" or a reference to modified camshafts ("hot rods"). The National Hot Rod Association (NHRA) was founded in 1951 by Wally Parks to organise drag racing and promote safety, moving racers off public streets. Hot rodding became a major strand of American car culture, reflected in magazines such as Hot Rod (founded 1948), films, and music.

Styles

Common modifications include engine swaps (classically a small-block Chevrolet V8), chopped roofs, channelled bodies, removed fenders and hoods, and custom paint such as flames. Traditional rods follow period styles of the 1940s–1960s, while "street rods" emphasise comfort and finish, and "rat rods" deliberately retain a rough, unfinished appearance. Original pre-war steel cars, especially the 1932 Ford "Deuce", remain the most prized basis for a build, and fibreglass replicas are common.
