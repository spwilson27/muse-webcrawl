Dharavi

Dharavi
Neighbourhood in Mumbai, Maharashtra, India
Area: approx. 2.39 km²
Population: estimated 700,000–1,000,000

Dharavi is a locality in Mumbai, Maharashtra, India, considered to be one of Asia's largest slums. It has an area of just over 2.39 square kilometres and a population of about 700,000 to 1 million, giving it one of the highest population densities in the world.

History

Dharavi was founded in 1884 during the British colonial era, when tanneries and other polluting industries — and the low-income workers employed in them — were expelled from the peninsular city centre. The settlement grew on marshy land between Mumbai's two main railway lines, attracting migrants from across India, including potters from Gujarat, leather tanners from Tamil Nadu, and embroidery workers from Uttar Pradesh.

Economy

Despite its poverty, Dharavi has a large informal economy, with thousands of small-scale industries producing leather goods, pottery, textiles, and recycled materials. Annual turnover has been estimated at over US$1 billion. Most homes double as workplaces, and goods produced in Dharavi are exported worldwide.

Redevelopment

Plans to redevelop Dharavi have been discussed since 2004. In 2022 the Maharashtra government awarded a redevelopment tender to the Adani Group; the project aims to rehouse eligible residents in new housing on site, though it remains contentious over eligibility and the fate of local businesses.
