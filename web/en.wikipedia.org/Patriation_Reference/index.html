Patriation Reference

The Patriation Reference (Re: Resolution to amend the Constitution, [1981] 1 SCR 753) is a landmark decision of the Supreme Court of Canada on the patriation of the Canadian constitution.

Background

In 1980 the federal government of Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau proposed to ask the Parliament of the United Kingdom to amend the British North America Acts, patriating the constitution with an amending formula and a Charter of Rights, without the consent of the provinces. Several provinces challenged the plan, and references were heard in Manitoba, Newfoundland, and Quebec.

Decision

The Court held, by a 7–2 majority, that Parliament could legally proceed unilaterally, since courts enforce law, not conventions. However, by a 6–3 majority it found that a constitutional convention required a "substantial measure" of provincial consent for amendments affecting provincial powers. The decision thus split the question into legality and convention.

Aftermath

The ruling forced renewed negotiations, leading to the November 1981 agreement between Ottawa and nine provinces (Quebec dissenting), the Constitution Act, 1982, and the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms.
