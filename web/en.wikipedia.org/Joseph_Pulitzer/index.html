Joseph Pulitzer
Born: 10 April 1847, Makó, Hungary
Died: 29 October 1911, Charleston, South Carolina
Known for: New York World, Pulitzer Prizes

Joseph Pulitzer (10 April 1847 – 29 October 1911) was a Hungarian-American newspaper publisher who built the St. Louis Post-Dispatch and the New York World into leading newspapers, pioneering techniques of "new journalism" and yellow journalism.

Career

Pulitzer emigrated to the United States in 1864, served in the Union Army, and became a reporter in St. Louis. He bought the St. Louis Dispatch and Post in 1878 and the New York World in 1883, raising its circulation dramatically with crusades, illustrations and publicity stunts, including the public campaign to fund the Statue of Liberty's pedestal.

Yellow journalism

His circulation war with William Randolph Hearst's New York Journal in the 1890s gave rise to sensational "yellow journalism", which helped inflame opinion before the Spanish-American War.

Legacy

Pulitzer endowed Columbia University's journalism school and the Pulitzer Prizes, first awarded in 1917, which remain among the most prestigious honours in American journalism and letters.
