Harald Fairhair

Harald Fairhair (Old Norse: Haraldr Hárfagri)
c. 850 – c. 932
First King of Norway

Harald Fairhair (c. 850–c. 932) is regarded in Norwegian tradition as the first King of a unified Norway. The sagas, written centuries after his lifetime, credit him with conquering the petty kingdoms of Norway through a series of campaigns culminating in the Battle of Hafrsfjord, conventionally dated to 872.

Legend

According to Heimskringla, Harald vowed not to cut or comb his hair until he had subjected all of Norway to his rule, in order to win the hand of Gyda Eiriksdatter; after ten years unkempt he was nicknamed "Shockhead" (Tanglehair), and only after his victory had his hair dressed, earning the epithet Fairhair. His unification is said to have driven many independent-minded chieftains to emigrate to Iceland, contributing to its settlement.

Historicity

Modern historians treat the saga account cautiously: contemporary skaldic poems confirm a King Harald who fought at Hafrsfjord, but the extent of his realm was likely limited mainly to the southwestern coast, and full unification took generations. He was succeeded by sons including Eric Bloodaxe and Haakon the Good.
