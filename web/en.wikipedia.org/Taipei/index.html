Taipei (Mandarin: 臺北 / 台北, Táiběi), officially Taipei City, is the capital and a special municipality of Taiwan (the Republic of China). Located in the Taipei Basin in northern Taiwan, it is the political, economic, educational and cultural centre of the island, and forms the core of a metropolitan area of about seven million people together with New Taipei City and Keelung.

History

The basin was originally inhabited by the Ketagalan, one of the Taiwanese indigenous peoples. Han Chinese settlement began in the early 18th century, and the walled city of Taipei was built under Qing rule in the 1880s, becoming the provincial capital. Under Japanese rule (1895–1945) the city, called Taihoku, was modernised. In 1949, after the Kuomintang's defeat in the Chinese Civil War, the Republic of China government relocated to Taipei, which has remained its seat ever since.

Landmarks and culture

Taipei 101, completed in 2004, was the world's tallest building until 2010 and remains the city's emblem. Other landmarks include the National Palace Museum, holding one of the world's largest collections of Chinese art; the Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall; Longshan Temple; and the Shilin Night Market. The city is known for its night markets, hot springs in Beitou, and the surrounding Yangmingshan National Park.

Transport

The Taipei Metro (MRT), opened in 1996, is the backbone of public transport, supplemented by buses, the Taiwan High Speed Rail terminus at Taipei Main Station, and Songshan Airport for domestic and regional flights; most international flights use Taoyuan International Airport.
