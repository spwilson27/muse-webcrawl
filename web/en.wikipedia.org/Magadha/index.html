Magadha

Magadha was an ancient Indian kingdom and one of the sixteen Mahajanapadas ("great kingdoms") of the Second Urbanization period, located in the eastern Ganges plain in what is now Bihar and parts of Bengal and Uttar Pradesh. Its early capitals were Girivraja (Rajgir) and later Pataliputra (modern Patna).

Rise to power

Blessed with fertile alluvial soil, iron deposits, elephants, and control of river trade on the Ganges, Magadha successively expanded under the Haryanka dynasty (including the kings Bimbisara and Ajatashatru), the Shishunaga dynasty, and the Nandas, becoming the dominant power of northern India. Magadha was the heartland of early Buddhism and Jainism: the Buddha spent much of his life there, and Mahavira, founder of Jainism, was associated with the region.

Empires

Magadha formed the core of the first great Indian empires. Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nandas around 321 BCE and founded the Mauryan Empire, which under Ashoka came to rule most of the subcontinent from Pataliputra. Centuries later, the Gupta Empire (c. 320–550 CE), also centred on Magadha, presided over a celebrated age of art, science, and literature. The region's great monastic university of Nalanda flourished under later dynasties.

Decline

After the Guptas, Magadha's political centrality faded, though it remained a populous and culturally important region under the Palas and subsequent rulers, and its name survives in the historic Magadh division of Bihar.
