Aulus Vitellius (AD 15 – AD 69) was Roman emperor for eight months in AD 69, the "Year of the Four Emperors." A bon vivant from a rising senatorial family, he had been a companion of emperors Caligula, Claudius, and Nero, and was appointed commander in Germania Inferior by the emperor Galba in late 68.

Reign

On 2 January 69 his legions proclaimed him emperor. His generals Caecina and Valens marched on Italy and defeated the emperor Otho at the First Battle of Bedriacum (April 69), after which Otho took his own life. Vitellius entered Rome and ruled indulgently, famed in hostile sources (Tacitus, Suetonius, Cassius Dio) for gluttony, banquets, and cruelty, and he disbanded the Praetorian Guard, replacing it with his own men. Meanwhile the eastern legions proclaimed their general Vespasian emperor on 1 July 69. Vespasian's forces under Antonius Primus invaded Italy, crushed Vitellius's army at the Second Battle of Bedriacum (October), and fought their way into Rome.

Death

Vitellius attempted to abdicate but was prevented; on 20 December 69 he was dragged from hiding by soldiers, tortured, killed, and his body thrown into the Tiber. Vespasian's accession ended the civil war and founded the Flavian dynasty.
