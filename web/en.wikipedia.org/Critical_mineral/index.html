Critical mineral

Critical minerals (or critical raw materials) are mineral resources considered essential to a nation's economy, technology, or national security, whose supply may be at risk due to geological scarcity, concentration of production or processing in a few countries, or geopolitical factors. Governments maintain official lists of critical minerals to guide industrial policy, stockpiling, permitting, and research into substitutes and recycling. Commonly listed minerals include lithium, cobalt, nickel, graphite, manganese, and the rare-earth elements, all of which are vital to batteries, electric vehicles, wind turbines, and defence technologies.

Lithium, essential for rechargeable batteries, is a widely designated critical mineral

Definitions and lists

There is no single international definition; criticality is assessed relative to a country's industrial needs and import dependence. The United States Geological Survey maintains a list of 50 critical minerals (expanded in 2022), the European Union publishes a list of critical raw materials (34 in its 2023 assessment), and countries including Canada, Australia, Japan, and India have adopted their own lists. Assessments generally weigh economic importance against supply risk, and are periodically revised.

Supply concentration

Production and especially processing of many critical minerals are highly concentrated. China dominates the refining of rare earths, graphite, and much lithium and cobalt processing; the Democratic Republic of the Congo supplies most of the world's mined cobalt; and Indonesia has become the largest source of nickel. This concentration has prompted consuming countries to pursue "friend-shoring", strategic partnerships, and domestic mining and refining projects.

Energy transition demand

The deployment of clean-energy technologies is expected to multiply demand for several minerals: the International Energy Agency estimates that an electric car requires about six times the mineral inputs of a conventional car, and that overall mineral demand for clean energy could grow several-fold by 2040 under climate-driven scenarios. Concerns about price volatility, environmental damage from mining, and deep-sea mining proposals accompany efforts to expand supply.

Policy responses

Responses include subsidies and tax credits for domestic production (such as those in the US Inflation Reduction Act), the EU Critical Raw Materials Act setting benchmarks for domestic extraction, processing, and recycling, export controls by producing countries, strategic stockpiles, and research into alternative chemistries such as lithium-iron-phosphate and sodium-ion batteries that avoid the scarcest inputs.
