Deer farming

Deer farming is the raising and breeding of deer species in an agricultural setting for the production of venison, velvet antler, hides and breeding stock. It is practised on a commercial scale in New Zealand, which has the world's largest farmed deer herd, as well as in Europe, North America, China and Australia.

Species and products

Red deer are the most commonly farmed species, followed by fallow deer, elk (wapiti), sika and reindeer. Velvet antler — immature antler removed while still growing — is sold mainly into Asian traditional-medicine markets, while venison is marketed as a lean, premium red meat. Farmed deer require tall, strong fencing and specialised handling facilities because of their agility and flightiness.

Industry

New Zealand's industry began in the 1970s, partly in response to the ecological damage caused by introduced wild deer, and grew into a major export sector. Deer farming has attracted controversy where escaped animals establish feral populations, and in North America over the spread of chronic wasting disease among captive herds.
