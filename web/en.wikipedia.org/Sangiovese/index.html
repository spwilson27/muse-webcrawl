Sangiovese is a red Italian wine grape variety that derives its name from the Latin sanguis Jovis, "the blood of Jupiter". It is most famous as the main component of the blended Chianti wines of Tuscany, and as the sole grape of Brunello di Montalcino and the primary grape in Vino Nobile di Montepulciano and Morellino di Scansano. Sangiovese is one of the most widely planted grape varieties in Italy.

History and clones

The variety has been grown in Tuscany since at least the 16th century and is believed to be much older. DNA profiling has suggested parentage involving Ciliegiolo and Calabrese di Montenuovo. Sangiovese exists in numerous clonal forms, broadly grouped into the large-berried Sangiovese Grosso (including the Brunello clone used in Montalcino and the Prugnolo Gentile of Montepulciano) and the smaller-berried Sangiovese Piccolo typical of Chianti Classico.

Viticulture and wines

Sangiovese is a late-ripening, thin-skinned grape that thrives in warm, well-drained, limestone-rich soils and can produce high yields that must be controlled for quality. Its wines are typically ruby in colour with high acidity, moderate to firm tannins, and aromas of red cherry, plum, dried herbs, tea leaf, and earth. Young, everyday versions are fresh and food-friendly, while the best riservas from Montalcino and Chianti Classico can age for decades. In the late 20th century Sangiovese was also blended with international varieties in the "Super Tuscan" movement and planted in California, Australia, and elsewhere.
