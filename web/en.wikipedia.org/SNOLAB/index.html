SNOLAB

SNOLAB is a Canadian underground physics laboratory located about 2 km (1.2 mi) beneath the surface in Vale's Creighton Mine near Sudbury, Ontario. It grew out of the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory (SNO) experiment and is the deepest clean-room facility in the world, dedicated primarily to neutrino and dark matter physics. The rock overburden reduces the cosmic-ray muon flux by a factor of about 50 million compared with the surface.

SNOLAB's underground experimental halls in the Creighton Mine.

History

The original SNO experiment, built in the 1990s to resolve the solar neutrino problem, demonstrated that neutrinos oscillate between flavours — work that earned Arthur B. McDonald a share of the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics. The SNOLAB expansion, completed around 2009–2011, excavated additional laboratory space adjacent to the original SNO cavity, operating the entire complex as an ultra-clean (Class-2000 cleanroom) facility in which all personnel and equipment enter through shower and airlock procedures.

Experiments

Resident experiments have included SNO+ (a successor to SNO using liquid scintillator to search for neutrinoless double beta decay), DEAP-3600 and DarkSide dark matter searches with argon, the PICO bubble-chamber experiments, the HALO supernova neutrino detector, and the SuperCDMS dark matter experiment. SNOLAB also hosts biology and geology work, including studies of life in deep subsurface environments.
