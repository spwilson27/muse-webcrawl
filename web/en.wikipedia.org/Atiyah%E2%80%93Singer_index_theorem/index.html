The Atiyah–Singer index theorem, proved by Michael Atiyah and Isadore Singer in 1963, is a landmark result linking analysis and topology: for an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold, the analytical index — the difference between the dimensions of the kernel and cokernel of the operator — equals the topological index, computed from characteristic classes of the manifold and the operator's symbol.

Statement and consequences

In its best-known form, the theorem computes the index of a Dirac-type operator as an integral over the manifold of a product of the Â-genus and Chern character. It unifies and vastly generalises earlier theorems: the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem of algebraic geometry, and the Hirzebruch signature theorem all emerge as special cases.

Proofs and impact

The original proof used cobordism and K-theory; later proofs employed the heat equation and supersymmetry ideas imported from physics, foreshadowing deep exchanges between geometry and quantum field theory. Atiyah and Singer received the Abel Prize in 2004 in large part for this work.
