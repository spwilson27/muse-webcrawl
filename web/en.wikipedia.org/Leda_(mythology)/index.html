Leda (mythology)

Leda is a figure in Greek mythology, queen of Sparta as wife of King Tyndareus. She is famous for the myth in which Zeus, in the form of a swan, seduced or raped her.

Myth

From the union, in various versions, Leda produced eggs from which hatched Helen of Troy, Clytemnestra, and the Dioscuri, Castor and Pollux — with accounts differing over which children were Zeus's and which Tyndareus's. Helen's abduction by Paris precipitated the Trojan War.

In art

"Leda and the Swan" is a recurring subject in classical, Renaissance and later art and literature, including a sonnet by W. B. Yeats.
