The War in Afghanistan was an armed conflict that began on 7 October 2001 when the United States and its allies invaded Afghanistan to topple the Taliban regime, which had harboured Osama bin Laden and al-Qaeda following the September 11 attacks. It ended on 30 August 2021 with the completion of the US withdrawal, weeks after the Taliban seized Kabul and the US-backed Islamic Republic of Afghanistan collapsed. At twenty years, it was the longest war in American history.

Invasion and fall of the Taliban

Operation Enduring Freedom began with air strikes and special forces working alongside the Northern Alliance. Kabul fell on 13 November 2001 and Kandahar in December; bin Laden escaped at the Battle of Tora Bora. The Bonn Agreement of December 2001 created an interim administration under Hamid Karzai, and the UN-authorised International Security Assistance Force (ISAF) was deployed to secure Kabul. A new constitution was adopted in 2004 and Karzai was elected president.

Insurgency

From about 2005 the Taliban, regrouped in Pakistan's tribal areas, waged an escalating insurgency using suicide bombings, roadside bombs and shadow governance. The war widened as ISAF expanded across the country, and violence intensified alongside the parallel war in Iraq. In 2009–2011 President Barack Obama ordered a "surge" that raised US troop numbers to about 100,000; on 2 May 2011 US Navy SEALs killed Osama bin Laden in Abbottabad, Pakistan.

Transition and drawdown

ISAF formally ended its combat mission on 28 December 2014, handing security to the Afghan National Defence and Security Forces, while a smaller NATO Resolute Support mission and US Operation Freedom's Sentinel continued training and counterterrorism. The Taliban nevertheless steadily gained territory, and by 2020 controlled or contested much of rural Afghanistan.

Doha agreement and final offensive

On 29 February 2020 the United States and the Taliban signed the Doha Agreement, committing the US to withdraw all forces within fourteen months in exchange for Taliban counterterrorism guarantees. The Taliban largely stopped attacking coalition forces but continued fighting the Afghan government. In April 2021 President Joe Biden announced a complete withdrawal by September. The Taliban launched a nationwide offensive in May 2021; provincial capitals fell in rapid succession in August, President Ashraf Ghani fled on 15 August as the Taliban entered Kabul, and a chaotic airlift evacuated over 120,000 people from Kabul airport — marred by a suicide bombing at Abbey Gate that killed 13 US service members and some 170 Afghans. The last US aircraft departed on 30 August 2021.

Costs and legacy

Estimates of the human cost include more than 170,000 direct war deaths in Afghanistan — among them roughly 2,400 US service members, tens of thousands of Afghan security personnel and civilians — with millions displaced. The United States spent over $2 trillion on the war by some accounting. The Taliban restored the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan, imposing severe restrictions, particularly on women and girls, while the economy contracted sharply and armed resistance persisted in pockets such as the Panjshir Valley.
