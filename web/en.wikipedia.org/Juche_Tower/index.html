Juche Tower

The Juche Tower (officially the Tower of the Juche Idea) is a monument in Pyongyang, North Korea, completed in 1982 to commemorate President Kim Il Sung's 70th birthday. It stands on the east bank of the Taedong River, directly opposite Kim Il Sung Square.

Description

The tower is 170 metres (560 ft) tall — a 150-metre stone spire topped by a 20-metre flaming torch — and was for a time the tallest stone tower in the world, marginally exceeding the San Jacinto Monument in Texas. It is built of 25,550 blocks of white stone, one for each day of Kim Il Sung's life up to his 70th birthday. The torch is illuminated at night.

At the base stands a group of statues representing workers, peasants and intellectuals, and the surrounding plaza displays 82 friendship plaques sent by foreign supporters of the Juche idea. An elevator takes visitors to an observation terrace below the torch.
