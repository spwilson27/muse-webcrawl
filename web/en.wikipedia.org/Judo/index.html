Judo

Judo (Japanese: 柔道, jūdō, "the gentle way") is a modern Japanese martial art and combat sport created in 1882 by Kanō Jigorō. It was developed from the older grappling art of jujutsu, removing its most dangerous techniques and emphasising throws (nage-waza), pins, joint locks, and strangles (katame-waza) that can be practised at full force in free sparring (randori). Judo became an Olympic sport for men at the Tokyo Games of 1964 and for women in 1992, and it is practised worldwide under the International Judo Federation.

History

Kanō Jigorō, an educator who had studied Tenjin Shin'yō-ryū and Kitō-ryū jujutsu, founded the Kodokan school in 1882 at Eishō-ji temple in Tokyo. His guiding principles were seiryoku zen'yō ("maximum efficient use of energy") and jita kyōei ("mutual welfare and benefit"). Kodokan judoka demonstrated the art's effectiveness in celebrated matches against jujutsu schools in the 1880s, and judo was adopted in Japanese schools and police training. Kanō, the first Asian member of the International Olympic Committee, promoted judo internationally throughout his life.

Technique and competition

Practitioners (judoka) wear a heavy cotton uniform (judogi) and train on mats (tatami). A contest is won outright by ippon — a throw that lands the opponent largely on their back with force and control, a pin (osaekomi) held for 20 seconds, or submission by strangle or armlock. Lesser scores (waza-ari) accumulate, and penalties (shido) punish passivity and rule infringements. Grades progress through coloured belts (kyū) to black-belt degrees (dan). Formal pre-arranged sequences (kata) preserve older techniques excluded from competition.

Influence

Judo profoundly influenced other grappling arts: Brazilian jiu-jitsu descends from the judo taught in Brazil by Mitsuyo Maeda, and the Soviet/Russian art of sambo drew heavily on it. Judo techniques are foundational in mixed martial arts, and the sport is practised by millions in nearly 200 countries; its most decorated Olympian is Japan's Tadahiro Nomura, with three consecutive gold medals.
