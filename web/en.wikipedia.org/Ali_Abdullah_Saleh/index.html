Ali Abdullah Saleh

Ali Abdullah Saleh (Arabic: علي عبدالله صالح; 21 March 1947 – 4 December 2017) was a Yemeni politician and military officer who served as the first President of the Republic of Yemen from Yemeni unification in 1990 until his resignation in 2012, following the Yemeni revolution. Before unification he had been President of the Yemen Arab Republic (North Yemen) from 1978. Ruling for more than three decades in total, he was the longest-serving leader in Yemen's modern history, famed for describing governing Yemen as "dancing on the heads of snakes."

Early life and rise to power

Saleh was born in the village of Bayt al-Ahmar, near Sanaa, into the Sanhan tribe of the Hashid tribal confederation. He joined the armed forces of the Yemen Arab Republic as a young man and rose through the ranks during the North Yemen Civil War, becoming a tank commander and then a provincial military governor. After the assassination of President Ahmad al-Ghashmi in June 1978, Saleh — then a colonel — was elected president by the People's Constituent Assembly on 17 July 1978, despite having little formal education and a reputation as an outsider to the traditional elite.

Presidency

Saleh consolidated power by balancing Yemen's tribes, military factions, and Islamist movements against one another, distributing patronage from oil revenues and state offices. On 22 May 1990, North and South Yemen unified, and Saleh became president of the unified republic, with southern leader Ali Salem al-Beidh as vice president. Relations with the south collapsed into the 1994 civil war, which Saleh's forces won, cementing his rule.

He was re-elected in 1999 and again in 2006 in Yemen's first direct presidential elections. His tenure saw economic liberalization, a long counterinsurgency against the Houthi movement in Saada (the Saada wars, 2004–2010), the rise of al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula, and close counterterrorism cooperation with the United States after the bombing of USS Cole in Aden in 2000 and the attacks of 11 September 2001.

Downfall and death

Mass protests inspired by the Arab Spring erupted in January 2011. In June 2011 Saleh was gravely wounded in a bombing of the presidential palace mosque and evacuated to Saudi Arabia for treatment. Under a Gulf Cooperation Council initiative, he transferred power to Vice President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi in February 2012 in exchange for immunity from prosecution.

Saleh remained a powerful political broker, and in 2014 his loyalists in the army allied with the Houthis to seize Sanaa, triggering the Yemeni civil war and a Saudi-led military intervention in 2015. In late November 2017 the alliance collapsed into fighting in Sanaa; on 4 December 2017 Saleh was killed by Houthi fighters while attempting to flee the capital. He was succeeded as leader of the General People's Congress by his son Ahmed Ali Saleh's factional rivals and supporters contested the party's future.
