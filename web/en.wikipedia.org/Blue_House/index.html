Blue House

The Blue House (Korean: 청와대, Cheong Wa Dae) is a public park that formerly served as the presidential office and residence complex of South Korea. Located in Jongno District, Seoul, it was traditionally the executive office and official residence of the president of South Korea from 1948 to 2022. Its name comes from the roughly 150,000 blue tiles covering the roof of the main building.

History

The site, backed by Mount Bugak, occupies part of the former grounds of Gyeongbok Palace and was considered auspicious in feng shui terms. It was the site of a royal garden in the Joseon era and, during Japanese rule, the residence of the Governor-General. After independence it became the presidential residence, known first as Gyeongmudae. President Yun Bo-seon renamed it Cheong Wa Dae in 1960. The present main office building was completed in 1991 under President Roh Tae-woo.

1968 raid

In January 1968, North Korean commandos (Unit 124) reached within a few hundred metres of the Blue House in an assassination attempt on President Park Chung-hee before being intercepted; the incident nearly triggered a major conflict.

Opening to the public

President Yoon Suk-yeol relocated the presidential office to the Ministry of National Defense building in Yongsan in May 2022, and the Blue House complex was opened to the public as a park, receiving millions of visitors. The complex includes the main office building, the Yeongbingwan guest house, the Chunchugwan press centre, and extensive gardens.
