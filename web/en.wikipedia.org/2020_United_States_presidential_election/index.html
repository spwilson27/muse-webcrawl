2020 United States presidential election

The 2020 United States presidential election was the 59th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 3, 2020. The Democratic ticket of former vice president Joe Biden and California senator Kamala Harris defeated the incumbent Republican president Donald Trump and vice president Mike Pence.

Background

The election took place during the COVID-19 pandemic, which led to a record number of ballots cast early and by mail. Biden secured the Democratic nomination after defeating Bernie Sanders in the primaries; the general election campaign centred on the pandemic response, the economy, healthcare and racial justice protests.

Results

Biden won 306 electoral votes to Trump's 232 and received over 81 million popular votes, the most ever cast for a candidate. Trump carried Florida, Ohio and Texas, while Biden flipped Arizona, Georgia, Michigan, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin. Turnout was the highest since 1900 as a share of eligible voters.

Aftermath

Trump refused to concede and made claims of widespread voter fraud that were rejected by courts and election officials. The certification of the results was disrupted by the attack on the US Capitol on January 6, 2021. Biden was inaugurated as the 46th president on January 20, 2021.
