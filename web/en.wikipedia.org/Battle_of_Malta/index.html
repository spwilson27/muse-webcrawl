Battle of Malta

This article is about the naval battle of 1283. For other uses, see Siege of Malta.

The Battle of Malta took place on 8 June 1283 in the entrance to the Grand Harbour of Malta, during the War of the Sicilian Vespers. An Aragonese fleet of galleys, commanded by Roger of Lauria, attacked and defeated a fleet of Angevin-Provençal galleys commanded by the admirals Guillaume Cornut and Barthélemy Bonvin. The victory confirmed Aragonese naval supremacy in the western Mediterranean and secured Malta for the Crown of Aragon.

Background

After the Sicilian Vespers uprising of 1282, Peter III of Aragon claimed the Sicilian crown against Charles I of Anjou. Malta, held by an Angevin garrison at the Castrum Maris in Birgu, had risen against the Angevins, who retained the fortress. An Aragonese relief and a Provençal relieving squadron converged on the island in the summer of 1283.

The battle

Roger of Lauria arrived at Malta by night and stationed his galleys in a line across the entrance of the Grand Harbour. Shortly before dawn he sent a boat in to challenge the Angevin fleet, which was anchored inside the harbour, to come out. Cornut and Bonvin accepted, expecting their heavier complements of crossbowmen to prevail. Lauria let the Angevins exhaust their missiles at long range, then closed once their ammunition ran low. The ensuing boarding fight was one-sided: the Catalan and Sicilian almogàvers — lightly armoured infantry famed for their aggression — swept the Provençal decks. Cornut was killed, tradition says by Lauria himself in single combat; Bonvin escaped with a handful of galleys.

Aftermath

Some ten Angevin galleys were captured and many more sunk or disabled, with heavy losses among their crews, while Aragonese losses were comparatively light. The Angevin garrison in Malta soon capitulated, and the island passed under Aragonese rule, beginning more than two centuries of integration into the Catalan-Aragonese maritime world. Together with Lauria's later victories at Naples (1284) and Les Formigues (1285), Malta established his reputation as the outstanding admiral of the age.
