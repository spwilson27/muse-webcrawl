The Imperial Japanese Army (IJA; Japanese: 大日本帝国陸軍, Dai-Nippon Teikoku Rikugun) was the official ground-based armed force of the Empire of Japan from 1868 to 1945. It was controlled by the Imperial Japanese Army General Staff Office and the Ministry of the Army, both nominally subordinate to the Emperor of Japan. Together with the Imperial Japanese Navy, it formed the Imperial Japanese Armed Forces.

History

Origins and modernisation

The army was founded during the Meiji Restoration, replacing the samurai levies of the feudal domains with a national conscript force (Conscription Ordinance of 1873). Modelled first on the French and then, decisively after 1885 under the influence of the Prussian adviser Jakob Meckel, on the German army, it proved itself by defeating Qing China in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) and Tsarist Russia in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05) — the first victory of an Asian power over a European great power in modern warfare. Japan annexed Korea in 1910, and the army intervened in Siberia during the Russian Civil War.

Militarism and war in China

In the 1930s the army, particularly the Kwantung Army in Manchuria, acted increasingly independently of civilian government: the Mukden Incident (1931) led to the conquest of Manchuria and the creation of Manchukuo, while assassinations and coup attempts (notably the February 26 Incident, 1936) intimidated civilian politicians. Full-scale war with China began in 1937 after the Marco Polo Bridge Incident. The army's advance was accompanied by atrocities including the Nanjing Massacre (1937–38), the "Three Alls Policy" in North China, and the biological warfare programme of Unit 731.

Pacific War

In December 1941 the army, with the navy, launched offensives across Southeast Asia and the Pacific, conquering Malaya, Singapore, the Dutch East Indies, Burma and the Philippines. From 1942–43 the tide turned; the army fought costly defensive campaigns on Guadalcanal, in New Guinea, Burma and the Philippines, and by 1945 was preparing for a decisive defence of the home islands. Following the Emperor's surrender broadcast of 15 August 1945, the army was demobilised under Allied occupation, with some 6 million men under arms at the end.

Organisation and doctrine

The IJA emphasised the offensive, spiritual mobilisation (seishin), night attacks and infiltration, often underestimating logistics and firepower; its standard rifle was the Arisaka Type 99, and its tanks remained light compared with Allied designs. The military police arm, the Kempeitai, exercised wide powers over soldiers and civilians in occupied territories. After 1945, senior officers were tried at the Tokyo Trial for war crimes, and in 1954 Japan established the Ground Self-Defense Force as a non-successor defence organisation.
