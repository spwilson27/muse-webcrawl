Vickrey auction

A Vickrey auction is a type of sealed-bid auction in which bidders submit written bids without knowing the bids of the other participants, the highest bidder wins, but pays the price of the second-highest bid. It is named for William Vickrey, who described it in a 1961 paper; he later received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1996.

[Image: Bidders submitting sealed envelopes into a box at a Vickrey auction.]

Properties

The Vickrey auction is strategically equivalent to the English auction: the dominant strategy for each bidder is to bid their true valuation, since the price they pay is set by the second-highest bid rather than their own. This makes it the canonical example of a mechanism design success and the basis of the generalized Vickrey–Clarke–Groves auction for multiple goods.

Practical use

Though theoretically elegant, second-price auctions are rarely used in the open because bidders mistrust the auctioneer to report the true second bid, and because sellers dislike leaving money on the table. Variants are used in online advertising auctions.
