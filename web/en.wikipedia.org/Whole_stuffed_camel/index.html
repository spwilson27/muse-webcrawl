Whole stuffed camel

Whole stuffed camel is a dish consisting of a whole roasted camel stuffed with a sheep, chickens, fish and eggs, reputedly served at Bedouin wedding feasts and other celebrations in the Middle East. It is often cited as one of the largest dishes in the world and appears in the Guinness Book of Records tradition as the largest item on any menu, though its frequency is frequently exaggerated in retellings.

Description

Published recipes describe a nested preparation: boiled eggs are stuffed into fish, the fish into chickens, the chickens into a roasted sheep, and the sheep into a whole camel, seasoned with rice and spices and roasted over charcoal pits. The concept resembles the Western turducken and medieval "cockentrice" banquets of nested roasts.

Sources and skepticism

The dish entered Western awareness through travel and food writing, including references in Richard Burton's accounts of Arabia and mid-20th-century cookbooks; it was popularized in English by the "I Hate to Cook Book" tradition and by T. K. Mahadevan's compilation. Food historians note that while stuffed roasts are genuinely served at large Gulf celebrations, the full camel version is exceptionally rare and partly legendary.
