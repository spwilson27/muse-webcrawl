Alkyne

In organic chemistry, an alkyne is an unsaturated hydrocarbon containing at least one carbon—carbon triple bond. The simplest acyclic alkynes with only one triple bond and no other functional groups form a homologous series with the general chemical formula CnH2n−2. Alkynes are traditionally known as acetylenes, although the name acetylene also refers specifically to C2H2, known formally as ethyne using IUPAC nomenclature.

Unlike alkanes, which have a tetrahedral geometry at carbon, the carbons of a triple bond are sp-hybridised and the C≡C–C bond angle is 180°, giving the molecule a linear geometry around the triple bond.

Examples

The first few members of the series are acetylene (ethyne), propyne, the butynes, and pentynes. Internal alkynes have the triple bond within the chain, while terminal alkynes have it at the end; terminal alkynes are weakly acidic, with a pKa of about 25.

Synthesis

Alkynes are commonly prepared by the double dehydrohalogenation of vicinal or geminal dihalides using a strong base, or by the alkylation of acetylide anions derived from terminal alkynes. On an industrial scale, acetylene is produced by the partial combustion of methane or as a by-product of steam cracking of hydrocarbons; historically it was made from calcium carbide and water.

Reactions

Alkynes undergo many addition reactions across the triple bond, including hydrogenation (which can be stopped at the cis-alkene stage with Lindlar's catalyst), hydrohalogenation, hydration to ketones, and hydroboration. Terminal alkynes participate in coupling reactions such as the Sonogashira coupling and in the copper-catalysed azide–alkyne cycloaddition ("click chemistry").
