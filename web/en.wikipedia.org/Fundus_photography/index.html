Fundus photography

Fundus photography involves photographing the rear of the eye, known as the fundus, using a specialised fundus camera. The resulting images document the retina, optic disc, macula, and retinal blood vessels, and are used to diagnose and monitor eye diseases.

Technique

A fundus camera is a low-power microscope with an attached camera designed to image the interior surface of the eye through the pupil, often after pharmacologic dilation. Images typically capture a field of 30 to 50 degrees; widefield systems can exceed 100 degrees. Fluorescein angiography is a variant using injected dye to visualise retinal circulation.

Clinical uses

Fundus photography is central to screening for diabetic retinopathy, and to assessing age-related macular degeneration, glaucoma, hypertensive retinopathy, and retinal vascular occlusions. Teleophthalmology programmes increasingly use non-mydriatic cameras with automated image grading, including AI systems approved for autonomous diabetic retinopathy detection.
