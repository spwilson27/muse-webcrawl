Fillet (mechanics)

In mechanical engineering, a fillet is a rounding of an interior or exterior corner of a part design. An interior or exterior corner, with an angle or type of bevel, is called a "chamfer." Fillet geometry is often applied where two surfaces meet — for example, where a vertical shaft meets a horizontal flange, or where the wall of a casting meets its base.

Purpose

Fillets are used primarily to reduce stress concentration. Sharp internal corners act as stress raisers where fatigue cracks initiate; replacing them with a concave fillet of adequate radius spreads stress over a larger area and substantially increases fatigue life. Fillets also improve the flow of molten metal in casting, reduce turbulence in fluid passages, make parts safer to handle, and ease ejection from molds. On exterior corners, a rounding is more precisely called a round or radius, while fillet properly refers to interior corners — though usage varies.

Design considerations

Larger fillet radii reduce stress concentration more but may conflict with mating parts or add material and machining cost. Engineers select radii using stress-concentration factor charts and finite-element analysis. In CAD software (such as SolidWorks or Fusion 360), the fillet is a standard modeling feature applied by selecting edges and specifying a radius; constant-radius and variable-radius fillets are both common.

Welding

A fillet weld is a weld of approximately triangular cross-section joining two surfaces at roughly right angles, as in a lap or T-joint — the most common weld type in structural work. The term is distinct from, but related to, the geometric fillet.
