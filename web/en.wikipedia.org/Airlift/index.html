Airlift

An airlift is the organized transport of supplies, equipment, or personnel by aircraft, usually in military operations or disaster relief. Airlifts deliver cargo where ground or sea transport is too slow, impossible, or cut off, at the cost of high expense and limited tonnage per flight.

Famous airlifts

The Berlin Airlift of 1948–49 supplied West Berlin during the Soviet blockade, flying in millions of tonnes of food and coal over 15 months. During the Second World War, Allied aircraft flew "the Hump" over the Himalayas to supply China. Later operations include the 1973 Nickel Grass airlift to Israel, coalition airlifts in the Gulf War, and humanitarian airlifts and airdrops in Bosnia, Ethiopia, and other crises.

Types

Strategic airlift moves cargo between theatres using large transports such as the C-17 Globemaster III and C-5 Galaxy, while tactical airlift operates within a theatre using aircraft such as the C-130 Hercules, including airdrop delivery by parachute. Civilian airlift supports disaster response, evacuations, and remote communities.
