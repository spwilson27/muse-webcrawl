Home equity loan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A home equity loan is a type of loan in which the borrower uses the equity of their house as collateral. The loan amount is determined by the value of the property, and the value of the property is determined by an appraiser from the lending institution. Home equity loans are often used to finance major expenses such as home repairs, medical bills, or college education. A home equity loan creates a lien against the borrower's house and reduces actual home equity.

Most home equity loans require good to excellent credit history, reasonable loan-to-value and combined loan-to-value ratios. They are generally offered at market interest rates that are lower than unsecured consumer credit, because the lender's risk is reduced by the security of the property; however, the borrower's home is at risk of foreclosure if payments are not made.

Types

Home equity loans are commonly divided into two types. Closed-end loans deliver a lump sum at closing, repaid over a fixed term with fixed monthly payments. Open-end loans, usually called home equity lines of credit (HELOCs), are revolving credit arrangements in which the borrower may draw funds up to an approved limit during a draw period, typically paying interest only during that phase before a repayment period begins. Home equity loans are distinct from home equity lines of credit in the predictability of payments, and from reverse mortgages, which are available to older homeowners and require no repayment until the borrower leaves the home.

History and regulation

Home equity lending expanded rapidly in the United States in the 1980s after changes to the tax code preserved the deductibility of mortgage interest while ending deductions for other consumer interest. Such loans figured prominently in the subprime mortgage boom of the 2000s, when rising house prices encouraged heavy borrowing against equity; the subsequent housing crash left many borrowers in negative equity. In the United States, the interest deduction for home equity debt was suspended for most purposes by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, remaining available only where loan proceeds are used to buy, build, or substantially improve the home securing the loan.
