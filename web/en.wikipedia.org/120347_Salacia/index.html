120347 Salacia

120347 Salacia, provisional designation 2004 SB60, is a large trans-Neptunian object in the Kuiper belt and a likely dwarf planet. It forms a binary system with its moon, Actaea. Salacia was discovered on 22 September 2004 by American astronomers Henry Roe, Michael Brown and Kristina Barkume at the Palomar Observatory in California.

Orbit and classification

Salacia belongs to the classical Kuiper belt objects, sometimes called cubewanos, which orbit the Sun beyond Neptune on relatively circular, low-inclination orbits not in resonance with Neptune. Its semi-major axis is about 42 astronomical units, and it completes one orbit in roughly 274 years.

Physical properties

Observations with the Hubble Space Telescope and thermal measurements indicate a diameter of around 846 km, making Salacia one of the largest known trans-Neptunian objects. Its surface is relatively dark and spectrally fairly neutral, with weak evidence of water ice. Its moon Actaea, discovered in 2006, is about 290 km across and orbits Salacia every 5.5 days; measurements of the binary orbit allow the system's mass and density to be estimated.

Name

The object is named after Salacia, the Roman goddess of salt water and consort of Neptune; its moon is named for Actaea, one of the Nereids of Greek mythology.
