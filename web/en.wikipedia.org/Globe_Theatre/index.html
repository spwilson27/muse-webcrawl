The Globe Theatre was a theatre in London associated with William Shakespeare. It was built in 1599 at Southwark, close to the south bank of the Thames, by Shakespeare's playing company, the Lord Chamberlain's Men. It was destroyed by fire on 29 June 1613, rebuilt in the following year, closed by ordinance in 1642, and demolished in 1644.

History

The Globe was built using timber from an earlier playhouse, The Theatre in Shoreditch, which the company dismantled and ferried across the Thames after a dispute with its landlord. The polygonal, open-air amphitheatre was about 30 metres in diameter, three storeys high, and could hold around 3,000 spectators, with "groundlings" standing in the yard for a penny and wealthier patrons in roofed galleries.

The fire of 1613 began during a performance of Henry VIII, when a stage cannon ignited the thatched roof; remarkably, no deaths were reported. The second Globe, roofed with tile, stood on the same foundations until the Puritan authorities closed London's theatres in 1642.

Layout

The stage projected into the central yard, with a stage roof (the "heavens") supported by pillars, a trapdoor to the "cellarage", an inner discovery space, and a balcony above. The theatre's flag bore the motto Totus mundus agit histrionem — "all the world plays the actor".

Modern reconstruction

A modern reconstruction, Shakespeare's Globe, opened in 1997 approximately 230 metres from the site of the original, based on scholarly research into Elizabethan building practice, and stages Shakespeare's plays in conditions approximating those of the original.
