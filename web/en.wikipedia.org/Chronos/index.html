Chronos

Chronos (Greek: Χρόνος, "time"), also spelled Khronos or Chronus, is the personification of time in Greek mythology and pre-Socratic philosophy. He is often depicted as a wise, long-bearded old man turning the wheel of the zodiac.

Mythology

Chronos is not to be confused with the Titan Cronus, father of Zeus, though the two were frequently conflated in later tradition — a confusion encouraged by the similarity of their names and by the interpretation of Cronus devouring his children as time devouring all things. In Orphic cosmogony, Chronos is a primordial deity who, together with Ananke (Necessity), produced the cosmic egg from which the world emerged. He was also equated with the Zoroastrian deity Zurvan.

Legacy

The image of "Father Time" — an elderly bearded figure with a scythe or hourglass — descends in part from Chronos merged with Cronus. The Greek word chronos gives English many time-related terms, including chronology, chronic, and chronometer. Philosophically, chronos denotes quantitative, sequential time, in contrast to kairos, the opportune or qualitative moment.
