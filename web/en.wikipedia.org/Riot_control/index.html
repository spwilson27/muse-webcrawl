Riot control

Riot control measures are used by law enforcement, military, paramilitary or security forces to control, disperse, and arrest people who are involved in a riot, unlawful assembly or unlawful protest. If a riot is spontaneous and irrational, actions which cause people to stop and think for a few seconds — such as loud noise or issuing instructions in a calm tone — can be enough to stop it. However, these methods usually fail when there is severe anger, or the riot is planned.

Tactics and equipment

Officers deployed for riot control commonly wear protective gear including helmets with visors, body armour, fire-resistant clothing, gas masks and riot shields, and carry batons. Typical formations include shield walls and baton charges intended to push crowds back or channel them. Mounted police and police dogs are also used in many jurisdictions.

Less-lethal weapons

Tools classed as less-lethal ("non-lethal" in older terminology) include tear gas (CS and CN agents), pepper spray, water cannon, rubber and plastic bullets, bean bag rounds, sponge grenades, long-range acoustic devices and dazzlers. Their use is controversial: projectiles and chemical agents have caused serious injuries and deaths, and international human-rights bodies have issued guidance urging proportionality, medical safeguards and restrictions on firing into crowds.

Legal frameworks

Many countries historically empowered officials to read a "Riot Act" proclamation ordering dispersal, after which remaining assemblies became criminal. Modern public-order law usually relies instead on offences of riot, violent disorder and unlawful assembly, combined with principles of necessity and proportionality in police use of force.
