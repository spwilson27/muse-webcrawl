The Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) is a private research university in Cambridge, Massachusetts, United States. Founded in 1861 by geologist William Barton Rogers in response to the increasing industrialisation of the United States, MIT adopted a European polytechnic model emphasising laboratory instruction in applied science and engineering. Its 166-acre campus, which moved across the Charles River from Boston in 1916, sits along the river opposite Boston's Back Bay.

Academics and research

MIT comprises five schools — Architecture and Planning; Engineering; Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences; Management (Sloan); and Science — plus the Schwarzman College of Computing. It is consistently ranked among the world's top universities, particularly in engineering, computer science, economics, and the natural sciences. Affiliated researchers have included scores of Nobel laureates, Turing Award winners, and Fields medallists.

Contributions

MIT laboratories have made landmark contributions including the development of radar at the Radiation Laboratory during the Second World War, early work in artificial intelligence and computer science at Project MAC and the AI Lab, the Media Lab, the Human Genome Project contributions of the Whitehead and Broad Institutes, and the first detection of gravitational waves with LIGO. MIT also played a founding role in open education through OpenCourseWare, which published course materials freely online beginning in 2002.

Culture

The institute is known for its demanding workload, a strong hacker and maker culture, elaborate student pranks ("hacks"), and the motto Mens et Manus, "mind and hand", symbolised by its seal.
