Atoms for Peace
Speech: Dwight D. Eisenhower, UN General Assembly, 8 December 1953
Outcome: Creation of the IAEA (1957); spread of research reactors and nuclear training

Atoms for Peace was the title of a speech delivered by United States President Dwight D. Eisenhower to the United Nations General Assembly on 8 December 1953. The speech proposed that nations possessing nuclear knowledge share technology and fissionable material for peaceful purposes under an international atomic energy agency, rather than stockpiling weapons alone.

Programme

The resulting programme supplied research reactors, fuel, training, and technical literature to dozens of countries, and led to the founding of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) in 1957. The United States built research reactors abroad, established an atomic library programme, and trained foreign scientists at its national laboratories and at the first International School of Nuclear Science and Engineering.

Legacy

Atoms for Peace accelerated the global spread of civilian nuclear power and medicine, but it also diffused skills and materials with dual-use potential; several recipient states later pursued weapons programmes. The initiative is regarded as a landmark of Cold War diplomacy and of Eisenhower's effort to temper the arms race with a vision of beneficial atomic energy. The phrase also lends its name to the IAEA's motto, "Atoms for Peace and Development."
