Government of South Korea

The Government of South Korea is the national government of the Republic of Korea, structured under the Constitution of 1987 into executive, legislative, and judicial branches. South Korea is a unitary presidential republic: the President is both head of state and head of government, elected by popular vote for a single five-year term.

Executive

The President appoints the Prime Minister with the approval of the National Assembly, and appoints ministers and the heads of agencies, many on the Prime Minister's recommendation. The State Council deliberates on major policies. The executive ministries are headquartered largely in the Government Complex in Sejong City, an administrative capital built to reduce concentration in Seoul, while the presidential office, Cheong Wa Dae (the Blue House) in Seoul, was opened to the public in 2022 as the presidency moved to the Yongsan district.

Legislature and judiciary

The National Assembly is a unicameral legislature of 300 members elected for four-year terms, combining constituency seats with proportional representation. The judiciary consists of the Supreme Court, headed by the Chief Justice, and a separate Constitutional Court, established in 1988, which rules on constitutional review, impeachments, and party dissolutions; it has decided several landmark cases, including the impeachment proceedings of 2004, 2017, and 2024–25.

Local government

South Korea has had elected local autonomy since 1995, with provinces, special and metropolitan cities, and municipal governments (si, gun, gu) electing councils and executives.
