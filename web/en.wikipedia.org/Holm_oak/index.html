Holm oak

Quercus ilex
Family: Fagaceae
Native range: Mediterranean region

Quercus ilex, the evergreen oak, holly oak or holm oak, is a large evergreen oak native to the Mediterranean region. It is a characteristic tree of Mediterranean woodlands and the Spanish dehesa landscape.

Description

The tree grows to about 20-25 metres with a dense rounded crown. Leaves are dark green, glossy above and downy beneath, variable in shape, sometimes holly-like and spiny on young trees. Acorns ripen in autumn and feed wildlife and Iberian pigs.

Ecology and uses

Holm oak is drought-tolerant and long-lived. Its hard wood has been used for fuel and charcoal, its bark for tannin, and its acorns as fodder. It is widely planted as an ornamental and shade tree.
