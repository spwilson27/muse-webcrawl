Promotion and relegation

Promotion and relegation is a process in sports leagues in which teams are transferred between divisions according to their performance over a completed season: the best-ranked teams in a lower division are promoted to a higher division for the next season, and the worst-ranked teams in the higher division are relegated in their place. The system is the dominant structure of association football worldwide — including England's Premier League and Football League, and the leagues of virtually every European and South American country — and is also used in rugby, cricket, and other sports.

Operation

Typically the bottom two or three teams of a division drop down and the top two or three of the division below rise, often with play-offs deciding the final promotion place. Promotion brings greater revenue, television exposure, and prestige; relegation can be financially devastating, mitigated in England by "parachute payments" to relegated clubs. The possibility of movement between divisions keeps late-season matches between low-ranking clubs meaningful and allows ambitious small clubs to rise — Leicester City's climb from League One to Premier League champions (2016) is a celebrated example, as is the ascent of clubs through England's National League into the Football League.

History

The system originated in English football: the Football League's early "test matches" gave way to automatic promotion and relegation when the League absorbed the rival Football Alliance in 1892. It spread with the game across Europe and Latin America during the 20th century.

Contrast with closed leagues

North American major leagues (the NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, and Major League Soccer) use a closed, franchise model without relegation, redistributing competitive balance through drafts and salary caps instead. Critics of closed leagues argue they tolerate perennial losing teams; defenders of relegation systems credit them with meritocracy and drama, while critics note the financial instability they impose on relegated clubs. Occasional proposals to introduce promotion and relegation in the United States, and proposals such as the 2021 European Super League to abolish it for founding clubs, have been highly controversial.
