Etienne Wenger

Born: 1952, Switzerland
Fields: Social learning theory, education
Known for: Communities of practice, legitimate peripheral participation

Etienne Charles Wenger (born 1952) is a Swiss educational theorist and practitioner, best known for his work on communities of practice and, with Jean Lave, the concept of legitimate peripheral participation. He holds a PhD in artificial intelligence from the University of California, Irvine.

Work

Wenger's early research concerned artificial intelligence and education, including tutoring systems. His collaboration with anthropologist Jean Lave produced the theory of situated learning, presented in Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation (1991), which proposed that learning is fundamentally a process of participation in social practice rather than the internalization of abstract knowledge.

In Communities of Practice: Learning, Meaning, and Identity (1998), Wenger developed a social theory of learning built around communities of practice: groups of people who share a concern or a passion for something they do and learn how to do it better as they interact regularly. The concept has been widely applied in organizational development, education, healthcare, and knowledge management.

Later work

Wenger later worked as an independent consultant and researcher on learning in social systems, developing frameworks for "social learning spaces" and value creation in communities and networks, in collaboration with Beverly Wenger-Trayner.
