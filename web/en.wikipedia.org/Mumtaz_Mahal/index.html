Mumtaz Mahal

Born: Arjumand Banu, 27 April 1593, Agra
Died: 17 June 1631, Burhanpur
Spouse: Shah Jahan
Burial: Taj Mahal, Agra

Mumtaz Mahal (born Arjumand Banu Begum; 1593–1631) was the empress consort of the Mughal Empire as the chief wife of the emperor Shah Jahan. The Taj Mahal in Agra, one of the most famous buildings in the world, was commissioned by Shah Jahan as her mausoleum. Her name, given by her husband, means "the exalted one of the palace" in Persian.

Early life and marriage

Arjumand Banu was born in Agra into a family of Persian nobility; her father, Asaf Khan, was the elder brother of the empress Nur Jahan. Tradition holds that she met Prince Khurram (the future Shah Jahan) at a bazaar in 1607, when she was fourteen, and they married five years later, in 1612, on an astrologically auspicious date. Though Shah Jahan had other wives, the marriage was a deep attachment: Mumtaz travelled with him on military campaigns and was his trusted confidante and adviser, and the imperial seal was entrusted to her care.

Death

Mumtaz accompanied Shah Jahan on his campaign against rebels in the Deccan. At Burhanpur in June 1631 she died after a prolonged labour giving birth to her fourteenth child, a daughter, Gauhar Ara Begum. She was temporarily buried at Burhanpur and later reinterred at Agra in the garden tomb Shah Jahan raised for her on the bank of the Yamuna River — the Taj Mahal.

Children and legacy

Of her fourteen children, seven survived to adulthood, including the heir-apparent Dara Shikoh, the future emperor Aurangzeb, and the princess Jahanara Begum, who became a powerful figure at court. Mumtaz's death devastated Shah Jahan, whose hair reportedly turned grey with grief; the mausoleum he built, completed around 1653, made her memory immortal and became a symbol of conjugal devotion.
