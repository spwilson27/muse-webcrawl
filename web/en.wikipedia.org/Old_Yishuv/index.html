The Old Yishuv (Hebrew: היישוב הישן, ha-Yishuv ha-Yashan) were the Jewish communities of Ottoman Palestine that existed before the Zionist immigration (aliyah) waves beginning in the 1880s, which founded the "New Yishuv".

Communities

The Old Yishuv centred on the Four Holy Cities of Judaism: Jerusalem, Safed, Tiberias, and Hebron. Its members were largely religious Jews who had moved to the Land of Israel for religious reasons and supported themselves partly through the halukka system of charitable funds collected from Jewish communities abroad.

Population and life

Population estimates for the early 19th century range around 10,000, growing to perhaps 25,000–30,000 by 1880. The communities included long-established Sephardi and Musta'arabi Jews and later Ashkenazi arrivals, notably followers of the Vilna Gaon and Hasidic groups in the early 1800s.

Relations with the New Yishuv

Relations between the Old Yishuv and the secular Zionist settlers of the New Yishuv were often strained over ideology and control of funds, though the two populations gradually merged in the Mandate period.
