Turner Broadcasting System

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. was an American television and media conglomerate founded by Ted Turner in 1970 and based in Atlanta, Georgia. It created some of the foundational channels of American cable television before merging into Time Warner in 1996; its remaining units were absorbed into WarnerMedia and, following the 2022 Warner Bros. Discovery merger, the Turner name ceased to be used as a corporate division.

History

Turner grew from the billboard-advertising business of Ted Turner's father. Turner bought Atlanta UHF station WTCG in 1970 and in 1976 uplinked it by satellite as a national "superstation", renamed WTBS. In 1980 he launched CNN, the first 24-hour television news channel, followed by Headline News (1982), TNT (1988), the Cartoon Network (1992) and Turner Classic Movies (1994). Turner also owned the Atlanta Braves and Atlanta Hawks sports franchises and acquired the MGM film library, which seeded its classic-film channels.

Merger and dissolution

Time Warner acquired Turner Broadcasting in 1996 for about $7.5 billion, making Turner its largest shareholder. Under AT&T's ownership of WarnerMedia, the Turner division was broken up in 2019; its networks (TNT, TBS, CNN, Cartoon Network and others) passed to Warner Bros. Discovery units in 2022.

Legacy

Turner pioneered satellite-delivered cable networks, round-the-clock news and the use of vast film libraries as programming, reshaping the television industry worldwide.
