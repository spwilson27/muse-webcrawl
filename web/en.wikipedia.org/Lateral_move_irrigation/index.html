Lateral move irrigation, also called linear move irrigation, is a mechanised irrigation system similar in construction to center pivot irrigation — a self-propelled pipeline supported on wheeled towers, carrying sprinklers or drop tubes — except that instead of rotating around a fixed central point, the whole machine travels in a straight line across a rectangular field, irrigating a long strip as it goes. It can thereby water up to about 98% of a rectangular paddock, whereas a center pivot leaves the corners unirrigated (about 78% of a square field).

Operation

Water is supplied either from an open ditch running along one side of the field, from which the machine drafts water through a floating intake, or through a large flexible hose dragged behind the machine and connected to a line of hydrants. Guidance systems — furrow followers, buried wires, GPS, or alignment cables — keep the spans in line, and each tower's drive is controlled to maintain even advance. Corner "swing arms" are sometimes added to center pivots to approximate the rectangular coverage that linear machines achieve naturally.

Advantages and limits

Linear systems suit rectangular fields and can share one machine between fields, but they are generally more expensive and management-intensive than center pivots, require a continuous ditch or hose supply along the whole run, and cannot operate on as wide a range of terrain. Both systems are far more water-efficient than flood irrigation, especially when fitted with low-energy precision application (LEPA) nozzles.
