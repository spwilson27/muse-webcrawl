The Yale Whiffenpoofs are a collegiate a cappella singing group at Yale University. Founded in 1909, the Whiffenpoofs are the oldest collegiate a cappella group in the United States. Originally formed as a quartet, the group later expanded and traditionally consists of fourteen senior men, though women have been admitted since 2018 after a historic vote by the group's members.

History

The group traces its origins to informal weekly meetings at Mory's Temple Bar, a New Haven tavern long associated with Yale. The name "Whiffenpoof" was borrowed from a character in the 1908 operetta Little Nemo, itself adapted from an imaginary creature in a popular song. Early members included Cole Porter, who arranged some of the group's earliest repertoire.

The Whiffenpoof Song

The group's signature number, "The Whiffenpoof Song", was composed around 1910 with lyrics by Yale students Meade Minnigerode and George S. Pomeroy. Its famous refrain — "We are poor little lambs who have lost our way, baa, baa, baa" — was inspired by Rudyard Kipling's poem "Gentlemen-Rankers". The song became a hit recording for Bing Crosby in 1947 and remains the group's closing number, sung with raised glasses at the end of performances.

Modern era

Each year the Whiffenpoofs tour internationally and record an album. In 2010 the group appeared on the NBC competition series The Sing-Off. Following campus-wide debate, the group voted in 2018 to admit singers of all genders, ending more than a century as an all-male ensemble.
