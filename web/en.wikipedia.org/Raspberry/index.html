The raspberry is the edible fruit of numerous plant species in the genus Rubus of the rose family, most of which are in the subgenus Idaeobatus. The name also applies to the plants themselves. Raspberries are perennial with woody stems, called canes. World production of raspberries in recent years has been led by Russia, Mexico, Serbia, Poland and the United States.

Description

A raspberry is an aggregate fruit, developing from a single flower with multiple ovaries. Each individual unit, called a drupelet, surrounds a single seed and is covered in fine hairs. Unlike the closely related blackberry, the raspberry fruit separates from its receptacle (torus) when picked, leaving a hollow core. Raspberries may be red, black, purple, yellow or golden in colour when ripe, depending on the species and cultivar.

Cultivation

Raspberries are grown for the fresh fruit market, for processing into jam, purée and juice, and for freezing. Plants are typically planted as dormant canes and may be summer-bearing, producing fruit on two-year-old canes, or primocane-fruiting, producing on first-year canes in late summer and autumn. Raspberries grow best in well-drained, slightly acidic soil in temperate climates, and are an important commercial crop in regions with cool summers.

Nutrition

Raspberries are a rich source of vitamin C, manganese and dietary fibre, and contain antioxidant compounds including ellagitannins and anthocyanins, which give red raspberries their colour.
