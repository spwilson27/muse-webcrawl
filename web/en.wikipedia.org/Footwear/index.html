Footwear

Footwear serves protection, comfort, function, and fashion.

Footwear refers to garments worn on the feet, principally shoes, boots, sandals, and slippers. Footwear serves practical purposes — protecting the foot from the ground, weather, and injury, and providing grip and support — and social purposes as items of fashion and indicators of status or occupation. The craft of making footwear is called shoemaking, traditionally performed by shoemakers (cordwainers and cobblers).

History

Footwear is among the oldest human garments. The oldest known leather shoes, the Areni-1 shoes found in Armenia, date to about 3500 BC, and woven sagebrush sandals from Fort Rock Cave in Oregon are several thousand years older still. Ötzi the Iceman wore shoes of bearskin and deer hide stuffed with grass around 3300 BC. Ancient Egyptians wore palm and papyrus sandals; Greeks and Romans developed a range of sandals and boots. In medieval Europe, long pointed shoes (poulaines) became high fashion, while in China, foot binding was associated with lotus shoes. The modern shoe industry emerged with mechanized production in the 19th century, centred in places such as Northampton in England and Massachusetts in the United States.

Types

Footwear is commonly divided into shoes (not covering the ankle), boots (covering the ankle or leg), open footwear such as sandals and flip-flops, indoor footwear such as slippers, and specialist footwear: sports shoes and trainers, football boots with studs, ballet shoes, climbing shoes, safety boots with protective toe caps, orthopedic shoes, and traditional forms such as clogs, moccasins, and geta.

Construction and materials

A shoe consists of an upper, insole, midsole, outsole, heel, and fastenings such as laces, straps, or zippers. Traditional materials include leather, wood, and natural fibres; modern footwear uses rubber, synthetic fabrics, foam cushioning, and plastics extensively. Quality construction methods include Goodyear welting, which allows resoling, and cemented construction common in athletic shoes. Footwear is sized using systems such as UK, US, and European (Paris point) sizes, or the Mondopoint system.

Industry and culture

The global footwear industry produces more than twenty billion pairs a year, mostly in Asia. Footwear is also a major fashion and collecting culture, exemplified by "sneakerhead" culture around limited-edition athletic shoes, and luxury houses renowned for shoes. Growing attention is paid to labour conditions in production and to the environmental impact of synthetic materials and disposal.
