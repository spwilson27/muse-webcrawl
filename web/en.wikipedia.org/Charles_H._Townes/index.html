Charles H. Townes


Charles Hard Townes (1915–2015), Nobel laureate and inventor of the maser.

Charles Hard Townes (July 28, 1915 – January 27, 2015) was an American physicist. Townes worked on the theory and application of the maser, for which he obtained the fundamental patent, and on other work in quantum electronics associated with both maser and laser devices. He shared the 1964 Nobel Prize in Physics with Nikolay Basov and Alexander Prokhorov.

Early life and wartime work

Born in Greenville, South Carolina, Townes studied at Furman University and Duke University before earning his PhD at Caltech in 1939. At Bell Telephone Laboratories during the Second World War he designed radar bombing systems and developed microwave spectroscopy, the techniques that later underpinned his work on stimulated emission.

The maser and laser

At Columbia University, Townes conceived in 1951 a device that would amplify microwaves by stimulated emission of radiation; with James P. Gordon and Herbert Zeiger he built the first ammonia maser in 1953. In 1958, with his brother-in-law Arthur Schawlow, he published the theoretical design for an optical maser — the laser. Townes also applied maser technology to astronomy, co-discovering complex molecules in interstellar space and evidence for the supermassive black hole at the centre of the Milky Way.

Later career

Townes served as provost of MIT (1961–1967) and then at the University of California, Berkeley, for the rest of his career. He chaired the committee whose report recommended continuing the Apollo programme after the Apollo 1 fire, and worked on US defence advisory panels. A deeply religious man, he wrote widely on the convergence of science and faith and received the Templeton Prize in 2005.
