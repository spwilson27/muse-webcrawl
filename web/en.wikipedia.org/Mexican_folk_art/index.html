Mexican folk art

Mexican folk art encompasses handcrafts and art made by Mexico's indigenous peoples and other artisans, blending pre-Hispanic, Spanish colonial and modern influences. Major traditions include pottery, textiles, lacquerware, silverwork, bark paper painting (amate), and festive objects such as piñatas and Day of the Dead figures.

Traditions

Famous regional crafts include black pottery (barro negro) from Oaxaca, Talavera pottery from Puebla, alebrijes — brightly painted fantastical creatures — from Oaxaca and Mexico City, Mata Ortiz ceramics from Chihuahua, and the woven rebozos of Santa María del Río. Folk art is promoted by state institutions such as FONART and sold in mercados and specialised museums, including Mexico City's Museo de Arte Popular.

History

Colonial-era guilds and missions reshaped indigenous crafts with European techniques and Christian themes, while the post-Revolutionary government of the 1920s actively promoted folk art as an expression of national identity.
