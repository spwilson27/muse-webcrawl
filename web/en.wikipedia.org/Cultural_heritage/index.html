Cultural heritage

Cultural heritage is the heritage of tangible and intangible assets of a group or society that is inherited from past generations. Not all legacies of past generations are "heritage"; rather, heritage is a product of selection by society. Cultural heritage includes tangible culture (buildings, monuments, landscapes, books, works of art and artefacts), intangible culture (folklore, traditions, language and knowledge), and natural heritage (culturally significant landscapes and biodiversity).

Protection and preservation

The deliberate preservation of cultural heritage is known as heritage conservation. International protection was galvanised by the destruction of the Second World War and codified in the 1954 Hague Convention for the Protection of Cultural Property in the Event of Armed Conflict. UNESCO's 1972 World Heritage Convention established the World Heritage List of sites of outstanding universal value, which now contains well over a thousand properties, while the 2003 Convention addresses intangible cultural heritage — oral traditions, performing arts, rituals, craftsmanship and foodways.

Issues

Heritage is threatened by war and looting, development, tourism pressure, neglect and climate change. Long-running debates concern the repatriation of objects removed in colonial contexts (such as the Benin Bronzes and the Parthenon Marbles), the ethics of reconstruction after destruction, the digitisation of collections, and whose history gets selected as "heritage" at all — questions explored in critical heritage studies.

Heritage and identity

Cultural heritage underpins collective memory and identity at local, national and global scales, and heritage tourism is a major economic sector. At the same time, heritage can be contested: monuments associated with oppression have become the focus of public protest and removal campaigns in many countries.
