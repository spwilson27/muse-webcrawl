Tagesschau

Tagesschau (German for "Review of the Day") is a German national and international television news service produced by ARD-aktuell on behalf of the public broadcaster ARD. Its flagship 15-minute bulletin airs at 20:00 (8 p.m.) on Das Erste and several other channels simultaneously, and is the most-watched news programme in Germany, regularly drawing around ten million viewers.

History

Tagesschau began on 26 December 1952 from the NWDR studios in Hamburg, initially three times a week, modelled partly on cinema newsreels. By the late 1950s it was broadcast daily; from 1978 the later-evening Tagesthemen added background reports and commentary. The 20:00 bulletin became a fixed national ritual — in German idiom, events happen "before" or "after the Tagesschau". After reunification, the programme absorbed the role of East Germany's Aktuelle Kamera.

Format

The main edition presents the day's news in a sober, presenter-read format, followed by weather. Its dispassionate style contrasts with the more magazine-like Tagesthemen. Around 1997 the service expanded into a 24-hour presence through tagesschau.de and later the tagesschau24 channel and app; regular editions now run through the day, plus a simplified-news edition in easy language and a version in German Sign Language.

Presenters

Notable chief anchors (Chefsprecher) have included Karl-Heinz Köpcke, Dagmar Berghoff — the first woman to read the main news, from 1976 — Werner Veigel, Jan Hofer (chief anchor 2020–2024), and Jens Riewa. Long-serving presenters become nationally familiar figures, and their retirements are themselves news events.
