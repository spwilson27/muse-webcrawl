The Department of Cajamarca is a department and region in northwestern Peru. It is bordered by Ecuador to the north, the departments of Amazonas to the east, La Libertad to the south, and Lambayeque and Piura to the west. Its capital is the city of Cajamarca.

Geography

The department is crossed by the Andes; the western chain is at its lowest here, making it one of the more accessible Andean regions. The Marañón River and its tributaries drain much of the area. The jalca high-altitude grasslands are characteristic of the region.

History

Cajamarca was the site of the famous 1532 encounter between the Inca emperor Atahualpa and Francisco Pizarro's conquistadors, ending with Atahualpa's capture in the plaza of Cajamarca and his later execution — a pivotal moment in the Spanish conquest of Peru.

Economy

Agriculture, livestock and dairy production are central, and the department hosts major gold mining operations, including the Yanacocha mine, one of the largest gold mines in the world, which has also been the focus of social and environmental conflicts over water.
