Miaphysitism

Miaphysitism is the Christological doctrine held by the Oriental Orthodox Churches, according to which Jesus Christ, the Incarnate Word, is fully divine and fully human in one physis (nature), united without separation, without confusion, and without alteration. The term derives from the phrase of Cyril of Alexandria, "one nature of God the Word made flesh" (mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē).

History

Following the Council of Chalcedon (451), which defined Christ as being in two natures, opponents who emphasised the unity of Christ's nature were often labelled "Monophysites" by their critics — a term Oriental Orthodox churches reject, since it implies the absorption of Christ's humanity. Leaders of the tradition include Severus of Antioch and Dioscorus of Alexandria.

Churches

The Miaphysite Oriental Orthodox communion includes the Coptic Orthodox Church, the Syriac Orthodox Church, the Armenian Apostolic Church, the Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Churches, and the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church.

Modern dialogue

Twentieth-century ecumenical dialogues between Oriental and Eastern Orthodox theologians concluded that the historic dispute was largely terminological, leading to agreed statements on Christology, though full communion has not been restored.
