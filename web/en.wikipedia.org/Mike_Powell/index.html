Mike Powell
Born: 10 November 1963, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Sport: Track and field (long jump)
Personal best: 8.95 m (29 ft 4¼ in) — world record
Medals: 2 Olympic silvers, 2 World Championship golds

Michael Anthony Powell (born 10 November 1963) is an American former athlete who holds the world record in the men's long jump. At the 1991 World Championships in Tokyo he jumped 8.95 m (29 ft 4¼ in), breaking Bob Beamon's record of 8.90 m that had stood for almost 23 years since the 1968 Mexico City Olympics. Powell's record remains unbroken.

Career

Powell attended the University of California, Irvine, and later UCLA. He won silver medals at the 1988 Seoul and 1992 Barcelona Olympic Games, both times behind Carl Lewis. His defining contest came on 30 August 1991 in Tokyo, in one of the greatest long jump duels in history: Lewis produced the finest series of jumps ever recorded, including a wind-assisted 8.91 m, but Powell's fifth-round legal jump of 8.95 m took the world record and the gold medal.

Powell successfully defended his world title at Stuttgart in 1993, took bronze at the 1995 Worlds, and placed fifth at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics before retiring in 1997. He made a brief comeback attempt in 2001 aimed at the Sydney 2000 aftermath circuit. He later worked as a coach and athletics commentator, and has served as a World Athletics ambassador.

Legacy

Powell's 8.95 m is the longest-standing men's long jump world record in the modern era and is ranked among the greatest records in track and field. Only a handful of athletes — Lewis, Robert Emmiyan, Larry Myricks, Dwight Phillips and Juan Miguel Echevarría — have ever jumped beyond 8.80 m.
