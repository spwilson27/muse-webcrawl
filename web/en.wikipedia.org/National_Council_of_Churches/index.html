The National Council of the Churches of Christ in the United States of America, usually shortened to the National Council of Churches (NCC), is an ecumenical body of 38 Christian communions in the United States, including mainline Protestant, Orthodox, African American and peace-church traditions, representing tens of millions of adherents.

History

The council was constituted in 1950 by merging the Federal Council of Churches (founded 1908) with several other interdenominational agencies. Headquartered for decades at the Interchurch Center at 475 Riverside Drive in New York City, it became a prominent voice of "mainline" Protestantism, active in the civil rights movement, opposition to the Vietnam War, and later campaigns on poverty, peace and the environment. It is best known to scholars for sponsoring the Revised Standard Version (1952) and New Revised Standard Version (1989) translations of the Bible.

Work

The NCC convenes its member churches for theological dialogue (Faith and Order), joint advocacy in Washington, disaster relief cooperation with Church World Service, and ecumenical relations with the World Council of Churches and global partners.
