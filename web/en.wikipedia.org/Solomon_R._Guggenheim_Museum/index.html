The Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum, often referred to as The Guggenheim, is an art museum located at 1071 Fifth Avenue in the Upper East Side neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City. It is the permanent home of a continuously expanding collection of Impressionist, Post-Impressionist, early modern and contemporary art, and also features special exhibitions throughout the year.

The building

The museum was established by the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation in 1939 as the Museum of Non-Objective Painting. Its landmark building, designed by Frank Lloyd Wright, opened on 21 October 1959, six months after Wright's death. The cylindrical concrete structure, wider at the top than at the bottom, contains a single great atrium around which a continuous spiral ramp rises gently, allowing visitors to view art along the ramp while ascending. The building is an icon of modern architecture and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2019 as part of "The 20th-Century Architecture of Frank Lloyd Wright".

Collection

The collection centres on the holdings of the mining magnate Solomon R. Guggenheim, assembled with the artist Hilla von Rebay, and is especially strong in abstract and non-objective painting, including the world's largest collection of works by Wassily Kandinsky, along with major works by Picasso, Klee, Miró, and many others. The Guggenheim network includes the Peggy Guggenheim Collection in Venice and the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao.
