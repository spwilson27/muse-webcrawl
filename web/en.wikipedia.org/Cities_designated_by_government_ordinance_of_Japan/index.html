Cities designated by government ordinance of Japan

A city designated by government ordinance (Japanese: 政令指定都市, seirei shitei toshi), also called a designated city, is a Japanese city with a population greater than 500,000 that was designated as such by order of the Cabinet of Japan under Article 252, Section 19 of the Local Autonomy Law.

Designated cities are delegated many of the functions normally performed by prefectural governments in fields such as public education, social welfare, sanitation, business licensing and urban planning. The city government is generally delegated the various minor administrative functions normally performed by the prefectural government within that area.

History

The designated city system was introduced by the 1947 Local Autonomy Law. The first five designated cities were Yokohama, Nagoya, Kyoto, Osaka and Kobe, which were elevated in 1956. The system replaced the earlier "special city" framework. Over time more cities have been designated as their populations grew and merged with neighbouring municipalities.

Requirements

To become a candidate for designated city status, a city must have a population greater than 500,000. An application for designation proceeds through the prefectural assembly and ultimately requires approval by the national government. Cities have considerable incentive to merge with neighbours to reach the threshold.

List of designated cities

As of the 2010s there are 20 designated cities, including Sapporo, Sendai, Saitama, Chiba, Yokohama, Kawasaki, Sagamihara, Niigata, Shizuoka, Hamamatsu, Nagoya, Kyoto, Osaka, Sakai, Kobe, Okayama, Hiroshima, Kitakyushu, Fukuoka and Kumamoto.

Wards

Designated cities are subdivided into wards (ku), each of which has its own ward office carrying out administrative functions for the city government, similar to the special wards of Tokyo though they lack independent municipal status.
