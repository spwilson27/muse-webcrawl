Commewijne River

The Commewijne River (Sranan Tongo: Kawina-liba) is a river in northern Suriname. It originates in the hills southeast of the country and flows northward, joining the Suriname River at Nieuw Amsterdam shortly before the combined waters reach the Atlantic Ocean near Paramaribo. The river is about 198 kilometres (123 mi) long.

History

The fertile banks of the Commewijne were among the earliest parts of Suriname developed for plantation agriculture under Dutch colonial rule. From the late 17th century onward, sugar, coffee, and cacao plantations lined the river, worked first by enslaved Africans and later by indentured labourers from India and Java. Historic sites along the river include Fort Nieuw-Amsterdam at the confluence and the former plantations of Frederiksdorp, Mariënburg, and Rust en Werk. The name is thought to derive from Indigenous words, sometimes explained as "river of the tapir" (kama and wini).
