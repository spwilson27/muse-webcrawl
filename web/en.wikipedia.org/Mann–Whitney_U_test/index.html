Mann–Whitney U test

The Mann–Whitney U test (also called the Mann–Whitney–Wilcoxon test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test) is a nonparametric test of the null hypothesis that the distributions of two sampled populations are equal. It is one of the best-known nonparametric significance tests and is often described as the nonparametric alternative to the independent-samples t-test.

Procedure

All observations from both samples are ranked together. The statistic U is computed from the sum of the ranks in one sample: for sample sizes n1 and n2 and rank sum R1, U = n1n2 + n1(n1+1)/2 − R1. Under the null hypothesis, U has a known distribution, approximately normal for larger samples.

Assumptions and interpretation

The test requires independent samples and data that are at least ordinal. It is commonly interpreted as a test of whether one population tends to yield larger values than the other — equivalently, whether the probability that a random observation from one group exceeds a random observation from the other differs from one half.
