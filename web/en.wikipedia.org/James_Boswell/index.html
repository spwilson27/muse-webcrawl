James Boswell, 9th Laird of Auchinleck (29 October 1740 – 19 May 1795), was a Scottish lawyer, diarist and author. He is best known for his biography of the English writer Samuel Johnson, The Life of Samuel Johnson (1791), which is commonly regarded as one of the greatest biographies in the English language.

James Boswell (1740–1795)

Early life

Boswell was born in Edinburgh, the eldest son of Alexander Boswell, Lord Auchinleck, a judge of the Court of Session. He studied law at the University of Edinburgh and later at Glasgow, where he briefly attended the lectures of Adam Smith. His early journals record a young man torn between his father's Presbyterian strictness and the attractions of London society.

Friendship with Johnson

Boswell first met Samuel Johnson in the London bookshop of Tom Davies on 16 May 1763. Despite Johnson's initial mockery of Scotsmen, the two became close friends. Boswell's method of recording Johnson's conversation in meticulous detail — often written up the same evening — produced an unmatched portrait of Johnson's wit and character. In 1773 Boswell persuaded the 64-year-old Johnson to accompany him on a tour of the Scottish Highlands and the Hebrides, which both men published accounts of: Johnson's A Journey to the Western Islands of Scotland (1775) and Boswell's The Journal of a Tour to the Hebrides (1785).

Travels and other works

Before meeting Johnson, Boswell had travelled in Europe, meeting Rousseau and Voltaire, and befriending the Corsican independence leader Pasquale Paoli. His An Account of Corsica (1768) made his early reputation. He practised as an advocate in Edinburgh and later in London, but his legal career was overshadowed by his literary work and by struggles with alcohol and depression.

The Life of Johnson

Published in 1791, seven years after Johnson's death, The Life of Samuel Johnson, LL.D. drew on Boswell's journals, Johnson's letters and his own recollections. Its vivid dramatic scenes and fullness of recorded conversation revolutionised biography. Boswell's private journals, rediscovered in the twentieth century at Malahide Castle and Fettercairn House, revealed his remarkable candour and further raised his reputation as a diarist.

Death

Boswell died in London on 19 May 1795 and was buried in the family vault at Auchinleck in Ayrshire.
