Ligand


In coordination chemistry, a ligand is an ion or molecule that binds to a central metal atom to form a coordination complex.

In coordination chemistry, a ligand is an ion or molecule with a functional group that binds to a central metal atom to form a coordination complex. The bonding typically involves donation of one or more of the ligand's electron pairs into empty metal orbitals.

Classification

Ligands are classified by denticity: monodentate ligands (such as ammonia or chloride) bind through one atom, while polydentate or chelating ligands (such as ethylenediamine and EDTA) bind through several atoms and form especially stable complexes. Common ligands include water (aqua), ammonia (ammine), carbon monoxide (carbonyl) and halides.

Biochemistry

In biochemistry, a ligand is any molecule that binds to a specific site on a target protein, such as a receptor, to serve a biological purpose — for example hormones, neurotransmitters and drugs.
