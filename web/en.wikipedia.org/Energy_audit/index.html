Energy audit

An energy audit is an inspection, survey and analysis of energy flows in a building, process or system, carried out in order to reduce the amount of energy input into the system without negatively affecting its output. In commercial and industrial applications an audit is often the first step in identifying opportunities to reduce energy expense and carbon emissions.

Principles

An audit compares the amount of energy supplied to a system with the amount used productively, and identifies where losses occur — for example through poor insulation, air leakage, inefficient lighting, heating and cooling equipment, or outdated industrial processes. Recommendations are usually ranked by payback period.

Types of audit

Standard practice, following ASHRAE guidelines, distinguishes three levels: a Level 1 walk-through audit, a Level 2 energy survey and engineering analysis, and a Level 3 detailed analysis of capital-intensive modifications involving simulations and detailed field measurements. Home energy audits use tools such as blower-door tests and infrared thermography to locate air leaks and thermal bridges.

History

Energy audits became widespread after the 1973 oil crisis. In the European Union, the Energy Efficiency Directive requires large enterprises to carry out regular energy audits, and energy performance certificates for buildings are based on similar assessments.
