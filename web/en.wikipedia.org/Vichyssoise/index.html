Vichyssoise

Vichyssoise (pronounced [vi.ʃi.swaz]) is a thick soup made of boiled and puréed leeks, onions, potatoes, cream, and chicken stock. It is traditionally served cold but can be eaten hot. The soup is a staple of French-American cuisine.

Origin

Vichyssoise is usually ascribed to the French chef Louis Diat, chef at the Ritz-Carlton Hotel in New York City, who introduced it in 1917. Diat recalled a potato and leek soup his mother and grandmother made near Vichy, France, and how, in summer, he and his brother poured in cold milk to chill it. He named his refined, chilled version crème vichyssoise glacée in honour of his home region. Cold potato-leek soups had earlier precedents in French cooking, including a recipe in Jules Gouffé's 1869 Livre de cuisine.

Preparation

Leeks and onions are sweated in butter, simmered with potatoes in chicken stock, then puréed and enriched with cream and seasoned, often with chives for garnish. The soup is chilled thoroughly before serving, traditionally in a bowl set over crushed ice.
