Singel

LocationAmsterdam, Netherlands
Lengthabout 1.7 km (1.1 mi)
Flows intoAmstel / IJ

The Singel is a canal in Amsterdam which encircles the city centre, the innermost of the city's major ring canals. It runs from the IJ bay, near Central Station, to the Muntplein square, where it meets the Amstel river.

History

The Singel began as a moat dug in the late Middle Ages to mark the edge of the medieval city; the word singel derives from the old Dutch for a belt or girdle. From around 1480 until 1585 it formed Amsterdam's outer city limit, after which expansion produced the wider Herengracht, Keizersgracht, and Prinsengracht of the Canal Ring.

Points of interest

Landmarks along the Singel include the Bloemenmarkt, the world's only floating flower market, established on barges in 1862; the Munttoren tower at Muntplein; the round Lutheran church (Ronde Lutherse Kerk); and some of Amsterdam's narrowest houses. The canal is crossed by numerous bridges and lined with houseboats and historic canal houses.
