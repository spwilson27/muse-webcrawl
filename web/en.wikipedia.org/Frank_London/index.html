Frank London (born 1958) is an American trumpeter, composer, and bandleader, best known as a founding member of the Grammy Award-winning klezmer group The Klezmatics. A central figure in the klezmer revival and the broader "Radical Jewish Culture" movement, London has worked across jazz, world music, theater, and film.

Early life and education

London was born in New York City and raised in Plainview, Long Island. He studied music at the New England Conservatory in Boston, where he was exposed to both classical training and free improvisation, and he spent time in West Africa studying drumming traditions. These diverse influences shaped his eclectic approach to composition and performance.

Career

In 1986 London co-founded The Klezmatics, which became one of the most prominent ensembles in the modern klezmer revival, blending traditional Yiddish melodies with jazz, rock, and world-music elements. The group won the Grammy Award for Best Contemporary World Music Album for Wonder Wheel (2006), a setting of Woody Guthrie's Yiddish-themed lyrics.

Beyond the Klezmatics, London leads the Klezmer Brass Allstars and co-leads Hasidic New Wave, a project fusing Hasidic melodies with avant-garde jazz. He has composed for theater, dance, and film, and has collaborated with artists ranging from John Zorn to Itzhak Perlman and LL Cool J.

Style and influence

London's trumpet playing is noted for its expressive use of klezmer ornamentation — bends, growls, and krekhts (sobbing figures) — within contemporary compositional frameworks. As an educator and festival organizer, he has been an important mentor to younger generations of klezmer and Jewish-music performers.
