Pierre Corneille (1606–1684) was a French tragedian, one of the three great classical dramatists of 17th-century France together with Molière and Racine. He is often called the founder of French tragedy.

Le Cid and the quarrel

Corneille trained in law at Rouen and wrote comedies before achieving fame with Le Cid (1637), a tragicomedy about the Spanish hero Rodrigo Díaz. Its enormous success provoked the "Querelle du Cid," in which the newly founded Académie française, backed by Cardinal Richelieu, criticized the play for violating the classical unities and decorum — a controversy that shaped French dramatic rules for a century.

Major works

His masterpieces of the 1640s — Horace, Cinna, Polyeucte, and La Mort de Pompée — dramatize conflicts between love and duty, honour and the state, in elevated alexandrine verse. After a period of eclipse by the younger Racine, Corneille's reputation was restored; Voltaire edited his works and praised his grandeur.
