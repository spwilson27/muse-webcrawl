The Bardo Thodol (Tibetan: བར་དོ་ཐོས་གྲོལ, "Liberation through Hearing in the Intermediate State") is a funerary and instructional text of the Nyingma tradition of Tibetan Buddhism, known in the West as The Tibetan Book of the Dead. Traditionally read aloud to a dying or recently deceased person over a period of up to 49 days, it guides the consciousness through the intermediate states (bardo) between death and rebirth, instructing it to recognise the visions it encounters — brilliant lights, peaceful and wrathful deities — as projections of its own mind, and thereby to attain liberation rather than be drawn into another rebirth.

Origins

The work belongs to the Zhitro ("Peaceful and Wrathful Deities") cycle. According to tradition, its teachings were composed or concealed as a terma (treasure text) by Padmasambhava in the 8th century and rediscovered by the tertön Karma Lingpa in the 14th century on Mount Gampodar.

Contents: the three bardos

  1. Chikhai bardo — the moment of death, when the Clear Light of reality dawns; recognising it brings liberation.
  2. Chönyid bardo — the bardo of the experiencing of reality, in which the deceased encounters the mandalas of the 42 peaceful and 58 wrathful deities over successive days.
  3. Sidpa bardo — the bardo of rebirth, describing the judgement-like weighing of karma, the vision of mating couples, and instructions for choosing or closing the "womb door" to secure a good rebirth.

In the West

The first English edition (1927), translated by Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup and edited by Walter Y. Evans-Wentz — who coined the title "The Tibetan Book of the Dead" by analogy with the Egyptian Book of the Dead — introduced the text to a wide Western audience. Many translations followed, including Francesca Fremantle's (1975) and Robert Thurman's (1994), and a complete translation of the full cycle by Gyurme Dorje (2005). The book influenced figures from Carl Jung, who wrote a psychological commentary for one edition, to Timothy Leary, whose The Psychedelic Experience (1964) adapted it as a manual for psychedelic sessions.
