The Royal Irish Constabulary (RIC) was the police force in Ireland from 1836 until 1922, when the country was part of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. Unlike the unarmed civilian police of Britain, the RIC was an armed, centrally controlled, quasi-military gendarmerie, quartered in barracks across the countryside. At its height it numbered around 14,000 men.

Origins and organisation

The force grew out of Robert Peel's Peace Preservation Force (1814) and county constabularies created in 1822; a single national Irish Constabulary was established in 1836. After constables helped suppress the Fenian Rising of 1867, Queen Victoria conferred the prefix "Royal" and granted the force its harp-and-crown badge. Recruits — mostly Irish, and increasingly Catholic over time, though the officer class remained predominantly Protestant — served outside their home counties, lived in barracks under military-style discipline, and carried carbines as well as batons.

The Dublin Metropolitan Police policed the capital separately, while the RIC covered the rest of Ireland from more than 1,400 barracks, performing duties from criminal investigation and land-eviction protection to census-taking and weights-and-measures inspection.

War of Independence and disbandment

During the Irish War of Independence (1919–21) the RIC was the IRA's principal intelligence and security target: hundreds of constables were killed, rural barracks were abandoned and burned, and recruitment in Ireland collapsed. The British government reinforced the force with British ex-servicemen — the Black and Tans — and the Auxiliary Division, whose reprisals became notorious. Following the Anglo-Irish Treaty, the RIC was disbanded in 1922. In the new Irish Free State its successor was the unarmed Civic Guard, soon renamed the Garda Síochána; in Northern Ireland many former RIC men joined the new Royal Ulster Constabulary.

Legacy

The RIC model of an armed, centralised rural police was widely copied across the British Empire, influencing colonial forces from Canada (the North-West Mounted Police) to India and Palestine.
