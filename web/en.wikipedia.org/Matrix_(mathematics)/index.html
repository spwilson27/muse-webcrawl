Matrix (mathematics)

In mathematics, a matrix (plural matrices) is a rectangular array or table of numbers, symbols, or expressions, arranged in rows and columns, which is used to represent a mathematical object or property of such an object. Matrices are the central objects of linear algebra.

Basic operations

Matrices of the same size can be added entry by entry and multiplied by scalars. Matrix multiplication — defined when the number of columns of the first equals the number of rows of the second — is associative and distributive but generally not commutative. Every matrix has a transpose, formed by exchanging rows and columns. Square matrices have determinants, traces, eigenvalues and eigenvectors; a matrix with a non-zero determinant has an inverse.

Applications

Matrices represent linear transformations and systems of linear equations, and appear throughout physics (quantum mechanics, relativity), computer graphics (rotations and projections), statistics (covariance matrices), economics (input–output models) and machine learning. Gaussian elimination and LU decomposition are standard algorithms for solving matrix equations.

History

Arrays for solving simultaneous equations appear in the Chinese Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art. The term "matrix" was coined by James Joseph Sylvester in 1850, and Arthur Cayley developed matrix algebra in the 1850s.
