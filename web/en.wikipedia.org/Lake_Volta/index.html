Lake Volta, in Ghana, is the largest artificial lake in the world by surface area (8,502 km²) and the fourth largest by water volume. It was formed by the Akosombo Dam on the Volta River, completed in 1965, and covers about 3.6% of Ghana's total land area.

History

The lake was the centrepiece of the Volta River Project championed by Ghana's first president, Kwame Nkrumah. The Akosombo Dam's hydroelectric station (about 1,020 MW capacity) supplied cheap power to an aluminium smelter at Tema and to Ghana and neighbouring countries. Filling the reservoir displaced some 80,000 people from about 700 villages, who were moved to planned resettlement townships.

Geography and uses

The lake flooded the confluence of the Black Volta, White Volta, and Oti rivers. It provides a navigable waterway from the south to northern Ghana, supports a large capture fishery producing a significant share of the country's fish supply, and moderates local climate. Digya National Park occupies a peninsula on its western shore.

Concerns

Fisheries on the lake have been associated with child labour in fishing communities, a problem addressed by Ghanaian and international programmes. Snail-borne schistosomiasis spread along its shores after impoundment, and floating invasive weeds periodically block landing sites.
