Pagani Huayra

The Pagani Huayra (pronounced [ˈwai̯ra]) is a mid-engine sports car produced by Italian manufacturer Pagani. It succeeded the Zonda and was named after Huayra Tata, a Quechua god of the wind. The Huayra was named "The Hypercar of the Year 2012" by Top Gear magazine and received a very positive review when tested by Richard Hammond.

Specifications

The Huayra is powered by a Mercedes-AMG twin-turbocharged 6.0-litre V12 engine producing 730 PS (537 kW; 720 hp) in the standard version, with a seven-speed sequential single-clutch gearbox. The monocoque chassis is made of carbotanium — a combination of carbon fibre and titanium — giving the car a dry weight of about 1,350 kg. Active aerodynamic flaps at the front and rear adjust downforce and drag, and the car reaches a top speed of approximately 383 km/h (238 mph).

Variants

Production began in 2012 and was limited to 100 coupés. Later variants included the Huayra BC (2016), named after Benny Caiola, the first Zonda customer; the open-top Huayra Roadster (2017) and Roadster BC (2019); the track-focused Huayra R (2021) with a naturally aspirated V12; and the longtail Huayra Codalunga (2022). The Huayra's successor, the Utopia, was unveiled in 2022.

Design

Styled by company founder Horacio Pagani, the Huayra combines aerodynamic function with artisanal detail, including gullwing doors, machined aluminium switchgear, and exposed mechanical components in the cabin, reflecting Pagani's philosophy of blending art and engineering.
