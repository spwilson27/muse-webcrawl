Adriatic Sea

The Adriatic Sea is a body of water separating the Italian Peninsula from the Balkans, and the northernmost arm of the Mediterranean Sea, extending from the Strait of Otranto to the Po Valley.

Geography

Its shores are shared by Italy, Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro and Albania. The sea is about 800 km long and on average 160 km wide; its western (Italian) coast is low and lagooned (including Venice), while the eastern coast is rugged with more than a thousand islands, notably along Croatia's Dalmatian coast.

History and economy

The Adriatic was a vital trade route for Venice, whose republic dominated it for centuries. Today its coasts are major tourist destinations and its ports — Trieste, Rijeka, Split, Durrës — handle regional trade and ferries.
