Henry Jaynes Fonda (16 May 1905 – 12 August 1982) was an American actor whose career spanned more than five decades on stage and screen. With his understated manner and moral gravitas he came to embody the decent American everyman, in roles ranging from Tom Joad to Juror No. 8. He was the patriarch of a famous acting family including his daughter Jane Fonda, son Peter Fonda and grandchildren Bridget Fonda and Troy Garity.

Early career and stardom

Fonda was born in Grand Island, Nebraska, and began acting with the Omaha Community Playhouse, in a production directed by Dorothy Brando, mother of Marlon Brando. After work with summer stock and on Broadway — including an early role alongside James Stewart, his lifelong friend — he went to Hollywood in 1935. He rose quickly with films such as The Trail of the Lonesome Pine (1936) and, with director John Ford, Young Mr. Lincoln (1939), The Grapes of Wrath (1940), for which he was Oscar-nominated as Tom Joad, and My Darling Clementine (1946), playing Wyatt Earp. During the Second World War he served in the US Navy in the Pacific.

Postwar career

Returning to the stage, Fonda originated the title role in Mister Roberts (1948) on Broadway and reprised it on film in 1955. He produced and starred in the courtroom classic 12 Angry Men (1957), played the reluctant president in Fail Safe (1964), and startled audiences by playing a cold-blooded villain in Sergio Leone's Once Upon a Time in the West (1968). His other notable films include The Lady Eve (1941), The Ox-Bow Incident (1943), War and Peace (1956) and The Longest Day (1962).

Final years and honours

Fonda received an Honorary Academy Award in 1981, and the following year won the Academy Award for Best Actor for his final film, On Golden Pond (1981), co-starring Katharine Hepburn and his daughter Jane; he died of heart disease a few months later, aged 77. The American Film Institute ranked him sixth on its list of the greatest male screen legends of American cinema.
