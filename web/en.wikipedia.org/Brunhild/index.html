Brunhild

Also known asBrünnhilde, Brynhildr
SourcesNibelungenlied, Völsunga saga, Poetic Edda
RoleShieldmaiden / valkyrie (Norse tradition)

Brunhild, also spelled Brünnhilde (German) or Brynhildr (Old Norse), is a female figure in Germanic heroic legend. She appears in the Middle High German Nibelungenlied as the superhumanly strong queen of Iceland, and in Norse tradition—as in the Völsunga saga and poems of the Poetic Edda—as a valkyrie who is punished by Odin and awakened by the hero Sigurd (Siegfried).

In the Nibelungenlied

In the Nibelungenlied, Brunhild is a queen of immense strength who agrees to marry only a man who can defeat her in three feats of strength. King Gunther, aided invisibly by Siegfried, wins her hand; on the wedding night she overpowers Gunther and humiliates him, and Siegfried must subdue her in secret. The deception later fuels a bitter quarrel between Brunhild and Siegfried's wife Kriemhild, leading to Siegfried's murder.

In Norse tradition

In the Eddic poems and Völsunga saga, Brynhildr is a valkyrie who disobeys Odin and is condemned to sleep within a ring of fire until a hero fearless enough to cross the flames awakens her. Sigurd does so, and they exchange vows; through treachery Sigurd later woos her in the shape of Gunnar, and the resulting betrayal leads Brynhildr to engineer Sigurd's death and then take her own life on his funeral pyre.

Legacy

Brunhild is a central figure in Richard Wagner's opera cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen, where Brünnhilde is a valkyrie daughter of Wotan whose love for Siegfried and final immolation drive the drama's conclusion.
