Copula (linguistics)

In linguistics, a copula (plural: copulas or copulae; abbreviated COP) is a word or phrase that links the subject of a sentence to a subject complement, such as the word is in the sentence "The sky is blue" or the phrase "becomes" in the sentence "The sky becomes grey." The word copula derives from the Latin noun for a "link" or "tie" that connects two different things.

Uses

Copulas express identity ("I am the walrus"), class membership ("She is a doctor"), properties ("The soup is hot"), and location ("The book is on the table"). The English copula be is highly irregular, with forms am, is, are, was, were, been, being. Other English verbs can function as copulas, including seem, become, appear, feel, look, taste and get in some uses.

Across languages

Many languages have no copula in some constructions: Russian and Hungarian omit the present-tense copula, and Arabic has no copula in present-tense nominal sentences. Some languages have multiple copulas with distinct uses, such as Spanish ser and estar, and Irish is and tá. Mandarin Chinese uses shì before noun predicates but not before adjectival (stative verb) predicates.

Zero copula

The zero copula is the absence of an overt copula where one might appear in English, common in certain tenses, registers, headline style, and in some varieties such as African American Vernacular English ("He working").
