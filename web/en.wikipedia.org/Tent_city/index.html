Tent city

A tent city is a temporary housing facility made using tents or other temporary structures. Such settlements may be established by governments or relief organizations to house displaced people after disasters, by military forces in the field, or informally by homeless people in cities.

Homeless encampments

In North America, tent cities have become a visible feature of homelessness in cities such as Seattle, Portland, San Francisco, and Vancouver. Some are unauthorized encampments, while others are sanctioned or self-managed communities — for example, Seattle's licensed encampment program and Portland's Dignity Village. Debate surrounds whether tent cities should be cleared, tolerated, or formally provided with services.

Other uses

Tent cities have also housed protesters (such as Resurrection City in Washington, D.C., in 1968), pilgrims during the Hajj at Mina in Saudi Arabia, and workers at remote industrial or construction sites.
