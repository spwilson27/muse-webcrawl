Shandur Pass

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Shandur Pass (Urdu: شندور درہ) is a high mountain pass in northern Pakistan connecting the Chitral District of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa with the Ghizer District of Gilgit-Baltistan. At an elevation of about 3,700 metres (12,200 ft), the flat, grassy Shandur plateau — often called the "Roof of the World" locally — links the watersheds of the Gilgit River to the east and the Chitral River to the west, and contains the scenic Shandur Lake.

The polo festival

Shandur is famous for the annual Shandur Polo Festival, held since 1936 at what is regarded as the highest polo ground in the world. Teams from Chitral and Gilgit meet in early July in freestyle mountain polo — played under traditional rules with few fouls, little protective equipment, and periods called chukkers — before thousands of spectators who camp on the plateau. Tradition credits the festival's founding to the Balti prince Ali Sher Khan Anchan and its modern organisation to British political agent Evelyn Hey Cobb.

Access

The pass is usually open from late spring to early autumn; snow closes it in winter. The Shandur road forms part of the route between Gilgit and Chitral via Mastuj and Phander, and has long been of strategic and trade significance along the margins of the Hindu Kush and Karakoram ranges.
