Subregion

A subregion is a large subdivision of a region or continent. Subregions usually rest on geographic grounds: cardinal directions are commonly used to delineate subregions, as in "Northern Europe", "Southern Africa", or "Central America".

United Nations geoscheme

The most widely used formal system is the United Nations geoscheme, maintained by the Statistics Division of the United Nations. It divides the world into continental regions (such as the Americas, Africa, Asia, Europe, and Oceania) and further into subregions — for example, Asia is divided into Central, Eastern, South-eastern, Southern, and Western Asia; Europe into Northern, Western, Eastern, and Southern Europe; and Africa into Northern, Western, Middle, Eastern, and Southern Africa. The assignment of countries to subregions in the geoscheme is made for statistical convenience and implies no political judgement.

Other uses

The term is also used by international organisations, wine-appellation systems (where a subregion is a named area within a larger wine region), and biogeography, where subregions subdivide faunal and floral realms. In administrative terms some countries, such as Chile, Finland, and Serbia, have official sub-regional units below the regional level.
