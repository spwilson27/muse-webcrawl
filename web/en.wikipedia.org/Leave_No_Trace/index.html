Leave No Trace

Leave No Trace (LNT) is a set of outdoor ethics promoting conservation in the outdoors. The organization Leave No Trace, initially a program developed by the United States Forest Service in the 1960s and 1970s, teaches people how to enjoy the outdoors responsibly and minimize their impacts.


A backcountry campsite managed under Leave No Trace principles

The Seven Principles

  1. Plan ahead and prepare
  2. Travel and camp on durable surfaces
  3. Dispose of waste properly
  4. Leave what you find
  5. Minimize campfire impacts
  6. Respect wildlife
  7. Be considerate of other visitors

History

Concern over the impact of growing recreational use of public lands in the mid-20th century led land management agencies to promote "pack it in, pack it out" campaigns. The Leave No Trace program was formalized through partnerships between the Forest Service, the Bureau of Land Management, the National Park Service, and the nonprofit outdoor leadership school NOLS; the Leave No Trace Center for Outdoor Ethics was founded in 1994.
