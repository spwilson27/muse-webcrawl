The Foundation series is a science fiction book series written by American author Isaac Asimov. First published as a series of short stories and novellas in 1942–1950, and subsequently in three collections in 1951–1953, for nearly thirty years the series was widely known as The Foundation Trilogy: Foundation (1951), Foundation and Empire (1952), and Second Foundation (1953). It won the one-time Hugo Award for "Best All-Time Series" in 1966. Asimov later added new volumes, beginning with Foundation's Edge (1982), and eventually linked the series to his Robot and Empire series.

Premise

Mathematician Hari Seldon develops psychohistory, a science that can predict the future of large populations. Foreseeing the fall of the Galactic Empire and a dark age of 30,000 years, he establishes two Foundations to reduce the interregnum to a single millennium. The stories follow the Foundation at Terminus through successive "Seldon crises", guided by holograms of Seldon recorded centuries earlier.

Influence

The series, inspired partly by Edward Gibbon's Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire, has been enormously influential in science fiction and beyond, shaping ideas from galactic empires in popular culture to real-world attempts at predictive social science. A television adaptation, Foundation, premiered on Apple TV+ in 2021.
