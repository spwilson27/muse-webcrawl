HMS Endeavour

Launched1764 (as Earl of Pembroke)
TypeWhitby collier, converted to bark
Tonnage368 tons (bm)
Length97 ft 8 in (29.8 m)
FateScuttled 1778, Newport, Rhode Island

HMS Endeavour was a British Royal Navy research vessel that Lieutenant (later Captain) James Cook commanded to Tahiti, New Zealand and Australia on his first voyage of discovery from 1768 to 1771. She was launched in 1764 as the collier Earl of Pembroke, purchased by the Navy in 1768 for a scientific mission to the Pacific Ocean.

The voyage

The expedition's stated purpose was to observe the 1769 transit of Venus from Tahiti; its secret orders directed Cook to search for the supposed southern continent, Terra Australis. After Tahiti, Cook charted the coasts of New Zealand (proving it was not part of a southern continent) and then the eastern coast of Australia, making landfall at Botany Bay in April 1770 — the first recorded European contact with Australia's east coast. The ship nearly foundered on the Great Barrier Reef and was beached and repaired at the Endeavour River, near modern Cooktown.

Company and legacy

Aboard were the naturalists Joseph Banks and Daniel Solander, whose collections — thousands of plant and animal specimens, many new to science — made the voyage a landmark of natural history. After returning to England the Endeavour served in lesser roles, was sold, renamed Lord Sandwich, and was scuttled by the British in Newport Harbour, Rhode Island, in 1778 during the American Revolutionary War; her wreck has been the subject of long-running archaeological investigation.

Namesakes

The Space Shuttle Endeavour and SpaceX's Crew Dragon Endeavour were named after Cook's ship, as was the Endeavour River. A full-scale replica, launched in 1993, is berthed at the Australian National Maritime Museum in Sydney.
