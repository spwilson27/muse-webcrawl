Unconventional warfare

Unconventional warfare (UW) is a form of warfare in which a state or external actor supports an insurgency or resistance movement to coerce, disrupt, or overthrow a government or occupying power, typically through or with underground, auxiliary, and guerrilla forces in a denied area.

In United States doctrine, unconventional warfare is a core activity of US Army Special Forces (the "Green Berets"), whose classic missions include organizing, training, advising, and fighting alongside indigenous resistance forces—as with the Office of Strategic Services' Jedburgh teams and Detachment 101 in the Second World War, tribal allies in Vietnam, and the Northern Alliance in Afghanistan in 2001.

UW is distinguished from foreign internal defense, in which advisors help a friendly government defeat an insurgency (counterinsurgency), and from direct-action special operations. Its methods include subversion, sabotage, intelligence collection, escape and evasion networks, and psychological operations, usually pursued for strategic effect at relatively low cost and with deniability.
