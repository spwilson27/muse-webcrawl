Jachin and Boaz

Boaz and Jachin were two copper, brass or bronze pillars which stood on the porch of Solomon's Temple, the first Temple in Jerusalem. They are described in the books of 1 Kings and 2 Chronicles in the Hebrew Bible.

LocationPorch of Solomon's Temple, Jerusalem
MaterialBronze
HeightAbout 18 cubits each
CasterHiram of Tyre

Description

According to 1 Kings 7, the pillars were cast by Hiram, a Tyrian bronze worker. Boaz stood on the left and Jachin on the right; each was about 18 cubits tall with a capital of five cubits, decorated with lily work, pomegranates and chainwork. The names are commonly interpreted as "He shall establish" (Jachin) and "In Him is strength" (Boaz).

Symbolism and later use

The pillars are thought to have been free-standing rather than structural, a motif known from other temples in the ancient Near East. They later became important symbols in Freemasonry, where representations of Boaz and Jachin feature in lodge rooms and ritual, and they appear on the High Priestess card of the Tarot.
