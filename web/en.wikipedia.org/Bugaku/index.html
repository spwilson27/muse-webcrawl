Bugaku

Bugaku (舞楽) is a traditional Japanese court dance performed to the accompaniment of gagaku music. It developed at the imperial court during the Heian period (794–1185) from dance traditions imported from China, Korea and other parts of Asia, and has been preserved for over a thousand years. Bugaku is the dance counterpart of the instrumental kangen repertoire within gagaku, which UNESCO inscribed on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009.

Characteristics

Bugaku dances are slow, stately and highly formalised, performed on a square stage by dancers in elaborate, richly coloured costumes and, for some pieces, striking masks. The repertoire is divided into "Dances of the Left", accompanied by music of Chinese and Indian origin (tōgaku), and "Dances of the Right", of Korean and Manchurian origin (komagaku), traditionally performed by separate groups distinguished by red and green costumes. Movements are symmetrical and deliberate, often imitating animals, warriors or supernatural beings.

History and performance today

Bugaku was patronised by the imperial court and major shrines and temples, and its performance knowledge was guarded by hereditary guilds of court musicians. After the Meiji Restoration, the tradition was centralised under the Imperial Household, whose Music Department still performs it today. Public performances are given at imperial court ceremonies, at shrines such as Itsukushima and Kasuga, and occasionally abroad. In the 20th century, composers including Tōru Takemitsu drew on bugaku's sounds and imagery. See also: Buddhism in Japan, Gagaku.
