World Scout Jamboree

The World Scout Jamboree is a gathering of Scouts from around the world, organised by the World Organization of the Scout Movement (WOSM) approximately every four years. It typically brings together tens of thousands of young people aged 14 to 17, along with adult leaders and staff, for a programme of outdoor activities, cultural exchange and community service under a shared camp.

History

The first World Scout Jamboree was held in 1920 at Olympia, London, where Robert Baden-Powell was acclaimed "Chief Scout of the World." Subsequent jamborees were held in Denmark (1924), England (1929, at Arrowe Park), Hungary (1933) and the Netherlands (1937). After a Second World War interruption, the event resumed in France in 1947. Notably, the 1957 jamboree at Sutton Park, England marked Scouting's golden jubilee and the centenary of Baden-Powell's birth, and the 2007 jamboree at Hylands Park marked the movement's centenary.

Recent and upcoming jamborees

  • 2011 — Rinkaby, Sweden (22nd)
  • 2015 — Kirara-hama, Yamaguchi, Japan (23rd)
  • 2019 — Summit Bechtel Reserve, West Virginia, United States (24th), jointly hosted by the United States, Canada and Mexico
  • 2023 — Saemangeum, South Korea (25th), an event disrupted by heat and an approaching typhoon that led to early evacuation of contingents
  • 2027 — scheduled for Gdańsk, Poland (26th)

Programme

Participants live in subcamps, often named for places or themes, and take part in activities such as hiking, water sports, science and technology centres, global development villages, faith and cultural celebrations, and "solidarity" projects hosted with local communities. Those unable to attend in person have historically been supported through the Jamboree On The Air and Jamboree On The Internet (JOTA-JOTI).
