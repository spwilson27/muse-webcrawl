Guyanese cuisine

Guyanese cuisine is the culinary tradition of Guyana, on the northern coast of South America. It reflects the country's multicultural population — East Indian, African, Indigenous Amerindian, Chinese, Portuguese and other European influences — blending into a distinctive Creole food culture closely related to that of the wider Caribbean.

Influences and staples

East Indian indentured labourers brought curries, roti (including paratha, called "oil roti" or "buss up shut" when flaky and shredded), dhal and sweets such as gulab jamun and mithai. African traditions contributed cook-up rice — a one-pot dish of rice, beans, coconut milk and meats — and metemgee, a hearty stew of ground provisions in coconut milk. The Indigenous cassava yields cassava bread, pepperpot and the potent cassareep sauce, made from bitter cassava juice, which gives pepperpot its characteristic flavour and preserving quality.

Signature dishes

Pepperpot, made with beef or pork stewed in cassareep with cinnamon and hot peppers, is Guyana's national dish, traditionally served at Christmas with homemade bread. Other favourites include chicken curry with roti, chow mein and fried rice in a distinctly Guyanese-Chinese style, garlic pork of Portuguese origin, black cake rich with rum-soaked fruit, and pine tarts and cheese rolls from bakeries. Street snacks include egg balls, pholourie with mango sour, and plantain chips.

Drinks

Popular drinks include mauby (from tree bark), sorrel, ginger beer, peanut punch and swank (a lime drink), as well as Demerara rum renowned worldwide. Fresh coconut water and fruit juices from mango, soursop and golden apple are common.
