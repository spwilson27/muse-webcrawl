Breeches role

Breeches role
TypeTheatre and opera casting
Also calledPants role, trouser role (Hosenrolle)
Origin17th–18th century stage

A breeches role (also known as a pants role or trouser role) is a role in a theatrical or operatic production in which a male character is played by a female performer. Such roles are named for the breeches or trousers the actor wears as part of her male costume, a striking sight in eras when women ordinarily wore skirts on stage.


A singer as a young nobleman in a trouser role.

History

When women first appeared on the English Restoration stage in the 1660s, breeches parts let actresses show their legs — then considered daring — and became popular in comedy. In opera, breeches roles frequently depict adolescent boys whose unbroken voices suit a female singer's range: the page Cherubino in Mozart's The Marriage of Figaro, Octavian in Richard Strauss's Der Rosenkavalier, and the Composer in Ariadne auf Naxos are famous examples.

In opera

Such parts are usually written for mezzo-sopranos. Other notable trouser roles include Romeo in Bellini's I Capuleti e i Montecchi, Sesto in Mozart's La clemenza di Tito, and Hänsel in Humperdinck's Hänsel und Gretel. The practice continues today, sometimes with the gender-crossing serving thematic ends, as in productions that play on the layering of a woman performing masculinity.

Related practices

Breeches roles differ from travesti in some terminology but overlap heavily; they are distinct from the historical castrato roles they sometimes replaced, and from female characters in disguise as men, which are usually sung by women written as female characters.
