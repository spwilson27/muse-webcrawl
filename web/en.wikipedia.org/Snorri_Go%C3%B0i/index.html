Snorri Goði


The Snæfellsnes peninsula, Snorri's home region in western Iceland

Snorri Þorgrímsson (963–1031), better known as Snorri Goði ("Snorri the Chieftain-Priest"), was a prominent chieftain (goði) in western Iceland during the Viking Age and the early Commonwealth period. He is one of the best-known Icelanders of the saga age, figuring prominently in several Icelandic sagas, including Eyrbyggja saga (in which he is the central figure), Njáls saga, Laxdæla saga, and Grettis saga.

Life

Snorri was born at Helgafell on the Snæfellsnes peninsula, the son of Þorgrímr Þorsteinsson, who was killed before his birth in a feud arising from the events surrounding Gísli Súrsson. He inherited his father's chieftaincy and, after a fostering with his uncle Þorgrímr's killer's family connections, built up extensive power in the Breiðafjörður region. He acquired the goðorð and lands around Helgafell and later moved to Tunga in Sælingsdalur.

Snorri was known more for cunning and political calculation than for martial prowess; the sagas portray him as a wily, patient, and often ruthless operator who arranged marriages, settlements, and killings to extend his influence. He played a part in the Christianization of Iceland around the year 1000, building a church at Helgafell, though the sagas suggest his piety was pragmatic. He was involved in the aftermath of the burning of Njáll, in the feud following the death of Bolli Þorleiksson, and in numerous local disputes.

In the sagas

Eyrbyggja saga is in large part a biography of Snorri, tracing his feuds with Arnkell goði and the Þórsnesingar, his role in the haunting at Fróðá, and his later years. His death in 1031 is recorded in several sources; he was buried at the church he had built.
