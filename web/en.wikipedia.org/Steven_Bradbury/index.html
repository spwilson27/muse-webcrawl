Steven Bradbury

Steven John Bradbury (born 14 October 1973) is an Australian former short-track speed skater. He won the gold medal in the 1000 metres at the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City — Australia's first Winter Olympic gold medal.

Steven Bradbury (born 14 October 1973) is an Australian former short track speed skater best known for his unexpected victory in the men's 1000 metres at the 2002 Winter Olympics, when all four of his rivals crashed on the final turn, allowing him to skate through and win Australia's first-ever Winter Olympic gold medal. The phrase "doing a Bradbury" entered Australian slang for an unlikely come-from-behind win.

Early career

Bradbury was part of the Australian 5000 metre relay team that won a bronze medal at the 1994 Winter Olympics in Lillehammer — Australia's first Winter Olympic medal of any kind. His career was repeatedly interrupted by severe injuries, including a 1994 World Cup crash in which another skater's blade severed his femoral artery, costing him 4 litres of blood, and a broken neck from a training accident in 2000.

Salt Lake City 2002

At the Salt Lake City Games, Bradbury reached the 1000 m final after crashes and a disqualification cleared his path in earlier rounds. In the final, skating at the back of the field, he watched the four skaters ahead of him — including favourite Apolo Ohno — collide and fall at the last corner, and glided past to take gold. He later said he had deliberately held back, knowing his rivals might tangle while racing aggressively.

Later life

Bradbury retired after 2002 and became a motivational speaker, commentator and television presenter. He was awarded the Medal of the Order of Australia and inducted into the Sport Australia Hall of Fame.
