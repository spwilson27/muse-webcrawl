Fallout shelter

A fallout shelter is an enclosed space specially designed to protect occupants from radioactive debris or fallout resulting from a nuclear explosion. Many such shelters were constructed as civil defence measures during the Cold War.

Design

Fallout shelters rely on shielding: dense materials such as earth, concrete, and lead attenuate gamma radiation from deposited fallout. A shelter with earth packed at least three feet thick reduces radiation to about 1/125 of the outside level. Shelters are stocked with food, water, sanitation supplies, and a battery-powered radio, typically for an occupancy of about two weeks, after which fallout radiation has decayed substantially.

History

During the 1950s and 1960s, the United States and other nations promoted public fallout shelters, which were marked with yellow-and-black signs and stocked under federal civil defence programmes. Switzerland required shelter space in new dwellings, resulting in capacity for nearly its entire population. Interest declined with the end of the Cold War.
