Matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization

In mass spectrometry, matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization (MALDI) is an ionization technique that uses a laser energy-absorbing matrix to create ions from large molecules with minimal fragmentation. It has been applied to the analysis of biomolecules (biopolymers such as DNA, proteins, peptides and carbohydrates) and various organic molecules, which tend to be fragile and fragment when ionized by more conventional ionization methods.

MALDI
AnalytesProteins, peptides, polymers, lipids
AnalyzerCommonly time-of-flight (TOF)
DevelopersKoichi Tanaka; Michael Karas and Franz Hillenkamp

Principle

The sample is mixed with a small organic matrix compound that absorbs at the laser wavelength. Pulses from a UV laser vaporize and ionize the mixture; the matrix protects the analyte and transfers charge to it, producing mainly singly charged ions. MALDI is commonly coupled with time-of-flight analyzers (MALDI-TOF).

History

Koichi Tanaka demonstrated soft laser desorption of large proteins in the late 1980s, work for which he shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Michael Karas and Franz Hillenkamp developed the matrix-assisted method using organic matrices at the same period.

Applications

MALDI-TOF is used in proteomics for peptide mass fingerprinting, in clinical microbiology for rapid identification of bacteria and fungi, in polymer analysis, and in MALDI imaging mass spectrometry of tissue sections.
