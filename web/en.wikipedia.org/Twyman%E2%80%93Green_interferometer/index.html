Twyman–Green interferometer

Twyman–Green interferometer
Invented by: Frank Twyman and Arthur Green (1916)
Use: testing optical components
Based on: Michelson interferometer

The Twyman–Green interferometer, invented in 1916 and patented by Frank Twyman and Arthur Green, is a variant of the Michelson interferometer used to test optical components such as lenses, prisms and mirrors with high precision.

Design

Unlike the original Michelson instrument, it uses a point source of monochromatic light and collimating optics so that a plane wavefront traverses the test and reference arms. Imperfections in the test component distort the returning wavefront and produce characteristic deformations of the interference fringes.

Applications

Interferograms from a Twyman–Green setup allow optical workshops to measure surface figure errors to fractions of a wavelength. With laser sources and computerised phase-shifting analysis, the design remains a standard in precision optical metrology.
