Détente

Détente (French for "relaxation", pronounced [detɑ̃t]) is a term in international relations describing the easing of strained relations, especially between states. In the Cold War context it refers to the phase of improved relations between the United States and the Soviet Union from the late 1960s to the late 1970s, marked by arms-control agreements, expanded trade, and summit diplomacy.

Origins

The idea of a thaw had precedents after Stalin's death in 1953 and the "spirit of Camp David" of 1959. Practical steps included the Limited Test Ban Treaty (1963), the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968), and West Germany's Ostpolitik under Willy Brandt, which normalised relations with Eastern Europe. Under President Richard Nixon and National Security Advisor (later Secretary of State) Henry Kissinger, the United States pursued détente through "linkage"—connecting Soviet cooperation on arms control and Vietnam to trade and technology benefits—while also exploiting the Sino-Soviet split through rapprochement with China in 1972.

High point

Nixon's visit to Moscow in May 1972 produced the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty and the SALT I Interim Agreement limiting strategic missiles—the first major arms-control accords of the nuclear age. Trade expanded, including large American grain sales to the USSR. The 1975 Helsinki Final Act of the Conference on Security and Co-operation in Europe confirmed post-war European borders in exchange for human-rights commitments ("Basket III") that later empowered dissidents. Soviet leader Leonid Brezhnev and Presidents Gerald Ford and Jimmy Carter continued the process, signing the SALT II treaty in 1979.

Criticism and collapse

American critics, both conservative hawks and liberal human-rights advocates, attacked détente: the Jackson–Vanik Amendment (1974) linked trade to Soviet Jewish emigration, embarrassing Moscow; Ronald Reagan campaigned against it as one-sided accommodation. Competition continued in the Third World (Angola, the Horn of Africa, Nicaragua). The Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in December 1979 ended the era: Carter withdrew SALT II from Senate consideration, embargoed grain, and boycotted the 1980 Moscow Olympics. Reagan's election in 1980 brought confrontation and a renewed arms build-up, often called the "Second Cold War."
