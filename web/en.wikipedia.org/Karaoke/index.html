Karaoke

Karaoke
Origin: Japan, 1970s
Etymology: kara ("empty") + orchestra
Key figure: Daisuke Inoue, inventor of the karaoke machine

Karaoke is a form of interactive entertainment in which amateur singers sing along with recorded music using a microphone, while the lyrics are displayed on a screen, usually with a moving symbol or changing colours to guide the singer. The word combines the Japanese kara ("empty") and ōkesutora ("orchestra").

History

Karaoke emerged in Japan in the early 1970s. Musician Daisuke Inoue built the first karaoke machine, the 8 Juke, in Kobe in 1971, leasing tape decks that played instrumental versions of popular songs. The concept spread through Japanese bars and later, with dedicated private rooms called karaoke boxes, throughout Asia and the world.

Culture

Karaoke became a global phenomenon in forms ranging from pub nights to home video games and smartphone apps. In South Korea the equivalent venue is the noraebang; in China and Taiwan, KTV. See also Daisuke Inoue.
