Primal Scream are a Scottish rock band formed in Glasgow in 1982 by singer Bobby Gillespie, formerly the drummer of The Jesus and Mary Chain. Initially an indie pop group associated with the C86 scene, the band achieved a breakthrough by fusing rock with the emerging acid house and rave culture on their third album, Screamadelica (1991).

Career

The band's first two albums, Sonic Flower Groove (1987) and Primal Scream (1989), drew on 1960s psychedelia and hard rock. Working with producers including Andrew Weatherall, who transformed their song "Loaded" into a euphoric dance anthem, Primal Scream created Screamadelica, which won the first Mercury Prize in 1992 and is regularly ranked among the greatest albums of all time. The follow-up Give Out But Don't Give Up (1994) turned toward Southern rock and soul.

Later albums explored dub, krautrock and electronica (Vanishing Point, 1997, featuring the hit "Kowalski"), aggressive political electronica (XTRMNTR, 2000), and country-rock (Riot City Blues, 2006). Long-serving bassist Gary "Mani" Mounfield, formerly of The Stone Roses, played with the band from 1996 to 2011. The band have remained a prominent live act, touring extensively and marking anniversaries of Screamadelica with full-album performances.
