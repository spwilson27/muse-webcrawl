Lijiang

Lijiang is a prefecture-level city in the northwest of Yunnan Province, China. It has an area of about 21,000 square kilometres and a population of over a million. The city is famous for the Old Town of Lijiang, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1997.

Geography

Lijiang lies on the southeastern edge of the Tibetan Plateau near the Jinsha River (the upper Yangtze). The region is mountainous, with Jade Dragon Snow Mountain (Yulong Xueshan) rising to 5,596 metres north of the old town.

History and culture

The area has long been home to the Nakhi (Naxi) people, known for their Dongba pictographic script and matrilineal traditions in some communities. The old town, with its canals, stone bridges, and wooden architecture, developed as a trading post on the Tea Horse Road. The Mu family ruled as local chieftains for centuries under imperial Chinese suzerainty.

Tourism

Tourism is the dominant industry, drawing visitors to the old town, Jade Dragon Snow Mountain, Tiger Leaping Gorge on the nearby Jinsha River, and Lugu Lake to the northeast.
