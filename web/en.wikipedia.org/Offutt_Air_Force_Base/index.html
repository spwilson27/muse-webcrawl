Offutt Air Force Base

Offutt Air Force Base is a United States Air Force base near Bellevue, Nebraska, south of Omaha. It is the headquarters of United States Strategic Command (USSTRATCOM), the 557th Weather Wing, and the 55th Wing of Air Combat Command. The base played a central role in Cold War strategic deterrence as the headquarters of Strategic Air Command (SAC) from 1948 to 1992.

Offutt AFB lies on the west bank of the Missouri River.

History

The site began as Fort Crook, an Army post established in 1890. Its airfield was named in 1924 for Jarvis Offutt, an Omaha aviator killed in the First World War. During the Second World War the Glenn L. Martin plant at the field built B-26 Marauders and B-29 Superfortresses, including the Enola Gay and Bockscar. SAC moved its headquarters to Offutt in 1948, and its underground command post directed US nuclear forces throughout the Cold War.

September 11, 2001

On 9/11, President George W. Bush was flown to Offutt, where he used the USSTRATCOM command facilities for a secure video conference before returning to Washington — the episode made the base briefly famous worldwide.

Modern role

USSTRATCOM, established in its current form in 2002 and reorganised in 2018–2019, oversees nuclear deterrence, global strike, and missile defence; its new command and control facility opened in 2019. The 55th Wing flies RC-135 reconnaissance aircraft. Severe Missouri River flooding in March 2019 inundated a third of the base, prompting a major reconstruction programme.
