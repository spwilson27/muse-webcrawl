The Africa Cup of Nations, commonly referred to as AFCON, is the main international men's association football competition in Africa. It is sanctioned by the Confederation of African Football (CAF) and was first held in 1957 in Khartoum, Sudan, where Egypt defeated Ethiopia in the final.

History

The tournament grew from three founding nations to sixteen teams in 1996 and twenty-four in 2019. It was long held every two years; from 2013 it moved to odd-numbered years to avoid clashing with the FIFA World Cup, and from 2019 it was played in mid-year rather than January–February.

Records

Egypt is the most successful team with seven titles, including three consecutive wins in 2006, 2008, and 2010. Cameroon has won five titles and Ghana four. Notable individual records include Samuel Eto'o's eighteen tournament goals.
