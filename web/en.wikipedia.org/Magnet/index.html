Magnet

A magnet is a material or object that produces a magnetic field. This field is invisible but is responsible for the force that pulls on ferromagnetic materials, such as iron, nickel, and cobalt, and attracts or repels other magnets. A permanent magnet retains its field without external power; an electromagnet produces a field only while electric current flows.

Magnetic poles and fields

Every magnet has two poles, north and south: like poles repel and opposite poles attract. Cutting a magnet in two produces two smaller magnets, each with both poles. The field around a magnet can be visualised with iron filings and is quantified as magnetic flux. The Earth itself behaves as a giant magnet, which is why compass needles align with its field.

Materials and types

Common permanent magnets include ferrite (ceramic) magnets, alnico alloys, samarium–cobalt, and the exceptionally strong neodymium magnets developed in the 1980s. Magnetism arises from the alignment of magnetic domains and ultimately from electron spin and orbital motion.

Uses

Magnets are fundamental to electric motors, generators, loudspeakers, magnetic resonance imaging, data storage, compasses, and levitating trains such as those described under maglev. Instruments called magnetometers measure the strength and direction of magnetic fields.
