Crowd control

Illustration: Crowd control

Crowd control is the practice of managing large groups of people to prevent disorder, crushes, and stampedes. It is applied at public events such as concerts, sporting fixtures, protests, festivals, and religious gatherings, and by police and private security personnel.

Methods

Techniques include barriers and fencing, controlled entry and exit points, ticketing and capacity limits, stewarding, clear signage, and communication with the crowd. Modern practice emphasises crowd management—planning, monitoring crowd density and mood, and intervening early—rather than reactive force.

Disasters and reform

Tragedies such as the Hillsborough disaster of 1989, which killed 97 Liverpool football supporters, led to major reforms in stadium design and crowd safety science. Research by crowd psychologists has shown that crowds rarely "panic" irrationally, and that treating crowds as dangerous can itself escalate tension.

Equipment

Crowd control barriers, turnstiles, public-address systems, and CCTV monitoring are standard tools. Riot police may additionally deploy shields, batons, water cannon, and chemical irritants in confrontational situations.
