Non-commissioned officer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A non-commissioned officer (NCO) is a military officer who has not pursued a commission. NCOs usually obtain their position of authority by promotion from the enlisted ranks and hold command over junior soldiers. In contrast to commissioned officers, who receive their authority from a commission issued by the head of state, NCOs derive their authority from higher-ranking officers and from their experience and leadership.

Function

NCOs are often described as the "backbone" of the armed forces. They are responsible for the day-to-day training, discipline, welfare, and supervision of enlisted personnel, and they translate officers' orders into practical action at squad, section, and platoon level. Senior NCOs also advise officers and mentor junior leaders.

Ranks

Typical NCO ranks in armies include corporal, sergeant, staff sergeant, and warrant officer grades, as well as senior appointments such as sergeant major and command sergeant major. In navies, petty officers and chief petty officers fill equivalent roles. The exact structure varies between countries and services.

History

The modern NCO corps developed over the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries as armies professionalised. The United States Army formalised NCO duties in publications such as von Steuben's Regulations of 1779, and most professional armed forces now operate dedicated NCO academies and career courses.
