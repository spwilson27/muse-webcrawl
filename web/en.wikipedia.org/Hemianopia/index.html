Hemianopia

Visual field defect in homonymous hemianopia

Hemianopia (or hemianopsia) is a loss of vision or blindness in half of the visual field of one or both eyes. It results from damage to the visual pathways behind the optic chiasm, most commonly from stroke, traumatic brain injury, or tumours affecting the occipital lobe, optic radiations, or optic tract.

Types

In homonymous hemianopia, the same half of the visual field is lost in both eyes — for example, loss of the left half of vision in each eye following a lesion of the right retrochiasmal pathway. In heteronymous hemianopia, different halves are lost: bitemporal hemianopia, loss of the outer (temporal) fields of both eyes, typically caused by a pituitary tumour pressing on the optic chiasm, and binasal hemianopia, a rare loss of the inner fields. Quadrantanopia is loss of a quarter, rather than a half, of the visual field.

Diagnosis and management

The defect is mapped by visual field testing (perimetry), and imaging of the brain identifies the underlying lesion. There is no cure that reliably restores the lost field; management centres on treating the cause and on rehabilitation, including scanning training, compensatory eye-movement strategies, and in some cases prism lenses. Hemianopia can severely impair reading and driving, and may be accompanied by hemispatial neglect when the right parietal lobe is involved.
