Richardson number

The Richardson number (Ri), named after Lewis Fry Richardson, is a dimensionless number in fluid dynamics that expresses the ratio of the buoyancy term to the flow shear term. It is used to characterise the stability of a stratified flow and the likelihood of turbulence.

Definition

The gradient Richardson number is defined as Ri = (g/ρ) (∂ρ/∂z) / (∂u/∂z)2, where g is gravity, ρ is density, and u is velocity varying with height z. Related forms include the bulk Richardson number, computed over a whole layer.

Significance

When the Richardson number is small (much less than one), flow shear dominates and buoyancy is unimportant. The critical value is about 0.25: if the gradient Richardson number falls below about 1/4 somewhere in the flow, velocity shear can overcome stratification and Kelvin–Helmholtz instability and turbulence can occur. Large values indicate stable stratification that suppresses vertical mixing.

Applications

The number is used in meteorology and oceanography to assess atmospheric stability, clear-air turbulence, and mixing in the ocean and in engineering flows, including thermal stratification in nuclear reactors.
