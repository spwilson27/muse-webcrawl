Arthur Antunes Coimbra (born 3 March 1953), better known as Zico, is a Brazilian football coach and former player who played as an attacking midfielder. Widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time, he was renowned for his playmaking, dribbling and free kicks, and is often called the "White Pelé". He was chosen as one of the greatest living footballers by Pelé in the FIFA 100 list of 2004.

Club career

Zico spent most of his career with Flamengo of Rio de Janeiro, where he became the club's all-time top scorer. He led Flamengo to a golden era, winning four Brazilian league titles, the 1981 Copa Libertadores and the 1981 Intercontinental Cup against Liverpool. From 1983 to 1985 he played for Udinese in Italy's Serie A, before returning to Flamengo. He ended his playing career in Japan with Kashima Antlers, helping to popularise football in the country.

International career

Zico earned 71 caps for Brazil and scored 48 goals. He starred at the 1978, 1982 and 1986 World Cups; the 1982 side, featuring Zico, Sócrates and Falcão, is often remembered as one of the finest teams never to win the tournament. A penalty miss against France in the 1986 quarter-finals marked the end of his World Cup story.

Coaching career

After retiring, Zico coached the Japan national team to the 2004 Asian Cup title and the 2006 World Cup, then managed Fenerbahçe, CSKA Moscow, Olympiacos and the Iraq national team, among others. He also served as Brazil's Minister of Sports in 1990–1991.
