Makars

The makars were the poets of Renaissance Scotland, writing in Middle Scots in the 15th and early 16th centuries. The word makar means "maker" or poet. Their work, influenced by Chaucer and continental models, is noted for its variety: courtly romance, moral allegory, satire, comic flyting and religious verse.

Principal figures

Leading makars include Robert Henryson, William Dunbar, Gavin Douglas, and David Lyndsay, with King James I of Scotland also remembered for The Kingis Quair. Henryson's Morall Fabillis rework Aesop's fables; Dunbar produced dazzling court and satirical poetry; Douglas's Eneados was the first complete translation of Virgil's Aeneid into a form of English.

Legacy

The makars established Scots as a major literary language. The modern title of Makar — Scotland's national poet laureate, instituted in 2004 — consciously revives their name.
