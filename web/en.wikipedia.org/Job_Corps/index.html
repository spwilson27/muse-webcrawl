Job Corps

Founded1964
AgencyU.S. Department of Labor
PurposeEducation and vocational training
ParticipantsAges 16–24

Job Corps is a voluntary program administered by the United States Department of Labor that offers free education and vocational training to young men and women ages 16 to 24. It is the largest nationwide residential career training program in the United States.

History

Job Corps was established in 1964 as part of President Lyndon B. Johnson's War on Poverty, under the Economic Opportunity Act of 1964. Modelled in part on the Civilian Conservation Corps of the 1930s, its first director was Sargent Shriver. The first centre opened in 1965 at Camp Catoctin, Maryland.

Program

Students live at Job Corps centres — there are over 100 across the United States, many operated by contractors and some conservation centres run by the U.S. Forest Service. Participants can earn a high school diploma or equivalent, learn a trade in areas such as construction, healthcare, hospitality, manufacturing, automotive repair and information technology, and receive assistance with job placement. Room, board, basic medical care and a living allowance are provided at no cost.

Eligibility and outcomes

Applicants must be low-income, U.S. citizens or legal residents, and face barriers to employment or education. Studies by Mathematica and others have examined earnings gains, finding modest positive effects particularly for older participants. The program has periodically faced scrutiny over cost, safety incidents and outcomes, and proposals to reform or reduce it.

Administration

The program is administered by the Employment and Training Administration of the Department of Labor. Annual funding has been around $1.7 billion in recent years.
