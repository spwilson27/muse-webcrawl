Satya Yuga

Satya Yuga (Sanskrit: सत्य युग), also known as Krita Yuga (कृत युग), in Hinduism is the first and best of the four yugas (world ages) in a Yuga Cycle, preceded by Kali Yuga of the previous cycle and followed by Treta Yuga. Satya Yuga lasts for 1,728,000 years (4,800 divine years). It is a period of peace, harmony, stability, and prosperity, in which humankind is governed by the gods and manifests their most virtuous qualities.

Description

In the Satya Yuga, dharma (righteousness) is said to stand on all four legs, like a bull, in contrast to later ages in which it stands on progressively fewer. Ignorance, vice, and suffering are absent; human beings live extremely long lives—traditionally up to 100,000 years in some Puranic accounts—sustained by meditation and virtue rather than by labour. There is only one Veda and one occupation per person, and people are said to attain whatever they desire by contemplation alone.

The age begins after Kali Yuga ends, when the avatar Kalki destroys the wicked and restores dharma. Hindu texts such as the Mahabharata, the Manusmriti, and the Vishnu Purana describe the Satya Yuga as a time when there was no disease, no old age, no buying or selling, and no distinction of class as later known.

Duration and chronology

Each yuga consists of a main period preceded by its dawn (sandhya) and followed by its dusk (sandhyamsa), each one-tenth of the main period. For Satya Yuga: a dawn of 144,000 years, a main period of 1,440,000 years, and a dusk of 144,000 years, totalling 1,728,000 human years, equivalent to 4,800 divine years. The four yugas together form a chaturyuga of 4,320,000 years, and one thousand chaturyugas make a kalpa, a single day of Brahma.
