Zecharia Sitchin (11 July 1920 – 9 October 2010) was an author of books proposing an explanation for human origins involving ancient astronauts. He attributed the creation of the ancient Sumerian culture to the Anunnaki, whom he claimed were a race of extraterrestrials from a hypothetical planet called Nibiru, which he asserted approaches the inner Solar System on a 3,600-year elliptical orbit. His ideas are rejected by scientists and historians as pseudoscience and pseudohistory.

Life

Sitchin was born in Baku, then part of the Soviet Union, and raised in Mandatory Palestine, where he learned Hebrew and studied the Old Testament. He earned a degree in economics from the University of London and worked in Israel as a journalist and editor before moving to New York.

Theories

His best-known work, The 12th Planet (1976), was the first volume of the "Earth Chronicles" series. Drawing on his own translations of Sumerian cuneiform texts, Sitchin argued that the Anunnaki genetically engineered Homo sapiens as a slave species to mine gold. He also associated Nibiru's passages with catastrophic events in Earth's past.

Criticism

Sumerologists and astronomers have criticised Sitchin's translations as inaccurate, his reading of the texts as selective, and the Nibiru hypothesis as astronomically implausible; no planet with the orbit he described has been detected.
