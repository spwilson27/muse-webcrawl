Razorback

Razorback is a term for a feral pig (Sus scrofa domesticus) of the southern United States, especially in Arkansas, Texas, and Florida. The name refers to the ridge of coarse bristles along the animal's back. Razorbacks descend from domestic pigs brought by Spanish explorers and early settlers that escaped or were released and reverted to a lean, wary, wild form over generations.

Characteristics and impact

Feral hogs are highly adaptable omnivores that root up soil, damage crops and pastures, prey on ground-nesting birds and livestock, and spread disease; US wildlife agencies regard them as a destructive invasive pest causing billions of dollars in annual damage. They are hunted extensively, both for control and as game.

In culture

The razorback is the mascot of the University of Arkansas: the Arkansas Razorbacks adopted the name in 1910 after coach Hugo Bezdek declared his team had fought "like a wild band of razorback hogs". The term also titles the 1984 Australian horror film Razorback.
