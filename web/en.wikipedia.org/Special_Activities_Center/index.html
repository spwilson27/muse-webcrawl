Special Activities Center

The Special Activities Center (SAC) is a division of the United States Central Intelligence Agency responsible for covert operations and paramilitary operations. Until 2016 it was known as the Special Activities Division (SAD). The Center conducts deniable operations — including direct action, special reconnaissance, unconventional warfare and the training of foreign forces — under presidential findings authorising covert action.

Organisation

The SAC contains two main groups. The Special Operations Group (SOG) is the paramilitary arm, staffed by Paramilitary Operations Officers, many recruited from US special operations units such as Delta Force, SEAL Team Six and Marine Raiders; its Ground Branch, Air Branch (which operates unmanned aerial vehicles and aircraft), Maritime Branch and Armor and Special Programs Branch carry out tactical paramilitary tasks. The Political Action Group (PAG) handles covert political influence, economic operations and cyber operations. A small number of women serve in the Center; its officers account for a large share of the stars on the CIA Memorial Wall honouring officers killed in the line of duty.

History

The SAC descends from the wartime Office of Strategic Services and the CIA's Cold War paramilitary programmes in Korea, Tibet, Laos, Cuba and Nicaragua. More recently, SOG teams were among the first American forces into Afghanistan in October 2001, working with the Northern Alliance, and operated in Iraq before and during the 2003 invasion. Paramilitary officer Johnny Micheal Spann was the first American killed in combat in Afghanistan, in November 2001.
