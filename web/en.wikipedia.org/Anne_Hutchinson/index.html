Anne Hutchinson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Anne Hutchinson (née Marbury; baptised 20 July 1591 – 20 August 1643) was a Puritan religious leader in colonial Massachusetts whose teachings sparked the Antinomian Controversy and led to her trial and banishment. She is regarded as an early advocate of religious freedom and of women's ministry in America.

Life in New England

Hutchinson emigrated from England in 1634, following the minister John Cotton to Boston. A skilled midwife, she began holding meetings in her home to discuss sermons, attracting large followings, including men. She taught that Christians were saved by a "covenant of grace" rather than a "covenant of works", and criticised most of the colony's ministers as legalistic preachers — a direct challenge to the Puritan establishment.

Trial and banishment

In November 1637 she was tried before the General Court, Governor John Winthrop presiding, and convicted of sedition and heresy after she claimed direct divine revelation. Banished from Massachusetts, she settled first at Portsmouth on Aquidneck Island (Rhode Island), then moved to New Netherland in 1642. In August 1643 she and most of her household were killed by Siwanoy warriors during Kieft's War.

Legacy

Hutchinson has been reinterpreted over the centuries as a heroine of conscience and dissent; a statue of her stands outside the Massachusetts State House.
