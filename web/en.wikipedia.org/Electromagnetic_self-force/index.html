Electromagnetic self-force

The electromagnetic self-force is the force that a charged particle exerts on itself through its own electromagnetic field. Because an accelerating charge radiates energy, momentum conservation implies a recoil — radiation reaction — on the charge.

Theory

In classical electrodynamics the self-force for a point charge is described by the Abraham–Lorentz force, proportional to the derivative of acceleration. The resulting Abraham–Lorentz–Dirac equation is notorious for unphysical runaway solutions and pre-acceleration, reflecting the breakdown of the classical point-particle picture. Treatments model the charge as a small extended body or use the Landau–Lifshitz reduction of order.

Self-force calculations are also important in gravitational physics, where a small mass orbiting a black hole is corrected for its own gravitational field — key to predicting waveforms for space-based gravitational-wave detectors such as LISA.
