Nintedanib

Nintedanib, sold under the brand names Ofev and Vargatef, is a medication used to treat idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and certain types of non-small cell lung cancer. It is a small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor developed by Boehringer Ingelheim, targeting the vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR), fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR) and platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR).

Medical uses

Nintedanib was approved by the United States Food and Drug Administration in October 2014, on the same day as pirfenidone, as the first drug treatments specifically for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Clinical trials (the INPULSIS studies) showed that it slows the decline in forced vital capacity in people with IPF. It has also been approved for systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease and for chronic fibrosing interstitial lung diseases with a progressive phenotype.

In the European Union, nintedanib (as Vargatef) is approved in combination with docetaxel for non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology after first-line chemotherapy.

Mechanism of action

Nintedanib is an intracellular inhibitor of multiple receptor tyrosine kinases. By blocking VEGFR, FGFR and PDGFR signalling, it interferes with the proliferation and migration of the fibroblasts and endothelial cells that drive fibrosis and tumour angiogenesis.

Side effects

The most common adverse effects are gastrointestinal, including diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting and abdominal pain. Liver enzyme elevations also occur, and the drug carries warnings about bleeding risk and gastrointestinal perforation.
