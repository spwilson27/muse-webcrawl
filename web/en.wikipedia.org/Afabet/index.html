Afabet (Arabic: عفابت) is a town in northern Eritrea and the administrative center of the Afabet Subregion in the Anseba Region. It lies in a valley northwest of Asmara and is known as the site of a decisive battle of the Eritrean War of Independence.

Battle of Afabet

In March 1988, the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) attacked the Ethiopian army's Nadew Command at Afabet. Between 17 and 20 March 1988, the EPLF destroyed the garrison, killing or capturing thousands of Ethiopian soldiers and capturing heavy equipment including tanks and artillery, in what historians regard as one of the largest battles in Africa since the Second World War. The victory was a turning point in the war, leading toward Eritrean independence in 1991.

Geography

Afabet sits in a basin surrounded by mountains, with a hot climate moderated by elevation. The surrounding district is largely agricultural and pastoral.
