Birth certificate

A birth certificate is a vital record that documents the birth of a person. The term "birth certificate" can refer to either the original document certifying the circumstances of the birth or to a certified copy of, or representation of, the ensuing registration of that birth. Depending on the jurisdiction, a record of birth might or might not contain verification of the event by authorities such as a midwife or doctor.

A certified copy of a birth registration.

History and function

Systematic civil registration of births developed in the 19th century — in England and Wales from 1837 — replacing reliance on parish baptismal registers. A birth certificate records at minimum the child's name, date and place of birth, sex, and the names of the parents. It serves as foundational proof of identity, age, parentage and often citizenship, and is required to obtain passports, driving licences, social benefits, school enrollment and marriage licences.

Registration worldwide

The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child recognizes every child's right to be registered immediately after birth. Nonetheless, UNICEF has estimated that the births of roughly one in four children under five worldwide are unregistered, concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, leaving them at risk of statelessness and exclusion from services.

Forms

Many jurisdictions issue both a long form, reproducing the full registration entry, and a short form or wallet card containing only basic details. In the United States, birth certificates are issued by states, counties or other local authorities rather than the federal government, and formats vary by state.
