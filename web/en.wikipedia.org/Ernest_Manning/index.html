Ernest Manning

Ernest Charles Manning PC CC AOE (20 September 1908 – 19 February 1996) was a Canadian politician who served as the eighth premier of Alberta between 1943 and 1968, the longest-serving premier in the province's history and one of the longest-serving elected leaders in Canadian history. He led the Social Credit Party of Alberta, succeeding William Aberhart, whose protégé he had been, and later served in the Senate of Canada from 1970 to 1983.

Early life and the Social Credit movement

Manning was born in Carnduff, Saskatchewan, and moved to Alberta as a young man. He enrolled at the Calgary Prophetic Bible Institute, founded by the radio evangelist and politician William Aberhart, and became Aberhart's close associate, broadcasting on his religious radio programme. When Aberhart's Social Credit movement swept the 1935 Alberta election, the 26-year-old Manning became provincial secretary and one of the youngest cabinet ministers in Canada; he later also held the trade and industry, health, and other portfolios.

Premier of Alberta

On Aberhart's sudden death in 1943, Manning became premier. He steered Social Credit away from its founding monetary theories toward pragmatic, socially conservative free-enterprise government, while managing the province's transformation after the 1947 Leduc No. 1 oil discovery. Manning's governments built modern highways and public services, maintained balanced budgets, and won eight consecutive elections. He also reached a national audience through the "Back to the Bible Hour" radio broadcasts, which he continued throughout his premiership. His son, Preston Manning, later founded the Reform Party of Canada.

Later life

Manning retired as premier in 1968 and was appointed a Companion of the Order of Canada. In 1970 Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau appointed him to the Senate, where he sat until 1983. He died in Calgary in 1996.
