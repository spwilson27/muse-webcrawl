Latvia

Latvia (Latvian: Latvija), officially the Republic of Latvia (Latvijas Republika), is a country in the Baltic region of Northern Europe. It is bordered by Estonia to the north, Lithuania to the south, Russia to the east, Belarus to the southeast, and shares a maritime border with Sweden to the west. Latvia covers an area of about 64,589 km² and has a population of roughly 1.9 million. Its capital and largest city is Riga. Latvians belong to the ethnolinguistic group of the Balts and speak Latvian, one of only two surviving Baltic languages.

History

The territory of modern Latvia was settled by Baltic tribes by around 3000 BC. In the 13th century the region was conquered by German crusaders during the Livonian Crusade, and Riga was founded in 1201. Over the following centuries the land passed between Polish-Lithuanian, Swedish and Russian rule. After the First World War, Latvia declared independence on 18 November 1918. The republic was occupied by the Soviet Union in 1940, then by Nazi Germany, and again by the USSR in 1944, remaining a Soviet republic — the Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic — until independence was restored in 1991 during the Singing Revolution.

Geography

Latvia is a low-lying country of plains, forests and lakes, with its highest point, Gaiziņkalns, reaching only 312 metres. Forests cover more than half of the territory. The longest river is the Daugava, which flows into the Gulf of Riga. The climate is temperate and maritime-influenced, with cold winters and mild summers.

Politics

Latvia is a parliamentary republic. The unicameral parliament, the Saeima, has 100 members elected every four years. The President is head of state, elected by the Saeima, and the Prime Minister heads the government. Latvia joined the European Union and NATO in 2004 and adopted the euro in 2014.

Economy

Latvia has an open market economy with services, transit trade, timber, food processing and information technology among its main sectors. Riga is a major Baltic port and transport hub.

Culture

Latvian culture is known for its folk songs (dainas), choral traditions and the Latvian Song and Dance Festival, held every five years since 1873. Midsummer (Jāņi) is the most widely celebrated holiday.
