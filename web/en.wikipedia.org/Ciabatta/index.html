Ciabatta (Italian for "slipper") is an Italian white bread made from wheat flour, water, yeast, and salt, created in 1982 by Arnaldo Cavallari, a baker in Adria, in the province of Rovigo in the Veneto region.

History

Cavallari developed the bread in response to the popularity of French baguettes in Italy, which he feared would displace Italian sandwich breads. Working with bakers including Francesco Favaron, he produced a high-hydration loaf with a crisp crust and an open, holey crumb. The bread was named ciabatta Polesana after the Polesine area, and the recipe was licensed to bakers in many countries; it was introduced to the United Kingdom in 1985 and the United States in 1987.

Characteristics

Ciabatta is made with a very wet dough, often using a preferment such as biga, and is characterised by an irregular, open crumb structure, a crisp crust dusted with flour, and a somewhat elongated, flat shape resembling a slipper. Variations include ciabatta integrale (whole wheat) and versions enriched with milk or olive oil.

Use

The bread is widely used for sandwiches and panini, and served alongside soups and salads.
