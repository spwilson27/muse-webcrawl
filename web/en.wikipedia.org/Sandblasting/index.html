Sandblasting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sandblasting, sometimes known as abrasive blasting, is the operation of forcibly propelling a stream of abrasive material against a surface under high pressure to smooth a rough surface, roughen a smooth surface, shape a surface, or remove surface contaminants. A pressurised fluid, typically compressed air, or a centrifugal wheel is used to propel the blasting material (often called the media). The first abrasive blasting process was patented by Benjamin Chew Tilghman on 18 October 1870.

Types

Several variants of the process exist, including bead blasting, soda blasting, wet abrasive blasting, and vacuum blasting. Blasting media range from the original silica sand to steel shot and grit, aluminium oxide, crushed glass, plastic beads, walnut shells, and dry ice. The choice of medium depends on the substrate and the desired finish: harder, angular media cut aggressively, while softer or spherical media clean or polish with less surface damage.

Applications

Sandblasting is used to remove rust, scale, and old paint from metal before repainting or coating; to clean building façades and monuments; to etch glass and stone; to deburr machined parts; and in the preparation of surfaces for thermal spraying and other coating processes. A related consumer use is the distressing of denim garments.

Health and safety

Blasting with crystalline silica sand generates respirable dust that can cause silicosis, a serious and irreversible lung disease, and many jurisdictions have banned or restricted silica sand as a blasting medium. Operators require protective equipment including supplied-air helmets, and blasting is often performed in enclosed cabinets or rooms with dust extraction. Environmental rules also govern the containment and disposal of spent media contaminated with removed coatings such as lead paint.
