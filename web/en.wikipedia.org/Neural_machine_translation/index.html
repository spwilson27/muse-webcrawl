Neural machine translation

Neural machine translation (NMT) is an approach to machine translation that uses an artificial neural network to predict the likelihood of a sequence of words, typically modelling entire sentences in a single integrated model. It largely replaced statistical machine translation in production systems during the mid-2010s.

Architecture

Early NMT systems used an encoder–decoder architecture of recurrent neural networks: the encoder converts a source sentence into a sequence of vectors, and the decoder generates the translation, guided by an attention mechanism that lets it focus on relevant source words. The Transformer architecture, introduced in 2017, replaced recurrence with self-attention and became the standard design.

Adoption

Google Translate switched to neural models (GNMT) in 2016, reporting large reductions in translation errors compared with its phrase-based predecessor. NMT systems generally produce more fluent output than earlier methods but can make errors such as silently omitting content or hallucinating fluent but wrong translations.
