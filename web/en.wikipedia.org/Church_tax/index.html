Church tax

Type: Tax or membership levy

Examples: Germany, Austria, Nordic countries

A church tax is a levy collected from members of recognized religious communities to finance their activities, sometimes administered by the state. Systems exist in countries including Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Denmark, Finland, and Sweden, with different legal forms.

Operation

In Germany the tax is generally calculated as a percentage of income tax and withheld through payroll for registered members of participating churches. Leaving the church through a formal declaration ends liability but may also affect access to some church services.

Debate

Supporters value stable funding for worship, heritage, education, and social services. Critics question state involvement, administrative fees, disclosure of religious affiliation to employers, and treatment of people baptized as infants.
