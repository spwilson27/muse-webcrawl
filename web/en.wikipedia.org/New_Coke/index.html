New Coke was the unofficial name of a reformulation of the soft drink Coca-Cola, launched by the Coca-Cola Company in April 1985. It was renamed Coke II in 1990 and discontinued in July 2002.

Background

By the early 1980s Coca-Cola was losing market share to Pepsi, whose "Pepsi Challenge" blind taste tests showed consumers preferring Pepsi's sweeter taste. Coca-Cola's own extensive testing — some 200,000 taste tests — indicated a new, smoother, sweeter formula outperformed both original Coke and Pepsi. On 23 April 1985 Coca-Cola announced the change, the first alteration of the secret formula in 99 years, and discontinued the original.

Backlash and reversal

The reaction was overwhelmingly negative. The company received more than 1,500 angry calls a day; protest groups such as the Society for the Preservation of Old Coke Drinkers formed, and consumers hoarded the original. The fiasco became a cultural touchstone — later often called the greatest marketing blunder of all time. Just 79 days later, on 11 July 1985, Coca-Cola announced the return of the original formula as "Coca-Cola Classic". Some conspiracy theorists claimed the whole episode was a deliberate strategy to reinvigorate the brand — a suggestion company president Donald Keough dismissed: "we're not that dumb, and we're not that smart".

Aftermath

The return of the original restored and increased Coke's lead over Pepsi, and the episode is now a standard business-school case study in brand loyalty and the limits of market research. In 2019, New Coke was briefly re-released in a limited promotion tied to the Netflix series Stranger Things.
