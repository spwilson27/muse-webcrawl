Waijia

Waijia (Chinese: 外家; pinyin: wàijiā; literally "external family") is a category within Chinese martial arts, denoting styles classified as "external" arts, in contrast to the "internal" arts known as neijia. External styles are generally characterized by fast, explosive movements, athletic strength, and an emphasis on muscular power and hard techniques, at least in their earlier stages of training.

The internal–external distinction

The distinction between neijia and waijia is traditionally traced to the 17th century. An epitaph of 1669 written by Huang Zongxi for the fighter Wang Zhengnan credits Zhang Sanfeng with founding an internal school distinct from the Shaolin-associated external arts. Most Shaolin kung fu styles, along with many other hard or athletic systems, are classed as waijia, while tai chi, baguazhang, and xingyiquan are the canonical internal arts.

Practice

Waijia training typically begins with developing external qualities — strength, speed, stamina, and conditioned striking surfaces — and many traditions hold that advanced practice gradually internalizes these skills, so that the external/internal divide blurs at high levels. The saying "internal and external are one" (內外合一) reflects the view that the classification describes emphasis and training method rather than an absolute division. Some historians regard aspects of the dichotomy as a later construction tied to lineage politics and the mythology of the Wudang and Shaolin schools.
