Sumerian King List

The Sumerian King List is an ancient literary composition in the Sumerian language, probably created and redacted to legitimise the claims to power of various city-states and kingdoms in ancient Mesopotamia. It lists kings of Sumer (in southern Mesopotamia) and their supposed reign lengths, from the mythical antediluvian kings to the rulers of the dynasty of Isin in the early second millennium BC.

Content

The list begins: "After the kingship descended from heaven, the kingship was in Eridu." The antediluvian kings are credited with fantastically long reigns — eight kings reigning a total of 241,200 years — before "the Flood swept over." After the Flood, reign lengths gradually decrease toward historically plausible figures. Famous rulers in the list include Etana of Kish, who "ascended to heaven," and Gilgamesh of Uruk, assigned a reign of 126 years.

Sources and scholarship

The list survives in multiple copies, the most complete being the Weld-Blundell prism in the Ashmolean Museum. The copies differ in details, and the list presents successive dynasties as sequential even where they were partly contemporary, reflecting an ideological view of a single kingship passing from city to city. Historians use the later portions of the list, cross-checked against royal inscriptions, as a framework for Mesopotamian chronology, while treating the early reign lengths as mythical.

Parallels

The antediluvian section has often been compared with the biblical Genesis account of long-lived patriarchs before the Flood, and with Berossus's Babylonian history, which preserves a similar scheme of ten antediluvian kings.
