King William's War

King William's War (1688–1697) was the North American theatre of the Nine Years' War, fought between England and France and their respective Indigenous allies in New England, Acadia, and New France. It was the first of the French and Indian Wars.

Course of the war

Fighting included the French and Indigenous raids on Schenectady (1690), Salmon Falls, and York, the English capture of Port Royal in Acadia, and Sir William Phips's failed expedition against Quebec in 1690. Count Frontenac's invasion of Iroquois country in 1696 devastated the Oneida and Onondaga. The Peace of Ryswick (1697) ended the war and restored the pre-war status quo, leaving the underlying colonial rivalries unresolved; fighting resumed in Queen Anne's War in 1702.
