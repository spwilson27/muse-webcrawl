Easter, also called Pascha (from Aramaic and Greek, derived from the Hebrew Pesach) or Resurrection Sunday, is a Christian festival and cultural holiday commemorating the resurrection of Jesus from the dead, described in the New Testament as having occurred on the third day of his burial following his crucifixion by the Romans at Calvary.

Date

Easter is a movable feast determined by a lunisolar system: following the Council of Nicaea (325 AD), it is held on the first Sunday after the ecclesiastical full moon on or after 21 March. Western and Eastern churches use different calendars (Gregorian and Julian), so their Easter dates often differ. Easter falls between 22 March and 25 April in the West.

Observance

Easter concludes Holy Week, which includes Maundy Thursday, Good Friday and Holy Saturday, and ends the penitential season of Lent. Churches hold sunrise services, vigils and processions; the Paschal greeting "Christ is risen" is exchanged in many traditions. Customs vary widely and include decorating and hunting for Easter eggs, the Easter Bunny, hot cross buns, simnel cake and festive meals. The fifty-day Eastertide season ends at Pentecost.

Theology

The resurrection is central to Christian faith: according to the Apostle Paul, without it Christian preaching and faith would be "in vain". Easter's origins connect it to the Jewish Passover in timing and symbolism, and its English and German names may derive from a dawn goddess, Ēostre, mentioned by Bede.
