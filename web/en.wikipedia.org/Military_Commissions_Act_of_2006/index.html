Military Commissions Act of 2006

Long title
An Act to authorize trial by military commission for violations of the law of war, and for other purposes.
EnactedOctober 17, 2006
Public law109-443

The Military Commissions Act of 2006, also known as HR-6166, was an Act of Congress signed by President George W. Bush on October 17, 2006. It authorized the President to set up military commissions to try "unlawful enemy combatants" held at Guantanamo Bay and elsewhere.

Background

The Act followed the Supreme Court's decision in Hamdan v. Rumsfeld (2006), which held that the military commissions established by the administration lacked congressional authorization and violated the Uniform Code of Military Justice and the Geneva Conventions.

Provisions

The Act defined "unlawful enemy combatant", authorized trials by military commission, restricted habeas corpus petitions by detainees, and provided retroactive protections for US personnel regarding interrogation practices. Portions of it were later modified by the Military Commissions Act of 2009.

Legal challenges

In Boumediene v. Bush (2008), the Supreme Court struck down the Act's suspension of habeas corpus for Guantanamo detainees as unconstitutional.
