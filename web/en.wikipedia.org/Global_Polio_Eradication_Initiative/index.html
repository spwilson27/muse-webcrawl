Global Polio Eradication Initiative

The Global Polio Eradication Initiative (GPEI) is a public–private partnership launched in 1988 to eradicate poliomyelitis worldwide. Led by national governments together with the World Health Organization, Rotary International, the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, UNICEF and the Gates Foundation, it is the largest international public health initiative in history.

Progress

When the initiative began, polio paralysed an estimated 350,000 children per year in 125 countries. Mass immunisation with oral polio vaccine reduced cases by more than 99.9 percent. Wild poliovirus type 2 was certified eradicated in 2015 and type 3 in 2019; wild type 1 transmission has persisted only in Afghanistan and Pakistan, with occasional imported cases elsewhere.

Strategy and challenges

The GPEI's strategy combines routine immunisation, supplementary mass campaigns, environmental and case surveillance, and rapid outbreak response. Remaining obstacles include insecurity, vaccine hesitancy, hard-to-reach populations, and outbreaks of vaccine-derived poliovirus in under-immunised communities, which the programme addresses with newer vaccines and targeted campaigns.
