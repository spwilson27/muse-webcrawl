Coös County, New Hampshire

Coös County (pronounced KOH-oss, with two syllables) is the northernmost county in the U.S. state of New Hampshire. As of the 2020 census its population was about 31,268, making it the least populous county in the state. Its county seat is Lancaster.

Geography

It is the largest county by area in New Hampshire, covering much of the White Mountains and the Great North Woods, bordering Maine, Vermont and the Canadian province of Quebec. Mount Washington, the highest peak in the northeastern United States, lies in the county.

History

Created in 1803 from Grafton County, its name derives from an Algonquian word meaning 'small pines'. The diaeresis in Coös indicates separate vowel sounds.
