Combatives

Combatives is the term for hand-to-hand combat training systems used by military forces, law enforcement and security personnel. Unlike martial arts practised as sport or tradition, combatives emphasise techniques that can be learned quickly and applied under stress in real combat, including strikes, grappling, weapon retention and defence against weapons.

History

Modern combatives draw heavily on the methods developed during the Second World War by instructors such as William E. Fairbairn and Eric A. Sykes for Allied special forces and intelligence services, popularised as "defendu" and close combat shooting and fighting techniques. After the war, many armed forces formalised their own systems.

Contemporary systems

The United States Army's Modern Army Combatives Program (MACP), founded in 1995 at Fort Benning and influenced by Brazilian jiu-jitsu, trains soldiers in progressively advanced levels and runs unit competitions. The U.S. Marine Corps teaches the Marine Corps Martial Arts Program (MCMAP), a belt-ranked system integrating hand-to-hand techniques with warrior studies. Other notable systems include Israel's Krav Maga, developed for the Israel Defense Forces, and Russia's Systema and Sambo traditions.
