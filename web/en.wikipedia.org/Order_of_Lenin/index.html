Order of Lenin

The Order of Lenin (Russian: Орден Ленина, Orden Lenina) was an award of the Soviet Union, named after Vladimir Lenin, leader of the October Revolution. Established on 6 April 1930, it was the highest civilian decoration of the USSR — and, as it could also be awarded for military distinction in wartime, effectively the highest Soviet order overall until the separate Order of Victory was created for senior commanders.


Badge of the Order of Lenin

Criteria

The order was awarded to civilians for outstanding services to the state, to servicemen for exemplary military merit, and to institutions, enterprises, cities, and regions — recipients ranging from Hero of Socialist Labour titleholders (who received it automatically with that title) to factories and newspapers such as Pravda and Komsomolskaya Pravda. It could be awarded multiple times; some individuals received it nine or more times. Roughly 431,000 orders were awarded in total, the last in December 1991 shortly before the dissolution of the Soviet Union.

Design

The badge in its final form is a round medallion of gold and platinum bearing a platinum bust portrait of Lenin within a wreath of wheat ears, with a red enamel banner inscribed "ЛЕНИН" and a red flag bearing the hammer and sickle. It was worn on the left breast suspended from a red ribbon with yellow edges.
