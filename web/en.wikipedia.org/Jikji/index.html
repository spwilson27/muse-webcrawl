Jikji (Korean: 직지) is the abbreviated title of a Korean Buddhist document — Baegun hwasang chorok buljo jikji simche yojeol, the "Anthology of Great Buddhist Priests' Zen Teachings Pointing to the Heart Directly" — printed in 1377 during the Goryeo dynasty. It is the oldest extant book in the world printed with movable metal type, predating Gutenberg's Bible by 78 years, and was inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2001.

Contents and printing

The work was compiled by the monk Baegun (1298–1374) and condenses the sayings and teachings of eminent Zen (Seon) masters. In July 1377 his disciples printed it at Heungdeok Temple in Cheongju using cast metal type. It was produced in two volumes; only the second volume survives.

Provenance

The surviving volume was acquired in the late 19th century by Collin de Plancy, a French diplomat in Korea, and later belonged to collector Henri Vever before entering the collection of the National Library of France (Bibliothèque nationale de France) in Paris, where it remains. The book came to wide scholarly attention at the 1972 International Book Year exhibition, when Korean librarian Park Byeong-seon identified its colophon, which records the metal-type printing date.

Legacy

UNESCO established the Jikji Memory of the World Prize in 2004 to commemorate the book. South Korea has long sought the volume's return or long-term loan from France, and replicas are displayed at museums in Cheongju, where an early-printing museum stands near the reconstructed Heungdeok Temple site.
