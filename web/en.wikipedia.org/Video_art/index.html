Video art

Video art is an art form which relies on using video technology as a visual and audio medium. It emerged during the late 1960s as new consumer video technology became available outside corporate broadcasting, and took many forms: recordings broadcast or shown in galleries, installations combining monitors and sculpture, and live or recorded performances incorporating video.

History

The Korean-born artist Nam June Paik, often called the founder of video art, exhibited manipulated televisions in Germany in 1963 and later used Sony's portable Portapak recorder, released in 1967, which let artists record and play back tape immediately — unlike film, which required processing. Artists such as Joan Jonas, Bruce Nauman, Vito Acconci, Bill Viola and Pipilotti Rist developed the medium's languages of performance documentation, sculptural installation and immersive projection. Unlike cinema, video art often has no linear narrative or dialogue and may loop indefinitely.
