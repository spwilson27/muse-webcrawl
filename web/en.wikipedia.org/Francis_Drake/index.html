Sir Francis Drake (c. 1540 – 28 January 1596) was an English explorer and naval commander best known for his circumnavigation of the world in a single expedition between 1577 and 1580. Working as a privateer sanctioned by Elizabeth I, he raided Spanish shipping and settlements in the Americas, and he played a major role in the defeat of the Spanish Armada in 1588. To the Spanish he was known as El Draque, a feared pirate; in England he was celebrated as a hero.

Early career

Born in Tavistock, Devon, Drake went to sea as a youth and sailed with his cousin John Hawkins on slaving and trading voyages to West Africa and the Caribbean. The Spanish attack on their fleet at San Juan de Ulúa in 1568 embittered him against Spain and set the course of his career.

Circumnavigation

Sailing in December 1577 with five ships, Drake passed through the Strait of Magellan and raided Spanish ports along the Pacific coast of South America, capturing the treasure ship Nuestra Señora de la Concepción. He claimed the coast of California for England as "Nova Albion", crossed the Pacific and Indian Oceans, rounded the Cape of Good Hope and returned to Plymouth in September 1580 aboard the Golden Hind, laden with treasure. Elizabeth knighted him in 1581.

Later career and death

Drake's 1587 raid on Cádiz — the "singeing of the King of Spain's beard" — delayed the Armada, in whose defeat he served as a vice-admiral the following year. He died of dysentery in 1596 during an expedition against Spanish America and was buried at sea off Portobelo.
