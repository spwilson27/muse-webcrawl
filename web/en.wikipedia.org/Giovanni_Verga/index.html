Giovanni Carmelo Verga di Fontanabianca (2 September 1840 – 27 January 1922) was an Italian realist (verista) writer, best known for his depictions of life in his native Sicily. He is regarded as one of the greatest Italian novelists of the 19th century and the leading figure of the verismo movement.

Life and work

Born in Catania to a prosperous family, Verga began writing historical and romantic novels, then moved in the literary salons of Florence and Milan, where encounters with French naturalism transformed his style. Returning to Sicilian subjects, he produced the masterpieces of verismo: the short-story collections Vita dei campi (1880) and Novelle rusticane (1883), and the novels I Malavoglia (The House by the Medlar-Tree, 1881), about a family of fishermen ruined by fate and "progress", and Mastro-don Gesualdo (1889), the story of a self-made man's empty rise. The two novels were to open a projected "Cycle of the Vanquished" (Ciclo dei Vinti), never completed.

His short story "Cavalleria rusticana" (1880), which he adapted for the stage, became Pietro Mascagni's celebrated one-act opera of 1890, a landmark of operatic verismo. Verga's impersonal narrative technique, regional speech rhythms and pessimistic vision profoundly influenced later Italian literature and cinema, including the neorealist filmmakers. He was made a senator of the Kingdom of Italy in 1920 and died in Catania in 1922.
