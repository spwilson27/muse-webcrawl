Pericarditis

Pericarditis is inflammation of the pericardium, the fibrous sac surrounding the heart. Its characteristic symptom is sharp chest pain that worsens when breathing in or lying flat and improves sitting up and leaning forward. Pain may spread to the shoulder or trapezius ridge.

Pericarditis
Symptoms: sharp chest pain, fever
Signs: pericardial friction rub, diffuse ST elevation
Treatment: NSAIDs, colchicine
Complications: tamponade, constrictive pericarditis

Causes

Most cases are idiopathic or follow viral infection. Other causes include bacterial infection (notably tuberculosis), heart attack, heart surgery, autoimmune diseases, kidney failure, cancer and certain medications.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on symptoms, a friction rub heard with a stethoscope, characteristic electrocardiogram changes (widespread ST elevation and PR depression) and imaging showing fluid around the heart. Treatment is usually with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and colchicine; corticosteroids are reserved for specific cases. Most episodes resolve in weeks, though recurrence is common.
