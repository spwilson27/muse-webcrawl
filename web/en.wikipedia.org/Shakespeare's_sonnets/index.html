Shakespeare's sonnets are 154 poems in sonnet form written by William Shakespeare, first published together in a 1609 quarto titled Shake-speares Sonnets. The sonnets were probably composed over many years, and Francis Meres already praised Shakespeare's "sugared sonnets among his private friends" in 1598. The collection is preceded by a mysterious dedication to "the only begetter of these ensuing sonnets Mr. W. H.," whose identity has generated centuries of speculation.

Sequences and characters

Scholars conventionally divide the sequence into groups. Sonnets 1–126 are addressed to a young man, the "Fair Youth," beginning with the seventeen "procreation sonnets" urging him to marry and beget an heir, and moving through themes of beauty, time, rival poets, absence, and estrangement. Sonnets 127–152 are addressed to a mistress, the "Dark Lady," so called for her dark hair and complexion, in a tone by turns desiring, bitter, and self-lacerating — anti-Petrarchan poems such as Sonnet 130, "My mistress' eyes are nothing like the sun." Sonnets 153–154 are mythological exercises on Cupid. Famous individual sonnets include 18 ("Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?"), 29 ("When, in disgrace with fortune and men's eyes"), 55 ("Not marble nor the gilded monuments"), 71, 73 ("That time of year thou mayst in me behold"), and 116 ("Let me not to the marriage of true minds").

Form and themes

Shakespeare mostly used the English or Shakespearean sonnet form — three quatrains and a concluding couplet in iambic pentameter, rhyming ABAB CDCD EFEF GG — with occasional variations, including the fifteen-line Sonnet 99 and the tetrameter Sonnet 145. Recurring themes are the ravages of time and mortality, the immortality conferred by verse, procreation, jealousy, and the paradoxes of love and desire. Whether the poems are autobiographical remains one of the great unsettled questions of Shakespeare scholarship.

Influence

The sonnets rank among the most influential poems in English; they fixed the sonnet as a vehicle of intense personal lyric and have been endlessly quoted, set to music, and translated into many languages.
