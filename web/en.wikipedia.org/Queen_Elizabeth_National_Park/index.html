Queen Elizabeth National Park

LocationWestern Region, Uganda
Area1,978 km²
Established1952
Governing bodyUganda Wildlife Authority

Queen Elizabeth National Park is a national park in western Uganda, spanning the districts of Kasese, Kamwenge, Rubirizi, and Rukungiri. Covering about 1,978 square kilometres, it is Uganda's most visited national park and is known for its diverse ecosystems, which include savanna, wetlands, crater lakes, and the forests of the Maramagambo.

History

The park was founded in 1952 as Kazinga National Park and renamed two years later to commemorate a visit by Queen Elizabeth II. It borders the Rwenzori Mountains and lies along the Kazinga Channel, which connects Lake Edward and Lake George and supports one of the world's largest concentrations of hippos.

Wildlife

The park is home to some 95 mammal species, including African elephants, lions — the Ishasha sector is famous for its tree-climbing lions — leopards, buffalo, hippos, and chimpanzees in the Kyambura Gorge, as well as over 600 bird species, among the highest counts of any protected area in Africa.

Tourism and conservation

Boat cruises on the Kazinga Channel, game drives on the Kasenyi plains, and chimpanzee tracking in Kyambura Gorge are major attractions. The park faces pressures from poaching and human–wildlife conflict in surrounding fishing villages.
