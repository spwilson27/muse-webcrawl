Tánaiste

The Tánaiste (Irish: [ˈt̪ˠaːn̪ˠəʃtʲə]) is the deputy head of government of Ireland. The office corresponds roughly to that of a vice prime minister in other states. The Tánaiste is appointed by the President of Ireland on the nomination of the Taoiseach (prime minister) and must be a member of the Government of Ireland, normally drawn from Dáil Éireann.

Role and functions

The Tánaiste acts in place of the Taoiseach during the Taoiseach's temporary absence and, in the event of the Taoiseach's death or permanent incapacity, acts as Taoiseach until a successor is appointed. In practice, the Tánaiste also holds a senior cabinet portfolio, frequently that of Minister for Foreign Affairs or Minister for Enterprise, Trade and Employment. In coalition governments the office is traditionally held by the leader of the second-largest party in the coalition.

History

The word tánaiste originally meant the heir or deputy of a Gaelic chief, the second in command chosen under the system of tanistry. Under the Irish Free State, the deputy head of government was titled Vice-President of the Executive Council; Éamon de Valera's 1937 Constitution replaced the title with Tánaiste. The first holder under the constitution was Seán T. O'Kelly, who served from 1937 to 1945 before becoming President of Ireland.

Succession

Unlike a vice president in a presidential system, the Tánaiste does not automatically succeed to the office of Taoiseach, but must be nominated by Dáil Éireann and appointed by the President. Upon the death or resignation of a Taoiseach, the Tánaiste serves in an acting capacity in the interim.
