The Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center (JSC) is NASA's centre for human spaceflight activity, located in the Clear Lake area of Houston, Texas. It is home to the astronaut corps, the Christopher C. Kraft Jr. Mission Control Center, and the training facilities for the International Space Station and Artemis programmes.

History

The centre was established in 1961 as the Manned Spacecraft Center on land donated by Rice University, and operations moved there in 1963–64. From Mission Control, flight controllers directed the Gemini, Apollo, Skylab, Space Shuttle and ISS missions; the first words spoken from the Moon in 1969 were answered with "Houston". In 1973 the centre was renamed in honour of President Lyndon B. Johnson, a Texan who had championed the space programme.

Facilities

The 1,620-acre campus includes the Sonny Carter Training Facility with its Neutral Buoyancy Laboratory — one of the world's largest indoor pools — the Space Vehicle Mockup Facility, astronaut training aircraft operations at Ellington Field, and the Saturn V rocket displayed at the visitor centre, Space Center Houston.

Role today

JSC leads the Orion programme and houses mission control for the ISS, coordinating crew operations, flight dynamics and spacewalk planning around the clock.
