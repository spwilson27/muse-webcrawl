Norepinephrine

Norepinephrine (NE), also called noradrenaline, is an organic chemical in the catecholamine family that functions in the brain and body as both a hormone and a neurotransmitter. The name is derived from the Greek roots meaning "beside the kidneys", reflecting its production in the adrenal glands.

Structure and synthesis

Norepinephrine is a catecholamine with a phenethylamine backbone. It is synthesised from dopamine by the enzyme dopamine beta-hydroxylase, primarily in the locus coeruleus of the brainstem and in the adrenal medulla. It can be further methylated to form epinephrine (adrenaline).

Functions

As a stress hormone, norepinephrine increases arousal, alertness, and vigilance, enhances the formation and retrieval of memory, and focuses attention. In the body it increases heart rate and blood pressure, triggers the release of glucose from energy stores, and increases blood flow to skeletal muscle while reducing blood flow to the gastrointestinal system.

Pharmacology

Drugs that affect norepinephrine signalling are used as antidepressants, including serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) and tricyclic antidepressants. Norepinephrine itself is given intravenously as a vasopressor in cases of shock and severe hypotension.
