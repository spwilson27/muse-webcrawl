LocationCoasts of Spain and Portugal (A Coruña, Lisbon)

The English Armada (Spanish: Contra Armada), also known as the Drake–Norris Expedition, was a fleet of warships sent to Spain by Queen Elizabeth I of England in 1589, during the undeclared Anglo-Spanish War (1585–1604) and the Eighty Years' War. It was led by Sir Francis Drake as admiral and Sir John Norris as general, and failed to achieve its objectives. The campaign resulted in the defeat of the English fleet and heavy losses.

Objectives

The expedition was a response to the Spanish Armada of 1588. Its orders were to destroy the surviving Spanish warships being repaired at Santander, San Sebastián and A Coruña; to land at Lisbon and raise a Portuguese revolt in favour of the claimant Dom António, Prior of Crato; and, if possible, to seize the Azores and intercept the Spanish treasure fleet from the Indies.

Campaign

The fleet attacked A Coruña in May 1589, taking the lower town but failing against the upper town, defended in part by María Pita, who became a local heroine. The English then landed near Lisbon, but the expected Portuguese uprising did not materialise, the city was not taken, and disease began to ravage the expedition. Attempts on the Azores and the treasure fleet were abandoned, and the fleet straggled home.

Aftermath

Of the roughly 27,000 men who sailed, thousands died from combat, disease and shipwreck; dozens of ships were lost. The failure revived Spanish naval power — Spain rebuilt its fleet and sent its own armadas against England in 1596 and 1597 — and it damaged Drake's and Norris's reputations. Historians often pair the expedition with the Spanish Armada as a comparable catastrophe, largely forgotten in English popular memory.
