Hindi (हिन्दी, Hindī), often Modern Standard Hindi, is an Indo-Aryan language spoken chiefly in northern and central India. It is the official language of the Government of India (alongside English) and one of the 22 scheduled languages of the Republic of India. By number of native speakers it is the third most spoken language in the world, and the most widely spoken language in India, where about 44% of the population reported it as their mother tongue in the 2011 census.

History

Hindi developed from the Khari Boli dialect of the Delhi region, descended through Prakrit and Apabhraṃśa from Sanskrit. Its standard form is closely related to Urdu — together called Hindustani — from which it differs mainly in script (Devanagari versus Perso-Arabic) and in learned vocabulary (Sanskrit-derived versus Persian- and Arabic-derived). Literary Hindi flourished in the 19th century, and after independence in 1947 the Indian constitution adopted Hindi in Devanagari as an official language.

Grammar and script

Hindi is written in the Devanagari abugida, read left to right, with characters hanging from a horizontal headline. It is a subject–object–verb language with postpositions, grammatical gender (masculine and feminine), split ergativity in the perfective aspect, and a rich system of compound verbs. Its core vocabulary is Indo-Aryan, supplemented by extensive borrowing from Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic, Portuguese, and English.

Usage

Hindi predominates in the "Hindi Belt" states such as Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan, Haryana, and Delhi, and serves as a lingua franca across much of India. It is the language of Bollywood cinema and much Indian popular media, and diaspora varieties are spoken in Fiji, Mauritius, Suriname, Guyana, and Trinidad and Tobago.
