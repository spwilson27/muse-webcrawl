Doubs is a department in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region of eastern France, named after the Doubs River. Its prefecture is Besançon, and its other major towns include Montbéliard and Pontarlier. Bordering Switzerland along the Jura Mountains, the department is known for watchmaking, cheese production, and outdoor tourism.

Geography

The Doubs River rises near Mouthe and winds through the Jura, forming for part of its course the border with Switzerland, where it creates the dramatic Saut du Doubs waterfall. The department's terrain is largely mountainous in the east and plateau-like in the west, with forests covering much of its area.

Economy and culture

Historically a centre of the French watch and clock industry, particularly around Besançon and Morteau, Doubs is also famous for Comté cheese and smoked Morteau sausage. The Vauban citadel of Besançon, a UNESCO World Heritage site, overlooks a loop of the river.
