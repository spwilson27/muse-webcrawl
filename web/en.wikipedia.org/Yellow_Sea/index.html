The Yellow Sea (Chinese: 黄海; Korean: 황해, also called the West Sea in Korea) is a marginal sea of the western Pacific Ocean lying between mainland China and the Korean Peninsula. Its name comes from the yellowish silt carried into it by the Yellow River and other rivers, which colours the water.

Geography

The sea covers about 380,000 square kilometres and is shallow, with an average depth of only 44 metres, being a flooded portion of the continental shelf. Its northern extension between China's Liaodong Peninsula and North Korea's west coast is the Korea Bay, and the arm between the Liaodong and Shandong peninsulas is the Bohai Sea. Major rivers discharging into it include the Yellow River, Hai, Liao, and Yalu.

Ecology and economy

The Yellow Sea's intertidal mudflats, especially along the Korean coast, are critical stopover sites for migratory shorebirds on the East Asian–Australasian Flyway; parts are protected as UNESCO World Heritage sites. The sea supports major fisheries and busy shipping lanes serving ports such as Qingdao, Dalian, Tianjin, and Incheon, though it faces pressures from overfishing, pollution, and algal blooms.
