Key (music)

A key signature at the beginning of a staff indicates the key of the music.

In music theory, the key of a piece is the group of pitches, or scale, that forms the basis of a musical composition in Western classical music, art songs and most popular music. A key is identified by its tonic — the note to which the music gravitates and on which it usually ends — and by its mode: a piece is in a major key if it uses a major scale, or a minor key if it uses a minor scale. Thus one speaks of C major, A minor, and so on.

Key signatures and the circle of fifths

Keys are specified at the start of a score by a key signature, a set of sharp or flat symbols placed on the staff. Each major key has a relative minor a minor third below, sharing the same signature, and keys are arranged by their signatures in the circle of fifths. In equal temperament there are 24 commonly used keys: 12 major and 12 minor, with enharmonic equivalents (such as F♯ major and G♭ major) treated as distinct spellings of the same sounding key.

Key relationships and modulation

Themes in classical forms are typically stated in the tonic key and restated in a closely related key — for major works usually the dominant — before returning home; the change of key within a piece is called modulation. Relationships of "closeness" between keys structure much of the harmony of the common-practice era. Key characteristics — alleged distinctive moods attached to individual keys — were widely discussed from the 17th to the 19th centuries, though with the spread of equal temperament the physical basis for such differences largely vanished and their existence remains debated.

Key in other traditions

Non-Western and pre-modern music organises pitch around analogous centres: the modes of Gregorian chant, the ragas of Indian music and the maqamat of Arabic music each define characteristic melodic material around a final or tonic.
