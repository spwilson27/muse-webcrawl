Friedrich Hartogs

Friedrich Moritz Hartogs (20 May 1874 – 18 August 1943) was a German mathematician, known for his work on set theory and foundational results in the theory of functions of several complex variables.

Life

Hartogs was born in Brussels to the merchant Gustav Hartogs and his wife Elise Strupp, and grew up in Frankfurt am Main. He studied at the universities of Hannover, Berlin, and Munich, receiving his doctorate in Munich in 1903. He became a professor at the University of Munich. Being of Jewish descent, he suffered under the Nazi regime: he was dismissed from his post in 1935 under the Nuremberg Laws, was briefly interned in Dachau in 1938, and, facing deportation in 1943 after his divorce from his non-Jewish wife removed his remaining protection, he took his own life.

Work

In set theory, Hartogs proved in 1915 that for every set there exists a well-ordered set (an ordinal, now called the Hartogs number) that cannot be injected into it — a result that yields the comparability of cardinals without assuming the axiom of choice. In complex analysis, Hartogs's lemma and Hartogs's theorem (1906) showed that a function holomorphic in each variable separately in several complex variables is automatically jointly holomorphic — a phenomenon with no analogue in one variable. The Hartogs extension phenomenon, that holomorphic functions extend across compact holes in dimension two or more, is also named for him.
