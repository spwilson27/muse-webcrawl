Power electronics

Scope: Conversion and control of electric power

Key devices: Power MOSFET, IGBT, thyristor, diode

Converters: Rectifiers, inverters, DC–DC converters, cycloconverters

Power electronics is the application of electronics to the control and conversion of electric power. Where ordinary electronics process signals and information, power electronics processes energy, handling voltages and currents from watts to gigawatts with high efficiency. Modern power electronics is built around semiconductor switching devices — power diodes, thyristors, MOSFETs and insulated-gate bipolar transistors (IGBTs) — operated as switches rather than as linear amplifiers.

Converter types

Four basic conversions cover the field: AC to DC (rectification), DC to AC (inversion), DC to DC (chopping and switched-mode conversion) and AC to AC (frequency and voltage conversion). Pulse-width modulation (PWM) allows precise control of output voltage and current by varying the duty cycle of the switches.

Applications

Applications include switched-mode power supplies in virtually all electronic equipment, variable-frequency motor drives, high-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission, renewable-energy interfaces for solar panels and wind turbines, electric vehicle traction and charging, and uninterruptible power supplies.

History and materials

The field began with mercury-arc rectifiers in the early twentieth century and was transformed by the silicon controlled rectifier (1957) and later power transistors. Wide-bandgap semiconductors — silicon carbide and gallium nitride — now allow higher voltages, temperatures and switching frequencies than silicon.
