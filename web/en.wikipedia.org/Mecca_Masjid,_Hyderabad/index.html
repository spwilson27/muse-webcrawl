Mecca Masjid, Hyderabad

LocationHyderabad, Telangana, India
FoundedConstruction begun 1617; completed 1693/94
FounderMuhammad Quli Qutb Shah
StyleQutb Shahi / Indo-Islamic
CapacityAbout 20,000 worshippers

Mecca Masjid is one of the oldest and largest mosques in India, located in the old city of Hyderabad, Telangana, near the Charminar and Chowmahalla Palace. Named for the bricks made from soil said to have been brought from Mecca that form part of its central arch, the mosque can accommodate some 20,000 worshippers in its courtyard and prayer hall.

History

Construction was begun in 1617 by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, the fifth sultan of the Qutb Shahi dynasty and founder of Hyderabad. Work continued under Abdullah Qutb Shah and Abul Hasan Tana Shah, but the mosque was finally completed only in 1693–94, under the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb after his conquest of Golconda. The vast prayer hall is hewn from huge single granite slabs quarried nearby; its facade has five arched entrances, flanked by two minarets, and the courtyard contains the tombs of several Nizams of Hyderabad in a chamber at its western end.

Architecture

The main hall is about 67 metres long and 54 metres wide, rising some 23 metres, and is supported on fifteen great arches. Belgian crystal chandeliers donated by a Nizam adorn the interior. The mosque's legends include protective inscriptions and the belief that whoever sits on certain stone benches in the courtyard will return to sit on them again.

2007 bombing

On 18 May 2007 a bomb exploded during Friday prayers, killing several people and sparking riots in which police firing killed more; the attack was attributed to militant groups and remains a painful episode in the mosque's history.
