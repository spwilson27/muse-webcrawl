Flatland

Flatland: A Romance of Many Dimensions is a satirical novella by the English schoolmaster Edwin Abbott Abbott, first published in 1884 by Seeley & Co. of London. Written pseudonymously by "A Square", the book uses the fictional two-dimensional world of Flatland to satirise Victorian culture and to explore, through analogy, the nature of dimensions.

Plot

The narrator, A Square, describes a world whose inhabitants are geometric figures: women are straight lines, and men are polygons whose number of sides indicates their social rank. A Square dreams of Lineland, a one-dimensional world, whose king cannot comprehend two dimensions. Later, a Sphere from three-dimensional Spaceland visits Flatland, and eventually takes A Square out of his plane to witness Spaceland — an experience that leads A Square to speculate about fourth and higher dimensions, for which he is imprisoned on his return as a heretic.

Themes and legacy

The novella satirises the rigid class hierarchy and gender roles of Victorian society. It remains popular in mathematics and physics education as an accessible introduction to thinking about higher dimensions, and has inspired sequels and adaptations including Sphereland (1965) by Dionys Burger and several films.
