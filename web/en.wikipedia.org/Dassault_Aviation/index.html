Dassault Aviation

The Dassault Rafale, the company's flagship combat aircraft

Dassault Aviation SA is a French aerospace manufacturer of military and business aircraft. Founded by Marcel Bloch (later Marcel Dassault), the company is the maker of the Mirage and Rafale fighter jets and of the Falcon family of business jets. It remains controlled by the Dassault family through Groupe Dassault and is one of Europe's largest defence contractors.

History

Marcel Bloch began designing aircraft before the First World War and founded his company in 1929. After the Second World War — during which he was imprisoned at Buchenwald for refusing to collaborate — he adopted the name Dassault, his brother's Resistance codename. The postwar company built France's first jet fighters, the Ouragan and the Mystère, followed by the delta-winged Mirage III, exported worldwide. The Mirage 2000 and the twin-engine Rafale continued the line; the Rafale entered French service in the 2000s and has been exported to Egypt, India, Qatar, Greece, Croatia and others.

Business aviation

The Falcon line began with the Falcon 20 in 1963, developed with Pan Am; the family now includes the Falcon 7X/8X trijets and the ultra-long-range Falcon 10X in development. Dassault is also the industrial leader of the European nEUROn unmanned combat air vehicle demonstrator and a partner in the Future Combat Air System programme with Airbus and Indra.
