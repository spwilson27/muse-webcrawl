CREB

CREB (cAMP response element-binding protein) is a cellular transcription factor. It binds to certain DNA sequences called cAMP response elements (CRE), thereby increasing or decreasing the transcription of genes containing them.

Mechanism

Signals that raise intracellular cyclic AMP activate protein kinase A, which phosphorylates CREB at serine 133. Phosphorylated CREB recruits the coactivator CREB-binding protein (CBP) and initiates transcription. CREB can also be activated by calcium signalling and growth-factor pathways through other kinases.

Functions

CREB regulates many processes, including gluconeogenesis, circadian rhythms, and neuronal plasticity. It is best known for its role in memory formation: CREB activity is required for the consolidation of long-term memories, demonstrated in organisms from the sea slug Aplysia and fruit flies to mice. Dysregulation of CREB is implicated in depression, addiction and some cancers.
