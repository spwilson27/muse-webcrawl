State: New York

Founded: 1794

Area: approx. 806 sq mi (2,088 km²)

Named for: the Onondaga people

Onondaga County is a county in the U.S. state of New York, located in the central part of the state. Its county seat and largest city is Syracuse. The county is named after the Onondaga Nation, one of the five original nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy), whose capital and reservation lie within the county south of Syracuse. The Onondaga, "keepers of the fire", hosted the confederacy's council.

History

The county was formed in 1794 from part of Herkimer County, when the area was being organised as part of New York's Military Tract, used to compensate Revolutionary War soldiers. Salt production from the brine springs around Onondaga Lake — giving Syracuse its nickname "Salt City" — drove early growth, and the Erie Canal, completed through Syracuse in the 1820s, made the region a significant manufacturing and transport centre. In the nineteenth century the area was a hotbed of abolitionism and women's suffrage activity.

Geography

The county covers about 806 square miles, of which roughly 25 square miles are water. It includes most of Onondaga Lake, parts of Oneida Lake, and numerous smaller lakes such as Skaneateles Lake at its western edge. The terrain ranges from flat lake plains in the north to rolling hills in the south, where the landscape rises toward the Allegheny Plateau.

Communities and economy

Beyond Syracuse, the county includes towns and suburbs such as DeWitt, Manlius, Clay, Liverpool, Camillus, and Baldwinsville. Syracuse University, SUNY Upstate Medical University, and Le Moyne College anchor higher education and healthcare as major employers. Historically a manufacturing centre — home over the years to typewriters, air conditioning (Carrier), and automotive parts — the county's economy has shifted toward services, education, and healthcare, with large-scale semiconductor investment announced for the town of Clay in the 2020s.
