Hydro-Québec
Founded: 14 April 1944
Headquarters: Montreal, Quebec, Canada
A provincial Crown corporation generating and distributing electricity, overwhelmingly from hydroelectric power.

Hydro-Québec is a Canadian Crown corporation (state-owned enterprise) headquartered in Montreal. It manages the generation, transmission and distribution of electricity in the province of Quebec, and is one of the largest hydroelectric power producers in the world, with an installed capacity of more than 37,000 megawatts.

History

The company was created by the government of Adélard Godbout in 1944, amid the Quiet Revolution's push for state control of natural resources. The nationalisation of the remaining private electricity companies in 1962–63, under the slogan "Maîtres chez nous" ("masters in our own house"), made Hydro-Québec the province's monopoly utility and a symbol of Quebec nationalism.

Major projects

Its defining undertakings include the Manicouagan–Outardes complex and the James Bay Project, launched in 1971, whose La Grande River stations — including the Robert-Bourassa generating station, among the world's largest — supply a large share of Quebec's power. The 1995 Great Whale River project was shelved after opposition from the Cree and Inuit, whose rights were recognised in the landmark James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement of 1975.

Operations

Hydro-Québec delivers some of North America's lowest residential electricity rates, exports power to New England and New York, and its research institute, IREQ, is known for work on high-voltage transmission, including the 735 kV lines it pioneered.
