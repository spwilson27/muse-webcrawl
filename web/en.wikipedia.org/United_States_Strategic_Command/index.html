United States Strategic Command

The United States Strategic Command (USSTRATCOM) is one of the eleven unified combatant commands in the United States military. Headquartered at Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska, it is responsible for strategic deterrence, global strike, and operating the Department of Defense's Global Information Grid — in practice, it commands the United States' nuclear forces: intercontinental ballistic missiles, strategic bombers, and ballistic missile submarines.

History

USSTRATCOM was established in 1992 from the Strategic Air Command at the end of the Cold War, merging Air Force bombers and land-based missiles with Navy submarine-launched ballistic missiles under a single commander for the first time. In 2002 it absorbed the former U.S. Space Command and additional missions including missile defense and global strike planning; space forces were later split off again when U.S. Space Command was re-established in 2019. A new 1-million-square-foot headquarters building at Offutt was completed in 2019.

Responsibilities

The command prepares the operational plans for nuclear employment, maintains the nuclear command, control, and communications (NC3) system that links the President to the forces, and provides nuclear deterrence capabilities to the other combatant commands. Its service components include Air Force Global Strike Command (bombers and ICBMs) and the Navy's Submarine Force (the Ohio-class SSBN fleet carrying Trident II missiles). It also coordinates missile warning and defense support with U.S. Northern Command and Space Command.

Deterrence posture

STRATCOM is central to debates over nuclear modernization, including the Sentinel ICBM replacing Minuteman III, the Columbia-class submarines, and the B-21 Raider bomber. Its commander testifies annually before Congress on the deterrent posture relative to Russia and China.
