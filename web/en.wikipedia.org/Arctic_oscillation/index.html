TypeAtmospheric circulation pattern
Identified byDavid W. J. Thompson and John M. Wallace (1998)

The Arctic oscillation (AO), also called the Northern Annular Mode, is a climate pattern describing the seesaw of atmospheric pressure between the Arctic and the mid-latitudes of the Northern Hemisphere. When Arctic pressure is lower than average, mid-latitude pressure tends to be higher than average, and vice versa. The AO is the dominant pattern of non-seasonal sea-level pressure variability north of 20°N.

Phases

In its positive phase, a strong polar vortex keeps frigid air bottled up over the Arctic, mid-latitude westerly winds strengthen, and northern Europe, Russia and parts of North America tend to be warmer and wetter than usual. In the negative phase, the vortex weakens, cold Arctic air spills southward, and blocking patterns bring colder, stormier winters to the mid-latitudes while the Arctic itself is relatively warm. The winter of 2009–10, with its strongly negative AO, brought severe cold to Europe and eastern North America.

Definition and relationships

The AO index is typically defined from the leading empirical orthogonal function of monthly sea-level pressure anomalies, following the 1998 analysis by Thompson and Wallace. The AO closely resembles the North Atlantic oscillation, which some scientists regard as a regional expression of the same phenomenon; its Southern Hemisphere counterpart is the Antarctic oscillation (Southern Annular Mode). Variations in the AO are linked to stratospheric polar vortex behaviour and sudden stratospheric warming events.
