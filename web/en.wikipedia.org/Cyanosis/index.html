Cyanosis

Cyanosis is the bluish or purplish discolouration of the skin and mucous membranes due to inadequate oxygenation of the blood. It becomes apparent when the concentration of deoxygenated haemoglobin in the capillaries exceeds about 5 g/dL.

Types

Central cyanosis affects the whole body, including the tongue and lips, and results from low arterial oxygen saturation, as in congenital heart disease with right-to-left shunting, severe lung disease, or high altitude. Peripheral cyanosis is confined to the extremities and may result from reduced local blood flow — cold exposure, Raynaud's phenomenon, or heart failure — with normal arterial oxygen levels. Acrocyanosis is a persistent, painless peripheral cyanosis of the hands and feet.

Diagnosis and significance

Cyanosis is a sign rather than a disease; its presence warrants urgent evaluation of the underlying cardiopulmonary cause. It may be harder to detect in patients with dark skin or anaemia, and pulse oximetry and arterial blood gas analysis provide objective measures of oxygenation.
