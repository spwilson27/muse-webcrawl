Gilbert Islands

The Gilbert Islands (Gilbertese: Tungaru; formerly the Kingsmill Islands or King's-Mill Islands) are a chain of sixteen atolls and coral islands in the western Pacific Ocean. They are the main part of the Republic of Kiribati, whose name is a rendering of "Gilberts" in the native Gilbertese language.

The atolls and islands of the Gilberts lie roughly in a north-to-south line. From north to south they include Makin, Butaritari, Marakei, Abaiang, Tarawa, Maiana, Abemama, Kuria, Aranuka, Nonouti, Tabiteuea, Beru, Nikunau, Onotoa, Tamana, and Arorae. The capital of Kiribati, Tarawa, is in the central part of the chain.

History

The islands were inhabited for centuries by Micronesian peoples before European contact. They were named by the British explorer Captain Thomas Gilbert, who sighted several of the islands in 1788 while sailing between Port Jackson (Sydney) and Canton. In 1892 the islands became part of the British Gilbert and Ellice Islands protectorate, and in 1916 they became a Crown colony.

During the Second World War the islands were occupied by Japanese forces from December 1941. They were the scene of heavy fighting in November 1943, most famously the Battle of Tarawa on Betio atoll and the Battle of Makin, among the first major amphibious assaults by United States forces in the Central Pacific. The Gilberts became part of the independent Republic of Kiribati on 12 July 1979.

Geography and climate

The islands are low-lying coral atolls, rarely rising more than a few metres above sea level, and are vulnerable to drought and to sea-level rise. The climate is warm and humid, moderated by trade winds, with a wet season from October to March. The population of the Gilbert chain is a little over 100,000, about half of whom live on South Tarawa.
