Bicycle theft

Bicycle theft is the crime of stealing a bicycle. It is a common crime in many cities worldwide; hundreds of thousands of bicycles are reported stolen each year in the United States alone, and many thefts go unreported. Recovery rates are low, with only a small percentage of stolen bicycles returned to their owners.

Methods

Thieves may steal unlocked bicycles opportunistically, cut locks and cables with portable tools, lift bicycles over signposts or fencing, or remove components such as wheels and saddles. Organised theft targets high-value bicycles for resale, sometimes through online marketplaces, while other thefts are for temporary transport ("joyriding").

Prevention

Prevention measures include using hardened locks correctly, parking in visible and secure locations, and removing valuable accessories. Registration schemes and frame-number databases, such as BikeIndex and national police registers, help identify recovered bicycles. Some cities operate bait-bike programmes using GPS-tracked bicycles to catch thieves.

Statistics

Studies in several countries suggest that a substantial share of regular cyclists experience theft during their riding lives, and fear of theft is a barrier to cycling uptake. Insurance and reporting practices vary widely between jurisdictions.
