Starship Troopers is a military science fiction novel by American writer Robert A. Heinlein. Written in a few weeks in reaction to the US suspending nuclear tests, the story was first published as a two-part serial in The Magazine of Fantasy & Science Fiction as Starship Soldier, and published as a book by G. P. Putnam's Sons in December 1959.

Plot

The story follows Juan "Johnny" Rico through his military career in the Mobile Infantry, from recruit to officer, during an interstellar war between the Terran Federation and an insect-like species known as the Arachnids, or "Bugs". Much of the novel consists of flashbacks and classroom discussions in Rico's History and Moral Philosophy course, in which the political philosophy of the Federation—where full citizenship is earned through federal service—is expounded.

Themes

The novel explores civic virtue, the necessity of war, and the idea of suffrage earned through service. It popularized several concepts in science fiction, including powered armor and orbital drop infantry. It has long been controversial for its political views, which some critics describe as militaristic or fascistic, a debate that continued with Paul Verhoeven's 1997 film adaptation, which satirized the novel's themes.

Reception

Starship Troopers won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1960. It remains on recommended reading lists of the United States armed services and influenced works including Joe Haldeman's The Forever War and the film Aliens.
