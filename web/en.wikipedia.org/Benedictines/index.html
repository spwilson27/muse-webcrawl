Benedictines

The Benedictines, officially the Order of Saint Benedict (OSB), are a monastic religious order of the Catholic Church following the Rule of Saint Benedict. Founded by Benedict of Nursia in the 6th century, they are the oldest Western monastic order.

History

Benedictine monasteries spread across Europe in the early Middle Ages, preserving learning, copying manuscripts and developing agriculture. Houses such as Cluny and Monte Cassino became major religious and cultural centres.

Life

Monks and nuns take vows of stability, obedience and conversion of life, dividing the day between communal prayer (the Divine Office), work and sacred reading, under the motto ora et labora — pray and work.

Organisation

Each abbey is autonomous under its abbot or abbess; congregations of houses are linked in the Benedictine Confederation, headed by an Abbot Primate resident in Rome.
