Jean-Claude Mézières

Jean-Claude Mézières (23 September 1938 – 23 January 2022) was a French comic book artist best known as the co-creator, with writer Pierre Christin, of the science fiction series Valérian and Laureline (1967–2019), one of the most influential works of Franco-Belgian comics. His detailed, imaginative designs for alien worlds, creatures, and spacecraft helped define the visual language of European science fiction, and his concept art for Luc Besson's film The Fifth Element (1997) carried his influence into cinema.

Early life and career

Mézières was born in Paris and grew up in Saint-Mandé. A childhood friend of Pierre Christin, he published his first comic strip at fifteen and studied at the École des Arts Appliqués. In the mid-1960s he travelled to the United States, where he worked variously as a cowboy in Utah and in advertising, an experience that enriched the visual repertoire he later brought to science fiction. Returning to France, he reunited with Christin to create a new series for the magazine Pilote, edited by René Goscinny.

Valérian and Laureline

Valérian: spatio-temporal agent debuted in Pilote in 1967 with the story later collected as Bad Dreams. The series follows Valérian and his resourceful partner Laureline, agents of Galaxity, Earth's capital in the 28th century, through time-and-space adventures notable for their ecological and political themes, strong female lead, and Mézières's exuberant alien designs. Key albums include The City of Shifting Waters, Empire of a Thousand Planets, World Without Stars, Welcome to Alflolol, The Ambassadors of the Shadows, and On the Frontiers. Some two dozen albums appeared over five decades, concluding in the 2010s. The series has been credited with influencing the look of films such as Star Wars and was adapted by Luc Besson as the film Valerian and the City of a Thousand Planets (2017).

Film and other work

Besson engaged Mézières as a concept designer for The Fifth Element; devices such as the flying taxis of its future New York drew directly on imagery Mézières had drawn for the Valérian album The Circles of Power. Mézières also worked in advertising and illustration, and published the art book The Extras of Mézières collecting his designs. With Christin he also produced journalistic comics reportage and other short works.

Recognition and death

Mézières received the Grand Prix de la ville d'Angoulême in 1984, the highest honour of the Angoulême International Comics Festival, and numerous other awards in France and abroad. He died in Paris on 23 January 2022, aged 83. Tributes stressed his generosity as a draughtsman — dense, playful pages marrying architectural precision to biological fantasy — and his role, alongside Christin and colourist Évelyne Tranlé (his sister), in creating one of the longest-running and best-loved series in European comics.
