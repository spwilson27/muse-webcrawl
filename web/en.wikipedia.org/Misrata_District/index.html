Misrata (Arabic: مصراتة, Miṣrātah) is one of the districts (shaabiyat) of Libya, in the northwestern part of the country along the Mediterranean coast. Its capital and largest city is Misrata, Libya's third-largest city and a major commercial port. The district borders the Mediterranean Sea to the north, and the districts of Murqub to the west and Sirte to the east and south.

Geography and economy

The district occupies a coastal plain backed by desert, centred on the city of Misrata near the western shore of the Gulf of Sidra, about 187 km (116 mi) east of Tripoli. Its economy rests on the port of Qasr Ahmad, the Misrata free zone, and heavy industry, notably the Libyan Iron and Steel Company, one of the largest steel producers in North Africa. Agriculture in the district includes dates, olives, and cereals supported by irrigation.

History

The area corresponds roughly to the ancient emporium of Kephalai ("headlands"), later known as Thubactis. The modern district was repeatedly redrawn as Libya's administrative divisions changed under Italian colonial rule, the monarchy, and the Gaddafi era. During the Libyan Civil War of 2011, the city of Misrata endured a prolonged siege by Gaddafi forces and became a symbol of the revolution; its militias subsequently became among the most powerful armed groups in post-war Libya.
