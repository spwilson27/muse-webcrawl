Plan of Ayala

The Plan of Ayala (Spanish: Plan de Ayala) was a document drafted by revolutionary leader Emiliano Zapata during the Mexican Revolution. Announced on 28 November 1911 from the town of Ayala in the state of Morelos, the plan repudiated President Francisco I. Madero, accusing him of betraying the revolutionary cause by failing to carry out land reform and by preserving the social order of the Porfiriato.

Contents

The plan set out fifteen points. It declared Madero incapable of fulfilling the goals of the Revolution and recognised Pascual Orozco — and, if he refused, Zapata himself — as leader of the Liberation Army. Its agrarian articles demanded the restitution of lands, woods, and waters that haciendas had usurped from villages and individual citizens; the expropriation, with partial indemnity, of one-third of the monopolised landholdings for distribution to towns and individuals; and the confiscation of the property of those who opposed the plan.

Significance

The Plan of Ayala became the defining manifesto of the Zapatista movement and one of the foundational documents of Mexican agrarianism. Its demand for "land and liberty" (tierra y libertad) helped shape Article 27 of the Constitution of 1917, which provided the legal basis for the ejido system of communal landholding. The plan is commemorated annually and remains a reference point for land-rights movements in Mexico.
