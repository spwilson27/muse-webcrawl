Howdah

A howdah (Hindi: haudā), also known as houdah, is a carriage which is positioned on the back of an elephant, or occasionally some other animal such as a camel, used most often in the past to carry wealthy people or their luggage during travels or processions. It was also used as a fighting platform in war.

Description

Howdahs are typically wooden or metal frames fitted with seats, often covered by a canopy for shade and frequently richly decorated when used by royalty. Elaborate examples served as status symbols; the howdahs of Indian maharajas could be inlaid, gilded, and upholstered, and the elephant itself might be caparisoned in embroidered cloth.

Military use

In warfare, howdahs carried archers, spearmen, or commanders, giving them height and a vantage point over the battlefield. In the 19th century, howdah pistols — large-calibre multi-barrelled handguns — were carried by hunters and officers riding in howdahs for protection against wounded game such as tigers at close quarters.

Modern use

Today howdahs are chiefly associated with tourism and ceremonial processions in South and Southeast Asia, though their use for tourist rides has declined in some places on animal-welfare grounds.
