Bristol Bay

Bristol Bay is the easternmost arm of the Bering Sea, at the southwestern coast of Alaska, between the Alaska Peninsula to the south and the mainland to the north. It extends roughly 250 miles (400 km) east–west and 180 miles (290 km) north–south, and is fed by rivers including the Kvichak, Nushagak, Naknek, Egegik, Ugashik, Wood, Igushik and Togiak.

Salmon fishery

Bristol Bay supports the largest sockeye salmon fishery in the world. Roughly half of the world's harvest of sockeye salmon comes from the bay's river systems, with annual runs numbering in the tens of millions of fish; the 2022 run exceeded 70 million sockeye. The commercial fishery, prosecuted mainly by drift gillnetters and setnetters in a short summer season, is the economic mainstay of communities such as Dillingham and Naknek (King Salmon), alongside subsistence and sport fishing. Five species of Pacific salmon spawn in the watershed, whose spawning grounds in lakes such as Iliamna Lake — Alaska's largest — are exceptionally productive.

Pebble Mine controversy

The bay's headwaters are also the site of the proposed Pebble Mine, a very large copper–gold–molybdenum deposit. The project has been opposed for decades by commercial and subsistence fishers, Alaska Native communities and environmental groups because of the risk of acid mine drainage to the salmon watershed. In 2020 the US Army Corps of Engineers denied a key permit, and in 2023 the US Environmental Protection Agency finalised protections under the Clean Water Act restricting mine waste disposal in the watershed.
