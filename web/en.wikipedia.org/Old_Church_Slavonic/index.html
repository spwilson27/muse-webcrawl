Old Church Slavonic

Old Church Slavonic is the first Slavic literary language, developed in the ninth century by the Byzantine missionary brothers Saints Cyril and Methodius, who translated the Bible and liturgical texts for their mission to Great Moravia beginning in 863. Based largely on the South Slavic dialect spoken around their native Thessalonica, it became the liturgical and literary language of much of the Orthodox Slavic world.

Scripts and texts

Old Church Slavonic was first written in the Glagolitic alphabet devised by Cyril; the later Cyrillic alphabet, developed by his disciples in Bulgaria, eventually predominated. Surviving canonical manuscripts, such as the Codex Zographensis and Codex Marianus, date from the tenth and eleventh centuries. As the language spread, regional recensions (Russian, Serbian, Bulgarian, Croatian) developed, collectively termed Church Slavonic, which remains in liturgical use today.

Influence

Old Church Slavonic profoundly shaped the literary traditions and vocabularies of the South and East Slavic languages, much as Latin did in Western Europe, and its texts are a key source for reconstructing Proto-Slavic.
