Doctor Who

Doctor Who
Genre: Science fiction
Created by: Sydney Newman, C. E. Webber, Donald Wilson
Original release: 23 November 1963 – 6 December 1989; revived 26 March 2005 – present
Network: BBC One
Country: United Kingdom

Doctor Who is a British science fiction television series broadcast by the BBC since 1963. The series, created by Sydney Newman, C. E. Webber and Donald Wilson, depicts the adventures of an extraterrestrial being called the Doctor, a member of a humanoid species called Time Lords. The Doctor travels in the universe and in time using a time-travelling spaceship called the TARDIS, which externally appears as a British police box. While travelling, typically in company with companions, the Doctor combats foes such as the Daleks and Cybermen, works to save civilisations, and helps people in need.

Beginning with William Hartnell, fourteen actors have headlined the series as the Doctor; in 2017 Jodie Whittaker became the first woman to officially assume the role in the television series. The transition to each succeeding actor is explained within the show's narrative through the plot device of regeneration, a biological process through which a Time Lord gains a new physical form and a somewhat different personality after mortal injury or old age.

History

The programme originally ran from 1963 to 1989, was cancelled, and returned with a television film in 1996 before being successfully relaunched in 2005 by Russell T Davies. The revived series has been produced in Wales by BBC Wales and, more recently, Bad Wolf. Doctor Who is listed in Guinness World Records as the longest-running science fiction television series in the world, as well as the "most successful" science fiction series of all time, based on overall broadcast ratings and sales.

The Doctor

The Doctor is a centuries-old Time Lord from the planet Gallifrey who fled their home world in a stolen TARDIS. The character's incarnations include the First Doctor (William Hartnell), the Fourth Doctor (Tom Baker, the longest-serving), the Tenth Doctor (David Tennant), the Eleventh (Matt Smith), the Twelfth (Peter Capaldi), the Thirteenth (Jodie Whittaker) and the Fifteenth (Ncuti Gatwa). Signature tools include the sonic screwdriver and psychic paper; the Doctor's greatest enemies include the Daleks, the Cybermen and the Master.

Cultural impact

The programme is a significant part of British popular culture and has influenced generations of television professionals. It has spawned spin-offs including Torchwood, The Sarah Jane Adventures and Class, plus extensive novel, audio drama and comic ranges. Fandom's study of the series even mirrors academic practices of archival research and textual analysis comparable to the Documentary hypothesis in biblical scholarship.
