Youth culture

Street style and youth culture

Youth culture refers to the social norms, practices, styles and shared interests of young people, as distinct from the culture of adults and children. It is expressed through music, fashion, language, media and leisure activities, and is often analysed in terms of subcultures — groups such as teddy boys, mods, punks, goths, skaters and hip-hop fans that develop recognisable styles and values.

History

A recognisable, commercially significant youth culture emerged in the post-Second World War period, when rising incomes, longer schooling and targeted marketing in the United States and Britain created the "teenager" as a consumer identity. Rock and roll in the 1950s, the mod and hippie movements of the 1960s, punk in the 1970s, hip hop and rave culture in the 1980s and 1990s each defined generational styles, often in conscious opposition to parental norms.

Theoretical views

Sociologists have treated youth subcultures variously as expressions of class resistance (the Birmingham Centre for Contemporary Cultural Studies), as scenes or "neo-tribes" organised around taste rather than class, or as lifestyle clusters shaped by media and consumption. Critics note that youth culture is heavily produced and marketed by cultural industries, and that digital media have blurred the boundaries between distinct subcultures.

Contemporary forms

Today youth culture is strongly mediated by social media platforms, streaming music, gaming and influencer culture. Fashions and slang can spread globally within days, producing a more fluid mix of styles alongside persistent local scenes.
