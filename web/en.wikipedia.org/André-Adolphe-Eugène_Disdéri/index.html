André-Adolphe-Eugène Disdéri

André-Adolphe-Eugène Disdéri (28 March 1819 – 4 October 1889) was a French photographer best known for patenting the carte de visite, a small-format portrait photograph mounted on card that became a worldwide craze in the 1860s.

The carte de visite

In 1854 Disdéri patented a camera with multiple lenses (usually four or eight) that exposed several small portraits on a single glass plate. Printing several images at once drastically cut the cost of portraiture, making photographs affordable to the middle classes. His Paris studio became one of the largest and most fashionable in Europe after Napoleon III famously sat for a carte portrait in 1859.

Career and decline

Disdéri photographed royalty, soldiers departing for war, and scenes of the Franco-Prussian War and the Paris Commune. Imitation by competitors eroded his profits, and the fashion eventually faded; despite other ventures he died in poverty in Nice in 1889. His invention nonetheless transformed photography into a mass medium and popularised the photographic album.
