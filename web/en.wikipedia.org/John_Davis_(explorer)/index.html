John Davis (explorer)

Bornc. 1550, Sandridge, Devon, England
Died29 December 1605, near Bintan Island, East Indies
OccupationNavigator and explorer
Known forArctic exploration; Davis Strait; the Davis quadrant

John Davis (c. 1550 – 29 December 1605) was one of the chief English navigators of the Elizabethan age. He led several voyages in search of the Northwest Passage and served as pilot on expeditions to the East Indies. The Davis Strait between Greenland and Canada is named after him, and he invented the backstaff, or Davis quadrant, an instrument widely used for measuring latitude.

Arctic voyages

Backed by London merchants and encouraged by Francis Walsingham, Davis made three voyages to the Arctic in 1585, 1586 and 1587. On the first he reached Greenland and the strait that now bears his name; on the third he sailed as far north as about 73°N along the Greenland coast and explored Cumberland Sound and Frobisher Bay. Though he found no passage, his surveys greatly improved knowledge of the region, and his logbooks and charts were used by later explorers such as William Baffin.

Later career

Davis sailed with Thomas Cavendish on his second circumnavigation attempt in 1591–93 and is credited with sighting the Falkland Islands in 1592. He later served as pilot-major on voyages to the East Indies for the Dutch and then the English East India Company. On his final voyage in 1605, his ship was attacked by Japanese pirates off the coast of Malaya, and Davis was killed in the fighting.

Works

Davis wrote The Seaman's Secrets (1594), a practical manual of navigation, and The World's Hydrographical Description (1595), arguing for the existence of a Northwest Passage.
