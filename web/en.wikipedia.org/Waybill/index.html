Waybill

A waybill is a document issued by a carrier giving details and instructions relating to the shipment of a consignment of goods. Typically it shows the names of the consignor and consignee, the point of origin, destination and route, and details of the freight, charges, and goods carried.

Types

An air waybill (AWB) accompanies goods shipped by air and serves as a receipt and contract of carriage; unlike a bill of lading it is generally non-negotiable. Rail and road transport use their own waybill forms — for example the CIM consignment note in international rail transport. The maritime equivalent is the sea waybill, which similarly differs from a negotiable bill of lading.

Function

Waybills enable carriers and customers to track shipments, document the condition and quantity of goods, and support customs clearance. In modern logistics they are usually generated electronically, with tracking numbers linking the waybill to online shipment records.
