Battle of Falkirk

Battle of Falkirk
Date: 22 July 1298
Location: Falkirk, Scotland
Result: English victory
Part of the First War of Scottish Independence

The Battle of Falkirk was fought on 22 July 1298 between the army of King Edward I of England and the Scottish forces led by William Wallace during the First War of Scottish Independence. Edward avenged the Scottish victory at Stirling Bridge the previous year, breaking Wallace's schiltron formations with archery and cavalry.

Background

Edward, recalled from campaigning in France, marched into Scotland in the summer of 1298. His army nearly starved as Scottish scorched-earth tactics withheld supplies, but reinforcements arrived by sea, and Wallace, shadowing the English, was brought to battle near Falkirk. Wallace arrayed his infantry in four great schiltrons — dense circles of spearmen — between woods and a marsh, with archers and a small body of cavalry.

The battle

The English cavalry, after initial checks against the boggy ground, drove off the Scottish horse and archers, isolating the schiltrons. Edward then held his knights back while Welsh and English longbowmen and crossbowmen shot into the static spear formations, opening gaps into which the cavalry charged. The schiltrons broke with heavy losses, though Wallace himself escaped.

Aftermath

Wallace resigned the Guardianship of Scotland soon afterwards and went into obscurity before his capture in 1305. Falkirk demonstrated the vulnerability of dense spear formations to combined arms — a lesson Edward's grandson would apply in reverse against the French at Crécy.
