Director of photography

The director of photography (sometimes shortened to DP or DOP), also known as the cinematographer, is the chief of the camera and light crews on a film set and is responsible for making artistic and technical decisions related to the image of the film. The study and practice of this field is referred to as cinematography.

Role

The director of photography works closely with the film director before and during production to establish the visual style of the film: choosing the camera system and lenses, film stock or digital format, framing and composition, and the lighting design that gives each scene its mood. The DP heads the camera, electrical (lighting), and grip departments, and supervises the colour grading of the finished film in post-production.

On many productions the DP does not personally operate the camera; a camera operator handles that duty under the DP's direction, though some cinematographers — notably Roger Deakins — prefer to operate themselves.

Selection and collaboration

Directors typically select a DP whose previous work suits the project, and long-running director–cinematographer partnerships are common, such as Ingmar Bergman with Sven Nykvist, and the Coen brothers with Roger Deakins. Professional societies include the American Society of Cinematographers (ASC) and the British Society of Cinematographers (BSC), whose initials members may append to their names.

Recognition

The Academy Award for Best Cinematography has been given since the first Oscars in 1929. Celebrated cinematographers include Vittorio Storaro, Emmanuel Lubezki — who won the award three consecutive years, for Gravity, Birdman, and The Revenant — and Janusz Kamiński.
