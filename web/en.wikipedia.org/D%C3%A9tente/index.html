Détente

Détente (French for "relaxation") is the easing of strained relations, especially between states. The term is most often applied to the period of improved relations between the United States and the Soviet Union during the Cold War, roughly from the late 1960s to the late 1970s.

Course of détente

Milestones included the Partial Test Ban Treaty era of the 1960s, West Germany's Ostpolitik, President Richard Nixon's 1972 visit to Moscow, and the SALT I arms limitation agreements and the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty. The 1975 Helsinki Accords addressed European borders and human rights. Trade, cultural exchanges and joint ventures such as the 1975 Apollo–Soyuz Test Project accompanied the diplomatic thaw.

Decline

Détente drew criticism from those who argued it conceded too much to the Soviet Union, and it unravelled with the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979, followed by the Carter administration's sanctions and the more confrontational policies of the early Reagan years. A renewed easing of tensions came in the late 1980s under Mikhail Gorbachev.
