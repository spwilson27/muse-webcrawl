Gerhard Fritz Kurt Schröder (born 7 April 1944) is a German former politician of the Social Democratic Party (SPD) who served as Chancellor of Germany from 1998 to 2005. He previously served as Minister President of Lower Saxony (1990–1998) and led the first federal coalition between the SPD and the Greens.

Early life

Schröder was born in Mossenberg, North Rhine-Westphalia, in the final year of the Second World War; his father, a soldier, was killed in Romania in 1944. Raised in modest circumstances, he worked as a sales assistant and construction labourer while attending night school, then studied law at the University of Göttingen and practised as a lawyer. He rose through the SPD's youth organisation, the Jusos, which he chaired from 1978, and entered the Bundestag in 1980.

Minister President and Chancellor

As Minister President of Lower Saxony from 1990, Schröder built a pragmatic, business-friendly reputation. In the 1998 federal election the SPD under his leadership defeated Helmut Kohl's CDU after sixteen years, and Schröder formed a red-green government with Joschka Fischer as foreign minister. His government legalised civil partnerships, reformed citizenship law, began the phase-out of nuclear power, and committed German troops to combat abroad for the first time since 1945 in Kosovo and Afghanistan.

Agenda 2010 and the Iraq War

Re-elected narrowly in 2002 — helped by his government's response to severe flooding and his firm opposition to the Iraq War, which strained relations with the United States — Schröder announced the Agenda 2010 programme in 2003. Its labour-market and welfare reforms (the Hartz reforms) cut unemployment benefits and liberalised employment rules; economists credit them with later German labour-market strength, but they were deeply unpopular within his own party and contributed to his defeat. After losing a confidence vote he engineered in 2005, early elections produced a grand coalition under Angela Merkel.

After politics

Schröder subsequently took senior roles with Russian energy companies, including the Nord Stream pipeline consortium and Rosneft, positions that drew criticism in Germany, greatly intensified after Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, when his closeness to Vladimir Putin and refusal to sever ties led to sanctions on his offices and moves within the SPD to expel him.
