Thermoelectric effect

The thermoelectric effect is the direct conversion between temperature differences and electric voltage, and vice versa. It encompasses three related phenomena: the Seebeck effect (a temperature difference creates a voltage), the Peltier effect (an electric current creates a temperature difference), and the Thomson effect (heating or cooling of a current-carrying conductor with a temperature gradient).

Applications

Thermoelectric devices include thermocouples for temperature measurement, Peltier modules for solid-state cooling, and thermoelectric generators used in radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs) powering deep-space probes such as Voyager and Cassini.

Efficiency

Device performance is characterised by the dimensionless figure of merit ZT; commercial materials such as bismuth telluride have ZT near 1, limiting efficiency below that of conventional heat engines.
