Northern Italy (Italian: Italia settentrionale, Nord Italia, or simply il Nord) is a geographical and cultural region in the northern part of Italy. It consists of eight administrative regions: Piedmont, Aosta Valley, Liguria, Lombardy, Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol, Veneto, Friuli-Venezia Giulia and Emilia-Romagna.

Geography

The region is bounded by the Alps to the north and west and includes the Po Valley, one of the most fertile and densely populated plains in Europe. Major rivers include the Po, Adige and Piave. The Italian Lakes — Garda, Maggiore and Como — lie at the foot of the Alps.

Economy

Northern Italy is the most developed and productive area of the country, home to much of Italy's industry, finance and technology sectors. Milan is its economic capital, and cities such as Turin, Genoa, Bologna and Venice are major centres of manufacturing, logistics and tourism.

History

The area was settled by Celtic and Ligurian peoples before Roman conquest. In the Middle Ages it was divided among communes, signorie and foreign powers, and in the 19th century it became the engine of the Risorgimento under the Kingdom of Sardinia.
