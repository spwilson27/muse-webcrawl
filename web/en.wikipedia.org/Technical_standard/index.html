Technical standard

Standards bodies publish documents that codify technical requirements and procedures.

A technical standard is an established norm or requirement for a repeatable technical task. It is usually a formal document that establishes uniform engineering or technical criteria, methods, processes, and practices. Standards ensure that materials, products, and services are fit for their purpose, and enable interoperability between products made by different manufacturers.

Standards organizations

Standards are developed by standards organizations at international, regional, and national levels. Prominent bodies include the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC), the IEEE, ASTM International, and national bodies such as ANSI (United States), BSI (United Kingdom), and DIN (Germany). In computing and the internet, standards are often produced by consortia and open bodies such as the IETF (whose documents are RFCs) and the W3C.

Types

Standards may be de jure (formally adopted), de facto (dominant through market use), or open standards, which are publicly available and developed by consensus. Some standards become mandatory when incorporated into law or regulation. Standards are usually developed through a consensus process involving industry, government, academia, and consumers, and are periodically reviewed and revised.

Examples

Well-known standards include the SI system of units, ISO 9000 quality management standards, screw thread standards, the USB and Ethernet specifications, and internet protocols such as TCP/IP and HTTP.
