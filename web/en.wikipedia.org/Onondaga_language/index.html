Onondaga language

The Onondaga language (Onondaga: Onǫda'gegahgwaḥ) is an Iroquoian language spoken by the Onondaga people, one of the original Five Nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy), whose homeland is in present-day central New York, south of Lake Ontario, and in Ontario, Canada at Six Nations of the Grand River.

Onondaga is a polysynthetic language with noun incorporation and a complex verb morphology. Like other Northern Iroquoian languages it is endangered, with only a small number of first-language speakers, most of them elderly, but revitalisation efforts include immersion programmes, dictionaries and teaching at Onondaga Nation School.

Phonology

The language has a relatively small consonant inventory, glottalised and aspirated stops in some analyses, and distinctive vowel length and nasalised vowels. A standard orthography uses special characters for nasal vowels and the glottal stop.
