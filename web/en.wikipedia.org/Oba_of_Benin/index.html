The Oba of Benin is the traditional ruler (oba) of the Edo people and formerly the head of state of the historic Kingdom of Benin, centred on Benin City in present-day southern Nigeria. The title oba was introduced, according to tradition, around 1200 when the Ogiso ("rulers of the sky") dynasty gave way to the Oranmiyan line and its first oba, Eweka I. The oba was a sacred king: political sovereign, military commander, and the ritual link between the Edo people and their ancestors and gods.

History

Under a series of warrior-kings in the 15th and 16th centuries — notably Ewuare the Great (r. c. 1440–1473), Ozolua, Esigie, and Orhogbua — Benin expanded into an empire dominating trade in pepper, ivory, and later enslaved people with the Portuguese, who arrived in 1485. Ewuare is credited with rebuilding Benin City, fortifying it with massive earthwork walls (the Benin Moats), and reorganising the state and its court rituals.

The oba's palace was famed for its brass-casting workshops, which produced the celebrated Benin Bronzes — plaques, heads, and figures cast by the lost-wax method that memorialised obas, queen mothers (iyoba), and court life. In 1897 a British punitive expedition captured Benin City after the ambush of a British delegation; Oba Ovonramwen was exiled to Calabar, the palace was looted and burned, and thousands of bronzes were removed to museums in Europe — many now the subject of restitution claims, with several institutions returning pieces to Nigeria in the 2020s.

Restoration and modern role

The monarchy was restored in 1914 under Eweka II. Later obas included Akenzua II and Erediauwa (r. 1979–2016), a former federal permanent secretary. The current oba, Ewuare II, a former Nigerian ambassador, was crowned on 20 October 2016. Today the oba is a traditional ruler within Nigeria's Edo State, without formal political power but with substantial cultural and moral authority, involved in campaigns against human trafficking and in the planned museum projects in Benin City intended to house returned bronzes.
