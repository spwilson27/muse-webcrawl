The U15 Group of Canadian Research Universities (commonly shortened to U15; French: U15 – Regroupement des universités de recherche du Canada) is an association of fifteen public research universities in Canada. The group functions as a collective voice for research-intensive universities in Canada, advocating for federal research funding, science policy, and the role of universities in innovation and economic growth.

U15 members are among the largest and most research-intensive institutions in the country. Collectively, they attract the majority of competitively awarded federal research funding in Canada, conduct a large share of Canadian university research, and produce most of the doctoral graduates trained in Canada. The member universities are home to many of Canada's research chairs, major research hospitals, and national research facilities.

History

The association traces its origins to 1991, when the heads of ten Ontario and other Canadian research universities began meeting informally as the Group of Ten (G10) to coordinate advocacy on research policy. As federal programs such as the Canada Research Chairs (established in 2000) and the Canada Foundation for Innovation (established in 1997) reshaped the research funding landscape, the group became a formal presence in Ottawa.

In 2011 the group expanded its membership to fifteen institutions and adopted the name U15, with a permanent executive office in Ottawa. The expansion brought in additional research-intensive universities from across the country, including institutions in the Atlantic provinces, the Prairies, and British Columbia. The U15 has since worked on issues including federal science policy reviews, graduate student funding, research security, and Canada's participation in international research programs such as the European Union's Horizon Europe, which Canada joined as an associated country in 2024.

Research role

According to the association, U15 institutions together attract roughly four-fifths of competitively allocated federal research funding in Canada and perform a comparable share of university research activity. They host the majority of Canada Research Chairs and Canada Excellence Research Chairs, operate many of the country's largest research hospitals through affiliated teaching hospitals, and train a large proportion of Canada's doctoral and postdoctoral researchers.

The group publishes analyses of the Canadian research ecosystem and makes submissions to federal budget processes and policy reviews, including the 2016–2017 Fundamental Science Review, whose report (often called the Naylor Report) recommended substantial reinvestment in investigator-led research. The U15 has also advocateded on research security, intellectual property, and the funding of graduate scholarships and postdoctoral fellowships.
