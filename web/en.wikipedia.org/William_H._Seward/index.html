William H. Seward

BornMay 16, 1801, Florida, New York
DiedOctober 10, 1872 (aged 71), Auburn, New York
OfficesU.S. Secretary of State (1861–1869); Governor of New York (1839–1842); U.S. Senator (1849–1861)

William Henry Seward (May 16, 1801 – October 10, 1872) was an American politician who served as United States Secretary of State from 1861 to 1869 under Presidents Abraham Lincoln and Andrew Johnson. He is best remembered for negotiating the 1867 purchase of Alaska from Russia, often called "Seward's Folly" at the time.

Early career

Seward practiced law in Auburn, New York, and rose through the Anti-Masonic and Whig parties, becoming governor of New York in 1839 and then a leading antislavery voice in the U.S. Senate, famous for his "higher law" and "irrepressible conflict" speeches.

Secretary of State

After losing the 1860 Republican presidential nomination to Lincoln, Seward became his secretary of state, working to keep European powers from recognizing the Confederacy during the Civil War. On the night of Lincoln's assassination in April 1865, Seward was seriously wounded in a coordinated attack at his home. Under Johnson he negotiated the Alaska purchase for $7.2 million and pursued an expansive vision of American commerce in the Pacific.
