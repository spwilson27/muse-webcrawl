Titus Caesar Vespasianus (30 December 39 – 13 September 81) was Roman emperor from 79 to 81, the second ruler of the Flavian dynasty and the eldest son of Vespasian. He is remembered for conquering Jerusalem in the First Jewish–Roman War and for a short, popular reign marked by disaster relief.

Military career

Titus served under his father in Judaea from 67. When Vespasian was proclaimed emperor in 69 and departed for Rome, Titus remained to finish the war; in 70, after a brutal siege, his legions stormed Jerusalem and destroyed the Second Temple — an event commemorated by the Arch of Titus in Rome, whose reliefs show the menorah and silver trumpets carried in the triumph of 71. Back in Rome he served as praetorian prefect and his father's effective partner, acquiring a reputation for severity that made his accession feared.

Reign

As emperor Titus confounded expectations, governing generously. His reign saw the completion and dedication of the Flavian Amphitheatre — the Colosseum — in 80, the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 that destroyed Pompeii and Herculaneum, and a great fire at Rome in 80; Titus personally organised and funded relief after each disaster. His liaison with the Jewish queen Berenice, sister of Agrippa II, scandalised Rome and was ended for reasons of state. He died of a fever after only two years in power and was deified; Suetonius reports that on his deathbed he lamented a single unnamed deed of wrongdoing.
