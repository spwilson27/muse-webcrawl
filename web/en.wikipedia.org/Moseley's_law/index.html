Moseley's law

Moseley's law is an empirical law concerning the characteristic X-rays emitted by atoms: the square root of the frequency of the emitted X-ray is approximately proportional to the atomic number.

Moseley's law

Discovered: 1913, by Henry Moseley

Formulation

For Kα lines: √ν ∝ (Z − 1). Moseley's plot of √ν against position in the periodic table gave straight lines, proving atomic number (nuclear charge) — not atomic weight — is the fundamental ordering property, and identifying gaps for elements 43, 61, 72 and 75.

Explanation

The law follows from the Bohr model: Kα emission occurs when an electron falls to the n=1 shell screened by the remaining electron, giving an effective charge of (Z−1).
