Hope Diamond

The Hope Diamond is a 45.52-carat deep-blue diamond famous for its size, colour, and the legend of a curse said to afflict its owners. It is housed at the Smithsonian Institution's National Museum of Natural History in Washington, D.C., where it is among the most visited museum objects in the world.

Origins

The stone was cut from a larger diamond mined in the Kollur mines of Golconda, India. The French merchant Jean-Baptiste Tavernier brought a triangular blue diamond of about 67 carats to Europe in the 1660s and sold it to Louis XIV, who had it recut as the "French Blue." That gem was stolen from the royal storehouse in 1792 during the French Revolution; a smaller blue diamond matching a recut shape surfaced in London decades later, by then in the collection of Henry Philip Hope, from whom it takes its present name.

Modern history

After passing through owners including Washington Post heiress Evalyn Walsh McLean, jeweller Harry Winston bought the diamond, exhibited it on tour, and in 1958 mailed it to the Smithsonian as a gift — famously sending it by ordinary registered post. Its blue colour comes from trace amounts of boron, and it fluoresces red under ultraviolet light.

The "curse"

Tales of misfortune attached to the diamond were largely embellished or invented by dealers and journalists in the early twentieth century to raise its mystique and value; historians have found little evidence for the dramatic deaths attributed to it.
