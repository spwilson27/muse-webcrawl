War in the Vendée

The War in the Vendée (1793–1796) was a counter-revolutionary insurrection in the Vendée region of western France during the French Revolution. Peasants, provoked by conscription, the Civil Constitution of the Clergy and hostility to the revolutionary government in Paris, rose in March 1793 and formed the Catholic and Royal Army.

Course of the war

The insurgents initially won a string of victories, capturing towns such as Cholet and Saumur, but failed to take Nantes. After defeat at Cholet in October 1793, the royalist army crossed the Loire in the Virée de Galerne and was destroyed at Le Mans and Savenay. Repression by republican forces, including the infamous "infernal columns" of General Turreau, devastated the region. Sporadic fighting continued until 1796, and the Vendée rose again in 1815 and 1832.

Historiography

The human cost is debated; estimates of total deaths run into the hundreds of thousands. The historian Reynald Secher controversially described the repression as a genocide, a characterisation most specialists reject, though the scale of atrocities on both sides is widely acknowledged.
