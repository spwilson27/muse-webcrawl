Pan European Game Information (PEGI) is a European video game content rating system established to help European consumers — especially parents — make informed decisions about buying games for children. Launched on 9 April 2003, it replaced a patchwork of national rating schemes with a single voluntary system that has since become legally enforceable in a number of countries, including the United Kingdom, where it became the sole statutory system in 2012.

Ratings

PEGI classifies games into five age categories — 3, 7, 12, 16 and 18 — shown as colour-coded labels (green for 3 and 7, orange for 12 and 16, red for 18). Alongside the age label, up to eight content descriptors pictorially flag the presence of violence, bad language, fear, gambling, sex, drugs, discrimination or in-game purchases. The ratings are advisory about age suitability rather than difficulty, and a game rated PEGI 18 is restricted to adults.

Administration

The system was developed by the Interactive Software Federation of Europe (now Video Games Europe) and is administered by PEGI S.A. in Brussels. Publishers license the labels after completing a content assessment; examination of games is carried out by independent administrators, historically including the Netherlands Institute for the Classification of Audiovisual Media (NICAM) and the UK's Video Standards Council. PEGI also operates a code of conduct covering advertising and packaging, and a complaints procedure.

Use

PEGI ratings are used in more than 38 countries across Europe as well as in Israel. Some countries, notably Germany, retain their own statutory system (the USK), while Finland and others adopted PEGI into law.
