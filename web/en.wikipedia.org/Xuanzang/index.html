Xuanzang (c. 602 – 664) was a Chinese Buddhist monk, scholar, traveler, and translator. He is famous for his seventeen-year overland journey to India (629–645), undertaken to obtain authoritative sutras and resolve doctrinal disputes, and for his founding role in the Faxiang school of East Asian Yogācāra.

Xuanzang is traditionally depicted carrying a rack of scrolls on his back.

Journey to India

Defying an imperial travel ban, Xuanzang crossed the Gobi Desert and Central Asia along the Silk Road, studied at Nalanda monastery under Śīlabhadra, debated before King Harsha, and visited sites associated with the Buddha. He returned to Chang'an in 645 with hundreds of Sanskrit texts and relics.

Translations and writings

With imperial support he led a large translation bureau at the Great Ci'en Temple, whose Giant Wild Goose Pagoda was built to house his texts. He translated or compiled 75 works, including the Heart Sutra version in common use and the massive Mahāprajñāpāramitā Sūtra. His travel account, the Great Tang Records on the Western Regions, remains a key source for seventh-century Central and South Asia.

Legacy

Xuanzang was later fictionalized as Tang Sanzang ("Tripitaka"), the monk hero of the Ming-dynasty novel Journey to the West.
