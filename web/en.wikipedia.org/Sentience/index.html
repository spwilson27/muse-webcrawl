Sentience

Sentience is the capacity of an organism to have subjective experiences, in particular the capacity to feel sensations such as pain and pleasure. In modern Western philosophy the term often denotes the ability to experience qualia — to be a subject of experience — rather than the higher cognitive capacities sometimes labelled sapience.

Philosophical usage

The concept is central to animal ethics: Jeremy Bentham famously argued that the morally relevant question about animals is not "Can they reason?" but "Can they suffer?" Utilitarian philosophers such as Peter Singer have built theories of animal moral status on the premise of sentience.

Animal sentience

Scientific consensus holds that mammals and birds are sentient, and evidence increasingly supports sentience in fish, cephalopods, and some crustaceans. The United Kingdom's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 formally recognises vertebrates, cephalopod molluscs, and decapod crustaceans as sentient beings.

Artificial intelligence

Whether artificial systems could be sentient is an open question in the philosophy of mind, tied to debates about consciousness, functionalism, and the "hard problem" articulated by David Chalmers.
