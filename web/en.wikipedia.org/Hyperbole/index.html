Hyperbole

The "fish story" is a classic vehicle of hyperbole.

Hyperbole (from Ancient Greek hyperbole, "excess, exaggeration") is the use of exaggeration as a rhetorical device or figure of speech. In rhetoric, it is also sometimes known as auxesis (literally "growth"). In poetry and oratory, it emphasises, evokes strong feelings, and creates strong impressions. As a figure of speech, it is usually not meant to be taken literally.

Usage

Hyperbole may also be used for instances of understatement, though this is less common. It is often used in literature, advertising, and everyday speech. Examples include "I'm so hungry I could eat a horse", "I've told you a million times", and "this bag weighs a ton". Advertisers use hyperbole in claims such as "the world's best coffee", which are understood as puffery rather than factual assertions.

Hyperbole is contrasted with meiosis (deliberate understatement) and litotes. Where hyperbole magnifies, meiosis minimises: describing a hurricane as "a bit breezy" is meiosis.

Etymology and history

The word derives from Greek hyper ("above, beyond") and bole ("a throw") — that which overshoots the mark. Classical rhetoricians including Quintilian discussed hyperbole as a straining of the truth in the service of emphasis, distinguishing it from lying because the audience is not expected to believe the literal claim.
