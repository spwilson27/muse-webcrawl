The Great Wave off Kanagawa

The Great Wave off Kanagawa
Artist: Katsushika Hokusai
Year: c. 1831
Type: Woodblock print (ukiyo-e)
Series: Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji
Dimensions: 25.7 cm × 37.8 cm

The Great Wave off Kanagawa (Japanese: 神奈川沖浪裏, Kanagawa-oki Nami Ura, lit. "Under the Wave off Kanagawa") is a woodblock print by Japanese ukiyo-e artist Katsushika Hokusai, created in late 1831 during the Edo period. The print depicts three boats moving through a stormy sea before an enormous wave, with Mount Fuji visible in the background. It is Hokusai's most famous work and is often considered the most recognizable work of Japanese art in the world.

Description

The print, in landscape format, shows a monstrous, foam-clawed wave about to break over three fast cargo boats (oshiokuri-bune) carrying fishermen in Sagami Bay, south of Kanagawa (present-day Yokohama), while snow-capped Mount Fuji rises calmly in the distance under a darkening sky. The drama of the composition is heightened by the contrast between the violent wave and the serene, almost fragile mountain. Hokusai's signature appears in the upper left corner.

Technique and history

The print is the first in Hokusai's series Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, produced when he was about seventy years old. It was printed with multiple woodblocks, one for each color, using pigments that include the imported synthetic color Prussian blue, which gives the wave its striking intensity. Thousands of impressions were printed; surviving copies are held by museums worldwide, including the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the British Museum, and the Art Institute of Chicago.

Influence

The image profoundly influenced Western art during the Japonisme craze of the late 19th century, affecting artists such as Vincent van Gogh and Claude Debussy, whose score for La mer bore the wave on its cover. Today the wave appears on T-shirts, merchandise, and the Japanese 1,000-yen banknote issued in 2024.
