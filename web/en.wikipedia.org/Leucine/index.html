Leucine

Leucine (symbol Leu or L) is an essential amino acid used in the biosynthesis of proteins. It is an α-amino acid with the chemical formula C6H13NO2, containing an α-amino group, an α-carboxylic acid group and a side-chain isobutyl group, making it a non-polar aliphatic amino acid.

Formula: C6H13NO2
Codons: UUA, UUG, CUU, CUC, CUA, CUG
Essential: Yes
Class: Branched-chain amino acid

Biological role

Like valine and isoleucine, leucine is a branched-chain amino acid (BCAA). It cannot be synthesised by humans and must be obtained from food — good sources include meat, dairy, eggs, soy and legumes. Leucine is particularly important for muscle protein synthesis: it activates the mTOR signalling pathway, which regulates cell growth and protein production, and it can serve as a source of energy for muscle during exercise.

Metabolism

Leucine is a purely ketogenic amino acid, degraded in muscle and liver to acetyl-CoA and acetoacetate. Defects in the enzymes that break down BCAAs cause maple syrup urine disease, a serious inherited metabolic disorder named for the sweet odour of affected infants' urine.

Uses

Leucine supplements are marketed to athletes for muscle building, though evidence for benefit beyond adequate dietary protein is limited. As a food additive it has the E number E641 and is used as a flavour enhancer.
