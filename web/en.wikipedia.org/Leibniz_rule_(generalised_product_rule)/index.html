Leibniz rule (generalised product rule)

In calculus, the Leibniz rule, also called the generalised product rule, extends the familiar product rule for differentiation to products of more than two functions and to derivatives of arbitrary order. It is named after Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz.

Statement

For two differentiable functions f and g, the n-th derivative of their product is: (fg)(n) = Σk=0n C(n,k) f(k) g(n−k), where C(n,k) are binomial coefficients. The formula closely parallels the binomial theorem.

Proof and generalisations

The rule is proved by induction on n, using the ordinary product rule and Pascal's identity for binomial coefficients. It generalises to products of several functions, to integrals depending on a parameter (differentiation under the integral sign), and in differential algebra to derivations satisfying a Leibniz identity.
