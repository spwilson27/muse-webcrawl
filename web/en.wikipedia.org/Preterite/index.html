Preterite

The preterite or preterit (abbreviated PRET or PRT) is a grammatical past tense. In English, the preterite is formed with the verb's preterite inflection, which in turn is the form used in the simple past tense. For example, in the sentence "He walked to the store," walked is a preterite form. Not all languages express the preterite tense: some, such as Chinese, may simply use other means to indicate that an event happened before the present moment.

Preterite forms in English and Spanish conjugation.

English

In English, the preterite is normally marked by adding -ed (regular verbs: walked, jumped) or by an internal vowel change in strong verbs (sang, wrote, broke). The preterite is identical in form to the simple past and is used for events viewed as completed at a definite time in the past. English grammarians sometimes use the term preterite specifically for the morphological form, reserving simple past for the construction as a whole.

Spanish

In Spanish, the pretérito (preterite) is one of two simple past tenses in the indicative mood, the other being the imperfect. The preterite is used for actions that were completed at a specific point in the past, while the imperfect describes ongoing, habitual, or background actions. The contrast is illustrated by "Ayer comí pan" ("Yesterday I ate bread," preterite) versus "Cuando era niño, comía pan" ("When I was a child, I used to eat bread," imperfect).

German

In German, the preterite (Präteritum) is used primarily in written language and formal speech for narration, while the perfect is preferred in everyday spoken German in the south. Common verbs retain the preterite in speech regardless of region (war, hatte, konnte).
