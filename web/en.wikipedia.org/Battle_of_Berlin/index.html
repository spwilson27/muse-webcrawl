Battle of Berlin

Date16 April – 2 May 1945
LocationBerlin, Nazi Germany
ResultSoviet victory; suicide of Adolf Hitler; fall of the Third Reich

The Battle of Berlin, designated as the Berlin Strategic Offensive Operation by the Soviet Union and also known as the Fall of Berlin, was one of the last major offensives of the European theatre of World War II. Two Soviet fronts — armies totalling some 2.5 million men — attacked Berlin from the east and south after crossing the Oder and Neisse rivers, while a third front overran German forces north of the city.

The offensive began on 16 April 1945 with the Battle of the Seelow Heights, the last great defensive line before Berlin. By 25 April the city was fully encircled. Fierce street fighting followed as Soviet troops advanced toward the government district. On 30 April, as Soviet soldiers fought within blocks of the Reich Chancellery, Adolf Hitler died by suicide in his bunker; that evening Soviet troops raised the red flag over the Reichstag. The Berlin garrison surrendered on 2 May, though fighting continued elsewhere until the general German surrender of 8 May.

The cost was immense: Soviet forces suffered around 80,000 dead in the operation, German military deaths were at least comparable, and tens of thousands of civilians were killed amid extensive destruction and widespread violence against the civilian population. The battle ended the Third Reich and set the stage for the division of Berlin and Germany between the victorious Allies.
