Assyrtiko


Assyrtiko vines on Santorini are traditionally trained into ground baskets against the wind.

Assyrtiko is a white Greek wine grape variety indigenous to the island of Santorini in the Aegean Sea. It is celebrated for producing dry white wines of high acidity and pronounced minerality, and it retains its acidity even when grown in hot climates, an unusual quality that has attracted plantings in many other regions.

Viticulture

On Santorini, Assyrtiko is grown in volcanic ash soils and the vines are traditionally trained into coiled baskets close to the ground to protect the fruit from the island's fierce winds. The variety is resistant to many vine diseases and was unaffected in quality terms by the phylloxera era on the island, where many vines grow on their own roots in ash soils hostile to the louse.

Wines

Dry Assyrtiko wines are typically bone-dry, citrusy and mineral, and age well. The grape is also used in the traditional sweet wine Vinsanto of Santorini, blended with Aidini and Athiri, and in retsina-style and sparkling wines. Plantings have spread to mainland Greece (including in blends) and to Australia, South Africa and the United States.
