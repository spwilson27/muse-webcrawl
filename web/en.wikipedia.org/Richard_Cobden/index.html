Richard Cobden (1804–1865)

Richard Cobden (3 June 1804 – 2 April 1865) was an English manufacturer, Radical and Liberal statesman, and the leading campaigner for free trade in 19th-century Britain. He is best known as the co-founder, with John Bright, of the Anti-Corn Law League, whose campaign led to the repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846, and as a tireless advocate of peace, non-intervention and international commerce.

Early life

Cobden was born at Heyshott in Sussex, the son of a poor farmer. He made his fortune in Manchester as a calico printer and merchant, travelling widely in Europe and America, and published influential pamphlets arguing that British prosperity depended on open markets and peaceful engagement rather than colonies and armaments.

Anti-Corn Law League

In 1838–39 Cobden helped found the Manchester Anti-Corn Law Association, which grew into the national Anti-Corn Law League. Through mass meetings, pamphlets, electoral registration drives and brilliant oratory, the League pressed for repeal of the tariffs that kept bread prices high. Cobden entered Parliament for Stockport in 1841. In 1846, amid the Irish famine, Prime Minister Sir Robert Peel repealed the Corn Laws — a triumph Cobden was widely credited with engineering, and which split the Conservative Party.

Later career

Cobden opposed the Crimean War and Britain's war with China, losing his seat in 1857 for his unpopular stand, but he returned to Parliament for Rochdale in 1859. In 1860 he negotiated the Cobden–Chevalier Treaty with France, a landmark free-trade agreement that cut tariffs between the two countries and included a most-favoured-nation clause that helped spread tariff reductions across Europe. He declined cabinet office and died in London in 1865; he is buried at West Lavington in Sussex near his birthplace.

Legacy

"Cobdenism" became shorthand for the belief that free trade, peace and retrenchment in public spending go together. His name is commemorated in statues in Manchester and elsewhere and in the Cobden Club, founded in his honour.
