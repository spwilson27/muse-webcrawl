Shanto Iyengar


Occupation: Political scientist
Institution: Stanford University
Fields: Political communication, media effects, affective polarisation

Shanto Iyengar is an American political scientist and professor at Stanford University, where he holds the Harry & Norman Chandler Professorship in Communication and is a professor of political science. He directs the Political Communication Laboratory at Stanford and is known for his experimental research on the effects of mass media on political attitudes and behaviour.

Research

Iyengar's early work, including the book News That Matters (with Donald Kinder), examined how television news framing and agenda-setting shape what citizens think about politics. His book Is Anyone Responsible? studied how television framing of poverty and crime influences attributions of responsibility. Later research has focused on political polarisation: with co-authors he documented rising "affective polarisation" — growing dislike and distrust between partisans — in the United States and other democracies, and investigated the role of media choice and partisan news in deepening social division.

Career

He previously taught at UCLA and Stony Brook University before joining Stanford. He has received awards from the American Political Science Association and is a fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.
