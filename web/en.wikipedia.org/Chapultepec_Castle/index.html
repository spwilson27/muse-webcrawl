Chapultepec Castle

LocationChapultepec, Mexico City, Mexico
Construction1785–1864
Current useNational Museum of History
Elevation2,325 m (7,628 ft) above sea level

Chapultepec Castle (Spanish: Castillo de Chapultepec) is located on top of Chapultepec Hill in Mexico City's Chapultepec park. The name Chapultepec is the Nahuatl word Chapoltepēc, which means "at the grasshopper's hill." The castle stands 2,325 metres (7,628 ft) above sea level. The building has been used for several purposes during its history, including as a military academy, an imperial and presidential residence, an observatory, and since 1939 as the National Museum of History (Museo Nacional de Historia).

Chapultepec Castle is the only royal castle in the Americas to have served as the residence of a sovereign: Emperor Maximilian I of Mexico, who lived there with his wife, Empress Carlota, during the Second Mexican Empire.

History

Construction began in 1785 under Viceroy Bernardo de Gálvez as a summer house for the viceroy of New Spain, on a site sacred to the Aztecs and associated with the tlatoani Moctezuma. After independence the building fell into neglect before becoming home to the Military Academy in 1841.

During the Mexican–American War, the castle was stormed by United States forces at the Battle of Chapultepec on 13 September 1847. Mexican tradition commemorates six young cadets, the Niños Héroes ("Boy Heroes"), who died defending the castle, one of them, Juan Escutia, said to have wrapped himself in the Mexican flag and leapt from the walls.

Imperial and presidential residence

Emperor Maximilian and Empress Carlota remodelled the castle in the 1860s in neoclassical style, adding gardens, a rooftop terrace, and the boulevard now known as Paseo de la Reforma to connect it with the city centre. After the restoration of the republic, presidents including Porfirio Díaz used it as a residence; it remained the official presidential home until 1939, when Lázaro Cárdenas moved the residence to Los Pinos and converted the castle into a museum.

Museum

The National Museum of History displays artefacts from the Spanish conquest to the Mexican Revolution, including Maximilian's carriage, murals by Juan O'Gorman, José Clemente Orozco, and David Alfaro Siqueiros, and the mural depicting the Niños Héroes by Gabriel Flores. The castle's gardens and views over Chapultepec Park and Paseo de la Reforma make it one of Mexico City's most visited landmarks.
