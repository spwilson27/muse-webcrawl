Onam sadya

The Onam sadya (also spelled sadhya) is the grand vegetarian feast served during Onam, the harvest festival of Kerala in southern India. It is one of the largest traditional banquets in the world: a multi-course meal of typically 24 to 28 dishes — and sometimes more — served on a banana leaf and eaten by hand, without cutlery. The sadya is the festive peak of Onam, celebrating the legendary homecoming of King Mahabali.

Serving

Diners sit cross-legged on mats before a full banana leaf, with dishes placed in a fixed order: salt, banana chips and sharkara upperi (jaggery-coated banana chips) on the left tip, then pickles, injipuli (ginger-tamarind), kichadi, pachadi, olan, avial, thoran, erissery, kalan and koottu curry arranged clockwise, with rice in the centre. Sambar, rasam and spiced buttermilk (moru) follow in courses, and the meal closes with payasam — often several kinds, such as palada pradhaman and parippu payasam — sometimes eaten mixed with a small banana and pappadam.

Rules and traditions

Tradition dictates the order of serving, the position of each item on the leaf, and that the leaf be folded in a particular direction at the end (toward oneself to signify satisfaction). Grand community sadyas are served to thousands of people at temples, churches and public halls during Onam; the Aranmula Valla Sadya, served to oarsmen of snake boats, is one of the largest, with over sixty dishes. Every dish is vegetarian, and coconut, curry leaves and coconut oil define the flavour profile.
