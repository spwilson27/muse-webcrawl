Liberation of France

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Parisians celebrate the liberation of their city in August 1944.

The liberation of France (French: Libération) in the Second World War was accomplished through diplomacy, politics and the combined military efforts of the Western Allies, Free French forces and the French Resistance, ending the four-year period of German occupation and the rule of the Vichy regime. It began on 6 June 1944 with the Allied invasion of Normandy (D-Day) and was largely complete by the end of the year.

Background

After the armistice of June 1940, northern and western France were occupied by Germany, while Marshal Philippe Pétain's Vichy government ruled the nominally free southern zone until Germany occupied it too in November 1942. General Charles de Gaulle's Free France, based in London and later Algiers, organized French forces abroad, while resistance movements inside France sabotaged the occupier and gathered intelligence.

The landings and the breakout

On 6 June 1944, Allied forces landed in Normandy in Operation Overlord, the largest seaborne invasion in history. After weeks of fighting in the bocage, the Allied breakout (Operation Cobra) and the encirclement of German forces in the Falaise pocket opened the road to Paris. On 15 August, a second landing, Operation Dragoon, took place on the Mediterranean coast, with French forces playing a major role and rapidly liberating the south.

Liberation of Paris

On 19 August 1944, the French Forces of the Interior launched an uprising in Paris; on 25 August the city was liberated as General Leclerc's French 2nd Armored Division and the U.S. 4th Infantry Division entered, and the German commander surrendered. The next day de Gaulle marched down the Champs-Élysées to scenes of jubilation.

Completion and aftermath

Allied armies swept east and north, liberating most of France by September, though pockets such as Strasbourg (liberated in November) and the Atlantic ports held out longer. De Gaulle's Provisional Government took power, collaborators were tried during the épuration, and French forces joined the final invasion of Germany. The liberation restored the French Republic and France's status as a major power, with a seat among the victorious Allies.
