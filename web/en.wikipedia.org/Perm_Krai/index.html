Perm Krai (Russian: Пермский край) is a federal subject of Russia (a krai) located in the east of the European part of the country, in the Volga Federal District. Its administrative centre is the city of Perm. Perm Krai was formed on 1 December 2005 as a result of the merger of Perm Oblast and Komi-Permyak Autonomous Okrug, approved by referendum in 2004 — the first such merger of Russian federal subjects in the post-Soviet period.

Geography

The krai covers 160,236 km² on the western slopes of the Middle and Northern Urals and the adjoining plain. The Kama River, a major tributary of the Volga, flows through the region. About 70 per cent of the territory is forested taiga. The climate is temperate continental with long cold winters.

Economy and culture

Perm Krai is rich in oil, potash and magnesium salts (around Berezniki and Solikamsk, among the world's largest potash deposits), coal and diamonds. Its capital, Perm, is an industrial city and a cultural centre known for the Perm Opera and Ballet Theatre and for contemporary art. The geological Permian period is named after the Perm region, where strata of that age were identified by Roderick Murchison in 1841.
