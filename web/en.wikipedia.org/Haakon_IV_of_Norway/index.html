Haakon IV of Norway

Haakon IV Haakonsson (c. March/April 1204 – 16 December 1263), sometimes called Haakon the Old in contrast to his namesake son, was King of Norway from 1217 to 1263. His reign lasted for 46 years, longer than any Norwegian king since Harald Fairhair. Haakon was born into the troubled period known as the Bagler Wars, a period of civil war between rival claimants to the Norwegian throne, which had begun in 1184.

Early life

Haakon was the posthumous son of King Haakon III, of the Birkebeiner faction. His mother, Inga of Varteig, had to flee with the infant from Lillehammer to Trondheim in the winter of 1205–06, carried by two Birkebeiner skiers — an episode remembered as the origin of the Birkebeinerrennet ski race. After the death of King Inge II in 1217, Haakon was elected king, defeating the rival claim of Inge's half-brother Skule Bårdsson in an ordeal of hot iron undergone by his mother to prove his paternity.

Reign

Skule Bårdsson, who had been made earl and effective co-ruler, rebelled in 1239 and proclaimed himself king, but was defeated and killed in 1240, ending the era of civil wars in Norway. Haakon was crowned in Bergen in 1247. His reign brought Norway to a cultural and territorial peak: he consolidated royal authority, issued laws, and maintained a court notable for translating European chivalric literature into Old Norse.

Haakon extended Norwegian influence abroad. Greenland accepted Norwegian overlordship in 1261, and Iceland followed in 1262–64. His attempt to maintain Norwegian control of the Hebrides and the Isle of Man, however, led to conflict with Scotland. In 1263 Haakon led a large fleet to Scotland; after the inconclusive Battle of Largs he withdrew to Orkney, where he fell ill and died at Kirkwall on 16 December 1263.

Legacy

Haakon was succeeded by his son Magnus VI, under whom Norway ceded the Hebrides and Man to Scotland by the Treaty of Perth in 1266. Haakon's saga, Hákonar saga Hákonarsonar, written by the Icelandic historian Sturla Þórðarson, is one of the principal sources for his reign.
