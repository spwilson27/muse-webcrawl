Tendon (Japanese cuisine)

Tendon (天丼) is a Japanese rice bowl dish (donburi) consisting of a bowl of steamed white rice topped with tempura — battered and deep-fried seafood and vegetables — dressed with a sweet-savory soy-based sauce called tare or tentsuyu. The name is a contraction of tempura and donburi.

Composition

A typical tendon includes tempura shrimp (often two), white fish, squid, and vegetables such as sweet potato, pumpkin, aubergine (eggplant), green beans, shiitake mushroom and shiso leaf, arranged over rice and drizzled with sauce. More elaborate versions may add kakiage (mixed vegetable and seafood fritters) or anago (conger eel). The dish originated in the Edo period in Tokyo (then Edo), where tempura stalls and soba shops popularised tempura over rice as an inexpensive, filling meal.

Variations

Regional and modern variations include salt-seasoned tendon without sauce, spicy versions, and seasonal specialities. Tendon is commonly served with miso soup and pickles, and is a staple of tempura restaurants and casual dining chains across Japan.
