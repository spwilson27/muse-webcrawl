Irish mythology

Irish mythology is the body of myths native to the island of Ireland. It was originally passed down orally, and most was written down by Christian monks during the Irish medieval era. Irish mythology is the best-preserved branch of Celtic mythology.

The cycles

The tales are traditionally grouped into four cycles. The Mythological Cycle tells of the successive invasions of Ireland and the gods, the Tuatha Dé Danann. The Ulster Cycle centres on the hero Cú Chulainn and the court of Conchobar mac Nessa, and includes the epic Táin Bó Cúailnge (The Cattle Raid of Cooley). The Fenian Cycle follows Fionn mac Cumhaill and his band of warriors, the Fianna. The Historical Cycle, or Cycle of the Kings, recounts the deeds of legendary and semi-historical Irish kings.

Themes and sources

Recurring figures include the goddess Mórrígan, the hero-god Lugh, and the Otherworld (Tír na nÓg), reached across the sea or through burial mounds. Major manuscript sources include the Lebor Gabála Érenn (Book of Invasions) and the Book of Leinster.
