Pecan pie


Pecan pie, a staple of Southern US cuisine.

Pecan pie is a pie of pecan nuts mixed with a filling of eggs, butter, and sugar (typically corn syrup). It is popularly served at holiday meals in the United States and is considered a specialty of Southern U.S. cuisine.

Origins

Pecans are native to North America and were used by Native American peoples long before European settlement. The pie in its modern form is generally credited to the Karo syrup company: the wife of a Karo sales manager is said to have created the recipe, which the company printed on its cans in the 1930s and promoted heavily. French settlers in Louisiana, who knew pecans through Native trade and made pies with them, are an earlier influence, though their versions used molasses or maple syrup rather than corn syrup.

Variations

Variations include bourbon or whiskey in the filling, chocolate (making a chocolate pecan pie), and maple syrup in place of corn syrup. Pecan pie is named in the Texas state song tradition and is the official state pie of Texas; in 2013 it was also designated the state pie of Oklahoma.
