Christoff Rudolff

Born: 1499, Jauer, Silesia

Died: before 1543

Field: Mathematics

Christoff Rudolff (1499–before 1543) was a German mathematician best known for introducing the radical sign √ for square roots in his 1525 algebra book Die Coss.

Life and work

Rudolff studied and taught in Vienna, where he was influenced by scholars at the university and wrote for students learning practical and theoretical arithmetic. His work belonged to the German Cossist tradition, named for cosa, the unknown quantity in algebra.

Die Coss

Published in Strasbourg, Die Coss presented rules for equations, radicals, progressions, and commercial problems in German rather than Latin. Its compact radical notation eventually displaced more cumbersome verbal descriptions, although modern notation developed over subsequent centuries.

Other writings

Rudolff also produced books on arithmetic and measurement. His combination of clear notation and worked examples made advanced algebra more accessible to German-speaking readers.
