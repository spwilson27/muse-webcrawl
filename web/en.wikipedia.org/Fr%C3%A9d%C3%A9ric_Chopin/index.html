Frédéric Chopin

Frédéric François Chopin (born Fryderyk Franciszek Chopin; 1 March 1810 – 17 October 1849) was a Polish composer and virtuoso pianist of the Romantic period who wrote primarily for solo piano.

Born1 March 1810, Żelazowa Wola, Duchy of Warsaw
Died17 October 1849 (aged 39), Paris, France
OccupationComposer, pianist
EraRomantic

Life

A child prodigy born near Warsaw to a Polish mother and a French father, Chopin gave his first concert at age eight and published music in his teens. He left Poland in 1830, just before the November Uprising, and settled in Paris, where he built a reputation as a teacher and salon performer rather than a public virtuoso. His long relationship with the writer George Sand lasted from 1838 to 1847. He died in Paris in 1849, probably of tuberculosis.

Music

Chopin's works include 27 études, 25 preludes, 21 nocturnes, ballades, scherzos, mazurkas, waltzes, polonaises, two piano concertos, and three sonatas. His music combines singing bel canto melody with innovative harmony and a uniquely pianistic idiom that profoundly influenced later composers.
