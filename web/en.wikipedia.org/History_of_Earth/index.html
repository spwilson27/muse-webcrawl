The history of Earth spans roughly 4.54 billion years, from the planet's accretion out of the solar nebula to the present day. Geologists divide this span into eons, eras, periods and epochs based on the rock record and the fossil history of life.

Formation and Hadean eon

Earth formed by accretion about 4.54 billion years ago. A Mars-sized body, Theia, collided with the early Earth, ejecting material that formed the Moon. The Hadean eon saw a molten surface, heavy bombardment, the first crust, oceans and atmosphere.

Archean and Proterozoic

The oldest evidence of life dates to roughly 3.8–4.3 billion years ago. Photosynthetic cyanobacteria oxygenated the atmosphere in the Great Oxidation Event (~2.4 billion years ago), enabling aerobic life and an ozone layer. Eukaryotic cells arose, followed much later by multicellular organisms; the Proterozoic also saw severe "Snowball Earth" glaciations.

Phanerozoic

The current eon, beginning 541 million years ago with the Cambrian explosion, saw the diversification of complex life: fish, land plants, amphibians, reptiles, dinosaurs, mammals and finally humans in the last few hundred thousand years. Five mass extinctions punctuate the record, including the end-Permian event and the asteroid impact that ended the non-avian dinosaurs 66 million years ago.
