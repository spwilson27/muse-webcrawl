Islamic Courts Union

Activec. 2000–2006 (as a governing movement)
HeadquartersMogadishu, Somalia
Key leadersSharif Sheikh Ahmed, Hassan Dahir Aweys
Successor elementsAl-Shabaab and other factions

The Islamic Courts Union (ICU; Somali: Midowga Maxkamadaha Islaamiga) was a legal and political movement of sharia courts in Somalia that in 2006 took control of Mogadishu and much of southern Somalia, before being driven from power by an Ethiopian military intervention in December 2006.

Origins

After the collapse of the Somali state in 1991, clan-based sharia courts emerged in Mogadishu to provide policing and dispute resolution amid anarchy. In 2000 several courts formed a combined council, and by 2004 the union of courts had become a significant armed and administrative force, funded partly by local businesses tired of warlord checkpoints and insecurity.

Rule in 2006

In mid-2006 the ICU defeated a CIA-backed alliance of warlords, capturing Mogadishu in June and then much of the south without heavy resistance. It reopened Mogadishu's seaport and international airport, removed militia checkpoints and restored a measure of order welcomed by many residents. Its leadership combined moderates under Sharif Sheikh Ahmed with hardliners such as Hassan Dahir Aweys; its youth militia, al-Shabaab, was growing more radical.

Downfall

Viewing the ICU as a terrorist threat, Ethiopia — backing the internationally recognised Transitional Federal Government — invaded in December 2006. The courts' forces collapsed within weeks and Mogadishu fell. ICU leaders fled or went into exile; an insurgency followed, and al-Shabaab emerged as its most powerful element, while Sharif Sheikh Ahmed later became president of Somalia in 2009 through a peace process.
