Spiritual gift

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Holy Spirit, depicted as a dove, source of spiritual gifts in Christian theology

A spiritual gift or charism (plural charisms or charismata; Greek χάρισμα charisma, "gift of grace") is a power or ability attributed to the Holy Spirit and given to Christians for the benefit of the church. The principal biblical passages listing such gifts are Romans 12, 1 Corinthians 12, Ephesians 4 and 1 Peter 4.

Lists of gifts

The gifts mentioned in the New Testament include prophecy, teaching, exhortation, giving, leadership, mercy, words of wisdom and knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, discernment of spirits, speaking in tongues and interpretation of tongues, as well as ministries such as apostles, prophets, evangelists and pastors.

Theological views

Christians differ over whether the more miraculous gifts continue today. Pentecostal and charismatic traditions are "continuationist", holding that all the gifts remain available; many other traditions are "cessationist", holding that certain gifts ceased with the apostolic age. Catholic theology also recognises charisms given to the faithful for the common good, affirmed by the Second Vatican Council.
