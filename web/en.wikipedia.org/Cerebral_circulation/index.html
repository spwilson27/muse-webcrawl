Cerebral circulation

Cerebral circulation is the movement of blood through the network of cerebral arteries and veins supplying the brain. The brain requires a continuous supply of oxygen and glucose, receiving roughly 15% of cardiac output despite being about 2% of body weight.

Anatomy

Blood reaches the brain via the internal carotid and vertebral arteries, which connect at the base of the brain in the circle of Willis, an anastomotic ring providing collateral circulation. The anterior, middle, and posterior cerebral arteries supply respective territories. Venous blood drains into dural venous sinuses and then the internal jugular veins.

Regulation and pathology

Cerebral blood flow is autoregulated to remain relatively constant across a range of blood pressures. Interruption causes ischaemic stroke, while vessel rupture causes haemorrhagic stroke. The blood–brain barrier tightly regulates exchange between blood and brain tissue.
