Sail plan

A sail plan is the set of sails and rigging arrangement carried by a sailing vessel — in effect, the vessel's propulsion configuration. The term also denotes the drawing or diagram showing the arrangement of masts, sails and rigging.

Common sail plans

Fore-and-aft rigs include the sloop (one mast, mainsail and jib), cutter, ketch and yawl (two masts), and the schooner. Square-rigged plans include the brig, brigantine, barque and the full-rigged ship, in which all masts carry square sails. Cat rigs dispense with headsails entirely.

Design considerations

The sail plan determines a vessel's handling, speed and crew requirements. Naval architects balance sail area against stability, since the heeling force of the sails must be countered by the righting moment of the hull. Traditional plans evolved regionally with materials, trade routes and the size of available crews.
