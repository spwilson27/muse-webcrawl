Lever escapement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The lever escapement, the standard escapement in most modern mechanical watches.

The lever escapement, invented by the English clockmaker Thomas Mudge in around 1755, is a type of escapement used in almost all mechanical watches, some small clocks, and (modified) in marine chronometers. It is named for the lever, a component that pivots to alternately lock and unlock the escape wheel, and that transmits impulses to the balance wheel, which controls the rate of the timepiece.

History

Mudge invented the lever escapement around 1755 while working in London; his earliest known example was made for a watch presented to King Ferdinand VI of Spain. The design was slow to be adopted because of the difficulty of making its delicate parts, but during the 19th century it was refined—notably into the form now called the Swiss lever escapement—and became dominant in watchmaking. It displaced earlier escapements such as the verge and the cylinder in most watches because it is detached: the balance wheel is free of contact with the gear train for most of its oscillation, improving accuracy.

Operation

The escapement consists of an escape wheel with specially shaped club teeth, a lever carrying two jewelled pallets (entry and exit) and a forked end, and a roller on the balance staff carrying an impulse jewel. As the balance wheel swings, the jewel enters the fork of the lever, throwing it to the other side. This unlocks the escape wheel, which advances one tooth; a tooth lands on the other pallet and locks the wheel again. During the motion, the geometry of the pallets gives a small impulse to the balance wheel, replacing energy lost to friction and air resistance. Each full swing of the balance thus allows the escape wheel to advance by a fixed amount.

Advantages and use

The lever escapement is reliable, robust enough for wristwatches, and largely self-starting. Variants are used in virtually all modern mechanical watch movements, typically beating at frequencies between 18,000 and 36,000 vibrations per hour.
