Boischatel is a municipality in the Capitale-Nationale region of Quebec, Canada. Located at the mouth of the Montmorency River, just east of Quebec City, it is best known as the site of Montmorency Falls (Chute Montmorency), which are higher than Niagara Falls and draw many visitors each year.

Geography

Boischatel lies on the north shore of the St. Lawrence River opposite Île d'Orléans. Its terrain rises quickly from the river shoreline to the Laurentian foothills, and the Montmorency River forms its western boundary.

History

The area was settled in the 17th century under the Seigneurie de Beaupré. The municipality took its name from Jean-François de Galaup, comte de La Pérouse era associations with the Bois family name; its development was tied to the falls' use for hydroelectric power and industry from the 19th century onward. A cable car and park now serve the falls site.

Demographics and economy

Growth in recent decades has made Boischatel a residential suburb of Quebec City, with a population of roughly 8,000. Tourism around Montmorency Falls and proximity to the Beaupré coast ski areas shape its local economy.
