Roman Holiday is a 1953 American romantic comedy film directed and produced by William Wyler. It stars Audrey Hepburn as Princess Ann, a European princess who, while on a tightly scheduled tour of Rome, slips away from her official party and explores the city incognito, and Gregory Peck as Joe Bradley, an American reporter who recognises her and hopes for an exclusive story.

Plot

Overwhelmed by her regimented itinerary, Princess Ann rebels one night in Rome and, sedated by a doctor's injection, falls asleep on a park bench, where Bradley finds her. When he realises her identity, he plans to sell an exclusive interview, but over a day spent visiting the sights of Rome — including a ride on a Vespa scooter, a visit to the Mouth of Truth, and a dance on a barge on the Tiber — the two fall in love. In the end, Bradley and his photographer friend Irving suppress the story, and Ann returns to her duties, bidding Bradley a restrained farewell at a press reception.

Production

The film was shot entirely on location in Rome, unusual for a Hollywood production of the time. The screenplay was written by Ian McLellan Hunter and John Dighton from a story by Dalton Trumbo, who, being blacklisted, was not credited until decades later; his name was restored to the credits in 2011. Hepburn, in her first major film role, won the Academy Award for Best Actress. The film also won Oscars for Best Story and Best Costume Design (Edith Head), and was a major commercial success.

Legacy

Roman Holiday launched Hepburn to international stardom and popularised the Vespa scooter and Italian locations with American audiences. In 1999 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically and aesthetically significant.
