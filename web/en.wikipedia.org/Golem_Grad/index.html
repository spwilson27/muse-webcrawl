Golem Grad (Macedonian: Голем Град, "Big Town") is an island in Lake Prespa, in the Resen Municipality of North Macedonia, near the tripoint with Greece and Albania. Only about 600 metres long, it is nonetheless one of the most archaeologically and biologically notable islands in the Balkans, and is popularly known as Snake Island for its large population of dice snakes and vipers.

History

The island's plateau preserves remains from Neolithic settlement through the Roman era — including a Roman cistern and acropolis — to the medieval period, when it was a centre of monastic life. Several medieval churches survive in whole or in part, among them the church of St Peter (14th century) with notable frescoes, and the ruins of the church of St Demetrius. The island was largely abandoned after the medieval period and had no permanent population in modern times.

Nature

Golem Grad is protected as a strict nature reserve within the Lake Prespa ecosystem. Its ancient juniper and wild fig stands, steep cliffs and isolation shelter colonies of Dalmatian pelicans and pygmy cormorants nearby, alongside thousands of harmless dice snakes and a population of nose-horned vipers — protected from disturbance by the island's restricted access, visitable by boat from the village of Konjsko with a permit and guide.
