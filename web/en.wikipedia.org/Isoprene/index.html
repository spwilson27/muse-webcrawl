Isoprene (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene) is a colourless volatile liquid hydrocarbon of formula C5H8. It is produced by many plants and animals and is the fundamental building block of the terpenes: its activated derivatives, isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP) and dimethylallyl pyrophosphate (DMAPP), combine head-to-tail to form terpenoids, steroids, carotenoids and the polyisoprene chain of natural rubber.

Natural occurrence and atmosphere

Vegetation emits enormous quantities of isoprene — on the order of 500 million tonnes of carbon per year — making it the most abundant non-methane hydrocarbon released to the atmosphere, principally by oak and poplar trees. In the atmosphere it reacts with hydroxyl radicals and ozone, contributing to aerosol formation and affecting the oxidising capacity of air. Its emission by plants is thought to protect leaves against heat stress and reactive oxygen species.

Industrial production and uses

Industrially, isoprene is recovered from the C5 fraction of naphtha cracking and is polymerised to polyisoprene, a synthetic analogue of natural rubber used in tyres and medical goods. It is also used to make other elastomers and fine chemicals. In the human body, isoprene is a minor end product of cholesterol synthesis and is detectable in exhaled breath.
