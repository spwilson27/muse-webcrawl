Latte

Type: Coffee drink
Origin: Italy
Ingredients: Espresso, steamed milk, milk foam

A latte, or caffè latte, is a coffee drink made with espresso and steamed milk, typically topped with a small layer of milk foam. The term comes from the Italian caffè e latte, "coffee and milk."

Preparation

A standard latte combines one or more shots of espresso with steamed milk in roughly a 1:3 to 1:5 ratio, finished with about a centimetre of microfoam. Compared with a cappuccino it contains more steamed milk and less foam. Baristas often pour latte art — rosettas and hearts — into the foam. Variations include the flat white (thinner milk, common in Australia and New Zealand) and flavoured lattes with syrups; the chai latte substitutes spiced tea for espresso.

History

Milk coffee drinks are centuries old in Europe; the modern café latte was popularised in the United States, where Berkeley's Caffe Mediterraneum is sometimes credited with an early version, and chains such as Starbucks spread it worldwide from the 1980s.
