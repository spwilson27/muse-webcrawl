Copper Canyon

Copper Canyon (Spanish: Barrancas del Cobre) is a group of six distinct canyons in the Sierra Madre Occidental in the southwestern part of the state of Chihuahua, in northwestern Mexico, that is larger and in places deeper than the Grand Canyon in Arizona.

Geography

The six main canyons — Urique, Sinforosa, Batopilas, Candameña, Chinipas, and Oteros — were formed by rivers running into the Rio Fuerte, which flows west toward the Gulf of California. The overall canyon system covers roughly 25,000 square miles (65,000 km²). Urique Canyon, the deepest, reaches about 6,136 feet (1,870 m). The region's copper-green lichen-covered canyon walls gave the system its name.

People

The canyons are the ancestral home of the Rarámuri (Tarahumara) people, renowned for long-distance running in the rugged terrain. Settlements such as Creel, Batopilas (a former silver-mining town founded in the early 1700s), and Urique are located in the region.

Tourism and transport

The Chihuahua al Pacífico Railway, known as the El Chepe train, traverses the canyon region between Chihuahua City and Los Mochis, crossing dramatic bridges and tunnels, and is one of Mexico's most famous rail journeys. The Barrancas del Cobre Adventure Park near Divisadero offers a cable car and zip lines over the canyon confluence.
