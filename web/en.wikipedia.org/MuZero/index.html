MuZero

DeveloperGoogle DeepMind
Released2019 (announced); 2020 (paper)
TypeReinforcement learning algorithm
PredecessorAlphaZero

MuZero is a reinforcement learning algorithm developed by Google DeepMind. Announced in 2019 and described in a 2020 paper in Nature, MuZero masters games such as chess, shogi, Go and Atari video games without being given the rules of the environment, instead learning a model of the environment from interaction alone.

Overview

Like its predecessor AlphaZero, MuZero plans using Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS). The key difference is that AlphaZero had access to a perfect simulator of the game rules, whereas MuZero learns a representation of the environment, a dynamics function that predicts future latent states, and a prediction function that outputs policy and value estimates. Because the learned model focuses on the aspects of the environment relevant to planning, MuZero can be applied to domains where the rules are unknown or hard to simulate, such as visually complex Atari games.

Performance

MuZero matched AlphaZero's performance on Go, chess and shogi, and achieved state-of-the-art results on the Atari 57 benchmark suite of classic video games. It has also been applied to video compression problems.
