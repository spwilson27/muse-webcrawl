Malta, officially the Republic of Malta, is an island country in Southern Europe consisting of an archipelago in the Mediterranean Sea between Sicily and North Africa. Its capital is Valletta, the smallest national capital in the European Union by area. Malta has been inhabited since around 5900 BC and successively ruled by Phoenicians, Romans, Arabs, Normans, the Knights Hospitaller, France and Britain.

History

The Knights of St. John ruled Malta from 1530 to 1798 and famously withstood the Great Siege of 1565 by the Ottoman Empire. British rule followed from 1800; Malta was heavily bombed during the Second World War and the island was awarded the George Cross in 1942. It became independent in 1964 and a republic in 1974, joining the European Union in 2004.

Geography

The main islands are Malta, Gozo and Comino. The country has no rivers or lakes and a hot-summer Mediterranean climate. Its strategic location has made it a naval base and trading crossroads throughout history.

Economy and culture

Tourism, financial services, iGaming and maritime registration are major industries. Maltese, a Semitic language written in the Latin script, is the only Semitic language that is an official language of the EU. The megalithic temples of Malta, among the world's oldest free-standing structures, are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
