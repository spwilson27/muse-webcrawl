Joseph Priestley

Joseph Priestley
Born: 24 March 1733, Birstall, Yorkshire
Died: 6 February 1804, Northumberland, Pennsylvania
Known for: Discovery of oxygen (as "dephlogisticated air")

Joseph Priestley (24 March 1733 – 6 February 1804) was an English chemist, natural philosopher, separatist theologian, grammarian and political theorist who published over 150 works. He is credited with the discovery of oxygen, having isolated it in its gaseous state.

Science

Priestley isolated and described several gases, including nitrous oxide, ammonia, hydrogen chloride and oxygen (1774), invented soda water, and wrote influential works on electricity. He defended the phlogiston theory against Lavoisier's new chemistry.

Theology and politics

A dissenting minister and Unitarian, Priestley's support for the French Revolution made him a target: a mob burned his Birmingham home and laboratory in the Priestley Riots of 1791. He emigrated to Pennsylvania in 1794.

Legacy

The American Chemical Society's Priestley Medal is named for him, and he is remembered as a founder of Unitarianism in England and a pioneer of pneumatic chemistry.
