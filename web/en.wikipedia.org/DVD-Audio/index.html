DVD-Audio


Illustration: DVD-Audio

DVD-Audio (commonly abbreviated as DVD-A) is a digital format for delivering high-fidelity audio content on a DVD. Introduced in 2000, it was developed by Panasonic and other members of the DVD Forum as the successor to the compact disc for audiophile applications.

Specifications

DVD-Audio supports linear PCM audio at sampling rates up to 192 kHz and bit depths up to 24 bits, in configurations from mono to 5.1-channel surround sound. It uses Meridian Lossless Packing (MLP) to fit high-resolution multichannel audio on a disc. Discs may also carry still images and menus, and many include a Dolby Digital or DTS track for compatibility with ordinary DVD players.

Format war

DVD-Audio competed with Sony and Philips' Super Audio CD (SACD) in a format war that neither side decisively won; both formats remained niche as downloads and streaming took over. Compare Advanced Audio Coding and Dolby Stereo.
