Creepshow

Directed by: George A. Romero
Written by: Stephen King
Released: 1982
Genre: Horror anthology

Creepshow is a 1982 American horror anthology film directed by George A. Romero and written by Stephen King, in his screenwriting debut. Modelled on the EC horror comics of the 1950s such as Tales from the Crypt, it presents five short tales framed by a story about a boy whose father throws his horror comic in the trash; King himself appears as an actor in one segment.

Segments

The five tales are "Father's Day", about a murdered patriarch returning from the grave for his cake; "The Lonesome Death of Jordy Verrill", in which a farmer (King) is consumed by an alien plant growth; "Something to Tide You Over", a revenge tale of drowning victims returning for vengeance; "The Crate", about a murderous ape-like creature found in a university crate; and "They're Creeping Up on You!", in which a germophobic executive is overwhelmed by cockroaches.

Production and legacy

Tom Savini created the film's practical makeup effects, and the cast included Hal Holbrook, Adrienne Barbeau, Leslie Nielsen, Ed Harris, and Ted Danson. Shot around Pittsburgh, the film was a commercial success, spawning Creepshow 2 (1987), an unofficial Creepshow 3 (2006), and a well-received television series on Shudder beginning in 2019 under Greg Nicotero. Bernie Wrightson illustrated the official comic adaptation.
