Ventricular system

The ventricular system is a set of four interconnected cavities in the brain filled with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). It consists of the paired lateral ventricles within the cerebral hemispheres, the midline third ventricle in the diencephalon, and the fourth ventricle between the pons, medulla, and cerebellum. The system is continuous with the central canal of the spinal cord.

Flow of cerebrospinal fluid

CSF is produced mainly by the choroid plexus within the ventricles. It flows from the lateral ventricles through the interventricular foramina (of Monro) into the third ventricle, then through the cerebral aqueduct (of Sylvius) into the fourth ventricle, and out through the median and lateral apertures into the subarachnoid space, where it cushions the brain before being reabsorbed into the venous system.

Development and clinical significance

The ventricles develop from the hollow neural tube of the embryo. Blockage of CSF flow or absorption causes hydrocephalus, an accumulation of fluid that raises intracranial pressure, treated by shunting or endoscopic surgery. Enlargement of the ventricles is also observed in some neurodegenerative and psychiatric conditions.
