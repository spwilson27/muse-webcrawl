Meat pie

Meat pie
TypeSavoury pie
Main ingredientsPastry shell, meat and gravy filling
Regional formsAustralian meat pie, steak pie (Britain), tourtière (Quebec), empanada

A meat pie is a pie with a filling of meat and often other savoury ingredients, enclosed in pastry and baked. Meat pies are found in many cuisines around the world, from the individual hand-held pies of Australia and New Zealand to the steak and kidney pies of Britain and the tourtière of French Canada.

History

Pastry cases originally served as a durable container for preserving and transporting cooked meat; the early crusts were often not eaten. By the medieval period in Europe, pies with spiced meat fillings were festive dishes, and the form spread with European settlement to the Americas, southern Africa and Australasia.

Regional varieties

In Australia and New Zealand the meat pie — minced beef in gravy in a shortcrust base with a puff pastry lid, often eaten with tomato sauce — is an iconic snack sold at bakeries, service stations and sporting grounds, where it is traditionally eaten at Australian rules football and rugby matches. British varieties include steak and ale, steak and kidney, and the cold raised pork pie of Melton Mowbray. In North America, related dishes include the pot pie and the Quebec tourtière, a spiced minced-meat pie associated with Christmas and New Year.
