Făgăraș Mountains

Elevation2,544 m (8,346 ft)
CountryRomania
Parent rangeSouthern Carpathians

The Făgăraș Mountains (Romanian: Munții Făgăraș) are the highest mountains of the Southern Carpathians and of Romania. The range lies in the centre of the country, in Transylvania, and contains Moldoveanu Peak (2,544 m), the nation's highest summit, as well as Negoiu (2,535 m) and Viștea Mare (2,527 m).

Geography and features

The massif stretches about 70 km east–west and is characterised by a long, sharp main ridge, glacial lakes such as Bâlea Lake, and deep U-shaped valleys. The Transfăgărășan highway, built in the 1970s, crosses the range via a tunnel near Bâlea and is famed as one of Europe's most spectacular mountain roads.

Tourism

The Făgăraș are a major destination for hiking and mountaineering, with a demanding multi-day ridge traverse. The range's alpine terrain, chamois and bear populations, and relative wildness make it a centrepiece of Romanian outdoor tourism.
