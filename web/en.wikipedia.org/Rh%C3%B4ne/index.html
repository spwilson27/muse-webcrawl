Rhône

SourceRhône Glacier, Switzerland

The Rhône is a major river of Europe, 813 kilometres long, rising in the Alps of Switzerland and flowing through Lake Geneva before crossing southeastern France to the Mediterranean Sea near Arles. It is one of the great historic waterways of the continent, used for trade since antiquity.

Course

The river begins at the Rhône Glacier in the canton of Valais, flows southwest through the Valais valley to Lake Geneva, exits at Geneva, then turns south through France. At Lyon it is joined by the Saône, its principal tributary, and flows past Vienne, Valence, Avignon and Arles, where it splits into the Grand Rhône and Petit Rhône, forming the Camargue delta.

Navigation and power

The French section was extensively developed in the 20th century by the Compagnie Nationale du Rhône with a series of dams providing hydroelectricity, irrigation and navigation locks, making the river navigable for large vessels from the sea to Lyon.

Wine

The Rhône valley is a famous wine region: the northern Rhône produces Syrah wines such as Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie, while the southern Rhône is known for blends such as Châteauneuf-du-Pape.
