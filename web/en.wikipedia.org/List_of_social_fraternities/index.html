List of social fraternities

Social fraternities are membership organisations of (mostly) undergraduate students at colleges and universities, primarily in North America, characterised by Greek-letter names, secret rituals, selective recruitment ("rush" and pledging), and a lifelong bond of brotherhood. The modern system began with the founding of Kappa Alpha Society at Union College in 1825; together with Sigma Phi (1827) and Delta Phi (1827) it formed the "Union Triad". Women's counterparts are usually called sororities, a term coined in 1882 for Gamma Phi Beta. Umbrella bodies include the North American Interfraternity Conference (NIC) for men's groups and the National Panhellenic Conference for women's.

Selected fraternities (by founding)

  • Kappa Alpha Society (1825, Union College) — oldest continuously existing social fraternity.
  • Sigma Phi (1827), Delta Phi (1827) — completing the Union Triad.
  • Alpha Delta Phi (1832, Hamilton), Psi Upsilon (1833, Union), Delta Upsilon (1834, Williams — founded as the Social Fraternity/anti-secret confederation), Chi Psi (1841), Delta Kappa Epsilon (1844, Yale), Zeta Psi (1847), Delta Psi/St Anthony Hall, Theta Delta Chi (1847), Phi Gamma Delta (Fiji) (1848), Phi Delta Theta (1848, Miami University), Phi Kappa Sigma (1850), Phi Kappa Psi (1852), Sigma Chi (1855, Miami), Sigma Alpha Epsilon (1856), Chi Phi, Delta Beta Xi.
  • The "Miami Triad" of Beta Theta Pi (1839), Phi Delta Theta (1848) and Sigma Chi (1855) all arose at Miami University in Ohio.
  • Theta Xi (1864), Alpha Sigma Phi (1845), Kappa Alpha Order (1865), Pi Kappa Alpha (1868), Sigma Nu (1869), Kappa Sigma (1869), Lambda Chi Alpha (1909), Tau Kappa Epsilon (1899), Sigma Phi Epsilon (1901), Pi Kappa Phi (1904), Alpha Phi Alpha (1906, Cornell — the first intercollegiate Black Greek-letter fraternity, of the "Divine Nine" National Pan-Hellenic Council organisations, which include Kappa Alpha Psi, Omega Psi Phi, Phi Beta Sigma and Iota Phi Theta), FarmHouse (1905), and Triangle (1907, for engineering students).

Notes

Many fraternities expanded from single chapters into international organisations with hundreds of chapters and alumni networks; others remain local. The list conventionally excludes professional, honorary and service fraternities (such as Alpha Phi Omega), which are treated separately.
