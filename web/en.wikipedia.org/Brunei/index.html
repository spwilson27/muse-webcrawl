Brunei, officially the Nation of Brunei, the Abode of Peace, is a country in Southeast Asia, situated on the northern coast of the island of Borneo. Apart from its South China Sea coastline, it is completely surrounded by the Malaysian state of Sarawak, which separates it into two parts. It is the only sovereign state entirely on Borneo; the remainder is divided between Malaysia and Indonesia.

History

The Sultanate of Brunei once controlled much of Borneo and parts of the Philippines, reaching its height under Bolkiah the Singing Captain in the 15th–16th centuries. It ceded territory to the Brooke "White Rajahs" of Sarawak and the British North Borneo Company, became a British protectorate in 1888, and regained full independence in 1984. Oil discovered at Seria in 1929 transformed its economy.

Government and economy

Brunei is an absolute monarchy ruled by Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah, among the world's longest-reigning monarchs, under the national philosophy of Melayu Islam Beraja (Malay Islamic Monarchy). A Syariah penal code was phased in from 2014, drawing international scrutiny. Hydrocarbons account for roughly 90% of exports and over half of GDP, funding generous welfare—no income tax, subsidized housing, free education and healthcare—though diversification is an official priority as reserves decline.

Geography and culture

About 80% of Brunei is forested, including the pristine rainforest of Ulu Temburong National Park. Kampong Ayer, a vast stilt water village in the capital, houses tens of thousands. The population is about two-thirds ethnic Malay, with significant Chinese and indigenous minorities; Islam is the official religion.
