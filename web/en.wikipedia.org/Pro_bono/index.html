Pro bono

Pro bono publico (Latin: "for the public good"), usually shortened to pro bono, is a Latin phrase for professional work undertaken voluntarily and without payment. The term typically refers to legal services provided free of charge to people who are unable to pay, or to charitable and public-interest organisations.

Legal pro bono

In the United States, the American Bar Association's Model Rules of Professional Conduct recommend that lawyers aspire to provide at least 50 hours of pro bono service per year. Many large law firms have dedicated pro bono partners and count such work toward billable-hour targets. Pro bono representation is particularly significant in immigration, death penalty, housing, and civil rights cases.

In the United Kingdom, organisations such as Advocate (formerly the Bar Pro Bono Unit) match barristers with unrepresented litigants. In South Korea and other jurisdictions, courts may appoint pro bono counsel in certain proceedings.

Other professions

The concept extends beyond law: architects, accountants, physicians, engineers, and advertising professionals may all offer pro bono services to nonprofits and community groups, and skilled volunteering is promoted through organisations such as the Taproot Foundation.
