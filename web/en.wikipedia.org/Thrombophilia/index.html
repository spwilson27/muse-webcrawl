Thrombophilia

Thrombophilia (sometimes called hypercoagulability or a prothrombotic state) is an abnormality of blood coagulation that increases the risk of thrombosis (blood clots in blood vessels). Such abnormalities can be inherited or acquired, and they typically manifest as venous thromboembolism — deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism.

Causes

The most common inherited thrombophilias are factor V Leiden and the prothrombin G20210A mutation, followed by deficiencies of protein C, protein S, and antithrombin. Acquired causes include antiphospholipid syndrome, cancer, pregnancy, oral contraceptives, and prolonged immobility.

Diagnosis and treatment

Testing for thrombophilia is usually considered after unprovoked or recurrent venous thrombosis, thrombosis at a young age, or a strong family history. Management centres on anticoagulant therapy, and routine population screening is not recommended because most carriers never develop a clot.
