Kinect

Kinect
Developer: Microsoft
Type: Motion-sensing input device
Released: November 2010 (Xbox 360 version)
Discontinued: 2017 (original line); Azure Kinect in 2023

Kinect is a line of motion-sensing input devices developed by Microsoft, launched in November 2010 for the Xbox 360 console. Using an RGB camera, an infrared depth sensor and a microphone array, Kinect allowed users to control games and interfaces through body gestures and voice commands without holding a controller, tracking the skeletal movement of up to several players at once.

Technology

The depth-sensing technology was based on structured-light pattern projection developed by the Israeli company PrimeSense. Software converted the depth map into a skeletal model of 20 joints per player, enabling full-body play in titles such as Kinect Sports, Dance Central and Kinect Adventures!, the latter bundled with the sensor.

Sales and versions

Kinect sold 8 million units in its first 60 days, earning a Guinness World Record as the fastest-selling consumer electronics device at the time, and reached about 24 million units by early 2013. A Windows version and software development kit followed, and a redesigned Kinect shipped with the Xbox One in 2013, though it was later unbundled. Microsoft discontinued the sensor in 2017; its depth technology lived on in HoloLens and the Azure Kinect developer kit, itself discontinued in 2023.

Beyond gaming

Researchers and hobbyists adopted the inexpensive sensor for robotics, 3D scanning, gesture interfaces, medical rehabilitation and art installations, a community Microsoft eventually supported with an official PC SDK.
