Radio City Music Hall

Radio City Music Hall, the "Showplace of the Nation", at Rockefeller Center in Manhattan.

Radio City Music Hall is an entertainment venue and theater at 1260 Avenue of the Americas, within Rockefeller Center, in the Midtown Manhattan neighborhood of New York City. Nicknamed the Showplace of the Nation, it is the headquarters for the Rockettes, the precision dance company.

Designed by Edward Durell Stone and Donald Deskey in the Art Deco style, the Music Hall opened on December 27, 1932, as part of the construction of Rockefeller Center. Its auditorium was the largest indoor theater in the world at the time, seating around 6,000 people, and its stage — framed by a great golden proscenium arch evoking a setting sun — remains one of the largest in the United States.

History

Radio City was developed by John D. Rockefeller Jr. with the Radio Corporation of America during the Great Depression. For decades it combined first-run films with lavish stage shows. When movie palace economics collapsed in the 1970s, the hall was threatened with closure and demolition; a preservation campaign led to its designation as a New York City landmark in 1978 and to its continuation as a concert and special-events venue. A major restoration in 1999 returned the interiors to their 1930s appearance.

The Christmas Spectacular

Since 1933 the hall has hosted the annual Christmas Spectacular, featuring the Rockettes and famous numbers such as the "Parade of the Wooden Soldiers" and the "Living Nativity". It is one of the most attended theatrical productions in the United States.
