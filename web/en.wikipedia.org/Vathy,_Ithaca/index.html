Vathy, Ithaca

Vathy (Greek: Βαθύ) is a town on the island of Ithaca, in the Ionian Islands of Greece. It is the capital and largest settlement of the island, with a population of around 2,000, and serves as the island's principal port.

Geography and history

The town is built in an amphitheatre around a deep, sheltered bay — its name derives from the Greek word for "deep" — on the island's eastern coast, beneath the slopes of Mount Aetos and Mount Neion. The small islet of Lazareto, with a chapel and a former quarantine station, sits in the bay.

Vathy was rebuilt after the catastrophic Ionian earthquake of 1953, retaining a traditional character with Venetian influences. Notable sites include the Archaeological Museum, the Maritime and Folklore Museum, the cathedral of Panagia, and the nearby Cave of the Nymphs, associated in local tradition with the Odyssey; Ithaca is famed as the home of Homer's Odysseus. Ferries connect Vathy with Lefkada, Kefalonia (via the port of Pisaetos), and Patras.
