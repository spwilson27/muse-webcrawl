The American Red Cross (ARC), also known as the American National Red Cross, is a humanitarian organisation that provides emergency assistance, disaster relief, and disaster-preparedness education in the United States. It is the US affiliate of the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies and the United States movement to the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement. Clara Barton founded the organisation in 1881 after campaigning for the United States to ratify the Geneva Convention.

History

Barton, who had nursed soldiers in the American Civil War and worked with the International Red Cross during the Franco-Prussian War, led the American Red Cross until 1904. The organisation received its first congressional charter in 1900 and a second, still in force, in 1905. It grew enormously during the two world wars, organising nursing services, blood collection, parcels for prisoners of war, and support for refugees.

The American Red Cross began its civilian blood programme in 1941, following the wartime Blood for Britain effort, and it is today one of the largest suppliers of blood products in the United States, providing roughly 40% of the nation's blood supply through millions of annual donations.

Services

The organisation responds to tens of thousands of disasters each year, most of them home fires, providing shelter, food, and assistance. It trains millions of people annually in first aid, CPR, lifeguarding, and swimming. Its Service to the Armed Forces programme supports military members, veterans, and their families, and its international services include measles and rubella vaccination campaigns and restoring family links for people separated by conflict or migration.

Governance and criticism

The American Red Cross is governed by a board of governors, some appointed by the President of the United States, who serves as honorary chairman. The organisation has periodically faced criticism over fundraising and relief operations — for example after the September 11 attacks (when donations designated for victims were partly reserved for other purposes) and following the 2010 Haiti earthquake — prompting governance reforms and increased transparency measures.
