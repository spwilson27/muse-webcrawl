The Great Ocean Road is an Australian National Heritage-listed stretch of road along the south-eastern coast of Australia between the Victorian cities of Torquay and Allansford, near Warrnambool. Built by returned soldiers between 1919 and 1932 and dedicated to soldiers killed during World War I, it is the world's largest war memorial.

Geography and attractions

The road hugs dramatic limestone and sandstone cliffs along the Southern Ocean. Its best-known landmark is the Twelve Apostles, a group of limestone stacks in Port Campbell National Park, alongside London Arch (formerly London Bridge), Loch Ard Gorge, and the Grotto. The route passes through rainforest in the Otway Ranges and coastal towns such as Lorne, Apollo Bay, and Port Campbell.

History

Construction began in 1919, largely by hand using explosives, picks, and wheelbarrows, providing work for some 3,000 returned servicemen. The road was officially opened in 1932 and today is one of Australia's most popular scenic touring routes, also hosting the Great Ocean Road Marathon.
