2021 Western North America heat wave

The 2021 Western North America heat wave was an extreme heat wave that affected much of Western North America from late June to mid-July 2021. The heat wave was caused by a strong ridge of high pressure, often described as a "heat dome", that trapped hot air over the region.

Records

The village of Lytton, British Columbia, recorded 49.6 °C (121.3 °F) on 29 June 2021, the highest temperature ever measured in Canada. Portland, Oregon reached 46.7 °C (116 °F) and Seattle, Washington 42 °C (108 °F), both all-time records.

Impacts

More than 1,400 deaths were attributed to the event, including hundreds of sudden deaths in British Columbia. The heat damaged infrastructure, buckled roads, strained power grids and caused mass die-offs of intertidal marine life along the coast.

Lytton fire

On 30 June 2021, a wildfire swept through Lytton the day after its record temperature, destroying most of the village and killing two people.

Attribution

Rapid attribution studies concluded that such extreme temperatures would have been virtually impossible without human-caused climate change.
