Chaguaramas, Trinidad and Tobago

Chaguaramas is a coastal area on the northwestern peninsula of Trinidad, west of Port of Spain, in Trinidad and Tobago. Named after the palm trees that once grew there, it is known for its marinas, beaches, hiking trails, and the Chaguaramas National Park.

History

Under the Destroyers for Bases Agreement of 1940, the United States leased Chaguaramas as a naval base during the Second World War. The base became politically contentious; Eric Williams campaigned for its return, and the 1960 march to Chaguaramas was a landmark in the independence movement. The revised Treaty of Chaguaramas (1973) established the Caribbean Community (CARICOM) and was signed here.

Present day

Today Chaguaramas is a recreation and yachting centre, with boardwalks at Chagville beach, golf, zip-lining, and boat trips to offshore islands such as Gasparee Island and the Bocas Islands.
