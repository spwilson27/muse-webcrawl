Battle of Yamama

DateDecember 632 CE
LocationYamama, central Arabia
ResultRashidun victory
OpponentsMuslim forces vs followers of Musaylima

The Battle of Yamama was fought in December 632 during the Ridda Wars, the campaigns by which the first caliph, Abu Bakr, reasserted the authority of Medina over Arabian tribes that had rebelled after the death of the Prophet Muhammad.

The battle

Muslim forces under Khalid ibn al-Walid confronted the army of Musaylima, a rival claimant to prophecy, in the region of Yamama in present-day Saudi Arabia. The fighting was exceptionally hard: Musaylima's followers initially drove the Muslims back before Khalid reorganised his lines. Musaylima was killed in the enclosed orchard remembered as the "Garden of Death," and his army was destroyed.

Significance

So many reciters of the Qur'an fell at Yamama that, according to tradition, Abu Bakr ordered Zayd ibn Thabit to compile the Qur'anic text into a single written collection, a step toward the standard codex produced under Caliph Uthman.
