Touring car racing

Touring car racing is a form of motor racing in which competitors drive heavily modified production-based saloon cars (sedans). Unlike open-wheel or prototype racing, touring cars retain the basic silhouette and many components of road cars, and the racing is famous for close, contact-heavy battles, large grids, and strong manufacturer involvement.

Characteristics

Touring cars typically feature aerodynamic aids, wide tyres, racing suspension and brakes, and engines ranging from near-standard units to fully built racing motors. Regulations aim to keep costs manageable and performance comparable between different makes, often through balance-of-performance measures, success ballast, or spec components.

Major championships

  • British Touring Car Championship (BTCC) — founded 1958; one of the world's longest-running and most popular series.
  • Supercars Championship (Australia) — V8-powered Ford versus Holden (later Chevrolet) rivalry, home of the Bathurst 1000.
  • World Touring Car Championship / TCR World Tour — the FIA's global touring car platform; the TCR formula, launched in 2015, unified national series around front-wheel-drive 2.0-litre turbo cars.
  • Deutsche Tourenwagen Masters (DTM) — Germany's premier series; for much of its history used bespoke silhouette cars rather than true production touring cars.
  • Super GT and Super Taikyu (Japan), TCR regional series worldwide, and the historic European Touring Car Championship.

History

Production-car racing grew in the 1950s and 1960s with events mixing classes of saloons. Group 5 and Group A rules in the 1980s produced legendary machinery such as the Ford Sierra RS500 and BMW M3. The 1990s "Super Touring" era — exemplified by the BTCC and Germany's STW — brought big manufacturer budgets before costs forced simpler regulations. The TCR concept revived affordable, globally compatible touring car racing from the mid-2010s.

Famous races

Classic touring car events include the Bathurst 1000 in Australia, the Spa 24 Hours (in its touring car era), the Guia Race of Macau, and the Nürburgring 24 Hours classes. Touring car racing remains a fan favourite for its accessibility: spectators can identify with cars resembling those on the road.
