Electric power

Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred by an electric circuit, measured in watts. In a broader sense, the term also refers to the industry and infrastructure that generate, transmit, and distribute electrical energy to consumers. Electric power is typically produced by electric generators at power stations and delivered through an electrical grid.

Electricity is generated centrally and delivered over transmission networks.

Definition

In direct-current circuits, power P equals voltage V multiplied by current I (P = VI). In alternating-current systems the delivered (real) power depends also on the phase relationship between voltage and current, expressed by the power factor; the combination of real and reactive power gives the apparent power, measured in volt-amperes.

Generation

Most electricity is generated by rotating turbines coupled to generators, driven by steam from fossil fuels, nuclear fission, or concentrated sunlight, by falling water, or directly by wind. Photovoltaic cells convert sunlight to electricity without moving parts. Generation must be balanced continuously with demand, since large-scale storage remains limited.

Transmission and distribution

Power is transmitted at high voltage to reduce losses, then stepped down through substations for distribution to homes and businesses; see electric power transmission. The industry is operated by electric utilities, which may be publicly or privately owned and are often regulated as natural monopolies or restructured into competitive markets.
