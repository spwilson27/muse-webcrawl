Swedish Antarctic Expedition

Image placeholder: the ship Antarctic in Antarctic ice, 1902.

The Swedish Antarctic Expedition of 1901–1904 was a scientific expedition led by the geologist Otto Nordenskjöld, which conducted the first explorations of the eastern coast of the Antarctic Peninsula. The expedition is remembered both for its scientific results and for the dramatic survival and rescue of its members after their ship was lost.

Course of the expedition

The expedition sailed from Gothenburg on 16 October 1901 aboard the former whaler Antarctic, captained by Carl Anton Larsen. A wintering party under Nordenskjöld established a base on Snow Hill Island in February 1902 and carried out geological and meteorological work. When the Antarctic failed to return in the following summer — the ship was crushed by ice and sank in the Weddell Sea in February 1903 — the expedition became split into three stranded parties: at Snow Hill Island, at Hope Bay, and on Paulet Island, where Larsen's crew had sought refuge.

Rescue

Remarkably, all three parties survived the winter of 1903. In October 1903 the Hope Bay party sledged to Snow Hill, and in November the Argentine corvette ARA Uruguay, sent to search for the expedition, rescued the Snow Hill party and then the Paulet Island group. All but one member of the expedition survived.

Legacy

The expedition collected important fossil material demonstrating Antarctica's once-temperate climate, conducted the first sledge journeys on the peninsula's east coast, and its story remains one of the classic tales of Antarctic survival.
