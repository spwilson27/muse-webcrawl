Dark wave

Dark wave (also written darkwave) is a music genre that emerged from the new wave and post-punk movement of the late 1970s. Dark wave compositions are largely based on minor key tonality and introspective, dark, romantic, and bleak lyrics, often expressing sorrow. The genre embraces a wide stylistic range, drawing on new wave, gothic rock, synth-pop, neoclassical music, neofolk, and ethereal wave.

History

The term was first used in the 1980s in Europe to describe the dark, melancholic side of new wave and post-punk acts such as Joy Division, Bauhaus, The Cure, Siouxsie and the Banshees, and Cocteau Twins. In the 1990s, the American label Projekt Records popularized the term for its roster of ethereal and gothic acts. Dedicated festivals such as Wave-Gotik-Treffen in Leipzig became central to the scene.

Characteristics

Dark wave music frequently features synthesizers, drum machines, and reverb-heavy guitars, with vocals ranging from baritone crooning to operatic soprano. Lyrical themes include death, loss, longing, and the supernatural, influenced by Gothic literature and Romantic poetry.
