Vassal

A vassal is a person regarded as having a mutual obligation to a lord or monarch, in the context of the feudal system in medieval Europe. While the subordinate party is called a vassal, the dominant party is called a suzerain. The rights and obligations of the vassal are called vassalage, while the rights and obligations of a suzerain are called suzerainty.

Obligations

In a typical feudal relationship (homage and fealty), the vassal swore an oath of loyalty and undertook to provide military service, counsel, and sometimes financial aid or hospitality to his lord. In return the lord granted the vassal a fief — usually land and the peasants who worked it — and owed him protection and justice. Vassals could themselves have vassals (subinfeudation), producing the layered lordship characteristic of feudal society, summarized in the saying "the vassal of my vassal is not my vassal."

Beyond Europe

Analogous relationships existed elsewhere: in Japan, retainers (gokenin, later samurai) served daimyo lords in return for land or stipends; in the Ottoman and other systems, tributary rulers governed under a suzerain's overlordship.
