Joshua

Joshua (Hebrew: יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, Yehoshua), also spelled Jehoshua, was a figure in the Torah, being one of the spies for which Israel is sent by Moses to explore the land of Canaan, and after Moses' death he led the Israelite tribes in the conquest of Canaan and allocated the land to the tribes. According to biblical chronology, Joshua lived in the Bronze Age.

Biblical narrative

Joshua, of the tribe of Ephraim and son of Nun, first appears as Moses' aide in the battle against Amalek. He was one of the twelve spies sent into Canaan; only he and Caleb brought a favourable report and so only they of their generation were permitted to enter the Promised Land.

The Book of Joshua recounts the crossing of the Jordan River, the capture of Jericho and Ai, the covenant renewal, and the campaigns in the south and north, followed by the division of the land among the Twelve Tribes and Joshua's farewell address and death at the age of 110.

Historicity

Most scholars regard the conquest account as largely unhistorical in its details, reflecting later theological ideals rather than a documented rapid conquest; archaeological evidence suggests a more gradual and complex emergence of Israel in Canaan. Joshua remains an important figure in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.
