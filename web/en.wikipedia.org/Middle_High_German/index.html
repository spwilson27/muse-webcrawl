Middle High German

Middle High German (MHG; German: Mittelhochdeutsch) is the stage of the German language between 1050 and 1350, succeeding Old High German and preceding Early New High German. It was the language of a celebrated courtly literature, including the heroic epic Nibelungenlied, Wolfram von Eschenbach's Parzival, Gottfried von Strassburg's Tristan, and the love lyrics (Minnesang) of poets such as Walther von der Vogelweide.

Middle High German is known largely from literary texts written in a relatively standardised courtly language; everyday speech remained dialectally diverse. Characteristic features include full final syllables (later reduced), the completion of the High German consonant shift inherited from the earlier period, and vowel changes such as diphthongisation beginning at the transition to Early New High German.

Study

The language is reconstructed and taught from manuscripts such as the Codex Manesse, a richly illuminated collection of courtly song compiled in Zurich around 1300–1340. Standard dictionaries (Lexer) and grammars support its study in medieval German philology.
