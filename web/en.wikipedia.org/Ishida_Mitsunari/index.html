Ishida Mitsunari

Ishida Mitsunari (1559 – November 6, 1600) was a Japanese samurai and military commander of the late Sengoku period. A close retainer of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, he served as one of the five commissioners (go-bugyō) administering the Toyotomi government.

Career

Born in Ōmi Province, Mitsunari entered Hideyoshi's service and became renowned for administrative and logistical skill, including organizing supplies during the invasions of Korea. He was granted the fief of Sawayama. After Hideyoshi's death in 1598, Mitsunari's disputes with Fukushima Masanori and other generals, and his role in purging rivals, deepened the split in the Toyotomi camp.

Battle of Sekigahara and death

In 1600 Mitsunari led the Western Army against Tokugawa Ieyasu's Eastern Army at the Battle of Sekigahara. Betrayal by Kobayakawa Hideaki turned the battle, and the Western Army was destroyed. Mitsunari was captured and executed by beheading at Rokujōgawara in Kyoto. He remains a controversial figure, remembered both as a loyal administrator and as a scheming bureaucrat.
