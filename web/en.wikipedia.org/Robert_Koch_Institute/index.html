Robert Koch Institute

Headquarters of the Robert Koch Institute, Berlin

The Robert Koch Institute (RKI; German: Robert Koch-Institut) is a German federal government agency and research institute responsible for disease control and prevention. It is located in Berlin and Wernigerode and forms part of the Federal Ministry of Health.

History

The institute was founded in 1891 as the Royal Prussian Institute for Infectious Diseases, with Nobel laureate Robert Koch as its first director. Koch's group there developed foundational methods of bacteriology. The institute later bore Koch's name and expanded into epidemiology, health monitoring and biological security.

Tasks

The RKI's duties include identifying, surveillance and prevention of diseases, especially infectious diseases; epidemiological analysis and evaluation; health reporting for the federal government; and risk assessment for highly pathogenic agents. It operates reference laboratories and the standing committee on vaccination (STIKO).

COVID-19 pandemic

During the COVID-19 pandemic the institute's daily situation reports, risk assessments and its president Lothar Wieler became central to Germany's public response and public communication.
