SOLAS

Full nameInternational Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea
First adopted1914
Current versionSOLAS 1974, as amended
DepositaryInternational Maritime Organization

SOLAS, the International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea, is the most important international treaty governing the safety of merchant ships. It sets minimum standards for the construction, equipment and operation of ships, covering areas such as fire protection, life-saving appliances, radio communications, safety of navigation and the carriage of cargoes and dangerous goods.

History

The first SOLAS convention was adopted in 1914 in direct response to the sinking of the Titanic in 1912; it required, among other things, sufficient lifeboats for everyone aboard and continuous radio watches. Successor conventions followed in 1929, 1948 and 1960. The 1974 convention, which remains in force, introduced the "tacit acceptance" procedure, allowing technical amendments to enter into force automatically unless objected to, so the rules could keep pace with technology.

Content

SOLAS is organised into chapters addressing surveys and certificates, subdivision and stability, fire safety, life-saving appliances, radiocommunications (including the GMDSS), navigation safety, dangerous goods, nuclear ships, safety management (the ISM Code) and maritime security (the ISPS Code, added after 2001). Flag states are responsible for ensuring their ships comply, and port state control officers may inspect foreign vessels.
