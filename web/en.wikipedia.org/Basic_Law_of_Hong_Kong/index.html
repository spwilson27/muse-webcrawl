Basic Law of Hong Kong

The Basic Law of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China is a Chinese national law that serves as the constitutional document of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR). It was adopted by the National People's Congress on 4 April 1990 and came into effect on 1 July 1997, when sovereignty over Hong Kong was transferred from the United Kingdom to China.

The Basic Law serves as Hong Kong's constitutional document.

Contents

The Basic Law implements the "one country, two systems" principle set out in the Sino-British Joint Declaration of 1984. It provides that Hong Kong's capitalist system and way of life shall remain unchanged for 50 years, guarantees a "high degree of autonomy" except in defence and foreign affairs, preserves the common law legal system, and protects rights and freedoms including those in the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. It provides for an executive-led government headed by a Chief Executive, a Legislative Council, and an independent judiciary with the Court of Final Appeal at its apex; Articles 45 and 68 name universal suffrage as the "ultimate aim" for selecting the Chief Executive and all legislators.

Interpretation and disputes

Under Article 158, the power of final interpretation rests with the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, which has issued several interpretations — including on the right of abode (1999), electoral reform (2004), the Chief Executive's term (2005), state immunity (2011), and the oath-taking controversy (2016) — each of which has been politically contentious. In 2020 the Standing Committee imposed a National Security Law on Hong Kong via Annex III of the Basic Law, bypassing the local legislature, an action critics said undermined the autonomy the Basic Law promises.
