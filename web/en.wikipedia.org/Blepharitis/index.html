Blepharitis

Inflammation and crusting of the eyelid margin in blepharitis.

Blepharitis is a common chronic inflammation of the eyelids, specifically of the eyelid margins, characterized by redness, irritation, burning, itching, and crusting of scales around the base of the eyelashes. It is one of the most frequently encountered conditions in eye care and is a major cause of dry eye symptoms.

Types and causes

Blepharitis is usually classified by location. Anterior blepharitis affects the front edge of the lid where the lashes attach and is associated with staphylococcal bacteria and seborrheic dermatitis. Posterior blepharitis involves the inner edge of the lid and results from dysfunction of the meibomian glands (meibomian gland dysfunction), which secrete the oily layer of the tear film; it is strongly associated with rosacea. Infestation with Demodex mites can contribute in some patients. The condition is chronic and relapsing but is not contagious and usually does not permanently damage vision, though severe cases can cause lash loss, lid scarring, styes, chalazia, and corneal irritation.

Management

Treatment centers on long-term eyelid hygiene: warm compresses to soften gland secretions, gentle lid massage, and cleaning of the lash line with diluted cleansers or commercial lid scrubs. Antibiotics (topical or oral tetracyclines), anti-inflammatory drops, or in-office procedures may be prescribed for more severe cases. Because blepharitis rarely resolves completely, maintenance hygiene is typically continued indefinitely.
