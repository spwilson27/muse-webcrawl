The Iraqi Armed Forces are the military forces of the Republic of Iraq, comprising the Iraqi Army, the Iraqi Air Force, the Iraqi Navy, air defence forces, and the elite Counter Terrorism Service. They operate under the Ministry of Defence, with the prime minister as commander-in-chief.

History

Iraq's army was founded in 1921 under the British mandate and grew into one of the largest militaries in the Arab world under Saddam Hussein, fighting in the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), the invasion of Kuwait (1990), and the Gulf War (1991). After the 2003 US-led invasion, the Coalition Provisional Authority dissolved the armed forces; a new force was built from scratch with American and allied training and equipment.

Recent operations

The rebuilt military suffered a collapse in northern Iraq in June 2014, when Islamic State fighters seized Mosul, but it regrouped with coalition support, retook the country's cities in the war against ISIL (2014–2017), and declared victory in December 2017. The Popular Mobilization Forces, a state-sponsored umbrella of mostly Shia militias formed in 2014, operate alongside — and partly within — the formal security structure, raising continuing questions about command and control.
