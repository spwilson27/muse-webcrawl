The Big Sleep (1946 film)

The Big Sleep is a 1946 American film noir directed by Howard Hawks and starring Humphrey Bogart as private detective Philip Marlowe and Lauren Bacall as Vivian Rutledge. The film was based on the 1939 novel of the same name by Raymond Chandler; the screenplay was written by William Faulkner, Leigh Brackett and Jules Furthman. It is remembered for the crackling Bogart–Bacall chemistry and a plot so convoluted that even Chandler reportedly could not explain one of its murders.

Plot

General Sternwood hires Marlowe to resolve a blackmail attempt against his wild younger daughter Carmen. The case draws the detective through a chain of murders, pornographers, gamblers and dope rackets involving Carmen, her elder sister Vivian, gangster Eddie Mars, and a missing Irish gunman-adventurer, Sean Regan. Marlowe eventually establishes that Carmen killed Regan, with Eddie Mars covering it up; he confronts Mars and leaves Vivian and Marlowe together as sirens approach.

Production

Filmed in late 1944 and early 1945, the film was held back while Warner Bros. cleared its backlog of war films, then partly reshot in January 1946 to expand Bacall's role and add wisecracking scenes capitalizing on the couple's success in To Have and Have Not and their real-life marriage. Both the 1945 prerelease version and the 1946 theatrical version survive; the earlier cut was restored and released in the 1990s. In 1997 the film was selected for the United States National Film Registry.
