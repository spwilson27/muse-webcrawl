The African National Congress (ANC) is a political party in South Africa. It originated as a liberation movement known for its opposition to apartheid, and has been the governing party since the country's first democratic election in 1994. It was founded on 8 January 1912 in Bloemfontein as the South African Native National Congress, and was renamed the ANC in 1923.

History

The ANC was founded to unite Africans against discrimination, responding to the Natives Land Act of 1913 and other injustices. Early decades were marked by petitions and deputations; the 1940s brought a more militant generation — Nelson Mandela, Walter Sisulu, and Oliver Tambo — who founded the ANC Youth League in 1944. The 1952 Defiance Campaign and the 1955 Freedom Charter ("South Africa belongs to all who live in it") defined the party's non-racial vision.

Struggle against apartheid

After the Sharpeville massacre in 1960, the ANC was banned and went underground and into exile. Mandela and others founded uMkhonto we Sizwe (MK), its armed wing, in 1961; Mandela was imprisoned in 1962 and sentenced to life at the Rivonia Trial in 1964. Under Tambo's leadership in exile, the ANC built international solidarity and internal resistance grew through the 1976 Soweto uprising and the union and civic movements of the 1980s.

Government

Unbanned in 1990, the ANC negotiated the transition to democracy and won the 1994 election; Mandela became president. Successor governments under Thabo Mbeki, Jacob Zuma, and Cyril Ramaphosa expanded services and social grants but faced criticism over corruption, including the state capture scandal of the Zuma years. In the 2024 general election the ANC lost its parliamentary majority for the first time and formed a Government of National Unity with rival parties.
