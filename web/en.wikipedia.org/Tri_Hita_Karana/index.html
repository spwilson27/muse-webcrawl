Tri Hita Karana

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tri Hita Karana is a traditional philosophy for life on the Indonesian island of Bali. The Sanskrit-derived phrase literally translates as the "three causes of well-being" or "three reasons for prosperity": harmony among people, harmony with nature or the environment, and harmony with God. It is a central concept of Balinese Hinduism and underlies many aspects of Balinese communal life.

The three relationships

The three relationships are termed pawongan (harmony among fellow humans), palemahan (harmony with nature and the environment), and parahyangan (harmony with God). Each Balinese compound, village and temple embodies the triad, with spaces for worship, for habitation and communal activity, and for gardens and nature.

Applications

Tri Hita Karana informs the subak cooperative irrigation system that has managed Bali's rice terraces for roughly a millennium, organising water sharing through water temples — a landscape recognised by UNESCO in 2012 as a World Heritage Site. The philosophy also guides Balinese tourism policy, architecture, and provincial planning, where it is invoked as a standard for sustainable development.
