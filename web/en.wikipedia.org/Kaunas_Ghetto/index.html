Kaunas Ghetto

The Kaunas Ghetto (also known as the Kovno Ghetto) was a Nazi ghetto established in the Slobodka (Vilijampolė) district of Kaunas, Lithuania, during the German occupation in the Second World War. Between 1941 and 1944 some 30,000 Jews were confined there; most were murdered in mass shootings at the nearby Ninth Fort or deported to concentration camps, and only a few thousand survived.

Establishment

Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, and Kaunas fell within days; pogroms and massacres by German units and Lithuanian auxiliaries killed thousands of Jews before the ghetto was even formed. In July and August 1941 the Jewish population was ordered into the poor district of Slobodka across the Neris River, where they were placed under a Jewish Council (Ältestenrat) headed by Dr Elchanan Elkes.

Life and murder

The ghetto's inhabitants were exploited as forced labour in workshops and at the Aleksotas airfield. In the "Great Action" of 28–29 October 1941, some 9,200 Jews — nearly a third of the ghetto — were selected and shot at the Ninth Fort. Further actions, including the murder of hundreds of children in March 1944, steadily reduced the population. Despite the conditions, the ghetto sustained clandestine schools, cultural life and a secret documentation effort; the council's secretary Avraham Tory kept a diary and archive that survived the war.

Liquidation and aftermath

In July 1944, as the Red Army approached, the Germans liquidated the ghetto, burning it to the ground and deporting the remaining Jews to camps including Stutthof and Dachau. Of Kaunas's prewar Jewish community of some 35,000–40,000, only about 2,000 survived. The Ninth Fort is now a museum and memorial, and Elkes's letters and Tory's diary remain key sources on the ghetto's history.
