NCSA Mosaic was a web browser that popularised the World Wide Web. It was developed at the National Center for Supercomputing Applications (NCSA) at the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign, led by Marc Andreessen with Eric Bina, and released in 1993 — for Unix, then Microsoft Windows and Macintosh.

Features and impact

Earlier browsers displayed images only in separate windows; Mosaic's decisive innovation was to display images inline, mixed with text in a single window, together with a friendly graphical interface, easy installation and support for sound and video clips. These features made the Web accessible to non-technical users and sparked the explosive growth of the web in the mid-1990s; Mosaic is credited with popularising the World Wide Web outside academia.

Legacy

Andreessen left NCSA to co-found Netscape, whose Netscape Navigator browser was Mosaic's commercial heir. Microsoft licensed Mosaic technology from Spyglass, Inc. as the basis of Internet Explorer. NCSA ended Mosaic development in 1997 as other browsers surpassed it, but Mosaic's influence on the design of every subsequent browser is widely acknowledged.
