Baby boom

A baby boom is a period of sharply increased birth rates. The term most often refers to the post–Second World War baby boom in the United States and other Western countries, roughly from 1946 to 1964, during which annual births rose dramatically above pre-war levels.

Causes

The post-war boom combined several forces: demobilised soldiers returning and marrying, economic prosperity, rising suburban home ownership, and social expectations favouring earlier marriage and larger families. In the United States the total fertility rate peaked in the late 1950s at well over three children per woman before falling during the 1960s.

Consequences

The children of the boom — the "baby boomer" generation — reshaped schools, housing, consumer culture and politics as they moved through each stage of life, and their retirement in the twenty-first century has strained pension and health-care systems. Similar, though shorter, booms followed both world wars; the term is also applied to birth-rate surges in other countries and eras, and in ecology to sudden population increases of animals.
