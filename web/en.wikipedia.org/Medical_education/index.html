Medical education

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Medical education is education related to the practice of being a medical practitioner, including the initial training to become a physician (medical school) and additional training thereafter, such as residency, fellowship and continuing medical education. Medical education systems vary substantially around the world, though they typically combine classroom study in the basic sciences with supervised clinical experience.

Entry-level education

In many countries, including most of Europe, students enter medical school directly from secondary school for a course of five or six years. In the United States and Canada, medical school is a graduate programme, usually four years, requiring a prior bachelor's degree and admission tests such as the MCAT.

Postgraduate training

After graduation, newly qualified doctors undertake supervised practice: internship and residency in North America, foundation and specialty training in the United Kingdom, and similar structured programmes elsewhere. Further subspecialty training may follow in fellowships.

Continuing education

Licensed physicians are typically required to complete continuing medical education (CME) credits throughout their careers to maintain licensure and certification.
