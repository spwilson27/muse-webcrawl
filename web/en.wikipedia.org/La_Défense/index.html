La Défense is a major business district in the Paris metropolitan area, located about 3 kilometres west of the city limits of Paris in the departments of Hauts-de-Seine, spanning the communes of Puteaux, Courbevoie, Nanterre and La Garenne-Colombes. It is Europe's largest purpose-built business district, home to the headquarters of many of France's largest companies, and is named after the statue La Défense de Paris, commemorating the soldiers who defended the city in the Franco-Prussian War of 1870–71.

Development

The district was planned from 1958 by the public development agency EPAD to relieve the historic centre of Paris of modern office growth. The first office building, the Esso Tower, opened in 1963, and generations of towers followed; the pedestrian deck, or dalle, that covers roads and railways was progressively extended. La Défense lies at the western end of Paris's Axe historique, the alignment running from the Louvre through the Champs-Élysées and the Arc de Triomphe.

Grande Arche and landmarks

The district's emblem is the Grande Arche de la Fraternité (1989), a hollow cube designed by Danish architect Johann Otto von Spreckelsen that frames the axis and houses government offices and an exhibition space. Other notable structures include the Tour First (the tallest building in France at 231 m after its 2011 reconstruction), the Coeur Défense complex, and numerous corporate towers over the esplanade's public art collection. The district is served by Métro Line 1, RER A, trams and Transilien trains, and expanded management passed to the local authority Paris La Défense in 2018.
