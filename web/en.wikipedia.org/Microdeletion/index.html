Microdeletion

DefinitionChromosomal deletion too small (< ~5 Mb) to detect by conventional karyotyping
DetectionFISH, chromosomal microarray, MLPA, NGS-based copy number analysis
Associated syndromesDiGeorge, Williams, Prader–Willi, Angelman, Smith–Magenis, Miller–Dieker

A microdeletion is a chromosomal deletion that is too small to be detected by conventional cytogenetic karyotyping, generally spanning less than about 5 million base pairs (5 Mb). Microdeletions remove a contiguous set of genes and give rise to the group of conditions known as microdeletion or contiguous gene syndromes.

Mechanism

Many recurrent microdeletions arise through non-allelic homologous recombination between flanking low-copy repeats (segmental duplications), which mispair during meiosis and produce both the deletion and a reciprocal microduplication. Others occur through non-homologous end joining or replication-based mechanisms and are non-recurrent, with unique breakpoints in each patient.

Examples

Well-known microdeletion syndromes include the 22q11.2 deletion (DiGeorge/velocardiofacial syndrome), the most common human microdeletion; the 7q11.23 deletion causing Williams syndrome; 15q11–q13 deletions, which cause Prader–Willi syndrome when paternally inherited and Angelman syndrome when maternally inherited; the 17p11.2 deletion of Smith–Magenis syndrome; and the 4p− (Wolf–Hirschhorn) and 5p− (cri du chat) deletions, the latter two sometimes large enough to be visible cytogenetically.

Diagnosis

Because microdeletions escape routine karyotyping, they are detected by targeted fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA), and especially chromosomal microarray, which is a first-tier test for unexplained developmental delay and congenital anomalies. Non-invasive prenatal screening using cell-free DNA now includes panels for several common microdeletions, though positive results require diagnostic confirmation.
