Youth baseball

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Youth baseball is the sport of baseball played by children and teenagers, typically from about age 4 or 5 through age 18. It is organised by national and local bodies that adapt the rules of the adult game — shorter base paths, smaller fields, pitch counts, and modified pitching — to players' age and development. Youth baseball is especially popular in the United States, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Latin America.

Organisations and levels

The largest governing organisation is Little League Baseball, founded in 1939 in Williamsport, Pennsylvania, whose divisions progress from Tee-ball (ages 4–7) through Minor and Major leagues to Intermediate, Junior, and Senior leagues for teenagers. Its flagship event, the Little League World Series, is held each August in Williamsport. Other major programmes include Babe Ruth League (with its Cal Ripken division), PONY Baseball, USSSA, and American Legion Baseball for older teens. In Japan, school-based baseball — culminating in the famous national high school tournament at Koshien Stadium — is the dominant youth pathway.

Adaptations and safety

Youth rules commonly reduce the distance between bases (for example, 60 feet in Little League majors versus 90 feet in the adult game) and the pitching distance, use coach-pitch or machine-pitch formats for beginners, and impose strict pitch-count and rest rules to protect young arms from overuse injuries such as "Little League elbow". Sliding rules, bat standards, and mandatory protective equipment are also age-regulated.

Development

Beyond recreation leagues, a large travel-ball and showcase circuit has developed in the United States, feeding school teams, college recruitment, and professional drafts. Critics have noted concerns about early specialisation, cost, and burnout, while supporters emphasise teamwork, discipline, and community participation.
