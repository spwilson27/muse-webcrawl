Prize money

Prize money refers to money paid out in competition, particularly in sport and naval warfare. In sport, it denotes the purse awarded to competitors based on finishing position, as in golf, tennis, and motor racing.

Naval prize money

Historically, prize money was the monetary reward paid to the crew of a warship for capturing an enemy vessel (a "prize") or destroying it. In the Royal Navy, the value of a captured ship and its cargo was assessed by an Admiralty court, and the proceeds distributed by fixed shares — a system that could make captains wealthy, famously so during the Napoleonic Wars. The practice was reformed in the 19th century: head money replaced some awards, and the British system of prize money was finally abolished after the Second World War, with the remaining fund distributed following the Prize Act 1948.

In sport

Purse money in professional sport has grown enormously with television revenue; debates continue over equal prize money for men's and women's events, for instance at Wimbledon, which equalized payouts in 2007.
