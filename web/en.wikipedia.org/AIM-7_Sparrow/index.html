The AIM-7 Sparrow is an American medium-range semi-active radar homing air-to-air missile operated by the United States Air Force, United States Navy, United States Marine Corps, and various other air forces and navies. It was the primary beyond-visual-range missile of Western air forces from the late 1950s until the 1990s, when it was progressively replaced by the more capable AIM-120 AMRAAM. A surface-launched derivative, the RIM-7 Sea Sparrow, remains widely used as a naval point-defense weapon.

Development

The Sparrow originated in a 1947 United States Navy requirement for a rocket-propelled beam-riding weapon that could be launched from aircraft. The project passed through the Navy's Bureau of Aeronautics and the Sperry Corporation before Raytheon became the principal contractor. The first version, the Sparrow I, entered service in 1956 but proved limited, and the beam-riding guidance was soon superseded by semi-active radar homing, in which the missile homes on radar energy reflected from a target illuminated by the launching aircraft.

Variants

The AIM-7C introduced a storable liquid propellant motor. The AIM-7D and AIM-7E were widely used during the Vietnam War, where early combat experience exposed poor reliability and rules of engagement that limited the missile's beyond-visual-range advantage; its probability of kill in that conflict was low, prompting extensive redesign. The AIM-7F, entering service in the mid-1970s, added a solid-state guidance section, a more powerful motor, and a larger warhead. The AIM-7M of the 1980s incorporated an inverse monopulse seeker with better look-down/shoot-down capability and resistance to electronic countermeasures, along with a digital autopilot. The AIM-7P added improved guidance and an uplink for mid-course updates.

Combat use

Sparrows fired by American fighters achieved numerous aerial victories during the Vietnam War, the Gulf War of 1991, and other conflicts. In the Gulf War, United States Air Force F-15 Eagles used AIM-7Ms to down a large share of the Iraqi aircraft destroyed in air-to-air combat. Egypt, Israel, Iran, and other operators also employed the missile in regional wars.

RIM-7 Sea Sparrow

The RIM-7 Sea Sparrow adapts the AIM-7 airframe for shipboard launch from an eight-cell box launcher. It forms the basis of the NATO Sea Sparrow Missile System, used by many navies for defense against aircraft and anti-ship missiles. The vertically launched Evolved Sea Sparrow Missile (ESSM, RIM-162) is a much more advanced descendant sharing the Sparrow lineage.

Operators

Past and present operators include Australia, Canada, Egypt, Greece, Iran, Israel, Italy, Japan, Jordan, Kuwait, Malaysia, the Netherlands, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Korea, Spain, Taiwan, Thailand, Turkey, the United Kingdom, and the United States. Many have retired the air-launched version in favor of the AMRAAM while retaining naval derivatives.
