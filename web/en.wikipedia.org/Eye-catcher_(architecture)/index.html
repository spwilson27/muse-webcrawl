Eye-catcher (architecture)

An eye-catcher is a decorative building or structure, usually in an extensive garden or park, whose purpose is to be looked at rather than used. Eye-catchers provided the visual focus of designed landscapes in the eighteenth century and were often placed on hills or at the ends of vistas.

Typical forms included sham ruins, Gothic or classical temples, obelisks, towers and grottoes. Many were follies with no practical function, though some doubled as tea houses, viewing platforms or lodges. The eye-catcher at Shugborough and the ruins at Hagley Park are well-known English examples.
