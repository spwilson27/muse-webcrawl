Ballala Sena

Ballala Sena
Reign: c. 1160 – c. 1179 CE
Dynasty: Sena
Predecessor: Vijaya Sena
Successor: Lakshmana Sena
Region: Bengal

Ballala Sena (Bengali: বল্লাল সেন) was a ruler of the Sena dynasty of Bengal in the Indian subcontinent, reigning in the 12th century, approximately from 1160 to 1179 CE. He succeeded his father Vijaya Sena, the dynasty's founder, and was in turn succeeded by his son Lakshmana Sena.

Reign

Ballala Sena is credited with consolidating Sena rule over Bengal, and tradition associates him with founding the Kulin system of social rank among Bengali Brahmins, Rarhi Brahmins and Kayasthas — a system described in later Kulagrantha texts, though historians debate its historicity. He is also remembered as a scholar-king: works such as the Dānasāgara (a digest on gifts and donations) and the unfinished Adbhutasāgara (on omens and astronomy) are attributed to him, the latter completed by his son.

Legacy

His reign marks the height of Sena power before Lakshmana Sena's later expansions and the dynasty's eventual fall to the forces of Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khalji in the early 13th century.
