Second-countable space

In topology, a second-countable space is a topological space whose topology has a countable base. A space is second-countable if it has a family of open sets, countable in number, such that every open set is a union of members of that family.

Properties

Second-countability is a strong restriction: every second-countable space is separable, first-countable, and Lindelöf. The real line with its usual topology is second-countable, with the open intervals having rational endpoints as a countable base. Every second-countable regular space is metrizable by the Urysohn metrization theorem.

Examples

Euclidean spaces, their subspaces, and countable products of second-countable spaces are second-countable. The discrete topology on an uncountable set is not second-countable.
