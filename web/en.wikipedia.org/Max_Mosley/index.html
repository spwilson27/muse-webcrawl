Max Rufus Mosley (13 April 1940 – 23 May 2021) was a British businessman, lawyer and racing driver who served as president of the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA), the governing body for Formula One and other international motorsport, from 1993 to 2009. He was a co-founder and owner of the March Engineering racing car constructor and worked as a representative of the Formula One Constructors' Association (FOCA) alongside Bernie Ecclestone.

As FIA president, Mosley championed major safety improvements following the deaths of Ayrton Senna and Roland Ratzenberger in 1994, and promoted road-safety programmes and greener technologies in motorsport. His tenure ended after controversies including a 2008 privacy case against the News of the World, in which he won damages for invasion of privacy and subsequently became a campaigner for press regulation and privacy rights. He did not seek re-election in 2009 and was succeeded by Jean Todt.

Early life

Mosley was the younger son of Oswald Mosley, leader of the British Union of Fascists, and Diana Mitford. He studied physics at Christ Church, Oxford, and later qualified as a barrister. He raced cars competitively in the 1960s, competing in Formula Two, before retiring from driving and becoming involved in team management.
