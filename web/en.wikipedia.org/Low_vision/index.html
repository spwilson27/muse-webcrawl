Low vision

Low vision is the loss of sight that is not correctable with prescription eyeglasses or contact lenses. It is often characterised by partial sight, such as blurred vision, blind spots or tunnel vision, but also includes legally defined levels of blindness. People with low vision have reduced visual acuity or visual field loss that interferes with daily activities.

Definition

The World Health Organization classifies visual impairment by visual acuity in the better-seeing eye with best correction. Low vision is generally taken to be visual acuity worse than 6/18 (20/60) but equal to or better than 3/60 (20/400), or a corresponding visual field loss, in the better eye with best possible correction. In the United States, "legal blindness" is defined as acuity of 20/200 or worse in the better eye with correction, or a visual field of 20 degrees or less.

Causes

Common causes of low vision include:

  • Age-related macular degeneration
  • Diabetic retinopathy
  • Glaucoma
  • Cataract (where surgery is unavailable or unsuccessful)
  • Retinitis pigmentosa
  • Albinism and other congenital conditions

Management

Low-vision rehabilitation aims to maximise the use of remaining sight. Optical aids include magnifiers, telescopes and high-power reading glasses; non-optical approaches include large-print materials, improved lighting and contrast, text-to-speech and screen-reader software, and orientation and mobility training. Many people with low vision use a white cane or a guide dog.
