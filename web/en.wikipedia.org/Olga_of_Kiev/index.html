Olga (c. 890–925 – 969), also known as Saint Olga, was a regent of Kievan Rus' from 945 until 960, ruling on behalf of her infant son Sviatoslav I after the assassination of her husband, Prince Igor. She is remembered for her ruthless vengeance against the Drevlians who killed her husband, for administrative reforms, and for becoming the first ruler of the Rus' to convert to Christianity — for which the Orthodox Church canonized her as Equal-to-the-Apostles.

Revenge on the Drevlians

The Primary Chronicle recounts four episodes of Olga's revenge after the Drevlians murdered Igor, who had demanded excessive tribute. She allegedly buried alive a party of Drevlian envoys, burned a second embassy in a bathhouse, slaughtered thousands at a funeral feast, and finally burned the Drevlian capital of Iskorosten by tying burning tinder to birds that flew back to their nests in the city. Modern historians regard the details as legendary, though a punitive campaign likely occurred.

Reforms

Olga reorganized the tribute system, replacing arbitrary collection with fixed amounts levied at established trading posts (pogosty) — among the earliest administrative reforms recorded in Eastern Europe. She maintained peace and defended Kiev against steppe peoples.

Conversion and legacy

Olga was baptized in Constantinople, probably in 955 or 957, taking the Christian name Helena, after the mother of Constantine the Great; Byzantine sources describe her reception by Emperor Constantine VII. Although she could not persuade her son Sviatoslav to convert, her grandson Vladimir eventually Christianized the Rus' in 988, and Olga came to be honored as the forerunner of that conversion. She died in 969 and was later canonized; her relics were transferred to the Church of the Tithes in Kiev.
