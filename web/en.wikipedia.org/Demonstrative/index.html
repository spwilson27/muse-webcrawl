Demonstrative

In linguistics, a demonstrative is a word or morpheme that identifies a specific entity relative to the speaker's position in space or time, such as English this, that, these and those. Demonstratives are deictic expressions: their reference depends on the context of utterance, and they are often accompanied by pointing gestures.

Forms and functions

Demonstratives may serve as determiners ("this book"), as pronouns ("this is mine"), or as adverbs of place and manner ("here", "there", "thus"). Languages commonly distinguish two degrees of distance — proximal (near the speaker) versus distal (far) — as in English this/that. Others make finer distinctions: Spanish has este, ese and aquel (near speaker, near listener, remote), and many languages add contrasts for visibility, elevation or position relative to the hearer. Latin had a three-way system: hic, iste, ille.

Discourse use

Beyond physical pointing, demonstratives track items in discourse — "that argument" may refer to something just mentioned — and can signal emotional closeness or distance. Cross-linguistically, demonstratives are among the most frequent words in speech and are acquired early by children, often together with the pointing gesture itself.
