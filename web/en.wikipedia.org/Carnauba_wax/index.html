Carnauba wax

SourceLeaves of Copernicia prunifera palm
Melting point82–86 °C (180–187 °F)
E numberE903

Carnauba wax (Brazilian Portuguese: cera de carnaúba) is a wax obtained from the leaves of the carnauba palm, Copernicia prunifera, native to northeastern Brazil. Known as the "queen of waxes", it is among the hardest natural waxes and has a high melting point, properties that make it valuable in many applications.

Production

Harvesters cut the palm's leaves, dry them in the sun so the powdery wax coating loosens, and beat them to release flakes that are melted, strained, and graded (types T1 to T4 by purity and color). Production is concentrated in the states of Piauí and Ceará.

Uses

Carnauba wax provides gloss and hardness in car waxes, floor and furniture polishes, shoe polish, and cosmetics such as lipstick. In food it is a glazing agent (E903) on candies and pills, and it is used to coat dental floss, paper, and surfboards as a plant-based alternative to paraffin.

Properties

It consists mainly of aliphatic esters and cinnamic acid derivatives, is almost insoluble in water and ethyl alcohol, and softens significantly only above about 80 °C.
