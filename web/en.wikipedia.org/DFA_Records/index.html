DFA Records is an American independent record label founded in 2001 by musician James Murphy, British producer Tim Goldsworthy, and Jonathan Galkin. The initials stand for "Death from Above", a name the founders also used for their production team, The DFA.

History and artists

DFA became the defining label of the early-2000s dance-punk revival, releasing records by Murphy's own band LCD Soundsystem as well as The Rapture, whose "House of Jealous Lovers" (2002) was an early signature release, Hot Chip, The Juan MacLean, Holy Ghost!, Factory Floor, and Shit Robot. The DFA production duo's remixes — for artists including Britney Spears, Nine Inch Nails, and Gorillaz — helped spread the label's raw, live-band-meets-disco sound.

The label is noted for its meticulous, analogue-influenced production aesthetic and for the influence of its New York scene on indie and electronic music worldwide. LCD Soundsystem's albums, from their 2005 debut through American Dream (2017), remain its best-known releases.
