Aneto (Spanish: Pico Aneto) is the highest mountain in the Pyrenees and in Aragon, and Spain's third-highest mountain, reaching a height of 3,404 metres (11,168 ft). It lies in the Spanish province of Huesca, in the Posets-Maladeta Natural Park, close to the border with France.

Geography

Aneto forms part of the Maladeta massif, built of granite, and carries on its northern flank the largest glacier in the Pyrenees, covering roughly 80 hectares though shrinking steadily with climate change. The summit is crowned by a small cross and a statue of the Virgin of Pilar, and offers views across the whole chain.

Climbing

The first ascent was made on 18 July 1842 by the Russian officer Platon de Tchihatcheff with French guides, approaching from France. The normal route today starts from the Renclusa refuge (2,140 m) on the Spanish side, crossing the glacier to the ridge; the short final step, a narrow exposed section, is known as the Paso de Mahoma ("Muhammad's bridge") for the care it demands of climbers. Hundreds of climbers summit Aneto each summer, but the mountain remains dangerous, with frequent accidents on the glacier and ridge.

Name

The mountain takes its name from the village of Aneto in the valley below, on the other side of the massif from the usual approach.
