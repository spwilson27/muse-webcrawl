Surfactant

Surfactants are chemical compounds that decrease the surface tension or interfacial tension between two liquids, a liquid and a gas, or a liquid and a solid. The word is a blend of surface-active agent. Surfactant molecules are amphiphilic, containing both a hydrophilic head and a hydrophobic tail, which makes them effective in cleaning, wetting, emulsifying, foaming, and dispersing.

Classification

Surfactants are classified by the charge of their head group: anionic (e.g. sodium dodecyl sulfate, soaps), cationic (e.g. benzalkonium chloride, also used as disinfectants), non-ionic (e.g. polysorbates), and zwitterionic/amphoteric. In water, surfactants above a critical concentration aggregate into structures such as micelles.

Applications

Surfactants are key ingredients in detergents, soaps, shampoos, and toothpaste; they are also used in paints, inks, pharmaceuticals, oil recovery, and firefighting foams. In biology, pulmonary surfactant produced in the lungs reduces surface tension in the alveoli and prevents lung collapse; its absence in premature infants causes respiratory distress syndrome, treated with surfactant replacement therapy.

Environmental concerns

Some surfactants are toxic to aquatic life or persist in the environment; per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) used as surfactants have attracted particular regulatory concern.
