Card manipulation

Card manipulation is the branch of stage magic concerned with creating artistic effects using sleight of hand with playing cards. It ranges from the secret moves used in card tricks — passes, false shuffles, cuts, palming, and forces — to openly flourished displays of dexterity.

Techniques

Core sleights include the classic pass (secretly transposing cut packets), the double lift (showing two cards as one), palming (concealing a card in the hand), the second deal and bottom deal, and false shuffles that leave the deck order intact. Manipulators also produce cards from the air, fan them, and perform card springs and aerial catches as visual flourishes.

Cardistry

The purely visual, non-magical performance of flourishes is called cardistry (or card flourishing). Practitioners perform cuts such as the Charlier cut and Sybil, elaborate fans, cascades, and packet displays, shared widely through online video. Modern cardistry emphasises aesthetics and difficulty rather than deception.

Notable performers

Richard Turner, a blind American card mechanic, is famed for his mastery of gambling sleights. Ricky Jay was celebrated both as a performer and historian of card manipulation. Earlier masters such as Cardini built stage acts around silent, elegant productions of cards.
