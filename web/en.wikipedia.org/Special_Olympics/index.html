Special Olympics

Special Olympics
Founded: 1968
Founder: Eunice Kennedy Shriver
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Athletes: Millions in over 200 programmes worldwide

Special Olympics is a global sports movement for children and adults with intellectual disabilities, providing year-round training and athletic competition in a variety of Olympic-type sports. Founded by Eunice Kennedy Shriver, it held its first International Games in Chicago in 1968. Today it involves millions of athletes in more than 200 accredited programmes around the world, and it is distinct from the Paralympic Games, which serve athletes principally with physical disabilities and are held in conjunction with the Olympics.

History

Shriver, whose sister Rosemary had an intellectual disability, began by hosting "Camp Shriver" for children with disabilities at her Maryland home in 1962. With support from the Joseph P. Kennedy Jr. Foundation, the first International Special Olympics Summer Games took place at Soldier Field, Chicago, in July 1968, with about 1,000 athletes from the United States and Canada. The first World Winter Games followed in 1977 in Steamboat Springs, Colorado.

Programme

Special Olympics offers training and competition in more than 30 summer and winter sports, with athletes grouped by age and ability through "divisioning" so that contests are equitable. Its athlete oath is: "Let me win. But if I cannot win, let me be brave in the attempt." Beyond sport, the movement runs health screening programmes (Healthy Athletes), Unified Sports pairing athletes with and without disabilities, and leadership and anti-bullying initiatives in schools.

World Games

The Special Olympics World Games alternate between summer and winter editions roughly every four years in recent decades, with recent hosts including Abu Dhabi (2019), Berlin (2023, Summer) and Turin (2025, Winter).
