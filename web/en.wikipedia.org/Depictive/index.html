Depictive

A depictive is a secondary predicate in linguistics that describes the state of an argument (typically the subject or object) during the action denoted by the main verb. For example, in the sentence "She ate the fish raw", the adjective raw is an object-oriented depictive: it describes the state of the fish at the time of eating. In "He arrived exhausted", exhausted is a subject-oriented depictive.

Properties

Depictives differ from resultatives, which describe the state resulting from the event (as in "She hammered the metal flat"), and from attributive modifiers, which are not temporally tied to the event. Depictives are typically optional adjuncts, stage-level predicates, and may show agreement with their controller in languages with adjective agreement.

Cross-linguistic behaviour

Depictive constructions occur in many languages, including English, German, Russian and Japanese, though their syntactic analysis — whether as adjuncts, small clauses or complex predicates — remains debated among linguists.
