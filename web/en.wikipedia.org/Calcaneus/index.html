Calcaneus


The calcaneus of the left foot
Part ofTarsus (foot)
ArticulationsTalus, cuboid
AttachmentsAchilles tendon, plantar fascia

In humans and many other primates, the calcaneus (from Latin calcaneum, meaning heel) or heel bone is a bone of the tarsus of the foot which constitutes the heel. It is the largest of the tarsal bones and the largest bone of the foot. In some animals it forms the point of the hock.

Structure

The calcaneus is a roughly cuboid irregular bone. Its superior surface bears three facets articulating with the talus; its anterior surface articulates with the cuboid. The posterior tuberosity receives the Achilles tendon (tendo calcaneus), the largest tendon in the body, while the plantar surface anchors the plantar fascia and intrinsic foot muscles. A shelf-like projection, the sustentaculum tali, supports the talus medially.

Function and clinical significance

The calcaneus acts as a lever for the calf muscles and transmits body weight to the ground, bearing the brunt of heel strike in walking and running. Calcaneal fractures—typically from falls from height—are the commonest tarsal fractures and may involve the subtalar joint. Heel spurs, retrocalcaneal bursitis, and Sever's disease (calcaneal apophysitis in children) are other common conditions.
