Higher education in Quebec

Higher education in Quebec differs from the systems of other provinces in Canada. Following secondary school (which ends at grade 11), students attend a college-level institution known as a CEGEP (Collège d'enseignement général et professionnel) before university. Quebec's system was created during the Quiet Revolution of the 1960s, following the recommendations of the Parent Commission, with the first CEGEPs opening in 1967 in order to democratise access to post-secondary education.

Structure

CEGEPs offer two-year pre-university programs and three-year technical (career) programs; public CEGEPs charge no tuition to Quebec residents. Because of the CEGEP year, Quebec bachelor's degrees typically take three years, making the total school path the same length as in the rest of Canada. The province's universities include the Université du Québec network (founded 1968), Université de Montréal, Université Laval, Université de Sherbrooke, McGill University and Concordia University, among others. Quebec university tuition has historically been the lowest in Canada, and government attempts to raise it triggered the 2012 Quebec student protests (the "Maple Spring").
