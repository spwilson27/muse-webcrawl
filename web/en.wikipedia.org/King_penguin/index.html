The king penguin (Aptenodytes patagonicus) is the second largest species of penguin, smaller than but similar in appearance to the emperor penguin. King penguins breed on subantarctic islands at the northern reaches of Antarctica and on South Georgia, Falkland Islands and other temperate islands in the region.

Description

Adults stand 85 to 95 cm tall and weigh 11 to 16 kg. The plumage is silver-grey on the back and white on the front, with vivid orange-gold patches on the sides of the head and upper breast. Chicks are covered in brown down. The bill is long, black and slightly down-curved, with orange-pink sides to the lower mandible.

Distribution and habitat

Major breeding colonies are found on South Georgia, the Crozet Islands, Prince Edward Islands, Kerguelen Islands, Macquarie Island and Heard Island. The total population is estimated at over two million breeding pairs. Birds forage in the Southern Ocean, often travelling hundreds of kilometres on a single trip.

Behaviour and diet

King penguins dive to depths of 100–300 metres in search of lanternfish, squid and krill. They are strong swimmers, using their flippers for propulsion. On land they walk upright or toboggan on their bellies across snow and ice.

Breeding

The breeding cycle lasts 14 to 16 months, unusually long for a bird, so pairs can raise at most two chicks in three years. A single egg is incubated on the parents' feet under a fold of skin called the brood pouch, with parents taking turns for about 54 days. Chicks gather in crèches during winter while both parents forage.

Conservation

The species is listed as Least Concern, though climate change shifting prey further south is a long-term concern for colonies at the northern edge of the range.
