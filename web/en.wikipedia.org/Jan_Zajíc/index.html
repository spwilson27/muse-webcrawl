Jan Zajíc (3 July 1950 – 25 February 1969) was a Czech student who set himself on fire in Prague in protest against the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia and the onset of political "normalization". He was the second such protester, following Jan Palach, who had immolated himself six weeks earlier.

Background

Zajíc was born in Vítkov in northern Moravia and studied at the railway technical school in Šumperk. Deeply affected by the August 1968 invasion that crushed the Prague Spring, he travelled to Prague for Jan Palach's funeral in January 1969 and took part in a hunger strike by students near the National Museum. He called himself "Torch No. 2", carrying on Palach's sign of protest.

Self-immolation

Zajíc chose 25 February 1969 — the 21st anniversary of the Communist coup d'état of 1948 — for his act. In the entryway of a building at 39 Wenceslas Square in Prague, he drank acid to prevent himself from crying out, then set his fuel-soaked clothes alight. He collapsed before reaching the square and died shortly afterwards. In his farewell letters he urged his countrymen not to give in to apathy and demanded the abolition of censorship and the banning of the occupation's newspaper Zprávy, writing: "Remember Jan [Palach]. ... Do not be indifferent to the day when the light of the truth will shine."

Aftermath and remembrance

The communist authorities tried to suppress his memory; his grave in Vítkov was watched and in 1973 his remains were exhumed at police insistence and cremated to prevent pilgrimages. After the Velvet Revolution, Zajíc was publicly honoured: a memorial was erected in Šumperk, streets were named after him, and he was posthumously awarded the Order of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk. The memorial to Palach and Zajíc stands on Wenceslas Square outside the National Museum.
