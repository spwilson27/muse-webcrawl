The 24 Hours Nürburgring (German: ADAC RAVENOL 24h Nürburgring) is a 24-hour endurance race for touring cars and GT cars held annually since 1970 on the Nürburgring in Germany. The race uses a combined layout of the Grand Prix circuit and the legendary Nordschleife, producing a lap of more than 25 kilometres — one of the longest racing circuits in the world.

Format

Unlike most professional endurance races, the event mixes professional factory teams with amateur racers. Fields regularly exceed 150 cars across more than twenty classes, from top-tier SP9 (GT3) machines to near-production hatchbacks. Up to three or four drivers share each car, and around 200,000 spectators camp around the Nordschleife for the festival-like weekend.

History and records

Porsche is the most successful manufacturer at the event, while Manthey Racing is its most successful team. The race has been interrupted by weather on several occasions, most famously by fog in 2018 and torrential rain in 2024, when it was shortened to the shortest running in its history. Overall winners receive one of the most coveted prizes in GT racing, judged by many drivers as harder to win than comparable 24-hour races because of the circuit's danger, traffic, and variable microclimates.
