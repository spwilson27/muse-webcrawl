Authentication (philosophy)

Authentication
FieldPhilosophy, aesthetics, ethics
Key thinkersCharles Taylor; Lionel Trilling (related)
Related conceptsAuthenticity, identity, sincerity

In philosophy, authentication refers to the process by which an identity, self or work is established or confirmed as authentic — genuinely what it purports to be. The notion is closely connected with the ideal of authenticity, especially in existentialist and moral philosophy.

Ethics of authenticity

The Canadian philosopher Charles Taylor, in The Ethics of Authenticity (1991), defended a moderated ideal of authenticity: the idea that each person has an original way of being human and should define their own identity, while recognising that identity is formed dialogically, against shared horizons of significance. Taylor criticised both a purely self-centred individualism and cultural relativism.

Other uses

In aesthetics and the study of art, authentication concerns the procedures by which works, manuscripts and artefacts are verified as genuine products of an author or period, a process with legal and commercial as well as philosophical dimensions.
