Sexual reproduction is a type of reproduction that involves a complex life cycle in which a gamete (such as a sperm or egg cell) with a single set of chromosomes (haploid) combines with another gamete to produce a zygote that develops into an organism composed of cells with two sets of chromosomes (diploid). It is the predominant form of reproduction in eukaryotes, including nearly all animals and plants.

Mechanism

The two key processes are meiosis, a specialised cell division that halves the chromosome number while shuffling genes through crossing over and independent assortment, and fertilisation, the fusion of two gametes which restores the diploid number. Meiosis originated in early eukaryotes, probably over a billion years ago, possibly as a by-product of DNA repair mechanisms.

Evolutionary significance

Sex is costly — an asexual female can produce twice as many reproducing descendants (the "twofold cost of sex") — yet it persists because genetic recombination generates variation, allowing populations to adapt to changing environments and parasites (the Red Queen hypothesis) and to purge harmful mutations (avoiding Muller's ratchet). Sexual selection, arising from competition for mates, further shapes the evolution of sexually reproducing species.
