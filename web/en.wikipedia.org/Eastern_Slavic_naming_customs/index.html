Eastern Slavic naming customs

Eastern Slavic names
Structure: Given name + patronymic + family name
Used in: Russia, Ukraine, Belarus

Eastern Slavic naming customs are the traditional ways of identifying a person in Russia, Ukraine and Belarus. A full name consists of three parts: a given (first) name, a patronymic derived from the father's given name, and a family name (surname).

Patronymic

The patronymic is formed from the father's first name with a suffix: for men, -ovich, -evich or -ich in Russian (Ukrainian -ovych); for women, -ovna, -evna or -ichna (-ivna in Ukrainian). For example, the son of Ivan is Ivanovich and the daughter is Ivanovna. In formal and polite address, a person is addressed by first name plus patronymic.

Family names

Family names came into general use among peasants only in the 19th century. Many are derived from occupations, patronymics or place names, with common endings such as -ov/-ev, -in, -sky and Ukrainian -enko and -chuk. Most surnames have distinct masculine and feminine forms (e.g., Ivanov / Ivanova).

Diminutives

Given names have rich systems of diminutive and affectionate forms — for example, Aleksandr yields Sasha and Shura, while Ekaterina yields Katya — used among family and close friends.
