Dolly (sheep)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dolly (5 July 1996 – 14 February 2003) was a female Finn-Dorset sheep and the first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell. She was cloned by Keith Campbell, Ian Wilmut and colleagues at the Roslin Institute, part of the University of Edinburgh, Scotland, and the biotechnology company PPL Therapeutics, based near Edinburgh. Dolly's birth proved that specialised cells could be reprogrammed to create an entire organism.

Birth

Dolly was created using somatic cell nuclear transfer (SCNT): the nucleus of an adult mammary-gland cell from a six-year-old Finn Dorset ewe was transferred into an enucleated oocyte (egg cell) from a Scottish Blackface ewe, and the resulting embryo was carried by a surrogate Scottish Blackface mother. Of 277 attempts, only one embryo survived to term. She was named after the American singer Dolly Parton, because the donor cell came from a mammary gland.

Life

Dolly lived her entire life at the Roslin Institute. She was bred with a Welsh Mountain ram and produced six lambs in total, the first, Bonnie, born in April 1998. Her birth was announced in February 1997 and attracted worldwide media attention and intense public debate about the ethics of cloning.

Death and legacy

Dolly was euthanised on 14 February 2003, aged six, because of a progressive lung disease (sheep pulmonary adenomatosis) and severe arthritis. Her preserved body is exhibited at the National Museum of Scotland in Edinburgh. Her cloning demonstrated that the nucleus of a differentiated adult cell retains the full genetic information needed to direct development, a finding that helped pave the way for induced pluripotent stem cell research. Four further clones from the same donor cell line, the "Dolly sisters", were reported healthy in old age in 2016.
