Ahu Tongariki

Location: Hanga Nui, Easter Island (Rapa Nui), Chile
Platform length: about 220 m (720 ft)
Moai: 15

Ahu Tongariki is the largest ahu — a stone ceremonial platform — on Easter Island (Rapa Nui). It stands at Hanga Nui bay near the Rano Raraku quarry and carries fifteen moai statues, including one of the heaviest ever erected, weighing about 86 tonnes. The platform is approximately 220 metres long, making it the largest megalithic structure in Polynesia.

History

The ahu was built during the island's statue-building period. Its moai were toppled during the island's civil conflicts in the eighteenth century. In 1960, an earthquake off Chile triggered a tsunami that swept across Hanga Nui and scattered the already fallen statues and masonry up to hundreds of metres inland.

Restoration

Between 1992 and 1995 the site was restored by a team led by Chilean archaeologists Claudio Cristino and Patricia Vargas with support from the Japanese crane company Tadano Ltd., which supplied a large crane to re-erect the fifteen moai on their platform. The project returned Ahu Tongariki to something close to its original configuration, and it is now one of the island's most visited and photographed sites, particularly at sunrise, when the sun rises behind the statues as seen from the front.

The site lies within Rapa Nui National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
