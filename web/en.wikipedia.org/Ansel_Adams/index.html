Ansel Adams

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ansel Easton Adams (February 20, 1902 – April 22, 1984) was an American landscape photographer and environmentalist known for his black-and-white images of the American West, especially Yosemite National Park. His sharply focused, high-contrast photographs helped establish photography as a fine art.

Career

Adams first visited Yosemite in 1916 and returned throughout his life. In 1932 he co-founded the Group f/64 with Edward Weston and Imogen Cunningham, championing "straight photography" with maximum depth of field. With Fred Archer he developed the Zone System, a method of pre-visualising exposure and development to control tonal range. Famous works include Monolith, the Face of Half Dome (1927), Moonrise, Hernandez, New Mexico (1941) and Clearing Winter Storm (c. 1944).

Conservation and legacy

A longtime director of the Sierra Club, Adams used his photographs to lobby for wilderness protection and the expansion of the national parks. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1980. A wilderness area and a mountain peak in California are named for him, and his images remain among the most reproduced in photographic history.
