Twitter

Twitter is an American microblogging and social networking service on which users publish and interact with messages known as "tweets". Founded in 2006 by Jack Dorsey, Noah Glass, Biz Stone, and Evan Williams, it grew into one of the world's most visited websites and a central platform for breaking news, political communication, and public conversation. Registered users can post, like, and repost ("retweet") messages; posts were originally limited to 140 characters, doubled to 280 in 2017.

History

Twitter originated as a side project at the podcasting company Odeo. Dorsey's idea of an SMS-based status service was developed in early 2006, and the first tweet was sent by Dorsey on March 21, 2006. The service launched publicly that July and spun off into its own company in 2007. Its popularity surged during the 2007 South by Southwest festival, and hashtag and @-reply conventions introduced by users became core features. Twitter held its initial public offering in November 2013.

Acquisition and rebrand

In October 2022, businessman Elon Musk acquired Twitter for approximately US$44 billion after a protracted legal dispute over his attempt to withdraw from the deal. Under Musk, the company laid off much of its workforce, revised content-moderation and verification policies, and in July 2023 was rebranded as X, retiring the bird logo and the word "tweet" in official usage, though the old terms remain widespread.

Features and usage

Core features include the home timeline, hashtags for topic grouping, mentions, direct messages, lists, and live audio conversations (Spaces). The platform has been credited with a role in protest movements and rapid news dissemination, and criticised for harassment, misinformation, and bot activity. Heads of state, journalists, and celebrities use it as a primary channel for public statements.
