Samgyeopsal

Samgyeopsal (Korean: 삼겹살, lit. "three-layer meat") is a popular Korean dish consisting of thick slices of pork belly that are grilled, usually at the table, and eaten wrapped in lettuce or perilla leaves with rice and condiments. It is one of the most common dishes in Korean barbecue restaurants.

TypeGrilled meat dish
Place of originSouth Korea
Main ingredientsPork belly
AccompanimentsLettuce, perilla leaves, garlic, ssamjang, kimchi

Preparation and eating

Unmarinated slabs of pork belly, showing alternating layers of meat and fat, are grilled on a metal plate or grate, often tilted so rendered fat drains away. Diners cut the cooked meat with scissors and place a piece in a lettuce or perilla leaf (ssam) together with steamed rice, sliced garlic, green chillies and ssamjang, a paste of doenjang and gochujang. Grilled kimchi, mushrooms and onions frequently share the grill. The wrap is eaten in a single bite.

Popularity

Samgyeopsal rose to mass popularity in South Korea in the 1990s and 2000s, aided by low pork belly prices and company dining culture; 3 March (3/3) is informally celebrated as Samgyeopsal Day. The dish has spread internationally with the growth of Korean barbecue restaurants abroad.
