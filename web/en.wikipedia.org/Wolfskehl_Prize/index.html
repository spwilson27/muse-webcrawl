Wolfskehl Prize

The Wolfskehl Prize was a monetary prize offered by the Göttingen Academy of Sciences for a proof of Fermat's Last Theorem. It was endowed by Paul Wolfskehl (1856–1906), a German physician and mathematician from Darmstadt.

History

According to a popular story, Wolfskehl, despairing over an unhappy love affair, planned to kill himself at midnight but spent the night reading Ernst Kummer's work on Fermat's Last Theorem, finding a gap in the reasoning that kept him occupied until dawn — past the appointed hour. He rewrote his will to leave 100,000 marks for the prize. The story's details are uncertain, but the bequest is documented: the prize was announced in 1908, to remain open until 13 September 2007.

Resolution

The prize attracted thousands of incorrect submissions from amateurs. Inflation after the First World War greatly reduced its real value. In June 1997, Andrew Wiles, whose 1994 proof (completed with Richard Taylor) resolved the theorem, received the prize, then worth about 75,000 Deutschmarks.
