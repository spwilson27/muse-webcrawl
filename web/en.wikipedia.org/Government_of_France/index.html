The Government of France, officially the Government of the French Republic, is the executive branch of the French Republic. Under the Constitution of the Fifth Republic, adopted in 1958, executive power is shared between the President of the Republic and the Government led by the Prime Minister—a system often described as semi-presidential.

Composition

The Government consists of the Prime Minister and the ministers, secretaries of state, and ministerial delegates appointed by the President on the Prime Minister's proposal. The Prime Minister directs the action of the Government, is responsible for national defence administration and the execution of laws, and holds regulatory power. The Council of Ministers, chaired by the President, meets weekly at the Élysée Palace.

Powers and role

The Government determines and conducts the policy of the nation, controls the civil service and the armed forces' administration, and is accountable to Parliament. The National Assembly can dismiss the Government through a motion of censure. When the President and the parliamentary majority belong to opposing camps, France experiences "cohabitation," in which the Prime Minister's domestic authority expands considerably—as occurred in 1986–1988, 1993–1995, and 1997–2002.

Administration

France is a unitary state, though decentralization laws since the 1980s have given significant powers to regions, departments, and communes. The senior civil service is dominated by graduates of the grandes écoles, historically the École nationale d'administration (ENA), replaced in 2022 by the Institut national du service public (INSP).
