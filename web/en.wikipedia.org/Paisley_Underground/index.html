Paisley Underground

The Paisley Underground was a music scene centred in Los Angeles, California in the early 1980s, associated with a revival of 1960s psychedelic rock, folk rock, and garage rock. The term was coined by Michael Quercio of the band The Three O'Clock during a radio interview, as a wry comment on the scene's retro fashion sense — "paisley" being a hallmark pattern of 1960s psychedelic style.

Bands and sound

Key bands included The Dream Syndicate, The Bangles, The Three O'Clock, Rain Parade, Green on Red, The Long Ryders, and Opal (later Mazzy Star). Their music drew on The Byrds, The Velvet Underground, Buffalo Springfield, Love, and The Doors, typically combining jangling guitars, droning textures, and melancholic or dreamlike lyrics. Steve Wynn's Dream Syndicate, with the album The Days of Wine and Roses (1982), was a critical touchstone, while The Bangles achieved mainstream pop success later in the decade.

Context and legacy

The scene emerged partly in reaction to punk and hardcore, reclaiming melody and 1960s influences while keeping an underground ethos. Members of the bands frequently collaborated and shared bills at clubs such as the Music Machine. The Paisley Underground influenced later jangle pop, alternative rock, and the 1980s neo-psychedelia movement in Britain. A reunion tour featuring several of the principal bands took place in 2013.

Relationship with Prince

The scene's name coincidentally echoed Prince's Paisley Park label and aesthetic; Prince was an admirer, signing The Bangles' "Manic Monday" (which he wrote) and taking The Three O'Clock's contemporaries under his influence, though the Los Angeles scene predated and developed independently of Paisley Park.
