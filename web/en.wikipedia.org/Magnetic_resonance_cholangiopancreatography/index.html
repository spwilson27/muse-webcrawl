Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) is a medical imaging technique that uses magnetic resonance imaging to visualise the biliary and pancreatic ducts in a non-invasive manner. It is used to diagnose conditions such as gallstones in the bile ducts, strictures, congenital abnormalities, and tumours, and to plan surgery.

An MRCP image: static fluid in the ducts appears bright against a suppressed background.

Technique

MRCP uses heavily T2-weighted MRI sequences in which stationary or slowly moving fluid — bile and pancreatic juice — appears very bright, while surrounding tissues give little signal. No contrast agent, ionising radiation, or instrumentation of the ducts is required. Images are often acquired in thick slabs and as maximum-intensity projections to display the whole duct system in a single picture.

Comparison with ERCP

Unlike endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), which involves endoscopy, cannulation of the ducts, and a risk of pancreatitis, MRCP is purely diagnostic and essentially risk-free. ERCP retains advantages when therapy — such as stone extraction or stent placement — is anticipated, so in modern practice MRCP is generally used first for diagnosis, reserving ERCP for intervention.
