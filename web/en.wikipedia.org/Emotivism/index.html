Emotivism

Emotivism is a theory in meta-ethics claiming that moral judgments do not state facts but express emotional attitudes. On this view, saying "murder is wrong" is akin to saying "boo to murder" — it expresses disapproval and seeks to influence others, rather than describing a moral property.

Development

Emotivism emerged within logical positivism in the 1930s, notably in A. J. Ayer's Language, Truth and Logic (1936). Charles Stevenson developed a more sophisticated version distinguishing descriptive and emotive meaning and analyzing moral disagreement as disagreement in attitude.

Arguments and criticism

Supporters argued moral language cannot be verified empirically like factual statements. Critics raised the Frege–Geach problem: moral sentences appear in unasserted contexts such as conditionals, where they do not seem merely to express emotion, challenging non-cognitivist accounts.
