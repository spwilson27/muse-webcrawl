Páramo

The páramo is a high-altitude neotropical ecosystem found in the Andes of South America, between the upper forest line (around 3,100 m) and the permanent snow line (around 5,000 m). It is sometimes described as "tropical alpine tundra" and consists of mostly grassland, bogs, and shrubland adapted to cold, wet, and intensely sunny conditions.

Geography and ecology

Páramos occur from Venezuela and Colombia through Ecuador to northern Peru, with related formations in Costa Rica and Panama. Characteristic plants include tussock grasses and giant rosettes such as the frailejones (Espeletia), whose hairy leaves harvest moisture from clouds. Wildlife includes the spectacled bear, mountain tapir, and many endemic hummingbirds. The ecosystem hosts an extraordinary concentration of endemic species that evolved rapidly as the Andes rose.

Water and conservation

Páramo soils act like sponges, capturing cloud and rain water and releasing it steadily to streams that supply cities such as Bogotá and Quito — making páramos critical "water factories". They are threatened by agriculture, grazing, mining, pine plantations, and climate change, and are protected by law in Colombia and Ecuador.
