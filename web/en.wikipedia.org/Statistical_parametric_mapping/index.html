Statistical parametric mapping (SPM) is a statistical technique for analysing functional neuroimaging data — such as fMRI, PET, and EEG/MEG recordings — in order to identify brain regions whose activity differs between conditions or correlates with experimental variables. The term also denotes the widely used software package of the same name, developed under Karl Friston at the Wellcome Centre for Human Neuroimaging at University College London.

Method

In an SPM analysis, brain images from multiple subjects or sessions are realigned, spatially normalized into a common stereotactic space (such as MNI space), and smoothed. A general linear model is then fitted independently at every voxel, with regressors describing the experimental design. Contrasts between conditions produce statistical parametric maps — volumes of test statistics (t or F) — which are thresholded to locate significant activations, correcting for the many thousands of simultaneous comparisons using random field theory or false-discovery-rate procedures.

Related techniques

SPM underlies related voxel-wise methods such as voxel-based morphometry (VBM), used to compare grey-matter volume between groups. The SPM software, written in MATLAB, is distributed freely to the neuroimaging community and has been a standard analysis platform since the 1990s, together with comparable packages such as FSL and AFNI.
