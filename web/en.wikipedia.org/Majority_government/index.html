Majority government

A majority government is a government formed by a political party (or coalition) that holds an absolute majority of seats in a legislature. Such a government can normally pass legislation and survive votes of confidence without support from other parties.

Characteristics

Majority governments are generally more stable and durable than minority governments and can implement their programme more readily. Critics argue they can concentrate power, particularly in parliamentary systems where the executive controls the legislature's agenda.

Formation

Majorities may arise from a single party winning more than half the seats, or from a coalition of parties whose combined seats exceed half. In first-past-the-post systems, a party may win a majority of seats with well under half the popular vote.

Contrasts

The opposite is a minority government, which must negotiate support from other parties. A "hung parliament" describes an election result in which no party wins an outright majority.
