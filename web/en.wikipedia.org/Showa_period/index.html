Shōwa era

Period: 25 December 1926 – 7 January 1989
Emperor: Hirohito (Emperor Shōwa)
Preceded by: Taishō era
Followed by: Heisei era

The Shōwa era (昭和時代) was the period of Japanese history corresponding to the reign of Emperor Shōwa (Hirohito), from 25 December 1926 until his death on 7 January 1989. At over 62 years it was the longest reign of any Japanese emperor, spanning militarism and war, defeat and occupation, and Japan's rise to become the world's second-largest economy.

War and occupation

The first part of the era saw growing military influence, the invasion of Manchuria (1931) and China (1937), and entry into the Second World War with the attack on Pearl Harbor in 1941. Japan's defeat in 1945, after the atomic bombings and Soviet entry into the war, was followed by the Allied occupation, a new constitution, and Hirohito's renunciation of divinity.

Postwar recovery

From the 1950s Japan experienced rapid economic growth — the "Japanese economic miracle" — symbolised by the 1964 Tokyo Olympics and the Shinkansen. By the 1980s Japan was an industrial and financial superpower, though the decade ended with an asset price bubble that burst shortly after the era closed.
