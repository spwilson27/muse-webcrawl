Brighton and Hove

Brighton and Hove
County: East Sussex, England
Status: City and unitary authority (city status 2000)
Population: approx. 277,000 (2021 census)
Constituent towns: Brighton, Hove, Portslade, Rottingdean

Brighton and Hove is a city and unitary authority area on the south coast of England, in the county of East Sussex. It was formed as a unitary authority in 1997 from the formerly separate boroughs of Brighton and Hove, and was granted city status in 2000 as part of the millennium celebrations. The conurbation stretches along the English Channel coast at the foot of the South Downs.

History

Brighton grew from the fishing village of Brighthelmstone into a fashionable seaside resort in the 18th century, patronised by the Prince Regent, whose Royal Pavilion — built in an Indo-Saracenic style by John Nash — remains the city's most famous landmark. The arrival of the railway in 1841 made Brighton an easy day trip from London, and it developed a bohemian, tolerant reputation that persists. Hove, to the west, developed as a more sedate residential town of Regency squares and crescents, such as Brunswick Square and Adelaide Crescent.

Geography and economy

The city lies about 47 miles (76 km) south of London. Its economy is based on tourism and conferences, creative and digital industries, education (the University of Sussex and the University of Brighton), and a large LGBTQ+ community centred on the Kemp Town and St James's Street areas. Landmarks include the Palace Pier (opened 1899), the West Pier (ruined by fire and storms), the British Airways i360 observation tower (opened 2016), and the shingle beach.

Sport

The city is home to Brighton & Hove Albion F.C., who play at Falmer Stadium, and to Sussex County Cricket Club, based at the County Cricket Ground in Hove.
