The FIS Cross-Country World Cup is an annual cross-country skiing competition, arranged by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS) since 1981 (with unofficial series from 1973). The competition is a points-scored tour of races across Europe and North America, held from late autumn to early spring, and it forms the principal season-long circuit in the sport, ranking alongside the Winter Olympics and the FIS Nordic World Ski Championships.

Competition format

Events include individual and team sprints, distance races in classical and freestyle technique with interval and mass starts, skiathlons, relays, and team sprints. Points are awarded to the top finishers of each race — 100 points for a win under the traditional scale — and accumulate toward overall, distance, and sprint crystal globe standings, as well as the Nations Cup. Stage races modelled on cycling's grand tours are a distinctive feature: the Tour de Ski, held around New Year since 2006–07 and ending with a climb up the Alpe Cermis ski slope, and the season-opening Ruka Triple / Nordic Opening mini-tour, are scored both as events in themselves and toward the overall standings.

History and notable athletes

Norwegian skiers have dominated the World Cup. Bjørn Dæhlie won six overall men's titles in the 1990s; in later years Petter Northug, Martin Johnsrud Sundby, and Johannes Høsflot Klæbo — who became the most successful male sprinter in the circuit's history — led the standings. Among women, Yelena Välbe, Marit Bjørgen, Justyna Kowalczyk, Therese Johaug, and American Jessie Diggins are among the most successful overall champions. The calendar has survived disruptions such as the COVID-19 pandemic's truncated seasons, and Russian and Belarusian athletes have been excluded since 2022 following the invasion of Ukraine.
