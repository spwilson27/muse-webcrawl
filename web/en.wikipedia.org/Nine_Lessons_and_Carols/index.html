Nine Lessons and Carols

The Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols is a service of Christian worship traditionally celebrated on or near Christmas Eve. The story of the fall of humanity, the promise of the Messiah, and the birth of Jesus is told in nine short Bible readings (lessons) from Genesis, the prophetic books, and the Gospels, interspersed with the singing of Christmas carols, hymns, and choir anthems.

Origins and King's College

The format was devised by Edward Benson, then Bishop of Truro, for use on Christmas Eve 1880 in a makeshift cathedral in Cornwall. It was famously adapted by Eric Milner-White for King's College Chapel, Cambridge, where it was first held on Christmas Eve 1918 as a way of marking the end of the First World War. The King's service has been broadcast by the BBC since 1928 and is now heard worldwide; it traditionally opens with a solo chorister singing the first verse of "Once in Royal David's City", chosen only moments before the service begins.

Structure

The nine lessons trace the narrative from the Fall and the promises to Abraham and the prophets, to the Annunciation, the Nativity, the visit of the shepherds and Magi, and the prologue of John's Gospel. Lessons are read by members of the college and city community, and the service includes the bidding prayer written by Milner-White.
