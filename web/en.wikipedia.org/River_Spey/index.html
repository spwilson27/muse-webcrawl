River Spey

The River Spey (Scottish Gaelic: Uisge Spè) is a river in the Scottish Highlands, flowing from Loch Spey in Lochaber north-east to the Moray Firth at Spey Bay. At about 172 km (107 mi) it is among Scotland's longest rivers, and it is noted as one of the fastest-flowing large rivers in the country.

Course and catchment

The Spey passes through Newtonmore, Kingussie, Aviemore, Grantown-on-Spey, and Rothes before entering the sea near Kingston. Its catchment, Strathspey, is famous for salmon fishing and for the concentration of whisky distilleries along its banks — more than half of Scotland's malt whisky distilleries lie within the Speyside region.

Wildlife and recreation

The river supports Atlantic salmon, sea trout, and freshwater pearl mussels, and much of its length is protected as a Site of Special Scientific Interest and Special Area of Conservation. The Speyside Way long-distance path follows the valley.
