Nouméa is the capital and largest city of New Caledonia, a French special collectivity in the South Pacific. It sits on a hilly peninsula at the southern end of Grande Terre, the main island, protected by barrier reefs and lagoons listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site.

History

Founded in 1854 as Port-de-France shortly after France took possession of the archipelago, the settlement became a penal colony administrative centre and later grew with the nickel-mining industry. During the Second World War it served as a major US naval and military base in the Pacific.

Geography and climate

Nouméa has a tropical climate moderated by trade winds, with a warm wet season and a cooler dry season. Its bays — including Anse Vata and the Baie des Citrons — are centres of beach tourism.

Landmarks

Sights include the Tjibaou Cultural Centre, designed by Renzo Piano and opened in 1998 to honour Kanak culture, the Nouméa Cathedral, and the Amédée Lighthouse offshore.
