The Return of the Condor Heroes

AuthorJin Yong (Louis Cha)
Original title神鵰俠侶 (Shén Diāo Xiá Lǚ)
Serialised1959–1961, Ming Pao
GenreWuxia

The Return of the Condor Heroes, also known in some translations as The Giant Eagle and Its Companion, is a wuxia novel by Jin Yong (Louis Cha). It was first serialised between 20 May 1959 and 5 July 1961 in the Hong Kong newspaper Ming Pao, which Jin Yong had co-founded. It is the second part of the Condor Trilogy, following The Legend of the Condor Heroes and preceding The Heaven Sword and Dragon Saber.

Plot

Set during the Mongol invasion of the Southern Song dynasty, the novel centres on Yang Guo, the orphaned son of the villain Yang Kang from the preceding novel. Taken in briefly by Guo Jing and Huang Rong at Peach Blossom Island and then ill-treated at the Quanzhen school on Mount Zhongnan, Yang Guo finds a true master in Xiaolongnü ("Little Dragon Girl"), the icy young mistress of the Ancient Tomb sect, who teaches him her school's arts in the tomb beneath the mountain. Their deepening love violates the taboo against romance between master and disciple and draws the condemnation of the orthodox martial world, forcing the lovers through separations, poisonings and a sixteen-year parting before their reunion, set against the defence of Xiangyang against the Mongols.

Yang Guo's other companion is a giant divine condor, the former friend of the sword master Dugu Qiubai, whose training grounds forge Yang Guo's melancholic "Sad Palms" technique after he loses an arm.

Themes and reception

The novel is the most romantic of Jin Yong's works, and its defence of a love that defies social convention — and its unusually strong, detached heroine — made it among the most beloved and debated of his books. It has been adapted into films and television series many times across Hong Kong, Taiwan, China and Singapore, most famously the 1983 TVB series with Andy Lau and Idy Chan and the 1995 TVB version with Louis Koo and Carman Lee.
