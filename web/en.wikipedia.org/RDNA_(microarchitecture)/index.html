RDNA (microarchitecture)


Placeholder image: RDNA (microarchitecture)

RDNA (Radeon DNA) is a graphics processing unit (GPU) microarchitecture and accompanying instruction set architecture developed by AMD as the successor to its Graphics Core Next (GCN) architecture. It was first launched in 2019 with the Radeon RX 5000 series.

Design

RDNA restructured the compute units of GCN, widening wavefronts and improving instruction throughput per clock. Dual compute units, an improved geometry pipeline, and a multi-level cache hierarchy were introduced to raise performance per watt, particularly for gaming workloads.

Generations

RDNA 2 (2020) added hardware-accelerated ray tracing, variable rate shading, and support in the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S consoles. RDNA 3 (2022) introduced a chiplet design separating graphics and memory-cache dies. RDNA 4 followed in 2025 with improved ray tracing and AI acceleration.

Products

The architecture powers AMD's discrete Radeon RX graphics cards, integrated graphics in Ryzen processors, and semi-custom chips for game consoles.
