Buffy the Vampire Slayer is an American supernatural drama television series created by Joss Whedon, based on the 1992 film of the same name. It stars Sarah Michelle Gellar as Buffy Summers, a teenage girl chosen by fate to battle vampires, demons and other forces of darkness in the fictional California town of Sunnydale, which sits atop a portal to hell known as the Hellmouth. She is aided by her friends — collectively the "Scooby Gang" — and her mentor, Watcher Rupert Giles.

Overview

The series ran for seven seasons and 144 episodes and used its horror elements as metaphors for the difficulties of adolescence and young adulthood: high school is literally hell in the first three seasons, while later seasons deal with college, work, depression and loss. Landmark episodes include "Hush" (season 4), performed largely in silence; the musical episode "Once More, with Feeling" (season 6); and "The Body" (season 5), a stark depiction of grief.

Reception and legacy

Although modest in ratings, the series was a major critical success and is frequently cited among the greatest television series of all time. It spawned the spin-off Angel (1999–2004), comic book continuations, and an entire academic field sometimes called "Buffy studies", analysing the show's treatment of feminism, philosophy and narrative structure.
