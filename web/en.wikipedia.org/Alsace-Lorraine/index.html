Alsace-Lorraine

Alsace-Lorraine (German: Elsass-Lothringen) is a historical territory in eastern France, comprising most of Alsace and the Moselle department of Lorraine. It is best known as the Imperial Territory (Reichsland) that the German Empire annexed from France after the Franco-Prussian War in 1871 and that France recovered after the First World War in 1918.

Annexation and German rule

Under the Treaty of Frankfurt (1871), France ceded Alsace (except Belfort) and northern Lorraine, including Metz, to the new German Empire. The annexation — justified in Germany by language and history, resented in France as an amputation — became the central grievance of French revanchism for nearly half a century. Administered first from Berlin and later granted its own constitution and diet in 1911, the Reichsland had about 1.87 million inhabitants in 1910, the great majority German-speaking, though a significant Francophone minority remained, especially in Lorraine.

Twentieth century

The territory returned to France under the Treaty of Versailles in 1919 without a plebiscite. Nazi Germany annexed it de facto again in 1940, conscripting some 130,000 Alsatians and Mosellans into the Wehrmacht (the malgré-nous), before it reverted to France in 1945. Since the war the region's cross-border identity has made Strasbourg a symbol of Franco-German reconciliation and seat of the European Parliament. Alsace-Moselle retains distinctive local law, including the Concordat and Good Friday as a public holiday.
