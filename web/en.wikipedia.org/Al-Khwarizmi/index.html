Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī (c. 780 – c. 850) was a Persian polymath of the Islamic Golden Age who produced influential works in mathematics, astronomy, and geography. Working at the House of Wisdom in Baghdad under the caliph al-Ma'mun, he helped shape the development of algebra as an independent discipline; the word algebra derives from the title of his most famous book, and the word algorithm derives from the Latinised form of his name, Algorithmi.

Mathematics

His Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion and Balancing (Al-Kitāb al-mukhtaṣar fī ḥisāb al-jabr wa'l-muqābala, c. 820) presented the first systematic solution of linear and quadratic equations, treating them through the operations of "completion" (al-jabr) and "balancing". His book on Hindu numerals, known in Latin as Algoritmi de numero Indorum, introduced the decimal positional number system — including the concept of zero — to the Latin West, where its methods eventually displaced Roman numerals and the abacus.

Astronomy and geography

Al-Khwarizmi compiled astronomical tables (a zīj) based on Indian and Persian sources, and his Book of the Description of the Earth revised Ptolemy's geography with corrected coordinates for thousands of localities, accompanied by some of the earliest world maps of the Islamic tradition. The lunar crater Al-Khwarizmi is named in his honour.
