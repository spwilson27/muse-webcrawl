Liberalia

The Liberalia was an ancient Roman festival celebrated on 17 March in honour of Liber Pater and his consort Libera, gods of fertility, wine and freedom. It was a festival largely of the common people, marked by processions, sacrifices, masked revelry and the sale of honey cakes by priestesses.

Coming of age

The Liberalia was traditionally the occasion on which Roman boys, at about fifteen or sixteen years of age, laid aside the bulla and the bordered toga praetexta of childhood and assumed the plain white toga virilis of adult citizenship, being formally enrolled among the citizens. The poet Ovid discusses the festival and its myths in Book 3 of the Fasti.
