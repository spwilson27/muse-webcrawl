Eye examination

An eye examination is a series of tests performed to assess vision and the ability to focus on and discern objects, as well as other tests and examinations pertaining to the eyes and visual system. Eye examinations are primarily performed by optometrists, ophthalmologists, and orthoptists.

Basic examination

A routine examination typically includes a case history, measurement of visual acuity with a Snellen chart or logMAR chart, refraction to determine the lens prescription (often using a phoropter), and assessment of pupil responses, ocular motility, and peripheral vision. The examiner also checks the alignment of the eyes and binocular vision.

Health assessment

The health of the eye is assessed using a slit lamp to examine the anterior segment, tonometry to measure intraocular pressure (screening for glaucoma), and ophthalmoscopy — often after dilating the pupil — to inspect the retina and optic disc. Visual field testing, colour vision testing (such as Ishihara plates), and imaging such as optical coherence tomography may be added.

Importance

Regular eye examinations can detect refractive errors as well as diseases such as glaucoma, cataracts, macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy, some of which show no early symptoms. Health authorities commonly recommend examinations every one to two years, and annually for older adults and people with diabetes or other risk factors.
