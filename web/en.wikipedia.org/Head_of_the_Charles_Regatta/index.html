The Head of the Charles Regatta, also known as HOCR, is a head race held annually on the penultimate full weekend of October on the Charles River, which separates Boston and Cambridge, Massachusetts. First held in 1965, it is the largest two-day rowing event in the world, attracting around 11,000 athletes in more than 1,900 boats and hundreds of thousands of spectators.

History

The regatta was founded in 1965 by Cambridge Boat Club members D'Arcy MacMahon, Howard McIntyre, and Jack Vincent, with advice from Harvard sculling coach Ernest Arlett, who suggested the "head of the river" format used in England, in which crews start sequentially and are timed over a set course. It grew from a modest one-day event into a two-day festival central to the autumn rowing calendar.

Course

The course is 3 miles (about 4.8 km) long, beginning at Boston University's DeWolfe Boathouse near the Charles River Dam and finishing just after the Eliot Bridge at Herter Park in Allston. Crews must navigate the river's bends and pass under six bridges — the Boston University, River Street, Western Avenue, Weeks Footbridge, Anderson Memorial, and Eliot bridges — with the arches of the Weeks and Anderson bridges notorious choke points for steering and collisions.

Events

Races are held in dozens of categories by boat class (singles through eights), age (youth, collegiate, club, masters), and sex, including para-rowing events and the premier Championship eights and fours. The regatta is a showcase for colleges, clubs, and national-team athletes from around the world.
