Linda (mythology)

Linda is a figure in Estonian mythology, best known from the national epic Kalevipoeg, compiled by Friedrich Reinhold Kreutzwald in the 19th century. She is the wife of Kalev, the mythical king, and the mother of Kalevipoeg, the hero of the epic.

In the epic

According to the epic, Linda was born from a grouse's egg (in some tellings found by Kalev) and became Kalev's bride. After Kalev's death she grieved deeply; her tears were said to have formed Lake Ülemiste near Tallinn, and she carried great stones to cover her husband's grave, forming the hill of Toompea. One stone she dropped became a boulder still associated with her sorrow. She resisted the unwanted courtship of the Finnish sorcerer Tuuslar and was eventually transformed, in some versions, into stone.

Significance

Linda is a symbol of maternal devotion and national endurance in Estonian culture; the Linda monument in Tallinn, depicting her mourning, is a well-known memorial to victims of Soviet deportations.
