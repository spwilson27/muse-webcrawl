Borax

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Borax (also referred to as sodium borate, tincal, and tincar) is a salt (ionic compound), a hydrated or anhydrous borate of sodium, with the chemical formula Na2H20B4O17. It is a colorless crystalline solid that dissolves in water to make a basic (alkaline) solution. It is commonly available in powder or granular form and has many industrial and household uses, including as a component of laundry detergents, cosmetics, and enamel glazes.

Borax is a component of many detergents, cosmetics, and enamel glazes. It is used to make buffer solutions in biochemistry, as a fire retardant, as an anti-fungal compound, in the manufacture of fiberglass, as a flux in metallurgy, as a texturing agent in cooking, and as a precursor to other boron compounds.

Occurrence and production

Borax occurs naturally in evaporite deposits produced by the repeated evaporation of seasonal lakes. The largest known deposits are found in Turkey and California. Commercially important sources include the deposits at Boron, California, in the Mojave Desert, where the famous "20-mule team" wagons once hauled borax to railheads, and the great Turkish mines near Kırka. Borax is typically mined and refined from minerals such as tincal and kernite.

Uses

  • Laundry and cleaning — as a water softener and laundry booster, famously sold as 20 Mule Team Borax.
  • Glass and ceramics — a precursor to borosilicate glass and enamel glazes; see borosilicate glass.
  • Metallurgy — as a flux that dissolves metal oxides in welding and soldering.
  • Biochemistry — as a buffer in solutions such as TBE used in gel electrophoresis.
  • Other — fire retardant, anti-fungal wood treatment, and neutron absorber in some applications.

Chemistry

The term borax is used for several related minerals, including the decahydrate Na2B4O7·10H2O, the most common form. In water it hydrolyzes to give a mildly alkaline solution (pH about 9.5), and it is a precursor to boric acid and perborates used in detergents.
