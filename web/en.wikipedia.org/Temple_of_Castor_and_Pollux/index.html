Temple of Castor and Pollux

The Temple of Castor and Pollux is an ancient temple in the Roman Forum, Rome, Italy. It was originally built in gratitude for victory at the Battle of Lake Regillus (495 BC). Castor and Pollux (Greek Polydeuces) were the Dioscuri, the twin sons of Zeus and Leda, who according to legend appeared on the battlefield to aid the Romans and afterwards announced the victory in the Forum, watering their horses at the Spring of Juturna.

History

The dictator Aulus Postumius Albus vowed the temple during the battle; his son dedicated it in 484 BC. The building was repeatedly restored, most notably by Lucius Caecilius Metellus Dalmaticus in 117 BC and by Tiberius in 6 AD, rebuilding it in marble. The temple stood on a high podium and served at times as a meeting place for the Senate and as the office of weights and measures; its podium also housed bankers' offices and a barber shop.

Ruins

Today only three Corinthian columns, about 12.5 metres tall, survive, along with part of the podium — one of the most iconic images of the Roman Forum. The columns date from the Augustan-era rebuilding and were a landmark for travellers on the Grand Tour.
