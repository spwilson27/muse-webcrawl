Bursera

Bursera is a genus of flowering plants in the family Burseraceae, comprising roughly one hundred species of trees and shrubs native to tropical regions of the Americas, from northern Mexico and the Caribbean to southern Brazil. The genus was named for the seventeenth-century Danish botanist Joachim Burser.

Description

Many Bursera species are characteristic trees of tropical dry forests. They often have smooth, peeling, reddish or coppery bark and produce aromatic resins. The copal resins obtained from species such as Bursera bipinnata have been used since pre-Columbian times as incense in Mesoamerican ritual, as well as in varnishes and traditional medicine.

Ecology

Bursera species are a classic study system in plant–insect coevolution: the plants defend their leaves with pressurised resin canals that squirt toxic resin at herbivores, while specialist Blepharida flea beetles have evolved behaviours to disarm the defences. The genus is most diverse in Mexico, where numerous endemic species occur in the Balsas River basin.
