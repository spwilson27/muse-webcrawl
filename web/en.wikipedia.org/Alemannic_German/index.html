Alemannic, or Alemannic German, is a group of High German dialects spoken in Switzerland (as Swiss German), Baden-Württemberg and Swabia in Germany, Alsace in France, and parts of Austria and Liechtenstein. The name derives from the Alemanni, a confederation of Germanic tribes.

Varieties

Major subgroups include Swabian in the northeast, Low Alemannic around Lake Constance and in Alsace, High Alemannic in most of German-speaking Switzerland and Vorarlberg, and Highest Alemannic in the high valleys of the Alps, such as Valais German. The varieties differ in consonant shifts, vowel systems, and vocabulary, and are not always mutually intelligible.

Status

In Switzerland, Alemannic dialects are the everyday spoken language at all levels of society, while Standard German is used largely in writing — a classic diglossic situation. In Germany and France the dialects are more strongly in retreat in favour of the standard language.
