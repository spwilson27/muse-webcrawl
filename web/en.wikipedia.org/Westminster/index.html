Westminster is the main settlement and historic centre of the City of Westminster in London, England. It is the site of the Palace of Westminster — the meeting place of the Parliament of the United Kingdom — and of Westminster Abbey, the coronation church of English and British monarchs since 1066. As a result, "Westminster" is commonly used as a metonym for the UK Parliament and the British Government.

The area lies on the north bank of the River Thames, in London's West End, southwest of the City of London. Historically a separate settlement from London, it developed around the royal palace and abbey established on Thorney Island in the eleventh century, when Edward the Confessor built his palace and the great abbey church beside the Thames.

History

Westminster's prominence dates from the eleventh century, when Edward the Confessor moved the royal court there and rebuilt St Peter's Abbey (Westminster Abbey). William the Conqueror was crowned in the abbey on Christmas Day 1066, and it has remained the coronation church ever since. Westminster Hall, built in 1097, survives as the oldest part of the present Palace of Westminster.

Parliament gradually settled at Westminster during the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries. After a fire destroyed most of the old palace in 1834, the present Gothic Revival palace was built to designs by Charles Barry and Augustus Pugin, incorporating Westminster Hall. Its clock tower — commonly called Big Ben after its Great Bell — is one of London's most famous landmarks.

Landmarks

Principal landmarks include the Palace of Westminster and Westminster Abbey (together a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Westminster Bridge, the Cenotaph in Whitehall, Downing Street, the Supreme Court in Parliament Square, Westminster Cathedral, and the government departments along Whitehall. The district is served by Westminster tube station.
