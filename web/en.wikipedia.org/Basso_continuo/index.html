Basso continuo

Also known asThoroughbass, figured bass
PeriodBaroque era, c. 1600–1750
InstrumentsHarpsichord, organ, lute, theorbo, cello, viola da gamba, bassoon

Basso continuo parts, almost universally used in the Baroque era (c. 1600–1750), provided the harmonic structure of a piece of music by supplying a bassline and a chord progression. The phrase is often shortened to continuo, and the instrumentalists playing the continuo part are called the continuo group. The continuo part typically consisted of a bass instrument — such as a cello, viola da gamba, or bassoon — playing the bassline, together with a chord-playing instrument — harpsichord, organ, lute, or theorbo — whose player improvised the harmonies.

Figured bass

The notation of a continuo part, called figured bass or thoroughbass, consists of a bassline with numerals and accidentals written beneath the staff indicating the intervals above the bass note that should be played, and hence the chord to be realized. The realization itself was left to the performer's skill and taste: a competent continuo player supplied chord voicings, arpeggiation, and ornamentation in keeping with the style of the piece, much as a jazz rhythm-section player works from chord symbols today.

History

Continuo practice emerged around 1600 in Italy, in the works of composers such as Emilio de' Cavalieri, Giulio Caccini, and Claudio Monteverdi, as part of the new monodic and concertato styles. It became the foundation of virtually all Baroque ensemble music, from solo song to opera and the concerto grosso, and was central to the work of Bach, Handel, and Vivaldi. As the Classical style developed in the mid-18th century, composers increasingly wrote out all parts in full, and continuo practice declined, surviving longest in church music and in operatic recitative accompaniment.

Modern performance

Historically informed performance revived continuo practice in the 20th century. Modern Baroque ensembles routinely employ harpsichord or organ continuo, and the interpretation of figured bass remains a required skill for early-music keyboard players.
