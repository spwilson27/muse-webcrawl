Glencoe

Glencoe, or Glen Coe (Scottish Gaelic: Gleann Comhann), is a glen in the Scottish Highlands, in the Lochaber area of Highland council area. Formed by an ancient volcano and sculpted by glaciers, it is celebrated for its dramatic mountain scenery and is a centre for hillwalking and climbing.

Massacre of Glencoe

The glen is infamous for the Massacre of Glencoe on 13 February 1692, when government soldiers billeted with the MacDonalds of Glencoe murdered 38 members of the clan, ostensibly for failing to swear allegiance to William III and Mary II in time. The killings, carried out by troops who had accepted the MacDonalds' hospitality, became an enduring symbol of betrayal in Scottish memory.

Geography and tourism

The glen runs from Rannoch Moor eastward to Loch Leven. Peaks include Buachaille Etive Mòr and the Three Sisters of Bidean nam Bian. Much of the area is cared for by the National Trust for Scotland. The village of Glencoe lies at the glen's western end, and the A82 road passes through the valley, carrying heavy tourist traffic.
