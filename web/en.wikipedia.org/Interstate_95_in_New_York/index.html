Interstate 95 (I-95) is the main north–south Interstate Highway on the East Coast of the United States, running from Miami, Florida, to the Canadian border at Houlton, Maine. The portion of I-95 in the U.S. state of New York extends 23.5 miles (37.8 km) — one of its shortest state segments — through New York City and suburban Westchester County, yet it is among the most heavily travelled parts of the route.

Route description

I-95 enters New York from New Jersey on the George Washington Bridge, crossing Manhattan on the Trans-Manhattan Expressway. It continues across the Harlem River into the Bronx as the Cross Bronx Expressway, one of the most congested urban freeways in the United States. In the northeastern Bronx, the route splits: I-95 continues onto the New England Thruway (operated by the New York State Thruway Authority) through New Rochelle and Rye to the Connecticut state line at Port Chester, where it becomes the Connecticut Turnpike; the Bruckner Expressway carries I-278 and traffic toward I-295 and Long Island.

History

The Cross Bronx Expressway, championed by Robert Moses, was built between 1948 and 1972 through densely populated neighbourhoods, displacing thousands of residents and becoming a famous case study in the social costs of urban highway construction. The New England Thruway opened in 1958.
