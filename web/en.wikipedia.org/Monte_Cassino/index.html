Monte Cassino
Location: near Cassino, Lazio, Italy
Elevation: 520 m
Known for: Abbey founded c. 529 by St Benedict; Battle of Monte Cassino (1944)

Monte Cassino (today usually spelled Montecassino) is a rocky hill about 130 kilometres southeast of Rome, in the Latin Valley of Italy, crowned by the Abbey of Monte Cassino, one of the most famous monasteries in the world. Saint Benedict of Nursia founded the abbey on the site around 529, on the ruins of a Roman fortification and a temple of Apollo, and there wrote his Rule, the foundation of Western monasticism.

History of the abbey

The abbey was destroyed and rebuilt repeatedly: sacked by Lombards in the 570s, by Saracens in 883, ruined by an earthquake in 1349, and looted by Napoleonic troops in 1799. It was suppressed in 1866 but continued as a monastic community and became a national monument.

Battle of Monte Cassino

In the Second World War the hill dominated the German Gustav Line across the Italian peninsula. Between January and May 1944 Allied forces fought four battles to break through toward Rome. On 15 February 1944 Allied bombers destroyed the abbey — whose ruins German paratroopers then occupied as a defensive position. The position was finally taken on 18 May 1944 by the Polish II Corps, which raised the Polish flag over the ruins. The abbey was rebuilt after the war and reconsecrated by Pope Paul VI in 1964.
