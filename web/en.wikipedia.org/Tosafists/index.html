Tosafists

Tosafot printed alongside the Talmud text

The Tosafists were medieval rabbis from France, Germany, Bohemia, Austria, England, and Italy who wrote critical and explanatory glosses (questions, notes, interpretations, rulings, and sources) on the Talmud. These commentaries are known collectively as the Tosafot, meaning "additions" or "supplements" — additions to the commentary of Rashi, the 11th-century French scholar whose commentary had become the standard companion to the Talmud text.

History

The Tosafist period spans roughly from the 12th to the mid-14th centuries. The first Tosafists were Rashi's own students and descendants, including his grandsons Rabbi Samuel ben Meir (the Rashbam) and Rabbi Jacob ben Meir (Rabbeinu Tam), who became the most prominent Tosafist of his generation. The movement flourished in the rabbinic academies (yeshivot) of northern France and Germany before the persecutions associated with the Crusades and later expulsions curtailed Jewish life in those regions.

Method

The Tosafists' characteristic method was dialectical: they compared parallel Talmudic passages, identified apparent contradictions, and resolved them through fine distinctions (chilukim). Their glosses often begin by raising a difficulty in Rashi's interpretation and proceed to harmonize the texts. The Tosafot also cite customs, earlier authorities, and the Jerusalem Talmud, and they sometimes record practical halakhic rulings.

The printed Tosafot

Of the many Tosafot collections produced, the version printed in standard editions of the Talmud derives largely from the Tosafot of Touques, edited by Rabbi Eliezer of Touques in the late 13th century. In the classic Vilna edition layout, the Tosafot appear on the outer margin of the page, opposite Rashi's commentary on the inner margin.

Notable Tosafists

  • Rashbam (Samuel ben Meir)
  • Isaac ben Samuel of Dampierre (the Ri)
  • Samson ben Abraham of Sens
  • Eliezer of Touques
  • Meir of Rothenburg (the Maharam)
