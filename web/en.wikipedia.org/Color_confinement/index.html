Color confinement

Color confinement, often simply called confinement, is the phenomenon in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) that color-charged particles such as quarks and gluons cannot be isolated and therefore cannot be directly observed in normal conditions.

Explanation

Unlike electromagnetism, the force between quarks does not diminish with distance: the gluon field collapses into a flux tube with roughly constant tension, so separating quarks requires ever more energy until it becomes favourable to create a new quark–antiquark pair. Consequently quarks are always bound within color-neutral hadrons such as protons, neutrons and mesons.

Status

Confinement is strongly supported by lattice QCD simulations and experiment, but a rigorous mathematical proof remains one of the Millennium Prize Problems via the Yang–Mills existence and mass gap problem.
