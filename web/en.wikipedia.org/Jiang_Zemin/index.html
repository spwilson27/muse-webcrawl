Jiang Zemin


Jiang Zemin (17 August 1926 – 30 November 2022) was a Chinese politician who led China from 1989 to 2002.

Jiang Zemin (17 August 1926 – 30 November 2022) was a Chinese politician who served as general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) from 1989 to 2002, as chairman of the Central Military Commission from 1989 to 2004, and as president of the People's Republic of China from 1993 to 2003. He was the paramount leader of China from 1989 to 2002 and the core of the third generation of Chinese leadership.

Rise to power

Born in Yangzhou, Jiangsu, Jiang trained as an electrical engineer and joined the CCP in 1946. He rose through industrial ministries, served as mayor and party secretary of Shanghai, and entered the Politburo in 1987. After the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests and massacre, Deng Xiaoping selected Jiang as a compromise successor to the purged Zhao Ziyang.

Leadership

Jiang consolidated power with the support of party elders and presided over a decade of rapid economic reform and growth, guided by his political theory of the "Three Represents," which admitted private entrepreneurs into the party and was written into the CCP constitution. His tenure saw the handover of Hong Kong in 1997 and Macau in 1999, China's accession to the World Trade Organization in 2001, Beijing's successful bid for the 2008 Olympics, and the suppression of Falun Gong beginning in 1999. He transferred the party leadership to Hu Jintao in 2002 and the presidency in 2003, retaining the military commission chairmanship until 2004.

Death and legacy

Jiang died in Shanghai on 30 November 2022, aged 96, of leukemia and multiple organ failure, according to state media. He received a state funeral in Beijing. In retirement he remained an influential elder, and his loyalists formed the so-called Shanghai clique in Chinese politics.
