Common cold
Symptoms: runny or stuffy nose, sneezing, sore throat, cough
Causes: chiefly rhinoviruses; also coronaviruses and others
Duration: usually 7–10 days
Frequency: adults 2–3 per year; children 6–8

The common cold is a viral infectious disease of the upper respiratory tract that primarily affects the nose and throat. It is the most frequent infectious disease in humans and a leading cause of absence from work and school.

Cause

More than 200 virus strains can cause colds; rhinoviruses account for the largest share, with seasonal coronaviruses, parainfluenza and respiratory syncytial virus also involved. Different causative agents produce indistinguishable symptoms.

Transmission and symptoms

The viruses spread through airborne droplets, direct contact and contaminated surfaces. Symptoms typically begin one to three days after infection and include nasal congestion, sneezing, sore throat, cough and low-grade malaise. Complications such as sinusitis or otitis media occasionally follow.

Treatment

There is no cure and no vaccine, owing to the diversity of causative viruses. Management is symptomatic: rest, fluids, analgesics and decongestants. Antibiotics are ineffective against viruses, and evidence for vitamin C and echinacea remains limited.
