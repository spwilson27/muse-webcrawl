Aresti Catalog

The Fédération Aéronautique Internationale Aresti Catalog is a document enumerating the manoeuvres permitted in aerobatic competitions. Designed by Spanish aviator Colonel José Luis Aresti Aguirre (1919–2003), each figure in the catalog is represented by lines, arrows, geometric shapes and numbers representing the precise form of a manoeuvre.

The catalog broadly separates manoeuvres into families. Each family has figures represented by numbers within a grid; the complexity of a figure is defined by a numerical value, known as its K factor. When constructing a sequence for competition, pilots select figures from the catalog and add their K factors to determine the total difficulty value of the sequence.

Aresti symbols

Aresti diagrams use a standardized notation: solid lines represent upright or positive-g flight, dashed lines represent inverted or negative-g flight, arrows indicate the direction of flight and any rolls performed on a line, and triangles and other shapes denote spins, snap rolls, and tumbles. Lines begin and end with small perpendicular ticks marking the start and finish of each figure.

Organisation

The catalog is divided into families of manoeuvres, including horizontal and vertical lines, angles, turns and rolling turns, loops and parts of loops, hammerheads (stall turns), tailslides, spins, and combinations thereof. Each figure is assigned a catalog number and a K factor reflecting its judged difficulty.

Use in competition

Aerobatic sequences at all levels of competition, from primary through unlimited, are written using Aresti notation and must be drawn from the figures in the catalog. Judges use the same diagrams to follow and score a competitor's flight.
