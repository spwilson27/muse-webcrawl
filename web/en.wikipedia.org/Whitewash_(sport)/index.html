Whitewash (sport)

A whitewash in sport is a series victory in which one side wins every match, or a single match in which the loser fails to score at all. In North American English the equivalent term for winning every game of a series is a sweep. A notable early use of "whitewash" in sport appears in cricket reporting of the 19th century.

Usage by sport

In Test cricket, a whitewash means winning every match of a series — famously, the only 5–0 Ashes whitewashes include Australia's victory in 1920–21 and England's defeat in 2006–07. In rugby, a team finishing last in the Six Nations without a win is said to receive the "wooden spoon", while a clean sweep of victories earns a Grand Slam. In snooker and darts, "whitewash" also describes a match won without the opponent winning a frame or leg.

Related terms

Closely related concepts include the shutout (preventing an opponent from scoring in a single game) and bagel scores in tennis. Whitewashes are often cited as measures of dominance by great teams and of humiliation for heavily favoured losers.
