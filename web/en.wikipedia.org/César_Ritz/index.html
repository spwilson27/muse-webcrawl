César Ritz

César Ritz (23 February 1850 – 24 October 1918) was a Swiss hotelier widely regarded as the founder of the modern luxury hotel. His name became synonymous with elegance and high standards of service, giving English the words "ritzy" and "putting on the Ritz".

Early life and career

Born in Niederwald in the Swiss canton of Valais, the youngest of thirteen children of a farming family, Ritz began as an apprentice sommelier and worked his way through the grand hotels of Paris, Nice and Monte Carlo. His maxim was that "the customer is never wrong" (le client n'a jamais tort).

The Ritz hotels

With the celebrated chef Auguste Escoffier as his partner in the kitchen, Ritz managed the Savoy Hotel in London from 1889, transforming it into a fashionable destination. Dismissed from the Savoy in 1898, he went on to open the Hôtel Ritz in Paris's Place Vendôme the same year, followed by the Ritz Hotel in London in 1906 and the Ritz in Madrid in 1910.

Innovations

Ritz pioneered features now standard in luxury hotels: private bathrooms en suite, unobtrusive but meticulous service, elegant lighting designed to flatter guests, and dining rooms that welcomed women in public society. A nervous breakdown in 1902 ended his active career.

Legacy

The Ritz name survives in hotels around the world and in the Ritz-Carlton company, which later licensed the name. Ritz died in Küssnacht, Switzerland, in 1918 and is buried in his home village of Niederwald.
