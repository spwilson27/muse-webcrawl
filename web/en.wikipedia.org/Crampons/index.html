Crampons

Crampons are traction devices attached to footwear to improve mobility on snow and ice during ice climbing, mountaineering, glacier travel and related activities.

Design

Modern crampons are made of steel or aluminium alloy with 10–14 downward points, including forward-angled front points for steep ice. They attach by straps, clip (step-in) bindings, or hybrid systems, and must be matched to compatible boots.

History

Early designs were used by hunters and miners; Oscar Eckenstein designed a 10-point crampon in 1908, and Laurent Grivel added front points in 1929.
