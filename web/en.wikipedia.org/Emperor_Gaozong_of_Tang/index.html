Emperor Gaozong of Tang (21 July 628 – 27 December 683), personal name Li Zhi, was the third emperor of the Tang dynasty of China, reigning from 649 to 683. The ninth son of Emperor Taizong, he succeeded his father after being named crown prince in 643. His reign saw the Tang reach its greatest territorial extent, but chronic illness from the 660s led him to yield increasing authority to his powerful wife, Wu Zetian, who would eventually found her own dynasty.

Emperor Gaozong, third emperor of the Tang dynasty

Accession

Li Zhi was made crown prince after his elder brothers Li Chengqian and Li Tai were disgraced in a succession struggle. He ascended the throne on Taizong's death in 649, initially guided by veteran officials including his uncle Zhangsun Wuji.

Reign

Gaozong's armies completed the conquest of the Western Turks (657) and, with Silla, destroyed Baekje (660) and Goguryeo (668) in Korea, bringing Tang territory to its maximum extent. In 655 he deposed Empress Wang and installed Wu Zhao — formerly a concubine of his father — as empress, a controversial act that permanently shifted court power. After suffering strokes and deteriorating eyesight beginning around 660, Gaozong relied on Empress Wu to review memorials and decide policy; the two were known as the "Two Saints". In 674 he took the title Heavenly Emperor (Tian Huang), with Wu as Heavenly Empress.

Death and succession

Gaozong died at Luoyang in 683 and was buried in the Qianling Mausoleum, where Wu Zetian would later join him beneath the famous Wordless Stele. He was succeeded by his son Zhongzong, but real power passed to Wu Zetian, who deposed two sons before proclaiming herself emperor in 690.
