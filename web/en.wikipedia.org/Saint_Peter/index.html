Saint Peter

Saint Peter (died AD 64–68), also known as Peter the Apostle, Simon Peter, or Cephas, was one of the Twelve Apostles of Jesus Christ and one of the earliest leaders of the Christian Church. According to the New Testament, he was a fisherman from Bethsaida in Galilee, called by Jesus together with his brother Andrew. Catholic tradition holds that Jesus gave him a position of special leadership — the "keys of the kingdom" — and regards him as the first pope; Eastern Orthodox and Protestant traditions honour him as a pre-eminent apostle while differing on the institutional claims built upon his role.

In the Gospels

The Gospels present Peter — born Simon son of Jonah — as the first-called and spokesman of the apostles. He confesses Jesus as the Christ at Caesarea Philippi, whereupon Jesus declares him the "rock" (Greek Petros, Aramaic Kepha) on which the church will be built, and promises him the keys of the kingdom of heaven. Peter witnesses the Transfiguration, walks briefly on water, and at the Last Supper protests his loyalty — yet, as Jesus predicted, denies him three times before the cock crows on the night of the arrest. After the Resurrection, the risen Christ commissions him: "Feed my sheep."

Leadership of the early church

In the Acts of the Apostles, Peter leads the Jerusalem community: he preaches at Pentecost, performs healings, admits the first Gentile converts at the house of Cornelius, and speaks at the Council of Jerusalem. Two epistles of the New Testament bear his name, though their authorship has long been debated. Paul's letters show both cooperation and tension between the two apostles, notably the "incident at Antioch."

Martyrdom and relics

Tradition holds that Peter was martyred in Rome during the persecution under Nero, crucified head-downwards at his own request, feeling unworthy to die in the same manner as Christ. St Peter's Basilica in the Vatican is built over the site venerated since antiquity as his tomb; excavations beneath the basilica in the twentieth century uncovered an early necropolis and a shrine identified with his burial.

Legacy

Peter's figure — impulsive, fallible, forgiven, and steadfast — has made him one of the most humanly appealing saints. The doctrine of papal primacy in Catholic theology rests largely on the Petrine texts, while art through the centuries has depicted him with keys, chains, or an inverted cross from catacomb frescoes to Michelangelo's Last Judgment.
