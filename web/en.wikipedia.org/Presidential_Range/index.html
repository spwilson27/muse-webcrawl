Presidential Range

Presidential Range
Location: Coös County, New Hampshire, United States
Highest point: Mount Washington, 6,288 ft (1,917 m)
Parent range: White Mountains

The Presidential Range is a mountain range located in the White Mountains of the U.S. state of New Hampshire. Contained almost entirely in the White Mountain National Forest, the range's highest peaks are named after presidents of the United States, with later additions named for other notable Americans.

Peaks

The highest peak in the range is Mount Washington at 6,288 feet (1,917 m), the highest mountain in the northeastern United States. Other major summits include Mount Adams (5,774 ft), Mount Jefferson (5,712 ft), Mount Monroe (5,372 ft), Mount Madison (5,367 ft), Mount Lafayette is in the separate Franconia Range; within the Presidentials further peaks include Mount Eisenhower, Mount Pierce, Mount Jackson and Mount Webster, some of which were named for figures such as Daniel Webster rather than presidents.

Weather

The range is notorious for severe and rapidly changing weather. Mount Washington held the world record for a directly measured surface wind speed — 231 mph (372 km/h), recorded in 1934 — for decades. The combination of high winds, cold and exposure has led to many hiker fatalities, and the peaks are above treeline, forming the largest alpine zone in the eastern United States.

Recreation

The Appalachian Trail crosses the range, and the Appalachian Mountain Club maintains a chain of high huts, including Lakes of the Clouds Hut and Madison Spring Hut. The Mount Washington Cog Railway and the Mount Washington Auto Road provide access to the summit of Mount Washington. Traversing all the major summits in a single outing — the "Presidential Traverse" — is a popular challenge for strong hikers.
