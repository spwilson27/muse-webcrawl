Archivist

An archivist is an information professional who appraises, acquires, arranges, describes, preserves, and provides access to archives — records and other materials with long-term or permanent value. Archivists work for governments, universities, businesses, religious bodies, museums, and community organizations, and they are responsible both for the physical care of holdings and for making them discoverable and usable.

Duties

Core archival functions include appraisal (selecting which records merit permanent retention), accessioning, arrangement according to the principles of provenance and original order, description using standards such as ISAD(G) and Encoded Archival Description (EAD), preservation and conservation management, reference service, and outreach. Since the late twentieth century, the management of born-digital records and digital preservation has become a central part of the profession.

Education and professional bodies

Archivists commonly hold a master's degree in archival studies, library and information science, or history, often supplemented by certification such as that of the Academy of Certified Archivists. Professional associations include the Society of American Archivists, the Archives and Records Association, and the International Council on Archives. The profession's theory rests on foundational writings by figures such as Sir Hilary Jenkinson and Theodore Schellenberg.
