Stalwarts (politics)

Stalwarts
Party: Republican Party (US)
Era: Late 19th century
Leader: Roscoe Conkling
Opponents: Half-Breeds

The Stalwarts were a faction of the United States Republican Party in the late 19th century, led by Senator Roscoe Conkling of New York. They defended the spoils system of political patronage and opposed civil service reform.

Rivalry with the Half-Breeds

The Stalwarts feuded with the rival "Half-Breed" faction, associated with James G. Blaine, which favoured moderate reform. The split shaped the deadlocked 1880 Republican convention, which nominated compromise candidate James A. Garfield.

Decline

Garfield's assassination in 1881 by a self-described Stalwart office-seeker discredited the faction and spurred the Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act of 1883, after which Stalwart influence faded.
