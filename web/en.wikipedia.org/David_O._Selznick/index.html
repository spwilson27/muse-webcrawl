David O. Selznick (born David Selznick; May 10, 1902 – June 22, 1965) was an American film producer, screenwriter, and film studio executive who produced Gone with the Wind (1939) and Rebecca (1940), films that won consecutive Academy Awards for Best Picture. He also produced King Kong (1933), Dinner at Eight (1933), A Star Is Born (1937), and Duel in the Sun (1946).

Early life and studio career

Selznick was born in Pittsburgh to Lewis J. Selznick, a silent-film producer and distributor. After his father's business collapsed, David worked his way up through the studio system at MGM, Paramount, and RKO, becoming head of production at RKO in 1931, where he supervised productions including King Kong. He returned to MGM in 1933 as a vice-president and producer of prestige literary adaptations such as David Copperfield (1935) and A Tale of Two Cities (1935).

Selznick International Pictures

In 1935 Selznick founded his own company, Selznick International Pictures. Its productions included The Garden of Allah (1936), A Star Is Born (1937), and The Prisoner of Zenda (1937). The company's greatest triumph was Gone with the Wind (1939), an enormous production — famous for its long search for an actress to play Scarlett O'Hara — which became the highest-grossing film of all time (a record it still holds when adjusted for inflation). The following year, Rebecca, the first Hollywood film directed by Alfred Hitchcock under Selznick's contract, also won Best Picture.

Later years

Selznick was renowned — and sometimes resented — for his perfectionism, his minute involvement in every aspect of production, and his famously long and detailed memos. Later productions included Since You Went Away (1944), Spellbound (1945), and the lavish western Duel in the Sun (1946). His output declined in the 1950s; his final production was A Farewell to Arms (1957). He died of a heart attack in 1965.
