Lorenzo Ghiberti

The Gates of Paradise, Florence Baptistery

Lorenzo Ghiberti (1378 – 1 December 1455), born Lorenzo di Bartolo, was an Italian Renaissance artist of the Early Renaissance, best known as the creator of the bronze doors of the Florence Baptistery, called by Michelangelo the Gates of Paradise. Trained as a goldsmith and sculptor, he established a large, important workshop in Florence.

The Baptistery doors

In 1401 Ghiberti won the famous competition for the north doors of the Florence Baptistery, defeating rivals including Filippo Brunelleschi; both artists' trial panels of the Sacrifice of Isaac survive. The north doors (1403–1424) contain 28 quatrefoil panels of New Testament scenes. His second set, the east doors (1425–1452), has ten large square panels of Old Testament scenes rendered with sophisticated perspective and modelling in gilded bronze — the work Michelangelo praised as fit to be the gates of Paradise. Ghiberti also produced bronze statues for Orsanmichele and worked on Siena Cathedral's baptistery.

Other work and writings

Ghiberti's workshop trained artists including Donatello, Masolino, Michelozzo and Paolo Uccello. His three-volume Commentarii contains biographies of artists and the earliest surviving autobiography by an artist, and is an important source for Renaissance art history, though never completed.
