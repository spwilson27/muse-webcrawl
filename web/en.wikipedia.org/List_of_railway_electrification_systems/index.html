List of railway electrification systems

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Railway electrification systems supply electric power to trains through overhead lines (catenary) or a third rail. Systems differ by voltage and by whether current is direct (DC) or alternating (AC) and at what frequency. The lack of a single global standard means multi-system locomotives are needed where networks meet. This article lists the principal systems in use worldwide.

History

Electric traction began with low-voltage DC systems in the late 19th century, suited to urban railways. Low-frequency AC (16.7 Hz in central Europe, 25 Hz in the United States) allowed efficient transmission with the motor technology of the early 20th century. After the Second World War, advances in rectifiers made industrial-frequency 25 kV AC at 50 or 60 Hz practical, and it became the standard for new main-line electrification, including most high-speed railways.
