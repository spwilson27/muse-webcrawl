Tawba

Tawba (Arabic: توبة, tawba) is the Islamic concept of repentance to God for sins and misdeeds. Literally meaning "return" or "turning back", tawba denotes a sincere turning away from sin and toward God, and is a major theme of the Qur'an, which frequently describes God as al-Tawwāb, "the Most Relenting" or "Oft-Returning (to His servants in mercy)".

Conditions

Classical scholars, notably al-Ghazali and Ibn Qayyim, set out conditions for valid repentance: giving up the sin, sincere remorse for having committed it, a firm resolve never to return to it, and — where the rights of other people are involved — restoring those rights or seeking their pardon. Repentance must be made before death or before the rising of the sun from the west, a sign of the end of time in Islamic eschatology.

In the Qur'an and Sunnah

The ninth chapter of the Qur'an is itself named Surah At-Tawba ("The Repentance"). Qur'anic verses present repentance as open for every sin, including polytheism, provided it is sincere, and the Prophet Muhammad is reported to have said that all the children of Adam err, and the best of those who err are those who repent. In Sufi tradition, tawba is the first station (maqām) on the spiritual path, marking the awakening of the soul to its state.
