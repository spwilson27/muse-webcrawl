Fraser Valley

The Fraser Valley, the lowland of the lower Fraser River in southwestern British Columbia.

The Fraser Valley is a geographical region in southwestern British Columbia, Canada, and northwestern Washington state in the United States, defined by the lowland along the lower course of the Fraser River between the Cascade Mountains and the river's mouth at the Strait of Georgia. In common Canadian usage it refers to the communities east of Metro Vancouver, including Abbotsford, Chilliwack, Langley, Mission, Hope and Agassiz.

Geography and economy

The valley is a fertile floodplain with a mild, wet maritime climate, making it the most important agricultural region in British Columbia, particularly for dairy, poultry, berry and greenhouse production. The Fraser Valley Regional District administers part of the area. Major transportation routes — the Trans-Canada Highway, railways and the river itself — follow the valley, and the inland port and airport at Abbotsford serve the growing population, much of it commuting into the Vancouver metropolitan area.

The valley lies within the traditional territories of the Stó:lō and other Coast Salish peoples, whose history along the river stretches back thousands of years. The region was reshaped by the Fraser Canyon Gold Rush of 1858 and by later settlement, and suffered catastrophic flooding in November 2021 when an atmospheric river overwhelmed the drained Sumas Lake bed near Abbotsford.
