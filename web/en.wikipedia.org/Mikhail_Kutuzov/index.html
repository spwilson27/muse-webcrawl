Prince Mikhail Illarionovich Golenishchev-Kutuzov (16 September 1745 – 28 April 1813) was a Russian field marshal, regarded as one of the finest military officers and diplomats of his time. He served under the rulers Catherine the Great, Paul I and Alexander I, and is best remembered as the commander-in-chief who defeated Napoleon's Grande Armée during the French invasion of Russia in 1812.

Early career

Kutuzov entered the artillery and engineering school in 1759 and served his apprenticeship under Alexander Suvorov during the Russo-Turkish War of 1768–1774. He survived two severe head wounds — in 1774 near Alushta and in 1788 at Ochakov — each of which damaged his right eye, an injury that became his most recognisable feature. He distinguished himself in the storming of Izmail in 1790 and later served as ambassador to Constantinople and as governor-general of Saint Petersburg.

Wars against Napoleon

In 1805 Kutuzov commanded the Russian army sent to assist Austria, fighting a skilful rearguard campaign before the coalition defeat at Austerlitz, where he had counselled against giving battle. After victory over the Ottomans in the Russo-Turkish War of 1806–1812, concluded by the Treaty of Bucharest in May 1812, he was appointed commander-in-chief against Napoleon in August 1812.

Kutuzov fought the bloody Battle of Borodino on 7 September 1812, then took the controversial decision to abandon Moscow in order to preserve the army. From the fortified camp at Tarutino he rebuilt his forces and waged a partisan war against the French. When Napoleon began his retreat in October, Kutuzov's strategy of parallel pursuit, hunger and winter destroyed the Grande Armée, culminating in the catastrophe of the Berezina crossing in November.

Death and legacy

Created Prince of Smolensk for his services, Kutuzov followed the retreating French into Germany but fell ill and died at Bunzlau in Silesia on 28 April 1813. He was buried in the Kazan Cathedral in Saint Petersburg. Kutuzov remains a central figure of Russian national memory: he is a major character in Tolstoy's War and Peace, and the Order of Kutuzov, instituted in 1942, is still among Russia's highest military decorations.
