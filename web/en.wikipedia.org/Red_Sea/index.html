The Red Sea is a sea inlet of the Indian Ocean, lying between Africa and Asia. Its connection to the ocean is in the south, through the Bab el-Mandeb strait and the Gulf of Aden. To its north lie the Sinai Peninsula, the Gulf of Aqaba, and the Gulf of Suez (leading to the Suez Canal). It is underlain by the Red Sea Rift, which is part of the Great Rift Valley.

The Red Sea has a surface area of roughly 438,000 km², is about 2,250 km long, and has a maximum width of 355 km. It has an average depth of 490 m, and in the central Suakin Trough it reaches its maximum depth of 3,040 m. It is one of the most saline bodies of water in the world and one of the world's most heavily travelled waterways, carrying shipping between Europe and Asia through the Suez Canal.

Geology

The Red Sea occupies a young oceanic rift that began forming in the Oligocene as the African and Arabian plates separated. Spreading continues today, and geologically the sea is an ocean in the making; the process divided what was once a single landmass and is pushing Arabia northeastward toward Eurasia.

Climate and water

Evaporation greatly exceeds precipitation and river inflow — no significant rivers drain into the sea — producing salinities of 36–41‰, well above the global ocean average. Surface temperatures of 26–30 °C in summer make it among the warmest seas on Earth.

Ecosystem

The Red Sea is a rich and diverse ecosystem. More than 1,200 fish species, around 10% of them endemic, inhabit its waters, and some 2,000 km of fringing coral reef line its shores. Its reefs support major dive tourism at resorts such as Sharm El Sheikh, Hurghada, and Aqaba. Species adapted to its unusually warm, saline waters are studied as potential models of coral resilience to climate change.

History and trade

The sea has been a trade artery since antiquity: ancient Egyptians mounted expeditions to the Land of Punt from its ports, and it later carried Roman, Arab, and Ottoman commerce in spices and coffee. The opening of the Suez Canal in 1869 made it the principal shipping route between Europe and Asia; in recent years, attacks on shipping in the Bab el-Mandeb area have led many carriers to reroute around the Cape of Good Hope.
