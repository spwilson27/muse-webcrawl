United States Capitol Police

The United States Capitol Police (USCP) is a federal law enforcement agency charged with protecting the United States Congress, its members, employees, visitors and facilities. It is one of the few US federal police forces that answers to the legislative branch rather than the executive. The department employs roughly 2,000 sworn officers and several hundred civilian staff, guarding the Capitol complex in Washington, D.C. and providing protection to members of Congress throughout the United States and its territories.

History

The force traces its origin to 1801, when a single watchman guarded the Capitol after Congress moved to Washington; the Capitol Police was formally created by Congress in 1828 after an assault on the son of President John Quincy Adams in the Rotunda. Over the twentieth century its remit grew to include the House and Senate office buildings, the Library of Congress (whose separate police force merged into the USCP in 2009), and protective details for congressional leadership.

January 6, 2021

The department came under intense scrutiny after the attack on the Capitol of 6 January 2021, when rioters overwhelmed its defences; more than a hundred officers were injured, and Officer Brian Sicknick died the following day. Chief Steven Sund resigned, and subsequent reviews criticized intelligence failures and planning. Congress passed emergency supplemental funding for security upgrades, fencing and officer support, and Inspector General investigations led to reforms in leadership, intelligence sharing and mutual-aid arrangements.

Structure

The USCP is governed by the Capitol Police Board — the Sergeants at Arms of the House and Senate and the Architect of the Capitol — with the Chief of Police as an ex officio member. Specialized units include the Containment and Emergency Response Team, K-9, hazardous devices, dignitary protection and criminal investigations.
