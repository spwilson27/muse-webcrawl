Plastic surgery restores form and function, and includes aesthetic procedures.

Plastic surgery is a surgical specialty involving the restoration, reconstruction or alteration of the human body. The word "plastic" derives from the Greek plastikos, "to mould". The specialty divides into reconstructive surgery, which repairs defects from trauma, burns, disease or congenital conditions, and aesthetic (cosmetic) surgery, which alters appearance by choice.

History

Reconstructive techniques are ancient: the Indian surgeon Sushruta described forehead-flap rhinoplasty around the 6th century BCE, and skin grafting for noses destroyed by punishment became known in Europe in the 16th century. Modern plastic surgery took shape during the First World War, when Harold Gillies treated thousands of soldiers with facial wounds in England, and expanded further in the Second World War under surgeons such as Archibald McIndoe. Microsurgery from the 1960s onward enabled replantation of limbs and free tissue transfer.

Techniques

Core techniques include skin grafts, local and free flaps (tissue moved with its blood supply), tissue expansion, liposuction, implants, and endoscopic approaches. Subspecialties include craniofacial surgery, hand surgery, burn care, microsurgery, and paediatric plastic surgery.

Reconstructive surgery

Reconstruction addresses burns, traumatic injuries, cancer defects (such as breast reconstruction after mastectomy), congenital anomalies including cleft lip and palate, and pressure sores. The first successful hand transplantation and face transplants in the 2000s extended the frontier of reconstruction into vascularised composite allotransplantation.

Aesthetic surgery

Common cosmetic procedures include breast augmentation, liposuction, rhinoplasty, facelift (rhytidectomy), abdominoplasty and blepharoplasty, alongside minimally invasive treatments such as botulinum toxin and dermal fillers. Tens of millions of procedures are performed worldwide each year, making regulation, patient safety and psychological screening continuing concerns within the specialty.
