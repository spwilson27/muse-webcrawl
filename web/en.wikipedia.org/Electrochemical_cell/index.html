Electrochemical cell

An electrochemical cell is a device capable of either generating electrical energy from chemical reactions or using electrical energy to facilitate chemical reactions. It consists of two half-cells, each with an electrode immersed in an electrolyte.

Galvanic cells

A galvanic (or voltaic) cell converts the energy of a spontaneous redox reaction into electricity. The classic example is the Daniell cell, with zinc and copper electrodes in sulfate solutions connected by a salt bridge. Oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode.

Electrolytic cells

An electrolytic cell uses an external power supply to drive a non-spontaneous reaction, as in the electrolysis of water or the industrial production of aluminium and chlorine.

Cell potential

The voltage produced by a cell is determined by the difference in standard electrode potentials of its two half-reactions, and under non-standard conditions by the Nernst equation.

Applications

Electrochemical cells underpin batteries, fuel cells, corrosion, electroplating, and many analytical techniques.
