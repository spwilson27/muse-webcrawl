The Wakhan Corridor is a narrow strip of territory in Badakhshan Province in north-eastern Afghanistan, extending eastward between the Pamir Mountains and the Hindu Kush to give Afghanistan a short border — about 92 km — with China. It separates Tajikistan from Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan.

History

The corridor is a product of the 19th-century Great Game: the Anglo-Russian boundary commissions of 1873 and 1895 assigned the Wakhan to Afghanistan as a buffer so that British India and the Russian Empire would not share a border. The eastern boundary with China was never formally demarcated by treaty, though in practice both sides recognised the line. Historically the valley was a route of the Silk Road, traversed by traders and, by tradition, by Marco Polo.

Geography and people

The corridor follows the Panj and Wakhan rivers westward and climbs to high passes — the Wakhjir Pass (4,923 m) into China, closed in winter and without a road crossing. The population consists mainly of Wakhi farmers in the lower valley and Kyrgyz pastoralists on the high Pamir plateau, among the most isolated communities in the world.

Modern period

The corridor remained largely untouched by Afghanistan's wars, though its Kyrgyz inhabitants suffered after the 1978 revolution drove many into Pakistan. China has kept its border closed, though there is periodic discussion of trade or road links through the Wakhjir. The district returned to Taliban control in 2021 along with the rest of Afghanistan.
