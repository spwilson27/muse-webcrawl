The Kingdom of Israel, also known as the Kingdom of Samaria after its capital city, was an Israelite kingdom of the Southern Levant during the Iron Age. It emerged after the traditional division of the united Kingdom of Israel around 930 BC and existed until its conquest by the Neo-Assyrian Empire in 722 BC, when Samaria fell after a three-year siege and much of its population was deported.

History

The northern kingdom, comprising the territories of ten of the twelve tribes of Israel, was established when Jeroboam I led a revolt against Rehoboam, the son of Solomon. Its early capitals were Shechem and Tirzah before Omri founded Samaria around 880 BC, which remained the capital for the rest of the kingdom’s history. Assyrian inscriptions refer to the kingdom as the "House of Omri".

After the fall of the Omrides, the dynasty of Jehu ruled for about a century. Israel prospered under Jeroboam II in the eighth century BC, but a rapid succession of coups after his death weakened the state. Assyria under Tiglath-Pileser III annexed Galilee and Gilead in 732 BC, and the conquest was completed with the fall of Samaria in 722 BC.

Religion and society

The kingdom’s religion centred on the worship of Yahweh, with major sanctuaries at Bethel and Dan, though the Hebrew Bible accuses its kings of idolatry. The prophets Elijah, Elisha, Amos and Hosea are all associated with the northern kingdom. Finds such as the Samaria ostraca and the Mesha Stele illuminate its administration and wars.

Legacy

The deportation of Israelites by Assyria gave rise to the tradition of the Ten Lost Tribes. The region of Samaria later became home to the Samaritans, who trace their descent in part to the population that remained.
