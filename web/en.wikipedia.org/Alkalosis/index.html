Alkalosis

Alkalosis is the condition of excess alkali (base) in the body fluids, in which arterial blood pH rises above the normal range of 7.35–7.45. It is the opposite of acidosis. Alkalosis is divided into respiratory alkalosis, caused by low arterial carbon dioxide, and metabolic alkalosis, caused by excess bicarbonate or loss of acid.

Blood pH is normally maintained between 7.35 and 7.45; alkalosis is a rise above this range.

Respiratory alkalosis

Hyperventilation blows off CO2, raising pH. Causes include anxiety and panic attacks, fever, pain, hypoxia at high altitude, salicylate poisoning, and liver failure. Symptoms include light-headedness, paraesthesia of the hands and feet, and in severe cases carpopedal spasm, since alkalosis reduces ionised calcium.

Metabolic alkalosis

Metabolic alkalosis arises from loss of hydrogen ions (prolonged vomiting, nasogastric suction), excess bicarbonate intake, or volume and chloride depletion, and from mineralocorticoid excess. Diuretic therapy is a common cause. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause, often with saline and potassium repletion in chloride-responsive cases.

Compensation

The body compensates: metabolic alkalosis prompts hypoventilation to retain CO2, while in respiratory alkalosis the kidneys excrete bicarbonate over several days. Mixed acid–base disorders are common in seriously ill patients and are assessed with arterial blood gas analysis.
