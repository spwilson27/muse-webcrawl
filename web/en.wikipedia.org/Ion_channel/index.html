Ion channel

TypeMembrane protein / pore
FunctionPassive transport of ions across membranes
GatingVoltage, ligand, mechanical, light
ExamplesNav, Kv, Cav, nicotinic acetylcholine receptor
Studied byPatch clamp electrophysiology

Ion channels are pore-forming membrane proteins that allow ions to pass through the channel pore. Their functions include establishing a resting membrane potential, shaping action potentials and other electrical signals by gating the flow of ions across the cell membrane, and controlling the flow of ions across secretory and epithelial cells.

Basic properties

Ion channels are distinguished by ion selectivity — the extent to which they discriminate among different species of ion — and by gating, the process of opening and closing. An open channel conducts ions down their electrochemical gradient at very high rates, up to about 100 million ions per second, without consuming ATP; this distinguishes channels from ion pumps and transporters.

Classification

Voltage-gated channels open in response to changes in membrane potential and include the sodium, potassium and calcium channels underlying nerve impulses. Ligand-gated channels, such as the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor, open when a neurotransmitter or other chemical binds. Mechanically gated channels respond to membrane stretch and mediate touch and hearing. Other classes are gated by light, temperature or intracellular messengers such as cyclic nucleotides.

Biological roles

Channels are fundamental to the excitability of neurons and muscle, hormone secretion, cell volume regulation, fertilization and sensory transduction. Mutations in channel genes cause "channelopathies" including cystic fibrosis (a chloride channel defect), certain epilepsies, long-QT syndrome and periodic paralyses.

Research

The patch clamp technique, developed by Erwin Neher and Bert Sakmann, allowed recording of currents through single channels and earned the 1991 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. In 2003 Roderick MacKinnon shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for determining the first atomic-resolution structure of a potassium channel. Many widely used drugs — local anesthetics, calcium channel blockers, antiepileptics — act on ion channels.
