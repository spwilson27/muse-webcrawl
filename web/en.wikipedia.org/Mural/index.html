Mural

A mural is any piece of graphic artwork painted or applied directly to a wall, ceiling, or other permanent substrate. Murals have been created since prehistoric times, from cave paintings to the large-scale public works of the modern era.

History

Ancient Egyptian tombs, Roman villas, and Minoan palaces all featured wall paintings. In the Renaissance, artists such as Michelangelo and Leonardo da Vinci produced famous murals, including the Sistine Chapel ceiling, using the fresco technique of painting into wet plaster.

Mexican muralism

In the 1920s and 1930s, Mexican muralism became an influential movement, with artists Diego Rivera, José Clemente Orozco, and David Alfaro Siqueiros — "Los Tres Grandes" — painting monumental works depicting Mexican history and social themes on public buildings.

Contemporary murals

Modern murals include community art projects, advertising, and works connected with graffiti and street art. Murals are used to revitalise neighbourhoods, commemorate events, and express political messages.
