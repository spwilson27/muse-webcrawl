Manoeuvre warfare

Manoeuvre warfare is a military strategy which attempts to defeat the enemy by incapacitating their decision-making through shock and disruption, rather than by destroying their forces through attrition. It emphasises movement, tempo, deception and the concentration of effort against critical weaknesses.

Principles

Key ideas include acting inside the opponent's decision cycle (the OODA loop described by John Boyd), mission-type orders that give subordinates freedom of action, and combined-arms teams able to move faster than the enemy can react. The German concept of Blitzkrieg in the early Second World War is a famous historical example.

History

Manoeuvre ideas informed the United States Marine Corps' doctrine published in Warfighting (1989) and influenced the US Army's AirLand Battle doctrine. Critics note that manoeuvre approaches still depend on logistics and can fail against enemies who refuse decisive engagement.
