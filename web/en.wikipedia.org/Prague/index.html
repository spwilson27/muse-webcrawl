Prague (Czech: Praha) is the capital and largest city of the Czech Republic and the historical capital of Bohemia. On the Vltava River, Prague is home to about 1.4 million people. The city has a temperate oceanic climate, with relatively warm summers and chilly winters.

Prague is a political, cultural, and economic hub of central Europe, with a rich history and Romanesque, Gothic, Renaissance and Baroque architectures. It was the capital of the Kingdom of Bohemia and residence of several Holy Roman Emperors, most notably Charles IV (r. 1346–1378) and Rudolf II (r. 1575–1611).

History

Founded during the Romanesque period and flourishing by the Gothic and Renaissance eras, Prague was the capital of the historical Bohemian state. It played a major role in the Protestant Reformation and the Thirty Years' War. The Defenestration of Prague in 1618 triggered the latter conflict. After the dissolution of Austria-Hungary in 1918, Prague became the capital of Czechoslovakia. It endured Nazi occupation during World War II and communist rule until the Velvet Revolution of 1989, after which it became the capital of an independent Czech Republic in 1993.

Landmarks

The historic centre of Prague, built between the 11th and 18th centuries, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Principal attractions include Prague Castle — the largest ancient castle complex in the world — Charles Bridge, Old Town Square with its Astronomical Clock (installed in 1410, the third-oldest in the world and the oldest still operating), the Jewish Quarter (Josefov), St. Vitus Cathedral, and Wenceslas Square.

Culture

Prague has been the home of writers such as Franz Kafka and Milan Kundera, composers Antonín Dvořák and Bedřich Smetana, and the artist Alphonse Mucha. The city hosts major institutions including Charles University (founded 1348, the oldest university in Central Europe), the National Theatre, and the Rudolfinum concert hall. Prague is also famous for its beer culture and its historic pubs.
