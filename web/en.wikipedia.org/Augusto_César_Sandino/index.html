Augusto César Sandino (18 May 1895 – 21 February 1934) was a Nicaraguan revolutionary and leader of a rebellion against the United States military occupation of Nicaragua between 1927 and 1933. He is a national hero of Nicaragua and the namesake of the Sandinista movement.

Rebellion

After working in Mexico, where he was influenced by revolutionary and anti-imperialist ideas, Sandino returned to Nicaragua during its civil war. Refusing a U.S.-brokered settlement in 1927, he led the Ejército Defensor de la Soberanía Nacional in guerrilla warfare from the Segovia mountains against U.S. Marines and the Nicaraguan National Guard. His forces eluded capture for six years, and he became a symbol of resistance across Latin America.

Death and legacy

After the Marines withdrew in 1933, Sandino negotiated with President Juan Bautista Sacasa, but on 21 February 1934 he and his generals were seized and executed by National Guard forces under Anastasio Somoza García. The Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN), founded in 1961 and named for him, overthrew the Somoza dynasty in 1979. Sandino's wide-brimmed hat became an enduring emblem of the movement.
