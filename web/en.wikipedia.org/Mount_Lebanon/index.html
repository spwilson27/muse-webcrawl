Mount Lebanon (Arabic: جبل لبنان, romanized: Jabal Lubnān) is a mountain range in Lebanon. It averages above 2,500 m (8,200 ft) in elevation, with its peak at 3,088 m (10,131 ft). The range extends along the entire country, about 170 km (110 mi) long, parallel to the Mediterranean coast. The country of Lebanon takes its name from the mountain range.

The range forms a natural barrier between the Mediterranean Sea and the Beqaa Valley to the east. Its western slopes are forested with oak, pine and the famous Cedars of Lebanon, while deep gorges cut by seasonal rivers run down toward the coast. The name "Lebanon" is rooted in the Semitic word lbn, meaning "white", probably a reference to the snow-covered peaks.

Geography

The Mount Lebanon range runs roughly southwest to northeast from near Tripoli in the north to the Litani River in the south. Its highest peak, Qurnat as Sawda' (3,088 m), is the highest point in the Levant east of the Mediterranean. Other notable peaks include Jabal el-Mnaitra (2,702 m) and Mount Sannine (2,628 m). Snow covers the higher elevations for several months each year, and the range hosts Lebanon's ski resorts, including those in the Cedars area and at Faraya.

History

In antiquity the range's cedar forests were famed throughout the Near East; cedar timber was exported to Egypt and used in Solomon's Temple in Jerusalem. The mountains historically served as a refuge for religious minorities, including Maronite Christians and Druze, who settled its valleys and founded villages there. During the Ottoman period, the Mutasarrifate of Mount Lebanon was an autonomous district from 1861 to 1918. In the modern era, the historic core of the country around the range gave its name to the Republic of Lebanon.

Ecology

Mount Lebanon's varied altitudes create distinct vegetation zones, from Mediterranean maquis on the lower slopes to cedar, fir and juniper forests at higher elevations. Nature reserves such as the Al Shouf Cedar Nature Reserve and Horsh Ehden protect remnants of the ancient forests.
