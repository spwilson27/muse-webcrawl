Medical thermometer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A medical thermometer (also called a clinical thermometer) is a device used for measuring a patient's body temperature. Measurements may be taken in the mouth (oral), under the arm (axillary), in the rectum, at the ear or on the forehead. Accurate temperature measurement helps in diagnosing fever and monitoring illness.

History

The mercury-in-glass thermometer was adapted for clinical use in the nineteenth century; Thomas Clifford Allbutt is credited with designing a practical pocket clinical thermometer in 1866. Carl Wunderlich's studies established the normal range of human body temperature and the clinical value of temperature curves.

Types

Modern devices include digital thermometers using thermistors, infrared ear and forehead thermometers, and disposable chemical dot thermometers. Mercury thermometers have been phased out in many countries because of the toxicity of mercury.

Measurement sites

Rectal measurements are generally closest to core temperature; oral readings are slightly lower and axillary readings lower still. Normal body temperature varies between individuals and across the day.
