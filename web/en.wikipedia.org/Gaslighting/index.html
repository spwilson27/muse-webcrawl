Gaslighting

Gaslighting is a form of psychological manipulation in which a person or group causes someone to doubt their own perceptions, memories or judgement. The term derives from Patrick Hamilton's 1938 play Gas Light and its film adaptations, in which a husband dims the household gas lights and denies the change to destabilise his wife.

Techniques

Common behaviours described as gaslighting include persistent denial of events, countering memories, trivialising feelings, withholding information, staging events and diverting blame. The target may become anxious, confused and dependent on the manipulator's account of reality.

Contexts

The term is used in abusive intimate relationships, families, workplaces, medicine and politics. Clinicians distinguish deliberate sustained manipulation from ordinary disagreement or forgetfulness; popular use of the word has broadened considerably.

Recognition and response

Keeping records, maintaining outside relationships, seeking therapy or advocacy and setting boundaries are often recommended. In coercive-control situations, safety planning and specialist support may be important.
