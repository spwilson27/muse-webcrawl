Quotation

A dictionary of quotations

A quotation or quote is the repetition of a sentence, phrase, or passage from speech or text that someone has said or written. In written form, quotations are set off with quotation marks and are typically accompanied by an attribution to the original author or speaker; in oral speech, a quotation is often introduced by a quotative such as "he said".

Use

Quotations are used to present well-known statements, to support or illustrate an argument, and to reproduce the words of an authority or witness exactly. Famous quotations are collected in dictionaries of quotations such as Bartlett's Familiar Quotations and the Oxford Dictionary of Quotations.

Misquotation

Many famous lines are commonly misquoted: "Let them eat cake" was never said by Marie Antoinette in that form, and "Elementary, my dear Watson" appears verbatim nowhere in the canonical Sherlock Holmes stories. Misattribution can also arise when a quotation becomes so famous that it is attached to more renowned figures, such as Mark Twain, Winston Churchill, or Albert Einstein.

In scholarship

Academic style guides regulate how quotations are formatted, shortened with ellipses, interpolated with bracketed alterations, and cited, and excessive unacknowledged quotation amounts to plagiarism.
