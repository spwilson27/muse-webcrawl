City of Bath

The City of Bath, in Somerset, England, is a historic city celebrated for its Roman baths and Georgian architecture, and inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987. An article on Bath itself is at Bath, Somerset; the World Heritage listing recognises the whole city as a cultural landscape of outstanding universal value.

Heritage

The city grew around hot springs sacred to the goddess Sulis, whom the Romans identified with Minerva; their temple and bathing complex, Aquae Sulis, is among the best-preserved Roman remains in Britain. In the 18th century Bath became England's most fashionable spa town under Beau Nash, and architects John Wood the Elder and Younger laid out the Circus, the Royal Crescent and the Assembly Rooms in honey-coloured Bath stone. In 2021 the city received a second UNESCO inscription as part of the Great Spa Towns of Europe.
