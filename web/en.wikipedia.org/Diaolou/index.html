Diaolou

Diaolou (Chinese: 碉樓; pinyin: diāolóu) are fortified multi-storey watchtowers found mainly in Kaiping and neighbouring counties of Guangdong Province, China. Built largely in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, they combine defensive functions with residential use and blend Chinese and Western architectural styles.

History

The earliest diaolou date to the late Ming dynasty, when local communities built simple stone towers against bandit raids and flooding. The building boom came in the 1920s and 1930s, financed by overseas Chinese emigrants—many of whom had worked in North America—who sent remittances home and incorporated foreign architectural elements such as Greek columns, Gothic arches, and Baroque domes into the towers.

Types

Diaolou are generally classified into three types: communal towers built by several families as refuges, residential towers built by wealthy individual families combining defense with comfortable living quarters, and watchtowers erected at village entrances or along rivers as lookouts funded by the community.

World Heritage

In 2007, UNESCO inscribed "Kaiping Diaolou and Villages" as a World Heritage Site, recognising twenty diaolou and four village clusters as outstanding examples of the fusion of Chinese and Western architectural traditions.
