Beersheba

Country: Israel
District: Southern
Population: about 210,000 (2021)

Beersheba (Hebrew: בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע‎; Arabic: بئر السبع) is the largest city in the Negev desert of southern Israel and the capital of the Southern District. Often called the "Capital of the Negev", it is home to Ben-Gurion University of the Negev and serves as a centre for Israel's technology and health sectors in the south.

History

The name means "well of the oath" or "well of seven", and the biblical patriarchs Abraham and Isaac are associated with wells at Beersheba in the Book of Genesis. The ancient settlement of Tel Beersheba, east of the modern city, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The modern town was founded by the Ottoman Empire around 1900 as an administrative centre for the Bedouin of the Negev.

Beersheba was captured by Australian light horsemen of the ANZAC Mounted Division in the famous mounted charge of 31 October 1917 during the First World War. After 1948 it developed rapidly with immigration and today is the eighth-largest city in Israel.

Geography and climate

The city lies on the northern edge of the Negev, about 115 kilometres southeast of Tel Aviv, in an arid zone of hot summers and cool winters with roughly 200 millimetres of annual rainfall.
