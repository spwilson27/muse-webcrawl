Wilton Diptych

Yearc. 1395–1399
MediumTempera on oak panel
Dimensions53 cm × 74 cm (open)
LocationNational Gallery, London

The Wilton Diptych is a small portable altarpiece consisting of two hinged oak panels, painted on both sides, made in England around 1395–1399 for King Richard II. It is one of the finest surviving examples of the International Gothic style and a masterpiece of English medieval painting.

Description

With the diptych open, the left panel shows Richard II kneeling, presented by three saints: Edmund the Martyr, Edward the Confessor and John the Baptist. In the right panel the Virgin Mary, holding the Christ Child, stands amid a host of angels, eleven of them wearing Richard's badge of the white hart. The king receives a banner of St George from the Christ Child, linking his rule to divine sanction and to England, shown as a tiny island map on the banner's orb.

History

The artist is unknown; French, Italian and Bohemian origins have all been proposed. The work is first recorded in the collection of the Earl of Pembroke at Wilton House, from which it takes its name. The National Gallery in London acquired it in 1929. Its exterior panels bear Richard's arms and the white hart badge.
