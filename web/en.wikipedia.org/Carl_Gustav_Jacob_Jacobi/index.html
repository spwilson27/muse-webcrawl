Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi

Born: 10 December 1804, Potsdam
Died: 18 February 1851, Berlin
Fields: Mathematics
Known for: Elliptic functions, Jacobian, Hamilton–Jacobi equation

Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi (10 December 1804 – 18 February 1851) was a German mathematician who made fundamental contributions to elliptic functions, dynamics, differential equations, determinants, and number theory. Along with Niels Henrik Abel, he was a founder of the theory of elliptic functions, and the Jacobian determinant and Jacobian matrix of multivariable calculus bear his name.

Life

Jacobi was born in Potsdam into a prosperous Jewish banking family and was educated at the University of Berlin, where he obtained his doctorate in 1825. He taught at the University of Königsberg from 1826, becoming a full professor in 1832; his lectures there helped found the first great German school of mathematics, attracting students such as Kirchhoff and Hesse. Ill health (he suffered from diabetes) forced him to leave Königsberg in 1843; after a stay in Italy he settled in Berlin, where he died of smallpox in 1851.

Work

In his Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum (1829), Jacobi developed the theory of elliptic functions based on the theta functions, independently complementing Abel's approach. He introduced the Jacobi elliptic functions sn, cn, and dn, and proved transformation and addition theorems. In dynamics, the Hamilton–Jacobi equation provided a powerful reformulation of classical mechanics, and his work on the rotation of a rigid body and on the figure of the Earth was influential. In algebra and analysis he studied determinants — the word "determinant" conventions and the Jacobian functional determinant are his — partial differential equations, and the theory of numbers, where the Jacobi symbol generalizes the Legendre symbol. His name is also attached to the Jacobi identity in Lie theory, the Jacobi method for eigenvalues, and the Jacobi triple product.
