Agra Fort

Agra Fort
Location: Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India
Built: 1565–1573 (main phase)
UNESCO World Heritage Site: inscribed 1983

Agra Fort (Hindi: आगरा का क़िला) is a historical fort in the city of Agra in India. It was the main residence of the emperors of the Mughal dynasty until 1638, when the capital was shifted from Agra to Delhi. It is also known as the Red Fort of Agra.

Description

The fort stands on the right bank of the Yamuna River, about 2.5 km northwest of the Taj Mahal, which it overlooks. Enclosed by massive double walls of red sandstone forming a rough semicircle of about 94 acres, it contains a maze of palaces, audience halls, and mosques built by successive emperors.

History

The Mughal emperor Akbar rebuilt the earlier brick fort in red sandstone between 1565 and 1573. His grandson Shah Jahan added white-marble palaces, including the Khas Mahal and the Pearl Mosque. Shah Jahan spent his final years imprisoned in the fort's Musamman Burj, gazing at the Taj Mahal, after his son Aurangzeb seized the throne in 1658.

The fort later passed to the Marathas and then to the British, and was a site of fighting during the Indian Rebellion of 1857. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.
