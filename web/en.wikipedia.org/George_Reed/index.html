George Reed (1939–2023) was an American-born Canadian football running back who played his entire 13-season career with the Saskatchewan Roughriders of the Canadian Football League. He retired as professional football's all-time leading rusher and remains one of the most revered figures in Saskatchewan history.

Career

Reed played college football at Washington State before joining the Roughriders in 1963. Teaming with quarterback Ron Lancaster, he led Saskatchewan to its first Grey Cup championship in 1966, when the Roughriders defeated Ottawa 29–14 and Reed was named the game's most valuable player. He rushed for 16,116 yards and a record 134 rushing touchdowns in his career, and his 16,116 yards stood as a professional football record until surpassed in the NFL years later.

Honours and later life

Reed was a ten-time CFL All-Star, won the league's Most Outstanding Player Award in 1965, and was inducted into the Canadian Football Hall of Fame in 1979. His number 34 was retired by the Roughriders. Off the field he served as president of the CFL Players' Association and worked for decades in Regina business and community life; he was appointed to the Order of Canada in 1978. He died in Regina in 2023, one day before his 84th birthday.
