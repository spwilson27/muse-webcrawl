Yokozuna (Japanese: 横綱, IPA: [jokodzɯna]) is the highest rank in professional sumo wrestling. The name derives from the thick white rope (tsuna) worn around the waist during the ring-entering ceremony, which resembles the shimenawa ropes hung at Shinto shrines. Unlike lower ranks, a yokozuna can never be demoted; a wrestler whose performance falls below the standard is instead expected to retire.

Promotion

Promotion is decided by the Japan Sumo Association on the recommendation of the Yokozuna Deliberation Council. A candidate, who must hold the second-highest rank of ōzeki, is generally expected to win two consecutive tournament championships, or an equivalent record, and to demonstrate the strength, skill, and dignity (hinkaku) appropriate to the rank. Since the formalisation of the rank, only a few dozen wrestlers — beginning with Akashi Shiganosuke, recognised retrospectively as the first — have been promoted.

Ceremony

The yokozuna performs a distinctive ring-entering ceremony (dohyō-iri) wearing the rope, which weighs about 15–20 kg and is made afresh for each tournament with the help of his stablemates. He stamps his feet to drive away demons, accompanied by a sword-bearing attendant and a dew sweeper. Notable yokozuna include Taihō, Chiyonofuji, Asashōryū, and Hakuhō, the most successful wrestler in sumo history.
