Ice-rafted debris

Ice-rafted debris (abbreviated IRD or IBRD) is sediment of any grain size that has been transported by floating ice — chiefly icebergs and sea ice — and dropped into the sea floor or lake bed as the ice melts. Because icebergs can carry coarse material far from its source, ice-rafted debris in marine sediment cores is an important proxy for past ice-sheet behaviour and ocean conditions.

Formation and transport

As glaciers calve into the ocean, they release icebergs containing rock debris eroded from the land. Ocean currents carry the bergs, sometimes thousands of kilometres, and as they melt they rain their load of gravel, sand and mud onto the deep-sea floor, where it appears as isolated dropstones within fine marine sediments.

Paleoclimate significance

Layers rich in ice-rafted debris in North Atlantic sediment cores mark Heinrich events — episodes during the last glaciation when armadas of icebergs surged from the Laurentide Ice Sheet, freshening the ocean and disrupting Atlantic circulation. The composition of the debris can be traced to source regions such as Hudson Strait, Greenland or Iceland.
