Lester B. Pearson

Lester Bowles Pearson PC CC OM (23 April 1897 – 27 December 1972) was a Canadian scholar, statesman, diplomat, and politician who served as the 14th prime minister of Canada from 1963 to 1968. Born in Newtonbrook (Toronto), Pearson pursued a career in the Department of External Affairs and served as Canadian ambassador to the United States and as President of the United Nations General Assembly. He won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1957 for organizing the United Nations Emergency Force to resolve the Suez Canal Crisis. During his tenure as prime minister, Pearson's government implemented universal health care, the Canada Pension Plan, the Canada Student Loans program, and the Order of Canada, and introduced the current Maple Leaf flag of Canada.

Early life and diplomatic career

Pearson was educated at the University of Toronto and served as a pilot in the Royal Flying Corps during the First World War. After earning a degree at Oxford, he joined Canada's foreign service in 1928. He represented Canada at international conferences through the 1930s and 1940s, was ambassador to the United States (1945–46), and in 1948 became Secretary of State for External Affairs in Louis St. Laurent's government. He presided over the UN General Assembly in 1952–53 and was a central figure in the creation of NATO-era Canadian foreign policy.

Nobel Peace Prize and the Suez Crisis

During the Suez Crisis of 1956, Pearson proposed the creation of the first large-scale UN peacekeeping force — the United Nations Emergency Force — to supervise the withdrawal of invading forces from Egypt and patrol the border. The proposal defused an explosive confrontation, and Pearson received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1957, the first Canadian to win it individually. The episode made "peacekeeping" a signature element of Canadian identity.

Prime minister

Pearson became leader of the Liberal Party in 1958 and prime minister after the 1963 election, governing with minorities. His governments introduced the Maple Leaf flag (1965), national medicare legislation (1966), the Canada Pension Plan, bilingualism and biculturalism commissions, and the Order of Canada (1967). He retired in 1968, succeeded by Pierre Trudeau, and died in Ottawa in 1972. Toronto's international airport is named in his honour.
