Summis desiderantes affectibus

Summis desiderantes affectibus ("Desiring with supreme ardor"), sometimes called the Witch Bull, is a papal bull issued by Pope Innocent VIII on 5 December 1484. The bull, which was promulgated in response to the request of the Dominican inquisitors Heinrich Kramer and Jacob Sprenger, confirmed their authority to prosecute witchcraft in Germany and is frequently printed as a preface to the Malleus Maleficarum (1487).

Content

The bull laments that "many persons of both sexes, unmindful of their own salvation and straying from the Catholic Faith, have abandoned themselves to devils, incubi and succubi," and describes alleged harms including blighted crops, afflicted livestock, and impeded reproduction. It deplores the obstruction of the inquisitors by local clergy and officials, grants Kramer and Sprenger full authority to proceed, and orders the Bishop of Strasbourg to lend them support, threatening obstructors with excommunication.

Context and significance

The bull did not invent witch belief, nor did it order a general witch hunt; rather, it removed jurisdictional obstacles for two inquisitors in the Rhineland. Nevertheless, by lending papal endorsement to the prosecution of witchcraft, and through its association with the Malleus Maleficarum, it became one of the most notorious documents of the early European witch trials. Modern scholars debate its practical influence compared with secular courts and local panics.
