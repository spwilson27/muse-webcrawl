Kempner function

In number theory, the Kempner function S(n) is defined for a positive integer n as the smallest positive integer s such that n divides the factorial s!. It is also called the Smarandache function, having been rediscovered by Florentin Smarandache in 1980; Aubrey J. Kempner first studied it in 1918.

For a prime p, S(p) = p. For composite n, S(n) is determined by the largest prime-power factor: if n = ∏pᵢaᵢ, then S(n) is the maximum over i of S(pᵢaᵢ). Relatedly, S(n) is the largest exponent appearing when n is expressed in factorial number systems, and the function grows roughly like the largest prime factor of n.
