Lincoln Steffens

Joseph Lincoln Steffens (April 6, 1866 – August 9, 1936) was an American investigative journalist and one of the leading muckrakers of the Progressive Era. He specialised in exposing corruption in municipal government.

Career

Steffens worked for the New York Commercial Advertiser and later joined McClure's Magazine, where his series on corrupt city governments — beginning with "Tweed Days in St. Louis" (1902) — was collected as The Shame of the Cities (1904). The book documented graft and the alliance of businessmen and politicians in cities including St. Louis, Minneapolis, Pittsburgh, and Philadelphia, arguing that corruption was systemic rather than the fault of individual "bad men".

Later life

Steffens later reported on the Mexican Revolution and the Russian Revolution; after visiting Soviet Russia in 1919 he famously declared, "I have seen the future, and it works" — often quoted in shortened form. His Autobiography (1931) remains a classic of American journalism.
