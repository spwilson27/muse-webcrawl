Hyperparathyroidism

Hyperparathyroidism is an increase in parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels in the blood. It occurs when one or more of the parathyroid glands (usually four small glands in the neck, behind the thyroid) produce excess PTH. PTH raises blood calcium by increasing bone resorption, calcium reabsorption in the kidneys and intestinal absorption via activated vitamin D.

Types

  • Primary hyperparathyroidism: a disorder of the glands themselves, usually a benign single adenoma (~85% of cases), sometimes hyperplasia of all glands or, rarely, parathyroid carcinoma. It may occur as part of multiple endocrine neoplasia syndromes.
  • Secondary hyperparathyroidism: appropriate gland stimulation by low calcium or high phosphate, most often in chronic kidney disease or vitamin D deficiency.
  • Tertiary hyperparathyroidism: autonomous PTH secretion developing after long-standing secondary hyperparathyroidism, typically after renal transplantation or in end-stage renal disease.

Symptoms

Excess calcium produces the classic "stones, bones, groans and moans": kidney stones, bone pain and osteoporosis (including the cystic bone changes of osteitis fibrosa cystica), abdominal complaints, and psychiatric symptoms such as fatigue and depression. Many cases of primary disease are now found incidentally on blood tests.

Treatment

The definitive treatment for symptomatic primary hyperparathyroidism is parathyroidectomy. Asymptomatic patients may be monitored, and calcimimetic drugs such as cinacalcet can lower calcium and PTH.
