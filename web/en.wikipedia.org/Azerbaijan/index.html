Azerbaijan, officially the Republic of Azerbaijan, is a transcontinental country located at the boundary of Eastern Europe and West Asia. It is part of the South Caucasus region and is bounded by the Caspian Sea to the east, Russia's Dagestan to the north, Georgia to the northwest, Armenia and Turkey to the west, and Iran to the south. The exclave of Nakhchivan is separated from the main territory by Armenia. The capital and largest city is Baku.

History

The territory was successively ruled by Caucasian Albanians, Persian empires, Arabs, Seljuk Turks, Mongols and Safavids; the modern Azerbaijani identity emerged among Turkic-speaking Muslims of the region. After the Russo-Persian wars, the treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828) divided Azerbaijani lands between the Russian and Persian empires. Following the Russian Revolution, the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic (1918–1920) became the first secular democratic Muslim-majority state, before incorporation into the Soviet Union as the Azerbaijan SSR. Azerbaijan declared independence from the USSR on 30 August 1991, just before the Soviet collapse.

Independence coincided with the First Nagorno-Karabakh War with Armenia, which left the Nagorno-Karabakh region and surrounding districts under Armenian control. After the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War of 2020 and an Azerbaijani offensive in September 2023, Baku restored control over the entire territory, and the ethnic Armenian population of the region largely fled to Armenia.

Geography and economy

Azerbaijan spans the Greater and Lesser Caucasus ranges, the Kura-Aras lowland and the Caspian coast; mud volcanoes and the burning gas vents of Yanar Dag reflect its rich hydrocarbon geology. Oil and gas dominate the economy — Baku's oil industry dates to the 19th-century boom, and the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline carries Caspian crude to the Mediterranean. Agriculture, including cotton, grain and viticulture, and services make up most of the remainder.

Culture

Azerbaijani culture blends Turkic, Persian, Islamic and Caucasian influences. Mugham music, ashik minstrel poetry, miniature painting, carpets, and Novruz (spring new year) celebrations are inscribed on UNESCO's heritage lists. The country is constitutionally secular, with a majority Shia Muslim population.
