An oblast is a type of federal subject of the Russian Federation. Russia has 48 oblasts, the most numerous category of its federal subjects, which also include republics, krais, federal cities, okrugs and one autonomous oblast. Unlike the republics, oblasts have no ethnic basis and no constitution of their own; they are governed under charters, each headed by a governor and an elected regional legislature.

Overview

Oblasts range from Kaliningrad, an exclave on the Baltic, to Sakhalin in the Pacific. The most populous is Moscow Oblast, surrounding but administratively separate from the federal city of Moscow; others include Leningrad Oblast (around Saint Petersburg), Sverdlovsk, Novosibirsk and Rostov oblasts. Their administrative centres are generally the largest city of the same name.

History

The oblast system descends from the administrative divisions of the Russian Empire and was greatly reshaped in the Soviet period, when many new oblasts were created by subdividing earlier gubernias. The current set largely dates from the late Soviet era, with a few mergers of smaller subjects in the 2000s reducing the number of okrugs rather than oblasts.
