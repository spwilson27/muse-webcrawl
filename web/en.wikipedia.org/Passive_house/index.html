Passive house

Passive house (German: Passivhaus) is a voluntary standard for energy efficiency in a building, which reduces the building's carbon footprint. It results in ultra-low-energy buildings that require little energy for space heating or cooling, typically using up to 90% less heating energy than conventional buildings.

Principles

The standard rests on five principles: high levels of insulation, high-performance windows (usually triple-glazed), an airtight building envelope, elimination of thermal bridges, and mechanical ventilation with heat recovery (MVHR), which supplies fresh air while retaining most of the heat from exhaust air. Careful orientation and shading exploit passive solar gains.

History

The concept was developed by Wolfgang Feist in Germany and Bo Adamson in Sweden. The first certified passive house was built in Darmstadt-Kranichstein in 1991. The Passivhaus Institut in Darmstadt oversees certification. Tens of thousands of passive houses have since been built worldwide, mostly in Europe, and the approach has been extended to schools, offices, and retrofits (the EnerPHit standard).
