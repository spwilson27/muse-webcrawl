Moghalrajpuram Caves

LocationVijayawada, Andhra Pradesh, India
Periodc. 5th century CE
PatronageEastern Chalukya era
DeitiesShiva, Ardhanarishvara, Vinayaka

The Moghalrajpuram Caves are a group of ancient rock-cut cave temples in Vijayawada, Andhra Pradesh, India. Excavated into a hillock in the Moghalrajpuram locality of the city, they date to roughly the 5th century CE and are among the earliest Hindu rock-cut shrines in the Deccan region.

Description

The group comprises several small cave shrines carved into the rock face. The caves are noted for their sculptures, including a celebrated figure of Ardhanarishvara (the half-male, half-female form of Shiva), a dancing Shiva (Nataraja), and images of Vinayaka (Ganesha) and other deities. One cave contains a rock-cut lingam shrine. The carvings are regarded as important early examples of the transition from Buddhist rock-cut architecture — long established in the Krishna valley — to Hindu cave temples.

Protection

The caves are protected monuments under the Archaeological Survey of India. Urban growth around the hill has long posed conservation challenges, and the site lies close to other historic Vijayawada landmarks such as the Undavalli Caves across the Krishna River.
