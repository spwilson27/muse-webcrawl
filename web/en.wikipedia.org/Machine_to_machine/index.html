Machine to machine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Machine to machine (M2M) is direct communication between devices using any communications channel, including wired and wireless systems. M2M communication allows networked devices to exchange information and perform actions without manual human assistance, and it underlies applications such as telemetry, remote monitoring, fleet tracking, smart metering and industrial automation. M2M is closely related to, and historically preceded, the broader concept of the Internet of things (IoT).

Definition and architecture

A typical M2M system consists of end devices fitted with sensors or actuators and a communications module; a network — cellular, satellite, Wi-Fi, low-power wide-area radio such as LoRa, or wired Ethernet; and an application platform that collects, processes and presents the data. Devices may report measurements (temperatures, meter readings, vehicle positions) at intervals or on events, receive commands or configuration updates, and operate unattended for years on battery power or energy harvesting.

Applications

Early M2M applications emerged in industrial telemetry and supervisory control (SCADA) systems. In the 1990s and 2000s, GSM and later cellular data modems made M2M economical for automatic utility meter reading, vending-machine stock monitoring, point-of-sale terminals, vehicle telematics and insurance "black box" recorders, and remote monitoring of lifts, alarms and medical devices. Network operators created dedicated M2M service divisions, and standardisation bodies such as ETSI and the oneM2M partnership developed common service-layer standards.

Distinction from IoT

M2M generally refers to isolated, point-to-point or centrally managed systems using embedded hardware and specific networks, whereas IoT describes the wider integration of such devices with internet protocols, cloud platforms and data analytics. In practice the terms overlap, and much present-day IoT infrastructure descends directly from M2M practice. Security, device management and scalability are central concerns in both fields.
