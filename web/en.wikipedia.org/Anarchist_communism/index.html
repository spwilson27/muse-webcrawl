Anarchist communism

Anarchist communism, also known as anarcho-communism or libertarian communism, is a political philosophy within anarchism advocating the abolition of the state, capitalism, wage labour, and private property, in favour of common ownership of the means of production and a society organized through voluntary associations and communes, summarized in the principle "from each according to his ability, to each according to his needs".

History

The current took shape in the late 19th century within the First International. Peter Kropotkin became its most influential theorist, setting out the doctrine in works such as The Conquest of Bread (1892) and Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution (1902). Errico Malatesta and Carlo Cafiero were also key early proponents, and anarcho-communists were active in revolutionary movements in Russia, Ukraine, Spain, and elsewhere.

Principles

Anarchist communists propose replacing markets and money with a gift economy of freely distributed goods organized by communes and federations. They generally reject transitional state structures, arguing that the means of revolution must prefigure its ends, and favour direct action and mass organization.
