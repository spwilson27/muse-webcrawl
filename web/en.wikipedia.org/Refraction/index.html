Refraction

In physics, refraction is the redirection of a wave as it passes from one medium to another. The redirection can be caused by the wave's change in speed or by a change in the medium. Refraction of light is the most commonly observed phenomenon, but other waves such as sound and water waves also refract.

Snell's law

The amount of bending is described by Snell's law: n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2, where n is each medium's refractive index. Because a medium's index varies slightly with wavelength (dispersion), refraction separates white light into a spectrum, as in prisms and rainbows.

Applications

Lenses exploit refraction to focus light in eyes, cameras, telescopes and microscopes. Refraction also explains mirages, the apparent displacement of stars, and the shallower appearance of submerged objects. When light passes to a less dense medium beyond the critical angle, total internal reflection occurs, the principle behind optical fibres.
