Linji Yixuan (Chinese: 臨濟義玄; died 866 or 867) was a Chinese Chan Buddhist master of the Tang dynasty and the founder of the Linji school of Chan, known in Japan as Rinzai Zen. He was a student of Huangbo Xiyun, in the lineage descending from Mazu Daoyi.

Teaching

Linji taught at a small temple in Zhenzhou (in modern Hebei). His recorded sayings, the Línjì lù ("Record of Linji"), compiled by his disciples, are famous for their iconoclastic style: shouts (katsu), blows with a stick, and paradoxical injunctions such as "If you meet the Buddha on the road, kill him" — urging students to abandon dependence on external authority and concepts. He distinguished the "true person of no rank" and classified teachings using devices such as the Three Statements, Three Essentials, and Four Shouts.

Legacy

The Linji school became one of the two dominant branches of Chinese Chan and, transmitted to Japan by Eisai in the 12th century, the Rinzai school of Zen, famous for kōan practice. The Record of Linji remains one of the most influential classics of Zen literature.
