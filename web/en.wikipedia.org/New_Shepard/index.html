New Shepard

New Shepard is a fully reusable sub-orbital launch vehicle developed by the American aerospace company Blue Origin. Named after Alan Shepard, the first American in space, it is designed to carry passengers and research payloads on brief flights above the Karman line, the conventional boundary of space at 100 kilometres altitude.

Design

The vehicle consists of a single-stage booster powered by a BE-3 engine burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen, topped by a crew capsule with large windows. After liftoff from Launch Site One in West Texas, the capsule separates from the booster; the booster returns for a powered vertical landing, while the capsule coasts above 100 km, giving occupants several minutes of weightlessness, before descending under three parachutes.

Flight history

New Shepard first flew on 29 April 2015. Its first crewed flight, NS-16 on 20 July 2021, carried Blue Origin founder Jeff Bezos, his brother Mark, aviator Wally Funk and student Oliver Daemen. Subsequent crewed flights have carried celebrities, researchers and paying tourists, including William Shatner, who at 90 became the oldest person to reach space in October 2021. The program paused after an uncrewed booster failure in September 2022 (flight NS-23) and returned to flight in December 2023.
