Baccarat

Baccarat

Type: Comparing card game

Players: Variable; bets on two hands

Cards: Multiple standard 52-card decks

Main variants: Punto banco, chemin de fer, baccarat banque

Baccarat is a card game played at casinos. It is a comparing card game played between two hands, the "player" and the "banker". Each baccarat coup has three possible outcomes: "player" (player has the higher score), "banker", and "tie".

Scoring

Cards two through nine are worth face value, tens and face cards are worth zero, and aces are worth one. A hand's score is the units digit of the sum of its cards, so the best possible score is nine. A two-card total of eight or nine is a "natural" and ends the coup; otherwise fixed tableau rules determine whether the player and banker hands draw a third card. Players simply bet on which hand will win (or on a tie) and have no decisions to make in the dominant punto banco variant.

History and variants

The game is of Italian origin (from baccara, medieval Italian for zero, referring to the value of face cards) and was popularised at the French court of the 19th century. In chemin de fer and baccarat banque, players themselves can hold the bank and make drawing decisions. Punto banco, developed in Cuba and popularised in Las Vegas and Macau, is played purely as a game of chance against the house.

Popularity

Baccarat is the most important game in Macau's casinos, generating the majority of gaming revenue there, and is a staple of high-stakes salons privés worldwide. It also features prominently in Ian Fleming's James Bond novels, most notably Casino Royale.
