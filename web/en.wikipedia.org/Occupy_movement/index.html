Occupy movement

The Occupy movement was an international populist socio-political movement that expressed opposition to social and economic inequality and to the perceived lack of "real democracy" around the world. It aimed primarily to advance social and economic justice and different forms of democracy. The movement has had many different scopes; local groups often had different focuses, but many of the movement's prime concerns were how large corporations (and the global financial system) control the world in a way that disproportionately benefits a minority, undermines democracy and discourages self-determination.

Origins

The first Occupy protest to receive widespread attention, Occupy Wall Street, began on 17 September 2011 in Zuccotti Park, located in New York City's Wall Street financial district, and lasted until its eviction on 15 November. The Canadian anti-consumerist magazine Adbusters initiated the call for a protest, inspired by the Arab Spring and by Spain's anti-austerity movement. The slogan "We are the 99%" referred to income and wealth inequality in the United States.

Spread and methods

By October 2011, Occupy protests had spread to more than 900 cities across the world. The movement used encampments in public spaces, horizontal organisation, general assemblies and consensus decision-making, and techniques such as the "human microphone". Spin-offs included Occupy Sandy, a relief effort after Hurricane Sandy, and campaigns against student and household debt.

Legacy

Although most encampments were cleared by authorities by the end of 2011, Occupy popularised public discussion of inequality and influenced later movements and political campaigns in several countries.
