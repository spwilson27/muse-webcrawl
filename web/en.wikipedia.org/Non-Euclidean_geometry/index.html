Non-Euclidean geometry

Non-Euclidean geometry
FieldMathematics
Key figuresNikolai Lobachevsky, János Bolyai, Bernhard Riemann, Carl Friedrich Gauss (unpublished)

OriginAttempts to prove Euclid's parallel postulate, 1820s–1850s

In mathematics, non-Euclidean geometry consists of geometries based on axioms closely related to those of Euclid's classical geometry but in which the parallel postulate is replaced: through a point not on a given line there may be either many lines parallel to the given line (hyperbolic geometry) or none at all (elliptic geometry). The discovery that such geometries are internally consistent was one of the landmark achievements of 19th-century mathematics, transforming the understanding of axioms, proof and physical space itself.

History

For two thousand years mathematicians tried to derive Euclid's fifth postulate from the others. In the 1820s and 1830s, Nikolai Lobachevsky in Russia and János Bolyai in Hungary independently published consistent hyperbolic geometries, while Carl Friedrich Gauss arrived at similar ideas but never published them. In 1854 Bernhard Riemann delivered his famous habilitation lecture, generalising geometry to spaces of any curvature — the framework that, sixty years later, would underpin Einstein's general theory of relativity, in which the geometry of spacetime is non-Euclidean.

Properties

In hyperbolic geometry, the angles of a triangle sum to less than 180°, there are no similar triangles of different sizes, and area is determined directly by the angle deficit. In elliptic geometry, exemplified by geometry on a sphere, "lines" are great circles, all such lines meet, and triangle angle sums exceed 180°. Eugenio Beltrami's models (1868) proved that if Euclidean geometry is consistent, so is hyperbolic geometry.

Significance

Non-Euclidean geometry showed that the axioms of geometry are not self-evident truths about the world but hypotheses to be tested, opening the way to modern differential geometry, topology and the geometric formulation of cosmology.
