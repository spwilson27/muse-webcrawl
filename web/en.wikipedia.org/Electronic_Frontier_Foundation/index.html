Electronic Frontier Foundation

The Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) is an international non-profit digital rights group based in San Francisco, California. Founded in 1990, it provides funds for legal defense in cases involving digital civil liberties, advocates for user privacy and free expression, and develops privacy-enhancing technologies.

EFF was founded by Mitch Kapor, John Perry Barlow, and John Gilmore and is supported mainly by donations.

History

The EFF was established in July 1990 in response to government raids on computer bulletin board systems, including the Secret Service raid on Steve Jackson Games. Early cases included Bernstein v. United States, which established that software source code is protected speech and helped end export restrictions on cryptography.

Activities

EFF litigates and files amicus briefs in cases concerning surveillance, copyright, patents, and encryption. It publishes research on government surveillance (including litigation over NSA mass surveillance revealed in 2013), maintains tools such as the browser extension Privacy Badger, and co-developed the HTTPS Everywhere extension and the Let's Encrypt certificate authority. It also supports the Tor anonymity network and publishes the long-running newsletter EFFector.
