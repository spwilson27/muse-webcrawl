Anne Boleyn (c. 1501 or 1507 – 19 May 1536) was Queen of England from 1533 to 1536, as the second wife of King Henry VIII. Henry's marriage to her, and her subsequent execution, were part of the complex beginnings of the considerable political and religious upheaval known as the English Reformation, in which Anne herself actively promoted the cause of Church reform.

Early life

Anne was the daughter of Thomas Boleyn and Lady Elizabeth Howard. Educated in the Netherlands and France, she served as a lady-in-waiting to Queen Claude of France before returning to England in 1522. She was pursued by the king after becoming maid of honour to his first wife, Catherine of Aragon, but refused to become his mistress, insisting on marriage.

Marriage and queenship

Henry's efforts to annul his first marriage — the "King's Great Matter" — led to England's break with the Roman Catholic Church. Anne married Henry in January 1533 and was crowned queen consort on 1 June 1533. In September she gave birth to the future Elizabeth I. The expected male heirs did not survive, and her position weakened.

Fall and execution

In the spring of 1536 Anne was arrested and charged with adultery, incest with her brother George Boleyn, and plotting the king's death — charges historians generally regard as fabricated. She was convicted and beheaded at the Tower of London on 19 May 1536.

Legacy

Anne has been called "the most influential and important queen consort England has ever had", since she provided the impetus for Henry's annulment and England's separation from Rome. Her daughter Elizabeth became one of England's most celebrated monarchs.
