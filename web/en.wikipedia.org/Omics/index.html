Omics

FieldBiology, systems biology
Major branchesGenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics
ApproachHigh-throughput, whole-set measurement

Omics is a collective term for branches of biology whose names end in the suffix -omics and which aim to characterize and quantify entire sets of biological molecules of a given kind. The suffix derives from -ome (as in genome), denoting a totality.

Principal fields

Genomics studies the complete set of DNA; transcriptomics the complete set of RNA transcripts; proteomics the proteins; and metabolomics the small-molecule metabolites. Other recognized omes include the epigenome, lipidome, glycome, microbiome (together with metagenomics), and exposome. Enabled by high-throughput technologies — next-generation sequencing, mass spectrometry, microarrays — these fields generate large datasets analyzed computationally, often in combination ("multi-omics") to model an organism or disease state as a system.

Applications

Omics approaches underpin precision medicine (pharmacogenomics), cancer classification, biomarker discovery, crop improvement, and environmental monitoring. The style has also spread beyond biology, inspiring terms such as radiomics and culturomics.
