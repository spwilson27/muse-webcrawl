Travel visa

A travel visa (from Latin charta visa, meaning "paper that has been seen") is a conditional authorisation granted by a polity to a foreigner that allows them to enter, remain within, or leave its territory. Visas typically include limits on the duration of the foreigner's stay, areas within the country they may enter, the dates they may enter, the number of permitted visits, or the individual's right to work in the country.

Types

Common categories include tourist, transit, business, student, work, working holiday, diplomatic, and immigrant visas. Entry mechanisms range from visa-free entry, visa on arrival, and electronic visas (eVisas) requiring prior online approval, to visas obtained in advance from an embassy or consulate. The Schengen Area operates a common visa policy for short stays.

Application and refusal

Applicants usually must show a valid passport, evidence of funds and onward travel, and the purpose of the visit; refusals may be based on security, immigration risk, or incomplete documentation. A visa does not guarantee entry: border officials make the final decision on admission.

History

Modern passport and visa controls largely date from the First World War, when temporary wartime measures became permanent. Visa "power rankings" of passports measure the number of destinations holders can access without a prior visa.
