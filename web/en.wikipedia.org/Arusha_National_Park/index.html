Arusha National Park

Location: Arusha Region, Tanzania

Nearest city: Arusha

Area: 552 km² (213 sq mi)

Established: 1960

Governing body: Tanzania National Parks Authority (TANAPA)

Arusha National Park covers Mount Meru, a prominent volcano with an elevation of 4,566 m, in the Arusha Region of north eastern Tanzania. The park is small but varied with spectacular landscapes in three distinct areas.

In the west, the Meru Crater funnels the waters of the Jekukumia River; the peak of Mount Meru lies on its rim. Ngurdoto Crater in the south-east is grassland. The shallow alkaline Momella Lakes in the north-east have varying algal colours and are known for their wading birds.

Wildlife

The park has abundant wildlife but visitors should not expect the same game-viewing experience they find in Serengeti National Park. Its animal population includes African bush elephants, Cape buffaloes, hippopotamuses, zebras, giraffes (the park has the largest population of giraffes in Tanzania), warthogs, black-and-white colobus monkeys, blue monkeys, flamingos, and leopards. Lions are absent and there are no rhinos, but walking safaris with armed rangers are a popular way to experience the park up close.

Mount Meru

Mount Meru, Tanzania's second-highest mountain, dominates the park. Its multi-day climb — typically three to four days — serves both as a destination in itself and as acclimatisation for Mount Kilimanjaro, which lies about 60 km to the east and is visible from the park on clear days.

History

The area was first established as the Ngurdoto Crater National Park in 1960 and was expanded and renamed Arusha National Park in 1967, incorporating the Momella Lakes and Mount Meru.
