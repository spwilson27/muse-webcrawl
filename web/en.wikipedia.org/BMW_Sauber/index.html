BMW Sauber was a Formula One constructor that competed in the FIA Formula One World Championship from 2006 to 2009. The team was formed in late 2005 when the German automobile manufacturer BMW purchased the Swiss-based Sauber team, which had competed in Formula One since 1993. The team was officially known as the BMW Sauber F1 Team, retaining the Sauber name in recognition of founder Peter Sauber and the team's base at Hinwil, Switzerland.

History

Formation

BMW had previously supplied engines to the Williams team from 2000 to 2005, winning ten races. Dissatisfied with the partnership, BMW bought a majority stake in Sauber in June 2005 and took full control for the 2006 season, while Williams switched to Cosworth engines. Peter Sauber retained a 20 percent stake and took an advisory role.

2006–2007

In its debut season of 2006, BMW Sauber finished fifth in the Constructors' Championship, with two podium finishes by Nick Heidfeld and Jacques Villeneuve. Villeneuve was replaced mid-season by Polish test driver Robert Kubica, who scored a podium in only his third race at Monza. In 2007 the team improved to second place in the championship (after McLaren's disqualification), establishing itself as the third-fastest team behind Ferrari and McLaren.

2008: First victory

The 2008 season brought the team's only victory: at the Canadian Grand Prix, Robert Kubica won the race with Heidfeld second, exactly one year after Kubica's serious accident at the same circuit. Kubica briefly led the Drivers' Championship, and the team finished third in the Constructors' standings.

2009 and withdrawal

The 2009 season, run under radically revised aerodynamic regulations, was disappointing; the F1.09 was uncompetitive and the team scored only two podium finishes. On 29 July 2009, in the wake of the global financial crisis, BMW announced its withdrawal from Formula One at the end of the season. The team was sold back to Peter Sauber in November 2009 and competed from 2010 onward once again under the Sauber name.
