Reflection (physics)

In specular reflection, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.

Reflection is the change in direction of a wavefront at an interface between two different media so that the wavefront returns into the medium from which it originated. Common examples include the reflection of light, sound and water waves. The law of reflection states that for specular reflection (as from a mirror) the angle at which the wave is incident on the surface equals the angle at which it is reflected, both measured from the normal to the surface at the point of incidence.

Reflection of light

Light reflecting from a smooth surface undergoes specular reflection, producing a clear image, as in a mirror. Rough surfaces scatter light in many directions — diffuse reflection — which is how most objects are visible by reflected ambient light. The amount of light reflected depends on the materials and the angle of incidence, described by the Fresnel equations; at grazing angles even matte surfaces become glossy. When light travels toward a denser-to-rarer boundary beyond the critical angle, it undergoes total internal reflection, the principle behind optical fibres and the sparkle of cut diamonds.

Reflection of sound and other waves

Sound waves reflect from hard surfaces, producing echoes and reverberation, exploited in sonar, medical ultrasound and architectural acoustics. Seismic waves reflect from boundaries between rock layers, allowing geophysicists to image the Earth's interior. Radio waves reflect from the ionosphere and from conducting surfaces, and radar depends on detecting reflections from distant objects. Reflection also occurs for particles and for waves on strings, where a fixed end inverts the reflected pulse.

Applications

Reflection underlies mirrors, telescopes, periscopes, retroreflectors (including the arrays left on the Moon for lunar laser ranging), echo sounding, ultrasound imaging, and reflective safety materials. Anti-reflective coatings work by arranging destructive interference between reflections from successive interfaces.
