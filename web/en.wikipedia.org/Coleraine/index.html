Coleraine

Coleraine (from Irish Cúil Rathain, "nook of the ferns") is a town and civil parish in County Londonderry, Northern Ireland, near the mouth of the River Bann. It had a population of around 24,000 at the 2011 census. The town lies about 55 miles (89 km) northwest of Belfast and forms part of the Causeway Coast and Glens district.

History

The area has evidence of some of the earliest human settlement in Ireland at nearby Mount Sandel, dating to around 7000 BC. Coleraine received its charter in 1613 during the Plantation of Ulster, when the Honourable The Irish Society rebuilt the town as a fortified settlement. It was besieged during the Williamite war in 1689. In the twentieth century the town grew as a market and services centre for the north coast.

Modern town

Coleraine is the main campus town of Ulster University, whose Coleraine campus was founded in 1968. The town is a gateway to the Causeway Coast, including Portrush, Portstewart and the Giant's Causeway. It sits on the Belfast–Derry railway line and the A26 and A29 roads. The River Bann, the longest river in Northern Ireland, flows through the town.
