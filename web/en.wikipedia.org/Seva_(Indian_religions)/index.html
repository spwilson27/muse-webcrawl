Seva (Indian religions)

Volunteers serving food at a langar, a central expression of seva in Sikhism

Sevā is a Sanskrit word meaning selfless service. It is a central concept in several Indian religions, including Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and especially Sikhism, in which performing service without expectation of reward is regarded as a path to spiritual growth and a duty toward humanity.

In Sikhism

In Sikh tradition, seva is one of the foundations of religious life, alongside honest work (kirat karo) and sharing with others (vaṇḍ chakkō). Its best-known expression is the langar, the free community kitchen attached to every gurdwara, in which volunteers cook and serve meals to all visitors regardless of religion, caste or wealth. Seva also includes cleaning the gurdwara, caring for the sick and poor, and volunteering in disaster relief, activities for which Sikh organizations are widely noted.

In Hinduism and other traditions

In Hinduism, seva is service offered to God, a guru or society, closely related to the ideas of karma yoga — the discipline of selfless action taught in the Bhagavad Gita — and to charitable giving (dāna). Monasteries and temples run schools, hospitals and kitchens as forms of seva. In Jainism and Buddhism, service to monks, the poor and all living beings is likewise a means of accumulating merit and weakening ego and attachment.

Meaning

The essential feature of seva is that it is performed selflessly, without desire for recognition or reward; the word for one who performs it is sevak or sevadar. Modern usage in India extends the term broadly to public and community service.
