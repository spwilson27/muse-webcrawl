Arthur Hallam

Born: 1 February 1811, London, England

Died: 15 September 1833, Vienna, Austria

Occupation: Poet, essayist

Known for: Subject of Tennyson's In Memoriam

Arthur Henry Hallam (1 February 1811 – 15 September 1833) was an English poet and essayist, best known as the subject of his close friend Alfred, Lord Tennyson's celebrated elegy In Memoriam A.H.H. His early death at the age of twenty-two cut short a career of exceptional intellectual promise.

Life

Hallam was the son of the historian Henry Hallam. He was educated at Eton College and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he became a member of the Cambridge Apostles and formed his intense friendship with Tennyson. The two travelled together in France and the Pyrenees, and Hallam became engaged to Tennyson's sister Emily. In 1831 he published a notable essay on Tennyson's early poems, defending their "poetry of sensation", and wrote poetry of his own that contemporaries admired.

Death and legacy

In the summer of 1833 Hallam travelled to Austria with his father and died suddenly in Vienna of a cerebral haemorrhage. Tennyson's grief produced In Memoriam, published in 1850, which meditated on grief, faith and evolutionary science and became one of the defining works of Victorian literature. Hallam is buried at St Andrew's Church, Clevedon, in Somerset.
