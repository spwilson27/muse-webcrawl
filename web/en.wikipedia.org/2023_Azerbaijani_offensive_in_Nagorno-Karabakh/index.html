The 2023 Azerbaijani offensive in Nagorno-Karabakh was a military operation launched by Azerbaijan on 19 September 2023 against the self-proclaimed Republic of Artsakh in Nagorno-Karabakh, a region internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan but governed since the 1990s by its ethnic Armenian majority.

Background

Azerbaijan had recovered surrounding territories in the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War of 2020. From December 2022 Azerbaijan effectively blockaded the Lachin corridor, the region's only road link to Armenia, causing severe shortages of food, fuel and medicine for the remaining Armenian population.

Offensive and outcome

Describing the operation as an "anti-terrorist" measure, Azerbaijan attacked Artsakh Defence Army positions with artillery, drones and aircraft. After roughly 24 hours of fighting, the Artsakh authorities accepted a Russian-mediated ceasefire requiring the disarmament and dissolution of their forces. On 28 September the president of Artsakh signed a decree dissolving the republic effective 1 January 2024. In the following days, more than 100,000 ethnic Armenians — nearly the entire population — fled to Armenia, an exodus widely described as ethnic cleansing by international observers and condemned by Armenia.
