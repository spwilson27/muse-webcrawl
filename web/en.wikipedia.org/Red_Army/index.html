The Workers' and Peasants' Red Army, often shortened to the Red Army, was the army and air force of the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic and, after 1922, of the Soviet Union. Founded by Leon Trotsky as the armed force of the Bolsheviks during the Russian Civil War, it grew into the largest land force of the Second World War and the principal military instrument of the USSR. In February 1946 it was renamed the Soviet Army.

Russian Civil War

The Council of People's Commissars created the Red Army by decree on 28 January 1918. Trotsky, as People's Commissar for Military and Naval Affairs, built it into a mass conscript force, recruiting former Imperial officers ("military specialists") supervised by political commissars. It defeated the White Armies in the Civil War (1917–1923) and fought the Polish–Soviet War, securing the survival of the Bolshevik state.

Interwar period and purges

Under Mikhail Frunze and later reformers the army developed the theory of deep battle. Stalin's Great Purge of 1937–1938, however, executed or imprisoned much of the high command, including Marshal Mikhail Tukhachevsky, with lasting damage to readiness — evident in the Winter War against Finland (1939–1940).

Great Patriotic War

Germany's invasion in June 1941 (Operation Barbarossa) inflicted catastrophic losses, but the Red Army recovered: it halted the Wehrmacht before Moscow, won the Battle of Stalingrad (1942–1943) and the Battle of Kursk (1943), then advanced through Eastern Europe in operations such as Bagration (1944) to capture Berlin in May 1945. The Red Army bore the brunt of the land war against Nazi Germany; Soviet military deaths are estimated at around 8.7 million. After victory in Europe it invaded Manchuria in August 1945.

Postwar

In 1946 the Red Army was renamed the Soviet Army within the Armed Forces of the USSR. It remained stationed across Eastern Europe and became the core of the Warsaw Pact during the Cold War.
