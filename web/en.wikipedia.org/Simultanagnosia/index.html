Simultanagnosia

Simultanagnosia (or simultagnosia) is a rare neurological disorder characterised by the inability of an individual to visually perceive more than one object at a time. This is distinct from visual agnosia, where the patient suffers an inability to categorise objects. Patients with simultanagnosia can identify the fine details of an image but cannot grasp the overall meaning.

Presentation

Patients typically describe seeing a complex scene in a piecemeal fashion: they may report one object at a time while being unaware of others, so that, for example, they can see a fork on a table but not the plate beside it, and when their attention shifts to the plate, the fork disappears from awareness. Reading is often severely impaired, because reading requires perceiving several letters simultaneously, yet single letters and words presented alone are recognised.

Causes

Simultanagnosia is associated with bilateral lesions of the parieto-occipital region, and forms one of the three components of Bálint syndrome, alongside optic ataxia and optic apraxia. It can occur in neurodegenerative conditions such as posterior cortical atrophy, as well as after stroke. A milder "dorsal simultanagnosia" follows bilateral parietal damage, while a "ventral" form has been described after left inferior occipito-temporal lesions, in which patients can see several objects but process them unusually slowly.

Diagnosis

Standard tests include describing complex pictures such as the Cookie Theft picture from the Boston Diagnostic Aphasia Examination, identifying overlapping figures, and reporting multiple letters or shapes presented together. Patients characteristically give local rather than global descriptions of such stimuli.
