Vallam kali (Malayalam: വള്ളംകളി, literally "boat game/play") is a traditional boat race held in the backwaters and rivers of Kerala, in southern India. It is a form of canoe racing, principally conducted during the harvest festival of Onam in spring (the Malayalam month of Chingam, August–September), though the racing season spreads across the monsoon months. The most famous races draw crowds of tens of thousands and are major tourist attractions.

Snake boats

The stars of vallam kali are the chundan vallam ("beaked boats"), the famous Kerala snake boats: wooden war canoes 100–138 feet (30–42 m) long with a raised, curving prow resembling a snake's hood, crewed by 64 to 128 oarsmen, several helmsmen with long steering oars, and singers and beat-keepers who set the stroke rhythm with the boat songs, vanchipattu. Other classes include churulan (coiled boats), iruttukuthi, odi vallam, and smaller women's and juniors' categories.

History

Boat racing in Kerala is several centuries old, growing out of warfare between the region's petty kingdoms in the 13th–18th centuries, when chundan boats served as speedy naval craft, and out of temple rituals on the rivers. The Aranmula Uthrattathi race is tied to the Parthasarathy Temple at Aranmula: according to tradition a devotee carried provisions across the Pamba by boat, and today dozens of palliyodam "temple boats" take part in a festive water procession rather than a purely competitive race.

Major races

  • Nehru Trophy Boat Race — held on Punnamada Lake at Alappuzha on the second Saturday of August; named for Jawaharlal Nehru, who witnessed a race in 1952 and donated the trophy.
  • Champakulam Moolam Boat Race — one of the oldest, on the Pamba river, traditionally opening the season around June–July.
  • Aranmula Uthrattathi Vallamkali — a ritual water festival at Aranmula during Onam.
  • President's Trophy Boat Race — held on Ashtamudi Lake at Kollam on 1 November.

Since 2019 many leading races have been organized as the Champions Boat League (CBL), a points-based league modelled on franchise sport, with prize money professionalizing the oarsmen's training.

Culture

Races are village enterprises: boats are maintained by villages, temples or clubs, oarsmen train for weeks and observe traditional vows, and local pride runs high. The vanchipattu — most famously the songs of Ramapurathu Warrier about the travels of the Maharaja — is sung in a distinctive accelerating rhythm that synchronizes the rowing.
