Dirichlet beta function

In mathematics, the Dirichlet beta function (also known as the Catalan beta function) is an important special function, closely related to the Riemann zeta function. It is defined by the alternating series β(s) = Σn=0∞ (−1)n / (2n+1)s for Re(s) > 0, and by analytic continuation elsewhere in the complex plane.

Properties

The beta function is the Dirichlet L-function for the non-principal character modulo 4. It satisfies a functional equation relating β(s) and β(1−s). At positive odd integers it takes rational multiples of powers of π: for example β(1) = π/4 (the Leibniz formula) and β(3) = π³/32. Its value at s = 2 is Catalan's constant G ≈ 0.915965, whose irrationality remains unproven.

Relation to other functions

The beta function can be expressed through the Hurwitz zeta function as β(s) = 4−s [ζ(s, 1/4) − ζ(s, 3/4)]. It has no zeros on the line Re(s) = 1 apart from behaviour tied to its pole structure; unlike the zeta function it is entire, having no pole at s = 1 because the series alternates.
