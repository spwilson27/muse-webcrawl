Terrorism financing

Terrorism financing is the provision or collection of funds, by any means, directly or indirectly, with the intention or knowledge that they are to be used to carry out terrorist acts. Unlike money laundering, which disguises the proceeds of crime, terrorism financing may involve funds from legitimate sources that are directed toward illicit ends; this is sometimes called "reverse money laundering".

Methods

Terrorist groups raise and move money through many channels: charitable donations diverted from their stated purpose, criminal activity such as drug trafficking, smuggling and extortion, informal value-transfer systems such as hawala, trade-based schemes, and increasingly through digital assets and online crowdfunding. Because individual attacks can be inexpensive, the sums involved are often small and difficult to distinguish from ordinary transactions.

International response

The United Nations Security Council Resolution 1373, adopted after the September 11 attacks, requires states to criminalize terrorism financing and freeze the assets of designated persons and entities. The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) sets global standards through its recommendations, and the 1999 International Convention for the Suppression of the Financing of Terrorism provides the principal treaty framework.

Criticism

Counter-terrorism financing measures have been criticized for "de-risking" behaviour by banks, which has cut off legitimate charities and remittance flows to fragile states, and for imposing heavy compliance burdens relative to the small sums typically involved.
