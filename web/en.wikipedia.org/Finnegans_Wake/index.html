Finnegans Wake

Finnegans Wake
Author: James Joyce
Published: 4 May 1939
Publisher: Faber and Faber / Viking Press
Preceded by: Ulysses (1922)
Genre: Experimental novel

Finnegans Wake is a novel by Irish writer James Joyce, published in 1939 after seventeen years of composition under the name "Work in Progress". Written in an experimental idiom of portmanteau words, puns and multilingual allusions, it is widely considered one of the most difficult works of fiction in English.

Structure and themes

The book loosely follows the Earwicker family — HCE, his wife Anna Livia Plurabelle, and their sons Shem and Shaun — through a dreamlike night in Dublin, structured in four books echoing Vico's cycles of history. It famously begins mid-sentence, "riverrun, past Eve and Adam's…", and ends with a fragment that loops back to the opening.

Reception

Initial reviews were baffled or hostile, but the Wake has generated a large scholarly literature, including Joseph Campbell and Henry Morton Robinson's A Skeleton Key to Finnegans Wake (1944). Its opening also gave physics the word "quark", via the line "Three quarks for Muster Mark".
