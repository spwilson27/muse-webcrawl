Byway (road)

A byway is a minor secondary or side road, and the word is used in several formal legal and administrative senses in the United Kingdom and the United States. In general usage a byway is any little-traveled road off the main route — the source of the expression "byways" for obscure or indirect paths.

United Kingdom

In England and Wales, a byway open to all traffic (BOAT) is a highway over which the public has a right of way for vehicular and all other kinds of traffic, but which is used mainly as a footpath or bridleway. Byways are recorded on the definitive map of public rights of way maintained by local authorities. A related category, the restricted byway, created by the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000, permits a right of way on foot, on horseback, cycling, and for non-motorized vehicles such as horse-drawn carriages, but not for motor vehicles. Many byways are ancient unsurfaced tracks, sometimes called green lanes.

United States

In the United States, "byway" appears most prominently in scenic-road designations. The National Scenic Byways Program, administered by the Federal Highway Administration, recognizes roads for their scenic, historic, recreational, cultural, archaeological or natural qualities; the most distinguished are designated All-American Roads. Many states run their own scenic byway systems. The term also survives in older road names and in the names of historic routes such as the National Old Trails Road era "Ocean to Ocean" byways.
