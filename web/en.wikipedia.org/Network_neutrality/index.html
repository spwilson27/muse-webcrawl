Net neutrality

Net neutrality
ConceptInternet service providers must treat all data equally
Term coined byTim Wu (2003)
PrinciplesNo blocking, no throttling, no paid prioritization
Key jurisdictionsUnited States, European Union, India, Chile, Netherlands

Net neutrality (also network neutrality) is the principle that Internet service providers (ISPs) must treat all communications over the Internet equally, without discriminating or charging differently based on user, content, website, platform, application, or method of communication. Columbia Law professor Tim Wu coined the term in a 2003 paper, building on the older common-carriage tradition in telecommunications law and the Internet's end-to-end design principle.

Arguments

Supporters argue that neutrality preserves the Internet as an open platform for innovation and free expression: without it, ISPs could block competitors' services, throttle disfavored content, or sell "fast lanes" that entrench large incumbents able to pay. Critics, including many ISPs and some economists, argue that prohibiting differentiated services restricts investment in networks, prevents beneficial traffic management and new business models, and that competition among providers is a better safeguard.

History and regulation

United States

The U.S. debate has seesawed with administrations. The FCC's 2015 Open Internet Order, under Chairman Tom Wheeler, classified broadband as a Title II common-carrier service and banned blocking, throttling, and paid prioritization. In 2017, under Chairman Ajit Pai, the FCC repealed those rules (the "Restoring Internet Freedom" order, effective 2018), a decision largely upheld in Mozilla v. FCC (2019), while several states — notably California — enacted their own neutrality laws. In April 2024 the FCC voted to reinstate federal net-neutrality rules under Title II; in January 2025 the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals struck the rules down, holding after the end of Chevron deference that the FCC lacked authority for the classification.

Elsewhere

Chile (2010) and the Netherlands (2012) were early adopters of net-neutrality legislation. The European Union's Regulation 2015/2120 established neutrality principles across the EU, with regulators (BEREC) issuing guidelines permitting "reasonable" traffic management and specialized services. India adopted strong rules in 2018, banning discriminatory tariffs including zero-rating arrangements after the controversy over Facebook's Free Basics.

Issues

Contested practices include zero-rating (exempting certain services from data caps), interconnection fees, deep packet inspection, and throttling of peer-to-peer or video traffic — episodes such as Comcast's 2007 interference with BitTorrent and the 2014 Netflix–Comcast interconnection dispute helped drive the regulatory cycle.
