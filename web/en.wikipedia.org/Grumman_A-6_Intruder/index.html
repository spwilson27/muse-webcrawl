The Grumman A-6 Intruder was an American twinjet all-weather subsonic attack aircraft developed and manufactured by Grumman Aerospace. Operated by the United States Navy and Marine Corps from aircraft carriers and land bases, it was designed to deliver conventional and nuclear weapons in all weather conditions, day or night.

Design

The Intruder carried a crew of two — pilot and bombardier/navigator — seated side by side. Its DIANE (Digital Integrated Attack Navigation Equipment) system allowed accurate low-level attack in poor visibility. The type had a large weapons load of up to about 18,000 lb (8,200 kg) carried on five hardpoints.

Operational history

The A-6 entered service in 1963 and saw extensive combat in the Vietnam War, where it formed the backbone of the Navy's medium attack force. It later flew in operations over Lebanon, Libya, Iraq during the 1991 Gulf War, and Bosnia. The four-seat EA-6B Prowler electronic-warfare derivative remained in service much longer. The Navy retired its last Intruders in 1997, the attack role passing to the F/A-18 Hornet.
