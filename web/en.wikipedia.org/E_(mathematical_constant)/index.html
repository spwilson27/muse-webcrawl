e (mathematical constant)

The number e is a mathematical constant approximately equal to 2.71828, and is the base of the natural logarithm. It can be characterised in many ways: as the sum of the infinite series 1 + 1/1! + 1/2! + 1/3! + …, as the limit of (1 + 1/n)n as n grows, or as the unique number whose natural logarithm is 1.

History

The constant arose implicitly in early 17th-century work on compound interest and logarithms by John Napier. Jacob Bernoulli encountered the limit definition in 1683 while studying continuously compounded interest. Leonhard Euler introduced the letter e and proved the number irrational in the 18th century; Charles Hermite proved it transcendental in 1873.

Properties

The exponential function ex is its own derivative, making e fundamental to calculus, differential equations and complex analysis, where Euler's identity eiπ + 1 = 0 links it with π. It appears throughout probability (for example in the normal distribution and Poisson processes) and in the mathematics of growth and decay.
