Syr Darya

Syr Darya
Length: about 2,256 km (2,870 km including the Naryn)
Basin: about 219,000 km²
Source: confluence of the Naryn and Kara Darya, Fergana Valley
Mouth: North Aral Sea

The Syr Darya is a river in Central Asia, known in antiquity by the Greek name Jaxartes. It rises in the Tian Shan mountains of Kyrgyzstan, where the Naryn and Kara Darya rivers join in the Fergana Valley, and flows west and north-west through Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan to the northern remnants of the Aral Sea.

At about 2,256 kilometres in length (2,870 km counting the Naryn, its longest headstream), it is one of the two great rivers of the region, the other being the Amu Darya. Its waters are heavily used for irrigation, particularly for cotton cultivation.

History

The river marked the northern limit of Alexander the Great's conquests; he founded Alexandria Eschata ("Alexandria the Furthest") on its banks, the site of modern Khujand. In the Soviet era, diversion of the Syr Darya and Amu Darya for irrigation, begun in the 1960s, caused the Aral Sea to shrink dramatically, one of the twentieth century's worst environmental disasters. Partial recovery of the North Aral Sea followed the completion of the Kokaral dam in Kazakhstan in 2005.

Geography

Major cities along or near the river include Khujand, Tashkent (on its tributary the Chirchiq), Turkistan, Kyzylorda and Kazaly.
