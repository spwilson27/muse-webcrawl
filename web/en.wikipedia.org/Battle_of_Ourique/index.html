Battle of Ourique

The Battle of Ourique (Portuguese: Batalha de Ourique) was fought on 25 July 1139 between the forces of the Portuguese count Afonso Henriques and a Muslim Almoravid army. The traditional account holds that, after a great victory, Afonso's troops proclaimed him King of Portugal on the battlefield, a founding moment in Portuguese national history.

LocationOurique, Alentejo, Portugal
ResultPortuguese victory

Background

In the 1130s Afonso Henriques consolidated his rule over the County of Portugal and turned toward raiding and expansion into the Muslim-held south. In the summer of 1139 he led a chevauchée deep into Alentejo. A substantial Almoravid force assembled to intercept his return and confronted the Portuguese near Ourique.

The battle and its tradition

Contemporary documentation is sparse; the earliest near-contemporary source, the Chronica Gothorum, records a great Portuguese victory in 1139. Later medieval tradition elaborated the story: before the battle Afonso is said to have received a vision of Christ on the cross promising victory (the "Miracle of Ourique"), and after defeating five Muslim kings his men acclaimed him king. The five shields (quinas) on Portugal's coat of arms are traditionally said to commemorate the five kings defeated at Ourique.

Significance

From 1139 Afonso began styling himself rex (king), asserting Portuguese independence from the Kingdom of León. León formally recognised the title in the Treaty of Zamora (1143), and papal recognition followed in 1179 with the bull Manifestis Probatum. Historians caution that details of the battle—including its exact site, which remains disputed—were heavily embellished by later chroniclers, but Ourique remains a cornerstone of Portuguese national mythology.
