The Place de la Bastille is a square in Paris where the Bastille prison stood until the storming of the Bastille and its subsequent physical destruction between 14 July 1789 and 14 July 1790 during the French Revolution. No vestige of the prison remains, though the outline of the fortress is marked in the paving of the square. The square straddles several major streets radiating outward, including the Rue du Faubourg Saint-Antoine, and lies at the junction of the 4th, 11th and 12th arrondissements.

The July Column

At the centre of the square stands the July Column (Colonne de Juillet), a Corinthian column of bronze some 47 metres high topped by the gilded Génie de la Liberté ("Spirit of Freedom"), a winged figure by Auguste Dumont. The column commemorates not the Revolution of 1789 but the July Revolution of 1830 (the Trois Glorieuses), and the names of the Parisians killed in that uprising are inscribed upon it; a necropolis beneath the column holds their remains.

Modern square

The square is a major traffic hub and a traditional rallying point for demonstrations, marches and celebrations, including annual Bastille Day festivities on 14 July. On its southeastern side stands the Opéra Bastille, the modern opera house inaugurated in 1989 for the bicentennial of the Revolution, designed by Carlos Ott. The Bassin de l'Arsenal, part of the Canal Saint-Martin network, opens just south of the square.
