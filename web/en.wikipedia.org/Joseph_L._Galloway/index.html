Joseph Lee Galloway (November 13, 1941 – August 18, 2021) was an American newspaper correspondent and columnist, best known for his coverage of the Vietnam War and as co-author, with Lt. Gen. Harold G. Moore, of We Were Soldiers Once… and Young (1992).

Vietnam and Ia Drang

Galloway arrived in Vietnam in 1965 as a 23-year-old UPI reporter. In November 1965 he was flown into Landing Zone X-Ray during the Battle of Ia Drang, where he helped carry wounded soldiers to safety under fire. For his actions he was awarded the Bronze Star with "V" device in 1998 — the only civilian so decorated by the U.S. Army during the Vietnam War. His reporting and later book, adapted as the film We Were Soldiers (2002), in which he was played by Barry Pepper, became the definitive account of the battle.

Later career

Galloway went on to senior roles at UPI and U.S. News & World Report, covered wars from the Indo-Pakistani war to the Gulf War, and with Moore wrote a sequel, We Are Soldiers Still (2008). As a syndicated military columnist he became an outspoken critic of the Iraq War. He died in 2021 and was widely eulogised by the soldiers he had covered.
