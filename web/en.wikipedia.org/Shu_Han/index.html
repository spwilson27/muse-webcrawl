Shu Han was a dynastic state of China and one of the three major kingdoms that competed for supremacy during the Three Kingdoms period (220–280 AD). Founded in 221 by Liu Bei, who claimed descent from the imperial Liu clan of the Han dynasty, Shu Han was based in the Sichuan Basin with its capital at Chengdu, and controlled the Hanzhong valley and parts of the south-west.

History

Liu Bei, a warlord of the late Han dynasty, seized Yi Province from Liu Zhang in 214 and took Hanzhong from Cao Cao in 219. After Cao Pi usurped the Han throne in 220, Liu Bei declared himself emperor in 221 to continue the Han line. Following Liu Bei's death in 223, the chancellor Zhuge Liang governed as regent, allied with Eastern Wu, subdued the southern Nanzhong region, and launched five Northern Expeditions against Cao Wei. His successor Jiang Wei continued campaigns that strained the state's resources.

Fall

In 263, Wei generals Zhong Hui and Deng Ai invaded; Deng Ai took a daring route through difficult terrain and appeared before Chengdu, where the emperor Liu Shan surrendered. Shu Han thus became the first of the Three Kingdoms to fall. Its registered population of roughly one million made it the smallest of the three states.

Legacy

The state is romanticised in the 14th-century novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, which portrays Liu Bei and Zhuge Liang as paragons of benevolence and wisdom, and in the cult of the general Guan Yu.
