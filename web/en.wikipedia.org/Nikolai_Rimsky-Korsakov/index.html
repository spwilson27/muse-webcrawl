Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov

Born18 March 1844, Tikhvin, Russian Empire
Died21 June 1908, Lyubensk, Russian Empire
OccupationComposer, conductor, teacher, naval officer
Known forScheherazade, Capriccio Espagnol, 15 operas

Nikolai Andreyevich Rimsky-Korsakov (18 March 1844 – 21 June 1908) was a Russian composer, a member of the group of composers known as The Five. He was a master of orchestration, and his best-known orchestral compositions — Capriccio Espagnol, the Russian Easter Festival Overture and the symphonic suite Scheherazade — are staples of the classical repertoire, along with suites drawn from his 15 operas. Scheherazade is an example of his frequent use of fairy-tale and folk subjects.

Life and career

Born in Tikhvin to an aristocratic family with a naval tradition, Rimsky-Korsakov entered the Imperial Russian Navy's cadet school in St Petersburg, composing in his spare time. He came under the influence of Mily Balakirev, the leader of The Five, and began composing seriously while still a naval officer; a world cruise aboard the clipper Almaz (1862–65) inspired his lifelong fascination with the sea. In 1871, despite limited formal training, he became professor of composition and orchestration at the St Petersburg Conservatory, a post he held for most of his life, diligently studying harmony and counterpoint alongside his students.

Works and legacy

His operas include The Snow Maiden, Sadko, The Tale of Tsar Saltan (which contains the famous "Flight of the Bumblebee"), The Golden Cockerel and Kashchey the Deathless. Rimsky-Korsakov edited and completed works left unfinished by his fellow nationalists — most famously Mussorgsky's Boris Godunov and Night on Bald Mountain, and Borodin's Prince Igor (with Glazunov) — though his polished versions have sometimes been criticised for smoothing away their originality. His textbook Principles of Orchestration, published posthumously, remains influential. His pupils included Glazunov, Respighi (briefly), and Igor Stravinsky, whose early style bears his imprint.
