Ross Lake National Recreation Area

Ross Lake and the North Cascades
Location:Whatcom and Skagit counties, Washington, U.S.
Area:118,849 acres (48,096 ha)
Established:October 2, 1968
Governing body:National Park Service

Ross Lake National Recreation Area is a United States National Recreation Area in the North Cascades of northern Washington state, administered by the National Park Service as part of the North Cascades National Park Service Complex. Established in 1968 alongside North Cascades National Park, it covers about 118,849 acres (48,096 hectares) along the upper Skagit River valley.

Geography

The recreation area centres on three hydroelectric reservoirs built by Seattle City Light as the Skagit River Hydroelectric Project: Ross Lake, impounded by Ross Dam; Diablo Lake, behind Diablo Dam; and Gorge Lake, behind Gorge Dam. Ross Lake, at about 370 metres elevation, stretches some 37 kilometres northward, nearly reaching the Canadian border. The North Cascades Highway (State Route 20) runs through the southern part of the area, providing its principal road access; Ross Lake itself is reached by trail, boat, or a gravel road from British Columbia at its north end.

Recreation

Ross Lake is popular for boating, kayaking, canoeing and fishing for rainbow trout and bull trout, and is home to the floating Ross Lake Resort, whose cabins are reachable only by water or trail. Campgrounds, hiking trails and boat-in campsites line the lakes, and the area serves as a gateway to the surrounding Stephen Mather Wilderness and the two units of North Cascades National Park. Diablo Lake is noted for its striking turquoise colour, produced by suspended glacial rock flour.

History

The Skagit dams were built between the 1920s and 1961 to supply Seattle with electricity; Ross Dam was raised in stages to a height of 165 metres. The 1968 legislation that created the national park also designated the recreation area to accommodate the reservoirs and their continued operation, along with heavier recreational use than is typical within the park proper.
