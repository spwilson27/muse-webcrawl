Wilcoxon signed-rank test

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is a non-parametric statistical hypothesis test used either to test the location of a population based on a sample, or to compare the locations of two populations using matched samples. It is named after Frank Wilcoxon, who proposed it in 1945 alongside the rank-sum test. It serves as a non-parametric alternative to the paired Student's t-test and does not assume that the data are normally distributed.

Procedure

For paired data, the test proceeds as follows. For each pair, compute the difference between the two values. Discard differences equal to zero. Rank the remaining absolute differences from smallest to largest, assigning average ranks to ties. Then sum the ranks of the positive differences (W+) and of the negative differences (W−). Under the null hypothesis that the differences are symmetrically distributed about zero, the two sums should be similar; a large discrepancy provides evidence against the null hypothesis. When using the test on a single sample, the differences are taken against a hypothesised median.

Assumptions

The test assumes that the data are paired and come from the same population, that pairs are independent, and that the differences can be meaningfully ranked — in particular, the distribution of differences is assumed symmetric under the null hypothesis. For small samples, exact critical values are tabulated; for large samples, a normal approximation with continuity correction is used.

Related tests

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is related to the Mann–Whitney U test (also called the Wilcoxon rank-sum test), which compares two independent samples, and to the simpler sign test, which uses only the signs of the differences and is therefore less powerful but requires weaker assumptions.
