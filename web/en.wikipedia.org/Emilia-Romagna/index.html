Emilia-Romagna (Emilian: Eméggia-Rumâgna; Romagnol: Emélia-Rumâgna) is one of the 20 administrative regions of Italy, situated in the north of the country, comprising the historical regions of Emilia and Romagna. Its capital is Bologna. It has an area of 22,446 km² (8,666 sq mi) and a population of about 4.4 million, making it one of the most densely populated regions of Italy.

Emilia-Romagna is one of the wealthiest and most developed regions in Europe, with the third-highest gross domestic product per capita in Italy. Bologna, its capital, has one of Italy's highest quality-of-life indices and advanced social services. The region is also a major cultural and tourist centre, being the home of the University of Bologna, the oldest university in the world; Romanesque and Renaissance cities such as Modena, Parma and Ferrara; and a food and car production centre, home to manufacturers such as Ferrari, Lamborghini, Maserati, Pagani and Ducati, and to internationally renowned food products including Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese, Parma ham, balsamic vinegar of Modena, and pasta varieties such as tortellini and tagliatelle.

Geography

The region consists of nine provinces and covers an area bounded by the Po River to the north, which separates it from Lombardy and Veneto, the Apennine Mountains to the south and west, separating it from Liguria and Tuscany, and the Adriatic Sea to the east, facing Marche. Nearly half of the region consists of the Po Valley plain, one of the most fertile agricultural areas in Italy; the remainder is divided between hills and the Apennine mountains. Major rivers include the Po, the Reno, the Secchia and the Trebbia.

History

The name Emilia derives from the Via Aemilia, the Roman road built in 187 BC by the consul Marcus Aemilius Lepidus, running from Piacenza to Rimini. In Roman times the region was part of Cisalpine Gaul. After the fall of the Western Roman Empire it was contested by Byzantines (whose Exarchate of Ravenna dominated the Romagna portion), Lombards, and later the Papal States and free communes. During the Renaissance, cities such as Ferrara (under the Este), Modena, Parma (under the Farnese) and Bologna flourished as centres of art and learning. The region was unified with the Kingdom of Italy in 1860 following plebiscites. The modern administrative region was formally established by the Italian constitution of 1948.

Economy

Emilia-Romagna has a diversified economy based on advanced agriculture and food processing, precision engineering and motor vehicles (the so-called "Motor Valley"), ceramics (centred on Sassuolo), textiles and tourism along the Adriatic Riviera. The region has a strong cooperative movement and consistently records among the lowest unemployment rates in Italy.

Cuisine

The region is regarded as the gastronomic heart of Italy. Bologna gave its name to ragù alla bolognese, mortadella and tortellini; Parma is famed for Prosciutto di Parma and Parmigiano-Reggiano; Modena for traditional balsamic vinegar. Piadina flatbread is characteristic of Romagna.
