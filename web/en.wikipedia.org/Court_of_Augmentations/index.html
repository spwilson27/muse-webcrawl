Court of Augmentations

The Court of Augmentations, also called the Court of Augmentation of the Revenues of the King's Crown, was established by Henry VIII of England in 1536 to administer the estates confiscated during the Dissolution of the Monasteries.

History

Created by statute 27 Hen. 8 c. 27, it managed the vast lands, rents and revenues transferred to the Crown from monasteries, chantries and other religious houses. Its chancellor and officials surveyed, leased and sold properties, greatly augmenting royal income. In 1547 it absorbed the Court of General Surveyors, and in 1554 it was merged into the Exchequer under Mary I.
