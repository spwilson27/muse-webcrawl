The Caucasus is a region between the Black Sea and the Caspian Sea, mainly comprising Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, and parts of southern Russia. It is home to the Caucasus Mountains, including Mount Elbrus, Europe's highest peak at 5,642 m. The region is traditionally divided by the Greater Caucasus range into the North Caucasus (Ciscaucasia), on the Russian side, and the South Caucasus (Transcaucasia), where the independent states of Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan lie. Straddling the boundary between Europe and Asia, the Caucasus is one of the most linguistically and ethnically diverse regions on Earth.

Geography

The Greater Caucasus stretches about 1,100 km from the Taman Peninsula on the Black Sea to the Absheron Peninsula on the Caspian, with a parallel Lesser Caucasus range to the south connected by the Likhi Range. Between them lie lowlands — the Colchis plain in the west and the Kura-Aras basin in the east. Major rivers include the Kuban, Terek, Kura and Aras. The region forms a major biodiversity hotspot with high endemism.

Languages and peoples

More than 50 ethnic groups live in the Caucasus, speaking languages of three indigenous families found nowhere else — Kartvelian (Georgian), Northwest Caucasian (Abkhaz, Circassian) and Northeast Caucasian (Chechen, Dagestanian languages) — alongside Indo-European (Armenian, Ossetian, Russian) and Turkic (Azerbaijani, Karachay-Balkar) languages. This density led medieval Arab geographers to call the region the "mountain of tongues".

History

Armenia and Georgia were among the first states to adopt Christianity, in the early 4th century. The region was contested for centuries by Roman, Persian, Arab, Mongol, Ottoman and Russian empires; Russia annexed the Caucasus in the 19th century after long wars, including the Caucasian War (1817–1864). Briefly independent after 1917, the South Caucasus states were absorbed into the Soviet Union, then regained independence in 1991. Frozen and active conflicts — over Nagorno-Karabakh, Abkhazia, South Ossetia and Chechnya — have shaped the region since, including the 2008 Russo-Georgian War and Azerbaijan's recovery of Nagorno-Karabakh in 2020–2023.

Economy

The Caspian basin's oil and gas, pipelines such as Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan, wine production, mining and growing tourism (Georgia's mountain regions, Sochi's ski resorts, host of the 2014 Winter Olympics) anchor the regional economy.
