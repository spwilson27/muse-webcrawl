Tyrrhenian Sea

The Tyrrhenian Sea (Italian: Mar Tirreno) is part of the Mediterranean Sea off the western coast of Italy. It is named for the Tyrrhenian people (the Etruscans), an ancient civilisation of Italy. The sea is bordered by the islands of Corsica and Sardinia to the west, the Italian Peninsula (Tuscany, Lazio, Campania, Basilicata and Calabria) to the east, and Sicily to the south.

Geography

The Tyrrhenian Sea is connected to the Ligurian Sea to the northwest through the Corsica Channel, to the Ionian Sea to the southeast through the Strait of Messina, and to the western Mediterranean through the Strait of Bonifacio between Corsica and Sardinia and the Sardinia Channel. Its major ports include Naples, Palermo, Cagliari, Civitavecchia (the port of Rome), Salerno and Trapani.

Geology

The Tyrrhenian is a back-arc basin that formed during the last 10 million years due to the rollback of the subducting Ionian slab beneath the Calabrian Arc. Its floor includes two deep plains, the Vavilov and Marsili plains, and several seamounts, including the Marsili Seamount, one of the largest undersea volcanoes in Europe. The basin is seismically and volcanically active; the Aeolian Islands, including Stromboli and Vulcano, are volcanic in origin.

Islands

Major island groups in the Tyrrhenian include the Tuscan Archipelago (including Elba, where Napoleon Bonaparte was first exiled), the Pontine Islands, Capri and Ischia in the Bay of Naples, the Aeolian Islands off Sicily, and the Egadi and Pelagie groups. Ustica lies north of Sicily.

Name

The name derives from the Greek Tyrrhenoi, the name given to the Etruscans. In antiquity the sea was also the scene of Roman naval power projection and later of medieval trade by the maritime republics, notably Amalfi, Pisa and Genoa.
