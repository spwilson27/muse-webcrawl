Deltiology

Deltiology (from Greek deltion, diminutive of deltos, "writing tablet, letter"; and -logy) is the study and collection of postcards. Professor Rendell Rhoades of Ashland, Ohio, coined the term in 1945.

History

Postcards became hugely popular in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, during the so-called "golden age" of postcards (roughly 1898–1919), when billions were mailed worldwide. Collected postcards provide a rich visual record of places, buildings, transport, and social customs, many of which have since vanished or changed, making deltiology a valued resource for historians, genealogists, and local-history researchers.

Collecting

Collectors (deltiologists) may specialize by topic (topicals), by place, by artist or publisher, or by era — for example, divided-back versus undivided-back cards, white-border cards, or chrome (photochrome) cards. Postally used cards are often prized for their postmarks, stamps, and messages, which help date and contextualize the image. Postcard clubs, shows, and dealers support the hobby, and large institutional collections are held by libraries and museums.

Identification

Features used to date and identify cards include the style of the back, publisher's logos and series numbers, the stamp box design, and the printing process — such as collotype, lithography, or real-photo postcards printed directly from negatives.
