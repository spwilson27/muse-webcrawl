Cliffs of Moher

LocationCounty Clare, Ireland
LengthAbout 14 km (9 mi)
Highest point214 m (702 ft) above the Atlantic
GeologyNamurian shale and sandstone

The Cliffs of Moher (Irish: Aillte Mothair) are sea cliffs located at the southwestern edge of the Burren region in County Clare, Ireland. They run for about 14 kilometres along the Atlantic coast and rise to a maximum height of 214 metres, making them among the highest sea cliffs in Europe. They are one of Ireland's most popular tourist attractions, receiving well over a million visitors a year.

Geology

The cliffs are composed of layers of dark shale and sandstone laid down more than 300 million years ago during the Namurian stage of the Carboniferous period, when a great river delta spread sediment into a shallow sea. The harder sandstone bands resist erosion better than the shales, giving the cliff face its stepped profile. Fossils, including goniatites and trace fossils of burrowing organisms, are common in the strata.

Wildlife

The cliffs are an important seabird site, designated a Special Protection Area. Around 30,000 pairs of seabirds nest on the ledges and the offshore stack, Branaunmore, including Atlantic puffins, razorbills, guillemots, and fulmars. Choughs and peregrine falcons are also present, and the waters below are frequented by seals, dolphins, and basking sharks.

Tourism

A visitor centre, opened in 2007 and built largely underground to protect the landscape, serves the site near O'Brien's Tower, a small castellated viewing tower built in 1835 by the landlord Cornelius O'Brien for Victorian tourists. The cliffs have featured in films including Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince and The Princess Bride.
