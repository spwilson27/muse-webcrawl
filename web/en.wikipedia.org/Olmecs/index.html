Olmecs

RegionGulf Coast of Mexico (Veracruz, Tabasco)
Periodc. 1500–400 BCE (Formative/Preclassic)
Major sitesSan Lorenzo, La Venta, Tres Zapotes
Known forColossal heads, early Mesoamerican civilisation

The Olmecs were the earliest known major civilisation of Mesoamerica, flourishing in the tropical lowlands of the modern Mexican states of Veracruz and Tabasco from roughly 1500 to 400 BCE. Their heartland centres — San Lorenzo, La Venta and Tres Zapotes — featured monumental architecture, planned ceremonial precincts and some of the earliest evidence in the Americas for writing, calendrics and organised religion. The name "Olmec", from Nahuatl Ōlmēcah, "rubber people", was applied by the much later Aztecs to inhabitants of the rubber-producing Gulf Coast.

Colossal heads and art

The Olmecs are best known for their colossal stone heads — at least seventeen have been found — carved from basalt boulders transported over long distances and depicting individual rulers wearing helmet-like headgear. Their art also includes jade figurines, "were-jaguar" baby motifs, thrones (once mislabelled altars) and cave paintings. The greenstone axes and masks traded far beyond the heartland attest to Olmec influence across Formative Mesoamerica.

Society and achievements

Olmec society was stratified, supported by maize agriculture in rich riverine soils, and capable of mobilising large labour forces for earthworks such as the great pyramid at La Venta. The Cascajal Block may bear the oldest writing in the Americas, and the Mesoamerican Long Count calendar and the ballgame both have plausible Olmec antecedents. Whether the Olmecs were a "mother culture" to later civilisations such as the Maya and Zapotecs, or one of several interacting "sister cultures", remains debated among archaeologists.

Decline

San Lorenzo declined around 900 BCE and La Venta around 400 BCE, possibly through environmental change, river shifts or internal conflict; the causes are not fully understood. Epi-Olmec culture continued in the region into the early centuries CE.
