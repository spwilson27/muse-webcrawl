Regenerative agriculture

Regenerative agriculture is a conservation and rehabilitation approach to food and farming systems. It focuses on topsoil regeneration, increasing biodiversity, improving the water cycle, enhancing ecosystem services, supporting biosequestration of carbon, increasing resilience to climate change, and strengthening the health and vitality of farm soil.

Practices

Common practices include minimizing tillage (no-till or reduced-till farming), planting cover crops, rotating diverse crops, composting and applying organic matter, integrating livestock through managed rotational grazing, and avoiding or reducing synthetic fertilizers and pesticides. Many of the methods draw on older traditions including organic farming, permaculture, agroecology, agroforestry, and holistic management.

Claims and evidence

Proponents argue that regenerative methods rebuild soil organic matter depleted by industrial agriculture, increase water infiltration and retention, and can draw down atmospheric carbon. Scientists agree on several benefits for soil health, while estimates of the achievable scale of carbon sequestration and the generality of yield effects remain subjects of active research and debate.

History

The term was popularized in the 1980s by the Rodale Institute and gained wider currency in the 2010s through films, books, and adoption programmes by major food companies and retailers, as well as certification schemes such as Regenerative Organic Certified.
