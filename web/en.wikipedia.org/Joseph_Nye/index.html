Joseph Samuel Nye Jr. (born January 19, 1937) is an American political scientist. He and Robert Keohane co-founded the international relations theory of neoliberalism, developed in their 1977 book Power and Interdependence. He is best known for coining the concept of soft power — the ability of a state to influence others through attraction rather than coercion or payment.

Career

Nye, a Princeton graduate and Rhodes Scholar who took his PhD at Harvard, has spent most of his career at Harvard University, where he became University Distinguished Service Professor and served as dean of the John F. Kennedy School of Government from 1995 to 2004. In government he chaired the National Intelligence Council (1993–94) and served as Assistant Secretary of Defense for International Security Affairs in the Clinton administration, playing a role in renewing the U.S.–Japan alliance.

Ideas

In Bound to Lead (1990) and later works such as Soft Power (2004), The Future of Power (2011) and Do Morals Matter? (2020), Nye argued that American influence depends on combining hard power (military and economic) with soft power (culture, values and legitimate policies) into "smart power". He has been ranked among the most influential scholars of international relations in surveys of the field.
