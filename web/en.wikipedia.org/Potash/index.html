Potash

Potash is a general name for various mined and manufactured salts that contain potassium in water-soluble form, most commonly potassium chloride (KCl), as well as potassium carbonate, potassium sulfate, and potassium nitrate. The name derives from the historical method of production: leaching wood ashes and evaporating the solution in large iron pots, leaving a white residue called "pot ash".

Potash is mined from ancient evaporite deposits, including vast reserves in Saskatchewan, Canada (placeholder image).

History

Wood-ash potash was an important commodity in pre-industrial economies, used for making soap, glass, gunpowder, and fertilizer; its production drove deforestation in parts of Europe and North America. The first United States patent ever issued, in 1790, was for a process of making potash and pearl ash. In 1861, German miners near Staßfurt began extracting potassium salts from underground deposits, ending dependence on wood ash and founding the modern potash industry.

Production

Modern potash comes mainly from deep underground mines in evaporite beds formed by ancient seas, and from solution mining and evaporation of brines. Canada (Saskatchewan), Russia, and Belarus have historically been the largest producers, with major output also from Israel, Jordan, Germany, and China. Output is measured in tonnes of potassium oxide (K2O) equivalent.

Uses

About 90–95% of potash is used as fertilizer, supplying potassium, one of the three primary plant macronutrients (N-P-K). The remainder goes into industrial applications including glass, soaps, ceramics, and chemical manufacture. Potassium is essential for human nutrition, though usually obtained from food rather than potash salts directly.

Terminology

Because "potash" long predates modern chemistry, it can refer ambiguously to several compounds; the chemical symbol for potassium, K, comes from kalium, derived from alkali, itself from the Arabic word for ashes. Caustic potash is potassium hydroxide (KOH); muriate of potash is potassium chloride; sulfate of potash is potassium sulfate.
