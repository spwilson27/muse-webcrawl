Bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers

The bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers on September 15, 2008, was the largest bankruptcy filing in United States history, with the investment bank holding over US$600 billion in assets. It became the emblematic collapse of the 2007–2008 financial crisis.

Background

Lehman Brothers, founded in 1850, had loaded its balance sheet with mortgage-backed securities and real-estate assets financed with high leverage. As the US housing market collapsed, losses mounted, its share price plunged, and counterparties lost confidence. Attempts to arrange a sale to Barclays or Bank of America failed when the US government declined to provide financial support.

Consequences

The filing triggered a global financial panic: money-market funds "broke the buck", interbank lending froze, and governments around the world intervened with bailouts and guarantees. The bankruptcy and its aftermath led to inquiries into Lehman's accounting, notably its "Repo 105" transactions, and fed the argument that some institutions were "too big to fail" — while Lehman itself was allowed to fail.
