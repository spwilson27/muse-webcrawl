Juan Sebastián Elcano

Born: c. 1486, Getaria, Gipuzkoa, Crown of Castile
Died: 4 August 1526 (aged about 40), Pacific Ocean
Nationality: Basque / Spanish
Known for: Completing the first circumnavigation of the Earth (1519–1522)

Juan Sebastián Elcano (also spelled del Cano; c. 1486 – 4 August 1526) was a Spanish navigator of Basque origin best known for completing the first circumnavigation of the Earth. He took command of Ferdinand Magellan's expedition after Magellan's death in the Philippines, and brought the ship Victoria and 17 other survivors back to Spain in 1522.

Early life

Elcano was born in the fishing port of Getaria in Gipuzkoa. He served as a soldier in Italy and North Africa and became a ship's master, but fell foul of the law after surrendering a vessel to Genoese bankers to settle debts over unpaid wages. Pardoned on condition of joining Magellan's expedition, he sailed in 1519 as master of the Concepción, one of five ships seeking a westward route to the Spice Islands (the Moluccas).

The circumnavigation

Elcano took part in the mutiny against Magellan at Puerto San Julián in 1520 and was among those pardoned. After Magellan was killed at the Battle of Mactan in April 1521, and further losses reduced the fleet, the survivors burned the Concepción and continued with two ships. When the fleet reached the Moluccas and loaded cloves, the leaking Trinidad stayed behind for repairs while Elcano, now in command of the Victoria, chose the daring westward route home across the Indian Ocean and round the Cape of Good Hope, avoiding Portuguese ports. Starving and scurvy-ridden, the Victoria reached Sanlúcar de Barrameda on 6 September 1522, with 18 of the original 270 men, completing the first voyage around the world.

Rewards and death

Emperor Charles V granted Elcano a coat of arms bearing a globe and the motto Primus circumdedisti me ("You first went around me") and a pension. In 1525 Elcano sailed as second-in-command of the Loaísa expedition to the Moluccas via the Strait of Magellan; like most of its leaders he died at sea, of scurvy or malnutrition, in the Pacific in August 1526.

Legacy

Though long overshadowed by Magellan — who died halfway round — Elcano's achievement has been increasingly recognised; the Spanish Navy's training tall ship Juan Sebastián de Elcano, launched in 1927, is named in his honour, as are monuments in Getaria and elsewhere.
