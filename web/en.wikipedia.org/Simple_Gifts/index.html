Simple Gifts

Written1848
ComposerJoseph Brackett
OriginShaker community, Alfred, Maine
GenreShaker dance song / hymn

"Simple Gifts" is a Shaker song written and composed in 1848, generally attributed to Elder Joseph Brackett of the Shaker community at Alfred, Maine. Its best-known lines — "'Tis the gift to be simple, 'tis the gift to be free" — praise humility and simplicity, central virtues of the United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing, commonly known as the Shakers. Originally a "quick dance" song for worship, it has become one of the most familiar folk hymns in the United States.

Lyrics and meaning

The single original verse describes bowing and bending without shame and "turning, turning" until coming "round right", reflecting the whirling movements of Shaker dancing worship. The imagery of simplicity and freedom was a rejection of worldly pride: true freedom, in Shaker teaching, lay in obedience and plain living. A second verse, occasionally sung, is a later non-Shaker addition.

Popularisation and Appalachian Spring

The song remained little known outside Shaker communities until the composer Aaron Copland used its melody as the basis for the variations in his 1944 ballet score Appalachian Spring, choreographed by Martha Graham. Copland later included it in his Old American Songs (1950). The melody's fame through Copland helped spark a revival of interest in Shaker music and craftsmanship.

Later uses

"Simple Gifts" has been widely arranged and recorded: Sydney Carter adapted its tune for the hymn "Lord of the Dance" (1963); Judy Collins recorded it; it was performed at the inauguration of US President Barack Obama in 2009 in John Williams's arrangement Air and Simple Gifts; and it served for years as the theme associated with the American television programme Meet the Press in adaptation. The tune also appears in countless school songbooks and is a staple of American folk music.
