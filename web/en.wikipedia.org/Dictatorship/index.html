Dictatorship

A dictatorship is a form of government in which one person or a small group possesses absolute power without effective constitutional limitations. Dictatorships are generally characterised by the suppression of political opposition, limited or absent free elections, restrictions on civil liberties, and rule sustained through coercion, propaganda, and control of the military and security services.

Origins of the term

The term derives from the Roman dictator, an official appointed in emergencies for a limited term with extraordinary powers — famously Cincinnatus, who returned to his farm after the crisis. The modern meaning, of permanent and unconstrained one-person rule, developed in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Types

Political scientists distinguish military dictatorships, one-party states, personalist regimes, and absolute monarchies. Dictatorships may come to power through coups d'état, revolution, or the gradual erosion of democratic institutions. Many maintain a façade of democratic institutions such as elections and legislatures that do not effectively constrain the ruler.
