The Yeoman Warders of His Majesty's Royal Palace and Fortress the Tower of London, and Members of the Sovereign's Body Guard of the Yeoman Guard Extraordinary, known popularly as Beefeaters, are the ceremonial guardians of the Tower of London. They were formed in 1485 by King Henry VII, making them one of the oldest military corps in existence.

Role

Historically the warders guarded prisoners held in the Tower and protected the Crown Jewels. Today they act principally as guides and a tourist attraction in their own right, conducting the well-known Yeoman Warder tours, while security is provided by the Tower guard and police. They take part in the nightly Ceremony of the Keys. The Chief Yeoman Warder also maintains the Tower's famous ravens; care of the ravens is entrusted to the Ravenmaster, one of the warders.

Recruitment and dress

Warders are drawn from retired senior non-commissioned officers of the British Armed Forces with at least 22 years' service and a Long Service and Good Conduct Medal. Their scarlet-and-gold Tudor state dress, reserved for state occasions, differs from the everyday dark blue uniform with red trim. The origin of the nickname "Beefeater" is uncertain; popular explanations link it to a privilege of eating beef from the king's table.
