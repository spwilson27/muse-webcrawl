Bhartrihari

Bhartṛhari (5th century CE) was an Indian philosopher, grammarian and poet. He is known by two great works: the Vākyapadīya, a foundational text of Sanskrit grammar and philosophy of language, and the Śatakatraya, three collections of one hundred verses each on conduct, love and renunciation. Whether the grammarian and the poet were the same person has long been debated.

Philosophy of language

In the Vākyapadīya, Bhartrihari argues that the sentence, not the individual word, is the primary unit of meaning, and develops the doctrine of sphoṭa — the indivisible linguistic unit that "bursts forth" in understanding. He identifies ultimate reality with śabda-brahman, the Word-Absolute, placing grammar at the centre of Indian metaphysics. The Chinese pilgrim Yijing, writing in the 7th century, records traditions about Bhartrihari's life, including his repeated movement between monastic and worldly life.

The Śatakas

The Śatakatraya comprises the Nītiśataka (on ethics and conduct), Śṛṅgāraśataka (on love) and Vairāgyaśataka (on renunciation). Its verses, attributed to a legendary King Bhartrihari of Ujjain who renounced his throne, remain among the most quoted in Sanskrit literature.
