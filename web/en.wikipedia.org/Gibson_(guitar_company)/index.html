Gibson, Inc. is an American manufacturer of guitars, other musical instruments, and professional audio equipment, founded in 1902 in Kalamazoo, Michigan, and now based in Nashville, Tennessee. The company's founder, Orville Gibson, pioneered archtop mandolins and guitars carved like violins, and Gibson went on to create some of the most iconic electric guitars in history.

History

Orville Gibson began making mandolins in the 1890s with carved, arched tops and backs. The Gibson Mandolin-Guitar Manufacturing Company was incorporated in 1902. Under Lloyd Loar in the early 1920s, Gibson produced the celebrated Master Model instruments, including the F-5 mandolin and L-5 guitar. In the 1930s Gibson introduced influential archtop and flattop guitars, and in 1936 the ES-150, one of the first commercially successful electric guitars.

In 1952 Gibson launched the Les Paul solid-body electric guitar, developed with guitarist Les Paul, which — along with later models such as the ES-335 (1958), the SG, the Flying V and the Explorer — became a standard of rock music. In 1975 guitar production moved from Kalamazoo to Nashville. The company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in 2018 and emerged later that year under new ownership led by KKR, refocusing on its core guitar business.

Products

Gibson's best-known models include the Les Paul, SG, ES-335, Flying V, Explorer, Firebird and the J-45 acoustic. The company also owns the Epiphone, Kramer, Steinberger and Mesa Boogie brands, and operates the Gibson Custom Shop. Gibson is a longtime rival of Fender, and vintage Gibson instruments — especially the 1958–1960 Les Paul Standard "Bursts" — are among the most valuable production guitars ever made.
