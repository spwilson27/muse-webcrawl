Paris Is Burning is a 1990 American documentary film directed by Jennie Livingston. Filmed in New York City in the mid-to-late 1980s, it chronicles the ball culture of African-American, Latino, gay, and transgender communities, and remains one of the most celebrated documentaries of its era.

Content

The film explores the elaborately structured competitions ("balls") in which participants, organised into "houses" led by mothers and fathers, compete in categories judged on dance skills, costumes, and "realness" — the ability to embody a social archetype convincingly. It documents practices such as voguing, reading, and shade, and features figures including Pepper LaBeija, Dorian Corey, Willi Ninja, Octavia St. Laurent, and Venus Xtravaganza, whose murder during production became one of the film's most sobering threads.

Reception and legacy

The film won the Grand Jury Prize at the Sundance Film Festival and numerous other awards, though it also sparked debate about the ethics of its production and the compensation of its subjects, and some participants sued unsuccessfully over profits. Scholars including bell hooks and Judith Butler debated the film's portrayal of gender performance. In 2016 it was selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry. Its vocabulary — voguing, shade, "yas queen" lineage — has deeply influenced mainstream culture, notably through Madonna's "Vogue" (1990) and the television series Pose (2018–2021).
