Floating dry dock

A floating dry dock is a type of movable dry dock used to lift ships out of the water for maintenance and repair. It consists of a pontoon deck with wing walls containing ballast tanks; flooding the tanks submerges the dock so a vessel can be floated in, and pumping them out raises the dock and ship clear of the water.

Operation

Before lifting, the ship is centred over keel blocks placed on the dock floor, and side shores stabilise the hull as the dock surfaces. Floating docks can be towed between ports and are used where building a permanent graving dock is impractical.

Notable examples

Large floating docks served naval fleets during the Second World War, including the US Navy's sectional auxiliary floating drydocks (AFDB), which could lift capital ships at forward bases. Modern floating docks can lift vessels of more than 80,000 tonnes, though the largest ships still require fixed graving docks.
