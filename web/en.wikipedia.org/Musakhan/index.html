Musakhan

Musakhan served on taboon bread

Musakhan (Arabic: مسخّن, "something that is heated") is a Palestinian dish composed of roasted chicken baked with onions, sumac, allspice, and saffron, served over taboon flatbread and finished with generous amounts of olive oil and toasted pine nuts. It is considered the national dish of Palestine.

Preparation

Large quantities of sliced onions are slowly cooked in olive oil with sumac — which gives the dish its characteristic deep red colour and tang — until soft and sweet. The onions are spread over rounds of taboon bread, topped with pieces of roasted chicken, sprinkled with more sumac and pine nuts, and briefly baked. The dish is traditionally eaten by hand, tearing the oil-soaked bread together with the chicken and onions.

Cultural significance

Musakhan originates in the Tulkarm and Jenin area of the northern West Bank and is closely tied to the olive harvest season, when fresh-pressed olive oil is abundant. In 2010, Palestinians in the village of Arraba prepared a record-breaking musakhan about 4 metres across, recognised at the time as the largest ever made.
