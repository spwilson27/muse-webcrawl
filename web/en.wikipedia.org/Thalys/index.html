Thalys was a high-speed train service between France, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Germany, operated from 1996 to 2023. Its distinctive deep-red trains ran from Paris via Brussels to Amsterdam and Cologne, using dedicated high-speed lines and multi-voltage TGV-derived trainsets able to run under the four countries' different electrification and signalling systems. In October 2023 the Thalys brand was retired and its services were folded into the Eurostar brand following the merger of the two companies.

History

Thalys grew out of cooperation between the French SNCF, Belgian SNCB/NMBS, Dutch NS, and German Deutsche Bahn, and was named after a combination evoking its destinations. Services began in June 1996 between Paris, Brussels, and Amsterdam; the completion of the LGV Nord, HSL-Zuid, and the Belgian high-speed lines progressively cut journey times, making Paris–Brussels about 1 hour 22 minutes and Paris–Amsterdam about 3 hours 18 minutes. Thalys was the first rail service to offer onboard Wi-Fi on an international route.

Merger into Eurostar

In 2019 the shareholders of Thalys and Eurostar announced a merger (the Green Speed project), which was approved by the European Commission in 2022. The combined Eurostar Group adopted Eurostar as its single customer-facing brand from 1 October 2023, with Thalys's red trains progressively repainted in Eurostar livery while continuing to serve the same routes.

Rolling stock

Thalys used two fleets of TGV-derived trains: the PBA sets (Paris–Brussels–Amsterdam) and the quadri-current PBKA sets able to reach Cologne. They operated at up to 300 km/h on high-speed lines.
