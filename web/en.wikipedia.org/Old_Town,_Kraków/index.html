Old Town, Kraków

LocationKraków, Lesser Poland, Poland

CriteriaCultural: (iv)
Area149.65 ha (buffer zone 907.35 ha)

The Kraków Old Town (Polish: Stare Miasto) is the historic central district of Kraków, Poland. It is one of the most famous old districts in Poland and was the centre of the country's political life from 1038 until King Sigismund III Vasa relocated his court to Warsaw in 1596. The district was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1978, among the very first sites granted that status.

History

The Old Town was rebuilt in 1257, the year of the city's incorporation under Magdeburg law, after the Mongol invasion of 1241 destroyed the earlier settlement. Its street plan — a large central market square ringed by a grid of streets — has survived largely intact. Defensive walls with towers and the Barbican protected the city; most walls were demolished in the 19th century and replaced by the Planty park, a green ring around the district.

Main Market Square

At the centre lies the Main Market Square (Rynek Główny), the largest medieval town square in Europe at roughly 200 by 200 metres. It is dominated by the Renaissance Cloth Hall (Sukiennice), St Mary's Basilica with its uneven towers and hourly hejnał trumpet call, the small Church of St Adalbert, and the Town Hall Tower.

Other landmarks

The Royal Road runs from the Barbican and St Florian's Church through the square to Wawel Hill, where Wawel Castle and Wawel Cathedral overlook the Vistula River. The district also includes the Collegium Maius of the Jagiellonian University, founded in 1364, and dozens of historic churches, palaces and tenement houses.
