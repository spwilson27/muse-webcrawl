Somaliland

Somaliland, officially the Republic of Somaliland, is an unrecognised sovereign state in the Horn of Africa, recognised internationally as part of Somalia. It lies in northwestern Somalia, on the southern coast of the Gulf of Aden, and is bordered by Djibouti to the northwest, Ethiopia to the south and west, and Somalia to the east. Its claimed territory has an area of about 176,120 square kilometres (68,000 sq mi), with approximately 6.2 million residents in 2024. The capital and largest city is Hargeisa.

The territory was briefly independent in 1960 as the State of Somaliland before voluntarily uniting with the Trust Territory of Somaliland (former Italian Somaliland) to form the Somali Republic. Following the collapse of the Siad Barre regime and a brutal civil war, delegates at the Burao conference declared the restoration of Somaliland's independence on 18 May 1991.

Government and status

Somaliland functions as a de facto independent republic with its own government, currency (the Somaliland shilling), military, and regular multi-party elections, but it has not been formally recognised by any UN member state. It maintains informal relations with several governments and hosts representative offices abroad. Its eastern regions of Sool and Sanaag have been disputed with Puntland.

Economy

The economy is based largely on livestock exports to Gulf states, remittances from the diaspora, and the port of Berbera on the Gulf of Aden, which has been expanded under agreements with DP World and Ethiopia.
