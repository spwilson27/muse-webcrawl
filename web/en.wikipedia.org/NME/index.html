NME, originally the New Musical Express, is a British music magazine and website founded in 1952. It was the first British paper to include a singles chart, and for decades it was one of the most influential voices in British popular music, championing punk, post-punk, indie rock, and Britpop.

History

The paper began in 1949 as the Musical Express and was relaunched as the New Musical Express in 1952. Its rivalry with Melody Maker defined British music journalism. In the 1970s and 1980s, writers such as Nick Kent, Charles Shaar Murray, and Julie Burchill gave it a combative, literary style. Circulation declined in the digital era; the magazine became free in 2015 and ceased print publication in 2018, continuing as an online brand. It was acquired by BandLab Technologies in 2019.

NME Awards

The NME Awards, held annually since the 1950s, honour the year's music with irreverent categories — alongside Best Album and Best Band, prizes such as Villain of the Year and Worst Dressed became fixtures.
