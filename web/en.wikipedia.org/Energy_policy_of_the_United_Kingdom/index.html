Energy policy of the United Kingdom

The energy policy of the United Kingdom refers to the United Kingdom's efforts to supply energy while reducing carbon emissions and maintaining security of supply and affordability. Responsibility rests largely with the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero. The Climate Change Act 2008 set legally binding carbon budgets, amended in 2019 to require net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050.

History

British energy was transformed by North Sea oil and gas from the 1970s, the privatisation of electricity and gas industries in the 1980s–1990s, and the subsequent 'dash for gas'. Coal's share of electricity generation fell from around 40 per cent in 2012 to zero with the closure of the last coal-fired station, Ratcliffe-on-Soar, in 2024.

Current directions

Policy promotes offshore wind, in which the UK is a world leader, new nuclear power (Hinkley Point C and Sizewell C), solar, hydrogen and carbon capture and storage. Great British Energy, a publicly owned company announced in 2024, is intended to invest in clean power. Energy markets are regulated by Ofgem, and a retail price cap has operated since 2019.
