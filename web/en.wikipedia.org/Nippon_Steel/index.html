Nippon Steel

Industry: Steel
Founded: 1950 (original); 2012 (merger)
Headquarters: Chiyoda, Tokyo, Japan
Products: Steel, steel products

Nippon Steel Corporation (日本製鉄株式会社) is Japan's largest steelmaker and one of the world's largest steel producers by volume. It was formed in 2012 through the merger of the old Nippon Steel and Sumitomo Metal Industries, and adopted its current name in 2019.

History

The original Nippon Steel was created in 1970 by the merger of Fuji Iron & Steel and Yawata Iron & Steel, the latter tracing its roots to the state-owned Yawata Steel Works founded in 1901. The combined company was for a time the world's largest steelmaker.

Operations

Nippon Steel operates integrated steelworks across Japan, including at Kimitsu, Kashima, Nagoya, Wakayama, and Oita, and produces flat products, plates, bars, wire rods, pipes and tubes, and stainless steel for automotive, construction, energy, and shipbuilding customers. It has extensive overseas joint ventures and subsidiaries. In 2023 it agreed to acquire U.S. Steel, a deal that drew prolonged political and regulatory scrutiny in the United States before its completion in 2025.
