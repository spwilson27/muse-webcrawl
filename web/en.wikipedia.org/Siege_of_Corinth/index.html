Siege of Corinth

The siege of Corinth (also known as the First Battle of Corinth) was fought from April 29 to May 30, 1862, in Corinth, Mississippi, during the American Civil War. Union forces under Major General Henry Halleck cautiously advanced on the vital Confederate railroad junction and compelled its evacuation.

Background

After the Confederate defeat at Shiloh, General P. G. T. Beauregard withdrew his army to Corinth, where the Memphis and Charleston Railroad crossed the Mobile and Ohio Railroad. Halleck concentrated over 100,000 men and advanced with extreme caution, entrenching each night and building roads through the swampy terrain.

Evacuation

Outnumbered and short of clean water, Beauregard evacuated Corinth on the night of May 29–30, using trains and cheering troops to deceive the Federals into believing reinforcements were arriving. Union forces occupied the town the next morning.

Aftermath

The loss of the junction crippled Confederate logistics in the western theatre. Corinth remained in Union hands, despite a Confederate attempt to retake it in the Second Battle of Corinth in October 1862.
