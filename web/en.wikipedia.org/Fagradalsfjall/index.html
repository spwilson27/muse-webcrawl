Fagradalsfjall

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fagradalsfjall (Icelandic: [ˈfaɣraˌtalsˌfjatl̥]) is a tuya volcano formed in the Last Glacial Period on the Reykjanes Peninsula in south-west Iceland, located approximately 40 kilometres (25 mi) from the capital, Reykjavík. It is a hyaloclastite ridge roughly 385 metres (1,263 ft) high, and its name can be loosely translated as "fair dale mountain".

The mountain became famous worldwide as the site of a series of eruptions beginning in March 2021 — the first volcanic activity on the Reykjanes Peninsula in about 800 years — followed by further eruptions in 2022 and 2023. The eruptions were largely effusive, producing lava fields in the Geldingadalir and Meradalir valleys and drawing large numbers of visitors because of their relative accessibility and modest scale.

2021 eruption

After weeks of intense seismic activity — more than 50,000 earthquakes were recorded on the peninsula in the weeks before the event — an eruption began on the evening of 19 March 2021, when a fissure about 200 metres long opened in Geldingadalir, south-east of Fagradalsfjall. Lava fountains up to 100 metres high fed flows that gradually filled the valley. The eruption, though a fissure eruption near the Fagradalsfjall system rather than from the mountain's summit, was commonly referred to as the "Fagradalsfjall eruption". It continued for roughly six months, making it the longest eruption in Iceland in more than 50 years, and ended in September 2021 after producing about 151 million cubic metres of lava covering some 4.85 square kilometres.

Later eruptions

A second eruption began on 3 August 2022 in the Meradalir valley, lasting about three weeks. A third eruptive episode started on 10 July 2023 near Litli-Hrútur, north-east of Fagradalsfjall, and continued for about a month. These events formed part of a broader reawakening of volcanism on the Reykjanes Peninsula, which continued with a series of eruptions on the Sundhnúkur crater row near Grindavík beginning in December 2023.

Geology

Fagradalsfjall is part of the Krýsuvík volcanic system. The tuya was formed by subglacial eruptions during the Pleistocene. The recent eruptions are associated with a magmatic dyke intruded beneath the Fagradalsfjall area and are monitored by the Icelandic Meteorological Office.
