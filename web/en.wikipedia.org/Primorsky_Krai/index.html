Primorsky Krai (Russian: Приморский край, "maritime region") is a federal subject of Russia located in the country's far southeast, on the coast of the Sea of Japan. Its administrative centre is the port city of Vladivostok, the terminus of the Trans-Siberian Railway and headquarters of Russia's Pacific Fleet. The krai borders China to the west and shares a short border with North Korea along the Tumen River.

Geography

The krai covers about 165,000 square kilometres. Much of its interior is covered by the Sikhote-Alin mountain range, whose mixed forests — the Ussuri taiga — support exceptional biodiversity, including the Siberian tiger and the Amur leopard. The climate is monsoonal, with cold dry winters and warm humid summers, and the coast is occasionally affected by typhoons.

History

The territory was acquired by the Russian Empire from Qing China under the Treaties of Aigun (1858) and Peking (1860). Vladivostok was founded in 1860. Primorsky Krai was formally established in 1938 when the Far Eastern Krai was divided. During the 1930s the region's large Korean population was deported to Central Asia.

Economy

The economy centres on fishing and seafood processing, shipping through the ports of Vladivostok and Nakhodka, shipbuilding, timber, and coal mining. The free port regime introduced in 2015 and the development of Far Eastern Federal University on Russky Island were intended to strengthen the region's role as Russia's gateway to the Asia-Pacific.
