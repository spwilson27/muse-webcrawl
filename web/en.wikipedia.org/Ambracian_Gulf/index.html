Ambracian Gulf

The Ambracian Gulf, also known as the Gulf of Arta or Gulf of Actium, is a gulf of the Ionian Sea in northwestern Greece. About 40 km long and 15 km wide, it is one of the largest enclosed gulfs in Greece, connected to the Ionian Sea by a narrow channel near Preveza.

Ambracian Gulf
Location: Epirus / Western Greece
Length: ~40 km; Area: ~654 km²
Rivers: Louros, Arachthos

Geography and ecology

The Louros and Arachthos rivers discharge into the gulf, forming extensive delta wetlands protected under the Ramsar Convention and as the Amvrakikos Wetlands National Park. The shallows support important fisheries (including the bottarga-producing flathead grey mullet), loggerhead turtles, and one of Greece's resident bottlenose dolphin populations, plus Dalmatian pelicans and other waterbirds.

History

At its entrance Octavian's fleet defeated Mark Antony and Cleopatra at the Battle of Actium in 31 BCE; Octavian founded Nicopolis on its shore. The ancient city of Ambracia (modern Arta) gave the gulf its name. An undersea road tunnel (opened 2002) links Preveza with Actium beneath the entrance channel.
