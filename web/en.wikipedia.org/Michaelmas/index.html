Michaelmas

Michaelmas (also known as the Feast of Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael, the Feast of the Archangels, or the Feast of Saint Michael and All Angels) is a Christian festival observed in some Western liturgical calendars on 29 September. The word derives from "Michael's Mass", the feast of St Michael the Archangel.

History and customs

Because it falls near the autumn equinox, Michaelmas marked the end of the harvest season in the British Isles and was one of the traditional "quarter days" on which rents were due, servants were hired, and school and university terms began. One of the four terms of the academic year at Oxford and Cambridge is still called Michaelmas term, as are sittings of the law courts in England and Wales.

Customs included eating a roast goose — "Eat goose on Michaelmas day, want not for money all the year" — and in Scotland the baking of the St Michael's bannock (Struan Micheil). In folklore, Michaelmas was the last day on which blackberries could safely be picked, it being said that the Devil spat or stamped on them after that date; the Michaelmas daisy (an aster) is named for its flowering at this season. In the Roman Catholic calendar after the Second Vatican Council, the feast of 29 September honours the three archangels Michael, Gabriel and Raphael together; in Eastern Orthodoxy the Synaxis of the Archangel Michael is celebrated on 8 November.

Observance

The Book of Common Prayer tradition celebrates Saint Michael and All Angels on 29 September. The quarter day in Ireland and Scotland differs in date due to calendar reforms, with "Old Michaelmas" sometimes observed on 10 or 11 October.
