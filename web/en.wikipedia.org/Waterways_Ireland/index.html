Waterways Ireland manages the navigations of the whole island of Ireland.

Waterways Ireland (Irish: Uiscebhealaí Éireann) is one of the six all-Ireland North/South implementation bodies established under the Belfast/Good Friday Agreement of 1998. Created in 1999, it is responsible for the management, development and restoration of the inland navigable waterways of Ireland, north and south, primarily for recreational purposes. Its headquarters are in Enniskillen, County Fermanagh, with regional offices including Dublin and Carrick-on-Shannon.

Waterways managed

Waterways Ireland manages more than 1,000 kilometres of navigations: the River Shannon and the Shannon–Erne Waterway, the Grand Canal and Royal Canal, the Barrow Navigation, the Lower Bann, the Erne System and the Ulster Canal. The Royal Canal, reopened to through navigation in 2010 after decades of dereliction, links Dublin with the Shannon. The body has also led the phased restoration of the Ulster Canal, intended to reconnect the Erne system with Lough Neagh; the first restored section, between Clones and Clonfad, opened in the 2020s.

Functions

The organisation maintains locks, weirs, harbours and towpaths, issues navigation bye-laws and permits, promotes boating tourism and angling, and develops towpath greenways such as the Royal Canal Greenway. It is accountable jointly to the Northern Ireland Assembly and the Oireachtas through the North/South Ministerial Council, with sponsorship from departments in both jurisdictions.
