Panama–California Exposition

The Panama–California Exposition was a world's fair held in Balboa Park, San Diego, California, in 1915–1916, celebrating the opening of the Panama Canal and promoting San Diego as the first American port of call for ships travelling north from the canal.

Architecture

The exposition popularised the Spanish Colonial Revival style in California. Its landmark buildings, including the California Building with its ornate tower and the Cabrillo Bridge, remain icons of Balboa Park; many now house museums.

Legacy

Unlike most world's fairs, the exposition's buildings were constructed to be permanent. A second fair, the California Pacific International Exposition, reused the grounds in 1935–1936.
