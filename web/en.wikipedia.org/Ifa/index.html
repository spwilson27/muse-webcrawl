Ifá

Ifá is a Yoruba religion and system of divination originating among the Yoruba people of what is now southwestern Nigeria. It is centered on the worship of Orunmila, the orisha of wisdom, and employs an extensive corpus of oral literature — the odu — comprising 256 sections with hundreds of verses each, memorized by priests known as babalawo ("father of secrets").

Divination practice

In a consultation, the babalawo casts palm nuts (ikin) or a divining chain (opele) to generate a binary pattern identifying one of the 256 odu, then recites the verses associated with that sign, from which the client's situation and prescribed sacrifices or actions are interpreted. Ifá is recognized by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.

Spread and influence

Through the African diaspora, Ifá strongly influenced Afro-Caribbean and Afro-Brazilian traditions including Cuban Santería (Regla de Ocha) and Brazilian Candomblé, in which Ifá divination remains central. Scholarly study of the odu corpus has preserved one of the largest bodies of oral literature in Africa.
