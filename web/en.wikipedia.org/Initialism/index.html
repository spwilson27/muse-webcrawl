Initialism

DefinitionAbbreviation pronounced letter by letter
ExamplesFBI, ATM, HTML, USA, DVD
ContrastAcronym (pronounced as a word, e.g. NASA)

An initialism is an abbreviation formed from the initial letters of a phrase and pronounced as a sequence of individual letters, rather than as a word. Examples include FBI (Federal Bureau of Investigation), ATM (automated teller machine), BBC, and HTML. The term is distinguished by many style guides from acronym, which in the narrow sense denotes initial-letter abbreviations pronounced as words, such as NASA and laser.

Usage and style

Initialisms are pervasive in government, the military, technology, medicine, and business, where long organisational and technical names recur frequently. Style conventions vary: British usage generally writes initialisms without full stops (BBC, MP), while American usage historically favoured periods (F.B.I.) but now usually omits them. Initialisms typically take capital letters and often become so familiar that the expanded form is forgotten — most speakers know what a DVD player is without expanding "digital versatile disc".

Ambiguity of terms

Usage of the words acronym and initialism is not fully consistent: many dictionaries define acronym broadly to cover all initial-letter abbreviations, whether pronounced as words or spelled out, making initialism the marked, narrower term used chiefly by editors and linguists. Hybrid pronunciations also occur, as in JPEG ("jay-peg") or SQL, pronounced either letter by letter or as "sequel".
