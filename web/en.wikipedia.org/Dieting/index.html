Dieting

Dieting is the practice of eating food in a regulated way to decrease, maintain, or increase body weight, or to prevent and treat diseases such as diabetes and obesity. When undertaken to lose weight, dieting typically involves a calorie deficit; diets may also prescribe particular proportions of macronutrients or exclude specific foods.

Types of diets

  • Low-calorie diets restrict total energy intake.
  • Low-carbohydrate diets, such as the ketogenic and Atkins diets, restrict carbohydrate in favour of protein and fat.
  • Low-fat diets restrict fat intake, historically promoted for heart health.
  • Intermittent fasting alternates periods of eating with fasting.
  • Pattern-based diets, such as the Mediterranean diet, emphasise whole dietary patterns rather than single nutrients.

Effectiveness

Meta-analyses generally find that most named diets produce modest weight loss at six months with differences between diets being small, and that adherence is the strongest predictor of success. Long-term maintenance is difficult: metabolic adaptation and behavioural factors lead many dieters to regain weight, a phenomenon sometimes called weight cycling.

Health considerations

Very restrictive or fad diets can cause nutrient deficiencies and are discouraged by health authorities, which generally recommend gradual, sustainable changes. In people vulnerable to eating disorders, dieting is a known risk factor and is approached cautiously in clinical guidance.
