Matilda of Tuscany (Italian: Matilde di Canossa; c. 1046 – 24 July 1115), also called Matilda of Canossa and la Gran Contessa ("the Great Countess"), was a powerful Italian noblewoman who was among the most important figures of the Italian Middle Ages. As heiress of the House of Canossa she ruled a vast territory spanning much of present-day Tuscany, Lombardy, and Emilia, and she was the papacy's chief military supporter during the Investiture Controversy with the Holy Roman Emperors.

Rise to power

Matilda was the youngest child of Boniface III, Margrave of Tuscany, once the most powerful prince in Italy, who was assassinated in 1052. After the deaths of her siblings and stepfather, she and her mother Beatrice became the papacy's key allies in Italy. Her first marriage, to Godfrey the Hunchback of Lower Lorraine, ended with his murder in 1076; she thereafter ruled her lands in her own right with great ability, commanding troops in person.

The Walk to Canossa

Matilda is best remembered for the episode of January 1077, when Emperor Henry IV, excommunicated by Pope Gregory VII, came to her castle of Canossa in the Apennines and — according to tradition — stood barefoot in the snow for three days seeking absolution, which Gregory, Matilda's guest, finally granted. The meeting, which she helped mediate, became a lasting symbol of the humbling of secular power before the Church.

Wars and later life

Matilda remained Gregory's and his successors' staunchest defender, defeating Henry IV's forces in Italy — including at Sorbara in 1084 and at Monteveglio — and hosting Pope Urban II. When Henry invaded Italy in the 1090s she resisted from her Apennine fortresses until his withdrawal. In 1111 Emperor Henry V visited her and granted her the title of imperial vicar in Italy. She founded and endowed many churches and monasteries.

Death and legacy

Matilda died in 1115 without surviving children, bequeathing her allodial lands — the "Matildine lands" — to the Church, a donation contested by emperors for centuries. She is one of only a handful of women buried in St. Peter's Basilica in Rome, where Bernini designed her monument.
