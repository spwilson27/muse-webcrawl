Duomo

Florence Cathedral (Santa Maria del Fiore), the city's duomo

Duomo (Italian: [ˈdwɔːmo]) is an Italian term for a church that serves or served as a cathedral — the seat of a bishop. The word derives from the Latin domus ("house"), in the sense of the house of God or the bishop's church. A duomo need not today be a functioning cathedral, but the name is retained for many historic churches in Italy and Italian-speaking Switzerland.

Usage

Famous examples include the Duomo di Milano (Milan Cathedral), one of the largest Gothic churches in the world; Florence Cathedral, crowned by Brunelleschi's dome; Pisa Cathedral, part of the Piazza dei Miracoli complex; Siena Cathedral; and the cathedrals of Orvieto, Modena and Monreale. The modern Italian word for a cathedral in the strict sense is cattedrale, but in many cities the principal church is simply called "il Duomo".

Terminology

Related terms include the German Dom, which likewise denotes a cathedral or major collegiate church (as in Cologne Cathedral, the Kölner Dom), and the Scandinavian domkirke. The English word "dome" is not directly related in meaning, though the association of great cathedrals with domed roofs has influenced popular usage.
