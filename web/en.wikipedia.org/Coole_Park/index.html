Coole Park

Coole Park is a nature reserve of approximately 405 hectares (1,000 acres) located in the townland of Coole, near Gort in County Galway, Ireland. It is managed by the Irish National Parks and Wildlife Service. The park was formerly the estate of the Gregory family, and is best known as the home of Lady Augusta Gregory, dramatist and co-founder of the Abbey Theatre, and as a gathering place of the writers of the Irish Literary Revival.

History

Coole House was built in the late 18th century by the Gregory family. Lady Gregory (1852–1932), widow of Sir William Henry Gregory, made the house a centre of Irish cultural life from the 1890s onward: W. B. Yeats, George Bernard Shaw, John Millington Synge, Seán O'Casey, George William Russell (AE) and others were frequent guests. The famous "Autograph Tree", a copper beech in the walled garden, bears the carved initials of many of these visitors. Yeats celebrated the estate in poems including "The Wild Swans at Coole" (1917), written about the swans on Coole Lough, and "Coole Park, 1929" and "Coole and Ballylee, 1931".

The house was demolished in 1941, after the estate passed to the state Forestry Department, but the grounds — including lawns, a walled garden, woodland of oak and beech, and the seasonal lake (turlough) of Coole Lough — survive as a public nature reserve with visitor facilities.

Wildlife

Coole Lough is a turlough that fills in winter and empties in summer, supporting whooper swans and other wintering waterfowl. The woods are home to red squirrels, pine martens and fallow deer, and the reserve forms part of the Coole–Garryland complex of protected habitats.
