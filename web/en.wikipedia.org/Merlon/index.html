Merlon

A merlon is the solid upright section of a battlement or crenellated parapet in medieval architecture and fortifications. The gaps between merlons are called crenels or embrasures, through which defenders could shoot or drop missiles while sheltering behind the solid merlons. Battlements of alternating merlons and crenels are known together as crenellation.

Forms

Merlons most often take a simple rectangular form, but regional and political variations developed. In medieval Italy the shape of merlons signalled allegiance: square-topped merlons were associated with the Ghibellines (supporters of the Holy Roman Emperor), while swallowtail or "dovetail" merlons — notched in a V shape — were associated with the Guelphs (supporters of the Pope). Other forms include pointed, rounded and stepped merlons, some pierced with cross-shaped or slit openings for observation and archery.

History

Crenellated parapets were used from antiquity, appearing on Assyrian and Roman fortifications, and became a defining feature of medieval castles and city walls across Europe, the Islamic world and beyond. With the advent of gunpowder artillery merlons lost their military function but survived as a decorative motif in Gothic Revival and other historicist architecture.
