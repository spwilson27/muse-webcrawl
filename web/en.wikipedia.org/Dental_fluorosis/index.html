Dental fluorosis

Dental fluorosis is a change in the appearance of tooth enamel caused by excessive fluoride intake during enamel formation in childhood, typically up to age eight.

Signs

Mild cases show faint white flecks or streaks; severe cases show brown staining, pitting and loss of enamel. The condition is cosmetic in mild forms and is not considered harmful to health at those levels, though severe fluorosis can weaken teeth.

Causes and prevention

Sources include naturally high-fluoride groundwater, fluoridated water, swallowed toothpaste and supplements. Prevention involves monitoring total fluoride intake in young children. Dean's index is used to grade severity.
