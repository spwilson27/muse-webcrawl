List of Roman aqueducts

The Romans built aqueducts throughout their republic and empire to bring water from distant sources into cities for baths, fountains, latrines and private households.

Aqueducts of Rome

  • Aqua Appia (312 BC) — the first Roman aqueduct.
  • Anio Vetus, Aqua Marcia, Aqua Tepula, Aqua Julia — Republican aqueducts.
  • Aqua Virgo, Aqua Claudia, Anio Novus, Aqua Traiana, Aqua Alexandrina — Imperial aqueducts; by the 3rd century AD eleven aqueducts supplied Rome.

Provincial aqueducts

  • Pont du Gard (France) — part of the Nîmes aqueduct, a famous three-tiered bridge.
  • Aqueduct of Segovia (Spain) — well-preserved granite arcade.
  • Valens Aqueduct (Constantinople), Aqua Augusta (Naples), aqueducts of Tarragona, Mérida, Lyon, Cologne, Caesarea and many others across the empire.
