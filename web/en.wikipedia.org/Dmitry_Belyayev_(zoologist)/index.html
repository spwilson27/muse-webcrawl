Dmitry Belyayev (zoologist)

Dmitry Konstantinovich Belyayev (1917–1985) was a Soviet geneticist and zoologist best known for the farm-fox experiment, a long-running study of domestication conducted at the Institute of Cytology and Genetics in Novosibirsk, Siberia, which he directed for many years.

The farm-fox experiment

Beginning in 1959, Belyayev and his colleague Lyudmila Trut selectively bred silver foxes (a color variant of the red fox) solely for tameness — choosing each generation the animals least fearful of and least aggressive toward humans. Within a few dozen generations the foxes began to show not only dog-like behavior — wagging tails, seeking human contact, whimpering — but also physical changes: floppy ears, piebald coats, curled tails, shortened snouts and altered reproductive timing. Belyayev interpreted the results as evidence that selection for behavior destabilized developmental processes and produced correlated changes, supporting his theory of destabilizing selection and offering a model for how wolves may have been domesticated into dogs.

Context

Belyayev pursued genetics during the era of Lysenkoism, when orthodox Mendelian genetics was suppressed in the Soviet Union, and he worked to restore the discipline. The fox experiment continues to this day under Trut's direction and is a landmark in evolutionary and behavioral biology.
