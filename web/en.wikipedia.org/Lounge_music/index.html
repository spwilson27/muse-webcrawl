Lounge music

Lounge music is a genre of easy listening music popular in the 1950s and 1960s, intended to evoke in listeners the feeling of being in a place — a jungle, an island paradise, outer space or another exotic location — while drinking cocktails in a hotel lounge or tiki bar. It encompasses the related styles of exotica, space-age pop and some forms of easy-listening orchestral music.

Characteristics and artists

Classic lounge music featured lush orchestration, unusual instrumentation (vibraphone, bongos, bird calls, wordless vocals) and, in the hi-fi era, stereo effects designed to show off home sound systems. Key figures include Martin Denny and Les Baxter (exotica), Esquivel (space-age bachelor pad music), and performers such as the organists and vocalists of the cocktail-lounge circuit. Singers in the genre ranged from jazz vocalists performing standards to the inimitable Yma Sumac, famed for her multi-octave range.

Revival

In the 1990s a lounge revival, often called "cocktail nation", renewed interest in the genre: record labels reissued space-age pop, bands such as Combustible Edison and Stereolab drew on its sounds, and compilation series like Ultra-Lounge introduced it to new audiences. Modern descendants include chill-out and downtempo electronic music, and the term "lounge" is now loosely applied to relaxed background music in bars and hotels worldwide.
