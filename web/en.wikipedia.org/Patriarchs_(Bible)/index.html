The patriarchs (Hebrew: אבות Avot, "fathers") of the Bible, when narrowly defined, are Abraham, his son Isaac, and Isaac's son Jacob, also named Israel, the ancestors of the Israelites. These three are collectively referred to as the patriarchs, and the period in which they lived is known as the patriarchal age. Their stories are told in the Book of Genesis and they play significant roles in Judaism, Christianity and Islam.

The patriarchal narratives

The narratives describe Abraham's call and migration from Mesopotamia to Canaan, the covenant and promise of descendants and land, the birth of Isaac, the binding of Isaac, and Jacob's dreams, marriages and twelve sons — the eponymous ancestors of the Twelve Tribes of Israel. Joseph, Jacob's favoured son, brings the family to Egypt, setting the stage for the Exodus.

Related female figures, the matriarchs, are Sarah, Rebekah, Leah and Rachel. In a broader usage, "patriarchs" may also include the antediluvian figures of Genesis 1–11, such as Adam, Noah and their descendants, or the sons of Jacob.

Historicity

Scholars debate the historical background of the patriarchal traditions. Earlier 20th-century scholarship (notably William F. Albright) sought parallels in second-millennium BC Near Eastern customs; later critical studies (such as those of Thomas L. Thompson and John Van Seters) argued the narratives largely reflect the concerns of much later periods, likely the first millennium BC. The stories are generally treated by historians as foundational traditions rather than verifiable biography.

Burial

According to Genesis, Abraham purchased the cave of Machpelah at Hebron as a family tomb. Tradition holds that Abraham and Sarah, Isaac and Rebekah, and Jacob and Leah are buried there, at the site known as the Cave of the Patriarchs (the Ibrahimi Mosque), one of the most contested holy places in the world.

In Islam

In Islam, Abraham (Ibrahim), Isaac (Ishaq) and Jacob (Yaqub) are revered as prophets, and the Quran recounts their stories extensively; Abraham is honoured as a model of monotheistic faith (hanif) and ancestor of Muhammad through Ishmael.
