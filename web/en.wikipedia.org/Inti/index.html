Inti

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Inti is the ancient Inca god of the sun. He was among the most important deities of the Inca religion and the patron of the Inca state, whose rulers, the Sapa Inca, claimed descent from him. Inti was worshipped throughout the Inca Empire, especially at his great temple, the Coricancha in Cusco, and his festival Inti Raymi at the winter solstice remains celebrated in Peru today.

Mythology

In Inca mythology, Inti was the son of the creator god Viracocha and the father, in some accounts, of Manco Cápac and Mama Ocllo, the legendary founders of Cusco and the Inca dynasty. He was married to Mama Killa, the moon goddess, who was also regarded as his sister. Inti was believed to have given the Inca the gift of agriculture and civilisation, teaching them to cultivate maize and other crops.

Worship

The most sacred site of Inti's cult was the Coricancha ("Golden Enclosure") in Cusco, whose interior walls were once covered with sheets of gold representing the sun's radiance. A great golden image of Inti, the Punchao, was kept there; it was said to contain the ashes of the hearts of past emperors. Priests of the sun, headed by the Willaq Umu, conducted daily offerings of maize beer and food, and sacrifices — usually of llamas, and on great state occasions, in the capacocha rite, of children.

Festivals

The principal feast of Inti was Inti Raymi, held at the June (winter) solstice, when the sun was farthest from the earth and the Inca prayed for its return; ceremonies lasted several days and included processions, sacrifices and feasting. A second great solar festival, Capac Raymi, took place at the December solstice. After the Spanish conquest and suppression of the cult, Inti Raymi was revived in the twentieth century as a theatrical and civic festival, held each 24 June at the fortress of Sacsayhuamán above Cusco.

Legacy

The sun with a face from Inca iconography appears on the flags of Argentina and Uruguay (the Sun of May) and on various Peruvian symbols; the former Peruvian currency, the inti (1985–1991), was named after the god.
