History of role-playing games

The history of role-playing games begins in the early 1970s, when tabletop wargaming merged with fantasy fiction to produce Dungeons & Dragons (1974), the first commercially published role-playing game (RPG). The hobby has since grown to encompass tabletop, live-action, and video game forms played worldwide.

Origins: wargames and Blackmoor

Role-playing grew out of miniature wargaming: the Midwest Military Simulation Association's games in the late 1960s, Dave Arneson's Blackmoor campaign (from 1971), and the fantasy supplement to Gary Gygax and Jeff Perren's Chainmail (1971) combined to give players individual characters, experience, and dungeon exploration.

Dungeons & Dragons and the first wave

Gygax and Arneson's Dungeons & Dragons, published by TSR in 1974, launched the industry. Competitors and successors followed: RuneQuest (1978), Traveller (1977) for science fiction, Call of Cthulhu (1981) for horror, and GURPS (1986). The 1980s brought mainstream popularity alongside a moral panic linking games to occultism. Vampire: The Masquerade (1991) led a 1990s wave emphasizing storytelling.

The d20 era and beyond

Wizards of the Coast bought TSR in 1997 and released D&D third edition (2000) with the Open Game License, enabling third-party publishing and games like Pathfinder (2009). D&D fifth edition (2014), aided by actual-play streaming shows and mainstream media, drove record growth in the late 2010s and 2020s, while independent and "old school revival" games diversified design. Video game RPGs — from Ultima and Final Fantasy to modern CRPGs — form a parallel and intertwined history.
