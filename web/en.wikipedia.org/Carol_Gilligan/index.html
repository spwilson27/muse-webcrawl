Carol Gilligan (born November 28, 1936) is an American feminist, ethicist, and psychologist best known for her work on moral development and the ethics of care. Her landmark 1982 book In a Different Voice: Psychological Theory and Women's Development challenged the prevailing psychological theories of the time, which had been developed largely from studies of men and boys.

Early life and education

Gilligan was born in New York City. She studied English literature at Swarthmore College, graduating summa cum laude in 1958, earned a master's degree in clinical psychology from Radcliffe College in 1961, and completed her Ph.D. in social psychology at Harvard University in 1964.

Career

Gilligan joined the faculty of Harvard University in 1967 and taught at the Harvard Graduate School of Education for many years. Working alongside Lawrence Kohlberg, whose stage theory of moral development had been based on studies of males, she argued that women often approach moral problems with an orientation toward relationships, care, and responsibility rather than toward abstract rules and justice alone. Her work helped found the field known as the ethics of care.

After leaving Harvard, Gilligan became a professor at New York University School of Law. She later founded, with colleagues, a research collaborative focused on girls' development and relational psychology.

Selected works

  • In a Different Voice (1982)
  • Meeting at the Crossroads (1992, with Lyn Mikel Brown)
  • The Birth of Pleasure (2002)
  • Kyra (2008, a novel)
  • Joining the Resistance (2011)
  • Why Does Patriarchy Persist? (2018, with Naomi Snider)

Legacy

In a Different Voice is widely regarded as one of the most influential works of twentieth-century psychology and feminist thought. It reshaped debates about moral reasoning, gender, and human development, and remains a foundational text in care ethics.
