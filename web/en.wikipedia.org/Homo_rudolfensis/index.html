Homo rudolfensis

Homo rudolfensis is an extinct species of archaic human from the Early Pleistocene of East Africa about 2 million years ago. The species is known from fossils found around Lake Turkana (formerly Lake Rudolf) in Kenya and northern Malawi. The holotype is the skull KNM-ER 1470, discovered in 1972 at Koobi Fora by Bernard Ngeneo in a team led by Richard Leakey.

Classification

KNM-ER 1470 was originally assigned to Homo habilis, but differences in cranial capacity (around 750 cm³), a flatter and broader face, and larger molars led Valery Alekseyev to propose the species name rudolfensis in 1986 (originally in the genus Pithecanthropus). Whether these fossils represent a separate species from H. habilis, sexual dimorphism within one species, or variation within a single lineage remains debated. In 2012, Meave Leakey and colleagues described new fossils from Koobi Fora — a face and two jawbones — argued to confirm H. rudolfensis as a distinct species contemporaneous with H. habilis.

Anatomy

Compared with H. habilis, H. rudolfensis is reconstructed with a larger brain, a flatter face lacking pronounced brow ridges, and large post-canine teeth with thick enamel, suggesting a diet requiring heavy chewing.
