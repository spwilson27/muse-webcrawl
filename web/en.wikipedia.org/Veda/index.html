Vedas

The Vedas are a large body of religious texts originating in ancient India. Composed in Vedic Sanskrit, they are the oldest scriptures of Hinduism and among the oldest sacred texts in the world. Orthodox Hindu tradition regards them as śruti ("what is heard"), revealed to ancient sages rather than authored by humans.

The four Vedas

There are four Vedas: the Rigveda, a collection of hymns to the gods; the Samaveda, melodies and chants derived largely from the Rigveda; the Yajurveda, prose mantras and ritual instructions; and the Atharvaveda, spells, hymns and philosophical speculations. Each Veda comprises four layers: the Samhitas (hymns and mantras), the Brahmanas (ritual commentaries), the Aranyakas ("forest texts") and the Upanishads (philosophical teachings).

Dating and transmission

Scholars generally date the composition of the earliest portions, the Rigveda, to roughly 1500–1200 BCE in the northwestern Indian subcontinent, with the corpus taking shape by the end of the Vedic period around the middle of the first millennium BCE. The texts were transmitted orally with extraordinary precision for centuries, using mnemonic recitation patterns, before being written down.

Influence

The Vedas form the foundation of Hindu ritual, philosophy and law, and the Upanishads in particular shaped Indian thought, including concepts such as karma, samsara and moksha.
