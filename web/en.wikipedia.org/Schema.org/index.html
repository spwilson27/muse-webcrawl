Schema.org

Schema.org is a collaborative, community activity creating, maintaining, and promoting schemas for structured data on the Internet — markup that webmasters can use to annotate pages so that search engines can better understand their content. It was launched on 2 June 2011 by Bing, Google, and Yahoo!, with Yandex joining later that year.

Vocabulary and formats

The schema.org vocabulary defines hundreds of types — such as Person, Organization, Event, Product, Recipe, and CreativeWork — arranged in a hierarchy beneath the root type Thing, each with defined properties. Markup can be embedded using Microdata, RDFa, or JSON-LD; Google has recommended JSON-LD as its preferred format. Search engines use the annotations to power rich results such as star ratings, event listings, recipe cards, and knowledge panels.

Governance and adoption

Development is coordinated by a steering group drawn from the founding companies together with the W3C community and public contributors, with releases published on the schema.org website and GitHub. Millions of websites use schema.org markup, making it the most widely deployed structured-data vocabulary on the web and a central component of the semantic web in practice.
