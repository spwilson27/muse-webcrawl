Trondheim is a city and municipality in Trøndelag county, Norway, and the country's third most populous municipality. Founded in 997 by Viking king Olav Tryggvason at the mouth of the Nidelva river, it was Norway's capital until 1217 under its old name Nidaros.

History

Trondheim was a major pilgrimage destination in the Middle Ages because of the shrine of Saint Olav in Nidaros Cathedral, the world's northernmost medieval cathedral and the traditional coronation church of Norwegian monarchs. Fires repeatedly destroyed the largely wooden city, leading to wide street plans after 1681.

Modern city

Today Trondheim is a technology and education centre, home to the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU), SINTEF research institute and St. Olavs University Hospital. Landmarks include the colourful wharves of Bakklandet, the Old Town Bridge and Kristiansten Fortress.
