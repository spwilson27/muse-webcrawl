Wilhelm Wien

Born13 January 1864, Gaffken, East Prussia
Died30 August 1928, Munich, Germany
FieldsPhysics
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (1911)
Known forWien's displacement law; Wien distribution law; Wien filter

Wilhelm Carl Werner Otto Fritz Franz Wien (1864–1928) was a German physicist who used theories about heat and electromagnetism to deduce Wien's displacement law, which calculates the emission of a blackbody at any temperature from the emission at any reference temperature. He received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1911 "for his discoveries regarding the laws governing the radiation of heat."

Radiation laws

In 1893 Wien derived the displacement law relating the wavelength of peak black-body emission to temperature, and in 1896 he proposed an approximate spectral distribution law that fit experimental data at short wavelengths; Max Planck's correction of Wien's formula in 1900 produced Planck's law and launched quantum theory. Wien also did important work on canal rays (positive ion beams), showing that they could be deflected by magnetic fields and laying groundwork for mass spectrometry, and he proposed the velocity selector now known as the Wien filter.

Career

Wien studied at Göttingen, Heidelberg, and Berlin, working with Hermann von Helmholtz, and held professorships at Aachen, Giessen, Würzburg — where he succeeded Röntgen — and finally Munich. His cousin Max Wien was known for contributions to radio technology.
