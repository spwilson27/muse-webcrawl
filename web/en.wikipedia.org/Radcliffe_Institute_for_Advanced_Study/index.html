Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study

Radcliffe Yard, the historic heart of Radcliffe College and now of the Radcliffe Institute.

The Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study at Harvard University — usually known simply as the Radcliffe Institute — is an institute of Harvard University in Cambridge, Massachusetts, dedicated to interdisciplinary research across the arts, humanities, sciences and social sciences. It was founded in 1999 through the merger of Radcliffe College with Harvard, transforming the former women's college into a research institute while continuing its commitment to the study of women, gender and society.

Programs

The Institute's best-known activity is its fellowship program, which each year brings around 50 artists, scholars, scientists and professionals to Radcliffe Yard for a year of independent work and interdisciplinary exchange. Past fellows have included Pulitzer Prize winners, MacArthur fellows and leading researchers in fields from astrophysics to poetry. The Institute also runs conferences, lectures and public programs under the umbrella of Harvard Radcliffe Institute.

Schlesinger Library

The Institute is home to the Arthur and Elizabeth Schlesinger Library on the History of Women in America, one of the world's foremost research libraries for women's history. Its collections include the papers of figures such as Betty Friedan, Julia Child and the records of organisations including the National Organization for Women, alongside a celebrated culinary collection.

History

Radcliffe College began in 1879 as the "Harvard Annex", offering Harvard instruction to women who were barred from Harvard College itself, and was chartered as Radcliffe College in 1894. It granted degrees jointly with Harvard from the 1960s, and from 1977 shared admissions with Harvard College. The 1999 agreement dissolved the undergraduate college and created the Institute in its place; the Radcliffe name, endowment and campus were preserved within Harvard.
