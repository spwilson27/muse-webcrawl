Wei Zheng

Wei Zheng (魏徵, 580–643), courtesy name Xuancheng, was a Chinese politician and historian of the early Tang dynasty. Celebrated as the model of the honest remonstrating official, he served as chancellor under Emperor Taizong and is famous for his fearless criticism of the emperor, which Taizong accepted as essential to good government.

Career

Wei Zheng first served the rebel leader Li Mi and then the Tang crown prince Li Jiancheng. After Taizong killed Li Jiancheng in the Xuanwu Gate Incident of 626, he spared Wei and took him into his own service, impressed by his candour. As chancellor and later as an imperial adviser, Wei submitted more than two hundred memorials urging frugality, light taxes and reliance on virtuous officials — advice that helped shape the prosperous Zhenguan era.

Historian

Wei compiled and edited the Book of Sui, the official history of the preceding dynasty, and wrote the influential commentaries and prefaces in the Qunshu Zhiyao, an anthology of statecraft prepared for Taizong. After Wei's death, Taizong lamented: "With bronze as a mirror one can correct one's dress; with history as a mirror one can see the rise and fall of dynasties; with a man as a mirror one can see one's own errors. Now that Wei Zheng is gone, one of my mirrors is lost."
