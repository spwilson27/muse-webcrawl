Uniform

A uniform is a set of standard clothing worn by members of an organisation while participating in that organisation's activity. Uniforms promote a sense of identity and cohesion, signal the wearer's role and authority, and can serve practical and protective purposes.

Uses

Modern uniforms are worn by armed forces and paramilitary organisations such as police and emergency services; by workers in many service industries; by students in many school systems; by sports teams; and by members of some religious and civic organisations. Military uniforms distinguish friend from foe, display rank and unit insignia, and in modern combat forms provide camouflage. School uniforms are intended to reduce visible social differences among pupils, though their use remains debated.

History

Standardised dress for soldiers became common in European armies in the 17th and 18th centuries, when states issued soldiers with coats in regimental colours. Civil and occupational uniforms spread widely in the 19th and 20th centuries with the growth of large bureaucracies, railways, and public services.
