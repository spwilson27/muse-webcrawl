Regent Street

Regent Street is a major shopping street in the West End of London, England. Running from Langham Place in the north to Piccadilly Circus in the south, it is famous for its flagship stores, grand curved facades, and annual Christmas lights.

History

The street was designed by architect John Nash for the Prince Regent (later George IV) and built between 1811 and 1825 as a ceremonial and commercial route separating the fashionable squares of Mayfair from Soho. The original buildings were replaced in the late 19th and early 20th centuries by the present Portland stone facades, much of them designed by Sir Reginald Blomfield and others under the Crown Estate.

Retail and landmarks

Notable occupants have included Hamleys toy shop, Liberty, Apple, and Burberry. The street's Christmas lights, first installed in 1954, are a well-known seasonal tradition.
