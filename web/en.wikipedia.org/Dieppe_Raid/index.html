Dieppe Raid

Dieppe Raid (Operation Jubilee)
Date19 August 1942
LocationDieppe, Normandy, France
Allied forcec. 6,050 troops (mostly Canadian), 237 ships
ResultGerman victory; heavy Allied losses

The Dieppe Raid, codenamed Operation Jubilee, was an Allied amphibious attack on the German-occupied French port of Dieppe on 19 August 1942, during the Second World War. More than 6,000 infantry, predominantly Canadian, landed with naval and air support to seize and briefly hold the port, destroy coastal defences and installations, and gather intelligence. The raid ended in disaster: most objectives were not achieved and over half the landing force was killed, wounded or captured.

Landing craft approach the beaches at Dieppe, 19 August 1942.

The raid

Landings began before dawn across several beaches. Surprise was lost early — an encounter with a German convoy alerted defenders — and troops landing at Puys and Pourville were pinned down. On the main beach at Dieppe, infantry and Churchill tanks arrived late under intense fire; most tanks that landed could not cross the seawall or the chert beach. By early afternoon the surviving force was evacuated.

Aftermath and significance

Of the roughly 4,963 Canadians who embarked, 3,367 were killed, wounded or taken prisoner. The Royal Air Force lost over 100 aircraft, its heaviest single-day loss of the war. Historians debate the legacy: painful lessons about the need for overwhelming fire support, specialised armour, port logistics (leading to the Mulberry harbours) and intelligence shaped the planning of later landings in North Africa, Sicily and Normandy on D-Day.
