Congo Crisis

UN peacekeeping troops (ONUC) deployed during the Congo Crisis, one of the defining crises of decolonisation.

The Congo Crisis (French: Crise congolaise) was a period of political upheaval and conflict between 1960 and 1965 that began immediately after the Republic of the Congo gained independence from Belgium. The crisis began with a mutiny in the army, was compounded by the secession of the mineral-rich provinces of Katanga and South Kasai with Belgian support, and drew in the Cold War superpowers and a major United Nations peacekeeping operation (ONUC).

Background and outbreak

Congo became independent on 30 June 1960 with Joseph Kasa-Vubu as president and Patrice Lumumba as prime minister. Within days the Force Publique mutinied against its Belgian officers; Belgium intervened militarily, and on 11 July Moise Tshombe declared Katanga's secession, backed by Belgian mining interests. Lumumba appealed to the UN and then the Soviet Union, alarming the United States. In September, Colonel Joseph Mobutu seized power in a coup.

Key events

Lumumba was dismissed, arrested, and in January 1961 flown to Katanga and murdered, with Belgian and (as later inquiries found) American and British complicity alleged in plotting against him. UN Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld died in a plane crash in Northern Rhodesia in September 1961 while negotiating with Tshombe. UN forces ended the Katanga secession in January 1963. In 1964, the leftist Simba rebellion seized much of the east before being crushed with Belgian, American and mercenary assistance (including the Stanleyville hostage rescue, Operation Dragon Rouge). In November 1965 Mobutu seized power again, beginning his 32-year rule of what he renamed Zaire.

Legacy

The crisis became a symbol of Cold War interference in the decolonising world — a pattern of external intervention in resource-rich states that echoes earlier contests over trade and colonies going back to ancient Carthage. Estimates of deaths during the crisis reach around 100,000. Belgium formally apologised in 2002 for its role in Lumumba's death, and returned a relic of Lumumba to his family in 2022.
