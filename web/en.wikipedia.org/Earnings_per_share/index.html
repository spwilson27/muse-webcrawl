Earnings per share

Earnings per share (EPS) is the monetary value of earnings per outstanding share of common stock for a company. It is a key measure of corporate profitability and is commonly used to price stocks: the price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio divides the share price by EPS.

Calculation

Basic EPS equals net income minus preferred dividends, divided by the weighted-average number of common shares outstanding during the period. Diluted EPS adjusts the denominator for the dilutive effect of convertible securities, stock options and warrants, showing a more conservative figure. Continuing operations EPS excludes discontinued operations and extraordinary items, and is the variant most commonly reported in the financial press.

Standards

EPS reporting is governed by IAS 33 under International Financial Reporting Standards and by ASC 260 under US GAAP, both of which require listed companies to present basic and diluted EPS on the face of the income statement. Analysts often focus on "adjusted" or non-GAAP EPS, which strips out items management regards as non-recurring; this practice is both widely used and controversial, since it can flatter underlying performance.
