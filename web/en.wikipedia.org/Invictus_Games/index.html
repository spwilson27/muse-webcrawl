Invictus Games

The Invictus Games is an international multi-sport event for wounded, injured and sick servicemen and women, both serving and veterans. The Games use the power of sport to inspire recovery, support rehabilitation and generate a wider understanding and respect of all those who serve their country. The word "invictus" is Latin for "unconquered", and the event was founded by Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex, in 2014.

History

Prince Harry was inspired after attending the Warrior Games in the United States in 2013. The inaugural Invictus Games were held in London in September 2014, with over 400 competitors from 13 nations competing in nine sports at the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park.

Subsequent editions were held in Orlando (2016), Toronto (2017), and Sydney (2018). The Games scheduled for The Hague in 2020 were postponed by the COVID-19 pandemic and held in 2022, followed by Düsseldorf in 2023 and the first winter hybrid edition in Vancouver–Whistler in 2025. Birmingham was selected to host the 2027 Games.

Sports and governance

Sports have included athletics, archery, indoor rowing, powerlifting, road cycling, sitting volleyball, swimming, wheelchair basketball, and wheelchair rugby, with winter sports added in 2025. The Invictus Games Foundation oversees the selection of host cities and the development of the event, guided by the motto "I AM", drawn from William Ernest Henley's poem Invictus and its line "I am the master of my fate".
