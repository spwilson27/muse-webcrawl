Bland–Allison Act

Bland–Allison Act
Enacted: 28 February 1878
Sponsor: Rep. Richard P. Bland
Effect: Required Treasury silver purchases

The Bland–Allison Act of 1878 was an act of the United States Congress requiring the Treasury to buy between $2 million and $4 million of silver bullion each month and coin it into silver dollars. Congress passed it over President Rutherford B. Hayes's veto.

Background

The Coinage Act of 1873 had ended the minting of silver dollars, angering miners and debtors who favoured the inflationary effects of "free silver". Bland's original bill would have restored free coinage; Representative William B. Allison's amendment limited it to monthly purchases.

Legacy

The act led to the minting of the Morgan dollar. It was superseded by the Sherman Silver Purchase Act of 1890.
