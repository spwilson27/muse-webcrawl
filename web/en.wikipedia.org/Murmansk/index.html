Murmansk

Murmansk and its port on the Kola Bay

Murmansk (Russian: Мурманск) is a port city and the administrative centre of Murmansk Oblast, Russia, located in the northwest of the Kola Peninsula on the Kola Bay, an inlet of the Barents Sea. With a population of roughly 270,000, it is the largest city in the world located north of the Arctic Circle.

History

Murmansk was founded in 1916 as Romanov-on-Murman during the First World War, to serve as a year-round ice-free port — kept ice-free by the warm North Atlantic Current — for supplies shipped from Russia's allies. After the 1917 revolution it took its present name. During the Second World War, Murmansk was the destination of the Arctic convoys carrying Lend-Lease supplies to the Soviet Union, and the city withstood heavy bombing. Soviet leader Joseph Stalin awarded it the title Hero City in 1985.

Port and military significance

The port remains ice-free year-round and is a major fishing and shipping centre, as well as the home port of Russia's fleet of nuclear-powered icebreakers. Nearby Severomorsk hosts the headquarters of the Russian Northern Fleet.

Climate

Murmansk has a subarctic climate moderated by the sea. It experiences polar night from early December to mid-January and midnight sun in summer. The monumental Alyosha statue, commemorating the defenders of the Soviet Arctic, overlooks the city.
