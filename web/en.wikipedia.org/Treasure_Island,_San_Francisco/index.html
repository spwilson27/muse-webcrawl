Treasure Island is an artificial island in San Francisco Bay, in the U.S. state of California, connected by a short causeway to the natural Yerba Buena Island. It was built in 1936–37 of dredged fill as the site of the Golden Gate International Exposition of 1939–40, a world's fair celebrating the completion of the San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge and the Golden Gate Bridge, to which the island is attached midway along the Bay Bridge's route. It is named after the novel Treasure Island by Robert Louis Stevenson, who lived in San Francisco in 1879–80.

History

After the fair closed, the island was intended to become San Francisco's airport, but with the approach of the Second World War the U.S. Navy took it over in 1941 as Naval Station Treasure Island, a major departure and processing centre for the Pacific fleet — about 12,000 personnel passed through on a typical day at the war's peak. The Navy presence continued through the Cold War until the base closed in 1997 under the base realignment process.

Since closure the island has been transferred in stages to the City and County of San Francisco, which has pursued a long-term redevelopment plan for thousands of new homes, hotels, parks and commercial space, alongside Yerba Buena Island. The island is known for its panoramic views of the San Francisco skyline, a monthly flea market, wineries in former hangars, and the surviving fair-era Administration Building and hangars, which are listed historic structures. Radiation contamination left from naval activities, including radium and training materials, has been the subject of extensive remediation work and public controversy.

Geography

The flat, low-lying island sits at roughly sea level — its elevation averages only about 4 metres — making liquefaction in earthquakes and sea-level rise significant planning concerns addressed in the redevelopment's geotechnical improvements.
