Red deer

The red deer (Cervus elaphus) is one of the largest deer species. A male red deer is called a stag or hart, and a female is called a hind. The species inhabits most of Europe, the Caucasus, Anatolia, Iran and parts of western and central Asia, with a related population in North Africa.

Description

Stags stand about 1.2 m at the shoulder and bear large branching antlers that are shed and regrown each year. The coat is reddish-brown in summer, darkening in winter. During the autumn rut, stags roar and clash antlers to gather harems of hinds.

Distribution and introductions

Red deer have been introduced to New Zealand, Australia, Argentina and Chile, where they are farmed for venison and antler velvet and hunted as game. In their native range they shape woodland ecology through grazing and browsing, and are prey for wolves and lynx where those persist.
