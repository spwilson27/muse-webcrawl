Traffic law


A traffic light, a central instrument of traffic regulation

Traffic law (or road traffic law) is the body of law governing traffic and regulating vehicles, usually codified as traffic codes. It covers the rules of the road for drivers, cyclists and pedestrians, vehicle and driver licensing, registration, insurance, and penalties for violations.

Scope

Traffic codes typically specify which side of the road to drive on (right-hand or left-hand traffic), speed limits, priority rules at intersections, traffic signs and signals, overtaking rules, seat-belt and helmet requirements, and prohibitions on driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs and on using mobile phones while driving. Violations may be treated as civil infractions, misdemeanours, or serious criminal offences such as reckless or dangerous driving and vehicular homicide.

History and harmonisation

Early motor-vehicle laws appeared around the turn of the 20th century; Britain's Locomotive Acts of the 1860s famously required a man carrying a red flag to walk ahead of self-propelled vehicles. International harmonisation is promoted by the Vienna Convention on Road Traffic (1968) and the Vienna Convention on Road Signs and Signals. In the United States, traffic law is primarily state law, loosely harmonised by the Uniform Vehicle Code; in the United Kingdom the Highway Code and the Road Traffic Act 1988 are central.
