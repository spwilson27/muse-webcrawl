Elections in France

France holds elections at national, regional, departmental and municipal levels under the institutions of the Fifth Republic, founded in 1958. French citizens vote by universal suffrage from the age of 18. Most national elections use a two-round system: if no candidate wins an absolute majority in the first round, a run-off is held between the leading candidates.

National elections

The President of the Republic is elected directly for a five-year term (reduced from seven years in 2000) by two-round majority vote. Deputies to the National Assembly (577 seats) are elected in single-member constituencies, also in two rounds; candidates reaching 12.5% of registered voters advance to the second round, which can produce three- or four-way triangulaires. The Senate (348 seats) is elected indirectly by an electoral college of local officials for six-year terms, with half renewed every three years. France also elects deputies to the European Parliament by proportional representation.

Local elections

Municipal councils, departmental councils and regional councils are all elected for six-year terms. Municipal elections in larger communes combine majority and proportional elements. Regional and departmental elections are held concurrently. Referendums may be called by the President under Article 11 of the Constitution or on constitutional revision under Article 89.

Features of the system

Elections are traditionally held on Sundays. Campaign finance is tightly regulated, public funding is provided to parties, and official campaign broadcasts are allocated on public media. Voter turnout, once very high in presidential contests, has declined markedly in legislative and local elections in recent decades.
