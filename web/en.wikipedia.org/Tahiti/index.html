Tahiti

Location: Society Islands, French Polynesia

Capital: Papeete

Highest point: Mount Orohena, 2,241 m

Tahiti is the largest island of the Society Islands in French Polynesia in the South Pacific Ocean. Formed by two volcanic massifs joined at the isthmus of Taravao, it hosts the capital Papeete and most of French Polynesia's population.

History

Settled by Polynesian voyagers, Tahiti was first visited by Europeans in 1767–68 (Wallis, Bougainville, and Cook's voyages). The Pomare dynasty unified the island with European support. Missionaries such as John Williams spread Christianity from the late 1790s. France made Tahiti a protectorate in 1842 and annexed it in 1880.

Culture

Tahitian culture is famed for dance (ori Tahiti), tattooing, outrigger canoeing, and the paintings Paul Gauguin made there in the 1890s. Tourism, black pearls and copra are economic mainstays.

Geography

The island is mountainous and lush, ringed by a barrier reef and lagoon. Its climate is tropical, with a warm wet season from November to April.
