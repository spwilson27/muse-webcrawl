Marius Barnard

Born3 November 1927, Robertson, South Africa
Died14 November 2014, Cape Town
OccupationCardiac surgeon

Marius Stephanus Barnard (3 November 1927 – 14 November 2014) was a South African cardiac surgeon and inventor of critical illness insurance. He was a member of the team, headed by his younger brother Christiaan Barnard, that performed the world's first successful human-to-human heart transplant in 1967.

Medical career

Barnard studied medicine at the University of Cape Town and worked at Groote Schuur Hospital. In the December 1967 operation on Louis Washkansky, he was a key member of the surgical team alongside his brother. He also campaigned to bring heart surgery to patients in rural areas and abroad.

Critical illness insurance

Observing that patients often survived serious illness but were ruined financially by treatment costs and lost income, Barnard conceived "dread disease insurance" — a policy paying a lump sum on diagnosis of a specified serious condition. The first critical illness policy was launched in South Africa in 1983, and the product spread worldwide.

Politics

Barnard served as a Member of Parliament for the Progressive Federal Party from 1980 to 1989, where he opposed apartheid and advocated health reform. He received numerous honours for both his medical and humanitarian work.
