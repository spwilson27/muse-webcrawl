Environmental education

Outdoor field study is a central method of environmental education, connecting learners directly with natural environments.

Environmental education (EE) is a field of teaching and learning concerned with how natural environments function, how human beings interact with ecosystems, and how people can develop the knowledge, skills, values and motivation to address environmental challenges. It is taught in formal settings such as schools and universities and in non-formal settings such as nature centres, zoos, aquaria, parks and community programmes.

History

The roots of environmental education lie in the nature study movement of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, conservation education of the 1930s Dust Bowl era, and the modern environmental movement of the 1960s and 1970s. The 1975 Belgrade Charter, produced at a UNESCO-UNEP workshop, set out a global framework for environmental education, and the 1977 Tbilisi Intergovernmental Conference produced the widely used Tbilisi Declaration, which defined the field's goals in terms of awareness, knowledge, attitudes, skills and participation. The term was in use by the late 1940s, and bodies such as the IUCN helped develop it in the post-war period.

Goals and focus

Environmental education aims to develop an environmentally literate citizenry. Its components typically include awareness and sensitivity to the environment, knowledge and understanding of ecological and social systems, attitudes of concern, skills to identify and help resolve environmental issues, and participation in activities that lead to their resolution. Modern approaches often emphasise systems thinking, place-based education, climate change education and education for sustainable development.

Policy

In the United States, the National Environmental Education Act of 1990 established an Office of Environmental Education within the Environmental Protection Agency. UNESCO coordinates the global Education for Sustainable Development (ESD) agenda, and the UN Decade of Education for Sustainable Development ran from 2005 to 2014. Environmental education also appears in the UN Sustainable Development Goals, notably Target 4.7 on education for sustainable development and Target 13.3 on climate change education.
