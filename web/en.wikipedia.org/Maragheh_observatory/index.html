Maragheh observatory

The Maragheh observatory (Persian: رصدخانه مراغه) was an astronomical observatory founded in 1259 in Maragheh, in present-day northwestern Iran, under the patronage of the Ilkhanid ruler Hulagu and the direction of the Persian polymath Nasir al-Din al-Tusi. It is regarded as one of the most advanced scientific institutions of its time and a model for later observatories in the Islamic world and beyond.

History

After the Mongol conquest of Persia, Hulagu Khan commissioned al-Tusi to build an observatory for making precise astronomical tables. Construction began in 1259 on a hill west of Maragheh and took about twelve years. Al-Tusi assembled a distinguished team of astronomers and mathematicians from across the Islamic world and China, and the institution was supported as a religious endowment (waqf), which helped it survive for over half a century.

Work and legacy

The observatory housed large instruments, including a mural quadrant of four metres radius, armillary spheres and astrolabes, and a famous library said to hold some 400,000 volumes. Its principal achievement was the Zij-i Ilkhani (Ilkhanic Tables), a set of planetary tables based on twelve years of observations.

Mathematicians at Maragheh, including al-Tusi himself, developed new geometrical devices — most famously the "Tusi couple", which generates linear motion from two circular motions — to reform Ptolemaic planetary models. These techniques later appear in the work of Copernicus, and the observatory's organisational model influenced Ulugh Beg's observatory at Samarkand and, through it, observatories in Mughal India and Europe. The site fell into ruin by the early 14th century; a protective dome now covers the remains.
