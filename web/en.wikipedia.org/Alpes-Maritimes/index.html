Alpes-Maritimes

Alpes-Maritimes (French: [alp maʁitim]; Occitan: Aups Maritims) is a department of France located in the country's southeast corner, on the Mediterranean coast, in the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region. Its prefecture is Nice. The department takes its name from the Maritime Alps, the southwestern range of the Alps that forms its northern border with Italy.

Geography

The department stretches from the resorts of the French Riviera — including Cannes, Antibes, Menton, and Nice itself — to the high Alpine valleys of the Mercantour National Park, where peaks exceed 3,000 metres. It borders Italy to the east and the principality of Monaco is enclaved within its coastline. The Var river crosses the department west of Nice.

History

A first department of Alpes-Maritimes existed from 1793 to 1814, when the County of Nice belonged to revolutionary France. The modern department was created in 1860, when the Treaty of Turin ceded Nice and Savoy from the Kingdom of Sardinia to France, and was enlarged in 1947 with the communes of Tende and La Brigue transferred from Italy. The coastal area became one of Europe's earliest and most famous tourist regions, popularised by British and Russian aristocracy in the 19th century.

Economy

Tourism dominates the coastal economy, while inland the Sophia Antipolis technology park near Antibes is one of Europe's largest science parks. The department is also known for perfume production around Grasse.
