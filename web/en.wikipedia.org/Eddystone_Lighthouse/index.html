Eddystone Lighthouse

The Eddystone Lighthouse is a lighthouse on the dangerous Eddystone Rocks, about 9 miles (14 km) south of Rame Head in Cornwall, England, in the approaches to Plymouth Sound. The present tower, the fourth to occupy the site, was built by James Douglass and lit in 1882. Its predecessor, John Smeaton's tower of 1759, became the model for lighthouse design worldwide.

The fourth Eddystone Lighthouse, in service since 1882.

Winstanley's towers

The first lighthouse, an octagonal wooden structure designed by Henry Winstanley, was lit in 1698 and rebuilt in modified form in 1699. The Great Storm of 1703 destroyed it entirely, killing Winstanley and five other men. John Rudyard's replacement, a timber-and-stone tower completed in 1709, served until it burned down in 1755.

Smeaton's tower

John Smeaton's third lighthouse (1759) was a landmark of civil engineering: a tapered tower of interlocking granite blocks modelled on the trunk of an oak tree, using dovetail joints and hydraulic lime. It stood for over 120 years and was only taken down because the rock beneath it was eroding. The upper portion was re-erected on Plymouth Hoe as a memorial.

Douglass's tower

The current lighthouse, built beside Smeaton's foundations, is 49 metres tall and was automated in 1982. It is operated by Trinity House.
