Ukrainian (українська мова, ukrainska mova) is an East Slavic language of the Indo-European language family spoken primarily in Ukraine. It is the native language of a majority of Ukrainians, with roughly 40 million speakers, and serves as the country's sole official state language. Ukrainian is written in a variant of the Cyrillic script consisting of 33 letters.

Classification and history

Ukrainian descends from Old East Slavic, the language of Kievan Rus'. During the 17th and 18th centuries it developed distinct features under the influence of Polish and through the literary work of writers in the Hetmanate. The modern literary standard emerged in the late 18th century, notably with Ivan Kotliarevsky's Eneida (1798), the first published work entirely in vernacular Ukrainian. The language was suppressed at various times under the Russian Empire, including by the Valuev Circular (1863) and the Ems Ukaz (1876). After periods of Ukrainization in the 1920s and renewed Russification, Ukrainian's status strengthened following Ukrainian independence in 1991.

Phonology and grammar

Ukrainian has seven vowel sounds and a rich consonant system with palatalized consonants. Stress is free and mobile. The grammar retains seven cases for nouns, three genders, and a verbal system distinguishing perfective and imperfective aspect. Word order is relatively flexible because case endings carry much of the grammatical information.

Dialects

Ukrainian dialects are traditionally grouped into three clusters: Northern (Polissian), Southwestern, and Southeastern. The Southeastern dialects of the Kyiv–Poltava region form the basis of the modern standard language. Transitional varieties include Rusyn, sometimes considered a separate language, and Surzhyk, a mixed Ukrainian–Russian speech widespread in parts of the country.
