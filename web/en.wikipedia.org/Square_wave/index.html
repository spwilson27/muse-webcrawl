Square wave

A square wave is a non-sinusoidal periodic waveform in which the amplitude alternates at a steady frequency between fixed minimum and maximum values, with the same duration at minimum and maximum. In an ideal square wave, the transitions between minimum and maximum are instantaneous. Square waves are commonly encountered in electronics and signal processing.

Fourier analysis

An ideal square wave can be expressed as an infinite series of sine waves containing only odd harmonics of the fundamental frequency, with amplitudes falling off as 1/k for the k-th harmonic. When a square wave is synthesised with a finite number of harmonics, overshoot appears near the transitions — the Gibbs phenomenon.

Applications

Square waves are used as clock signals in digital circuits, as test signals for amplifiers, and in pulse-width modulation. A square wave with unequal high and low durations is more generally called a pulse wave, characterised by its duty cycle.
