Surface Transportation Board

The Surface Transportation Board (STB) is an independent federal agency of the United States government charged chiefly with economic regulation of freight railroads. It was established on January 1, 1996, by the ICC Termination Act of 1995, succeeding functions of the Interstate Commerce Commission.

Surface Transportation Board
Formed: January 1, 1996
Preceding agency: Interstate Commerce Commission
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Members: Five-member board appointed by the President

Responsibilities

The Board oversees railroad rates and service disputes, mergers and acquisitions of rail carriers, line constructions and abandonments, and certain matters involving trucking, pipelines, and intercity buses. Major rail mergers — such as the combination creating Canadian Pacific Kansas City — require its approval.
