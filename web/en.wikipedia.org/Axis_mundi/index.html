Axis mundi

In certain beliefs and philosophies, the axis mundi (Latin for "axis of the world") is the world center, or the connection between Heaven and Earth. It is a widespread symbol found in many cultures and religions, representing the point where the celestial and earthly realms meet and where communication between them becomes possible.

Forms

The axis mundi commonly takes the form of a natural object: a mountain (such as Mount Meru in Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist cosmology, or Mount Olympus in Greek tradition), a great tree (such as Yggdrasil in Norse mythology or the Tree of Life in many traditions), or a pillar. Human-made structures — temples, pyramids, ziggurats, minarets, church steeples, and totem poles — can serve the same function.

Function

The axis marks the sacred center of the world, the place of creation and the point from which the world is ordered and oriented. Religious rituals, prayers, and offerings performed at the axis are understood to reach the divine most directly. In shamanic traditions, the shaman journeys along the axis between the worlds.

In comparative religion

The concept was used extensively by the historian of religion Mircea Eliade, who treated the axis mundi as a defining feature of sacred space: the point at which a community establishes contact with the transcendent and founds its world.
