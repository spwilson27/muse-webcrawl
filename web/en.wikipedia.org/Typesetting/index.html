Typesetting

Hand composition of metal type in a composing stick
FieldTypography, printing
Key technologiesMovable type, Linotype, phototypesetting, digital typesetting

Typesetting is the composition of text by means of arranging physical type (in the form of individual letter blocks or sorts) or its digital equivalents. The set material is stored in type cases or in digital files, and reproduced by printing or display. Typesetting has been a central craft of publishing since the spread of movable type in the 15th century.

History

For centuries, compositors set type by hand, picking individual metal sorts from a type case and assembling them in a composing stick. The invention of hot metal typesetting machines — Ottmar Mergenthaler's Linotype (1884) and the Monotype system — allowed operators to cast whole lines or sorts from a keyboard, greatly speeding newspaper and book production. In the mid-20th century, phototypesetting replaced metal with photographic film, and from the 1980s digital typesetting and desktop publishing systems such as TeX, PageMaker, and later InDesign and web technologies made typesetting a software task.

Typesetting involves choices of typeface, point size, line length, leading, justification, hyphenation, and kerning, all of which affect readability and appearance.
