From today's featured article

[Image: Mount Everest, seen from the Tibetan Plateau — a snow-covered peak rising above brown foothills]

Mount Everest, known in Nepali as Sagarmatha and in Tibetan as Chomolungma, is Earth's highest mountain above sea level, located in the Mahalangur Himal sub-range of the Himalayas, on the border between Nepal and the Tibet Autonomous Region of China. Its elevation is most commonly given as 8,848.86 metres (29,031.7 ft), established by a joint survey by Nepal and China in 2020. The mountain was named in honour of George Everest, a former Surveyor General of India, although the surveyor who first established its height was Radhanath Sickdar. The first confirmed ascent was made on 29 May 1953 by Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay. Everest remains a major mountaineering objective, climbed via the southeast ridge from Nepal and the north ridge from Tibet, and is known both for its mountaineering history and for concerns about overcrowding, waste and climber safety in the so-called "death zone" above 8,000 metres.
