Catalepsy

Catalepsy is a nervous condition characterised by muscular rigidity and a fixed posture regardless of external stimuli, together with decreased sensitivity to pain. A person in a cataleptic state may hold a limb in whatever position it is placed — a feature called waxy flexibility when the limbs remain passively mouldable.

Causes

Catalepsy is a symptom associated with disorders of the nervous system, notably catatonia in schizophrenia and other psychiatric conditions, Parkinson's disease, and epilepsy. It can also occur as a side effect of antipsychotic medications and in cases of drug withdrawal or overdose.

History

The term derives from Greek words meaning "seizing" or "holding down". Catalepsy features prominently in 19th-century literature and medicine, where it was sometimes confused with death, contributing to fears of premature burial. Modern treatment addresses the underlying condition, with benzodiazepines and electroconvulsive therapy used for catatonia.
