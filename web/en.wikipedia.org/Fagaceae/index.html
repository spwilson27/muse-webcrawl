Fagaceae

Fagaceae
Kingdom: Plantae
Order: Fagales
Family: Fagaceae Dumort.
Genera: ~8, including Quercus, Fagus, Castanea, Lithocarpus

Fagaceae is a family of flowering plants that includes the beeches, oaks and chestnuts. The family comprises roughly 900–1,000 species of deciduous and evergreen trees and shrubs, mostly in the Northern Hemisphere, and is one of the most ecologically important groups of woody plants in temperate and subtropical forests. Its members are characterised by alternate simple leaves, unisexual flowers, and fruit in the form of nuts partially or wholly enclosed in a spiny or scaly cup called a cupule — the acorn cup of oaks being the familiar example.

Genera and distribution

The largest genera are the oaks (Quercus, about 430 species), the stone oaks (Lithocarpus, about 340 species, chiefly in Southeast Asia) and the chestnuts (Castanea). The beeches (Fagus) dominate temperate forests in Europe and eastern North America, while the southern beeches, once placed in Fagaceae, are now classified in their own family, Nothofagaceae. Fagaceae are dominant canopy trees in many forests of North America, Europe and East Asia, and their nuts (acorns, beechnuts, chestnuts) are crucial food for wildlife.

Uses

Oak and beech timber is valued for furniture, flooring, cooperage and construction; cork comes from the bark of the cork oak, Quercus suber. Sweet chestnuts are an important food crop, and acorns were historically used as famine food and pig forage. The family also has great horticultural importance.

Evolution

Fossils show the family was diverse by the Late Cretaceous and Paleogene, and molecular studies place Fagaceae in the order Fagales alongside the birch, walnut and southern-beech families.
