Facial expression

A facial expression is one or more motions or positions of the muscles beneath the skin of the face. According to one set of controversial theories, these movements convey the emotional state of an individual to observers. Facial expressions are a form of nonverbal communication.

Universality

Charles Darwin's The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals (1872) argued that facial expressions of emotion are universal and biologically innate. In the twentieth century, psychologist Paul Ekman's research proposed a set of basic emotions — including happiness, sadness, fear, anger, surprise, and disgust — each associated with a distinctive expression recognized across cultures, though the strength of this claim remains debated.

Measurement

The Facial Action Coding System (FACS), developed by Ekman and Wallace Friesen, describes facial movements in terms of individual "action units" of the underlying muscles.
