Respiratory system

The respiratory system is the biological system responsible for gas exchange in animals and plants. In humans it consists of the airways, lungs, and muscles of respiration, and its function is to deliver oxygen to the blood and remove carbon dioxide produced by cellular metabolism.

Anatomy in humans

Air enters through the nose and mouth, passing through the pharynx and larynx into the trachea. The trachea divides into two main bronchi, one to each lung, which branch repeatedly into bronchioles and terminate in clusters of alveoli. The conducting zone (nose to terminal bronchioles) conditions the air — warming, humidifying, and filtering it — while the respiratory zone (respiratory bronchioles and alveoli) performs gas exchange.

The lungs sit in the thoracic cavity, each enclosed by the pleural membranes. The diaphragm and intercostal muscles power ventilation.

Physiology

Ventilation moves air in and out; external respiration exchanges gases between alveoli and pulmonary capillaries; the blood transports oxygen largely bound to haemoglobin and carbon dioxide largely as bicarbonate; internal respiration exchanges gases between systemic capillaries and tissues. Cellular respiration then uses oxygen to produce ATP.

Other animals

Birds possess air sacs and a flow-through lung that supports unidirectional airflow and highly efficient exchange. Fish use gills with countercurrent exchange; insects deliver air directly to tissues through a tracheal network opening at spiracles; plants exchange gases through stomata in their leaves.

Diseases

Common diseases include asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), pneumonia, tuberculosis, and lung cancer.
