Duduk

The duduk is an ancient double-reed woodwind instrument made of apricot wood, indigenous to Armenia and widely played across the Caucasus and neighbouring regions. It is known for its warm, soft, slightly nasal timbre and its expressive, melancholic sound.

Characteristics

The duduk has a wide double reed and a long cylindrical body with finger holes. Players often perform in pairs: one holds a continuous drone using circular breathing while the other plays the melody. Its range is about one octave, extended by technique.

Recognition

In 2005 UNESCO proclaimed the duduk and its music a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity. The instrument gained international prominence through performers such as Djivan Gasparyan and through film soundtracks.
