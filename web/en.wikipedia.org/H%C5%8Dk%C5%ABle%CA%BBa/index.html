Hōkūleʻa

Hōkūleʻa is a performance-accurate waʻa kaulua (double-hulled voyaging canoe) built by the Polynesian Voyaging Society and launched in Hawaiʻi in 1975. Her name means "star of gladness" and refers to Arcturus, the zenith star of Hawaiʻi. Sailing without modern instruments, Hōkūleʻa demonstrated the feasibility of the deliberate two-way voyaging by which Polynesians settled the Pacific, and became a central symbol of the Hawaiian Renaissance.

Construction and first voyages

The canoe was designed from historical descriptions and rock art by artist and historian Herb Kawainui Kāne and built with modern materials — fibreglass hulls and plywood — on traditional lines, with two crab-claw sails. In 1976 she sailed from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti, navigated without instruments by Mau Piailug, a master navigator from Satawal in Micronesia, whose knowledge proved that wayfinding by stars, swells, and birds could guide such voyages. A 1978 attempt ended in capsizing south of Molokaʻi, in which crew member Eddie Aikau was lost paddling for help; the canoe was redesigned and continued voyaging.

Later voyages

Hōkūleʻa subsequently sailed throughout the Polynesian Triangle, including voyages to Aotearoa (New Zealand) and Rapa Nui, training a new generation of Hawaiian navigators, most prominently Nainoa Thompson. Between 2014 and 2017 she completed the Mālama Honua Worldwide Voyage, circumnavigating the globe to promote sustainability, followed by the Moananuiākea voyage around the Pacific begun in 2023.
