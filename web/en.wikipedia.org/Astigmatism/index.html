Astigmatism


Astigmatism causes blurred vision at all distances.

Astigmatism is a type of refractive error in which the eye does not focus light evenly on the retina, because of an irregular curvature of the cornea or lens. This results in blurred or distorted vision at all distances. Other symptoms can include eyestrain, headaches and difficulty driving at night.

Causes

Most astigmatism is regular corneal astigmatism, present from birth and possibly hereditary, in which the cornea is curved more steeply in one meridian than the perpendicular one, like a rugby ball rather than a football. Irregular astigmatism can result from scarring, injury or keratoconus. The condition often occurs together with near-sightedness or far-sightedness.

Diagnosis and treatment

Astigmatism is diagnosed by an eye examination including refraction and keratometry. It is corrected with cylindrical lenses in glasses or contact lenses, or by refractive surgery such as LASIK, which reshapes the cornea. Toric intraocular lenses can correct it during cataract surgery.

In optics

The same term names an off-axis aberration in optical systems, in which rays in two perpendicular planes focus at different distances; lenses exhibiting it are called anastigmats when corrected.
