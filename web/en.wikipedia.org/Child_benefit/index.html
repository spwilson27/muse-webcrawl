Child benefit

Child benefit or family allowance is a social security payment made to parents or guardians of children, designed to help with the costs of raising children. Schemes exist in most developed countries, varying in whether they are universal or means-tested and in the amounts paid by the age of the child.

History

Family allowances developed in early 20th-century Europe; France's system grew out of employer funds in the 1930s, and the United Kingdom introduced a Family Allowances Act in 1945 following the Beveridge Report. The UK's payment, renamed Child Benefit in 1977, was paid to the mother and credited with reducing child poverty.

Models

Countries such as Germany (Kindergeld), Canada (Canada Child Benefit), and Australia operate substantial programs. Debates over child benefit typically concern universality versus targeting, effects on poverty, and, in some countries, links between benefits and population policy.
