Radiopharmaceutical

A radiopharmaceutical, or medicinal radiocompound, is a drug that contains a radioactive isotope and is used in nuclear medicine for diagnosis or therapy. Radiopharmaceuticals combine a biologically active molecule — which determines where the drug goes in the body — with a radionuclide whose emissions can be detected externally (for diagnosis) or deliver a therapeutic dose of radiation to diseased tissue (for therapy). Most diagnostic radiopharmaceuticals are used in very small ("tracer") amounts with negligible pharmacological effect.

Diagnostic use

Diagnostic agents emit gamma rays or positrons that can be imaged with a gamma camera, SPECT scanner, or PET scanner. Because the radionuclides involved usually have short half-lives, the compounds are often prepared in hospital radiopharmacies or produced on site with generators and cyclotrons, and must be administered quickly.

Therapeutic use

Therapeutic radiopharmaceuticals deliver cytotoxic radiation — typically beta particles or, increasingly, alpha particles — to target tissue. Examples include iodine-131 for thyroid cancer and hyperthyroidism, radium-223 dichloride for bone metastases in prostate cancer, lutetium-177 DOTATATE (Lutathera) for neuroendocrine tumors, lutetium-177 PSMA for prostate cancer, and yttrium-90 microspheres for liver tumors.

Production and regulation

Short-lived radionuclides are produced by cyclotrons or eluted from radionuclide generators (such as the molybdenum-99/technetium-99m generator). Radiopharmaceuticals are regulated as drugs but are also subject to radiation-safety rules; preparation typically follows radiopharmacy practice standards, and doses are prescribed in activity units (becquerels or curies) rather than mass.

Theranostics

The pairing of a diagnostic and a therapeutic agent targeting the same molecule — for example, gallium-68 and lutetium-177 versions of a PSMA or somatostatin-receptor ligand — is known as theranostics, an approach that allows clinicians to image a target before treating it.
