ETH Zurich (German: Eidgenössische Technische Hochschule Zürich; English: Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Zurich) is a public research university in Zurich, Switzerland. Founded in 1855 as the Swiss Federal Polytechnic School, it is one of two Swiss federal institutes of technology and is consistently ranked among the world's leading universities, particularly in engineering and the natural sciences.

History

ETH Zurich was created by the Swiss federal government to train engineers and scientists for the young federal state. Its monumental main building, designed by Gottfried Semper, overlooks the city from the Zürichberg. The institution received the right to award doctorates in 1909 and adopted its present name in 1911.

Academics

The university comprises 16 departments covering architecture, civil and mechanical engineering, computer science, mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, earth sciences, management and the humanities. It has two main campuses: Zentrum in central Zurich and Hönggerberg, which houses science departments and research facilities such as the Swiss National Supercomputing Centre's partner installations. More than 20 Nobel laureates are associated with ETH Zurich, including Albert Einstein, who studied and later taught there. Other famous alumni include Wilhelm Röntgen and the architect Max Frisch.
