Ottoman wars in Europe

The Ottoman wars in Europe were a series of military conflicts between the Ottoman Empire and various European states from the late 14th century to the early 20th century. Beginning with campaigns in the Balkans, the Ottoman Empire expanded at the expense of the Byzantine Empire, Serbia, Bulgaria, Hungary, and the Venetian and Habsburg possessions, and at its height threatened Vienna in 1529 and again in 1683.

Expansion

Ottoman forces crossed into Europe in the 1350s, taking Gallipoli and Adrianople (Edirne). Victories at the Maritsa (1371), Kosovo (1389), and Nicopolis (1396) secured most of the Balkans. After a setback at the hands of Timur in 1402, the empire recovered; Varna (1444) defeated a crusading army, and in 1453 Sultan Mehmed II conquered Constantinople. Belgrade fell in 1521, Hungary was crushed at Mohács in 1526, and much of it was annexed. Ottoman armies besieged Vienna in 1529 and Malta in 1565 and were checked at sea at Lepanto in 1571.

Turning point and decline

The failed second siege of Vienna in 1683 and the defeat at Zenta (1697) led to the Treaty of Karlowitz (1699), the first major Ottoman territorial retreat in Europe. Through the 18th and 19th centuries, a series of Russo-Turkish wars, Greek independence (1821–1832), and the Balkan Wars of 1912–13 stripped the empire of nearly all its European territory, leaving only Eastern Thrace — roughly the empire's European remnant in modern Turkey — by 1913.
