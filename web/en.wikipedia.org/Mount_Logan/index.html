Mount Logan
Elevation5,959 m (19,551 ft)
LocationYukon, Canada
RangeSaint Elias Mountains

Mount Logan is the highest mountain in Canada and the second-highest peak in North America after Denali. It stands in Kluane National Park and Reserve in southwestern Yukon and is named after Sir William Edmond Logan, the founder of the Geological Survey of Canada.

Geography

Mount Logan has one of the largest base circumferences of any non-volcanic mountain on Earth and an enormous summit plateau some 25 km long. Because of active tectonic uplift, the mountain continues to grow in height. It is a source of the Hubbard and Logan glaciers.

Climate

The massif generates its own severe weather; temperatures of −77.5 °C have been recorded on the summit plateau, among the coldest measured in the Northern Hemisphere outside Greenland.

Climbing

The mountain was first climbed in 1925 by an international expedition led by A. H. MacCarthy after an epic approach. The standard King Trench route is not technically difficult but is long, remote and exposed to extreme cold and storms.
