The Aztec Empire, or the Triple Alliance (Classical Nahuatl: Ēxcān Tlahtōlōyān, "the three places where orders are given"), began as an alliance of three Aztec city-states: Tenochtitlan, Texcoco, and the smallest, Tlacopan. These three city-states ruled the Valley of Mexico and surroundings from 1428 to 1521, when the combined forces of the Spanish conquistadores and their native allies who had sided against the Aztecs decisively defeated them.

Formation and expansion

The alliance was formed after Tenochtitlan and Texcoco overthrew the Tepanec empire of Azcapotzalco. Under emperors (tlatoque) such as Itzcoatl, Moctezuma I, Axayacatl, Ahuitzotl and Moctezuma II, the alliance expanded by conquest to cover much of central Mexico, from the Gulf Coast to the Pacific, though some peoples — notably Tlaxcala and the Purépecha (Tarascan) state — successfully resisted.

Administration and tribute

Unlike many empires, the Aztec Empire generally did not administer conquered territory directly. Local rulers were left in place provided they paid regular tribute in goods — maize, beans, cloaks, feathers, cacao, jade, gold and warrior costumes — recorded in tribute registers such as the Codex Mendoza. Trading and tribute networks linked the empire's provinces to the markets of Tenochtitlan, a city of perhaps 200,000 inhabitants, larger than London at the time.

Fall

The arrival of Hernán Cortés in 1519 began the Spanish conquest. After the death of Moctezuma II, the massacre during the Toxcatl festival, the Spanish retreat of La Noche Triste, and a smallpox epidemic that devastated the population, Tenochtitlan fell on 13 August 1521 following a siege of several months.
