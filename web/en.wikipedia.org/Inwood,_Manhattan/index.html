Inwood is the northernmost neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City, bounded by the Hudson River to the west, the Harlem River and Spuyten Duyvil Creek to the north and east, and Dyckman Street and Fort George Hill to the south. It is part of Manhattan Community District 12, together with Washington Heights.

Geography and landmarks

Inwood Hill Park contains the last natural forest and salt marsh on Manhattan Island, with caves once used by the Lenape and views over the Hudson. According to legend, the area near the park is where Peter Minuit purchased Manhattan in 1626. The Dyckman House, built about 1784, is Manhattan's last Dutch farmhouse. Baker Athletics Complex of Columbia University, including Robert K. Kraft Field at Lawrence A. Wien Stadium, occupies the northern waterfront.

History and community

Long farmland after Dutch and English colonisation, Inwood developed rapidly after the subway reached it in the early 20th century. It has had large Irish, Jewish, Greek, and — since the 1960s — Dominican communities; today it is known for its Dominican culture along Dyckman Street and Broadway, its pre-war apartment buildings, and relatively low housing costs for Manhattan.

Transportation

The neighbourhood is served by the A and 1 trains of the New York City Subway, the Metro-North Hudson Line at Marble Hill (across the river), and several bus routes; the Henry Hudson Parkway runs along its western edge.
