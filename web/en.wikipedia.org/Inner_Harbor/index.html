Inner Harbor

LocationBaltimore, Maryland, United States
Water bodyPatapsco River (Northwest Branch)
Known forWaterfront redevelopment, museums, aquarium, historic ships

The Inner Harbor is a historic seaport and tourist attraction in Baltimore, Maryland, located at the mouth of Jones Falls on the Northwest Branch of the Patapsco River. Once a working port of piers, warehouses, and shipyards, it was transformed in the second half of the 20th century into a celebrated model of urban waterfront redevelopment, lined with museums, shops, restaurants, and historic ships.

History

Baltimore's port grew up around the basin in the 18th and 19th centuries, serving the tobacco, grain, and clipper-ship trades — the frigate USS Constellation, launched in 1797, is associated with the harbour. As shipping moved to larger facilities downriver in the early 20th century, the inner piers declined into dereliction. Beginning in the 1960s, under mayors including William Donald Schaefer, the city cleared the old wharves, creating parkland (Rash Field), the Maryland Science Center (1976), the festival-marketplace Harborplace pavilions (1980), and the National Aquarium (1981), which together drew millions of visitors annually.

Attractions

Today the Inner Harbor hosts the National Aquarium, the Maryland Science Center, the American Visionary Art Museum nearby, and a fleet of museum ships including USS Constellation, the submarine USS Torsk, the lightship Chesapeake, and the Coast Guard cutter USCGC Taney. The World Trade Center Baltimore (1977), designed by I. M. Pei's firm, rises over the east shore. The area remains central to Baltimore's identity and to debates about the festival-marketplace model of downtown revival.
