Ralph Nader

Ralph Nader (born February 27, 1934) is an American lawyer, author and political activist known for his work in consumer protection, environmentalism and government reform. His 1965 book Unsafe at Any Speed, a critique of the American automobile industry's safety record, helped spur the creation of federal auto-safety regulation. Nader founded numerous public-interest organisations and ran for President of the United States several times, most prominently as the Green Party candidate in 2000.

Early life and Unsafe at Any Speed

Nader was born in Winsted, Connecticut, to Lebanese immigrant parents. He graduated from Princeton University and Harvard Law School. While practising law and writing, he investigated automobile safety, and Unsafe at Any Speed attacked manufacturers — particularly General Motors and the Chevrolet Corvair — for resisting safety features. GM's attempt to investigate and discredit him backfired: its president apologised before Congress, and Nader won an invasion-of-privacy settlement, using the proceeds to fund further advocacy. The scandal helped pass the National Traffic and Motor Vehicle Safety Act of 1966.

Nader's Raiders and organisations

From the late 1960s, Nader recruited young lawyers and students — dubbed "Nader's Raiders" — to investigate federal agencies and industries, producing influential reports. Organisations he founded or inspired include Public Citizen, the Center for Auto Safety, the US Public Interest Research Group (PIRG), Clean Water Action and the Center for Study of Responsive Law. His campaigns contributed to legislation on clean air and water, freedom of information, occupational safety, and the creation of agencies such as the EPA, OSHA and the Consumer Product Safety Commission.

Presidential campaigns

Nader ran for president in 1996, 2000, 2004 and 2008. His 2000 Green Party campaign won 2.74 percent of the popular vote; in Florida his vote total exceeded George W. Bush's margin over Al Gore, leading some Democrats to blame him as a "spoiler" — a charge he disputes. He continued campaigns as an independent advocating third-party access and corporate accountability.

Later work

Nader has written many books, including Crashing the Party, The Seventeen Solutions and Unstoppable, and founded the American Museum of Tort Law in Winsted in 2015.
