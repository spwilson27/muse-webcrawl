Bartholomew I (born Dimitrios Arhondonis, 29 February 1940) is the 270th Archbishop of Constantinople and Ecumenical Patriarch, a position he has held since 1991. As Ecumenical Patriarch he is regarded as the spiritual leader and primus inter pares (first among equals) of the Eastern Orthodox Church worldwide.

Early life and education

Bartholomew was born on the island of Imbros (now Gökçeada, Turkey) into the island's Greek community. He studied theology at the Patriarchal Theological School of Halki, which was closed by Turkish authorities in 1971, and later pursued postgraduate studies in canon law in Rome, Munich, and at the Ecumenical Institute of Bossey in Switzerland. He is fluent in numerous languages, including Greek, Turkish, Latin, Italian, French, English, and German.

Patriarchate

Elected Ecumenical Patriarch in October 1991 following the death of Patriarch Demetrios, Bartholomew has emphasized interfaith dialogue, reconciliation with the Roman Catholic Church, and the unity of the Orthodox churches. He convened the Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church in Crete in 2016, the first such council in centuries. In 2019 he granted autocephaly to the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, a decision that deepened tensions with the Moscow Patriarchate.

Environmental advocacy

Bartholomew is widely known as the "Green Patriarch" for his pioneering advocacy on environmental protection, having declared harm to the environment a sin and convened a series of symposia on religion, science, and the environment focusing on the world's seas and rivers.
