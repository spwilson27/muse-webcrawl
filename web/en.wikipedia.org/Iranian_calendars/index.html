Iranian calendars

The Iranian calendars or Iranian chronology (Persian: گاه‌شماری ایرانی) are a succession of calendars created and used for over two millennia in Iran, also known as Persia. One of the longest chronological records in human history, the Iranian calendar has been modified many times for political, religious, and administrative purposes.

Solar Hijri calendar

The current official calendar of Iran is the Solar Hijri calendar, a solar calendar whose epoch is the Hijra of Muhammad in 622 CE and whose year begins at the moment of the March equinox, celebrated as Nowruz. It consists of twelve months, the first six of 31 days, the next five of 30 days, and the last of 29 or 30 days. Its leap-year rule, based on astronomical observation at Tehran, makes it among the most accurate solar calendars in use.

Historical calendars

Earlier systems included the Zoroastrian calendar, a 365-day calendar of twelve 30-day months plus five epagomenal days, and the Jalali calendar, reformed in 1079 under Sultan Jalal al-Din Malik Shah by a committee of astronomers including Omar Khayyam.
