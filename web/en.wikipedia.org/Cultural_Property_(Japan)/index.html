Cultural Property (Japan)

Himeji Castle in Hyōgo Prefecture, designated a National Treasure and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Cultural Properties (文化財, bunkazai) are the tangible and intangible products of Japanese culture that are officially registered, designated, selected or recognized by the Japanese government under the Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties, first enacted in 1950. The system is administered by the Agency for Cultural Affairs and is designed to protect artefacts, buildings, works of art, performing arts, skills, landscapes and archaeological remains of high historical, artistic or academic value.

Categories

Cultural Properties are broadly divided into:

  • Tangible Cultural Properties — buildings, paintings, sculptures, crafts, books, documents and archaeological materials. Those of especially high value may be designated Important Cultural Properties, and the most outstanding as National Treasures.
  • Intangible Cultural Properties — drama, music, dance and craft techniques, such as Noh theatre, kabuki and traditional pottery skills. Master practitioners may be recognized individually or collectively; individual holders are popularly known as Living National Treasures.
  • Folk Cultural Properties — customs, manners, folk performing arts, clothing, utensils and houses connected with everyday life.
  • Monuments — historic sites, places of scenic beauty and natural monuments.
  • Cultural Landscapes and Groups of Traditional Buildings (Preservation Districts).
  • Buried Cultural Properties — archaeological deposits in the ground.

History

Protection of cultural heritage in Japan began with the Ancient Shrines and Temples Preservation Law of 1897. The modern framework dates to 1950, when the Law for the Protection of Cultural Properties was passed after a fire in January 1949 damaged the celebrated Kondō and wall paintings of Hōryū-ji, one of Japan's oldest temples. The law has been amended many times, adding categories for intangible folk culture (1954), preservation districts (1975) and cultural landscapes (2004).
