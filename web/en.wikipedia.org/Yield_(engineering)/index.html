Yield (engineering)

Yield point
DefinitionStress at which a material begins to deform plastically
Symbolσy
SI unitMegapascal (MPa)

In materials science and engineering, the yield point is the point on a stress–strain curve that indicates the limit of elastic behaviour and the beginning of plastic (permanent) deformation. Below the yield point, a material deforms elastically and returns to its original shape when the load is removed; above it, some deformation remains after unloading.

Definitions

Some materials, notably mild steel, show a distinct upper and lower yield point, with a drop in stress and the propagation of Lüders bands. Most metals, however, yield gradually, so the yield strength is conventionally defined by the 0.2% offset method: the stress at which the stress–strain curve departs from the elastic line by a plastic strain of 0.002. This value is also called the proof stress.

Yield criteria

For multiaxial stress states, yielding is predicted by criteria such as the von Mises yield criterion (based on distortional strain energy) and the Tresca criterion (based on maximum shear stress). At the microscopic level, yielding in crystalline metals occurs by the motion of dislocations; strengthening mechanisms — grain refinement, solid-solution alloying, precipitation, and work hardening — raise the yield strength by impeding dislocation motion.
