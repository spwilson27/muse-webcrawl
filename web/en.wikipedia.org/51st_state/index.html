51st state

"51st state" is a phrase referring to possible additional U.S. states beyond the current fifty. Since Hawaii's admission in 1959, various territories and jurisdictions have been discussed as candidates, most prominently the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico. The phrase is also used internationally, sometimes pejoratively, to describe countries or regions perceived as heavily influenced by the United States.

District of Columbia

Residents of Washington, D.C., lack voting representation in Congress despite a population larger than some states. Statehood bills proposing admission of a smaller federal district under names such as the "State of Washington, Douglass Commonwealth" have passed the House of Representatives but stalled in the Senate. A 2016 referendum saw D.C. voters overwhelmingly endorse statehood.

Puerto Rico

Puerto Rico, a U.S. territory since 1898, has held several status referendums. In the 2020 referendum, a slim majority voted in favor of statehood, though turnout and the non-binding nature of the vote left the question unresolved.

Other proposals

Other historical and speculative candidates have included Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, the division of existing states such as California, and even — in jest or in rhetoric — Canada, whose hypothetical annexation featured in political discourse in 2024–2025.
