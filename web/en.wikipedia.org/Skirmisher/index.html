Skirmisher

[Image: Nineteenth-century light infantry skirmishers firing from behind cover ahead of the main line]
Skirmisher
RoleLight infantry
FormationLoose, extended order (skirmish line)
TasksScreening, harassment, reconnaissance, sniping
Historical examplesPeltasts; velites; voltigeurs; Jäger; tirailleurs; riflemen

Skirmishers are light infantry or cavalry soldiers deployed in a loose formation — a skirmish line — in front of, or on the flanks of, the main body of an army. Their role is to screen the main force, harass and delay the enemy, probe for weaknesses, engage in reconnaissance and protect against surprise attack. Unlike troops of the line, skirmishers fight in open order, make use of cover and terrain, and aim their fire at individual targets.

History

Light troops have existed since antiquity: Greek peltasts and Roman velites screened heavier hoplites and legionaries, throwing javelins before retiring behind the main line. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, specialised light infantry, riflemen and German and Austrian Jäger units became standard in European armies. The French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars saw extensive use of skirmishers (tirailleurs, voltigeurs), who could disrupt enemy formations with aimed fire before a column attack.

During the American Civil War and the wars of the later nineteenth century, improved rifled firearms greatly increased the value of dispersed, aimed fire; sharpshooter units such as Berdan's Sharpshooters played prominent roles. By the First World War the distinction between skirmishers and ordinary infantry largely dissolved as all infantry adopted open-order tactics, though the word survives in the general sense of small-unit firefights.

Characteristics

Skirmishers typically operate in pairs or small groups with wide intervals, advancing by rushes or infiltration and relying on initiative rather than rigid drill. Historically they were often armed with longer-ranged or more accurate weapons — notably rifles, while line infantry carried smoothbore muskets — and were selected for marksmanship and fieldcraft.
