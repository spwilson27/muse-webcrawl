Speyside single malt

Speyside single malts are single malt Scotch whiskies distilled in Strathspey, the area around the River Spey in Moray and Badenoch and Strathspey in northeastern Scotland. Speyside has the greatest number of distilleries of any whisky-producing region in Scotland — more than sixty, roughly half of the national total.

Character

Speyside malts are generally sweet and fruity, ranging from the light, grassy style of distilleries such as Glen Grant to rich, heavily sherried whiskies such as those of The Macallan, Glenfarclas and Aberlour. Little or no peat smoke is used in most of the region's whiskies, though there are exceptions such as Benromach and Benriach.

Notable distilleries

The best-selling single malts in the world, Glenlivet and Glenfiddich, are both Speysides. Other famous distilleries include Balvenie, Cardhu, Cragganmore, Glen Moray, Glenrothes, Mortlach and Strathisla, the oldest continuously operating distillery in the Highlands, founded in 1786.

Legal status

Under the Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009, "Speyside" is a protected locality designation; a single malt may only bear the name if it is distilled in the defined area. The region's abundance of distilleries is historically attributed to pure water supplies, local barley, and a railway network that made transport easy — as well as a tradition of illicit distilling in remote glens.
