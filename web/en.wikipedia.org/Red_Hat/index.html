Red Hat, Inc. is an American multinational software company that provides open source software products to enterprises. Founded in 1993, the company is best known for Red Hat Enterprise Linux (RHEL), a commercial Linux distribution sold on a subscription model. Red Hat was acquired by IBM in 2019 for approximately US$34 billion, the largest software acquisition in history at the time, and operates as an independent subsidiary.

History

Red Hat was formed in 1995 from the merger of Bob Young's ACC Corporation and Marc Ewing's Red Hat Software, named after the red fedora Ewing had worn at Carnegie Mellon University. The company's initial public offering in August 1999 was one of the most successful of the dot-com era. Red Hat built its business on packaging open source software with support, training and certification, pioneering the subscription model for enterprise Linux.

In 2003, Red Hat discontinued its consumer-oriented Red Hat Linux line in favour of Red Hat Enterprise Linux for businesses and endorsed the community-developed Fedora project for home use. The company expanded into middleware with the acquisition of JBoss in 2006, virtualization, and later container technology with OpenShift, its Kubernetes-based platform, and automation with Ansible, acquired in 2015.

IBM acquisition

IBM announced its acquisition of Red Hat on 28 October 2018, valuing the company at about $34 billion. The deal closed on 9 July 2019. Red Hat retained its headquarters in Raleigh, North Carolina, and its leadership, and IBM positioned the purchase as central to its hybrid cloud strategy, combining Red Hat's OpenShift platform with IBM's cloud services.

Products

Red Hat's principal products include Red Hat Enterprise Linux, Red Hat OpenShift, Red Hat Ansible Automation Platform, Red Hat OpenStack Platform and a range of middleware derived from JBoss. The company is one of the largest corporate contributors to the Linux kernel and other open source projects.
