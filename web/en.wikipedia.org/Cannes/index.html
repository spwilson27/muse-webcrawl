Cannes (French: [kan]; Occitan: Canas) is a city located on the French Riviera in the Alpes-Maritimes department of southeastern France. It is a busy tourist destination, famous for hosting the annual Cannes Film Festival, Midem, and Cannes Lions International Festival of Creativity. The city is known for its association with the rich and famous, its luxury hotels and restaurants, and for conferences and festivals.

History

The area was settled by Ligurians and later by the Romans, who established a post at Le Suquet, the old town hill. In the Middle Ages, Cannes was a small fishing and agricultural village watched over by monks of Lérins Abbey on the offshore islands. The town's transformation began in 1834 when the former British Chancellor Lord Brougham, forced to stop there by a cholera outbreak in Nice, built a villa and promoted Cannes as a health resort for the European aristocracy.

The building of the railway, grand hotels along the seafront boulevard La Croisette, and winter visits by royalty made Cannes a fashionable resort. The first film festival was held in 1946 after a pre-war edition was cancelled by the outbreak of World War II.

Landmarks and culture

The Promenade de la Croisette curves along the bay past Belle Époque palaces such as the Carlton and Martinez hotels. Le Suquet preserves medieval streets and a 14th-century watchtower. Ferries run to the Lérins Islands: Île Sainte-Marguerite, where the "Man in the Iron Mask" was imprisoned, and Île Saint-Honorat, home to Cistercian monks who produce wine and liqueurs.

Economy

Tourism and conferences dominate the economy through the Palais des Festivals et des Congrès. Beyond the film festival, Cannes hosts MIPTV and MIPIM trade fairs, the Cannes Lions advertising festival, and the Cannes Yachting Festival. The nearby Sophia Antipolis technology park adds a high-tech sector.
