Rangeland

Rangelands are grasslands, shrublands, woodlands, wetlands, and similar landscapes where the native vegetation is predominantly grasses, grass-like plants, forbs, or shrubs and which are managed as natural ecosystems. They include prairies, steppes, savannas, tundra, desert shrublands, and mountain meadows, and cover roughly 40–50% of Earth's land surface.

Use and management

Rangelands provide forage for livestock grazing and wildlife habitat, store carbon, protect watersheds, and support recreation. Rangeland management is the professional field concerned with balancing grazing, fire, invasive species, and conservation on these lands. Threats include overgrazing, woody-plant encroachment, fragmentation, desertification, and climate change.
