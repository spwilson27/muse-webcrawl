The Battles of Saratoga (19 September and 7 October 1777) marked the climax of the Saratoga campaign of the American Revolutionary War and are widely regarded as a turning point of the war. The surrender of General John Burgoyne's British army convinced France to enter the war openly as an ally of the United States.

Background

Burgoyne's plan was to advance from Canada down the Hudson valley to Albany, cutting New England off from the other colonies. After early successes at Ticonderoga, his army was slowed by supply problems, the loss of its foraging expedition at Bennington, and growing American militia forces. General Horatio Gates took a strong fortified position at Bemis Heights, designed by Tadeusz Kościuszko, blocking the road south.

The battles

At Freeman's Farm (19 September), Burgoyne's advancing columns clashed with American riflemen under Daniel Morgan and troops led aggressively by Benedict Arnold; the British held the field but suffered heavy losses. At Bemis Heights (7 October), a British reconnaissance-in-force was repulsed, Arnold — though relieved of command — rode into the fighting and led the assault that carried the Breymann redoubt before being wounded in the leg. Burgoyne retreated to Saratoga, was surrounded, and on 17 October surrendered some 6,000 troops under the Convention of Saratoga.

Significance

The victory demonstrated that the Continental Army could defeat a major British force, and it directly precipitated the Franco-American alliance of February 1778, transforming the war into a global conflict. Saratoga is frequently ranked among the most decisive battles in history.
