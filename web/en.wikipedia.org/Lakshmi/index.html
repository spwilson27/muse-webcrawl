Lakshmi (Sanskrit: लक्ष्मी, Lakṣmī), also known as Shri, is one of the principal goddesses in Hinduism, revered as the goddess of wealth, fortune, prosperity, beauty, fertility, and abundance. She is the consort of Vishnu, one of the principal deities of Hinduism, and together with Parvati and Saraswati forms the Tridevi, the feminine counterpart of the Trimurti. Lakshmi is venerated in Jainism and in some Buddhist traditions as well.

Iconography

Lakshmi is depicted as a beautiful woman of golden complexion, seated or standing on a full-bloomed lotus, holding lotuses in two of her four hands, with gold coins flowing from one hand and the other in a blessing gesture. Two or four elephants, symbolising royal power and rain-bearing clouds, often flank her, pouring water over her. Her red or golden sari signifies prosperity and activity.

Mythology

According to the Puranas, Lakshmi emerged from the churning of the Ocean of Milk (Samudra Manthana) seated on a lotus, and chose Vishnu as her eternal consort. She is said to accompany Vishnu in his avatars — as Sita beside Rama and as Rukmini beside Krishna. Traditions describe her as restless by nature, staying only where there is virtue, cleanliness, and enterprise.

Ashtalakshmi and worship

Lakshmi is worshipped in eight forms, the Ashtalakshmi, representing different sources of wealth: grain, courage, progeny, victory, knowledge, and others. Her most important festival is Lakshmi Puja during Diwali, when homes and businesses are cleaned, lit with lamps, and opened to invite her blessing for the year ahead. Fridays and the month of Shravana (Varalakshmi Vratam) are also sacred to her.
