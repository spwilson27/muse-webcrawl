Electron gun

An electron gun (also called an electron emitter) is an electrical component in some vacuum tubes that produces a narrow, collimated electron beam that has a precise kinetic energy.

Operation

Most electron guns use a heated cathode that emits electrons by thermionic emission. The electrons are accelerated by a high voltage toward an anode and shaped into a beam by electrodes such as the Wehnelt cylinder, then focused by electrostatic or magnetic lenses.

Applications

The largest use was in cathode ray tubes (CRTs) for television sets and computer displays, which used one or three guns (for red, green, and blue). Electron guns are also essential in electron microscopes, electron-beam welders, X-ray tubes, microwave tubes such as klystrons, and particle accelerators.

Types

Emitters range from simple oxide-coated cathodes to tungsten filaments, lanthanum hexaboride crystals, and field emission sources capable of extremely fine, bright beams.
