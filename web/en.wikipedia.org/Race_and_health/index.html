Race and health
Field: Public health, epidemiology, medical sociology
Related topics: Health disparities, social determinants of health

Race and health refers to the relationships — often involving disparities in outcomes — between racial and ethnic identity and physical and mental health. Extensive research, particularly in the United States, documents differences among racial groups in life expectancy, infant mortality, and the prevalence of conditions such as hypertension, diabetes, and asthma.

Causes of disparities

Scholars attribute most disparities not to innate biological differences — race is a social rather than a biological category — but to social determinants of health: income and wealth inequality, residential segregation, environmental exposures, access to and quality of health care, and chronic stress associated with discrimination ("weathering"). Historical abuses, such as the Tuskegee syphilis study, have also shaped distrust of medical institutions.

Debates

Debate continues over the use of race in clinical algorithms (for example, race corrections in kidney-function estimates, many now being abandoned) and in research design, where genetic ancestry may be more precise than self-identified race. Public-health policy responses include targeted screening, community health programs, and measurement of outcomes by demographic group.
