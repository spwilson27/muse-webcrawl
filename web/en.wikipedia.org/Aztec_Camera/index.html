Aztec Camera

Aztec Camera
Origin: East Kilbride, Scotland
Years active: 1980–1995
Genre: Jangle pop, new wave, indie pop
Key member: Roddy Frame

Aztec Camera were a Scottish pop/new wave band founded by Roddy Frame, the group's singer, songwriter, and only consistent member. Formed in East Kilbride in 1980, the band were associated with the Sound of Young Scotland and the Postcard Records scene before signing to Rough Trade and later Warner Bros.

History

The band's debut album, High Land, Hard Rain (1983), was a critical success and included the singles "Oblivious" and "Walk Out to Winter." Subsequent albums included Knife (1984), produced by Mark Knopfler, and Love (1987), which produced the UK top-ten hit "Somewhere in My Heart." Frame's later albums moved toward a smoother pop sound; he disbanded Aztec Camera in 1995 and continued recording under his own name.

Legacy

Aztec Camera's blend of intricate acoustic guitar playing, jazz and Latin influences, and melodic pop songwriting has been widely praised, with High Land, Hard Rain frequently cited among the finest Scottish albums of its era.
