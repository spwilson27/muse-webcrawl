Homeless shelter

A homeless shelter is a temporary residence service for homeless individuals and families. Shelters provide a place to sleep, often meals, and sometimes additional services, with the goal of providing safety from the weather and a base from which residents can work toward more stable housing. They are operated by charities, religious organizations, and government agencies.

Types

Emergency shelters provide short-term, often night-by-night accommodation, sometimes with curfews and limits on belongings. Transitional housing programs offer stays of months to two years combined with case management and support services. Specialized shelters serve particular populations, such as families, youth, veterans, or people fleeing domestic violence. "Warming centers" and "cooling centers" open during extreme weather.

Issues

Shelters vary widely in capacity, rules, and conditions. Common concerns include safety, privacy, storage of possessions, separation of family members, and rules that may exclude people with pets, partners, or substance-use issues. Some people experiencing homelessness avoid shelters and sleep rough or in vehicles instead. Housing First approaches prioritize permanent housing without preconditions, with shelters forming one part of a broader continuum of care.
