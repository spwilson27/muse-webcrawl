Shigeru Miyamoto (born 16 November 1952) is a Japanese video game designer, producer and game director at Nintendo, where he serves as a Representative Director and Fellow. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in the history of video games, having created several of the medium's best-selling and most acclaimed franchises.

Career

Miyamoto joined Nintendo in 1977 as an industrial designer, recruited with the help of his father's connection to president Hiroshi Yamauchi. His breakthrough came with the arcade game Donkey Kong (1981), which introduced the character who became Mario. He went on to design Super Mario Bros. (1985) for the Nintendo Entertainment System, a landmark platform game credited with helping revive the video game industry after the crash of 1983, and The Legend of Zelda (1986), a pioneering open-world action-adventure.

Later work and legacy

Miyamoto produced or supervised Super Mario 64 (1996), which defined 3D platforming, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (1998), frequently ranked among the greatest games ever made, as well as the Pikmin, Star Fox, F-Zero and Wii Sports series. He played a key role in the concept of the Wii console. Among many honours, he was the first inductee into the Academy of Interactive Arts and Sciences Hall of Fame and received a BAFTA Fellowship in 2010.
