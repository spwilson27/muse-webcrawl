Man-portable air-defense system

A man-portable air-defense system (MANPADS) is a portable surface-to-air missile system small enough to be carried and fired by a single soldier or a small team. MANPADS typically use infrared homing to track aircraft engines and are effective mainly against low-flying aeroplanes and helicopters.

History

The first widely deployed MANPADS of the missile era, such as the American FIM-43 Redeye and the Soviet 9K32 Strela-2, appeared in the 1960s. Later systems — including the U.S. FIM-92 Stinger, famous for its use in the Soviet–Afghan War — improved range, seekers, and resistance to countermeasures.

Proliferation concerns

Because of their portability and potential use against civilian airliners, MANPADS are the subject of international export-control efforts, including through the Wassenaar Arrangement and the G8 action plan of 2003.
