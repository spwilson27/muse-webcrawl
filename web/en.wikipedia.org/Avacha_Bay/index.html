Avacha Bay (Russian: Авачинская губа) is a large bay on the Pacific coast of the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East. One of the largest natural harbours in the world, it is home to the city of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky and to the Russian Pacific Fleet's submarine base at Vilyuchinsk.

Geography

The bay cuts about 24 kilometres into the coast and is ringed by volcanic hills, including the active Avachinsky and Koryaksky volcanoes, which tower over Petropavlovsk. Three sea stacks known as the "Three Brothers" guard the narrow entrance and are a symbol of the city. The Avacha River flows into the bay's inner part, Avacha Inlet.

History

Petropavlovsk was founded by Vitus Bering's expedition in 1740 and named for Bering's ships St Peter and St Paul. During the Crimean War, in 1854, the small garrison famously repelled an Anglo-French attack on the port. In the Soviet era the closed military town of Vilyuchinsk (Rybachy) on the bay's southern shore became a major base for ballistic-missile submarines.

Wildlife

The bay supports rich marine life, including Steller sea lions, sea otters and seasonally migrating orcas and whales, and its shores are noted birdwatching sites.
