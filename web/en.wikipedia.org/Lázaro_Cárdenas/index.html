Lázaro Cárdenas del Río (21 May 1895 – 19 October 1970) was a Mexican army officer and politician who served as President of Mexico from 1934 to 1940. A general in the Constitutional Army during the Mexican Revolution, he is best known for the expropriation of foreign oil companies in 1938, creating the state oil monopoly Pemex, and for an ambitious programme of land reform that redistributed some 18–20 million hectares to communal villages (ejidos), more than all his predecessors combined.

Cardenismo, the political movement associated with his presidency, combined agrarian reform, labour mobilisation, socialist education and economic nationalism. Cárdenas remains one of the most revered figures in modern Mexican history; the term cardenismo still denotes the left-nationalist tradition within Mexican politics, later carried by his son Cuauhtémoc Cárdenas.

Early life and military career

Cárdenas was born in Jiquilpan, Michoacán, to a mestizo family of modest means. After his father's death he worked as a tax collector's clerk, printer's apprentice and jail keeper. In 1913, following the coup against President Francisco I. Madero, he joined the revolutionary forces, rising through the Constitutionalist ranks under generals including Álvaro Obregón and Plutarco Elías Calles. By the early 1920s he was a general and military commander in several regions, and in 1928 he became governor of Michoacán, where he promoted land reform, secular education and temperance, combating the Cristero rebellion's aftermath in his home state.

The Maximato and the break with Calles

Cárdenas was chosen as the ruling party's presidential candidate for 1934 by Calles, the Jefe Máximo who had dominated Mexico through a succession of puppet presidents (the Maximato). Once in office, however, Cárdenas built his own base among peasants and workers, encouraged strikes that Calles denounced, and in 1935–36 forced Calles and his allies into exile, ending the Maximato and concentrating power in the presidency. He reorganised the official party as the Party of the Mexican Revolution (PRM, 1938) with corporate sectors for labour, peasants, the military and the "popular" classes — a structure inherited by the later PRI.

Presidency

Land and labour reform

Cárdenas accelerated the redistribution of hacienda land into ejidos, expropriating the great cotton estates of La Laguna (1936) and the henequen plantations of Yucatán (1937). He supported the formation of the Confederation of Mexican Workers (CTM) under Vicente Lombardo Toledano and backed workers in major strikes, including the railway and oil disputes.

Oil expropriation

When foreign oil companies refused to accept a Supreme Court-backed labour award in 1937–38, Cárdenas decreed the expropriation of their assets on 18 March 1938 — a date still celebrated in Mexico as the Expropiación Petrolera. The state company Petróleos Mexicanos (Pemex) was created in June 1938. Britain broke diplomatic relations, the United States applied economic pressure, but President Franklin D. Roosevelt's Good Neighbor policy kept the dispute from escalating; compensation was eventually negotiated.

Education and foreign policy

His government promoted "socialist education" in public schools, expanded rural schooling, and founded the National Polytechnic Institute (1936). In foreign policy Cárdenas condemned the Italian invasion of Ethiopia and the Axis interventions in the Spanish Civil War, and Mexico gave asylum to some 20,000–25,000 Spanish Republican refugees, as well as to Leon Trotsky, who lived in Coyoacán from 1937 until his assassination in 1940.

Later life and legacy

Cárdenas chose the moderate Manuel Ávila Camacho as his successor and served as Secretary of National Defence (1942–45) during Mexico's participation in the Second World War, thereafter acting as an elder statesman of the revolutionary left. He died in Mexico City on 19 October 1970. His reputation for personal honesty and social commitment made him the moral benchmark of the post-revolutionary regime; his son Cuauhtémoc Cárdenas founded the Party of the Democratic Revolution (PRD) in 1989 after a disputed presidential election.
