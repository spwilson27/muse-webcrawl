Two-nation theory

Key proponentMuhammad Ali Jinnah; All-India Muslim League
Key documentLahore Resolution (1940)
OutcomePartition of India, creation of Pakistan (1947)

The two-nation theory is the ideology that Muslims and Hindus of the Indian subcontinent constitute two distinct nations, with their own religions, laws, cultures and heroes, and that Muslims therefore require a separate homeland. It formed the ideological basis of the Pakistan Movement led by the All-India Muslim League under Muhammad Ali Jinnah.

Origins and development

Ideas of distinct Muslim identity were articulated by figures such as Syed Ahmad Khan in the 19th century and given poetic form by Muhammad Iqbal, who in his 1930 Allahabad address called for a consolidated Muslim state in north-western India. Jinnah's 1940 presidential address and the Lahore Resolution of the same year converted the theory into a formal political demand.

Opposition and consequences

The Indian National Congress and leaders such as Mahatma Gandhi and Jawaharlal Nehru advocateded a united, secular India and rejected the theory; some Muslim scholars and parties, including the Jamiat Ulema-e-Hind, also opposed it on theological grounds. The theory ultimately prevailed in the 1947 Partition, which created India and Pakistan amid mass displacement and violence. Bangladesh's independence in 1971, on linguistic and ethnic grounds, is often cited as a challenge to the theory's religion-based premise.
