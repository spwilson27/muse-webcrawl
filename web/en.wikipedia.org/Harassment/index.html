Harassment

Harassment covers a wide range of behaviours of an offensive, humiliating, or intimidating nature. It is commonly understood as behaviour that demeans, threatens, or disturbs another person, and which is often repeated or persistent. Harassment may be motivated by characteristics such as race, gender, religion, sexual orientation, disability, or age, and is prohibited in many legal systems in contexts including employment, housing, and public services.

Forms

Recognised forms include sexual harassment (unwelcome sexual advances or conduct, including quid pro quo demands and hostile-environment behaviour), workplace bullying and mobbing, racial and ethnic harassment, stalking, and online harassment or cyberbullying. Harassment can also occur through repeated unwanted communications, threats, or vandalism, and military and police contexts use the term for harassing fire and similar tactics.

Legal treatment

Many jurisdictions criminalise harassing conduct and provide civil remedies. In the United States, workplace harassment based on protected characteristics violates federal civil rights law; the United Kingdom's Protection from Harassment Act 1997 prohibits a course of conduct amounting to harassment. Employers and institutions are often required to maintain policies, training, and complaint procedures.

Effects

Harassment can cause substantial psychological harm, including anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress, as well as workplace and social consequences for victims.
