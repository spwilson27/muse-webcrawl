Gaius Duilius (fl. 260 BC) was a Roman general and statesman who won the first great Roman naval victory over Carthage at the Battle of Mylae during the First Punic War. A novus homo with no consular ancestors, he was elected consul for 260 BC with Gnaeus Cornelius Scipio Asina as his colleague.

Battle of Mylae

When Scipio was captured at the Lipari Islands, Duilius took command of the newly built Roman fleet in Sicily. Rome, traditionally a land power, had constructed its warships by copying a wrecked Carthaginian quinquereme and had fitted them with the corvus, a hinged boarding-bridge tipped with a spike, which converted naval combat into infantry fighting — negating Carthaginian seamanship. Off Mylae (modern Milazzo) in 260 BC Duilius defeated the fleet of Hannibal Gisco, capturing or sinking some fifty ships. It was the first naval triumph celebrated at Rome.

Honours

Duilius was awarded a triumph and a unique civic honour: the right to be escorted home from dinner parties by a torchbearer and flute-player for the rest of his life. In the Forum a columna rostrata — a column decorated with the rams (rostra) of captured ships — was erected bearing an inscription, the surviving copy of which is one of the oldest monuments of Latin prose. In 258 BC he served as censor, and later tradition made him an emblem of the citizen-soldier of the early Republic.
