Safety glass

Safety glass is glass with additional safety features that make it less likely to break, or less likely to become a hazard when broken. Common designs include toughened glass (also known as tempered glass), laminated glass, and wire mesh glass (traditional armoured glass). Building codes in many jurisdictions require safety glass in locations where breakage poses a risk of injury, such as doors, low windows, bathrooms, and balustrades.

Toughened glass

Toughened glass is processed by controlled thermal or chemical treatments to increase its strength compared with normal glass. When broken, it crumbles into small granular chunks instead of splintering into jagged shards, making injury much less likely. It is used for vehicle windows, shower doors, and architectural glazing.

Laminated glass

Laminated glass consists of two or more layers of glass bonded with an interlayer, typically polyvinyl butyral (PVB). Invented by French chemist Édouard Bénédictus in 1903, it holds together when shattered, with fragments adhering to the interlayer. It is the standard material for automobile windshields and is also used in skylights, hurricane-resistant construction, and security glazing.
