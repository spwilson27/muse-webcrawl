Automath ("automating mathematics") was a formal language developed by the Dutch mathematician Nicolaas Govert de Bruijn from 1967 for expressing complete mathematical theories in such a way that an automated proof checker could verify their correctness. It is regarded as the first practical system of its kind and a foundation of modern interactive theorem provers.

Overview

In Automath, mathematical statements and proofs are written in a precisely defined notation based on a form of typed lambda calculus. The system pioneered ideas later central to type theory, including the use of dependent types and the "propositions as types" correspondence.

Influence

A substantial body of mathematics — notably Edmund Landau's Grundlagen der Analysis — was formalised in Automath. Concepts from the project influenced the development of later systems such as Coq, Agda and Lean, and De Bruijn's work on the Automath archive remains a reference point for the history of formalised mathematics.
