A CEGEP (pronounced "SAY-zhep") is a publicly funded pre-university and technical college in the province of Quebec, Canada. The name is an acronym of the French Collège d'enseignement général et professionnel. CEGEPs form a unique intermediate level of education between secondary school and university that exists only in Quebec.

History

The CEGEP system was created in 1967 during Quebec's Quiet Revolution, following the recommendations of the Parent Commission, which sought to democratise and modernise education in the province. The first CEGEPs opened that year, replacing a patchwork of classical colleges and specialised schools, and made post-secondary education widely accessible at little or no tuition cost to Quebec residents.

Programs

Students enter CEGEP after completing Grade 11 (Secondary V), one year earlier than in the rest of Canada. Two main program streams lead to the Diploma of College Studies (DEC): pre-university programs, lasting two years, which prepare students for university (Quebec bachelor's degrees then generally take three years); and technical programs, lasting three years, which train students for direct entry into professions and trades such as nursing, engineering technology and computer science. All students take a common core of French, English, humanities and physical education.
