Osnabrück


Country: Germany
State: Lower Saxony
Population: about 165,000
Known for: Peace of Westphalia negotiations, 1648

Osnabrück is a city in the German state of Lower Saxony, situated in a valley between the Wiehen Hills and the northern tip of the Teutoburg Forest, about 80 km from Hanover. With a population of roughly 165,000 it is one of the four largest cities in Lower Saxony and serves as a regional centre for industry, logistics, education and culture.

History

Osnabrück was founded as a bishop's seat by Charlemagne around 772 and grew into a member of the Hanseatic League. In 1648 the city, together with Münster, hosted the negotiations of the Peace of Westphalia that ended the Thirty Years' War: Catholic envoys negotiated in Münster while Protestant delegations met in Osnabrück, and the treaty was proclaimed from the steps of Osnabrück's town hall. The city calls itself the Friedensstadt, "City of Peace". Heavily bombed in the Second World War, its old town was largely rebuilt.

Culture and people

Osnabrück is the birthplace of the writer Erich Maria Remarque, author of All Quiet on the Western Front, and of the painter Felix Nussbaum, whose work is displayed in the Felix Nussbaum Haus designed by Daniel Libeskind. The city hosts a university founded in 1974 and the annual European Media Art Festival. The surrounding Osnabrück Land forms part of the UNESCO Global Geopark TERRA.vita.
