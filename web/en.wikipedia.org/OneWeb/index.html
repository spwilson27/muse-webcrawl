OneWeb, legally Eutelsat OneWeb since its merger with Eutelsat in 2023, is a satellite communications company operating a constellation of more than 630 satellites in low Earth orbit that deliver broadband internet connectivity to governments, businesses, aviation, and remote communities — particularly in polar and high-latitude regions poorly served by geostationary satellites.

History

The company was founded in 2012 by Greg Wyler as WorldVu Satellites. It raised major investment from SoftBank, Airbus, Qualcomm, Virgin Group, and others, and began launching satellites in February 2019 on Soyuz rockets. In March 2020, its financing collapsed amid the COVID-19 pandemic and it filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection. In November 2020 it was rescued by a consortium led by the UK government and India's Bharti Global, each taking major stakes. Launches resumed, but in March 2022, following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, OneWeb's launches from Baikonur were suspended; it completed its constellation using SpaceX and Indian LVM3 rockets instead, declaring global coverage readiness in 2023.

Merger with Eutelsat

In September 2023 OneWeb merged with the French geostationary operator Eutelsat to form Eutelsat Group, combining GEO and LEO services with the UK government retaining a special share. Commercial services focus on enterprise, maritime, aviation, and government markets rather than direct-to-consumer sales, distinguishing OneWeb from SpaceX's Starlink.

Technology

OneWeb satellites orbit at about 1,200 km in near-polar planes, providing low-latency Ku-band links through a network of gateway earth stations. A second-generation constellation ("Gen2") is planned to add capacity and new services, with the European Union's IRIS² program set to use related infrastructure.
