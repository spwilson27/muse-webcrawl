Angaur (Japanese: アンガウル, Angauru) is an island and state in the island nation of Palau in the western Pacific Ocean. It is the southernmost island of the Palau archipelago, lying southwest of Peleliu, and has a small population concentrated in the village of Ngeremasch.

History

Angaur was administered by Spain, then sold to Germany in 1899, and was occupied by Japan during the First World War, after which it was governed under a League of Nations mandate. Phosphate mining, begun under the Germans, was a major activity. In September 1944 the island was the scene of the Battle of Angaur, fought between United States Army forces and the Imperial Japanese Army in parallel with the better-known Battle of Peleliu.

Geography and life

The island is a raised coral platform partly ringed by a rocky coast, with a small lagoon harbour on the west side. Angaur is known for its population of feral monkeys — descendants of animals released during the German period — and for Japanese-language signage reflecting its mandate history; it is one of the few places outside Japan where Japanese is an official language at the state level.
