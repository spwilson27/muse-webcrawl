Nashville, Tennessee

Nashville is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Tennessee. Located on the Cumberland River in north-central Tennessee, it has a population of around 690,000 and is the centre of a metropolitan area of about two million.

History

Founded in 1779 and named for Francis Nash, a general of the Continental Army, Nashville grew as a river port and railroad centre. It became Tennessee's permanent capital in 1843 and was the first Confederate state capital to fall to Union forces during the Civil War.

Music City

Nashville is known worldwide as "Music City", the centre of the American country music industry. It is home to the Grand Ole Opry, the Country Music Hall of Fame, and numerous recording studios on Music Row.

Economy and education

Health care, publishing, banking, and transportation are major industries. The city hosts Vanderbilt University and several other colleges, and its full-scale replica of the Parthenon in Centennial Park earned it the nickname "Athens of the South".
