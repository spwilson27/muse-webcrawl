The One Ring, also called the Ruling Ring, is a fictional artifact in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, the central object of The Lord of the Rings. Forged by the Dark Lord Sauron in the fires of Mount Doom during the Second Age, it was made to control the bearers of the other Rings of Power. Sauron poured much of his own power into it, and it bears an inscription in the Black Speech, revealed in fire: "One Ring to rule them all, One Ring to find them, One Ring to bring them all and in the darkness bind them."

Properties

The Ring grants invisibility to most wearers by shifting them into the wraith-world, prolongs life unnaturally, and enhances the bearer's innate power — but it corrupts them, fostering possessiveness and domination. It has a will of its own, resizing itself to slip from a finger and seeking to return to Sauron. It cannot be destroyed by ordinary means; only the fires of Mount Doom, where it was made, can unmake it.

History in the narrative

After Sauron's defeat, Isildur cut the Ring from his hand but was later killed, losing it in the river Anduin. It was found by the hobbit-like Déagol, taken by Sméagol (Gollum), lost to Bilbo Baggins in The Hobbit, and passed to Frodo Baggins. The quest to destroy it forms the plot of The Lord of the Rings; at the Cracks of Doom, Frodo claims the Ring, but Gollum bites it from his hand and falls with it into the fire, destroying it and Sauron.

Themes

The Ring is widely read as a symbol of the corrupting nature of power and, in Tolkien's own framing, of the temptation to use evil means for good ends; he resisted simple allegorical readings such as identifying it with the atomic bomb.
