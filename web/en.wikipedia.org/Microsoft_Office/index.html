Microsoft Office, now largely branded as part of Microsoft 365, is a family of productivity software developed by Microsoft. It consists primarily of Word (word processing), Excel (spreadsheets), PowerPoint (presentations) and Outlook (email and personal information management), together with applications such as Access, Publisher and OneNote, plus services including OneDrive and Teams.

History

Announced by Bill Gates in 1988, Office first appeared for the Macintosh in 1989 and for Windows in 1990, bundling previously separate programs. Through the 1990s it defeated rivals such as WordPerfect and Lotus 1-2-3 to dominate the office suite market. Major releases included Office 95, 97, 2000, XP, 2003, and Office 2007, which introduced the Ribbon interface and the Office Open XML formats (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx) that became an ISO standard.

Microsoft 365

In 2011 Microsoft launched Office 365, a subscription service combining the applications with cloud storage and collaboration; it was rebranded Microsoft 365 in 2020 and later gained AI features under the Copilot brand. Perpetual-license versions continue alongside the subscription, most recently Office 2024. Office also runs on the web, macOS, iOS and Android.

Influence

Office is among the most widely used software suites in the world, and its file formats function as de facto standards for documents, spreadsheets and presentations.
