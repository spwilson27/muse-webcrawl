Clement Richard Attlee, 1st Earl Attlee (3 January 1883 – 8 October 1967) was a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1945 to 1951 and Leader of the Labour Party from 1935 to 1955. He led Labour to a landslide victory in the 1945 general election and presided over the establishment of the post-war consensus and the modern British welfare state.

Attlee government

The Attlee government of 1945–51 implemented the Beveridge Report's vision of social security, creating the National Health Service in 1948 under Aneurin Bevan, expanding national insurance, and nationalising major industries including coal, railways, electricity, gas and the Bank of England. Overseas, his government granted independence to India and Pakistan in 1947, and committed Britain to the Marshall Plan and the founding of NATO in 1949. Attlee also served as Deputy Prime Minister in Winston Churchill's wartime coalition from 1942 to 1945.

Legacy

Often ranked among the greatest British prime ministers in academic surveys, Attlee's quiet, understated style contrasted with his government's radical achievements. He was made an earl in 1955 and remained an influential elder statesman until his death in 1967.
