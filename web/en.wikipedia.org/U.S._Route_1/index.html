U.S. Route 1 (US 1) is a major north–south United States Numbered Highway that serves the East Coast of the United States. It runs 2,369 miles (3,813 km) from Key West, Florida, north to Fort Kent, Maine, at the Canadian border, making it the longest north–south road in the United States.

The highway connects most of the major cities of the Atlantic Seaboard, including Miami, Jacksonville, Raleigh, Richmond, Washington, D.C., Baltimore, Philadelphia, New York City, Boston and Portland. In many places it has been bypassed or paralleled by Interstate 95, but it remains an important local and regional arterial.

History

US 1 was one of the original U.S. Highways designated in the 1926 numbering plan. It largely followed the Atlantic Highway, an auto trail assembled from earlier roads including the historic King's Highway, the Fall Line Road, and in Florida the Dixie Highway. The southern terminus was originally in Miami and was later extended over the Overseas Highway to Key West.

In the mid-20th century, US 1 was the principal route for motorists travelling between the Northeast and Florida. The construction of Interstate 95 from the 1950s through the 1980s diverted most long-distance traffic, and many sections of US 1 became urban boulevards lined with motels, diners and roadside businesses dating from the highway's heyday.

Route description

From Key West, US 1 crosses the island chain of the Florida Keys on the Overseas Highway, then follows the coast through Miami and Fort Lauderdale as Biscayne Boulevard and Federal Highway. It continues through Jacksonville, then inland through the Carolinas and Virginia, passing through Raleigh and Richmond before reaching Washington, D.C. North of Washington it follows the Baltimore–Washington corridor, crosses the Susquehanna River, and passes through Philadelphia.

In New Jersey, US 1 runs concurrently with US 9 for a stretch and crosses into New York City via the George Washington Bridge approach roads and the Harlem River Drive corridor. Through New England it follows the Boston Post Road alignment through Connecticut, Rhode Island and Massachusetts, passing Boston before continuing along the coast of New Hampshire and Maine to its northern terminus at the Clair–Fort Kent Bridge over the Saint John River.
