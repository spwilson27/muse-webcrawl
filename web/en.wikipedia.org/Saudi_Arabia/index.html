Saudi Arabia, officially the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA), is a country in Western Asia occupying most of the Arabian Peninsula. It is bordered by Jordan, Iraq and Kuwait to the north, the Persian Gulf, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates to the east, Oman and Yemen to the south, and the Red Sea to the west. It contains Islam's two holiest cities, Mecca and Medina, and the king's official title is Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques.

History

The modern kingdom was founded by Ibn Saud (Abdulaziz ibn Saud), who retook Riyadh in 1902 and reunified Najd and Hejaz, proclaiming the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in 1932. Oil was discovered in commercial quantities in 1938 (Dammam No. 7), transforming the economy; the state oil company Saudi Aramco made the kingdom one of the world's largest petroleum producers and exporters, and a founding member of OPEC (1960).

Economy

The economy is dominated by oil and petrochemicals, though the Vision 2030 programme of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman seeks diversification into tourism, entertainment, technology and mega-projects such as the planned city of Neom. A large share of the workforce consists of expatriates. The Public Investment Fund is among the world's largest sovereign wealth funds.

Society

Saudi Arabia is an Islamic state whose legal system is based on Sharia as interpreted by the Hanbali school; the Wahhabi movement has historically shaped religious life. Since 2017, reforms have lifted the ban on women driving (2018), curtailed the religious police, and opened cinemas and tourism, alongside continued criticism of the human-rights record.
