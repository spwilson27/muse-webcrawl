Grindcore

Grindcore is an extreme fusion of heavy metal and hardcore punk that originated in the mid-1980s, drawing inspiration from abrasive-sounding industrial music, hardcore punk, and thrash metal. It is characterized by a noise-filled sound, heavily distorted down-tuned guitars, extreme tempos, blast beats, and vocals that range from high-pitched shrieks to low, guttural growls. Songs are typically very short; the genre is famous for "microsongs" lasting only a few seconds.

History

The genre emerged in the United Kingdom and the United States. The Birmingham band Napalm Death is widely credited with defining grindcore; their debut album Scum (1987) fused the speed of hardcore with metal's heaviness, and its track "You Suffer" — lasting about 1.3 seconds — is often cited as the shortest recorded song. Other pioneers included Carcass, whose early gore-obsessed lyrics spawned the "goregrind" offshoot, and American bands such as Terrorizer and Repulsion. The Birmingham label Earache Records became the genre's principal home in its formative years.

Characteristics and subgenres

Grindcore lyrics in the Napalm Death tradition frequently address social and political themes — anti-capitalism, anti-racism, animal rights — reflecting the genre's punk roots, while other bands focus on horror and pathology. Related styles include deathgrind (grindcore combined with death metal), goregrind, and powerviolence, a closely related form of fast hardcore. Influential later acts include Pig Destroyer, Nasum, Agoraphobic Nosebleed, and Brutal Truth.
