Action-adventure game

An action-adventure game is a video game genre that combines core elements from both the action game and adventure game genres. Action-adventure games are the most popular and broadly encompassing genre in video games, typically featuring real-time combat, traversal, exploration, puzzle-solving, and item-based progression within a story-driven structure.

Definition and characteristics

Action-adventure games focus on exploration, with the player character moving through an environment, gathering tools or items to overcome obstacles and enemies, and often unlocking new areas with new abilities — a structure sometimes called "Metroidvania" in side-scrolling form. Unlike pure action games, success depends partly on solving problems and managing resources; unlike traditional adventure games, action and reflexes play a direct role. Common subgenres and neighbouring forms include survival horror, stealth games, platform-adventure games, and open-world action-adventure titles.

History

The genre traces to Atari's Adventure (1980), which transposed the text-adventure concept into a graphical action format. Nintendo's The Legend of Zelda (1986) established the template of an explorable world, dungeons, and item-based progression. Later landmark series include Metroid, Tomb Raider, Resident Evil, Grand Theft Auto, Assassin's Creed, Uncharted, God of War, and Batman: Arkham. In 3D, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (1998) was highly influential for its targeting system and contextual controls, and the open-world design of Breath of the Wild (2017) reshaped expectations for exploration-driven design.
