Military organisation or military organization is the structuring of the armed forces of a state so as to offer the military capability required by the national defence policy. In some jurisdictions these formations are also called military units or task forces, and in modern usage a unit is any military body small enough to operate on its own budget with its own commander.

Hierarchy

Modern land forces follow a common hierarchy of nested units, each roughly an order of magnitude larger than the one below. A squad (or section) of about 8–12 soldiers is grouped into platoons of 30–50; platoons form companies of 100–200 commanded by a captain or major; companies form battalions of 500–1,000; and battalions are combined into brigades and divisions of 10,000–20,000 commanded by generals. Above division stand corps and field armies, and in wartime, army groups comprising hundreds of thousands of troops. Naval and air forces have parallel structures — fleets and squadrons respectively.

Organisation by function

Within this hierarchy, forces are divided by branch or arm — infantry, armour, artillery, engineers, signals and logistics — and supported by staff systems, chains of command and tables of organisation and equipment that define the authorised strength and kit of each unit type. Ad hoc groupings such as battlegroups and joint task forces are assembled for particular operations, combining units from several branches or services under a single commander.
