Presbyterian Church in America

The Presbyterian Church in America (PCA) is the second-largest Presbyterian church body in the United States, after the Presbyterian Church (USA). Formed in 1973 by congregations separating from the Presbyterian Church in the United States over theological liberalism, the PCA is theologically conservative, holding to the Westminster Standards as its doctrinal confession.

History

The founding General Assembly met in Birmingham, Alabama, in December 1973, with 190 congregations under the name National Presbyterian Church; the body adopted its present name in 1974. In 1982 the Reformed Presbyterian Church, Evangelical Synod joined the PCA in the "joining and receiving" merger. The denomination has grown to roughly 400,000 members in more than 1,900 congregations, mainly in the Southeast and metropolitan areas.

Beliefs and polity

As a Presbyterian body, the PCA is governed by elders through local sessions, regional presbyteries and an annual General Assembly. It affirms the inerrancy of Scripture, maintains a complementarian position restricting ordained office to men, and supports institutions including Covenant Theological Seminary and Covenant College. Mission to the World is its missions agency, and Reformed University Fellowship its campus ministry.
