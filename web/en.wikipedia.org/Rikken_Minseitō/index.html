Rikken Minseitō

The Rikken Minseitō (立憲民政党, Constitutional Democratic Party) was one of the main political parties in pre-war Imperial Japan. It was formed on 1 June 1927 by a merger of the Kenseikai and the Seiyū Hontō parties, and was the principal rival of the Rikken Seiyūkai. Together, the two parties dominated Japanese party government during the era of Taishō democracy.

History

The Minseitō platform favoured parliamentary government, retrenchment in public spending, fiscal austerity under Finance Minister Hamaguchi Osachi, ratification of the London Naval Treaty of 1930, and conciliation toward China and the Western powers. Hamaguchi became Prime Minister in 1929; his government's deflationary policies, combined with the onset of the Great Depression, proved deeply unpopular. Hamaguchi was shot by an ultranationalist in 1930 and died of his wounds the following year.

Under Wakatsuki Reijirō the party returned to power in 1931, but party cabinets collapsed after the Manchurian Incident and the May 15 Incident of 1932, which ended the era of party government. The Minseitō remained a major Diet bloc through the 1930s but dissolved in 1940 to join the Imperial Rule Assistance Association.
