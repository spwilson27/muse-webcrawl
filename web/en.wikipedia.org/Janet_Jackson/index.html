Janet Jackson

Janet Damita Jo Jackson (born May 16, 1966) is an American singer, songwriter, actress and dancer. The youngest child of the Jackson family, she is noted for her innovative, socially conscious and sexually provocative recordings, elaborate stage shows and choreography. With record sales of more than 100 million worldwide, she is one of the best-selling music artists of all time.

Born: May 16, 1966, Gary, Indiana, US

Occupations: Singer, songwriter, actress, dancer

Genres: R&B, pop, dance

Years active: 1974–present

Career

Jackson began her career as a child actress and signed with A&M Records in 1982. Her breakthrough came with the album Control (1986), produced with Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, which yielded hits such as "What Have You Done for Me Lately" and "Nasty" and defined a new sound in R&B. Its follow-up, Janet Jackson's Rhythm Nation 1814 (1989), addressed social injustice and produced a record seven top-five singles. Subsequent albums janet. (1993), The Velvet Rope (1997) and All for You (2001) consolidated her status as a pop icon, and she starred in films including Poetic Justice (1993).

Legacy

Jackson was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019. Her choreography and visual style influenced a generation of pop performers, and her socially conscious work is regarded as a landmark in popular music.
