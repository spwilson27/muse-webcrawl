Peritoneum

The peritoneum is the serous membrane forming the lining of the abdominal cavity and covering most of the intra-abdominal organs. It consists of a parietal layer lining the abdominal wall and a visceral layer wrapping the organs, with a potential space between them containing a small amount of lubricating fluid.

Peritoneum
Type: serous membrane
Layers: parietal and visceral
Structures: mesentery, omentum
Clinical: peritonitis, dialysis

Structure

Folds of peritoneum connect organs to each other and to the abdominal wall: the mesentery suspends the intestines and carries their blood vessels and nerves; the greater and lesser omenta hang from the stomach. Organs fully covered are called intraperitoneal; those behind the peritoneum, such as the kidneys, are retroperitoneal.

Clinical significance

Inflammation, usually from a perforated viscus or infection, is peritonitis — a surgical emergency. The peritoneum's absorptive surface is exploited in peritoneal dialysis for kidney failure, and fluid accumulation in the cavity is ascites.
