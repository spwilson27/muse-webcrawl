Riparian water rights

Riparian water rights (or simply riparian rights) is a system of allocating water among those who possess land along its path. The doctrine has its origins in English common law and exists in many jurisdictions with a British heritage, including the eastern United States, where water is relatively plentiful.

Principles

Under the riparian doctrine, owners of land adjoining a watercourse (riparian owners) have the right to make reasonable use of the water on their land, subject to the equivalent rights of other riparian owners. Water generally may not be transferred out of the watershed, and rights are not lost by non-use. In times of shortage, all riparian users share the burden proportionally.

Comparison with prior appropriation

The western United States instead generally follows the prior appropriation doctrine ("first in time, first in right"), in which rights are acquired by diverting and beneficially using water and can be severed from riparian land. Some jurisdictions, such as California, combine elements of both systems.

Modern regulation

Many jurisdictions have overlaid the common-law system with statutory permit schemes (sometimes called regulated riparianism), requiring registration or permits for significant withdrawals while retaining riparian principles as a background rule.
