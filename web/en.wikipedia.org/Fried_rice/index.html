Fried rice

Fried rice
Place of originChina
Main ingredientsRice, cooking oil, egg, vegetables
VariationsYangzhou, nasi goreng, chāhan, bokkeum-bap, khao phat

Fried rice is a dish of cooked rice that has been stir-fried in a wok or frying pan, usually mixed with other ingredients such as eggs, vegetables, seafood, or meat. It is a component of several East, Southeast, and South Asian cuisines and a popular home-cooked dish made to use up leftover rice and ingredients.

History

Fried rice originated in China, where it developed as a way to use leftover rice; a celebrated early centre of the dish is Yangzhou, whose elaborate Yangzhou fried rice combines char siu pork, shrimp, scallions, egg, and vegetables. From China the technique spread across Asia and, with the Chinese diaspora, worldwide.

Preparation

Day-old, refrigerated rice is preferred because its firmer, drier grains separate rather than clump when stir-fried over high heat. Ingredients are typically cooked in stages in a hot wok with oil, seasoned with soy sauce, salt, and aromatics such as garlic and scallion, and finished with beaten egg folded through the rice.

Varieties

Notable regional versions include Indonesia's nasi goreng, sweetened with kecap manis; Japan's chāhan; Korea's bokkeum-bap, often made with kimchi; Thailand's khao phat; and Cantonese salted-fish fried rice. In the Americas, Peruvian chaufa reflects Chinese influence on Peruvian cuisine.
