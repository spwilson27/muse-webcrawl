Berkshire Hathaway Inc. is an American multinational conglomerate holding company headquartered in Omaha, Nebraska. Originally a textile manufacturer, it was transformed by investor Warren Buffett into one of the largest companies in the world, built around insurance operations whose premiums ("float") fund investments in wholly owned businesses and marketable securities.

History

Berkshire Hathaway's textile roots run to 1839; the modern company resulted from the 1955 merger of Berkshire Fine Spinning Associates and Hathaway Manufacturing. Buffett began buying shares in 1962, took control in 1965, and gradually wound down the textile operations (closed in 1985) while redeploying capital — famously into National Indemnity (1967) and GEICO insurance, See's Candies, and later the BNSF Railway (2010, its largest acquisition), energy utilities, and manufacturers. Long-time vice chairman Charlie Munger, Buffett's partner since 1978, died in November 2023 aged 99. Buffett announced in 2025 that Greg Abel would succeed him as chief executive at year-end, with Buffett remaining chairman.

Businesses and investments

Wholly owned subsidiaries span insurance (GEICO, General Re), rail (BNSF), energy (Berkshire Hathaway Energy), aviation services (NetJets, FlightSafety), building products, chemicals (Lubrizol), retail (Nebraska Furniture Mart) and food (Dairy Queen, See's Candies). Berkshire also holds a large portfolio of listed equities, historically concentrated in companies such as Apple, Bank of America, American Express and Coca-Cola, and is known for holding very large cash balances. Its Class A shares are the highest-priced listed shares in the world, having never been split.

Culture

Berkshire operates with an unusually small head office, extreme decentralisation, and famous annual shareholder meetings in Omaha that draw tens of thousands of investors. Buffett's annual letters to shareholders are widely read as texts on investing and management, and the company has never paid a dividend, preferring to reinvest earnings.
