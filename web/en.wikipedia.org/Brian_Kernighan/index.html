Brian Kernighan

Brian Wilson Kernighan (born January 30, 1942) is a Canadian computer scientist. He worked at Bell Labs and contributed to the development of Unix alongside creators Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie. Kernighan's name became widely known through co-authorship of the first book on the C programming language, The C Programming Language (1978, 1988), with Ritchie — universally known as "K&R". He coined the terms "Unix" (a pun on Multics) and "hello, world!", and is a co-creator of the AWK and AMPL programming languages. Since 2000 he has been a professor of computer science at Princeton University.

Early life and education

Kernighan was born in Toronto and studied engineering physics at the University of Toronto, graduating in 1964. Summer jobs introduced him to computing — including work at Ferranti-Packard and a project at the University of Calgary — before he completed a Ph.D. in electrical engineering at Princeton in 1969, with a dissertation on graph partitioning heuristics, supervised by Edward Sueltenfuss and Peter Weiner.

Bell Labs and Unix

Joining Bell Labs in 1969, Kernighan worked in the same computing sciences group as Thompson, Ritchie, Doug McIlroy and others who created Unix and its tool-based philosophy. He did not write Unix itself, but he advocated it, documented it, and christened it: his suggested name "Unics" (Uniplexed Information and Computing Service) was adopted as "Unix". His popularisation of "Hello, World!" — in a 1973 memo on B and then in The C Programming Language — made it the canonical first program in programming pedagogy. With P. J. Plauger he wrote the influential style guides The Elements of Programming Style (1974) and Software Tools (1976), promoting "ratfor" and structured programming.

Languages and books

With Alfred Aho and Peter Weinberger, Kernighan created AWK (1977), the pattern-action text-processing language named from their initials, and with Robert Fourer and David Gay he designed AMPL, an algebraic modelling language for optimisation. His many books include The Practice of Programming (1999, with Rob Pike), The Go Programming Language (2015, with Alan Donovan), and books for general readers: D is for Digital (2011), Millions, Billions, Zillions (2018), Understanding the Digital World (2017/2021) and UNIX: A History and a Memoir (2019). At Princeton he teaches courses on computers in society and digital literacy for non-majors, as well as systems courses.
