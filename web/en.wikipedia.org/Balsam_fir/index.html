Balsam fir

The balsam fir (Abies balsamea) is a North American fir native to most of eastern and central Canada and the northeastern United States, from Newfoundland west to Alberta and south to West Virginia and Minnesota. It is a medium-sized evergreen tree, typically 15–20 metres tall, with a narrow conical crown.

Description

The bark of young trees is smooth and grey, marked with resin blisters that give the tree its name; the resin, known as Canada balsam, was historically used as a cement for lenses and microscope slides. The leaves are flat, dark green needles 1.5–3 cm long, with two white bands of stomata beneath. The seed cones stand upright on the branches and disintegrate when mature.

Uses

Balsam fir is a popular Christmas tree, valued for its fragrance, dark green colour and good needle retention. Its wood is used for pulp and light construction, and the tree is an important food and shelter species for wildlife, including moose, which browse it heavily in winter.
