The Fu-Go balloon bomb (風船爆弾, fūsen bakudan, "fire balloon") was a balloon weapon deployed by Japan against the United States during World War II. It consisted of a hydrogen-filled balloon 10 metres (33 ft) in diameter carrying a load of one 15 kg (33 lb) antipersonnel bomb and two 5 kg (11 lb) incendiary devices.

Development

The balloons were launched during the winter of 1944–1945 from the Japanese home islands, riding the newly understood jet stream across the Pacific Ocean. Japan launched some 9,300 balloons, of which around 300 were found or observed in North America.

Effects and casualties

The campaign resulted in the death of six people — a woman and five children in Bly, Oregon, on May 5, 1945 — the only known fatalities on the U.S. mainland from enemy attack during the war. A resulting forest fire near Hanford, Washington, briefly interrupted power to the Manhattan Project's plutonium plant.

Legacy

News of the balloons was suppressed by American authorities so that Japan would receive no confirmation of their effectiveness. The Fu-Go remains the longest-ranged intercontinental weapon used in combat at the time of its deployment.
