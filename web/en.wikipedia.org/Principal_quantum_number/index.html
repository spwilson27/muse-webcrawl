Principal quantum number

In quantum mechanics, the principal quantum number (symbolized n) is one of four quantum numbers assigned to each electron in an atom to describe that electron's state. Its values are natural numbers (1, 2, 3, ...) making it a discrete variable.

Overview

The principal quantum number roughly determines the energy of an electron and its most probable distance from the nucleus — that is, the size of the electron shell. Electrons with the same n occupy the same shell, historically labelled K, L, M, N for n = 1, 2, 3, 4. Higher n corresponds to higher energy and a larger average radius.

Related quantum numbers

Together with the azimuthal quantum number (ℓ), the magnetic quantum number (mℓ), and the spin quantum number (ms), the principal quantum number fully specifies the quantum state of an electron, in accordance with the Pauli exclusion principle.
