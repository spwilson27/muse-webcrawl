Endodermis

The endodermis is the innermost layer of cells in the cortex of a plant root (and in some stems), surrounding the vascular tissue known as the stele. It regulates the flow of water and dissolved substances into the plant's vascular system.

Structure

Endodermal cells are characterized by the Casparian strip, a band of suberin and lignin deposited in their radial and transverse walls. The strip is impermeable to water, forcing water and solutes moving through the root to pass through the selectively permeable plasma membranes of endodermal cells rather than between them.

Function

By controlling what enters the xylem, the endodermis protects the plant against toxins and pathogens and helps regulate mineral uptake. In older roots, endodermal cells may become further thickened with suberin lamellae, leaving only certain "passage cells" thin-walled.
