The Tsar Cannon (Russian: Царь-пушка, Tsar'-pushka) is a large early modern artillery piece on display in the Moscow Kremlin. Cast in bronze in 1586 by the Russian master founder Andrey Chokhov during the reign of Tsar Feodor Ivanovich, it is, by calibre, the largest bombard by calibre ever made, and a celebrated monument of Russian casting art.

Description

The cannon weighs approximately 40 tonnes, is 5.34 metres long, and has a calibre of 890 millimetres. Its barrel is decorated with reliefs, including a depiction of Tsar Feodor Ivanovich on horseback. The decorative gun carriage and the hollow decorative cannonballs beside it were cast in iron much later, in 1835. The cannon was intended to fire grapeshot for the defence of the Kremlin, but it was apparently never used in warfare.

History

The Tsar Cannon originally stood near Red Square before being moved into the Kremlin in the eighteenth century, where it was placed alongside captured Napoleonic artillery. It now stands near the Tsar Bell, another gigantic casting that was never used for its intended purpose. Guinness World Records lists it as the largest bombard by calibre in the world.
