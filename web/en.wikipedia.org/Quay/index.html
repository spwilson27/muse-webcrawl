Quay

A quay (pronounced "key") is a structure on the shore of a harbour or river, parallel to the water's edge, where ships and boats can lie alongside to load and discharge cargo or passengers. Quays are typically solid constructions of stone, concrete or sheet piling with a level paved surface, in contrast to piers and jetties, which project out into the water.

Terminology

The terms quay, wharf and berth are closely related and often used interchangeably; "wharf" is more common in North American usage, while "quay" is widespread in Britain, Ireland and in the names of famous waterfront districts such as Singapore's Clarke Quay and Dublin's quays along the River Liffey. The word comes from Old North French cai, of Celtic origin.

Design

Modern container quays are reinforced to bear heavy cranes and stacks of containers, equipped with bollards, fenders, and deep-water berths dredged to accommodate large vessels.
