Raška (region)

Raška (Serbian Cyrillic: Рашка) is a geographical and historical region in southwestern Serbia, centred on the Raška river and the town of Raška, and corresponding roughly to the medieval Serbian heartland. The name is closely associated with the medieval Serbian state of Raška, whose grand princes (župans) of the Vukanović dynasty, and afterwards the Nemanjić dynasty, laid the foundations of the Serbian kingdom.

History

In the early Middle Ages, Raška was a frontier province of the early Serbian principality, ruled from centres such as Stari Ras, a site now listed by UNESCO together with the monastery of Sopoćani. Under Stefan Nemanja (r. 1166–1196), Raška became the nucleus of the unified Serbian state; his son Stefan was crowned king in 1217, and another son, Saint Sava, founded the autocephalous Serbian Orthodox Church. The medieval monasteries of Studenica and Sopoćani stand in the region.

Modern usage

Today the name survives administratively in the Raška District of Serbia, with its seat at Kraljevo. The historical region partly overlaps with the area also known as Sandžak (Sanjak of Novi Pazar), which has a substantial Bosniak population; "Raška" is sometimes used by Serbian speakers for much the same area.
