Vemork is a hydroelectric power plant and industrial site near Rjukan in Telemark, Norway. Built by Norsk Hydro and opened in 1911, it was at the time the largest power station in the world, supplying electricity for the production of artificial fertilizer using the Birkeland–Eyde process. Vemork became famous during the Second World War as the site of the Norwegian heavy water sabotage operations.

Heavy water production

From 1934, Vemork housed the world's first plant capable of producing heavy water (deuterium oxide) in commercial quantities, as a by-product of ammonia production via electrolysis. After the German occupation of Norway in 1940, heavy water became strategically important because of its use as a moderator in nuclear reactor research, prompting Allied efforts to deny it to Germany.

Sabotage operations

In February 1943, a team of Norwegian commandos trained by the British Special Operations Executive parachuted onto the Hardangervidda plateau and, in Operation Gunnerside, destroyed the heavy water electrolysis cells at Vemork without losing a man. Later that year American bombers attacked the plant, and in February 1944 Norwegian resistance fighters sank the ferry SF Hydro on Lake Tinn, carrying heavy water drums to Germany. The story was dramatised in the 1965 film The Heroes of Telemark.

Present day

The original power station ceased generation in 1971, replaced by a new underground plant. The old building now houses the Norwegian Industrial Workers Museum. Archaeologists excavated the heavy water cellar in 2017–2018, recovering equipment from the wartime plant.
