Lucchese crime family

The Lucchese crime family is an Italian-American Mafia crime family and one of the "Five Families" that dominate organized crime activities in New York City, within the nationwide criminal phenomenon known as the American Mafia. Founded in the early 1920s and reorganized after the Castellammarese War of 1931, the family takes its name from longtime boss Tommy Lucchese.

Lucchese crime family
Founded: c. 1920s
Founder: Gaetano Reina (original family)
Named after: Tommy Lucchese
Territory: New York City, New Jersey, Florida
Activities: racketeering, extortion, labor racketeering, illegal gambling, loansharking, drug trafficking

History

The organization began as the Reina crime family under Gaetano "Tommy" Reina. After Reina's murder in 1930 during the Castellammarese War, Joseph Pinzolo and then Tommy Gagliano led the family under the new Commission structure created in 1931. Gagliano kept a low profile until his death in the early 1950s.

Lucchese era

Tommy Lucchese, Gagliano's underboss, succeeded him in 1951 and led until his death from a brain tumor in 1967. Lucchese expanded the family's influence in the garment industry, trucking, and labor unions, and cultivated political connections, making the family one of the most powerful and profitable in the country. Later bosses included Carmine Tramunti and Anthony "Tony Ducks" Corallo.

Amuso and Casso era

In 1986 Vittorio "Vic" Amuso became boss, with Anthony "Gaspipe" Casso as underboss; their violent reign saw dozens of murders and a feud with federal authorities. Both were eventually convicted — Amuso received life imprisonment in 1992 — and the family was further weakened by defections and RICO prosecutions. After years of acting bosses, Michael DeSantis became acting boss in 2017 with Amuso's approval from prison.
