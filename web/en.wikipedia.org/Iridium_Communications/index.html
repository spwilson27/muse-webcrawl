Iridium Communications

Type: Public

Industry: Telecommunications

Founded: 2001 (as Iridium Satellite LLC)

Headquarters: McLean, Virginia, United States

Key people: Matt Desch (CEO)

Iridium Communications Inc. (formerly Iridium Satellite LLC) is a publicly traded American company headquartered in McLean, Virginia, United States. It operates the Iridium satellite constellation, a system of 66 active communication satellites plus spares in low Earth orbit used for worldwide voice and data communication from hand-held satellite phones and other transceiver units.

History

The original Iridium company was founded by Motorola in 1991 and launched service in 1998, but filed for bankruptcy in 1999 after failing to attract enough subscribers. In December 2000, a group of private investors purchased the constellation's assets for a fraction of their construction cost and resumed service in 2001 through Iridium Satellite LLC. The company became Iridium Communications in 2009 after merging with a special-purpose acquisition company, and trades on the Nasdaq under the ticker IRDM.

Services

Iridium serves aviation, maritime, government, military, and emergency markets, and is a key provider of communications in polar regions where geostationary satellites cannot reach. Its products include satellite phones, the Iridium GO! hotspot, broadband terminals (Iridium Certus), and short-burst data modems used in asset tracking and machine-to-machine applications. The US Department of Defense is among its largest customers. In 2023 Iridium announced it would supply satellite connectivity to Qualcomm's Snapdragon Satellite programme for smartphones, though that partnership later ended; the company has since pursued standards-based direct-to-device services.
