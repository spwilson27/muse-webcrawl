Biological classification

Biological classification is the process by which scientists group living organisms into categories based on shared characteristics. It is a fundamental part of taxonomy, the science of naming, describing and classifying organisms.

History

Systems of classification date back to Aristotle, who grouped animals by habitat and mode of reproduction. The modern system was established by Carl Linnaeus in the 18th century, who introduced binomial nomenclature and a hierarchy of ranks including kingdom, class, order, genus and species.

Principal ranks

The principal taxonomic ranks in modern use are domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species. Intermediate ranks such as subfamily or tribe are frequently inserted. In the three-domain system of Carl Woese, life is divided into Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya.

Modern developments

Molecular phylogenetics, which uses DNA and RNA sequences, has substantially revised traditional classifications, revealing evolutionary relationships invisible to morphology alone.
