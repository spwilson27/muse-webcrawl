Edwin Herbert Land (7 May 1909 – 1 March 1991) was an American scientist and inventor, best known as the co-founder of the Polaroid Corporation. He invented inexpensive filters for polarizing light, a practical system of in-camera instant photography, and the retinex theory of colour vision. He held 535 United States patents, among the largest totals for any inventor.

Early work on polarization

Land left Harvard University as a young man to pursue his idea of a synthetic sheet polarizer. By embedding microscopic crystals of herapathite in a plastic sheet, he produced the first inexpensive polarizing filter, marketed as Polaroid film. Applications ranged from sunglasses and camera filters to glare reduction in headlights and windows.

Instant photography

According to company lore, Land conceived instant photography in 1943 after his young daughter asked why she could not see a photograph immediately. Within a few years his team had solved the problem of developing and fixing an image inside the camera. The Polaroid Land Camera went on sale in 1948, and instant photography became a mass consumer phenomenon, culminating in the folding SX-70 camera of 1972, which Land regarded as his masterpiece.

Later career

Land advised the United States government on reconnaissance during the Cold War, playing a key role in the development of the U-2 spy plane's camera systems. He founded the Rowland Institute for Science in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and pursued research on colour vision, arguing in his retinex theory that colour perception depends on relative lightness across a scene. He retired from Polaroid in 1982.
