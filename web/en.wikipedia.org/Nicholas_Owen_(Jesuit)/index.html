Saint Nicholas Owen (c. 1562 – March 2, 1606) was an English Jesuit lay brother who became famous as a builder of priest holes, hiding places for persecuted Roman Catholic priests in the homes of English Catholic families during the reigns of Elizabeth I and James I. He was arrested after the Gunpowder Plot and died in the Tower of London under torture, refusing to reveal the locations of the hiding places he had built.

Owen, a carpenter and mason by trade and a man of short stature, served the Jesuit priest Henry Garnet for nearly two decades. His priest holes were so ingeniously constructed—using false walls, disguised entrances behind panelling, and spaces within chimneys—that some remained undiscovered for centuries.

Capture and death

Following the Gunpowder Plot of November 1605, Owen left his hiding place at Hindlip Hall in Worcestershire and gave himself up, hoping to divert attention from his master Garnet. He was imprisoned in the Marshalsea and then the Tower, where he was manacled and tortured on the rack despite laws exempting those with his hernia condition from such treatment. He died on March 2, 1606, without revealing any secrets.

Owen was canonized by Pope Paul VI in 1970 as one of the Forty Martyrs of England and Wales.
