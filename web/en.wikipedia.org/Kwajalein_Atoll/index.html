Kwajalein Atoll (Marshallese: Kuwajleen) is part of the Republic of the Marshall Islands. The southernmost and largest island in the atoll is Kwajalein Island, which plays host to a United States Army installation, the Ronald Reagan Ballistic Missile Defense Test Site (Kwajalein Missile Range). The atoll's vast lagoon, one of the largest in the world at 2,174 km², has for decades been used as a target for intercontinental ballistic missile test launches from California.

History

Kwajalein was administered by Germany, then by Japan after World War I under a League of Nations mandate. In February 1944, during World War II, U.S. forces captured the atoll from Japan in the Battle of Kwajalein, a major amphibious assault. After the war the atoll became part of the U.S.-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, and in 1986 part of the independent Marshall Islands under a Compact of Free Association, which preserves the U.S. Army presence. Marshallese workers commute to the base from the densely populated island of Ebeye.

Geography

The atoll consists of 97 islands and islets around one of the largest coral atoll lagoons in the world. Kwajalein Island lies at the southern end; other significant islands include Ebeye, Roi-Namur, Ebadon and Meck Island, the last used as a missile launch site.
