Papeete (Tahitian: Papeʻete, "water basket") is the capital city of French Polynesia, an overseas collectivity of France in the Pacific Ocean. The commune of Papeete lies on the northwestern coast of the island of Tahiti, the largest island in the Windward group of the Society Islands. It is French Polynesia's primary port, administrative centre, and commercial hub.

History

The area was settled by Polynesian voyagers centuries before European contact. In the late 18th century the missionary William Ellis established a printing press and mission station in the area, and the Pomare dynasty made Papeete the centre of the Kingdom of Tahiti. Queen Pomare IV ruled from 1827 to 1877 amid growing French pressure; in 1842 Tahiti became a French protectorate, and the kingdom was annexed outright in 1880. Papeete grew as the administrative capital of the French Établissements d'Océanie. Early in the First World War, on 22 September 1914, the German armoured cruisers Scharnhorst and Gneisenau bombarded Papeete, sinking a French gunboat and a freighter and damaging the town. The town's growth accelerated after 1960 with the construction of Faaʻa International Airport and the influx associated with the French nuclear testing programme at Moruroa.

Geography and climate

Papeete occupies a narrow coastal strip between the lagoon and the steep volcanic interior of Tahiti Nui, facing the neighbouring island of Moorea across the Sea of the Moon. It has a tropical monsoon climate, with a warm, rainy season from November to April and a relatively drier season from May to October; temperatures remain near 26–31 °C year-round.

Economy and landmarks

The economy centres on government, the port, tourism, and services for the territory's scattered archipelagos. Notable sites include the Papeete Market (Marché de Papeete), a two-storey market selling produce, monoi oil, vanilla, and handicrafts; the Notre Dame Cathedral (completed 1875); the Paofai Temple, French Polynesia's largest Mormon temple complex of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints' predecessor — the Protestant Paofai church; the waterfront Jardins de Paofai; and the Robert Wan Pearl Museum. The annual Heiva festival in July, centred in Papeete, is the highlight of Tahitian dance and sport. Paul Gauguin lived and painted in Papeete in the 1890s, and the town retains associations with the Bounty story — the mutineers' descendants and HMS Bounty called at Tahiti in 1788–89.
