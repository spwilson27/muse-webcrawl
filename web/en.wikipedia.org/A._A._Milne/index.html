A. A. Milne

Alan Alexander Milne (18 January 1882 – 31 January 1956) was an English writer best known for his children's books about the teddy bear Winnie-the-Pooh and the child Christopher Robin, based on his son. Before the Pooh books, Milne was a successful playwright, novelist, and contributor to the humour magazine Punch.

Life

Milne was educated at Westminster School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he edited the student magazine Granta. He served in the First World War and afterwards wrote plays such as Mr Pim Passes By (1919) and the detective novel The Red House Mystery (1922). In 1920 his son Christopher Robin was born, and the stuffed toys Milne gave him inspired the stories set in the Hundred Acre Wood.

Winnie-the-Pooh

Winnie-the-Pooh (1926) and The House at Pooh Corner (1928), illustrated by E. H. Shepard, introduced Pooh, Piglet, Eeyore, Owl, Rabbit, Kanga, Roo, and Tigger. Milne also published two beloved poetry collections for children, When We Were Very Young (1924) and Now We Are Six (1927). Though the books brought him fame, Milne came to resent being typecast as a children's author.
