Mesosphere

The mesosphere is the third layer of the Earth's atmosphere, directly above the stratosphere and directly below the thermosphere. It extends from about 50 km (31 mi) above the surface to about 85 km (53 mi). Temperature decreases with height throughout the mesosphere; its top, the mesopause, is the coldest naturally occurring place on Earth, with temperatures below −143 °C (−225 °F).

Characteristics

The mesosphere is where most meteors burn up on entering the atmosphere. It is too high for aircraft and weather balloons, and too low for satellites to orbit, so it is mainly studied with sounding rockets — making it one of the least understood regions of the atmosphere. The strange noctilucent (night-shining) clouds form in the mesosphere near the poles in summer, from water vapor condensing on meteoric dust.

Dynamics

Atmospheric tides and gravity waves generated in the lower atmosphere propagate into the mesosphere and drive its circulation, including a summer-to-winter pole flow that makes the summer mesopause the coldest part despite continuous sunlight. Transient luminous events such as sprites occur in the mesosphere above large thunderstorms.
