The Old Man and the Sea

The Old Man and the Sea
Author: Ernest Hemingway
Published: 1952
Genre: Novella
Awards: Pulitzer Prize for Fiction (1953)

The Old Man and the Sea is a novella written by the American author Ernest Hemingway in 1951 in Bimini, Bahamas, and published in 1952. It was the last major work of fiction by Hemingway published during his lifetime.

Plot

Santiago, an aging Cuban fisherman, has gone 84 days without catching a fish. On the 85th day he sails far into the Gulf Stream and hooks a giant marlin. For two days and nights he holds the line, enduring pain and exhaustion, before killing the fish and lashing it to his skiff. Sharks attack on the return journey, and despite Santiago's fight they devour the marlin, leaving only its skeleton — yet the old man returns with his dignity intact, dreaming of lions on the beaches of Africa.

Reception

The novella restored Hemingway's reputation, won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 1953, and contributed to his Nobel Prize in Literature in 1954. It has been adapted for film several times, notably the 1958 film starring Spencer Tracy.
