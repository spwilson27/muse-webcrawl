Shadow banning

Shadow banning is the practice of partially blocking or reducing the visibility of a user's content without notifying them.

Shadow banning, also called stealth banning, hell banning, ghost banning or comment ghosting, is the practice of blocking or partially blocking a user or the content they produce from an online community in such a way that the ban is not readily apparent to the user. By deliberately making the user's interactions invisible or less visible to others (and sometimes to the user as well), the hope is that the banned user will not create new accounts to circumvent the restriction, and may eventually become discouraged and leave.

History

The practice originated in early online forums and bulletin board systems. Variants included "hellbanning" on forums such as Something Awful, where a user could continue posting but no one else could see the posts. In the 2010s the term gained wide currency in debates about content moderation on large social media platforms, with allegations that platforms suppressed particular viewpoints without disclosure; platforms generally described such measures as reducing the reach of "borderline" or low-quality content rather than banning accounts outright.

Purposes

Shadow banning is used to combat spam, trolling and abusive behaviour: because spammers who are immediately aware of a ban can simply register a new account, a hidden restriction can waste their effort on posts nobody sees. It has also been used to demote content that violates platform policies short of outright removal. Critics argue that lack of transparency undermines trust and can suppress legitimate speech without recourse to appeal.
