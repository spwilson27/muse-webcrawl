Taxicab

[Placeholder image: Taxicab.png]

A taxicab (also called a taxi or cab) is a type of vehicle for hire with a driver, used by a single passenger or small group of passengers, often for a non-shared ride. A taxicab conveys passengers between locations of their choice, in contrast to public transport, where the pick-up and drop-off locations are decided by the service provider, not by the passengers.

History

Horse-drawn hackney carriages plied for hire in London and Paris from the early 17th century. The first documented public hackney coach service in London was launched in 1605. The electrically powered Hansom cab appeared in the 1830s, and gasoline-powered taxis with taximeters became common in European cities in the early 20th century.

Operation

Taxis are typically regulated by local authorities. Fares may be calculated by a taximeter measuring distance and waiting time, or agreed in advance. Passengers may hail a taxi in the street, at a taxi rank, or book by telephone or app. Ride-hailing services have expanded the market in the 2010s.
