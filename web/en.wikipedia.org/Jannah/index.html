Jannah

In Islam, Jannah (Arabic: جَنّة, romanized: Janna, plural: Jannāt, lit. "garden") is the final destination and abode of the righteous, described as a place of eternal bliss, promised to believers by God (Allah). According to Islamic belief, one's fate in the afterlife is determined by one's deeds in the earthly life: those whose good deeds outweigh their sins may enter Jannah, while wrongdoers may be consigned to Jahannam (hellfire).

The Qur'an describes Jannah as a paradise of gardens beneath which rivers flow, where the blessed will dwell eternally, freed from pain, sorrow, fatigue, and death. Among its promised delights are rivers of pure water, milk, honey, and non-intoxicating wine; abundant fruit; companions; and palaces of gold and silver.

Description in the Qur'an

The Qur'an mentions Jannah many times, offering vivid imagery of shade trees, flowing springs, and gardens. The highest level of paradise is often identified as Al-Firdaws. Other names and levels traditionally enumerated include Jannat al-Mawa, Jannat an-Na'im, Dar al-Khuld, Dar as-Salam, Dar al-Muqamah, Jannat al-'Adn ("Garden of Eden"), and Jannat al-Firdaws. The gates of Jannah are described as eight in number, each associated with a particular category of virtuous deeds, such as prayer, charity, fasting, and pilgrimage.

Entry and conditions

Islamic theology generally holds that entry into Jannah depends on God's mercy as well as on faith and righteous action during earthly life. The concept is closely tied to the Day of Judgment (Yawm al-Qiyamah), when the dead are resurrected and judged. Jannah is contrasted with Jahannam, the place of punishment for the wicked.
