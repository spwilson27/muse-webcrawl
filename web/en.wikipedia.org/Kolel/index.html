Kolel


A kolel is an institute for advanced Talmudic study for married men.

A kolel (or kollel, Hebrew: כולל, pl. kolelim) is an institute for full-time, advanced study of the Talmud and rabbinic literature, primarily attended by married men in Orthodox and Haredi Jewish communities. Students in a kolel typically receive a stipend that enables them to devote themselves to study after marriage, rather than entering the workforce immediately.

History

The modern kolel movement is often traced to Rabbi Yisrael Lipkin Salanter in 19th-century Lithuania and to institutions founded in Kovno (Kaunas); an earlier model existed in the "Kolel Perushim" of the Old Yishuv in Jerusalem, supported by charitable funds (halukka) raised from the diaspora. After the Second World War, kolelim multiplied in Israel and in diaspora communities such as Lakewood, New Jersey, home to Beth Medrash Govoha, one of the largest yeshivas in the world with an extensive kolel.

Structure

Study in a kolel is usually organised around a particular tractate or area of halakha, often in paired study (chavruta) and daily lectures (shiurim), and may prepare students for rabbinic ordination or for communal leadership positions. The growing number of men in long-term kolel study, particularly in Israel, has been a subject of social and economic debate.
