Borghese Gallery

Italian name: Galleria Borghese

Location: Villa Borghese gardens, Rome, Italy

Building: Villa Borghese Pinciana (built 1633–1635, architect Flaminio Ponzio)

Collection founded by: Cardinal Scipione Borghese

The Borghese Gallery (Italian: Galleria Borghese) is an art museum in Rome, Italy, housed in the former Villa Borghese Pinciana within the Villa Borghese gardens. It holds much of the Borghese collection of paintings, sculpture, and antiquities, begun by Cardinal Scipione Borghese, nephew of Pope Paul V.

Collection highlights

The gallery is famed for Gian Lorenzo Bernini's early masterpieces — Apollo and Daphne, The Rape of Proserpina, and David — and for Antonio Canova's Paolina Borghese as Venus Victrix. Paintings include several major works by Caravaggio (David with the Head of Goliath, Boy with a Basket of Fruit, Saint Jerome Writing), Titian's Sacred and Profane Love, Raphael's Deposition, and works by Rubens, Correggio, and Domenichino.

Visiting

The villa's frescoed rooms, including Mariano Rossi's ceiling in the entrance hall, form part of the display. Because of the museum's small size, entry is by reservation, with visitors admitted in timed groups. The surrounding Villa Borghese gardens are Rome's third-largest public park.
