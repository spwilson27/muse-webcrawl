Nibiru cataclysm

The Nibiru cataclysm is a supposed disastrous encounter between Earth and a large planetary object, variously called Nibiru or Planet X, that certain groups believed would occur in the early 21st century. The idea is considered pseudoscience by astronomers and planetary scientists, who note that such an object would already be visible and gravitationally disruptive.

Origins

The concept originated with Nancy Lieder, who claimed in 1995 to receive messages from inhabitants of the Zeta Reticuli system warning of a close passage in 2003. Lieder borrowed the name Nibiru from Zecharia Sitchin's "ancient astronaut" writings about a supposed Sumerian planet on a 3,600-year orbit. When 2003 passed uneventfully, believers shifted the prediction to 2012, linking it to popular interpretations of the end of a cycle in the Maya calendar, and later to further dates promoted by figures such as David Meade in 2017.

Scientific response

NASA and other scientists repeatedly stated there is no evidence for such a planet: a Sun-bound object big enough to matter and due to arrive within years would be among the brightest objects in the sky and would perturb the known planets' orbits. The idea is frequently cited as an example of an internet-age doomsday hoax.
