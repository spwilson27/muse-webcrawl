Arthur Strong Wightman (30 March 1922 – 13 January 2013) was an American mathematical physicist best known as one of the founders of axiomatic quantum field theory. His formulation of the Wightman axioms in the 1950s gave a rigorous mathematical framework for relativistic quantum fields.

Career

Wightman studied at Yale University and completed his doctorate at Princeton University under John Archibald Wheeler, with a dissertation on the hydrogen atom problem in quantum electrodynamics. He joined the Princeton faculty and spent most of his career there, becoming Thomas D. Jones Professor of Mathematical Physics.

Work

Working with Lars Gårding, Wightman set out axioms that any relativistic quantum field theory should satisfy, treating fields as operator-valued distributions. The framework led to important structural theorems, including the PCT theorem and the spin–statistics connection, proved rigorously within the axiomatic approach. His book PCT, Spin and Statistics, and All That (1964), written with Ray Streater, became a standard reference.

Honours

Wightman received the Dannie Heineman Prize for Mathematical Physics in 1969 and the Henri Poincaré Prize in 1997. He supervised many students who went on to shape mathematical physics, including Barry Simon and Arthur Jaffe.
