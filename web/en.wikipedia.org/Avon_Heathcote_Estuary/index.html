Avon Heathcote Estuary

The Avon Heathcote Estuary (Māori: Te Ihutai) is a shallow tidal estuary of about 880 hectares on the coast of Christchurch, New Zealand, where the Avon River (Ōtākaro) and the Heathcote River (Ōpāwaho) meet the Pacific Ocean at Pegasus Bay, between the suburbs of New Brighton and Sumner.

Ecology

Its mudflats, salt marshes and eelgrass beds support large numbers of wading birds, including migratory eastern bar-tailed godwits that arrive each spring from Alaska, black swans, and the endangered black stilt's relatives among other shorebirds; it is recognised as a wetland of international significance. For Māori the estuary was a major mahinga kai (food-gathering) area for shellfish, flounder and eels.

Changes

For much of the twentieth century treated sewage was discharged into the estuary; since 2010 Christchurch's treated wastewater has instead been discharged offshore. The 2010–2011 Canterbury earthquakes caused liquefaction, subsidence and large volumes of silt to enter the estuary, altering its bed level and ecology, from which it has been gradually recovering.
