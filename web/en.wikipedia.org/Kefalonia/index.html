Kefalonia or Cephalonia (Greek: Κεφαλονιά), formerly also known as Kefallinia or Kephallenia, is the largest of the Ionian Islands in western Greece and the 6th largest island in Greece overall, after Crete, Euboea, Lesbos, Rhodes and Chios. It is also a separate regional unit of the Ionian Islands region. The capital of Kefalonia is Argostoli.

Geography

Kefalonia lies in the Ionian Sea, north of Zakynthos and south of Lefkada, off the western coast of mainland Greece. The island covers an area of about 781 km² (302 sq mi) and has a rugged coastline about 254 km long. Its highest mountain is Mount Ainos (also rendered Aenos), at 1,628 m (5,341 ft), famous for its fir forest of Abies cephalonica, the Cephalonian fir. Notable beaches include Myrtos, Antisamos, Lourdas and Skala, and natural attractions include the Melissani cave lake and the Drogarati Cave.

History

The island has been inhabited since the Palaeolithic era and, in the Bronze Age theories of some scholars, has been identified with Homeric Ithaca — a view famously promoted by the amateur archaeologist Robert Bittlestone in his book Odysseus Unbound. In antiquity the island contained four city-states (a tetrapolis): Pale, Cranii, Sami and Pronnoi. Later ruled by Rome and Byzantium, it passed through Norman, Venetian, Ottoman and French hands, and was a British protectorate as part of the United States of the Ionian Islands before union with Greece in 1864. During the Second World War the island was occupied first by Italy and then by Germany; the Italian Acqui Division was massacred by German forces in September 1943, an event depicted in the novel and film Captain Corelli's Mandolin. In August 1953 a catastrophic earthquake (magnitude 7.2) destroyed most buildings on the island, sparing only the northern Fiskardo area.

Economy and tourism

The island's economy rests largely on tourism, agriculture and fishing. Kefalonia is known for Robola wine, olive oil, and honey. The port town of Fiskardo, the castle of Saint George, and the villages of Assos and Sami attract large numbers of summer visitors. Kefalonia International Airport connects the island with Athens and seasonal European destinations.
