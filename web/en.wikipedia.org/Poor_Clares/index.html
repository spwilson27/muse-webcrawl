The Poor Clares, officially the Order of Saint Clare (Latin: Ordo Sanctae Clarae, OSC), are a contemplative order of nuns in the Catholic Church. Founded by Saint Clare of Assisi with the guidance of Saint Francis of Assisi in 1212, they were the second Franciscan order to be established and are sometimes called the Second Order of Saint Francis.

History

On Palm Sunday 1212, the young Clare Offreduccio left her noble family in Assisi and received the religious habit from Francis at the Porziuncola. She and her first companions settled at the church of San Damiano, where the community — first called the "Poor Ladies" — lived in radical poverty and enclosure. Clare fought for decades to secure approval of her own Rule, which insisted that the sisters own nothing, individually or in common: the so-called "Privilege of Poverty". Her Rule was approved by Pope Innocent IV in 1253, two days before her death.

Life and branches

Poor Clares live an enclosed, contemplative life centred on prayer, the Liturgy of the Hours, adoration of the Blessed Sacrament and manual work, supported by alms. Following later reforms the order divided into several observances — including the Colettine Poor Clares (reformed by Saint Colette in the 15th century), the Capuchin Poor Clares (founded 1535) and the Poor Clares of Perpetual Adoration — differing in details of austerity and enclosure.

Today

There are several thousand Poor Clare nuns in monasteries around the world. Well-known modern houses include the monastery at Arundel in England and communities associated with Mother Angelica in the United States, whose sisters staff the Eternal Word Television Network's shrine in Alabama.
