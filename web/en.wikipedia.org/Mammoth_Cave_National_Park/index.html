Mammoth Cave National Park

Mammoth Cave National Park is a national park of the United States in south-central Kentucky. It encompasses portions of Mammoth Cave, the longest known cave system in the world. The park's 52,830 acres (21,380 ha) are located primarily in Edmonson County, with small areas extending eastward into Hart and Barren counties. The Green River runs through the park, with a tributary called the Nolin River feeding into the Green just inside the park.

The cave

The Mammoth Cave system has more than 426 miles (686 km) of surveyed passageways, a figure that increases with continued exploration. The cave formed in thick Mississippian limestone beneath a sandstone caprock, dissolved by slightly acidic groundwater over millions of years. It preserves evidence of use by prehistoric peoples, including cane-reed torches and artefacts dating back several thousand years, and of saltpetre mining during the War of 1812. In the 19th century the cave became a famous tourist attraction; the enslaved guide Stephen Bishop was among the first to map large sections and crossed the "Bottomless Pit" in 1838.

History of the park

Private owners promoted the cave for tours from 1816 onward, and rival cave attractions fuelled the "Kentucky Cave Wars" of the early 20th century. The Mammoth Cave National Park Association, formed in 1924, raised funds to buy land; the park was authorised in 1926, established in 1941, and dedicated in 1946. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981 and the core of an International Biosphere Reserve in 1990.

Ecology and recreation

Above ground, the park's forests, sinkholes, springs and river corridors host white-tailed deer, wild turkey and a rich diversity of plants. The cave harbours specialised fauna including eyeless cavefish and the endangered Kentucky cave shrimp, and its bat colonies have been affected by white-nose syndrome. Ranger-led cave tours range from short lantern walks to strenuous wild-cave expeditions; surface activities include hiking, camping, canoeing on the Green River and horseback riding.
