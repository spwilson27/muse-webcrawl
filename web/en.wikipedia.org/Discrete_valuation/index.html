Discrete valuation

Discrete valuation
FieldAbstract algebra, algebraic number theory, algebraic geometry
Codomainℤ ∪ {∞}
Key examplep-adic valuation on ℚ

In abstract algebra, a discrete valuation is an integer-valued function defined on a field K that measures the "size" or divisibility of elements. Formally, a discrete valuation is a surjective function

v : K× → ℤ

satisfying v(xy) = v(x) + v(y) and v(x + y) ≥ min(v(x), v(y)) for all x, y, extended by v(0) = ∞. The image is a discrete subgroup of the additive reals, which accounts for the name. The set of elements with non-negative valuation forms a discrete valuation ring (DVR), a local principal ideal domain with a unique maximal ideal generated by a uniformizer π with v(π) = 1.

Examples

The canonical example is the p-adic valuation: for a prime p, vp(a/b) counts the exponent of p in the prime factorization of the rational number a/b (positive if p divides the numerator, negative if it divides the denominator). Other examples arise from the order of vanishing of a rational function at a point on an algebraic curve, and from power series expansions. Discrete valuations are central to algebraic number theory, where they describe prime ideals in rings of integers, and to algebraic geometry, where they are associated with irreducible divisors on varieties.
