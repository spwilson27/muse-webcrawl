Multilineal evolution

FieldAnthropology
Key figureJulian Steward
Contrasts withUnilineal evolution

Multilineal evolution is a mid-20th-century anthropological theory, developed chiefly by Julian Steward, which holds that cultures evolve along multiple, divergent pathways rather than through a single universal sequence of stages.

Background

Nineteenth-century unilineal theories (such as those of Lewis Henry Morgan) arranged all societies on one ladder from "savagery" to "civilisation". Steward instead argued that similar environments and technologies could produce similar cultural adaptations — the approach he called cultural ecology — while different environments sent societies along different tracks, so that parallels in development occur only in limited, comparable cases.

Influence

Multilineal evolution helped move anthropology toward ecological and comparative explanations of cultural change and influenced later work in archaeology and political anthropology.
