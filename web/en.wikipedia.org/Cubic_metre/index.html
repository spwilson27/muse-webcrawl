Cubic metre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The cubic metre (in Commonwealth English and the BIPM spelling) or cubic meter (in American English) is the unit of volume in the International System of Units (SI). Its symbol is m³. It is the volume of a cube with edges one metre in length. One cubic metre equals 1,000 litres, about 35.3 cubic feet, or about 1.31 cubic yards.

Conversions

A cubic metre of pure water at maximum density (approximately 3.98 °C) and standard atmospheric pressure has a mass of 1,000 kilograms, or one tonne. Subdivisions include the cubic decimetre (equal to one litre) and the cubic centimetre (equal to one millilitre). Large volumes, such as reservoirs and ocean quantities, may be expressed in cubic kilometres.

Usage and names

The cubic metre is the standard unit for measuring volumes of liquids, gases, bulk materials, timber, and concrete in most of the world. Alternative names include the stère, used for firewood and equal to one cubic metre, and the kilolitre, which is exactly one cubic metre. Unicode provides a dedicated character for the unit, ㎥ (U+33A5).
