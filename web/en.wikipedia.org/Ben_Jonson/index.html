Ben Jonson

Benjamin Jonson (c. 11 June 1572 – 18 August 1637) was an English playwright and poet. Jonson's artistry exerted a lasting influence upon English poetry and stage comedy. He popularised the comedy of humours; he is best known for the satirical plays Every Man in His Humour (1598), Volpone, or The Fox (c. 1606), The Alchemist (1610) and Bartholomew Fair (1614) and for his lyric and epigrammatic poetry. He is generally regarded as the second most important English dramatist, after William Shakespeare, during the reign of James I.

Life and career

Jonson was educated at Westminster School under the antiquarian William Camden. After brief work as a bricklayer and soldier in Flanders, he became an actor and playwright. In 1598 he killed fellow actor Gabriel Spenser in a duel and escaped execution by pleading benefit of clergy. Under James I he became the leading writer of court masques, often collaborating with designer Inigo Jones. In 1616 he published his collected Works — a then-unusual step for a playwright — and received a royal pension, making him effectively the first poet laureate. Younger writers, the "Tribe of Ben", gathered around him at the Devil's Head tavern.

Legacy

Jonson was buried in Westminster Abbey under the inscription "O rare Ben Jonson". His classical learning, theory of the humours and disciplined plotting shaped Restoration comedy and later criticism.
