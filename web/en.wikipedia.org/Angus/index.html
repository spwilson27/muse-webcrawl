Angus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Angus (Scots: Angus; Scottish Gaelic: Aonghas) is one of the 32 local government council areas of Scotland, and a historic county and registration county on the east coast. It borders Aberdeenshire, Perth and Kinross, and Dundee City. The council area has a population of roughly 116,000, and its administrative centre is Forfar.

Geography

Angus divides into distinct landscapes: the fertile lowland of Strathmore in the centre, the Angus Glens — Glen Isla, Glen Prosen, Glen Clova and Glen Esk — cutting into the Grampian Mountains in the north, and a North Sea coastline of cliffs and sandy beaches between Dundee and Montrose. Towns include Arbroath, Brechin, Carnoustie, Kirriemuir, Monifieth and Montrose.

History

The county was formerly known as Forfarshire. Arbroath Abbey is famous as the place where the Declaration of Arbroath, asserting Scottish independence, was drawn up in 1320. Angus has strong associations with the Picts, whose carved stones survive at Aberlemno and elsewhere. Carnoustie is internationally known for its championship golf links.

Economy

Agriculture, food processing and tourism are important, along with the soft fruit for which the area is noted. The Arbroath smokie — haddock smoked over hardwood — is a protected local delicacy.
