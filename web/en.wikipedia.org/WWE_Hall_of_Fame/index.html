WWE Hall of Fame


Established: 1993 (as the WWF Hall of Fame)
First inductee: André the Giant
Ceremony: Annually, during WrestleMania weekend
Inductees: More than 200 individuals and groups

The WWE Hall of Fame is a hall of fame that honours professional wrestlers and personalities associated with the American promotion WWE. It was established in 1993 as the WWF Hall of Fame; unlike most sports halls of fame, it has no physical building, existing instead as an annual induction ceremony and a body of honorees.

History

The hall's first and only inductee in 1993 was André the Giant, honoured shortly after his death. Annual inductions continued until 1996, followed by a hiatus. The ceremony returned in 2004 as part of WrestleMania weekend, and it has since grown into one of WWE's flagship annual events, typically held the night before — or, in recent years, the Friday before — WrestleMania. The 2020 and 2021 classes were inducted together in 2021 after the pandemic disrupted the 2020 ceremony.

Categories

In addition to individual wrestlers, the Hall of Fame includes tag teams and stables (such as D-Generation X and The Four Horsemen), a Celebrity Wing (inductees have included Mr. T, Drew Carey, Snoop Dogg and Donald Trump), a Legacy Wing recognising early pioneers of the sport, and the annual Warrior Award, created in 2015 to honour individuals who have shown strength and perseverance in their communities.

Ceremony and criticism

Inductees receive a Hall of Fame ring and are introduced by a chosen presenter. Because selections are made by WWE itself rather than by voters or a committee, the Hall of Fame has periodically been criticised for omissions and for the timing of inductions; some major stars of rival promotions were inducted only decades after their prime years.
