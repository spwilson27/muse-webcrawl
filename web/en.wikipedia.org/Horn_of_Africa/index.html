Horn of Africa

The Horn of Africa (HoA), also known as the Somali Peninsula, is a large peninsula of East Africa located on the easternmost part of the African mainland. It lies along the southern side of the Red Sea and extends hundreds of kilometres into the Guardafui Channel, the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean. The region comprises the countries of Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia and Somalia, and is home to well over 140 million people.

Geography

The Horn is dominated by the Ethiopian Highlands, a rugged massif split by the Great Rift Valley, which gives rise to some of Africa's highest peaks, including Ras Dashen. Lowlands along the coasts are arid or semi-arid; the Danakil Depression in the Afar region is one of the hottest and lowest places on Earth. The region's long coastline — the longest of any African region — borders the Red Sea, the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean, and commands the strategic Bab el-Mandeb strait.

History

The Horn is one of the cradles of humankind: the Afar Depression has yielded famous hominin fossils, including "Lucy" (Australopithecus afarensis). In antiquity the region was known to Egyptians as the Land of Punt, and it later hosted the kingdoms of D'mt and Aksum, the latter a major trading empire and one of the first states to adopt Christianity. Medieval centuries saw the rise of Muslim sultanates such as Ifat and Adal alongside the Christian Ethiopian Empire. In the late nineteenth century the region was partitioned among Italy, Britain and France, while Ethiopia under Menelik II famously repelled Italian invasion at Adwa in 1896. The twentieth century brought Italian occupation, decolonisation, the Ogaden War between Ethiopia and Somalia, and prolonged conflicts, droughts and famines that remain central concerns.

People and culture

The region is ethnically and linguistically diverse. Major peoples include the Oromo, Amhara, Tigray, Somali, Afar and Sidama; languages belong chiefly to the Cushitic and Ethiosemitic branches of the Afroasiatic family, and Ge'ez script survives in liturgical use. Ethiopia counts among the world's oldest Christian nations, while Islam has deep roots along the coasts and in Somalia. Pastoralism, coffee — first domesticated in Ethiopia's Kaffa region — and the port trade of Berbera, Zeila and Massawa have long shaped its economies.
