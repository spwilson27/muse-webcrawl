José Guadalupe Posada
Born: 2 February 1852, Aguascalientes
Died: 20 January 1913, Mexico City
Known for: Printmaking, engraving, calaveras

José Guadalupe Posada Aguilar (2 February 1852 – 20 January 1913) was a Mexican political printmaker and engraver whose work has influenced many Latin American artists and cartoonists because of its satirical acuteness and social engagement.

Career

Posada produced thousands of images for penny broadsheets (hojas volantes) published by Antonio Vanegas Arroyo, covering sensational crimes, disasters, politics and religion. He worked mainly in zinc relief etching and type-metal engraving.

Calaveras

His most famous images are the calaveras — satirical skeleton figures — notably La Calavera Catrina, originally called La Calavera de la Catrina, mocking the Mexican upper classes. These images became closely associated with the Day of the Dead (Día de Muertos).

Legacy

Posada died in relative obscurity in 1913, but was rediscovered in the 1920s by artists of the Mexican muralist movement, including Diego Rivera, who championed him as a progenitor of modern Mexican art.
