The Battle of Lechfeld, also known as the Battle of Augsburg, was fought on 10 August 955 between the forces of King Otto I of Germany and the Hungarian harka Bulcsú's army of Magyar horsemen. The German victory ended the Magyar invasions of Western Europe that had continued for decades.

Background

Hungarian raiders had ravaged Central and Western Europe since the late ninth century. In the summer of 955 a large Hungarian army besieged Augsburg. Otto marched to relieve the city with an army drawn from the German duchies, supported by Bohemian troops.

The battle

On the Lechfeld plain south of Augsburg, Otto's heavy cavalry engaged the Hungarian horse archers. A Hungarian detachment that crossed the river to attack the German rear was defeated, and in the main battle the German armoured riders broke through the Magyar ranks. Many Hungarians drowned in the Lech during the rout, and captured leaders including Bulcsú were executed at Regensburg.

Aftermath

The victory consolidated Otto's kingship — his troops are said to have acclaimed him imperator on the field — and permanently ended Hungarian raids to the west. The Hungarians subsequently settled and founded the Christian kingdom of Hungary under Stephen I.
