Douglas Mawson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sir Douglas Mawson OBE FRS FAA (5 May 1882 – 14 October 1958) was an Australian geologist, Antarctic explorer and academic. He led the Australasian Antarctic Expedition (1911–1914) and is famous for his lone survival trek, one of the great feats of endurance in exploration history.

Exploration

Born in Shipley, England, Mawson emigrated to Sydney as a child and studied at the University of Sydney. He joined Ernest Shackleton's Nimrod Expedition (1907–1909), taking part in the first ascent of Mount Erebus and the first journey to the South Magnetic Pole. Declining a place on Scott's Terra Nova expedition, he organised his own Australasian Antarctic Expedition, based at Cape Denison in Commonwealth Bay — one of the windiest places on Earth.

The Far Eastern Trek

In November 1912 Mawson set out with Xavier Mertz and Belgrave Ninnis to survey eastward. Ninnis fell into a crevasse with the main sledge and most supplies; on the return journey Mertz died, and Mawson, starving and frostbitten, hauled himself over some 160 km alone, surviving a fall into a crevasse by climbing out on his rope. He reached base hours after the relief ship had left, and had to winter another year. He later led the BANZARE voyages (1929–1931), claiming Macquarie Island and Antarctic territory for Australia.

Later life

Knighted in 1914, Mawson became professor of geology at the University of Adelaide, working on the geology of South Australia and the Flinders Ranges. He died in Adelaide in 1958. Mawson Station in Antarctica, founded in 1954, bears his name, and his image appeared on the Australian $100 note.
