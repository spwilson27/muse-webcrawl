Spruce

A spruce is a tree of the genus Picea, a genus of about 40 species of coniferous evergreen trees in the family Pinaceae, found in the northern temperate and boreal (taiga) regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

Description

Spruces are large trees, reaching 20–60 metres tall when mature, with whorled branches and a conical form. Their needle-like leaves are attached singly to the branches on small peg-like structures (pulvini) and are roughly square in cross-section, unlike the flat needles of firs. Cones hang downward and have thin scales.

Uses

Spruce wood (often sold as whitewood) is used for construction timber, paper pulp, and soundboards of stringed instruments; Norway spruce is a common Christmas tree. Species include the Norway spruce (Picea abies), Sitka spruce (P. sitchensis) and blue spruce (P. pungens).
