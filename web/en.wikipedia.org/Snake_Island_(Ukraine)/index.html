Snake Island (Ukraine)

Snake Island and its lighthouse, viewed from the air

Snake Island (Ukrainian: Острів Зміїний, Ostriv Zmiinyi; Romanian: Insula Șerpilor), also known as Serpent Island or Zmiinyi Island, is a Ukrainian island in the Black Sea, near the Danube Delta and the maritime border with Romania. The island is about 35 km from the coast, covers roughly 17 hectares, and rises to around 41 m above sea level. It is part of Izmail Raion of Odesa Oblast and hosts a small settlement, Bile, a lighthouse, and border guard and research facilities.

History

In antiquity the island was associated with Achilles and known as Leuke, the "White Island", with a temple to the hero. It passed through Byzantine, Genoese and Ottoman hands before the Russian Empire annexed it in the 19th century. Romania controlled it after the First World War; in 1948 it was transferred to the Soviet Union, a transfer Romania long contested. A 2009 ruling of the International Court of Justice delimited the surrounding continental shelf between Romania and Ukraine, granting most of the disputed area to Romania while confirming Ukrainian sovereignty over the island itself.

2022 battle

Snake Island became internationally famous on 24 February 2022, the first day of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, when the Russian cruiser Moskva demanded the surrender of the island's Ukrainian border guards and a guardsman replied with a profane refusal — "Russian warship, go fuck yourself" — which became a rallying cry in Ukraine and was commemorated on a postage stamp. The garrison was captured after bombardment. Ukraine subjected the Russian occupation to repeated strikes, and Russian forces withdrew on 30 June 2022; Ukrainian forces subsequently re-established control.
