Portamento

In music, portamento (plural: portamenti; from the Italian portamento della voce, "carriage of the voice") is a pitch sliding from one note to another without a break in the sound. The term originated from the Italian expression "portamento della voce" and was first used in the 17th century in Italy for the vocal technique of gliding smoothly between pitches.

Usage

Portamento is natural to instruments capable of continuous pitch variation, such as the human voice, string instruments, trombone, and theremin. It was a prominent expressive device in 19th- and early 20th-century vocal and string performance practice, heard in historical recordings of singers and violinists, and fell somewhat out of fashion in later 20th-century classical performance, where it came to be considered old-fashioned if overused — though it remains common in popular music styles, including blues, jazz, and rock singing.

Portamento is distinguished from glissando: while usage is inconsistent, glissando is often reserved for a rapid slide passing audibly through intermediate discrete pitches (as in a scale played by sliding a finger along a piano's keys), whereas portamento is a smooth, continuous slide in which intermediate pitches are not distinctly articulated. On synthesizers, "portamento" is the standard name for the glide function that interpolates pitch between successive notes, a hallmark of monophonic synthesizer solos and electronic music.
