Episcopal see

An episcopal see is, in the usual meaning, the area of a bishop's ecclesiastical jurisdiction — essentially synonymous with a diocese. The word "see" derives from the Latin sedes, meaning "seat", referring to the bishop's throne (cathedra) in the cathedral church, which symbolizes the bishop's authority to teach and govern.

A cathedra, the ceremonial seat from which the concept of the see takes its name

Usage

The term is especially common in the Roman Catholic Church, where phrases such as "the See of Rome" refer to the office and jurisdiction of the Pope as bishop of Rome; the Holy See (Sancta Sedes) denotes the central governing authority of the Catholic Church and is a sovereign subject of international law. Sees are commonly identified by the name of their principal city: for example, the See of Canterbury in the Church of England, whose archbishop's throne is at Canterbury Cathedral.

Related terms

A see whose bishop has died or resigned is described as sede vacante ("the seat being vacant"). A "titular see" is a diocese that no longer functions residentially but whose title is conferred on auxiliary or diplomatic bishops. Sees founded by apostles may be styled "apostolic sees".
