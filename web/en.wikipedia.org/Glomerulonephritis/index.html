Glomerulonephritis

Glomerulonephritis
SpecialtyNephrology
SymptomsBlood or protein in urine, swelling, high blood pressure, reduced urine output
CausesImmune-mediated injury, infections, autoimmune disease
DiagnosisUrinalysis, blood tests, kidney biopsy

Glomerulonephritis (GN) is a group of diseases characterised by inflammation of the glomeruli, the tiny capillary tufts in the kidney that filter the blood. It may present acutely or chronically and ranges from mild, asymptomatic urinary abnormalities to rapidly progressive kidney failure.

Presentation

Glomerular inflammation classically produces a nephritic syndrome: haematuria (blood in the urine, often with red-cell casts), proteinuria, hypertension, oedema and a fall in glomerular filtration rate. Heavy protein leakage may instead produce a nephrotic syndrome with marked oedema and low serum albumin. Some patients have an overlap of both.

Causes

Most forms are immune-mediated. IgA nephropathy is the commonest primary glomerulonephritis worldwide. Other causes include post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis following throat or skin infection, lupus nephritis in systemic lupus erythematosus, anti-glomerular basement membrane (Goodpasture) disease, and ANCA-associated vasculitis. Rapidly progressive ("crescentic") glomerulonephritis can destroy kidney function within days to weeks and is a medical emergency.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation includes urinalysis, measurement of kidney function and protein excretion, serological tests and, in most adults, a kidney biopsy, which defines the pattern of injury under light microscopy, immunofluorescence and electron microscopy. Treatment targets the underlying cause — immunosuppression for many primary and autoimmune forms — together with blood-pressure control, salt restriction and renin–angiotensin system blockade to limit progression to chronic kidney disease.
