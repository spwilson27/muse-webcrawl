Thor Odinson is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by editor-plotter Stan Lee, scripted by Larry Lieber and designed by artist Jack Kirby, the character first appeared in Journey into Mystery #83 (cover dated August 1962). Based on the Norse mythological god Thor, he is the Asgardian God of Thunder, whose enchanted hammer Mjolnir can only be lifted by the worthy, and a founding member of the superhero team the Avengers.

Publication history

Lee wanted a hero stronger than any human and hit upon a god as the protagonist. In the original stories, the lame physician Donald Blake discovered a walking stick in a Norwegian cave that transformed into Mjolnir and granted him Thor's form and powers; later retcons established Blake as an artificial identity and Thor as the true person. The series was renamed The Mighty Thor, and notable runs include Walt Simonson's celebrated 1980s tenure, which introduced Beta Ray Bill and retold the Ragnarök cycle, and the stories of J. Michael Straczynski and Jason Aaron, the latter introducing Jane Foster as a new Thor.

Fictional biography

Thor is the son of Odin, king of Asgard, and the elder goddess Gaea. Banished to Earth to learn humility, he became one of its foremost protectors. His recurring adversaries include his adoptive brother Loki, the Enchantress, the Executioner, Hela, Surtur, Malekith and Gorr the God Butcher. Wielder of Mjolnir — and later of the axe Stormbreaker and the Odinforce — Thor has died and returned more than once, lost and regained his worthiness, and served as king of Asgard.

In other media

In the Marvel Cinematic Universe, Thor is portrayed by Chris Hemsworth, debuting in Thor (2011) and appearing in the Avengers films and sequels including Thor: Ragnarok (2017) and Thor: Love and Thunder (2022). The character has also appeared in numerous animated series, video games and merchandise.
