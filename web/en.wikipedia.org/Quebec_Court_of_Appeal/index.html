Quebec Court of Appeal

LocationMontreal and Quebec City, Quebec
Established1849
Appeals toSupreme Court of Canada
CompositionChief Justice of Quebec and puisne judges (22 authorized)

The Quebec Court of Appeal (French: Cour d'appel du Québec) is the highest judicial court in the province of Quebec, Canada. It hears appeals from the Superior Court and the Court of Québec, as well as references from the provincial government on questions of law.

Organization

The court sits principally in Montreal, at the Ernest-Cormier Building, and in Quebec City. Its judges are appointed by the federal government; the head of the court, the Chief Justice of Quebec, is the province's chief justice. Panels usually consist of three judges, though five may sit in important cases.

Role in the bijural system

Because Quebec maintains a civil-law tradition for private law within Canada's otherwise common-law federation, the Court of Appeal plays a distinctive role, interpreting the Civil Code of Québec; by convention, three of the nine justices of the Supreme Court of Canada come from Quebec.
