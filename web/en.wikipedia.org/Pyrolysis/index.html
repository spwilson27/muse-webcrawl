Pyrolysis
TypeThermochemical decomposition
ConditionsHigh temperature, absence of oxygen
ProductsChar (biochar), pyrolysis oil, syngas
ApplicationsCharcoal production, waste treatment, biofuels, chemical feedstocks

Pyrolysis is a process involving the separation of covalent bonds in organic matter by thermal decomposition at elevated temperatures, typically between 400 and 800 °C, in the near-absence of oxygen. The word derives from the Greek pyr (fire) and lysis (separation). Unlike combustion and hydrolysis, pyrolysis does not involve reaction with oxygen or water.

Process and products

Pyrolysis of organic materials yields three product streams: a solid rich in carbon (char or biochar), a condensable liquid (pyrolysis oil, tar or bio-oil), and non-condensable gases (syngas, containing hydrogen, methane, carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide). Product ratios depend on temperature and heating rate: slow pyrolysis maximises char, while fast (flash) pyrolysis at around 500 °C with rapid heating maximises liquid yield.

Applications

  • Charcoal making — one of humanity's oldest chemical processes
  • Production of biochar for soil improvement and carbon sequestration
  • Conversion of waste plastics and tyres into fuels and feedstocks
  • Industrial production of chemicals such as methanol (historically from wood distillation) and activated carbon
  • Torrefaction and carbonisation of biomass for energy
