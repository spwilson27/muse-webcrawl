Marble House

Location: 596 Bellevue Avenue, Newport, Rhode Island
Built: 1888–1892
Architect: Richard Morris Hunt
Style: Beaux-Arts
Owner: Preservation Society of Newport County (museum)

Marble House is a Gilded Age mansion in Newport, Rhode Island, built between 1888 and 1892 for William Kissam Vanderbilt and his wife Alva. Designed by architect Richard Morris Hunt in the Beaux-Arts style, it was one of the most lavish houses in America and helped transform Newport into a resort of palatial summer "cottages".

History

The house was a 39th-birthday present from William to Alva Vanderbilt, reportedly costing about $11 million, of which some $7 million was spent on marble — half a million cubic feet of it, much imported from Italy and North Africa. After the Vanderbilts divorced, Alva remarried Oliver Belmont and later used Marble House in the women's suffrage cause, hosting rallies and building a Chinese-style tea house on the grounds. The house was sold in 1932 and acquired by the Preservation Society of Newport County in 1963.

Architecture

Modelled in part on the Petit Trianon at Versailles, the U-shaped mansion fronts Bellevue Avenue with a Corinthian portico. Interiors by the Paris firm Allard and Sons include a gold ballroom, a Gothic Revival library and a grand hall faced in yellow Sienna marble. The building is a National Historic Landmark and is open to the public as a museum.
