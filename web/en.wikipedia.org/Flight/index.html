Flight

Flight or flying is the process by which an object moves through a space without contacting any planetary surface, either within an atmosphere (aeroplane flight) or through the vacuum of outer space (spaceflight). This can be achieved by generating aerodynamic lift associated with gliding or propulsive thrust, aerostatically using buoyancy, or by ballistic movement.

Types of flight

Animals such as birds, bats and insects achieve powered flight by flapping wings; many other creatures glide. Human flight began with kites and gliders, progressed to airships and balloons, and culminated in powered aeroplanes after the Wright brothers' first sustained controlled flight in 1903. Spaceflight, achieved in the mid-20th century, extends flight beyond the atmosphere using rockets.

Physics

Flight in an atmosphere involves four principal forces: lift, weight (gravity), thrust and drag. Lift is generated as air flows over a wing-shaped surface; thrust overcomes drag, and lift must equal or exceed weight for the aircraft to rise. The study of these principles is aerodynamics.
