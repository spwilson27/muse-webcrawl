Arthur's Seat
Elevation251 m (823 ft)
LocationHolyrood Park, Edinburgh, Scotland
GeologyExtinct volcano (Carboniferous)

Arthur's Seat is the main peak of the group of hills in Edinburgh, Scotland, which form most of Holyrood Park. It lies about a mile east of Edinburgh Castle and rises 251 metres (823 ft) above the city, providing panoramic views. The hill is the eroded remnant of a volcano that was active around 340 million years ago during the Carboniferous period.

Name

The origin of the name is uncertain. It is sometimes associated with legends of King Arthur, though it may derive from Gaelic Àrd-na-Said, "height of arrows". William Maitland proposed "Archer's Seat", and other derivations connect it to the legendary hero.

Geology and history

Arthur's Seat is a classic example of a crag-and-tail landscape sculpted by glacial action, and its volcanic structure helped James Hutton develop foundational ideas in geology. A hill fort once occupied its slopes. In 1836, seventeen miniature coffins were found in a cave on the hill, an enduring mystery sometimes linked to the Burke and Hare murders.
