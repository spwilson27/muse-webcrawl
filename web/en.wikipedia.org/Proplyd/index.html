Proplyd

A proplyd, short for ionized protoplanetary disk, is a protoplanetary disk around a young star undergoing photoevaporation by ultraviolet radiation from nearby massive stars. The term arose from Hubble Space Telescope observations of the Orion Nebula in the 1990s.

Characteristics

Proplyds appear as bright teardrop or silhouette disks, often with ionization fronts and tails pointing away from the hot stars of the Trapezium cluster. Their study shows planet formation proceeding in harsh environments, while photoevaporation may truncate disk lifetimes and limit planet growth. Hundreds are catalogued in Orion, with analogues found in other star-forming regions such as Carina.
