The Justice League (originally the Justice League of America) is a team of superheroes appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. Created by writer Gardner Fox, the team first appeared in The Brave and the Bold #28 (1960) and became one of the most popular superhero teams of the Silver Age of Comic Books.

History of publication

Inspired by the success of the revived Flash and Green Lantern, editor Julius Schwartz asked Gardner Fox to revive the Justice Society of America; Fox instead created a new team, renamed the "Justice League of America" because he judged "League" would appeal to young readers familiar with baseball leagues. The founding line-up — Superman, Batman, Wonder Woman, Aquaman, Barry Allen's Flash, Hal Jordan's Green Lantern and the Martian Manhunter — soon received its own title, Justice League of America (1960).

The roster has expanded and changed over decades, adding heroes such as Green Arrow, the Atom, Hawkman, Black Canary, Cyborg and Shazam. Major reboots include the 1987 Justice League International, Grant Morrison's 1997 JLA revival restoring the "big seven", and the 2011 New 52 relaunch.

In other media

The team has starred in animated series including Super Friends (1973–1985), Justice League (2001–2004) and Justice League Unlimited (2004–2006). A live-action film, Justice League, was released in 2017, followed by the director's cut Zack Snyder's Justice League in 2021.

Fictional team biography

Within DC continuity the League unites Earth's greatest heroes against threats no single hero can face, typically operating from the Hall of Justice in Washington, D.C., or the orbital Watchtower. Recurring adversaries include Darkseid, the Legion of Doom, the Crime Syndicate and the Secret Society of Super Villains.
