Norte Chico civilization

The Norte Chico civilization (also Caral or Caral-Supe civilization) was a complex Pre-Columbian society of the Late Archaic period that flourished on the north-central coast of present-day Peru between roughly 3700 and 1800 BC. It is regarded as the oldest known civilization in the Americas.

The pyramids of Caral in the Supe valley

Society

Some thirty major population centres are known across the valleys of the Fortaleza, Pativilca, Supe, and Huaura rivers. The sites feature monumental platform mounds and sunken circular plazas, most famously at Caral, one of the largest urban centres of its era. Remarkably, the civilization produced almost no ceramics and little visual art, but it built with stone and adobe and used knotted-string records resembling later quipu.

Economy

Norte Chico society rested on an exchange between coastal fishing communities — rich in anchovies and sardines — and inland agricultural communities growing cotton, beans, squash, and guava. Cotton for fishing nets linked the two economies, a model proposed by archaeologist Michael Moseley as a maritime foundation for Andean civilization.
