Mordent

In music, a mordent is an ornament indicating that a note should be played with a single rapid alternation with the note above or below. Like trills, they can be chromatically modified. The term and its execution changed over time: in the Baroque period a mordent typically meant alternation with the note below, while in modern usage the ornament alternating with the note above is often called a mordent and the one alternating below an inverted mordent.

TypeMusical ornament
RelatedTrill, turn, appoggiatura
EraBaroque onward

Types

The (upper) mordent consists of the principal note, the note above, and the principal note again. The inverted mordent (or lower mordent) alternates with the note below the principal. Exact realisation depends on period, tempo and style, and composers' notation varied considerably.

History

Ornaments including mordents were central to Baroque performance practice, and treatises of the period gave detailed tables of their execution. As notation became more literal in the 19th century, the interpretation of such signs was standardised along the modern pattern.
