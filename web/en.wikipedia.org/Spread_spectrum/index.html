Spread spectrum

In telecommunications, spread spectrum techniques are methods by which a signal generated with a particular bandwidth is deliberately spread in the frequency domain, resulting in a signal with a wider bandwidth. These techniques are used for a variety of reasons, including the establishment of secure communications, increasing resistance to natural interference and jamming, preventing detection, and enabling multiple users to share the same frequency band.

Techniques

The two principal techniques are frequency-hopping spread spectrum (FHSS), in which the carrier jumps among many frequency channels according to a pseudorandom sequence, and direct-sequence spread spectrum (DSSS), in which the signal is multiplied by a high-rate pseudonoise code. A famous early patent for frequency hopping was granted to actress Hedy Lamarr and composer George Antheil in 1942 for a torpedo guidance system.

Applications

Spread spectrum is used in CDMA mobile telephony standards, in Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11), Bluetooth, and the Global Positioning System. Its resistance to interference and low probability of interception made it attractive first for military communications before widespread civilian adoption.
