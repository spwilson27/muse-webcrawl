Super Bowl

The Super Bowl is the annual league championship game of the National Football League (NFL) in the United States, played between the champions of the National Football Conference (NFC) and the American Football Conference (AFC). Held since January 1967, it is played on a Sunday in early February — "Super Bowl Sunday" — and is consistently the most-watched television broadcast of the year in the US, as well as a major cultural event known for its halftime show and high-profile commercials.

History

The game grew out of the AFL–NFL merger agreement of 1966, which pitted the champions of the rival leagues against each other; the first four editions were styled the AFL–NFL World Championship Game. Kansas City Chiefs coach Lamar Hunt is credited with suggesting the name "Super Bowl", inspired by his children's Super Ball toy. Super Bowl III (1969), in which Joe Namath's New York Jets upset the Baltimore Colts, established the AFL's credibility. The winning team receives the Vince Lombardi Trophy, named for the legendary Green Bay Packers coach who won the first two editions.

Records and spectacle

The Pittsburgh Steelers and New England Patriots share the record with six championships; the Patriots have the most appearances. Tom Brady holds the record for a player with seven rings. The halftime show has grown into a headline concert — Michael Jackson's 1993 performance set the modern template — and thirty seconds of advertising time costs several million dollars. The game rotates among warm-weather or domed stadiums chosen years in advance.

Recent championship games

Super Bowl LIX was played on February 9, 2025, at the Caesars Superdome in New Orleans, where the Philadelphia Eagles defeated the Kansas City Chiefs 40–22, denying Kansas City a third consecutive title. Super Bowl LVIII (2024) saw the Chiefs beat the San Francisco 49ers 25–22 in overtime in Las Vegas.
