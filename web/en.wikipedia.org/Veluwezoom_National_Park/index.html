Veluwezoom National Park

Location: Gelderland, Netherlands
Area: 50 km²
Established: 1930
Governing body: Natuurmonumenten

Veluwezoom National Park (Dutch: Nationaal Park Veluwezoom) is a national park in the province of Gelderland in the Netherlands, on the southeastern edge of the Veluwe region north of Arnhem. Covering about 5,000 hectares, it was the first national park established in the Netherlands, in 1930, and is owned and managed by the conservation organisation Natuurmonumenten.

Landscape

The park is characterised by rolling moraine hills left by the Saalian glaciation, including the Posbank viewpoint, dry heathlands of heather that flower purple in late summer, drift sands, and extensive oak and beech woodland. At about 110 metres, the Signaal Imbosch is among the highest points of the Veluwe.

Wildlife and recreation

Red deer, wild boar, roe deer, pine martens and Highland cattle (used for grazing management) live in the park. It is a popular destination for walking, cycling and horse riding, with a visitor centre at Rheden near the main entrance.
