Arkose

Arkose is a detrital sedimentary rock, specifically a type of sandstone containing at least 25% feldspar. It is commonly pinkish or reddish in colour because of its abundant potassium feldspar (orthoclase and microcline) grains, and typically also contains quartz and rock fragments in a matrix of sand and clay. Arkose grains are usually angular and poorly sorted, indicating that the sediment was transported only a short distance from its source before deposition.

Formation

Arkose forms from the rapid erosion and deposition of granitic or gneissic source rocks. Because feldspar is chemically less stable than quartz and weathers readily, arkose accumulates where mechanical erosion outpaces chemical weathering — typically in arid climates, in tectonically active settings, and in alluvial fans, braided rivers, and fault-bounded basins adjacent to uplifted basement rock. Well-known examples include the Fountain Formation of Colorado, which forms the slabs of the Flatirons near Boulder and the outcrops of Red Rocks and the Garden of the Gods, and the Torridonian sandstones of Scotland.

Significance

To geologists, arkose indicates a nearby granitic source terrain and conditions of rapid burial. Arkose should be distinguished from greywacke, another immature sandstone, which is richer in rock fragments and matrix and typically darker in colour.
