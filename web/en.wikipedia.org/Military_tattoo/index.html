Military tattoo

TypeMilitary music performance
OriginNetherlands, 17th century
Notable examplesRoyal Edinburgh Military Tattoo

A military tattoo is a performance of music or display of armed forces in general. The term comes from the early-17th-century Dutch phrase doe den tap toe ("turn off the taps"), a signal sounded by drum or bugle in garrison towns around 9:30 p.m. to recall soldiers to their billets; over time it evolved from a simple evening call into an elaborate entertainment by military musicians.

Development

During the First and Second World Wars, tattoos in Britain developed into large-scale pageants combining massed bands, drill displays and re-enactments, beginning with the Aldershot Tattoo in the 1920s. Following the Second World War the format spread internationally as a showcase of precision drill, martial music and military ceremony.

Famous tattoos

The Royal Edinburgh Military Tattoo, first staged in 1950 on the esplanade of Edinburgh Castle during the Edinburgh Festival, is the best known: it has never cancelled a performance and has spawned sister tattoos in Sydney, Melbourne, Norway and elsewhere. Other major events include the Royal Nova Scotia International Tattoo, the Basel Tattoo, and the Norwegian Military Tattoo. Performances traditionally conclude with a lone piper or a massed-band finale.
