Living in Bondage

Living in Bondage
Directed by: Chris Obi Rapu
Written by: Kenneth Nnebue, Okechukwu Ogunjiofor
Starring: Kenneth Okonkwo, Nnenna Nwabueze, Okechukwu Ogunjiofor
Release date: 1992
Country: Nigeria
Language: Igbo (with English subtitles)

Living in Bondage is a 1992 Nigerian two-part drama thriller film directed by Chris Obi Rapu, written by Kenneth Nnebue and Okechukwu Ogunjiofor, and produced by Ogunjiofor. Shot directly on VHS video and released straight to video, it is widely credited with launching the modern Nigerian film industry known as Nollywood.

Plot

Andy Okeke, a struggling young man desperate for wealth, joins a secret satanic cult and sacrifices his wife Merit in a money ritual. He becomes rich, but is haunted by her ghost and descends into madness, eventually seeking redemption. The film's themes — the moral cost of sudden wealth, occult rituals and marital betrayal — resonated strongly with audiences and set templates that Nigerian video films would follow for decades.

Production and impact

The film was financed by businessman Kenneth Nnebue, who had a stock of blank videocassettes; shooting direct-to-video allowed production without cinema infrastructure. It sold an enormous number of copies — often said to be in the hundreds of thousands — and demonstrated that a profitable home-video market existed in Nigeria. A sequel, Living in Bondage: Breaking Free, directed by Ramsey Nouah, was released in 2019 and became a major box-office success.

Legacy

Living in Bondage is routinely cited by film historians as the starting point of Nollywood, which grew into one of the world's largest film industries by volume of output. The original film was shot mainly in Igbo with English subtitles, and its star Kenneth Okonkwo became one of the era's defining actors.
