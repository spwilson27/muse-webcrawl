The Little League World Series (LLWS) is an annual baseball tournament for children aged 10 to 12, held each August in South Williamsport, Pennsylvania. Organized by Little League International, it is the culminating event of the world's largest youth sports program and is broadcast nationally in the United States, typically by ESPN/ABC.

History

Little League Baseball was founded in Williamsport in 1939 by Carl Stotz; the first World Series was played in 1947 with only American teams. International participation began in the 1950s, and a team from Monterrey, Mexico, won in 1957 and 1958. From the late 1960s, teams from Taiwan dominated for two decades, prompting rule and eligibility scrutiny. The tournament expanded repeatedly, adding a second stadium in 2001 and expanding to 20 teams in 2022.

Qualification and format

Teams qualify through regional tournaments in the United States and international regions (including Asia-Pacific, Latin America, Europe-Africa, Canada, Japan, Mexico, Australia, and the Caribbean). In Williamsport, the US and international brackets proceed in modified double-elimination format, culminating in the championship game between the US and international champions, plus a consolation game.

Notable moments

Famous episodes include the 1982 victory of Kirkland, Washington, over Taiwan's long winning streak; the 2001 Danny Almonte age scandal, in which a Bronx team's pitcher proved to be 14, forfeiting its wins; Mo'ne Davis's celebrated pitching in 2014; and frequent appearances by future major leaguers. Age and residency eligibility rules have been tightened repeatedly over the tournament's history.
