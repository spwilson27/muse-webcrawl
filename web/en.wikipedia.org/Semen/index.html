Semen

Semen, also known as seminal fluid, is an organic bodily fluid created to contain spermatozoa. It is secreted by the gonads (sexual glands) and other sexual organs of male or hermaphroditic animals and can fertilize the female ovum. In humans, seminal fluid contains several components besides spermatozoa: proteolytic and other enzymes as well as fructose are elements of seminal fluid which promote the survival of spermatozoa, and provide a medium through which they can move or "swim".

Physiology

Semen is produced and originates from the seminal vesicle, which is located in the pelvis. The process that results in the discharge of semen is called ejaculation. During this process, sperm cells produced in the testes pass through the vas deferens and are mixed with fluids from the seminal vesicles (which contribute roughly 65–75% of the volume, including fructose and prostaglandins), the prostate gland (roughly 25–30%, including zinc, citric acid, and enzymes such as prostate-specific antigen), and the bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands, which add a small amount of mucus-like fluid.

Composition

Human semen is a viscous, creamy, slightly yellowish or grayish fluid. A normal ejaculate typically contains between 15 million and more than 200 million sperm per millilitre, although sperm make up only a small fraction of the total volume. The fluid also contains amino acids, enzymes, fructose and other sugars, phosphorylcholine, prostaglandins, potassium, and zinc.

Fertilization

The purpose of semen is fertilization: the spermatozoa it carries can fuse with a female gamete (ovum) to begin the development of a new organism. In mammals, semen is deposited in the female reproductive tract during copulation, and the sperm swim through the cervix and uterus toward the fallopian tubes.

Health and analysis

Semen analysis is a standard test in the evaluation of male fertility, measuring volume, sperm concentration, motility, and morphology. Semen can also transmit sexually transmitted infections, including HIV, because the fluid can carry viral particles. In forensic science, the detection of semen — for example through acid phosphatase or prostate-specific antigen tests — is used as evidence.

Etymology and culture

The word "semen" derives from the Latin word for "seed", itself from serere, "to sow". Historically, thinkers from Aristotle onward speculated about its role in generation; Aristotle regarded semen as the carrier of the form of the future organism. The Dutch scientist Antonie van Leeuwenhoek observed spermatozoa under a microscope in 1677.
