Mantling

In heraldry, mantling or lambrequin is drapery tied to the helmet above the shield in a coat of arms. It is depicted flowing outwards from behind the helm, usually in the principal colours (the "livrées") of the arms.

Origin

The mantling derives from the cloth worn by knights over their helmets in battle and tournament, which protected the metal from the sun's heat and from rust, and softened sword blows. In depictions it is often shown torn and cut, as if damaged in combat.

Conventions

In British heraldry the mantling is conventionally shown with the principal metal of the arms on one side (doubled, as a lining) and the principal colour on the other. Styles vary widely between national traditions and between artistic periods, from simple cloth to elaborate acanthus-like scrollwork.
