Generalized inverse

In mathematics, and in particular linear algebra, a generalized inverse (or g-inverse) of a matrix is a generalization of the inverse matrix. Unlike an ordinary inverse, a generalized inverse exists for matrices that are not square, or that are square but singular. There are many types of generalized inverses, defined by imposing different subsets of the so-called Penrose conditions.

Motivation

Generalized inverses arise naturally in solving systems of linear equations. For a system Ax = b, if A has a generalized inverse Ag satisfying AAgA = A, then the system is consistent exactly when AAgb = b, in which case the general solution can be expressed in terms of Agb.

The Moore–Penrose inverse

The best-known generalized inverse is the Moore–Penrose inverse, usually written A+, which is the unique matrix satisfying all four Penrose conditions: AA+A = A, A+AA+ = A+, and the conditions that AA+ and A+A are Hermitian. It can be computed from the singular value decomposition and gives the least-squares, minimum-norm solution of a linear system.

Other types

Other named generalized inverses include the Drazin inverse, used in the theory of differential equations and Markov chains; the group inverse; and the Bott–Duffin inverse. Reflexive g-inverses satisfy the first two Penrose conditions.

Applications

Generalized inverses are used in statistics (for example, in linear models with singular design matrices), signal processing, control theory, and numerical analysis.
