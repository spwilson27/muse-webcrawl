RLC circuit

A series RLC circuit: resistor, inductor and capacitor in a single loop.

An RLC circuit is an electrical circuit consisting of a resistor (R), an inductor (L), and a capacitor (C), connected in series or in parallel. The name of the circuit is derived from the letters that are used to denote the constituent components of this circuit, where the sequence of the components may vary from RLC.

The circuit forms a harmonic oscillator for current, and resonates in a manner similar to an LC circuit. Introducing the resistor increases the decay of these oscillations, which is also known as damping. The resistor also reduces the peak resonant frequency. In ordinary conditions, even if the resistor is not specifically part of the circuit, some resistance is unavoidable.

Basic concepts

There are two fundamental parameters that describe the behaviour of RLC circuits: the resonant frequency and the damping factor. In addition, other parameters derived from these two are the bandwidth, the quality factor (Q factor), the characteristic impedance and the attenuation.

The resonant frequency is defined as the frequency at which the impedance of the circuit is at a minimum (series circuit) or maximum (parallel circuit). At resonance, the inductive reactance and capacitive reactance cancel each other out.

Series circuit

In the series configuration, the resistor, inductor and capacitor are connected end to end in a single loop with the voltage source. The same current flows through all three components. The impedance of the series circuit is Z = R + j(ωL − 1/ωC). At the resonant frequency ω0 = 1/√(LC), the reactive terms cancel and the impedance is purely resistive and minimal, so the current is maximal.

Parallel circuit

In the parallel configuration, the three components are connected across the same two nodes. The parallel RLC circuit behaves as the dual of the series circuit: at resonance its impedance is maximal and the circulating current between the inductor and capacitor can be much larger than the current drawn from the source, an effect described by the circuit's Q factor.

Applications

RLC circuits are widely used as band-pass and band-stop filters in radio receivers and transmitters, where resonance selects a narrow band of frequencies. They also appear in oscillator circuits, impedance matching networks, and in the tuning circuits of television and communication equipment.
