Airbnb

Type: Public company

Founded: 2008, San Francisco, California

Founders: Brian Chesky, Joe Gebbia, Nathan Blecharczyk

Headquarters: San Francisco, California, U.S.

Industry: Lodging, hospitality, tourism

Website: airbnb.com

Airbnb, Inc. is an American company that operates an online marketplace for short-term and long-term homestays and experiences in various countries and regions. The name is a portmanteau of "air mattress" and "bed and breakfast." Airbnb acts as a broker and charges a commission from each booking; it does not own any of the real estate listed on the platform.

History

Airbnb was founded in 2008 by Brian Chesky and Joe Gebbia, two industrial designers who, unable to afford their San Francisco rent, rented out air mattresses in their living room during a design conference. Nathan Blecharczyk joined as the third co-founder and chief technology officer. The company received seed funding from the Y Combinator accelerator in 2009. Initially named AirBed & Breakfast, the company shortened its name to Airbnb in 2009.

The company grew rapidly through the 2010s, reaching a valuation of over $1 billion in 2011 and becoming one of the best-known examples of the sharing economy. Airbnb went public in December 2020 with an initial public offering on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ABNB, debuting at more than double its IPO price.

Business model

Airbnb hosts list properties ranging from single rooms to entire homes, treehouses, and boats. Guests pay a nightly rate plus fees, and Airbnb takes a service fee from both hosts and guests. The platform provides a reputation system of reviews, identity verification, and host guarantees. Airbnb also offers "Experiences," host-led activities launched in 2016.

Criticism and regulation

Airbnb has faced criticism over its effects on housing affordability and availability in major cities, where the conversion of long-term rentals to short-term tourist accommodation can reduce housing supply. Many cities have enacted regulations limiting short-term rentals, requiring registration, or taxing stays. The company has also faced disputes over party houses, safety incidents, and discrimination, to which it has responded with policies such as a ban on parties and anti-discrimination measures.
