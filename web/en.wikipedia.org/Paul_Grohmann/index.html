Paul Grohmann

Born12 June 1843, Vienna, Austrian Empire
Died1 August 1908, Vienna, Austria-Hungary
OccupationMountaineer, co-founder of the Austrian Alpine Club
Known forFirst ascents in the Dolomites and Eastern Alps

Paul Grohmann (12 June 1843 – 1 August 1908) was an Austrian mountaineer and one of the most important pioneers of climbing in the Eastern Alps, particularly in the Dolomites. He made numerous first ascents in the 1860s and 1870s and was a co-founder of the Austrian Alpine Club (Österreichischer Alpenverein) in 1862, one of the earliest organisations of its kind.

Climbing career

Born into a wealthy Viennese industrial family, Grohmann began climbing in the early 1860s. His first ascents include the Hochalmspitze (1865), the Olperer (1867), and, in the Dolomites, the Tofana di Mezzo and Sorapiss (1864), Monte Cristallo (1865), and the Marmolada — the highest peak of the Dolomites — first climbed via its south side in 1864 with local guides. In 1869 he made the first ascent of the Großer Wiesbachhorn and other major summits. He often climbed with guides such as Franz Innerkofler and published widely, including a detailed map of the Dolomites (1875) and essays promoting the range, which had previously been little visited compared with the Western Alps.

Legacy

Several features bear his name, including the Grohmannspitze (Piz Grohmann) in the Langkofel group, named in his honour. A monument to Grohmann stands in Ortisei in Val Gardena. Financial difficulties in the 1890s forced him to sell much of his property, and he died in Vienna in 1908. He is remembered as the "discoverer" of the Dolomites for mountaineering, opening dozens of summits and popularising the region in German-speaking Europe.
