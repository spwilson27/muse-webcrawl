Nicodemus

Nicodemus is a New Testament figure, a Pharisee and a member of the Sanhedrin, who is presented in the Gospel of John as a ruler of the Jews who comes to Jesus by night. His nighttime visit gives rise to Jesus' discourse on being "born again" (or "born from above"), one of the most quoted passages in Christian teaching.

In the Gospel of John

Nicodemus appears three times in John. First, he visits Jesus at night and hears the teaching about rebirth by water and the Spirit, including the verse "For God so loved the world that he gave his only Son" (John 3:16). Later he speaks in Jesus' defence before fellow Pharisees, asking whether the law condemns a man without a hearing. Finally, after the crucifixion, he helps Joseph of Arimathea prepare Jesus' body for burial, bringing a large quantity of myrrh and aloes.

Veneration and legacy

Nicodemus is venerated as a saint in several Christian traditions. Apocryphal writings, including the Gospel of Nicodemus (also associated with the Acts of Pilate), were later attributed to him. He is often interpreted as a cautious but sincere seeker whose gradual movement toward open discipleship mirrors a journey of faith.
