Erich Auerbach

Erich Auerbach (9 November 1892 – 13 October 1957) was a German philologist and comparative scholar of literature. His best-known work is Mimesis: The Representation of Reality in Western Literature, a history of representation in three thousand years of Western literature, written while Auerbach was in exile in Istanbul and published in 1946.

Life

Auerbach was born in Berlin into a Jewish family. After serving in the First World War, he completed a doctorate in Romance philology at the University of Greifswald in 1921, with a dissertation on the Italian and French novella of the Renaissance. He worked at the Prussian State Library in Berlin and then, from 1929, as professor of Romance philology at the University of Marburg, where he succeeded Leo Spitzer. Dismissed in 1935 under the Nazi racial laws, he emigrated to Turkey, where he chaired Romance philology at Istanbul University. In 1947 he moved to the United States, teaching at Pennsylvania State University and then at Yale University, where he became Sterling Professor of Romance Philology in 1950. He died in Wallingford, Connecticut, in 1957.

Mimesis

Mimesis proceeds through a series of chapters, each beginning with a close reading of a single passage — from the Odyssey and Genesis through Dante, Shakespeare, Cervantes, Stendhal, Flaubert, and Virginia Woolf — and expanding outward into an account of the changing representation of reality. Its opening chapter, "Odysseus' Scar", famously contrasts the externalised, fully illuminated style of Homer with the fragmentary, background-laden style of the Hebrew Bible. Auerbach later noted that the book's breadth was possible precisely because Istanbul lacked a large specialised research library, forcing him to work from the texts themselves. Edward Said, who wrote an introduction to the fiftieth-anniversary edition, praised the book as a monumental act of humanist synthesis born of exile.

Other works

Auerbach's earlier work Dante: Poet of the Secular World (1929) introduced his concept of figura, developed further in the influential essay "Figura" (1938) on typological interpretation. His late methodological essay "Philology and Weltliteratur" (1952) reflected on the future of his discipline in a globalising world.
