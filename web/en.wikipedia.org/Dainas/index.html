Dainas

Dainas are traditional Latvian folk songs or poems, a defining element of Latvian culture and identity. A daina is typically a short, unrhymed quatrain — most often four lines, sometimes two or six — written in a trochaic or dactylic meter. More than 1.2 million daina texts have been collected and catalogued, making the corpus one of the largest collections of folk poetry in the world.

Content

Dainas cover the whole span of traditional life: birth, childhood, courtship, weddings, work in the fields, death, and the cycles of nature. Many preserve pre-Christian Latvian mythology, invoking deities and figures such as Saule (the sun), Mēness (the moon), Pērkons (thunder), Laima (fate), and Jānis, associated with the summer solstice festival of Jāņi. Singing dainas was central to seasonal celebrations and daily work.

Collection

The monumental collection of the dainas was assembled by the folklorist Krišjānis Barons, whose Latvju dainas was published between 1894 and 1915 with support from the Latvian businessman Krišjānis Valdemārs's circle and the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences. The original card catalogue, known as the Dainu skapis ("Cabinet of Folk Songs"), is preserved in Riga and is inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register.
