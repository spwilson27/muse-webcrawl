House of Este

The House of Este (Italian: Casa d'Este) is a European princely dynasty of Longobard origin. It ruled parts of Italy for several centuries, most famously the city of Ferrara and later Modena and Reggio Emilia, and was one of the great patron families of the Italian Renaissance. A junior branch of the family, the House of Welf, produced rulers in Germany and, ultimately, the British Hanoverian monarchs.

Origins

The family descends from the Obertenghi, a powerful Frankish-Lombard kindred of northern Italy. Alberto Azzo II, Margrave of Milan (died 1097), is regarded as the founder of the house; his sons split the inheritance, with Welf IV continuing the German line and Folco I the Italian Este line. The family took its name from the town of Este in the Veneto, where it held extensive estates.

Lords of Ferrara

From the 13th century the Este established themselves in Ferrara, where Obizzo II was proclaimed perpetual lord in 1264. Under rulers such as Niccolò III, Leonello, Borso, and Ercole I, Ferrara became a brilliant Renaissance court, attracting artists and writers including Ludovico Ariosto, who celebrated the family in Orlando Furioso. Borso received the ducal title for Modena and Reggio in 1452 and for Ferrara in 1471.

Dukes of Modena

In 1598 the Este lost Ferrara to the Papal States after the death of Alfonso II without a legitimate direct heir, but retained Modena and Reggio as an imperial duchy under Cesare d'Este. The Modena line continued until 1803, when Ercole III died and the male line became extinct; his inheritance passed through his daughter Maria Beatrice to the Archduke Ferdinand of Austria, founding the Austria-Este branch.

Legacy

The Este name survives in the Austrian Habsburg-Este line and in numerous palaces, including the Palazzo dei Diamanti in Ferrara and the Ducal Palace of Modena. The family's patronage left an enduring mark on Renaissance art, music, and garden design, notably the Villa d'Este at Tivoli, built for Cardinal Ippolito II d'Este.
