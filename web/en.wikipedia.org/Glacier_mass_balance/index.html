Glacier mass balance


Accumulation and ablation zones of a glacier.

Glacier mass balance is the difference between the accumulation (gain) and ablation (loss) of ice and snow on a glacier over a period of time, usually a year. It is the most direct measure of a glacier's health and its response to climate.

Definitions

Mass balance is typically expressed in metres of water equivalent (m w.e.) averaged over the glacier's area. Accumulation includes all processes that add mass: snowfall, wind-blown snow, avalanches, rime and refreezing rain. Ablation includes all processes that remove mass: melting, sublimation, evaporation, calving of icebergs, and wind erosion of snow.

The equilibrium line divides the accumulation zone, where net balance is positive over the year, from the ablation zone, where it is negative. The altitude of this line at the end of the melt season (the equilibrium line altitude, ELA) and the accumulation area ratio are key diagnostics.

Measurement

The traditional glaciological method uses stakes drilled into the ice and snow pits to measure changes directly. The geodetic method compares the glacier's volume from repeated elevation surveys, increasingly from satellites such as GRACE and ICESat. The World Glacier Monitoring Service coordinates a network of reference glaciers with records extending back decades.

Climate significance

Most glaciers worldwide have had strongly negative mass balances since the late 20th century, a trend attributed to climate warming. Cumulative losses contribute significantly to global sea level rise, and sustained negative balance leads to glacier retreat and eventual disappearance.
