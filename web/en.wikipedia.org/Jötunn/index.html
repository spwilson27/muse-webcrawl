Jötunn

Jötunn (Old Norse: jǫtunn; plural: jötnar)
Also called: Giants (in English translations)
Realm: Jötunheimr, in Útgarðr (the outer world)
Opponents/kin of: The gods (Æsir and Vanir)

A jötunn (Old Norse jǫtunn, plural jötnar) is a class of beings in Norse mythology who live in Jötunheimr, one of the Nine Realms, and are frequently rendered in English as "giants," though they are not always enormous and many are described as strikingly beautiful or profoundly wise. They stand in a complex relation to the gods: opposed to them in many myths, yet also their ancestors, in-laws, and occasional allies.

Origins

In the Norse creation account, the first being, the primordial jötunn Ymir, arose from the rime of Élivágar where it met the heat of Muspelheim. From Ymir the gods fashioned the world — his flesh became earth, his blood the sea, his skull the sky. The jötnar descend from Ymir's offspring, and the gods themselves are partly of jötunn descent: Odin's mother Bestla was a jötunn, and many gods took jötunn wives, including Njörðr (Skaði) and Freyr (Gerðr).

Roles in myth

Jötnar figure throughout the Poetic and Prose Eddas. Some, like the wise Mímir and the riddling Vafþrúðnir, possess ancient knowledge; others, such as Þrymr, who stole Thor's hammer, are adversaries. Fire jötnar under Surtr will fight the gods at Ragnarök, when the world is destroyed and reborn. The Old English cognate eoten appears in Beowulf, and the word survives in English folklore in forms such as "ettin."
