Erdene Zuu Monastery

Erdene Zuu Monastery
Location: Kharkhorin, Övörkhangai Province, Mongolia
Founded: 1585
Tradition: Tibetan Buddhism (Gelug)
Erdene Zuu Monastery, surrounded by a wall with 108 stupas

The Erdene Zuu Monastery (Mongolian: Эрдэнэ Зуу хийд) is probably the earliest surviving Buddhist monastery in Mongolia. Located in Övörkhangai Province, about two kilometres northeast of the town of Kharkhorin, it stands adjacent to the ruins of the ancient Mongol capital Karakorum. It is part of the Orkhon Valley Cultural Landscape, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Erdene Zuu was founded in 1585 by Abtai Sain Khan, who met the 3rd Dalai Lama and declared Tibetan Buddhism the state religion of Mongolia. Stones from the ruins of Karakorum were used in its construction. The monastery is enclosed by a wall with 108 stupas — 108 being a sacred number in Buddhism. At its height it housed between 60 and 100 temples and up to 1,000 monks.

Destruction and revival

The temples were badly damaged during the Stalinist purges of the late 1930s, in which many Mongolian monasteries were destroyed and monks persecuted. Erdene Zuu survived largely because it was preserved as a museum. After the democratic revolution of 1990, the monastery was returned to religious use and again became an active centre of Mongolian Buddhism, while also functioning as a museum.

Architecture

The three principal temples, dedicated to the three stages of Buddha's life, combine Tibetan and Mongolian architectural elements with Chinese-style tiling. The central temple square also preserves the foundations of a great ger (yurt) erected for the Dalai Lama's visit of 1586. A famous phallic rock and stone turtles from old Karakorum stand near the site.
