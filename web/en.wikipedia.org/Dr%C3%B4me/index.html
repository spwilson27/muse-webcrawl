Drôme is a department in south-eastern France, in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region, named after the Drôme River, a tributary of the Rhône. Its prefecture is Valence. The department stretches from the Rhône valley in the west to the foothills of the Alps in the east, and its southern part, the Drôme Provençale, shares the lavender fields, olive groves and hilltop villages of neighbouring Provence.

Geography

The Drôme contains the Vercors massif, a limestone plateau that was a stronghold of the French Resistance during the Second World War, and the Diois and Baronnies hill country. The enclave of the Papal Enclave of Valréas, belonging to Vaucluse, lies entirely within Drôme.

Economy

Agriculture includes lavender, apricots, olives (notably from Nyons) and wine, including Crozes-Hermitage and Clairette de Die, a sparkling wine made by an ancestral method. The department is a leading area for organic farming in France.
