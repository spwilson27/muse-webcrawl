Saint Thomas Christians

RegionKerala, India, and worldwide diaspora
TraditionEast Syriac and West Syriac liturgical traditions
Traditional founderThomas the Apostle (traditionally AD 52)

The Saint Thomas Christians, also called Nasranis, are an ethnoreligious community of Christians in Kerala, India, who trace their origins to the evangelising activity of Thomas the Apostle in the first century AD. For many centuries they maintained communion with the Church of the East and used the East Syriac liturgy.

History

Tradition holds that Thomas landed at Muziris in AD 52 and founded seven churches. Historically documented links with Persian Christianity date from late antiquity. Portuguese colonisation from the sixteenth century brought Latin pressure, leading to the Coonan Cross Oath of 1653 and a lasting division into Catholic and Orthodox branches.

Today

The community today comprises several churches, including the Syro-Malabar Catholic Church, the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, the Jacobite Syrian Church and the Mar Thoma Church, with a large diaspora in the Gulf states, North America and Europe.
