Cycling in Switzerland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cycling in Switzerland is a significant mode of transport and recreation, supported by one of Europe's densest networks of signposted cycle routes. Despite the country's mountainous terrain, a substantial share of everyday trips — around a tenth of commuter journeys — is made by bicycle, and cycle tourism is a major part of the Swiss tourism economy.

Cycle network

The national network, coordinated by SwitzerlandMobility (formerly Cycling in Switzerland), comprises nine national routes (such as the Rhone Route, the Rhine Route, the North–South Route and the Lakes Route) totalling over 3,000 kilometres, supplemented by dozens of regional and hundreds of local routes. Routes are uniformly signposted with red markers and integrated with public transport: most Swiss trains carry bicycles, and rail-plus-bike services are promoted under the SwitzerlandMobility banner.

Urban cycling

Swiss cities such as Bern, Basel, Winterthur and Zurich record some of the highest cycling mode shares in Europe, aided by dense networks of cycle lanes, bicycle parking at stations, and bike-share schemes. Federal law has required cantons and communes to plan coherent cycle networks since the national cycle-path legislation approved by voters in 2018 and in force from 2023.

Sport

Switzerland has a distinguished racing tradition: the Tour de Suisse (founded 1933) is a leading stage race, and Swiss riders including Hugo Koblet, Ferdi Kübler, Tony Rominger and Fabian Cancellara have won major international honours. Mountain biking is also highly developed, with thousands of kilometres of waymarked trails in the Alps.
