The Galerie des Glaces at Versailles

The Hall of Mirrors (French: Grande Galerie, Galerie des Glaces, Galerie de Versailles) is a grand Baroque gallery and one of the most emblematic rooms in the Palace of Versailles, France. Built between 1678 and 1684 by Jules Hardouin-Mansart for Louis XIV, it replaced a terrace between the king's and queen's apartments and served as the setting for court ceremony, receptions of foreign ambassadors, and masked balls.

Description

The hall is 73 metres long. Seventeen arched windows overlooking the gardens are matched by seventeen mirrored arcades on the opposite wall, comprising 357 individual mirror plates—at the time a striking demonstration of French manufacturing, since mirror-making had been a Venetian monopoly. Charles Le Brun's ceiling paintings celebrate the triumphs of Louis XIV's early reign, from the Peace of the Pyrenees to the Dutch War. Marble revetment, gilded trophies of arms, and (in the Sun King's time) solid silver furniture completed the display of power.

Historical events

The hall has hosted turning points of European history: the proclamation of the German Empire on 18 January 1871, staged by Bismarck in the defeated France of the Franco-Prussian War; and the signing of the Treaty of Versailles on 28 June 1919, deliberately chosen to answer 1871. It remains a ceremonial space—used for state receptions during Charles de Gaulle's presidency—and the centrepiece of the palace museum, restored in a major campaign completed in 2007.
