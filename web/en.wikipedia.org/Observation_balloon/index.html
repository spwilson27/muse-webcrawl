Observation balloon

An observation balloon is a type of balloon that is employed as an aerial platform for gathering intelligence and spotting artillery. Use of observation balloons began during the French Revolutionary Wars, reaching their zenith during World War I, and they continue in limited use today.

History

The first military use of balloons occurred in 1794, when the French Aerostatic Corps used the tethered balloon L'Entreprenant at the Battle of Fleurus to observe Austrian movements. During the American Civil War, the Union Army Balloon Corps under Thaddeus S. C. Lowe used balloons for reconnaissance and artillery spotting.

First World War

During World War I, observation balloons were deployed by all major combatants along the front lines. Observers in wicker baskets hung beneath hydrogen-filled balloons reported enemy positions by telephone line running down the tether cable, and directed artillery fire. Balloons were heavily defended by anti-aircraft guns and fighter patrols, and attacking them was so dangerous that successful pilots earned the nickname "balloon busters". Observers were among the first military aircrew issued with parachutes.

Later use

By the Second World War, observation balloons had largely been superseded by reconnaissance aircraft, though barrage balloons remained in use for defence. Modern tethered aerostats equipped with radar and cameras are used for border surveillance and persistent ground monitoring.
