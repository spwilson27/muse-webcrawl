The Challenge Cup is a knockout cup competition in rugby league, organised by the Rugby Football League. First played in the 1896–97 season, it is one of the oldest cup competitions in British sport and is contested by professional and amateur clubs, with the final traditionally held at Wembley Stadium in London.

History

The competition was established shortly after the 1895 schism that created the Northern Union. Batley won the first final in 1897, defeating St Helens. Since 1929 the final has usually been staged at Wembley, and the occasion became a celebrated annual pilgrimage for supporters of the northern game.

Format

Amateur and community clubs enter the early rounds, with Championship and League 1 clubs joining next and Super League teams entering in the later rounds. Ties are single-leg knockouts, culminating in the final at Wembley.

Notable records

Wigan Warriors are the most successful club in the competition's history, with a record number of cup wins including eight consecutive victories between 1988 and 1995. The Lance Todd Trophy is awarded to the player of the match in the final.
