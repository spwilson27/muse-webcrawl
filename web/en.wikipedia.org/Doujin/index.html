Doujin

Doujinshi for sale at a convention.

Dōjin (同人), often romanised as doujin, is a Japanese term for a group of people who share a common interest or hobby, and by extension for the self-published creative works they produce. Such works, called dōjinshi when in printed or comic form, span manga, novels, music, games and artwork, and are typically created by amateurs, though professionals also participate.

Culture

Doujin works are sold at conventions (doujinshi sokubaikai), the largest of which is Comiket in Tokyo, held twice a year and drawing hundreds of thousands of attendees. Much doujin output is derivative, based on existing anime, manga or video game franchises, and exists in a legal grey area generally tolerated by Japanese rights holders because it sustains fan culture and can launch professional careers.

Related forms

Doujin music and doujin games form substantial subcultures; well-known examples of the latter include the Touhou Project series. Circles (groups of creators) publish under shared names, and specialist bookshops in districts such as Akihabara retail their output.
