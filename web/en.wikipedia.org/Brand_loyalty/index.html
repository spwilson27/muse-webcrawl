Brand loyalty

Brand loyalty is the tendency of consumers to continue buying the same brand of goods rather than competing brands. It reflects a positive attitude toward a brand combined with repeat-purchase behaviour, and is considered one of the most valuable outcomes of marketing because loyal customers are less price-sensitive, cheaper to serve and more likely to recommend the brand to others.

Dimensions

Scholars distinguish behavioural loyalty — simply buying the brand repeatedly, which may result from habit or lack of alternatives — from attitudinal loyalty, a genuine commitment and emotional attachment that resists competitors' inducements. Dick and Basu's influential framework crosses repeat purchase with relative attitude to identify true loyalty, spurious loyalty, latent loyalty and no loyalty. Aaker describes a loyalty pyramid from switchers at the base to committed buyers at the top.

Building loyalty

Firms cultivate loyalty through consistent product quality, customer satisfaction, service recovery, loyalty programmes (points, tiers and rewards), brand communities and emotional branding. Metrics such as retention rate, share of wallet and the Net Promoter Score are used to track it. Switching costs — contractual, technical or psychological — can lock customers in without true attitudinal commitment.

Criticism and change

Research by the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute argues that loyalty metrics mainly follow market share (the "double jeopardy" law) and that most buyers are polygamously loyal across a small repertoire of brands, challenging strategies built on deep exclusivity.
