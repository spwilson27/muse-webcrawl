Extended evolutionary synthesis

The EES adds development, plasticity and niche construction to evolutionary theory.

The extended evolutionary synthesis (EES) is a set of theoretical concepts in evolutionary biology that extend and, its proponents argue, revise the modern synthesis (neo-Darwinism) established in the mid-20th century. Where the modern synthesis emphasized genes as the principal units of inheritance and natural selection on random mutation as the central cause of adaptation, the EES gives greater causal weight to developmental processes, phenotypic plasticity, extra-genetic inheritance, and the reciprocal influence of organisms on their environments.

Components

Key elements include evolutionary developmental biology ("evo-devo"), which shows how developmental systems structure the variation on which selection acts; niche construction, in which organisms modify their own selective environments; epigenetic and ecological inheritance alongside genetic inheritance; and inclusive inheritance and plasticity-first evolution, where environmentally induced traits may later become genetically accommodated.

Debate

A widely discussed 2014 exchange in Nature ("Does evolutionary theory need a rethink?") set proponents such as Kevin Laland, Eva Jablonka, and Massimo Pigliucci against critics who held that the phenomena invoked are already accommodated within standard theory. The dispute concerns not the facts but their theoretical organization and causal emphasis, and remains active within evolutionary biology and the philosophy of biology.
