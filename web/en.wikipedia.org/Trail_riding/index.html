Trail riding

Trail riders on a woodland path.

Trail riding is riding outdoors on trails, bridle paths, and forest roads, as opposed to riding in an arena or on roads. Trail rides may be informal leisure rides of an hour or two, organised group rides, or multi-day journeys with pack animals. The term also encompasses competitive trail riding and endurance riding, in which horse and rider cover long distances over natural terrain.

Leisure trail riding

Recreational trail riding is one of the most popular equestrian activities. Riders explore designated trails in forests, mountains, deserts, and along beaches. In the United Kingdom, riders make use of the bridleway network; in the United States, trails in national forests, national parks, and state parks are widely used. Riders are generally expected to follow "leave no trace" principles and to yield the right of way appropriately to hikers.

Horses used for trail riding are expected to be calm, sure-footed, and habituated to wildlife, water crossings, bridges, and other trail obstacles.

Competitive trail riding

Competitive trail riding (CTR) is a judged sport in which horses cover a set distance—typically 25 to 40 miles in one day, or up to 100 miles over multiple days—within a prescribed time window. Horses are evaluated by veterinarians and judges on condition, soundness, and way of going; the winner is the horse that completes the course in the best condition rather than simply the fastest.

Endurance riding

Endurance riding is a race over very long distances, most famously 100 miles (160 km) in a single day, with mandatory veterinary checkpoints. It is governed internationally by the Fédération Équestre Internationale (FEI). Prestigious events include the Tevis Cup in California and the Mongol Derby.

Safety and etiquette

  • Wear a properly fitted helmet and suitable footwear.
  • Carry water, a map or GPS, and basic first-aid supplies for horse and rider.
  • Announce your presence calmly when approaching hikers or cyclists.
  • Keep to designated trails and close gates behind you.
