Tomás Luis de Victoria

Bornc. 1548, Ávila, Spain
Died27 August 1611, Madrid
OccupationComposer, organist, priest

Tomás Luis de Victoria (c. 1548 – 27 August 1611) was the most famous Spanish composer of the Renaissance and one of the great figures of Counter-Reformation church music. Alongside Palestrina and Orlando di Lasso, he forms the triumvirate of late Renaissance polyphonists.

Life

Born in Ávila, Victoria sang as a choirboy at Ávila Cathedral before being sent to Rome, where he studied at the Collegium Germanicum and succeeded Palestrina as maestro at the Roman Seminary. Ordained a priest, he published extensively in Rome before returning to Spain in 1587 to serve the Dowager Empress Maria at the convent of Las Descalzas Reales in Madrid, where he remained until his death.

Music

Victoria wrote exclusively sacred music: some twenty masses, forty-four motets, hymns, Magnificats and a famous Officium Defunctorum (1605), a Requiem written for the Empress Maria's funeral, often considered his masterpiece. His music combines Palestrinian clarity with an intensity and warmth regarded as distinctively Spanish.
