Quebec Autoroute 20

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Autoroute 20 is a major autoroute in the Canadian province of Quebec. It forms part of the Trans-Canada Highway and is the principal road link between Montreal and Quebec City on the south shore of the Saint Lawrence River, paralleled on the north shore by Autoroute 40.

Route

The highway begins at the Ontario border, where it continues from Ontario Highway 401, passes through the Montreal metropolitan area (where segments are also known as the Autoroute du Souvenir and, historically, the Boulevard Métropolitain corridor), then runs northeast through Drummondville and along the south shore to Quebec City and beyond, ending near Mont-Joli in the Bas-Saint-Laurent region. Its total length is roughly 640 kilometres, making it one of the longest autoroutes in Quebec.

History

The first sections opened in the 1960s as Quebec built its autoroute network; the Montreal–Quebec City corridor was completed in stages through the 1970s. Near its western end, the interchange with Autoroute 30 and the ongoing reconstruction of Montreal's Turcot Interchange have been major projects, and a planned extension toward the Gaspé Peninsula has been discussed for decades.
