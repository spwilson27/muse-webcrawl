Salt

Sea salt crystals harvested from evaporation ponds.

Salt, in its most common form, is a mineral composed primarily of sodium chloride (NaCl). It is essential to life in general — saltiness is one of the basic human tastes — and it has been one of the most widely traded commodities in human history, used to season and preserve food for thousands of years.

Production

Salt is produced by mining underground halite (rock salt) deposits, by solution mining, and by evaporating seawater or brine in shallow ponds. Major producers include China, the United States and India. Table salt is usually refined and may contain iodine (as iodised salt, introduced in the 1920s to prevent goitre) and anti-caking agents.

History

Salt production in China dates back more than 4,000 years. Salt routes such as the Via Salaria in Italy shaped trade networks, salt taxes (such as the French gabelle) provoked revolts, and Mahatma Gandhi's Salt March of 1930 became a defining act of civil disobedience against British rule in India. The word "salary" is often linked, perhaps apocryphally, to Roman salt payments.

Health

Sodium is an essential electrolyte, but high dietary intake is associated with hypertension and cardiovascular disease. Health authorities such as the World Health Organization recommend that adults consume less than 5 grams of salt per day; average intakes in many countries are roughly double that.

Chemistry

In chemistry, a salt is any ionic compound formed by the neutralisation of an acid and a base. This broader class includes compounds such as copper sulfate and sodium carbonate, in addition to common sodium chloride.
