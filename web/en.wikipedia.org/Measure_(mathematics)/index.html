Measure (mathematics)

In mathematics, a measure is a generalisation of the notions of length, area and volume. Formally, a measure on a set X is a function that assigns a non-negative real number (or +∞) to certain subsets of X, in a way that generalises the intuitive idea of size. A measure must be countably additive: the measure of a countable disjoint union of measurable sets equals the sum of their measures.

Definition

Let (X, Σ) be a measurable space, that is, Σ is a σ-algebra of subsets of X. A function μ : Σ → [0, +∞] is a measure if μ(∅) = 0 and, for every countable collection of pairwise disjoint sets in Σ, μ of their union equals the sum of their measures. The triple (X, Σ, μ) is called a measure space.

Examples

The most important example is Lebesgue measure on the real line, which extends the length of intervals to a large class of sets and underpins the Lebesgue integral. Other examples include counting measure, Dirac measure concentrated at a point, probability measures (total measure 1), and Hausdorff measures used in fractal geometry.

Properties

Measures are monotone: if A ⊆ B then μ(A) ≤ μ(B). They are continuous from below for increasing sequences of sets and, under finiteness conditions, continuous from above for decreasing sequences. Not every subset of the real line is Lebesgue measurable: the Vitali set, constructed using the axiom of choice, is a standard example of a non-measurable set.

Applications

Measure theory is the foundation of the modern theory of integration, of probability theory (following Kolmogorov's axioms), and of ergodic theory and functional analysis.
