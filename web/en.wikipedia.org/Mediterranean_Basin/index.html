The Mediterranean Basin is the region of lands around the Mediterranean Sea that have a Mediterranean climate, characterized by mild, rainy winters and hot, dry summers. It spans parts of three continents — Europe, Africa, and Asia — and has been a crossroads of trade, migration, and empire since antiquity, sometimes called the cradle of Western civilization for the Phoenician, Greek, Roman, and other cultures that flourished on its shores.

Geography

The basin is bounded by mountain systems including the Pyrenees, Alps, Dinaric Alps, Taurus Mountains, and Atlas Mountains. Major peninsulas — Iberian, Italian, Balkan, and Anatolian — project into the sea, and large islands include Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, Crete, Cyprus, and the Balearics. Great rivers draining into the sea include the Nile, Rhône, Ebro, and Po. The sea connects to the Atlantic through the Strait of Gibraltar and, since 1869, to the Red Sea through the Suez Canal.

Climate and ecology

The Mediterranean climate supports sclerophyllous evergreen vegetation — maquis and garrigue in Europe, chaparral-like formations elsewhere — adapted to summer drought and recurrent fire. The basin is one of the world's recognized biodiversity hotspots, with roughly 25,000 plant species, about half of them endemic. Olive, grape, wheat, and citrus cultivation, developed over millennia, defines its agricultural landscape and underpins the celebrated Mediterranean diet.

History

The basin was unified politically under the Roman Empire, whose citizens called the sea Mare Nostrum ("Our Sea"). After Rome's fall, the region was divided among Byzantine, Islamic, and Latin Christian powers, yet trade networks continued to link its ports. Fernand Braudel's classic history The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II (1949) treated the sea and its hinterlands as a single historical system shaped by geography and long-term structures.

Modern issues

Today more than 500 million people live in the Mediterranean watershed states. The region faces acute pressures from tourism, coastal urbanization, overfishing, marine pollution, and climate change, which is warming the basin faster than the global average and intensifying water scarcity and wildfires. It is also a major route for migration from Africa and the Middle East to Europe.
