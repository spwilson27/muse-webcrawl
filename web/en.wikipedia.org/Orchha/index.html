Orchha

Orchha is a historic town in the Niwari district of Madhya Pradesh, India, lying on the Betwa River about 15 km south of Jhansi. Founded in the 16th century by the Bundela Rajput chief Rudra Pratap Singh, it was the capital of the princely state of Orchha and is famed for its palaces, temples and cenotaphs.

History

The Bundela dynasty made Orchha its capital in 1531. Under Bir Singh Deo (r. 1605–1627), a favourite of the Mughal emperor Jahangir, the town received its grandest buildings, including the Jahangir Mahal, built to honour the emperor's visit. The capital later shifted to Tikamgarh, and Orchha declined, preserving its early fabric largely intact.

Architecture

The fort complex on an island in the Betwa contains the Raj Mahal, Jahangir Mahal and Rai Praveen Mahal, noted for murals and Bundela painting. The Ram Raja Temple is unusual in worshipping Rama as a king, while the Chaturbhuj Temple and the Lakshmi Temple display a blend of temple and fort architecture. Along the river stand the royal chhatris (cenotaphs) of the Bundela rulers.

Tourism

Today Orchha is a popular heritage destination, reachable from Jhansi on the Delhi–Chennai rail corridor, with river rafting on the Betwa and an annual cultural calendar centred on its temples.
