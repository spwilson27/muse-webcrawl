The Mali Empire (Manding: Nyeni or Niani) was an empire in West Africa from c. 1226 to 1670. Founded by Sundiata Keita after his victory over the Sosso at the Battle of Kirina (c. 1235), it became one of the largest and wealthiest empires in African history, controlling the trans-Saharan trade in gold and salt.

Mansa Musa

Under Mansa Musa, the empire reached its greatest extent and fame. His pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324–1325 distributed so much gold in Cairo that it depressed the metal's value there for years. Musa patronised the construction of mosques and madrasas in Timbuktu and Gao, making Timbuktu a renowned centre of Islamic scholarship.

Government and society

The empire was a confederation of tributary kingdoms under the mansa (emperor). The oral Epic of Sundiata preserves the founding traditions, including the Kouroukan Fouga charter, sometimes described as one of the earliest charters of human rights.

Decline

After the 15th century, succession disputes, Tuareg and Mossi raids, and the rise of the Songhai Empire eroded Mali's power. By the 17th century it had fragmented into small chiefdoms.
