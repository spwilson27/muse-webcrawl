The abdomen (colloquially the belly or tummy) is the part of the body between the thorax (chest) and the pelvis. It encloses the abdominal cavity, which contains most of the organs of digestion as well as the kidneys and parts of the urinary and reproductive systems. The abdomen is bounded above by the thoracic diaphragm and below by the pelvic brim.

Contents

Organs within the abdomen include the stomach, liver, gallbladder, spleen, pancreas, small intestine, most of the large intestine, the kidneys and adrenal glands, and major blood vessels such as the abdominal aorta and inferior vena cava. The cavity is lined by the peritoneum, a serous membrane that also partly covers many of the organs.

Muscles and surface anatomy

The abdominal wall consists of skin, fascia and several muscles — the external and internal obliques, transversus abdominis and rectus abdominis — which protect the organs, support the trunk and assist in breathing and posture. For clinical description the surface of the abdomen is divided into four quadrants, or into nine regions. In arthropods and other invertebrates, the abdomen is the posterior body segment (tagma) behind the thorax.
