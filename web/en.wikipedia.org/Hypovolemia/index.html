Hypovolemia
A medical condition in which the volume of circulating blood plasma falls abnormally low; severe cases progress to hypovolemic shock.

Hypovolemia, also known as volume depletion or volume contraction, is a state of abnormally low extracellular fluid in the body, particularly a decreased volume of circulating blood plasma. It may result from loss of blood (haemorrhage), loss of plasma through burns, or loss of water and electrolytes through vomiting, diarrhoea, sweating or diuretic use.

Signs and stages

Early compensation includes rapid pulse, narrowed pulse pressure, thirst, cool extremities and reduced urine output, driven by sympathetic activation and the hormonal stress response of the HPA axis. In haemorrhage classification, loss of up to 15% of blood volume is usually well tolerated by healthy adults; losses of 30–40% produce marked tachycardia and falling blood pressure, and losses above 40% cause life-threatening hypovolemic shock with confusion and organ failure, including acute kidney injury reminiscent of the damage seen in obstructive conditions such as hydronephrosis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on history, orthostatic vital signs, skin turgor, mucous membranes and laboratory findings such as rising urea relative to creatinine. Treatment is directed at the cause — stopping bleeding, for instance — together with rapid volume replacement using intravenous crystalloids such as saline or lactated Ringer's solution, and blood products when haemorrhage is the cause. Untreated or under-treated severe hypovolemia leads to tissue hypoxia and death.
