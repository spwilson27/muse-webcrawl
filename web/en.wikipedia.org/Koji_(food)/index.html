Koji (food)


Koji is grain or beans fermented with the mould Aspergillus oryzae.

Koji (Japanese: 麹, kōji) is a food ingredient made by fermenting cooked grains or soybeans with the mould Aspergillus oryzae. It is a cornerstone of Japanese cuisine, used to produce sake, miso, soy sauce, mirin, rice vinegar and amazake, among other fermented foods.

Production and chemistry

Steamed rice (or barley, or soybeans) is inoculated with spores of A. oryzae and incubated in warm, humid conditions for about two days, during which the mould grows through the grains and produces enzymes — amylases that convert starch to sugars, proteases that break down proteins into amino acids (giving umami flavour), and lipases. The finished koji is fragrant and covered in white mycelium.

Aspergillus oryzae was designated the "national fungus" of Japan in 2006 in recognition of its cultural importance. Related koji moulds are used elsewhere in East and Southeast Asia in the production of fermented foods and liquors.
