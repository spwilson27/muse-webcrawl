Maharashtra

Maharashtra is a state in the western peninsular region of India, occupying a substantial portion of the Deccan Plateau. It is the second-most populous state in India (over 120 million people) and the third-largest by area (307,713 km²). Its capital is Mumbai, India's financial and entertainment centre, and its principal language is Marathi.

History

The region was ruled in turn by the Satavahanas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Yadavas, the Delhi Sultanate's successors, the Bahmani Sultanate, and the Mughals. In the 17th century Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj founded the Maratha Empire, which came to dominate much of India in the 18th century before falling to the British East India Company in 1818. The modern state was created on 1 May 1960 (celebrated as Maharashtra Day) when the bilingual Bombay State was split along linguistic lines into Maharashtra and Gujarat, following the Samyukta Maharashtra movement.

Geography and economy

The Western Ghats (Sahyadri range) run parallel to the Konkan coast, separating the narrow coastal plain from the Deccan plateau, drained by the Godavari and Krishna rivers. Maharashtra is India's most industrialised state, with the largest state economy; Mumbai hosts the Bombay Stock Exchange, the Reserve Bank of India, and the Hindi film industry (Bollywood). Agriculture employs a large share of the workforce, with major crops including sugarcane, cotton, and pulses. Other major cities include Pune, Nagpur, Nashik, and Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad).

Culture

The state is known for the rock-cut caves of Ajanta and Ellora (UNESCO sites), the Wari pilgrimage to Pandharpur, Ganesh Chaturthi celebrations popularised in Mumbai, and Marathi theatre and cinema.
