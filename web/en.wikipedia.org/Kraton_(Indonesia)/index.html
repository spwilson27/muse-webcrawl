Kraton (Indonesia)

Kraton
MeaningRoyal palace of a Javanese or related Indonesian ruler
Notable examplesKraton Yogyakarta; Kraton Surakarta; Kasepuhan (Cirebon); Mangkunegaran
FeaturesPendopo audience hall, alun-alun square, sacred heirlooms (pusaka)

A kraton or keraton is the Javanese word for a royal palace, the residence and ceremonial centre of a sultan or susuhunan in Indonesia. Derived from ratu ("ruler") with circumfix ke-…-an, the term denotes both the physical palace complex and the court centred on it. Comparable palaces exist in Cirebon, Surakarta, Yogyakarta and in Malay courts of Sumatra and Kalimantan.

Layout

A kraton is typically laid out as a walled complex aligned on a cosmological axis, with a broad open square (alun-alun) to the north shaded by banyan trees, a grand mosque nearby, and a series of courtyards leading to open pillared audience halls called pendopo. Inner quarters house the ruler's family, court retainers (abdi dalem), workshops for gamelan and wayang, and the treasury of sacred heirlooms, or pusaka.

Surviving courts

The most prominent are the Kraton Ngayogyakarta Hadiningrat in Yogyakarta — still home to the Sultan, who serves as governor of the Special Region — and the Kraton Surakarta Hadiningrat in Solo, seat of the Susuhunan of the Surakarta Sunanate since 1745. Cirebon has several kratons, including Kasepuhan and Kanoman. Although their political power is now largely ceremonial, the kratons remain guardians of Javanese dance, gamelan music, batik and court etiquette.
