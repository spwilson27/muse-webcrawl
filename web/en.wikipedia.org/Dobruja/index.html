Dobruja

LocationSoutheast Europe, between the lower Danube and the Black Sea
CountriesRomania (Northern Dobruja), Bulgaria (Southern Dobruja)
Area~23,100 km²
Largest cityConstanța

Dobruja (Romanian: Dobrogea; Bulgarian: Добруджа, Dobrudzha) is a geographical and historical region of Southeastern Europe situated between the lower Danube River and the Black Sea, including the Danube Delta. It is shared by Bulgaria and Romania: Northern Dobruja belongs to Romania (the counties of Constanța and Tulcea) and Southern Dobruja to Bulgaria (the provinces of Dobrich and Silistra).

Geography

The region is a hilly plateau rising to about 467 m in the Măcin Mountains (Romanian side), with a steppe-like climate in the south. The Danube Delta, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and biosphere reserve, lies at its northern end. The port of Constanța is Romania's principal seaport, linked to the Danube by the Danube–Black Sea Canal.

History

Greek colonies such as Histria, Tomis and Callatis dotted the coast from the 7th century BC. Dobruja later passed through Roman, Byzantine, Bulgarian, Genoese and Ottoman rule. Mircea the Elder of Wallachia held it briefly before Ottoman annexation in the early 15th century. After the Russo-Turkish War, the Treaty of Berlin (1878) awarded Northern Dobruja to Romania; Southern Dobruja was contested between Romania and Bulgaria — Romanian from 1913 to 1940, when the Treaty of Craiova returned it to Bulgaria, accompanied by a population exchange.

Population

Dobruja is ethnically diverse, with Romanians, Bulgarians, Turks, Tatars, Lipovan Russians, Greeks, Roma and others, reflecting its history as a crossroads between the Balkans, the steppe and the sea.
