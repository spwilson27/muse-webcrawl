Xining

Xining (西宁; pinyin: Xīníng) is the capital and largest city of Qinghai province in northwestern China, and the largest city on the Tibetan Plateau. Sitting at an elevation of about 2,275 metres (7,464 ft) in the Huangshui River valley, it has long been a trading post linking China proper with Tibet and Central Asia.

History

The valley has been inhabited for over 2,100 years; a Han dynasty garrison was established here after Huo Qubing's campaigns against the Xiongnu. Xining stood on a southern branch of the Silk Road and later on the Tea-Horse Road to Lhasa. It was successively governed by Chinese dynasties, Tibetan peoples, the Western Xia, and the Mongols. In 1929 it became the capital of the newly created Qinghai province. Under the warlord Ma Bufang in the Republic era, Xining was developed as the seat of the Ma clique.

Geography and climate

Xining occupies a narrow east–west valley where the Huangshui (a tributary of the Yellow River) passes between mountains. Its high-altitude continental climate brings cool summers — the city is a popular summer retreat from China's hot eastern cities — and cold, dry, sunny winters.

People and culture

The population of the prefecture-level city is about 2.5 million, ethnically mixed among Han Chinese, Hui Muslims, Tibetans, Tu, Mongols, and others. Landmarks include the Dongguan Grand Mosque, one of China's oldest and largest mosques, and the Northern Monastery (Beishan). About 100 km southwest is Kumbum Monastery (Ta'er Si) at Huangzhong, one of the great monasteries of Tibetan Buddhism and the birthplace of Tsongkhapa.

Transport

Xining is the starting point of the Qinghai–Tibet railway to Lhasa, the highest railway in the world, which opened in 2006. Lanzhou–Xinjiang high-speed rail also passes through the city.
