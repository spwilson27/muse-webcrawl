Choice

A choice is the act of selecting or making a decision when faced with two or more possibilities. The concept is central to psychology, economics, philosophy, and everyday life: to choose is to pick one course of action from a set of alternatives, usually after some process of deliberation, whether conscious or unconscious.

In psychology

Psychological research on choice examines how people evaluate options, weigh costs and benefits, and commit to a course of action. Studies of decision-making show that choices are influenced by framing, defaults, emotions, and cognitive biases. Having too many options can produce "choice overload", making people less likely to decide and less satisfied with the outcome.

In economics

In economics, choice is foundational: because resources are scarce, agents must allocate them among competing uses. Rational choice theory models individuals as choosing the option that maximises their utility, subject to constraints. Revealed preference theory infers preferences from observed choices rather than from stated intentions.

In philosophy

Philosophers debate whether choices are genuinely free or determined by prior causes, a question tied to the problem of free will. Existentialist thinkers such as Jean-Paul Sartre emphasised that human beings are "condemned to be free", responsible even for the choice not to choose.
