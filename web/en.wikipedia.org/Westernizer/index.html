Westernizer

[Image: Westernizer]

The Westernizers (Russian: западники, zapadniki) were a group of 19th-century Russian intellectuals who believed that Russia's development depended on the adoption of Western European technology, thought, government and culture. They stood in opposition to the Slavophiles, who argued that Russia should develop according to its own unique traditions and Orthodox heritage.

Beliefs

Westernizers admired the reforms of Peter the Great, which they saw as an attempt to bring Russia into the European mainstream. They generally advocated constitutional government, the abolition of serfdom, freedom of the press, and the rule of law. Prominent figures included the literary critic Vissarion Belinsky, the writer Ivan Turgenev, the philosopher Alexander Herzen (in his early period), and the historian Timofey Granovsky.

The debate

The controversy between Westernizers and Slavophiles, which flourished in the 1840s and 1850s in Moscow literary salons, shaped Russian intellectual life for decades. While the Slavophiles idealized pre-Petrine Russia and the peasant commune, the Westernizers saw European liberalism and science as the universal path of progress. The debate echoes in later arguments about Russia's identity between East and West.
