Modal realism

Modal realism is the philosophical view, most famously defended by David Lewis (1941–2001), that all possible worlds are real — as concrete and actual-to-their-inhabitants as the world we inhabit is to us. On this view, "actual" is an indexical term like "here" or "now": each world is actual from the perspective of its own inhabitants, and no world is metaphysically privileged.

Lewis's account

Lewis presented the theory in Counterfactuals (1973) and systematically in On the Plurality of Worlds (1986). Key claims include:

  • There are infinitely many possible worlds, causally and spatiotemporally isolated from one another.
  • Every way a world could possibly be is a way some world is.
  • Individuals exist in only one world; talk of the same person in another world is handled by counterpart theory — a person has counterparts, similar individuals, in other worlds rather than existing in several.
  • Modal claims (about possibility and necessity) are analysed in terms of what happens at other worlds: something is possible if it happens at some world, necessary if it happens at all worlds.

Advantages claimed

Lewis argued that modal realism yields a reductive, unified account of modality, counterfactuals, propositions, and properties, comparing its theoretical payoff to the usefulness of set theory in mathematics. He held the theory to be a form of philosophical "paradise" purchased at the price of an extravagant ontology, which he considered a fair bargain.

Objections

The best-known response is the "incredulous stare": many philosophers find the existence of infinitely many concrete universes — with talking donkeys and other possibilities realised — simply unbelievable, an objection Lewis acknowledged but did not consider decisive. Other criticisms target counterpart theory, the isolation of worlds, and the theory's implications for ethics and epistemology. Alternatives include ersatz modal realisms, which represent possible worlds as abstract objects such as sets of sentences or propositions.
