The Grand River, formerly known as the River Ouse, is a large river in southwestern Ontario, Canada. It flows 280 kilometres from headwaters near Dundalk southward through the cities of Kitchener, Waterloo, Cambridge, Paris, and Brantford before emptying into Lake Erie at Port Maitland. It is the largest river flowing entirely within southern Ontario, draining a watershed of about 6,800 square kilometres.

History

The river valley was home to the Neutral Nation and later the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois). Under the Haldimand Proclamation of 1784, lands six miles deep on each side of the river were granted to the Six Nations in recognition of their alliance with Britain during the American Revolution; disputes over this land, centred on the Six Nations of the Grand River reserve near Brantford, continue to this day. European settlement brought mills, canals, and the growth of the manufacturing cities of the lower valley.

Environment and recreation

The river was designated a Canadian Heritage River in 1994. Its watershed, managed by the Grand River Conservation Authority (founded 1934, one of Ontario's oldest), includes the Elora Gorge, cold-water trout fisheries on the upper river, and reservoirs such as Belwood Lake that provide flood control and low-flow augmentation for the roughly one million people now living in the basin.
