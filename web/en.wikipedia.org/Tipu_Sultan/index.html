Tipu Sultan

Ruler of Mysore
Reign: 1782–1799
Born: 1 December 1751, Devanahalli

Tipu Sultan (1 December 1751 – 4 May 1799), also known as the Tiger of Mysore, was the ruler of the Kingdom of Mysore in southern India from 1782 until his death. The son of Hyder Ali, he continued his father's resistance to the expansion of the British East India Company.

Wars with the British

Tipu fought the British in the Second, Third, and Fourth Anglo-Mysore Wars, allying at times with the French. His forces made pioneering use of iron-cased rockets, which influenced the later British Congreve rockets. In the Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–1792) he lost roughly half his territory under the Treaty of Seringapatam. In 1799 a British force under General Harris stormed Seringapatam, and Tipu was killed defending the city.

Administration and legacy

Tipu introduced administrative and economic reforms, promoted sericulture, and maintained an extensive diplomatic network reaching France, the Ottoman Empire, and Afghanistan. His legacy remains contested: he is remembered both as a fierce resistor of colonial expansion and, by critics, for persecution of religious minorities in Malabar and Kodagu.
