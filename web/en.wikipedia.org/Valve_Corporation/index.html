Valve Corporation, also known as Valve Software, is an American video game developer, publisher, and digital distribution company headquartered in Bellevue, Washington. It is the developer of the software distribution platform Steam and the game franchises Half-Life, Counter-Strike, Portal, Day of Defeat, Team Fortress, Left 4 Dead, and Dota.

Valve was founded in 1996 by former Microsoft employees Gabe Newell and Mike Harrington. Their debut game, the first-person shooter Half-Life (1998), was a critical and commercial success; its sequel Half-Life 2 (2004) launched with Valve's Steam platform and the Source game engine. Steam grew into the dominant digital storefront for PC games, with tens of millions of users and thousands of titles. In 2012 Valve released Dota 2, one of the world's most popular esports. Valve later entered hardware with the Steam Machine initiative, the Valve Index virtual reality headset (2019), and the Steam Deck handheld console (2022).

History

Newell and Harrington left Microsoft in 1996 and licensed the Quake engine from id Software to build Half-Life. Harrington sold his stake and left in 2000. Counter-Strike, begun as a fan modification of Half-Life, was acquired by Valve and became one of the most played online shooters. In 2003 Valve launched Steam, initially controversial for its required online activation, which evolved into a storefront hosting most PC game sales. The company released The Orange Box (2007), including Portal and Team Fortress 2, and Half-Life: Alyx (2020), a flagship VR title.

Company structure

Valve is privately held and noted for its flat organisational structure: since its founding, employees have largely chosen their own projects without formal managers, a model described in the company's employee handbook, which attracted wide attention when it leaked in 2012. The company employs only a few hundred people yet generates among the highest revenue per employee in the technology industry, chiefly from its percentage of Steam sales.

Steam

Steam, launched in September 2003, is Valve's digital distribution service and storefront. It provides digital rights management, matchmaking servers, anti-cheat (Valve Anti-Cheat), cloud saves, community features, and the Steam Workshop for user-generated content. Valve typically takes a 30% revenue share on sales, a figure that has drawn criticism and competition from rival stores.
