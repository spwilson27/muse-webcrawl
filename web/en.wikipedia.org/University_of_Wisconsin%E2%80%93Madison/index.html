The University of Wisconsin–Madison (University of Wisconsin, Wisconsin, UW, UW–Madison, or simply Madison) is a public land-grant research university in Madison, Wisconsin, United States. Founded when Wisconsin achieved statehood in 1848, it is the oldest and largest public university in the state and the flagship campus of the University of Wisconsin System.

History

The university held its first classes in 1849 and became a land-grant institution under the Morrill Act in 1866. Under President Charles R. Van Hise in the early twentieth century it articulated the "Wisconsin Idea" — the principle that the university's research and expertise should serve the entire state — a philosophy that shaped American public higher education. The university was a founding member of the Association of American Universities in 1900. Notable research milestones include the discovery of vitamins A and B by Elmer McCollum, the development of warfarin, and Harry Steenbock's vitamin D irradiation process, whose patents funded the Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation (WARF).

Academics

UW–Madison is organised into colleges and schools including Letters and Science, Agricultural and Life Sciences, the Wisconsin School of Business, Engineering, Education, Law, Medicine and Public Health, Nursing, Pharmacy and Veterinary Medicine. It awards over 9,000 degrees annually and spends more than $1.5 billion on research each year, ranking among the top American universities in research expenditure. The university is the home of the Wisconsin Institutes for Discovery and the Morgridge Institute for Research.

Campus and athletics

The campus stretches along the southern shore of Lake Mendota, encompassing Bascom Hill, the Memorial Union terrace, and the Lakeshore Nature Preserve. The Wisconsin Badgers compete in the NCAA Division I Big Ten Conference; Camp Randall Stadium is one of the oldest college football stadiums in the United States, and the student tradition of "Jump Around" between the third and fourth quarters is nationally famous.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include 20 Nobel laureates, among them John Bardeen (alumnus) and Herbert Gasser; architect Frank Lloyd Wright attended briefly; aviator Charles Lindbergh studied engineering there; and novelist and filmmaker alumni range from Eudora Welty (graduate studies) to the creators of The Onion, founded in Madison in 1988.
