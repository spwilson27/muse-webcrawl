Turpan

Turpan (Uyghur: تۇرپان; Chinese: 吐鲁番, Tǔlǔfān), also known as Turfan, is a prefecture-level city in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region of China. It lies in the Turpan Depression — one of the lowest and hottest places in China — on the northern route of the Silk Road around the Taklamakan.

Geography and climate

The depression's floor at Ayding Lake, about 154 m below sea level, is China's lowest point. Summers are among China's hottest — the "Fire Land" near the Flaming Mountains — while rainfall is minimal; agriculture depends on karez (qanat) underground channels carrying mountain snowmelt.

History

Ancient kingdoms centred on the ruined cities of Jiaohe and Gaochang; the region was the Qocho (Gaochang) Uyghur kingdom, a centre of Buddhism and Manichaeism from which the Turfan manuscripts derive. It passed through Mongol, Ming-era and Qing control, and the Emin Minaret (1778) commemorates a local ruler loyal to the Qing.

Economy

Famed for seedless white grapes dried into raisins in brick drying houses, melons and wine; tourism centres on the ruins, the Karez Museum, Grape Valley and Bezeklik Thousand Buddha Caves.
