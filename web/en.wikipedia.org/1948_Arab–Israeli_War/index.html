1948 Arab–Israeli War

Date15 May 1948 – 10 March 1949 (armistices signed through 20 July 1949)
LocationFormer British Mandate of Palestine, Sinai Peninsula, southern Lebanon
ResultIsraeli victory; establishment of the 1949 Armistice Lines (Green Line)
BelligerentsIsrael; Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon, Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Yemen and Palestinian Arab forces

The 1948 Arab–Israeli War was the first war between the State of Israel and its Arab neighbours. Known in Israel as the War of Independence (Hebrew: מלחמת העצמאות) and in Arabic as part of the Nakba (النكبة, "the catastrophe"), it began on 15 May 1948, the day after Israel's declaration of independence and the expiry of the British Mandate for Palestine.

Origins

The war followed months of civil conflict in Mandatory Palestine between Jewish and Arab communities after the United Nations General Assembly passed Resolution 181(II) on 29 November 1947, recommending partition into two states. The Arab states and the Arab Higher Committee rejected partition, and when Israel declared independence, five Arab armies invaded the former Mandate territory.

Fighting

Initial Arab gains included the capture of the Jewish Quarter of Jerusalem's Old City by Transjordan's Arab Legion and an Egyptian advance along the coastal plain that was halted at Ashdod. Two UN-brokered truces (June–July and July onwards) punctuated the fighting. Israel used the pauses to import arms — including aircraft and artillery from Czechoslovakia — and to consolidate its forces into the IDF, eventually fielding over 100,000 troops.

Israeli offensives in July 1948 captured the towns of Lydda and Ramle and much of the Galilee; operations in October and December broke the Egyptian front in the Negev and opened the road to Eilat, reached in Operation Uvda in March 1949, the war's final operation.

Outcome

Armistice agreements signed on Rhodes in 1949 left Israel in control of roughly 78 per cent of Mandatory Palestine, substantially more than the Partition Plan had allotted. Jordan took the West Bank and East Jerusalem, and Egypt retained the Gaza Strip. No Palestinian Arab state was established.

More than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled during the 1947–49 war and were not permitted to return, becoming the core of the Palestinian refugee problem. Israel, describing the war as one of survival, lost about one per cent of its population, some 6,000 people. The conflict set the pattern for subsequent Arab–Israeli wars and remains central to the national narratives of both peoples.
