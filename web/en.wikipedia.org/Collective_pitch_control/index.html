Collective pitch control

The collective pitch control is one of the primary flight controls of a helicopter. It changes the pitch angle of all main-rotor blades simultaneously ("collectively"), increasing or decreasing the total lift produced by the rotor and thereby making the helicopter climb or descend.

Operation

The collective is typically a lever on the pilot's left side that is raised to increase pitch (and lift) and lowered to decrease it. Because increasing pitch also increases drag and torque, raising the collective requires more engine power; in most modern helicopters a governor or FADEC automatically adjusts the throttle to maintain rotor speed, while on simpler types the pilot twists a throttle grip on the collective lever.

The collective works together with the cyclic pitch control, which tilts the rotor disc for directional movement, and the anti-torque pedals, which counteract the torque changes produced when collective (and power) is changed.
