Pierre-Simon Laplace

Pierre-Simon, Marquis de Laplace (23 March 1749 – 5 March 1827) was a French scholar and polymath whose work was important to the development of engineering, mathematics, statistics, physics, astronomy, and philosophy. He summarised and extended the work of his predecessors in his five-volume Mécanique céleste (1799–1825), translating the geometric study of classical mechanics into one based on calculus, and opening up a broader range of problems. He was one of the first scientists to propose the existence of black holes and the notion of gravitational collapse.

Work

Laplace proved the stability of the Solar System on long timescales, developed the nebular hypothesis of its origin, and pioneered Bayesian probability in his Théorie analytique des probabilités (1812). His name is attached to the Laplace transform, Laplace's equation, the Laplacian operator, and Laplace's demon — the thought experiment expressing his deterministic philosophy that a sufficiently vast intellect knowing all positions and forces could embrace past and future in a single formula. When Napoleon asked why God did not appear in his system of the world, Laplace is said to have replied, "I had no need of that hypothesis."

Politics

Laplace served briefly as Minister of the Interior under Napoleon in 1799 and later supported the Bourbon Restoration; he was made a marquis in 1817.
