Imperial units

The imperial system of units, imperial system or imperial units (also known as British Imperial or Exchequer Standards of 1826) is the system of units first defined in the British Weights and Measures Act 1824 and continued to be developed through a series of Weights and Measures Acts and amendments.

The imperial system developed from earlier English units as did the related United States customary units. The imperial units replaced the Winchester Standards, which were in use from 1588 to 1826.

Units

  • Length: inch, foot (12 inches), yard (3 feet), mile (1,760 yards), and nautical mile.
  • Mass/weight: ounce, pound (16 ounces), stone (14 pounds), hundredweight (112 pounds), and ton (2,240 pounds).
  • Volume: fluid ounce, gill, pint (20 imperial fluid ounces), quart, and the imperial gallon (4.54609 litres, larger than the US gallon).

Current use

The system came into official use across the British Empire. By the late 20th century most nations of the former empire had officially adopted the metric system (SI) as their main system of measurement, though imperial units remain in everyday use in the United Kingdom — for road distances (miles), draught beer (pints), and personal height and weight (feet/inches, stones) — and Canada uses a mixture of metric and imperial. In the United Kingdom, metrication began in 1965 and is largely complete, with defined exceptions.
