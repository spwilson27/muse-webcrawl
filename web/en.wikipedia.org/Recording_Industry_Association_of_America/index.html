The Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA) is a trade organisation that represents the US recording industry. Founded in 1952, its members include the major record labels—Universal Music Group, Sony Music Entertainment, and Warner Music Group—along with hundreds of independent labels and distributors, which together create, manufacture, and distribute the great majority of recorded music sold in the United States.

Activities

The RIAA engages in government relations and lobbying on copyright and music policy, works to protect the intellectual property rights of artists and labels, conducts research on the music industry, and operates the best-known music certification programme in the US.

Gold and Platinum programme

Since 1958 the RIAA has certified sales of singles and albums: Gold (500,000 units), Platinum (1,000,000), Multi-Platinum, and Diamond (10,000,000), introduced in 1999. The first Gold single was Perry Como's "Catch a Falling Star" (1958) and the first Gold album the soundtrack of Oklahoma!. In 2013 and 2016 the programme was updated to count on-demand streams and track-equivalent units. The RIAA also created the Los Premios De Oro y Platino programme for Latin recordings in 2000.

Technical standards and controversies

In its early years the RIAA standardised technical matters such as the RIAA equalisation curve for vinyl records. The organisation became controversial in the 2000s for suing individuals over peer-to-peer file sharing, a litigation campaign ended in 2008 in favour of education and ISP cooperation. It also played a role in the 1985 Senate hearings that led to the "Parental Advisory" sticker system.
