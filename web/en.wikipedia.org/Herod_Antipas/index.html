Herod Antipas

Herod Antipas (c. 20 BC – after AD 39) was a 1st-century ruler of Galilee and Perea, son of Herod the Great. Bearing the title tetrarch, he governed from 4 BC until his deposition in AD 39. He founded the city of Tiberias on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee as his capital.

Herod Antipas
Reign: 4 BC – AD 39
Title: Tetrarch of Galilee and Perea
Brother: Herod Agrippa (half-brother)

John the Baptist and Jesus

Antipas is best known from the New Testament: he imprisoned and executed John the Baptist, who had condemned his marriage to Herodias, his half-brother's former wife. The Gospels also describe Jesus being sent before Antipas by Pontius Pilate during the Passion, and Jesus referring to him as "that fox."

Downfall

After a disastrous war with the Nabataean king Aretas IV, Antipas sought the title of king at Rome; accusations brought by his nephew Agrippa led Caligula to depose and exile him to Gaul in AD 39. See also Herodian dynasty.
