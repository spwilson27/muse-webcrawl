Government


A seat of government: parliaments house the legislative branch.

A government is the system or group of people governing an organized community, generally a state. In its broadest sense, government consists of the institutions and processes by which a society makes and enforces collective decisions: typically a legislature that makes laws, an executive that implements them, and a judiciary that interprets them. Governments exercise authority through a monopoly, claimed to be legitimate, on the use of force within a territory, and they finance their activities mainly through taxation.

Forms of government

Political systems are classified by who holds power and how it is exercised. Democracies vest authority in elected representatives or in citizens directly; monarchies in a hereditary sovereign, either absolute or constitutional; republics in elected heads of state; and authoritarian and totalitarian systems concentrate power in a ruler or party that tolerates little opposition. Governments also differ by territorial structure — unitary states concentrate authority centrally, while federations divide it constitutionally between a centre and regions — and by the relation between legislature and executive, as in parliamentary and presidential systems.

Functions

Beyond law-making and enforcement, modern governments provide public goods and services such as defence, infrastructure, education and health care, regulate markets, redistribute income through welfare programmes, and conduct foreign relations. The size and role of government — measured by public spending as a share of GDP, which ranges from under 20% to over 50% among developed economies — is a central question of political debate. Theories of the state's origin range from social contract accounts (Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau) to explanations based on conquest, economic surplus and the management of irrigation or war.

Levels

Most states have several tiers: national government, regional or state governments, and local government (counties, municipalities), each with defined competences. Above the state, supranational organizations such as the European Union exercise limited governmental functions pooled by member states.
