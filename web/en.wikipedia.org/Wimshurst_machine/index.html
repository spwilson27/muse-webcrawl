Wimshurst machine

TypeElectrostatic generator
InventorJames Wimshurst
Developed1880–1883

The Wimshurst machine is an electrostatic generator developed between 1880 and 1883 by British inventor James Wimshurst. It uses two counter-rotating insulating disks with metal sectors to accumulate high-voltage static electricity by electrostatic induction, producing dramatic sparks.

Description

Two glass or acrylic disks rotate in opposite directions, driven by a hand crank or motor. Metal foil sectors on the disks pass under brushes connected by neutralizing rods; charge is collected by combs into Leyden jars or terminals, building potentials of tens of thousands of volts. Unlike earlier friction machines, the Wimshurst machine is self-exciting and works reliably in most weather.

Uses

Wimshurst machines were used in early X-ray experiments and high-voltage demonstrations and remain popular as classroom demonstration devices.
