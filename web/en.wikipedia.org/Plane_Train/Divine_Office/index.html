Divine Office

The Divine Office, also called the Liturgy of the Hours or canonical hours, is the daily cycle of prayer of the Catholic Church (with counterparts in Eastern Orthodoxy, Anglicanism, and Lutheranism), consisting of psalms, hymns, scripture readings, and prayers appointed for fixed hours of the day. Its traditional hours are Matins (night office), Lauds (dawn), Prime, Terce, Sext, None, Vespers (evening), and Compline (before sleep).

History

The practice descends from Jewish daily prayer and the early Church's custom of praying at the third, sixth, and ninth hours. The Rule of Saint Benedict (c. 530) organized the monastic office around the weekly recitation of all 150 psalms, and Benedictine usage shaped the Roman office spread across the West. The chants of the office — antiphons, responsories, and hymns — form much of the Gregorian chant repertory; Eastern churches developed parallel hours served by Byzantine chant.

Modern forms

The Breviary collects the office's texts; after the Second Vatican Council, Paul VI's Laudis Canticum (1970) promulgated a revised Liturgy of the Hours with a four-week psalter and expanded readings, intended for clergy, religious, and laity alike. Anglican worship preserves the office as Morning and Evening Prayer in the Book of Common Prayer.
