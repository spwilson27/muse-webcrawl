CubeSat

Unit (1U)10 × 10 × 10 cm, ≤ 2 kg (modern spec)
Proposed1999, Cal Poly / Stanford
First launches2003
Typical orbitLow Earth orbit

A CubeSat is a class of small satellite built to a standard form factor of 10-centimetre cubes, called units (U). A 1U CubeSat is one cube; common configurations are 1U, 2U, 3U, 6U, and 12U. The standard was proposed in 1999 by Jordi Puig-Suari of California Polytechnic State University and Bob Twiggs of Stanford University so that students could design, build, and test satellites cheaply, using a common deployer (the P-POD) that rides as a secondary payload.

Development and uses

The first CubeSats reached orbit in June 2003. Built from commercial off-the-shelf components, CubeSats moved from university teaching tools to mainstream use: technology demonstration, science missions, and commercial Earth observation constellations such as Planet's Dove satellites, plus remote sensing and communications experiments. In 2018 NASA's MarCO-A and MarCO-B became the first CubeSats to fly to another planet, relaying data from the InSight Mars landing.

Challenges

CubeSats face tight power, volume, and communications budgets, limited propulsion (a contributor to orbital-debris concerns), and high failure rates among first-time builders; nevertheless, well over two thousand had been launched by the mid-2020s, and the standard is maintained by Cal Poly with an openly published specification.
