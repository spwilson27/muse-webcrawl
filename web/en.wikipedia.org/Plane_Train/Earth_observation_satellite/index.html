Earth observation satellite

An Earth observation satellite or Earth remote sensing satellite is an artificial satellite designed to observe the Earth from orbit for environmental monitoring, weather forecasting, mapping, agriculture, disaster response, and reconnaissance. Most operate in low Earth orbit, especially sun-synchronous polar orbits that image each place at the same local solar time; weather satellites also fly in geostationary orbit.

Instruments

Payloads range from optical and multispectral imagers (visible and infrared cameras) to synthetic-aperture radar, which images through cloud and darkness; lidar altimeters measuring ice and canopy height; microwave radiometers and sounders profiling temperature and moisture; and ocean-colour and gravity instruments. Spatial resolution varies from kilometres for weather imagers to under a metre for commercial and military systems.

Programmes and history

The field began with TIROS-1 (1960) for weather imagery and the classified Corona reconnaissance programme, and widened to civil science with Landsat 1 (1972), whose archive underpins half a century of land-change studies. Modern fleets include the United States' Landsat and GOES series, Europe's Copernicus Sentinels, India's Resourcesat and Cartosat series, China's Gaofen satellites, and commercial constellations of CubeSats imaging the whole land surface daily.
