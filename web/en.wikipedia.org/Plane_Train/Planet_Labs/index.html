Planet Labs PBC is an American public Earth imaging company based in San Francisco, California. Founded in 2010 by former NASA scientists Will Marshall, Robbie Schingler, and Chris Boshuizen (originally as Cosmogia Inc.), the company builds and operates fleets of small satellites — CubeSats known as "Doves" — that together photograph the entire land mass of the Earth every day.

Satellite constellations

Planet's PlanetScope constellation of roughly 200 Dove satellites produces medium-resolution imagery (about 3–5 metres per pixel) on a daily cadence. Its SkySat constellation, acquired from Terra Bella (formerly Skybox Imaging) in 2017, provides higher-resolution imagery (around 0.7 metres per pixel) with tasking capability. The company's newer Pelican satellites are designed to succeed SkySat with still higher resolution and faster revisit, and its Tanager hyperspectral satellites detect emissions such as methane plumes.

Applications

Planet's daily global imagery is used in agriculture (crop monitoring), forestry and deforestation tracking, climate science, defence and intelligence, journalism (including documenting conflicts and disasters), mapping, and finance. Because the archive is continuous, users can compare images over time to detect change.

History

Planet launched its first demonstration CubeSats in 2013 and began deploying Doves in large batches ("flocks") from the International Space Station and on rockets such as the PSLV, which in 2017 carried a record flock of 88 Doves. The company went public in December 2021 through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company, reorganising as a public benefit corporation.
