Constable

A constable is a person holding a particular office, most commonly in law enforcement. The office developed from the medieval constable, an officer of a noble household responsible for the stables (from Latin comes stabuli, "count of the stable") who became a high military and state official — in France, the Constable of France was commander of the royal armies.

Modern police usage

In the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth countries, police constable (PC) is the lowest rank of a sworn police officer; all officers, regardless of rank, hold the original office of constable and derive their powers from it. Ranks above are sergeant, inspector, chief inspector, superintendent and chief officers. Special constables are volunteer officers with full police powers. The term also survives in parish constables, historically unpaid local officers, and in bodies such as the Royal Parks Constabulary.

United States

In the United States, constables are elected or appointed peace officers in many states, typically serving process, executing court orders and providing courtroom security; in Texas, constables are fully empowered peace officers elected by precinct, often with patrol and investigative functions. In Pennsylvania and other states the office is largely process-serving.

Other uses

Constable is also a military or ceremonial title in some countries, and "Constable of the Tower" remains a senior office of the Tower of London.
