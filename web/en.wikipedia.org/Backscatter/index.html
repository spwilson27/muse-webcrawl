Backscatter

In physics, backscatter (or backscattering) is the reflection of waves, particles, or signals back to the direction from which they came. It is a diffuse reflection caused by scattering, as opposed to specular (mirror-like) reflection. Backscattering is widely exploited in imaging, ranging, and measurement because a detector can be placed at the same location as the source.

Backscatter
Type: Wave/particle scattering phenomenon
Applications: Radar, lidar, electron microscopy, X-ray security imaging
Related: Rutherford backscattering spectrometry

Applications

Radar systems measure the backscatter from raindrops, aircraft, and terrain; weather radar estimates precipitation from the intensity of the returned signal. In oceanography and atmospheric science, lidar backscatter profiles reveal aerosols and water properties. Backscattered-electron imaging in scanning electron microscopy gives atomic-number contrast, while Rutherford backscattering spectrometry analyses thin films by detecting ions bounced back from a sample. Backscatter X-ray scanners, used for airport security, form images from low-energy X-rays reflected off the subject rather than transmitted through it.
