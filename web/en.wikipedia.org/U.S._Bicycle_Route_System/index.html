United States Bicycle Route System

The United States Bicycle Route System (abbreviated USBRS) is a national network of long-distance bicycle touring routes across the United States. The system links urban, suburban and rural corridors using existing roads and trails, and routes are designated with numbers in a manner analogous to the U.S. Highway System.

The USBRS is coordinated by the Adventure Cycling Association, working with the American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO), state departments of transportation, and local agencies. When fully built out, the network is planned to exceed 50,000 miles (80,000 km).

History

The concept of a national bicycle route network dates to the 1970s, when AASHTO approved the designation of the first two U.S. Bicycle Routes in 1978: U.S. Bicycle Route 1, following the Atlantic coast from Florida to Maine, and U.S. Bicycle Route 76, the "Bikecentennial" or TransAmerica route crossing the country from Virginia to Oregon, originally established for the United States Bicentennial in 1976.

Development stalled for many years, with only a handful of routes designated. In the 2000s, AASHTO adopted a National Corridor Plan laying out a grid of proposed corridors across the country, and from 2010 onward states began designating new routes and realigning existing ones at a much faster pace. By the early 2020s, more than 18,000 miles of routes had been officially designated in over 30 states.

Route numbering

Following a scheme similar to U.S. Highways, odd-numbered routes generally run north–south and even-numbered routes run east–west. Principal routes carry one- or two-digit numbers, while alternate and spur routes may carry three-digit numbers. Routes are signed with a distinctive green-and-white marker showing a bicycle symbol and the route number, though signage is installed by states and municipalities and coverage varies.

Notable routes

  • U.S. Bicycle Route 1 – Atlantic coast route from Key West, Florida, to the Canadian border in Maine.
  • U.S. Bicycle Route 76 – the TransAmerica Trail corridor from Yorktown, Virginia, to Astoria, Oregon.
  • U.S. Bicycle Route 20 – route across Michigan and the northern tier, including a ferry crossing of Lake Michigan.
  • U.S. Bicycle Route 50 – coast-to-coast corridor roughly following the Pony Express and Lincoln Highway alignments through Nevada, Utah and the Midwest.

Relationship to other networks

The USBRS is distinct from, but often overlaps, long-distance trails such as the East Coast Greenway and the Great American Rail-Trail, and several corridors correspond to Adventure Cycling Association touring routes. International connections link the system with Canada's Route Verte in Quebec and other Canadian cycling networks.
