Displacement (fluid)

In fluid mechanics, displacement occurs when an object is wholly or partly immersed in a fluid, pushing the fluid aside. The volume of the displaced fluid equals the immersed volume of the object; for a fully submerged object it equals the object's total volume.

Buoyancy

According to Archimedes' principle, the buoyant force on an immersed body equals the weight of the fluid it displaces. This is the basis of the classic story of Archimedes measuring the volume — and hence the density — of Hiero's crown by water displacement.

Applications

The displacement of a ship, expressed as a mass or weight, is a standard measure of vessel size: a floating ship displaces a weight of water equal to its own weight. Displacement is also used in laboratories to measure the volume of irregular solids using a displacement (Eureka) can or a graduated cylinder.
