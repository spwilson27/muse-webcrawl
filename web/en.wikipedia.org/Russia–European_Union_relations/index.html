Russia–European Union relations

Flags of Russia and the European Union.

Russia–European Union relations are the international relations between the Russian Federation and the European Union. Formal relations were established after the dissolution of the Soviet Union under the Partnership and Cooperation Agreement, signed in 1994 and in force from 1997. For many years the EU was Russia's largest trading partner, and Russia supplied a large share of the EU's natural gas, oil and coal.

Development and deterioration

Cooperation frameworks included the "Four Common Spaces" agreed in 2003 and regular summits. Relations deteriorated after the Russo-Georgian War of 2008 and sharply after Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, which prompted EU sanctions. Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, the EU adopted successive packages of sanctions, expelled Russian diplomats, and moved to end dependence on Russian fossil fuels, while most political dialogue was suspended.
