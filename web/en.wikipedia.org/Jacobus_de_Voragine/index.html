Jacobus de Voragine

Jacobus de Voragine
Born: c. 1228, Varazze, Italy
Died: 13/14 July 1298, Genoa
Occupation: Dominican friar, Archbishop of Genoa, author

Jacobus de Voragine (Italian: Giacomo da Varazze; c. 1228 – 1298) was an Italian Dominican friar who became Archbishop of Genoa and is best known as the author of the Golden Legend (Legenda aurea), a collection of saints' lives that became one of the most popular books of the later Middle Ages.

Life

Born at Varazze near Genoa, Jacobus joined the Dominican Order in 1244. He rose to become prior and then provincial of Lombardy, represented his province at general chapters, and served briefly in the order's government after the death of the Master General. In 1292 he was appointed Archbishop of Genoa, a post he held until his death in 1298. He was beatified by Pope Pius VII in 1816.

The Golden Legend

Compiled around 1260, the Legenda aurea arranges some 180 lives of saints and accounts of feasts according to the liturgical calendar, drawing on earlier collections and adding popular narrative detail. It was translated into every major European language, survives in roughly a thousand manuscripts, and was among the first books printed by William Caxton in English (1483). Its vivid stories shaped medieval and Renaissance art, providing the source for innumerable paintings and sculptures of saints and their attributes.

Other works

Jacobus also wrote a chronicle of Genoa, collections of sermons, and a life of the Virgin. His chronicle is a valuable, if local, source for the history of 13th-century Genoa.
