Japanese aircraft carrier Ryūjō

Ryūjō (Japanese: 龍驤, "Prancing Dragon") was a light aircraft carrier built for the Imperial Japanese Navy during the 1930s. Designed to exploit a loophole in the Washington Naval Treaty, which exempted carriers under 10,000 tons, she proved top-heavy and had to be rebuilt with a bulged hull and a raised forecastle. She was sunk by American carrier aircraft at the Battle of the Eastern Solomons on 24 August 1942.

Launched: 1931

Commissioned: 9 May 1933

Fate: Sunk 24 August 1942, Battle of the Eastern Solomons

Displacement: ~10,600 long tons (after rebuild)

Aircraft carried: ~36–48

Design

Ryūjō's small hull was originally intended for a single hangar, but the design was revised to two hangars stacked atop one another, giving her an unusually tall profile for her tonnage. Trials revealed dangerous instability; after the Tomozuru incident exposed stability problems across the fleet, she was reconstructed in 1934–36 with ballast, bulges and a reduced armament.

Service

Ryūjō served in the Second Sino-Japanese War and, in the Pacific War, supported the invasions of the Philippines and the Dutch East Indies. In April 1942 her aircraft raided British shipping in the Indian Ocean. At the Aleutians in June 1942 her planes attacked Dutch Harbor. In August 1942 she was detached to lure American forces during the Guadalcanal campaign; attacked by aircraft from USS Saratoga, she was hit by bombs and a torpedo and sank that evening with the loss of about 120 men.
