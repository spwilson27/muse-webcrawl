North Vancouver (district municipality)

The District of North Vancouver is a district municipality in British Columbia, Canada, and part of the Metro Vancouver Regional District. It surrounds the City of North Vancouver on three sides and stretches from the Capilano River in the west to Deep Cove in the east, along the north shore of Burrard Inlet at the foot of the North Shore Mountains. As of the 2021 census, its population was 88,168.

History

The District was incorporated in 1891, originally covering the whole north shore from Horseshoe Bay to Deep Cove. The City of North Vancouver separated in 1907, and the District of West Vancouver in 1912. Forestry, particularly logging of the mountain slopes and the mills of Moodyville, drove early settlement. The Lions Gate Bridge (1938) and the Upper Levels Highway improved access, and the district grew rapidly as a residential suburb after the Second World War.

Geography and neighbourhoods

Much of the district is steep, forested mountainside cut by canyons, including Lynn Canyon and the Capilano River canyon, spanned by the Capilano Suspension Bridge. Neighbourhoods include Lynn Valley, Capilano, Edgemont Village, Upper Lonsdale, and Deep Cove on Indian Arm. Grouse Mountain and Mount Seymour ski areas lie within or adjacent to the district. Large areas are protected as watershed lands of the Capilano and Seymour reservoirs, and as parks such as Lynn Headwaters Regional Park.

Recreation

The district is renowned for mountain biking trails on Mount Fromme and Mount Seymour, which played a central role in the development of freeride mountain biking, as well as hiking, skiing, and kayaking. Its motto and branding emphasise its outdoor setting.
