Sesquilinear form

In mathematics, a sesquilinear form is a generalisation of a bilinear form that is linear in one argument and conjugate-linear (antilinear) in the other. The prefix sesqui- means "one and a half".

Definition

On a complex vector space V, a sesquilinear form is a map B : V × V → ℂ that is linear in one variable and conjugate-linear in the other; authors differ on which variable carries which property. Every inner product on a complex vector space is a sesquilinear form (linear in one argument, conjugate-linear in the other), and Hermitian forms—satisfying B(x, y) = conjugate of B(y, x)—play the role that symmetric bilinear forms play over the reals.

Applications

Sesquilinear forms arise throughout functional analysis and quantum mechanics, where expectation values and transition amplitudes are naturally sesquilinear in the state vectors.
