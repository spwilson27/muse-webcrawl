Kim Il-sung

Kim Il-sung (15 April 1912 – 8 July 1994) was a Korean revolutionary and politician who founded North Korea (the Democratic People's Republic of Korea) and led it from its establishment in 1948 until his death in 1994. As premier and later president, he built a highly centralised one-party state around the Workers' Party of Korea and the ideology of Juche, and developed an extensive personality cult in which he is still venerated as the country's "Eternal President".

Early life and guerrilla years

Born Kim Song-ju in Mangyongdae near Pyongyang during the Japanese occupation of Korea, he grew up in Manchuria, where his family had fled. He joined anti-Japanese guerrilla units in the 1930s and later served as a captain in the Soviet Red Army's 88th Independent Rifle Brigade.

Rule of North Korea

Installed with Soviet backing in 1945, Kim proclaimed the DPRK in 1948. In 1950 he ordered the invasion of South Korea, beginning the Korean War, which ended in a 1953 armistice that left the peninsula divided. In the following decades he purged rival factions, collectivised agriculture and industry, and survived the Sino-Soviet split by balancing between Moscow and Beijing. Economic stagnation and famine marked his final years.

Death and legacy

Kim died of a heart attack in July 1994 and was succeeded by his son Kim Jong-il, establishing the first hereditary succession in a communist state. His embalmed body lies in the Kumsusan Palace of the Sun in Pyongyang, and his birthday, the "Day of the Sun", is North Korea's most important holiday.
