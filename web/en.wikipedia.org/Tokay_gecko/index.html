Tokay gecko

Tokay gecko
Scientific name: Gekko gecko
Family: Gekkonidae
Range: South and Southeast Asia

The tokay gecko (Gekko gecko) is a nocturnal arboreal gecko native to South and Southeast Asia, from northeastern India and Bangladesh through Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines, and introduced into places such as Florida and Hawaii. It is one of the largest geckos, reaching 30–35 cm in length.

Appearance and behaviour

Tokays are bluish-grey with orange or red spots and have large, prominent eyes with vertical pupils. Males produce a loud, distinctive mating call — a croaking "to-kay" — from which the species takes its name. They are aggressive, territorial, and capable of a painful bite, often gaping their mouths in threat display. Their adhesive toe pads let them climb smooth vertical surfaces and even ceilings.

Relationship with humans

Tokays are kept in the pet trade and are used in traditional medicine in parts of Asia, where unfounded claims about medicinal properties have driven heavy collection. They commonly live in and around houses, where they feed on insects and small vertebrates.
