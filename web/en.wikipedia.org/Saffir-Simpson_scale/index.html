Saffir–Simpson scale

The Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale (SSHWS) classifies hurricanes in the Atlantic and northern Pacific Oceans into five categories distinguished by the intensities of their sustained winds. It was developed in 1971 by civil engineer Herbert Saffir and meteorologist Robert Simpson, then director of the US National Hurricane Center.

Category 3 and above are described as major hurricanes. Earlier versions of the scale included central pressure and storm surge, but since 2010 the scale has been based purely on wind speed, renamed the Saffir–Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale.

Limitations

Because the scale measures only wind, it does not account for damage from flooding, storm surge or tornadoes, and forecasters caution that a lower-category storm can still be extremely dangerous.
