Shiraz

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: view of Shiraz, Iran]

Shiraz (Persian: شیراز) is the fifth-most-populous city of Iran and the capital of Fars province. At the 2016 census its population was about 1.57 million. Situated in the southwest of the country on the seasonal Rudkhaneye Khoshk river, Shiraz has a moderate climate and has been a regional trade centre for over a thousand years.

Shiraz is known as the city of poets, literature, wine (historically), and flowers. It is the birthplace and burial place of the celebrated Persian poets Hafez and Saadi, whose tombs are major pilgrimage and tourist sites. The city's gardens, such as Eram Garden, exemplify the Persian garden tradition, and its historic quarters preserve the Vakil Bazaar, Vakil Mosque, and the Arg of Karim Khan from the Zand period.

History

The area near Shiraz lies close to Persepolis, the ceremonial capital of the Achaemenid Empire, destroyed by Alexander the Great. The present city was founded or rebuilt in the early Islamic period, around 693 CE. Under the Buyids, the Safavids, and especially Karim Khan Zand — who made it his capital in the 18th century — Shiraz flourished as a centre of art and scholarship.

Shiraz wine was famous for centuries, lending its name (by folk association) to the Syrah grape style known in Australia as Shiraz, though modern scholarship links the grape's origin to France's Rhône valley instead.
