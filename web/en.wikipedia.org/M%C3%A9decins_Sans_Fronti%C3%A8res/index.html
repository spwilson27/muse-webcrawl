Médecins Sans Frontières

Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF), known in English as Doctors Without Borders, is a charity that provides humanitarian medical care in conflict zones and countries affected by endemic diseases.

TypeMedical humanitarian NGO
AwardNobel Peace Prize 1999

History

MSF was founded by French doctors and journalists, including Bernard Kouchner, after the Biafran famine and in response to perceived neutrality limits of the Red Cross. It embraces témoignage — bearing witness and speaking out.

Operations

MSF runs hospitals and clinics in over 70 countries, responds to epidemics such as Ebola and cholera, treats malnutrition, and has worked extensively in Syria, Sudan and Gaza. Its staff have faced attacks, notably the 2015 US bombing of its Kunduz hospital.
