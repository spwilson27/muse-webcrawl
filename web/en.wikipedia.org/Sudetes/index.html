Sudetes

The Sudetes (German: Sudeten; Czech: Sudety; Polish: Sudety) are a mountain range in Central Europe extending from eastern Germany through northern Bohemia (Czech Republic) into southwestern Poland. The range is about 300 kilometres long and forms part of the northeastern rim of the Bohemian Massif.

Geography

The Sudetes are conventionally divided into the Western, Central and Eastern Sudetes. The highest subrange is the Giant Mountains (Krkonoše/Karkonosze), whose peak Śnieżka (1,603 m) is the highest point of the Czech Republic. Other subranges include the Jizera, Ore-adjacent Lusatian, Orlické and Jeseníky mountains. The range is drained by the Elbe, Oder and Morava river systems and includes the Karkonosze National Park, a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

History

The mountains were largely German-speaking for centuries, and the term "Sudetenland" became notorious for the border regions of Czechoslovakia annexed by Nazi Germany under the 1938 Munich Agreement — the crisis that precipitated the Second World War. After 1945 the German population was expelled and the region resettled by Poles and Czechs.

Tourism

The Sudetes are a major area for hiking and skiing, with resorts such as Karpacz, Szklarska Poręba, Špindlerův Mlýn and Harrachov, and landmarks including the rock towns of Adršpach and the Table Mountains.
