Robert Gillespie (born 22 June 1961) is a Scottish musician, singer-songwriter, and multi-instrumentalist. He is best known as the lead singer and founding member of the alternative rock band Primal Scream. He was also the drummer for The Jesus and Mary Chain in the mid-1980s.

Early life and career

Born in Glasgow and raised in the Mount Florida district, Gillespie worked as a roadie and played in local bands before joining The Jesus and Mary Chain as drummer, appearing on their debut album Psychocandy (1985). He left to concentrate on Primal Scream, which he had founded in 1982 with guitarist Jim Beattie.

Primal Scream

Primal Scream began as an indie pop/jangle band (Sonic Flower Groove, 1987) before embracing rock and then rave culture. Their third album, Screamadelica (1991) — produced with Andrew Weatherall and including "Loaded" and "Movin' On Up" — fused house music with rock and gospel, won the first Mercury Prize in 1992, and is widely ranked among the greatest British albums. Subsequent albums ranged across dub, electronica, krautrock, and political rock: Vanishing Point (1997), XTRMNTR (2000), Riot City Blues (2006), Beautiful Future (2008), More Light (2013), Chaosmosis (2016), and the retrospective-era release Come Ahead (2024).

Other work

Gillespie has collaborated with artists including the Chemical Brothers (singing on "Out of Control," 1999). In 2021 he released Utopian Ashes, a duets album with Jehnny Beth, and published his memoir Tenement Kid, recounting his working-class Glasgow upbringing and musical career.
