Whale shark

ClassChondrichthyes (cartilaginous fish)

The whale shark (Rhincodon typus) is a slow-moving, filter-feeding carpet shark and the largest known extant fish species. Whale sharks inhabit the open waters of all tropical and warm-temperate seas and are known to migrate long distances to feed at seasonal aggregations of plankton.

Description

The whale shark has a broad, flattened head with a very large mouth, up to 1.5 m wide, and is marked by a distinctive checkerboard pattern of pale spots and stripes that is unique to each individual, allowing researchers to identify animals photographically. Despite its size — confirmed specimens reach over 12 m and reports suggest individuals approaching 19 m — it feeds on tiny organisms, filtering plankton, krill, fish eggs, and small squid through modified gill rakers.

Life history

Whale sharks are ovoviviparous: a female captured off Taiwan in 1995 carried some 300 embryos, the largest litter recorded for any shark. They grow slowly, are thought to mature at around 25–30 years of age, and may live 70 years or more. Seasonal aggregations occur at sites such as Ningaloo Reef (Australia), Yucatán (Mexico), and in the Gulf of Tadjoura (Djibouti).

Conservation

The species is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List, threatened by targeted fisheries, bycatch, vessel strikes, and habitat degradation. It is protected in many countries and is a popular species for ecotourism, including regulated swimming and diving encounters.
