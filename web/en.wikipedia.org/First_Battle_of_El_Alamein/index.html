First Battle of El Alamein

Date: 1–27 July 1942
Location: El Alamein, Egypt
Result: British defensive victory; Axis advance into Egypt halted
Belligerents: United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, India vs. Germany and Italy
Commanders: Claude Auchinleck vs. Erwin Rommel

The First Battle of El Alamein (1–27 July 1942) was a battle of the Western Desert campaign of the Second World War, fought in Egypt between Axis forces of the Panzer Army Africa, commanded by Erwin Rommel, and Allied forces of the British Eighth Army, commanded by General Claude Auchinleck.

Background

After defeating the Eighth Army at Gazala in June 1942 and capturing Tobruk, Rommel pursued the retreating British deep into Egypt. The British fell back to the El Alamein position, where the Mediterranean Sea to the north and the impassable Qattara Depression to the south formed a narrow defensive front that could not easily be outflanked.

The battle

Rommel attacked on 1 July but was checked by determined resistance and heavy artillery fire. Throughout July, Auchinleck launched a series of counterattacks against the weaker Italian formations holding parts of the Axis line, inflicting heavy losses, notably at Ruweisat Ridge and Tel el Eisa. Axis counterattacks in turn failed, and by late July both sides were exhausted. Rommel suspended the offensive, having failed to break through to Alexandria and the Nile Delta.

Aftermath

The battle halted the final Axis advance into Egypt and preserved the Suez Canal for the Allies. Although tactically inconclusive, it was strategically decisive: Rommel's overextended supply lines from Tripoli could not sustain a renewed offensive. In August, Auchinleck was replaced by Harold Alexander and Bernard Montgomery, who would win the Second Battle of El Alamein in October–November 1942.
