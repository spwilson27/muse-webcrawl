Pejorative

A pejorative word or grammatical form is an expression that connotes a negative or disrespectful attitude toward its subject, or carries a sense of disapproval. Pejoratives contrast with melioratives (amelioratives), which express approval, and with neutral terms; the same word can shift categories over time through pejoration or reclamation.

Pejorative
Type: Linguistic connotation
Opposite: Meliorative

Pejoration

Semantic change often drags words downward: terms such as "silly" (once "blessed") and "villain" (once "farmhand") acquired negative meanings. Titles and occupational words frequently undergo pejoration as social attitudes change.

Reclamation

Groups sometimes reclaim slurs and pejoratives as badges of identity, neutralizing or reversing their force within the in-group while they may remain offensive from outsiders.

Usage

Speakers deploy pejoratives—sometimes called "snarl words"—to persuade or disparage rather than describe, a rhetorical device long studied in logic and propaganda analysis alongside their positive counterparts, "purr words".
