Tintin

Tintin is the title character of The Adventures of Tintin, the comics series created by Belgian cartoonist Hergé (Georges Remi). A young reporter with a distinctive quiff, he travels the world with his fox terrier Snowy (Milou), becoming involved in adventures involving crime, exploration and politics. The series, first published in 1929, comprises 24 albums and has been translated into more than 70 languages, selling over 200 million copies.

Character and series

Tintin's age and background are deliberately vague — he is a reporter who rarely files a story, functioning as an all-purpose adventurer and moral centre. The albums, drawn in Hergé's ligne claire ("clear line") style with meticulous documentary research, range from detective stories (The Secret of the Unicorn) to science fiction (Destination Moon and Explorers on the Moon, which depicted a lunar landing 15 years before Apollo 11). Supporting characters include the blustering Captain Haddock, the deaf inventor Professor Calculus, and the incompetent detectives Thomson and Thompson.

Reception and legacy

The series is a cornerstone of Franco-Belgian comics, though some early albums are criticised for colonial and racial stereotypes, and Hergé revised several works. Adaptations include animated series and Steven Spielberg's 2011 film The Adventures of Tintin. The Hergé Museum opened in Louvain-la-Neuve in 2009.
