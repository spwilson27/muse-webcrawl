Charreada

The charreada (Spanish: [tʃareˈaða]) is a competitive rodeo event and the national sport of Mexico, stemming from the traditional working practices of charros, Mexican horsemen, on haciendas.

Events

A charreada consists of nine events, called suertes, performed in a lienzo charro arena. They include the cala de caballo (reining demonstration), piales (roping wild mares by the hind legs), coleadero (bull tailing), jineteo de toro (bull riding), terna en el ruedo (team roping of a bull), jineteo de yegua (bareback mare riding), manganas (forefooting a mare, on foot and on horseback), and the paso de la muerte (passing to a running mare at speed).

Escaramuza

Women compete in the escaramuza charra, a precision drill team event performed sidesaddle in traditional dress.

Recognition

In 2016 UNESCO inscribed charrería on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
