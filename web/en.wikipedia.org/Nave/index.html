Nave

The nave is the central part of a church, stretching from the main entrance (narthex) to the transepts, or, in a church without transepts, from the entrance to the chancel (area around the altar). Where the building has side aisles separated from the nave by colonnades or arcades, the term properly denotes the central open space. The nave is the part of the church where the congregation worships.

Etymology and history

The word derives from the Latin navis, "ship" — an early Christian symbol of the Church — and the form of early basilican churches. In medieval cathedrals the nave was often separated from the sanctuary by a rood screen, and in larger churches it may be divided from the aisles by rows of piers supporting a clerestory that admits light above the aisle roofs. The longest nave in England is at Winchester Cathedral.

Architecture

Naves vary from the timber-roofed halls of Anglo-Saxon churches to the stone-vaulted spans of Gothic cathedrals. Their scale, decoration, and acoustics reflect their role as the principal congregational space, traditionally furnished with pews facing the altar and pulpit.
