Rendang

Rendang is a Minangkabau dish originating from the Minangkabau region in West Sumatra, Indonesia. It consists of meat — most commonly beef — slow-cooked in coconut milk and a paste of mixed ground spices, which includes ginger, galangal, turmeric, lemongrass, garlic, shallots, and chillies. The cooking process takes hours and produces a dark, dry, intensely flavoured dish in which the sauce has reduced and caramelized around the meat.

Beef rendang, traditionally served with steamed rice at ceremonial occasions.

History and cultural significance

Rendang has been a centrepiece of Minangkabau feasts for centuries, served at ceremonial occasions such as weddings, Eid celebrations, and traditional ceremonies. In Minangkabau philosophy, the ingredients carry symbolic meanings: the meat represents clan leaders, the coconut milk the intellectuals, the chillies the religious scholars, and the spice mixture the rest of society. The dry cooking method also served a practical purpose, preserving meat for long journeys in the tropical climate before refrigeration.

Varieties and spread

As the Minangkabau migrated across the Malay world, rendang spread throughout Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, and Brunei, and into the Netherlands through the colonial connection. Most Indonesian restaurants worldwide serve a version of it. Variations include rendang made with chicken, lamb, duck, liver, or jackfruit. In a 2011 CNN online poll, rendang was voted the world's most delicious food, and it again topped the list in 2017. Indonesia's Ministry of Tourism named it one of the country's national dishes in 2018.
