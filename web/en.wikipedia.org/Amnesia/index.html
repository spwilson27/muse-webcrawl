Amnesia is a deficit in memory caused by brain damage or brain diseases, but it can also be temporarily caused by the use of various sedatives and hypnotic drugs. The memory can be either wholly or partially lost due to the extent of damage that is inflicted. There are two main types of amnesia: retrograde amnesia and anterograde amnesia. Retrograde amnesia is the inability to retrieve information that was acquired before a particular date, usually the date of an accident or operation. Anterograde amnesia is the inability to transfer new information from short-term storage into long-term storage; people with this type cannot remember things for long periods of time after the onset.

Causes

Amnesia can result from damage to brain regions vital for memory processing, particularly the medial temporal lobe and hippocampus. Causes include traumatic brain injury, stroke, tumours, encephalitis, chronic alcoholism (leading to Korsakoff syndrome, associated with thiamine deficiency), and neurodegenerative conditions. Transient global amnesia is a sudden, temporary episode of memory loss whose cause remains debated. Dissociative (psychogenic) amnesia arises from psychological trauma rather than structural injury.

Famous cases

The most famous case in neuroscience is Henry Molaison, known for decades as "Patient H.M.", whose bilateral medial temporal lobectomy in 1953 left him with profound anterograde amnesia while sparing his intellect and short-term memory; studying him revolutionised understanding of memory systems. Other notable cases include Clive Wearing, a British musician whose herpes encephalitis produced one of the most severe amnesias ever recorded, and Phineas Gage, whose case involved personality rather than memory change.

Treatment

There is no specific drug treatment for most amnesias. Management focuses on treating the underlying cause, rehabilitation, occupational therapy, and compensatory strategies such as structured routines, note-taking, and electronic memory aids. In Korsakoff syndrome, thiamine replacement may prevent further deterioration.
