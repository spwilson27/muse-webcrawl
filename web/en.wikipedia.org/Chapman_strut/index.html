Chapman strut


The Chapman strut, a strut-type independent rear suspension.

A Chapman strut is a design of independent rear suspension used especially in light sports and racing cars. It is named after Colin Chapman, founder of Lotus Cars, who introduced it on the Lotus Twelve in 1957 and on the Lotus Elite road car. The design adapts the principle of the MacPherson strut front suspension for use at the rear.

Design

In a Chapman strut, a long coil-over damper strut runs from the chassis down to the wheel hub carrier and, together with a lower transverse link and a fixed-length driveshaft acting as an upper link, locates the wheel. Because the strut itself is a structural, load-bearing member, the design is lighter and more compact than a double-wishbone arrangement, though wheel geometry control is more limited.

Applications

Chapman struts were fitted to many Lotus models, including the Elite, Elan, Europa, Esprit and early Eclat, as well as to racing cars such as the Lotus 12, 15, 16 and 17. The arrangement suited Chapman's philosophy of light weight and simplicity, and its low cost also appealed to other small manufacturers.

Comparison

Unlike the MacPherson strut, the Chapman strut does not steer, so it can be mounted more rigidly. Like its front-suspension counterpart it requires a stiff body structure at the strut top mount and permits some compliance steer, characteristics that modern multi-link designs were developed to overcome.
