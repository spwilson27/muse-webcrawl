Trinidad Scorpion Butch T pepper


Butch T pods, showing the characteristic scorpion-like tail

The Trinidad Scorpion Butch T pepper is a cultivar of Capsicum chinense derived from the Trinidad Scorpion pepper. It is named for Butch Taylor, the grower who propagated the strain. In 2011 it was recognized by Guinness World Records as the world's hottest chili pepper at that time.

Heat

Testing by the Australian team that submitted the record measured the pepper at approximately 1,463,700 Scoville heat units, surpassing the previous record holder, the Naga Viper. It was later surpassed by the Trinidad Moruga Scorpion and the Carolina Reaper.

Appearance

Like other Trinidad Scorpion varieties, the pepper is roughly golf-ball sized, wrinkled, and often bears a small pointed tail resembling a scorpion's stinger, which gives the group its name. The pods ripen from green to a bright red.

Use

Because of its extreme pungency, the pepper is used sparingly in hot sauces and cooking, and handlers typically wear gloves. It is also used in military-grade pepper sprays and marine paints intended to deter barnacles.
