Czesław Miłosz

Czesław Miłosz (1911–2004), Polish poet and Nobel laureate.

Czesław Miłosz (30 June 1911 – 14 August 2004) was a Polish-American poet, prose writer, translator and diplomat. Regarded as one of the great poets of the twentieth century, he won the 1980 Nobel Prize in Literature, cited by the Swedish Academy as a writer who "voices man's exposed condition in a world of severe conflicts". His best-known prose work, The Captive Mind (1953), is a classic analysis of intellectual life under totalitarianism.

Life

Miłosz was born in Šeteniai in present-day Lithuania, then part of the Russian Empire, and educated in Wilno (Vilnius), where he co-founded the Żagary poetry group and published his first collection in 1933. He survived the war in Warsaw, writing and participating in underground cultural life. After the war he served as a cultural attaché of communist Poland in New York, Washington and Paris — until 1951, when he broke with the regime and sought asylum in France, a defection that scandalised both the Polish government and parts of the Parisian left when The Captive Mind appeared.

In 1960 he moved to the United States, becoming professor of Slavic languages and literatures at the University of California, Berkeley. From California he produced much of his finest poetry and essays, translated Polish poetry into English (and his own work with Robert Pinsky and others), and waited out the decades in which his books were banned in Poland. The Nobel Prize made him a national icon there; after 1989 he divided his time between Berkeley and Kraków, where he died in 2004. He is buried in the crypt of Skałka church among Poland's honoured artists.

Work

Miłosz's poetry moves between metaphysical meditation, historical witness and the celebration of the sensory world, from the wartime "Campo dei Fiori" and "A Poor Christian Looks at the Ghetto" through the long philosophical poem A Treatise on Poetry (1957) to the late clarity of Second Space (2002). His essays — The Captive Mind, Native Realm (1959), The Land of Ulro — combine autobiography with a lifelong argument about faith, history and the seductions of ideology, often conducted in dialogue with his cousin, the French poet Oscar Miłosz.
