Tuna el-Gebel

Tuna el-Gebel (Arabic: تونا الجبل) is an archaeological site and necropolis in Middle Egypt, in Minya Governorate near the modern city of Mallawi, on the western edge of the Nile valley at the border of the desert. It served as the cemetery of the nearby city of Hermopolis Magna (Khmun), sacred to the god Thoth.

Archaeology

The site is best known for the tomb of Petosiris, a high priest of Thoth from the early Ptolemaic period (around 300 BCE), whose chapel-like tomb blends Egyptian and Greek artistic styles, and for vast underground galleries where millions of mummified ibises and baboons — animals sacred to Thoth — were buried as votive offerings. Other remains include the tomb of the priestess Isadora, Roman-period burials, and a boundary stela of Akhenaten marking the limits of his city of Akhetaten (Amarna) across the river region. Excavations, long led by Cairo University, continue to uncover burials and funerary houses.
