Tennessee Senate

The Tennessee Senate is the upper house of the Tennessee General Assembly, the state legislature of Tennessee. It comprises 33 members, each representing a district of roughly equal population for a four-year term, with about half the seats contested every two years. The Senate meets in the Tennessee State Capitol in Nashville.

Composition and elections

Senators must be at least 30 years old, citizens of the United States, residents of Tennessee for at least three years, and residents of their county for at least one year before election. Terms are staggered as required by the state constitution. Republicans have controlled the Senate since 2005 and hold a supermajority in recent assemblies.

Leadership

Uniquely among American states, the presiding officer of the Tennessee Senate — the Speaker of the Senate — also serves as Lieutenant Governor of Tennessee. The Speaker is elected by the Senate itself and appoints committees and their chairs. Randy McNally, a Republican from Oak Ridge, has held the office since January 2017. Other leaders include the Speaker Pro Tempore and the majority and minority leaders and caucus chairs.

Powers and role

The Senate shares the legislative power with the Tennessee House of Representatives. It tries impeachments brought by the House and has a major role in confirming or rejecting certain gubernatorial appointments, including members of state boards and commissions. The lieutenant governor's dual role gives the Senate's presiding officer influence over appointments to joint committees and commissions as well.
