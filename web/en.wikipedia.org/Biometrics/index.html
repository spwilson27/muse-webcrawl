Biometrics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Biometrics are body measurements and calculations related to human characteristics, used in computer science as a form of identification and access control. Biometric authentication identifies individuals by physiological traits (such as fingerprints, facial features, or iris patterns) or behavioural traits (such as voice, gait, or typing rhythm).

History

Fingerprints were used for identification in 19th-century India and systematised by Francis Galton and Edward Henry for policing. Automated systems emerged in the late 20th century, and consumer biometrics became ubiquitous with fingerprint and face-unlock smartphones in the 2010s.

How systems work

A biometric system enrols a user by capturing a sample and extracting a template, then later compares a fresh sample against the stored template to verify identity (1:1 matching) or search a database (1:N identification). Performance is measured by false-match and false-non-match rates, and by resistance to spoofing; modern systems add liveness detection.

Applications and concerns

Biometrics are used in passports and border control, national ID programmes such as India's Aadhaar, device security, and law enforcement. Because biometric traits cannot be changed like passwords, their collection raises privacy and security concerns, and several jurisdictions regulate them specially — for example the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act and the EU's GDPR.
