Northern Ireland Executive

[Image placeholder: Northern Ireland Executive]

The Northern Ireland Executive is the devolved government of Northern Ireland, an administrative branch of the legislature, the Northern Ireland Assembly. It was established under the Good Friday Agreement of 1998 and consists of the First Minister and deputy First Minister and nine departmental ministers.

Structure

Under the power-sharing arrangements, the First Minister and deputy First Minister — one unionist and one nationalist — hold a joint office with equal powers. Ministerial portfolios are allocated to parties proportionally using the d'Hondt method. The Executive governs on matters transferred from Westminster, including health, education, agriculture and justice (transferred in 2010).

Suspensions and collapses

Devolution has repeatedly been suspended or collapsed: the institutions were suspended from 2002 to 2007, and the Executive fell in 2017 over the Renewable Heat Incentive scandal, returning in January 2020 under the New Decade, New Approach agreement. It collapsed again in 2022 over the Northern Ireland Protocol and was restored in February 2024, when Michelle O'Neill of Sinn Féin became the first nationalist First Minister, serving alongside Emma Little-Pengelly as deputy First Minister.
