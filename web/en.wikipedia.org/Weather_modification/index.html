Weather modification

Main techniqueCloud seeding
Seeding agentsSilver iodide, dry ice, salts
PurposesRain enhancement, hail suppression, fog dispersal
Key treatyENMOD Convention (1977)

Weather modification is the act of intentionally manipulating or altering the weather. The most common form is cloud seeding, which increases rain or snow, usually for the purpose of increasing the local water supply. Weather modification can also have the goal of preventing damaging weather, such as hail or hurricanes, from occurring, or of dispersing fog at airports.

History

Modern weather modification began in 1946, when Vincent Schaefer and Irving Langmuir at General Electric discovered that dry ice could induce ice-crystal formation in supercooled clouds; Bernard Vonnegut soon found silver iodide effective as a seeding agent. Military and civilian experiments followed, including the controversial Project Stormfury attempts to weaken hurricanes (1962–1983) and Operation Popeye, a U.S. military cloud-seeding programme during the Vietnam War intended to extend the monsoon over the Ho Chi Minh trail. Revelations about Popeye contributed to the 1977 ENMOD Convention, which prohibits hostile environmental modification.

Practice today

Dozens of countries operate seeding programmes. China maintains the world's largest, using rockets and aircraft to induce rain and to clear skies before major events such as the 2008 Beijing Olympics. In the United States, ski resorts, water districts and insurers fund programmes to increase snowpack and suppress hail. Scientific assessments, including those of the U.S. National Research Council and the World Meteorological Organization, find evidence that seeding can modestly enhance precipitation under suitable conditions, though quantifying the effect remains difficult.

Criticism and law

Critics question efficacy, raise concerns about environmental effects of silver iodide, and dispute the ethics of diverting rain. Legally, weather modification is regulated in many jurisdictions, and liability for unintended consequences remains unsettled.
