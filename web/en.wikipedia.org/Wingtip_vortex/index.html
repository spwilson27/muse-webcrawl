Wingtip vortices

Wingtip vortices are circular patterns of rotating air left behind a wing as it generates lift. When a wing produces lift, air from below the wing is drawn around the wingtip to the region above the wing by the lower surface's higher pressure, resulting in a vortex of air coming off each wingtip. Wingtip vortices are sometimes named wake vortices or lift-induced vortices because they also occur at points other than the wingtips.

Formation

The vortex is strongest when the aircraft is heavy, slow, and in a "clean" configuration (flaps and gear retracted), because the angle of attack is high. The rotating air masses trail behind the aircraft as two counter-rotating line vortices, which can persist for several minutes and sink below the flight path.

Hazards

Wake turbulence from a large aircraft can be hazardous to following aircraft, especially smaller ones; separation minima are therefore prescribed between aircraft at airports. Winglets and other devices reduce the strength of wingtip vortices and the induced drag they cause.
