Happy ending problem

The happy ending problem is a problem in discrete geometry: for any sufficiently large set of points in the plane in general position (no three collinear), there exists a subset forming the vertices of a convex polygon of a given size.

History

The problem was posed by Esther Klein in the 1930s and named by Paul Erdős because Klein's work on it led to her marriage to George Szekeres. Erdős and Szekeres proved that a number N(n) exists for every n, and gave upper and lower bounds.

Known values

The smallest numbers are known exactly only for small cases: five points guarantee a convex quadrilateral, and the exact values for pentagons and hexagons required substantial work, with nine points guaranteeing a convex pentagon and 17 points a convex hexagon. The general exact formula remains conjectural. See also Ramsey theory.
