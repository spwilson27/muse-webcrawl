Vylkove (Ukrainian: Вилкове) is a small city in Izmail Raion, Odesa Oblast, in south-western Ukraine, near the border with Romania. It lies in the Danube Delta where the river splits into branches before reaching the Black Sea, and is sometimes called the "Ukrainian Venice" because canals — known locally as yeryky — serve as streets, with boats the traditional means of transport.

History

Vylkove was founded in 1746, largely by Old Believers (Lipovans) fleeing religious persecution in the Russian Empire, along with Cossacks and other settlers. Fishing, especially for Danube herring and sturgeon, long formed the basis of the local economy. The town passed between the Ottoman Empire, the Russian Empire, Romania and the Soviet Union before becoming part of independent Ukraine in 1991.

Geography and nature

The town sits at the edge of the Danube Biosphere Reserve, a protected wetland of international importance for migratory birds, including pelicans. Residents traditionally build houses on islands of silt and reeds, and gardens on reclaimed plots fertilised with river mud.

Economy

Fishing, fish processing, agriculture (including strawberries and grapes grown on island plots) and growing eco-tourism centred on the delta are the main activities. The Ukrainian Danube port facilities nearby connect the town to river trade.
