Chivalric romance

A chivalric romance is a prose or verse narrative popular in medieval and early modern Europe, telling of knightly adventure, courtly love, quests and marvels.

Traditions

Romances drew on the Matter of France (Charlemagne and his paladins), the Matter of Britain (King Arthur and the Knights of the Round Table) and the Matter of Rome (classical antiquity). Chrétien de Troyes pioneered Arthurian romance in the 12th century; later landmarks include Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, Le Morte d'Arthur and Amadis of Gaul.

Legacy

Miguel de Cervantes parodied the genre in Don Quixote (1605), transforming it into the modern novel, while romance motifs persist in fantasy literature.
