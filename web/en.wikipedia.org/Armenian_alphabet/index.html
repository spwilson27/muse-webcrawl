Armenian alphabet

Armenian alphabet
Type: Alphabet
Created: c. 405 AD
Creator: Mesrop Mashtots
Letters: 39 (originally 36)

The Armenian alphabet is an alphabetic writing system used to write the Armenian language. It was created around 405 AD by Mesrop Mashtots, an Armenian linguist and ecclesiastical leader, largely to make the Bible accessible in Armenian.

History

Before its creation, Armenian had been written with Greek, Syriac, or other scripts unsuited to its sounds. Mashtots, supported by Catholicos Sahak Partev and King Vramshapuh, designed an alphabet of 36 letters. Three more letters (օ, ֆ, and the ligature և later counted separately) were added in the Middle Ages, bringing the total to 39.

Characteristics

Armenian is written left to right. It has distinct uppercase and lowercase forms, and its letter shapes are unrelated to those of Greek or Syriac. The alphabet has remained essentially unchanged for over sixteen centuries, a notable point of national and cultural pride. The first sentence written in it is traditionally said to be the opening of the Book of Proverbs: "To know wisdom and instruction; to perceive the words of understanding."

Usage

Two literary standards use the alphabet: Eastern Armenian, spoken in Armenia and Iran, and Western Armenian, historically spoken by the diaspora in the Middle East and West.
