Executive order

An executive order is a directive by the president of the United States that manages operations of the federal government. The legal or constitutional basis for executive orders comes from Article Two of the United States Constitution, which vests "the executive Power" in the president and requires the president to "take Care that the Laws be faithfully executed," as well as from acts of Congress that delegate authority to the president.

History

Presidents have issued executive orders since 1789, initially unnumbered; the numbering system began retrospectively in 1907, reaching back to an order of Abraham Lincoln from 1862. Executive orders have been used for major policy actions: Abraham Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation was an executive order, as was Harry Truman's Executive Order 9981 desegregating the armed forces and Franklin D. Roosevelt's Executive Order 9066 authorizing the internment of Japanese Americans. Theodore Roosevelt significantly expanded the use of the instrument, and Franklin D. Roosevelt issued the most executive orders of any president, over 3,700.

Legal status and review

Executive orders have the force and effect of law once published in the Federal Register, but they are subject to judicial review and can be overturned by Congress through legislation or rendered moot by appropriations riders. A subsequent president may amend or revoke a predecessor's orders freely. The Supreme Court's decision in Youngstown Sheet & Tube Co. v. Sawyer (1952), which struck down Truman's seizure of the steel mills, established the influential framework — associated with Justice Robert Jackson's concurrence — for evaluating the scope of presidential power.

Use and criticism

Presidents of both parties have been criticized for using executive orders to circumvent Congress, particularly on immigration, environmental regulation, and labor policy. Supporters respond that orders are an indispensable tool for administering the executive branch, conducting foreign policy, and acting where Congress has delegated authority. Related instruments include presidential proclamations, memoranda, and national security directives.
