Battle of Arfderydd
Datec. 573
LocationArthuret, near Longtown, Cumbria (traditionally)
ResultVictory of Rhydderch Hael and allies
BelligerentsRhydderch Hael, Maelgwn's sons vs. Gwenddoleu ap Ceidio

The Battle of Arfderydd (also Arderydd) was fought around 573 AD in the Brittonic "Old North" of post-Roman Britain, traditionally located near Arthuret by modern Longtown in Cumbria. It is recorded in the Welsh annals and is famous in Welsh tradition because the bard Myrddin — a figure later absorbed into the legend of Merlin — was said to have gone mad with grief after the battle.

Sources and narrative

The Annales Cambriae note the battle for the year 573 and record the death of Gwenddoleu son of Ceidio, the defeated lord. Welsh triads name the opposing coalition as including Rhydderch Hael of Strathclyde and the sons of Eliffer, Peredur and Gwrgi. According to later tradition, the battle was fought over a trivial cause — the triads class it among the "futile battles" of Britain, said to have been caused by a lark's nest.

Myrddin and the Merlin legend

Myrddin Wyllt ("Myrddin the Wild"), a warrior or bard in Gwenddoleu's service, was said to have fled into the Caledonian forest after the slaughter and lived as a wild man, acquiring prophetic powers. Geoffrey of Monmouth drew on this tradition for his Vita Merlini, merging Myrddin with the figure of Merlin Ambrosius.
