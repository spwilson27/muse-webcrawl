Salt March

Date12 March – 6 April 1930
LocationSabarmati Ashram to Dandi, Gujarat, British India
Distance390 km (240 mi)
LeaderMahatma Gandhi
OutcomeMass civil disobedience across India; Gandhi–Irwin Pact (1931)

The Salt March, also known as the Salt Satyagraha or Dandi March, was an act of nonviolent civil disobedience in colonial India led by Mahatma Gandhi. The 24-day march of 390 kilometres (240 mi) ran from 12 March to 6 April 1930 as a direct-action campaign of tax resistance and nonviolent protest against the British salt monopoly.

Background

British law made it illegal for Indians to collect or produce salt independently, forcing them to buy it from the colonial government at prices inflated by a salt tax that fell hardest on the poor. Gandhi chose salt precisely because it was a necessity used by every Indian, Hindu and Muslim, rich and poor — a symbol of the Raj's injustice that no one could dismiss. After the Indian National Congress declared Purna Swaraj (complete independence) in January 1930, Gandhi announced the march in a letter of ultimatum to Viceroy Lord Irwin.

The march

Gandhi set out from his Sabarmati Ashram in Ahmedabad with 78 trusted volunteers, walking about 16 km a day and addressing crowds in village after village. By the time the procession reached the coastal village of Dandi on 5 April, thousands had joined. On the morning of 6 April 1930, Gandhi picked up a lump of natural salt from the shore, breaking the law before the world's press. Millions of Indians followed his example, making and buying illegal salt, boycotting foreign cloth, and courting arrest; more than 60,000 were imprisoned, including Gandhi himself in May.

Impact

The march drew unprecedented international attention to the Indian independence movement and is widely credited with transforming it into a mass movement. It led to the Gandhi–Irwin Pact of 1931 and Gandhi's participation in the second Round Table Conference in London. The Salt March became a template for nonviolent protest worldwide, influencing Martin Luther King Jr. and the American civil rights movement. See also Mahatma Gandhi and Non-cooperation movement.
