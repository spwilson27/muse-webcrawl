Frequency comb

A frequency comb is a spectrum consisting of a series of discrete, equally spaced elements forming a "comb" of frequencies, typically generated by a mode-locked laser producing a train of ultrashort pulses. The frequencies of the comb lines are given by fn = n·frep + fceo, where frep is the pulse repetition rate and fceo is the carrier–envelope offset frequency.

Applications

Frequency combs link microwave frequencies, which can be counted electronically, to optical frequencies, enabling optical clocks of extraordinary precision, high-resolution spectroscopy, calibration of astronomical spectrographs for exoplanet searches, and precision distance measurement. The technique was developed principally by John L. Hall and Theodor W. Hänsch, who shared part of the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physics for their contributions to laser-based precision spectroscopy, including the frequency comb technique.
