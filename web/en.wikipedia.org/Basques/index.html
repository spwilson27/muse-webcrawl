The Basques (Basque: Euskaldunak; Spanish: Vascos; French: Basques) are an ethnic group indigenous to the Basque Country, a region located around the western end of the Pyrenees in north-central Spain and southwestern France. They are known for speaking Basque (Euskara), a language isolate unrelated to any other living language, and for a distinctive culture with roots that may predate the arrival of Indo-European languages in Western Europe.

History

The ancestors of the Basques are often linked to the Vascones, a pre-Roman tribe mentioned by classical authors such as Strabo and Pliny the Elder. Unlike much of Iberia, the Basque region was only lightly Romanized, and Basque language and customs survived the Visigothic and Frankish periods. During the Middle Ages the Kingdom of Navarre, with its capital at Pamplona, became the principal Basque political power. The southern Basque territories were gradually incorporated into the Crown of Castile, whose rulers confirmed the local charters known as fueros, which granted substantial self-government.

In the 19th century the fueros were largely abolished following the Carlist Wars, and Basque nationalism emerged as a political movement, symbolized by Sabino Arana, founder of the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV). During the Spanish Civil War, the bombing of Guernica in 1937 by the German Condor Legion became an enduring symbol of the conflict. Under Francisco Franco, public use of the Basque language was suppressed. The armed group ETA, founded in 1959, carried out a campaign of violence for Basque independence until it announced a definitive cessation of armed activity in 2011 and dissolved in 2018.

Language

Basque is an ergative, agglutinative language with no known relatives. It is spoken in several historical dialects, alongside the standardized form, Euskara Batua, created in 1968. Co-official with Spanish in the Basque Autonomous Community and parts of Navarre, Basque has experienced a revival through the ikastola school movement and public broadcasting, though the number of speakers in the French Basque Country continues to decline.

Culture

Basque culture is noted for its rural sports (herri kirolak), including stone lifting and wood chopping; the ball game pelota; bertsolaritza, improvised sung verse; and a celebrated gastronomy of pintxos, cider houses, and restaurants such as Arzak and Mugaritz. Genetic studies indicate the Basques descend largely from early Neolithic farmers with comparatively little later admixture, preserving a distinctive genetic profile in Western Europe.
