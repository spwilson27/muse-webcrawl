HM Prison Dartmoor is a Category C men's prison, located in Princetown in the Dartmoor area of Devon, England. Its high granite walls dominate this moorland village, the highest in southern England. The prison is owned by the Duchy of Cornwall and operated by His Majesty's Prison Service. Once notorious as one of Britain's most severe prisons, its remote moorland setting and grim history have made "Dartmoor" a byword for harsh incarceration.

History

Construction began in 1806, to a design by Daniel Asher Alexander, to hold French prisoners of the Napoleonic Wars; the first prisoners arrived in 1809. During the War of 1812 it also held American prisoners of war, hundreds of whom died of disease and are buried in the prison grounds. From 1850 the site became a convict prison for British criminals, who worked the surrounding moorland farms, quarries, and peat works.

The prison gained a fearsome reputation in the 20th century. In January 1932 a major mutiny saw prisoners set fire to buildings and take over the prison for several hours before order was restored. In later decades Dartmoor's role changed: it became a lower-security training prison, mainly for non-violent and older offenders, and housed many prisoners convicted of sexual offences for much of the late 20th and early 21st centuries.

Recent years

The prison's future has been debated repeatedly, owing to its age, cost, and listed buildings. In 2024 prisoners were temporarily moved out after elevated levels of radon gas were detected, and remedial work was undertaken. The prison museum, housed in the old dairy across the road, tells the story of the site from the Napoleonic era to the present day.
