THX

THX Ltd. is an American company founded in 1983 by George Lucas and headquartered in San Francisco, California. It develops the THX high-fidelity quality standard for movie theaters, screening rooms, home theaters, speakers, car audio and video games. The name comes from Lucas's first film, THX 1138 (1971), combined with the "X" for "crossover" or "experiment".

History

THX was created so that the soundtrack of Star Wars: Episode VI – Return of the Jedi (1983) would be reproduced faithfully in cinemas. Engineer Tomlinson Holman developed a set of standards for theater acoustics, speaker placement and playback equipment, along with a crossover system; a theater certified to the standard could guarantee the audience heard the mix as intended. The famous THX trailer note — the "Deep Note", a synthesized crescendo — was composed by James A. Moorer.

Certification

THX certifies cinemas, consumer electronics, and media. Products carrying the logo are tested against specifications for frequency response, output and distortion. THX was a division of Lucasfilm until 2002, later owned by Creative Technology, then Razer (2016), and from 2023 by the company itself under new ownership arrangements.
