Mugham

Mugham (Azerbaijani: Muğam) is one of the many folk musical compositions from Azerbaijan, resembling the Middle Eastern maqam tradition. It is a highly complex art form that weds classical poetry — typically ghazals by poets such as Nizami, Fuzuli and Khurshidbanu Natavan — with musical improvisation in specific local modal systems.

Performance

A mugham performance is typically a suite unfolding through contrasting sections in a single mode, sung by a khananda accompanied by a trio of tar (long-necked lute), kamancha (spike fiddle) and daf or naghara percussion. The principal modes include Rast, Shur, Segah, Chahargah, Bayaty-Shiraz, Shushtar and Humayun. In 2008, Azerbaijani mugham was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The composer Uzeyir Hajibeyov famously fused mugham with Western classical forms in works such as the 1908 opera Leyli and Majnun.
