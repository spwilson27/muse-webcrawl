The Ryman Auditorium is a historic live-performance venue in Nashville, Tennessee. Built as the Union Gospel Tabernacle by riverboat captain and businessman Thomas Ryman, it opened in 1892 for religious revivals led by evangelist Sam Jones, and was renamed in Ryman's honour after his death in 1904.

Grand Ole Opry years

From 1943 to 1974 the Ryman was the home of the Grand Ole Opry radio show, hosting legends including Hank Williams, Patsy Cline, Johnny Cash and Elvis Presley, and earning its nickname the "Mother Church of Country Music". After the Opry moved to a new house in 1974, the building was threatened with demolition but was saved, restored, and reopened in 1994 as a celebrated concert venue noted for its church-pew seating and exceptional acoustics. It is a National Historic Landmark.
