Champlain Canal

The Champlain Canal is a 60-mile (97 km) canal in New York State that connects the Hudson River to Lake Champlain, running from Waterford through Fort Edward to Whitehall. It is part of the New York State Canal System.

History

Authorised alongside the Erie Canal, the original Champlain Canal opened in 1823, enabling trade between New York City, Albany, and Montreal via the Richelieu River. It was enlarged in the nineteenth century and replaced by the modern barge canal channel in the early twentieth century, partly following the Hudson River itself.

Today

The canal now principally serves recreational boating and forms part of the Lakes to Locks Passage and the route between the Hudson and Canada used by long-distance cruisers. Eleven locks lift vessels between the Hudson and Lake Champlain.
