Helenio Herrera Gavilán (10 April 1910 – 9 November 1997) was an Argentine-French football manager. Nicknamed Il Mago ("The Magician"), he is best remembered for his success with Inter Milan in the 1960s, where his defensive catenaccio system made the "Grande Inter" side one of the most dominant in European football history.

Career

Born in Buenos Aires to Spanish parents, Herrera's family emigrated to Casablanca, Morocco, where he began his playing career as a defender before moving to French clubs and taking French citizenship. After early managerial success in France and Spain — winning La Liga titles with Atlético Madrid (1950, 1951) and Barcelona (1959, 1960) — he moved to Inter in 1960.

At Inter, Herrera built a team around goalkeeper Giuliano Sarti, sweeper Armando Picchi, playmaker Luis Suárez and counter-attacking forwards such as Sandro Mazzola and Giacinto Facchetti, the prototype of the attacking full-back. Inter won three Serie A titles, two European Cups (1964, 1965) and two Intercontinental Cups. Herrera was a pioneer of modern management methods: strict control of players' diet and lifestyle, psychological motivation — famous motivational phrases decorated the dressing room — and meticulous tactical preparation. He also managed the Italian and Spanish national teams and later coached Roma, winning the Coppa Italia in 1969.

Legacy

Herrera remains one of the most influential and controversial coaches in football history: a forerunner of the modern celebrity manager, he is widely ranked among the greatest managers of all time, though his association with catenaccio made his name synonymous with defensive football.
