Flag officer

A flag officer is a commissioned officer in a nation's armed forces senior enough to be entitled to fly a flag to mark the position from which the officer exercises command. Historically the term applied to naval officers: an admiral commanding a fleet flew his distinctive flag (ensign) from the mainmast of his flagship, and the ship itself was thereafter called a "flagship". Today the term is used in many services — navies, and by extension armies, air forces, marine corps, and coast guards — to denote the senior officer corps, roughly equivalent to "general officer" in land and air forces.

Naval usage

In the Royal Navy and the United States Navy, flag officers are officers of the rank of rear admiral (or commodore, in some usages) and above: rear admiral, vice admiral, admiral, and admiral of the fleet / fleet admiral. Their flags historically indicated their squadron and seniority; in the age of sail, British admirals were graded by the colour of their squadron — red, white, and blue — a system from which the ranks of "Admiral of the Red" and so on derived. In the U.S. Navy, the number of stars on the flag corresponds to the officer's grade, and flag officers are accordingly often called "stars" collectively, their staffs forming the machinery of operational-level command.

Extended usage

By analogy, "flag officer" is used in the United States for general officers of the Army, Air Force, Marine Corps, and Space Force, and in legislation governing their numbers, pay, and retirement. Many non-military organisations have borrowed the term: some police forces, fire services, merchant navies, and even sailing organisations designate their most senior office-holders as flag officers — in yacht clubs, the commodore, vice commodore, and rear commodore are collectively the flag officers of the club.
