Caledonian Maritime Assets Ltd (CMAL) is a public corporation owned by the Scottish Government. It owns the ferries, ports and harbours used by the state-supported ferry services serving the Clyde, the Hebrides and the Northern Isles of Scotland, and leases them to the operating companies.

History

CMAL was established in 2006 when the former Caledonian MacBrayne organisation was split into an asset-owning company and an operating company, CalMac Ferries Ltd, in order to comply with European Union rules on the tendering of subsidised ferry services. Ownership of the vessels and shore infrastructure passed to CMAL, while CalMac bid to operate the routes.

Role and fleet

The company owns a fleet of around thirty ferries, ranging from small double-ended vessels on short crossings to large ships serving the Outer Hebrides and the Northern Isles, together with dozens of harbours and slipways. CMAL is responsible for procuring new vessels and for major harbour works. Its newbuilding programme has included hybrid diesel-electric ferries and the controversial dual-fuel (LNG) ferries MV Glen Sannox and MV Glen Rosa, built at Ferguson Marine, whose long delays and cost overruns drew sustained political and media scrutiny in Scotland.

Criticism

The split between owning and operating companies, and CMAL's procurement record, have been criticised by island communities and parliamentary committees, particularly over the ageing fleet and the delayed dual-fuel vessels.
