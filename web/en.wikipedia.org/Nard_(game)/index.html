Nard (game)

Nard (also spelled narde or nardi) is a tables game played across Persia (Iran), Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, and parts of the Middle East and Eastern Europe. It is widely regarded as a close relative or direct ancestor of backgammon, and the two games share the same board of 24 points (triangles) and the use of two dice.

Gameplay

Each player has fifteen pieces, called checkers or men, arranged on the board. Players roll two dice and move their pieces according to the numbers shown, racing around the board and eventually bearing them off. Unlike Western backgammon, traditional Persian nard does not use a doubling cube, and the initial arrangement of pieces and some movement rules differ: in the Persian variant, all fifteen pieces begin stacked on the head point of the board. A notable feature is that a single piece alone on a point may not be hit and sent back in some regional rule sets, which changes tactics considerably.

History

Race games on marked boards are ancient in Mesopotamia and Persia; the board game in the epic tradition is associated with the Sasanian Empire and the vizier Bozorgmehr, who is credited in the Persian text Chatrang-namak tradition with devising nard in response to the arrival of chess from India. The game spread through the Islamic world and into Europe, where its descendants include backgammon and other tables games.
