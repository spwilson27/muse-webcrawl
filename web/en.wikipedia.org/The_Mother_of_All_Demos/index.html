The Mother of All Demos

"The Mother of All Demos" is the name retrospectively given to a landmark computer demonstration by Douglas Engelbart and his team at the Stanford Research Institute (SRI), presented on 9 December 1968 at the Fall Joint Computer Conference in San Francisco. In a single live session, Engelbart publicly introduced the computer mouse, hypertext, video conferencing, teleconferencing, word processing, and collaborative real-time editing.

The presentation

Engelbart, seated before a console with a keyboard, mouse, and a chorded keyset, demonstrated the oN-Line System (NLS) developed at SRI's Augmentation Research Center. The audience of about 1,000 computer professionals watched him edit hypertext documents, navigate linked information, and hold a video conference with colleagues back at Menlo Park, including Bill English, who directed the technical production. The 90-minute demonstration outlined nearly every fundamental element of modern personal computing decades before it became commonplace.

Legacy

The retrospective name "Mother of All Demos" was coined by journalist Steven Levy in 1994 and popularised by Andries van Dam. The demonstration influenced researchers at Xerox PARC and, through them, the development of the graphical user interface at Apple and Microsoft. It is regarded as one of the most important events in the history of computing.
