Dignāga

Dignāga (c. 480 – c. 540 CE) was an Indian Buddhist scholar and one of the founders of Indian logic (hetu vidyā). He is known for his influential work on logic and epistemology, laying the foundation for the later school developed by Dharmakīrti. Dignāga's works had a profound influence on Buddhist philosophy in India, Tibet, China, and East Asia.

Dignāga, founder of the Buddhist school of logic.

Philosophy

Dignāga held that there are only two means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa): perception (pratyakṣa) and inference (anumāna). He defined perception as free of conceptual construction, and analyzed inference through the "three modes" (trairūpya) of a valid reason. His theory of apoha (exclusion) explained how words refer: a word signifies its object by excluding everything else.

Works

His most famous work is the Pramāṇasamuccaya ("Compendium of the Means of Valid Knowledge"), which survives mainly in Tibetan translation. Other works include the Hetucakra ("Wheel of Reasons"), which classifies valid and invalid inferential reasons in a ninefold scheme, and the Ālambanaparīkṣā on the object of cognition.
