Sitka deer

Scientific nameOdocoileus hemionus sitkensis
FamilyCervidae
RangeCoastal British Columbia and the Alexander Archipelago, Alaska
StatusLeast Concern

The Sitka deer or Sitka black-tailed deer (Odocoileus hemionus sitkensis) is a subspecies of mule deer native to the temperate rainforests of the Pacific coast of western North America, named for Sitka, Alaska.

Description

Sitka deer are smaller than other mule deer subspecies, an example of insular dwarfism in island populations. Adults typically weigh 36 to 54 kilograms. Their coat is reddish-brown in summer and grey-brown in winter; like other black-tailed deer, the tail is black on top.

Ecology

They inhabit coastal old-growth forest and muskeg, feeding on forbs, shrubs, and conifer browse. Deep-snow winters can cause heavy mortality when deer congregate on beaches and low-elevation old growth. Sitka deer were introduced to Haida Gwaii, where they have substantially altered forest understories, and to Kodiak Island. They are an important game animal in Alaska and a prey species for wolves and bears.
