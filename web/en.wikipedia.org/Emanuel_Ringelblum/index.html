Emanuel Ringelblum

Emanuel Ringelblum (November 21, 1900 – March 7, 1944) was a Polish-Jewish historian, politician and social worker, known for his Notes from the Warsaw Ghetto and for founding the secret Oyneg Shabes archive, which documented life and death in the Warsaw Ghetto during the Holocaust.

Trained as a historian at the University of Warsaw, Ringelblum worked for the American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee organising relief for Jewish refugees. After the German invasion of Poland in 1939, he remained in Warsaw and established a clandestine network of writers, rabbis, teachers and ordinary people who collected diaries, documents, newspapers, posters and artefacts of Jewish life under occupation.

The archive was buried in milk cans and metal boxes in 1942–43. Ringelblum escaped the ghetto but returned to rescue others; he and his family were discovered in hiding in March 1944 and executed. Two of the three buried caches were recovered after the war, comprising some 35,000 pages, and are now held by the Jewish Historical Institute in Warsaw.
