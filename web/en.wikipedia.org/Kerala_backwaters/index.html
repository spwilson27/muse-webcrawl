Kerala backwaters

The Kerala backwaters are a network of brackish lagoons and canals lying parallel to the Arabian Sea coast of Kerala state in southern India, as well as inland rivers. The network includes interconnected canals, rivers, lakes and inlets fed by 38 rivers and extending about 900 kilometres. The freshwater of the rivers meets the seawater of the Arabian Sea in a landscape of unique ecology, supporting rice paddies cultivated below sea level in the Kuttanad region, known as the "Rice Bowl of Kerala".

Geography

The backwaters were formed by the action of waves and shore currents creating low barrier islands across the mouths of rivers flowing from the Western Ghats. The largest body is Vembanad Lake, about 200 square kilometres, bordered by Alappuzha, Kottayam and Ernakulam districts. The eight-kilometre-wide Ashtamudi Lake at Kollam is the gateway to the system. The Thanneermukkom bund (spillway) and the Thottappally spillway regulate saltwater intrusion for the paddy fields of Kuttanad.

Ecology

The backwaters are home to distinctive aquatic life, including crabs, frogs, mudskippers, otters, turtles and birds such as terns, kingfishers, darters and cormorants. Palm trees, pandanus shrubs and other vegetation line the banks. Environmental pressures — pollution, land reclamation and the spread of water hyacinth — have prompted conservation efforts, and Vembanad is designated a Ramsar wetland of international importance.

Tourism and transport

The backwaters are one of India's most famous tourist attractions. Traditional cargo kettuvallams have been converted into houseboats with bedrooms and kitchens, cruising routes centred on Alappuzha (Alleppey), Kumarakom and Kollam. The Nehru Trophy Boat Race, held on Punnamada Lake each August, draws enormous crowds to watch snake boats (chundan vallam) crewed by over a hundred oarsmen. The Kerala State Water Transport Department runs passenger ferries linking the villages of the region.
