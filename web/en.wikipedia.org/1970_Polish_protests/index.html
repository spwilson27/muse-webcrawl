The 1970 Polish protests (Polish: Grudzień 1970, "December 1970") were strikes and demonstrations by workers in northern Poland, principally in the coastal cities of Gdańsk, Gdynia, Elbląg and Szczecin, against sudden increases in the prices of food and other everyday items announced shortly before Christmas.

Events

On 14 December 1970 workers at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk went on strike. Protests spread across the Baltic coast; in Gdynia, on 17 December ("Black Thursday"), soldiers fired on workers arriving for their shifts, killing at least 18. Across the region at least 44 people were killed and more than 1,000 wounded, while around 3,000 were arrested.

Aftermath

The crackdown, authorised by party leader Władysław Gomułka, led to his removal: on 20 December Edward Gierek became First Secretary of the Polish United Workers' Party, and the price increases were frozen and then withdrawn after further strikes in early 1971. The events of December 1970 were a formative precursor to the Solidarity movement of 1980; the victims are commemorated by the Monument to the Fallen Shipyard Workers in Gdańsk, unveiled in December 1980.
