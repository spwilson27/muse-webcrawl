Voussoir


Material: Stone or brick
Used in: Arches, vaults
Centre stone: Keystone

A voussoir is a wedge-shaped element, typically of stone or brick, used in building an arch or vault. Each voussoir is one of the individual blocks that, fitted together in a curve, convert the vertical load above an opening into compressive forces carried down the sides of the arch.

Terminology

The central voussoir at the crown of the arch is the keystone, the last piece set in place during construction, which locks all the others into position. The lowest voussoirs on each side, resting on the supporting imposts, are called springers. The visible face of a voussoir is its head; the inner curved surface of the arch is the intrados and the outer curve the extrados. Decorative treatment of voussoirs — alternating stones of different colours, or projecting and recessed ("rusticated") blocks — is a hallmark of classical, Romanesque and Renaissance masonry, and elaborately moulded arch stones are prominent in Islamic architecture as well.

Construction

Voussoirs are laid over temporary wooden centring that supports them until the keystone is placed and the mortar (if any) has set, after which the arch is self-supporting and the centring is struck. In dry-stone and Roman construction the precision of the wedge cuts alone could carry the load.
