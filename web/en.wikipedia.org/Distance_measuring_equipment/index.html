Distance measuring equipment

Distance measuring equipment (DME) is a radio navigation technology that measures the slant range (distance) between an aircraft and a ground station by timing the propagation delay of radio signals in the frequency band between 960 and 1215 megahertz (MHz). Line-of-sight between the aircraft and ground station is required. An interrogator (airborne) initiates an exchange by transmitting a pulse pair on an assigned "channel" to the transponder ground station. The channel assignment specifies carrier frequency and spacing of the pulses. After a known delay, the transponder replies by transmitting a pulse pair on a frequency that is offset from the original by 63 MHz. This frequency offset differs depending on the channel.

Operation

An aircraft uses DME to determine its distance from a ground station by sending and receiving pulse pairs — two pulses of fixed duration and separation. The ground stations are typically co-located with VOR or ILS transmitters. A low-power DME can also be co-located with an ILS localizer, in which case the DME distance measures the distance to the runway touchdown point.

The aircraft interrogator measures the elapsed time between transmitting its interrogation and receiving the reply, subtracts the fixed 50-microsecond delay introduced by the ground transponder, and halves the result to compute the one-way slant range. DME accuracy is typically within 0.2 nautical miles or 3% of distance, whichever is greater.

Frequencies and channels

DME operates on 252 channels between 960 and 1215 MHz. Channels are designated X and Y, with pulse spacings of 12 and 30 microseconds respectively for interrogations. DME is usually paired with a VHF navigation frequency so that tuning the VOR automatically selects the corresponding DME channel.

Uses

DME is used for en-route and terminal navigation, for defining airways intersections and fixes, and as part of instrument approach procedures. When combined with a VOR bearing, DME provides a complete rho-theta position fix. DME/DME area navigation is also used as a backup positioning source when GNSS is unavailable.
