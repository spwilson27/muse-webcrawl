Ireland (Irish: Éire) is an island in the North Atlantic Ocean, in north-western Europe. It is separated from Great Britain to its east by the North Channel, the Irish Sea, and St George's Channel. Ireland is the second-largest island of the British Isles, the third-largest in Europe, and the twentieth-largest in the world. Geopolitically, the island is divided between the Republic of Ireland (officially named Ireland), an independent state covering five-sixths of the island, and Northern Ireland, which is part of the United Kingdom. As of 2023 the population of the entire island was over 7 million, with over 5 million living in the Republic and roughly 1.9 million in Northern Ireland, ranking it the second-most populous island in Europe after Great Britain.

History

The first evidence of human presence in Ireland is dated to around 33,000 years ago, during the last ice age; settled farming arrived in the Neolithic, which saw the construction of monumental passage tombs such as Newgrange, older than the Egyptian pyramids. Celtic languages and culture reached the island in the late Bronze Age and Iron Age. Christianity arrived in the 5th century, traditionally with Saint Patrick, and Irish monasteries became centres of learning that preserved Latin texts through the early Middle Ages.

From the late 12th century, Norman and then English rule progressively extended over the island, punctuated by rebellion and plantation. The Acts of Union of 1801 incorporated Ireland into the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. The Great Famine of 1845–1852, in which about a million people died and more than a million emigrated, reshaped Irish society and fuelled nationalism. After the Easter Rising of 1916 and the Irish War of Independence, the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 partitioned the island: the Irish Free State (later the Republic of Ireland) in the south, and Northern Ireland remaining in the United Kingdom. From the late 1960s to the Good Friday Agreement of 1998, Northern Ireland experienced the sectarian conflict known as the Troubles.

Geography

The island is ringed by low mountains surrounding a central plain of limestone, bogland, and farmland. The River Shannon, at about 360 km, is the longest river in Ireland or Britain. The climate is temperate oceanic, moderated by the North Atlantic Current: winters are mild, summers cool, and rainfall frequent — the origin of Ireland's poetic name, the Emerald Isle. The highest peak is Carrauntoohil in County Kerry.

Politics

The Republic of Ireland is a parliamentary republic whose head of state is the President and whose head of government is the Taoiseach; its parliament is the Oireachtas and its capital and largest city is Dublin. Northern Ireland has a devolved assembly at Stormont within the United Kingdom. Both jurisdictions cooperate through institutions established under the Good Friday Agreement, and both are committed by that agreement to the principle that constitutional change requires the consent of majorities in both parts of the island.

Economy

The Republic's economy, once heavily agricultural, transformed from the late 20th century into a hub for pharmaceuticals, medical devices, software, and financial services, with many multinational firms choosing Ireland for its English-speaking workforce and (historically) low corporate tax. Northern Ireland's economy centres on services, public administration, agri-food, and a growing technology and creative sector. Tourism, drawing on Ireland's landscapes, music, and literary heritage, is significant across the island.

Culture

Ireland has made an outsized contribution to literature in English, producing writers such as W. B. Yeats, James Joyce, Samuel Beckett, and Seamus Heaney — four Nobel laureates in literature. The Irish language (Gaeilge), a Goidelic Celtic language, is the first official language of the Republic and is spoken daily by a minority, concentrated in Gaeltacht regions. Traditional music, dance, and sports governed by the Gaelic Athletic Association — hurling and Gaelic football — remain central to cultural life, and Saint Patrick's Day is celebrated worldwide.
