Country: Japan

Area: 4,017 km²
Population: approx. 1.4 million

Shiga Prefecture (滋賀県, Shiga-ken) is a landlocked prefecture of Japan located in the Kansai region of Honshu. Its capital and largest city is Ōtsu. Shiga is dominated geographically by Lake Biwa, the largest freshwater lake in Japan, which covers roughly one-sixth of the prefecture's area and supplies drinking water to some 14 million people in the Kansai region.

History

Before the Meiji Restoration, the area corresponded to Ōmi Province, a strategic region controlling routes between eastern and western Japan. It was famous for the Ōmi merchants, traders whose networks spread across the country. Battles at Anegawa and the presence of castles at Azuchi — where Oda Nobunaga built Azuchi Castle — made the province central to the reunification of Japan; the decisive Battle of Sekigahara (1600) was fought just beyond its border. Shiga Prefecture in its modern form was established in 1872.

Geography

Surrounded by mountains, Shiga borders Kyoto, Fukui, Gifu, and Mie prefectures. The lake plain around Biwa is fertile and heavily cultivated with rice, while the mountainous east includes Mount Ibuki, the prefecture's highest peak. The Seta River, Biwa's only natural outlet, flows south to become the Uji and Yodo rivers.

Cities and culture

Major cities include Ōtsu, Kusatsu, Higashiōmi, Hikone — home to Hikone Castle, one of Japan's twelve remaining original castles — and Nagahama, known for the Kurokabe glass district. Enryaku-ji temple on Mount Hiei, a UNESCO World Heritage site, was the head temple of the Tendai school of Buddhism. The prefecture is also associated with the Kōka ninja tradition.

Economy

Shiga has a strong manufacturing sector, ranking among the top prefectures in Japan for factory output per capita, alongside agriculture centred on rice and Ōmi beef. Its proximity to Kyoto and Osaka makes its southern cities popular commuter communities.
