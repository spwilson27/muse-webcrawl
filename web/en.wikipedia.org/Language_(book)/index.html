Language (book)

Language: An Introduction to the Study of Speech is a 1921 book by the American linguist Edward Sapir. A classic introduction to linguistics for general readers, it surveys phonetics, grammar, language change, and the relationship between language, race and culture.

Contents

Sapir discusses sounds and sound patterns, words and concepts, grammatical processes and types of linguistic structure, historical change and language contact.

Influence

The book helped establish American structural linguistics and popularised the idea that languages differ profoundly in how they structure experience, a theme later associated with the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis.
