Charles Babbage (26 December 1791 – 18 October 1871) was an English polymath — a mathematician, philosopher, inventor, and mechanical engineer — who originated the concept of a digital programmable computer. Considered by many to be the "father of the computer", he designed the Difference Engine, a mechanical calculator for polynomial tables, and the far more ambitious Analytical Engine, a general-purpose computer whose design included an arithmetic logic unit ("the mill"), memory ("the store"), and control flow by conditional branching and loops.

Early life and education

Babbage was born in London, the son of a banker. Largely self-taught in mathematics because of ill health in childhood, he entered Trinity College, Cambridge, in 1810, where he co-founded the Analytical Society to promote continental (Leibnizian) calculus notation in Britain. He helped found the Astronomical Society in 1820 and was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1816.

The engines

Frustrated by errors in hand-computed mathematical tables, Babbage proposed in 1822 a machine to compute them mechanically by the method of finite differences. The British government funded Difference Engine No. 1, but the project collapsed amid cost overruns and disputes with his engineer, and it was never completed in his lifetime. From 1834 he developed the Analytical Engine, programmable with punched cards adapted from the Jacquard loom. Ada Lovelace, who translated and annotated a description of the engine, recognized its potential beyond pure calculation and wrote what is regarded as the first computer program. In 1991 the Science Museum in London built Difference Engine No. 2 from Babbage's plans, and it worked — demonstrating the soundness of his designs.

Other work

Babbage wrote influentially on the organization of factories in On the Economy of Machinery and Manufactures (1832), campaigned for the reform of British science in Reflections on the Decline of Science in England (1830), and made contributions ranging from actuarial science to the ophthalmoscope and railway safety devices. He famously campaigned against street music in his later years.
