John Peel

Born: John Robert Parker Ravenscroft, 30 August 1939, Heswall, Cheshire, England
Died: 25 October 2004 (aged 65), Cusco, Peru
Occupation: Radio presenter, disc jockey
Employer: BBC Radio 1 (1967–2004)
Known for: The "Peel Sessions"; championing punk, reggae, hip hop and indie music

John Robert Parker Ravenscroft OBE (30 August 1939 – 25 October 2004), better known as John Peel, was an English disc jockey and radio presenter. He was the longest-serving of the original BBC Radio 1 DJs, broadcasting regularly from 1967 until his death in 2004. He was one of the first broadcasters to play psychedelic rock and progressive rock records on British radio, and is widely credited with promoting punk rock, reggae, indie rock, alternative rock and hip hop.

Career

After national service and a period working in the United States — where he got his first radio jobs in Texas and Oklahoma, at one point posing as a Beatles expert for a Dallas station during Beatlemania — Peel returned to Britain in 1967 to join the pirate station Radio London. Adopting the surname Peel, he presented the late-night programme The Perfumed Garden. When Radio London closed he joined the newly launched BBC Radio 1, where his shows became a fixture for nearly four decades.

The Peel Sessions

Peel's great institution was the "Peel Session": bands were invited to record four tracks in a day at the BBC's Maida Vale studios, giving hundreds of unsigned and emerging acts national exposure. Sessions by artists from Pink Floyd and Led Zeppelin to The Smiths, Joy Division and Nirvana have become treasured documents, many released commercially. Peel famously championed The Fall, whose leader Mark E. Smith he adored; his favourite song was "Teenage Kicks" by The Undertones, whose opening lines are carved on his gravestone. His annual listeners' chart, the Festive Fifty, became a beloved tradition.

Death and legacy

Peel died suddenly of a heart attack while on a working holiday in Cusco, Peru, in October 2004. Tributes poured in from musicians and listeners alike; his funeral in Bury St Edmunds was attended by thousands. The BBC named a wing of Broadcasting House after him, and the Glastonbury Festival named its New Bands Tent the John Peel Stage in his honour. His record collection, one of the largest private collections in the world, has been catalogued as a cultural archive.
