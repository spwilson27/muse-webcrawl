Robert James Brown (born 27 December 1944) is an Australian former politician, environmentalist, and doctor. He was a senator for Tasmania from 1996 to 2012 and the first parliamentary leader of the Australian Greens, serving from 2005 until his retirement in 2012.

Brown trained and worked as a general practitioner before becoming prominent in environmental campaigns in Tasmania, most famously the campaign to save the Franklin River in the early 1980s, during which he was imprisoned for protesting. He was elected to the Tasmanian House of Assembly in 1983 and to the Australian Senate in 1996, becoming the first openly gay member of the Parliament of Australia.

Political career

As Greens leader, Brown led the party to its strongest results, and in 2010 the Greens won a record vote and held the balance of power in the Senate. He negotiated the agreement supporting the Gillard Labor minority government. Brown campaigned on climate change, opposition to the Iraq War, same-sex marriage, and forest protection.

Later life

After retiring from the Senate, Brown founded the Bob Brown Foundation to campaign for environmental causes, including opposition to the proposed Adani coal mine and protection of Tasmania's Tarkine forests.
