Hadamard transform

The Hadamard transform (also known as the Walsh–Hadamard transform) is an example of a generalized class of Fourier transforms. It performs an orthogonal, symmetric, involutive, linear operation on 2m real numbers, decomposing a signal into a superposition of Walsh functions. The transform is defined by the Hadamard matrices, whose entries are +1 and −1 and whose rows are mutually orthogonal.

Definition

The Hadamard matrix of order 2 is H = [[1, 1], [1, −1]], and higher-order matrices are built recursively by the Kronecker product H2n = H2 ⊗ Hn. The fast Walsh–Hadamard transform computes the operation in O(n log n) additions and subtractions, with no multiplications.

Applications

It is used in signal processing and data compression (including early JPEG XR and video coding experiments), error-correcting codes such as the Reed–Muller codes used by the Mariner spacecraft, and quantum computing, where the Hadamard gate creates superpositions of qubit states.
