President of the Senate (Australia)

The President of the Senate is the presiding officer of the Australian Senate, the upper house of the Parliament of Australia. The President is elected by senators from among their own number at the start of each parliament.

Role

The President presides over debates, maintains order under the standing orders, and represents the Senate in dealings with the House of Representatives and the Governor-General. Unlike the Speaker of the House, the President retains a deliberative vote equal to that of other senators, but does not exercise a casting vote; tied votes are resolved in the negative.

History

The office was established at Federation in 1901, modelled loosely on the Lord Chancellor's role in the British House of Lords, adapted to an elected chamber.
