William Tell (opera)

William Tell (Guillaume Tell)
Opera by Gioachino Rossini
Libretto: Étienne de Jouy and Hippolyte Bis, after Schiller
Premiere: 3 August 1829, Paris Opéra

William Tell (French: Guillaume Tell) is an opera in four acts by Italian composer Gioachino Rossini to a French libretto by Étienne de Jouy and Hippolyte Bis, based on Friedrich Schiller's play about the Swiss folk hero William Tell. It premiered at the Paris Opéra on 3 August 1829 and was Rossini's last opera, though he lived forty more years.

Overview

Set in 13th-century Switzerland under Austrian domination, the opera dramatises Tell's resistance to the bailiff Gesler: the famous scene in which Tell is forced to shoot an apple from his son Jemmy's head, his capture, escape, and the Swiss cantons' rising that ends in liberation. Its original length (over four hours of music) and demanding tenor role — Arnold's music, written for Adolphe Nourrit, contains numerous high Cs — led to frequent cuts and abridgements in performance.

The overture

The overture is among the most famous in classical music. Its four parts depict dawn, a storm, a pastoral scene (with the ranz des vaches for cor anglais), and a galop finale — the galloping "March of the Swiss Soldiers," indelibly associated in popular culture with the Lone Ranger. Rossini later quipped about the work and its reception, and famously remarked of its length in performance that he expected audiences to need patience.

Legacy

The opera profoundly influenced French grand opera and composers including Meyerbeer. Though long considered difficult to stage complete, modern revivals and recordings have restored the full score, in both the original French and the Italian version (Guglielmo Tell).
