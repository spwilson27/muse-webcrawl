Mandatory Palestine

The territory of the British Mandate for Palestine.

Mandatory Palestine was a geopolitical entity that existed between 1920 and 1948 in the region of Palestine under the terms of the League of Nations Mandate for Palestine. Britain received the mandate at the San Remo conference in 1920, following the defeat of the Ottoman Empire in the First World War, and civil administration began in 1920; the mandate formally entered into force in 1923.

The Mandate

The mandate document incorporated the Balfour Declaration of 1917, committing Britain to facilitate the establishment of a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine while safeguarding the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities. Transjordan was administered separately after 1921 and became fully independent in 1946.

History

The Mandate period saw waves of Jewish immigration (aliyah), the growth of Jewish and Arab national movements, and escalating conflict, including the 1929 riots, the 1936–1939 Arab revolt, and the 1939 White Paper restricting Jewish immigration. After the Second World War and the Holocaust, Britain referred the problem to the United Nations, whose 1947 partition plan proposed two states. The Mandate ended on 14 May 1948, when Israel declared independence, followed by the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.
