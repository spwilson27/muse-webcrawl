Blessed Hugh Faringdon

Died14 November 1539, Reading, England
OfficeLast Abbot of Reading Abbey (1520–1539)

Hugh Faringdon (c. 1490 – 14 November 1539), born Hugh Cook, was an English Benedictine monk and the last Abbot of Reading Abbey. He was executed for high treason during the Dissolution of the Monasteries under Henry VIII and was later beatified as a martyr by the Roman Catholic Church.

Early life

Cook took the name Faringdon, probably from his birthplace in Berkshire, when he entered Reading Abbey as a novice. He rose through the community, serving as sub-prior, and was elected abbot in 1520. He maintained cordial relations with Henry VIII, entertained the court at the abbey, and initially cooperated with royal policy.

Dissolution and trial

During the Dissolution, Faringdon refused to surrender his abbey voluntarily and was accused — on disputed evidence — of denying the royal supremacy and aiding rebels. He was tried at Reading in November 1539, convicted of treason, and hanged, drawn and quartered outside the abbey gate along with two companions, John Eynon (Rugg) and John Oglethorpe (Onion). Reading Abbey was dissolved and its buildings plundered.

Beatification

Faringdon was beatified by Pope Leo XIII in 1895 as one of the martyrs of the English Reformation. His feast day is 14 November, shared locally with his companions.
