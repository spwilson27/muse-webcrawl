Salishan languages

Map of the historical distribution of Salishan languages in the Pacific Northwest.

The Salishan (or Salish) languages are a family of Indigenous languages of the Pacific Northwest of North America, spoken in what is now British Columbia in Canada and the US states of Washington, Oregon, Idaho and Montana. They are characterised by complex consonant systems, including glottalised and uvular consonants, and by polysynthetic word structure.

Branches

The family is usually divided into several branches: Coast Salish languages such as Halkomelem, Squamish, Lushootseed and Northern Straits Salish; Interior Salish languages such as Shuswap (Secwepemctsín), Okanagan and Spokane–Kalispel–Flathead; and the more divergent Nuxalk (Bella Coola), spoken in an isolated enclave on the central British Columbia coast, and Tillamook in Oregon.

Characteristics

Salishan languages are famous among linguists for their large consonant inventories, consonant clusters without vowels, lexical suffixes, and reduplication marking plurality, diminution and aspect. Some analyses argue that they may lack a clear noun–verb distinction, a claim that has been influential and debated in linguistic theory.

Status and revitalisation

All Salishan languages are endangered, many with only a small number of elderly fluent speakers, a result of colonisation and residential school policies. Community-led revitalisation programmes, immersion schools and university partnerships are working to document the languages and train new speakers.
