Pitch (music)

Pitch is a perceptual property of sounds that allows their ordering on a frequency-related scale, or more commonly, pitch is the quality that makes it possible to judge sounds as "higher" and "lower" in the sense associated with musical melodies. Pitch is a major auditory attribute of musical tones, along with duration, loudness, and timbre.

Pitch may be quantified as a frequency, but pitch is not a purely objective physical property; it is a subjective psychoacoustical attribute of sound. Oscillations in sound waves can often be characterized in terms of frequency, and the pitch is generally associated with the fundamental frequency of the sound.

Pitch and frequency

Generally, the higher the frequency of a sound wave, the higher the perceived pitch. A doubling of frequency is heard as an octave. The standard reference point is concert pitch: A440, in which the A above middle C vibrates at 440 hertz. In the equal temperament system, pitch classes are divided into 12 semitones per octave.

Notation and pitch classes

In Western music notation, pitch is represented by the vertical placement of notes on the staff, in combination with a clef and key signature, and by letter names (A through G) with accidentals. Pitch class refers to a pitch considered modulo octave equivalence; there are twelve pitch classes in the chromatic scale.

Perception

Humans typically hear pitches from about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, with sensitivity declining at the extremes. Some individuals have absolute pitch (perfect pitch), the ability to identify or produce a note without a reference tone. Pitch perception can be influenced by timbre, loudness, and context, and complex phenomena such as the missing fundamental show that pitch is constructed by the auditory system rather than read directly off the waveform.

History of pitch standards

Pitch standards varied widely by place and period before the 20th century; Baroque pitch was often around a semitone lower than modern pitch. Attempts at standardisation culminated in the international adoption of A = 440 Hz, confirmed by the International Organization for Standardization in 1955 (ISO 16), though some orchestras tune slightly sharper, to 442–445 Hz.
