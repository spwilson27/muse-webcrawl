Trumpet

ClassificationBrass instrument, aerophone
RangeWritten F♯ below middle C to about three octaves above
RelatedCornet, flugelhorn, piccolo trumpet

The trumpet is a brass instrument commonly used in classical and jazz ensembles. It is the highest-register instrument of the brass family, producing sound when the player's vibrating lips cause the air column inside the tube to vibrate. The most common type is the B♭ trumpet, whose tubing is about 1.48 metres long.

Construction

A modern trumpet has three piston valves (or, in some traditions, rotary valves) that add lengths of tubing and lower the pitch by a whole step, a half step and a minor third respectively. Combined, the valves give the instrument a fully chromatic range. The bell, leadpipe and valve section are usually made of yellow brass.

History

Trumpet-like instruments date back to ancient Egypt and were used for military and ceremonial signalling for millennia. The invention of valves in the early 19th century transformed the natural trumpet into a fully chromatic instrument, and composers such as Haydn and Hummel had already written famous concertos for the keyed trumpet. In the 20th century the trumpet became a central solo instrument in jazz through players such as Louis Armstrong, Dizzy Gillespie and Miles Davis.
