Gregorian chant

Gregorian chant is the central tradition of Western plainchant: monophonic, unaccompanied sacred singing in Latin, developed mainly in western and central Europe during the 9th and 10th centuries for use in the Roman Catholic liturgy. It is named after Pope Gregory I, to whom legend attributed its composition.

Characteristics

Chants are organised into the eight church modes and notated with neumes — early signs indicating melodic contour — before staff notation developed. The repertoire includes antiphons, responsories, hymns and settings of the Mass Ordinary and Proper. Gregorian chant profoundly influenced later Western music, from organum to modern chant revivals.
