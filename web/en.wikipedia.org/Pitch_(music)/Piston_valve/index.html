Piston valve

A piston valve is a device used to control the motion of a fluid along a tube or pipe by means of the linear motion of a piston within a chamber or cylinder. The term is used in two main contexts: the valves of brass instruments, and valves in steam engines and industrial piping.

Brass instruments

Most trumpets, cornets, flugelhorns, euphoniums and many tubas use three (sometimes four) piston valves. Each valve contains a piston — a cylindrical plug with holes, or ports, drilled through it — that slides in a casing. When the player presses a valve, the piston shifts so its ports redirect the air through an extra length of tubing, lowering the instrument's pitch by a fixed interval: the second valve by a semitone, the first by a whole tone, and the third by a minor third. Combinations of valves make the full chromatic range available. Piston valves compete with rotary valves, which are favored on French horns and some tubas.

Steam engines

In steam engines, a piston valve consists of two piston heads on a common spindle that alternately admit steam to, and release it from, each end of the cylinder. Replacing the earlier slide valve, piston valves suited the higher pressures and superheated steam of later locomotives and were widely used in the twentieth century.
