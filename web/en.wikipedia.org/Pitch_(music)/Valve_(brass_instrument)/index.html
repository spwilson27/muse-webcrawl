Valve (brass instrument)

A valve on a brass instrument changes the pitch by altering the effective length of the vibrating air column. When a valve is engaged, it diverts the air through an extra loop of tubing, lowering the pitch by a fixed interval. Most brass instruments have three valves, some have four, and the French horn uses rotary valves operated by levers.

Types

The two main families are piston valves, which move up and down inside a casing and are standard on trumpets, cornets and many low brass instruments, and rotary valves, which rotate and are standard on French horns and some tubas and trumpets. The first valve typically lowers the pitch by a whole step, the second by a semitone and the third by a minor third.

History

Before valves, brass players relied on harmonics and, on horns, hand-stopping. Valve systems were patented in the 1810s and 1820s (Stölzel, Blühmel, Périnet and others) and spread rapidly, making brass instruments fully chromatic and revolutionising orchestral and band writing.
