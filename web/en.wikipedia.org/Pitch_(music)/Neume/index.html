Neume

A neume is the basic element of Western and Eastern systems of musical notation used before the invention of the five-line staff. Neumes are signs written above chant texts indicating melodic gestures — single notes or groups of notes sung to one syllable — rather than exact pitches.

Development

Early unheighted neumes (from the 9th century) reminded singers of melodies they already knew by heart. Heighted neumes, placed at relative heights around a reference line, gradually made pitch recoverable, leading through the innovations of Guido d'Arezzo to modern staff notation. Square-notation neumes remain in use in printed books of Gregorian chant.
