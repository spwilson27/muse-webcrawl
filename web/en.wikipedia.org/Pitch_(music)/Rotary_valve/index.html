Rotary valve

A rotary valve is a type of valve used in brass instruments, most famously the French horn, in which the player changes the pitch by pressing levers that rotate cylindrical rotors inside the instrument's tubing. Rotating the valve redirects the airstream through additional lengths of tubing, lowering the pitch of the instrument in the same way that piston valves do on trumpets and tubas.

Mechanism

Each rotary valve contains a solid rotor with internal ports. When the lever is depressed, the rotor turns through roughly a quarter turn, aligning its ports with a different combination of tubes. Modern rotors are usually driven by a mechanical linkage or a string linkage, the latter being traditional on French horns. Rotary valves are also used on some tubas, euphoniums and rotary trumpets, which are common in German and Austrian orchestras.

History

The rotary valve was developed in the early 19th century, around the same period as the piston valve. Heinrich Stölzel and Friedrich Blühmel patented valve designs in 1818, and Joseph Riedl is credited with an early rotary valve design in Vienna in 1832. Rotary valves became standard on horns and remained dominant in central European brass design.
