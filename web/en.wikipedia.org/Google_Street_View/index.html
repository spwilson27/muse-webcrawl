Google Street View

DeveloperGoogle
Launched25 May 2007
CoverageOver 100 countries; millions of kilometres of road

Google Street View is a technology featured in Google Maps and Google Earth that provides interactive panoramas from positions along many streets in the world. It was launched in 2007 in several cities in the United States and has since expanded to include cities and rural areas worldwide.

[ Image placeholder: A Google Street View car with its camera mast ]

Street View imagery is captured mostly by specially equipped cars carrying cameras, GPS, and laser range scanners on a mast, stitched into 360-degree panoramas. Backpack-mounted "Trekker" units, trikes, boats, snowmobiles, and even camels have extended coverage to trails, interiors, waterways, museums, and landmarks such as Machu Picchu and the International Space Station.

Privacy

Google automatically blurs faces and licence plates, and users can request additional blurring. Street View has nevertheless provoked privacy controversies in many countries; its camera cars were also found to have collected Wi-Fi network data, leading to investigations and fines in the early 2010s. Some countries, notably Germany for many years, had limited residential coverage because of public opposition.

Uses

Beyond navigation and virtual tourism, Street View data is used in urban research, real-estate assessment, journalism, games such as GeoGuessr, and as a time machine of sorts through archived imagery of the same locations across years.
