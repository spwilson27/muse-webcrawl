Superior Court of Quebec

The Montreal courthouse, seat of the Superior Court in the district of Montreal.

The Superior Court of Quebec (French: Cour supérieure du Québec) is a superior court and court of general jurisdiction in the province of Quebec, Canada. It is the province's trial court of inherent jurisdiction, hearing civil, commercial, family, and criminal matters, and it also exercises appellate and judicial-review functions over certain lower courts and administrative bodies.

Jurisdiction

The Superior Court has original jurisdiction over civil claims above the monetary limits of the Court of Québec, including actions in damages, contract disputes, successions, and family law matters such as divorce and custody. In criminal law it tries the most serious indictable offences, including jury trials. It also hears applications for judicial review of decisions of provincial tribunals and appeals from certain decisions of the Court of Québec.

Organization

The court is headed by a Chief Justice, assisted by a Senior Associate Chief Justice and an Associate Chief Justice. Its judges — roughly 150 in number — are appointed by the federal government under section 96 of the Constitution Act, 1867. The court sits in judicial districts throughout Quebec, with major seats in Montreal and Quebec City, and is divided for administrative purposes into chambers, including a Civil Chamber, a Commercial Chamber (which handles bankruptcy and insolvency), a Family Chamber, and a Criminal and Penal Chamber.

History

The court traces its origins to the courts established after the British conquest of New France and was formally organized in its modern form in the nineteenth century. It is the successor to the Court of Queen's Bench (later King's Bench) for civil matters in Lower Canada.
