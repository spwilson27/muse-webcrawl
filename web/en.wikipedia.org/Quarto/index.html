Quarto

Quarto (abbreviated Qto, 4to or 4º) is the size of a book or pamphlet produced by folding a full sheet of paper twice to form four leaves (eight pages). Each printed sheet produces four leaves; the size of a quarto page depends on the size of the full sheet, but a common historical quarto is about 24 by 30 centimetres (9.5 by 12 inches) or smaller.

In printing history

Quarto is one of the traditional format sizes alongside folio (sheet folded once) and octavo (folded three times). In the hand-press period, quartos were cheaper than folios and were the usual format for plays, pamphlets and sermons.

Shakespeare quartos

The term is famous in literary scholarship because many of Shakespeare's plays were first printed as quartos: inexpensive pamphlet editions such as the first quarto of Hamlet (1603, the "bad quarto") and the second quarto (1604/05, the "good quarto"). Early modern play quartos are distinguished from the posthumous First Folio of 1623 in textual scholarship, and the differences between quarto and folio texts are a major topic in editing Shakespeare.

Modern usage

In British paper sizes, "quarto" historically denoted a sheet of 10 by 8 inches, yielding a book size in between foolscap and A4. The term is now used mainly by bibliographers, antiquarian booksellers and librarians.
