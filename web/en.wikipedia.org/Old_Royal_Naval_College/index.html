Old Royal Naval College

The Old Royal Naval College is the architectural centrepiece of Maritime Greenwich in London, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Built on the site of the Tudor Palace of Placentia, where Henry VIII and Elizabeth I were born, the complex was designed by Sir Christopher Wren and built between 1696 and 1712 as the Royal Hospital for Seamen, a home for retired sailors of the Royal Navy.

History

Queen Mary II ordered the creation of the hospital after the Battle of La Hogue in 1692. Wren's design, with contributions from Nicholas Hawksmoor, features twin domes and a symmetrical layout that preserves the view of the Queen's House from the River Thames. The hospital closed in 1869, and from 1873 to 1998 the buildings housed the Royal Naval College, Greenwich, a training establishment for naval officers.

Features

The site is noted for the Painted Hall, decorated by Sir James Thornhill between 1707 and 1726 and often called the "Sistine Chapel of the UK", and the Chapel of St Peter and St Paul, rebuilt by James "Athenian" Stuart after a fire. Since 1998 the site has been managed by the Old Royal Naval College Foundation; parts are used by the University of Greenwich and Trinity Laban Conservatoire of Music and Dance.
