Baltica

Baltica is a palaeocontinent that formed in the late Proterozoic and Paleozoic from crustal blocks now making up northwestern Eurasia, including Scandinavia, the Baltic states, and much of European Russia. Its core, the East European Craton, is among the oldest continental crust on Earth.

History

Baltica existed as an independent continent by about 550 million years ago, separated from Gondwana by the Iapetus Ocean. During the Ordovician and Silurian it drifted northward and collided with Laurentia in the Caledonian orogeny, forming the combined continent of Euramerica (Laurussia). In the late Paleozoic, Baltica and Euramerica were incorporated into the supercontinent Pangaea.

Legacy

The Baltic Shield and the Baltic Sea region preserve the ancient basement of Baltica. The name Baltica is also used by geologists for the tectonic plate that carried the continent.
