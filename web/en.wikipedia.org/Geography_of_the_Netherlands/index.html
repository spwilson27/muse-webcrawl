The Netherlands is a country in Western Europe bordering Germany to the east, Belgium to the south, and the North Sea to the northwest. Its geography is dominated by its low elevation and flat terrain: roughly a quarter of the country lies below sea level, and much of the land has been reclaimed from the sea, lakes, and marshes in the form of polders.

Land reclamation and water management

Over many centuries the Dutch have built an elaborate system of dikes, dunes, pumping stations, and canals to hold back the sea and the great rivers — the Rhine, Meuse, and Scheldt — whose deltas occupy the southwest of the country. The catastrophic North Sea flood of 1953 prompted the Delta Works, a series of dams and storm surge barriers considered one of the great engineering projects of the twentieth century. The Zuiderzee Works earlier transformed the Zuiderzee inlet into the freshwater IJsselmeer and created the province of Flevoland on reclaimed land.

Landscape

The western and northern parts of the country are flat polderland. The east and southeast rise slightly into sandy plains and low hills; the highest point, the Vaalserberg in Limburg, reaches 322.7 metres near the tripoint with Belgium and Germany. The Frisian Islands line the Wadden Sea coast in the north.

Climate

The Netherlands has a temperate maritime climate with mild summers and cool winters, influenced by the North Sea and the prevailing westerly winds.
