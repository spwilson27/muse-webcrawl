Alan Moore

Born: 18 November 1953, Northampton, England

Occupation: Writer

Notable works: Watchmen, V for Vendetta, From Hell, The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen, Swamp Thing

Genres: Comics, graphic novel, fiction

Alan Moore (born 18 November 1953) is an English writer widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in comics history. Working first in British comics and then in the American industry, he brought literary ambition and formal experimentation to the medium with works such as Watchmen, V for Vendetta, and From Hell.

Career

Moore began writing for British magazines including 2000 AD and Warrior, where he created V for Vendetta and The Ballad of Halo Jones. Hired by DC Comics in 1983, he transformed the horror title Swamp Thing and then wrote Watchmen (1986–87) with artist Dave Gibbons, a landmark deconstruction of superheroes that remains one of the best-selling graphic novels ever published and the only one to win a Hugo Award.

After disputes over rights and merchandising, Moore left mainstream American comics, publishing works such as the Jack the Ripper study From Hell (with Eddie Campbell) and The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen (with Kevin O'Neill). He famously disowns film adaptations of his work and has had his name removed from them. Moore has also written prose novels, notably Voice of the Fire (1996) and the vast Jerusalem (2016), as well as the short story collection Illuminations (2022) and the novel The Great When (2024). He is a practicing ceremonial magician and an anarchist, and lives in Northampton.
