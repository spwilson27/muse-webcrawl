Dubrovnik

Dubrovnik (Croatian pronunciation: [dǔbroːʋniːk]), historically known as Ragusa, is a city on the Adriatic Sea in southern Croatia. It is one of the most prominent tourist destinations in the Mediterranean, a seaport and the centre of Dubrovnik-Neretva County. Its population was about 41,600 in 2021.

History

The city was founded in the 7th century, traditionally by refugees from Epidaurum (modern Cavtat) fleeing Slav and Avar invasions. As Ragusa it grew into a wealthy maritime republic, a rival of Venice, protected first by Byzantium and later, after 1358, under nominal Hungarian suzerainty. The Republic of Ragusa flourished on trade across the Adriatic and the Balkans, maintained a famous diplomatic corps, abolished slavery in 1416 — among the first in Europe — and was renowned for its quarantine laws (1377), pharmacy (1317) and orphanage. An earthquake in 1667 killed some 5,000 people and levelled much of the city. The republic was abolished by Napoleon in 1808, and in 1815 the city passed to Austria.

The Old Town

Dubrovnik's medieval walls — up to 6 metres thick in places and 25 metres high — completely enclose an old town of limestone-paved streets, including the main thoroughfare, the Stradun (Placa), lined with Baroque churches and palaces such as the Sponza and Rector's palaces, Onofrio's Fountain and the Franciscan monastery. The Old Town was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979. During the Croatian War of Independence, the city was besieged by Yugoslav forces in 1991–92 and shelled despite its protected status; restoration followed. Dubrovnik later gained worldwide fame as a filming location for the television series Game of Thrones, in which it stood in for King's Landing.
