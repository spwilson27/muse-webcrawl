Myofibroblasts combine features of fibroblasts and smooth muscle cells.

The myofibroblast is a cell type intermediate between a fibroblast and a smooth muscle cell, central to wound healing and tissue repair. Myofibroblasts synthesise extracellular matrix — especially collagen — and contract it using bundles of actin microfilaments (stress fibres) that incorporate alpha-smooth muscle actin, drawing wound edges together.

Formation

Myofibroblasts differentiate from local fibroblasts and other precursor cells under the influence of TGF-β1, mechanical tension in the matrix, and specialised matrix proteins such as the ED-A splice variant of fibronectin. They develop through a "proto-myofibroblast" stage bearing actin stress fibres before acquiring α-SMA.

Role in disease

In normal healing, myofibroblasts disappear by apoptosis once the wound closes. Their persistence drives fibrosis in organs such as the liver, lung and kidney, hypertrophic scarring, and the desmoplastic stroma of many cancers, where cancer-associated fibroblasts support tumour growth. Myofibroblasts also cause contractures in conditions such as Dupuytren's contracture.
