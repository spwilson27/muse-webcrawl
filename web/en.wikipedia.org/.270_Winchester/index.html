.270 Winchester

The .270 Winchester is a rifle cartridge developed by the Winchester Repeating Arms Company in 1923 and introduced in 1925 for its Model 54 bolt-action rifle. It was created by necking down the .30-03 case to accept .277-inch (7.04 mm) bullets. It became one of the most popular big-game hunting cartridges in the world, helped greatly by decades of advocacy by outdoor writer Jack O'Connor, for whom it was a favourite sheep and deer cartridge.

Performance

Typical factory loads drive a 130-grain (8.4 g) bullet at about 3,060 feet per second (930 m/s), with 150-grain loads around 2,850 ft/s. The cartridge is noted for a flat trajectory and moderate recoil, making it well suited to deer-sized game and, with suitable bullets, larger game such as elk at reasonable ranges. It has been chambered in countless bolt-action rifles and inspired descendants and competitors including the .270 Winchester Short Magnum and the later 6.8 Western and .277 Fury.

Use

The .270 Winchester remains a standard chambering offered by virtually every major rifle maker and ammunition manufacturer, and is widely used for hunting across North America and for plains game abroad.
