Aerobic respiration

Aerobic respiration is a set of metabolic reactions by which cells break down nutrient molecules in the presence of oxygen to produce adenosine triphosphate (ATP). It is the principal means by which eukaryotic cells, and many prokaryotes, obtain energy from glucose.

Aerobic respiration
Stages: Glycolysis, Krebs cycle, oxidative phosphorylation
Reactants: Glucose and oxygen
Products: CO2, water, ATP (~30–32)

Stages

Glycolysis in the cytoplasm splits glucose into pyruvate. Pyruvate enters the mitochondrion, where the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) releases carbon dioxide and generates electron carriers NADH and FADH2. In oxidative phosphorylation, electrons pass along the electron transport chain to oxygen, the final acceptor, driving ATP synthase.

Comparison with anaerobic respiration

Aerobic respiration yields far more ATP per glucose than fermentation because glucose is completely oxidized. In the absence of oxygen, cells rely on glycolysis coupled to fermentation, regenerating NAD+ at much lower energy yield.
