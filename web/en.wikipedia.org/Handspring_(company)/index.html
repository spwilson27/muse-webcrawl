Handspring (company)

IndustryConsumer electronics
Founded1998
FoundersJeff Hawkins, Donna Dubinsky, Ed Colligan
Defunct2003 (acquired by Palm, Inc.)

Handspring, Inc. was an American electronics company founded in 1998 by Jeff Hawkins, Donna Dubinsky, and Ed Colligan, the same team that had earlier founded Palm, Inc. The company was best known for the Visor line of personal digital assistants (PDAs) and the Treo line of smartphones.

History

Hawkins and Dubinsky left Palm after disagreements with its parent company, 3Com, over licensing of the Palm OS. Handspring licensed the Palm OS itself and built hardware around it, competing directly with its founders' former company. Its first product, the Visor, launched in 1999 and was an immediate success.

Visor

The Visor distinguished itself with the Springboard expansion slot, which accepted modules such as cameras, modems, GPS receivers, MP3 players, and backup storage. Models included the Visor Deluxe, Visor Prism (with a color screen), and the slim Visor Edge.

Treo

In 2002 Handspring pivoted toward smartphones with the Treo line, beginning with the Treo 180 and the popular Treo 270 and Treo 300, which combined a PDA, phone, and thumb keyboard. The Treo 600, released shortly after the Palm merger, became a landmark smartphone design.

Acquisition

In 2003 Palm, Inc. acquired Handspring, reuniting the two companies; the combined firm was renamed palmOne. The Treo line continued under the Palm brand for several years.
