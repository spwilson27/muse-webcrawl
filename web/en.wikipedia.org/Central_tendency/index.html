Central tendency

In statistics, a central tendency (or measure of central tendency) is a central or typical value for a probability distribution. Colloquially, measures of central tendency are often called averages. The term dates to the 1920s.

Common measures

The most familiar measures are the arithmetic mean (the sum of values divided by the count), the median (the middle value when observations are ordered), and the mode (the most frequent value). Other measures include the geometric mean, harmonic mean, midrange, trimmed mean, and various generalised means. The choice of measure depends on the type of data and the purpose of the summary: the median is often preferred for skewed distributions, such as incomes, because it is resistant to outliers, while the mean uses all the data but can be distorted by extreme values.

Properties

A good measure of central tendency is generally expected to be equivariant under changes of location and scale, to be computable from the data, and to summarise the distribution's centre together with a measure of spread such as the standard deviation or interquartile range.
