Pearl (poem)

Pearl is a late 14th-century Middle English poem that is considered one of the most significant surviving works of Middle English poetry. Written in a North-West Midland dialect, it survives in a single manuscript (Cotton Nero A.x) alongside Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, Patience and Cleanness, and is attributed to the same anonymous author, the "Pearl Poet" or "Gawain Poet".

Pearl
Author: Anonymous (the Pearl Poet)
Date: Late 14th century
Form: Alliterative verse, 101 stanzas
Manuscript: Cotton Nero A.x

Summary

A grieving father, mourning his lost "pearl"—widely read as his young daughter—falls asleep in a garden and dreams of a jewelled otherworldly landscape. Across a stream he sees a maiden, the Pearl herself, now a bride of Christ in the New Jerusalem. Their dialogue on death, grace and redemption unfolds until the dreamer tries to cross the stream and wakes.

Form

The poem comprises 101 twelve-line stanzas grouped in sections linked by repeated refrain words, with intricate alliteration, rhyme and numerical symbolism built around the number twelve and the perfection of heaven.
