IPTV

Internet Protocol television (IPTV) is the delivery of television content over Internet Protocol (IP) networks. Unlike downloaded media, IPTV offers the ability to stream the source media continuously. Unlike traditional terrestrial, satellite and cable television, IPTV services are transmitted over managed IP-based networks, typically operated by a telecommunications provider, which can guarantee quality of service.

A client media player can stream the content from a set-top box connected to a television, or directly through applications on computers, smartphones and smart TVs. IPTV services may be classified into live television, time-shifted television (such as catch-up TV and restart TV) and video on demand (VOD).

Definition and architecture

The term IPTV first appeared in 1995 with the founding of Precept Software. IPTV systems commonly use IP multicast (via IGMP) for live channels, so that a single stream serves all viewers watching the same channel, and unicast streaming for on-demand content. Video is usually compressed with codecs such as MPEG-2, H.264/MPEG-4 AVC or H.265/HEVC. Middleware handles channel guides, billing and conditional access; content protection is provided by IPTV-specific digital rights management systems.

Services

Telecom operators worldwide offer IPTV as part of "triple play" bundles with broadband and voice telephony; prominent examples include services built on Microsoft's Mediaroom platform (acquired by Ericsson) and numerous national deployments in France, South Korea, China and the United States. Because the network is managed end-to-end by the provider, IPTV is generally distinguished from over-the-top (OTT) streaming services such as Netflix, which are delivered over the public Internet without quality guarantees.

History and market

Early streaming products such as RealPlayer (1995) and services like the Kingston Interactive Television trial in the United Kingdom pioneered television-like delivery over telephone networks. Through the 2000s and 2010s IPTV subscriber numbers grew steadily to hundreds of millions worldwide, driven by fibre and fast DSL rollouts, even as OTT services grew alongside them.
