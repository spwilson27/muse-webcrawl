Fort Jefferson is a massive coastal fortress located on Garden Key in the Dry Tortugas, about 68 miles (109 km) west of Key West, Florida. Constructed of more than 16 million bricks, it is the largest brick masonry structure in the Americas. Built by the United States government between 1846 and 1875 to protect shipping lanes in the Gulf of Mexico, the fort was never fully completed and never came under attack, yet it served as a military prison during and after the American Civil War.

History

The Dry Tortugas were named by Spanish explorer Ponce de León in 1513, after the sea turtles (tortugas) found there and the lack of fresh water ("dry"). Recognising the strategic value of the islands, which command the shipping channel between the Gulf of Mexico and the Atlantic Ocean, the United States began construction of the fort in 1846. The hexagonal fort was designed with three tiers of casemates and more than 400 heavy guns.

During the American Civil War, the fort remained in Union hands and was used to house prisoners, mostly Union deserters. Its most famous prisoner was Dr. Samuel Mudd, imprisoned there from 1865 to 1869 for his role in treating John Wilkes Booth, the assassin of President Abraham Lincoln. Mudd won a pardon from President Andrew Johnson in 1869 after helping to treat a yellow fever epidemic at the fort.

Later use and preservation

The army abandoned the fort in 1874 after hurricanes and disease made it untenable. It later served as a naval coaling station, a quarantine station, and a seaplane base. In 1935 President Franklin D. Roosevelt designated it a national monument, and in 1992 it became the centrepiece of Dry Tortugas National Park. Today the fort is accessible only by boat or seaplane and is a popular destination for snorkelling, birdwatching, and history tours.
