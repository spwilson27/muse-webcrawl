John D. Rockefeller

John Davison Rockefeller Sr. (July 8, 1839 – May 23, 1937) was an American business magnate and philanthropist. He co-founded the Standard Oil Company in 1870 and built the first great American business trust, dominating the oil industry until the Supreme Court ordered Standard Oil's dissolution in 1911. He is widely regarded as the richest American of all time and among the wealthiest people in modern history.


Rockefeller in later life

Standard Oil

Rockefeller entered the oil business in Cleveland, Ohio, in the 1860s, building a refinery and pursuing relentless cost control, vertical integration and secret railroad rebates. Through the Standard Oil Trust (1882) he came to control some 90% of American oil refineries and pipelines. Ida Tarbell's exposé The History of the Standard Oil Company (1904) made him the emblem of the predatory "robber baron." In Standard Oil Co. of New Jersey v. United States (1911), the Supreme Court dissolved the trust into 34 companies, including ExxonMobil, BP and Chevron — and Rockefeller's personal fortune grew further as the shares rose.

Philanthropy

A devout Baptist, Rockefeller gave away much of his fortune, founding the University of Chicago (1890), Rockefeller University (1901) and the Rockefeller Foundation (1913), which funded public health campaigns against hookworm, yellow fever and malaria worldwide, as well as the Peking Union Medical College. He retired from daily business in the late 1890s and died in 1937, aged 97.
