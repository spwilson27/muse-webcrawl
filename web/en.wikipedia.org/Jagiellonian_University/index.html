The Jagiellonian University (Polish: Uniwersytet Jagielloński) is a public research university in Kraków, Poland. Founded in 1364 by King Casimir III the Great, it is the oldest university in Poland and one of the oldest surviving universities in the world.

History

The university was founded as the Kraków Academy in 1364, modeled on the universities of Bologna and Padua. After a period of decline it was restored in 1400 by King Władysław II Jagiełło — from whom it takes its name — with funds from the bequest of Queen Jadwiga. Its most famous early student was Nicolaus Copernicus, who studied there in the 1490s. During the Second World War, 184 of its academics were arrested by the Gestapo in the Sonderaktion Krakau of November 1939 and deported to concentration camps; the university operated underground during the occupation.

Today

The Jagiellonian University comprises 16 faculties, including medicine, law, philosophy, physics, and computer science, and teaches tens of thousands of students. Its library, the Jagiellonian Library, holds major collections of medieval manuscripts. The university consistently ranks among Poland's top institutions.
