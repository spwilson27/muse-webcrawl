HIV/AIDS

HIV/AIDS is a spectrum of conditions caused by infection with the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV). HIV attacks the immune system, particularly CD4 T cells; without treatment, infection progressively weakens immunity until the most advanced stage, acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), in which opportunistic infections and certain cancers can develop.

Transmission and course

HIV spreads through specific body fluids, mainly via unprotected sex, contaminated needles, blood products, and from mother to child during pregnancy, birth, or breastfeeding. It is not spread by casual contact. After an initial flu-like illness, infection may remain asymptomatic for years while CD4 counts decline. AIDS is diagnosed when the CD4 count falls below 200 cells/µL or an AIDS-defining illness occurs.

Treatment and prevention

There is no cure or vaccine, but antiretroviral therapy (ART) suppresses the virus to undetectable levels, allowing near-normal life expectancy and eliminating sexual transmission. Prevention includes condoms, needle exchange, pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP).

Epidemiology

Since the pandemic began in the early 1980s, roughly 40 million people have died of AIDS-related illnesses, and around 39 million people were living with HIV in the early 2020s, with sub-Saharan Africa most heavily affected.
