Palembang is the capital and largest city of the Indonesian province of South Sumatra, on the island of Sumatra. The city is situated on the banks of the Musi River in the eastern lowlands of southern Sumatra, some 90 kilometres from the river's mouth on the Bangka Strait. It is one of the oldest cities in Indonesia and in Southeast Asia, with a recorded history extending back to the 7th century.

History

Palembang was the centre of Srivijaya, a powerful Malay Buddhist maritime empire that dominated trade through the Strait of Malacca from the 7th to the 13th century. The Kedukan Bukit inscription, dated 682 CE and found near the city, is among the oldest surviving texts in the Malay language and records the founding of Srivijaya. The Chinese monk Yijing studied Sanskrit and Buddhist texts in Palembang in the late 7th century, describing a flourishing centre of learning with more than a thousand monks. After Srivijaya's decline, the city passed under Majapahit influence and became a haunt of pirates before the rise of the Palembang Sultanate in the 17th century, which grew wealthy on the pepper and tin trades. The Dutch abolished the sultanate in 1823 and incorporated the region into the Netherlands East Indies.

Geography and economy

The city straddles the Musi River, whose two banks — Seberang Ilir (downtown) and Seberang Ulu — are linked by the red Ampera Bridge, opened in 1965 and Palembang's signature landmark. The surrounding region is a major producer of oil and natural gas, coal, rubber, and palm oil, and the city's river port remains an important hub for trade in southern Sumatra. Palembang's floating houses (rumah rakit) and stilt settlements along the Musi preserve the riverine character of the old city.

Culture

Palembang is famous throughout Indonesia for pempek, a savoury fishcake made from ground fish and tapioca, served in a sweet-sour vinegar sauce. The local Malay dialect, songket gold-thread weaving, and lacquerware are distinctive cultural products. Palembang co-hosted the 2011 Southeast Asian Games with Jakarta and, again with Jakarta, the 2018 Asian Games, for which the Jakabaring Sport City complex and a light-rail line — Indonesia's first — were built.
