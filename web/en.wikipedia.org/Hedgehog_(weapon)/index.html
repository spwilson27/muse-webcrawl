Hedgehog (weapon)

The Hedgehog (also known as an Anti-Submarine Projector) was a forward-throwing anti-submarine weapon developed by the Royal Navy during the Second World War. It fired a pattern of mortar bombs ahead of a ship so that the attacking vessel could remain in sonar contact with the target submarine.

Design and use

The Hedgehog consisted of 24 spigot mortars arranged in a box-like mount, firing 65 lb (29.5 kg) projectiles containing about 35 lb of Torpex explosive in an elliptical pattern roughly 200 yards ahead of the ship. Unlike depth charges, the Hedgehog's bombs were contact-fuzed: they exploded only if they struck the submarine, so a miss did not disturb the water and sonar contact could be maintained.

First used in 1942, the Hedgehog achieved a considerably higher kill rate per attack than depth charges and was widely fitted to Royal Navy and United States Navy escort vessels. It was later complemented and partly succeeded by the Squid and Limbo mortar systems.
