East Asia Summit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The East Asia Summit (EAS) is a regional forum held annually by leaders of, initially, 16 countries in the East Asian, Southeast Asian, South Asian and Oceanian regions, based on the ASEAN Plus Six mechanism. Membership expanded to 18 countries including Russia and the United States at the Sixth Summit in 2011.

History

The concept of an East Asian grouping was first promoted in 1991 by Malaysian Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad. The modern summit emerged from the ASEAN Plus Three process, and the first East Asia Summit was held in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, on 14 December 2005. Meetings are held after the annual ASEAN leaders' meetings and are chaired by the ASEAN member holding the rotating chairmanship.

Members and participants

The 18 members are the ten ASEAN states (Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam), plus Australia, China, India, Japan, New Zealand, South Korea, Russia and the United States. The United States and Russia formally joined at the 2011 summit in Bali, with President Barack Obama becoming the first sitting US president to attend.

Role

The EAS is a leaders-led forum for strategic dialogue on political, security and economic issues of common regional concern. It operates alongside, and is often contrasted with, APEC and the ASEAN Regional Forum. Priority areas of cooperation include energy, education, finance, global health and communicable diseases, environment and disaster management, and ASEAN connectivity.
