Indra (Sanskrit: इन्द्र) is the king of the devas (gods) and ruler of Svarga (heaven) in Hinduism. He is associated with the sky, lightning, weather, thunder, storms, rains, river flows, and war. Indra is the most frequently praised deity in the Rigveda, with about 250 hymns dedicated to him; his defining myth is the slaying of the serpent Vritra, who withheld the waters of the world, releasing the rivers and bringing rain.

Indra on his white elephant Airavata, bearing the vajra.

Mythology

Fortified by soma, Indra shattered Vritra with his vajra (thunderbolt), crafted by Tvashtr, and released the seven rivers. He also defeated the demon Vala and recovered the stolen cows of the Panis. In later Hinduism, Indra's status declined relative to Vishnu and Shiva, and "Indra" became a title or office held by a succession of kings of the gods. In epics and Puranas, he is guardian of the east, father of Arjuna in the Mahabharata, and a frequent tester — and victim — of heroes and ascetics.

In other traditions

Indra appears in Buddhism as Śakra, lord of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven who questions the Buddha, and in Jainism as Saudharmendra. Cognate storm gods in other Indo-European traditions include Zeus, Jupiter, Thor, and Perun; Indra's Mitanni and Avestan counterparts attest to his antiquity in the Indo-Iranian world.
