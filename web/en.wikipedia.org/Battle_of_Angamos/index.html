Battle of Angamos

Date8 October 1879
LocationOff Punta Angamos, Antofagasta, Chile
ResultChilean victory; capture of the Huáscar
BelligerentsChile vs. Peru
CommandersGalvarino Riveros (Chile); Miguel Grau (Peru, killed)

The Battle of Angamos (Spanish: Combate Naval de Angamos) was a naval encounter of the War of the Pacific, fought on 8 October 1879 between the navies of Chile and Peru off Punta Angamos in present-day northern Chile. It concluded with the capture of the Peruvian ironclad Huáscar and the death of her commander, Admiral Miguel Grau, and is regarded as the decisive naval engagement of the war.

Background

Since the opening of the war in 1879, the Peruvian monitor Huáscar, under Grau, had raided Chilean ports and shipping, capturing the transport Rímac and delaying Chilean invasion plans for months. Chile determined to hunt her down with two divisions centred on the armoured frigates Blanco Encalada and Almirante Cochrane, whose faster speed and heavier guns gave them the advantage.

The battle

On the morning of 8 October, Huáscar and the corvette Unión were sighted near Angamos. Unión escaped north, but Huáscar was cut off by the second Chilean division. A shell from Almirante Cochrane struck Huáscar's turret, and another destroyed her bridge, killing Admiral Grau. Successive Peruvian commanders were killed or wounded; with steering damaged and guns disabled, the ship was boarded and captured after her crew attempted to scuttle her by opening the sea cocks — Chilean boarding parties closed them and took the ship in tow.

Significance

With Huáscar captured (she was repaired and taken into Chilean service), Peru lost effective command of the sea. Chile was free to land its army in Peruvian territory, beginning the land campaigns that would occupy Tarapacá, Arica, and eventually Lima. In Peru, Grau is revered as the nation's foremost naval hero, and 8 October is commemorated by the Peruvian Navy.
