The Tower of London, officially His Majesty's Royal Palace and Fortress of the Tower of London, is a historic castle on the north bank of the River Thames in central London, England. It was founded toward the end of 1066 as part of the Norman Conquest. The White Tower, which gives the entire castle its name, was built by William the Conqueror in 1078 and was a resented symbol of oppression, inflicted on London by the new Norman ruling class. The castle was also used as a prison from 1100 (Ranulf Flambard) until 1952 (the Kray twins), although that was not its primary purpose.

History

The castle was expanded under Richard I, Henry III, and Edward I, reaching roughly its present form by the end of the 13th century: concentric rings of defensive walls, a moat, and some twenty towers around the central keep. Over its history the Tower served successively as a royal residence, the Royal Mint, the Royal Menagerie, the repository of the Crown Jewels, an armory and treasury, and the home of the Royal Observatory and the Public Record Office.

Its reputation as a place of torture and death derives largely from 16th-century religious propagandists and 19th-century popular writers; in fact only seven people were executed inside the Tower before the 20th century — among them Anne Boleyn, Catherine Howard, and Lady Jane Grey — though many notable prisoners were held there, and the two young "Princes in the Tower" disappeared in 1483. During the two World Wars it was again used for prisoners and spies; eleven men were shot there for espionage in the First World War and Josef Jakobs in 1941.

The Tower today

The Tower is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of London's most popular tourist attractions. It is guarded by the Yeoman Warders ("Beefeaters"), who also give tours, and the Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom are displayed in the Jewel House. The Tower is famously home to a community of ravens, kept under the legend that the kingdom will fall if they leave; the Ceremony of the Keys, the nightly locking of the gates, has been performed for some 700 years. The complex is managed by Historic Royal Palaces.
