Plebs

The plebs formed the mass of ordinary Roman citizens.

In ancient Rome, the plebeians, also called the plebs, were the general body of free Roman citizens who were not members of the patrician class. The distinction between patricians and plebeians is one of the oldest features of Roman society, traditionally dated to the foundation of the city under Romulus, who is said to have appointed the first senators — the patres — from whose descendants the patrician order derived. The word is the origin of the English terms "pleb" and "plebeian", now often used pejoratively for common or unsophisticated people.

Conflict of the Orders

Early in the Republic, plebeians were excluded from most magistracies, priesthoods, and intermarriage with patricians, and suffered from harsh laws of debt. Their long struggle for equality, the Conflict of the Orders (traditionally 494–287 BC), began with the first secession of the plebs to the Mons Sacer, which won them their own officers, the tribunes of the plebs, whose persons were sacrosanct and who could veto magistrates' actions. The plebeians also gained their own assembly, the Concilium Plebis, whose enactments were called plebiscites.

Equalisation

Step by step the barriers fell: the Twelve Tables (451–450 BC) published the law; the Lex Canuleia (445 BC) permitted intermarriage; the Licinian-Sextian laws (367 BC) opened the consulship; and the Lex Hortensia (287 BC) made plebiscites binding on all citizens. Wealthy plebeian families joined the patricians to form a mixed senatorial aristocracy, the nobiles, while "plebeian" ceased to carry legal disability, though it retained social connotations.

Later usage

In the late Republic and Empire, plebs urbana referred to the city populace of Rome, maintained in part by the grain dole (annona) and famously characterised by Juvenal as caring only for "bread and circuses" (panem et circenses). In modern English, "pleb" has been revived as a class-tinged insult; in 2012 the British "Plebgate" affair, involving a minister accused of calling police officers plebs, became a national scandal.
