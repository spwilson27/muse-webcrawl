Chevrolet (colloquially known as Chevy) is an American automobile division of General Motors (GM). Founded in 1911 by Swiss racing driver Louis Chevrolet and ousted GM founder William C. Durant, the brand was acquired by GM in 1918 and positioned as its volume leader, competing directly with Ford.

History

The first Chevrolet, the Series C Classic Six, appeared in 1912. Durant used Chevrolet's profits and stock to regain control of GM, merging the companies in 1918. Under leaders such as Alfred P. Sloan, Chevrolet overtook Ford as America's best-selling make in the late 1920s and held the position for much of the century, with landmark models including the Suburban (1935), Bel Air, Corvette (1953), Impala, Camaro (1966) and the Silverado pickup line.

Products and markets

Chevrolet sells a full range of cars, crossovers, trucks, and performance vehicles in over 100 markets, though it withdrew from Europe in 2016 and several other regions during GM's restructuring. The brand has been central to GM's electrification, from the Volt plug-in hybrid and Bolt EV to the electric Silverado and Equinox.

In culture

Chevrolet is deeply embedded in American popular culture, referenced in hundreds of songs, and its "bowtie" emblem, introduced in 1913, is among the most recognised automotive logos in the world.
