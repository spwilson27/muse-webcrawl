Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation

The Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation (IPO) is a long-period oscillation of sea surface temperatures in the Pacific Ocean that can last from 20 to 30 years in each phase. It resembles a long-lived El Niño-like pattern of Pacific climate variability, with a positive phase marked by warmer-than-average waters in the tropical eastern Pacific and cooler waters in the north and south Pacific.

Effects

The IPO modulates the frequency and strength of El Niño and La Niña events and influences rainfall and temperature around the Pacific rim, including drought patterns in Australia and the southwestern United States, and the rate of global surface warming. Phases identified in the instrumental record include a positive phase from about 1922 to 1944, a negative phase from 1945 to 1977, and a positive phase from 1978 to about 1998. The IPO is closely related to — and by some measures nearly indistinguishable from — the Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO), which is defined over the North Pacific.
