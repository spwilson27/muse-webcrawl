Tariq ibn Ziyad

Ṭāriq ibn Ziyād (Arabic: طارق بن زياد; c. 670 – c. 720) was an Umayyad commander, probably of Berber origin, who initiated the Muslim conquest of Visigothic Hispania (present-day Spain and Portugal) in 711. Gibraltar, where his forces landed, is named after him: Jabal Ṭāriq, "the mountain of Tariq".

Background

Tariq served under Musa ibn Nusayr, the Umayyad governor of Ifriqiya (North Africa), and was appointed governor of Tangier after its capture. In 711, reportedly invited by dissident Visigothic factions or by Count Julian of Ceuta, Tariq crossed the strait with an army of some 7,000–12,000 men, mostly Berbers, landing at Gibraltar in the spring of 711.

Conquest of Hispania

At the Battle of Guadalete (July 711), Tariq's army destroyed the Visigothic host and King Roderic was killed. Tariq then advanced rapidly — taking Córdoba, and Toledo, the Visigothic capital — going beyond Musa's instructions. Musa crossed with a larger army in 712, and together they subdued most of the peninsula within a few years, founding al-Andalus. Later legend has Tariq burn his ships after landing to commit his men to victory, though the story is not in early sources.

Later life

In 714 both Tariq and Musa were recalled to Damascus by the caliph al-Walid I. Musa was disgraced and fined; Tariq's later fate is obscure, and he is reported to have died in Damascus around 720. His name endures in Gibraltar and in the Arabic word ṭarīq (path) associations of popular etymology.
