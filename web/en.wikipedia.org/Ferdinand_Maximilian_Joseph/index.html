Ferdinand Maximilian Joseph

Maximilian I of Mexico

Ferdinand Maximilian Joseph Maria (6 July 1832 – 19 June 1867) was an Austrian archduke who became emperor of Mexico as Maximilian I, reigning from 1864 until his execution in 1867. A younger brother of Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria, he had a distinguished career in the Austrian navy and served as governor-general of Lombardy–Venetia.

Emperor of Mexico

After French forces intervened in Mexico, Napoleon III offered Maximilian the Mexican throne. He accepted in 1864 and arrived with his wife, Carlota of Belgium, establishing the Second Mexican Empire. Despite liberal intentions, his regime depended on French troops, and he alienated conservatives by upholding many of the liberal reforms of Benito Juárez.

Downfall and execution

When France withdrew its forces under pressure from the United States, Maximilian refused to abdicate. He was captured by republican forces at Querétaro and, despite international appeals for clemency, was executed by firing squad on 19 June 1867 alongside generals Miguel Miramón and Tomás Mejía. The scene was famously painted by Édouard Manet.
