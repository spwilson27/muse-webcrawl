Ndola

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ndola is the third largest city in Zambia and the capital of its Copperbelt Province, with a population of over 450,000. It is the commercial and industrial centre of the Copperbelt, connected by road and rail to Lusaka, Kitwe, and the Congolese border to the north.

History

Ndola began as a trading post on an Arab slave-trading route; the town was founded in 1904 near the ancient fig tree known as the Slave Tree (Mukuyu), under which traders held auctions. It grew with the copper industry in the 1920s and 1930s and became a city in 1967. In September 1961 a plane carrying UN Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld crashed on approach to Ndola airport, killing all aboard — an event still the subject of investigation and speculation.

Economy

Copper refining, cement, lime, and oil refining (the Indeni refinery) anchor the economy, along with Zambia's only oil pipeline terminus from Dar es Salaam. The city hosts the Zambia International Trade Fair and a campus of the Copperbelt University. See also Copperbelt and Zambia.
