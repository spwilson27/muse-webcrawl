Blend modes

Blend modes
Used inDigital image editing, compositing
Common modesNormal, Multiply, Screen, Overlay, Difference
InputsBase color, blend color
OutputResult color, per channel

Blend modes (alternatively blending modes or mixing modes) in digital image editing and computer graphics are used to determine how two layers are blended with each other. Most graphics applications, including Adobe Photoshop and GIMP, allow users to modify the basic blend modes — for example by applying different levels of opacity — to produce a wide variety of effects.

Common modes

Multiply multiplies the base and blend colors, always producing a darker result, useful for shadows and darkening. Screen is its inverse, producing a lighter result. Overlay combines the two, multiplying dark areas and screening light ones, increasing contrast. Difference subtracts the colors and inverts, so identical layers yield black. Soft light and hard light give gentler or stronger contrast effects, while modes such as hue, saturation, color, and luminosity blend only selected components of the colors.

Mathematics

Blend modes are typically applied per RGB channel with color values normalized to the range 0 to 1. For example, multiply is simply result = base × blend, while screen is result = 1 − (1 − base)(1 − blend). Separable modes apply independently per channel; non-separable modes operate on hue/saturation/lightness representations.
