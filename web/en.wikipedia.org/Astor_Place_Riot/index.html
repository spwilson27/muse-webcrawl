Astor Place Riot

DateMay 10, 1849
LocationAstor Opera House, Astor Place, New York City
CauseRivalry between actors Edwin Forrest and William Charles Macready; class and nationalist tensions
DeathsAt least 22–25

The Astor Place Riot occurred on May 10, 1849, outside the Astor Opera House in Manhattan, New York City. A crowd of working-class supporters of the American actor Edwin Forrest attacked the theatre during a performance of Macbeth by his English rival, William Charles Macready. When the mob could not be dispersed, the state militia fired into the crowd, killing more than twenty people — among the deadliest civil disturbances in American history to that date.

Background

Forrest and Macready were the leading Shakespearean tragedians of their respective nations, and their rivalry had become a proxy for Anglo-American antagonism and New York class resentment: Forrest was the idol of the Bowery's working-class "b'hoys", while the fashionable Astor Opera House, with its dress code, catered to the elite. Tensions boiled over after Forrest was hissed during a London tour and Macready was pelted with objects at an earlier New York performance on May 7.

The riot and aftermath

On May 10 a crowd estimated in the tens of thousands gathered outside the theatre, throwing paving stones and attempting to storm the building. Called out by the authorities, militia troops eventually fired volleys over the crowd and then into it; at least 22 people were killed and more than 100 injured on all sides. The riot deepened the cultural separation between popular and elite theatre in America, hastening the split between "legitimate" theatre and working-class entertainment forms such as minstrelsy and vaudeville.
