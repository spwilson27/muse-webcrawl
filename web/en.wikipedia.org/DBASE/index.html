dBase

dBase was one of the first widely used database management systems for microcomputers, and the line of products and programming languages descended from it. Originally released in 1980 by Ashton-Tate as dBASE II for CP/M, it became a dominant standard on the IBM PC during the 1980s.

History

dBase grew out of a system called Vulcan, written by Wayne Ratliff, who marketed it himself before Ashton-Tate took it over and renamed it. dBASE II, and especially dBASE III (1984) and dBASE IV (1988), made Ashton-Tate one of the "big three" PC software companies alongside Microsoft and Lotus. The buggy, delayed release of dBASE IV damaged the company's fortunes, and Borland acquired Ashton-Tate in 1991.

The language and file formats

dBase combined a simple interactive "dot prompt" with a procedural programming language (often called xBase) for building complete applications. Its table file format, .dbf, and memo and index formats were so widely copied that they outlived the product: FoxPro, Clipper and other "xBase" clones built on them, and .dbf files remain in use, for example in GIS shapefiles.

Legacy

Versions of dBase continued under various owners long after its peak, and its design influenced generations of desktop database tools. The xBase family demonstrated that non-specialists could build data-driven business applications on personal computers.
