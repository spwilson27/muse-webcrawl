Torticollis

Torticollis
Also known as: Wry neck
Specialty: Neurology, orthopaedics, paediatrics
Symptoms: Head tilted and rotated to one side
Treatment: Physiotherapy, stretching, botulinum toxin, surgery

Torticollis, also known as wry neck, is a condition in which the head is tilted to one side and the chin rotated toward the opposite side, caused by shortening or spasm of the sternocleidomastoid muscle (SCM). The name derives from the Latin tortus ("twisted") and collum ("neck").

Types and causes

Congenital muscular torticollis is present at or shortly after birth, often associated with a fibrous mass (a "pseudotumour") in the SCM and with intrauterine positioning or birth trauma; it affects roughly 0.3–2% of newborns and is more common on the right side. Acquired torticollis in older children and adults may arise from infection, trauma, atlantoaxial subluxation, tumours or drug reactions. In adults, spasmodic torticollis (cervical dystonia) is a focal dystonia causing involuntary neck-muscle contractions.

Treatment

Most cases of congenital torticollis resolve with early physiotherapy and a programme of stretching and positioning; surgery to release the SCM is reserved for resistant cases. Cervical dystonia is commonly managed with botulinum toxin injections into the affected muscles.
