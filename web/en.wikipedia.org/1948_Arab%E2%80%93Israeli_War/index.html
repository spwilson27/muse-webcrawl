The 1948 Arab–Israeli War, also known as the First Arab–Israeli War, was the second phase of the 1947–1949 Palestine war. It began on 15 May 1948, the day after the Israeli Declaration of Independence and the termination of the British Mandate, when the armies of Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon, and Iraq invaded the territory of the former Mandate.

Course of the war

The war was fought in three main rounds punctuated by UN-brokered truces in June–July and July–October 1948. Arab forces initially advanced in the south and contested Jerusalem, where Jordan's Arab Legion captured the Old City's Jewish Quarter. Israel consolidated its forces into the Israel Defense Forces, absorbed arms shipments (notably from Czechoslovakia), and from October 1948 launched offensives — Operations Yoav, Hiram, and Horev — that secured the Negev and Galilee.

Aftermath

Armistice agreements signed at Rhodes in 1949 established the Green Line boundaries. The war resulted in the flight and expulsion of over 700,000 Palestinian Arabs and, in its aftermath, the departure of Jewish communities from Arab countries. No Palestinian state was created; the West Bank came under Jordanian rule and Gaza under Egyptian administration.
