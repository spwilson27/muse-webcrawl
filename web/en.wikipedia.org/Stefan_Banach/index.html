Stefan Banach

Stefan Banach (30 March 1892 – 31 August 1945) was a Polish mathematician who is generally regarded as one of the most important and influential mathematicians of the 20th century. He was the founder of modern functional analysis and a leading figure of the Lwów School of Mathematics. His best-known work is his 1932 book Théorie des opérations linéaires (Theory of Linear Operations).

Life

Born in Kraków, Banach was largely self-taught in mathematics. His talent was recognised by Hugo Steinhaus after the two met by chance in 1916 — an encounter Steinhaus later called the greatest discovery of his life. Banach became a professor at the Jan Kazimierz University in Lwów, where he and his colleagues famously worked in the Scottish Café, writing problems and solutions in a thick notebook — the "Scottish Book." During the Second World War he survived the Nazi occupation partly by working as a lice-feeder at a typhus research institute. He died of lung cancer in Lwów in August 1945, shortly after the war's end.

Mathematical work

Banach's doctoral thesis of 1922 laid the foundations of functional analysis. He introduced complete normed vector spaces — now called Banach spaces — and proved several cornerstone results: the Hahn–Banach theorem (extension of linear functionals), the Banach–Steinhaus theorem (uniform boundedness principle), the open mapping theorem and the closed graph theorem. With Alfred Tarski he proved the Banach–Tarski paradox (1924), showing that a ball can be decomposed into finitely many pieces and reassembled into two balls of the same size, using the axiom of choice. He also contributed the Banach fixed-point theorem, foundational to the theory of differential equations and iterative methods.

Legacy

The Stefan Banach International Mathematical Center in Warsaw bears his name, as do numerous theorems, spaces and the Banach Medal of the Polish Academy of Sciences. Stanislaw Ulam, one of his students, said that Banach's influence on modern analysis was exceeded by no one in his generation.
