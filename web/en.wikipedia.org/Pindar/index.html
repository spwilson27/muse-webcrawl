Pindar

Pindar (c. 518 – c. 438 BC) was an Ancient Greek lyric poet from Thebes. Of the canonical nine lyric poets of Hellenistic Alexandria, his work is the best preserved, and he was held in the highest regard: Quintilian called him "by far the greatest of the nine lyric poets". His poetry, written in an artificial literary dialect based on Doric, celebrates athletic victories, gods, and heroes.

Victory odes

Pindar's surviving works are almost entirely epinicia—victory odes composed for victors at the Panhellenic games (Olympic, Pythian, Nemean, and Isthmian), commissioned by athletes and their cities across the Greek world, including tyrants of Sicily such as Hieron of Syracuse. The odes weave praise of the victor with myth, gnomic moralizing, and prayers, in intricate triadic structures of strophe, antistrophe, and epode, performed by a chorus.

His poetry fell out of fashion in the Hellenistic and Roman periods as difficult, but Horace admired and imitated him, and his reputation revived in the Renaissance. The Pindaric ode influenced English poets from Ben Jonson to Wordsworth and Tennyson.
