The Old Swiss Confederacy (German: Alte Eidgenossenschaft) was a loose confederation of independent small states — initially rural communes and later also city states — in the Alpine valleys and Swiss Plateau, existing from the late 13th century until the French invasion of 1798. It began, according to tradition, with the Federal Charter of 1291 in which Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden pledged mutual defence, and grew by alliance and conquest to thirteen cantons plus associates and shared subject territories.

Formation and growth

The founding pact of early August 1291 was a defensive alliance among valley communities amid the uncertainty following the death of Rudolf I of Habsburg; later legend placed the oath on the Rütli meadow and associated it with the hero William Tell. Victory over Habsburg forces at Morgarten (1315) secured the confederation's survival, and Lucerne (1332), Zürich (1351), Glarus, Zug and Bern (1353) joined to form the Confederacy of Eight Cantons. Further Habsburg defeats at Sempach (1386) and Näfels (1388), and victory over Burgundy in the Burgundian Wars (1474–1477), made Swiss infantry the most feared in Europe and Swiss soldiers prized mercenaries.

The Thirteen Cantons

Fribourg and Solothurn joined in 1481, Basel and Schaffhausen in 1501, and Appenzell in 1513, completing the Thirteen Cantons. The Swabian War of 1499 ended with the Peace of Basel, exempting the Confederacy in practice from imperial taxes and jurisdiction — a de facto independence formally recognised in the Peace of Westphalia (1648). Conquered territories such as Aargau, Thurgau and Ticino were governed jointly as condominiums (subject bailiwicks).

Government and society

There was no capital, common constitution or joint citizenship: the Tagsatzung, an assembly of cantonal envoys, coordinated war, foreign affairs and the subject territories. Cantons differed in regime — patrician city republics such as Bern and Zürich, and rural Landsgemeinde democracies in the forest cantons. The Reformation split the confederation into Catholic and Protestant blocs that fought the Wars of Kappel (1529, 1531, in which Zwingli was killed) and the Villmergen Wars (1656, 1712), yet the alliance survived through compromise.

End

In 1798 French revolutionary armies invaded, and the confederacy collapsed into the unitary Helvetic Republic. After Napoleon's Act of Mediation (1803) restored a confederation of cantons, the Congress of Vienna (1815) confirmed Swiss neutrality and borders; the modern federal state emerged with the constitution of 1848.
