Laws of the Game (association football)

The Laws of the Game are the codified rules of association football (soccer). They are maintained by the International Football Association Board (IFAB), which was founded in 1886 by the football associations of England, Scotland, Wales, and Ireland; FIFA joined in 1913. The Laws consist of seventeen individual laws governing everything from the field of play to fouls, offside, and the method of scoring.

History

The first unified laws were drawn up by the Football Association in London in 1863, under the leadership of Ebenezer Cobb Morley. Before standardisation, different schools and clubs played by their own rules, such as the Sheffield Rules. The IFAB was formed in 1886 to take control of the Laws, and it remains their custodian; changes require a three-quarters majority at the Board's annual general meeting, with FIFA holding four votes and each of the four British associations one.

The seventeen laws

  1. The Field of Play
  2. The Ball
  3. The Players
  4. The Players' Equipment
  5. The Referee
  6. The Other Match Officials
  7. The Duration of the Match
  8. The Start and Restart of Play
  9. The Ball In and Out of Play
  10. Determining the Outcome of a Match
  11. Offside
  12. Fouls and Misconduct
  13. Free Kicks
  14. The Penalty Kick
  15. The Throw-in
  16. The Goal Kick
  17. The Corner Kick

Application

The Laws are applied at all levels of the game worldwide, with limited modifications permitted for youth, veterans', disability, and grassroots football. Law 5 gives the referee full authority to enforce the Laws, and Law 11 defines the offside offence, one of the most debated aspects of the sport. Major modern changes include the introduction of the video assistant referee (VAR) protocol in 2018 and revisions to the handball and kick-off laws.
