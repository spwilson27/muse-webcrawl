Supply curve

A supply curve is a graph of the relationship between the price of a good or service and the quantity supplied for a given period of time. In a typical representation, price appears on the left vertical axis and quantity supplied on the horizontal axis. Because higher prices generally induce producers to supply more, the curve usually slopes upward from left to right.

Movements and shifts

Economists distinguish a change in quantity supplied — a movement along a fixed curve caused by a change in the good's price — from a change in supply, in which the entire curve shifts. Shifts result from changes in production costs, technology, prices of related goods, expectations, taxes or subsidies, and the number of sellers. A rightward shift represents an increase in supply at every price.

Forms and derivation

For a competitive firm, the short-run supply curve is the portion of the marginal cost curve above average variable cost; market supply aggregates firms' curves horizontally. Supply may be vertical (perfectly inelastic), as with a fixed stock of land, or horizontal in long-run constant-cost industries. The elasticity of supply varies along the curve and with the time horizon considered.
