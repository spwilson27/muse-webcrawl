Bra–ket notation

FieldQuantum mechanics
Introduced byPaul Dirac
Year1939

Bra–ket notation, also called Dirac notation, is a standard notation in quantum mechanics for describing quantum states as vectors in a complex Hilbert space. It was introduced by Paul Dirac in 1939 and is used throughout physics and quantum chemistry.

Kets and bras

A vector is written as a ket, |ψ⟩, representing a state of a quantum system. Its dual (conjugate transpose) is a bra, ⟨ψ|. The inner product of two states is then the "bracket" ⟨φ|ψ⟩, a complex number whose squared modulus gives the probability of finding the system in one state when prepared in the other.

Operators and properties

Linear operators act on kets to produce new kets, and expectation values take the form ⟨ψ|A|ψ⟩. Outer products |ψ⟩⟨φ| form operators, enabling elegant expressions such as completeness relations and projection operators. The notation's algebraic convenience made Dirac's formulation of quantum mechanics especially transparent and remains the default language of the field.
