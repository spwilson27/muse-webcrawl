Cytokine storm

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A cytokine storm, also called cytokine release syndrome (CRS) or hypercytokinaemia, is a severe immune reaction in which the body releases too many cytokines into the blood too quickly. In a cytokine storm the immune system's positive feedback loop between cytokines and immune cells spirals out of control, causing high fever, redness, swelling, extreme fatigue and nausea, and potentially progressing to multiple organ failure and death.

Causes

Cytokine storms can be triggered by severe infections — influenza (including the 1918 pandemic strain and H5N1 avian influenza), sepsis, Ebola and SARS-CoV-2 — as well as by acute graft-versus-host disease and immunotherapies such as CAR T-cell therapy and certain monoclonal antibodies. The term was popularised after the 2006 Northwick Park trial of the antibody TGN1412, in which six healthy volunteers suffered life-threatening cytokine release.

Mechanism and symptoms

Activated immune cells release large quantities of mediators including interleukin-6, interleukin-1, tumour necrosis factor alpha and interferon gamma. These drive systemic inflammation: capillary leak, hypotension, coagulopathy, acute respiratory distress syndrome and organ dysfunction. Symptoms range from mild flu-like illness to high fever, confusion, seizures and cardiovascular collapse.

Treatment

Management is primarily supportive — oxygen, fluids, vasopressors and organ support — combined with immunosuppression targeted at the storm itself: corticosteroids, the IL-6 receptor blocker tocilizumab, and the IL-1 antagonist anakinra are used, including in severe COVID-19, where cytokine-driven inflammation was a major cause of death.
