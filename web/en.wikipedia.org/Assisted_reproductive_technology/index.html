Assisted reproductive technology

Assisted reproductive technology (ART) includes medical methods used primarily to address infertility. It covers procedures involving the handling of eggs, sperm, or embryos outside or inside the body, most notably in vitro fertilisation (IVF) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI).

ICSI: a single sperm is injected directly into an egg.

Techniques

Major techniques include IVF, in which eggs are fertilised in a laboratory dish and resulting embryos transferred to the uterus; ICSI, used chiefly for severe male-factor infertility; intrauterine insemination (IUI); controlled ovarian stimulation; donor eggs, sperm, and embryos; gestational surrogacy; and preimplantation genetic testing of embryos. Cryopreservation allows eggs, sperm, and embryos to be stored for later use.

History

The field began with the birth of Louise Brown in 1978, the first child conceived by IVF, work for which Robert Edwards later received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Since then, millions of children have been born through ART worldwide.

Regulation and ethics

ART raises ethical and legal questions concerning embryo status, multiple pregnancy, access and cost, and the welfare of donor-conceived people. Regulation varies widely by country, and success rates depend heavily on age and diagnosis, with live-birth rates per IVF cycle declining sharply after age 40 using a patient's own eggs.
