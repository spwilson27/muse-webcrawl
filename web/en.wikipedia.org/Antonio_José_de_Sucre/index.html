Antonio José de Sucre y Alcalá (3 February 1795 – 4 June 1830), known as the "Gran Mariscal de Ayacucho," was a Venezuelan general and statesman who led the independence armies of South America to decisive victories and served as president of Peru and of Bolivia. He was one of Simón Bolívar's closest friends and most trusted lieutenants.

Independence campaigns

Born in Cumaná into a family of military tradition, Sucre joined the patriot cause as a teenager. After distinguished service in Venezuela, he became Bolívar's chief of staff and negotiated the Armistice of Santa Ana (1820) with the Spanish general Pablo Morillo, which regularised the conduct of the war. Sent south, Sucre liberated Quito with his victory at the Battle of Pichincha (24 May 1822) and annexed the region to Gran Colombia. In Peru, his triumph over the main royalist army at the Battle of Ayacucho (9 December 1824) effectively ended Spanish rule in mainland South America and earned him the title Grand Marshal of Ayacucho.

Bolivia and death

Sucre convened the assembly that declared Upper Peru independent as Bolivia (1825) and became its second president, pursuing liberal reforms in education, church property and public finance. Wounded in a mutiny in 1828, he resigned and returned to Ecuador. He presided over the "Admirable Congress" in Bogotá in 1830 and opposed the dissolution of Gran Colombia. On 4 June 1830 he was assassinated in the mountains of Berruecos near Pasto, a crime widely attributed to political enemies of unionism. The Bolivian capital city of Sucre and the former Ecuadorian currency are named after him.
