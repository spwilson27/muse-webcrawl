Home counties

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The counties commonly reckoned among the home counties surround London.

The home counties are the counties of England that surround London. The term has no exact geographical definition, but the counties generally considered to be in the group are Berkshire, Buckinghamshire, Essex, Hertfordshire, Kent, Surrey and Sussex (historically one county, now East Sussex and West Sussex). Middlesex, the historic county largely absorbed into Greater London in 1965, was also historically a home county. Hampshire, Bedfordshire, Oxfordshire and others are sometimes included.

Usage and character

The term originated in the sixteenth century and became common in the nineteenth, when wealthy Londoners built country houses in the rural ring around the capital. Since the coming of the railways, and especially with the growth of "Metroland" suburbs in the early twentieth century, the home counties have been associated with commuter towns, stockbroker belts, prosperous middle-class life, and the "home counties" accent — a form of Received Pronunciation. The area is among the most affluent in the United Kingdom and forms much of the London commuter belt within the South East region.

"Home Counties" is also used administratively in various contexts — for example historically in the military (the Home Counties Brigade), in policing and in cricket — and colloquially to denote respectable suburban England. Definitions vary: government usage has sometimes listed only the counties directly bordering London, while cultural usage is broader.
