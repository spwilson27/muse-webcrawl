Modus operandi

Identifying an offender's characteristic method helps link related crimes

A modus operandi (often abbreviated MO; Latin for "mode of operation") is someone's habits of working, particularly in the context of business or criminal investigations. In criminology it describes the methods, techniques, and habits a particular offender uses to commit a crime — how the victim is approached, how the scene is entered, what is taken, and how the offender escapes. Because an MO is learned behaviour shaped by what works, it tends to remain recognisably consistent across an offender's crimes while gradually evolving with experience.

In criminal investigation

Recording MOs allows investigators to link crimes in a series and to search databases of known offenders for similar methods; systems such as the FBI's Violent Criminal Apprehension Program (ViCAP) were built for this purpose. Analysts distinguish the MO — the practical behaviours necessary to commit the crime and avoid capture — from the offender's "signature", expressive or ritualistic behaviours that satisfy a psychological need and are therefore more stable over time.

Other uses

Outside criminology the phrase is used loosely for any characteristic way of operating: the modus operandi of a company, a negotiator, or a political campaign. The plural is modi operandi.
