Ghadir Khumm

The Ghadir Khumm (Arabic: غَدِير خُمّ) was a gathering of Muslims addressed by the Islamic prophet Muhammad on 16 March 632 CE (18 Dhu al-Hijjah 10 AH) at a pond (ghadir) near al-Juhfa in present-day Saudi Arabia, on the return journey from Muhammad's Farewell Pilgrimage to Mecca. There, according to the widely transmitted hadith of Ghadir Khumm, Muhammad took Ali ibn Abi Talib by the hand and declared: "Whoever I am his mawla, this Ali is his mawla."

The meaning of the Arabic word mawla — which can signify master, patron, leader, or friend/ally — is the crux of a major division in Islam. Shia Muslims regard the statement as an explicit designation of Ali as Muhammad's successor; Sunni Muslims interpret it as an affirmation of Ali's closeness and merit rather than an appointment to leadership.

The event

Reports in both Sunni and Shia sources state that Muhammad halted the caravan at the pond of Khumm, had a pulpit of saddles erected, and addressed the assembled pilgrims. In addition to the mawla declaration, accounts mention the hadith of the thaqalayn (the two weighty things: the Quran and Muhammad's household) and congratulatory remarks by Umar ibn al-Khattab to Ali. Shia sources add that the Quranic verses 5:3 and 5:67 were connected with the occasion.

Interpretations

For Shia Islam, Ghadir Khumm is the clearest public investiture of Ali and grounds the doctrine of the Imamate; the day is celebrated as Eid al-Ghadir, one of the most important Shia festivals. Sunni scholarship generally accepts the authenticity of the hadith while reading mawla as "friend" or "beloved", and points to the context — reportedly complaints against Ali from a detachment returning from Yemen — as the reason for Muhammad's public defence of him.
