Snowboarding is a recreational and competitive winter sport that involves descending a snow-covered surface while standing on a snowboard that is attached to a rider's feet. It was developed in the United States in the 1960s, drawing inspiration from skiing, sledding, surfing, and skateboarding.

History

In 1965, engineer Sherman Poppen of Muskegon, Michigan, fastened two skis together and attached a rope for control, creating the "Snurfer" as a toy for his daughter. Jake Burton Carpenter founded Burton Snowboards in 1977 and worked to legitimise the sport at ski resorts, while Tom Sims and Dimitrije Milovich also developed early boards. Snowboarding debuted at the Winter Olympic Games at Nagano in 1998.

Styles

Major styles include freeriding (off-piste riding in natural terrain), freestyle (tricks in terrain parks and halfpipes), and alpine or carving (gated racing on groomed runs). Competitive disciplines include halfpipe, slopestyle, big air, snowboard cross (boardercross), and parallel giant slalom.

Equipment

A snowboard is a flat board, typically of wood-core composite construction, ridden with both feet strapped into bindings in a sideways stance. Riders wear soft boots for freestyle and freeriding, or hard boots with plate bindings for alpine racing. Protective gear includes helmets, wrist guards, and padded clothing.
