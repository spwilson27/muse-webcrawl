Robert Bristow

Sir Robert Bristow (1880–1966) was a British harbour engineer best known for transforming the port of Cochin (Kochi) on India's Malabar Coast. Appointed harbour engineer to the Government of Madras in 1920, he oversaw the dredging of the Cochin channel and the creation of Willingdon Island from the dredged material.

Cochin port works

Bristow's bold scheme cut a deep channel through the sandbar at the harbour mouth and used the spoil to build the artificial Willingdon Island, on which port facilities, the Cochin Port Trust offices, and later an airport and naval base were developed. The work, completed in stages through the 1920s and 1930s, turned Cochin into a major all-weather port, earning it the title "Queen of the Arabian Sea".

Later life

Bristow wrote about his experiences in Cochin Saga (1949), an account of the port's development and of the region's history. He was knighted for his services and remains a celebrated figure in Kochi, where a road and the Bristow Hotel site recall his name.
