Trout is the generic name for a number of species of freshwater fish belonging to the genera Oncorhynchus, Salmo and Salvelinus, all of the salmon family Salmonidae. The word is also used for the sea-run form of some species, such as steelhead (anadromous rainbow trout).

Species and distribution

Well-known species include the brown trout (Salmo trutta), native to Europe and widely introduced worldwide; the rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) of North America; brook trout and lake trout (Salvelinus), technically chars; and golden trout of California mountain streams. Trout generally live in cool, well-oxygenated water.

As food and sport

Trout are popular game fish, prized by fly anglers, and important in aquaculture; rainbow trout are farmed extensively for food. Their flesh ranges from white to deep orange-red depending on diet.
