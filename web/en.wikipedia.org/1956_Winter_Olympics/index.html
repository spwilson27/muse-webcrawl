The 1956 Winter Olympics, officially the VII Olympic Winter Games, were held from 26 January to 5 February 1956 in Cortina d'Ampezzo, Italy. Cortina, which had been awarded the 1944 Games that were cancelled because of the Second World War, hosted the first Winter Olympics to be televised to a broad international audience.

Highlights

The Games marked the Winter Olympic debut of the Soviet Union, which immediately topped the medal table with seven gold medals, including a victory in ice hockey that ended Canadian dominance of the event.

Austrian alpine skier Toni Sailer became the star of the Games by winning all three men's alpine skiing events — downhill, slalom, and giant slalom. In figure skating, Americans Tenley Albright and Hayes Alan Jenkins won the singles titles.

Organisation

The Cortina Games were compact, with most venues within a few kilometres of the town. The Olympic flame was lit at the Capitol in Rome and carried to Cortina. Skater Guido Caroli, carrying the torch into the stadium, famously tripped over a television cable but kept the flame alight.

The next Winter Games were awarded to Squaw Valley in the United States for 1960.
