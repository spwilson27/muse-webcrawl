Breathless (French: À bout de souffle, "out of breath") is a 1960 French crime drama film written and directed by Jean-Luc Godard, in his feature directorial debut. It stars Jean-Paul Belmondo as Michel Poiccard, a small-time criminal modeled on Humphrey Bogart characters, and Jean Seberg as Patricia Franchini, an American journalism student in Paris. The film is regarded as one of the defining works of the French New Wave (Nouvelle Vague) and one of the most influential films ever made.

Plot

Michel, a car thief on the run after shooting a policeman, hides out in Paris and tries to persuade Patricia to flee with him to Italy. She hesitates, torn between attraction and doubt, and eventually informs the police of his whereabouts; Michel, resigned, refuses to run and is shot in the street, dying with a final ambiguous exchange between the two.

Style and production

Made cheaply and quickly — shot in about four weeks in the summer of 1959, largely on location in Paris with a handheld camera and natural light — the film broke with classical continuity conventions. Its most famous innovation is the extensive use of jump cuts, partly a practical solution when the distributor required a shorter running time: Godard cut within scenes rather than removing whole sequences. The film also features direct address to the camera, improvised dialogue, and a jazz score by Martial Solal. Godard dedicated the film to Monogram Pictures, a Hollywood B-movie studio, signaling its dialogue with American genre cinema.

Reception and legacy

Breathless was a commercial success in France and won Godard the Silver Bear for Best Director at the 1960 Berlin International Film Festival. Together with Truffaut's The 400 Blows and Resnais' Hiroshima mon amour (both 1959), it launched the New Wave internationally; its informal technique and youthful energy influenced generations of filmmakers. An American remake, Breathless (1983), starred Richard Gere.
