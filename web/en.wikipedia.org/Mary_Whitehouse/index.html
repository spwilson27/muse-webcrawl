Mary Whitehouse

Constance Mary Whitehouse CBE
Born: 13 June 1910, Nuneaton, Warwickshire, England
Died: 23 November 2001 (aged 91), Colchester, Essex
Occupation: Campaigner, teacher
Known for: Clean-Up TV campaign; National Viewers' and Listeners' Association

Constance Mary Whitehouse CBE (née Hutcheson; 13 June 1910 – 23 November 2001) was a British teacher and conservative activist. She became the best-known campaigner against what she saw as the permissive society and declining moral standards in British television, founding the Clean-Up TV campaign in 1964 and the National Viewers' and Listeners' Association (later Mediawatch-UK) in 1965, which she led for nearly three decades.

Career and campaigns

Whitehouse taught art and sex education in schools, experiences she later said convinced her that young people were being harmed by permissive media. Her Clean-Up TV petition gathered hundreds of thousands of signatures, and under her leadership the NVALA monitored broadcasts, lobbied Parliament and pressured the BBC. She campaigned against swearing, sex and violence on screen, targeting programmes such as Doctor Who and the comedy of Dave Allen, and she met several prime ministers.

Blasphemy case and later influence

In 1977 Whitehouse brought a private prosecution for blasphemous libel against Gay News over a poem by James Kirkup depicting Christ — the first successful blasphemy prosecution in Britain in over half a century. She was appointed CBE in 1980. Though frequently mocked by comedians, Whitehouse significantly influenced British broadcasting regulation and public debate about taste and decency; the Mary Whitehouse Experience radio and television comedy shows of the early 1990s took their name ironically from her fame. She retired from the renamed Mediawatch-UK in 1994 and died in 2001.
