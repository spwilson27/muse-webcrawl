4711 is a traditional German eau de cologne produced in Cologne since the late 18th century. It is one of the best-known classic cologne water brands and remains an iconic product of the company Mäurer & Wirtz.

History

The name derives from the house number 4711 at Glockengasse in Cologne, assigned during the French occupation of the city in the 1790s when houses were numbered consecutively. The merchant Wilhelm Mülhens received the recipe for a miracle water as a wedding gift, according to company legend, and marketed it from the Glockengasse address. The Farina family of Cologne disputed the right to the name "Eau de Cologne," and after decades of litigation the brand was sold under the number 4711 instead.

Composition

4711 Original Eau de Cologne is a citrus-based fragrance built on notes of lemon, bergamot, and orange, with lavender, rosemary, and neroli. Its recipe has remained largely unchanged for over two centuries, and its distinctive turquoise-and-gold label and Bremer bottle shape are recognized worldwide.

Cultural significance

The flagship store at Glockengasse 4 in Cologne is a tourist landmark. 4711 has been referenced in literature and film, and during the Second World War the scent was famously carried by German submariners, while Allied troops also brought bottles home as souvenirs.
