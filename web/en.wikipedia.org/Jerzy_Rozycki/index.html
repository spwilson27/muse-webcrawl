Jerzy Różycki

Jerzy Witold Różycki

Born: 24 July 1909, Olchowiec, Russian Empire

Died: 9 January 1942, Mediterranean Sea

Occupation: Mathematician, cryptologist

Known for: Breaking the Enigma cipher

Jerzy Witold Różycki (24 July 1909 – 9 January 1942) was a Polish mathematician and cryptologist who worked with Marian Rejewski and Henryk Zygalski at the Polish General Staff's Cipher Bureau to break the German Enigma machine cipher in the 1930s.

Cryptological work

Różycki studied mathematics at Poznań University and was recruited to the Cipher Bureau's cryptology course in 1929. With Rejewski and Zygalski he developed methods and machines for attacking Enigma, including the cryptologic bomb and the "grille" and "clock" methods; Różycki devised the clock method, which exploited differences in rotor turnover points to identify which rotor was in the rightmost position. The team's achievements were handed to Britain and France at a meeting in July 1939, weeks before the outbreak of war.

War and death

After the fall of Poland, Różycki worked at the clandestine PC Cadix station near Uzès in southern France. He died in January 1942 when the passenger ship Lamoricière, carrying him back from a posting in Algiers, sank in a storm in the Mediterranean near the Balearic Islands.
