Crown jewels

Crown jewels are the objects of metalwork and jewellery in the regalia of a current or former monarchy. They are often used for the coronation of a monarch and a few other ceremonial occasions. A monarch may often be shown wearing them in portraits, as they symbolise the power and continuity of the monarchy. Additions may be made to crown jewels between reigns, but since ancient times the items for at least the main ceremonies have traditionally been passed down unchanged, and most have been designed to be kept unaltered.

Notable collections

  • The Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom, held at the Tower of London, include St Edward's Crown, the Imperial State Crown (set with the Cullinan II diamond and the Black Prince's Ruby), the Sovereign's Orb, and the Jewelled Sword of Offering. The Stone of Scone was returned to Scotland in 1996 but was used again for the coronation of Charles III in 2023.
  • The Honours of Scotland — crown, sceptre, and sword of state — are displayed at Edinburgh Castle and are among the oldest surviving regalia in Europe.
  • The French Crown Jewels, largely sold off by the Third Republic in 1887, survive in part, including the Regent Diamond, in the Louvre.
  • The Imperial Crown Jewels of Iran, including the Daria-i-Noor diamond and the Pahlavi Crown, are held in the Treasury of National Jewels in Tehran.
  • The Austrian Crown Jewels, including the Imperial Crown of the Holy Roman Empire, are kept in the Imperial Treasury in Vienna.
  • The Crown Jewels of India, such as the Koh-i-Noor diamond (now in the British collection), remain subjects of restitution claims.

Symbolism and custody

Crown jewels typically belong to the state or to the crown as an institution rather than to the monarch personally, and in many countries they are inalienable. They are guarded as national treasures, displayed in treasuries and museums, and used only on state occasions such as coronations and the state opening of parliament. Stolen crown jewels have occasionally made headlines — as with the 2019 Dresden Green Vault heist, in which Saxon royal treasures were stolen, many later recovered.
