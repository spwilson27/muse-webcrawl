The Royal New Zealand Navy (RNZN; Māori: Te Taua Moana o Aotearoa, literally "Sea Warriors of New Zealand") is the maritime arm of the New Zealand Defence Force. The fleet currently consists of nine ships. The Navy had its roots in the Naval Defence Act 1913, and the subsequent purchase of the cruiser HMS Philomel, which by 1921 had been moored in Auckland. These forces operated as a division of the Royal Navy.

The division was made an independent force in 1941 and granted the name Royal New Zealand Navy by King George VI. The Royal New Zealand Navy was involved in the Second World War, where its cruisers HMNZS Achilles and HMNZS Leander saw action, notably at the Battle of the River Plate in December 1939. From 1948 to 1951 the RNZN also took part in the Korean War.

History

The first recorded action of a New Zealand naval force was in the Flagstaff War of 1845–46. Formal naval defence began with the Naval Defence Act 1913, which created the New Zealand Naval Forces as a division of the Royal Navy. The cruiser HMS Philomel was purchased in 1914 and commissioned for New Zealand service. During the First World War these forces participated in operations against German colonies in the Pacific and escorted the New Zealand Expeditionary Force.

On 1 October 1941, the New Zealand Naval Forces became the Royal New Zealand Navy. In the Second World War, New Zealand naval personnel served worldwide; Achilles fought at the River Plate alongside HMS Ajax and HMS Exeter against the German pocket battleship Admiral Graf Spee. After the war, the navy participated in the Korean War, the Malayan Emergency, and later in peacekeeping and fisheries protection duties.

The fleet today

The modern fleet is based at Devonport Naval Base in Auckland. Its principal combatants are the two Anzac-class frigates HMNZS Te Kaha and HMNZS Te Mana. Other vessels include the offshore patrol vessels HMNZS Otago and HMNZS Wellington, the replenishment ship HMNZS Aotearoa (commissioned in 2020, the largest ship ever operated by the RNZN), the multi-role vessel HMNZS Canterbury, and inshore patrol vessels. In 2024 the hydrographic survey ship HMNZS Manawanui was lost after grounding off Samoa; all personnel were safely evacuated.

Personnel and roles

The RNZN maintains a strength of roughly 2,000–2,500 regular personnel plus reserves. Its roles include maritime security, search and rescue support, hydrographic survey, explosive ordnance disposal, and support to other government agencies in New Zealand's exclusive economic zone, one of the largest in the world.
