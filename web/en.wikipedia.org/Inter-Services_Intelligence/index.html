The Inter-Services Intelligence (Urdu: بین الخدماتی استخبارات), commonly known as the ISI, is the premier intelligence agency of Pakistan, responsible for gathering, processing and analysing national security information. It was established in 1948, shortly after the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947 exposed weaknesses in intelligence coordination.

History

The ISI was founded by Major-General Walter Cawthorn, a British officer serving in the Pakistan Army. It expanded greatly during the Soviet–Afghan War of the 1980s, when it channelled support to the Afghan mujahideen in cooperation with the United States and Saudi Arabia.

Organisation

The agency is headed by a Director-General, typically a serving three-star general of the Pakistan Army, and is headquartered in Islamabad. Its directorates cover political, external, counter-intelligence and technical matters.
