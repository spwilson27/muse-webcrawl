Coffee culture of Australia and New Zealand

The coffee culture of Australia and New Zealand is characterised by a strong café tradition and a preference for espresso-based drinks, most famously the flat white — a shot of espresso with steamed milk and a thin layer of microfoam — whose invention is disputed between Sydney (Moors Espresso Bar, 1985, Alan Preston) and Wellington (Derek Townsend and Darrell Ahlers).

History

Italian and Greek immigration after the Second World War brought espresso machines and café culture to cities such as Melbourne, which became known for its laneway cafés. From the 1980s onward, independent specialty cafés and local roasters proliferated; international chains such as Starbucks achieved only limited success and closed most Australian stores in 2008.

Characteristics

Distinctive features include the flat white and long black, early-morning café opening hours, coffee served with brunch, and a culture of skilled professional baristas. Australia and New Zealand consistently rank among the highest per-capita coffee consumers in the world.
