The Royal Court Theatre, at different times known as the Court Theatre, the New Chelsea Theatre, and the Belgravia Theatre, is a non-commercial West End theatre in Sloane Square, London, England. In 1956 it was acquired by the English Stage Company and became known as the home of new British playwriting.

History

The first theatre on the site opened in 1870 as a converted chapel. The present building dates from 1888. Under J. E. Vedrenne and Harley Granville-Barker in the early 1900s, the theatre staged pioneering seasons of plays by George Bernard Shaw and other modern dramatists.

The English Stage Company

In 1956 George Devine founded the English Stage Company at the Royal Court. Its production on 8 May 1956 of John Osborne's Look Back in Anger is widely regarded as a turning point in post-war British theatre, launching the era of the "angry young men". The theatre went on to premiere work by Arnold Wesker, Edward Bond, Caryl Churchill, Sarah Kane and many others.

Spaces

The building contains the Theatre Downstairs, the main auditorium, and the Theatre Upstairs, a studio space opened in 1969 that has launched many significant new plays. The building was extensively redeveloped between 1997 and 2000.
