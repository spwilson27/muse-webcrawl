Galveston Bay is a bay in the western Gulf of Mexico along the upper coast of Texas. It is the seventh-largest estuary in the United States and the largest on the Texas coast. Bounded by the city of Galveston on Galveston Island and the mainland communities of the Houston metropolitan area, the bay system includes East Bay, West Bay, Trinity Bay, and several smaller inlets, and it connects to the Gulf through Bolivar Roads and Galveston Ship Channel.

Ecology

Fed by the Trinity and San Jacinto rivers, the bay's mix of fresh and salt water supports oyster reefs, marshes, and seagrass beds that serve as nurseries for shrimp, crabs, and finfish. The bay sustains major commercial and recreational fisheries and lies on the Central Flyway for migratory birds.

Commerce

The Houston Ship Channel, dredged through the bay, links the Port of Houston — one of the busiest ports in the United States — to the Gulf. Petrochemical complexes line the western shore, and the bay has been repeatedly affected by hurricanes, most severely the 1900 Galveston hurricane and Hurricane Ike in 2008, whose storm surge inundated bay communities.
