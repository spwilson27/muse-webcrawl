Leukopenia

Leukopenia is a decrease in the number of white blood cells (leukocytes) in the blood, typically a total count below about 4,000 cells per microlitre in adults. Because white cells defend against infection, leukopenia increases the risk of infectious disease.

Leukopenia
DefinitionWhite blood cell count below normal range
Common subtypeNeutropenia (low neutrophils)
CausesDrugs, infections, bone-marrow disorders, autoimmune disease
Main riskIncreased susceptibility to infection

Causes

Leukopenia can result from chemotherapy and other medications, viral infections, bone-marrow failure or infiltration, autoimmune conditions such as lupus, nutritional deficiencies, or inherited disorders. The most clinically important form is neutropenia, a shortage of neutrophils; lymphocytopenia is a shortage of lymphocytes.

Management

Treatment addresses the underlying cause — stopping an offending drug, treating infection, or using growth factors such as G-CSF to stimulate white-cell production. People with severe neutropenia may need protective precautions and prompt antibiotics for fever.
