Electromagnetically induced transparency

Electromagnetically induced transparency
AbbreviationEIT
FieldQuantum optics
ApplicationsSlow light, quantum memory, optical switching

Electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) is a coherent optical nonlinearity in which a medium that is normally opaque to a probe laser beam is rendered transparent when a second, coupling laser beam is applied. The effect arises from quantum interference between absorption pathways in a three-level atomic system.

Mechanism

In a typical lambda or ladder configuration of atomic energy levels, the coupling field creates a narrow window of transparency within an absorption line. Within this window the refractive index varies steeply with frequency while absorption stays low, which greatly reduces the group velocity of light pulses — the basis of "slow light" and stopped-light experiments.

Applications

EIT underlies proposals for quantum memories that store photonic qubits in atomic ensembles, highly sensitive magnetometers, and nonlinear optical devices operating at very low light levels. The phenomenon was predicted and demonstrated in the early 1990s, notably by the group of Stephen E. Harris.
