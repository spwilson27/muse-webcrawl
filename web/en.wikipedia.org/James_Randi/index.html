James Randi (born Randall James Hamilton Zwinge; August 7, 1928 – October 20, 2020) was a Canadian-American stage magician, author and scientific skeptic who extensively challenged paranormal and pseudoscientific claims. Billed in his performing days as "The Amazing Randi," he became one of the world's best-known debunkers of psychics, faith healers and other claimants of supernatural powers.

James Randi, magician and skeptic

Magician and escape artist

Randi was born in Toronto and took up magic after seeing Harry Blackstone Sr. perform. Dropping out of school at seventeen to join a carnival, he built a career as a stage magician, mentalist and escape artist, performing on television and radio in Canada and the United States and touring internationally. He set records for escapes and endurance feats, including surviving in a sealed metal coffin under water, and toured in the 1970s as a special effects consultant and performer with rock musician Alice Cooper, for whose stage show Randi was mock-executed nightly by guillotine and gallows.

Skeptical career

Randi grew increasingly frustrated with performers who claimed genuine psychic powers. In the 1970s he famously challenged Uri Geller, the Israeli spoon-bender, arguing that Geller's feats were standard magic tricks; their feud, documented in Randi's The Magic of Uri Geller (1975), brought lawsuits and lasting controversy. In 1976 he co-founded the Committee for the Scientific Investigation of Claims of the Paranormal (CSICOP, later CSI) with Martin Gardner, Isaac Asimov, Carl Sagan and others, though he resigned a few years later. He investigated faith healers — exposing Peter Popoff's use of a hidden radio receiver in a celebrated 1986 investigation — and devised "Project Alpha," hoaxing a university parapsychology laboratory with two young conjurors posing as psychics.

The Million Dollar Challenge

In 1996 Randi founded the James Randi Educational Foundation (JREF), which offered a prize — raised to one million dollars in 1996 — to anyone who could demonstrate paranormal ability under controlled, agreed test conditions. Hundreds applied; none passed even preliminary tests, and the challenge was retired in 2015. His books include Flim-Flam! (1980) and The Faith Healers (1987). Randi received a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 1986 and an asteroid, 3163 Randi, was named in his honour. He died in Florida in 2020, aged 92.
