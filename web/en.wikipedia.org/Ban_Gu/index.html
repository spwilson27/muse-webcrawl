Ban Gu (Chinese: 班固; 32–92 CE), courtesy name Mengjian, was a Chinese historian and court scholar of the Eastern Han dynasty. He is best known as the principal author of the Book of Han, the dynastic history of the Western Han, which established the format followed by the standard histories of imperial China for nearly two millennia.

Life

Ban Gu was born into a prominent family of scholars and soldiers. His father, Ban Biao, had begun a continuation of Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian; after Ban Biao's death, Emperor Ming ordered Ban Gu to continue the work, which grew into the Book of Han. Ban Gu served at court, took part in the famous scholarly discussions at the White Tiger Hall in 79 CE whose conclusions shaped Confucian orthodoxy, and accompanied the general Dou Xian on campaign against the Xiongnu, composing celebratory inscriptions. When Dou Xian fell from power in 92 CE, Ban Gu was arrested and died in prison. His younger sister Ban Zhao, herself a noted scholar, later completed the unfinished portions of the history.

Book of Han

The Han Shu covers the Western Han dynasty from 206 BCE to 23 CE in 100 chapters arranged as imperial annals, chronological tables, treatises on subjects such as law, astronomy, geography and economics, and ranked biographies. By confining each history to a single dynasty and organising it in this annal-biography (jizhuanti) composite form, Ban Gu created the template for all subsequent official dynastic histories, making him one of the most influential historians in the Chinese tradition.

Other works

Ban Gu was also a leading writer of fu (rhapsodic prose-poems); his "Two Capitals Fu" (《兩都賦》), contrasting Chang'an and Luoyang, is considered a masterpiece of the genre.
