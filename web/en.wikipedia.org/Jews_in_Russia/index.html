Jews in Russia

The history of the Jews in Russia and historically associated territories spans many centuries. Jews have been present in territories now forming part of Russia since ancient times, but the largest Jewish population entered the Russian Empire through the partitions of Poland in the late 18th century, which brought hundreds of thousands of Jews under Russian rule.

Imperial period

From 1791 the empire confined most Jews to the Pale of Settlement in its western provinces, and imposed restrictions on residence, occupations, and education. Jews were subject to special taxes and, under Nicholas I, to conscription of cantonists. Waves of pogroms in 1881–82 and 1903–06 drove mass emigration, chiefly to the United States. Despite restrictions, Russian Jewry produced flourishing religious scholarship, Yiddish and Hebrew literature, and vigorous political movements including Zionism, the Bund, and revolutionary socialism.

Soviet period and after

The 1917 revolutions abolished the Pale and formal disabilities, and Jews played prominent roles in Soviet life, though the regime suppressed religious practice and, later, independent Jewish culture. Around two million Soviet Jews were murdered in the Holocaust on occupied Soviet territory. Post-war state antisemitism included the campaign against "rootless cosmopolitans" and the Doctors' plot. From the 1970s many Soviet Jews emigrated, especially to Israel and the United States, a movement accelerated by the collapse of the USSR in 1991. The Jewish population of Russia today numbers in the low hundreds of thousands by core definitions, with larger numbers of people of Jewish ancestry.
