The Chinese space program is the space program of the People's Republic of China, directed by the China National Space Administration (CNSA). It is one of the most advanced in the world, and the most active by annual launch count since 2018. Its origins lie in the late 1950s missile program led by Qian Xuesen; China became the fifth nation to launch a satellite independently with Dong Fang Hong I in 1970.

Human spaceflight

Project 921 Shenzhou placed China's first astronaut, Yang Liwei, in orbit on October 15, 2003, making China the third country with independent human spaceflight capability. The program progressed through the Tiangong-1 and Tiangong-2 test laboratories to the modular Tiangong space station ("Heavenly Palace"), whose core module Tianhe launched in 2021; the station was completed with the Wentian and Mengtian modules in 2022 and is intended to remain permanently crewed. China has announced goals of landing astronauts on the Moon before 2030.

Lunar and planetary exploration

The Chang'e program has achieved a string of firsts: Chang'e 3 made the first lunar soft landing since 1976 (2013); Chang'e 4 performed the first soft landing on the Moon's far side (2019); Chang'e 5 returned lunar samples (2020); and Chang'e 6 returned the first samples from the lunar far side (2024). Tianwen-1 (2021) placed China second to the United States in successfully landing a rover on Mars (Zhurong). The asteroid-sample-return mission Tianwen-2 launched in 2025.

Launch capability and infrastructure

The Long March (Chang Zheng) rocket family covers all classes, including the heavy-lift Long March 5. Launches fly from four sites: Jiuquan (the historic and crewed site), Taiyuan, Xichang, and the coastal Wenchang site in Hainan. China operates the BeiDou navigation satellite constellation (global coverage completed 2020), the Gaofen and Yaogan remote-sensing families, and extensive communications satellites. Commercial spaceflight has grown rapidly with private firms such as LandSpace, i-Space, and Galactic Energy.
