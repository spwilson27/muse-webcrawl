Abdurrahman Wahid

Born: 7 September 1940, Jombang, Dutch East Indies

Died: 30 December 2009, Jakarta, Indonesia

Office: 4th President of Indonesia (1999–2001)

Abdurrahman Wahid (7 September 1940 – 30 December 2009), more colloquially known as Gus Dur, was an Indonesian Muslim religious and political leader who served as the fourth president of Indonesia from 1999 to 2001. He was the long-time chairman of Nahdlatul Ulama (NU), the world's largest independent Muslim organisation, and the founder of the National Awakening Party (PKB).

Early life and education

Wahid was born in Jombang, East Java, into one of Indonesia's most prominent Muslim families; his grandfather Hasyim Asy'ari founded Nahdlatul Ulama, and his father Wahid Hasyim was Indonesia's first minister of religious affairs. He studied at Al-Azhar University in Cairo and the University of Baghdad, and travelled widely in Europe and North America before returning to Indonesia in 1971.

Religious leadership

Elected chairman of NU in 1984, Wahid steered the organisation away from party politics and toward social and educational work. A prominent advocate of pluralism, democracy and interfaith dialogue, he defended the rights of Indonesia's Chinese and Christian minorities and opposed the political Islamisation of the state.

Presidency

Following the fall of Suharto, Wahid was elected president by the People's Consultative Assembly in October 1999, defeating Megawati Sukarnoputri. His administration lifted restrictions on ethnic Chinese culture, pursued reform of the military and attempted decentralisation, but was marked by erratic policy, cabinet turmoil and conflict with parliament. He was removed from office by the legislature in July 2001 and succeeded by Megawati. Wahid remained an influential public figure until his death in Jakarta in 2009.
