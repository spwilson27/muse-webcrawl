Isabella Mary Beeton (née Mayson; 14 March 1836 – 6 February 1865), known as Mrs Beeton, was an English journalist and editor, famous as the author of Mrs Beeton's Book of Household Management (1861), one of the most successful cookery and household guides ever published in Britain.

Life

Isabella was the eldest of a large blended family raised at Epsom, Surrey. Educated partly in Heidelberg, Germany, she married publisher Samuel Orchart Beeton in 1856. She began contributing cookery and domestic columns to her husband's Englishwoman's Domestic Magazine, translating French fiction and compiling recipes sent in by readers and adapted from earlier writers such as Eliza Acton.

Book of Household Management

First issued in monthly parts from 1859 and published as a single volume in 1861, the Book of Household Management ran to more than a thousand pages with some 900 recipes and advice on servants, childcare, etiquette, household accounts and "the mistress's" duties. Its systematic format — ingredients, costs, timings and serving numbers for each recipe — set the template for modern cookbooks. "Mrs Beeton" became a byword for domestic competence, although Isabella was in her early twenties, with limited cooking experience, when she compiled it.

Beeton died of puerperal fever in 1865, aged 28, after the birth of her fourth child. Her husband and later Oxford University Press kept the book in print, heavily revised, for more than a century.
