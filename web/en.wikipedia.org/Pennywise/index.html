Pennywise

Pennywise, also known as It, is the title character and main antagonist of Stephen King's 1986 horror novel It. Pennywise is an ancient, shape-shifting cosmic entity that preys on the children of the fictional town of Derry, Maine, awakening roughly every 27 years to feed, preferring victims who are frightened because fear "salts the meat".

Character

It's true form is incomprehensible to humans; the closest approximation is a giant spider, while its essence resides in the "deadlights", a writhing mass of orange light in a dimension called the Macroverse. On Earth it most often appears as Pennywise the Dancing Clown, also calling itself Bob Gray, luring children with balloons. It can take the shape of whatever its victim fears most, appearing variously as a leper, a werewolf, a giant bird, or the apparition of Georgie Denbrough.

In the novel, It arrived on Earth in an asteroid impact in prehistory and is opposed by the Losers' Club, a group of seven children who wound it in 1958 and return as adults in 1985 to destroy it in the Ritual of Chüd. Its natural enemy is Maturin the Turtle, another ancient being of the Macroverse.

Portrayals

Pennywise was portrayed by Tim Curry in the 1990 television miniseries It, a performance widely praised and credited with popularising the "scary clown" image. Bill Skarsgård played the role in the films It (2017) and It Chapter Two (2019), which together grossed over a billion dollars worldwide. The character has become one of the most recognisable villains in popular culture and is frequently cited in discussions of coulrophobia.
