Brushless DC electric motor

A brushless DC electric motor (BLDC), also known as an electronically commutated motor, is a synchronous electric motor powered by direct-current electricity via an inverter or switching power supply that produces alternating current in the motor windings. Unlike brushed DC motors, it has no brushes or mechanical commutator: an electronic controller switches current to the windings in sequence, creating a rotating magnetic field that the permanent-magnet rotor follows.

Construction

In the most common arrangement the permanent magnets are mounted on the rotor and the coil windings on the stationary stator — the inverse of a brushed motor. Rotor position is sensed with Hall effect sensors, or estimated sensorlessly from back electromotive force. Outrunner designs, common in drones and model aircraft, place the magnets on an outer rotating bell around the stator.

Advantages and applications

Without brushes there is no brush wear, sparking or associated electrical noise, giving longer life, higher efficiency and quieter operation. Drawbacks include cost and the need for an electronic speed controller. BLDC motors are used in computer fans and disk drives, cordless power tools, electric vehicles, heating and ventilation systems, industrial servos, and multirotor drones.

Control

Controllers range from simple trapezoidal (six-step) commutation to sinusoidal drive and field-oriented control, which gives smoother torque and better efficiency at low speed. Speed is set by the switching rate, and torque by current.
