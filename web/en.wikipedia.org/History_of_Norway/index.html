History of Norway

The history of Norway stretches from the first settlement after the last Ice Age, through the Viking Age and medieval kingdom, four centuries of union with Denmark, union with Sweden, full independence in 1905, and Norway's emergence as a prosperous petroleum economy.

Prehistory and the Viking Age

Humans settled Norway's coasts as the ice retreated, around 10,000 BCE; rock carvings at Alta record later hunter and farming cultures. Petty kingdoms were gradually unified, tradition crediting Harald Fairhair's victory at Hafrsfjord (c. 872). From the raid on Lindisfarne in 793, Norse Vikings ranged across Europe, settling Iceland, Greenland and reaching North America around 1000 CE. Kings Olaf Tryggvason and Olaf Haraldsson (St Olaf) imposed Christianity around the turn of the millennium.

Medieval kingdom

After civil wars in the twelfth century, Norway reached its greatest extent under Haakon IV (r. 1217–1263), ruling Iceland, Greenland and the Hebrides, though the Scottish campaign of 1263 and the Treaty of Perth (1266) ceded the Hebrides and Man. The Black Death of 1349 killed perhaps half the population and weakened the kingdom fatally.

Union with Denmark

Through dynastic accident Norway entered the Kalmar Union (1397) with Denmark and Sweden, and from 1536 was effectively a Danish province; the Reformation was imposed, and Norwegian institutions were dismantled. Yet trade in fish and timber enriched coastal towns, and the Danish era gave Norway peace for long stretches, broken by wars with Sweden in which Norway lost territory (1645, 1658).

1814 and union with Sweden

By the Treaty of Kiel (1814) Denmark ceded Norway to Sweden, but Norwegians declared independence and adopted a liberal constitution at Eidsvoll on 17 May 1814. After a brief war, Norway accepted a personal union with Sweden while keeping its constitution and parliament. Growing nationalism, romantic national culture and economic development fuelled demands for full independence.

Independence and modern Norway

The union was dissolved peacefully in 1905; Prince Carl of Denmark became King Haakon VII. Norway remained neutral in the First World War. In April 1940 Nazi Germany invaded; the king and government went into exile while Vidkun Quisling's collaborationist regime ruled at home, and a large resistance movement operated until liberation in 1945. Post-war Norway joined NATO (1949), rebuilt under Labour governments, twice rejected EU membership in referendums (1972, 1994) while joining the European Economic Area, and from 1969 channelled North Sea oil wealth into industry and a sovereign wealth fund, now the world's largest.
