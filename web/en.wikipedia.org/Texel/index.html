Texel

Texel (pronounced [ˈtɛsəl]; Texels dialect: Tessel) is a municipality and an island in the Netherlands, in the province of North Holland. It is the largest and most populated island of the West Frisian Islands in the Wadden Sea. The island is situated north of Den Helder, separated from the mainland by the Marsdiep strait, and is accessible by a regular ferry from Den Helder operated by TESO.

Geography

The island is about 25 km long and 9 km wide, with an area of roughly 162 km² of land. Its landscape of dunes, polders, beaches and nature reserves — including the De Slufter tidal inlet and the Dunes of Texel National Park — makes it a major domestic tourist destination. The seven villages are Den Burg (the largest), De Koog, Den Hoorn, De Cocksdorp, Oosterend, Oudeschild and De Waal.

History

Texel's roadstead was an important anchorage for Dutch shipping in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, and many ships of the Dutch East India Company began their voyages from the Reede van Texel. In April 1945, near the end of the Second World War, the island was the scene of the Georgian uprising on Texel, in which Georgian soldiers of the German Wehrmacht revolted against their German officers; the fighting, sometimes called "Europe's last battle", continued even after the German surrender in the Netherlands and ended only with the arrival of Canadian troops.

Nature and economy

Texel is noted for birdwatching, sheep farming (the Texel sheep breed originates here), and the Ecomare seal sanctuary and nature museum. Tourism is the mainstay of the local economy, with the number of visitors far exceeding the resident population each year.
