Berghain

Berghain is a nightclub in Berlin, Germany, widely regarded as one of the world's most famous techno clubs. It is housed in a former East German power station near Ostbahnhof, on the border of Friedrichshain and Kreuzberg — from which its name is derived.

History

The club opened in 2004, succeeding the Ostgut club. Its main floor occupies a vast turbine hall with 18-metre ceilings, with a Funktion-One sound system; the smaller Panorama Bar upstairs plays house music.

Culture

Berghain is famous for long weekend parties running from Saturday night into Monday, a strict and unpredictable door policy, a ban on photography inside, and its resident DJs such as Marcel Dettmann and Ben Klock. In 2016 a German court recognised the club as a cultural institution for tax purposes.

Music

Its associated label Ostgut Ton released defining minimal and industrial techno. The club grew out of Berlin's post-reunification techno scene and its gay club culture.
