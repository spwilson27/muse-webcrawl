Proteolysis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Proteolysis is the breakdown of proteins into smaller polypeptides or amino acids. Uncatalysed, the hydrolysis of peptide bonds is extremely slow, taking hundreds of years. Proteolysis is typically catalysed by cellular enzymes called proteases, but may also occur by intramolecular digestion. Proteolysis serves many purposes in living organisms: it activates precursor proteins and zymogens, removes damaged or misfolded proteins, regulates the concentration of active proteins, processes dietary protein during digestion, and recycles amino acids.

Mechanisms

Proteases cleave peptide bonds either within a protein chain (endoproteases or proteinases) or from the ends (exopeptidases, including aminopeptidases and carboxypeptidases). Proteases are classified by their catalytic mechanism into serine, cysteine, aspartic, metallo-, threonine, and glutamic proteases. Many are synthesized as inactive precursors (zymogens) that are activated by proteolytic cleavage — a cascade seen in digestive enzymes such as trypsin, in blood clotting, and in the caspases that execute apoptosis.

Regulated protein degradation

In eukaryotic cells, most regulated degradation is carried out by the ubiquitin–proteasome system. Proteins destined for destruction are tagged with chains of the small protein ubiquitin by E1, E2, and E3 enzymes; the E3 ubiquitin ligases confer substrate specificity. The tagged protein is unfolded and translocated into the 26S proteasome, whose threonine protease core degrades it into short peptides. Autophagy, by contrast, delivers larger structures, organelles, and aggregates to the lysosome, where acid hydrolases including cathepsins degrade them. Together, these systems maintain protein homeostasis (proteostasis).

Biological roles

  • Digestion: pepsin, trypsin, and chymotrypsin break dietary proteins into absorbable units.
  • Activation: proinsulin is cleaved to insulin; procaspases are activated during apoptosis.
  • Regulation: degradation of cyclins drives cell-cycle progression; turnover of transcription factors shapes gene expression.
  • Defense and remodeling: complement activation, blood coagulation, and extracellular matrix remodeling all depend on controlled proteolysis.

Disease and applications

Dysregulated proteolysis contributes to disease: excessive matrix metalloproteinase activity aids cancer invasion, and impaired proteasomal or autophagic clearance is linked to neurodegenerative diseases. Protease inhibitors are important drugs — HIV protease inhibitors transformed AIDS treatment, and proteasome inhibitors such as bortezomib are used against multiple myeloma. Industrially, proteases are used in detergents, leather processing, and food production such as cheese making.
