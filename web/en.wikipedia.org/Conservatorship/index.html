Conservatorship

A conservatorship is a legal arrangement, used in the United States and some other jurisdictions, in which a court appoints a person or organization (the conservator) to manage the personal affairs, finances, or both, of another person (the conservatee) who is judged unable to manage them because of mental or physical limitations, advanced age, or other incapacity. A conservatorship of the person covers decisions about health care, residence, and daily life; a conservatorship of the estate covers property and money. Closely related concepts include guardianship (a term used for minors in many states and for adults in others) and, in California, the LPS conservatorship for people gravely disabled by mental disorder.

Process and powers

Conservatorships are established by probate courts, usually after a petition, medical evidence, and a hearing; the proposed conservatee is entitled to notice, a lawyer, and a jury trial in some jurisdictions. A conservator may gain sweeping powers — controlling bank accounts, contracts, medical consent, and even where the conservatee lives and whom they see — and owes fiduciary duties, including periodic accountings to the court. Because the arrangement removes fundamental civil rights, courts are supposed to impose the least restrictive alternative and to consider supported decision-making where possible.

Controversy and notable cases

Critics argue the system is prone to abuse and exploitation, citing cases in which professional conservators isolated elderly people and drained their estates. Public attention peaked with the conservatorship of singer Britney Spears, imposed in 2008 and terminated in 2021 after a widely supported "Free Britney" campaign and her testimony about coercive control; the case spurred legislative reforms in California and elsewhere to strengthen conservatees' rights, require consideration of alternatives, and increase oversight.
