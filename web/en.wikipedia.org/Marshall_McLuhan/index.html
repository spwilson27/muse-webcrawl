Herbert Marshall McLuhan (21 July 1911 – 31 December 1980) was a Canadian philosopher whose work is among the cornerstones of media theory. McLuhan coined the expression "the medium is the message" in the first chapter in his Understanding Media: The Extensions of Man (1964), as well as the term "global village", and predicted the World Wide Web almost 30 years before it was invented.

Life and career

Born in Edmonton and raised in Winnipeg, McLuhan studied at the University of Manitoba and then at the University of Cambridge, where he earned his PhD in English literature in 1943 with a dissertation on Thomas Nashe and the classical trivium. He taught at several universities before settling at St. Michael's College, University of Toronto, where he founded the Centre for Culture and Technology in 1963.

Ideas

In The Gutenberg Galaxy (1962) McLuhan argued that print technology reshaped human consciousness toward linear, visual thinking. In Understanding Media he classified media as "hot" (high-definition, low participation) or "cool" (low-definition, high participation), and insisted that the content of any medium is another medium. His aphoristic, mosaic style made him a celebrity intellectual of the 1960s — he made a cameo appearance as himself in Woody Allen's Annie Hall (1977) — even as academic critics disputed his methods. Interest in his work revived strongly with the rise of the internet and social media.
