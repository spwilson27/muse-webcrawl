Flavius Honorius (9 September 384 – 15 August 423) was Roman emperor in the West from 393 to 423. He was the younger son of emperor Theodosius I and his first wife Aelia Flaccilla. After the death of his father in 395, Honorius succeeded to the western half of the Roman Empire, while his elder brother Arcadius ruled in the East.

Early reign and Stilicho

Honorius was elevated to Augustus in 393 at the age of eight. Effective power lay with the magister militum Stilicho, a general of Vandal descent who married into the imperial family and whose daughters Maria and Thermantia were successively married to Honorius. Stilicho repelled the Visigothic king Alaric I at Pollentia and Verona in 402, and destroyed the invasion of Radagaisus in 406.

Fall of Stilicho and sack of Rome

In 408, amid court intrigue, Stilicho was arrested and executed. The purge of his supporters drove many federate soldiers to defect to Alaric, who besieged Rome three times and sacked the city on 24 August 410 — the first time Rome had fallen to a foreign enemy in almost 800 years. Honorius, safe in the marsh-protected capital of Ravenna, refused to negotiate a settlement.

Later reign

The 410s saw the loss of effective control over Gaul, Hispania and Africa to usurpers and barbarian kingdoms, though the general Constantius (later co-emperor Constantius III) recovered much territory. Honorius died of dropsy in 423 without an heir; after the usurpation of Joannes, the eastern court installed Valentinian III as western emperor.

Legacy

Honorius is traditionally judged one of the weakest emperors, presiding over the disintegration of the Western Empire, though modern historians stress the structural crises he inherited and the competence of his generals.
