Chandragupta Maurya

Chandragupta Maurya (reigned c. 324 – c. 297 BCE) was the founder of the Maurya Empire in ancient India, the first empire to unify most of the Indian subcontinent under one ruler. Guided by his mentor Chanakya, he overthrew the Nanda dynasty and established his capital at Pataliputra.

Rise to power

Historical details of Chandragupta's early life are obscure and interwoven with legend from Hindu, Jain and Buddhist sources, as well as Greek accounts. After Alexander the Great's campaigns in the northwest, Chandragupta built an army, seized Magadha from the unpopular Nanda king Dhana Nanda, and then moved west to conquer the Macedonian satrapies in the Indus valley.

War with Seleucus

Around 305 BCE Chandragupta fought Seleucus I Nicator, a successor of Alexander. The resulting treaty ceded large territories — including parts of present-day Afghanistan — to Chandragupta in exchange for 500 war elephants, and was sealed by a matrimonial alliance. The Seleucid ambassador Megasthenes visited Chandragupta's court and wrote the Indica, a famous account of India.

Later life

Jain tradition holds that late in life Chandragupta abdicated in favour of his son Bindusara, became a Jain monk under the teacher Bhadrabahu, and ended his life by ritual fasting (sallekhana) at Shravanabelagola in present-day Karnataka.
