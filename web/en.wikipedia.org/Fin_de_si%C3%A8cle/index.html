Fin de siècle

Fin de siècle (French for "end of the century") is a term associated with the end of the 19th century and the cultural mood of the 1890s, characterized by decadence, aestheticism, and a sense of both exhaustion and anticipation of a new era.

Periodc. 1880–1914 (especially the 1890s)
Associated movementsDecadence, Symbolism, Art Nouveau, Aestheticism

Character

The phrase came to describe the spirit of late-Victorian and Belle Époque Europe: cynicism, ennui, and pessimism about civilization mingled with excitement about new artistic and scientific possibilities. Figures associated with the period include Oscar Wilde, Joris-Karl Huysmans, Aubrey Beardsley, and the French Symbolist poets.

Usage

Originally applied to French culture, the term is now used more broadly for the close of any era, and historians use it to discuss the cultural climate that preceded the upheavals of the First World War.
