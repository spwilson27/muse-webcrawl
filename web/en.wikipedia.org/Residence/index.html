Residence

A residence is a place (a building or structure) used as a home or dwelling, or, more generally, the fact or status of living somewhere. The term is used in law, taxation, immigration and everyday speech to distinguish a person's usual abode from other kinds of presence, such as a temporary stay.

Legal meaning

In many legal systems, residence is a connecting factor for jurisdiction, taxation and voting eligibility. It is usually a question of fact: a person resides where they ordinarily live, even if they are temporarily absent. Residence is contrasted with domicile, which in common-law countries also requires an intention to remain permanently or indefinitely.

Types

Residences range from single-family houses and apartments to official residences (such as a head of state's palace), student residences (halls of residence or dormitories), and care residences for the elderly. A "principal residence" or "main residence" is the dwelling a household treats as its primary home, a status that often carries tax advantages.
