The economy of Japan is a highly developed free-market economy. It is one of the largest economies in the world by nominal GDP, and among the largest by purchasing power parity. Japan is a member of the G7 and one of the world's foremost manufacturing and exporting nations. Its currency, the yen, is one of the most traded in the world.

Tokyo, Japan's financial centre

History

Following the Meiji Restoration of 1868, Japan industrialised rapidly. After devastation in the Second World War, Japan achieved sustained high growth from the 1960s to the 1980s — the "Japanese economic miracle" — becoming a leader in automobiles, consumer electronics, and shipbuilding. An asset price bubble burst around 1990, ushering in the "Lost Decades" of slow growth and deflation. In 2012, Prime Minister Shinzō Abe launched "Abenomics", a programme of monetary easing, fiscal stimulus, and structural reform.

Sectors

Services account for roughly 70% of GDP, industry about 25%, and agriculture a small share. Japan is a major producer of motor vehicles (Toyota, Honda, Nissan), electronics, robotics, and precision machinery. Scarce in natural resources, Japan imports most of its energy and raw materials and much of its food. It has one of the world's largest fishing fleets and a vast network of infrastructure, including the Shinkansen high-speed rail system.

Challenges

Japan faces an ageing and shrinking population, a public debt that is the highest in the developed world relative to GDP, and persistent deflationary pressure. Labour productivity in services lags that of manufacturing. Successive governments have debated reforms to immigration, female labour-force participation, and corporate governance.
