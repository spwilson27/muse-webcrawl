Aden Protectorate

The Aden Protectorate (Arabic: محمية عدن) was a British protectorate in South Arabia which evolved in the hinterland of the port of Aden and in the Hadramawt following the conquest of Aden by Great Britain in 1839, and it continued until the 1960s.

History

Beginning with a treaty with the Sultan of Lahej in 1839, Britain gradually concluded protection treaties with the rulers of the tribal states surrounding Aden, formalised during the 1880s as the Ottoman Empire pressed into Yemen. In 1937 the protectorate was administratively divided into the Western Aden Protectorate and the Eastern Aden Protectorate, the latter comprising the Hadramawt states such as the Qu'aiti and Kathiri sultanates.

Federation and end

In 1959 several western states formed the Federation of Arab Emirates of the South, renamed the Federation of South Arabia in 1962 when Aden Colony joined. The eastern states largely refused to join and formed the separate Protectorate of South Arabia. Both arrangements collapsed in 1967 amid the Aden Emergency; the rulers were deposed and the territory became part of the People's Republic of South Yemen.
