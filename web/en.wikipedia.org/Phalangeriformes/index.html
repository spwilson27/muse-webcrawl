Phalangeriformes

Phalangeriformes is a suborder of marsupials comprising the possums of Australia and New Guinea. The group includes around seventy species in six families, ranging from tiny pygmy possums weighing only a few grams to the brushtail possums roughly the size of a domestic cat.

PhalangeriformesOrder: Diprotodontia
Families: 6 (incl. Phalangeridae, Pseudocheiridae, Petauridae)
Range: Australia, New Guinea
Species: ~70

Characteristics

Phalangeriform possums are mostly arboreal and nocturnal, with large eyes, grasping hands, and often a prehensile tail; several lineages, such as the sugar glider, have evolved gliding membranes. Diets vary from nectar and sap to leaves, fruit, and insects. Together with the wombats and koalas (Vombatiformes) and the kangaroos and their kin (Macropodiformes), they make up the order Diprotodontia.
