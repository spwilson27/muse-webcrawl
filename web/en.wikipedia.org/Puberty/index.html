Puberty

Stages of adolescent development

Puberty is the process of physical changes through which a child's body matures into an adult body capable of sexual reproduction. It is initiated by hormonal signals from the brain to the gonads: the ovaries in a girl, the testes in a boy. In response to the signals, the gonads produce hormones that stimulate libido and the growth, function, and transformation of the brain, bones, muscle, blood, skin, hair, breasts, and sex organs. Physical growth—height and weight—accelerates in the first half of puberty and is completed when an adult body has been developed.

Physical changes

In girls, puberty usually begins between ages 10 and 14 (most often with breast development, or thelarche) and is marked by the growth spurt, widening of the hips, growth of pubic and underarm hair, and the onset of menstruation (menarche), typically around age 12–13. In boys, puberty usually begins between ages 11 and 16 with enlargement of the testes, followed by growth of the penis, deepening of the voice, increased muscle mass, facial hair, and the first ejaculation (spermarche). Both sexes may experience acne and changes in body odor and composition. Pediatricians describe progression using the five Tanner stages.

Hormonal control

Puberty begins when the hypothalamus increases pulsatile secretion of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), which prompts the pituitary gland to release luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). These stimulate the gonads to produce sex steroids—mainly estrogen in girls and testosterone in boys—which drive the visible changes. A separate process, adrenarche, involves rising adrenal androgens. Timing is influenced by genetics, nutrition, body fat, and general health; over the past two centuries the average age of puberty has declined in industrialized countries in parallel with improving childhood nutrition.

Variations and significance

Puberty beginning unusually early is called precocious puberty, while delayed puberty may reflect hormonal or chronic health problems; both can be evaluated medically. Beyond the physical changes, puberty is a period of major brain development and psychological adjustment, and its onset carries social and cultural significance marked in many societies by rites of passage.
