Tuskegee Airmen

The Tuskegee Airmen were a group of African American military aviators — fighter and bomber pilots, navigators, bombardiers, and support staff — who fought in World War II. They trained at Tuskegee Army Air Field in Alabama and formed the 332nd Fighter Group and the 477th Bombardment Group of the United States Army Air Forces.

Background

Before 1940, African Americans were barred from flying for the U.S. military. Civil rights pressure and the looming war led to the creation of an all-Black pursuit squadron, the 99th, in 1941.

Combat record

Flying P-51 Mustangs with distinctive red tails, the 332nd escorted bombers over Europe, earning a reputation for protecting their charges and destroying enemy aircraft, including German jets. Their success helped pave the way for President Truman's 1948 executive order desegregating the U.S. armed forces.

Legacy

In 2007 the Tuskegee Airmen collectively received the Congressional Gold Medal. Their story has been told in books and films, including Red Tails (2012).
