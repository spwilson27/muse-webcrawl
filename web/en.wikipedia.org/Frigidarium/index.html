Frigidarium

A frigidarium is a large cold pool at the Roman baths. The water in the pool is cold, and bathers would use it after spending time in the caldarium (hot bath) and tepidarium (warm room), whose heat opened the pores of the skin; the cold water was believed to close them. The frigidarium was generally the last stop in the bathing sequence.

Design

In the large imperial thermae, the frigidarium was often a monumental vaulted hall containing a swimming pool, sometimes open to the sky. The frigidarium of the Baths of Caracalla in Rome was a vast groin-vaulted space that later inspired architects; the main hall of Pennsylvania Station in New York was modelled in part on it. In smaller bath complexes the frigidarium could be a rectangular room with one or more plunge pools, sometimes preceded by an apodyterium (changing room) and combined with a palaestra for exercise.

Use

Roman bathing was a daily social ritual. After exercise and heating through the warm and hot rooms, immersion in the cold pool refreshed the bather. Some frigidaria included a larger natatio, an open-air swimming pool. The design of the frigidarium influenced later Turkish baths and European spa architecture, though the cold plunge took different forms in those traditions.
