Nixon shock

The Nixon shock was a series of economic measures undertaken by United States President Richard Nixon in 1971, announced in a nationally televised address on 15 August 1971. Its centrepiece was the unilateral suspension of the US dollar's convertibility into gold, effectively ending the Bretton Woods system of fixed exchange rates that had governed international finance since 1944.

Measures

The New Economic Policy combined: (1) a 90-day freeze on wages and prices to combat inflation; (2) a 10 percent import surcharge; and (3) closure of the "gold window", ending the right of foreign central banks to redeem dollars for gold at $35 per ounce. The measures were devised with Treasury Secretary John Connally and announced without prior consultation with America's allies.

Consequences

The Smithsonian Agreement of December 1971 attempted a realignment of currencies, but by 1973 the major currencies were floating against each other, inaugurating the modern era of fiat money and floating exchange rates. The shock also marked the beginning of the stagflationary decade of the 1970s, though historians debate how directly it caused that era's inflation and unemployment.
