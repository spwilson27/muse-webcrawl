Y-chromosomal Adam

In human genetics, the Y-chromosomal most recent common ancestor (Y-MRCA), informally known as Y-chromosomal Adam, is the most recent male from whom all currently living humans are descended through an unbroken line of male ancestors — that is, the man whose Y chromosome is ancestral to the Y chromosomes of every living man.

The concept

Because the Y chromosome is passed essentially unchanged from father to son (apart from mutations), all Y chromosomes coalesce, going backwards in time, to a single ancestor. This is a mathematical consequence of genealogy, not evidence that only one man lived at the time: Y-chromosomal Adam lived among thousands of contemporaries, most of whose male lines have since died out through drift. His identity is not fixed — as some paternal lines go extinct, the MRCA moves forward in time.

Dating

Estimates of when Y-chromosomal Adam lived have shifted with better data. Studies in the 2000s placed him roughly 60,000–90,000 years ago. The 2013 discovery of the very divergent A00 haplogroup in an African American man and in Mbo men of Cameroon pushed estimates to roughly 200,000–340,000 years ago. Later large-sample analyses (e.g., Karmin et al. 2015) estimated around 192,000–307,000 years, with subsequent refinements clustering near 200,000–250,000 years. He is estimated to have lived in Africa.

Relation to Mitochondrial Eve

Y-chromosomal Adam is the male counterpart to Mitochondrial Eve, the matrilineal most recent common ancestor traced through mitochondrial DNA, dated to roughly 150,000–200,000 years ago. Despite the biblical names, the two were not a couple and need not have lived at the same time or place; the names are analogies for single-ancestor coalescence along one uniparental line.
