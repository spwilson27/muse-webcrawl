Airfix

Founded: 1939

Founder: Nicholas Kove

Headquarters: United Kingdom

Products: Plastic model kits

Parent: Hornby Hobbies

Airfix is a British brand of plastic model kits, founded in 1939 by Hungarian-born businessman Nicholas Kove. The name was chosen so that the company would appear first in alphabetical trade directories and because air could be injected, or "fixed," into its inflatable products.

History

Airfix began by manufacturing inflatable rubber toys and later plastic combs. In 1949 it produced a promotional model of a Ferguson tractor, and in 1952 released a kit of the Golden Hind — often described as the first modern plastic model kit sold in Britain. Its 1953 Spitfire kit became iconic. Airfix kits, packaged in distinctive boxes with artwork by Roy Cross and others, defined scale modelling for generations of British hobbyists, covering aircraft, ships, vehicles, figures, and buildings, commonly in 1:72, 1:48, and 1:24 scales.

The company passed through receivership in 1981 and was acquired by Humbrol; in 2006 it became part of Hornby Hobbies after Humbrol's collapse. Under Hornby the brand was revived with new-tool kits using computer-aided design and laser-scanned subjects.

Legacy

Airfix is a cultural institution in the United Kingdom: the word is sometimes used generically for plastic kits, and the brand featured in the BBC series James May's Toy Stories, which built a full-size Spitfire from an Airfix-style kit in 2009.
