Kyahi Haji Ahmad Dahlan (born Muhammad Darwis; August 1, 1868 – February 23, 1923) was an Indonesian Islamic revivalist and scholar who founded Muhammadiyah in 1912, one of the largest Islamic organizations in the world.

Life and work

Born in the Kauman district of Yogyakarta, the son of an imam of the Sultan's mosque, Dahlan studied in Mecca, where he encountered reformist ideas influenced by Muhammad Abduh and Rashid Rida. Returning home, he campaigned to purify Islamic practice of what he saw as un-Islamic accretions and corrected the direction of prayer (qibla) in local mosques.

Muhammadiyah

Founded on November 18, 1912, Muhammadiyah promoted modern education combining religious and general subjects, and built schools, hospitals, and orphanages across Indonesia. Dahlan also encouraged the education of women, supporting the founding of the women's organization Aisyiyah. He was later declared a National Hero of Indonesia.
