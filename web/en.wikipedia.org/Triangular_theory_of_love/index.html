Triangular theory of love

Triangular theory of love
Proposed by: Robert Sternberg (1986)
Components: intimacy, passion, commitment

The triangular theory of love is a theory of love developed by psychologist Robert Sternberg. In the context of interpersonal relationships, the three components of love, according to the theory, are an intimacy component, a passion component, and a commitment component.

Components

Intimacy refers to feelings of closeness, connectedness and bondedness. Passion encompasses drives leading to romance, physical attraction and sexual consummation. Decision/commitment consists of the short-term decision that one loves another and the long-term commitment to maintain that love.

Forms of love

Combinations of the components yield distinct forms: liking (intimacy alone), infatuation (passion alone), empty love (commitment alone), romantic love (intimacy and passion), companionate love (intimacy and commitment), fatuous love (passion and commitment), and consummate love (all three).
