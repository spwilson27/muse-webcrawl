Citroën SM

Manufacturer: Citroën
Production: 1970–1975
Class: Grand tourer
Engine: 2.7 L / 3.0 L Maserati V6
Layout: Front-engine, front-wheel drive

The SM combined Citroën's hydropneumatics with a Maserati V6.

The Citroën SM is a high-performance coupé produced by the French manufacturer Citroën from 1970 to 1975. Unveiled at the Geneva Motor Show in March 1970, it combined front-wheel drive, hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension and a V6 engine supplied by Maserati, which Citroën then owned. The SM was Citroën's flagship and one of the fastest front-wheel-drive cars of its era, with a top speed of about 220 km/h (137 mph).

Design and technology

Styled by Robert Opron, the SM had a sleek, tapered body with partially skirted rear wheels and six headlamps behind a glass panel, the inner pair swivelling with the steering. It introduced DIRAVI fully powered, self-centring steering whose assistance varied with speed, variable-assist power steering that became a Citroën signature, and disc brakes all round with high-pressure hydraulic assistance. The Maserati-built 90° V6 was offered in 2.7-litre carburetted and fuel-injected forms and, for the North American market, a 3.0-litre version.

Reception and production

The SM placed third in the 1971 European Car of the Year contest and won Motor Trend's Car of the Year award in the United States in 1972. Production totalled 12,920 cars. Sales suffered after the 1973 oil crisis, and the SM was discontinued in 1975 when Citroën, bankrupt, was taken over by Peugeot and sold Maserati. Coachbuilt variants included convertibles by Chapron and the four-door Opéra saloon.

Legacy

The SM is regarded as a landmark of automotive design and a showcase of Citroën's technological ambition. Its engine also powered the Maserati Merak, and its steering and suspension concepts influenced later Citroën models such as the CX. See also Citroën DS.
