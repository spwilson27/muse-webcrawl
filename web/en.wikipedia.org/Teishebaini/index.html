Teishebaini

Teishebaini (also transliterated as Teišebaini) was a city of the ancient kingdom of Urartu, located on the hill of Karmir Blur ("Red Hill") in present-day Yerevan, Armenia. It was founded in the mid-7th century BC by the Urartian king Rusa II and named for Teisheba, the Urartian god of storms and thunder. Teishebaini served as a major administrative and storage center of Urartu's Trans-Caucasian provinces.

History

Teishebaini was built as a planned fortress city with massive mud-brick walls, a palace, temples, workshops, and enormous storerooms. Its granaries, oil and wine cellars, and arsenals supplied the Urartian administration in the Ararat plain. The city was destroyed by fire in the early 6th century BC, probably during attacks by Scythians or Medes as Urartu collapsed, and was never rebuilt.

Excavations

Excavations began in 1939 under the direction of Boris Piotrovsky and continued for decades. They recovered cuneiform inscriptions, bronze helmets and shields, jewelry, ceramics, and carbonized stores of grain, sesame, and wine that had been preserved by the fire. Many finds are now displayed in the History Museum of Armenia and the Erebuni Museum in Yerevan.

Significance

Teishebaini is one of the best-documented Urartian cities, providing detailed evidence for the kingdom's administration, economy, and material culture. The site lies within the modern suburbs of Yerevan, near the earlier Urartian fortress of Erebuni, founded by Argishti I in 782 BC.
