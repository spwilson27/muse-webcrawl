Pharynx

SystemRespiratory and digestive
PartsNasopharynx, oropharynx, laryngopharynx
Lengthabout 12–14 cm (adult human)

The pharynx (plural: pharynges) is the part of the throat behind the mouth and nasal cavity, and above the oesophagus and larynx. In humans it forms the upper part of both the digestive tract and the conducting zone of the respiratory system, conveying air to the larynx and food to the oesophagus. It is found in all vertebrates and in many invertebrates.

The three parts of the human pharynx

The human pharynx is conventionally divided into three sections. The nasopharynx lies behind the nose and above the soft palate, and contains the pharyngeal tonsil (adenoid) and the openings of the Eustachian tubes. The oropharynx lies behind the mouth and contains the palatine tonsils; it is the part visible when the mouth is opened wide. The laryngopharynx (hypopharynx) lies behind the larynx and leads into the oesophagus. During swallowing, the epiglottis folds over the laryngeal inlet so that food passes into the oesophagus rather than the airway.

Clinical significance

Inflammation of the pharynx — pharyngitis, the common "sore throat" — is usually viral but may be caused by group A streptococcus ("strep throat"). The pharynx is also implicated in obstructive sleep apnoea when its soft tissues collapse during sleep, and pharyngeal cancers are strongly associated with tobacco, alcohol and human papillomavirus infection.
