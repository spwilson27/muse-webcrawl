Duke

Duke is a male title either of a monarch ruling over a duchy, or of a member of royalty, or nobility. As rulers, dukes are ranked below emperors, kings, grand dukes, grand princes, and sovereign princes. As royalty or nobility, they are ranked below princes and grand dukes. The title comes from French duc, itself from the Latin dux, "leader", a term used in republican Rome to refer to a military commander without an official rank, and later coming to mean the leading military commander of a province. The female equivalent is a duchess.

History

In the Middle Ages, dukes were the rulers of feudal territories, some of which were sovereign principalities, such as the duchies of Normandy, Burgundy and Brittany in France and numerous duchies within the Holy Roman Empire. Over time, as monarchies centralised, many duchies lost sovereignty and the title became primarily a noble rank—the highest rank below a prince in most European peerage systems. In the United Kingdom, a duke is the highest rank of the peerage below the royal family, addressed as "Your Grace"; royal dukes are members of the royal family who hold dukedoms such as York, Edinburgh, Sussex and Cambridge.

Usage by country

In Britain there are currently around two dozen non-royal dukedoms, the premier being the Duke of Norfolk, hereditary Earl Marshal. In Spain, dukes are grandees of Spain. In Italy and Germany the cognate titles are duca and Herzog. The Portuguese title duque and Scandinavian hertig/hertug are equivalents. Some sovereigns remain dukes or grand dukes: the Grand Duke of Luxembourg is a reigning monarch, and the Prince of Monaco's subsidiary styles reflect similar history, while Liechtenstein is ruled by a prince (Fürst).
