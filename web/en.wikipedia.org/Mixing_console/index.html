Mixing console

A mixing console (also called a mixing desk, audio mixer, or sound board) is an electronic device for combining, routing, and changing the level, tone, and dynamics of audio signals. Mixers are used in recording studios, live sound reinforcement, broadcasting, and film and television post-production.

Structure

A typical console is organised into channel strips, each with an input (microphone or line level), gain control, equalisation section, auxiliary sends for effects and monitor mixes, a pan control, and a channel fader. Channels are summed to buses and to a master stereo or multichannel output. Additional features may include subgroups, mute and solo buttons, phantom power for condenser microphones, inserts for external processors, and talkback facilities.

Digital consoles

Digital mixing consoles convert audio to digital form and perform mixing with digital signal processing, offering total recall of settings, onboard effects, and remote control. In studio work, much mixing is now done "in the box" using a digital audio workstation (DAW), whose mixer interface emulates the layout of a physical console.

Applications

Live sound engineers use front-of-house and monitor consoles at concerts; broadcast mixers feed radio and television programmes; and DJs use compact mixers, often with crossfaders, to blend recorded tracks. Major manufacturers include Yamaha, Allen & Heath, Midas, Solid State Logic, and Neve.
