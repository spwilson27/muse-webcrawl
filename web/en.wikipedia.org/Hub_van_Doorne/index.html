Hubert Jozef ("Hub") van Doorne (1 January 1900 – 23 May 1979) was a Dutch industrialist and inventor who, with his brother Wim, founded the company that became DAF, the Netherlands' principal truck and former car manufacturer. He is best known as the inventor of the Variomatic continuously variable transmission.

Life and career

Born in the Limburg village of America, van Doorne trained as a blacksmith and machinist. In 1928, with backing from brewery director A. H. Huenges, he and his brother opened a small engineering workshop in Eindhoven, the Hub van Doorne Machinefabriek. In 1932 the firm began building trailers as Van Doorne's Aanhangwagenfabriek — DAF — and after the Second World War expanded into trucks (from 1949) and passenger cars (from 1958).

Variomatic

Van Doorne's signature invention, developed in the 1950s, was the Variomatic: a belt-driven continuously variable transmission (CVT) using rubber belts running between variable-diameter pulleys. It debuted in the DAF 600 of 1958 and made DAF cars fully automatic long before rivals. His steel push-belt development, pursued through Van Doorne's Transmissie (VDT) in Tilburg, became the basis of modern CVTs used by many manufacturers; VDT was later acquired by Bosch.
