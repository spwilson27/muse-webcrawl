Music

Music is the arrangement of sound to create some combination of form, harmony, melody, rhythm, or otherwise expressive content. Definitions of music vary depending on culture, though it is an aspect of all human societies and a cultural universal. Music may be performed with voices and a vast range of instruments, recorded and reproduced by technology, and experienced in settings from religious ritual to entertainment.

Elements

Common elements of music include pitch (which governs melody and harmony), rhythm (including tempo and metre), dynamics, and the sonic qualities of timbre and texture. Different styles or types of music may emphasise, de-emphasise or omit some of these elements. Music is generally composed, improvised, or some combination of both, and is studied within music theory, musicology and the sociology and psychology of music.

History

Music predates written history: bone flutes at least 40,000 years old are known from Paleolithic sites. Written traditions developed in ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt, India, China and Greece. Medieval chant, the growth of polyphony, the Baroque, Classical and Romantic eras, and the explosion of popular genres in the recording age trace the Western tradition, alongside rich independent traditions worldwide. In the 20th and 21st centuries, recording, radio, jazz, rock, electronic music and streaming transformed how music is made and heard.

Genres and culture

Major families of genre include classical, folk, jazz, blues, rock, pop, hip hop, electronic and countless regional and hybrid forms. Music serves functions in worship, celebration, storytelling, dance, protest and identity, and is a major global industry.
