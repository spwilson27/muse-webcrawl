Literacy

Literacy is the ability to read and write. Modern definitions extend it to the ability to understand, interpret, create and communicate using printed and written materials within varying contexts, and related concepts include numeracy, digital literacy and media literacy.

History

Writing emerged independently in Mesopotamia, Egypt, China and Mesoamerica. For most of history literacy was confined to small elites of scribes, clergy and officials. It spread with cheap print after the fifteenth century, and with compulsory mass schooling in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Global adult literacy rose from around 42 per cent in 1960 to roughly 87 per cent in the 2020s, though hundreds of millions of adults, disproportionately women, remain unable to read.

Measurement

UNESCO defines a literate person as one who can, with understanding, read and write a short simple statement on everyday life. Functional literacy — using reading and writing to participate fully in society — is a higher bar, assessed by surveys such as PIAAC.

Effects

Literacy is associated with better health, earnings and civic participation, and its spread underpins mass media, bureaucracy and modern economies.
