Indian art

Indian art consists of a variety of art forms, including painting, sculpture, pottery, and textile arts such as woven silk. Geographically, it spans the entire Indian subcontinent, including what is now India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, and at times eastern Afghanistan. A strong sense of design is characteristic of Indian art and can be observed in its modern as well as in its traditional forms.

Early art

The earliest Indian art was the rock art of pre-historic times, such as the petroglyphs found in places like the Bhimbetka rock shelters, some of them predating 5500 BCE. The Indus Valley Civilisation produced small seals, bronze and stone figures notable for their refinement. Under the Mauryan Empire (c. 322–185 BCE), monumental stone sculpture such as the Ashokan pillars and the Lion Capital of Ashoka set lasting standards.

Buddhist, Hindu and Jain art

The centuries around the start of the Common Era saw the development of Buddhist art at Bharhut, Sanchi, and Gandhara, the latter blending Indian subjects with Greco-Roman technique. The Gupta period (c. 320–550 CE) is often called a classical age, producing serene Buddha images and the beginnings of great Hindu temple sculpture. The rock-cut temples of Ajanta and Ellora preserve some of the finest early Indian painting and sculpture.

Medieval and Mughal periods

Regional temple styles — Nagara in the north and Dravida in the south — reached monumental scale in temples such as those at Khajuraho and Thanjavur. From the 16th century, the Mughal Empire fostered a distinctive court art, especially miniature painting, which combined Persian, Indian, and European influences. Rajput and Pahari schools developed their own lyrical miniature traditions.

Modern and contemporary art

Under British rule, academic oil painting and the Company school developed, followed by the Bengal School's nationalist revival in the early 20th century. After independence in 1947, the Progressive Artists' Group in Bombay and others sought a modern Indian idiom; contemporary Indian art now commands a substantial international market.
