Biosolids

Biosolids are solid organic matter recovered from a sewage treatment process and used as fertilizer. The term was introduced by the water treatment industry in the 1990s to distinguish treated sewage sludge that meets regulatory standards for land application from untreated sludge. Biosolids contain plant nutrients including nitrogen and phosphorus, as well as organic matter that can improve soil structure.

Treatment and classification

Treatment processes such as anaerobic digestion, composting, lime stabilization and heat drying reduce pathogens and odour. In the United States, the Environmental Protection Agency's Part 503 rule classifies biosolids: Class A biosolids have pathogens reduced to below detectable levels and may be used with few restrictions, while Class B biosolids are treated but still contain detectable pathogens and face site restrictions.

Controversies

Debate continues over contaminants that can be present in biosolids, including heavy metals, pharmaceuticals, microplastics and per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), and over the adequacy of regulation when biosolids are spread on farmland.
