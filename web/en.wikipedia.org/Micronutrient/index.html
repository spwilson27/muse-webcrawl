Micronutrient

CategoriesVitamins, dietary minerals
RequirementSmall quantities (milligrams or less per day)

Micronutrients are essential dietary elements required by organisms in small quantities throughout life to orchestrate a range of physiological functions to maintain health. Micronutrient requirements differ between organisms; in humans they include vitamins and dietary minerals such as iron, iodine, zinc, calcium and vitamin A, needed in amounts generally less than 100 milligrams per day — in contrast to macronutrients (protein, carbohydrate and fat), which are required in large quantities.

Functions and deficiency

Micronutrients serve as cofactors for enzymes, structural components of tissues and hormones, antioxidants and regulators of gene expression. Deficiencies — sometimes called "hidden hunger" — affect an estimated two billion people worldwide. Iodine deficiency is the leading preventable cause of intellectual disability; iron deficiency causes anaemia; vitamin A deficiency is a major cause of preventable blindness in children; and zinc deficiency impairs immune function and growth.

Public health interventions

Strategies to combat deficiency include dietary diversification, supplementation programmes, biofortification of staple crops, and food fortification, such as iodised salt, folic-acid fortification of flour to prevent neural tube defects, and vitamin D added to milk. Excess intake of some micronutrients can also be harmful, setting upper limits for safe consumption.
