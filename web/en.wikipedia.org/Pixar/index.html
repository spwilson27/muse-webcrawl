Pixar Animation Studios is an American computer animation studio based in Emeryville, California, known for creating critically and commercially acclaimed feature films using its proprietary RenderMan rendering software. It is a subsidiary of Walt Disney Studios, a division of the Walt Disney Company. The studio's mascot is Luxo Jr., a desk lamp from its 1986 short film of the same name.

History

Pixar began in 1979 as the Graphics Group, part of the computer division of Lucasfilm. In 1986 it was spun out as an independent company with investment from Apple co-founder Steve Jobs, who served as chairman and principal shareholder. Initially a computer hardware company selling the Pixar Image Computer, the studio's animation department produced short films — Luxo Jr. (1986), Tin Toy (1988) and Knick Knack (1989) — that pioneered computer animation. In 1991 Pixar signed a deal with Disney to produce feature films, beginning with Toy Story (1995), the first fully computer-animated feature film and a landmark in cinema history.

A string of hits followed, including Monsters, Inc., Finding Nemo, The Incredibles, Ratatouille, WALL-E, Up, Inside Out and Coco; many won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature. In 2006 Disney acquired Pixar for approximately $7.4 billion in stock, making Jobs Disney's largest individual shareholder, and Pixar leaders Ed Catmull and John Lasseter took charge of Walt Disney Animation Studios as well.

Technology and process

Pixar develops much of its own software, notably RenderMan for rendering and Presto for animation. Its productions follow a story-driven process refined by the "Braintrust", a group of senior creatives who review films in progress and give candid feedback. Short films continue to be made both as artistic exercises and as a training ground for new directors.
