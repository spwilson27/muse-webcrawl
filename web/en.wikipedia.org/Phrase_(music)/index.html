Phrase (music)

In music theory, a phrase is a unit of musical metre that has a complete musical sense of its own, built from figures and motifs, and combining to form melodies, periods and larger sections. A phrase typically ends with a cadence, a point of melodic or harmonic rest that gives a feeling of partial or complete closure.

Characteristics

Phrases in Classical music are often four bars long, and pairs of phrases form a period: an antecedent phrase ending with a weaker (half) cadence followed by a consequent phrase ending with a stronger (authentic) cadence. Performers shape phrases with dynamics and articulation, much as a speaker groups words into spoken phrases separated by breaths.

Phrasing

Phrasing refers to the expressive shaping of a line of music — the way a performer groups notes, places emphasis and articulates the beginnings and ends of phrases. Slurs in notation frequently indicate phrase markings.
