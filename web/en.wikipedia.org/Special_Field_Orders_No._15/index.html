Special Field Orders No. 15

Special Field Orders, No. 15 (series 1865) were military orders issued during the American Civil War, on January 16, 1865, by General William Tecumseh Sherman, commander of the Military Division of the Mississippi of the United States Army. They provided for the confiscation of 400,000 acres (160,000 ha) of land along the Atlantic coast of South Carolina, Georgia, and Florida and the dividing of it into plots of not more than 40 acres (16 ha), on which were to be settled approximately 18,000 families of formerly enslaved people and other Black refugees then following Sherman's army.

Background

The orders were issued following Sherman's March to the Sea. On January 12, 1865, Sherman met in Savannah, Georgia with twenty Black religious leaders and Secretary of War Edwin M. Stanton. The leaders stated that land ownership was the best way for freed people to secure their independence. Four days later Sherman issued the orders.

Contents

The orders reserved a strip of coastal land from Charleston, South Carolina, south to the St. Johns River in Florida, including the Sea Islands, for the exclusive settlement of Black families. Each family was to receive a plot of not more than forty acres of tillable ground. The phrase "forty acres and a mule," which entered American political folklore, derives from these orders and from subsequent practice under which some settlers were lent army mules.

Aftermath

By June 1865 about 40,000 freed people had settled on the reserved land. However, after the assassination of President Abraham Lincoln, his successor Andrew Johnson reversed the policy in the autumn of 1865, ordering that most of the confiscated land be returned to its former white owners. The episode became a lasting symbol of the federal government's unfulfilled promises of land reform during Reconstruction.
