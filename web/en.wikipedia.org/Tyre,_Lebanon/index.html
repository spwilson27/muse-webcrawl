Tyre (Arabic: صُور, Ṣūr; Phoenician: 𐤑𐤓, Ṣūr) is a city in Lebanon, one of the oldest continually inhabited cities in the world. It was one of the most important cities of the Phoenicians, famed for maritime trade, the production of purple dye from murex snails (Tyrian purple), and the founding of colonies across the Mediterranean, including Carthage.

Originally an island city just off the coast, Tyre was besieged by Alexander the Great in 332 BC, who built a causeway from the mainland to take it; the causeway silted up over time, permanently joining the island to the shore as a peninsula. The city later flourished under Roman rule, which left extensive remains including a hippodrome, triumphal arch, and necropolis.

History

Tyre resisted Nebuchadnezzar II for thirteen years and reached its height in the 10th century BC under King Hiram I, an ally of Solomon. Its sailors and merchants ranged across the Mediterranean and possibly beyond. After Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, and Ottoman periods, Tyre is today a predominantly Shia Muslim city and a major centre of southern Lebanon. Its archaeological sites were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.
