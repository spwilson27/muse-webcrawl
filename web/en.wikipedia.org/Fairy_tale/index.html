Fairy tale

A fairy tale is a short story that belongs to the folklore genre, typically featuring magical and fantastical elements such as fairies, giants, dragons, elves, goblins, dwarfs, witches, talking animals and enchantments. Fairy tales occur both in oral and literary form; the name now also covers well-known stories such as "Cinderella", "Snow White", "Sleeping Beauty" and "Hansel and Gretel". They are distinguished from legends (which claim a basis in fact) and from moral fables by their openly fantastical settings.

History

The literary fairy tale developed in early modern Europe — Straparola and Basile in Italy, then Charles Perrault's Tales of Mother Goose (1697) in France and Madame d'Aulnoy, who coined the term conte de fées. The Brothers Grimm's Children's and Household Tales (1812) collected German oral tales, and Hans Christian Andersen later wrote original literary tales that became classics.

Analysis

Scholars have classified tales using the Aarne–Thompson–Uther index of tale types, and Vladimir Propp's morphology identified recurring narrative functions across Russian wonder tales. Interpretations range from Bruno Bettelheim's psychoanalytic readings to feminist and historical critiques. Modern research suggests some tale types may be thousands of years old.
