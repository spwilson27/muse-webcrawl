Capital (Bourdieu)

Forms of capital (Bourdieu)
Economic, cultural, social, symbolic
Key essay: "The Forms of Capital" (1986)

In the work of French sociologist Pierre Bourdieu, capital is any resource that agents can accumulate and deploy within a social field. Bourdieu extended the concept beyond economics to explain how power and privilege are reproduced.

Forms of capital

Economic capital is money and assets. Cultural capital exists in embodied form (dispositions, knowledge, accents), objectified form (books, instruments), and institutionalized form (credentials). Social capital consists of resources available through networks of relationships. Symbolic capital is capital perceived as legitimate prestige or honour.

Significance

The theory explains how advantages are transmitted across generations — for instance through education — without direct inheritance of wealth. It was set out in the essay "The Forms of Capital" (1986) and works such as Distinction (1979).
