Virtual production

Virtual production is an area of media production combining conventional filmmaking with computer-generated imagery (CGI), augmented reality, motion capture, and real-time game engine technology. In its best-known form, actors perform on a soundstage surrounded by large LED video walls that display photorealistic digital environments in real time, rendered with correct parallax as the camera moves; the final image is largely captured in camera rather than composited in post-production.

Techniques

The central technology is the LED volume: high-resolution LED panels forming walls and a ceiling, fed by real-time rendering engines such as Unreal Engine. Camera-tracking systems report the physical camera's position so that the displayed background shifts in perspective, giving the illusion of depth. Lighting from the walls illuminates the actors naturally, reducing the mismatch typical of green-screen work. Other forms of virtual production include simulcam, which previews digital characters alongside live action, and fully virtual cinematography.

History

Precursors include rear projection and the front-projection effects used in films such as 2001: A Space Odyssey. The modern LED-volume approach was popularised by the Disney+ series The Mandalorian (2019), produced with Industrial Light & Magic's StageCraft system, whose success led to the construction of permanent volumes in Los Angeles, London, Toronto, and elsewhere. Virtual production also expanded rapidly during the COVID-19 pandemic, when location shooting was restricted.

Advantages and limitations

Advantages include creative control over time of day and weather, fewer company moves, and visual effects decisions made on set. Limitations include high stage cost, the need for assets to be completed before shooting, and moiré or colour artefacts when the camera and LED grid interact.
