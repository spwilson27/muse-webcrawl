Indian National Congress

The Indian National Congress (INC), colloquially the Congress Party, is one of the two major political parties in India, the other being the Bharatiya Janata Party. It was founded in 1885 and was a pivotal participant in the Indian independence movement, which it led under figures such as Mahatma Gandhi and Jawaharlal Nehru. The party's post-independence dominance earned it the description of a "big tent" party commanding support across India's social spectrum.

History

Founded by retired civil servant Allan Octavian Hume, the Congress first met in Bombay in December 1885 with 72 delegates, initially seeking greater Indian participation in governance. It evolved into a mass movement under Gandhi, leading campaigns of non-cooperation (1920–22), civil disobedience including the Salt March (1930), and the Quit India movement (1942). After independence in 1947, Nehru became India's first prime minister, and Congress governed almost continuously for decades.

Post-independence era

Under Nehru and later Indira Gandhi, Congress pursued planned economic development, non-alignment in foreign policy, and secular nationalism. The party split in 1969 and again after the Emergency of 1975–77, which led to its first loss of power at the centre in 1977. Rajiv Gandhi led the party to its largest-ever victory in 1984 after Indira Gandhi's assassination. From 2004 to 2014, Congress led the United Progressive Alliance government under Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, introducing welfare measures such as the rural employment guarantee (MGNREGA) and the Right to Information Act.

Recent period

The party suffered heavy defeats in the 2014 and 2019 general elections to the BJP but improved its position in the 2024 election as the largest member of the INDIA opposition alliance. The Nehru–Gandhi family has supplied many of the party's leaders, including presidents Sonia Gandhi and Rahul Gandhi; Mallikarjun Kharge became party president in 2022, the first non-Gandhi president in over two decades.
