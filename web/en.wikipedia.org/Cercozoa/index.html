Cercozoa

The Cercozoa are a group of single-celled eukaryotes, united largely by molecular phylogenies. They include many amoeboid forms with filose pseudopodia, flagellates, and the chlorarachniophytes, which possess green plastids acquired by secondary endosymbiosis.

Diversity

Cercozoans are abundant in soil and aquatic environments, where they feed on bacteria and other microbes. Some groups, such as the foraminifera-like retarians sometimes associated in the broader Rhizaria, form shells; plant-parasitic plasmodiophorids, such as the clubroot organism, are economically important.

Classification

Cercozoa belongs to the supergroup Rhizaria, together with Foraminifera and Radiolaria in many schemes. The group was established in the 1990s as molecular data revealed relationships among previously disparate protist lineages lacking obvious shared morphology.
