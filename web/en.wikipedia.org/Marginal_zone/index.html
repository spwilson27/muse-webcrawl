Marginal zone

The marginal zone is a region of the spleen situated at the interface between the red pulp and the white pulp (the lymphoid tissue), surrounding the periarteriolar lymphoid sheaths and follicles. It is a critical site for screening the blood for pathogens and antigens, and it hosts a specialised population of B lymphocytes known as marginal zone B cells. Comparable tissue, called mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue, exists in other organs.

Structure

Blood entering the spleen through the central arterioles passes into the marginal sinus and the marginal zone before draining into the red pulp. The zone contains a mixture of cell types: marginal zone B cells, macrophages of two specialised kinds (marginal metallophilic macrophages on the inner border and marginal zone macrophages within the zone), dendritic cells, and small numbers of T cells and neutrophils. In the human spleen the marginal zone is well developed, with an additional perifollicular zone; the rodent marginal zone is the most extensively studied experimentally.

Function

The marginal zone acts as a filter and immunological checkpoint for blood-borne antigens. Its macrophages capture particulate material, old cells, and microorganisms, while marginal zone B cells are poised to respond rapidly to blood-borne bacteria — especially encapsulated organisms such as Streptococcus pneumoniae — by producing natural IgM antibodies in a T-cell-independent response directed at polysaccharide antigens. Marginal zone B cells also shuttle between the marginal zone and follicles, carrying captured antigen to follicular dendritic cells and so helping to initiate adaptive immune responses.

Loss of splenic function (asplenia, as in sickle cell disease or after splenectomy) removes this protection and leaves patients vulnerable to overwhelming post-splenectomy infection by encapsulated bacteria, which is why vaccination against pneumococcus, meningococcus, and Haemophilus influenzae type b is recommended for such patients.

Clinical significance

The marginal zone gives its name to marginal zone lymphomas, a group of indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphomas thought to arise from marginal zone B cells. These include extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT lymphoma), splenic marginal zone lymphoma, and nodal marginal zone lymphoma.
