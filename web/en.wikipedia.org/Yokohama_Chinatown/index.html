Yokohama Chinatown

LocationNaka Ward, Yokohama, Japan
Established1859–1860s
Area~0.2 km²
Known forChinese restaurants and shops

Yokohama Chinatown (Yokohama Chūkagai) is the largest Chinatown in Japan, located in central Yokohama. It developed after the port of Yokohama opened to foreign trade in 1859, when Chinese traders and merchants settled in the foreign quarter.

History

Chinese residents, many from Guangdong, served as intermediaries and compradors for Western merchants. The neighbourhood was rebuilt after the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake and again after wartime bombing, growing into a district of hundreds of restaurants and shops.

Features

Ten ornate paifang gates mark the entrances. Landmarks include the Kanteibyō temple dedicated to Guan Yu, first built in 1862, and the Mazu Miao temple opened in 2006. The district is famous for dishes such as steamed buns, shumai, and shark fin soup, and hosts major Chinese New Year celebrations.
