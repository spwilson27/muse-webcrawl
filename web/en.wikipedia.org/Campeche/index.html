Campeche

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Campeche (Spanish pronunciation: [kamˈpetʃe]), officially the Free and Sovereign State of Campeche, is one of the 31 states that, with Mexico City, comprise the federal entities of Mexico. It is located in southeast Mexico on the western side of the Yucatán Peninsula, bordering the Gulf of Mexico. Its capital and largest city is San Francisco de Campeche. Campeche is Mexico's second-least populous state but one of its most important oil-producing regions.

History

The territory was part of the Maya civilisation, and major archaeological sites such as Calakmul, Edzná, and Becán testify to its Classic-period importance. The city of Campeche was founded by the Spanish in 1540 on the site of the Maya town of Ah-Kin-Pech. Repeated pirate attacks in the 17th century led to the construction of massive defensive walls and forts around the colonial port, which handled exports of logwood and chicle. Campeche became a separate state in 1863, split from Yucatán.

Economy

Offshore oil fields in the Bay of Campeche — including the giant Cantarell field, discovered in 1976 — long made the state one of Mexico's leading petroleum producers, and the island city of Ciudad del Carmen is a centre of the offshore industry. Agriculture (maize, rice, sugar cane), timber, fishing, and tourism are also significant; the walled historic centre of the capital was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999 for its outstanding colonial fortifications.

Geography

The state covers about 57,500 square kilometres of low-lying limestone plain and tropical forest, much of it protected within the Calakmul Biosphere Reserve, one of the largest tropical forest reserves in Mexico, and the Petenes and Laguna de Términos protected areas along its swampy coast.
