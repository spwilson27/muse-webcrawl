Dutch oven

A Dutch oven is a thick-walled cooking pot with a tight-fitting lid, traditionally made of seasoned cast iron, though modern versions may be made of aluminium, ceramic or enamelled cast iron.

History

The design traces to Dutch casting techniques of the late 17th century, which produced smooth, high-quality ironware; an English patent of 1707 by Abraham Darby adopted the Dutch sand-casting method. Dutch ovens became staples of frontier and campfire cooking in America, and were carried on the Lewis and Clark expedition.

Uses

Because they retain heat and cook evenly, Dutch ovens are used for braising, stewing, roasting, frying and baking bread. Camp-style ovens have short legs and a flanged lid designed to hold hot coals on top, allowing baking over an open fire.

In France, enamelled versions known as cocottes are standard kitchen equipment. The Dutch oven is the official state cooking pot of Utah and Texas.
