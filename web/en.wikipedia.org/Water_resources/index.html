Water resources are natural resources of water that are potentially useful to humans, for example as a source of drinking water supply or irrigation water. Water occurs as surface water in rivers, lakes, and wetlands, as groundwater in aquifers, and stored in glaciers and ice caps. Of Earth's roughly 1.4 billion cubic kilometres of water, about 97.5% is saline ocean water; of the 2.5% that is fresh, most is locked in ice or deep groundwater, leaving only a small fraction readily accessible in lakes, rivers, and shallow aquifers.

Sources

Surface water is replenished by precipitation and lost through discharge to the oceans, evaporation, and infiltration. Groundwater — water held underground in aquifers — supplies a large share of drinking water worldwide and is extracted by wells; some aquifers are "fossil" water, recharged in wetter climates thousands of years ago and effectively non-renewable. Desalination of seawater and wastewater reuse are growing supplementary sources.

Uses

Globally, agriculture accounts for about 70% of freshwater withdrawals, chiefly for irrigation; industry (including cooling for power generation) accounts for roughly 20%; and domestic use about 10%. Water is also used in-stream for hydropower generation, navigation, recreation, and maintaining aquatic ecosystems.

Stress and scarcity

Demand has grown with population and economic development, and many regions experience water stress: more than two billion people live in countries experiencing high water stress, according to the UN. Competition among users, depletion of aquifers such as the Ogallala in North America, pollution, and climate-change-driven shifts in precipitation and glacier melt are major challenges. Responses include improved irrigation efficiency, integrated water resources management, storage infrastructure, water pricing, and international agreements over shared rivers.

Availability measures

The Falkenmark indicator measures water availability per capita; below 1,700 m³ per person per year a country is said to experience "water stress", and below 1,000 m³ "water scarcity". Renewable resources vary enormously, from tens of thousands of cubic metres per capita in Canada or Brazil to a few hundred in parts of the Middle East and North Africa.
