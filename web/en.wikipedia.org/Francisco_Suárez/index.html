Francisco Suárez

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Francisco Suárez de Toledo Vázquez de Utiel y González de la Torre (5 January 1548 – 25 September 1617), known as Doctor Eximius, was a Spanish Jesuit priest, philosopher and theologian, one of the leading figures of the School of Salamanca and of the "Second Scholasticism". His work in metaphysics, law and political theory influenced Descartes, Leibniz, Grotius and later generations of philosophers.

Life

Suárez studied at Salamanca and entered the Society of Jesus in 1564, overcoming early doubts about his abilities to become an outstanding scholar. He taught philosophy and theology at Segovia, Valladolid, Rome (at the Collegio Romano), Alcalá, Salamanca and, from 1597 until his death, at Coimbra in Portugal, where his lecture courses formed the basis of his massive published work.

Philosophy and theology

His Disputationes Metaphysicae (1597) was the first systematic treatise of metaphysics written in the West that was not a commentary on Aristotle, and it structured metaphysics for Catholic and Protestant universities alike for over a century. Suárez defended a moderate realism about universals, a distinctive account of being by analogy, and theories of individuation and distinctions that were widely studied and criticized.

Law and political theory

The De Legibus (1612) developed a comprehensive theory of law — eternal, natural, human and positive — and treated the law of nations (ius gentium). Against James I of England, his Defensio Fidei (1613) argued that political authority derives from the community and is transferred to rulers, a doctrine with important consequences for later theories of popular sovereignty and resistance. His thought is a major source for Hugo Grotius and for early international law.
