Rolls-Royce Griffon

Type: Liquid-cooled V-12 piston aero engine
Manufacturer: Rolls-Royce Limited
First run: November 1939
Major applications: Supermarine Spitfire (later marks), Avro Shackleton, Fairey Firefly

The Rolls-Royce Griffon is a 36.7-litre (2,240 cu in), supercharged, liquid-cooled V-12 piston aero engine designed and built by Rolls-Royce. Developed from the smaller R-series racing engine lineage that also produced the Merlin, the Griffon powered the later marks of the Supermarine Spitfire and, in contra-rotating propeller form, the Avro Shackleton maritime patrol aircraft. About 8,108 Griffons were produced.

Design and development

Work began in the late 1930s on a larger engine following the dimensions of the 1929 "R" engine used in Schneider Trophy racing seaplanes. The Griffon combined a 6-inch bore and 6.6-inch stroke with single-stage and later two-stage supercharging, ultimately producing over 2,400 horsepower in late marks. Unlike the Merlin, its supercharger and magnetos were arranged so the engine retained compact frontal dimensions for fighter installation.

Service

The Griffon entered service in 1942, first in the Firefly and in the Spitfire Mk XII; the definitive combination was the Spitfire Mk XIV, whose performance matched the best German fighters at all altitudes. Post-war, Griffon 57 and 58 engines with contra-rotating propellers powered the Shackleton until 1991. Griffons remain popular in unlimited air racing and in restored warbirds.
