Peterloo Massacre

Date16 August 1819
LocationSt Peter's Fields, Manchester, England
Deaths18
Injuries400–700

The Peterloo Massacre took place at St Peter's Field, Manchester, Lancashire, England, on Monday 16 August 1819. Fifteen to eighteen people were killed and 400 to 700 injured when cavalry charged into a crowd of around 60,000 who had gathered to demand reform of parliamentary representation.

Contemporary engraving of the charge at St Peter's Fields

In the aftermath of the Napoleonic Wars, economic hardship, falling wages and the Corn Laws fuelled radical agitation in the industrial north. The meeting on 16 August was organised by the Manchester Patriotic Union and addressed by the radical orator Henry Hunt. Local magistrates, alarmed by the size of the crowd, ordered Hunt's arrest; when the Manchester and Salford Yeomanry attempted to seize the speakers, they became trapped in the crowd and drew their sabres. The 15th Hussars then charged to clear the field, cutting down demonstrators as they fled.

Aftermath and legacy

The name "Peterloo" was coined by the radical newspaper the Manchester Observer, ironically echoing the Battle of Waterloo four years earlier. The government responded with the repressive Six Acts, but Peterloo became a defining symbol of the struggle for democracy in Britain, commemorated by poets including Percy Bysshe Shelley, whose The Masque of Anarchy was written in response. A memorial now stands near the site in Manchester, and the event was dramatised in Mike Leigh's 2018 film Peterloo.
