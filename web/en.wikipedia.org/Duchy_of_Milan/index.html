Duchy of Milan

The Duchy of Milan in northern Italy

The Duchy of Milan (Italian: Ducato di Milano) was a state in northern Italy, created in 1395 when Gian Galeazzo Visconti received the ducal title from Emperor Wenceslaus. A constituent state of the Holy Roman Empire for much of its existence, it was ruled first by the Visconti family and, from 1450, by the Sforza.

History

The Visconti had dominated Milan since the late 13th century and expanded its territory across Lombardy. On the death of Filippo Maria Visconti in 1447, the short-lived Ambrosian Republic was proclaimed, but in 1450 the condottiero Francesco Sforza seized the city and founded a dynasty. Under Ludovico Sforza, Milan became a brilliant Renaissance court where Leonardo da Vinci worked.

Foreign domination

From 1499 the duchy became a prize in the Italian Wars, passing between France and Spain. The Treaty of Madrid and subsequent settlements left it to Spanish Habsburg rule from 1556; it remained Spanish until the War of the Spanish Succession, when it passed to Austria. Austrian Lombardy endured, with interruptions, until the Napoleonic era.

Dissolution

In 1796 French revolutionary armies occupied Milan; the Treaty of Campo-Formio (1797) abolished the duchy, incorporating its lands into the Cisalpine Republic.
