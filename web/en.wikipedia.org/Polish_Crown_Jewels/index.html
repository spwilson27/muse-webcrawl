Polish Crown Jewels

The Polish Crown Jewels (Polish: Polskie klejnoty koronne) were the regalia of the Kingdom of Poland, stored from the 14th century in the Crown Treasury at Wawel Castle in Kraków. The original set, inventoried in 1633, included several crowns, sceptres, orbs, swords and other insignia used at the coronations of Polish monarchs.

Fate of the jewels

During the Deluge (the Swedish invasion of the 1650s) the treasury was evacuated, but the jewels' ultimate destruction came after the Third Partition of Poland. In 1794, during the Kościuszko Uprising, the jewels were seized by Prussian troops; they were taken to Berlin and, in 1809, on the orders of King Frederick William III of Prussia, the crowns and other pieces were melted down, their gold and gems sold to finance the Prussian war effort against Napoleon. The destruction, discovered by Polish historians only later, is considered one of the great losses of Polish cultural heritage.

Szczerbiec

The only surviving original item is Szczerbiec, the coronation sword, preserved today in the Wawel Royal Castle Museum. A ceremonial sword dating from the 12th or 13th century, it was used at most Polish coronations from 1320 onward. Its name, meaning "the jagged sword", derives from a notch in the blade, around which later legends grew concerning Bolesław the Brave.

Replicas

Between 2001 and 2003 a team of goldsmiths created a reconstruction of the destroyed Crown of Bolesław the Brave and other regalia based on historical records; these replicas are displayed in Poland, including at Nowy Sącz. Modern Polish state ceremonies do not employ crown jewels, Poland being a republic, but the surviving and reconstructed regalia remain powerful national symbols.
