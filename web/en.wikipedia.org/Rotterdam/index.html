Rotterdam (Dutch: [ˌrɔtərˈdɑm]) is the second-largest city in the Netherlands after the national capital of Amsterdam. It is in the province of South Holland, part of the North Sea mouth of the Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta. Rotterdam's history goes back to 1270, when a dam was constructed in the Rotte. In 1340, Rotterdam was granted city rights.

The port of Rotterdam is the largest seaport in Europe, its oldest harbour areas dating back to the 15th century. From 1962 to 2004 it was the world's busiest port by annual cargo tonnage; it was subsequently overtaken by Asian ports including Singapore and Shanghai, but remains by far Europe's largest, handling hundreds of millions of tonnes of cargo annually via the Maasvlakte extensions at the North Sea.

History

The settlement grew around a dam on the Rotte river (hence "-dam"). Rotterdam became a major mercantile centre with the Dutch East and West India Companies, and by the 19th century its port was transformed by the digging of the Nieuwe Waterweg channel in 1872, opening it to large ocean-going ships.

On 14 May 1940, during the German invasion of the Netherlands, the Luftwaffe bombed Rotterdam, destroying almost the entire historic city centre and killing around 900 people. The city rebuilt itself after the war with striking modernist architecture rather than historical reconstruction.

Architecture

Rotterdam is known for its bold contemporary architecture: the Erasmus Bridge (1996, nicknamed "The Swan"), the Cube Houses designed by Piet Blom, the Markthal (2014) with its huge arched ceiling artwork, the Euromast observation tower, and the De Rotterdam complex by Rem Koolhaas's OMA. The Van Nelle Factory is a UNESCO World Heritage Site celebrated as a monument of Dutch modernism.

Culture and sport

The city hosts the Rotterdam Philharmonic Orchestra, the Museum Boijmans Van Beuningen, and the annual International Film Festival Rotterdam. Its principal football club, Feyenoord, plays at De Kuip stadium and is one of the Netherlands' "big three" clubs alongside Ajax and PSV.
