A pharmacist, also known as a chemist in Commonwealth usage, is a healthcare professional who prepares, dispenses and advises on the safe and effective use of medicines. Pharmacists are experts in drug action, interactions, dosing and formulation, and they serve as a key check between prescribers and patients.

Roles

Community pharmacists supply prescription and over-the-counter medicines, counsel patients, and increasingly deliver clinical services such as vaccinations, blood-pressure checks, smoking-cessation support and, in some jurisdictions, prescribing for minor ailments. Hospital pharmacists work within clinical teams, manage complex regimens such as chemotherapy and parenteral nutrition, and run medicines-optimisation and antimicrobial-stewardship programmes. Others work in the pharmaceutical industry, regulation, research and academia.

Education and regulation

Qualification typically requires a master's degree in pharmacy (MPharm) or a Doctor of Pharmacy (PharmD), followed by supervised pre-registration training and licensure with a national regulator such as the General Pharmaceutical Council in Great Britain or state boards of pharmacy in the United States. The profession descends from the apothecaries and druggists of earlier centuries, emerging as a distinct discipline as drug manufacture industrialised in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
