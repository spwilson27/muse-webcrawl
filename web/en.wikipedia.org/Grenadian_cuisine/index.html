Grenadian cuisine

Oil down, the national dish of Grenada, is a one-pot stew of breadfruit, salted meat and coconut milk.

Grenadian cuisine is the cooking of the Caribbean island nation of Grenada and its dependencies, shaped by African, Amerindian (Carib and Arawak), French, British and East Indian influences. It relies on local ground provisions, fresh seafood, tropical fruit, and the spices — above all nutmeg — for which Grenada is known as the "Isle of Spice".

National dish

The national dish is oil down: a hearty one-pot meal in which breadfruit, salted pig snout and other salted meats, chicken, dumplings, callaloo and root vegetables such as dasheen and plantain are simmered in coconut milk until the liquid is absorbed and the oil from the coconut settles at the bottom. It is traditionally eaten communally.

Other dishes and ingredients

Other staples include pelau (rice cooked with pigeon peas, meat and coconut milk), roti and curry of Indian derivation, lambi (conch), callaloo soup, saltfish, and "bakes" (fried or roasted dough). Nutmeg — Grenada is one of the world's largest exporters — flavours drinks, syrups and ice cream, while local cocoa, cinnamon, cloves and bay leaves are also widely used. The traditional under-the-counter rum of "river Antoine" style estates and spiced rums are part of the island's food culture, and nutmeg ice cream is a signature dessert.
