Korean barbecue
Place of originKorea
Main ingredientsBeef, pork, chicken; marinades of soy sauce, garlic, sesame oil, sugar
Typical dishesBulgogi, galbi, samgyeopsal

Korean barbecue (Korean: 고기구이, gogi-gui) is a popular method in Korean cuisine of grilling meat, typically beef, pork or chicken, often on gas or charcoal grills built into the dining table itself, so that diners cook the meat communally and eat it immediately.

Main dishes

The best-known dish is bulgogi, thinly sliced beef marinated in a mixture of soy sauce, sugar, sesame oil, garlic and pepper. Galbi refers to marinated beef or pork short ribs cut across the bone, while samgyeopsal — grilled pork belly — is usually served unmarinated and is among the most popular dishes in South Korea. Other favourites include dak-galbi (spicy stir-fried chicken) and jumulleok.

Accompaniments

Grilled meat is typically cut with scissors, wrapped in lettuce or perilla leaves with rice, garlic, and ssamjang sauce, and eaten in one bite, accompanied by an array of small side dishes called banchan, including kimchi. Meals are often paired with soju or beer.

History and spread

The tradition descends from the grilled meat dishes of earlier Korean kingdoms. Korean barbecue restaurants spread worldwide with the Korean diaspora and the growing global popularity of Korean culture, and the style has been widely adapted internationally.
