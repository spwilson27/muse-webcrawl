Kerala (Malayalam: കേരളം) is a state on the Malabar Coast of India, formed on 1 November 1956 by merging the Malayalam-speaking territories of Travancore, Cochin, and Malabar. Spread over 38,863 km², it is bordered by Karnataka to the north, Tamil Nadu to the east, and the Lakshadweep Sea (Arabian Sea) to the west. With 33 million inhabitants, it is India's thirteenth-most populous state but one of its most densely settled.

History

Kerala's spice trade with the wider world dates to the third millennium BCE; Phoenicians, Romans, Arabs, and Chinese all traded for black pepper and other spices at ports such as Muziris. The Chera dynasty ruled much of the coast in the early centuries CE. In 1498 Vasco da Gama's arrival at Calicut opened the European colonial era: the Portuguese were followed by the Dutch and the British, while Travancore and Cochin survived as princely states. In 1957 Kerala elected one of the world's first democratically chosen communist governments.

Geography

A narrow strip of land between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea, Kerala has a 590 km coastline, a network of brackish backwaters and lagoons, and 44 rivers. Its tropical climate brings heavy southwest and northeast monsoons. Anamudi, at 2,695 m, is the highest peak in India south of the Himalayas.

Society

Kerala is noted for social development indicators comparable to wealthy nations: the highest literacy rate in India (over 96%), the highest life expectancy, and the highest sex ratio. Its economy relies heavily on remittances from a large expatriate workforce, especially in the Gulf states, as well as on tourism, spice and seafood exports, and a growing IT sector. The "Kerala model" of development is widely studied internationally.

Culture

Kerala is the home of Kathakali dance-drama, the martial art Kalaripayattu, the temple art form Theyyam, and a major film industry — Malayalam cinema. The boat races of Onam, the state's harvest festival, draw visitors each year.
