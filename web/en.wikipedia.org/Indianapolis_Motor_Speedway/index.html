The Indianapolis Motor Speedway (IMS) is a motor racing circuit located in Speedway, Indiana, an enclave suburb of Indianapolis. Opened in 1909, it is home to the Indianapolis 500 and the Brickyard 400 and is the highest-capacity sports venue in the world, with total capacity estimated around 400,000 including infield spectators. Nicknamed "the Brickyard", the 2.5-mile (4.0 km) rectangular oval was originally paved with 3.2 million bricks, a strip of which remains at the start-finish line as the famous Yard of Bricks.

The Speedway's 2.5-mile oval, with the road course in the infield.

History

Built by Carl G. Fisher and partners as an automobile testing ground, the track held its first 500-mile race in 1911, won by Ray Harroun. Except during the world wars, the Indianapolis 500 has run annually and is part of the Triple Crown of Motorsport. The Brickyard 400 for NASCAR began in 1994, and the venue hosted the United States Grand Prix Formula One race on an infield road course from 2000 to 2007, and MotoGP from 2008 to 2015. Penske Corporation acquired the Speedway in 2019.

Facilities

The grounds include the Indianapolis Motor Speedway Museum and a golf course. The Speedway was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1975 and designated a National Historic Landmark in 1987.
