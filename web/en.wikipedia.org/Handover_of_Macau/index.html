Handover of Macau

The handover of Macau from Portugal to the People's Republic of China took place on 20 December 1999, ending more than four centuries of Portuguese presence and administration. Macau became a Special Administrative Region of China under the "one country, two systems" principle.

Background

Portuguese traders settled in Macau in the 1550s, and Portugal administered the enclave for centuries, with its status formalised by the 1887 Sino-Portuguese Treaty of Peking. After Portugal's 1974 Carnation Revolution, Lisbon moved to return the territory; the 1987 Joint Declaration set 1999 for the transfer, with autonomy guaranteed for 50 years.

Ceremony and aftermath

A midnight ceremony saw the Portuguese flag lowered beside President Jorge Sampaio and Chinese President Jiang Zemin. Edmund Ho became Macau's first chief executive. Macau's economy subsequently grew rapidly on gaming and tourism, and its historic centre was listed by UNESCO in 2005.
