Lightweight markup language

A lightweight markup language (LML) is a markup language with simple, unobtrusive syntax, designed to be easy to read and write in its raw form while converting cleanly to a full-featured format such as HTML. Lightweight markup languages emerged from conventions used in plain-text email and Usenet, where asterisks, underscores and indentation implied emphasis and structure.

Examples

Well-known examples include Markdown (created by John Gruber in 2004 and now the most widely used LML), reStructuredText, Textile, MediaWiki's wikitext, AsciiDoc, Org-mode syntax, BBCode and Creole, an attempt at a common wiki markup standard. Markdown's many flavours, such as GitHub Flavored Markdown and the CommonMark specification, illustrate the challenge of standardising these languages.

Characteristics

LMLs typically mark headings with leading symbols, lists with bullets or numbers, and links with bracketed text, and they generally cover only a subset of HTML features. Their documents are often published through converters such as Pandoc, which can translate between dozens of markup formats, making LMLs popular for documentation, readme files, static websites and note-taking.
