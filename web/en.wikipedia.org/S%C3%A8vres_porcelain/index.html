Sèvres porcelain is porcelain produced by the Manufacture nationale de Sèvres, one of the principal European porcelain factories. Founded at Vincennes in 1740, the factory moved to Sèvres, near Paris, in 1756 under the patronage of Madame de Pompadour and King Louis XV, who became its sole owner in 1759.

Characteristics

Sèvres became famous for its soft-paste porcelain with rich ground colours — including bleu céleste (turquoise), bleu lapis, and rose Pompadour — and for combining painted reserves with lavish gilding. It produced services for the French court and diplomatic gifts, and employed leading artists such as the painter Étienne Maurice Falconet.

Later history

In the 1760s–70s the factory began making hard-paste porcelain using kaolin from Limoges. It survived the Revolution, supplied Napoleon and later French heads of state, and today operates under the French Ministry of Culture, producing both historical reproductions and contemporary designs; its wares are held by major museums worldwide.
