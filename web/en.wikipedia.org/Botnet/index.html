Botnet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A botnet is a group of Internet-connected devices, each of which runs one or more bots. Botnets can be used to perform distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, steal data, send spam, and allow the attacker to access the device and its connection. The owner can control the botnet using command and control (C&C) software. The word "botnet" is a portmanteau of "robot" and "network"; infected devices are often called "zombies."

Architecture

Botnets are commanded either through a centralized client–server model — historically IRC channels or web servers — or through decentralized peer-to-peer structures that are harder to disable. Operators ("bot herders") infect computers, phones, and increasingly Internet of Things devices through malware delivered by phishing, drive-by downloads, or exploitation of default passwords.

Uses

  • DDoS attacks — overwhelming websites and infrastructure with traffic, sometimes for extortion or rent.
  • Spam and phishing — distributing junk email and scams at scale.
  • Credential theft and fraud — keylogging, banking trojans, and click fraud.
  • Cryptocurrency mining — secretly using victims' processors to mine coins.
  • Proxying — reselling infected devices as residential proxies to mask criminals' traffic.

Notable botnets

The Mirai botnet (2016), built largely from insecure IoT devices such as cameras and routers, launched some of the largest DDoS attacks then recorded, including one against the DNS provider Dyn that disrupted major websites across the United States. Other well-known examples include Conficker, Zeus, Emotet — described by Europol as "the world's most dangerous malware" before a multinational takedown in 2021 — and the Russian Business Network's Storm botnet.

Countermeasures

Defenses include patching and changing default credentials, ISP-level detection and notification, sinkholing of command-and-control domains by researchers and law enforcement, and coordinated international takedowns of botnet infrastructure.
