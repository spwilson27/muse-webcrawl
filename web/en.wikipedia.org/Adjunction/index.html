Adjunction

Adjunction is a term used in several areas of mathematics and linguistics for constructions that "attach" or pair structures together. In category theory, an adjunction is a pair of functors between two categories that stand in a particularly close relationship; in algebra, adjunction describes adjoining an element to a field or ring; in linguistics it describes a type of syntactic attachment.

Category theory

An adjunction between categories C and D consists of two functors F : D → C (the left adjoint) and G : C → D (the right adjoint), together with a natural isomorphism of hom-sets, Hom(FX, Y) ≅ Hom(X, GY), or equivalently unit and counit natural transformations satisfying the triangle identities. Adjoint functors arise "in all directions" in mathematics: free–forgetful pairs, tensor–hom adjunctions, limits and colimits, and Galois connections are examples. Daniel Kan introduced the concept in 1958.

Algebra

In field theory, adjoining an element α to a field F produces the smallest field F(α) containing both F and α, such as adjoining i to the reals to obtain the complex numbers.

Linguistics

In syntax, adjunction is the process of forming a complex constituent by attaching an adjunct (a modifier) to another constituent, as in some analyses of adverbial modification.
