Anti-austerity movement

The anti-austerity movement refers to the wave of protests, strikes and political campaigns that arose in many countries after the financial crisis of 2007–08, opposing austerity — government policies of spending cuts, tax rises and labour-market reforms intended to reduce budget deficits. The movement was especially prominent in Europe, where austerity was imposed most strictly in the eurozone crisis countries.

Course of the movement

Greece saw repeated general strikes and mass demonstrations from 2010 against measures attached to international bailout loans. In Spain the 15-M movement (the indignados) occupied city squares in 2011, later feeding into the party Podemos. In the United Kingdom, marches, student protests over tuition fees and the UK Uncut campaign targeted cuts by the coalition government. Portugal, Ireland, Italy and France also saw large mobilisations, and anti-austerity arguments influenced the global Occupy movement.

Political effects

The movement helped bring anti-austerity parties to prominence — most notably Syriza, which won the Greek election of January 2015 — and shifted public debate about the economic wisdom of rapid deficit reduction, a debate echoed by economists and by later revisions of IMF thinking on fiscal multipliers.
