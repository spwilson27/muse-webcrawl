Terraforming of Venus

The terraforming of Venus is the hypothetical process of engineering the global environment of the planet Venus in such a way as to make it suitable for human habitation. Venus's current surface conditions — temperatures around 464 °C and an atmospheric pressure 92 times that of Earth, with clouds of sulfuric acid — present enormous challenges.

Proposed methods

Carl Sagan proposed in 1961 seeding Venus's clouds with algae to convert carbon dioxide into oxygen, an idea later recognized as unworkable because of the atmosphere's density. Other proposals include building a solar shade at the Sun–Venus L1 point to cool the planet, precipitating out the carbon dioxide as carbonates or sequestering it, importing hydrogen to convert CO₂ into water and graphite, and removing atmosphere by impact events or mass drivers.

Challenges

Major obstacles include Venus's slow retrograde rotation (a solar day of about 117 Earth days), lack of a protective magnetosphere, and the sheer scale of its atmosphere. Some advocates instead propose floating habitats in the upper atmosphere, where temperature and pressure at about 50 km altitude are Earth-like.
