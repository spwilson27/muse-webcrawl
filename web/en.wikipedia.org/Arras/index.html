Arras

Country: France

Region: Hauts-de-France

Department: Pas-de-Calais

Arras is a commune in northern France, the prefecture of the Pas-de-Calais department in the Hauts-de-France region. It is known for its two grand squares, the Grand-Place and the Place des Héros, lined with baroque town houses, and for its belfry, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Arras was originally a Celtic settlement of the Atrebates tribe. It became an important cloth and tapestry town in the Middle Ages — the English word "arras" for a tapestry derives from it. The Treaty of Arras was signed here in 1435. During the First World War the town lay close to the front line, and the Battle of Arras was fought nearby in 1917; the Wellington Quarry tunnels beneath the town sheltered thousands of soldiers.

Landmarks

Highlights include the Hôtel de Ville and its belfry, Arras Cathedral, the abbey of Saint-Vaast, and the Citadel built by Vauban.
