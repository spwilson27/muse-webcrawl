The 1967 NFL Championship Game, commonly known as the Ice Bowl, was played on December 31, 1967, at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin, between the Green Bay Packers and the Dallas Cowboys. It is one of the most famous games in the history of American football, remembered for the severe cold and for its dramatic finish.

Conditions

The game-time temperature was about −13 °F (−25 °C), with a wind chill approaching −48 °F (−44 °C), making it one of the coldest games in NFL history. The field was frozen solid; the heating system beneath the turf malfunctioned, and the surface grew icier as the game went on. The referee's whistle froze to his lips on the opening play, and the marching band's instruments froze, forcing the pregame and halftime performances to be cancelled.

The game

Green Bay led 14–0 on two touchdown passes from Bart Starr to Boyd Dowler, but Dallas rallied to lead 17–14 in the fourth quarter, aided by a fumble return touchdown and a 50-yard halfback option pass from Dan Reeves to Lance Rentzel. With 4:50 remaining, the Packers began a final drive from their own 32-yard line.

The winning play

With 16 seconds left and the ball at the Dallas one-yard line, coach Vince Lombardi declined a field goal that would have tied the game. Bart Starr ran a quarterback sneak behind right guard Jerry Kramer's celebrated block on Jethro Pugh, scoring the winning touchdown. Green Bay won 21–17 and advanced to Super Bowl II, where they defeated the Oakland Raiders.

Legacy

The Ice Bowl cemented the legend of Lombardi's Packers and remains a byword for football played in extreme weather. Starr's sneak is among the most replayed plays in the sport's history.
