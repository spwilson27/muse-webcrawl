Dross

Dross is a mass of solid impurities floating on a molten metal or dispersed in the metal, including oxides and other material that forms on the surface of low-melting-point metals such as aluminium, zinc, lead, and tin. It is generally removed by skimming before the metal is cast, though in industrial processes it also forms inside furnaces and crucibles.

Formation and handling

Dross forms when molten metal is exposed to air: the surface oxidizes, and the oxide skin mixes with entrained metal and other contaminants such as flux residues. Aluminium dross, for example, can contain a large fraction of recoverable metallic aluminium mixed with aluminium oxide and nitrides, making it a valuable recycling feedstock; processors recover the metal by milling, salt-flux melting, or pressing while the dross is hot.

Environmental concerns

Some drosses are hazardous waste. Lead and zinc drosses, and salt slag from aluminium recycling, can leach toxic metals or release gases on contact with water, and are regulated accordingly in many jurisdictions.

Figurative use

By extension, "dross" is used metaphorically for anything worthless or of poor quality, as opposed to what is refined and valuable—a usage drawn from biblical and metallurgical imagery of impurities skimmed away in refining.
