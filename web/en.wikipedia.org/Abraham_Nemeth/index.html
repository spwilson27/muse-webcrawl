Abraham Nemeth

Born: 16 October 1918, New York City

Died: 2 October 2013, Grafton, Wisconsin

Known for: Nemeth Braille Code for mathematics

Abraham Nemeth (16 October 1918 – 2 October 2013) was an American mathematician and professor of mathematics who, though blind from birth, developed the Nemeth Code — the standard Braille code for mathematics and science notation used in the United States and many other countries.

Life and education

Born on New York's Lower East Side to Hungarian Jewish immigrants, Nemeth attended schools for the blind and initially studied psychology at Brooklyn College, discouraged from pursuing mathematics. He nevertheless earned a master's degree in psychology from Columbia University, taught himself advanced mathematics, and eventually completed a PhD in mathematics at Wayne State University in 1966. He joined the faculty of the University of Detroit Mercy, where he taught until retiring in 1985.

The Nemeth Code

Nemeth developed his Braille mathematics code beginning in the 1940s so that he could pursue his own studies; the code was adopted nationally in 1952 and first published by the American Printing House for the Blind in 1965. It allows mathematical and scientific notation — fractions, exponents, integrals, matrices — to be represented compactly in Braille, and it remains named in his honour. Later in life Nemeth also helped design the MathSpeak standard for rendering mathematics in speech, receiving several awards for his contributions to accessibility.
