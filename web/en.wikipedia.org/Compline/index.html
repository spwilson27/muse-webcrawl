Compline

Compline is the final church service of the day

Compline (from Latin completorium, "completion") is the final prayer service of the day in the Christian tradition of canonical hours, prayed at the end of the day before retiring to sleep. It is the last of the offices of the Liturgy of the Hours in the Roman Catholic Church, and equivalent night prayers exist in Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, and Lutheran practice.

Structure

The Roman rite of Compline includes an opening blessing, an examination of conscience and act of contrition, a hymn, psalmody (commonly Psalms 4, 91, and 134), a short reading, the responsory "Into your hands, Lord, I commend my spirit", the canticle of Simeon (Nunc dimittis) with its antiphon "Protect us, Lord, as we stay awake", a concluding prayer, and one of the seasonal Marian antiphons such as the Salve Regina.

History

The office developed in monastic practice; the Rule of Saint Benedict describes a concluding prayer before sleep, and by the early Middle Ages Compline had taken recognizable form as the seventh and final hour. Its emphasis on trust in God through the night and readiness for death gives the service a contemplative character.

Other traditions

In the Byzantine Rite, the corresponding services are Great Compline and Little Compline. Anglican practice includes a service of Night Prayer, and Compline has seen renewed popularity as a sung evening service in parishes and cathedrals; choral settings of its texts have been composed from the Renaissance to the present day.
