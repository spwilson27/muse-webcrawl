Norfolk, Virginia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Norfolk is an independent city in the Commonwealth of Virginia in the United States. At the core of the Hampton Roads metropolitan area, it had a population of about 239,000 in the 2020 census, making it the third-most populous city in Virginia. Norfolk sits at the mouth of the Chesapeake Bay, where the Elizabeth River meets the James River estuary, and is surrounded by water on three sides.

History

Founded in 1682 and incorporated as a borough in 1736, Norfolk grew as a major port exporting tobacco and timber. It was bombarded and burned in 1776 during the Revolutionary War, and rebuilt. During the Civil War the nearby Battle of Hampton Roads (1862) saw the first clash of ironclads, CSS Virginia — raised from the burned USS Merrimack at the Norfolk navy yard — against USS Monitor. The city resisted school desegregation in the 1950s under Massive Resistance, briefly closing its public schools.

Naval presence

Norfolk is home to Naval Station Norfolk, the world's largest naval station, headquarters of US Fleet Forces Command, as well as the Norfolk Naval Shipyard in neighbouring Portsmouth. The economy depends heavily on defence, shipping through the Port of Virginia, and tourism.

Education and culture

Old Dominion University and Norfolk State University are located in the city, as are the Chrysler Museum of Art, Nauticus and the battleship USS Wisconsin, and the Virginia Opera and Virginia Stage Company.
