Treaty of the Pyrenees

Signed7 November 1659
LocationPheasant Island, France/Spain
PartiesFrance; Spain

The Treaty of the Pyrenees was signed on 7 November 1659 on Pheasant Island in the Bidasoa River between France and Spain. Negotiated by Cardinal Mazarin for France and Luis de Haro for Spain, it ended the Franco-Spanish War that had begun in 1635 as part of the Thirty Years' War and continued after the Peace of Westphalia.

Terms

Spain ceded Roussillon and the northern part of Cerdanya to France, fixing the Franco-Spanish frontier along the Pyrenees, where it has largely remained. Spain also ceded Artois and other territories in the Spanish Netherlands. The treaty provided for the marriage of Louis XIV of France to Maria Theresa, the Spanish king's daughter, with a large dowry that was never fully paid — a failure France later used to justify claims on Spanish territory.

Significance

The treaty is conventionally taken to mark Spain's decline as the leading European power and the rise of Bourbon France to predominance on the continent. Because the ceded part of Cerdanya was defined as villages rather than towns, the Spanish town of Llívia remains an exclave surrounded by French territory.
