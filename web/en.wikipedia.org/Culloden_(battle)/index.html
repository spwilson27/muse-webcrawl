Battle of Culloden

The Battle of Culloden (Scottish Gaelic: Blàr Chùil Lodair) was fought on 16 April 1746 on Drummossie Moor near Inverness, Scotland. It was the final confrontation of the Jacobite rising of 1745: government forces under Prince William Augustus, Duke of Cumberland, decisively defeated the Jacobite army of Charles Edward Stuart ("Bonnie Prince Charlie"). It was the last pitched battle fought on British soil.

Background

The rising of 1745 sought to restore the exiled Stuart line to the British throne. After early successes at Prestonpans and Falkirk, the Jacobites advanced into England as far as Derby before turning back. By spring 1746, Charles's army — largely Highland clansmen, exhausted, hungry, and short of supplies — fell back to Inverness. A failed night attack on Cumberland's camp at Nairn on 15 April left the Jacobites tired and disorganized on the morning of the battle.

The battle

The Jacobites, about 5,000–6,000 strong, faced roughly 7,000–9,000 well-supplied government troops with superior artillery. On flat, boggy ground ill-suited to the Highland charge, Jacobite lines were battered by cannon fire before charging; although some clans broke through the first government line, musketry and bayonet drill — troops had been trained to thrust at the man to their right, under the targe — repelled them. The battle lasted about an hour. Jacobite losses were approximately 1,500–2,000 killed and wounded; government losses were around 300.

Aftermath

Wounded Jacobites were killed on the field and prisoners executed or transported, earning Cumberland the nickname "Butcher." The government then pacified the Highlands with measures including the Disarming Act and the Act of Proscription, which banned tartan and Highland dress (repealed 1782), and the abolition of heritable jurisdictions, dismantling the clan system. Charles escaped to France after months as a fugitive. The battlefield is now maintained by the National Trust for Scotland, with clan graves marked by stones erected in the 19th century.
