District of Columbia home rule

D.C. home rule

Key law: District of Columbia Home Rule Act (1973)

Local government: Mayor and Council of the District of Columbia

First mayor under home rule: Walter Washington

Ultimate authority: United States Congress

District of Columbia home rule is the ability of residents of the District of Columbia to govern their local affairs. As the federal capital, the District is under the exclusive legislative authority of the United States Congress under Article I of the Constitution, but since the passage of the District of Columbia Home Rule Act in 1973, Congress has delegated much of that authority to an elected mayor and a 13-member Council of the District of Columbia.

Background

For most of its history, Washington, D.C. was governed directly by Congress or by presidentially appointed commissioners. An elected territorial government existed briefly from 1871 to 1874 before being abolished amid financial difficulties. In 1967 President Lyndon B. Johnson reorganized the government under a single appointed commissioner. Residents could vote for President only after the Twenty-third Amendment (1961) and gained a non-voting delegate in the House of Representatives in 1971.

The Home Rule Act

Signed by President Richard Nixon on December 24, 1973, the Home Rule Act provided for an elected mayor and council, with the first elections held in 1974; Walter Washington became the first mayor under home rule. The Act reserves significant powers to Congress: District legislation is subject to a congressional review period, Congress controls the District's budget process, and Congress may legislate for the District at any time.

Limitations and debate

Unlike U.S. states, the District has no voting representation in Congress, and its residents' lack of full self-government has fueled a long-running statehood movement. Congress has periodically overridden local decisions, blocking or restricting District laws on issues from abortion funding to marijuana legalization, and a federal control board supervised the District's finances from 1995 to 2001.
