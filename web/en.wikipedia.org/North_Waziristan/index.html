North Waziristan

North Waziristan District
Country: Pakistan
Province: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
Headquarters: Miranshah
Area: 4,707 km²
Population: about 700,000 (2023 census, approx.)
Main tribe: Wazir and Dawar Pashtuns

North Waziristan (Pashto: شمالي وزیرستان; Urdu: ضلع شمالی وزیرستان) is a district in the Bannu Division of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, Pakistan. It borders Afghanistan to the west and South Waziristan to the south. Until 2018 it was an agency of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA). Its capital is Miranshah.

History

Inhabited principally by the Wazir and Dawar tribes, the area fiercely resisted British rule, producing figures such as the Faqir of Ipi, who waged guerrilla war against the British and later Pakistan. After 2001 the district became a stronghold of the Pakistani Taliban, al-Qaeda, and the Haqqani network, and a frequent target of United States drone strikes.

Operation Zarb-e-Azb

In June 2014 the Pakistan Army launched Operation Zarb-e-Azb, a major offensive to clear the district of militant groups. The operation displaced close to a million residents, many of whom returned only gradually as reconstruction proceeded. In 2018 North Waziristan, along with the rest of FATA, was merged into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, extending provincial law and courts to the district.

Geography

The district is mountainous and dry, drained by the Tochi (Gambila) and Kurram rivers. Its economy rests on agriculture, livestock, and trade with Afghanistan through crossings such as Ghulam Khan.
