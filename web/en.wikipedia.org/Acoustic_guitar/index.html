Acoustic guitar

An acoustic guitar is a musical instrument in the guitar family whose sound is produced by the vibration of its strings, amplified acoustically by the resonance of its hollow body, without electrical amplification. The term is often used to distinguish it from an electric guitar, which relies on electronic amplification.

Types

Common types include the classical guitar, with nylon (historically gut) strings and a wide neck, used in classical and flamenco music; the steel-string acoustic guitar, used in folk, country, blues, and popular music; the twelve-string guitar; and the resonator guitar, whose metal cones amplify the sound mechanically. Body shapes include dreadnought, jumbo, auditorium, and parlor sizes.

Sound production and construction

When a string is plucked, its vibration is transmitted through the bridge to the soundboard (top) of the guitar. The vibrating top and the enclosed air in the body radiate sound, with the sound hole projecting it outward. Tone depends on the woods used — spruce and cedar are common soundboard materials, with mahogany, rosewood, and maple used for backs and sides — and on the internal bracing pattern, such as the X-bracing developed by C. F. Martin & Co. in the 19th century.
