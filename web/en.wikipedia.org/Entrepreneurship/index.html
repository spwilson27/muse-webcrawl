Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying opportunities, organizing resources, and creating a new venture or innovation under uncertainty. Entrepreneurs may found small businesses, scale technology startups, develop social enterprises, or introduce new products inside existing organizations.

Concepts and theories

Economists have described the entrepreneur as an innovator, a bearer of risk, and a coordinator of production. Joseph Schumpeter emphasized creative destruction: new combinations of products, methods, and markets displace older arrangements and drive economic change.

Practice

Starting a venture requires validating demand, securing finance, hiring, complying with regulation, and adapting as customers respond. Access to capital, mentorship, infrastructure, and tolerant bankruptcy rules can influence how much entrepreneurship occurs in a society.

Forms and impact

Entrepreneurship includes necessity-driven self-employment as well as high-growth firms backed by venture capital. It can create jobs and diffuse technology, but failure rates are high and benefits are unevenly distributed. See also startup company, venture capital, and social entrepreneurship.
