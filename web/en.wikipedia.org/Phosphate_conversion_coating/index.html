Type: Surface treatment
Substrates: Steel, aluminium, zinc
Variants: Zinc, iron, and manganese phosphate

Phosphate conversion coating

Phosphate conversion coating is a chemical treatment applied to steel and other metal parts, in which a dilute solution of phosphoric acid and phosphate salts is applied by spraying or immersion, reacting chemically with the surface to form a layer of insoluble crystalline phosphates. Trade names include Parkerizing, Bonderizing, and Lubrizing.

Purpose

The coating's properties — high adherence and electrical non-conductivity — increase corrosion resistance, improve paint adhesion, and can hold lubricants for drawing, forming, and anti-galling applications on fasteners and firearms. Manganese phosphate coatings, known as Parkerizing on firearms, are used for wear resistance and oil retention.

Process

Parts are cleaned and degreased, then immersed in the heated phosphating bath, where free phosphoric acid etches the metal and phosphates precipitate as crystals on the surface. Zinc phosphate is widely used as a paint base in automotive manufacturing, while iron phosphate suits less demanding applications.
