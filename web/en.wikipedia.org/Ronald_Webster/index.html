James Ronald Webster (2 March 1926 – 9 December 2016) was an Anguillan politician who served as the first Chief Minister and later Premier of Anguilla. He is regarded as the father of the nation for leading the Anguilla Revolution of 1967, in which the island rejected rule from Saint Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla and ultimately became a separate British overseas territory.

The Anguilla Revolution

When Saint Kitts, Nevis and Anguilla moved toward statehood in association with Britain in 1967, most Anguillans feared domination by the government of Robert Bradshaw in Basseterre. In May 1967 islanders expelled the St Kitts police, and a referendum in July voted overwhelmingly to secede. Webster chaired the island's governing council and, after a further referendum in 1969, Anguilla unilaterally declared itself an independent republic. Britain restored direct control by an intervention in March 1969 (sometimes jokingly called a "Bay of Piglets" landing) and eventually agreed to Anguilla's separation, formalised in 1980.

Premier

Webster became Anguilla's first Chief Minister in 1976 under a new constitution, serving until 1977, and returned to office from 1980 to 1984, by then with the title of Premier following formal separation from Saint Kitts and Nevis. His later terms were marked by disputes over land policy and development. He remained a revered elder statesman; his birthday, 2 March, is a public holiday in Anguilla, and the island's main football stadium is named in his honour. He died in December 2016 at the age of 90.
