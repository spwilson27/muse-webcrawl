South Holland (Dutch: Zuid-Holland) is a province of the Netherlands located on the North Sea coast in the west of the country. It is the most populous and one of the most densely populated provinces, containing the country's largest port city, Rotterdam, and its seat of government, The Hague.

Geography and history

Much of the province lies at or below sea level in the Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta, protected by dunes and dikes and drained into polders; the rivers Lek, Waal and Maas cross it. Until 1840 North and South Holland formed the single province of Holland, historically the dominant part of the Dutch Republic; the split of 1840 (formalised in 1842) created the two modern provinces.

Economy and institutions

The Port of Rotterdam is Europe's largest seaport, anchoring a major petrochemical and logistics cluster. The Hague hosts the Dutch parliament and government, the International Court of Justice and the International Criminal Court. Other major cities include Leiden, Dordrecht, Delft, Gouda and Zoetermeer. The Westland greenhouses make the province a world centre of horticulture, while Keukenhof's bulb fields draw visitors each spring.
