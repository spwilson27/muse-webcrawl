The Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE) is a self-contained, individual field ration in lightweight packaging procured by the United States Department of Defense for its service members for use in combat or field conditions where organised food facilities are not available. The MRE replaced the canned Meal, Combat, Individual (C-ration) in 1981, and is the intended successor to the lighter Long Range Patrol (LRP) ration developed by the US Army during the Vietnam War.

Contents

Each MRE provides an average of about 1,250 kilocalories and contains an entrée (such as beef stew, chili, or a pasta dish), a side dish, crackers or bread with a spread (peanut butter, cheese, or jam), a dessert, powdered beverage mixes, condiments, and an accessory pack with items such as coffee, gum, and toilet paper. A flameless ration heater, activated by a small amount of water, uses an exothermic magnesium–iron reaction to heat the entrée.

Development and menus

Menus rotate over multi-year cycles, with 24 varieties in current production including vegetarian options. Entrées are cooked, sealed in retort pouches, and designed to withstand airdrops and rough handling, remaining shelf-stable for at least three years at 27 °C. Packaging is tan-coloured flexible laminate, and each case holds twelve meals.

Other uses

MREs are also distributed for humanitarian relief after disasters, and similar retort-pouch rations are issued by many other armed forces (such as the British Operational Ration Pack and the French RCIR). Surplus and commercial variants are popular with hikers, emergency preppers, and online reviewers.
