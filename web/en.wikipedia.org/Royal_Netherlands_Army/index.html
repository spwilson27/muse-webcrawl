The Royal Netherlands Army (Dutch: Koninklijke Landmacht, abbreviated KL) is the land branch of the Netherlands Armed Forces. Founded on 9 January 1814, its roots trace to the States Army of the Dutch Republic. The army is responsible for land operations and, together with the Royal Netherlands Navy, Royal Netherlands Air Force and Royal Marechaussee, defends the territory of the Kingdom of the Netherlands and contributes to international peacekeeping and crisis-management operations.

History

The modern army was established after the restoration of Dutch independence following the Napoleonic Wars. During the Second World War, the army resisted the German invasion of May 1940 before the capitulation that followed the bombing of Rotterdam. Dutch forces subsequently fought alongside the Allies, including in the liberation of the Netherlands in 1944–45.

During the Cold War, the army maintained a large conscript-based corps stationed in part in West Germany as part of NATO's Northern Army Group. Conscription was suspended in 1996, and the army became an all-volunteer professional force. Since the end of the Cold War, Dutch soldiers have served in peacekeeping and combat missions in Lebanon, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Iraq, Afghanistan and Mali.

Structure

The army's combat strength is organised into three brigades: 11 Airmobile Brigade, 13 Light Brigade and 43 Mechanised Brigade. The 43 Mechanised Brigade is integrated into the German Army's 1st Panzer Division, an example of the deep binational cooperation between the Dutch and German land forces. Support is provided by the Operational Support Command Land, and special operations are conducted by the Korps Commandotroepen.

Equipment

Main equipment includes Leopard 2 main battle tanks, CV90 infantry fighting vehicles, Boxer armoured vehicles, PzH 2000 self-propelled howitzers and Fennek reconnaissance vehicles. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the Netherlands announced significant reinvestment in heavy equipment and ammunition stocks.
