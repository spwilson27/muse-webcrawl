Denitrification

Denitrification is the process by which certain bacteria and archaea convert nitrate (NO3−) to nitrogen gases — principally dinitrogen (N2), with nitrous oxide (N2O) as a possible intermediate or by-product — thereby returning fixed nitrogen to the atmosphere. It is a key step in the nitrogen cycle, operating mainly in oxygen-poor soils, sediments and waters.

Process

Denitrifying organisms use nitrate as an electron acceptor in respiration when oxygen is scarce, reducing it stepwise: nitrate → nitrite → nitric oxide → nitrous oxide → nitrogen gas. Genera involved include Pseudomonas, Paracoccus and Thiobacillus. The process requires a supply of organic carbon or another electron donor and anoxic conditions.

Significance

In agriculture, denitrification removes plant-available nitrogen from soils, reducing fertiliser efficiency, and produces nitrous oxide, a potent greenhouse gas. In wastewater treatment, controlled denitrification is deliberately used to remove nitrogen from effluent and thereby limit eutrophication of receiving waters.
