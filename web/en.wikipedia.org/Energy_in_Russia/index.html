Energy in Russia

Energy in Russia describes energy production, consumption, and export in the Russian Federation. Russia is one of the world's largest producers and exporters of energy: it holds among the largest proven reserves of natural gas, ranks near the top in crude oil production alongside the United States and Saudi Arabia, and is a major producer of coal and uranium. Energy exports have historically been a pillar of the Russian economy and state budget.

Oil and natural gas

Russia's oil industry is dominated by companies such as Rosneft and Lukoil, with production concentrated in western Siberia and increasingly in eastern Siberia and the Arctic. Natural gas is dominated by the state-controlled Gazprom, which operates the vast Yamal peninsula fields and an extensive pipeline network. Before 2022, Russia supplied a large share of Europe's imported gas through pipelines including Nord Stream, Yamal–Europe, and transit routes through Ukraine. Following the Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the European Union sharply reduced purchases of Russian pipeline gas, and Russia redirected much of its oil trade toward China and India, while investing in expanded liquefied natural gas (LNG) capacity.

Electricity

Electricity generation in Russia comes mainly from natural gas, with substantial contributions from nuclear power (operated by Rosatom), hydroelectricity (notably large Siberian and Volga dams), and coal. Russia is a leading exporter of nuclear power technology and enriched uranium services. Renewable sources such as wind and solar remain a small share of generation.

Coal and other sources

Russia is among the world's top coal producers and exporters, with major basins in Siberia, including the Kuznetsk Basin. District heating, often supplied by combined heat and power plants, is widespread in Russian cities given the cold climate.

Policy and geopolitics

Because of the scale of its reserves and exports, Russia's energy sector is central to its foreign policy and to European and global energy security debates. Sanctions imposed after 2014 and especially after 2022 have restricted Russia's access to Western technology and markets, reshaping global energy trade flows.
