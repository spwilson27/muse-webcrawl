Fish oil

Fish oil is oil derived from the tissues of oily fish such as anchovies, sardines, menhaden, herring and mackerel. It is notable for containing the omega-3 fatty acids eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), which fish accumulate from eating microalgae or prey fish.

Production

The global fish oil industry processes millions of tonnes of small pelagic fish annually, largely from the anchoveta fishery off Peru and Chile. Whole fish are cooked, pressed and centrifuged to separate oil from fishmeal. Production is divided between aquaculture feed (the majority), direct human consumption as supplements and food ingredients, and industrial uses.

Health effects

Fish oil supplements became popular on evidence that omega-3 fatty acids reduce blood triglycerides; prescription EPA and DHA preparations are approved for severe hypertriglyceridemia. Large trials of over-the-counter supplements for preventing cardiovascular disease in the general population have produced mixed or negative results, and health authorities generally recommend obtaining omega-3s from eating oily fish. Cod liver oil is a traditional source of vitamins A and D.

Sustainability

Demand for fish oil has raised concerns about overfishing of forage species that support marine food webs. Algal oils and genetically modified oilseed crops are being developed as alternative omega-3 sources.
