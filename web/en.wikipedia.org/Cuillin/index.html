The Cuillin (Scottish Gaelic: An Cuiltheann) is a range of mostly jagged rocky mountains on the Isle of Skye in Scotland. The main Cuillin ridge is known as the Black Cuillin to distinguish it from the smoother, red granite Red Cuillin (Na Beanntan Dearga) to the east.

The Black Cuillin consists mainly of gabbro, a very rough black igneous rock that gives excellent grip, with summits of basalt. The ridge forms a horseshoe around Loch Coruisk and includes twelve Munros; its highest point, Sgùrr Alasdair (992 m), is the highest mountain on Skye. The full traverse of the main ridge — about 12 km with more than 3,000 m of ascent, including the rock-climbing obstacle of the Inaccessible Pinnacle ("In Pinn") — is regarded as the finest mountaineering expedition in the British Isles.

History and culture

The hills were first explored by Victorian climbers such as the Pilkington brothers and John MacKenzie of Sligachan, the first professional British mountain guide, after whom Sgùrr MhicChoinnich is named. The Cuillin feature in Gaelic folklore — the warrior Cú Chulainn is said to have trained here with the warrior-woman Scáthach — and in the poetry of Sorley MacLean.
