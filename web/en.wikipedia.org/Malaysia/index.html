Malaysia is a country in Southeast Asia consisting of two regions separated by the South China Sea: Peninsular Malaysia, on the Malay Peninsula bordering Thailand and Singapore, and East Malaysia, on the northern part of Borneo, bordering Indonesia and Brunei. It is a federation of thirteen states and three federal territories.

History

The Malay Peninsula was long a crossroads of trade, home to sultanates such as Malacca, whose wealth attracted Portuguese (1511), Dutch, and then British colonisers. British Malaya became a major producer of tin and rubber. After Japanese occupation in the Second World War, the Federation of Malaya gained independence from Britain in 1957 under Tunku Abdul Rahman. In 1963 Malaya joined with Sabah, Sarawak, and (briefly) Singapore to form Malaysia; Singapore was expelled in 1965. Malaysia endured the Malayan Emergency against communist insurgents and a confrontation with Indonesia in the 1960s, then industrialised rapidly in the late 20th century.

Government and society

Malaysia is one of the world's only elective monarchies: the Yang di-Pertuan Agong (king) is chosen for a five-year term from among the nine hereditary Malay rulers. The population is multi-ethnic — Malays and other indigenous (bumiputera) peoples form the majority, with large Chinese and Indian minorities — and multi-religious, with Islam the official religion alongside Buddhism, Christianity, and Hinduism. Since the racial riots of 1969, affirmative-action policies favouring bumiputera have shaped politics and the economy, which today combines electronics manufacturing, palm oil, petroleum, and tourism.

Geography

The country has a tropical rainforest climate, equatorial heat and humidity, and some of the world's oldest rainforests, home to orangutans, Malayan tigers, and the giant rafflesia flower. Mount Kinabalu in Sabah (4,095 m) is its highest peak.
