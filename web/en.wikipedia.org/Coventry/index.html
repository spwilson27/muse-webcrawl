Coventry is a city and metropolitan borough in the West Midlands county of England, on the River Sherbourne. It had a population of 345,324 at the 2021 census, making it the ninth-largest city in England. Historically part of Warwickshire, Coventry grew to be a major medieval city, famous for its cloth trade, and in the 20th century became a centre of the British motor industry. The city is associated with the legend of Lady Godiva, who is said to have ridden naked through its streets to protest her husband's taxes.

History

Coventry developed around a Benedictine abbey founded in 1043 by Leofric, Earl of Mercia, and his wife Godgifu (Godiva). By the 14th century it was one of England's largest cities, fortified with walls and visited by monarchs; its drapers' blue cloth — "Coventry blue" — was prized across Europe. In the 19th century the city turned to ribbon weaving and watchmaking, then bicycles, and finally motor cars: brands such as Jaguar, Triumph, and the Rootes Group made Coventry "Britain's motor city."

On the night of 14–15 November 1940, the city centre was devastated in the Coventry Blitz, a Luftwaffe raid that killed 568 people and destroyed St Michael's Cathedral. The provost's response — choosing reconciliation over revenge — made Coventry an international symbol of peace; the ruins were preserved beside Basil Spence's new cathedral, consecrated in 1962.

Modern city

Postwar reconstruction gave the city a pioneering pedestrian shopping precinct and a ring road. Coventry is home to the University of Warwick (on its southern edge) and Coventry University, and was UK City of Culture 2021. Its economy now mixes advanced manufacturing — including Jaguar Land Rover and the London EV Company — with education and logistics.
