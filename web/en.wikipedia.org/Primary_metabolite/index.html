Primary metabolite

A primary metabolite is a kind of metabolite that is directly involved in the normal growth, development, and reproduction of an organism. Primary metabolites are produced during the active growth phase of cells and are found, in essentially similar forms, across very many species.

Examples

Examples include amino acids, nucleotides, organic acids such as lactic acid and citric acid, vitamins, alcohols such as ethanol, and the building blocks of proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, and carbohydrates. Core pathways that produce them—glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and amino acid biosynthesis—are among the most conserved in biology.

Industrial significance

Primary metabolites are important products of industrial microbiology and biotechnology. Fermentation processes produce ethanol for fuels and beverages, citric acid for the food industry, and amino acids such as glutamate (for monosodium glutamate) and lysine for feed and nutrition at massive scale. Primary metabolites contrast with secondary metabolites, which are not directly required for growth and often serve ecological functions such as defence or signalling.
