Illinois River

The Illinois River is a principal tributary of the Mississippi River, approximately 273 miles (439 km) long, in the U.S. state of Illinois. It drains a large section of central Illinois, with a drainage basin of some 28,750 square miles (74,500 km²), and connects the Great Lakes at Chicago with the Mississippi, making it a vital inland shipping corridor.

Course

The river is formed by the confluence of the Kankakee and Des Plaines rivers near Channahon, southwest of Chicago. It flows generally southwest past Joliet, Ottawa, La Salle–Peru, Peoria — where it widens into Peoria Lake — Havana and Beardstown, joining the Mississippi at Grafton, a short distance upstream from St. Louis. The Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal links its headwaters to Lake Michigan.

History and use

Explored by French voyageurs in the 1670s, the river valley was home to the Illiniwek confederacy and later became a highway for settlement. The Illinois and Michigan Canal (1848) and its successor waterway established the Great Lakes–Mississippi connection; today the Illinois Waterway's locks and dams carry grain, coal and petroleum. The river is also noted for its backwater lakes, waterfowl habitat and ongoing battles with invasive Asian carp, addressed in part by electric barriers near Chicago.
