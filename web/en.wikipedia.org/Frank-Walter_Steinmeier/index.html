Frank-Walter Steinmeier

Frank-Walter Steinmeier (born 1956), President of Germany since 2017

Frank-Walter Steinmeier (born 5 January 1956) is a German politician serving as President of Germany since 2017. A member of the Social Democratic Party (SPD), he previously served as foreign minister from 2005 to 2009 and again from 2013 to 2017, and as vice-chancellor from 2007 to 2009.

Career

Born in Detmold, North Rhine-Westphalia, Steinmeier studied law and politics at Giessen and became a close aide to Gerhard Schröder, first in Lower Saxony and then, after 1998, as head of the Federal Chancellery in Berlin, where he was a key architect of the Agenda 2010 labour-market reforms. As foreign minister in Angela Merkel's grand coalitions he pursued Ostpolitik-style engagement with Russia and helped negotiate responses to the Iran nuclear programme and the Ukraine crisis; the "Steinmeier formula" for implementing the Minsk agreements in eastern Ukraine bears his name.

Presidency

The Federal Convention elected Steinmeier president on 12 February 2017, succeeding Joachim Gauck, and re-elected him in 2022 for a second five-year term. As head of state he has emphasised defence of democracy, remembrance of Nazi crimes, and social cohesion, including prominent speeches marking anniversaries of the Second World War and appeals against extremism.
