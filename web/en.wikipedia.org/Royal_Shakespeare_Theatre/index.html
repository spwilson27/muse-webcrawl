The Royal Shakespeare Theatre (RST) is a 1,040+ seat thrust stage theatre owned by the Royal Shakespeare Company dedicated to the English playwright and poet William Shakespeare. It is located in the town of Stratford-upon-Avon, in the West Midlands, England, on the banks of the River Avon.

History

The first Shakespeare Memorial Theatre opened on the site in 1879 but was destroyed by fire in 1926. The replacement theatre, designed by Elisabeth Scott, opened in 1932 and was the first major building in Britain designed by a woman architect. It was renamed the Royal Shakespeare Theatre in 1961 when the company received its royal charter.

Transformation project

Between 2007 and 2010 the theatre was transformed in a major redevelopment designed by Bennetts Associates. The new auditorium brought the furthest seat much closer to the stage, restored Art Deco features of the 1932 building, and added a rooftop restaurant, a riverside walkway and a 36-metre tower with views over the town. The theatre reopened in November 2010.

Productions

The RST is the principal home of the Royal Shakespeare Company, staging Shakespeare's plays alongside work by his contemporaries and new writing.
