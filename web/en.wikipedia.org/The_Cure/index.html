The Cure

The Cure are an English rock band formed in Crawley in 1976 as Easy Cure and later as The Cure after losing members. The band's primary songwriter and only constant member, guitarist and singer Robert Smith, has fronted the group throughout. Emerging from the post-punk scene, The Cure became defining figures in gothic rock with albums such as Seventeen Seconds (1980), Faith (1981) and Pornography (1982), then found mainstream success with pop hits in the mid-1980s and 1990s.

History

The debut album Three Imaginary Boys (1979) and the single "Boys Don't Cry" established the band in the post-punk wave. Smith's tenure briefly doubling as guitarist for Siouxsie and the Banshees coincided with the band's movement through the stark "trilogy" of dark albums to the psychedelic pop of The Top (1984) and the international breakthrough The Head on the Door (1985), with "In Between Days" and "Close to Me". Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me (1987) broke the US market ("Just Like Heaven"), and Disintegration (1989), featuring "Lovesong" and "Pictures of You", is often cited as their masterpiece. Wish (1992) produced the hit "Friday I'm in Love".

Legacy

The Cure have sold an estimated 30 million albums worldwide and are credited with shaping gothic rock and alternative music; Smith's smeared-lipstick, teased-hair image became iconic. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019 and continues to tour to large audiences, releasing the long-awaited album Songs of a Lost World in 2024.
