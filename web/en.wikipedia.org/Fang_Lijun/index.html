Fang Lijun

Fang Lijun (Chinese: 方力钧; born 1963) is a Chinese contemporary artist, one of the leading figures of the Cynical Realism movement that emerged in China after 1989. He is best known for paintings of bald-headed men — often resembling the artist himself — yawning, swimming or staring blankly, images that became icons of post-Tiananmen disillusionment and of China's new contemporary art on the international market.

Life and career

Fang was born in Handan, Hebei province, and graduated in printmaking from the Central Academy of Fine Arts in Beijing in 1989. Living in the artists' community at Yuanmingyuan near the Old Summer Palace, he developed his signature style: smooth, brightly coloured figures with shaved heads rendered against skies and water. His work featured in landmark exhibitions of Chinese avant-garde art in the 1990s, including the 1993 Venice Biennale presentations that introduced contemporary Chinese painting to a global audience.

Style and themes

The yawning, bored bald man — sometimes read as a rogue, a monk or an everyman — embodies the cynicism, detachment and suppressed frustration that critics identified in urban China after the suppression of the 1989 protests. Fang has also worked in woodblock prints, sculpture and ceramics, and co-founded art spaces and publications supporting independent Chinese art. His paintings have achieved major prices at auction and are held by museums worldwide.
