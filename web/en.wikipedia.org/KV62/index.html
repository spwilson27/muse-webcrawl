Tomb of Tutankhamun (KV62)

KV62 is the standard Egyptological designation for the tomb of the young pharaoh Tutankhamun in the Valley of the Kings near Luxor, Egypt. Discovered in November 1922 by Howard Carter, under the patronage of Lord Carnarvon, it is famous for being the most intact pharaonic tomb ever found — the only royal burial of the ancient Egyptian kings discovered with its grave goods largely undisturbed.

Discovery

Carter's team uncovered a descending stairway on 4 November 1922. On 26 November Carter peered through a small breach in the sealed doorway and, asked by Carnarvon whether he could see anything, replied with the famous words "Yes, wonderful things." The tomb had been robbed at least twice in antiquity, but the robbers were apparently caught and the burial resealed, and the entrance was later buried under debris from the cutting of the nearby tomb of Ramesses VI (KV9), which protected it from later looting.

Contents and layout

The tomb is small for a king, consisting of a corridor, an antechamber, an annexe, a burial chamber and a "treasury." It contained more than 5,000 objects, catalogued by Carter's team over ten years: gilded couches, chariots, shrines, the nested coffins and the gold funerary mask of the king, the quartzite sarcophagus, and everyday items from furniture to food and underwear. Only the burial chamber is decorated, with scenes from the funerary texts.

Legacy

The discovery triggered worldwide "Tutmania" and fed the popular legend of the "curse of the pharaohs." Most of the treasures are now displayed at the Grand Egyptian Museum in Giza; the king's mummy remains in the tomb.
