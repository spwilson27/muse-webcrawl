Christmas dinner

Christmas dinner is a meal traditionally eaten at Christmas, often the main celebratory meal of the festival. It is eaten on Christmas Day in many English-speaking countries, or on Christmas Eve in much of continental Europe and Latin America. Although its components vary widely by country and region, it typically centers on a roast meat served with seasonal accompaniments and a rich dessert.

A traditional British Christmas dinner of roast turkey with all the trimmings

United Kingdom and Commonwealth

The classic British Christmas dinner consists of roast turkey (historically goose or beef) with stuffing, gravy, cranberry sauce, roast potatoes, Brussels sprouts, carrots, parsnips, and pigs in blankets (chipolata sausages wrapped in bacon), followed by Christmas pudding flamed with brandy and served with brandy butter or custard, and mince pies. Christmas crackers are pulled at the table. A similar meal is standard in Ireland, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, though in the Southern Hemisphere it may be adapted with barbecued meats and seafood eaten outdoors in summer heat.

United States

American Christmas dinners often resemble Thanksgiving dinner, centering on roast turkey, ham, or roast beef, with mashed potatoes, sweet potatoes, green beans, and stuffing, followed by pumpkin, pecan, or apple pie and eggnog. Regional and ethnic traditions add dishes such as tamales in the Southwest, lasagna in Italian-American families, and the Christmas Eve Feast of the Seven Fishes.

Around the world

In France, the réveillon of Christmas Eve features oysters, foie gras, roast capon, and the bûche de Noël. In Germany, goose or carp is traditional on Christmas Eve; in Poland, the meatless Wigilia supper has twelve dishes; in the Philippines, the Noche Buena feast after Midnight Mass includes lechón and hamón; and in Japan, fried chicken and strawberry Christmas cake have become popular customs.
