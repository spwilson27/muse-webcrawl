Santiago de Querétaro (Spanish pronunciation: [sanˈtjaɣo ðe keˈɾetaɾo]), known simply as Querétaro City, is the capital and largest city of the state of Querétaro in central Mexico. Its historic center, with well-preserved Spanish colonial baroque architecture, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996. The city's name derives from the Purépecha word K'erhiretarhu, "place of the big rock", or the Otomí Ndämxei, "place of the ball game".

History

The city was founded on 25 July 1531, according to tradition after a battle between Otomí allies of the Spanish and local Chichimeca peoples in which an apparition of Saint James was said to appear. Querétaro prospered as a way-station on the road to the silver mines of Zacatecas and Guanajuato, and its landmark 74-arch aqueduct was built in the 1730s. The city played a central role in Mexican independence: the conspiracy that launched the movement was organized there in 1810, and Emperor Maximilian made his last stand and was executed on the Cerro de las Campanas in 1867. The Mexican Constitution of 1917 was drafted and signed in Querétaro.

Economy and geography

Located about 213 km (132 mi) northwest of Mexico City in the Bajío region, modern Querétaro is one of Mexico's fastest-growing industrial centers, with aerospace, automotive and data-center industries joining its traditional manufacturing base. The surrounding metropolitan area is among the most prosperous in the country.
