Sailfish

The sailfish is a species of billfish living in colder areas of all Earth's seas. Formerly divided into the Atlantic sailfish (Istiophorus albicans) and the Indo-Pacific sailfish (Istiophorus platypterus), the two are now generally treated as a single species, Istiophorus platypterus. The sailfish is named for its spectacular sail-like first dorsal fin, which can be raised or folded away into a groove along the back. It is among the fastest fish in the ocean and a prized game fish.

Description

The sailfish has a greatly elongated body, metallic blue above and silvery white below, with a long, spear-like bill. The first dorsal fin — the "sail" — is much taller than the body is deep and is flecked with black spots; it is erected when the fish is excited or herding prey, and folded into a groove at cruising speed. Two long pelvic fins and a stiff, crescent-shaped tail complete the streamlined form. Sailfish can change colour rapidly, flashing bright blue and silver patterns across the body, an ability thought to aid communication and confuse prey during group hunts.

A sailfish with its sail raised, hunting sardines near the surface.

Speed

Sailfish are famed for their speed. Older reports credited them with bursts of over 110 km/h, measured by timing line taken out by a hooked fish; more recent biomechanical studies suggest their true maximum sustained burst speeds are considerably lower, though they remain among the swiftest of all fish. Their bills and bodies are highly specialised for rapid acceleration in pursuit of schooling prey such as sardines and anchovies.

Behaviour and feeding

Sailfish hunt alone or in small groups, working together to herd schools of small fish toward the surface. They use their bills to slash, tap, and stun individual prey before turning to swallow them. Spawning occurs in warm waters, and females may release several million eggs in a season. Juveniles grow extremely fast, reaching a metre in length within their first year.

Fishing and conservation

Sailfish are a popular target of recreational anglers, and much of the sport fishery is now catch-and-release. They are also taken as bycatch in tuna longline and net fisheries, and populations have declined in parts of their range; the species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN. The meat is firm and is eaten in some regions, sometimes smoked.
