Cmax (pharmacology)

A plasma concentration–time curve; Cmax is the peak concentration, reached at Tmax

Cmax is the maximum (or peak) serum concentration that a drug achieves in a specified compartment or test area of the body after the drug has been administered and before the administration of a second dose. It is a standard measurement in pharmacokinetics, usually expressed in units such as µg/mL or ng/mL. The time at which Cmax occurs is called Tmax.

Measurement

Cmax is measured by sampling blood (or another fluid) at intervals after dosing and plotting the concentration–time curve. For an orally administered drug given at intervals, the curve typically rises during absorption, peaks, and then declines during elimination. The area under the curve (AUC) measures total exposure, while Cmax captures the peak exposure.

Clinical significance

Cmax is important both for efficacy and safety: a peak concentration that is too low may fail to produce the desired effect, while an excessively high peak may cause toxicity. Regulatory assessments of generic drug bioequivalence compare both AUC and Cmax between the generic and reference products. Cmax depends on the dose, the rate and extent of absorption, the formulation (immediate-release versus extended-release), food effects, and the patient's physiology.

For many analgesics, such as codeine, the onset of effect tracks the approach to peak concentration. After Cmax, concentration falls roughly exponentially with the drug's elimination half-life in the first-order phase.
