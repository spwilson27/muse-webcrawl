The Stations of the Cross or the Way of the Cross, also known as the Way of Sorrows or the Via Crucis, are a series of images depicting Jesus Christ on the day of his crucifixion, accompanied by prayers. The practice originated from the pilgrim route in Jerusalem — the Via Dolorosa — and developed as a devotion allowing those who could not travel to the Holy Land to retrace Christ's Passion in their own churches.

The fourteen stations

  1. Jesus is condemned to death
  2. Jesus carries his cross
  3. Jesus falls for the first time
  4. Jesus meets his mother
  5. Simon of Cyrene helps Jesus carry the cross
  6. Veronica wipes the face of Jesus
  7. Jesus falls for the second time
  8. Jesus meets the women of Jerusalem
  9. Jesus falls for the third time
  10. Jesus is stripped of his garments
  11. Jesus is nailed to the cross
  12. Jesus dies on the cross
  13. Jesus is taken down from the cross
  14. Jesus is laid in the tomb

History

Pilgrims had retraced Christ's path in Jerusalem since the early centuries of the Church. The Franciscans, custodians of the Holy Land sites from 1342, promoted the devotion in Europe; by the 18th century, popes fixed the number of stations at fourteen and authorised their erection in all Catholic churches. In 1991 Pope John Paul II introduced a "Scriptural Way of the Cross" drawn strictly from Gospel accounts, beginning with the Agony in the Garden and sometimes concluding with the Resurrection as a fifteenth station.

Practice

The devotion is prayed individually or communally, most commonly on Fridays during Lent and on Good Friday, with worshippers moving from station to station with prayers, readings and hymns such as the Stabat Mater.
