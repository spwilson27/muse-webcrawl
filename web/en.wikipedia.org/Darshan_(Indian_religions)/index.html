Darshan (Indian religions)

Darshan (Sanskrit: दर्शन, darśana, "viewing, seeing") in Indian religions, especially Hinduism, is the auspicious sight of a deity or a holy person. The term refers to the reciprocal act of seeing and being seen by the divine: the devotee gazes upon the image of a god or goddess and in turn receives the deity's gaze and blessing.

Practice

Darshan is a central element of temple worship (puja). Worshippers queue to pass before the sanctum, where the consecrated image (murti) of the deity is revealed, often at set times when the curtains are drawn back. Receiving darshan is believed to confer merit, protection and spiritual benefit. The concept also extends to viewing a revered guru, saint or sacred place.

In philosophy

In Indian philosophy, darśana also denotes a school of thought or system of philosophy — a "viewpoint" on reality — such as the six orthodox (astika) darshanas: Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa and Vedanta.
