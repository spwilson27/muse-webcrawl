Front de libération du Québec

The Front de libération du Québec (FLQ) was a militant Québec separatist group in Canada, active in the 1960s and early 1970s, that used violence in pursuit of an independent, socialist Quebec.

Active1963–1971
RegionQuebec, primarily Montreal
IdeologyQuebec nationalism, separatism, socialism

Activities

Founded in 1963, FLQ cells carried out more than 160 violent incidents, including bombings of mailboxes, military installations, and the Montreal Stock Exchange, as well as robberies to fund their operations.

October Crisis

In October 1970, FLQ cells kidnapped British trade commissioner James Cross and Quebec labour minister Pierre Laporte; Laporte was murdered. Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau invoked the War Measures Act, deploying troops and arresting hundreds. Cross was released in exchange for his kidnappers' exile to Cuba. The crisis effectively ended the FLQ, and mainstream separatism continued through the democratic Parti Québécois.
