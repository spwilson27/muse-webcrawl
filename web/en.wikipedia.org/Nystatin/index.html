Nystatin

ClassPolyene antifungal
Discovered1950
SourceStreptomyces noursei
UsesCandidiasis of skin, mouth and gut

Nystatin is an antifungal medication used to treat Candida infections of the skin, mouth, oesophagus and intestines. It was the first polyene antifungal, discovered in 1950 by Elizabeth Hazen and Rachel Fuller Brown from the bacterium Streptomyces noursei, found in soil from a farm in Virginia; the drug was named after the New York State health laboratory where they worked.

Mechanism and use

Nystatin binds to ergosterol in fungal cell membranes, forming pores that leak ions and kill the cell. Because it is barely absorbed from the gut or skin, it is used topically or orally for local infections such as oral thrush and nappy rash, rather than for systemic disease. It appears on the World Health Organization's list of essential medicines.
