The Book of Five Rings

The Book of Five Rings (Japanese: 五輪書, Go Rin no Sho) is a text on kenjutsu and the martial arts written by the Japanese swordsman Miyamoto Musashi circa 1645, shortly before his death. It has long been studied by martial artists and, in the West, by readers seeking lessons for business and strategy.

Structure

The work comprises five books, named for the elements of Buddhist cosmology: the Ground (Earth) Book, an introduction to strategy and the Niten Ichi-ryū school; the Water Book, on the school's techniques and spirit; the Fire Book, on battle and timing; the Wind Book, a critique of other schools; and the Void Book, on the intuitive understanding that lies beyond technique.

Musashi and his school

Musashi (1584–1645) fought and won more than sixty duels, most famously against Sasaki Kojirō on Ganryū Island in 1612. He developed nitō ichi, fighting with both the katana and the shorter wakizashi simultaneously. Retiring to Reigandō cave in Kumamoto in 1643, he wrote the book for his student Terao Magonojō.

Influence

Translations into English in the 1970s made the text a bestseller among executives, who read its maxims — on perception, timing and knowing one's opponent — as guides to competition, an application Musashi's emphasis on transcending fixed method arguably supports.
