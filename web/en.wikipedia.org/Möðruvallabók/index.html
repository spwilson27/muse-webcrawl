Möðruvallabók

ShelfmarkAM 132 fol
Datec. 1330–1370
OriginMöðruvellir, Eyjafjörður, Iceland
LanguageOld Norse
MaterialVellum

Möðruvallabók (AM 132 fol) is an Icelandic manuscript from the mid-14th century. It is the largest single surviving collection of Icelandic family sagas (Íslendingasögur) from the medieval period, and it is named after the farm Möðruvellir in Eyjafjörður, northern Iceland, where it was kept for a time.

Contents

The codex contains eleven sagas, including Njáls saga, Egils saga, Laxdæla saga, Eyrbyggja saga, Vatnsdæla saga, Hallfreðar saga, Kormáks saga, Víga-Glúms saga, Bandamanna saga, Droplaugarsona saga, and Finnboga saga. For several of these texts Möðruvallabók is the oldest or sole complete witness, making it one of the most important manuscripts for the study of Old Norse literature.

History

The manuscript was written around 1330–1370, probably by a single main scribe. In the 17th century it came into the possession of the Icelandic scholar Magnús Björnsson, and it was later acquired by Árni Magnússon, after whom its shelfmark AM 132 fol is named. It is now preserved at the Árni Magnússon Institute for Icelandic Studies in Reykjavík, having been repatriated from Denmark in the decades after 1971.
