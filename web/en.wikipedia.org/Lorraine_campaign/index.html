Lorraine campaign

The Lorraine campaign was the operations of the U.S. Third Army, commanded by General George S. Patton, in the Lorraine region of France from 1 September to 16 December 1944 during the Second World War. After the rapid Allied advance across France following the Normandy breakout, Third Army stalled before the fortified city of Metz amid logistical shortages, autumn rain, and determined German resistance.

Course of the campaign

Through September, fuel shortages halted Patton's advance, giving German forces under General Hermann Balck time to reorganize along the Moselle River and the old Maginot and Siegfried fortifications. Hard fighting around Nancy, Arracourt — one of the largest tank battles involving U.S. forces in the war, where the 4th Armored Division defeated German panzer brigades — and the grinding siege of Metz followed. Fort Driant resisted repeated assaults, and Metz itself, which had not fallen to assault since 451, finally surrendered on 22 November 1944, though some outlying forts held out into December.

Outcome

Third Army pushed on toward the Saar River and was preparing to breach the Siegfried Line when the German Ardennes offensive (the Battle of the Bulge) began on 16 December, forcing Patton to pivot his army northward to relieve Bastogne. The Lorraine campaign cost the Third Army heavy casualties, commonly estimated at around 55,000, and remains a study in the limits of logistics and weather on mobile operations.
