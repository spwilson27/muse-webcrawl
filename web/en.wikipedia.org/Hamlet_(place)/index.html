Hamlet (place)

A hamlet is a human settlement smaller than a village. Definitions vary by country, but a hamlet typically lacks its own church, parish administration or central place of worship and local government functions, which are provided by a nearby village or town.

By region

In Britain and Ireland a hamlet is a small settlement without a church, distinguishing it from a village, which has one. In many parts of continental Europe, hamlets are recognised subdivisions of municipalities. In the United States the term is informal in most states, but in New York a hamlet is an unincorporated community within a town. In Canada, hamlets can be designated settlements administered by municipal districts or counties.

History

The word derives from Old French hamelet, a diminutive of hamel, itself from a Germanic root related to "home". Historically hamlets often grew around a farm, mill, ford or crossroads, or as daughter settlements of a larger village.

Characteristics

Hamlets may consist of only a handful of houses with no shops or services. Because definitions are administrative rather than purely size-based, some designated hamlets are larger than some villages.
