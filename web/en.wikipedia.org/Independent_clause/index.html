Independent clause

An independent clause (or main clause) is a clause that can stand by itself as a simple sentence. It contains a subject and a predicate (a verb and, typically, its complements) and expresses a complete thought. For example, in the sentence "The dog barked," the single clause is independent.

Use in sentences

Two or more independent clauses can be joined by coordination — using a coordinating conjunction such as "and" or "but," or a semicolon — to form a compound sentence. An independent clause joined with one or more dependent (subordinate) clauses forms a complex sentence, and the independent clause provides the sentence's main assertion. In traditional grammar, distinguishing independent from dependent clauses is central to avoiding fragments and run-on sentences in writing instruction.

Related terms

The complementary term is the dependent clause, which begins with a subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun and cannot stand alone. In some frameworks, independent clauses are analyzed as root clauses in the syntax of the sentence.
