The Blue Nile (Amharic: ጥቁር አባይ, Ṭiqur Abbay; Arabic: النيل الأزرق, an-Nīl al-ʾAzraq) is a river originating at Lake Tana in Ethiopia. Together with the White Nile, it is one of the two major tributaries of the Nile; at Khartoum it joins the White Nile to form the main Nile, which flows north through Sudan and Egypt to the Mediterranean.

Course

From Lake Tana the river flows south, then west through spectacular gorges in the Ethiopian highlands — including the Blue Nile Falls (Tis Issat, "smoking water") near Bahir Dar — before turning northwest into Sudan, where it passes the Roseires and Sennar dams and reaches Khartoum after about 1,460 km.

Hydrology and importance

Though shorter than the White Nile, the Blue Nile supplies the majority of the Nile's water and nearly all of its fertile silt during the summer monsoon flood, historically the basis of Egyptian agriculture. Its flow is highly seasonal, peaking in August and falling to a fraction of that in the dry season.

Disputes and development

Ethiopia's Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, built on the Blue Nile near the Sudanese border, became the centre of a long diplomatic dispute with Egypt and Sudan over water security and the filling and operation of the reservoir.
