Theorem prover

A theorem prover is a computer program that establishes the truth of mathematical or logical statements by formal deduction. The term covers both automated theorem provers, which attempt to find proofs with little human guidance, and interactive theorem provers (proof assistants), in which a human guides the construction of a proof that the machine rigorously checks.

Automated theorem proving

Automated systems typically work in first-order logic or simpler systems, using methods such as resolution, superposition and satisfiability (SAT) solving. Provers such as Vampire and E have solved open problems in pure mathematics, though automated proving remains practical mainly for specialized domains.

Interactive theorem proving

Proof assistants such as Coq, Isabelle, Lean and Agda are used for the formal verification of mathematics, software and hardware. Landmark achievements include the fully machine-checked proofs of the four-colour theorem and the Kepler conjecture, and the verification of the CompCert optimizing C compiler.
