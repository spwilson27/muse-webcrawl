Ilulissat

Ilulissat, formerly Jakobshavn, is the municipal seat and largest town of the Avannaata municipality in western Greenland, located approximately 350 km (220 mi) north of the Arctic Circle. With a population of about 4,700, it is the third-largest city in Greenland. The town's name means "icebergs" in Greenlandic, referring to the enormous icebergs calved by the nearby Sermeq Kujalleq glacier into the Ilulissat Icefjord, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The area around the icefjord has been inhabited intermittently for more than 4,000 years by Inuit cultures. The Danish-Norwegian trading post of Jakobshavn was founded in 1741 by the merchant Jacob Severin and named in his honor. Ilulissat was the birthplace of the polar explorer Knud Rasmussen (1879–1933), whose childhood home is now a museum.

Geography and climate

Ilulissat sits on the shore of Disko Bay. The Sermeq Kujalleq (Jakobshavn Glacier) is one of the fastest-moving and most productive glaciers in the world, calving roughly 10% of Greenland's icebergs. The Ilulissat Icefjord was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2004. The town has a polar tundra climate with cold winters and cool, bright summers under the midnight sun.

Economy

Fishing, particularly for Greenland halibut and shrimp, is the main industry, alongside a growing tourism sector drawn by the icefjord, dog sledding, and whale watching. Ilulissat Airport connects the town with Reykjavík and other Greenlandic destinations. Notably, the town has nearly as many sled dogs as people.
