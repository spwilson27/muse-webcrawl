Requiem (Verdi)

Composer: Giuseppe Verdi
Composed: 1873–74
Premiere: 22 May 1874, San Marco, Milan
Text: Latin Requiem Mass
Occasion: First anniversary of the death of Alessandro Manzoni

The Messa da Requiem is a musical setting of the Catholic funeral mass (Requiem) for four soloists, double choir, and orchestra by Giuseppe Verdi. It was composed in memory of Alessandro Manzoni, an Italian poet and novelist whom Verdi admired, and premiered on 22 May 1874, the first anniversary of Manzoni's death, at the Church of San Marco in Milan. It is widely considered Verdi's greatest concert work; the conductor Hans von Bülow famously (and later ruefully) called it "an opera in ecclesiastical dress."

Verdi's Requiem is renowned for its dramatic force, especially the Dies irae.

Background and structure

Verdi had earlier proposed a collaborative Requiem for Gioachino Rossini in 1868, contributing a Libera me; the project collapsed, but he reused that movement in the Manzoni Requiem. The work follows the traditional mass for the dead: Requiem aeternam and Kyrie; the long Dies irae sequence, whose terrifying opening — pounding bass drum and full orchestra depicting the Day of Judgment — returns as a structural refrain; the Offertorio; Sanctus (a double fugue); Agnus Dei; Lux aeterna; and the concluding Libera me, with its whispered opening and dramatic soprano recitative.

Reception

The premiere, conducted by Verdi himself, was a triumph, and he soon took the work to Paris, London, and Vienna. Johannes Brahms wrote that "only a genius could have written such a work." Critics debated whether its theatricality suited a liturgical text, but the Requiem's emotional power has made it one of the most frequently performed large choral works. In a notable chapter of its history, performances were organized by prisoners at the Theresienstadt concentration camp under conductor Rafael Schächter, who saw the work as a coded act of defiance.
