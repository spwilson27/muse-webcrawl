GLUT4

Glucose transporter type 4
Gene: SLC2A4
Organism: Human
Chromosome: 17p13
Function: Insulin-regulated glucose transporter

Glucose transporter type 4 (GLUT4), also known as solute carrier family 2, facilitated glucose transporter member 4, is a protein encoded, in humans, by the SLC2A4 gene. GLUT4 is the insulin-regulated glucose transporter found primarily in adipose tissues and striated muscle (skeletal and cardiac). The protein was first reported by David James in 1988.

At the cell surface, GLUT4 facilitates the passive diffusion of circulating glucose down its concentration gradient into fat and muscle cells. Unlike most other glucose transporters, GLUT4 is stored in intracellular vesicles in the absence of insulin and is rapidly translocated to the plasma membrane when insulin binds to receptors on the cell surface.

Regulation

Insulin signalling through the PI3K–Akt pathway (including phosphorylation of AS160/TBC1D4) triggers the movement of GLUT4 storage vesicles to the cell membrane, where they fuse and expose the transporter, greatly increasing glucose uptake. Muscle contraction during exercise stimulates GLUT4 translocation through an insulin-independent pathway involving AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), which is one reason physical activity lowers blood glucose.

Clinical significance

Impaired GLUT4 translocation in adipose tissue and skeletal muscle is a key feature of insulin resistance in type 2 diabetes mellitus and obesity; in adipose tissue, GLUT4 expression itself is reduced, contributing to systemic insulin resistance. Drugs and interventions that improve GLUT4 trafficking or expression — including thiazolidinediones and exercise training — improve glycaemic control. Complete disruption of the gene in mouse models produces insulin resistance and cardiac hypertrophy.
