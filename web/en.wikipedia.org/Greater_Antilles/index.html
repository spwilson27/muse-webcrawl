Greater Antilles

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Greater Antilles (Spanish: Antillas Mayores) is a grouping of the larger islands of the Caribbean Sea, comprising Cuba, Hispaniola (shared by Haiti and the Dominican Republic), Puerto Rico, Jamaica, and sometimes the Cayman Islands. Together with the Lesser Antilles and the Lucayan Archipelago, they make up the West Indies.

Geography

The Greater Antilles form the northern rim of the Caribbean Sea, running from Cuba, just south of Florida, southeastward to Puerto Rico. They are mostly continental in origin — raised portions of the North American and Caribbean plate boundary — and are correspondingly mountainous: Pico Duarte in the Dominican Republic reaches 3,098 m, the highest point in the Caribbean, and Cuba's Sierra Maestra and Jamaica's Blue Mountains are substantial ranges. The islands account for the great majority of the Caribbean's land area and population.

History

Before European contact the islands were inhabited by Taíno and other Arawak-speaking peoples (with Carib communities in the east). Hispaniola was the site of Columbus's first landfalls and colonies from 1492–93, and the islands became the launching ground for Spanish colonisation of the Americas. Their history was shaped by indigenous collapse, African slavery and plantation economies (sugar especially), and successive colonial rule by Spain, France, and Britain. Today the Greater Antilles contain the independent states of Cuba, Haiti, the Dominican Republic, and Jamaica, plus the U.S. territory of Puerto Rico.
