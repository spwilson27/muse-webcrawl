Simply connected space

In topology, a simply connected space is a path-connected topological space in which every loop can be continuously contracted to a point. Equivalently, a space is simply connected if its fundamental group is trivial. Simply connected spaces are, informally, spaces made of a single piece with no "holes" that obstruct the shrinking of loops.

Definition

A topological space X is called simply connected if it is path-connected and every continuous map from the unit circle into X can be extended to a continuous map from the closed unit disk. This is equivalent to requiring the fundamental group of X, taken at any base point, to be the trivial group.

Examples

The Euclidean plane, the 2-sphere, and higher-dimensional spheres are simply connected. A torus is not simply connected, because a loop encircling its hole cannot be contracted to a point. The real projective plane is also not simply connected: its fundamental group is the cyclic group of order two.

Properties

Every covering space of a simply connected space is trivial. A simply connected, locally path-connected space serves as the universal cover of any of its quotients in the theory of covering spaces. Riemann surfaces that are simply connected are classified by the uniformization theorem as the Riemann sphere, the complex plane, or the unit disk.
