Liu Yan (Han dynasty warlord)

Liu Yan (Chinese: 劉焉; died AD 194), courtesy name Junlang, was a politician and warlord who lived in the late Eastern Han dynasty of China. A descendant of the imperial house of Han, he served as the governor of Yi Province (roughly covering present-day Sichuan and Chongqing), where he built up a semi-independent regime that his son Liu Zhang later inherited.

Life

Liu Yan was a descendant of Liu Yu, the fourth son of Emperor Jing of Han. He served in various offices at the Han imperial court, including as Minister of Ceremonies and as a provincial governor. In AD 188, as the authority of the central government crumbled amid court factionalism and rebellion, Liu Yan proposed that the court appoint officials of heavyweight status as provincial governors (州牧, zhou mu) with greater powers to restore order. The proposal was accepted, and Liu Yan himself secured appointment as Governor of Yi Province, a remote and wealthy southwestern region.

In Yi Province, Liu Yan consolidated his power. He suppressed local rebellions, recruited refugee soldiers from the Nanyang and Guanzhong regions to form the Dongzhou Corps (東州兵), and dealt harshly with powerful local gentry families who challenged his authority. He also had dealings with Zhang Lu, a leader of the Way of the Five Pecks of Rice religious movement, whom he used — and later failed to control — in the Hanzhong region.

Death and succession

Liu Yan's ambitions reportedly extended to imperial trappings, which drew suspicion at court. His elder sons in the capital suffered as a result of plots connected to him. Liu Yan died in 194, traditionally said to be of grief and illness after a fire destroyed his capital at Mianzhu and after the deaths of his sons. He was succeeded as governor of Yi Province by his youngest son, Liu Zhang, whose weak rule eventually led to the province falling to Liu Bei in 214.
