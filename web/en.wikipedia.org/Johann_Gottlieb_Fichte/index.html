Johann Gottlieb Fichte

J. G. Fichte
Born19 May 1762, Rammenau, Saxony
Died27 January 1814, Berlin, Prussia
SchoolGerman idealism

Johann Gottlieb Fichte (1762–1814) was a German philosopher, one of the founding figures of German idealism, which developed from the theoretical and ethical writings of Immanuel Kant.

Philosophy

Fichte's Wissenschaftslehre (Doctrine of Science), first presented at Jena in 1794, begins from the self-positing I and attempts to derive experience from the activity of the self-conscious subject. His anonymous Attempt at a Critique of All Revelation (1792) was initially mistaken for a work by Kant.

Later career

Dismissed from Jena amid an atheism controversy, Fichte moved to Berlin. His Addresses to the German Nation (1808), delivered under French occupation, became influential texts of German nationalism. He was the first rector of the University of Berlin.
