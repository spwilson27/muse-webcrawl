Parasite

A parasite is an organism that lives on or inside another organism, the host, deriving nutrients at the host's expense. Parasitism is one of the most successful modes of life on Earth: parasites are found in nearly every major group of organisms, from viruses and bacteria to fungi, plants, and animals, and they may make up a large fraction of all species.

Types

Ectoparasites, such as lice, fleas, ticks, and many mites, live on the host's surface. Endoparasites, including tapeworms, flukes, and the protozoa that cause malaria, live inside the body. Some parasites have complex life cycles requiring several hosts; others, like parasitoid wasps, eventually kill their hosts. Brood parasites, such as cuckoos, exploit the parental care of other species rather than feeding on them directly.

Effects and ecology

Parasites can cause disease (parasitosis), reduce host fitness, and alter host behaviour to aid transmission. They are powerful ecological and evolutionary forces, thought to drive sexual reproduction and immune diversity through host–parasite coevolution, the "Red Queen" dynamic.

Human parasites

Important human parasites include Plasmodium species (malaria), parasitic worms (helminths) such as roundworms and schistosomes, and ectoparasites such as head lice and scabies mites. The study of parasites is parasitology.
