Relative risk reduction

Relative risk reduction (RRR) is a measure used in epidemiology and clinical research describing the proportional reduction in the rate of a harmful outcome in an exposed or treated group compared with a control group.

Calculation

If the event rate in the control group is CER and in the experimental group is EER, then the relative risk is EER/CER and the relative risk reduction is 1 minus the relative risk, equivalently (CER − EER)/CER.

Interpretation

Relative risk reduction can make a treatment effect appear large even when the absolute benefit is small — for example, halving a risk from 0.2% to 0.1% is a 50% relative reduction but an absolute risk reduction of only 0.1 percentage points. For this reason it is generally recommended that relative measures be reported together with absolute risk reduction and the number needed to treat.
