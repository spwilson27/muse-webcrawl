Panoramic photography

Panoramic photography is a technique of photography, using specialised equipment or software, that captures images with horizontally elongated fields of view. Images with an aspect ratio of at least 2:1 are commonly considered panoramic, and some reach 10:1 or more, covering fields of view up to a full 360 degrees.

History

Panoramas were popularised in the 19th century, beginning with painted panoramas and then with early cameras that produced wide views of battlefields, cities and landscapes. Dedicated film cameras followed, including rotating-lens designs such as the Cirkut camera, swing-lens cameras, and fixed wide-angle panoramic models in 35 mm and medium formats.

Techniques

Modern methods include stitching: taking a series of overlapping frames — ideally rotating the camera about the lens's entrance pupil on a panoramic tripod head to avoid parallax error — and merging them with software. Single-shot solutions include catadioptric lenses, rotating slit cameras, and the panorama modes built into smartphones, which sweep the camera during exposure or automatically stitch frames. Immersive 360° panoramas are widely used in virtual tours and street-level mapping services.
