Andronovo culture

The Andronovo culture is a collection of closely related Bronze Age archaeological cultures of the Eurasian steppe and forest-steppe, flourishing roughly from 2000 to 1450 BC (with related later phases extending toward 900 BC), spanning from the Ural Mountains and western Siberia across Kazakhstan to the Yenisei River and south toward Central Asia. Named after the village of Andronovo in Siberia, where burials were first excavated in 1914, the horizon is characterized by fortified and unfortified settlements, pastoralism supplemented by farming, advanced bronze metallurgy, and flat-bottomed pottery with geometric incised ornament. Most scholars associate the Andronovo peoples with early Indo-Iranian speakers.

Subgroups and chronology

Archaeologists commonly divide the horizon into an earlier Alakul phase (c. 2000–1700 BC) in the Trans-Urals and northern Kazakhstan and a Fedorovo phase (c. 1700–1300 BC) extending further east, with the southern Alekseevka–Sargary cultures continuing latest. The preceding Sintashta culture (c. 2100–1800 BC), with its fortified circular towns such as Arkaim and the earliest known spoke-wheeled war chariots, is often treated as the formative stage of the same tradition.

Economy and society

Andronovo communities raised cattle, sheep, goats, and horses, practised some floodplain agriculture, and worked rich copper and tin deposits (notably in the Altai and central Kazakhstan) into tools, weapons, and ornaments traded over long distances. Settlements of semi-subterranean timber houses stood along rivers; cemeteries contained inhumations in stone-lined or timbered graves, sometimes with sacrificed horses and chariot gear, and — especially in the Fedorovo area — cremations. Horse sacrifice, fire ritual, and soma/haoma-like cult practices inferred from later Indo-Iranian religion are often projected back onto Andronovo society.

Genetics and Indo-Iranian identification

Ancient DNA studies show Andronovo populations descended largely from Sintashta/steppe Middle-Late Bronze Age groups, themselves combining Corded Ware-related and Yamnaya-related ancestry, with additional admixture from European farmers and, eastward, Siberian hunter-gatherers. Their genetic profile matches the "Steppe MLBA" ancestry that later spread into Central and South Asia, corroborating the traditional linguistic identification of Andronovo with the eastward expansion of Indo-Iranian languages — toward the Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex and ultimately Iron Age Iran and Vedic India.
