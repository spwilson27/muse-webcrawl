Mitra

Mitra (Sanskrit: मित्र, Mitrá) is a Vedic deity of friendship, contracts, and the morning sun, one of the Adityas in Hinduism. Together with Varuna, Mitra upholds ṛta, the cosmic and moral order, and watches over oaths, alliances, and honest dealings among men.

In the Rigveda

Mitra is invoked in the Rigveda, most often jointly with Varuna in the dual compound Mitrā́váruṇā. Only one hymn (RV 3.59) is addressed to Mitra alone. Where Varuna is associated with night, cosmic law, and binding punishment, Mitra is linked with daylight, benevolence, and the friendly ordering of human society. The word mitra in Sanskrit also means "friend," a meaning the god embodies.

Indo-Iranian origins

Mitra descends from the Proto-Indo-Iranian deity *Mitra, "contract," whose Iranian counterpart is Mithra, a major yazata of Zoroastrianism. A treaty between the Hittites and the Mitanni, dating to roughly the 14th century BC, invokes Mitra alongside Varuna, Indra, and the Nasatyas as divine witnesses — one of the earliest attestations of Vedic gods anywhere.

Later Hinduism

In later Hindu tradition Mitra's independent importance waned, and he remains chiefly one of the twelve Adityas, solar deities who are sons of the goddess Aditi. In the epics and Puranas each Aditya presides over a month of the year, and Mitra retains his associations with light, friendship, and the sanctity of agreements.
