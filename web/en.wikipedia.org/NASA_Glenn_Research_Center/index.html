Glenn Research Center


Labelled image: NASA Glenn Research Center, Cleveland

The NASA John H. Glenn Research Center at Lewis Field is a NASA center located in the cities of Brook Park and Cleveland in Ohio, United States, with an additional facility, the Neil A. Armstrong Test Facility (formerly Plum Brook Station), near Sandusky. Glenn is one of ten major NASA field centers. Its primary mission areas are aeronautics propulsion, space propulsion and power, communications, and microgravity science.

History

Founded in 1941 as part of the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA), the laboratory was successively known as the Flight Propulsion Research Laboratory and, from 1949, for NACA director George W. Lewis. It made major contributions to jet engine development, including early work on the icing research tunnel, altitude wind tunnels, and the Centaur upper-stage rocket. In 1999 it was renamed for astronaut and senator John Glenn, the first American to orbit the Earth.

Facilities and work

Glenn hosts the Space Power Facility, the world's largest thermal vacuum chamber; the Electric Propulsion Laboratory, which developed the NEXT ion thruster; the Icing Research Tunnel; and the 10×10 supersonic wind tunnel. The center leads NASA's work on solar electric propulsion used by the DART mission, aeronautics electrified aircraft propulsion, and the communications architecture for lunar exploration through the SCaN program.
