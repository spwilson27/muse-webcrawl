HafenCity is a quarter (Stadtteil) in the district of Hamburg-Mitte, Hamburg, Germany, and the site of one of the largest urban redevelopment projects in Europe. Built on the former port area of Grasbrook and the warehouse district along the northern bank of the Elbe, the project extends Hamburg's city centre by roughly 40 per cent, converting old docklands into a mixed-use district of housing, offices, culture, retail and education.

Development

The masterplan, adopted in 2000 following an international competition won by a Dutch-German team led by Kees Christiaanse, divides HafenCity into neighbourhoods built around preserved harbour basins. Because the site lies outside Hamburg's main dyke line, buildings stand on flood-protected artificial mounds (Warften) about eight metres above sea level, and public waterfront promenades remain deliberately floodable. Construction began in 2001; completion is expected in the 2030s.

Landmarks

The quarter adjoins the Speicherstadt, the historic warehouse district inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015. Its best-known building is the Elbphilharmonie concert hall, opened in 2017 atop the former Kaispeicher A warehouse. HafenCity also hosts the Kühne Logistics University, HafenCity University, and is served by the U4 underground line.
