The Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC) is an agency of the Government of Canada responsible for public health, emergency preparedness and response, and infectious and chronic disease control. It was created in 2004 following the SARS outbreak and the recommendations of the Naylor Report, which identified weaknesses in Canada's public health system.

Mandate

PHAC works to promote health, prevent diseases and injuries, and prepare for and respond to public health emergencies. It operates the National Microbiology Laboratory in Winnipeg, one of the few laboratories in the world with both Level 4 human and animal containment facilities, and runs surveillance systems such as the Global Public Health Intelligence Network.

COVID-19

The agency played a central role in Canada's response to the COVID-19 pandemic, including border measures, vaccine procurement support and public guidance, and faced criticism over pandemic preparedness and the state of the national emergency stockpile.
