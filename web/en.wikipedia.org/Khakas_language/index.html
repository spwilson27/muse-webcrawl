Khakas language

Khakas is spoken in the Republic of Khakassia in southern Siberia.

Khakas (Хакас тілі)
Native to: Russia (Khakassia)
Speakers: roughly 30,000–40,000
Family: Turkic, Siberian Turkic
Writing system: Cyrillic

Khakas is a Turkic language spoken by the Khakas people in the Republic of Khakassia in southern Siberia, Russia, mainly in the basin of the upper Yenisei and Abakan rivers. Together with Shor and Chulym it belongs to the Siberian branch of Turkic. The Khakas are descended from the Yenisei Kyrgyz, whose khaganate dominated the region in the early medieval period and whose language is documented in runic inscriptions.

Dialects and standard language

Traditional dialect divisions correspond to tribal groups: Sagai, Kacha, Kyzyl and Shor dialects are usually distinguished. The literary language, created in the Soviet period, is based mainly on the Sagai and Kacha dialects. A closely related variety, Fuyu Kyrgyz, is spoken by a small community in Heilongjiang, China — descendants of Khakas resettled under the Qing dynasty and often cited as the easternmost Turkic language.

Writing and status

A Cyrillic-based alphabet was created for Khakas in the 1920s; a Latin alphabet replaced it in 1929, before Cyrillic was reimposed in 1939. Khakas is co-official with Russian in the republic and is taught in schools and used in local media and literature, but the number of speakers has been declining under pressure from Russian, and UNESCO lists the language as endangered. Traditional oral literature, including heroic epics (alyptyg nymakh) performed by storytellers (khayji), is a prominent part of Khakas culture.

Features

Like other Turkic languages, Khakas is agglutinative, with vowel harmony, SOV word order and a rich system of cases and possessive suffixes. Its vowel system includes long vowels, and its consonant system has been influenced by contact with Mongolic and Samoyedic languages.
