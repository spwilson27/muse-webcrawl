Asterales

KingdomPlantae
CladeAngiosperms; eudicots; asterids
Families11 (APG IV), incl. Asteraceae, Campanulaceae
Species~35,000

Asterales is an order of flowering plants (dicotyledons) within the asterid clade of the APG classification. It is best known for the family Asteraceae — the daisy or composite family — one of the largest families of flowering plants, which includes sunflowers, lettuce, artichokes, and chrysanthemums.

Characteristics

Members of the order commonly store energy as the carbohydrate inulin rather than starch, and many have specialised floral structures. In Asteraceae the inflorescence is a capitulum, a dense head of many small florets that functions like a single flower. The order also includes the bellflower family (Campanulaceae) and smaller families such as Goodeniaceae and Menyanthaceae.

Importance

With roughly 35,000 species, the order is economically significant worldwide: crops include sunflower, safflower, lettuce, endive, and Jerusalem artichoke, and many species are grown as ornamentals. The APG IV system recognises eleven families in the order.
