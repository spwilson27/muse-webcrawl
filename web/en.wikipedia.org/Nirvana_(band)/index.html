Nirvana was an American rock band formed in Aberdeen, Washington, in 1987 by lead singer and guitarist Kurt Cobain and bassist Krist Novoselic. With drummer Dave Grohl (joined 1990), the band achieved worldwide fame with its second album, Nevermind (1991), whose single "Smells Like Teen Spirit" propelled alternative rock and the Seattle "grunge" scene into the mainstream and made Nirvana the defining band of Generation X.

History

Nirvana debuted on the independent label Sub Pop with Bleach (1989), recorded for about $600. Signing to DGC Records, they recorded Nevermind with producer Butch Vig; its unexpected success — it knocked Michael Jackson from the top of the Billboard chart in January 1992 and has sold tens of millions of copies — transformed the music industry. The rawer follow-up In Utero (1993), produced by Steve Albini, debuted at number one, and the band's MTV Unplugged performance in November 1993 became one of the most celebrated live recordings in rock.

Cobain struggled with heroin addiction, chronic illness and the pressures of fame. In April 1994 he was found dead at his Seattle home, aged 27; Nirvana disbanded. Grohl went on to found the Foo Fighters, while Novoselic pursued music and political activism.

Legacy

Nirvana is among the best-selling bands of all time, with estimated sales of over 75 million records. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2014, its first year of eligibility, and its music remains a touchstone of alternative rock.
