Apicius

Apicius is the name attributed to a collection of Roman recipes, conventionally dated to the 4th or 5th century AD and also known as De re coquinaria ("On the Subject of Cooking"). It is the oldest surviving cookbook from antiquity and the only one to survive substantially complete from the Roman world.

The text

The work contains roughly 500 recipes arranged in ten books, covering meats, vegetables, legumes, seafood, sauces, and delicacies for the wealthy table. Recipes are terse, written in a Vulgar Latin that differs from classical norms, and many call for the fish sauce liquamen (garum) and sweet-sour flavourings. The surviving manuscripts date from the Carolingian period (9th century); the text was first printed in 1498.

Marcus Gavius Apicius

The name refers to Marcus Gavius Apicius, a Roman gourmet of the 1st century AD under Tiberius, famed in anecdotes by Seneca and Pliny for extravagant luxury. He almost certainly did not write the surviving text, which accumulated under his name as a byword for fine cooking.

Legacy

Apicius is a key source for Roman cuisine and has been edited and translated many times; modern chefs have recreated its dishes.
