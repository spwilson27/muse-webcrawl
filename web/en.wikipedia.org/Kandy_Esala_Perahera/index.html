Kandy Esala Perahera

Kandy Esala Perahera
Location: Kandy, Sri Lanka
Timing: Esala (July/August), 10 nights
Honours: Sacred Tooth Relic of the Buddha

The Kandy Esala Perahera (the festival of the Sacred Tooth Relic) is a major Buddhist festival held annually in Kandy, Sri Lanka, during the month of Esala (July–August). Over ten nights of processions, lavishly decorated elephants, drummers, dancers, fire-breathers, whip-crackers, and flag bearers fill the streets to honour the Sacred Tooth Relic of the Buddha, enshrined at the Temple of the Tooth (Sri Dalada Maligawa).

Structure

The perahera combines processions of the Temple of the Tooth with those of four Hindu devales dedicated to Natha, Vishnu, Kataragama, and Pattini. The first five nights are the Kumbal Perahera, followed by the grander Randoli Perahera; on the final night a ceremonial casket (representing the relic, which itself no longer leaves the temple) is carried on the back of the Maligawa tusker elephant. The festival closes with a water-cutting ceremony (diya-kepeema) in the Mahaweli River at dawn.

Origins

The festival merges two older processions: the Esala Perahera, which prayed to the gods for rain, and the Dalada Perahera, honouring the Tooth Relic after its arrival in Kandy in the 16th–17th centuries. King Kirti Sri Rajasinghe formally joined the two in the 18th century. It remains one of the oldest and most spectacular religious pageants in Asia.
