Second breakfast

Second breakfast (German: zweites Frühstück; Polish: drugie śniadanie; Hungarian: tízórai) is a meal eaten after breakfast, but before lunch, traditional in parts of Central and Eastern Europe including Germany, Poland, Slovakia, Hungary, and Austria. It is typically a light mid-morning snack rather than a full meal. The concept gained wide recognition in the English-speaking world through J. R. R. Tolkien's hobbits, for whom second breakfast follows first breakfast and precedes elevenses.

Regional forms

In Bavaria the second breakfast traditionally features Weisswurst (white sausage) with sweet mustard and a pretzel. In Poland, drugie śniadanie is commonly a packed meal of sandwiches and fruit eaten at school or work. The Hungarian tízórai — literally "ten-o'clock snack" — is standard in schools.

In literature

In Tolkien's The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings, hobbits eat six meals a day when they can get them: breakfast, second breakfast, elevenses, luncheon, afternoon tea, and dinner (with supper besides). The 2001 film adaptation popularised the phrase, when Pippin asks whether Aragorn knows about second breakfast.
