Ping-pong diplomacy

Ping-pong diplomacy (Chinese: 乒乓外交) refers to the exchange of table tennis players between the United States and the People's Republic of China in 1971–72, which thawed two decades of hostility and opened the way to the rapprochement between the two countries, culminating in President Richard Nixon's visit to China in February 1972. The phrase has since become a byword for the use of sport to open political dialogue.

The 1971 exchange

During the World Table Tennis Championships in Nagoya, Japan, in April 1971, American player Glenn Cowan missed his team bus and was invited aboard the Chinese team's bus, where he struck up a conversation with the Chinese star Zhuang Zedong. Photographs of their friendly meeting made headlines worldwide. Days later, on 10 April 1971, the American team was invited to visit China—the first American delegation admitted since 1949. The players toured the country, played exhibition matches and were received by Premier Zhou Enlai, who told them they had opened a new page in relations between the two peoples.

Consequences

The visit was followed in July 1971 by Henry Kissinger's secret trip to Beijing and, in February 1972, by Nixon's historic visit and the Shanghai Communiqué, reshaping Cold War geopolitics. A Chinese team toured the United States in return in 1972. The episode demonstrated how an apparently minor sporting encounter, seized upon by leaders on both sides—Mao Zedong personally approved the invitation—could signal and accelerate a strategic realignment.
