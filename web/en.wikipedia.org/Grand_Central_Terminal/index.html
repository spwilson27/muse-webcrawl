Grand Central Terminal

Grand Central Terminal (also referred to as Grand Central Station or simply as Grand Central) is a commuter rail terminal located at 42nd Street and Park Avenue in Midtown Manhattan, New York City. Grand Central is the southern terminus of the Metro-North Railroad's Harlem, Hudson and New Haven Lines, and since 2023 it has also served Long Island Rail Road trains at the Grand Central Madison station below it. The terminal is the third station to occupy the site; it was built for the New York Central Railroad and opened on February 2, 1913.

Covering 48 acres (19 ha) with 44 platforms — more than any other railroad station in the world — Grand Central is celebrated for its Beaux-Arts architecture and is a designated National Historic Landmark.

Architecture

The terminal was designed by the firms of Reed and Stem and Warren and Wetmore in the Beaux-Arts style. Its main facade on 42nd Street is crowned by Jules-Félix Coutan's sculpture Glory of Commerce, featuring Mercury, Hercules, and Minerva, and a Tiffany glass clock. The Main Concourse, 275 feet long and 120 feet high, has a vaulted ceiling painted with an astronomical mural of the zodiac, and its famous four-faced opal glass clock above the information booth is a meeting point known to generations of New Yorkers. The terminal also contains the Whispering Gallery outside the Oyster Bar restaurant, tennis courts, and extensive underground passages.

History

The first station on the site, Grand Central Depot, was built by Cornelius Vanderbilt in 1871 and replaced by Grand Central Station in 1900. After a deadly 1902 train crash in the Park Avenue Tunnel, steam locomotives were banned from Manhattan, prompting the electrification of the lines and the complete rebuilding of the terminal with tracks on two underground levels. In the 1970s, a proposal by Penn Central to build an office tower over the terminal led to a landmark legal battle; in Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City (1978), the U.S. Supreme Court upheld the city's landmarks preservation law and saved the building. A major restoration completed in 1998 returned the terminal to its original splendor.

Services

Metro-North trains serve the Hudson Valley, Westchester County, and Connecticut, with destinations including Poughkeepsie, New Haven, and Danbury. The terminal connects to the New York City Subway at Grand Central–42nd Street station, serving the 4, 5, 6, 7, and S trains.
