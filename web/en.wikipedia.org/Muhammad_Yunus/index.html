Muhammad Yunus

Muhammad Yunus (born 28 June 1940) is a Bangladeshi economist, entrepreneur, and civil society leader who founded the Grameen Bank and pioneered the concepts of microcredit and microfinance. In 2006, Yunus and Grameen Bank were jointly awarded the Nobel Peace Prize. In August 2024, following the resignation of Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina, Yunus became Chief Adviser of the interim government of Bangladesh.

Grameen Bank and microfinance

An economics professor at Chittagong University during the 1974 famine, Yunus began by lending $27 of his own money to 42 bamboo stool makers in the village of Jobra in 1976. The Grameen Bank was formally established in 1983, lending small sums, mostly to poor women, in groups that provided mutual support. The model spread worldwide, though it has been debated by researchers on its measured impact. Yunus was removed as Grameen's managing director in 2011 by the Bangladeshi central bank.

Chief Adviser

After the July 2024 student-led uprising and Hasina's flight, Yunus, chosen by the protest movement, was sworn in as Chief Adviser of an interim government on 8 August 2024, tasked with restoring order and preparing elections.
