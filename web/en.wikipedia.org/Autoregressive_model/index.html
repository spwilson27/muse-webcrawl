Autoregressive model

FieldStatistics, signal processing
NotationAR(p)
TypeTime series model

In statistics, econometrics, and signal processing, an autoregressive (AR) model is a representation of a type of random process; as such, it is used to describe certain time-varying processes in nature, economics, behavior, etc. The autoregressive model specifies that the output variable depends linearly on its own previous values and on a stochastic term (an imperfectly predictable term).

Definition

The notation AR(p) indicates an autoregressive model of order p, in which the value at time t is a constant plus a weighted sum of the previous p values, plus a random error term. Together with the moving-average (MA) model, it forms the basis of the more general ARMA and ARIMA models of time series.

Estimation

Parameters of an AR model can be estimated by ordinary least squares, by the method of maximum likelihood, or by solving the Yule–Walker equations, which relate the model coefficients to the autocorrelations of the process.

Stationarity

An AR process is stationary when the roots of its characteristic polynomial lie outside the unit circle. For the AR(1) model this reduces to the condition that the single autoregressive coefficient has absolute value less than one.

Applications

Autoregressive models are used in speech coding (linear predictive coding), economics, and spectrum estimation, and they form the conceptual basis of modern autoregressive language models in machine learning.
