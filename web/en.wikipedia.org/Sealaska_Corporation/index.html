Sealaska Corporation

Sealaska Corporation is one of thirteen Alaska Native Regional Corporations created under the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act of 1971 (ANCSA). Headquartered in Juneau, Alaska, it is owned by more than 22,000 Tlingit, Haida, and Tsimshian shareholders and descendants, and represents the Native peoples of Southeast Alaska.

History

Under ANCSA, Sealaska received a settlement of cash and approximately 356,000 acres of surface estate (with additional subsurface rights) in Southeast Alaska, including lands around the Tongass National Forest. The corporation was incorporated in 1972 and held its first shareholder meeting the same year.

Business

Sealaska's businesses have historically included timber harvesting, natural resources, environmental services, manufacturing, and food production. In more recent years it has shifted toward sustainable businesses, including ocean-based foods through ventures such as its investment in sustainable seafood companies, as well as carbon-offset projects on its forest lands.

Culture and heritage

Sealaska supports the cultures of its shareholder tribes through the Sealaska Heritage Institute, which works to preserve and revitalise Tlingit, Haida, and Tsimshian languages, arts, and traditions, including the biennial Celebration festival in Juneau.
