Visit, board, search, and seizure

A naval boarding team conducting VBSS

Abbreviation: VBSS

Practised by: Navies and coast guards worldwide

Visit, board, search, and seizure (VBSS) is the term used by United States military and law enforcement agencies for maritime boarding actions and tactics. VBSS teams are designed to capture enemy vessels, combat terrorism, piracy, smuggling, and other illegal activities at sea, inspecting vessels to enforce blockades, sanctions, and maritime law.

Operations

In a typical VBSS operation, a team from a warship approaches a target vessel by rigid-hull inflatable boat or helicopter, boards it — with or without the cooperation of its crew, known as compliant and non-compliant boardings — secures the vessel, and searches it for contraband, weapons, or persons of interest. Boardings may be conducted under the law of the sea, United Nations sanctions regimes, or the right of visit on the high seas for stateless vessels or suspected piracy and slave trading.

Training

Members of VBSS teams receive specialized training in close-quarters combat, tactical movement aboard ship, handcuffing and detention, and evidence handling. In the United States Navy, VBSS duty is typically performed by ship's company teams, while the Coast Guard deploys specialized Maritime Security Response Teams and Law Enforcement Detachments. Similar capabilities exist in other navies, often as part of maritime interdiction operations.
