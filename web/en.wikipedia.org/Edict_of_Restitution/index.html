Edict of Restitution

The Edict of Restitution, issued on March 6, 1629, by Ferdinand II, Holy Roman Emperor, was an attempt to restore the religious and territorial situation in the Holy Roman Empire to what it had been at the time of the Peace of Augsburg in 1555. Issued during the Thirty Years' War, at the height of imperial and Catholic success following the campaigns of Wallenstein and Tilly, it ordered the return of all ecclesiastical property secularized by Protestants since 1552.

Provisions

The edict reaffirmed the reservatum ecclesiasticum of the Peace of Augsburg, under which a prince-bishop or abbot who converted to Protestantism was required to surrender his office and lands. It declared that the "ecclesiastical reservation" bound Protestants as well as Catholics, that Calvinists (the Reformed) enjoyed no legal protection under the Peace of Augsburg, which recognized only Catholics and Lutherans, and that all archbishoprics, bishoprics, monasteries, and church lands taken by Protestants since the Treaty of Passau (1552) were to be restored to the Catholic Church. In practice this meant the restoration of two archbishoprics (Magdeburg and Bremen), numerous bishoprics, and hundreds of monasteries and estates.

Implementation and reaction

Imperial commissioners, backed by Wallenstein's army, began enforcing restitution in northern Germany, provoking alarm and resistance among Protestant princes — including even Lutheran Saxony, Ferdinand's ally — and giving Sweden's Gustavus Adolphus an opening to intervene in the war in 1630. Moderates at the imperial court, and even some Catholic electors, regarded the edict as overreach that made a negotiated peace impossible.

Abrogation

The Peace of Prague (1635) effectively suspended the edict for forty years and set 1627 as the normative year for church property in the territories of the signatories. The Peace of Westphalia (1648) settled the matter definitively by fixing January 1, 1624, as the normative date for possession of ecclesiastical lands and extending legal recognition to Calvinists.
