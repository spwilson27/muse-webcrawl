Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing

The American Triple Crown series concludes with the Belmont Stakes

The Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing, often shortened to Triple Crown, is a series of horse races for three-year-old Thoroughbreds, consisting of the Kentucky Derby, the Preakness Stakes, and the Belmont Stakes. Winning all three races is considered the greatest accomplishment in American Thoroughbred racing. The term originated in mid-19th-century England for the 2,000 Guineas, St Leger, and Epsom Derby, and was later adopted in the United States.

The three races are run over five weeks each spring, testing a young horse's speed, stamina, and versatility at three different distances and tracks. The Belmont Stakes, at a mile and a half, is the longest and is nicknamed "the Test of the Champion".

Winners

Only thirteen horses have won the American Triple Crown. The first was Sir Barton in 1919, before the term was in common use. The most famous winners include Gallant Fox (1930), War Admiral (1937), Whirlaway (1941), Count Fleet (1943), Assault (1946), Citation (1948), Secretariat (1973) — whose Belmont victory by 31 lengths set a still-standing world record — Seattle Slew (1977), and Affirmed (1978). After a 37-year drought, American Pharoah ended the wait in 2015 and also won the Breeders' Cup Classic to complete the "Grand Slam"; Justify followed in 2018.

Other Triple Crowns

Many racing nations stage their own Triple Crown series, typically modelled on the English pattern of a Guineas, a Derby, and a St Leger. There are also series for fillies, and a Triple Crown of Harness Racing for pacers and trotters.
