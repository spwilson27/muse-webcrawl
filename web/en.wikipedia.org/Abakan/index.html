Abakan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Abakan is the capital city of the Republic of Khakassia, Russia.

Abakan (Russian: Абака́н; Khakas: Ағбан, Ağban) is the capital city of the Republic of Khakassia, Russia, located in the central part of the Minusinsk Depression, at the confluence of the Yenisei and Abakan rivers. Its population was about 165,000 at the 2010 census, rising to roughly 184,000 by the 2021 census.

History

A Russian fort, Abakansky ostrog, was founded near the mouth of the Abakan River in 1675. The settlement that became the modern city developed in the 19th century as Ust-Abakanskoye. In 1914 the construction of the Achinsk–Minusinsk railway gave the settlement new importance; the station of Abakan opened in 1925. Ust-Abakanskoye was granted town status and renamed Abakan in 1931, and it became the administrative centre of Khakas Autonomous Oblast, later the Republic of Khakassia.

Geography and climate

Abakan lies in southern Siberia, about 4,200 kilometres east of Moscow, in a steppe basin surrounded by the Sayan Mountains. It has a sharply continental climate, bordering on a cold semi-arid regime: winters are long and very cold, with January averages around −18 °C, while summers are warm, with July averages around 20 °C. Annual precipitation is low, about 300 mm, most of it falling in summer.

Economy and transport

Abakan is a regional centre for trade and for the processing of agricultural and forestry products; its industries include railway workshops, food processing, and the manufacture of building materials. The city is served by Abakan International Airport and by rail links on the line between Achinsk and Tayshet, with connections to the Trans-Siberian Railway. Across the Yenisei lies the neighbouring city of Minusinsk. The Sayano-Shushenskaya hydroelectric dam, one of the largest power plants in the world, is located upstream on the Yenisei.

Culture and education

The city is home to Khakass State University, founded in 1994, as well as the Khakass National Museum, theatres, and research institutes devoted to the language, history, and archaeology of the Khakas people and the ancient cultures of the Minusinsk Basin, famous for its Bronze Age kurgans and stone stelae.
