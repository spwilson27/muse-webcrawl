UGM-73 Poseidon

The UGM-73 Poseidon missile was the second US Navy submarine-launched ballistic missile (SLBM) system, powered by a two-stage solid-fuel rocket. It succeeded the Polaris missile and was the first SLBM equipped with multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles (MIRVs). It entered service in 1971 and was phased out in favour of the Trident I beginning in 1979.

Development

Development began in 1963 under the designation Polaris B3, intended to fit the same launch tubes as Polaris while carrying a heavier payload over a similar range. Renamed Poseidon C3, the missile was larger in diameter and weight, and introduced a multiple reentry vehicle bus able to dispense up to 14 W68 warheads in a "footprint" against multiple targets. The first submerged launch took place from USS James Madison in 1970.

Service

Poseidon entered operational service on 31 March 1971 aboard USS James Madison. A total of 31 of the US Navy's "41 for Freedom" ballistic missile submarines — the Lafayette, James Madison and Benjamin Franklin classes — were backfitted to carry 16 Poseidons each. The earlier Polaris boats retained that missile. Poseidon was progressively replaced by the longer-ranged Trident I (C4) from 1979 and the last Poseidon patrols ended in the early 1990s, with the remaining missiles removed under the START I treaty.

Accuracy

With a circular error probable of about 0.55 km, Poseidon was significantly more accurate than Polaris, though still intended primarily for countervalue (area) targets rather than hardened missile silos. Its MIRV capability roughly doubled the number of warheads the US sea-based deterrent could deliver.
