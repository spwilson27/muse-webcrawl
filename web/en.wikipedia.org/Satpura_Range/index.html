The Satpura Range is a range of hills in central India. It runs roughly east–west for about 900 kilometres, south of and parallel to the Vindhya Range, between the Narmada River to the north and the Tapti River to the south. The range spans the states of Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Chhattisgarh. Its name derives from the Sanskrit Satpura, "seven folds", referring to its many parallel ridges.

Geography and ecology

The Satpuras are largely composed of basalt and granite, heavily forested in their eastern and central sections with sal and teak. The highest peak, Dhupgarh (1,352 m), lies near the hill station of Pachmarhi, sometimes called the "Queen of Satpura". The range includes several protected areas — Satpura, Kanha (in the Maikal hills), Pench, and Melghat tiger reserves among them — which together form one of the largest contiguous tiger landscapes in India. The region is also home to Gond and other Adivasi communities.
