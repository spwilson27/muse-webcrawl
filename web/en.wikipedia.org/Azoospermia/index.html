Azoospermia

SpecialtyUrology, andrology
SymptomsAbsence of sperm in semen; usually discovered during infertility testing

Azoospermia is the medical condition of a man whose semen contains no sperm. It is associated with male infertility, though many forms are amenable to medical treatment. In humans, azoospermia affects about 1% of the male population and may be seen in up to 20% of male infertility situations in Canada. It should not be confused with aspermia, the complete absence of semen.

Classification

Azoospermia is classified into three main types:

  • Pretesticular — inadequate hormonal stimulation of otherwise normal testes, as in hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (including Kallmann syndrome). Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) is low.
  • Testicular (non-obstructive) — failure of sperm production within the testes themselves, caused by genetic conditions such as Klinefelter syndrome, Y-chromosome microdeletions, cryptorchidism, chemotherapy or radiotherapy. FSH is usually elevated.
  • Post-testicular (obstructive) — sperm are produced but not ejaculated because of a physical blockage, for example congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens (associated with cystic fibrosis mutations), prior vasectomy, or ejaculatory duct obstruction.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on semen analysis performed on at least two occasions, with centrifugation of the sample to confirm the complete absence of sperm. Hormone profiles (FSH, LH, testosterone), physical examination and genetic testing (karyotype and Y-microdeletion screening) help determine the cause.

Treatment

Pretesticular azoospermia can often be treated with hormone therapy. Obstructive azoospermia may be treated by surgical reconstruction or by sperm retrieval directly from the testis or epididymis, followed by in vitro fertilisation with intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). In some non-obstructive cases sperm can also be retrieved by microdissection testicular sperm extraction (micro-TESE).
