Nineteen Eighty-Four (also published as 1984) is a dystopian novel by the English writer George Orwell, published on 8 June 1949 by Secker & Warburg as Orwell's ninth and final book completed in his lifetime. Set in Airstrip One (formerly Great Britain), a province of the superstate Oceania, the novel depicts a totalitarian society ruled by the Party and its figurehead, Big Brother, in which mass surveillance, perpetual war, and the systematic manipulation of language and history suppress all independent thought.

Plot

The protagonist, Winston Smith, is a member of the Outer Party who works at the Ministry of Truth, falsifying historical records to align the past with the Party's present claims. Privately rebellious, Winston begins a forbidden love affair with his colleague Julia and is drawn toward the rumoured resistance movement, the Brotherhood, through the Inner Party member O'Brien. The couple are arrested by the Thought Police, and Winston is tortured and brainwashed in the Ministry of Love — culminating in Room 101, where he is confronted with his worst fear, rats — until he betrays Julia and comes to love Big Brother.

Themes and concepts

The novel introduced vocabulary that has entered common usage: Big Brother, doublethink (holding two contradictory beliefs simultaneously), thoughtcrime, Newspeak (the Party's deliberately impoverished language, designed to make heretical thought impossible), memory hole, and the Thought Police. The adjective "Orwellian" derives from the book. Orwell drew on Stalinist Russia, Nazi Germany, and wartime and post-war Britain — including censorship practices he observed at the BBC and the Ministry of Information — in constructing Oceania.

Composition and publication

Orwell wrote most of the novel on the island of Jura in Scotland during 1947–1948 while seriously ill with tuberculosis. Earlier working titles included The Last Man in Europe. The book was an immediate critical and commercial success and has never been out of print; it was banned or challenged in several countries, including the Soviet Union, where it circulated in samizdat. Sales repeatedly surge in response to political events, and the novel topped bestseller lists again in 2017 amid debates over "alternative facts" and mass surveillance.

Influence

Nineteen Eighty-Four is routinely ranked among the greatest English-language novels of the twentieth century. It shaped later dystopian fiction, political discourse about surveillance and propaganda, and popular culture, from reality television (Big Brother) to Apple's "1984" Macintosh advertisement. Together with Orwell's earlier allegorical novella Animal Farm, it cemented his reputation as one of the century's most influential political writers.
