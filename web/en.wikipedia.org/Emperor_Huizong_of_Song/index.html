Emperor Huizong of Song (2 November 1082 – 4 June 1135), personal name Zhao Ji, was the eighth emperor of the Song dynasty of China and, in his own time, the most famous Chinese artist of his period, as well as a great patron of the arts. His reign, however, ended in catastrophe: invasion by the Jurchen-led Jin dynasty, the fall of the capital Kaifeng, and his own capture and captivity.

Artistic life

Huizong was a painter, poet, musician and calligrapher of genuine talent. He developed the "Slender Gold" style of calligraphy, promoted detailed bird-and-flower painting in the imperial academy, and compiled catalogues of the imperial art collection. He was also a fervent patron of Daoism, styling himself a Daoist emperor and pursuing elixirs and omens with his minister Cai Jing.

Fall of Northern Song

Song foreign policy misjudged the rise of the Jurchen, allying with them against the Liao dynasty and then facing them alone. When the Jin invaded in 1125–26, Huizong abdicated in favour of his son, Emperor Qinzong, but both were captured at Kaifeng in the Jingkang Incident of 1127 and carried north to Manchuria, where Huizong died in 1135. The Song court re-established itself in the south as the Southern Song.

Legacy

Huizong is a classic figure of the brilliant but negligent ruler in Chinese historiography: his cultural achievements are undisputed, yet his court is traditionally blamed for corruption and for the loss of northern China.
