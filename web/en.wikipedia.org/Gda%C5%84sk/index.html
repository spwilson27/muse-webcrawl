Gdańsk (German: Danzig) is a city on the Baltic coast of northern Poland. With a population of around 487,000, it is the capital and largest city of the Pomeranian Voivodeship and Poland's principal seaport. Together with Gdynia and Sopot it forms the metropolitan area known as Tricity.

History

First mentioned in 997, Gdańsk grew into a wealthy Hanseatic port trading in grain and amber, passing between Polish, Teutonic, and Prussian rule over the centuries. Between the world wars it was the Free City of Danzig. The first shots of the Second World War were fired here, at Westerplatte, on 1 September 1939. Heavily destroyed in 1945, its old town was meticulously rebuilt. In August 1980, strikes at the Lenin Shipyard gave birth to the Solidarity movement led by Lech Wałęsa, a turning point in the fall of communism in Eastern Europe.

Economy and landmarks

The Port of Gdańsk is the largest Polish seaport, and the city is known for amber processing, shipbuilding, and a growing technology sector. Landmarks include the Main Town Hall, St Mary's Church (one of the largest brick churches in the world), the medieval Gdańsk Crane, the European Solidarity Centre, and the Museum of the Second World War.
