Uniform act

In the United States, a uniform act is a proposed law drafted by the Uniform Law Commission (ULC), formally the National Conference of Commissioners on Uniform State Laws, and offered to the legislatures of all the states for enactment in identical or near-identical form. The purpose is to promote consistency in state law on subjects where uniformity is desirable across state lines.

Process

The ULC, founded in 1892 and composed of commissioners appointed by each state, studies areas of law and drafts model legislation. States are free to adopt, modify, or reject a uniform act; when a state enacts one with significant alterations, the result is sometimes described as a "uniform act" in name only.

Examples

The most successful uniform act is the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), first published in 1952 and adopted, in whole or large part, by every state. Other widely adopted acts include the Uniform Probate Code, the Uniform Trade Secrets Act, the Uniform Child Custody Jurisdiction and Enforcement Act, and the Uniform Anatomical Gift Act.
