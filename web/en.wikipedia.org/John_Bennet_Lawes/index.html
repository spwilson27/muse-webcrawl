John Bennet Lawes

Sir John Bennet Lawes, 1st Baronet (28 December 1814 – 31 August 1900) was an English entrepreneur and agricultural scientist. He founded the Rothamsted Experimental Station in Hertfordshire in 1843, the world's oldest agricultural research institution, and patented superphosphate, the first commercial chemical fertiliser, launching the modern fertiliser industry.

Early life

Lawes was born at Rothamsted, near Harpenden, the only son of the estate's owner. He attended Eton and Brasenose College, Oxford, leaving without a degree, and inherited Rothamsted in 1822 (taking control on his majority). Fascinated by chemistry, he experimented with growing medicinal plants and with the effects of manures on crops in pots and field plots.

Superphosphate and Rothamsted

In 1842 Lawes patented a process for treating bones and mineral phosphates with sulphuric acid to produce superphosphate, a soluble and effective fertiliser, and built a factory at Deptford. The profits financed his research. In 1843 he employed the chemist Joseph Henry Gilbert, beginning a collaboration of nearly sixty years, and laid out the famous long-term field experiments at Rothamsted — including the Broadbalk wheat experiment (begun 1843) and Park Grass (1856) — which continue to this day and underpin the statistical design of field trials later formalised by R. A. Fisher at the same station.

Legacy

Lawes was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society, received a Royal Medal, and was created a baronet in 1882. In 1889 he placed Rothamsted under a trust (the Lawes Agricultural Trust) with an endowment, securing the station's future. He died at Rothamsted in 1900; the institution, now Rothamsted Research, remains a leading centre of agricultural science.
