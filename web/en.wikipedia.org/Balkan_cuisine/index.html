Balkan cuisine is a type of regional cuisine that combines characteristics of European cuisine with some from Western Asia. It is found across the Balkan Peninsula in Southeast Europe, shaped by centuries of Ottoman rule and by Mediterranean, Slavic and Central European influences.

Common dishes

Shared dishes throughout the region include grilled minced-meat sausages such as ćevapi, stuffed vegetables (sarma and dolma), phyllo pastries such as burek and banitsa, and roasted-pepper relishes such as ajvar and lutenica. Salads like shopska, yogurt-based drinks such as ayran, and strong fruit brandies such as rakia are widespread.

Regional variations

Coastal Croatia, Montenegro and Greece emphasize seafood and olive oil; inland Serbia, Bosnia and North Macedonia favor grilled meats and bean stews; Bulgaria is known for yogurt and sirene cheese; Romania and Moldova add cornmeal mămăligă and sour soups (ciorbă).
