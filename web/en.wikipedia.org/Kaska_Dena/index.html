Kaska Dena

The Kaska Dena are a Dene (Athabaskan-speaking) First Nations people whose traditional territory, known as Dene Kéh Kusán, spans parts of what is now northern British Columbia, southeastern Yukon, and the western Northwest Territories in Canada. Their language is Kaska, part of the Northern Athabaskan family.

Communities

Kaska communities include the Liard First Nation and Ross River Dena Council in Yukon, and the Daylu Dena Council (Lower Post), Dease River First Nation (Good Hope Lake), and Kwadacha First Nation in British Columbia. The Kaska Dena Council, founded in 1981, represents several of these nations in land claims negotiations.

Territory and land claims

The Kaska traditional territory covers roughly 110,000 square kilometres of boreal forest, mountains, and rivers, including parts of the Liard and Dease river watersheds, rich in wildlife and minerals. Unlike most British Columbia First Nations, the Kaska — like other Yukon and northern nations — have pursued comprehensive land claims; Kaska in Yukon are covered by separate processes from those in British Columbia, where negotiations under the BC treaty process have proceeded without a final agreement.

Culture and language

Traditionally the Kaska were semi-nomadic hunters, trappers, and fishers organised in family groups with exogamous moieties (Wolf and Crow). The Kaska language (Dene Ké) is endangered but is the focus of revitalisation programs, dictionaries, and community language nests.
