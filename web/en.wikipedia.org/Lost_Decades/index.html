Lost Decades

Lost Decades
Japanese: Ushinawareta Jūnen
Period: roughly 1991–2011 (sometimes extended to 2021)
Context: collapse of the Japanese asset price bubble

The Lost Decades are a period of economic stagnation in Japan that began with the collapse of the Japanese asset price bubble in 1991–92. Originally called the Lost Decade (1991–2001), the period was extended by commentators to cover the 2000s, and sometimes the 2010s as well, as growth remained low and deflation persisted.

Causes and course

In the late 1980s, loose monetary policy and financial liberalisation inflated stock and land prices; the Nikkei 225 peaked in December 1989. Tightening then pricked the bubble, leaving banks burdened with non-performing loans. Slow clean-up, "zombie" firms kept alive by cheap credit, weak demand, deflation, and demographic decline produced decades of near-zero growth despite near-zero interest rates, quantitative easing (pioneered by the Bank of Japan in 2001), and repeated fiscal stimulus that pushed public debt above 200% of GDP.

Responses and legacy

Prime Minister Shinzo Abe's "Abenomics" (from 2012) combined monetary easing, fiscal spending and structural reform. The Lost Decades became a cautionary case study worldwide, cited during the 2008 financial crisis and in debates about Japanification of other advanced economies.
