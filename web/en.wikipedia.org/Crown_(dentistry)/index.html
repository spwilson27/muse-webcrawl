Crown (dentistry)

Dental crown
Specialty: Prosthodontics
Synonyms: Dental cap

In dentistry, a crown is a type of dental restoration that completely caps or encircles a tooth or dental implant. Crowns are used to restore a tooth's shape, strength and appearance when a large cavity, fracture or extensive wear threatens its health, and are often placed after root canal treatment.

Indications

Crowns are indicated for badly broken-down or heavily filled teeth, cracked teeth, teeth weakened by endodontic treatment, cosmetic improvement, and as retainers for bridges or coverings for implants.

Materials and procedure

Crowns may be made of porcelain-fused-to-metal, all-ceramic materials such as zirconia or lithium disilicate, gold alloys, or resin. The tooth is shaped, an impression or digital scan is taken, a temporary crown is fitted, and the definitive crown is cemented or bonded at a second visit; CAD/CAM systems can mill a crown in a single appointment.

Longevity

Well-maintained crowns commonly last ten to fifteen years or more, with failure most often due to recurrent decay, fracture or periodontal disease.
