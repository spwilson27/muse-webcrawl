Agnes George de Mille (September 18, 1905 – October 7, 1993) was an American dancer and choreographer whose work transformed both ballet and Broadway musical theatre by integrating dance fully into dramatic storytelling.

Career

De Mille studied in London and choreographed for Ballet Rambert and later American Ballet Theatre. Her 1942 ballet Rodeo, set to Aaron Copland's score, was a triumph. In 1943 she choreographed Rodgers and Hammerstein's Oklahoma!, whose dream ballet advanced the plot and established dance as an essential narrative element of the American musical. She went on to choreograph Carousel, Brigadoon, and Paint Your Wagon, and created the dramatic ballet Fall River Legend (1948), based on the Lizzie Borden story.

Advocacy and legacy

De Mille was a tireless advocate for the arts, instrumental in the creation of the National Endowment for the Arts, and a prolific writer whose memoir Dance to the Piper (1952) remains a classic. She received the Kennedy Center Honors and the National Medal of Arts.
