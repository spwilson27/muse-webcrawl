Princeton University

Princeton University is a private Ivy League research university in Princeton, New Jersey. Founded in 1746 in Elizabeth as the College of New Jersey, Princeton is the fourth-oldest institution of higher education in the United States and one of the nine colonial colleges chartered before the American Revolution.

History

The college moved to Newark in 1747 and to Princeton in 1756, where Nassau Hall was built. Under president John Witherspoon (from 1768), a signer of the Declaration of Independence, it became a centre of revolutionary thought. The institution gained university status in 1896 and adopted the name Princeton University. Its graduate school was founded in 1900, and women were first admitted as undergraduates in 1969.

Academics

Princeton provides undergraduate and graduate instruction in the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, and engineering. It is organised around residential colleges for undergraduates and is known for its senior thesis requirement and preceptorial system of small discussion classes. Research centres include the Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory and close ties with the Institute for Advanced Study, which is independent but nearby.

Campus

The main campus sits on about 600 acres in Princeton, with Collegiate Gothic architecture, Lake Carnegie, and the Princeton University Art Museum. Notable alumni and faculty include US presidents James Madison and Woodrow Wilson, and dozens of Nobel laureates; Albert Einstein worked at the neighbouring Institute for Advanced Study from 1933 until his death.
