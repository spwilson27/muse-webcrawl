Voice procedure

Voice procedure
Used inMilitary, aviation, maritime, amateur radio
Key toolsProcedure words, NATO phonetic alphabet, call signs

Voice procedure is the set of conventions used in radio communication to maximize clarity and brevity, including the use of procedure words (prowords), the NATO phonetic alphabet, standardized call signs, and prescribed message formats. It is employed by military forces, aviation, maritime services, emergency services, and amateur radio operators, where noisy channels and international crews make misunderstood transmissions dangerous.

Procedure words

Common prowords include "Roger" (message received and understood), "Wilco" (will comply), "Over" (transmission finished, reply expected), "Out" (transmission finished, no reply expected), "Say again" (repeat the transmission), "Break", "Mayday" for distress, and "Pan-pan" for urgency. Contrary to popular usage in films, "over and out" is incorrect, since the two words are contradictory.

Standards

Allied military voice procedure is codified in publications such as ACP 125; aviation radiotelephony phraseology is standardized by ICAO, and maritime communication uses the IMO Standard Marine Communication Phrases. Common principles include speaking slowly and clearly, spelling difficult words phonetically, reading back critical instructions, and keeping transmissions short. Voice procedure is an element of communications security (COMSEC) discipline alongside authentication and encryption.
