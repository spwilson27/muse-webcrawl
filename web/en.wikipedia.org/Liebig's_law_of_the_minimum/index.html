Liebig's law of the minimum is a principle developed in agricultural science by Carl Sprengel (1840) and later popularized by Justus von Liebig. It states that growth is dictated not by total resources available, but by the scarcest resource (limiting factor).

The barrel analogy

The law is illustrated by "Liebig's barrel": just as the capacity of a barrel with staves of unequal length is limited by the shortest stave, a plant's growth is limited by the nutrient in shortest supply relative to its needs.

Applications

The principle underlies fertiliser management in agriculture and has been generalised in ecology to limiting factors of populations, and by analogy to economics and systems management, where the scarcest input constrains overall output.
