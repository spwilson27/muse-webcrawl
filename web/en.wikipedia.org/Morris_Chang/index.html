Morris Chang (Chinese: 張忠謀; born 10 July 1931) is a Taiwanese-American businessman who founded the Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) in 1987. He is widely regarded as the founder of the pure-play semiconductor foundry business model, in which a company manufactures chips designed by others without designing chips of its own.

Early life and education

Chang was born in Ningbo, China, and moved frequently during his childhood amid the turmoil of war. He left China in 1948 and studied at Harvard University before transferring to the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, where he earned bachelor's and master's degrees in mechanical engineering. He later received a PhD in electrical engineering from Stanford University in 1964.

Career

Chang began his career at Sylvania Semiconductor and then spent 25 years at Texas Instruments, rising to lead its worldwide semiconductor business. In the mid-1980s he moved to Taiwan to head the Industrial Technology Research Institute. In 1987, with support from the Taiwanese government and Philips, he founded TSMC and served as its chairman and, for long periods, chief executive. Under his leadership TSMC grew into the world's largest contract chip manufacturer, producing advanced processors for companies such as Apple and Nvidia.

Legacy

Chang retired as chairman in 2018. He is frequently described as the "father of Taiwan's chip industry" and has received numerous honors, including the IEEE Medal of Honor in 2011.
