Blended wing body

A blended wing body (BWB), also known as a blended body, hybrid wing body, or lifting body design, is a fixed-wing aircraft design in which the wing and fuselage are smoothly blended into a single lifting surface with no clear dividing line. The entire craft — including a thickened center section that houses passengers or cargo — contributes to lift, promising significant improvements in aerodynamic efficiency and fuel consumption over conventional tube-and-wing designs.

Advantages and challenges

Studies by NASA and industry suggest a BWB airliner could burn substantially less fuel and produce less noise and emissions, because of its lower wetted area and lift-to-drag ratio. Challenges include the difficulty of pressurizing a non-cylindrical cabin, passenger evacuation and window placement, airport compatibility, and control of a configuration that requires advanced fly-by-wire systems.

Development

The concept was explored earlier in designs such as the Junkers G.38 and Northrop flying wings. Modern research culminated in the Boeing X-48, a remotely piloted subscale demonstrator flown by NASA and Boeing between 2007 and 2013, which validated the flight characteristics of the configuration. Startups and major manufacturers continue to study BWB designs for future cargo and passenger aircraft.
