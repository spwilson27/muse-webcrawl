Von Neumann algebra

In mathematics, a von Neumann algebra (also called a W*-algebra) is a *-algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space that is closed in the weak operator topology and contains the identity operator. The theory was founded by John von Neumann (with Francis Murray) in a series of papers beginning in the 1930s, motivated by quantum mechanics, group representations, and ergodic theory. Von Neumann algebras are the noncommutative analogues of measure spaces, just as their norm-closed counterparts, C*-algebras, are analogues of topological spaces.

Von Neumann algebras consist of operators on a Hilbert space closed under weak limits.

Definition and the bicommutant theorem

Von Neumann's bicommutant theorem states that a *-subalgebra of the bounded operators on a Hilbert space, acting nondegenerately, is a von Neumann algebra — that is, is closed in the weak operator topology — if and only if it equals its own double commutant. This gives an algebraic characterisation of a topologically defined class of algebras.

Factors and classification

A von Neumann algebra whose centre consists only of scalar multiples of the identity is called a factor. Murray and von Neumann classified factors into types I, II, and III according to the structure of their projection lattices and dimension functions. Type II1 factors, which admit a finite trace but no minimal projections, arise from group von Neumann algebras of discrete groups with infinite conjugacy classes, and were historically surprising "continuous" analogues of matrix algebras. Alain Connes's work in the 1970s on the classification of amenable factors earned him the Fields Medal, and the theory remains central to quantum field theory, where local observable algebras are typically type III.
