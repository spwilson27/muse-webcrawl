Professor James Moriarty is a fictional character in the Sherlock Holmes stories written by Sir Arthur Conan Doyle. A criminal mastermind described by Holmes as the "Napoleon of crime", Moriarty is a mathematics professor turned crime lord who serves as the detective's archenemy. The character was created primarily as a device to allow Doyle to kill off Holmes in the 1893 story "The Final Problem", in which the two grapple at the Reichenbach Falls and apparently plunge to their deaths; Holmes was later revived, but Moriarty remained dead.

Character

Holmes describes Moriarty as the organiser of half that is evil and nearly all that is undetected in London: a criminal consultant who protects and directs other criminals for a share of their profits, while himself appearing a respectable academic. The author of a celebrated treatise on the binomial theorem and a work on the dynamics of an asteroid, Moriarty is Holmes's intellectual equal. He appears or is mentioned in only a handful of stories — principally "The Final Problem" and the novel The Valley of Fear — yet his cultural prominence far exceeds his page presence.

Legacy

Moriarty became the archetype of the supervillain and the hero's archnemesis in popular fiction. He has been portrayed on screen by actors including George Zucco, Henry Daniell, Laurence Olivier (voice), Vincent D'Onofrio, Andrew Scott in the BBC series Sherlock, and Jared Harris in the Guy Ritchie films. The character also features in countless pastiches, plays, games and works of science fiction.
