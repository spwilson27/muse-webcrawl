Lúcio Costa

Born: 27 February 1902, Toulon, France

Died: 13 June 1998, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

Known for: Pilot Plan of Brasília; Ministry of Education building

Lúcio Marçal Ferreira Ribeiro Lima Costa (27 February 1902 – 13 June 1998) was a Brazilian architect and urban planner, born in France to Brazilian parents, best known for the Pilot Plan (Plano Piloto) of Brasília, the new capital of Brazil inaugurated in 1960, and as a pioneer of Brazilian modernism.

Career

Trained at the National School of Fine Arts in Rio de Janeiro, Costa briefly directed the school and in the 1930s led the team — including a young Oscar Niemeyer and consultant Le Corbusier — that designed the Ministry of Education and Health building in Rio, a landmark of modernist architecture with brise-soleil façades and tile panels. He also oversaw Brazil's heritage service (IPHAN) and designed the Brazilian pavilion for the 1939 New York World's Fair with Niemeyer.

Brasília

In 1957 Costa won the national competition for the plan of the new capital with a strikingly simple submission of sketches and a report. His plan organised Brasília as two crossing axes — often compared to an aeroplane, a bird, or a cross — with a monumental axis of government buildings and a curved residential axis of self-contained superquadra housing blocks, separating traffic and functions in the manner of the modernist CIAM charter. Niemeyer's palaces gave the plan its sculptural architecture. Brasília was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987, and Costa's plan is celebrated for its vision while criticised for car-dependence and social segregation.
