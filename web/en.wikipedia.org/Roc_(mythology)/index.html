Roc (mythology)

The roc is an enormous legendary bird of prey in Middle Eastern mythology, popularised in Arabian fairy tales and sailors' folklore. It was said to be so large that it could carry off elephants and other great beasts to feed its young.

Accounts

The roc appears in the One Thousand and One Nights, notably in the voyages of Sinbad the Sailor, whose companions encounter the bird on a remote island; Sinbad escapes by tying himself to a piece of meat carried up by the roc. Marco Polo also reported the bird, locating it near Madagascar and describing a feather so long it required many paces to measure. Such tales may draw on the extinct elephant birds of Madagascar or on giant fossil eggs known to traders.

In culture

The spelling "rukh" is also used. The roc survives in modern fantasy literature, games and heraldic imagery as the archetype of the giant bird.
