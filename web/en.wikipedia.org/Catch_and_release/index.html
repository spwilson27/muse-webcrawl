Catch and release

Catch and release is a practice in recreational fishing in which fish are unhooked and returned alive to the water after capture. It is used as a conservation measure to sustain fish populations — especially of species such as trout, bass and Atlantic salmon — and is mandatory in some fisheries for fish outside slot limits or during closed seasons.

Techniques

Survival of released fish improves with practices such as using barbless or circle hooks, keeping the fish in the water while unhooking, wetting hands before handling, supporting the fish horizontally, minimising time out of water, and reviving exhausted fish by holding them facing the current. Deep-hooked fish and those caught from great depth (subject to barotrauma) have lower survival rates.

History and debate

The ethic was promoted in the United States from the 1930s, notably by angler Lee Wulff, who argued that a sport fish is too valuable to be caught only once. Catch and release is now widespread in fly fishing and bass tournament culture, though some ethicists and animal-welfare campaigners question a practice that harms fish for recreation.
