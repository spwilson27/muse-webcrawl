Mabo v Queensland (No 2)

Court: High Court of Australia
Decided: 3 June 1992
Plaintiffs: Eddie Mabo and others, Meriam people
Held: Native title exists; terra nullius rejected

Mabo v Queensland (No 2) [1992] HCA 23 was a landmark decision of the High Court of Australia recognising native title and rejecting the doctrine of terra nullius for Australia.

Background

Eddie Koiki Mabo and fellow Meriam plaintiffs from Mer (Murray Island) in the Torres Strait claimed rights to their traditional lands. Queensland attempted to extinguish any such rights with the Queensland Coast Islands Declaratory Act 1985, struck down in Mabo (No 1) (1988) under the Racial Discrimination Act.

Decision

By 6–1, the Court held that the Meriam people held native title under their traditional laws and customs, and that native title generally survived the acquisition of sovereignty unless validly extinguished.

Legacy

The decision led to the Native Title Act 1993 and reshaped Australian land law and Indigenous rights, though later cases such as Wik and Yorta Yorta defined its scope.
