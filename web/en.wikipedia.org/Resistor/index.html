TypePassive component

A resistor is a passive two-terminal electrical component that implements electrical resistance as a circuit element. In electronic circuits, resistors are used to reduce current flow, adjust signal levels, divide voltages, bias active elements, and terminate transmission lines, among other uses.

Theory of operation

The electrical resistance of a resistor is specified by its resistance value, measured in ohms (Ω), and follows Ohm's law: the current through a resistor is directly proportional to the voltage across it. Resistors dissipate electrical energy as heat, so they are also rated by the maximum power they can safely dissipate.

Types

Fixed resistors include carbon composition, carbon film, metal film, and wirewound types. Variable resistors include the potentiometer and rheostat. Small axial resistors are marked with coloured bands using the electronic colour code to indicate value and tolerance; surface-mount devices are marked numerically. Specialised types include the thermistor, whose resistance varies strongly with temperature, and the varistor, whose resistance depends on voltage.

Series and parallel

Resistors in series have a total resistance equal to the sum of the individual values, while resistors in parallel combine reciprocally, giving a total resistance lower than any single resistor.
