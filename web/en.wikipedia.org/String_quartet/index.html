Instrumentation2 violins, 1 viola, 1 cello

A string quartet is a musical ensemble of four string players — two violin players, a viola player and a cellist — and also a musical composition written for such a group. The string quartet is one of the most prominent chamber ensembles in classical music, with most major composers, from the mid-18th century onward, writing string quartets.

History

The string quartet emerged in the 1750s and 1760s out of earlier forms such as the divertimento. Joseph Haydn is generally credited with establishing the form: his early quartets of around 1757 were followed by the landmark sets Op. 20 (1772) and Op. 33 (1781), which Haydn said were written in "a new and special way", with equal interplay among the four voices. Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart dedicated six quartets to Haydn in admiration.

Ludwig van Beethoven's sixteen quartets transformed the genre. His early Op. 18 quartets followed the Classical model, the middle "Razumovsky" quartets expanded its scale, and the late quartets — including the Grosse Fuge — are among the most profound works in Western music.

Form

The standard Classical string quartet has four movements:

  • A sonata-form allegro
  • A slow movement
  • A minuet (later, with Beethoven and others, a scherzo)
  • A fast finale, often in sonata or rondo form

Later repertoire

Franz Schubert, Felix Mendelssohn, Robert Schumann, Johannes Brahms, Antonín Dvořák, and Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky all enriched the repertoire. In the 20th century, Béla Bartók's six quartets and Dmitri Shostakovich's fifteen quartets are central works, and the form remains a testing ground for contemporary composers.
