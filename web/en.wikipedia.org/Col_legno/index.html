Col legno

Col legno (Italian for "with the wood") is a musical direction for string instruments indicating that the player should strike or rub the strings with the wooden back of the bow rather than drawing the hair across them. The resulting sound is percussive and muted, with a distinctive clicking or tapping quality.

Technique and notation

Two variants are distinguished: col legno battuto, in which the stick is struck against the string to produce a short percussive note, and col legno tratto, in which the stick is drawn along the string, yielding a faint, ghostly tone. In notation, the instruction is written above the staff and cancelled by the direction arco when the player returns to normal bowing. Composers sometimes indicate the technique with a special notehead or symbol.

Use in repertoire

The technique appears as early as the 17th century in works by Carlo Farina. Famous later uses include Berlioz's Symphonie fantastique, where col legno battuto in the final movement evokes skeletons dancing; Saint-Saëns's Danse macabre; Mahler's symphonies; Holst's "Mars" from The Planets, which opens with a col legno ostinato; and numerous 20th- and 21st-century scores. Players are often wary of the technique because it can damage varnish and bow sticks, and some orchestras keep inexpensive bows for such passages.
