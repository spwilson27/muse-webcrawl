Carol W. Greider

Carol W. Greider, Nobel laureate in Physiology or Medicine (2009)

Carolyn Widney Greider (born April 15, 1961) is an American molecular biologist and Nobel laureate. She is a Distinguished Professor of Molecular, Cell, and Developmental Biology at the University of California, Santa Cruz. Greider discovered the enzyme telomerase in 1984, while working under Elizabeth Blackburn at the University of California, Berkeley. She also pioneered work on the structure and function of telomeres, the protective ends of chromosomes.

Discovery of telomerase

As a graduate student in Blackburn's laboratory, Greider set out to find the enzyme responsible for adding DNA repeats to chromosome ends in the ciliate Tetrahymena. On Christmas Day 1984 she obtained experimental evidence of the enzymatic activity, and in 1985 she and Blackburn reported the discovery of telomerase, the ribonucleoprotein enzyme that extends telomeres. The work illuminated how chromosomes are protected from progressive shortening and degradation during cell division.

Career and honours

Greider completed her doctorate in 1987 and carried out postdoctoral research at Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, where she later became a professor. In 1997 she moved to the Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine, where she directed the Department of Molecular Biology and Genetics. In 2020 she moved to UC Santa Cruz.

In 2009, Greider shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Elizabeth Blackburn and Jack W. Szostak "for the discovery of how chromosomes are protected by telomeres and the enzyme telomerase."
