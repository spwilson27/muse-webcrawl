Morelos, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Morelos (Spanish: Estado Libre y Soberano de Morelos), is one of the 32 states that comprise the Federal Entities of Mexico. It is divided into 36 municipalities and its capital city is Cuernavaca. Morelos is the second-smallest state in Mexico by area, after Tlaxcala.

Geography

Morelos is located in south-central Mexico, bordered by Mexico City to the north, the State of Mexico to the northeast and northwest, Puebla to the east, and Guerrero to the southwest. The state lies within the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt and the Balsas River basin, with elevations ranging from about 700 metres in the south to over 5,000 metres at the Popocatépetl volcano on its northeastern border. Its warm climate has made it a popular weekend retreat from Mexico City since Aztec times.

History

The region was home to the Tlahuica people, whose center was Cuernavaca (Cuauhnáhuac). After the Spanish conquest, Hernán Cortés made the area part of his marquisate, and Morelos became a major sugar-producing region. The state is named after José María Morelos, a hero of the Mexican War of Independence; it was created in 1869 by President Benito Juárez from territory formerly part of the State of Mexico.

Morelos was the home state of Emiliano Zapata, leader of the Liberation Army of the South during the Mexican Revolution and author of the Plan of Ayala, whose demand for land reform — tierra y libertad — began in the state's sugar haciendas.

Economy and tourism

Tourism centers on Cuernavaca, the "City of Eternal Spring," the lakeside town of Tepoztlán, the archaeological site of Xochicalco (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), and the pre-Hispanic ruins at Teopanzolco and El Tepozteco.
