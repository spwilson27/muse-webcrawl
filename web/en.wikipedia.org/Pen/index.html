Pen

A pen is a writing instrument that applies ink to a surface, usually paper, for writing or drawing. Pens are among humanity's oldest writing tools, evolving from reed pens and quills to modern ballpoint, rollerball, fountain, and stylus pens.

History

Ancient Egyptians wrote with reed pens on papyrus; the feather quill, introduced in the early Middle Ages, dominated Western writing for over a thousand years. Steel dip-pen nibs were mass-produced in nineteenth-century Birmingham, and the fountain pen — carrying its own ink reservoir — was patented in practical form in the 1880s (Lewis Waterman's feed patent of 1884). The ballpoint pen, perfected by László Bíró in the 1930s–40s, used a rotating ball bearing to dispense quick-drying, paste-like ink and became the world's most common writing instrument after the Second World War. The porous-tip (felt-tip) pen and the rollerball, using liquid or gel ink, followed in the 1960s–80s.

Types

Principal modern types are the ballpoint, rollerball, fountain pen, gel pen, felt-tip pen/marker, and stylus pens for touchscreens. Fountain pens remain prized for their smooth, expressive line, while disposable ballpoints are produced in billions annually.
