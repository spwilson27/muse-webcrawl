British royal family

The British royal family comprises King Charles III and his close relations. There is no strict legal or formal definition of who is or is not a member, although the Royal Household has issued various lists outlining who is a part of the royal family. Members typically support the monarch in carrying out public engagements, undertake charitable work, and represent the United Kingdom at home and abroad. The family belongs to the House of Windsor, whose name was adopted by George V in 1917 in place of the German House of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha.

Members

The core ("working") members of the royal family include the King and Queen Camilla; William, Prince of Wales, and Catherine, Princess of Wales; Anne, Princess Royal; Prince Edward, Duke of Edinburgh, and Sophie, Duchess of Edinburgh; and the King's cousins the Duke of Kent, Prince Michael of Kent, and Princess Alexandra. Prince Andrew, Duke of York, and Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex, remain members of the family but no longer carry out official duties on behalf of the monarch.

Role and duties

The head of the family is the sovereign, who is head of state of the United Kingdom and of the other Commonwealth realms. Under the British constitutional monarchy, the sovereign's public duties include the State Opening of Parliament, granting royal assent to legislation, receiving ambassadors, and conferring honours. Working royals collectively carry out thousands of engagements each year, act as patrons or presidents of hundreds of charities and military units, and undertake state visits and tours. The family's activities are financed principally through the Sovereign Grant and the private incomes of the Duchies of Lancaster and Cornwall.

History

The modern royal family descends from the Hanoverian succession of 1714. Queen Victoria's descendants intermarried with royal houses across Europe. The family's name was anglicised during the First World War amid anti-German sentiment. Elizabeth II reigned from 1952 until her death in 2022 — the longest reign of any British monarch — succeeded by her eldest son, Charles III, whose coronation took place on 6 May 2023 at Westminster Abbey.

Titles and styles

Members born into the family are generally styled His or Her Royal Highness with the title of prince or princess; senior members may be granted peerages (dukedoms) on marriage. Under letters patent issued by George V in 1917, the children and grandchildren of the sovereign in the male line, and the eldest son of the eldest son of the Prince of Wales, are entitled to royal status, extended by Elizabeth II in 2012 to all children of the eldest son of the Prince of Wales.
