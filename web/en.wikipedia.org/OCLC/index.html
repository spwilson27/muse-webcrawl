OCLC, Inc. is an American nonprofit cooperative library organization "dedicated to the public purposes of furthering access to the world's information and reducing information costs." Founded in 1967 as the Ohio College Library Center by Fred Kilgour, it later became the Online Computer Library Center before shortening its name to OCLC in 2017. Its headquarters are in Dublin, Ohio.

WorldCat

OCLC maintains WorldCat, the world's largest online public access catalog, which aggregates the collections of tens of thousands of libraries in many countries. Member libraries collectively create and maintain WorldCat's records, including holdings information that supports interlibrary loan.

Services and history

The cooperative began by sharing cataloging among Ohio academic libraries, cutting duplication of effort in producing catalog cards. Over time it expanded into digitization, reference services, and discovery tools. OCLC also administers the Dewey Decimal Classification system, having acquired the rights when it purchased Forest Press in 1988. Its classifications and identifiers, such as OCLC numbers, are widely used to link bibliographic records across systems.

Governance

OCLC is governed by a board of trustees and a global council representing member institutions. Membership is held by libraries and related cultural heritage organizations that use its services.
