Strike action

Strike action, also called simply a strike, is a work stoppage caused by the mass refusal of employees to work, typically taking place in response to employee grievances. Strikes became common during the Industrial Revolution, when mass labour became important in factories and mines.

Forms

Strikes may be organised by trade unions during collective bargaining to gain concessions from an employer, or be unofficial "wildcat" strikes. Variants include the general strike, involving workers across many industries; sit-down strikes, where workers occupy the workplace; work-to-rule; sympathy strikes in support of another group; and hunger strikes as a form of protest.

History

The first recorded strike in history took place in ancient Egypt around 1152 BC, when tomb builders at Deir el-Medina withheld labour over unpaid rations. In the modern era, notable strikes include the UK General Strike of 1926, the wave of sit-down strikes in 1930s America, the Polish Solidarność strikes of 1980, and numerous miners' and transport strikes worldwide. Strikes played major roles in labour-law reform, the eight-hour day movement, and democratisation movements.

Legal status

The right to strike is recognised in many countries and in international labour law, though it is often restricted for essential services and public employees, and employers may in some jurisdictions hire replacement workers. Picketing and secondary action may also be limited by law.
