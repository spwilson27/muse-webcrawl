Diya (lamp)

Diya
TypeOil lamp
MaterialsClay, brass, or other metal
FuelGhee, mustard or sesame oil
Used inDiwali, Karthika Deepam, puja

A diya, dīpam, or deepak is an oil lamp made from clay or mud with a cotton wick dipped in ghee or vegetable oil, used throughout the Indian subcontinent. Diyas are native to India and are strongly associated with the festival of Diwali, the festival of lights, during which homes, temples, and public places are lined with rows of flickering lamps; the word "Diwali" itself derives from Deepavali, "row of lights".

Significance

The lighted diya symbolizes the victory of light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance. In Hindu tradition, lamps are lit during daily worship (puja) and special ceremonies; a brass lamp is often the centerpiece of the South Indian festival of Karthika Deepam. Clay diyas are also lit on the banks of rivers during festivals such as Ganga Mahotsav and Dev Deepavali in Varanasi, and at Diwali celebrations in Jain, Sikh, and some Buddhist traditions — Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas, marking Guru Hargobind's release from prison.

Forms

Traditional diyas are shallow, unfired or baked clay bowls, often painted and decorated for festivals; more durable versions are cast in brass or bronze, sometimes in elaborate multi-tiered forms used in temples.
