Pontryagin's maximum principle

Pontryagin's maximum principle (also called Pontryagin's minimum principle) is used in optimal control theory to find the best possible control for taking a dynamical system from one state to another. It was formulated in 1956 by the Russian mathematician Lev Pontryagin and his students.

Statement

The principle states that any optimal control, together with the optimal state trajectory, must solve the so-called Hamiltonian system: the state evolves according to Hamilton's equations with a Hamiltonian built from the control variable and costate (adjoint) variables, and the Hamiltonian must be maximized (or minimized, depending on sign conventions) with respect to the control at almost every time.

Applications

The principle generalizes the calculus of variations and is foundational in aerospace trajectory optimization, robotics, and economics. It explains the "bang-bang" structure of many optimal controls, such as minimum-time problems.
