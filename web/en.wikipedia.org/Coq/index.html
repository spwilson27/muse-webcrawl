The Coq interactive proof environment, used to develop machine-checked proofs.

Coq (renamed The Rocq Prover in 2025) is an interactive theorem prover and proof assistant developed principally by the French national research institute Inria. It allows users to express mathematical statements and software specifications in a rich formal language and to construct proofs that are verified mechanically by a small trusted kernel.

Overview

Coq is based on the calculus of inductive constructions, a higher-order typed lambda calculus with dependent types and inductive definitions. Because the system follows the Curry–Howard correspondence, proofs are themselves programs, and computation can be used to discharge proof obligations. Users develop proofs interactively using a tactic language, while a small checker verifies each completed proof independently of the tactics used to find it.

History

The project grew out of the calculus of constructions of Thierry Coquand and Gérard Huet in the 1980s; the implementation was named Coq after Coquand and the French word for rooster. It has been developed continuously since, receiving the ACM Software System Award in 2013. In 2025 the project was renamed Rocq to give it a distinctive identity while retaining a connection to its French origins.

Notable applications

Coq has been used to produce fully machine-checked proofs of major results, including the four colour theorem (formalised by Georges Gonthier and collaborators in 2005) and the Feit–Thompson odd order theorem (completed in 2012). In software, the CompCert C compiler, developed by Xavier Leroy and others, is written and verified in Coq and is used in safety-critical industries. Large libraries such as Software Foundations and MathComp support teaching and research.
