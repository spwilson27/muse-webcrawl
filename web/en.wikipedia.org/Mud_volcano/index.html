Mud volcano

A mud volcano or mud dome is a landform created by the eruption of mud or slurries, water and gases. Several geological processes produce mud volcanoes; they are distinct from true igneous volcanoes, as most erupt cool material, although some — usually associated with petroleum deposits — can emit flammable methane and occasionally ignite.

A mud volcano in Gobustan, Azerbaijan

Formation

Mud volcanoes form where subsurface pressure forces water-saturated sediments upward along faults, often in areas of hydrocarbon accumulation and tectonic compression. They range from small cones a metre high to large edifices hundreds of metres high and kilometres wide. Azerbaijan has the greatest concentration in the world, with around 400 of the planet's estimated 1,000+ onshore mud volcanoes.

Eruptions

Most eruptions are gentle bubbling, but some are violent; the 2006 Sidoarjo mud flow (Lusi) in East Java, Indonesia, displaced tens of thousands of people and remains controversial as to its cause. Mud volcanoes have also been reported on Mars as possible analogues for sedimentary volcanism.
