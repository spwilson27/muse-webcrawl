Max Miedinger

Max Miedinger (24 December 1910 – 8 March 1980) was a Swiss typeface designer, best known for creating the Helvetica typeface in 1957 with Eduard Hoffmann. Helvetica is one of the most widely used sans-serif typefaces in the world, used in countless logos, signage systems, and official documents.

Born in Zurich, Miedinger trained as a typesetter and studied at the Kunstgewerbeschule Zürich. He worked as a typographer and later as a salesman and freelance designer for the Haas'sche Schriftgiesserei (Haas Type Foundry) in Münchenstein, near Basel.

Helvetica

In 1956, Eduard Hoffmann, director of the Haas foundry, commissioned Miedinger to design a new sans-serif typeface to compete with the success of Akzidenz-Grotesk. The result, released in 1957 as Neue Haas Grotesk, was renamed Helvetica in 1960 — after Helvetia, the Latin name for Switzerland — to make it more marketable internationally. Linotype licensed and distributed it widely, and it became the signature typeface of Swiss Style / International Typographic Style graphic design.

Miedinger also designed other typefaces, including Pro Arte and Horizontal, though none approached Helvetica's fame. He died in Zurich in 1980.
