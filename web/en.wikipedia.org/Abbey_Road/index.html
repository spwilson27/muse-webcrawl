Abbey Road is a thoroughfare in the borough of Camden and the City of Westminster in London, running north-west from St John's Wood. It is best known as the location of Abbey Road Studios and for the cover photograph of the Beatles' 1969 album Abbey Road, on which the four band members are shown walking across the zebra crossing outside the studio.

History

The road was built in the early nineteenth century as London expanded north-west. EMI's recording studios at No. 3 Abbey Road opened in 1931; Edward Elgar conducted the London Symphony Orchestra there at the opening. The studios were used by generations of artists including Cliff Richard, Pink Floyd and the Beatles, who recorded most of their albums there.

The album cover

The photograph for Abbey Road was taken by Iain Macmillan on 8 August 1969 from a stepladder in the middle of the road while a policeman held up traffic; only six frames were shot. Paul McCartney appears barefoot, a detail that fed the "Paul is dead" rumour. The crossing became a place of pilgrimage for Beatles fans, and in 2010 it was given Grade II listed status.
