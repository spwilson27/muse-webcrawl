Lumen gentium

Lumen gentium, the Dogmatic Constitution on the Church, is one of the principal documents of the Second Vatican Council. Promulgated by Pope Paul VI on 21 November 1964, its title, Latin for "Light of the Nations", comes from its opening words.

Contents

The constitution has eight chapters covering the mystery of the Church, the People of God, the hierarchical structure and the episcopate, the laity, the universal call to holiness, religious life, the pilgrim Church and eschatology, and the Blessed Virgin Mary in the mystery of Christ and the Church.

Teaching

It teaches that the Church of Christ "subsists in" the Catholic Church, emphasizes collegiality of bishops with the pope, the common priesthood of the faithful, and that elements of sanctification exist outside the Church's visible structure. Chapter II's placement of the People of God before the hierarchy was a notable shift in emphasis.

Influence

Lumen gentium underpins post-conciliar ecclesiology, ecumenism, and the theology of the laity.
