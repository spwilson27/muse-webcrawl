Congress of the People

The Congress of the People was a gathering held in Kliptown, Soweto, South Africa, on 25–26 June 1955, at which the Freedom Charter was adopted. It was organised by the Congress Alliance, a coalition led by the African National Congress together with the South African Indian Congress, the South African Congress of Democrats, the Coloured People's Congress and the South African Congress of Trade Unions.

Background

The campaign for the congress collected demands from communities across the country — for land, housing, work, education and political rights — which were drafted into the Freedom Charter. Around 3,000 delegates attended the open-air gathering despite intense police surveillance.

The Freedom Charter

The Charter opened with the declaration that "South Africa belongs to all who live in it, black and white," and demanded a democratic state based on the will of all the people, equal rights, land sharing, and free and compulsory education. Police broke up the second day of proceedings and seized documents; many leaders were later charged in the Treason Trial of 1956.

Legacy

The Freedom Charter became the defining programme of the anti-apartheid movement and strongly influenced South Africa's post-1994 constitution. The name Congress of the People was also later adopted by a breakaway political party (COPE) founded in 2008.
