Brigadier

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


British brigadier's rank insignia

Brigadier is a military rank used in many countries. It is the highest field rank or lowest general rank in various armies, ranking above colonel and below major general, and typically commands a brigade. In the British Army and Royal Marines — and in many Commonwealth forces following the British model — brigadier is the highest non-general rank (rated at NATO level OF-6), whereas in other systems, including the French and many Latin American armies, it corresponds to brigadier general, a one-star general officer rank.

Usage by country

In the United Kingdom, brigadier was historically an appointment rather than a substantive rank; the title "brigadier-general" was abolished in 1922 and replaced by colonel-commandant, then by brigadier from 1928. The rank insignia is a crown over three "pips" (Bath stars). In India, Pakistan and Bangladesh, brigadier is a one-star rank above colonel. In France, général de brigade is the first general rank with two stars. The United States does not use the bare title "brigadier", having brigadier general instead.

Other uses

The title is also used by some police forces, fire services and uniformed organisations for senior officers, and in historical contexts for the commander of a brigade in early modern armies. In the Salvation Army, brigadier was formerly a senior rank between major and colonel.
