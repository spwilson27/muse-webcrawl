William Labov

William Labov (born December 4, 1927) is an American linguist widely regarded as the founder of the discipline of variationist sociolinguistics — the quantitative study of how language varies and changes in social context. He was a professor at the University of Pennsylvania from 1971 until his retirement.

Research

Labov's 1961 master's study of Martha's Vineyard showed how a vowel shift expressed local identity, and his 1966 dissertation on the social stratification of English in New York City department stores — the celebrated "fourth floor" study — demonstrated orderly patterning in speech variation. His work on African American Vernacular English, beginning with Language in the Inner City (1972), argued that the dialect is a systematic, rule-governed variety, work later cited in the Ann Arbor and Oakland "Ebonics" debates. His three-volume Principles of Linguistic Change (1994–2010) and the Atlas of North American English (2006) mapped the chain shifts reorganizing North American vowels, including the Northern Cities Vowel Shift.

Labov is a member of the National Academy of Sciences and received the Benjamin Franklin Medal in 2013.
