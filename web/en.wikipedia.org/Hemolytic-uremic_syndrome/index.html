Hemolytic–uremic syndrome

Hemolytic–uremic syndrome (HUS) is a group of blood disorders characterised by a triad of microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia, low platelet count (thrombocytopenia), and acute kidney injury. It belongs to the thrombotic microangiopathies: small-vessel damage causes red blood cells to fragment as they pass through narrowed, clot-lined vessels, while platelet consumption produces bleeding risk and renal failure.

Classification and causes

The most common form, typical (STEC) HUS, follows infection with Shiga toxin–producing Escherichia coli (such as O157:H7), usually after bloody diarrhoea, and mainly affects young children; a similar syndrome follows Shigella dysenteriae infection. Atypical HUS arises from dysregulation of the alternative complement pathway, often due to inherited mutations in complement regulators (e.g. factor H, factor I, MCP/CD46) or autoantibodies, and carries a poorer prognosis. Secondary forms occur with pneumococcal infection, pregnancy, malignancy, transplantation, certain drugs, and other diseases. HUS is related to thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), which features more prominent neurological involvement and ADAMTS13 deficiency.

Signs and symptoms

After a diarrhoeal prodrome (in STEC-HUS), patients develop pallor and fatigue from anaemia, bruising or petechiae from thrombocytopenia, and reduced urine output, swelling, and hypertension from kidney failure; severe cases involve the brain, pancreas, heart, and gut.

Treatment

Management is mainly supportive: careful fluid and electrolyte balance, blood transfusion, antihypertensives, and dialysis when needed; antibiotics and antimotility agents are generally avoided in STEC infection as they may increase toxin release. Plasma exchange is used where TTP is possible, and the complement inhibitor eculizumab has transformed treatment of atypical HUS. Most children with typical HUS recover renal function, though a minority have lasting kidney damage or die, particularly in large outbreaks such as the 2011 German O104:H4 outbreak.
