Marketplace

A marketplace, market place, or just market, is a location where people regularly gather for the purchase and sale of provisions, livestock, and other goods. Historically the term has also come to denote the abstract arena in which buyers and sellers interact, and in the digital era, online platforms that connect third-party sellers with buyers.

Traditional marketplaces

Markets have existed since antiquity: the Greek agora and Roman forum combined commerce with civic life, while bazaars and souks served cities of the Middle East and Asia. In medieval Europe the market square stood at the centre of a market town. Marketplaces range from daily food markets and wet markets to periodic fairs, flea markets, farmers' markets and wholesale markets serving retailers.

Online marketplaces

An online marketplace is an e-commerce site where product or service information is provided by multiple third parties, while transactions are processed by the marketplace operator. eBay (founded 1995) and Amazon Marketplace (2000) are leading examples; others include Etsy for crafts, Alibaba and AliExpress, and app stores for software. Operators typically charge commissions or listing fees and provide search, payment processing, reviews and dispute resolution, benefiting from network effects while facing criticism over counterfeit goods, fees and competition with their own sellers.

Economic usage

In economics, "the marketplace" is used figuratively: the marketplace of ideas, a phrase drawn from John Stuart Mill and US Supreme Court jurisprudence, holds that truth emerges from the competition of ideas in free public discourse.
