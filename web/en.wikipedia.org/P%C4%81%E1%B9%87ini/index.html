Pāṇini

Bornc. 6th–4th century BCE, Salatura, Gandhara
OccupationGrammarian, linguist
Known forAṣṭādhyāyī, the foundational grammar of Sanskrit

Pāṇini was an ancient Indian Sanskrit grammarian, logician, and philologist who lived in the northwestern Indian subcontinent, traditionally placed in the 6th to 4th century BCE. He is widely regarded as the founder of linguistics and among the greatest scholars of the ancient world.

The Aṣṭādhyāyī

His masterwork, the Aṣṭādhyāyī ("Eight Chapters"), is a remarkably systematic and concise grammar of Sanskrit comprising nearly 4,000 rules (sūtras). It describes the spoken language of his day with extraordinary precision, using a sophisticated metalanguage, abbreviations, and a rule-ordering architecture that has often been compared to a computer program. It is the earliest known complete generative grammar of a natural language.

Influence

The Aṣṭādhyāyī became the canonical standard for Sanskrit and strongly influenced later Indian grammatical tradition, including the commentaries of Kātyāyana and Patañjali. Western scholars who encountered it in the 19th century, such as Ferdinand de Saussure and Leonard Bloomfield, were deeply influenced by its methods; Pāṇini's formal system is sometimes cited as a precursor to modern formal language theory and Backus–Naur form in computing.

Life

Little is known of Pāṇini's life. Tradition holds that he was born in Salatura near the Indus River in Gandhara, in present-day Pakistan, the son of a woman named Dākṣī. Legends in later literature describe him as a dull student blessed by the god Shiva, whose drumbeats were said to have revealed the sounds of Sanskrit to him.
