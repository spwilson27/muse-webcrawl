Picornaviridae is a family of viruses in the order Picornavirales. The name derives from pico-, meaning small, and RNA, referring to their small positive-sense single-stranded RNA genome. Virions are non-enveloped, with an icosahedral capsid about 30 nanometres in diameter. The family contains many well-known human and animal pathogens.

Genome and structure

The picornavirus genome is a single strand of positive-sense RNA roughly 7.2 to 8.5 kilobases long. It is translated as a single polyprotein that is cleaved by virally encoded proteases into structural and non-structural proteins. The capsid is composed of 60 protomers, each made up of four proteins: VP1, VP2, VP3 and VP4.

Genera and diseases

The family includes the genera Enterovirus (which includes poliovirus, coxsackieviruses and rhinoviruses), Cardiovirus, Aphthovirus (foot-and-mouth disease virus), Hepatovirus (hepatitis A virus), Parechovirus and many others. Diseases caused by picornaviruses range from the common cold and poliomyelitis to hepatitis A, myocarditis and foot-and-mouth disease in livestock.

Replication

Picornaviruses replicate in the cytoplasm of the host cell. Entry is by attachment to host receptors, followed by endocytosis or direct penetration. Replication follows the positive-stranded RNA virus model, and assembly and release occur in the cytoplasm, often by cell lysis.
