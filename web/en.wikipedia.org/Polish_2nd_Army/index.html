Second Polish Army (1944–45)

Active: August 1944 – August 1945
Country: Poland
Allegiance: Soviet Union / Polish Committee of National Liberation
Branch: Land forces (Polish Armed Forces in the East)
Engagements: Vistula–Oder Offensive, Battle of Bautzen, Prague Offensive
Commander: Karol Świerczewski

The Second Polish Army (Polish: Druga Armia Wojska Polskiego, 2 AWP) was a Polish Army unit formed in the Soviet Union in 1944 as part of the Polish Armed Forces in the East. It was commanded by General Karol Świerczewski. Formed in August–September 1944, it grew to roughly 90,000 soldiers by early 1945, although it suffered from shortages of officers and specialists that were partly filled by Soviet personnel.

Combat history

The army entered combat during the Vistula–Oder Offensive in January 1945, and in April 1945 took part in the drive toward Dresden and Berlin as part of the Soviet 1st Ukrainian Front. Its bloodiest action was the Battle of Bautzen (21–30 April 1945), where a German counteroffensive inflicted heavy casualties; the 2nd Army lost some 18,000 men killed, wounded and missing, a setback often attributed to Świerczewski's poor leadership. Elements of the army subsequently participated in the Prague Offensive in May 1945.

Postwar

After the war the army guarded Poland's new western border on the Oder–Neisse line and was reorganised into military districts in the autumn of 1945. Together with the First Polish Army, it formed the core of the armed forces of postwar Poland.
