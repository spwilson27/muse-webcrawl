The Austrian Netherlands was the territory of the Southern Netherlands under the rule of the Austrian branch of the House of Habsburg from 1714, following the Treaty of Rastatt at the end of the War of the Spanish Succession, until its annexation by France in the 1790s.

History

The region passed from Spain to Austria as part of the peace settlement of Utrecht and Rastatt. Under Charles VI and Maria Theresa the provinces were administered from Brussels by a governor-general, frequently a member of the imperial family. The reforming policies of Joseph II provoked strong resistance and helped trigger the Brabantine Revolution of 1789, which briefly established the United Belgian States before Austrian rule was restored.

End

French revolutionary armies conquered the territory in 1794, and Austria formally ceded it in the Treaty of Campo Formio (1797). The region later formed the core of the independent Kingdom of Belgium.
