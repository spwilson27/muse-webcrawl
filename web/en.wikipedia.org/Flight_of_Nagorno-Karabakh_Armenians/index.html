Flight of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians

The flight of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians was the mass exodus of ethnic Armenians from Nagorno-Karabakh in September–October 2023, following Azerbaijan's military offensive on 19–20 September 2023 against the self-declared Republic of Artsakh. Within roughly a week, nearly the entire Armenian population of the region — over 100,000 people out of an estimated population of about 120,000 — fled to Armenia through the Lachin corridor.

Background

Nagorno-Karabakh, internationally recognised as part of Azerbaijan, had been administered since the First Nagorno-Karabakh War by the unrecognised Republic of Artsakh with an ethnic Armenian majority. After the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War in 2020, a Russian peacekeeping contingent monitored the region, but from December 2022 Azerbaijan blockaded the Lachin corridor, the region's only road link to Armenia, producing a humanitarian crisis.

The exodus

Following a one-day offensive, the Artsakh authorities capitulated and agreed to disarm. On 24 September 2023 the first refugees began crossing into Armenia; within days the flow became near-total. By early October, Armenian officials reported that over 100,600 people had arrived, leaving only a handful of Armenians in the region. On 28 September, Artsakh's president signed a decree dissolving the republic effective 1 January 2024.

Reactions

Numerous international observers, legal scholars, and governments characterised the exodus as ethnic cleansing, a characterisation Azerbaijan rejected, stating that Armenians were free to remain and reintegrate. Armenia brought proceedings against Azerbaijan at the International Court of Justice, which in November 2023 ordered Azerbaijan to ensure the safe return of those who had fled and wished to go back.
