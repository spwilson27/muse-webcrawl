Anthropology of food

The anthropology of food is a sub-discipline of anthropology that connects an ethnographic and historical perspective with contemporary social issues in food production and consumption systems. It studies how food practices — production, preparation, sharing, taboo and feasting — express identity, kinship, religion, power and exchange.

Key themes

Classic works include Claude Lévi-Strauss's structuralist analysis of cooking in "The Culinary Triangle" and Mary Douglas's decoding of meal structures and purity rules in Purity and Danger. Sidney Mintz's Sweetness and Power traced how sugar linked Caribbean slavery to European consumption, founding much modern political economy of food. The field also examines food taboos, gift exchange and hospitality, famine, industrial food systems, globalization of cuisines, and movements such as slow food and food sovereignty.

Methods

Food anthropologists use participant observation in markets, kitchens, farms and restaurants, combined with archival and nutritional research, to understand how eating both sustains bodies and creates social meaning.
