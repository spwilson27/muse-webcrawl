Lindos (Greek: Λίνδος) is a town and archaeological site on the east coast of the island of Rhodes in the Dodecanese, Greece. It lies about 50 km south of Rhodes Town. In antiquity Lindos was one of the three great Dorian cities of Rhodes — with Ialysos and Kamiros — and a major trading port before the founding of Rhodes city in 408 BC.

History

Tradition held Lindos founded by the Dorians under Tlepolemus, a son of Heracles. Its merchants founded colonies including Gela in Sicily, and the Lindian law of the sea influenced the later Rhodian maritime code. The tyrant Cleobulus of Lindos, one of the Seven Sages of Greece, ruled in the 6th century BC. The acropolis was successively fortified by Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, the Knights of St John and the Ottomans; the Knights' castle walls enclose the Hellenistic stoa and the Temple of Athena Lindia (c. 300 BC). After Italian occupation (1912–1945) the Dodecanese were united with Greece in 1948.

The modern village

The village below the acropolis is a protected settlement of whitewashed houses, pebble-mosaic courtyards (chochlaki) and "captains' houses" from its seafaring prosperity of the 16th–18th centuries. The Byzantine church of Panagia (Our Lady) of Lindos, with 18th-century frescoes, stands at its centre. Cars are excluded from the narrow lanes. South of the village lies St Paul's Bay, traditionally where the Apostle Paul landed in AD 51. Lindos is one of the most popular tourist destinations in Greece, known also for some of the warmest temperatures in the country.
