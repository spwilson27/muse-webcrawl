The Haunting of Hill House is a 1959 Gothic horror novel by American author Shirley Jackson. The book — a finalist for the National Book Award in 1960 — is widely regarded as one of the finest ghost stories of the twentieth century. Stephen King has called it one of the most important horror novels of its era, praising its opening paragraph as among the best in the genre: "Hill House, not sane, stood by itself against its hills, holding darkness within…" Jackson herself expressed surprise at its success, reportedly having aimed for readers to be thoroughly frightened.

Plot

Dr. John Montague, an investigator of the supernatural, rents the eighty-year-old Hill House — a mansion with a history of violent deaths and reputed hauntings — for a summer study of paranormal phenomena. His guests are the heir Luke, the artistic Theodora, and Eleanor Vance, a lonely young woman who has spent her adult life caring for her invalid mother and who once experienced poltergeist phenomena. Over the following days, cold spots, closed doors, writing on walls, and terrifying nocturnal visitations focus increasingly on Eleanor, whose fragile psychology merges with the house's malevolence. After she is sent away for her own safety, Eleanor crashes her car into a tree on the drive, dying — in Jackson's deliberately ambiguous telling — either by suicide or by the house's will.

Adaptations and influence

The novel was filmed by Robert Wise as The Haunting (1963), a classic of psychological horror; a poorly received remake followed in 1999; and Mike Flanagan's 2018 Netflix series reimagined it as a family drama to critical acclaim. The novel's fusion of haunting and mental disintegration heavily influenced modern horror fiction.
