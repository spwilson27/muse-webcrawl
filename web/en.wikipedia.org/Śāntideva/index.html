Śāntideva (Sanskrit: शान्तिदेव; c. 685 – 763 CE) was an Indian Buddhist monk, philosopher, and poet of the great monastic university of Nālandā, revered throughout the Mahāyāna world as a master of the bodhisattva path.

Life

Tibetan biographies describe Śāntideva as a prince of Saurāṣṭra who renounced the throne on the eve of his coronation after a vision of the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, and travelled to Nālandā to become a monk under Jayadeva. There he acquired a misleading reputation for idleness: his fellow monks joked that his only three activities were eating, sleeping, and defecating. To expose him, they invited him to recite scripture before the whole assembly from a tall throne with no steps, expecting humiliation. Instead he levitated to the seat and delivered the poem that became the Bodhicaryāvatāra, ascending into the sky as he reached its ninth chapter, on wisdom.

Works

The Bodhicaryāvatāra ("A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life"), in ten chapters of Sanskrit verse, is one of the most beloved texts of Mahāyāna Buddhism. It teaches the generation of bodhicitta (the resolve to attain awakening for all beings), the six perfections (pāramitā), meditations on exchanging self and other, and a Madhyamaka analysis of emptiness. Its verses are still memorised and commented upon across the Tibetan tradition, and the present Dalai Lama has called it the single most important source of his own practice. Śāntideva's other surviving work, the Śikṣāsamuccaya ("Compendium of Training"), supports the same teachings with long quotations from earlier sutras, many of which survive only in his citations.
