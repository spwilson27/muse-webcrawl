Nosework
TypeDog sport / scent work
OriginSouthern California, USA (~2006)
Target odoursBirch, anise, clove

Nosework, also called scent work, is a dog sport modelled on professional detection work in which dogs search for hidden target odours and indicate their location to a handler. It was developed in Southern California in the mid-2000s by trainers including Ron Gaunt, Amy Herot and Jill Marie O'Brien, who founded the National Association of Canine Scent Work (NACSW).

How it works

Dogs search defined areas — interiors, exteriors, vehicles and containers — for cotton swabs scented with essential oils (typically birch, anise and clove at different levels). Handlers must read their dog's body language and call the alert when the dog locates the source.

Popularity

The sport is noted for accessibility: elderly dogs, reactive dogs and dogs with disabilities can participate because searches are done one dog at a time. Major registries including the AKC and UKC later adopted scent work programmes, and the activity is also used for shelter-dog enrichment.
