South Asian river dolphin

The South Asian river dolphin (Platanista gangetica) is a freshwater dolphin found in the great river systems of the Indian subcontinent. It is traditionally treated as one species with two subspecies — the Ganges river dolphin and the Indus river dolphin — though some recent research argues they should be recognised as separate species.

Description

These dolphins have a long, pointed snout lined with sharp teeth, a stocky body, very small eyes that lack a lens (they are effectively blind), and navigate and hunt by echolocation in muddy waters. They characteristically swim on one side, trailing a flipper along the bottom. Females, larger than males, reach about 2.6 metres.

Distribution and conservation

The Ganges subspecies inhabits the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna and Karnaphuli river systems of India, Nepal, and Bangladesh; the Indus subspecies survives mainly in a stretch of the Indus in Pakistan. Both are endangered, threatened by dam construction fragmenting their habitat, water extraction, pollution, and accidental capture in fishing nets. The Ganges dolphin is India's national aquatic animal.
