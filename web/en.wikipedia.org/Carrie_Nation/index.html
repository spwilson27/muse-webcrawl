Carrie Nation

Carrie Nation with her hatchet, c. 1900

Caroline Amelia Nation (November 25, 1846 – June 9, 1911), often rendered Carrie, Carry A. Nation, or Carry Nation, was an American temperance advocate who was known for attacking alcohol-serving establishments (most often taverns) with a hatchet. She was a leader of the temperance movement, which opposed alcohol before the advent of Prohibition.

Early life

Nation was born in Kentucky and had an unsettled childhood; her mother suffered from mental illness. Her first marriage, to an alcoholic physician, Charles Gloyd, ended with his death and strongly influenced her views on alcohol. She later married David Nation, a minister and lawyer, and the couple moved to Kansas.

Activism

After Kansas had adopted a constitutional amendment prohibiting alcohol in 1880 — a law widely ignored by saloon keepers — Nation began campaigning for its enforcement. In 1900 she destroyed the fixtures of a saloon in Kiowa, Kansas, believing she had received a divine calling. Her most famous raid was on the Carey Hotel in Wichita, where she smashed the bar with rocks and a hatchet. She was arrested many times — around thirty — and funded her fines and bail through lecture tours and the sale of souvenir hatchets.

Nearly six feet tall and dressed in severe black-and-white clothing, Nation presented herself as a "bulldog running along at the feet of Jesus, barking at what he doesn't like." Her violent methods were controversial even within the temperance movement, but they brought national attention to the cause.

Death and legacy

Nation collapsed during a speech in 1911 and died later that year. She was buried in Belton, Missouri; the Woman's Christian Temperance Union later inscribed her gravestone. Her home in Medicine Lodge, Kansas, is preserved as a museum.
