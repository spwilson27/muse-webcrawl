Onsen

Japanese: 温泉 (おんせん)

Definition (Onsen Act): water, vapour or gas emerging from underground at 25 °C or above, or containing specified mineral substances

Number in Japan: tens of thousands of sources; approx. 2,300 onsen inns/towns

In Japan, onsen (温泉) are hot springs and the bathing facilities and traditional inns located around them. Thanks to Japan's volcanic geography there are thousands of onsen across the country, in settings ranging from mountain valleys to the seashore. Onsen play a central role in Japanese tourism and culture, and bathing in their mineral waters is traditionally associated with relaxation and health benefits.

Definition and types

Under Japan's Onsen Act (Onsen Law), water qualifies as onsen if it emerges from the ground at 25 °C or warmer, or if it contains at least one of a list of designated mineral substances such as sulfur, iron, or radon, even at lower temperatures. Springs are categorised by their dominant chemistry — simple springs, chloride springs, hydrogen carbonate springs, sulfate springs, sulfur springs, iron springs, acidic springs, and radioactive springs among them — and traditional claims attach different therapeutic properties to each type.

Famous onsen towns

Well-known onsen resorts include Beppu and Yufuin in Ōita Prefecture, Kusatsu in Gunma, Hakone near Tokyo, Ginzan Onsen in Yamagata with its wooden ryokan-lined river, Kinosaki in Hyōgo where visitors stroll between public baths in yukata, and Dōgo Onsen in Matsuyama, one of Japan's oldest springs with a history of some 3,000 years. The three most celebrated springs of the Edo period, the "three famous springs" (三名泉), were Arima, Kusatsu, and Gero.

Etiquette

Bathing is done naked in gender-separated baths (mixed bathing, kon'yoku, survives in some rural areas). Guests must wash thoroughly at the shower stations before entering the water, must not let towels touch the bath water, and bathing suits are prohibited. Historically many onsen prohibited bathers with tattoos because of associations with organised crime, although rules have been relaxed in many places as international tourism has grown. Outdoor baths are called rotenburo, and private reservable baths are known as kashikiri.

Health

Onsen bathing (balneotherapy) is popularly credited with relieving muscle pain, joint stiffness, skin conditions, and fatigue, and onsen therapy has a long tradition in Japanese medicine, though the strength of scientific evidence varies by claim. Waters must meet hygiene standards, and bathers with certain conditions are advised to take care with very hot or acidic springs.
