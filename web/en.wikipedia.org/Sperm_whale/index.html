Sperm whale

Sperm whale (Physeter macrocephalus)
SizeMales to about 16–18 m and 45+ tonnes; females smaller
Distinguishing traitsEnormous block-shaped head (up to a third of body length), spermaceti organ, largest brain of any animal
DivingRegularly 400–1,000 m; over 2,000 m and two hours recorded
DietSquid, including giant and colossal squid; fish
ConservationVulnerable (IUCN); hunted historically for spermaceti oil

The sperm whale (Physeter macrocephalus) is the largest of the toothed whales and the largest toothed predator on Earth. Named for the milky spermaceti oil in its vast head, it is distinguished by its block-shaped forehead, narrow underslung jaw, and a single S-shaped blowhole set far forward on the left. Sperm whales live in all deep oceans, dive deeper than almost any other mammal to hunt squid, and communicate in patterned clicks called codas that vary between cultural clans.

Biology

The head contains the spermaceti organ, a reservoir of waxy oil possibly used in echolocation and buoyancy control; sperm whale clicks are the loudest sounds produced by any animal. Females and young live in stable matrilineal pods with communal calf care, while mature males roam high latitudes alone. Teeth — up to 36–50 large cones in the lower jaw — appear to matter more for grasping squid and social display than for chewing, as prey is often swallowed whole.

Whaling and culture

Commercial whaling targeted sperm whales from the early 1700s for spermaceti (used in candles and cosmetics) and ambergris, a rare intestinal secretion prized in perfumery. Industrial whaling in the 20th century killed hundreds of thousands before the IWC moratorium of the 1980s. The whale that rammed and sank the whaleship Essex in 1820 inspired Herman Melville's Moby-Dick (1851), whose white whale made the species a cultural icon.

Conservation

Populations are recovering slowly — sperm whales produce one calf every four to six years — and the species is listed as Vulnerable, threatened by entanglement, ship strikes, ocean noise and plastic ingestion.
