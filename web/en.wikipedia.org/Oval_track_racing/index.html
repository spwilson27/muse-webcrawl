Oval track racing

Oval track racing is a form of motorsport held on an oval-shaped circuit. It is the dominant form of auto racing in the United States, exemplified by NASCAR stock car racing and IndyCar, and ranges from local dirt short tracks to superspeedways over two miles long.

Tracks

Ovals are classified by length as short tracks (under one mile), intermediates, and superspeedways, and by surface—paved asphalt or concrete, or unpaved dirt and clay. Banking varies from flat to over 30 degrees, as at Talladega Superspeedway. Although most ovals turn left (counterclockwise), configurations and series differ worldwide; in the United Kingdom, oval racing features short-circuit stock cars and bangers.

Characteristics

Racing in a pack at sustained high speed, drafting, and frequent caution periods distinguish the discipline from road racing.
