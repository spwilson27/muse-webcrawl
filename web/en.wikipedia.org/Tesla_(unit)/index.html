Tesla (unit)

The tesla (symbol: T) is the unit of magnetic flux density (magnetic induction, the B-field) in the International System of Units (SI). One tesla equals one weber per square metre. The unit was announced in 1960 and is named after the inventor Nikola Tesla.

Definition

A particle carrying a charge of one coulomb and moving perpendicular to a magnetic field of one tesla, at a speed of one metre per second, experiences a force of one newton, according to the Lorentz force law. One tesla is also equal to 10,000 gauss, the unit used in the centimetre–gram–second system.

Examples

The Earth's magnetic field at the surface is roughly 25 to 65 microteslas. Magnetic resonance imaging scanners typically operate at 1.5 to 3 teslas, while the strongest continuous fields produced in laboratories exceed 40 teslas. The surface magnetic field of a neutron star can reach hundreds of millions of teslas.
