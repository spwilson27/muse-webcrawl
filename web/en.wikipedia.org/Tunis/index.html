Tunis (Arabic: تونس) is the capital and largest city of Tunisia. Situated on the Gulf of Tunis at the western end of the Mediterranean Sea, the metropolitan area is home to roughly a quarter of the country's population and serves as its political, economic and cultural centre.

History

Tunis grew as a settlement near ancient Carthage and rose to prominence under Arab rule from the 7th century. It became capital under the Almohads and flourished under the Hafsid dynasty in the 13th–16th centuries, when its medina — now a UNESCO World Heritage Site — took shape around the Zitouna Mosque. The city later came under Ottoman influence and, from 1881, French protectorate rule, until Tunisian independence in 1956.

Geography and layout

The historic medina, with its souks, palaces and mosques, contrasts with the European-style Ville Nouvelle built during the protectorate along the Avenue Habib Bourguiba. A ship canal connects the city to the port of La Goulette on the gulf.

Modern city

Tunis was the epicentre of the 2010–2011 Tunisian revolution that began the Arab Spring. Today it hosts government institutions, the University of Tunis, the Bardo National Museum and a growing service economy.
