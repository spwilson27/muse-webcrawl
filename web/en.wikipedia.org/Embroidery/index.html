Embroidery is the craft of decorating fabric or other materials using a needle to apply yarn or thread. Embroidery may also incorporate other materials such as pearls, beads, quills, and sequins. It is used to ornament clothing, religious objects, household linens, and wall hangings, and has been practised worldwide for thousands of years.

History

The earliest surviving embroideries date from Warring States–period China (5th–3rd century BC). Elaborate traditions developed in Persia, India, Japan, and medieval Europe, where church vestments and the famous Bayeux Tapestry — an embroidered cloth nearly 70 metres long depicting the Norman Conquest — testify to the craft's importance. In many cultures embroidery signalled status and wealth, employing gold and silver thread (goldwork).

Techniques and classification

Embroidery is commonly divided into surface embroidery, counted-thread embroidery (such as cross-stitch and blackwork), and canvas work (needlepoint), in which thread is stitched through a canvas mesh. Basic stitches include running, back, chain, satin, and blanket stitches; regional styles — from Japanese sashiko to Indian kantha and Hungarian written embroidery — combine them in distinctive ways.

Machine embroidery and contemporary practice

Hand-embroidery machines appeared in the 19th century, and computerised machines now digitise designs automatically. Hand embroidery persists as a folk art and a contemporary fine-art medium, and has seen a popular revival as a hobby.
