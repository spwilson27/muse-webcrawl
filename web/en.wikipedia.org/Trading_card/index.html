A trading card (or collectible card) is a small card, usually made out of paperboard or thick paper, which usually contains an image of a certain person, place or thing (fictional or real) and a short description of the picture, along with other text such as statistics or trivia. Cards are produced in sets and are meant to be collected and traded.

History

Precursors include 19th-century trade cards inserted into product packaging. Tobacco companies included cards of baseball players in cigarette packs from the 1880s, and the Goudey Gum Company popularized bubble-gum cards in the 1930s. Topps became the dominant sports-card maker after the Second World War.

Sports cards

Baseball, basketball, football, hockey, and soccer cards form the largest traditional category. Rarity, condition grading, autographs, and pieces of game-used memorabilia drive the secondary market; rare cards such as the T206 Honus Wagner have sold for millions of dollars.

Game and entertainment cards

Trading card games such as Magic: The Gathering (1993), Pokémon, and Yu-Gi-Oh! made cards functional game pieces. Non-sport cards tied to films, television, and franchises form another large collecting category.

Digital era

Digital card apps and blockchain-based collectibles extended the format online, though physical graded cards saw a major market boom in the early 2020s.
