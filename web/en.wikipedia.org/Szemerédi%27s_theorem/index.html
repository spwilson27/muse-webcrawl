Szemerédi's theorem

Szemerédi's theorem is a result in arithmetic combinatorics stating that any subset of the integers of positive upper density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. Formally: for every positive integer k and every δ > 0 there exists a number N such that any subset of {1, …, N} of size at least δN contains an arithmetic progression of length k.

History

The theorem was conjectured by Paul Erdős and Pál Turán in 1936. The case of 3-term progressions was proved by Klaus Roth in 1953 using Fourier-analytic methods, and the 4-term case by Endre Szemerédi in 1969. Szemerédi proved the full theorem in 1975 using an intricate combinatorial argument, for which his regularity lemma on graphs became a key tool. In 1977 Hillel Furstenberg gave a new proof using ergodic theory, establishing a correspondence principle between combinatorial number theory and multiple recurrence in dynamical systems. The theorem is also a far-reaching generalization of van der Waerden's theorem.

Related developments

Erdős offered a $1,000 prize for the theorem, one of the largest he ever offered. Quantitative bounds on N remain an active research area; the Green–Tao theorem (2004), which shows the primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions, relies on a relative version of Szemerédi's theorem.
