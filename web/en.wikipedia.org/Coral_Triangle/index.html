Coral Triangle

Area~10 million km² of ocean
CountriesIndonesia, Malaysia, Papua New Guinea, Philippines, Solomon Islands, Timor-Leste
Known forHighest marine biodiversity on Earth

The Coral Triangle is a roughly triangular area of tropical marine waters in the western Pacific Ocean encompassing parts of Indonesia, Malaysia, Papua New Guinea, the Philippines, the Solomon Islands and Timor-Leste. It is recognised as the global centre of marine biodiversity.

Biodiversity

The region contains more than 600 species of reef-building corals — about three quarters of the world's known total — and thousands of species of reef fish. It supports six of the world's seven marine turtle species and provides habitat for tuna spawning grounds of global commercial importance.

Threats and conservation

More than 100 million people depend on the region's fisheries and coastal resources. Threats include overfishing, destructive fishing practices, coastal development, pollution and coral bleaching from warming seas. The Coral Triangle Initiative on Coral Reefs, Fisheries, and Food Security, launched in 2009, coordinates conservation among the six nations.
