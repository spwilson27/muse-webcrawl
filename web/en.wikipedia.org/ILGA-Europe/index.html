ILGA-Europe

ILGA-Europe is the European region of the International Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Trans and Intersex Association (ILGA). It is an advocacy group promoting the human rights and equality of lesbian, gay, bisexual, trans and intersex (LGBTI) people at the European level. Its membership comprises over 700 organisations from 54 European and Central Asian countries, and it is headquartered in Brussels, Belgium.

ILGA-Europe's Rainbow Map ranks European countries on LGBTI equality laws and policies.

History

ILGA-Europe was founded in 1996 as a separate region of ILGA, which itself had been founded in 1978 (as the International Gay Association). It has consultative status with the Council of Europe and participatory status at its Conference of INGOs, and it engages with the institutions of the European Union, the OSCE and the United Nations on European LGBTI issues.

Activities

The organisation advocates for anti-discrimination legislation, legal gender recognition, protection from hate crime and hate speech, freedom of assembly and association, and the rights of LGBTI asylum seekers and families. It produces strategic litigation support, capacity-building for member organisations, and policy analysis for European institutions.

Rainbow Map and Annual Review

Since 2009 ILGA-Europe has published the Rainbow Map (now Rainbow Europe Map and Index), benchmarking 49 European countries against criteria in areas such as equality and non-discrimination, family, hate crime and hate speech, legal gender recognition, intersex bodily integrity, civil society space, and asylum; Malta has repeatedly ranked first. Its Annual Review of the Human Rights Situation of LGBTI People in Europe and Central Asia documents legal and social developments country by country.
