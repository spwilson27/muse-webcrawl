Biotechnology

Biotechnology is a multidisciplinary field that involves the integration of natural sciences and engineering sciences in order to achieve the application of organisms and parts thereof for products and services. The term was coined by the Hungarian engineer Károly Ereky in 1919. Modern biotechnology exploits recombinant DNA technology, cell culture, and bioinformatics.

History

Humans have used biotechnology for millennia in brewing, baking, and selective breeding. The modern era began in the 1970s with recombinant DNA techniques: Genentech, founded in 1976, produced human insulin in bacteria, approved in 1982 as the first recombinant drug. The polymerase chain reaction (1983), automated DNA sequencing, and the Human Genome Project further transformed the field, and CRISPR–Cas9 gene editing emerged after 2012.

Applications

In medicine, biotechnology produces monoclonal antibodies, vaccines (including mRNA vaccines), gene and cell therapies, and diagnostic tests. In agriculture it yields genetically modified crops with herbicide tolerance or insect resistance, such as Bt cotton and maize. Industrial biotechnology uses engineered microbes and enzymes to make biofuels, bioplastics, detergents, and specialty chemicals, while bioremediation uses organisms to clean up pollutants.

Regulation and ethics

Biotechnology products are regulated by agencies such as the FDA and EMA for safety and efficacy, and genetically modified foods remain controversial in many countries. Ethical debates concern human germline editing — dramatized by the 2018 CRISPR babies affair — patenting of genes and organisms, and equitable access to expensive biologic medicines.
