World War II casualties of Poland

Estimates of Polish casualties in World War II generally put the total number of deaths of Polish citizens at around six million, comprising roughly three million Polish Jews murdered in the Holocaust and around three million ethnic Poles and other Polish citizens killed through Nazi German and Soviet policies. Poland suffered the highest proportional population loss of any country in the war, losing approximately 17–20 percent of its pre-war population.

Background

Poland was invaded by Nazi Germany on 1 September 1939 and by the Soviet Union on 17 September 1939. The country was partitioned between the two occupiers, and both regimes pursued policies of terror against the Polish population, including mass executions of the intelligentsia, deportations, and forced labour.

Jewish victims

Approximately 90 percent of Poland's pre-war Jewish population of about 3.3 million was murdered in the Holocaust, most in German extermination camps established on occupied Polish territory, including Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka, Belzec, Sobibor, and Chelmno, as well as in ghetto liquidations and mass shootings.

Ethnic Polish victims

Ethnic Poles died in mass executions such as the Intelligenzaktion and AB-Aktion, in concentration and labour camps, in reprisals for resistance activity, during the pacification of villages, and in the Warsaw Uprising of 1944, in which about 150,000–200,000 civilians were killed. Soviet repression, including the Katyn massacre and deportations to Siberia and Kazakhstan, caused further deaths.

Postwar estimates

A 1947 Polish government report estimated 6,028,000 deaths attributable to the German occupation. The Institute of National Remembrance (IPN), in research published in 2009, estimated total deaths of Polish citizens at between 5.6 and 5.8 million, including victims of both German and Soviet occupation.
