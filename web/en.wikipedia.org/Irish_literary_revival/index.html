Irish literary revival

The Irish literary revival, also called the Irish literary renaissance or Celtic Revival, was a movement in the late 19th and early 20th centuries that renewed Irish literature by drawing on Gaelic mythology, folklore and the Irish language, and was closely linked to cultural nationalism.

Figures

Leading figures included W. B. Yeats, Lady Gregory, J. M. Synge, Douglas Hyde and George Russell (Æ). Hyde's Gaelic League promoted the Irish language.

Theatre

The Irish Literary Theatre and then the Abbey Theatre (founded 1904) staged plays such as Synge's The Playboy of the Western World (1907), which provoked riots.

Legacy

The revival shaped modern Irish identity and literature, influencing James Joyce and later writers who also reacted against its romanticism.
