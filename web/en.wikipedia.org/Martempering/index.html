Martempering

Martempering, also called marquenching, is an interrupted quenching heat treatment for steel designed to produce a martensitic structure while minimising distortion, residual stress and the risk of quench cracking. Instead of cooling the workpiece rapidly all the way to room temperature, the quench is interrupted just above the martensite-start temperature.

Process

The austenitised steel part is quenched in a hot bath — usually molten salt or hot oil — held slightly above the martensite-start (Ms) temperature, typically around 200 °C. The part is held in the bath only long enough for the temperature to equalise between its surface and centre, but not long enough for bainite to form. It is then cooled in air through the martensite transformation range, so that martensite forms nearly uniformly throughout the section at the same time, greatly reducing internal stresses. A subsequent tempering treatment is usually applied to give the required toughness.

Applications

Martempering is used for precision gears, bearings, dies and other alloy-steel components where dimensional stability and crack-free hardening are critical. A related process, austempering, holds the part at a higher temperature to produce a bainitic instead of martensitic structure.
