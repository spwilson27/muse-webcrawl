Subcontractor

A subcontractor is an individual or a business that signs a contract to perform part or all of the obligations of another's contract. A subcontractor is a company or person whom a general contractor, prime contractor or another subcontractor hires to perform a specific task as part of the overall project — most commonly in construction, engineering, manufacturing, and information technology — and is normally paid for services provided to the project by the originating general contractor.

In construction

While subcontracting is common in many industries, building construction is particularly dependent on it: general contractors typically self-perform a minority of the work and engage specialist "trades" — electrical, plumbing, HVAC, steel erection, roofing, drywall, painting, and others — as subcontractors. Subcontractors in turn sometimes engage lower-tier subcontractors, producing chains that raise questions of payment security; mechanisms such as mechanics liens, payment bonds, and prompt-payment laws protect lower tiers.

Legal and tax considerations

Subcontractors are usually independent contractors rather than employees, with consequences for taxation, labor law, insurance, and liability; misclassification is a recurrent legal issue. Contracts typically flow down the prime contract's terms, and disputes commonly concern scope, delay, defective work, and payment.
