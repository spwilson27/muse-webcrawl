Maine is a state in the New England region of the United States, bordered by New Hampshire to the west, the Gulf of Maine to the southeast, and the Canadian provinces of New Brunswick and Quebec to the northeast and northwest. It is the northernmost state in the contiguous United States east of the Great Lakes. Maine is known for its jagged, rocky coastline, low rolling mountains, heavily forested interior, and picturesque waterways, as well as its seafood cuisine, especially lobster and clams.

History

Indigenous peoples inhabited the region for thousands of years before European settlement. Maine was part of Massachusetts until 1820, when it was admitted to the Union as a free state under the Missouri Compromise. Its maritime and shipbuilding industries flourished in the 19th century.

Geography

Maine is the largest state in New England by area. About 90 percent of its land is forested, the highest proportion of any state. Acadia National Park, on Mount Desert Island, protects rugged coastal headlands and the Cadillac Mountain summit. The state's highest point is Mount Katahdin (5,270 ft), the northern terminus of the Appalachian Trail.

Economy

Tourism, commercial fishing (notably lobster), agriculture (wild blueberries and potatoes), and forest products are major industries. The Port of Portland and L.L.Bean, headquartered in Freeport, are significant employers.

Demographics

Maine has the oldest median age of any U.S. state and the highest proportion of residents of French ancestry in New England. Its population is concentrated along the southern coast.
