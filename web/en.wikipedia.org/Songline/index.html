Songline

A songline, also called a Dreaming track, is one of the paths across the land (or sometimes the sky) within the animist belief system of Aboriginal Australians, marking the route followed by localised "creator-beings" during the Dreaming. The paths of the songlines are recorded in traditional song cycles, dances, stories, and art.

Description

A songline is based on the travels of a creator-being — a totemic ancestor such as the Rainbow Serpent, a kangaroo, or an emu — who wandered over the continent in the Dreamtime, singing out the names of everything they encountered: birds, animals, plants, rocks, and waterholes. By singing the world into existence in this way, the ancestors created the landscape and established its laws.

Function

Songlines function simultaneously as sacred narrative, law, and practical map. A knowledgeable person can navigate vast distances by reciting the song, whose verses describe water sources, landmarks, and the boundaries of neighbouring territories. Because creator-beings crossed many language groups, songlines often extend for thousands of kilometres across the continent, and their melodies are shared even where the words change with the local language.

In popular culture

The concept was brought to wide international attention by Bruce Chatwin's 1987 travel book The Songlines.
