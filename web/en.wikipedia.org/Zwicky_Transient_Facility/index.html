Zwicky Transient Facility

Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF)
Location: Palomar Observatory, California
Telescope: Samuel Oschin Schmidt telescope (48-inch)
First light: 2017
Field of view: 47 square degrees

The Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) is a wide-field sky survey at Palomar Observatory in California, designed to discover transient astronomical events such as supernovae, asteroids and variable stars. It is named after astronomer Fritz Zwicky, a pioneer of supernova surveys at Palomar.

Instrument

ZTF uses a custom-built 576-megapixel camera mounted on the 48-inch Samuel Oschin Schmidt telescope, giving an enormous 47-square-degree field of view. It can image the entire visible northern sky in about three nights, producing alerts for objects that change brightness or position within minutes of observation.

Science

The survey has discovered thousands of supernovae, numerous near-Earth asteroids and unusual transients, and provides rapid alerts for follow-up of gravitational-wave and neutrino events. ZTF succeeded the Palomar Transient Factory and serves as a pathfinder for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory.
