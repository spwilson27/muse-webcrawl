The President of Iceland (Icelandic: Forseti Íslands) is the head of state of Iceland. The office was established in 1944 when Iceland became a republic, replacing the Danish monarch as head of state. Although the role is largely ceremonial, the constitution grants the president several significant reserve powers.

Powers

The president appoints the prime minister and cabinet following elections, chairs the State Council, signs bills into law, and represents Iceland abroad. Under Article 26, the president may refuse to sign a law, in which case it is put to a national referendum; this power was used by Ólafur Ragnar Grímsson in 2004, 2010 and 2011, notably over the Icesave bills. The president also holds powers of pardon and amnesty.

Election

Presidents are directly elected by popular vote for four-year terms, with no limit on re-election, using a simple plurality system. Any Icelandic citizen aged 35 or over who gathers the required endorsements may stand. If only one candidate stands, that person is declared elected unopposed, as happened several times during Vigdís Finnbogadóttir's tenure.

List of presidents

The presidents have been Sveinn Björnsson (1944–1952), Ásgeir Ásgeirsson (1952–1968), Kristján Eldjárn (1968–1980), Vigdís Finnbogadóttir (1980–1996)—the world's first democratically elected female head of state—Ólafur Ragnar Grímsson (1996–2016), Guðni Th. Jóhannesson (2016–2024) and Halla Tómasdóttir (2024–present).
