Shipbuilding


A ship under construction in a shipyard dry dock.

Shipbuilding is the construction of ships and other floating vessels. It normally takes place in a specialised facility known as a shipyard. Shipbuilders, also called shipwrights, follow a specialised occupation that traces its roots to before recorded history. Shipbuilding and ship repairs, both commercial and military, are referred to as naval engineering.

History

The earliest evidence of watercraft comes from prehistoric dugout canoes and rafts; the Egyptians, Phoenicians, Greeks, and Romans developed planked wooden sailing ships, and shipwrighting traditions matured in China with the junk, medieval Europe with the cog and carrack, and the great galleons of the age of sail. The Industrial Revolution transformed the craft: iron and then steel hulls, steam propulsion, and prefabricated construction replaced traditional timber framing. In the Second World War, mass-produced Liberty ships demonstrated the speed of welded, sectional construction, and ships have continued to grow in size ever since.

Modern process

Modern shipbuilding is a heavy industrial process. Designers prepare detailed plans and computer models; steel plates are cut by automated machines and assembled into blocks, which are joined on a slipway or in a building dock and lifted into place by large gantry cranes before the hull is launched or floated out. The ship is then fitted out with machinery, piping, electrical systems, and accommodation, and finally tested in sea trials before delivery. Ships are built to the rules of classification societies such as Lloyd's Register and under the safety regime of SOLAS.

Industry today

Commercial shipbuilding is concentrated in East Asia: China, South Korea, and Japan together account for the great majority of world output by tonnage, producing bulk carriers, container ships, tankers, and liquefied gas carriers. European yards specialise in cruise ships, ferries, and naval vessels, while the United States industry focuses chiefly on warships and vessels for the domestic Jones Act trade.
