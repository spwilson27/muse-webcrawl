Ivano-Frankivsk (Ukrainian: Івано-Франківськ) is a city in western Ukraine, the administrative centre of Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast, situated in the foothills region of the Carpathians between the Bystrytsia Solotvynska and Bystrytsia Nadvirnianska rivers.

History

The city was founded in 1662 by the Polish magnate Andrzej Potocki as the fortress town of Stanisławów, named for his father, Stanisław "Rewera" Potocki, and laid out in a Renaissance star-fort plan. It passed to the Habsburg Empire in the First Partition of Poland (1772), becoming Stanislau in Austrian Galicia. After World War I it lay in the Second Polish Republic. Annexed by the Soviet Union in 1939–1945, it suffered the destruction of its large Jewish community during the Holocaust. In 1962, for its 300th anniversary, the city was renamed in honour of the Ukrainian writer Ivan Franko.

Culture and economy

Ivano-Frankivsk is a centre of Ukrainian culture in the Precarpathian region, known for its preserved Austro-Hungarian-era old town, the Art Deco town hall tower, universities, IT sector and role as a gateway to Carpathian resorts such as Bukovel and Yaremche. It has consistently ranked among Ukraine's most liveable cities.
