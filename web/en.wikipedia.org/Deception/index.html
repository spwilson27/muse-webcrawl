Deception


Deception in art and literature

Deception is the act of misleading another person or concealing the truth, typically to gain an advantage or to avoid blame. It includes lying, omission of relevant facts, equivocation, exaggeration, and nonverbal misleading behavior. Deception occurs in everyday social interaction, in fraud and propaganda, in animal communication, and in warfare as military deception.

Psychology

Research in psychology examines why people deceive (for example, to protect themselves or others, or for material gain), how deception is detected, and developmental questions such as children's theory of mind. Studies suggest people detect lies at rates only modestly above chance, despite widespread confidence in so-called lie-detection cues. Polygraph testing remains controversial and is not generally accepted as scientifically reliable.

Types

Bella DePaulo's research distinguishes kinds of lies by motivation and seriousness. Common categories include white lies, exaggerations, lies of omission, and outright fabrication. In evolutionary biology, mimicry and camouflage are forms of deception between species, and some animals engage in tactical deception of their own kind.
