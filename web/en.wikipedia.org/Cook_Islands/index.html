Cook Islands

Capital: Avarua
Population: ~17,000
Status: Self-governing in free association with New Zealand
Currency: New Zealand dollar; Cook Islands dollar

The Cook Islands is a self-governing island country in the South Pacific Ocean in free association with New Zealand. It comprises 15 islands spread over nearly 2 million square kilometres of ocean, with a total land area of about 236 square kilometres. The capital, Avarua, is on the main island of Rarotonga.

History

The islands were settled by Polynesians and named after Captain James Cook, who visited in the 1770s. They became a British protectorate in 1888 and were administered by New Zealand from 1901. In 1965 the Cook Islands became self-governing in free association with New Zealand; Cook Islanders remain New Zealand citizens.

Economy and culture

Tourism is the main industry, supplemented by offshore banking, pearls, and marine and fruit exports. The population is predominantly Māori, closely related to the Māori of New Zealand, and Cook Islands Māori and English are official languages.
