Panama (Spanish: Panamá), officially the Republic of Panama, is a country in Central America, occupying the Isthmus of Panama that connects North and South America. It is bordered by Costa Rica to the west, Colombia to the southeast, the Caribbean Sea to the north and the Pacific Ocean to the south. Its capital and largest city is Panama City, whose metropolitan area is home to roughly half the country's population.

Geography

Panama's dominant feature is the narrow isthmus crossed by the Panama Canal, which links the Atlantic and Pacific oceans and handles a significant share of world maritime trade. The country is mountainous along its central spine, with the highest point the dormant Volcán Barú at 3,475 metres. The Darién region in the east, where the Pan-American Highway is interrupted (the Darién Gap), is dense rainforest. Panama has a tropical climate with a long rainy season.

History

The isthmus was inhabited by indigenous peoples including the Cueva, Coclé and Guna before Spanish colonisation in the 16th century; Vasco Núñez de Balboa crossed it in 1513 and reached the Pacific. Panama became a key transit route for Spanish silver. It declared independence from Spain in 1821 and joined Gran Colombia, separating from Colombia in 1903 with United States support, whereupon the US built the Panama Canal (opened 1914) and administered the surrounding Canal Zone. Military rule under Omar Torrijos and later Manuel Noriega ended with the US invasion of 1989. Under the Torrijos–Carter Treaties, the canal was transferred to Panama on 31 December 1999; an expanded set of locks opened in 2016.

Economy

Panama's economy is service-based and dollarised, centred on the canal, the Colón Free Trade Zone, banking, shipping registry (among the world's largest flags of convenience), logistics and tourism. It has been one of Latin America's fastest-growing economies in recent decades, though income inequality and rural poverty persist.
