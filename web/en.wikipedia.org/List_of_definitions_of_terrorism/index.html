List of definitions of terrorism

There is no single universal legal or academic definition of terrorism. Scholars such as Alex Schmid surveyed over 100 definitions; common elements include violence, political aims, intent to create fear, and targeting of civilians or non-combatants.

Examples

  • United Nations (1994 Declaration): criminal acts intended or calculated to provoke a state of terror in the general public, a group or particular persons for political purposes.
  • United States (18 U.S.C. § 2331): violent acts or acts dangerous to human life that violate criminal law and appear intended to intimidate or coerce a civilian population, influence government policy, or affect government conduct.
  • United Kingdom (Terrorism Act 2000): use or threat of serious violence, damage or disruption designed to influence the government or intimidate the public for a political, religious, racial or ideological cause.
  • European Union: intentional offences that, given their nature or context, may seriously damage a country or international organisation, committed to seriously intimidate a population, destabilise structures, or compel a government to act.
  • Academic (Schmid & Jongman): an anxiety-inspiring method of repeated violent action employing clandestine or semi-clandestine actors for idiosyncratic, criminal or political reasons.

Debates cover state terrorism, whether liberation movements are included, and the difficulty that has prevented a comprehensive UN terrorism convention.
