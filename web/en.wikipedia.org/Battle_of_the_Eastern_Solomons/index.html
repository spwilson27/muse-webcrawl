Battle of the Eastern Solomons

Date24–25 August 1942
LocationNorth of Santa Isabel Island, Solomon Islands, Pacific Ocean
ResultAllied strategic victory

The Battle of the Eastern Solomons, fought on 24–25 August 1942, was the third carrier battle of the Pacific War and one of the opening engagements of the Guadalcanal campaign. United States Navy carrier forces turned back a Japanese attempt to reinforce Guadalcanal and to destroy the American carriers, sinking the light carrier Ryūjō and damaging the fleet carrier USS Enterprise.

USS Enterprise, heavily damaged but operational, 24 August 1942.

Background

After the American landings on Guadalcanal on 7 August 1942, Japan mounted a major counter-operation: a convoy carrying some 1,500 troops was to reinforce the island, screened by Nagumo's carrier force of Shōkaku and Zuikaku, with the light carrier Ryūjō detached to strike Henderson Field and lure the Americans. Vice Admiral Fletcher commanded Task Force 61 with the carriers Saratoga, Enterprise and Wasp.

The battle

On 24 August American scouts found Ryūjō, and aircraft from Saratoga sank her. That afternoon Japanese carrier aircraft located the American carriers and attacked Enterprise, hitting her with three bombs and killing 74 men, though she remained able to operate aircraft. The Japanese failed to find Saratoga. The next day, American aircraft from Henderson Field and B-17s attacked the reinforcement convoy, sinking the transport Kinryū Maru and the destroyer Mutsuki; the convoy turned back.

Aftermath

Japan lost over 75 aircraft and much of the experienced aircrew aboard them — losses its training system could not easily replace — while the United States lost about 25 aircraft. Though Enterprise was out of action for repairs, the Japanese reinforcement effort had failed, and subsequent troops had to be delivered piecemeal by destroyer runs, the "Tokyo Express".
