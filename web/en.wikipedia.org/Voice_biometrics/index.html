Voice biometrics

Voice biometrics is the science and technology of measuring and analysing a person's unique vocal characteristics in order to verify their identity or characterise attributes such as age, gender or emotional state. Because the human voice is shaped by the physical anatomy of the vocal tract, lungs and nasal passages, as well as by learned speaking habits, no two speakers produce exactly the same acoustic signal.

How it works

A voice biometric system first records a speech sample and extracts a compact numerical representation, often called a voiceprint. Features commonly used include mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), formant frequencies, pitch contours and speaking rate. During enrolment a reference template is stored; during verification a new sample is compared with the template and a similarity score is produced. If the score passes a threshold, the speaker's claimed identity is accepted.

Systems may be text-dependent, requiring the speaker to repeat a fixed phrase, or text-independent, analysing free speech. Modern systems use deep neural networks to learn speaker embeddings that are robust to noise and channel variation.

Applications

Voice biometrics is widely used in call centres for customer authentication, in banking for telephone transactions, in forensic speaker comparison, and in smart devices for personalised access. It offers hands-free convenience but raises concerns about spoofing by recordings or synthetic speech, and about the privacy of stored voiceprints, which are biometric data under laws such as the EU's GDPR.
