Land law

Land law is the form of law that deals with the rights to use, alienate, or exclude others from land. In many jurisdictions, these kinds of property are referred to as real estate or real property (as distinct from personal property). Land use agreements, including renting, are an important intersection of property and contract law. Encumbrances on one person's right in the land of another, such as easements, are a feature of land law in many legal systems.

History

In the common law tradition, land law descends from the feudal system of medieval England, under which all land was ultimately held of the Crown and subjects held estates — such as the fee simple, fee tail, and life estate — rather than absolute ownership. The doctrine of estates and tenure was substantially reformed in most common law jurisdictions during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, but its terminology still shapes the subject.

Estates and interests

Land law distinguishes ownership interests (estates) from lesser interests such as leases, easements (rights over another's land), profits à prendre, restrictive covenants, mortgages and other security interests, and licences. Rules govern how such interests are created, transferred, and prioritised when they conflict, for example when land is sold to a good-faith purchaser.

Registration

Modern systems increasingly rely on public registers of title. Under the Torrens system, originating in South Australia and adopted across much of the Commonwealth and elsewhere, the register itself is conclusive of title. Other jurisdictions, such as most of the United States, use deed-recording systems, in which priority depends on filing and notice.

Land reform

Land law is also the vehicle for land reform: programmes of redistribution, tenancy protection, and restitution that have been major political questions in many countries, particularly where colonial or feudal patterns concentrated ownership. Zoning and planning law, though often treated as separate fields, operate as public-law overlays on private land rights.
