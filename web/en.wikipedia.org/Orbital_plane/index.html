Orbital plane

Orbital plane
Contains: The orbit of a body and the centre of mass of the system
Orientation described by: Inclination and longitude of the ascending node

The orbital plane of a revolving body is the geometric plane in which its orbit lies. A sufficient definition of the orbital plane is given by three non-collinear points: the central body, the orbiting body at one position, and the orbiting body at a later position.

Reference planes

By default, orbiting objects in the Solar System have their orbital planes described relative to the ecliptic plane — the plane of Earth's orbit. For satellites of a planet, the planet's equatorial plane or the invariable plane may be used instead. The orientation of the orbital plane is specified by two of the orbital elements: its inclination to the reference plane and the longitude of the ascending node.

Changes of plane

An orbital plane is fixed in inertial space unless perturbed. For spacecraft, changing the orbital plane requires a large expenditure of propellant — plane-change manoeuvres are among the most expensive in astrodynamics, with the required change in velocity growing with orbital speed and the angle of the change.
