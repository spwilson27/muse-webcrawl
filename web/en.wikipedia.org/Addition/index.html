Addition (usually signified by the plus symbol +) is one of the four basic operations of arithmetic, the others being subtraction, multiplication and division. The addition of two whole numbers results in the total amount or sum of those values combined: for example, 3 + 2 = 5, as in three apples and two apples making five apples. The numbers being added are called addends, and the result is the sum.

Properties

Addition is commutative (a + b = b + a) and associative ((a + b) + c = a + (b + c)), and zero is its identity element: adding zero to any number leaves it unchanged. As one of the simplest numerical tasks, addition of small numbers can be performed even by young children and, with training, by some animals.

Generalisation

The operation extends far beyond whole numbers: fractions, real and complex numbers, vectors, matrices, polynomials and many abstract algebraic structures have their own addition operations. In the Peano axioms, addition of natural numbers is defined recursively using the successor function. Repeated addition underlies multiplication, and column addition with "carrying" is the standard paper algorithm taught in elementary school.
