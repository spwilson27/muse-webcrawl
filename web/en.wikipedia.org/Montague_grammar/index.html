Montague grammar

Montague grammar is an approach to natural language semantics developed by the American logician Richard Montague in the late 1960s and early 1970s. It treats fragments of natural language — notably English — with the same formal tools used for mathematical logic, based on model theory, intensional logic, and the lambda calculus.

Core ideas

In his paper "The Proper Treatment of Quantification in Ordinary English" (1973), Montague argued there is no important theoretical difference between natural and formal languages. Syntax and semantics are defined in parallel by algebraic rules, so that each syntactic construction maps compositionally to a semantic interpretation.

Influence

Montague grammar founded modern formal semantics in linguistics, shaping work on quantification, tense, and intensionality. Some of his results were famously anticipated in a more restricted form by the Polish logician's school and paralleled Ajdukiewicz's categorial grammar.
