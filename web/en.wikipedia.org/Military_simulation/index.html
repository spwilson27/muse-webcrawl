Military simulation

Military simulations, also known informally as war games, are simulations of military operations and tactics used for training, analysis, and planning, in which theories of warfare can be tested and refined without the need for actual hostilities.

Types

Simulations are often classified as live (real troops and equipment in the field), virtual (simulators such as flight trainers), or constructive (computer models of units and engagements), and as manual (map and tabletop exercises), semi-automated, or fully computer-based.

History

The Prussian Kriegsspiel of the early 19th century formalized staff wargaming; naval war games were used by major powers before and during both World Wars. During the Cold War, large computer models and exercises such as Millennium Challenge 2002 became central to doctrine development.

Applications

Militaries use simulation for officer education, rehearsal of plans, weapons procurement analysis, and experimentation with future concepts, at a fraction of the cost and risk of live exercises.
