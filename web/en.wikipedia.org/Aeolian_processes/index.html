Aeolian processes

Aeolian processes, also spelled eolian, are the geological processes driven by wind: its ability to erode, transport and deposit materials. Named after Aeolus, the Greek god of the winds, they are most effective in arid environments with sparse vegetation and abundant loose sediment.

Erosion and transport

Wind erodes by deflation (removing loose particles, leaving desert pavement) and abrasion (sandblasting rock surfaces into ventifacts and yardangs). Particles move by suspension (dust), saltation (bouncing grains) and surface creep.

Deposition

Deposits form sand dunes of many types — barchan, transverse, star and longitudinal — and thick blankets of wind-blown silt called loess, which form fertile soils in parts of China, Europe and North America. Aeolian processes also shape landscapes on Mars and Titan.
