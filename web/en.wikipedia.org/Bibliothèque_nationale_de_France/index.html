Bibliothèque nationale de France

The Bibliothèque nationale de France (BnF) is the national library of France, located in Paris. It is the repository of all that is published in France and holds extensive historical collections, making it one of the largest libraries in the world. It also operates Gallica, a major open-access digital library.

History

The library grew out of the royal collections assembled by French kings from the Middle Ages onward. In 1537, King Francis I instituted the dépôt légal, requiring a copy of every book printed in France to be deposited in the royal library — one of the oldest legal deposit systems in the world. The collection expanded greatly under librarians such as Jacques Auguste de Thou and under Colbert, and it was opened to the public in the 17th century.

Sites

The historic Richelieu site, in the 2nd arrondissement, houses the departments of manuscripts, maps, prints, coins and medals, and performing arts; its famous oval reading room (salle Ovale) was designed by Henri Labrouste. In 1996 the library opened the François-Mitterrand site in the 13th arrondissement, designed by architect Dominique Perrault, whose four 79-metre glass towers are shaped like open books standing around a sunken garden. The site was named in honour of President François Mitterrand, who championed the project.

Collections

The BnF's holdings include millions of books and periodicals, medieval manuscripts, maps, prints, photographs, sound recordings and audiovisual material. Treasures include Gutenberg Bibles, medieval illuminated manuscripts, and the collections of the French national archives of print. Its digital library, Gallica, launched in 1997, provides free access to millions of digitized documents.
