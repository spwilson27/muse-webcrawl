Gaius Plinius Secundus (AD 23/24 – AD 79), called Pliny the Elder, was a Roman author, naturalist, and natural philosopher, and a naval and army commander of the early Roman Empire. He is best known for the Naturalis Historia (Natural History), an encyclopaedia in 37 books covering astronomy, geography, zoology, botany, medicine, and mineralogy, which became the model for later encyclopaedias and remains a crucial source for Roman knowledge and everyday life.

Life

Pliny was born at Novum Comum (modern Como) in northern Italy. He served as a cavalry commander in Germany, held procuratorships in Gaul, Africa, and Spain, and became a close adviser to the emperor Vespasian, while writing prodigiously—according to his nephew Pliny the Younger, he rose before dawn to work, had books read to him at meals and in the bath, and regarded any time not spent in study as lost.

Natural History

The Natural History, published shortly before his death, claims to compile some 20,000 facts drawn from about 2,000 volumes by 100 authors. Its compilation is uncritical—reporting marvels alongside sound information—but it preserves vast amounts of otherwise lost material and was continuously consulted through the Middle Ages and the early modern period.

Death at Vesuvius

Pliny died at Stabiae during the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in AD 79. As commander of the fleet at Misenum, he sailed across the bay both to observe the eruption and to rescue people from the shore; overcome by fumes or by the collapse of his health (he was corpulent and asthmatic), he died on the beach. His nephew's two letters to Tacitus describing the eruption and his uncle's death are among the most famous documents of classical antiquity and the source of the term "Plinian eruption."

Other works

Pliny's other writings—on cavalry tactics, rhetoric, grammar, and a history of Rome's German wars—are lost, though they were used by later historians including Tacitus and Suetonius.
