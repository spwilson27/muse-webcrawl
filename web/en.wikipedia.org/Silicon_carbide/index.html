Silicon carbide

Silicon carbide (SiC), also known as carborundum, is a hard chemical compound containing silicon and carbon. It occurs in nature as the extremely rare mineral moissanite and has been mass-produced as a powder since 1893 for use as an abrasive. Today it is a major semiconductor material for high-voltage, high-temperature electronics.

Production

Most silicon carbide is made by the Acheson process, heating silica sand and carbon in an electric resistance furnace at temperatures above 1,600 °C. High-purity single crystals for electronics are grown by physical vapour transport or from solution.

Applications

SiC is used in grinding wheels and cutting tools, in bulletproof armour and kiln furniture, and in the blue and green LEDs of early devices. Its wide bandgap makes it increasingly important in power electronics for electric vehicles, solar inverters, and fast chargers, where it outperforms silicon at high voltages and temperatures.
