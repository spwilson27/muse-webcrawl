Kakutani fixed-point theorem

The Kakutani fixed-point theorem is a result in mathematical analysis proved by Shizuo Kakutani in 1941. It generalizes the Brouwer fixed-point theorem from single-valued functions to set-valued functions (correspondences): if S is a nonempty, compact, convex subset of Euclidean space and φ maps each point of S to a nonempty, convex subset of S in an upper hemicontinuous way, then φ has a fixed point — some point x in S such that x belongs to φ(x).

Applications

The theorem is a foundational tool in mathematical economics and game theory. John von Neumann used a result of this type in his work on expanding economies, and John Nash's celebrated 1950 proof of the existence of equilibrium in non-cooperative games applied the Kakutani theorem to players' best-response correspondences. It is likewise used to prove the existence of competitive equilibrium in general equilibrium theory, following Arrow and Debreu.

Related results

The theorem holds in locally convex topological vector spaces in versions developed by Ky Fan and Irving Glicksberg. When the correspondence is single-valued and continuous, the statement reduces to Brouwer's fixed-point theorem.
