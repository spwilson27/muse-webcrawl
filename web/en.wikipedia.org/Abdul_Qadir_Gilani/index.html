Abdul Qadir Gilani

Died1166, Baghdad

Abdul Qadir Gilani (Arabic: عبد القادر الجيلاني; 1077/78–1166), known honorifically as al-Ghawth al-A'zam ("the Supreme Helper"), was a Hanbali scholar, preacher and Sufi mystic based in Baghdad. He is the eponymous founder of the Qadiriyya, one of the oldest and most widespread Sufi orders (tariqas) in the Muslim world.

Life

Born in Gilan, on the Caspian coast of Persia, Gilani travelled to Baghdad in 1095 to study. He studied Hanbali jurisprudence and hadith, and was initiated into Sufism by Abu al-Khayr Hammad al-Dabbas. After a period of ascetic withdrawal, he began preaching in 1127 and was given charge of a school and ribat (Sufi lodge) in Baghdad, where his sermons attracted large audiences.

Works and legacy

His works include Al-Ghunya li-Talibi Tariq al-Haqq, a compendium of religious practice, and collections of sermons such as Futuh al-Ghayb ("Revelations of the Unseen") and Al-Fath al-Rabbani. His tomb and mosque in Baghdad remain a major pilgrimage site, and his death is commemorated annually by followers, particularly on 11 Rabi' al-thani (Gyarvi Sharif). The Qadiriyya spread through his descendants and disciples across the Middle East, Africa and Asia.
