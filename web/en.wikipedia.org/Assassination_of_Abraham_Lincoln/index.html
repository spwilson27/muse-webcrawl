Assassination of Abraham Lincoln

DateApril 14, 1865 (shot); died April 15, 1865
LocationFord's Theatre, Washington, D.C.
TargetAbraham Lincoln, 16th president of the United States
PerpetratorJohn Wilkes Booth

The assassination of Abraham Lincoln took place on Good Friday, April 14, 1865, just days after the effective end of the American Civil War. Lincoln, the 16th president of the United States, was shot while attending a performance of Our American Cousin at Ford's Theatre in Washington, D.C., and died the following morning. He was the first U.S. president to be assassinated.

The attack

The assassin, actor John Wilkes Booth, was a Confederate sympathiser who had originally plotted to kidnap Lincoln. Entering the presidential box during the play's best-known comic line, Booth shot Lincoln once in the back of the head with a derringer pistol, then leapt to the stage — breaking his leg — and escaped from the city on horseback. Simultaneously, Booth's co-conspirators attacked Secretary of State William H. Seward, who survived, while another accomplice assigned to Vice President Andrew Johnson lost his nerve.

Aftermath

Lincoln was carried across the street to the Petersen House, where he died on the morning of April 15. Booth was tracked to a farm in Virginia and killed twelve days later. Eight conspirators were tried by a military commission; four, including Mary Surratt, were hanged. The nation mourned on an unprecedented scale as Lincoln's funeral train travelled to Springfield, Illinois, and Andrew Johnson succeeded to the presidency.
