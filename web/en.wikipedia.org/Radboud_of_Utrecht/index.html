Radboud of Utrecht

Radboud of Utrecht (c. 850 – 29 November 917) was a Frankish bishop of Utrecht from about 900 until his death, and is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church. He is the patron saint associated with learning in the Netherlands, and Radboud University Nijmegen is named after him.

Life

Radboud descended from a noble Frankish family; his maternal line was said to trace back to the Frisian king Radbod. He was educated at the court school of Charles the Bald and later at Cologne under Archbishop Gunther. After a period as a Benedictine monk, he was elected bishop of Utrecht around 899–900, though Viking raids forced him to reside chiefly in Deventer rather than Utrecht itself.

Writings and legacy

Radboud composed sermons, saints' lives, hymns and a chronicle, and was remembered as a patron of scholarship and church music. He died at Ootmarsum in 917 and was buried in the Lebuïnus Church in Deventer. A later medieval Vita Radbodi recounts miracles attributed to him.
