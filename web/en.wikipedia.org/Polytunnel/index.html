Polytunnel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A polytunnel protecting a crop of vegetables

A polytunnel (also known as a polyhouse, hoop greenhouse or high tunnel) is a tunnel-like structure, usually semi-circular or elongated in cross-section, made of steel hoops covered with polyethylene film. Unlike a glasshouse, a polytunnel is typically unheated and passively ventilated; it protects crops from wind and rain, raises internal temperature, and extends the growing season.

Construction and use

Galvanised steel tubes are bent into hoops, anchored to ground posts, and a sheet of UV-stabilised polyethylene — usually replaced every three to five years — is stretched over the frame and buried or tensioned at the edges. Sizes range from small garden tunnels a few metres long to commercial multi-span structures covering hectares. Doors and roll-up sides provide ventilation, and irrigation is commonly by drip lines or sprinklers.

Agricultural role

Polytunnels are widely used for soft fruit such as strawberries and raspberries, salad crops, tomatoes, peppers and nursery plants. By sheltering fruit from rain they reduce fungal disease and blemishing, improve yields and allow earlier and later harvests. Large-scale use has transformed production in regions such as Almería in Spain, where vast areas of plastic greenhouses and tunnels supply vegetables to northern Europe.

Issues

Critics raise concerns about landscape impact, disposal of used plastic sheeting, and the creation of warm, humid conditions favouring some pests and diseases. High temperatures inside on sunny days require careful ventilation management, and crops grown under plastic may still depend heavily on irrigation.
