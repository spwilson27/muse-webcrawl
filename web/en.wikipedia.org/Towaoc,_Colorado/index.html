Towaoc is a census-designated place (CDP) in Montezuma County, Colorado, United States. It is the capital and administrative centre of the Ute Mountain Ute Tribe, whose reservation occupies the southwestern corner of Colorado, adjoining New Mexico and Utah. The name comes from a Ute word meaning "all right" or "okay". The community sits on the high desert below Sleeping Ute Mountain, at an elevation of roughly 5,900 feet (1,800 m).

Geography

Towaoc lies along U.S. Route 160/491, about 15 miles south of Cortez and west of Mesa Verde National Park. The landscape is arid high desert with piñon-juniper woodland, dominated by the profile of Sleeping Ute Mountain, sacred to the Ute people. The Towaoc Canal, part of the Dolores Project, delivers irrigation water from McPhee Reservoir to tribal farmland.

Community and economy

The town hosts the tribal headquarters, Ute Mountain Casino Hotel, a travel plaza, and tribal enterprises including farming operations and the Weeminuche Construction Authority. Ute Mountain Tribal Park, administered from the community, offers guided visits to Ancestral Puebloan cliff dwellings and rock art comparable to those of nearby Mesa Verde, but preserved in a more natural, less developed state.

Demographics

The population of the CDP was about 1,087 at the 2010 census, with the great majority of residents being enrolled members or descendants of the Ute Mountain Ute Tribe. The community serves as the service centre for the roughly 200,000-acre reservation.
