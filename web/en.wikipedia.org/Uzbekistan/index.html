Uzbekistan, officially the Republic of Uzbekistan, is a doubly landlocked country in Central Asia, bordered by Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Afghanistan and Turkmenistan. It is the most populous country in the region, with about 36 million people, and its capital and largest city is Tashkent.

History

The land between the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers — ancient Transoxiana — hosted the civilizations of Bactria and Sogdia, was conquered by Alexander the Great, and became a hub of the Silk Road: Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva grew rich on trade between China and the Mediterranean. Arab conquest brought Islam in the 8th century; the region produced the scholars al-Khwarizmi, Ibn Sina (Avicenna), al-Biruni and Ulugh Beg. In the 14th century Timur (Tamerlane) made Samarkand the capital of a vast empire whose monuments — the Registan, the Bibi-Khanym Mosque — still dominate the city. Later Uzbek khanates ruled until the Russian Empire annexed the region in the 19th century.

The Uzbek Soviet Socialist Republic was created in 1924. Independence followed the Soviet collapse in 1991 under President Islam Karimov, whose authoritarian rule lasted until his death in 2016. His successor, Shavkat Mirziyoyev, liberalized the economy, floated the currency, improved relations with neighbours and released political prisoners, while political competition remained limited.

Geography

Much of Uzbekistan is desert — the Kyzylkum covers the centre and west — with fertile irrigated valleys in the east, especially the Fergana Valley. The catastrophic shrinking of the Aral Sea, caused by Soviet-era diversion of rivers for cotton irrigation, is one of the world's worst environmental disasters; the autonomous republic of Karakalpakstan occupies the west. The climate is continental, hot and dry.

Economy

Uzbekistan is a major producer of cotton and one of the world's largest gold producers (the Muruntau mine is among the largest open-pit gold mines on Earth), with significant natural gas, uranium and copper. Remittances, manufacturing, agriculture and growing tourism to the Silk Road cities also contribute. The Uzbek language, a Turkic language written in the Latin alphabet, is official; Tajik is widely spoken in Samarkand and Bukhara, and Russian remains in common use.

Culture

Culture blends Persian, Turkic, Arab, Chinese and Russian influences: plov (pilaf) is the emblematic dish, classical music follows the shashmaqam tradition, and historic centres of Samarkand, Bukhara, Khiva (Itchan Kala) and Shakhrisabz are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Islam, predominantly Sunni Hanafi, is the majority faith.
