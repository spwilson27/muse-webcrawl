Amália da Piedade Rodrigues (23 July 1920 – 6 October 1999) was a Portuguese fado singer and actress. Known as the "Rainha do Fado" ("Queen of Fado"), she was instrumental in popularizing the genre worldwide and remains one of Portugal's most celebrated cultural figures.

Career

Born into a poor family in Lisbon, Rodrigues began singing professionally in 1939 and soon dominated the city's fado houses. She recorded extensively, toured internationally, and performed in Paris, London, New York, and Rio de Janeiro. Her repertoire included traditional fado and settings of poems by Luís de Camões and contemporary poets, notably in the album Com que Voz (1970).

Film and theatre

She starred in Portuguese films such as Fado, História de uma Cantadeira (1947) and acted on stage in Lisbon and Paris.

Legacy

On her death in 1999, Portugal declared three days of national mourning. Her remains were transferred to the National Pantheon in Lisbon in 2001, an honor rarely accorded to artists.
