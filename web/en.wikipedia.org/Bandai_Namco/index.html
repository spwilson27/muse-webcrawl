Bandai Namco Holdings Inc. is a Japanese mass media and entertainment conglomerate formed in 2005 by the merger of Bandai, Japan's leading toy manufacturer, and Namco, a major video game and arcade company founded by Masaya Nakamura in 1955. The group is among the world's largest toy companies and a major force in video games, anime, and character merchandising, built around franchises such as Gundam, Dragon Ball, One Piece, Pac-Man, Tekken, and Tamagotchi.

History

Bandai was founded in 1950 by Naoharu Yamashina and built its business on model kits and licensed-character toys, securing the merchandising rights to properties such as Ultraman, Kamen Rider, Super Sentai (adapted abroad as Power Rangers), and, from 1980, the enormously successful Gunpla plastic models of Mobile Suit Gundam. Namco rose from amusement rides to arcade prominence with Pac-Man (1980), the best-selling arcade game of all time, and became a major home-console publisher with series like Ridge Racer, Tekken, Ace Combat, and Tales. Facing a consolidating entertainment industry, the two companies integrated management in September 2005.

Businesses

Group operations include toys and hobby products (Bandai), video games through Bandai Namco Entertainment — publisher of titles including Elden Ring and the Dark Souls series (developed by FromSoftware), Tekken, and licensed anime games — anime production through Bandai Namco Filmworks (the former Sunrise studio, home of Gundam and Code Geass), music and live events, and amusement facilities. The company is notable for its cross-media "IP axis" strategy, developing each franchise simultaneously across toys, games, animation, and events.
