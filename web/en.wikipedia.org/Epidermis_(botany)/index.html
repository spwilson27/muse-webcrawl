Epidermis (botany)

Epidermis

Tissue system
Dermal
Cell types
Epidermal cells, guard cells, trichomes
Key structures
Cuticle, stomata

The epidermis in plants is a single layer of cells (in most species) that covers the leaves, flowers, roots, and stems. It forms the boundary between the plant and the external environment, protecting against water loss, mechanical injury, and invasion by pathogens, while regulating gas exchange.

Structure

Epidermal cells are typically flattened and closely packed, with thickened outer walls. Their outer surface is usually coated with the cuticle, a waxy layer composed of cutin that reduces evaporation. Embedded in the epidermis of aerial parts are stomata — pores flanked by pairs of guard cells that open and close to control the exchange of carbon dioxide and water vapour. Hair-like outgrowths called trichomes may deter herbivores or reflect excess light.

Specialised cells

In roots, the epidermis (rhizodermis) bears root hairs that greatly increase absorptive surface area. Bulliform cells in grass leaves cause the leaf to roll during drought. In older stems and roots the epidermis is replaced by the periderm, whose outer cork cells form bark.
