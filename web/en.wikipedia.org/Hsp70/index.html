Hsp70

The 70 kilodalton heat shock proteins (Hsp70s or DnaK) are a family of conserved, ubiquitously expressed heat shock proteins. They are important parts of the cell's machinery for protein folding and help to protect cells from stress. Proteins with similar structure exist in virtually all living organisms, from bacteria to humans.

Hsp70 proteins are ATP-dependent molecular chaperones.
FamilyHeat shock proteins
Molecular weight~70 kDa
FunctionMolecular chaperone; protein folding

Discovery and structure

The heat-shock response was first observed in Drosophila in the 1960s, and Hsp70 was identified as one of the proteins strongly induced by heat and other stresses. Hsp70 proteins consist of two major domains: an N-terminal nucleotide-binding domain (NBD) with ATPase activity, and a C-terminal substrate-binding domain (SBD) that binds exposed hydrophobic stretches of unfolded or partially folded polypeptides, capped by a helical "lid".

Mechanism and function

Hsp70 cycles between an ATP-bound state with low substrate affinity and an ADP-bound state with high substrate affinity. Co-chaperones regulate the cycle: J-domain proteins (Hsp40 family) stimulate ATP hydrolysis and deliver substrates, while nucleotide exchange factors promote release. Through repeated binding and release, Hsp70 assists de novo protein folding, refolding of misfolded proteins, translocation of proteins across membranes, disassembly of protein complexes, and targeting of damaged proteins for degradation. In humans, key family members include the stress-inducible Hsp72 (HSPA1A), the constitutive Hsc70 (HSPA8), the endoplasmic-reticulum form BiP (HSPA5), and the mitochondrial mortalin (HSPA9).

Clinical relevance

Because many cancer cells depend heavily on chaperones to manage their abnormal protein load, Hsp70 is a target of interest in oncology. Elevated extracellular Hsp70 can also modulate immune responses, and the family is implicated in neurodegenerative diseases characterised by protein misfolding.
