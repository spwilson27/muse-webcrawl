The Battle of Largs (2 October 1263) was a minor and inconclusive engagement between Norwegian and Scottish forces on the shore of the Firth of Clyde near Largs, during the Scottish–Norwegian War. Though little more than a skirmish, it marked the effective end of King Haakon IV of Norway's expedition to reassert Norwegian authority over the Hebrides and western Scotland.

Background

The western isles of Scotland owed nominal allegiance to Norway, but Alexander III of Scotland had been pressing to buy or take them. In the summer of 1263 Haakon IV sailed with a large fleet through the Hebrides and into the Firth of Clyde. In the night of 30 September–1 October a violent storm drove several Norwegian ships ashore near Largs.

The engagement

Scottish forces harassed the Norwegians as they tried to salvage their beached vessels and protect the crews coming ashore. Fighting over several days consisted mainly of missile exchanges and skirmishes around the stranded ships; the Scots, cautious in the face of the main Norwegian fleet, did not commit to a full-scale assault, and most of the Norwegian crews were successfully re-embarked.

Aftermath

With autumn weather worsening, Haakon withdrew to Orkney, where he died at Kirkwall in December 1263. Three years later his son Magnus VI ceded the Hebrides and the Isle of Man to Scotland under the Treaty of Perth (1266) in return for a payment of 4,000 marks and an annuity. In Scottish tradition Largs was later celebrated as a great national victory, a view embellished by nineteenth-century romanticism far beyond the scale of the actual fighting.
