Cataphora

In linguistics, cataphora is the use of an expression whose reference depends on a later expression in the same sentence or text — the reverse of the more common anaphora, in which the referent comes first. The later expression supplying the reference is called the antecedent or postcedent. An example is: "When he arrived home, John went to bed," where the pronoun he refers forward to John.

Types and effects

Cataphora occurs within sentences (as above) and across discourse, for example when a text begins with a pronoun whose identity is revealed only later, a device used for suspense or stylistic effect: "She was unlike anyone he had met. Maria…" Cataphoric reference is constrained by syntax: in many configurations a pronoun cannot precede and command its antecedent, restrictions studied within binding theory in generative grammar.
