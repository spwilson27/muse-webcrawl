Sousveillance

Coined bySteve Mann
Meaning"Watching from below" — recording by participants/ordinary people
Contrasts withSurveillance (watching from above)

Sousveillance (from French sous, "below", as opposed to sur, "above") is the recording of an activity by a participant in the activity, using portable personal technologies such as wearable cameras, body cameras, and smartphones. The term was coined by Canadian engineer and inventor Steve Mann, a pioneer of wearable computing.

Concept

Surveillance describes watching by authorities over subjects; sousveillance inverts the hierarchy, as citizens record the powerful — police encounters filmed by bystanders being the best-known example. Mann distinguishes inverse surveillance (watching the watchers) from sousveillance proper, in which the recording is simply the participant's own account of events they are part of. Widespread smartphone video has made sousveillance a major force in public accountability since the 2010s.

Applications and debates

Sousveillance has been used in human-rights documentation, journalism, accessibility research, and art. Legal and ethical debates concern consent, privacy of bystanders, and laws restricting recording in some jurisdictions. Related concepts include participatory sensing and the "veillance" framework developed by Mann and others.
