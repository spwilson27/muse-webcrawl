Maud Menten

Maud Leonora Menten
Born: 20 March 1879, Port Lambton, Ontario, Canada
Died: 26 July 1960 (aged 81), Leamington, Ontario
Alma mater: University of Toronto; University of Chicago (PhD)
Known for: Michaelis–Menten kinetics

Maud Leonora Menten (20 March 1879 – 26 July 1960) was a Canadian physician and chemist. With Leonor Michaelis she formulated the Michaelis–Menten equation, the foundational mathematical description of enzyme reaction rates, in a landmark 1913 paper.

Life and career

Menten earned her BA and MD at the University of Toronto, becoming one of Canada's first women physicians. Because research posts were largely closed to women in Canada, she worked abroad: at the Rockefeller Institute in New York, in Berlin with Michaelis on enzyme kinetics, and at the University of Chicago, where she earned her PhD in 1916. She spent most of her career at the University of Pittsburgh School of Medicine and Children's Hospital of Pittsburgh, finally receiving a full professorship in 1949, at age 70.

Scientific work

Beyond enzyme kinetics, Menten worked on haemoglobin, blood sugar regulation, kidney function, bacterial toxins, and histochemistry — she developed an azo-dye method for staining alkaline phosphatase still in use. She published over 70 papers.

Personal life

Fluent in several languages, Menten was also an accomplished clarinettist, painter, and mountaineer. She was inducted posthumously into the Canadian Medical Hall of Fame in 1998.
