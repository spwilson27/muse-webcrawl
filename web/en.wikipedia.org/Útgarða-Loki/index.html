Útgarða-Loki

Útgarða-Loki (Old Norse: "Loki of the Outyards" or "Loki of Útgarðr") is a jötunn (giant) in Norse mythology. He is the ruler of the castle Útgarðr in Jötunheimr, and is best known from the tale in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda in which he subjects Thor and his companions to a series of deceptive contests.

The contests at Útgarðr

When Thor arrives at Útgarðr with Loki, Þjálfi, and Röskva, Útgarða-Loki demands that each guest demonstrate some exceptional skill. Loki loses an eating contest against Logi, who is revealed to be wildfire itself. Þjálfi loses footraces against Hugi, who is thought. Thor fails to drain a drinking horn connected to the sea, fails to lift a cat who is actually Jörmungandr, the World Serpent, and is wrestled to one knee by Elli, old age itself.

The revelation

As the party departs, Útgarða-Loki reveals that all of the contests were decided by illusions, and that Thor's feats were in fact terrifying demonstrations of strength: his drinking lowered the ocean tides, he very nearly lifted the World Serpent, and he resisted old age longer than anyone could have expected. He admits that he defended his hall with trickery and vows never to let Thor return. When Thor raises his hammer Mjölnir in rage, the castle and its king vanish.

Other attestations

A figure named Utgarthilocus appears in Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum as a chained giant in a subterranean hall visited by Thorkillus. Scholars debate the relationship between Útgarða-Loki, the god Loki, and the figure Skrýmir, who escorts Thor on the journey to Útgarðr and whose knapsack Thor cannot untie.
