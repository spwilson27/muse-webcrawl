Los Padres National Forest

Los Padres National Forest is a United States National Forest in southern and central California, administered by the U.S. Forest Service. Covering roughly 1.75 million acres, it stretches across the coastal mountain ranges from Ventura County to Monterey County, including much of the Big Sur coast, and extends inland to the edge of the San Joaquin Valley.

Geography and ecology

The forest encompasses the Santa Lucia, San Rafael, and Sierra Madre ranges, with elevations from sea level to nearly 9,000 feet at Junipero Serra Peak. Vegetation ranges from coastal redwood groves and oak woodlands to dense chaparral and high-elevation conifer forest. It provides habitat for the endangered California condor, and includes the Sespe Condor Sanctuary; reintroduced condors soar over the forest's rugged backcountry. Designated wilderness areas within the forest include the Ventana, Silver Peak, San Rafael, Dick Smith, and Sespe wildernesses.

Recreation and management

Popular activities include hiking, camping, hot springs such as those along the Sespe Creek, and scenic drives along Highway 1. The forest is also noted for frequent wildfires in its chaparral ecosystems, and for the prehistoric rock art of the Chumash people found within its boundaries.
