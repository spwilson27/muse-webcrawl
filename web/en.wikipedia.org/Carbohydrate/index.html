Carbohydrate

A carbohydrate is a biomolecule consisting of carbon (C), hydrogen (H) and oxygen (O) atoms, usually with a hydrogen–oxygen atom ratio of 2:1 (as in water) and thus with the empirical formula Cm(H2O)n. Carbohydrates are one of the four main classes of biomolecules, alongside proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids.

Classification

Carbohydrates are divided into four chemical groups: monosaccharides, disaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides. Monosaccharides such as glucose, fructose, and galactose are the simplest sugars. Disaccharides such as sucrose, lactose, and maltose consist of two monosaccharide units joined together. Polysaccharides such as starch, glycogen, and cellulose are long chains of monosaccharide units and serve as energy stores or structural materials.

Function

In living organisms, carbohydrates serve as a major source of energy: glucose is broken down in cellular respiration to produce ATP. Plants produce carbohydrates through photosynthesis, and cellulose — the most abundant organic polymer on Earth — forms the structural framework of plant cell walls.

Nutrition

In human nutrition, carbohydrates are found in grains, fruits, vegetables, and dairy products. Dietary guidelines generally recommend that carbohydrates provide roughly 45–65% of total daily calories, with an emphasis on whole grains, fibre, and limited added sugars.
