Agony in the Garden

The Agony in the Garden of Gethsemane is an episode in the life of Jesus, occurring after the Last Supper and before his betrayal by Judas and arrest. According to the Synoptic Gospels, Jesus went with his disciples to the garden of Gethsemane on the Mount of Olives and prayed in anguish, knowing what was to come, while the disciples fell asleep nearby.

Biblical account

The episode is described in Matthew 26:36–46, Mark 14:32–42 and Luke 22:39–46. Jesus, distressed and sorrowful "even to death", withdrew a short distance and prayed: "My Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as you will." Three times he returned to find Peter, James and John asleep. Luke's account adds that an angel appeared to strengthen him and that, in his agony, his sweat became like drops of blood falling to the ground (hematidrosis).

The event is followed by the arrival of Judas with a crowd sent by the chief priests, and Jesus's arrest.

In devotion and art

The Agony in the Garden forms the first of the Sorrowful Mysteries of the Rosary and is commemorated during Holy Week. It has been depicted by countless artists, including Andrea Mantegna, Giovanni Bellini, Paolo Veronese and Paul Gauguin. The Church of All Nations, also called the Basilica of the Agony, stands on the traditional site at the foot of the Mount of Olives in Jerusalem, enclosing a section of bedrock on which Jesus is believed to have prayed.
