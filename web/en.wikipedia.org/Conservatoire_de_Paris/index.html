The Conservatoire de Paris, officially the Conservatoire national supérieur de musique et de danse de Paris, is a college of music and dance founded in 1795. It is one of the most prestigious music schools in the world and a model for conservatories internationally.

History

The Conservatoire was created during the French Revolution by merging the École Royale de Chant (founded 1784) with the Institut National de Musique, under the leadership of Bernard Sarrette. Early directors included Luigi Cherubini, who shaped its curriculum and discipline. It moved several times before settling in 1990 in purpose-built premises at the Cité de la Musique in the Parc de la Villette.

Education

Admission is by competitive audition, and tuition is essentially free. The Conservatoire trains performers, composers, conductors, musicologists and dancers, with roughly 1,300 students and several hundred teachers. Its alumni include Berlioz, Debussy, Ravel, Messiaen, Boulez and many leading orchestral musicians. The Prix de Rome and the system of competitive examinations (concours) were long central to its pedagogy.
