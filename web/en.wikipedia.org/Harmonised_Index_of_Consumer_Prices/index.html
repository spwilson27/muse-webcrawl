The Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP) is an indicator of inflation and price stability compiled by Eurostat, the statistical office of the European Union, together with the national statistical institutes of the member states. It measures the change over time in the prices of consumer goods and services acquired by households, using a methodology harmonised across countries so that inflation rates are genuinely comparable — unlike national consumer price indices, which differ in coverage and construction.

Purpose and use

The HICP was developed in the 1990s to support the convergence criteria of the Maastricht Treaty and, above all, the monetary policy of the European Central Bank, whose definition of price stability for the euro area is framed in terms of the euro area HICP (an annual increase of 2 per cent in the medium term). Eurostat publishes a flash estimate at the end of each reference month and full results, including the Monetary Union Index of Consumer Prices (MUICP) and the European Index of Consumer Prices (EICP), around the middle of the following month.

Methodology

The HICP is a chain-linked, Laspeyres-type price index with weights updated annually. It covers household final monetary consumption expenditure, including purchases by foreign visitors and spending in rural and urban areas alike. Historically it excluded the cost of owner-occupied housing — a notable difference from some national indices such as the US CPI — although Eurostat compiles a separate owner-occupied housing price index, and its inclusion in the HICP has been under long-standing discussion. Quality adjustment, including hedonic methods for items such as computers, is applied according to common EU regulations.

Comparison with national CPIs

Because of harmonisation rules, a country's HICP inflation can differ from its national CPI inflation: the United Kingdom, for example, published both an HICP-based CPI and the older RPI, which uses a different formula and includes housing costs. Within the euro area, the HICP is the headline measure used by markets and the ECB.
