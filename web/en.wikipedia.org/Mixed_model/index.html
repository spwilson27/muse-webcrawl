Mixed model

A mixed model (also called a mixed-effects model) is a statistical model that contains both fixed effects and random effects. These models are widely used in research where measurements are made repeatedly on the same subjects or clustered within groups — for example, students within schools, patients within hospitals, or repeated observations over time — because they account for correlations among observations that share a group.

Structure

In a linear mixed model, the response is modelled as a combination of fixed effects, whose coefficients are the same for all observations, and random effects, whose values vary by group according to a probability distribution, typically assumed normal. Random intercepts allow each group its own baseline value, while random slopes allow the effect of a predictor to vary across groups. Models with only fixed effects are ordinary regression models; models with only random effects are used, for example, in variance-component estimation in quantitative genetics.

Estimation

Parameters are commonly estimated by maximum likelihood or restricted maximum likelihood (REML). Mixed models for non-normal outcomes, such as binary or count data, are called generalised linear mixed models (GLMMs). Software implementations include the lme4 package in R, SAS PROC MIXED, and Statsmodels in Python.

Applications

Mixed models are standard in longitudinal studies and clinical trials, multilevel educational research, ecology, and animal and plant breeding, where the related concept of the "animal model" is used to estimate breeding values. They are closely related to hierarchical linear models and Bayesian hierarchical models.
