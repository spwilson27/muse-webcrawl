European Social Forum

The European Social Forum (ESF) was a recurring conference held by members of the alter-globalisation movement, focusing on social issues and opposition to neoliberal globalisation. It was inspired by the World Social Forum in Porto Alegre, Brazil, and took place in different European cities under the slogan "Another Europe is possible."

History and meetings

The first ESF was held in Florence, Italy in November 2002, drawing tens of thousands of participants and culminating in a march of around one million people against the anticipated Iraq War. Subsequent forums were held in Paris (2003), London (2004), Athens (2006), Malmö (2008), and Istanbul (2010). Attendance declined after the mid-2000s, and no full-scale forum has been held since Istanbul; national and thematic social forums continued in some countries.

Organisation and themes

The ESF brought together trade unions, NGOs, peace and environmental groups, migrant organisations, and left-wing political currents in plenary sessions, seminars, and cultural events. Recurring themes included opposition to war, defence of public services and welfare states, workers' rights, migrant rights, environmental sustainability, and democratisation of European institutions. Protests associated with the forums targeted G8 summits and European Union policies.

Criticism

Critics questioned the forums' practical impact and internal divisions between institutional NGOs and more radical movements, while supporters credited the ESF with networking European movements and shaping anti-war mobilisation in 2003.
