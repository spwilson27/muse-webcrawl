Birmingham campaign

The Birmingham campaign was a movement organised in early 1963 by the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC), led by Martin Luther King Jr., to bring attention to the integration efforts of African Americans in Birmingham, Alabama, one of the most racially segregated cities in the United States.

Events

The campaign used boycotts, sit-ins and marches. In April 1963 King was arrested and wrote his famous "Letter from Birmingham Jail". In May, the Children's Crusade saw thousands of school students march; the violent response by police under Bull Connor, using dogs and fire hoses, was broadcast nationally.

Outcome

The campaign led to an agreement to desegregate lunch counters and remove segregation signs, and it helped build momentum for the Civil Rights Act of 1964.
