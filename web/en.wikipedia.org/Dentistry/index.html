Dentistry

Dentistry, also known as dental medicine or oral medicine, is the branch of medicine concerned with the study, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of diseases and conditions of the oral cavity, including the teeth, gums, and related structures of the mouth and jaw.

Scope

The dentist's supporting team — dental hygienists, assistants, and technicians — assists in providing oral health services. Common treatments include fillings for tooth decay, extraction of teeth, scaling and polishing, root canal treatment (endodontics), crowns and bridges, dentures, and dental implants. Preventive dentistry emphasizes regular examinations, fluoride, sealants, and oral hygiene instruction.

History

Evidence of dental practice dates back thousands of years, to the Indus Valley and ancient Egypt. Dentistry became a distinct profession in early modern Europe; Pierre Fauchard's 1728 work Le Chirurgien Dentiste earned him the title "father of modern dentistry." The first dental school, the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery, opened in 1840.

Specialties

Recognized specialties include orthodontics (alignment of teeth), periodontics (gums), prosthodontics (replacement of teeth), oral and maxillofacial surgery, pediatric dentistry, endodontics, dental public health, and oral pathology.

Education

Dentists typically earn a degree such as the Doctor of Dental Surgery (DDS) or Doctor of Dental Medicine (DMD) after undergraduate and dental school study, followed by licensure; many pursue additional residency training in a specialty.
