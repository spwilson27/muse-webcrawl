Appalachian Basin

The Appalachian Basin is a sedimentary foreland basin in the eastern United States, lying west of the Appalachian Mountains and extending from Alabama and Georgia northeastward through the Virginias, Ohio, Pennsylvania and New York. It contains a thick succession of Palaeozoic sedimentary rocks deposited as the Appalachian orogeny built the neighbouring mountain belt.

Geology

The basin's fill records three major mountain-building episodes — the Taconic, Acadian and Alleghanian orogenies. Cycles of marine shales, sandstones and limestones alternate with coal-bearing continental deposits of the Carboniferous. Important units include the Devonian Marcellus Shale and the deeper Utica Shale.

Energy resources

The basin was the site of the first commercial oil well in the United States (Drake Well, Pennsylvania, 1859) and has long produced coal, oil and conventional natural gas. In the 21st century, horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing in the Marcellus and Utica shales made it one of the world's most productive natural-gas regions.
