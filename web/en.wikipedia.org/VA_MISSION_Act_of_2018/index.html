VA MISSION Act of 2018

The VA MISSION Act of 2018, formally the John S. McCain III, Daniel K. Akaka, and Samuel R. Johnson VA Maintaining Internal Systems and Strengthening Integrated Outside Networks Act of 2018, is a United States federal law enacted to expand health care options for veterans. It was signed into law by President Donald Trump on June 6, 2018.

Background

The Act consolidated several community care programs administered by the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), including the Veterans Choice Program created in 2014, into a single Veterans Community Care Program. It was named in honor of Senators John McCain and Daniel Akaka and Representative Sam Johnson.

Provisions

The law allows eligible veterans to receive care from private-sector providers when VA facilities cannot offer timely or convenient treatment, establishes access standards based on drive time and wait time, expands telehealth and urgent care benefits, and extends caregiver support to veterans of all eras. It also authorized funding for VA infrastructure and created an asset review process.

Implementation

The community care provisions took effect on June 6, 2019, one year after enactment. Eligibility generally covers veterans who face drive times over 30 minutes for certain care or wait times over 20 days.
