Boeing XF7B

The Boeing XF7B (Boeing Model 273) was an American prototype naval fighter of 1933 — Boeing's first monoplane fighter, with a low cantilever wing, an enclosed cockpit and a Pratt & Whitney R-1535 radial engine. It marked Boeing's transition from biplane to monoplane fighter design and influenced the later P-26 Peashooter lineage.

Development and testing

First flown in September 1933, the XF7B reached about 233 mph (375 km/h), fast for its day. However, its high landing speed and poor carrier-suitability — limited forward view and handling at low speed — led the Navy to reject it. Only one prototype was completed; the aircraft was later used for testing before being struck off.

Significance

Though unsuccessful, the XF7B demonstrated the performance advantage of the monoplane layout for carrier fighters, a configuration the Navy adopted fully with types such as the Brewster F2A and Grumman F4F later in the decade.
