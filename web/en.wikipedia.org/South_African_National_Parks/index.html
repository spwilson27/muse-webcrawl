South African National Parks

South African National Parks (SANParks)
Formed: 1926
Headquarters: Groenkloof, Pretoria
Parks managed: 19 national parks
Area: over 4 million hectares

South African National Parks (SANParks) is the body responsible for managing South Africa's system of national parks. It was formed in 1926 with the proclamation of Kruger National Park and today manages 19 parks covering more than four million hectares — roughly three percent of the country's land area — together with a number of marine protected areas.

History

The National Parks Board of Trustees was established under the National Parks Act of 1926, which also created Kruger National Park from the Sabi and Singwitsi game reserves championed by conservationist James Stevenson-Hamilton. Over the following decades parks such as Kalahari Gemsbok (now part of Kgalagadi), Addo Elephant, Bontebok and Mountain Zebra were added. After 1994 the system expanded to represent all of South Africa's biomes, adding parks such as Mapungubwe, Marakele and the expanded Garden Route and West Coast parks.

Parks

The SANParks system includes:

  • Kruger National Park — the flagship big-game reserve
  • Table Mountain National Park
  • Addo Elephant National Park
  • Kgalagadi Transfrontier Park (shared with Botswana)
  • Garden Route National Park
  • Mapungubwe National Park, a World Heritage Site
  • Golden Gate Highlands, Augrabies Falls, Camdeboo, Karoo, Mountain Zebra, Namaqua, Tankwa Karoo, West Coast, Agulhas, Bontebok, Marakele, Mokala and Richtersveld parks

Conservation and tourism

SANParks combines biodiversity conservation with nature-based tourism; tourism revenue from rest camps, game drives and concessions funds much of its conservation work. It also runs community programmes such as the People's and Parks Programme and environmental education initiatives, and manages anti-poaching operations, notably against rhino poaching in Kruger.
