Arctic Circle

The Arctic Circle is one of the two polar circles, and the most northerly of the five major circles of latitude as shown on maps of Earth. It marks the northernmost latitude at which the centre of the noon-day sun is just visible on the December solstice, and the southernmost latitude at which the centre of the sun is visible without interruption for 24 hours around the June solstice—the phenomena known as polar night and the midnight sun.

Position

The position of the Arctic Circle is not fixed; it currently lies at about 66°33′50″ N and drifts northward by roughly 15 metres per year, following changes in Earth's axial tilt, which oscillates between about 22.1° and 24.5° over a 41,000-year cycle. North of the circle lie the Arctic Ocean and the northern coasts of North America, Greenland, Europe and Asia; the land within the circle is divided among eight countries—Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia, the United States (Alaska), Canada, Denmark (Greenland) and Iceland, where the circle passes just offshore through the island of Grímsey.

Climate and habitation

The region inside the circle is dominated by tundra and polar climates, sea ice and permafrost, though the North Atlantic Current keeps parts of coastal Norway comparatively mild. Several hundred thousand people live north of the circle, including Indigenous peoples such as the Sámi, Inuit, Nenets and Chukchi, and towns such as Murmansk, Norilsk and Tromsø.
