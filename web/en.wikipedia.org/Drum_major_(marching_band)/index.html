Drum major (marching band)

A drum major is the leader of a marching band, drum and bugle corps, or pipe band, responsible for conducting the ensemble in performance, giving commands, and setting the tempo. In modern scholastic and collegiate bands the drum major stands on a podium or ladder during field shows, conducts with distinctive arm movements, and serves as a visible symbol of the band.

Duties

The drum major keeps time for the entire ensemble, cues entrances, and leads rehearsals and parades. In parades the drum major traditionally carries a mace or baton, performs flourishes, and opens and closes the performance with a formal salute. Behind the scenes, drum majors act as student leaders and liaisons between the band director and the members, helping with discipline, morale, and instruction.

History and styles

The role derives from the British Army, where the drum major was the senior drummer responsible for military drumming signals, later becoming the band's ceremonial leader with an ornate staff. American universities developed distinctive styles: some drum majors perform elaborate routines with a mace (as at Ohio State, whose drum major's pregame backbend is a famous tradition), while others, such as the University of Michigan's, emphasise athletic showmanship. In drum and bugle corps the equivalent leader is often called the conductor or field commander.

Selection

Drum majors are usually selected by audition and interview from experienced members, and may attend specialised camps and training in conducting, leadership, and mace work.
