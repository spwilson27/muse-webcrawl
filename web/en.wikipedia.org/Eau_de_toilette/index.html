Eau de toilette

Eau de toilette (French for "grooming water") is a lightly scented perfume, or the fragrance concentration so described, typically containing about 5–15% perfume essence dissolved in alcohol. It sits between the weaker eau de Cologne (roughly 2–4%) and the stronger eau de parfum (roughly 15–20%) in the standard hierarchy of fragrance strengths.

Characteristics

Eau de toilette formulations emphasize bright top notes and are generally fresher and lighter than the same scent in eau de parfum form, in which base notes are more prominent. Longevity on skin is typically a few hours. Because the concentration differs, the same named fragrance may smell noticeably different across its toilette and parfum versions, and perfumers adjust the formula for each strength.

History and usage

The term derives from the French toilette, the act of washing and dressing. "Toilet water" in the 19th and early 20th centuries referred to scented waters and lotions used in grooming; the modern perfume-industry usage as a concentration grade became standard in the 20th century. Eau de toilette is the most common form in which men's fragrances are sold and is popular as a daytime or warm-weather strength.

Concentration grades

From strongest to lightest, the common grades are parfum (extrait), eau de parfum, eau de toilette, eau de Cologne and eau fraîche; percentages overlap between houses and are conventions rather than legal standards.
