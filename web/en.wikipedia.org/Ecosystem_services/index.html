Ecosystem services

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ecosystem services are the many and varied benefits that humans freely gain from the natural environment and from properly functioning ecosystems. Such ecosystems include, for example, agroecosystems, forest ecosystems, grassland ecosystems, and aquatic ecosystems. These ecosystems, functioning in healthy relationship, offer such things as natural pollination of crops, clean air, extreme weather mitigation, and human mental and physical well-being. Collectively, these benefits are becoming known as ecosystem services, and are often integral to the provisioning of clean drinking water, the decomposition of wastes, and resilience and productivity of food ecosystems.

Pollination is a regulating ecosystem service

Categories

The landmark Millennium Ecosystem Assessment (2005) grouped ecosystem services into four categories:

  • Provisioning services — products obtained from ecosystems, such as food, fresh water, wood, fibre, genetic resources, and medicines.
  • Regulating services — benefits from the regulation of ecosystem processes, including climate regulation, flood control, disease regulation, water purification, and pollination.
  • Cultural services — non-material benefits such as recreation, aesthetic enjoyment, tourism, and spiritual fulfilment.
  • Supporting services — services necessary for the production of all other services, such as nutrient cycling, soil formation, and primary production.

Valuation

Scientists and environmentalists have discussed ecosystem services for decades, but the term became widely used after the Millennium Ecosystem Assessment. Efforts to assign monetary values to ecosystem services — one famous 1997 study by Robert Costanza and colleagues estimated the total value of global ecosystem services in the tens of trillions of dollars per year — have influenced policy tools such as payments for ecosystem services (PES), in which landholders are compensated for managing land to provide services such as watershed protection or carbon storage.

Threats

Many ecosystem services are degraded by land-use change, overexploitation, pollution, invasive species, and climate change. The 2019 IPBES Global Assessment concluded that nature's contributions to people are deteriorating worldwide, with implications for human well-being and the Sustainable Development Goals.
