COVID-19 pandemic in Australia

The COVID-19 pandemic in Australia began on 25 January 2020, when the first case was confirmed in Melbourne, Victoria, in a man who had returned from Wuhan, China. Australia responded with border closures, hotel quarantine for arrivals, lockdowns and large-scale vaccination, keeping case and death rates far below those of most comparable countries in the pandemic's first two years.

Course

After an initial wave in March 2020, Australia pursued a suppression and at times elimination strategy. Melbourne endured some of the longest cumulative lockdowns in the world. The Delta outbreak of 2021 spread from Sydney across the country, and the Omicron variant from late 2021 caused very large case numbers as restrictions were lifted following high vaccination coverage.

Impact

The pandemic led to the closure of Australia's international border from March 2020 to late 2021, the formation of a National Cabinet of federal and state leaders, major economic support programs such as JobKeeper, and widespread working from home. More than eleven million cases and over twenty thousand deaths had been reported by the time the emergency phase ended.
