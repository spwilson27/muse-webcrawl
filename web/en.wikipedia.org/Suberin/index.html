Suberin

Suberin is a lipophilic macromolecule found in specialized plant cell walls, wherever insulation or protection toward the surrounding environment is needed. Suberized cells form a protective and insulating tissue in plants, including the bark (phellem) of trees and shrubs — cork is almost pure suberized tissue rich in suberin — the endodermis of roots (where it forms the Casparian strip), seed coats, wound-healing periderm, and the skin of tubers such as potatoes.

Structure and composition

Suberin is a polyester biopolymer built from long-chain ω-hydroxy fatty acids, α,ω-dicarboxylic acids, and glycerol, together with an aromatic domain containing ferulic acid and other hydroxycinnamic acids that cross-links it to the cell wall. Deposited as lamellae inside the cell wall, it is strongly hydrophobic and acts as a barrier to water loss and to movement of solutes and pathogens.

Function

In roots, suberized endodermal and exodermal layers regulate uptake of water and nutrients and help exclude sodium and toxins; suberization increases in response to stress, drought, and wounding. In bark, suberin-containing cork insulates stems and resists microbial degradation, properties exploited commercially in cork stoppers and insulation. Suberin is chemically related to, but distinct from, cutin, the polyester of aerial cuticles, and both are contrasted with the phenolic polymer lignin.
