The Fairmont Le Château Frontenac, commonly known as the Château Frontenac, is a grand hotel in Quebec City, Canada, and one of the most photographed hotels in the world. Operated by Fairmont Hotels and Resorts, it stands on a cliff overlooking the Saint Lawrence River within the UNESCO World Heritage district of Old Quebec, on the site of the former Château Saint-Louis, residence of the French governors of New France. It was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1981.

History

The hotel was built by the Canadian Pacific Railway beginning in 1892 to the designs of American architect Bruce Price, and opened in December 1893 as the first of Canada's château-style railway hotels. It was named after Louis de Buade, Count of Frontenac, governor of New France in the 17th century. The central tower and additional wings were added in stages, culminating in 1924; a fire in 1926 damaged part of the complex. During the Second World War, the hotel hosted the Quebec Conferences of 1943 and 1944, where Winston Churchill and Franklin D. Roosevelt planned the Normandy landings and the Italian campaign.

Architecture and fame

Built of brick with steep copper roofs, turrets, and asymmetrical massing, the château exemplifies the Châteauesque style. Its 610 rooms and silhouette dominating the Quebec skyline have made it an emblem of the city; Guinness World Records has recognized it as the most photographed hotel in the world. Alfred Hitchcock filmed scenes of I Confess (1953) there, and a major renovation completed in 2014 modernized the property for the 21st century.
