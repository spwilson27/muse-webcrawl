Carrara marble is a type of white or blue-grey marble of high quality, popular for use in sculpture and building decor. It is quarried in the city of Carrara in the province of Massa and Carrara, in the Lunigiana region of Tuscany, Italy, in the Apuan Alps.

History

The quarries were worked from Roman times; the nearby colony of Luna supplied marble for many buildings of ancient Rome. During the Renaissance, Carrara marble again became the prestige material for sculpture: Michelangelo personally selected blocks there for works including the Pietà and David. The Pantheon and Trajan's Column in Rome, Marble Arch in London, and the Peace Monument in Washington, D.C., all use Carrara marble.

Varieties

Grades include Statuario, prized for sculpture for its pure white ground and dramatic veining, Bianco Carrara for general architectural use, and Bardiglio, a darker grey variety. The marble is a metamorphosed Jurassic limestone, its veins formed by mineral impurities present during metamorphism.
