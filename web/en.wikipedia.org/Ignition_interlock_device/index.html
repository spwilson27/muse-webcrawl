Ignition interlock device

Ignition interlock device
Purpose: Prevent driving under the influence of alcohol
Method: Breath alcohol test before starting vehicle
Common users: Drivers convicted of DUI offences

An ignition interlock device or breath alcohol ignition interlock device (IID or BAIID) is a breathalyser mechanism installed in a motor vehicle that requires the driver to blow into a mouthpiece before the engine can be started. If the measured alcohol concentration exceeds a programmed limit, the device prevents the engine from starting.

Operation

Modern devices use an electrochemical fuel-cell sensor specific to alcohol. As well as the initial test, many systems require "rolling retests" at intervals while driving, to ensure the driver remains sober and that someone else did not blow the start test. Results are logged and reported to the supervising authority, and the vehicle must be serviced periodically at an approved centre.

Use

Interlocks are most often imposed as a condition of sentencing or licence reinstatement after driving-under-the-influence convictions. Many jurisdictions in North America, Australia, and Europe operate interlock programmes, and studies generally find that they reduce repeat offending while installed.
