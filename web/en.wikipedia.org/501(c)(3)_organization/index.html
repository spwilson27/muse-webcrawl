501(c)(3) organization

PurposesReligious, charitable, scientific, educational, literary, and others

A 501(c)(3) organization is a United States nonprofit organization exempt from federal income tax under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. It is the most common type of tax-exempt nonprofit in the United States, covering charities, churches, schools, hospitals, museums, and foundations.

Requirements

To qualify, an organization must be organized and operated exclusively for exempt purposes — religious, charitable, scientific, testing for public safety, literary, educational, amateur sports competition, or prevention of cruelty to children or animals. No part of its net earnings may benefit a private individual, and it must not be an "action organization": substantial lobbying is prohibited, and participation in political campaigns is barred entirely (the Johnson Amendment).

Types and compliance

The IRS divides 501(c)(3) entities into public charities and private foundations, which face different rules. Most must apply for recognition using Form 1023 or the streamlined Form 1023-EZ; churches and very small organizations are exceptions. Annual information returns (Form 990) are required, and exemption can be revoked for non-filing or loss of exempt purpose. Contributions to 501(c)(3) organizations are generally deductible by donors under section 170.
