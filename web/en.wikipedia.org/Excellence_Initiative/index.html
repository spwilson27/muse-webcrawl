Excellence Initiative

The Excellence Initiative (German: Exzellenzinitiative) was a funding programme of the German federal and state governments launched in 2005 to promote top-level research and strengthen Germany's universities in international competition. It was run by the German Research Foundation (DFG) and the German Council of Science and Humanities.

Funding lines

The initiative funded three lines: graduate schools to train early-career researchers, clusters of excellence for cutting-edge research in competitive fields, and institutional strategies to strengthen universities overall. Universities winning in all three lines were informally called "elite universities" (Eliteuniversitäten).

History

Funding decisions were made in 2006 and 2007 for the first phase and in 2012 for the second phase, which ran to 2017. Some 4.6 billion euros were distributed. In 2019 the programme was succeeded by the Excellence Strategy (Exzellenzstrategie), which funds clusters of excellence and, through a separate competition, "Universities of Excellence".

Criticism

Critics argued the initiative widened the gap between well-funded and other universities and privileged research over teaching, while supporters credited it with raising the international visibility of German higher education.
