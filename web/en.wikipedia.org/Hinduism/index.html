Hinduism is an Indian religion and dharma, or way of life. It is the world's third-largest religion, with over 1.2 billion followers, most of them in India and Nepal. Hinduism has been called the oldest living major religion, its roots tracing to the Indus Valley Civilisation and the historical Vedic religion of Iron Age India.

Hindu practice and belief are diverse, with no single founder, creed, or central authority. Prominent themes include dharma (ethics and duties), samsara (the cycle of rebirth), karma (action and its consequences), and moksha (liberation from the cycle), alongside devotion (bhakti) to deities such as Vishnu, Shiva, Devi, Ganesha, and Surya. The concept of Brahman, ultimate reality, is central to its philosophy.

History

Scholars divide Hindu history into periods including the Indus Valley period (c. 3300–1300 BCE), the Vedic period (c. 1500–500 BCE), in which the Vedas were composed, and a "second urbanisation" period (c. 600–200 BCE) that saw the rise of shramana movements such as Buddhism and Jainism. Classical and medieval Hinduism saw the composition of the epics and Puranas, the flourishing of bhakti movements, and the work of philosophers such as Adi Shankara (Advaita Vedanta), Ramanuja, and Madhva.

Practices

Hindu practices include puja (worship, often before a murti), yoga and meditation, recitation of mantras, rites of passage (samskaras), and festivals such as Diwali, Holi, Durga Puja, and the Kumbh Mela pilgrimage — the largest peaceful gathering in the world. The cow is revered, and many Hindus practise vegetarianism and ahimsa (non-violence).

Texts

Hindu texts are classified as shruti ("heard": the four Vedas and the Upanishads) and smriti ("remembered": the Mahabharata, Ramayana, and Puranas). The Bhagavad Gita, part of the Mahabharata, is among the most widely read.
