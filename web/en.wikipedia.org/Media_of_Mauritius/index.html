Media of Mauritius

The media of Mauritius comprise print, radio, television and online outlets serving the island nation's multilingual population in French, English, Mauritian Creole, Hindi and other languages. Mauritius has a long newspaper tradition dating to the 18th century, and its media are generally regarded as among the freest in Africa, although journalists' organisations periodically raise concerns about political pressure, state advertising leverage and defamation law.

Print media

Mauritius's first newspaper, Annonces, Affiches et Avis Divers, appeared in 1773, making the island one of the earliest centres of printing in the southern hemisphere. Today the leading French-language dailies include L'Express (founded 1963) and Le Mauricien (founded 1908), alongside the Le Défi Media Group titles and weeklies such as Week-End and 5-Plus Dimanche. English-language coverage is provided largely through the same groups' online editions.

Broadcasting

The state-owned Mauritius Broadcasting Corporation operates several television channels and radio services in multiple languages. Private radio arrived in 2002 with stations such as Radio Plus, Top FM and Radio One, ending the MBC's radio monopoly. Private free-to-air television remains limited, and pay television and streaming services supply much entertainment programming.

Internet and telecommunications

Internet penetration is high for the region. Online news portals associated with the newspaper groups — lexpress.mu and defimedia.info among them — draw large audiences, and social media are major channels for news and political debate. The Information and Communication Technologies Authority regulates telecommunications.

Press freedom

Mauritius consistently ranks near the top of African countries in international press-freedom indices. Critics nevertheless point to the absence of a freedom-of-information act, government influence over the MBC, and periodic proposals to tighten online-content regulation.
