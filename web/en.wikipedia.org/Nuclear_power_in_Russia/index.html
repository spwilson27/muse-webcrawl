Nuclear power in Russia is a major component of the country's energy system. Russia operates one of the largest fleets of nuclear reactors in the world, supplying roughly a fifth of its electricity, and is a leading exporter of reactor technology through the state corporation Rosatom.

History

The Soviet Union commissioned the world's first grid-connected nuclear power plant at Obninsk in 1954. Large-scale deployment followed with RBMK and VVER pressurised water reactors during the 1970s and 1980s. The 1986 Chernobyl disaster, in the Ukrainian SSR, reshaped safety policy across the Soviet fleet. After the dissolution of the USSR, Russia continued operating its inherited plants and gradually commissioned new VVER-1000 and VVER-1200 units.

Current fleet

Major stations include the Leningrad, Kursk, Balakovo, Kalinin and Rostov plants. Russia has also operated the floating nuclear power plant Akademik Lomonosov, moored at Pevek in Chukotka since 2020, and has pioneered fast breeder reactors such as the BN-600 and BN-800 at Beloyarsk.

Exports and policy

Rosatom holds a large share of the global market for new reactor construction, with projects in countries including Turkey, Bangladesh, Egypt, India and China. Russian policy treats nuclear power as a strategic export industry and a low-carbon domestic energy source.
