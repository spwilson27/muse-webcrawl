Porta Pia
LocationRome, Italy
Built1561–1565
ArchitectMichelangelo (outer façade); Giacomo Del Duca
Commissioned byPope Pius IV

Porta Pia is a gate in the Aurelian Walls of Rome, Italy, on the Via Nomentana. Commissioned by Pope Pius IV, after whom it is named, it was built between 1561 and 1565 to replace the Porta Nomentana. Its rusticated outer façade was among the last architectural works of Michelangelo.

The breach of Porta Pia

The gate is famous for the events of 20 September 1870, when Italian royal troops under General Raffaele Cadorna bombarded the walls nearby — the "breach of Porta Pia" — and entered Rome, ending the Papal States and completing the unification of Italy. A monument and the Museo Storico dei Bersaglieri near the gate commemorate the capture of Rome.

Description

The inner façade, facing the city, was rebuilt in neoclassical style in 1869 by Virginio Vespignani, incorporating a triumphal-arch design with statues and the arms of Pope Pius IX.
