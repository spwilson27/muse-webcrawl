Unitary group

In mathematics, the unitary group of degree n, denoted U(n), is the group of n × n unitary matrices, with the group operation of matrix multiplication. The unitary group is a subgroup of the general linear group GL(n, C). A complex square matrix U is unitary if its conjugate transpose U* is also its inverse, that is, if U*U = UU* = I, where I is the identity matrix.

The unitary group U(n) is a real Lie group of dimension n2. Its Lie algebra consists of the skew-Hermitian matrices. The subgroup of unitary matrices with determinant 1 is called the special unitary group, denoted SU(n).

Properties

  • Since the determinant of a unitary matrix is a complex number with absolute value 1, the map det: U(n) → U(1) is a group homomorphism whose kernel is SU(n).
  • The unitary group U(n) is compact and connected, but not simply connected; its fundamental group is infinite cyclic.
  • The group U(1) is isomorphic to the circle group, and appears in physics as the gauge group of electromagnetism.

Applications

Unitary groups play a central role in quantum mechanics, where the time evolution of a closed quantum system is given by a unitary operator, as well as in the theory of Lie groups and representation theory. The groups SU(2) and SU(3) describe aspects of the weak and strong interactions in the Standard Model of particle physics.
