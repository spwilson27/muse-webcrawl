Art rock

Art rock is a subgenre of rock music that generally reflects a challenging or avant-garde approach to rock, or which makes use of modernist, experimental, or unconventional elements. Art rock aspires to elevate rock music to a level of artistic credibility, and often treats albums as unified artistic statements rather than collections of songs.

History

The genre emerged in the mid-1960s as musicians influenced by classical music, jazz, and the avant-garde pushed beyond the three-minute single; landmark works include the Beach Boys' Pet Sounds (1966) and the Beatles' Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967). In the 1970s art rock overlapped with progressive rock, with acts such as Pink Floyd, Roxy Music, Genesis, and David Bowie, whose theatrical personae became a hallmark of the style.

Later developments

Post-punk and new wave bands including Talking Heads drew on art-school sensibilities, and later artists such as Kate Bush, Radiohead, and Björk have kept the art-rock approach prominent in alternative music.
