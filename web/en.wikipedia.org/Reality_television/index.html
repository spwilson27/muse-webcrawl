Reality television

Reality television presents supposedly unscripted real-life situations, often with ordinary people as participants.

Reality television is a genre of television programming that documents purportedly unscripted real-life situations, often starring ordinary people rather than professional actors. Reality television emerged as a distinct genre in the early 2000s with the success of shows such as Survivor, Big Brother and American Idol, though examples date back decades, to hidden-camera programmes like Candid Camera (1948) and the fly-on-the-wall documentary series An American Family (1973).

Subgenres

The genre spans documentary-style "docusoaps" following people in their jobs or private lives; competition and game shows in which contestants are progressively eliminated; talent searches; dating and relationship programmes; makeover and lifestyle shows; and celebrity reality, which follows famous people through their daily routines. Many formats are traded internationally and adapted country by country.

Production and authenticity

Although marketed as unscripted, reality programmes are shaped by casting, structured situations, producer prompts, selective editing and, in some cases, partially scripted dialogue — practices sometimes summarized by critics with the term "soft-scripted". Editing techniques such as "Frankenbiting" rearrange fragments of interviews to change meaning. Producers defend these methods as necessary storytelling compression, while critics charge that the genre routinely misrepresents participants and events.

Criticism and impact

Reality television is cheaper to produce than scripted drama and has been blamed for displacing it, including through the industry's reliance on non-union labour. Scholars and critics debate its effects on viewers' expectations of fame, privacy and behaviour, its treatment of participants' mental health, and its role in launching celebrity and even political careers. Despite critical disdain, the genre has proved durably popular worldwide and has migrated readily to streaming platforms.
