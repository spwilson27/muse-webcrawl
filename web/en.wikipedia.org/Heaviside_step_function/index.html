Heaviside step function

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Heaviside step function, or the unit step function, usually denoted by H, is a discontinuous function, named after Oliver Heaviside, whose value is zero for negative arguments and one for positive arguments. The value at zero is usually left undefined or assigned a conventional value, often 1/2 or 1, depending on the context.

H(x) = 0 for x < 0;   H(x) = 1 for x > 0

Applications

The step function is fundamental in signal processing and control theory, where it models a signal that switches on at a specified time and stays on indefinitely. In probability, it appears as the cumulative distribution function of a degenerate random variable. Its derivative, in the sense of distributions, is the Dirac delta function. Heaviside developed the function as part of his operational calculus for solving differential equations arising in electrical engineering, in particular the analysis of telegraph lines.

Related functions

The step function can be viewed as the integral of the Dirac delta. It is related to the ramp function, which is its integral, and to the rectangular function, which can be written as the difference of two step functions. In Fourier analysis the step function appears in the solution of initial-value problems via the Laplace transform, where the unit step is used to express piecewise-defined inputs.
