Circumzenithal arc

The circumzenithal arc, also called the circumzenith arc (CZA), the Bravais arc, or colloquially an "upside-down rainbow" or "smile in the sky", is an optical phenomenon similar in appearance to a rainbow but belonging to the family of halos arising from refraction of sunlight through ice crystals, generally in cirrus or cirrostratus clouds, rather than from raindrops.

Formation

The arc forms high above the Sun, curving around the zenith, when sunlight enters the top horizontal faces of plate-shaped ice crystals and exits through a vertical side face. It can only form when the Sun is lower than about 32.2° above the horizon, and is brightest when the Sun is about 22° high. Unlike a rainbow, the arc's colours are pure and vivid, with blue on the outer (lower, sunward-inverted) edge — the colour order is reversed relative to the circumhorizontal arc, its low-sky counterpart.

Observation

Because it lies near the zenith, the circumzenithal arc is often overlooked despite not being especially rare; observers looking up on days with thin cirrus and a low sun may see it. It never appears as a complete circle, spanning at most about a quarter of a circle around the zenith.
