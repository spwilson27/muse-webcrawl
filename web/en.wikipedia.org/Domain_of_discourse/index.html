Domain of discourse

Domain of discourse
Field: Logic, semantics, philosophy
Also called: Universe of discourse

In the formal sciences, the domain of discourse, also called the universe of discourse, is the set of entities over which certain variables of interest in a formal treatment may range. When one says "for all x" or "there exists an x," the domain of discourse specifies which objects x is allowed to be.

In logic

In model theory, a structure for a formal language includes a non-empty domain (universe) together with interpretations of the language's symbols. Quantifiers in first-order logic range over this domain. Specifying the domain matters: the sentence "every number has a successor" is true over the natural numbers but behaves differently over bounded sets.

History

The concept is associated with the 19th-century logician Augustus De Morgan and was developed by George Boole and later writers in philosophical logic. In ordinary language, the term also refers loosely to the collection of objects being discussed in a conversation or text.
