Magnificat (Bach)

ComposerJohann Sebastian Bach
CatalogueBWV 243 (D major); BWV 243a (E-flat major)
TextLuke 1:46–55 (Latin Vulgate)
Composed1723 (Leipzig); revised c. 1733
ScoringSSATB choir, soloists, orchestra

The Magnificat by Johann Sebastian Bach is a setting of the biblical canticle Magnificat, the Song of Mary from the Gospel of Luke. Bach first composed a version in E-flat major (BWV 243a) for Christmas 1723 in Leipzig, shortly after taking up the post of Thomaskantor, and later revised it into the better-known version in D major (BWV 243), one of his most popular sacred works.

Structure

The D-major version is in twelve movements, symmetrically arranged, for five-part choir (SSATB), five soloists, and a festive Baroque orchestra with trumpets and timpani. The opening chorus "Magnificat anima mea Dominum" returns in varied form at the close ("Sicut erat in principio"). Notable movements include the aria "Quia respexit humilitatem" for soprano with oboe d'amore, the duet "Et misericordia", and the buoyant chorus "Fecit potentiam".

History

The 1723 version included four Christmas interpolations (lauds) specific to the feast; Bach removed these in the D-major revision, making the work suitable for performance throughout the church year. The Magnificat was among the first of Bach's works to be published in the 19th-century Bach revival.
