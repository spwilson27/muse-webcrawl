Movie theater

A movie theater (American English), cinema (British English), or cinema hall, is a venue that contains an auditorium for viewing films (also called movies), generally for entertainment and typically for a paying audience. Most theaters are commercial operations with tiered or raked seating facing a large projection screen, a sound system, and a concession stand selling snacks such as popcorn.

History

Public film exhibition began in the 1890s with Edison's Kinetoscope parlors and the Lumière brothers' projected shows. Dedicated venues spread rapidly: cheap storefront nickelodeons in the 1900s, then lavish "movie palaces" of the 1920s seating thousands. Drive-in theaters flourished after the Second World War. The multiplex — many screens under one roof — from the 1960s onward, and the megaplex of the 1990s, reshaped the business, while digital projection replaced 35 mm film in the 2000s–2010s. Home television, video, and streaming have repeatedly pressured attendance, prompting premium formats (IMAX, Dolby Cinema, recliner seating, dine-in service).

Operation

Theaters license films from distributors and split box-office revenue, earning much of their profit from concessions. Typical elements include a box office or online ticketing, lobby, projection (today usually digital cinema servers with DCP files), surround sound, and auditoria designed for sightlines and acoustics. Ratings, trailers and advertisements precede the feature.
