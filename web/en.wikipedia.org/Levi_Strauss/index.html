Claude Lévi-Strauss

Claude Lévi-Strauss (28 November 1908 – 30 October 2009) was a French anthropologist and ethnologist whose work was central to the development of structural anthropology and structuralism. He argued that cultures can be understood through underlying structures of the human mind, expressed in kinship systems, myth and custom.

Life and work

After fieldwork among Indigenous peoples of Brazil in the 1930s, Lévi-Strauss published The Elementary Structures of Kinship (1949) and the memoir-like Tristes Tropiques (1955). His four-volume Mythologiques (1964–1971) analysed hundreds of myths from the Americas as variations on shared structures and binary oppositions.

Influence

Lévi-Strauss held the chair of social anthropology at the Collège de France from 1959 to 1982 and profoundly influenced philosophy, literary theory and the social sciences in the second half of the 20th century. (He is not to be confused with Levi Strauss, founder of the jeans company.)
