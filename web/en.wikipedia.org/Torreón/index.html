Torreón (Spanish pronunciation: [toreˈon]) is a city and seat of the municipality of the same name in the Mexican state of Coahuila. With a population of about 690,000 in 2020, it is one of the youngest major cities in Mexico and, together with the neighbouring Durango cities of Gómez Palacio and Lerdo, forms the La Laguna metropolitan area, a conurbation of roughly 1.5 million people in the arid north of the country.

History

The city grew from a railway junction established in 1883–1893 where the Mexican Central Railway crossed the line to Durango, beside a watchtower (torreón, "big tower") built earlier to guard the ranch of Leonardo Zuloaga against raids and to monitor the flooding of the Nazas River. Irrigation from the Nazas made La Laguna Mexico's leading cotton-growing region, and Torreón boomed as an agricultural, banking and industrial centre, receiving city status in 1907. During the Mexican Revolution the city was a strategic prize: it was captured by Pancho Villa's División del Norte in 1913 and 1914, and in May 1911 its capture was accompanied by a massacre of more than 300 members of the city's Chinese-Mexican community, one of the worst episodes of anti-Chinese violence in the Americas, for which the Mexican government formally apologised in 2021.

Geography and economy

Torreón has a hot desert climate with low rainfall and large diurnal temperature ranges. The traditional cotton economy has been supplemented by dairy production — La Laguna is Mexico's largest milk-producing region and home to Grupo Lala — as well as metallurgy and mining (through Peñoles, one of the world's largest silver refiners), textiles, and automotive and food industries. Dams on the Nazas River regulate the irrigation that sustains agriculture in the region.

Culture and sport

The city's best-known landmark is the Cristo de las Noas, a 22-metre statue of Christ on the Cerro de las Noas, reached by cable car and among the largest such monuments in Latin America. Torreón is home to the football club Santos Laguna, founded in 1983 and playing at the Estadio Corona, and has produced a strong tradition in Mexican professional football and boxing. Cultural institutions include the Museo Arocena and the Teatro Isauro Martínez.
