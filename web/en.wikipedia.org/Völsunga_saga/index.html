Völsunga saga

The Völsunga saga (Old Norse: Vǫlsunga saga) is a legendary saga written in Old Norse prose, probably in Iceland in the late 13th century. It tells the origin and decline of the Völsung clan, including the story of Sigurd the dragon-slayer, the cursed ring of Andvari, and the fall of the Burgundian kings — material closely paralleled in the heroic lays of the Poetic Edda and, in German form, in the Nibelungenlied.

Contents

The saga begins with Sigi, a son of Odin, and traces his descendants: Rerir, Völsung (whose ten children include the twins Sigmund and Signý), and Sigmund, who alone can draw the sword Gram from the tree Barnstokkr. After Sigmund's death his son Sigurd is fostered by Regin, reforges Gram, and kills the dragon Fáfnir, gaining wisdom by tasting the dragon's blood and understanding the speech of birds. Sigurd awakens the valkyrie Brynhildr (Sigrdrífa) but, bewitched by a potion of forgetfulness, marries Gudrun of the Gjúkungs instead. The deception leads to Sigurd's murder, Brynhildr's suicide on his funeral pyre, and eventually to Gudrun's marriage to Atli (Attila), who invites her brothers to his hall and kills them; Gudrun avenges them by killing Atli and their sons.

Manuscripts and influence

The principal manuscript, Ny kgl. Saml. 1824 b 4to, dates to about 1400. The saga inspired William Morris's verse rendering The Story of Sigurd the Volsung (1876) and was a major source for Richard Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen and for J. R. R. Tolkien's The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún.
