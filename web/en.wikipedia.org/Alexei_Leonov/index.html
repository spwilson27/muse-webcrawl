Alexei Arkhipovich Leonov (30 May 1934 – 11 October 2019) was a Soviet and Russian cosmonaut, Air Force major general, writer, and artist. On 18 March 1965, he became the first person to conduct a spacewalk, exiting the capsule during the Voskhod 2 mission for 12 minutes and 9 seconds. He was also selected to be the first Soviet person to land on the Moon, although the project was cancelled.

In July 1975, Leonov commanded the Soyuz capsule in the Apollo–Soyuz mission, which docked in space for two days with an American Apollo capsule.

Early life and education

Leonov was born in Listvyanka, West Siberian Krai, Russian SFSR, to Yevdokia and Arkhip. His grandfather had been exiled to Siberia for his role in the 1905 Russian Revolution. Leonov was the eighth of nine surviving children in the family. His father was arrested and declared an "enemy of the people" in 1936 during the Great Purge, though he was later released and vindicated.

Leonov graduated from secondary school in Kaliningrad in 1953 and was accepted into the Riga Academy of Arts, but chose instead to attend a Ukrainian aviation school in Kremenchug, taking his first solo flight in May 1955. He graduated from the Chuguyev Higher Air Force Pilots School in 1957 and became a fighter pilot.

Voskhod 2 spacewalk

Leonov was one of the 20 Soviet Air Forces pilots selected to be part of the first cosmonaut group in 1960. On 18 March 1965, at 8:34 UTC, Leonov exited the Voskhod 2 capsule through an inflatable airlock and spent 12 minutes and 9 seconds outside the spacecraft, becoming the first person to walk in space. During the spacewalk, his spacesuit ballooned in the vacuum of space, making it difficult for him to re-enter the airlock; he was forced to reduce the pressure in his suit by opening a valve, risking decompression sickness.

Apollo–Soyuz

In July 1975, Leonov commanded the Soviet half of the Apollo–Soyuz Test Project, the first joint space mission between the Soviet Union and the United States. His Soyuz 19 docked with an American Apollo capsule, and the crews conducted joint experiments and exchanged gifts. Leonov became known for the sketches and portraits he made of the American astronauts during the mission.

Later life and art

From 1976 to 1982, Leonov was the commander of the cosmonaut team and deputy director of the Yuri Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Center. He was also a talented artist, producing many works depicting spaceflight; his paintings were exhibited widely, and he co-authored several illustrated books. He retired in 1992 and later worked in private business. Leonov died in Moscow on 11 October 2019 and was buried at the Federal Military Memorial Cemetery.
