Trigonometry


The unit circle and the trigonometric functions

Trigonometry (from Greek trigōnon, "triangle", and metron, "measure") is a branch of mathematics that studies relationships between side lengths and angles of triangles. The field emerged in the Hellenistic world during the 3rd century BC from applications of geometry to astronomical studies.

Trigonometric functions

The principal functions are sine, cosine, and tangent, together with their reciprocals cosecant, secant, and cotangent. For an angle θ in a right triangle, the sine is the ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse, the cosine the ratio of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse, and the tangent the ratio of opposite to adjacent. These functions are extended to all real numbers using the unit circle.

Identities and laws

Key results include the Pythagorean identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, the law of sines, and the law of cosines, which generalize the Pythagorean theorem to any triangle.

Applications

Trigonometry is used in astronomy, navigation, surveying, engineering, physics, and computer graphics, and its functions describe periodic phenomena such as sound and light waves.
