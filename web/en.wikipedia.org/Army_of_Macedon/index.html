Army of Macedon

The Army of Macedon was the military force of the ancient Greek kingdom of Macedon. Reorganised by Philip II and wielded by his son Alexander the Great, it became one of the most effective armies of the ancient world, combining the sarissa phalanx with elite cavalry and flexible supporting arms.

Organisation under Philip II

Philip II created a professional, year-round army. Its core was the pezhetairoi, the "Foot Companions", heavy infantry armed with the sarissa, a pike some five to six metres long, arrayed in dense phalanx formations. The Companion Cavalry (hetairoi) provided the decisive shock arm, while hypaspists served as an elite infantry link between phalanx and cavalry. Light troops such as peltasts, archers, and slingers screened the army, and torsion artillery supported sieges.

Under Alexander the Great

Alexander used the army to conquer the Achaemenid Persian Empire between 334 and 323 BC, winning the battles of Granicus, Issus, and Gaugamela. His tactics typically fixed the enemy with the phalanx while he led the Companion Cavalry in a decisive charge, often in oblique order.

Hellenistic successors

After Alexander's death the army was divided among his successors, the Diadochi, whose kingdoms retained the phalanx-and-cavalry system. The last Macedonian field army was defeated by Rome at Pydna in 168 BC.
