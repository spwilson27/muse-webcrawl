Marineland of Florida, now the Marineland Dolphin Conservation Center, is an oceanarium in the town of Marineland, Florida, south of St. Augustine. Opened in 1938 as Marine Studios, it is considered the world's first oceanarium and a pioneer of dolphin display and research: bottlenose dolphins were first trained and bred in captivity there, and the facility was a major Florida roadside attraction for decades.

History

The founders—including Ilya Tolstoy, grandson of Leo Tolstoy—built two large tanks for filming and studying marine life. Marineland flourished in the post-war tourist boom, drawing 400,000–500,000 visitors a year, but declined with competition from newer attractions and changed ownership several times. The original structures were largely demolished in 2004 due to deterioration.

Modern facility

Reopened in 2006 in a smaller modern form, Marineland focuses on dolphin encounters, education and conservation, and was acquired by the Georgia Aquarium in 2011 and later by the Dolphin Company. Its bottlenose dolphin Nellie, who lived there from 1953 to 2014, was the longest-lived dolphin in human care at the time of her death.
