Geb (Ancient Egyptian: gbb) was the ancient Egyptian god of the Earth and a member of the Ennead of Heliopolis. He was the son of the air god Shu and the moisture goddess Tefnut, the brother and husband of the sky goddess Nut, and the father of Osiris, Isis, Set and Nephthys.

Mythology

Geb and Nut were said to have been born locked in an embrace, and were separated by their father Shu, who lifted Nut up to form the sky while Geb remained below as the earth — a scene frequently depicted in Egyptian art, with Geb reclining beneath the arched body of Nut. His laughter was believed to cause earthquakes. As god of the earth, Geb was associated with fertility, the harvest, and the plants and minerals of the ground; the dead were said to be imprisoned in his body.

Geb held an important place in the mythology of kingship. He was regarded as the divine ancestor and "father" of the pharaohs, whose throne was called the "throne of Geb", and in the Osiris myth he presided over the tribunal that awarded the kingship of Egypt in the contendings of Horus and Set. His sacred animal and hieroglyphic emblem was the goose, and he was sometimes called the "Great Cackler" whose egg brought forth the sun.

Iconography

Geb is usually depicted as a man reclining beneath Nut, sometimes with his skin coloured green to symbolise vegetation and rebirth, or wearing the crown of Lower Egypt or a goose upon his head.
