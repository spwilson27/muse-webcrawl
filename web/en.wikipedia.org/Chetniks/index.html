Chetniks

The Chetniks, formally the Chetnik Detachments of the Yugoslav Army and later the Yugoslav Army in the Homeland, were a Serb nationalist and royalist guerrilla movement in German-occupied Yugoslavia during World War II, led by General Draža Mihailović. The name derives from the Serbian word četa (company/troop) and had been used by earlier Serbian guerrilla bands in Ottoman Macedonia and the Balkan Wars.

World War II

After the Axis invasion of Yugoslavia in April 1941, Mihailović established his headquarters at Ravna Gora and organised resistance under the authority of the Yugoslav government-in-exile, which recognised him as commander and later Minister of War. The Chetniks initially cooperated with Tito's Partisans, but by late 1941 the two movements were fighting each other, and the Chetniks increasingly prioritised defeating the Partisans over attacking the Axis.

Collaboration and war crimes

Many Chetnik formations reached accommodations with Italian and later German occupation forces against the Partisans, and Chetnik units committed extensive massacres of Bosniak and Croat civilians as part of a programme of creating an ethnically homogeneous Greater Serbia. Allied support shifted to the Partisans in 1943–44 as evidence of collaboration mounted.

Aftermath

Mihailović was captured by the new communist authorities, tried for treason and war crimes, and executed in Belgrade in 1946. Chetnik veterans emigrated widely; the movement was suppressed in Yugoslavia, and its legacy remains contested, particularly since its partial rehabilitation in Serbia in the 2000s and the re-emergence of Chetnik symbolism during the Yugoslav wars of the 1990s.
