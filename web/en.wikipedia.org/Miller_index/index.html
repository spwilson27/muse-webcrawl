Miller index

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Miller indices form a notation system in crystallography for designating lattice planes and directions in crystals. Named after British mineralogist William Hallowes Miller, who introduced the system in 1839, the indices are a set of three integers (four for hexagonal crystals) derived from the intercepts a plane makes with the crystallographic axes.

Definition

To obtain the Miller indices of a plane: find its intercepts with the three axes in units of the lattice constants; take the reciprocals of these numbers; and reduce to the smallest integers with the same ratio. The result is written in parentheses, e.g. (100), (110), or (111). Negative intercepts are written with a bar over the number. A plane parallel to an axis intercepts it at infinity, giving an index of zero. Families of equivalent planes are enclosed in braces {hkl}, while directions are written in square brackets [hkl] and families of directions in angle brackets.

Use

Miller indices underlie the description of crystal structure by X-ray diffraction, the classification of cleavage and slip planes in metallurgy, and the specification of wafer orientations in the semiconductor industry, where (100) and (111) silicon surfaces are of special importance. For hexagonal and trigonal crystals a four-index system, Miller-Bravais indices (hkil), is used, with the redundant index i = −(h + k).
