Senate Intelligence Committee report on CIA torture

The Senate Intelligence Committee report on CIA torture is the commonly used name for the Committee Study of the Central Intelligence Agency's Detention and Interrogation Program, produced by the United States Senate Select Committee on Intelligence. A declassified 525-page executive summary was released on 9 December 2014; the full report runs to about 6,700 pages and remains classified.

Findings

The study, based on more than six million pages of CIA documents, concluded that the CIA's use of "enhanced interrogation techniques" — including waterboarding, sleep deprivation, stress positions, and confinement — was more brutal and extensive than the agency had represented to policymakers and the public; that the program was not an effective means of acquiring intelligence or gaining detainee cooperation; that the CIA repeatedly provided inaccurate information to the Department of Justice, Congress and the White House; and that interrogations of at least 39 detainees involved techniques, while at least 26 of the 119 known detainees were wrongfully held.

Reactions

Committee chair Dianne Feinstein described the program as a stain on American values. The CIA and several former directors disputed the study's conclusions, arguing the program produced useful intelligence, though the agency acknowledged serious mistakes. The release prompted renewed debate about torture, accountability and oversight of intelligence agencies.
