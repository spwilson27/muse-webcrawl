Vanillin

Vanillin is an organic compound with the molecular formula C8H8O3. It is a phenolic aldehyde whose functional groups include an aldehyde, a hydroxyl group and an ether (methoxy) group. It is the primary component of the extract of the vanilla bean and the principal flavour and aroma compound of vanilla.

History

Vanillin was first isolated in relatively pure form in 1858 by Nicolas-Théodore Gobley, who obtained it by evaporating a vanilla extract and recrystallising the residue. In 1874 Ferdinand Tiemann and Wilhelm Haarmann determined its chemical structure and synthesised it from coniferin; shortly afterwards vanillin was synthesised from eugenol, found in clove oil. Synthetic production made vanilla flavouring cheap and widely available.

Occurrence and production

Pure vanillin occurs naturally in cured vanilla pods at roughly 1–2% by weight. Because natural supply is small, most vanillin is manufactured synthetically. A large share has historically been produced from lignin waste in sulfite pulp processing; modern production largely uses the petrochemical precursor guaiacol, and "bio-based" vanillin can be made by microbial fermentation of ferulic acid derived from rice bran.

Uses

Vanillin is used extensively as a flavouring in foods (especially ice cream, chocolate and baked goods), beverages, pharmaceuticals and perfumes. It is also a chemical intermediate, used in the synthesis of drugs such as L-DOPA, and a staining agent in thin-layer chromatography.
