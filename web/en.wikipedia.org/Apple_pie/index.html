Apple pie

An apple pie is a pie in which the principal filling is apples, typically seasoned with sugar, cinnamon and sometimes nutmeg or lemon, enclosed in or topped with pastry. Apple pies may be double-crusted, lattice-topped, or open-faced (tarte tatin when caramelised and baked upside down). They are often served warm, with ice cream ("à la mode"), cream, or a slice of cheese in some regions.

History

Recipes for apple pies date to medieval England; a recipe from 1381 lists apples, spices, figs, raisins and pears baked in a pastry "coffin". Dutch and English settlers carried pie-making to North America, where varieties such as the crab apple and later orchard cultivars were plentiful. Johnny Appleseed's planting of orchards in the early 19th century helped spread apple cultivation across the frontier.

Cultural significance

Despite its English origins, apple pie became an emblem of the United States, giving rise to the phrase "as American as apple pie". In 1902 a newspaper editorial declared that no one frightened by pie-eating could be permanently defeated, and pie was famously requested by American soldiers during the Second World War when explaining what they were fighting for: "mom and apple pie".
