Sierra Madre Oriental

Sierra Madre Oriental
Location: Northeastern Mexico
Length: about 1,350 km (840 mi)
Highest point: Cerro el Potosí, 3,713 m (12,182 ft)
States: Coahuila, Nuevo León, Tamaulipas, San Luis Potosí, Hidalgo, Puebla, Querétaro, Veracruz

The Sierra Madre Oriental is a mountain range in northeastern Mexico, extending roughly 1,350 kilometres (840 mi) from the Rio Grande in the north, near the border with the U.S. state of Texas, southward to join the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt in Puebla and Hidalgo. It is one of three major mountain ranges — the others being the Sierra Madre Occidental and the Sierra Madre del Sur — that together frame the Mexican Plateau.

Geography

The range runs through the states of Coahuila, Nuevo León, Tamaulipas, San Luis Potosí, Querétaro, Hidalgo, Puebla and Veracruz. Its highest summit is Cerro el Potosí, at 3,713 metres (12,182 ft), near Galeana in Nuevo León. Other notable peaks include Cerro La Viga and El Coahuilón. The city of Monterrey lies at the eastern foot of the range, where the distinctive Cerro de la Silla dominates the skyline.

Geology

The Sierra Madre Oriental consists largely of folded and faulted sedimentary rocks — limestone, shale and sandstone — of Mesozoic age, uplifted during the Laramide orogeny in the Late Cretaceous and early Tertiary. The folds form long parallel ridges and valleys, and the range's eastern escarpment rises abruptly from the Gulf Coastal Plain.

Ecology

The range is biologically rich. Its northern parts support pine–oak forests of the Sierra Madre Oriental pine–oak forests ecoregion, home to species such as the maroon-fronted parrot. Cloud forests cloak some of the wetter eastern slopes, while the rain-shadowed western slopes and interior valleys are covered by the semi-arid Meseta Central matorral. Because the range runs north–south, it serves as a corridor between Nearctic and Neotropical biotas, and it hosts high levels of endemism.

Protected areas

Protected areas in the range include the El Cielo Biosphere Reserve in Tamaulipas, Cumbres de Monterrey National Park in Nuevo León, and the Sierra Gorda Biosphere Reserve in Querétaro.
