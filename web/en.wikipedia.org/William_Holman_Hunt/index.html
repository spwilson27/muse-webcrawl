William Holman Hunt

William Holman Hunt (2 April 1827 – 7 September 1910) was an English painter and one of the founders of the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood in 1848. His intensely detailed, brightly coloured works combined meticulous natural observation with elaborate moral and religious symbolism.

Pre-Raphaelitism

Hunt founded the Brotherhood with John Everett Millais and Dante Gabriel Rossetti, reacting against academic idealisation in favour of brilliant colour, sharp detail and "truth to nature". His early successes included The Hireling Shepherd (1851) and The Awakening Conscience (1853), contemporary moral subjects rendered with symbolic precision. The Light of the World (1851–54), showing Christ knocking at an overgrown door, became one of the most famous religious images of the Victorian age; a larger version toured the world and hangs in St Paul's Cathedral.

The Holy Land

Obsessed with biblical accuracy, Hunt travelled to Egypt and Palestine in 1854 and painted biblical scenes on the spot, including The Scapegoat, painted by the Dead Sea, and The Finding of the Saviour in the Temple. He remained devoted to Pre-Raphaelite principles throughout his long career, publishing his memoirs, Pre-Raphaelitism and the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood, in 1905.
