Single-elimination tournament

A single-elimination tournament, also called a knockout tournament, is a type of elimination tournament where the loser of each match-up is immediately eliminated. The winner of each match advances to the next round, until the final match-up, whose winner becomes the tournament champion.

Format

For n entrants, a single-elimination tournament requires n − 1 matches and ⌈log₂ n⌉ rounds when the field is a power of two. When the entry count is not a power of two, byes are awarded in the first round, typically to the highest seeds. Seeding arranges entrants so that the strongest competitors are not scheduled to meet until the late rounds.

Examples

The knockout stages of the FIFA World Cup, the NCAA basketball tournament ("March Madness"), and the draws of Grand Slam tennis tournaments all use single-elimination formats. Variations include consolation brackets, third-place playoffs, and re-entry formats in which a competitor may buy back into the field.

Criticism

The format is efficient and dramatic, but a single upset or bad performance eliminates a strong entrant, and the runner-up may not be the second-best competitor since the eventual champion's half of the draw may have been weaker. Double-elimination and Swiss-system formats address some of these concerns at the cost of more matches.
