Changthangi

The Changthangi or Changpa goat is a breed of cashmere goat native to the high-altitude Changthang plateau of Ladakh in northern India, extending into western Tibet. It is reared by the nomadic Changpa herders for its exceptionally fine undercoat, pashmina, used to make Kashmiri shawls.

Characteristics

Changthangi goats are sturdy, medium-sized animals, usually white but also black or pied, with large twisted horns. They are adapted to altitudes above 4,000 metres, where temperatures can fall below −20 °C. To survive, they grow a thick double coat; the soft downy undercoat is combed out in spring and yields some of the finest cashmere fibre in the world, typically 12–16 microns in diameter.

Economy

Pashmina production is the principal livelihood of Changpa nomads. The fibre is sold largely through traders in Leh and processed in Kashmir. The breed population has faced pressure from climate change, pasture degradation and the declining viability of nomadic herding.
