Multiethnic society


Related concepts: Multiculturalism, plural society, melting pot
Contrasted with: Ethnically homogeneous society

A multiethnic society is a society composed of people belonging to two or more distinct ethnic groups who live within a shared political and social framework. Such societies have existed throughout history — in empires, trading cities, and settler states — and are increasingly common worldwide through migration.

Models of integration

Scholars describe several approaches by which multiethnic societies manage diversity: assimilation, in which minority groups adopt the dominant culture; the "melting pot", in which groups blend into a new common culture; and multiculturalism or cultural pluralism, in which distinct identities are retained and publicly recognized, as in official Canadian and Australian policy. J. S. Furnivall's concept of the "plural society", developed in colonial Southeast Asia, described groups that mix economically but remain culturally separate.

Challenges and debates

Multiethnic societies debate language policy, religious accommodation, representation, and discrimination. Inter-ethnic relations can produce tension or conflict, particularly where groups compete for resources or political power, but also generate innovation, trade, and cultural exchange. Policies such as consociationalism in divided societies attempt to share power among groups.

Examples

Historically multiethnic states include the Roman, Ottoman, and Habsburg empires; modern examples include India, Indonesia, Nigeria, Canada, and the United States.
