Caulking

MaterialsSilicone, latex/acrylic, polyurethane, butyl sealants
ToolsCaulking gun; smoothing tool
UsesSealing joints in buildings, boats, plumbing

Caulk or caulking is a material used to seal joints or seams against leakage in various structures and piping. The term also denotes the process of applying the material. The oldest known form of caulk consisted of fibrous materials driven into the wedge-shaped seams between boards on wooden boats and ships; cast iron sewerage pipe joints were traditionally sealed in a related manner. In modern construction, caulk is a flexible sealing material applied from a cartridge with a caulking gun.

Boatbuilding origins

In traditional wooden shipbuilding, seams between hull planks were caulked by driving in strands of cotton or oakum (tarred hemp) with a caulking mallet and iron, then paying the seam with hot pitch or putty to make the hull watertight. Riveted steel ships were likewise caulked by deforming the metal edges of plates.

Modern caulks

  • Silicone caulk is waterproof, flexible and durable, widely used around baths, showers, and windows, but it cannot be painted and requires solvents to remove.
  • Acrylic latex ("painter's caulk") is water-cleanup and paintable, suited to interior trim and small gaps.
  • Polyurethane and hybrid sealants adhere strongly to masonry and concrete and tolerate movement in exterior joints.
  • Butyl rubber caulk is used for roofing and gutter seams.

Application

Caulk is typically dispensed from a tube through a caulking gun, then tooled smooth with a wet finger or tool before it skins over. Proper surface preparation — removing old sealant and ensuring a dry, clean joint, sometimes with backer rod for deep gaps — determines how long the seal lasts. Failed caulk is a common cause of water damage in bathrooms and building envelopes, so joints are periodically re-caulked.
