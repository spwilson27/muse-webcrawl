Roderic

Roderic
Reign: 710–711
Predecessor: Wittiza
House: Visigothic Kingdom of Toledo
Died: 711

Roderic (Spanish: Rodrigo; died 711) was the Visigothic King of Hispania from 710 to 711. He is known as "the last of the Goths". He occupies a distinguished place in Spanish history and in Spanish literature as the king who lost Spain to the Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula.

Accession

Roderic was the dux (duke) of Baetica before seizing the throne. Following the death of King Wittiza, the Visigothic nobility split; Roderic took Toledo by force while a rival king, Achila II, ruled in the northeast. Coins minted in Roderic's name are known from Toledo and Mérida.

Muslim invasion and death

In 711, the Berber commander Tariq ibn Ziyad crossed from North Africa at the invitation, according to later legend, of Count Julian of Ceuta. Roderic marched south from a campaign against the Basques to confront the invaders. In July 711, the two armies met at the Battle of Guadalete, where the Visigothic army was destroyed and Roderic was killed—his body was never reliably identified. Within a few years, nearly all of Hispania was under Muslim rule.

Legend

Roderic became the centre of many legends, including the story of La Cava, the daughter of Count Julian, whose dishonour was said to have provoked the invasion, and the tale that he survived the battle and lived as a hermit, or that his tomb was discovered at Viseu in Portugal.
