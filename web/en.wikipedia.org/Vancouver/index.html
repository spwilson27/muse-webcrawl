Vancouver is a major city in western Canada, located in the Lower Mainland region of British Columbia. It is the most populous city in the province and the third-largest metropolitan area in Canada. The city is named for Captain George Vancouver, who explored and charted the area in 1792.

Downtown Vancouver from across False Creek.

Geography

Vancouver sits beside Burrard Inlet and the Strait of Georgia, ringed by the snow-capped North Shore Mountains. Its climate is an oceanic one, among the mildest in Canada, though the city receives abundant rain from October to March. Stanley Park, a 405-hectare evergreen park, borders the downtown peninsula.

Economy

The Port of Vancouver is the largest in Canada and the third largest in North America. Vancouver is also a major centre for software development, biotechnology, and film production — nicknamed "Hollywood North". Tourism, forestry products, and international trade with Asia-Pacific nations round out the economy.

Demographics and culture

Vancouver is ethnically diverse: a large proportion of residents are of Chinese, South Asian, and Filipino heritage, and over half of residents speak a first language other than English. The city hosted the 2010 Winter Olympics. Vancouver frequently ranks among the world's most livable cities, though housing affordability is among the worst in North America.
