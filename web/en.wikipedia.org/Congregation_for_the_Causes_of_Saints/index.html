Congregation for the Causes of Saints

The Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, previously named the Congregation for the Causes of Saints (Latin: Congregatio de Causis Sanctorum), is the dicastery of the Roman Curia that oversees the complex process that leads to the canonization of saints, passing through the steps of a declaration of "heroic virtues" and beatification. After preparing a case, including the approval of miracles, the case is presented to the Pope, who decides whether or not to proceed toward canonization.

History

The antecedent of the congregation was the Congregation of Sacred Rites, founded by Pope Sixtus V in 1588, which handled both liturgy and saints' causes. In 1969 Pope Paul VI divided it, creating a separate Congregation for the Causes of Saints. Under the apostolic constitution Praedicate evangelium (2022) of Pope Francis, the body became a dicastery and took its present name.

Process

The dicastery supervises diocesan inquiries into the life and virtues of a Servant of God, examines claims of miracles (often medical healings reviewed by a board of physicians), and prepares the positio summarizing the evidence. Its officials include the prefect, secretary, and a promoter of the faith, a role historically associated with the expression "devil's advocate".
