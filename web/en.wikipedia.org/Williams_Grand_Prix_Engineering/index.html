Williams Grand Prix Engineering Limited, commonly known as Williams Racing, is a British Formula One team and constructor. It was founded by Sir Frank Williams and engineer Sir Patrick Head. The team was formed in 1977 after Frank Williams's two unsuccessful earlier attempts with Frank Williams Racing Cars and Wolf–Williams Racing. All of Williams's Formula One chassis since 1978 have been named "FW" followed by a number, in honour of team founder Frank Williams.

History

The team's first win came at the 1979 British Grand Prix with Clay Regazzoni, and its first championships in 1980, when Alan Jones won the drivers' title and Williams the constructors'. Keke Rosberg (1982), Nelson Piquet (1987), Nigel Mansell (1992), Alain Prost (1993), Damon Hill (1996) and Jacques Villeneuve (1997) added drivers' championships, making Williams one of the most successful teams in the sport, with nine constructors' titles, second only to Ferrari at the time of its last title. The dominant FW14B of 1992, designed under Adrian Newey with active suspension and traction control, is regarded as one of the most technologically advanced Formula One cars ever built.

The team was scarred by the death of Ayrton Senna in the Williams FW16 at the 1994 San Marino Grand Prix. Engine partnerships included Ford-Cosworth, Honda, Judd, Renault, Mecachrome/Supertec, BMW (2000–2005), Toyota, Cosworth again and Renault again, and since 2014 Mercedes power units. After two decades of declining results, the Williams family sold the team to the American investment firm Dorilton Capital in August 2020; Frank Williams died in November 2021. Under team principal James Vowles, appointed in 2023, the team has sought to rebuild, signing Carlos Sainz Jr. to partner Alexander Albon from 2025.

Williams Advanced Engineering

The group's technology arm, Williams Advanced Engineering (WAE), commercialised Formula One know-how — including flywheel energy storage, aerodynamics and battery systems for the Formula E and Extreme E racing series and the Jaguar C-X75 hybrid concept — before being sold to Fortescue in 2022. The Williams Heritage division maintains the team's collection of historic cars at Grove, where the factory also hosts the Williams Experience Centre and one of the largest Grand Prix car collections open by appointment.
