Anglo-Egyptian conquest of Sudan

The Anglo-Egyptian conquest of Sudan (1896–1899) was the reconquest of territory lost by the Khedivate of Egypt to the Mahdist State during the Mahdist War. An Egyptian army commanded by British officers, led by Sirdar Herbert Kitchener, advanced methodically from Egypt down the Nile, defeating the Mahdist forces in a series of battles and re-establishing joint Anglo-Egyptian rule.

Background

The Mahdiyya, proclaimed by Muhammad Ahmad in 1881, had destroyed Egyptian authority in Sudan; Khartoum fell in January 1885 and General Gordon was killed. Britain, occupying Egypt since 1882, declined to reconquer Sudan until strategic concerns — Italian defeat at Adwa in 1896, French ambitions on the Upper Nile, and the security of Egypt — prompted action.

The Dongola campaign, 1896

Kitchener advanced into Dongola province in 1896, building a military railway across the Nubian desert to sustain his army. Victories at Firket (7 June 1896) and Hafir secured Dongola by September.

Advance to Khartoum, 1897–1898

In 1897 the army took Abu Hamed and Berber. Advancing on the Atbara in April 1898, Kitchener defeated Mahmud Ahmad's zariba. On 2 September 1898, at the Battle of Omdurman outside Khartoum, his army of about 25,000, supported by gunboats and Maxim guns, destroyed the Khalifa Abdallahi's army of some 50,000 at a cost of fewer than 50 dead. Khartoum was reoccupied, and a memorial service was held for Gordon.

Aftermath

The Fashoda Incident with France followed within weeks, resolved diplomatically in Britain's favour. The Khalifa was hunted down and killed at Umm Diwaykarat in November 1899. The condominium agreement of January 1899 created Anglo-Egyptian Sudan.
