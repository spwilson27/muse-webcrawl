Williamina Paton Stevens Fleming (15 May 1857 – 21 May 1911) was a Scottish astronomer. At the Harvard College Observatory she helped develop a common system of classifying stars and catalogued thousands of stars and other astronomical phenomena, including the discovery of the Horsehead Nebula.

Career

Fleming emigrated to Boston in 1878 and, abandoned by her husband while pregnant, took work as a maid in the home of Edward Charles Pickering, director of Harvard College Observatory. Pickering hired her for clerical work at the observatory in 1881, making her one of the first of the "Harvard Computers", the women who measured and catalogued photographic plates. She quickly rose to supervise the team.

Scientific work

Fleming devised the Pickering–Fleming system of stellar spectral classification, assigning letters to spectral types, and applied it to the more than 10,000 stars of the Draper Catalogue of Stellar Spectra (1890). Over her career she discovered 59 gaseous nebulae, over 310 variable stars and 10 novae. In 1888 she identified, on a photographic plate, the dark nebula in Orion later famous as the Horsehead Nebula. In 1899 she became Harvard's first Curator of Astronomical Photographs, and in 1906 she was the first American woman elected an honorary member of the Royal Astronomical Society.
