Laryngeal mask airway

A laryngeal mask airway (LMA), also known as a laryngeal mask, is a supraglottic airway device developed by British anaesthetist Archie Brain in the early 1980s. It consists of an airway tube connected to an elliptical mask with an inflatable cuff that sits over the larynx, allowing ventilation without endotracheal intubation.

Use

LMAs are widely used in anaesthesia and emergency medicine for maintaining an open airway during surgery and in pre-hospital care. They are easier and faster to insert than endotracheal tubes but do not fully protect against aspiration.

Development

Since the original LMA Classic, second-generation devices with gastric drain tubes and improved seals, such as the LMA ProSeal and Supreme, have been introduced.
