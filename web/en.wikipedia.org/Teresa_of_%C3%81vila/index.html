Teresa of Ávila, also called Saint Teresa of Jesus (born Teresa Sánchez de Cepeda y Ahumada; 28 March 1515 – 4 October 1582), was a Spanish noblewoman who became a Carmelite nun, a prominent Spanish mystic, and a religious reformer. She founded the Discalced Carmelites together with Saint John of the Cross.

Life and work

Teresa entered the Carmelite convent in Ávila at age 20. After years of illness and intense prayer, she experienced mystical visions and raptures. Her writings, including her autobiography The Life of Teresa of Jesus, The Interior Castle, and The Way of Perfection, are classics of Christian mysticism.

Legacy

Canonized in 1622, Teresa was proclaimed a Doctor of the Church by Pope Paul VI in 1970, the first woman to receive that title jointly with Catherine of Siena. Bernini's sculpture The Ecstasy of Saint Teresa in Rome is a famous depiction of her mystical experience.
