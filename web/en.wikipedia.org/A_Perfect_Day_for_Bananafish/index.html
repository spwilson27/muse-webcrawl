A Perfect Day for Bananafish

"A Perfect Day for Bananafish"
Author: J. D. Salinger
Published: The New Yorker, January 31, 1948
Collection: Nine Stories (1953)
Series: Glass family

"A Perfect Day for Bananafish" is a short story by J. D. Salinger, originally published in the January 31, 1948 issue of The New Yorker. It was later included as the opening story of Salinger's 1953 collection Nine Stories. The story introduces Seymour Glass, the eldest of Salinger's fictional Glass family, and is among the most anthologized American short stories of the twentieth century.

Plot summary

At a Florida beach resort, Muriel Glass speaks by telephone with her mother, who is anxious about Muriel's husband Seymour, a World War II veteran recently released from an Army hospital and behaving erratically. Meanwhile, on the beach, Seymour befriends a young girl named Sybil Carpenter and, while floating with her in the sea, tells her about the fictional "bananafish," which swim into banana holes, gorge themselves, become too fat to escape, and die of banana fever. After returning to the hotel, Seymour has a strange confrontation with a woman in the elevator. He then goes to his room, takes out a pistol, and shoots himself.

Themes and significance

The story is noted for its withheld exposition: Salinger never explains Seymour's trauma directly, leaving readers to infer his alienation from the materialistic adult world represented by Muriel and her mother, and his affinity with children. Seymour became a central figure of the Glass saga, explored further in "Raise High the Roof Beam, Carpenters," "Seymour: An Introduction," and Franny and Zooey.
