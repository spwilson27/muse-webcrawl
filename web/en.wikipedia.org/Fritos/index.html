Fritos

Fritos is an American brand of corn chips introduced in 1932 by Charles Elmer Doolin, founder of the Frito Company, which later merged with H. W. Lay & Company in 1961 to form Frito-Lay, now a division of PepsiCo. Fritos are made from cornmeal extruded and deep-fried in oil, and are thicker and crunchier than tortilla chips.

History

Doolin, a San Antonio confectionery manager, bought a corn-chip recipe, a potato ricer and a retail store's customer list from Gustavo Olguin for $100 in 1932, and began producing the chips in his mother's kitchen. The Frito Company grew steadily, and Doolin is also credited with popularising the dish now known as the Frito pie — corn chips topped with chili and cheese — served at fairs, diners and (traditionally) straight from the bag.

Varieties

Besides the Original flavour, Fritos are sold in varieties including Scoops! (shaped for dipping), Chili Cheese, Flamin' Hot, and Honey BBQ twists. The chips are a standard ingredient in "walking tacos" and Frito pie, and are used as a topping for chili and casseroles.
