TET enzymes

The TET enzymes are a family of three proteins — TET1, TET2, and TET3 — that catalyse the oxidation of 5-methylcytosine in DNA, initiating active DNA demethylation. They are named for the "ten-eleven translocation" first identified in leukaemia.

Function

TET enzymes convert 5-methylcytosine (5mC) to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC) and further to 5-formylcytosine and 5-carboxylcytosine, which can be excised by thymine DNA glycosylase and replaced with unmodified cytosine.

Biological roles

They are important in embryonic development, stem-cell pluripotency, and neuronal function. Loss-of-function mutations in TET2 are common in myeloid malignancies and clonal haematopoiesis.

Regulation

TET activity requires iron(II), oxygen, and alpha-ketoglutarate, linking epigenetic regulation to cellular metabolism; vitamin C is known to enhance their activity.
