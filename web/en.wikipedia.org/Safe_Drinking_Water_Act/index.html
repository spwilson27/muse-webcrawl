Safe Drinking Water Act
Enacted16 December 1974
Administered byU.S. Environmental Protection Agency
Codified42 U.S.C. §§ 300f et seq.

The Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA) is the principal United States federal law ensuring the quality of Americans' drinking water. Passed in 1974 and substantially amended in 1986 and 1996, it authorises the Environmental Protection Agency to set national health-based standards for contaminants in public water systems.

Provisions

Under the act the EPA sets maximum contaminant levels (or treatment techniques) for more than 90 contaminants, and states usually hold primary enforcement responsibility ("primacy"). The law also protects underground sources of drinking water through wellhead protection and sole-source aquifer programmes, regulates underground injection wells, and created a Drinking Water State Revolving Fund to finance infrastructure. It does not generally regulate private wells or bottled water (the latter falls to the FDA).
