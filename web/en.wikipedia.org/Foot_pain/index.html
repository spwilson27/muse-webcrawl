Foot pain

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Foot pain is pain felt in the foot, which may arise from the bones, joints, muscles, tendons, ligaments, nerves, blood vessels, or skin of the foot. It is a very common complaint, affecting roughly a quarter of adults at any given time, and increases in prevalence with age, obesity, and conditions such as diabetes and arthritis.

Causes

Common causes by region of the foot include:

  • Heel: plantar fasciitis (the most common cause of heel pain), Achilles tendinitis, heel spurs, calcaneal stress fractures, and Sever's disease in adolescents.
  • Forefoot: metatarsalgia, Morton's neuroma, sesamoiditis, bunions (hallux valgus), hammer toes, and stress fractures of the metatarsals.
  • Midfoot and arch: flat feet (pes planus), posterior tibial tendon dysfunction, midfoot arthritis, and Lisfranc injuries.
  • General: osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, gout, corns and calluses, ingrown toenails, warts, peripheral neuropathy, and peripheral artery disease.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis relies on history (location, onset, aggravating factors), physical examination, gait assessment, and, where needed, imaging such as X-rays, ultrasound, or MRI. Nerve conduction studies may be used when neuropathy or tarsal tunnel syndrome is suspected.

Management

Most foot pain responds to conservative treatment: rest and activity modification, ice, analgesics and anti-inflammatory drugs, footwear changes, orthotic insoles, stretching and strengthening exercises, and physiotherapy. Specific conditions may require corticosteroid injections, night splints, or surgery — for example bunion correction or release of the plantar fascia. Foot pain with numbness, ulceration, or poor circulation in people with diabetes requires prompt medical attention.
