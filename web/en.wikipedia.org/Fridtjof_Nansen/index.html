Fridtjof Nansen

Fridtjof Wedel-Jarlsberg Nansen (10 October 1861 – 13 May 1930) was a Norwegian polymath: explorer, scientist, diplomat and humanitarian. He led the first crossing of the interior of Greenland in 1888 and, aboard the polar ship Fram in 1893–1896, drifted across the Arctic Ocean and reached a record northern latitude of 86°14′N. After the First World War he served as the League of Nations' High Commissioner for Refugees, creating the Nansen passport for stateless refugees, and received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1922.

Exploration

A champion skier and zoologist by training, Nansen crossed Greenland's ice cap from east to west in 1888, proving the interior was continuous ice. For his Arctic venture he had Fram built to withstand ice pressure and deliberately froze her into the Siberian ice in 1893; during the three-year drift he and Hjalmar Johansen made a dash for the Pole by dog sledge and kayak, wintering in a stone hut on Franz Josef Land before being rescued in 1896. The expedition revolutionized oceanography and confirmed a deep Arctic Ocean crossed by a transpolar current.

Diplomat and humanitarian

Nansen helped secure Norway's peaceful independence from Sweden in 1905 and served as its first minister to London. From 1920 he organized the repatriation of prisoners of war, famine relief in Soviet Russia (1921–22), and refugee resettlement, including the exchange of Greek and Turkish populations. The Nansen International Office for Refugees continued his work after his death.

Legacy

Nansen's name is borne by the Nansen Refugee Award, lunar and Martian craters, and mountains in Antarctica and the Yukon.
