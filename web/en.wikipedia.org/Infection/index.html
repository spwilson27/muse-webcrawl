Infection

An infection is the invasion of tissues by pathogens, their multiplication, and the reaction of host tissues to the infectious agents and the toxins they produce. An infectious disease, also known as a transmissible or communicable disease, is an illness resulting from an infection. Infections can be caused by bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, and prions.

Pathogens such as bacteria and viruses invade host tissues, multiply, and provoke an immune response.

Causes and transmission

Infectious agents are transmitted in many ways: directly from person to person (through contact, droplets, or bodily fluids), indirectly through contaminated objects or water, through the air over longer distances, and via vectors such as mosquitoes and ticks. The chain of infection requires a pathogen, a reservoir, a portal of exit, a means of transmission, a portal of entry, and a susceptible host; breaking any link can prevent spread.

Course of infection

After entry, an infection typically passes through an incubation period, a prodromal phase of nonspecific symptoms, a period of illness, and convalescence. Some infections are asymptomatic or subclinical, while others are chronic, latent (with periods of dormancy, as in herpesviruses and tuberculosis), or rapidly fatal. The host's immune system responds with innate defences such as inflammation and fever, followed by adaptive immunity involving antibodies and T cells.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on clinical signs, culture of the organism, microscopy, antigen and antibody tests, and molecular methods such as PCR. Treatment depends on the cause: antibiotics for bacteria, antivirals for some viruses, antifungals and antiparasitic drugs for their respective organisms. Prion diseases have no effective treatment. Prevention includes vaccination, sanitation, hand hygiene, safe food and water, vector control, and infection-control practices in healthcare. The rise of antimicrobial resistance is a major global health concern.

Burden

Infectious diseases remain among the leading causes of death worldwide, particularly in low-income countries, with respiratory infections, diarrhoeal diseases, HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis and malaria among the largest contributors.
