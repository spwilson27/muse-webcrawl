Colonial America

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Colonial America refers to the period from the first successful European settlements in the early seventeenth century until the independence of the United States in 1776 (with the broader era of European colonisation of the Americas beginning in 1492). Spain, France, the Netherlands, and above all Great Britain established colonies along the Atlantic seaboard of what is now the United States, transforming the land and the lives of its Indigenous peoples.

Early settlement

England's first permanent settlement was Jamestown, Virginia, founded in 1607 by the Virginia Company. Plymouth Colony followed in 1620, settled by the Pilgrims aboard the Mayflower, and the Massachusetts Bay Colony in 1630. The Dutch founded New Netherland (including New Amsterdam, later New York), which England seized in 1664; France controlled Canada and Louisiana; Spain held Florida and the Southwest.

The Thirteen Colonies

By the early eighteenth century the British colonies had coalesced into thirteen, grouped as New England, the Middle Colonies, and the Southern Colonies. Southern economies rested on plantation agriculture — tobacco, rice, indigo, and later cotton — worked increasingly by enslaved Africans brought through the transatlantic slave trade. Northern colonies developed mixed farming, fishing, shipbuilding, and commerce. Colonial society included indentured servants, free artisans and merchants, and large numbers of enslaved people, especially in the South.

Relations with Native Americans

European settlement brought catastrophic disease epidemics to Indigenous populations and repeated conflicts over land, including King Philip's War (1675–1676) in New England and the French and Indian War (1754–1763), whose outcome expelled France from Canada but left Britain with heavy debts.

Road to revolution

To pay war debts and administer an enlarged empire, Parliament imposed new taxes and regulations — the Stamp Act (1765), the Townshend Acts, and the Tea Act (1773) — provoking colonial resistance under the slogan "no taxation without representation". Escalating conflict led to the outbreak of the American Revolutionary War in 1775 and the Declaration of Independence on 4 July 1776.
