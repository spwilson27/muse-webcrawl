Sinbad the Sailor

Sinbad the Sailor (Arabic: سندباد البحري, Sindibād al-Baḥrī) is a fictional mariner and the hero of a story cycle of Middle Eastern origin, incorporated into the One Thousand and One Nights. Set in Baghdad during the Abbasid Caliphate, the tales recount Sinbad's seven fantastic voyages across the seas east of Africa and south of Asia, during which he encounters magical realms, terrifying monsters and supernatural phenomena, each time returning home richer than before.

Frame story

The cycle is framed as the narration of the wealthy Sinbad the Sailor to a poor porter, also named Sinbad (Sinbad the Porter), who has complained of the injustice of fortune outside the rich man's gate. Invited in and feasted, the porter hears how the sailor's wealth was earned through peril rather than granted by luck, one voyage per evening over seven evenings.

The seven voyages

On his voyages Sinbad mistakes a sleeping whale for an island; is carried off by the giant bird called the Roc and escapes a valley of diamonds guarded by serpents; is captured by the cannibalistic Old Man of the Sea, whom he intoxicates and kills; is shipwrecked among man-eating apes and a gigantic serpent; is buried alive with his dead wife according to local custom and escapes through a charnel cave; sails a raft down an underground river into a kingdom of pearl fisheries; and on his final voyage is enslaved by elephant hunters, whose mercy toward the elephants earns him freedom and ivory. After the seventh voyage he retires for good in Baghdad.

Origins and influence

The tales draw on Persian and Arabic sailors' lore, Indian story collections and echoes of Greek literature, including parallels with the Odyssey. They reflect the real maritime trade of the Abbasid era with India, Sri Lanka, Indonesia and China. Sinbad became one of the best-known figures of the Nights in the West, inspiring pantomimes, novels, animated films and Ray Harryhausen's celebrated film trilogy The 7th Voyage of Sinbad (1958), The Golden Voyage of Sinbad (1973) and Sinbad and the Eye of the Tiger (1977).
