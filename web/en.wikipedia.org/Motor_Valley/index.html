Motor Valley (Italian: Terra dei Motori, "Land of Motors") is an industrial and cultural district in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, renowned worldwide for its concentration of luxury sports car and racing motorcycle manufacturers, racing circuits, museums, and specialist engineering suppliers. The district stretches along the Via Emilia between Modena and Bologna.

Manufacturers

The area is home to some of the most famous names in automotive history: Ferrari in Maranello, Lamborghini in Sant'Agata Bolognese, Maserati in Modena, Ducati motorcycles in Borgo Panigale (Bologna), and Pagani in San Cesario sul Panaro. Dallara, the world's leading builder of racing car chassis, is based at Varano de' Melegari. The region also hosts a dense network of coachbuilders, component makers, and engineering firms, together with the racing team facilities of the former Formula One team Minardi at Faenza (now Racing Bulls).

Circuits and museums

Motor Valley includes the Imola circuit (Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari), the Misano World Circuit Marco Simoncelli, and the Modena autodrome. Factory museums — the Museo Ferrari in Maranello, the Museo Enzo Ferrari in Modena, the Lamborghini and Ducati museums — draw hundreds of thousands of visitors each year, and the annual Motor Valley Fest in Modena celebrates the district's heritage.

Economy

The automotive cluster employs tens of thousands of people in Emilia-Romagna and generates a significant share of Italian automotive exports. The MUNER (Motorvehicle University of Emilia-Romagna), a joint initiative of the region's universities and manufacturers, trains engineers specifically for the high-performance vehicle industry.
