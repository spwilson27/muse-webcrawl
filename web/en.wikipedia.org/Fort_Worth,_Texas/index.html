Fort Worth, Texas

Fort Worth is a city in the U.S. state of Texas and the seat of Tarrant County. It is the fifth-most populous city in Texas and one of the largest cities in the United States, anchoring the western half of the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex. Known for its Western heritage, the city styles itself "the City of Cowboys and Culture."

History

Fort Worth began in 1849 as Camp Worth, one of a line of U.S. Army forts established after the Mexican–American War to protect settlers, named for General William Jenkins Worth. After the army departed in 1853, the settlement grew into a trading post and, after the Civil War, a major stop on the Chisholm Trail cattle drives, earning the nickname "Cowtown." The arrival of railroads in the 1870s made it a major cattle-shipping and meatpacking centre; the historic Stockyards district preserves this heritage.

Economy and culture

Modern Fort Worth has major employers in defence and aviation — including Lockheed Martin's Aeronautics division and Bell Textron — as well as railroads, energy and logistics. Cultural institutions include the Kimbell Art Museum, the Modern Art Museum of Fort Worth, the Amon Carter Museum of American Art, and the Fort Worth Zoo.
