The Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW) is an intergovernmental organisation and the implementing body for the Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC), which entered into force on 29 April 1997. The OPCW, with its 193 member states, has its seat in The Hague, Netherlands, and oversees the global endeavour to permanently eliminate chemical weapons.

Mandate and activities

The organisation verifies the destruction of declared chemical weapons stockpiles and production facilities, inspects chemical industry facilities to ensure dual-use chemicals are not diverted to weapons, and can conduct challenge inspections and fact-finding missions into alleged use. By July 2023, all declared chemical weapons stockpiles of member states had been verifiably destroyed, totalling over 72,000 metric tonnes.

Structure

The OPCW's organs are the Conference of the States Parties, the Executive Council (41 members elected for two-year terms) and the Technical Secretariat, headed by the Director-General. Its official languages are Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian and Spanish.

Nobel Peace Prize

In 2013 the OPCW was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize "for its extensive efforts to eliminate chemical weapons". The award coincided with its work overseeing the destruction of Syria's declared chemical weapons following the Ghouta attack.
