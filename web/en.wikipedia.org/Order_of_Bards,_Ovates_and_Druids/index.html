The Order of Bards, Ovates and Druids (OBOD) is a British-based international Neopagan order dedicated to practising, teaching, and developing modern Druidry as a spiritual path. It is one of the largest druid organisations in the world, with members in dozens of countries.

History

The Order was founded in 1964 by the poet and artist Ross Nichols, a former member of the Ancient Druid Order, following a schism in that organisation. Nichols worked closely with Gerald Gardner, the founder of modern Wicca, and the two movements influenced one another. After Nichols's death in 1975, Philip Carr-Gomm was chosen to lead the Order in 1988 and developed its distance-learning course. In 2020 Eimear Burke became Chosen Chief.

Training and grades

Members progress through three grades—Bard, Ovate, and Druid—traditionally associated in Welsh and Irish sources with poets and storytellers, seers and healers, and priests or teachers. The Order's training is delivered largely through a correspondence course, and local groups known as groves and seed groups organise ceremonies, commonly marking the eight festivals of the Wheel of the Year, including the solstices and equinoxes.
