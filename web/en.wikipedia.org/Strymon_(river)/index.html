Strymon (river)

Strymon / Struma
Countries: Bulgaria, Greece
Length: 415 km
Mouth: Aegean Sea (Strymonian Gulf)

The Strymon (Greek: Στρυμών) — known as the Struma in Bulgaria — is a river in southwestern Bulgaria and northeastern Greece, flowing about 415 km from the Vitosha mountains south of Sofia to the Aegean Sea near Amphipolis.

Course and history

It passes through Pernik and Blagoevgrad provinces, Lake Kerkini in Greece, and the Roupel Gorge. In antiquity the Strymon valley was a contested frontier between Macedon and Thrace; the Athenian colony of Amphipolis guarded its mouth, and the Battle of Kleidion (1014) was fought in its upper valley region.

Ecology

Lake Kerkini, formed on its course, is a major wetland and bird reserve. The river's waters irrigate the Serres plain, an important agricultural area.
