ANFO

Composition~94% ammonium nitrate, ~6% fuel oil
TypeBlasting agent / tertiary explosive
Detonation velocityapprox. 3,200 m/s (confined, 100 mm diameter)
UsesMining, quarrying, construction blasting

ANFO (ammonium nitrate/fuel oil) is a widely used bulk industrial explosive consisting of about 94 percent porous prilled ammonium nitrate (NH4NO3), which acts as the oxidiser, and about 6 percent fuel oil, typically diesel, which acts as the fuel. It is the most common explosive used in mining and quarrying in North America and many other regions, accounting for the great majority of explosives consumed in the United States.

Properties

ANFO is classified as a blasting agent rather than a high explosive: it is relatively insensitive and normally requires a booster of a more sensitive explosive to initiate detonation. Properly mixed and confined, it detonates at roughly 3,200 metres per second and has an explosive power comparable to, though somewhat lower than, TNT on a weight basis. Its great advantages are low cost, safety in handling, and the ability to be poured or pumped directly into boreholes. Its chief weakness is a lack of water resistance: ammonium nitrate is hygroscopic and dissolves, so wet holes require packaged, waterproof variants or emulsion blends.

History

The explosive properties of ammonium nitrate mixed with fuels were recognised after disasters such as the Texas City explosion of 1947, and ANFO was commercialised in the 1950s, transforming the economics of large-scale mining and civil blasting. The bomb used in the 1995 Oklahoma City bombing, which destroyed the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building and killed 168 people, was an ammonium nitrate–based device using fuel oil and other fuels, prompting tighter regulation of fertiliser-grade ammonium nitrate in many countries.

Regulation

Because its ingredients are cheap agricultural and industrial commodities, ANFO and similar mixtures have been used in improvised explosive devices, and many jurisdictions now regulate the sale of ammonium nitrate, favour deterrent additives, or licence its use in blasting.
