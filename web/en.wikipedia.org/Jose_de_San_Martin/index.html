José de San Martín

José Francisco de San Martín y Matorras (25 February 1778 – 17 August 1850) was an Argentine general and statesman who led the independence movements of Argentina, Chile and Peru from Spanish rule. He is regarded, along with Simón Bolívar, as one of the Liberators of Spanish South America and is a national hero of Argentina.

Early life and career in Spain

San Martín was born in Yapeyú, in the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata, the son of a Spanish officer. He was educated in Spain and served in the Spanish army for more than twenty years, fighting against Napoleon's forces in the Peninsular War and rising to the rank of lieutenant colonel.

Liberation of South America

Returning to South America in 1812, he organized the Army of the Andes in Mendoza and in 1817 led the celebrated Crossing of the Andes into Chile, defeating the royalists at Chacabuco (1817) and Maipú (1818) and securing Chilean independence. He then sailed north and proclaimed the independence of Peru in Lima on 28 July 1821, serving as its Protector until 1822.

Later life

After his meeting with Bolívar at Guayaquil in 1822, San Martín resigned his powers and retired from public life. He spent his final years in exile in France, dying at Boulogne-sur-Mer in 1850. His remains were repatriated to Buenos Aires Cathedral in 1880.
