Pesto

Pesto (Italian: [ˈpesto]) is a sauce originating in Genoa, the capital of Liguria, Italy. The classic pesto alla genovese is made by pounding fresh basil leaves with coarse salt, garlic, pine nuts, grated hard cheese such as Parmigiano-Reggiano and Pecorino, and extra-virgin olive oil. The name derives from the Italian pestare, "to pound or crush", traditionally done with a mortar and pestle.

History

Pounded herb-and-garlic sauces date to Roman times — the Roman moretum described in a poem attributed to Virgil is a precursor — and medieval Ligurian agliata combined garlic and walnuts. Basil pesto in recognisable form appeared in the nineteenth century; a recipe was published in Giovanni Battista Ratto's La Cuciniera genovese (1863). Once a regional specialty, pesto became internationally popular in the late twentieth century.

Ingredients and serving

Authentic pesto genovese, protected by a consortium specification, uses Genoese basil DOP, ideally young leaves, and is traditionally served with trofie or trenette pasta, sometimes with potatoes and green beans cooked in the pasta water (avvantaggiati style). Because basil oxidises and darkens, the sauce is made quickly and used fresh.

Variations

Related sauces include Sicilian pesto alla trapanese with almonds and tomato, pesto rosso with sun-dried tomatoes, Provençal pistou (without nuts or cheese), and modern versions substituting walnuts, kale, spinach or other greens for basil or pine nuts.
