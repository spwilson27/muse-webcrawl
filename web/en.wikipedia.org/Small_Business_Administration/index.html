Small Business Administration

FormedJuly 30, 1953
HeadquartersWashington, D.C.
Parent agencyUnited States federal government (independent agency)

The Small Business Administration (SBA) is a United States government agency that provides support to entrepreneurs and small businesses. Its mission is to maintain and strengthen the nation's economy by enabling the establishment and viability of small businesses and by assisting in the economic recovery of communities after disasters.

History

The SBA was created on July 30, 1953, by President Dwight D. Eisenhower with the signing of the Small Business Act. It succeeded wartime and Depression-era lending bodies such as the Reconstruction Finance Corporation. The agency's programs have periodically been targeted for elimination, but it has survived and expanded, notably administering pandemic-era relief through the Paycheck Protection Program in 2020.

Programs

The SBA does not generally lend money directly; instead it guarantees loans made by partner lenders, reducing their risk. Its flagship is the 7(a) loan program. It also supports microloans, disaster assistance loans, government contracting goals for small businesses, and a network of Small Business Development Centers providing counselling and training.
