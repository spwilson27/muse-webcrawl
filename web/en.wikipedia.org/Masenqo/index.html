The masenqo (Amharic: ማሰንቆ; also spelled masinko or masenko) is a single-stringed bowed lute played in the musical traditions of Ethiopia and Eritrea. It is the characteristic instrument of the azmari, itinerant professional musicians who perform songs of praise, satire, and social commentary in bars, at celebrations, and at traditional venues.

Construction

The masenqo consists of a square or diamond-shaped wooden sound box covered with animal hide, a long neck or pole passing through the body, and a single string — traditionally of horsehair — stretched over a bridge. The string is tuned with a large peg and sounded with a light bow, also strung with horsehair. The player stops the string with the fingers of the left hand while bowing with the right, and the instrument is often held against the body. Its timbre is bright, nasal, and penetrating, well suited to cutting through noisy gatherings.

Musical role

The masenqo accompanies the singer's voice, doubling and ornamenting the melody. Azmari performers improvise verses in the qenet scales of Ethiopian music, often using the double-entendre poetic style known as sem-ena-werq ("wax and gold") to praise patrons or satirise public figures — a tradition that historically gave the masenqo player a licensed role as social critic, though it could also get performers into trouble with the authorities. The instrument features prominently in Ethio-jazz and modern Ethiopian popular music arrangements as well as in traditional tej bet (honey-wine house) performance.
