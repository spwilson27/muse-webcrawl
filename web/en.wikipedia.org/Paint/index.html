Paint

Paint is a liquid or mastic composition that, after application to a substrate in a thin layer, converts to a solid film. It is most commonly used to protect, colour, or provide texture to objects. Paint typically consists of a pigment, a binder, a solvent or carrier, and additives.

Components

Pigments provide colour and opacity; historically important pigments include ochre, lead white, ultramarine, and titanium white, the dominant modern white pigment. The binder (or vehicle) is the film-forming component — oils such as linseed oil in traditional oil paint, acrylic polymer emulsion in latex paints, or alkyd resins. Solvents adjust viscosity and evaporate as the paint dries; in water-based paints the carrier is water. Additives modify properties such as drying time, gloss, mildew resistance, and flow.

History

Paint is among humanity's oldest technologies: ochre pigments were used in cave painting more than 40,000 years ago, and prepared paint mixtures have been found at sites around 100,000 years old. Egyptian, Chinese, and medieval European painters developed sophisticated pigment and binder recipes. Industrial production began in the 19th century; the first ready-mixed paints and the collapsible paint tube (1841) transformed both decorative painting and fine art. In the 20th century, water-based emulsion ("latex") paints largely replaced lead- and solvent-based paints for buildings, and lead pigments were phased out for toxicity reasons.

Types and application

Paints include primers, enamels, varnishes, lacquers, watercolours, oil paints, and acrylic paints. Application methods include brushes, rollers, and spraying. Paint failure modes include chalking, cracking, peeling, and fading from ultraviolet light.
