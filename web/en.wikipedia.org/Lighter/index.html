Lighter


A lighter is a portable device used to create a flame, consisting of a metal or plastic container filled with flammable fluid or pressurised gas and a means of ignition.

A lighter is a portable device which creates a controlled flame, used to ignite a target such as a cigarette, a gas stove burner, or a campfire. It usually consists of a metal or plastic container filled with a flammable liquid or pressurized gas, a means of ignition to produce the flame, and some provision for extinguishing the flame.

History

One of the earliest lighters was the Döbereiner lamp (1823), which produced a flame from hydrogen ignited over platinum. Modern lighters use either naphtha-soaked wicks ignited by a flint spark wheel, or butane gas released and ignited by a spark or piezoelectric crystal.

Types

Disposable butane lighters, popularised by Bic from the 1970s, dominate the market; refillable metal lighters such as the Zippo remain in use. Jet lighters produce a hotter, wind-resistant flame.
