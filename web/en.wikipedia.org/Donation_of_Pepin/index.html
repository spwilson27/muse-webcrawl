Donation of Pepin

The Donation of Pepin in 756 was the grant by Pepin the Short, King of the Franks, of a strip of central Italian territory to Pope Stephen II, creating the Papal States, which endured until 1870.

Background

In 751 the Lombards had conquered the Byzantine Exarchate of Ravenna, threatening Rome. Pope Stephen II crossed the Alps to appeal to Pepin, who in return for papal anointing and the title Patrician of the Romans promised to restore the seized lands. At the Treaty of Quierzy (754) Pepin pledged the territories to the papacy.

The donation

After defeating the Lombard king Aistulf in campaigns of 755 and 756, Pepin compelled him to surrender the cities of the former exarchate and the Pentapolis. Rather than returning them to the Byzantine emperor, Pepin gave them to the Pope; the keys of the cities were laid on the tomb of St Peter. The donation shifted the papacy's allegiance from Constantinople to the Franks and laid the territorial foundation of papal temporal power. Pepin's son Charlemagne confirmed and expanded the grant in 774.
