Diners Club

Diners Club International Ltd., founded as Diners Club, is a charge card company owned by Discover Financial Services. Founded in 1950 by Frank X. McNamara, Ralph Schneider and Matty Simmons, it is credited with issuing the first independent multipurpose charge card, and is widely regarded as the originator of the modern credit card industry.

History

According to company lore, the idea came in 1949 when McNamara, dining in a New York restaurant, realised he had forgotten his wallet. The Diners Club card launched in 1950, initially accepted at 27 restaurants and held by some 200 members; it was made of cardboard and required the balance to be paid in full each month. Membership grew explosively through the 1950s as the card spread to hotels, airlines, car rental firms and retailers, and Diners Club franchised operations internationally, becoming the first international charge card network.

Later ownership

Citibank acquired Diners Club in 1981. The North American business was subsequently sold to the Bank of Montreal (BMO) in 2009, while in 2008 Discover Financial Services acquired Diners Club International's global network from Citi, aligning acceptance with the Discover network and allowing Diners Club cards issued abroad to run on Discover rails.

Products and position

Diners Club operates as a travel-and-entertainment focused charge card with airport lounge access and rewards programmes in many markets, issued by franchisees and licensees in dozens of countries. Though a small player compared with Visa, Mastercard and American Express, the brand retains recognition as the pioneer of the general-purpose payment card.
