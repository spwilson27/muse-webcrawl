Standard time

Standard time is the synchronization of clocks within a geographical region to a single time standard, rather than a local mean time standard. The concept arose in the 19th century with the needs of railway scheduling and telegraphy.

History

Before standard time, towns kept local solar time, making timetables chaotic. Railway companies in Britain adopted Greenwich Mean Time in the 1840s, and North American railroads introduced standard time zones in 1883. The 1884 International Meridian Conference established Greenwich as the prime meridian.

Modern use

Standard time is the baseline for civil time in most jurisdictions; many regions offset it seasonally with daylight saving time. Time zones are generally defined as offsets from Coordinated Universal Time (UTC).
