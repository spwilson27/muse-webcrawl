Shingles

Herpes zoster, commonly known as shingles, is a viral disease characterised by a painful skin rash with blisters in a localised area, typically a stripe on one side of the body. It is caused by reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus, the same virus that causes chickenpox.

Cause and course

After a person recovers from chickenpox, the virus remains dormant in nerve ganglia. Years or decades later it may reactivate, travelling down a sensory nerve to the skin of the corresponding dermatome. Risk increases with age and with weakened immunity. The rash usually resolves within two to five weeks, but some people develop postherpetic neuralgia, persistent nerve pain that can last months or years.

Antiviral medicines such as aciclovir can reduce severity if started early. Vaccines — the live vaccine Zostavax and the recombinant vaccine Shingrix — substantially reduce the risk of shingles and postherpetic neuralgia in older adults.
