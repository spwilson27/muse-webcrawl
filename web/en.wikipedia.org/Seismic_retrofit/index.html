Seismic retrofit

Seismic retrofit (or seismic rehabilitation) is the modification of existing structures to make them more resistant to seismic activity, ground motion, or soil failure due to earthquakes. Retrofitting is applied to buildings, bridges, and other infrastructure that were designed before modern seismic codes, or that have been found deficient under current standards.

Goals

Retrofit objectives range from preventing collapse and protecting life safety, to limiting damage so a structure remains operational after an event. Hospitals, fire stations, and other essential facilities are often retrofitted to an "immediate occupancy" performance level.

Techniques

  • Adding strength and stiffness: steel braced frames, reinforced concrete or steel shear walls, and moment frames.
  • Base isolation: inserting flexible bearings between the structure and its foundation to decouple it from ground motion.
  • Supplemental damping: installing viscous, friction, or hysteretic dampers that absorb seismic energy.
  • Strengthening connections: bolting houses to foundations, tying walls to floors and roofs, and jacketing columns with steel or fibre-reinforced polymer.
  • Mass reduction and configuration changes: removing heavy appendages or soft-storey irregularities.

Common building types at risk

Unreinforced masonry buildings, non-ductile concrete frames, soft-storey apartment buildings with open ground-floor parking, and older steel structures with vulnerable welded connections are widely recognised as high-risk types targeted by municipal retrofit ordinances, such as those adopted in Los Angeles and San Francisco.
