Known as: Allemansrätten (Sweden), jokamiehenoikeus (Finland), allemannsretten (Norway)
Covers: Walking, camping, foraging on uncultivated land

Right of public access to the wilderness

The right of public access to the wilderness, often called the freedom to roam, is a customary and in some countries statutory right allowing the public to access and enjoy privately owned land, lakes, and rivers for recreation and exercise, provided nature and property are not harmed. It is strongest in the Nordic countries.

Nordic versions

In Sweden (allemansrätten), Norway (allemannsretten, codified in the Outdoor Recreation Act of 1957), and Finland (jokamiehenoikeus), everyone may walk, ski, cycle, camp briefly, swim, and pick mushrooms, berries, and wildflowers on uncultivated land, subject to duties not to disturb residents, damage crops or plantings, or harm wildlife. Fires, fishing, and hunting are more restricted.

Elsewhere

Comparable rights exist in Scotland, where the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 established statutory access rights with a Scottish Outdoor Access Code, and in parts of the Baltic states and Central Europe, while in England and Wales access is more limited to designated access land and public rights of way.
