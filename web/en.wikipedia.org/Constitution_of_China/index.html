Constitution of China

The Constitution of the People's Republic of China is the supreme law of the People's Republic of China (PRC). It was adopted by the 5th National People's Congress on December 4, 1982, with further revisions in 1988, 1993, 1999, 2004, and 2018. It is the fourth constitution in the PRC's history, replacing the 1978 Constitution.

History

The first PRC constitution was adopted in 1954, establishing the National People's Congress (NPC) system. The radical 1975 constitution, issued during the Cultural Revolution, greatly shortened the document and subordinated state organs to the Chinese Communist Party (CCP). The post-Mao 1982 constitution, drafted under the leadership of Deng Xiaoping's reforms, restored state institutions and legal rights and has since been amended to incorporate the "socialist market economy", rule-of-law language (1999), human-rights protection (2004), and in 2018 the removal of term limits for the President and the inscription of Xi Jinping Thought.

Structure and content

The constitution has a preamble and 143 articles in four chapters: general principles; fundamental rights and duties of citizens; structure of the state; and the national flag, anthem, emblem, and capital. It proclaims China a socialist state under the people's democratic dictatorship, led by the CCP (a leadership role written into Article 1 in 2018), and vests all power in the people, exercised through the NPC and local people's congresses.

Implementation

Constitutional interpretation and enforcement rest with the NPC and its Standing Committee; Chinese courts do not exercise judicial review and cannot strike down laws as unconstitutional. In practice, the constitution's rights provisions operate within the framework of CCP leadership, and the document functions partly as a political program.
