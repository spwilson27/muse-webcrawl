Union Station (Toronto)

Union Station
Location: 65 Front Street West, Toronto, Ontario
Opened: August 6, 1927
Services: GO Transit, Via Rail, Amtrak, TTC subway, UP Express

Union Station is the principal railway station and intermodal transport hub of Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Located on Front Street in the downtown core, the Beaux-Arts station building is a National Historic Site and one of the country's busiest transport facilities, serving roughly 300,000 passengers a day.

History

Designed by the Montreal firm of Ross and Macdonald with Hugh Jones and John M. Lyle, the station was built between 1914 and 1920 but did not open until 1927, when it was inaugurated by Edward, Prince of Wales. Its Great Hall, with a vaulted ceiling rising some 27 metres, is among Canada's finest classical interiors.

Services and revitalization

The station is the hub of GO Transit commuter rail, Via Rail intercity services, the Union Pearson Express airport train, and the TTC's Union subway station. A major revitalization begun in 2010 expanded the concourses, added retail levels, and restored the heritage fabric.
