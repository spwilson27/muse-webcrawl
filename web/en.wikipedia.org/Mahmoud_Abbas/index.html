Mahmoud Abbas (Arabic: مَحْمُود عَبَّاس, romanized: Maḥmūd ʿAbbās; born 15 November 1935), also known by the kunya Abu Mazen, is a Palestinian politician who has served as president of the Palestinian Authority and of the State of Palestine since 2005, and as chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) since 2004. A founding member of Fatah, Abbas was a close associate of Yasser Arafat and a principal architect of the Oslo Accords, signing the 1993 Declaration of Principles on behalf of the PLO.

Early life

Abbas was born in Safed in British Mandatory Palestine. His family fled to Syria during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. He studied law at the University of Damascus and later earned a doctorate in history from Patrice Lumumba University in Moscow, writing a dissertation on relations between Zionism and Nazism that later drew controversy. In the 1950s he was among the founders, with Arafat and others, of the Fatah movement in exile in Qatar and Kuwait.

Role in the peace process

Abbas advocated of negotiations with Israel from the 1970s onward, maintained contacts with Israeli peace groups, and coordinated the Palestinian side of the secret Oslo channel in 1992–93. He signed the Oslo I Accord at the White House in September 1993 and the Oslo II (Taba) agreement in 1995. In 2003 he became the first Prime Minister of the Palestinian Authority under international pressure for reform, but resigned after a few months amid a power struggle with Arafat.

Presidency

After Arafat's death in November 2004, Abbas became PLO chairman and won the January 2005 presidential election with about 62% of the vote, campaigning on a platform of nonviolence and negotiations. His presidency has been marked by the 2007 split between Fatah and Hamas — which left his authority confined to the West Bank while Hamas governed Gaza — by stalled peace talks, by Palestinian statehood bids at the United Nations (including the UN General Assembly's 2012 grant of non-member observer state status), and by repeated postponements of elections; his four-year term, begun in 2005, has been extended indefinitely, drawing criticism of democratic backsliding and corruption within the PA.
