Nordic Council of Ministers

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Nordic Council of Ministers is the official body for intergovernmental co-operation between the Nordic countries: Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden, together with the autonomous territories of the Faroe Islands, Greenland, and Åland. It was established in 1971 and works alongside the Nordic Council, the parliamentary forum founded in 1952.

Organisation

The council is composed of national ministers; the prime ministers hold overall responsibility for co-operation, and a rotating presidency passes annually among the member states. A Secretary General heads the secretariat in Copenhagen. Decisions require consensus and are generally not binding on member states, though much Nordic co-operation is underpinned by the Helsinki Treaty of 1962.

Activities

The council funds and coordinates work on the environment, culture, education, research, labour markets, and regional policy, and supports institutions such as the Nordic Culture Fund and NordForsk. It has promoted the Nordic region as a single market for labour since the common labour market agreement of 1954 and passport-free travel under the Nordic Passport Union.
