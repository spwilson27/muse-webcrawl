Wok hei

Wok hei (Cantonese: 鑊氣, wok6 hei3) translates to "breath of the wok" and refers to the unique smoky flavour and aroma imparted to food stir-fried in a wok at very high heat. It is considered a hallmark of Cantonese cooking and an essential quality in dishes such as beef chow fun and fried rice.

Origins of the flavour

The flavour arises from a combination of effects: the Maillard reaction and partial caramelisation of sugars at temperatures above 200 °C, the combustion of oil droplets ignited by the flame, and compounds from the carbonised seasoning layer of a well-used wok. Restaurants achieve it with powerful jet burners sometimes called "dragon breath" stoves.

Technique

To produce wok hei, the cook heats the wok until it nearly smokes, adds oil, and tosses small batches of ingredients so that they sear rather than steam. Home stoves rarely generate enough heat, which is why the flavour is difficult to reproduce outside professional kitchens.
