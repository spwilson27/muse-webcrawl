Vito Genovese

Vito Genovese (November 21, 1897 – February 14, 1969) was an Italian-born American mobster who rose to power during Prohibition as an enforcer for Charles "Lucky" Luciano. He became boss of the Luciano crime family, which was renamed the Genovese crime family after him. Genovese is remembered as one of the most ruthless figures of the American Mafia, and the 1957 Apalachin meeting of mob bosses — which he convened — exposed the Mafia's existence to public scrutiny.

Early career

Born in a village near Naples, Genovese emigrated to New York in 1913. He became a close associate of Luciano and a leading bootlegger during Prohibition, and served as underboss of Luciano's family from 1931. In 1937, facing a murder indictment, he fled to Italy, where he ingratiated himself with Benito Mussolini's regime. Returned to the United States after the Second World War, he was acquitted when key witnesses were murdered or intimidated.

Boss and Apalachin

In 1957 Genovese moved against boss Frank Costello; the failed assassination attempt by Vincent Gigante nevertheless drove Costello into retirement, and Genovese took control of the family. That November he convened a national Mafia summit at Apalachin, New York, which was raided by police — a public humiliation that confirmed the Mafia's existence and badly damaged Genovese's standing. In 1959 he was convicted of narcotics trafficking in a case he believed was engineered by rivals, and sentenced to 15 years. He died of a heart attack in federal prison in 1969.
