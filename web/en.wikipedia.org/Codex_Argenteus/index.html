Codex Argenteus

A leaf of the Codex Argenteus, written in silver (and partly gold) on purple vellum

The Codex Argenteus ("Silver Book") is a 6th-century illuminated manuscript that originally contained a 4th-century translation of the Gospels into the Gothic language by the bishop Ulfilas. It is written in silver, and partly gold, ink on purple-dyed parchment in the Gothic alphabet devised by Ulfilas. Of its original 336 leaves, 188 survive, including a fragment discovered in Speyer in 1970.

History

The codex was probably produced in Ravenna under the Ostrogothic king Theodoric the Great in the early 6th century. After a long obscure history it surfaced at Werden Abbey in Germany, passed to Prague, and was taken to Sweden as war booty in 1648 at the end of the Thirty Years' War. It is now preserved at the Carolina Rediviva library of Uppsala University in Sweden, where it is displayed in a dedicated exhibition room.

Significance

The Codex Argenteus is the principal surviving text in Gothic, a language otherwise known only from fragments; consequently it is a document of the first importance for Germanic philology and for the reconstruction of early Germanic languages. Together with other luxury purple codices, it exemplifies late antique book production, a craft later also exercised in imaginary works such as the Codex Seraphinianus. A careful facsimile edition was published in 1927, and the manuscript has been inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register.
