The Battle of Riachuelo was a large and decisive naval battle of the Paraguayan War between Paraguay and the Empire of Brazil, fought on 11 June 1865 on the Paraná River near the Riachuelo stream, in present-day Argentina. The Brazilian fleet under Admiral Francisco Manuel Barroso da Silva defeated the Paraguayan squadron of Captain Pedro Ignacio Meza.

Background

Paraguayan forces had invaded the Argentine province of Corrientes. The Paraguayan plan was to surprise and capture the Brazilian fleet at anchor, using boarding parties, supported by artillery and troops concealed on the riverbank and on barges.

The battle

Alerted in time, Barroso led his ships downstream past the Paraguayan batteries and then turned back into action, ramming and sinking several Paraguayan vessels with his flagship Amazonas. The Paraguayans lost four steamers and all their barges; Meza was mortally wounded. Brazilian losses were much lighter, though the corvette Jequitinhonha grounded and was lost.

Aftermath

Riachuelo gave Brazil command of the rivers, isolating landlocked Paraguay from the outside world and preventing the Paraguayan advance toward Uruguay. The date is celebrated as the Brazilian Navy's principal battle honour.
