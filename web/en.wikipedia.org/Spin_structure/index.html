Spin structure

In differential geometry, a spin structure on an orientable Riemannian manifold (M, g) allows one to define associated spinor bundles, giving rise to the notion of a spinor field on the manifold. Spin structures have wide applications in mathematical physics, in particular quantum field theory where they are an essential ingredient in the definition of particles with fermionic behavior.

Definition

A spin structure is a lift of the structure group of the oriented orthonormal frame bundle from SO(n) to its double cover, the spin group Spin(n). A manifold admits a spin structure if and only if its second Stiefel–Whitney class vanishes. Spin manifolds are those orientable manifolds admitting such a structure.

Examples

Spheres, complex projective planes, and tori are spin manifolds, while the real projective plane and CP² with certain metrics illustrate obstructions in related contexts. Spin structures on a given manifold, when they exist, are classified by the first cohomology group with Z/2 coefficients.

Applications

Spin geometry underlies the Dirac operator, the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, and Seiberg–Witten theory. The existence of a spin structure is also an important condition in several formulations of quantum gravity and string theory.
