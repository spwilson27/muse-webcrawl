Ludlow Massacre

Date20 April 1914
LocationLudlow, Colorado, United States
Deaths~21, including 11 children

The Ludlow Massacre was a mass killing perpetrated by anti-striker militia during the Colorado Coalfield War. On 20 April 1914, Colorado National Guard troops and company guards attacked a tent colony of some 1,200 striking coal miners and their families at Ludlow, Colorado.

Events

The colony burned during the fighting; two women and eleven children suffocated in a pit dug beneath one of the tents. About 21 people died in total, including union organizer Louis Tikas. In the ten days of retaliatory violence that followed, strikers attacked mines across the region until federal troops restored order.

Legacy

The massacre galvanized public opinion against the mine owners, including the Rockefeller-owned Colorado Fuel and Iron Company, and became a landmark in American labor history. The United Mine Workers erected a monument at the site, now a National Historic Landmark.
