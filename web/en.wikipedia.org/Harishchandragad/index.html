Harishchandragad is a hill fort in the Ahmednagar district of Maharashtra, India, in the Malshej Ghat region of the Western Ghats. It is one of the most famous trekking destinations in the Sahyadris and is named after the legendary King Harishchandra. Its highest point, Taramati peak at 1,422 metres, is among the highest points in Maharashtra.

History

The fort and its rock-cut caves are ancient; the caves are traditionally dated to around the 6th century, and the fort is mentioned in the Skanda Purana and Matsya Purana. It passed through the hands of the Mughals and the Marathas — it was captured by the Maratha commander in the 18th century era struggles — before coming under British control in 1818. The sage Changdev is said to have meditated in one of its caves in the 14th century.

Attractions

The fort's best-known feature is Konkan Kada, a vast concave, overhanging cliff facing the Konkan lowlands, famous for sunsets and, in the monsoon, a rare "vertical cloudburst" effect in which wind drives mist and water upward. The Harishchandreshwar temple, built in the Hemadpanthi style of black stone with intricate carving, stands beside rock-cut cisterns (Saptatheertha). Other features include the Kedareshwar cave, with a large Shiva lingam surrounded by water, and the twin peaks Taramati and Rohidas. Popular trek routes ascend from Khireshwar village via Tolar Khind, from Pachnai (the easiest route), and the difficult Nalichi Vaat.
