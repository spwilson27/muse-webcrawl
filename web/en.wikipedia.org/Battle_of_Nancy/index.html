The Battle of Nancy was the final and decisive battle of the Burgundian Wars, fought on 5 January 1477 outside Nancy, capital of the Duchy of Lorraine. Duke René II of Lorraine, supported by a large contingent of Swiss mercenaries, attacked the army of Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy, who was besieging the city in mid-winter with a depleted and demoralised force. The Burgundians were routed, and Charles was killed in the rout — his frozen body was found days later in a pond, his head cleft by a halberd. His death ended the Burgundian state as a great power and triggered the partition of its lands between France and the Habsburgs.

Background

Charles the Bold's ambition to build a middle kingdom between France and the Empire had already suffered crushing defeats by the Swiss at Grandson and Morat in 1476. In October 1476 he nevertheless invaded Lorraine, whose duke had allied with the Swiss and France, and laid siege to Nancy, which had just been recovered by René. Despite the pleas of his advisers and the onset of a bitter winter that killed horses and men in the siege lines, Charles refused to withdraw. René assembled a relief army of Lorrainers, Alsatians and some 8,000–9,000 Swiss and marched to the city's rescue.

The battle

On 5 January 1477, in driving snow, the allied army descended on the Burgundian position south of Nancy. While a detachment demonstrated against the Burgundian front along the road, the main body — with the Swiss pike squares at its heart — made a wide flanking march over the wooded hills and attacked the Burgundian right. Charles's artillery fired ineffectively in the snow, his Italian and English mercenaries wavered, and his line collapsed toward the stream of the Jarville. Charles was last seen fighting in the press; three days later his corpse was identified by his physician and servants among the dead, half-eaten by wolves, with a halberd wound through the head.

Aftermath

With no male heir, the Burgundian inheritance passed to Charles's daughter Mary, who married Maximilian of Habsburg — a settlement that transferred the Burgundian Netherlands and Franche-Comté to the Habsburgs while Louis XI of France seized the Duchy of Burgundy itself. The resulting Franco-Habsburg rivalry shaped European politics for centuries. Nancy remained Lorraine's capital, and the battle is commemorated by the church of Notre-Dame de Bonsecours, built on the battlefield site.
