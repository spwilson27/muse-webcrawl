Rumble strip

A rumble strip (also known as a sleeper line, alert strip, or audibly as "singing" pavement) is a road safety feature applied to the driving surface of a road to alert inattentive drivers of potential danger by causing a tactile vibration and audible rumbling transmitted through the wheels into the interior of the vehicle. Rumble strips are placed along the direction of travel following an edgeline or centerline, or across the direction of travel as an alert before a hazard.

Types

Shoulder rumble strips are cut or rolled into the paved shoulder along the edgeline to warn drivers drifting off the roadway. Centerline rumble strips are used on undivided two-lane roads to discourage crossing into oncoming traffic. Transverse rumble strips run across the lane and are placed in advance of toll booths, intersections, sharp curves or work zones.

Effectiveness

Studies by highway safety agencies have found that shoulder rumble strips reduce run-off-road injury crashes substantially — commonly cited figures are reductions of roughly 15–70% depending on road type — while centerline strips reduce head-on and opposite-direction sideswipe crashes. They are considered one of the most cost-effective road safety countermeasures.

Concerns

Rumble strips generate noise for nearby residents and can affect cyclists, for whom gaps are sometimes provided in the strip pattern. Milled-in strips are preferred over raised types in snowy regions because they do not interfere with snowploughs and do not break away under traffic.
