Supervolcano

Supervolcano
DefinitionVolcano capable of an eruption with ejecta volume > 1,000 km³ (VEI 8)
Last super-eruptionOruanui eruption, ~25,400 years ago
Major examplesYellowstone, Toba, Taupō, Long Valley, La Garita

A supervolcano is a volcano whose eruptions can eject more than 1,000 cubic kilometres of material — a rating of 8 on the Volcanic Explosivity Index (VEI), the maximum value. Such super-eruptions are thousands of times larger than the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens and can blanket continents in ash and inject so much sulphur into the stratosphere that global temperatures fall for years, a "volcanic winter" capable of devastating agriculture worldwide.

Calderas

Super-eruptions empty a magma chamber so rapidly that the ground above collapses, forming a vast caldera rather than a cone. Yellowstone Caldera (72 by 55 km) was created by an eruption 640,000 years ago; the Toba caldera in Indonesia, now Lake Toba, formed about 74,000 years ago in the largest known eruption of the past two million years, which some scientists have linked to a genetic bottleneck in early human populations.

Known supervolcanoes

  • Yellowstone, United States — eruptions 2.1 million, 1.3 million, and 640,000 years ago.
  • Toba, Indonesia — ~74,000 years ago, VEI 8.
  • Taupō, New Zealand — Oruanui eruption ~25,400 years ago; Hatepe eruption ~232 CE was VEI 7.
  • La Garita, United States — Fish Canyon eruption ~28 million years ago, among the largest known.
  • Long Valley, Valles, Camp Flegrei (Italy) — large caldera systems with more recent smaller eruptions.

Frequency

VEI-8 eruptions occur on average once every tens of thousands of years; none has occurred in recorded history. Monitoring of ground deformation, gas emissions, and seismicity at restless calderas aims to give warning, though forecasting such events remains a major scientific challenge.
