Airports of London

The metropolitan area of London, England, is served by six international airports — Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted, Luton, London City, and Southend — which together form the busiest city airport system in the world by passenger traffic. In a typical pre-pandemic year the system handled roughly 180 million passengers. Each airport has a distinct role: Heathrow is the principal long-haul hub, Gatwick and Stansted serve a mix of full-service and low-cost traffic, Luton is dominated by low-cost carriers, City serves business travellers in the Docklands, and Southend is the smallest and newest of the six.

The six airports

Heathrow, west of the city, is the UK's largest airport and a major global hub for British Airways and the Star Alliance. Gatwick, to the south in West Sussex, is the world's busiest single-runway airport in regular operation. Stansted, in Essex, is a principal base for Ryanair. Luton, north of London, is a base for easyJet and Wizz Air. London City, closest to the financial districts, has a short runway restricting it to smaller aircraft. Southend, in Essex, reopened to scheduled services in 2012.

Capacity debate

London's airports operate close to capacity, and expansion has been debated for decades. A third runway at Heathrow was approved by Parliament in 2018 after years of inquiry, including the Airports Commission chaired by Sir Howard Davies, though legal and political challenges have repeatedly delayed construction; Gatwick has pursued plans to bring its standby runway into routine use.
