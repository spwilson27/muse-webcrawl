Australian Alps

Highest peak: Mount Kosciuszko (2,228 m)

Location: New South Wales, Victoria and the ACT, Australia

Parent range: Great Dividing Range

The Australian Alps are a mountain range in south-eastern Australia, forming the highest section of the Great Dividing Range. The range spans eastern Victoria, south-eastern New South Wales and the Australian Capital Territory, and contains mainland Australia's highest peak, Mount Kosciuszko (2,228 metres), along with the other members of the Seven Summits' mainland group such as Mount Townsend and Mount Bogong.

Ecology

The Alps are the only mainland Australian region to receive regular deep snow and support an alpine and sub-alpine environment found nowhere else on the continent, including snow gum woodlands, alpine ash forests, bogs and fens, and endemic species such as the mountain pygmy possum and the corroboree frog. Much of the range is protected in a chain of national parks, including Kosciuszko National Park and Alpine National Park.

Uses

The region supports Australia's ski industry at resorts such as Thredbo, Perisher, Falls Creek and Mount Hotham, and its catchments feed the Snowy Mountains Scheme and the Murray–Darling river system, providing water for irrigation and hydroelectricity. The Alps have a long history of Indigenous occupation and, later, summer cattle grazing by stockmen celebrated in Australian bush folklore.
