Degree of a field extension

In mathematics, more specifically field theory, the degree of a field extension is a rough measure of the "size" of the extension. The concept plays an important role in many areas of mathematics, including algebra and number theory — for example, the impossibility of certain classical straightedge and compass constructions was proved using the degree of associated field extensions.

Definition

Suppose E / F is a field extension. Then E may be regarded as a vector space over F. The dimension of this vector space is called the degree of the field extension and is denoted [E : F]. The extension is said to be finite if the degree is finite and infinite otherwise.

Examples

The complex numbers as an extension of the real numbers have degree 2, with basis {1, i}. The rationals extended by the square root of 2 also have degree 2. The field of rational functions in one variable over the rationals has infinite degree. Simple extensions generated by an algebraic element have degree equal to the degree of the element's minimal polynomial.

Multiplicativity

Degrees are multiplicative in towers of extensions: if K / E and E / F are field extensions, then [K : F] = [K : E] · [E : F], whenever the relevant degrees are defined.
