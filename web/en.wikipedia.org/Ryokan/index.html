Ryokan

A traditional tatami room in a ryokan.

A ryokan (Japanese: 旅館) is a traditional Japanese inn, dating back to the Edo period, when such lodgings served travellers along Japan's highways. Some establishments, such as Nishiyama Onsen Keiunkan (founded 705 AD), are among the oldest hotels in the world.

Features

A typical ryokan room has tatami-matted floors, low tables, and futon bedding laid out in the evening by staff. Guests are provided with a yukata robe, and communal baths — often onsen hot springs in resort areas — are a central feature. Meals typically consist of traditional multi-course kaiseki dinners and Japanese breakfasts, frequently served in the guest's room. Service is overseen by the okami (proprietress), and the emphasis is on personal hospitality (omotenashi) and seasonal detail.
