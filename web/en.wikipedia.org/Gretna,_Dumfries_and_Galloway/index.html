Gretna is a town in Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland, close to the border with England. Together with neighbouring Gretna Green, it is historically famous as a destination for runaway marriages, after the Marriage Act 1753 in England and Wales imposed restrictions on marriage that did not apply under Scots law.

History

Under Scots law, couples could marry by simple declaration before witnesses, without parental consent from the age of 16. Gretna Green, the first village across the border on the main London-to-Edinburgh coaching route, became famous for its "anvil marriages" performed by blacksmiths acting as "priests". The modern town of Gretna grew during the First World War around HM Factory Gretna, a vast cordite munitions works, and was built as a planned township designed in part by Raymond Unwin.

Marriage tourism

Gretna remains one of Scotland's most popular wedding destinations, hosting thousands of weddings each year. The Old Blacksmith's Shop at Gretna Green, dating to 1712, is now a visitor attraction and wedding venue.

Transport and sport

The town is served by Gretna Green railway station and lies near the A74(M) motorway. Gretna F.C., based in the town, famously rose through the Scottish leagues to reach the Scottish Premier League and the 2006 Scottish Cup Final before collapsing financially in 2008; its successor club is Gretna F.C. 2008.
