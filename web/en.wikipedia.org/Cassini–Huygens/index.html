Cassini–Huygens, commonly called Cassini, was a space-research mission by NASA, the European Space Agency (ESA), and the Italian Space Agency (ASI) to send a space probe to study the planet Saturn and its system, including its rings and natural satellites. The flagship-class robotic spacecraft comprised both NASA's Cassini orbiter — the first spacecraft to orbit Saturn — and ESA's Huygens lander, which landed on Saturn's largest moon, Titan, in January 2005, the most distant landing ever made by a spacecraft.

The mission is named after the astronomers Giovanni Cassini and Christiaan Huygens. Cassini discovered four of Saturn's moons and the large gap in its rings now called the Cassini Division; Huygens discovered Titan and deduced the true nature of Saturn's rings.

Mission overview

Cassini was launched on October 15, 1997, aboard a Titan IVB/Centaur rocket from Cape Canaveral. Its interplanetary trajectory used gravity assists from Venus (twice), Earth, and Jupiter, arriving at Saturn on July 1, 2004. The Huygens probe was released on December 25, 2004, and descended through Titan's atmosphere on January 14, 2005, transmitting data from the surface for over an hour after touchdown.

Discoveries

Over its thirteen years in the Saturn system, Cassini transformed understanding of the planet and its moons. Key findings include:

  • Active ice geysers erupting from the south pole of Enceladus, revealing a global subsurface ocean.
  • Lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane on Titan, with a full hydrological cycle.
  • Detailed structure and dynamics of Saturn's rings, including propeller-shaped disturbances from embedded moonlets.
  • A giant hexagonal polar vortex on Saturn and a hurricane-like storm at the north pole.
  • Evidence that Titan's interior also hosts a subsurface water ocean.

Grand Finale and end of mission

After the primary mission (2004–2008) and two extensions (the Equinox and Solstice missions), Cassini performed a series of daring passes between Saturn and its rings in 2017. To protect potentially habitable moons from contamination, the spacecraft was deliberately steered into Saturn's atmosphere on September 15, 2017, where it burned up, ending a mission of almost twenty years.
