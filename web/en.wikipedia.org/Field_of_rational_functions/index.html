Field of rational functions

Field of rational functions
FieldAbstract algebra, algebraic geometry
NotationK(X), K(X1, …, Xn)

In abstract algebra, the field of rational functions in one indeterminate X over a field K, denoted K(X), is the field of fractions of the polynomial ring K[X]. Its elements are quotients

f(X) / g(X), with f, g ∈ K[X] and g ≠ 0,

called rational functions, added and multiplied by the usual rules for fractions. K(X) is the smallest field containing both K and the polynomial ring.

Properties

K(X) is a transcendental extension of K of transcendence degree 1: X satisfies no polynomial equation over K. The construction generalises to several variables, K(X1, …, Xn), the field of fractions of the multivariate polynomial ring. Every finitely generated extension of K of transcendence degree n embeds in such a field.

In algebraic geometry

For an irreducible algebraic variety V, the field of rational functions K(V) consists of equivalence classes of functions defined by ratios of polynomials on open dense subsets of V. It is a fundamental invariant: two varieties are birationally equivalent exactly when their function fields are isomorphic. For a smooth projective curve, the function field determines the curve, linking the algebra of rational functions to the geometry of Riemann surfaces.
