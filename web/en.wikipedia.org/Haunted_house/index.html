Haunted house

A haunted house, spook house or ghost house is a house or other building traditionally believed to be inhabited by disembodied spirits of the deceased who may have lived there or been connected to the place. Belief in haunted houses is a staple of folklore worldwide and of Gothic and horror literature.

Reported phenomena

Commonly reported features of hauntings include apparitions, unexplained sounds such as knocks, footsteps and voices, moving or disappearing objects, cold spots, and feelings of being watched. Historical explanations proposed by sceptics include carbon monoxide poisoning, infrasound, sleep paralysis, and the power of suggestion in old, draughty buildings.

In literature and film

Famous fictional haunted houses include Hill House in Shirley Jackson's The Haunting of Hill House (1959) and the Overlook Hotel in Stephen King's The Shining (1977). Real locations famous for alleged hauntings include the Borley Rectory in England, the Amityville house in New York, and the Winchester Mystery House in California.

Attractions

"Haunted house" also denotes a seasonal commercial attraction, popular around Halloween, in which visitors walk through sets decorated with horror themes while costumed actors startle them. Such attractions range from small community fundraisers to large professional productions.
