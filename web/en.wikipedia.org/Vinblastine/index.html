Vinblastine (VBL), sold under the brand name Velban among others, is a chemotherapy medication used to treat several types of cancer, including Hodgkin lymphoma, non-small-cell lung cancer, bladder cancer, brain cancer, melanoma, and testicular cancer. It is given by injection into a vein.

Mechanism of action

Vinblastine is a vinca alkaloid isolated from the Madagascar periwinkle. It binds to tubulin and inhibits microtubule assembly, thereby arresting dividing cells in metaphase of mitosis. Because microtubules are also important for normal cellular functions, the drug is cytotoxic and must never be given intrathecally, where it is almost invariably fatal.

Medical uses and side effects

Vinblastine is a component of several classic regimens, including ABVD for Hodgkin lymphoma. Common side effects include hair loss, constipation, bone marrow suppression (chiefly leukopenia), shortness of breath and gastrointestinal upset. It was first isolated in 1958 by Robert Noble and Charles Thomas Beer at the University of Western Ontario, and is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
