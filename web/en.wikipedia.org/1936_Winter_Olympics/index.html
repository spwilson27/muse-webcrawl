The 1936 Winter Olympics, officially known as the IV Olympic Winter Games, were a winter multi-sport event held from 6 to 16 February 1936 in Garmisch-Partenkirchen, Germany. The country also hosted the 1936 Summer Olympics in Berlin later that year, making Germany the host of both Games in the same year, the last time this occurred.

Garmisch and Partenkirchen were separate towns that had merged in 1935 under pressure from the Nazi regime, which used the Games as a showcase ahead of the Berlin Summer Olympics. Adolf Hitler opened the Games, and the event was notable for improved organisation, large crowds, and extensive construction including a new ski stadium and ski jumps built under the direction of organising committee president Karl Ritter von Halt.

Sports

The programme included alpine skiing for the first time at a Winter Olympics, with combined events for men and women won by Franz Pfnür and Christl Cranz of Germany. The sports contested were bobsleigh, ice hockey, skating (figure skating and speed skating), and skiing (alpine skiing, cross-country skiing, Nordic combined and ski jumping). Military patrol and ice stock sport (Eisstockschießen) were demonstration events.

Highlights

Norwegian speed skater Ivar Ballangrud was the most successful athlete of the Games, winning three gold medals and one silver. Norway's Sonja Henie won her third consecutive Olympic figure skating title. In ice hockey, Great Britain — whose team was largely composed of Canadian expatriates — won a surprise gold medal, defeating Canada and ending its run of four consecutive titles; the United States took bronze. Norway topped the medal table with seven golds and fifteen medals overall.

Participating nations

A record 28 nations sent athletes, with Australia, Bulgaria, Greece, Liechtenstein, Spain and Turkey making their Winter Olympic debuts. The Games were the last Winter Olympics held before the Second World War; the 1940 and 1944 editions were cancelled, and the event resumed with the 1948 Winter Olympics in St. Moritz.
