Magnet wire

Magnet wire, or enamelled wire, is a copper or aluminium wire coated with a very thin layer of electrical insulation. It is used to wind coils for electric motors, transformers, inductors, generators, electromagnets, loudspeaker voice coils, and other devices that need tight coils of insulated wire. The thin enamel insulation, unlike bulky plastic sheathing, lets many turns fit in a small space while withstanding high voltages between adjacent turns.

Construction

The conductor is drawn to size and annealed, then coated with multiple passes of polymer enamel, each baked in an oven, building a film tens of micrometres thick. Common insulations include polyurethane (solderable — it can be tinned without stripping), polyester and polyester-imide (higher thermal endurance), and polyimide film for extreme temperatures. Wire may be round, rectangular (for dense "edge-wound" motor windings in electric vehicles), or stranded as litz wire to reduce skin-effect losses at high frequencies.

Properties and grading

Magnet wire is classified by its thermal class — the maximum continuous operating temperature, from about 105 °C up to 240 °C — and by film build (single, heavy, triple). Dielectric breakdown strength is typically several kilovolts despite the thin coating. Copper dominates for its conductivity and solderability; aluminium magnet wire, roughly 60% as conductive but a third of the weight, is used in some large transformers and motors where mass matters more than volume.

Applications

Every electric motor, from tiny vibration motors in phones to traction motors in electric vehicles and industrial machines, contains magnet-wire windings. Transformers at all scales, MRI magnet coils, guitar pickups, hard-disk voice coils, and amateur Tesla coils also depend on it. Repair shops strip and rewind burnt-out motor coils with fresh magnet wire as a routine service.
