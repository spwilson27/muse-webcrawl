Grand Paris (literally "Greater Paris") is a project and administrative structure aimed at transforming the Paris metropolitan area into a major world metropolis. It is embodied chiefly by the Métropole du Grand Paris, an intercommunal structure created on 1 January 2016, grouping the city of Paris and 130 surrounding communes.

The Grand Paris Express

The most visible part of the project is the Grand Paris Express, a major expansion of the Paris Métro and suburban rail network. It involves building some 200 kilometres of new automated metro lines — lines 15, 16, 17 and 18, plus extensions of lines 11 and 14 — and 68 new stations, at a cost estimated in the tens of billions of euros. The new lines are designed to connect the suburbs directly to one another instead of forcing journeys through central Paris.

Governance

The Métropole du Grand Paris is responsible for metropolitan planning, housing policy, economic development and environmental protection across its territory, which covers the inner suburbs of Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-Saint-Denis and Val-de-Marne plus several communes farther out.

Background

The idea of a "Grand Paris" was launched by President Nicolas Sarkozy in 2007, and an international consultation of architects and urban planners followed in 2008–2009. Legislation creating the metropolis was passed in 2014–2015. The project also became intertwined with preparations for the 2024 Summer Olympic Games in Paris.
