Rail (bird)

Rails
Family Rallidae
Small to medium ground-living birds of wetlands and grasslands

The rails, or Rallidae, are a large cosmopolitan family of small- to medium-sized terrestrial and/or semi-amphibian birds. The family exhibits considerable diversity and includes the crakes, coots and gallinules. Many species are associated with wetlands, although the family is found in every terrestrial habitat except dry deserts, polar regions and areas above the snow line.

Description

Rails typically have laterally compressed bodies — the origin of the expression "thin as a rail" — allowing them to slip through dense reed beds, with long toes for walking on floating vegetation and short rounded wings. Most are secretive, but their loud calls often betray their presence. Species range from the 12-cm white-browed crake to the flightless takahe of New Zealand, which is over 60 cm long.

Flightlessness and extinction

Rails readily colonize remote islands, where they repeatedly evolve flightlessness; consequently many island species were exterminated by introduced predators, making Rallidae among the most extinction-prone bird families. Survivors under conservation include the takahe and the weka.

Genera

Well-known members include the water rail (Rallus aquaticus), corn crake, common moorhen, Eurasian coot, purple swamphen and the giant extinct moa-like adzebills' ecological neighbors on Pacific islands.
