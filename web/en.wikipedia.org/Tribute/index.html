Tribute

A tribute (from Latin tributum, a contribution) is wealth, often in kind, that a party gives to another as a sign of submission, allegiance or respect, or as the price of protection. Various ancient states exacted tribute from the territories they had conquered or threatened to conquer. In the case where an alliance was formed, the inferior party paid tribute to the superior party in return for protection. The superior party may also ask the inferior party to provide soldiers to participate in military operations.

Historical examples

The Assyrian and Roman empires demanded tribute from client states; imperial China operated an extensive tributary system in which neighbouring states presented gifts at court; and the Byzantine Empire paid tribute at times to neighbouring powers to prevent attack. In medieval Europe, tribute-like payments included the Danegeld paid to Viking raiders. Tribute differs from taxation in that it is levied on a subordinate political community as a whole rather than administered as internal revenue, though in practice the distinction can blur.

Modern usage

Modern elements of the term include its figurative use for payments of respect or praise — a "tribute" to a person or event — and in phrases such as "tribute band" (a band performing the music of a famous act) and "tribute album". The word also survives in the names of rivers called tributaries, which "pay" their waters into a larger stream.
