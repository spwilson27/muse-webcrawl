Nadia Murad

Nadia Murad Basee Taha
Born: March 10, 1993, Kocho, Iraq
Nationality: Iraqi Yazidi
Occupation: Human rights activist
Award: Nobel Peace Prize (2018)

Nadia Murad (born 1993) is an Iraqi Yazidi human rights activist and a co-recipient of the 2018 Nobel Peace Prize, awarded "for efforts to end the use of sexual violence as a weapon of war and armed conflict."

Background and captivity

Murad grew up in the village of Kocho in the Sinjar district of northern Iraq. In August 2014, Islamic State (ISIL) fighters attacked Sinjar, massacring Yazidi men and enslaving thousands of women and girls in what the United Nations later recognized as a genocide. Murad's mother and six of her brothers were killed; she was captured and held as a sex slave in Mosul before escaping after three months with the help of a neighboring family.

Activism

After reaching a refugee camp and then Germany, Murad began speaking publicly about the Yazidi genocide and sexual violence in conflict. In December 2015 she addressed the UN Security Council — the first briefing on human trafficking in its history — and in 2016 she became the first UN Goodwill Ambassador for the Dignity of Survivors of Human Trafficking. With lawyer Amal Clooney she pressed for international prosecution of ISIL crimes, and she founded Nadia's Initiative, which advocates for survivors and helps rebuild the Sinjar region. Her memoir, The Last Girl (2017), recounts her experience.

Recognition

She shared the 2018 Nobel Peace Prize with Congolese physician Denis Mukwege and has received the Sakharov Prize and the Vaclav Havel Human Rights Prize. See also Yazidi genocide.
