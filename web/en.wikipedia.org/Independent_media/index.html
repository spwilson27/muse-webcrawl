Independent media

Independent media refers to media — such as newspapers, radio, television, film, and online outlets — that are free of influence by government or corporate interests. The term overlaps with alternative media, which challenges established viewpoints, and with community and citizen media produced by non-professionals. Independent outlets are often supported by subscriptions, donations, or small-scale advertising rather than by large media conglomerates.

Independent outlets typically operate outside major media conglomerates.

History and forms

Precursors include the underground press of the 1960s, pirate and community radio, public-access television, and zines. The Indymedia network, launched during the 1999 Seattle WTO protests, pioneered open-publishing citizen journalism online. The internet and social media have lowered distribution costs, enabling blogs, podcasts, newsletters, and video channels to reach audiences without traditional gatekeepers, while also raising questions about funding, fact-checking, and reach.

Challenges

Independent media face financial precarity, pressure from advertisers and platforms, and, in many countries, legal harassment or censorship. Press-freedom organisations argue that independent outlets are essential checks on power, particularly where mainstream media ownership is concentrated or state-controlled.
