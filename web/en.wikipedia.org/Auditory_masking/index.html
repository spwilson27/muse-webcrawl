Auditory masking

In psychoacoustics, auditory masking occurs when the perception of one sound is affected by the presence of another sound. A louder sound (the masker) can raise the threshold at which a quieter target sound is heard.

Types

Simultaneous masking occurs when masker and target coincide in time, and is strongest when their frequencies are close, reflecting the frequency selectivity of the basilar membrane. Temporal masking occurs just before (backward masking) or after (forward masking) the masker.

Applications

Masking models underpin perceptual audio codecs such as MP3 and AAC, which discard signal components predicted to be inaudible, and inform clinical audiometry and hearing-aid design.
