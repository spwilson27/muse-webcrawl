The Public Theater is an arts organization in New York City, founded by Joseph Papp as the Shakespeare Workshop in 1954 and built on the idea that theatre, like the public library, should be freely accessible to all. It is headquartered at 425 Lafayette Street in the former Astor Library building in the East Village, and presents an annual season of new plays, musicals, and classics, alongside the free Shakespeare in the Park productions at the Delacorte Theater in Central Park.

History

Papp began producing free Shakespeare in parks and church basements in 1954; the mobile productions led to the Delacorte Theater, opened in Central Park in 1962. In 1967 the Public opened its Lafayette Street home with the premiere of the rock musical Hair. The theatre became a powerhouse incubator: works developed there have won numerous Tony Awards and Pulitzer Prizes. George C. Wolfe directed the theatre from 1993 to 2004, followed by Oskar Eustis.

Notable productions

Productions originating at the Public include Hair, A Chorus Line, For Colored Girls Who Have Considered Suicide / When the Rainbow Is Enuf, Topdog/Underdog, Caroline, or Change, Fun Home, and Lin-Manuel Miranda's Hamilton, which premiered at the Public in 2015 before transferring to Broadway. The Public also runs the Under the Radar festival, the Mobile Unit touring Shakespeare to underserved communities, and Public Works, which stages large-scale productions with community members.

Shakespeare in the Park

The free summer Shakespeare productions at the Delacorte have hosted performers including Meryl Streep, Al Pacino, and Denzel Washington, and are among New York's most beloved cultural traditions; tickets are distributed free on the day of performance.
