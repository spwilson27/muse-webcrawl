River Bann

The River Bann (from Irish: An Bhanna, meaning "the goddess") is the longest river in Northern Ireland, its total length being about 129 kilometres (80 mi). The river winds its way from the south-eastern corner of Northern Ireland, in the Mourne Mountains, northwards through Lough Neagh — the largest lake in the British Isles — and finally to the Atlantic Ocean at Portstewart.

Course

The Bann is conventionally divided into the Upper Bann, which flows from the Mournes through Banbridge, Portadown and Lurgan into Lough Neagh, and the Lower Bann, which flows from the north-east corner of the lough through Toome, Kilrea, Portglenone and Coleraine to the sea. The river drains a large part of Northern Ireland and has long been important for navigation, milling, eel fishing and drainage of the Lough Neagh basin.

Fisheries and history

The Lower Bann was historically famous for its salmon and eel fisheries; the eel fishery at Toome was one of the largest in Europe. The river's valley was settled in the Mesolithic — the site at Mount Sandel near Coleraine is among the earliest known settlements in Ireland. Navigation works in the 19th century made the Lower Bann navigable, and a series of locks still operates today.
