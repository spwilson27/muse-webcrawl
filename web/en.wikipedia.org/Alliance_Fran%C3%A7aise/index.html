Alliance Française

Founded: 1883, Paris
Type: Non-profit cultural organisation
Purpose: Promoting the French language and culture
Network: Around 800 chapters in 130 countries

The Alliance Française is an international organisation that aims to promote the French language and francophone culture around the world. Founded in Paris in 1883 by figures including the scientist Louis Pasteur, the diplomat Ferdinand de Lesseps, and the writer Jules Verne, it operates a network of locally run, independent chapters that teach French language courses, administer examinations such as the DELF and DALF, and host cultural events.

History

The Alliance was created during the French Third Republic as an instrument of cultural diplomacy, at a time when France sought to extend its influence abroad through language. Chapters spread rapidly through Europe, Africa, Asia, and the Americas. In 2007 the Fondation Alliance Française was established to coordinate the global network and hold the Alliance Française trademark.

Activities

Chapters offer French classes for all levels, teacher training, libraries and media centres, and programmes of concerts, exhibitions, film screenings, and debates. The network teaches several hundred thousand students each year and is a central part of France's cultural diplomacy alongside the Institut français.
