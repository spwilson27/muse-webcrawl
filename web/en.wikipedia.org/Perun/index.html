Perun

Perun is the highest god of the Slavic pantheon and the god of sky, thunder, lightning, storms, rain, law, war, fertility and oak trees. His weapons were the hammer, the axe (Perun's axe, a common protective amulet) and flaming arrows of lightning.

Cult and evidence

Perun is known from the Primary Chronicle of Kievan Rus', which records that Prince Vladimir the Great set up idols of Perun and other gods on a hill in Kyiv in 980, before his conversion to Christianity in 988, after which the idol was thrown into the Dnieper. Treaties between the Rus' and Byzantium were sworn by Perun and the cattle god Veles, his divine adversary.

Mythology

In the reconstructed Slavic myth, Perun battles the serpentine god Veles, who has stolen his cattle, water or wife — a cosmic combat mirroring the Indo-European myth of the thunder god and the serpent, comparable to Indra and Vritra or Thor and Jörmungandr. Perun's name derives from the Proto-Indo-European root *perkʷ- (oak/strike), and his figure closely parallels the Baltic thunder god Perkūnas and the Norse Thor.

Legacy

After Christianisation, many of Perun's attributes passed to the prophet Elijah, whose fiery chariot was heard as thunder. Oaks, hilltops and place names across Slavic lands retain traces of his cult, and he remains a central figure in modern Slavic neopaganism.
