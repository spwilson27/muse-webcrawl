River Nene

The River Nene is a river that flows through east England from its source near Arbury Hill in Northamptonshire, about 161 km (100 mi) to its mouth in The Wash. It is the tenth-longest river in the United Kingdom and is navigable from Northampton to the sea. Its pronunciation varies: in Northamptonshire it is usually "Nen", while around Peterborough it is commonly "Neen".

Course

The Nene rises from three sources in west Northamptonshire and flows through Northampton, Wellingborough, Rushden, Irthlingborough and Oundle, then past Peterborough before crossing the Fens to Wisbech and out into The Wash near Sutton Bridge. Below Peterborough the river is tidal, and its fenland course was extensively straightened and embanked during drainage works from the medieval period onward.

Navigation and landmarks

The river was made navigable to Northampton by locks completed in the 18th century; today it connects with the Grand Union Canal via the Northampton Arm and with the Middle Level Navigations at Peterborough, making it popular with leisure boaters. Landmarks along its course include Northampton's Carlsberg brewery area, the Nene Valley Railway near Wansford, and the Guyhirn and Cross Keys bridges in the Fens.
