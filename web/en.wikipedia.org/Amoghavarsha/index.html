Amoghavarsha I (r. 814–878 CE) was a Rashtrakuta emperor, the greatest ruler of the Rashtrakuta dynasty of peninsular India. His reign of 64 years is one of the longest precisely dated monarchical reigns in Indian history. He moved the capital to Manyakheta (modern Malkhed in Karnataka) and presided over an empire stretching across much of the Deccan.

Patronage of learning

Amoghavarsha was a noted patron of scholars and a follower of Jainism, influenced by the monk Jinasena. He is traditionally credited with authoring (or commissioning) the Kavirajamarga (c. 850), the earliest surviving work on poetics and rhetoric in the Kannada language, which remains a landmark of Kannada literature. His court hosted mathematicians such as Mahavira, author of the Ganita Sara Sangraha.

Reign

Ascending the throne as a youth after his father Govinda III, Amoghavarsha faced revolts early in his reign but consolidated the empire and generally favored peace and cultural achievement over expansion, maintaining friendly relations with the Eastern Chalukyas and other neighbors, though he fought campaigns against the Eastern Gangas and others when challenged.
