Bail Reform Act of 1984

The Bail Reform Act of 1984 is a United States federal law that amended the bail laws of the country, enacted as part of the Comprehensive Crime Control Act of 1984. It established the modern framework for determining whether a criminal defendant should be released or detained while awaiting trial in federal court.

Provisions

The Act authorized pretrial detention of a defendant based on the danger they might pose to the community, in addition to the traditional consideration of flight risk. It set out the procedures for detention hearings, the factors judges must weigh, and the conditions of release available to the court.

Constitutionality

The preventive detention provisions were upheld by the Supreme Court in United States v. Salerno (1987), which held that detention under the Act was a permissible regulatory measure rather than punishment. See also bail and pretrial detention.
