Luxembourg (Luxembourgish: Lëtzebuerg), officially the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, is a landlocked country in Western Europe bordered by Belgium, Germany, and France. It is the world's only remaining grand duchy. With an area of 2,586 km², it is one of Europe's smallest states, yet it has one of the world's highest GDPs per capita, built on steel, then banking and finance, and increasingly on technology and satellite communications.

History

The country traces its origin to the castle of Lucilinburhuc, acquired by Count Siegfried in 963. Luxembourg's fortress, the "Gibraltar of the North", was contested for centuries by Burgundy, Spain, Austria, and France before the Congress of Vienna (1815) erected it into a grand duchy in personal union with the Netherlands. The Treaty of London (1867) confirmed its independence and perpetual neutrality; full independence from the Dutch crown followed in 1890. Occupied in both world wars, Luxembourg became a founding member of NATO, the Benelux union, and the European Communities.

Politics and economy

The head of state is the Grand Duke (Henri since 2000), and government is led by a prime minister responsible to the Chamber of Deputies. Luxembourg City hosts several EU institutions, including the Court of Justice of the European Union. The country is officially trilingual, and nearly half its residents are foreign nationals, with large Portuguese, French, and Italian communities; tens of thousands of cross-border workers commute daily. Since 2020 Luxembourg has made all public transport free nationwide, a world first.
