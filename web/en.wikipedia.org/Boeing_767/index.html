The Boeing 767 is an American wide-body airliner developed and manufactured by Boeing Commercial Airplanes. It was the first wide-body twinjet built by Boeing and, alongside the narrow-body 757, was designed with a common two-crew glass cockpit. The aircraft has a conventional tail and a supercritical wing, is powered by two underwing turbofan engines, and seats roughly 181 to 375 passengers depending on variant and configuration, with a range of about 3,850 to 6,590 nautical miles (7,130 to 12,200 km).

Development and variants

Launched in 1978, the 767 first flew in September 1981 and entered service with United Airlines in 1982 — initially on transcontinental United States routes and then, with regulatory approval for extended-range twin-engine operations (ETOPS), on transatlantic services, pioneering long overwater routes for twinjets. Variants include the original 767-200, the increased-capacity 767-200ER, the stretched 767-300 and 767-300ER, the longer-range 767-400ER, the 767-300F freighter, and converted freighters. Passenger production ended in 2014, but freighter and military derivatives — the KC-46 Pegasus tanker and the E-767 — remain in production.

Operators and safety record

The 767 has been widely used by airlines including Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, All Nippon Airways, and cargo carriers FedEx Express and UPS Airlines, the largest operators of the freighter version. The type has been involved in several notable accidents, including Lauda Air Flight 004 (1991), caused by an in-flight thrust reverser deployment, which led to fleet-wide design changes; overall, later 767 variants have a strong safety record.

Specifications (767-300ER, typical)

  • Length: 54.9 m (180 ft 3 in)
  • Wingspan: 47.6 m (156 ft 1 in)
  • Typical seating: 218–269 (two/three class)
  • Range: approximately 5,980 nautical miles (11,070 km)
  • Engines: two turbofans — GE CF6, Pratt & Whitney PW4000, or Rolls-Royce RB211
