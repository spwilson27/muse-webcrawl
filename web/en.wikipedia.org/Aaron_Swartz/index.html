Aaron Swartz

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Swartz in 2011.

Aaron Hillel Swartz (November 8, 1986 – January 11, 2013) was an American computer programmer, entrepreneur, writer, political organiser, and Internet hacktivist. As a programmer, Swartz helped develop the web feed format RSS, the technical architecture for Creative Commons, the website framework web.py, and Markdown, and was a co-founder of the social news site Reddit. He also founded the online group Demand Progress, known for its campaign against the Stop Online Piracy Act.

Early life and career

Swartz was born in Highland Park, Illinois, and became involved in Internet standards as a teenager. At age 14 he co-authored the RSS 1.0 specification. He later attended Stanford University but left after his first year. In 2005 his startup Infogami was merged with Reddit through Y Combinator; Reddit was acquired by Condé Nast in 2006. Swartz then co-founded Watchdog.net and worked on the Open Library project at the Internet Archive.

Activism

Swartz was a prominent advocate of open access to scholarly and public information. In 2008 he published the Guerilla Open Access Manifesto. In 2009 he downloaded millions of documents from PACER, the US federal court records system, during a free-access trial and shared the experience of the system's costs publicly. He founded Demand Progress in 2010, which played a leading role in the successful campaign to defeat the Stop Online Piracy Act (SOPA) in 2012.

JSTOR case and death

In 2011 Swartz was arrested by MIT police on charges of breaking and entering, after connecting a laptop to the MIT network in an unlocked closet and downloading a large number of academic journal articles from JSTOR. Federal prosecutors charged him with wire fraud, computer fraud, and other offences under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, carrying potential penalties of decades in prison. JSTOR itself declined to pursue a civil suit and MIT remained neutral, but the prosecution continued. Swartz rejected a plea bargain and died by suicide in his Brooklyn apartment on January 11, 2013, at the age of 26.

Legacy

Swartz's death prompted widespread criticism of the prosecution and renewed debate about computer-crime law; members of the US Congress introduced "Aaron's Law" to reform the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. He was posthumously inducted into the Internet Hall of Fame in 2013. The documentary The Internet's Own Boy: The Story of Aaron Swartz premiered at the Sundance Film Festival in 2014. His writings were published posthumously in the collection The Boy Who Could Change the World.
