Tyrol

CountryAustria (North & East Tyrol); Italy (South Tyrol)
CapitalInnsbruck (Austrian Tyrol); Bolzano (South Tyrol)
LanguagesGerman, Italian, Ladin

Tyrol is a historical region in the Alps, in western Austria and northern Italy. It comprises the Austrian state of Tyrol (North and East Tyrol) and the Italian autonomous provinces of South Tyrol and Trentino.

Geography

The region is dominated by the Eastern Alps, including the Ötztal, Stubai, Zillertal and Hohe Tauern ranges, and by the valleys of the Inn, Adige and Drava rivers. It is a major centre for skiing and mountaineering.

History

Ruled for centuries as a county within the Habsburg domains, Tyrol was divided after the First World War: the northern part remained with Austria while the south was annexed by Italy under the Treaty of Saint-Germain in 1919. South Tyrol later gained extensive autonomy within Italy.

Culture

Tyrolean folk traditions include yodelling, Schuhplattler dancing and distinctive dress; the region is also known for the freedom fighter Andreas Hofer, who led resistance against Bavarian and French occupation in 1809.
