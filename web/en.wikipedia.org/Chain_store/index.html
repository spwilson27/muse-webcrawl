Chain store

A chain store or retail chain is a network of retail outlets that share a brand, central management and standardized business methods. Chains range from a handful of shops to global networks of thousands; they may be company-owned or operated under franchise agreements.

History

Early chains emerged in the 19th century: the Great Atlantic & Pacific Tea Company (A&P) in the United States and Singer sewing-machine stores were pioneers. In the early 20th century, chains such as Woolworth's, Sears and J. C. Penney grew rapidly by exploiting economies of scale in purchasing and distribution, provoking "anti-chain" political movements that produced measures such as the Robinson–Patman Act of 1936 in the US.

Characteristics

Chains benefit from bulk buying, shared logistics, uniform store layouts and strong brand recognition, but are sometimes criticised for displacing independent retailers and homogenising shopping districts. Restaurant chains and hotel chains apply the same model to services.
