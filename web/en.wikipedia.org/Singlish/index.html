Singlish

RegionSingapore
FamilyEnglish-based creole
InfluencesHokkien, Malay, Mandarin, Tamil, Cantonese

Singlish (a portmanteau of Singapore and English) is an English-based creole language spoken in Singapore. It incorporates vocabulary and grammar from the country's other languages, including Hokkien, Cantonese, Malay, Mandarin, and Tamil.

Features

Singlish is characterised by discourse particles such as lah, lor, leh, and meh, topic-prominent sentence structures, copula deletion, and loanwords like kiasu (afraid to lose out), makan (to eat), and shiok (very enjoyable). Linguists describe a continuum from basilectal Singlish to acrolectal Standard Singapore English.

Status and debate

The Singapore government has promoted standard English through the Speak Good English Movement, launched in 2000, fearing Singlish harms the country's competitiveness. At the same time, Singlish is widely regarded as a marker of Singaporean identity and features prominently in local film, theatre, and everyday humour.
