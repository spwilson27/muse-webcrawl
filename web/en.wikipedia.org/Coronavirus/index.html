Coronaviruses are a group of related RNA viruses that cause diseases in mammals and birds. In humans and birds, they cause respiratory tract infections that can range from mild to lethal. Mild illnesses in humans include some cases of the common cold (which is also caused by other viruses, predominantly rhinoviruses), while more lethal varieties can cause SARS, MERS and COVID-19.

Discovery

The earliest reports of a coronavirus infection in animals occurred in the late 1920s, when an acute respiratory infection of domesticated chickens emerged in North America. Human coronaviruses were discovered in the 1960s, including the strains HCoV-229E and HCoV-OC43 isolated from patients with common colds. The name "coronavirus" derives from the Latin corona, meaning "crown" or "wreath", referring to the characteristic appearance of virions under the electron microscope: a fringe of large, bulbous surface projections formed by the viral spike peplomers.

Structure and genome

Coronaviruses are enveloped viruses with a positive-sense single-stranded RNA genome. At 26 to 32 kilobases, their genome is among the largest of all RNA viruses. The virions are roughly spherical, 60–140 nm in diameter, and bear club-shaped spike (S) proteins projecting from the envelope, alongside membrane (M) and envelope (E) proteins. The nucleocapsid inside the envelope is formed from copies of the nucleocapsid (N) protein bound to the RNA genome.

Human coronaviruses

Seven coronaviruses are known to infect humans. Four — 229E, NL63, OC43 and HKU1 — circulate endemically and cause mild respiratory illness. Three zoonotic betacoronaviruses have caused major outbreaks: SARS-CoV (2002–2004), MERS-CoV (identified in 2012), and SARS-CoV-2, which emerged in late 2019 and caused the COVID-19 pandemic. Coronaviruses frequently recombine and can cross species barriers, often via intermediate hosts such as civets, dromedary camels and, for SARS-CoV-2, a still-debated animal reservoir.

Replication

Infection begins when the spike protein attaches to its complementary host cell receptor — for example ACE2 for SARS-CoV and SARS-CoV-2, DPP4 for MERS-CoV. After entry, the viral genome is translated into the replicase polyproteins, which assemble replication–transcription complexes producing new genomic and subgenomic RNAs. New virions are assembled in the endoplasmic reticulum–Golgi intermediate compartment and released by exocytosis.
