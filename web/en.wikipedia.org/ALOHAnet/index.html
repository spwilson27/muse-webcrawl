ALOHAnet

Developed1970–1971, University of Hawaii
Key peopleNorman Abramson
Known forALOHA random-access protocol

ALOHAnet, also known as the ALOHA System, was a pioneering computer networking system developed at the University of Hawaii under Norman Abramson. It became operational in 1971 and was the first public demonstration of a wireless packet data network.

The ALOHA protocol

ALOHAnet connected terminals across the Hawaiian islands by radio to a central computer. Its medium-access method — transmit whenever data is ready, and retransmit after a random delay if an acknowledgement is not received — was simple and influential: "pure ALOHA" achieves at most about 18% channel utilization, while "slotted ALOHA" doubles that.

Legacy

The ALOHA protocol directly influenced the development of Ethernet's collision handling and later wireless standards, and ALOHAnet was one of the first networks connected to ARPANET.
