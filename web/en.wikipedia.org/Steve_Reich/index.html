Stephen Michael Reich (born October 3, 1936) is an American composer known for his contribution to the development of minimal music in the mid to late 1960s. Reich's work is marked by its use of repetitive figures, slow harmonic rhythm, and canons. His innovations include using tape loops to create phasing patterns, as in It's Gonna Rain (1965) and Come Out (1966), and exploring speech melody in works such as Different Trains (1988).

Early life and education

Born in New York City, Reich studied philosophy at Cornell (BA 1957) and composition at Juilliard and Mills College, where he worked with Darius Milhaud and Luciano Berio. A trip to Ghana in 1970 to study Ewe drumming, and study of Balinese gamelan, shaped his rhythmic language.

Major works

Key compositions include Piano Phase (1967), Clapping Music (1972), Music for 18 Musicians (1974–76), Tehillim (1981), Different Trains (1988) for string quartet and recorded speech, the video operas The Cave (1993) and Three Tales (2002) with Beryl Korot, and Double Sextet (2007), which won the Pulitzer Prize for Music in 2009.

Influence

Reich influenced generations of composers and popular musicians, from Philip Glass to Brian Eno and Radiohead's Jonny Greenwood; his techniques permeate electronic dance music and film scoring.
