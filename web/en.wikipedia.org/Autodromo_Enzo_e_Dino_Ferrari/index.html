The circuit at Imola, commonly known simply as Imola.

The Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari, better known as Imola, is a 4.909 km (3.050 mi) motor racing circuit in the town of Imola, in the Emilia-Romagna region of Italy, 40 kilometres (25 mi) east of Bologna. It is one of the few major international circuits to run in an anti-clockwise direction. The circuit is named after Enzo Ferrari, the founder of Ferrari, and his son Alfredo "Dino" Ferrari.

History

Opened in 1953, the circuit originally ran through the surrounding countryside on public roads before becoming a permanent track. Imola hosted the Italian Grand Prix in 1980 and then the San Marino Grand Prix from 1981 to 2006, the name reflecting the nearby microstate since Italy already had its own Grand Prix at Monza.

The 1994 San Marino Grand Prix

Imola is remembered most somberly for the weekend of the 1994 San Marino Grand Prix, during which Austrian driver Roland Ratzenberger was killed in qualifying and the three-time World Champion Ayrton Senna died in a crash at the Tamburello corner during the race. The tragedies prompted sweeping safety reforms in Formula One and modifications to the circuit, including chicanes at Tamburello and Villeneuve.

Return to Formula One

After a 14-year absence, Imola returned to the Formula One calendar in 2020 as the Emilia Romagna Grand Prix. The 2023 race was cancelled because of severe flooding in the region.
