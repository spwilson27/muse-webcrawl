Electroweak interaction

In particle physics, the electroweak interaction is the unified description of two of the four known fundamental interactions of nature: electromagnetism and the weak interaction.

Theory

The theory was developed in the 1960s by Sheldon Glashow, Abdus Salam, and Steven Weinberg, who shared the 1979 Nobel Prize in Physics. It describes the electromagnetic and weak forces as aspects of a single gauge theory based on the symmetry group SU(2) × U(1).

Force carriers

The interaction is mediated by four gauge bosons: the massless photon of electromagnetism and the massive W+, W−, and Z bosons of the weak interaction. The masses arise through the Higgs mechanism, in which the electroweak symmetry is spontaneously broken.

Experimental confirmation

Neutral weak currents were observed at CERN in 1973, and the W and Z bosons were discovered there in 1983 with masses matching predictions. The Higgs boson, the remaining predicted particle, was discovered in 2012.
