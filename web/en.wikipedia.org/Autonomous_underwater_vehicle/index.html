Autonomous underwater vehicle

An autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) is a robot that travels underwater without requiring continuous input from an operator. Unlike remotely operated vehicles (ROVs), AUVs carry their own power supply and control system, following pre-programmed missions and navigating with inertial sensors, Doppler velocity logs, and acoustic positioning.

Design

Most AUVs are torpedo-shaped for efficient cruising, propelled by a propeller and steered by fins; underwater gliders instead change buoyancy to move slowly for weeks or months. Because radio waves do not travel far underwater, AUVs communicate acoustically at low data rates and typically upload data when they surface.

Applications

AUVs are used for seafloor mapping with sonar, oceanographic measurement, pipeline and cable inspection, environmental monitoring, search for wreckage, and military tasks such as mine countermeasures. Commercial surveys for the offshore energy industry were an early driver of the technology.
