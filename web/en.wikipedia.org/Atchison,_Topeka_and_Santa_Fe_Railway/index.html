The Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway (reporting mark ATSF), often shortened to just the Santa Fe, was one of the larger railroads in the United States. The railroad was chartered in February 1859 to serve the cities of Atchison and Topeka in Kansas and Santa Fe in New Mexico. It grew into a system of some 13,000 miles connecting Chicago with the Gulf of Mexico and the Pacific coast, serving Kansas, Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona, California, Oklahoma and Texas.

History

Construction began in Topeka in 1869; the line reached the Colorado state line in 1872 and famously won the race for Raton Pass against the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad in 1878, reaching Albuquerque in 1880 and Deming in 1881, where it linked with the Southern Pacific to form the second transcontinental route. In the 1880s and 1890s the Santa Fe expanded aggressively into California, acquiring its own route to Los Angeles and San Diego and later to the San Francisco Bay Area. After a receivership in the depression of the 1890s, the reorganised company became known for well-engineered lines, famous passenger trains, and its partnership with Fred Harvey's trackside "Harvey House" hotels and restaurants.

Passenger service

The Santa Fe's flagship was the Super Chief, launched in 1936 between Chicago and Los Angeles — the first diesel-powered all-Pullman streamliner — celebrated as "the train of the stars" for its Hollywood clientele. Other famous trains included the Chief, El Capitan (whose double-deck Hi-Level cars pioneered modern bilevel equipment), the San Francisco Chief and the Texas Chief. Amtrak took over passenger operations in 1971 but continued to use the Super Chief name (later Southwest Chief) for many years.

Merger

A proposed merger with the Southern Pacific in the 1980s was rejected by the Interstate Commerce Commission, and the two companies were forced to divest. On 31 December 1996 the Santa Fe merged with the Burlington Northern Railroad to form the Burlington Northern and Santa Fe Railway, today's BNSF Railway, ending 137 years of independent operation. The Santa Fe's "Warbonnet" livery remains one of the most recognised paint schemes in railroading.
