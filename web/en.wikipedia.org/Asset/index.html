Asset

In financial accounting, an asset is any resource owned or controlled by a business or an individual that is expected to produce future economic benefits. Formally, under the IFRS framework, an asset is a present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events, where an economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits. Assets appear on the balance sheet and, together with liabilities, determine the owner's equity: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

Classification

Assets are commonly classified by convertibility and use. Current assets are expected to be converted to cash or consumed within one year — cash and equivalents, accounts receivable, inventory and short-term investments. Non-current (long-term) assets include property, plant and equipment, long-term investments and intangible assets such as patents, trademarks and goodwill. Assets may also be divided into tangible assets (physical things such as buildings and machinery) and intangible assets (rights and claims without physical form). Financial assets — stocks, bonds and bank deposits — represent claims on the assets of issuers.

Accounting treatment

Tangible long-term assets are generally recorded at cost and depreciated over their useful lives; intangible assets with finite lives are amortized. Assets are tested for impairment when their recoverable amount falls below carrying value.

Other uses

Outside accounting, "asset" is used broadly: in economics for anything of value, in computing and game development for digital resources (images, models, sounds), in intelligence for a source or agent, and in everyday language for any useful quality or possession.
