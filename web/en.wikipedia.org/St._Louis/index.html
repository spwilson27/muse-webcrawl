St. Louis is an independent city in the U.S. state of Missouri, located on the west bank of the Mississippi River just below its confluence with the Missouri River. Founded by French fur traders in 1764, it grew into the "Gateway to the West" and remains a major inland port and metropolitan centre.

History

Pierre Laclède and Auguste Chouteau founded St. Louis as a fur-trading post in 1764, naming it for King Louis IX of France. The city passed to Spain, back to France, and then to the United States in the Louisiana Purchase of 1803; the Lewis and Clark Expedition set out from nearby in 1804. In the 19th century St. Louis became a great river port and manufacturing centre, and in 1904 it hosted both the Louisiana Purchase Exposition (World's Fair) and the Summer Olympic Games. The city reached its peak population of over 850,000 in 1950 before decades of suburbanisation.

Gateway Arch

The 192-metre (630-foot) stainless-steel Gateway Arch, designed by Eero Saarinen and completed in 1965, is the tallest arch in the world and the symbol of the city, commemorating westward expansion. It anchors Gateway Arch National Park along the riverfront.

Economy and culture

St. Louis is a centre for healthcare, biotechnology, finance and brewing — historically home to Anheuser-Busch. It is known for the St. Louis Cardinals baseball team, the St. Louis Blues hockey team, Forest Park, toasted ravioli and gooey butter cake, and a rich blues and ragtime musical heritage associated with figures such as Scott Joplin and Chuck Berry.
