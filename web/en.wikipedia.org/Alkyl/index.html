Alkyl

In organic chemistry, an alkyl group is an alkane missing one hydrogen atom. The term "alkyl" is intentionally unspecific to include many possible substitutions, and an alkyl group can be considered a functional group or a substituent.

Nomenclature

Acyclic alkyl groups have the general formula CnH2n+1 and are named by replacing the -ane suffix of the parent alkane with -yl: methyl (CH3–), ethyl (C2H5–), propyl, butyl, and so on. Cyclic alkyl groups have the formula CnH2n−1, e.g. cyclopropyl and cyclohexyl.

Classification

Alkyl groups are classified as primary, secondary, or tertiary according to whether the carbon atom bonded to the rest of the molecule is attached to one, two, or three other carbons. Related species include alkyl radicals, carbocations (such as the tert-butyl cation), and carbanions, which appear as reactive intermediates in many reactions.

Occurrence

Alkyl groups occur throughout organic molecules — in fuels, pharmaceuticals, and polymers — and alkylation reactions that attach them are fundamental industrial processes, for example in refining petroleum to produce high-octane gasoline components.
