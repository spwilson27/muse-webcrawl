Ambrogio Spinola

Ambrogio Spinola Doria, 1st Marquis of Los Balbases
Born: 1569, Genoa
Died: 25 September 1630, Castelnuovo Scrivia
Allegiance: Spanish Empire
Service: Army of Flanders

Ambrogio Spinola Doria, 1st Marquis of Los Balbases (1569 – 25 September 1630) was a Genoese nobleman and soldier who served the Spanish crown as commander of the Army of Flanders during the Eighty Years' War. He is regarded as one of the most capable generals of his age, best remembered for the Siege of Ostend and the capture of Breda in 1625.

Career in Flanders

From one of Genoa's great banking families, Spinola raised troops at his own expense and took command in the Spanish Netherlands in 1602. After a three-year siege he captured Ostend in 1604, though at enormous cost. His campaigns in the eastern Netherlands and Germany in 1605–1606 and his financial skill kept Spanish forces in the field despite repeated royal bankruptcies; he helped negotiate the Twelve Years' Truce of 1609. When war resumed, he commanded in the Palatinate and took Breda after an eleven-month siege in 1625 — the event immortalised in Velázquez's painting The Surrender of Breda, in which Spinola receives the keys of the city with notable courtesy.

Later life

Denied adequate funds and support from Madrid, Spinola returned to Italy, where he died in 1630 during the War of the Mantuan Succession. His reputation for honourable conduct toward defeated enemies made him a byword for chivalric generalship.
