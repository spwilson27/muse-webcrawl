Presidency of Franklin D. Roosevelt
March 4, 1933 – April 12, 1945
President: Franklin D. Roosevelt
Party: Democratic
Preceded by: Presidency of Herbert Hoover
Succeeded by: Presidency of Harry S. Truman

The presidency of Franklin D. Roosevelt began on March 4, 1933, when he was inaugurated as the 32nd president of the United States, and ended with his death in office on April 12, 1945. Roosevelt, a Democrat from New York, is the only president to have served more than two terms, winning a record four presidential elections. His presidency spanned the Great Depression and most of World War II.

The New Deal

Taking office amid a banking crisis and unemployment near 25 percent, Roosevelt launched the New Deal, a sweeping program of relief, recovery, and reform. The First New Deal (1933–1934) included the Emergency Banking Act, the Civilian Conservation Corps, the Tennessee Valley Authority, and the National Industrial Recovery Act. The Second New Deal (1935–1936) produced the Social Security Act, the Wagner Act protecting organized labor, and the Works Progress Administration, which employed millions on public works projects.

World War II

After initially maintaining neutrality, Roosevelt supported the Allies through the Lend-Lease program. Following the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, the United States entered World War II. Roosevelt directed the wartime mobilization, worked closely with Winston Churchill and Joseph Stalin in the Grand Alliance, and oversaw the strategy that led to Allied victory in Europe and the Pacific, though he died weeks before Germany's surrender.

Legacy

Roosevelt is consistently ranked by historians among the three greatest American presidents, alongside George Washington and Abraham Lincoln. His presidency permanently expanded the role of the federal government in American economic life and established the modern liberal political order that dominated American politics for decades.
