Homing (biology)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Homing is the inherent ability of an animal to navigate towards an original location through unfamiliar areas. This may be when returning to a home territory, breeding ground, or a born location. Homing phenomena have been utilised by humans for many years for communication, as in the case of homing pigeons, and for sport, while scientific studies of homing have illuminated the sensory basis of animal navigation.

Animals noted for homing include salmon, which return from the ocean to spawn in the stream where they hatched; sea turtles, whose females return to their natal beaches to lay eggs; homing pigeons selectively bred from the rock dove; and many migratory birds that return to the same nesting territories year after year. Dogs and cats are also popularly reported to travel long distances toward home, though such anecdotes are harder to verify scientifically.

Mechanisms

Several mechanisms may act alone or in combination. Magnetoreception — sensitivity to the Earth's magnetic field — provides compass and possibly map information in birds, turtles, and fish; in pigeons and some other birds, magnetite-containing structures and light-dependent cryptochrome reactions in the retina have both been implicated. Olfactory navigation is well supported in pigeons, which learn a map of odours carried on winds, and in salmon, which imprint on the scent of their natal stream. Animals also use the sun and stars as compasses, polarised light patterns, landmarks, and infrasound. Experiments in which sensory channels are disrupted — for example, fitting pigeons with frosted lenses or disrupting their sense of smell — have been central to disentangling these cues.

Human use

Homing pigeons carried messages in the ancient world and remained militarily important through both World Wars; individual birds such as Cher Ami were decorated for delivering messages that saved soldiers' lives. Racing pigeons back to their home lofts remains a popular sport. Biologists also exploit homing experimentally: translocated animals that attempt to return home reveal navigational abilities, while failure to home after displacement is used to test hypotheses about navigation cues.
