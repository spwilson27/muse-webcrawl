Russia has competed at the modern Olympic Games under several designations over more than a century. Athletes from the Russian Empire first participated at the 1900 Summer Olympics in Paris, and the empire sent teams to the Games of 1908 and 1912. After the October Revolution, Russian athletes competed as part of the Soviet Union from 1952 to 1988, and as the Unified Team at the 1992 Games following the dissolution of the USSR.

The Russian Federation competed in its own right beginning with the 1994 Winter Olympics in Lillehammer and the 1996 Summer Olympics in Atlanta. Following doping sanctions, Russian athletes competed as "Olympic Athletes from Russia" at the 2018 Winter Olympics, and under the name "ROC" (Russian Olympic Committee) at the Tokyo 2020 and Beijing 2022 Games.

History

Russian Empire

The Russian Empire was one of the nations represented at the second modern Olympiad in Paris in 1900, though it did not win a medal there. At the 1908 London Games, figure skater Nikolai Panin won Russia's first Olympic gold medal, in the special figures event. Russian shooters and wrestlers also won medals at the 1912 Stockholm Games.

Soviet era

From 1952 to 1988, Russian athletes formed the backbone of the Soviet Union team, which became one of the two dominant forces in Olympic sport alongside the United States. The USSR topped the medal table at numerous Summer and Winter Games during this period.

Russian Federation

As an independent team, Russia finished near the top of the medal standings at every Games it attended through 2016. Russia hosted the 2014 Winter Olympics in Sochi, where it initially topped the medal table before a series of doping disqualifications arising from the state-sponsored doping scandal documented by the McLaren Report. Russia also hosted the Summer Games once under the Soviet flag, at Moscow 1980.

Sanctions and neutral participation

In December 2019, the World Anti-Doping Agency banned Russia from major international sporting events for four years (later reduced to two by the Court of Arbitration for Sport) over manipulation of laboratory data. Russian athletes who could prove they were clean competed under neutral designations. After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the International Olympic Committee recommended the exclusion of Russian and Belarusian athletes; a limited number later competed as Individual Neutral Athletes at the Paris 2024 Games.
