In algebraic and differential geometry, a Calabi–Yau manifold is a compact complex manifold with vanishing first Chern class. By Yau's solution of the Calabi conjecture, such a manifold admits a Ricci-flat Kähler metric. Calabi–Yau manifolds are of central importance in string theory, where the extra dimensions of spacetime are thought to be compactified on a six-dimensional Calabi–Yau space, whose shape determines the physical properties of the observable universe.

History

Eugenio Calabi conjectured in 1954 that a compact Kähler manifold with vanishing first Chern class carries a Ricci-flat metric. Shing-Tung Yau proved the conjecture in 1977, work that contributed to his Fields Medal in 1982. Interest exploded in 1985 when Philip Candelas, Gary Horowitz, Andrew Strominger and Edward Witten showed that compactifying ten-dimensional superstring theory on a Calabi–Yau threefold yields a four-dimensional theory with the extended supersymmetry needed for realistic physics.

Examples and classification

In one complex dimension, the only Calabi–Yau manifold is the torus; in two dimensions they are tori and K3 surfaces. In three dimensions a vast number of examples exist, constructed for example as hypersurfaces in weighted projective spaces; complete classification is an open problem. The topology of a threefold is largely captured by its Hodge numbers, which count its independent cycles.

Mirror symmetry

Calabi–Yau manifolds come in mirror pairs: two topologically different manifolds can yield identical physics when used for compactification, exchanging quantities that are easy to compute on one side with hard ones on the other. Mirror symmetry has led to striking predictions in enumerative geometry, such as counts of rational curves on the quintic threefold, and remains a major area of research connecting physics and pure mathematics.
