Android version history

The version history of the Android operating system began with the public release of Android 1.0 in September 2008. Developed by Google and the Open Handset Alliance, Android versions were named after confectionery in alphabetical order from 1.5 Cupcake to 9 Pie; from Android 10 onward the public names are simply numbers, though dessert codenames continue internally.

The first commercial device, the HTC Dream, launched in October 2008. Honeycomb was a tablet-only branch; Ice Cream Sandwich reunified phone and tablet interfaces.

Material design and beyond

Lollipop (2014) introduced the Material Design language and the Android Runtime (ART). Marshmallow (6.0, 2015) added runtime permissions and Doze; Nougat (7.0, 2016) split-screen multitasking; Oreo (8.0, 2017) Project Treble's modular architecture; and Pie (9, 2018) gesture navigation and Digital Wellbeing. Android 10 (2019) dropped public dessert names and added a system-wide dark theme.

Numbered era

Android 11 (2020) refined conversations and privacy controls; Android 12 (2021) debuted the expressive “Material You” design; Android 13 (2022) expanded per-app languages and the photo picker; Android 14 (2023) and Android 15 (2024) continued incremental improvements in security, large-screen support, and satellite connectivity. Pre-release development builds are distributed several times a year ahead of each autumn launch.
