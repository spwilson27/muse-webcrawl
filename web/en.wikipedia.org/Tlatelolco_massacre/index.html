Tlatelolco massacre

The Tlatelolco massacre was the killing of student and civilian protesters by Mexican government forces on 2 October 1968, in the Plaza de las Tres Culturas in the Tlatelolco district of Mexico City. It took place ten days before the opening of the 1968 Summer Olympics in Mexico City.

Background

Throughout the summer of 1968, a student movement had protested against the authoritarian rule of the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) under President Gustavo Díaz Ordaz, demanding democratic reforms and an end to police repression. The government, determined to present a stable image before the Olympics, occupied the National University and arrested movement leaders.

The massacre

On the evening of 2 October, thousands of demonstrators gathered in the plaza. Soldiers surrounded the area, and members of the plainclothes Olympia Battalion signalled the attack; troops then opened fire. The death toll remains disputed: official figures initially claimed only a few dozen deaths, while journalists and later investigations estimated several hundred. Documents declassified decades later confirmed government planning and the role of snipers positioned to provoke the army's response.

Legacy

The massacre became a defining trauma in modern Mexican history, commemorated annually on 2 October under the slogan "2 de octubre no se olvida" ("October 2 is not forgotten"). It galvanised opposition movements and eroded the legitimacy of the PRI regime.
