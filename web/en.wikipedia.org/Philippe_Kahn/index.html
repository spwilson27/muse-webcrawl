Philippe Kahn (born March 16, 1952) is a French-American technology entrepreneur and inventor. He founded Borland, a major software company of the personal computer era, as well as Starfish Software, LightSurf Technologies, and Fullpower Technologies. He is widely credited with creating the first camera phone solution, sharing the first camera phone photograph in 1997.

Early life and Borland

Kahn was born in Paris and studied mathematics at ETH Zurich and the University of Nice. He emigrated to the United States and in 1982 founded Borland International in Scotts Valley, California. Borland's first product, Turbo Pascal, was a breakthrough in price and performance for programming tools and sold by mail order. Under Kahn, Borland grew into a leading PC software firm with products such as Sidekick, Reflex, Quattro Pro, dBASE, and Paradox, competing directly with Microsoft and Lotus.

Camera phone

On June 11, 1997, while his wife was giving birth to their daughter, Kahn jury-rigged a connection between a mobile phone and a digital camera so that he could share photos of the newborn instantly with friends and family around the world. The infrastructure he improvised became the basis of picture messaging; the image he took that day is regarded as the first camera phone photograph shared publicly.

Later ventures

After leaving Borland, Kahn founded Starfish Software (creator of the TrueSync synchronization software), LightSurf Technologies (a multimedia messaging company later acquired by Verisign), and Fullpower Technologies, which works on wearable and sleep-tracking technology. An accomplished sailor, Kahn has also set records and won major offshore sailing races.
