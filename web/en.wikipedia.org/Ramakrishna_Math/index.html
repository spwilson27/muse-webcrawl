Ramakrishna Math

Ramakrishna Math
Type: Monastic order
Founded: 1886, Baranagar, Kolkata
Headquarters: Belur Math, Howrah, India
Founder: Swami Vivekananda (with brother disciples)

Ramakrishna Math is a monastic order for men founded by the disciples of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa (1836–1886), the Bengali saint of Dakshineswar. After Ramakrishna's death, his young monastic disciples, led by Swami Vivekananda, formed a fellowship that first settled at Baranagar in 1886 and later moved to Alambazar and finally to Belur, where Belur Math was consecrated in 1898.

Purpose and organisation

The Math is the monastic wing of the Ramakrishna movement; its sister organisation, the Ramakrishna Mission, conducts the movement's charitable and educational work. Both have their headquarters at Belur Math and share the same administrative body, though they are legally distinct. The Math's motto, drawn from Vivekananda's teaching, is "For one's own salvation and for the welfare of the world" (Atmano mokshartham jagat hitaya cha).

Activities

Monks of the order devote themselves to spiritual practice, teaching Vedanta, publishing literature on Ramakrishna, Vivekananda and Sarada Devi, and maintaining temples and centres in India and abroad. The order runs maths (monasteries) and sub-centres across the world, propagates the ideals of harmony of religions, and trains novices through a period of probation and brahmacharya before sannyasa.

Presidents

The president of the Math and Mission is elected by the board of trustees. Past presidents include Swami Brahmananda, Swami Shivananda and Swami Atmasthanandaji. The general secretary administers day-to-day affairs from Belur.
