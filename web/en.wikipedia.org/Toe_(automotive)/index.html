Toe (automotive)

In automotive engineering, toe is the symmetric angle that each wheel makes with the longitudinal axis of the vehicle, viewed from above. It is one of the principal wheel alignment parameters, along with camber and caster.

Toe-in and toe-out

When the fronts of a pair of wheels point toward each other, the setting is called toe-in (positive toe); when they point away from each other it is toe-out (negative toe). Toe is usually measured as the difference in distance between the front and rear edges of the rims, or as an angle in degrees or fractions of a degree.

Effects

Toe strongly influences tyre wear and directional stability. Excessive toe-in or toe-out scrubs the tyres and causes rapid, feathered tread wear. Road cars typically use a small amount of static toe-in at the rear for stability and slight toe-in or near-zero toe at the front; some front-wheel-drive cars use slight toe-out to compensate for compliance under power. Racing setups may use toe-out at the front to quicken turn-in response, at the cost of straight-line stability.
