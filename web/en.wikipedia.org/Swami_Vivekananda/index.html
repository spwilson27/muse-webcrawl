Swami Vivekananda

Born: Narendranath Datta, 12 January 1863, Calcutta, British India
Died: 4 July 1902, Belur Math, Bengal
Guru: Ramakrishna
Founded: Ramakrishna Mission; Ramakrishna Math

Swami Vivekananda (12 January 1863 – 4 July 1902), born Narendranath Datta, was an Indian Hindu monk, philosopher, author, and religious teacher. He was a chief disciple of the Indian mystic Ramakrishna and a key figure in the introduction of the Indian philosophies of Vedanta and Yoga to the Western world.

Chicago and the West

Vivekananda rose to prominence with his speech beginning "Sisters and brothers of America..." at the Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago in 1893, where he introduced Hinduism to an American audience and called for religious tolerance and an end to fanaticism. He subsequently lectured widely in the United States and England and founded the Vedanta Society of New York.

Work in India

Returning to India in 1897, he founded the Ramakrishna Mission, dedicated to social service and education, and the Ramakrishna Math, a monastic order headquartered at Belur Math near Kolkata. His teachings emphasised the divinity of the soul, service to humanity as worship of God ("Shiva in the poor"), and the harmony of religions. His birthday is celebrated in India as National Youth Day.
