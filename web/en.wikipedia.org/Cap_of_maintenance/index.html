Cap of maintenance
Ceremonial crimson velvet cap lined with ermine, borne before sovereigns and in heraldry

A cap of maintenance is a ceremonial cap of crimson velvet lined with ermine, worn or carried as a symbol of high rank. In British tradition it is borne before the sovereign at the State Opening of Parliament by a peer.

Heraldry

In heraldry, a cap of maintenance appears beneath the helmet in some coats of arms, notably in Scottish heraldry, and famously supports the red dragon of Wales. It is said to have been granted by the pope to certain princes as a mark of favour.

History

Pope Julius II presented a cap of maintenance and a sword to Henry VIII in 1513; similar caps became part of the regalia of English and Scottish sovereigns.
