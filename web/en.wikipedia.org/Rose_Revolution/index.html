Rose Revolution

Rose Revolution
Date: November 2003
Location: Georgia
Result: Resignation of President Eduard Shevardnadze; election of Mikheil Saakashvili

The Rose Revolution was a nonviolent change of power in Georgia in November 2003, in which President Eduard Shevardnadze was forced to resign following mass protests over disputed parliamentary elections. It is regarded as the first of the "colour revolutions" in the post-Soviet states.

Events

After the parliamentary election of November 2, 2003, which observers criticised as flawed, opposition parties led by Mikheil Saakashvili, Nino Burjanadze, and Zurab Zhvania mobilised weeks of demonstrations in Tbilisi. On November 22, protesters led by Saakashvili — carrying roses — stormed the parliament during Shevardnadze's opening speech. Shevardnadze resigned the following day after negotiations involving Russian foreign minister Igor Ivanov, avoiding bloodshed.

Aftermath

Burjanadze became acting president, and Saakashvili won the January 2004 presidential election overwhelmingly. The new government pursued anti-corruption reforms, market liberalisation, and closer ties with the United States, NATO, and the European Union.
