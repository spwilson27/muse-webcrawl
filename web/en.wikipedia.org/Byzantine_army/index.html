Byzantine army

The Byzantine army was the military body of the Byzantine (Eastern Roman) Empire, inheriting the traditions of the Roman army and evolving over the empire's thousand-year history, from the 5th century to 1453, to meet Persian, Arab, Slav, Turkish and Western enemies.

Thematic system

From the 7th century the empire was defended largely by the themes — military districts whose soldier-farmers served under a strategos — supplemented by the professional, centrally paid tagmata regiments stationed near Constantinople. Heavy cavalry cataphracts, versatile infantry and horse archers formed the core of field armies, guided by an unusually rich literature of military manuals.

Later developments

After the losses of the 11th century, the Komnenian emperors rebuilt the army around pronoia land grants, imperial guards such as the Varangians, and foreign mercenaries. The shrinking empire of the Palaiologan period could field only small forces, supplemented by Catalan and Turkish mercenaries, until the fall of Constantinople in 1453.
