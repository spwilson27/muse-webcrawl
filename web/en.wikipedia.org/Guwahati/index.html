Guwahati is the largest city in the Indian state of Assam and the largest metropolis in northeastern India. It lies on the south bank of the Brahmaputra River, nestled among the hills of the Shillong Plateau. Dispur, the capital of Assam, is a locality within the city. Guwahati serves as the principal commercial, educational and transport hub of the North-East region and is often called the "Gateway to North East India".

The ancient cities of Pragjyotishpura and Durjaya, located in present-day Guwahati, were capitals of the ancient kingdom of Kamarupa. The city is home to the famous Kamakhya Temple, one of the 51 Shakti Peethas, situated atop the Nilachal Hill, which draws pilgrims from across India, especially during the Ambubachi Mela festival.

History

Archaeological evidence suggests habitation in the region since at least the 4th century BCE. Guwahati features prominently in the Mahabharata as Pragjyotishpura, the capital of King Bhagadatta. The city changed hands repeatedly between the Ahom kingdom and the Mughals during the 17th century; the decisive Battle of Saraighat in 1671 saw the Ahom general Lachit Borphukan defeat the Mughal army on the Brahmaputra near the city.

Geography

The city is surrounded by hills and the Brahmaputra flows along its northern edge. Umananda Island, in the middle of the river, is one of the smallest inhabited river islands in the world. The climate is humid subtropical, with monsoon rains from June to September.

Economy and transport

Guwahati is a major river port and trade centre. The Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi International Airport and the Guwahati railway station connect the city to the rest of India. It hosts the Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati, Gauhati University and the Assam State Zoo.
