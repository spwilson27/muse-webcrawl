Dirk Gently

Dirk Gently (real name Svlad Cjelli) is a fictional character created by the English writer Douglas Adams. He is a self-described "holistic detective", operating on the principle of the fundamental interconnectedness of all things, which he uses to justify expenses and methods that his clients rarely appreciate.

The novels

Dirk appears in two completed novels: Dirk Gently's Holistic Detective Agency (1987) and The Long Dark Tea-Time of the Soul (1988). A third book, The Salmon of Doubt, was left unfinished at Adams's death in 2001 and was published posthumously in fragmentary form in 2002.

Character

Operating from a shabby office in Islington, London, Dirk refuses conventional deductive methods, insisting instead that following apparently irrelevant threads — a missing cat, an electric monk, a sofa stuck in a stairwell — will reveal the whole case. He studied at St Cedd's College, Cambridge, where his rumour-spreading about exam papers led to his imprisonment for fraud.

Adaptations

The character has been adapted for radio, stage, comics, and twice for television: a 2010–2012 BBC Four series starring Stephen Mangan, and a 2016–2017 BBC America/Netflix series, Dirk Gently's Holistic Detective Agency, starring Samuel Barnett.
