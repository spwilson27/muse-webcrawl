Long John Silver

Long John Silver is a fictional character and the main antagonist in the novel Treasure Island (1883) by Robert Louis Stevenson. Silver is the one-legged ship's cook aboard the Hispaniola, secretly the leader of the pirates seeking Captain Flint's buried treasure, and the most richly drawn character in the book — charming, cunning and ambiguous. He has become the archetype of the popular image of a pirate.

Character

Silver lost his leg in naval service — he claims under Admiral Hawke — and walks with a crutch, carrying his parrot, Captain Flint (named mockingly after his former captain), on his shoulder. As cook ("Barbecue" to his shipmates) he befriends the cabin boy and narrator Jim Hawkins, who initially admires him, before overhearing his plot to mutiny once the treasure is found. Silver's blend of warmth and ruthlessness — he murders a loyal sailor yet protects Jim — gives the novel much of his moral complexity. At the novel's end he escapes with a portion of treasure, and Jim is relieved never to see him again.

In popular culture

Silver has shaped the stereotypical pirate: the West Country "arrr" accent (cemented by Robert Newton's performances in Disney's 1950 Treasure Island and sequels), the parrot, the peg leg and phrases like "shiver my timbers" (Stevenson's own invention). He has been portrayed by many actors on stage and screen and inspired prequels, notably the television series Black Sails, in which he is a central character. The American seafood restaurant chain Long John Silver's is named after him.
