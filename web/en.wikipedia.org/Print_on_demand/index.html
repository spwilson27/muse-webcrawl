Print on demand

Print on demand (POD) is a printing technology and business process in which book copies (or other documents) are not printed until the company receives an order, allowing prints of single or small quantities. While build to order has been an established business model in many other industries, "print on demand" developed only after digital printing began, because it was not economical to print single copies using traditional lithography or letterpress offset printing.

History

Print on demand emerged in the 1990s as digital printing technology matured. Lightning Source, founded in 1997, became one of the largest POD providers, later part of the Ingram Content Group. Amazon's CreateSpace (later merged into Kindle Direct Publishing) brought POD within reach of self-publishing authors.

Process

In a typical workflow, a publisher supplies digital files (usually PDF) for the book's interior and cover. When a customer places an order, the file is sent to a printer near the customer, printed, bound, and shipped — often within days. Because no inventory is warehoused, backlist titles can remain perpetually "in print" and the financial risk of unsold stock is eliminated.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include low setup costs, no warehousing, and easy updating of content. The per-unit cost, however, is higher than for large offset print runs, and print quality and binding options can be more limited.
