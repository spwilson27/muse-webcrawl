Taxicab number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the nth taxicab number, typically denoted Ta(n) or Taxicab(n), is the smallest number that can be expressed as a sum of two positive cube numbers in n distinct ways. The name derives from a famous anecdote involving the mathematicians G. H. Hardy and Srinivasa Ramanujan and the number 1729, the cab number of the taxi Hardy had ridden to visit Ramanujan in hospital.

The Hardy–Ramanujan number 1729

Hardy remarked that 1729 seemed "rather a dull number", to which Ramanujan replied that it was very interesting, being the smallest number expressible as the sum of two cubes in two different ways: 1729 = 13 + 123 = 93 + 103. Thus Ta(2) = 1729. The smallest taxicab number Ta(1) is 2 = 13 + 13.

Known values

The first taxicab numbers are Ta(1) = 2, Ta(2) = 1729, Ta(3) = 87,539,319, Ta(4) = 6,963,472,309,248, and Ta(5) = 48,988,659,276,962,496; Ta(6) is a 49-digit number found by computer search. Bernard Frénicle de Bessy knew examples of numbers expressible as sums of two cubes in several ways as early as 1657, and Leonhard Euler gave a parametric family in the eighteenth century.
