Project Orion (nuclear propulsion)

CountryUnited States
OrganizationGeneral Atomics; U.S. Air Force; DARPA
Period1958–1965
PropulsionNuclear pulse propulsion

Project Orion was a study conducted in the late 1950s and early 1960s by the United States Air Force, DARPA, and General Atomics to design a spacecraft propelled by repeated nuclear explosions behind the vehicle — a technique known as nuclear pulse propulsion. The project was led by physicists Ted Taylor and Freeman Dyson. The Orion concept promised both high thrust and high specific impulse, potentially enabling crewed missions to Mars and Saturn with 1960s-era technology.

Design

The Orion spacecraft consisted of a large pusher plate mounted on shock absorbers at the rear of the vehicle. Small nuclear devices would be ejected and detonated a short distance behind the plate; the expanding plasma would strike the plate and push the craft forward. The design studies ranged from small vehicles of a few hundred tonnes to enormous "super Orions" of millions of tonnes. Chemical-explosive test models, using conventional charges, successfully demonstrated stable flight.

Cancellation

Project Orion was cancelled in the mid-1960s. The Partial Test Ban Treaty of 1963, which prohibited nuclear explosions in space, together with concerns about radioactive fallout from ground launches and the shifting priorities of the space race, ended serious development. The concept nonetheless remains a reference point in discussions of high-performance propulsion, and declassified studies have influenced later designs studied under names such as Project Daedalus.
