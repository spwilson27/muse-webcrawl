Fraser fir

Abies fraseri, the Fraser fir, endemic to high elevations in the southern Appalachians.

The Fraser fir (Abies fraseri) is a species of fir native to the Appalachian Mountains of the southeastern United States, where it grows only at high elevations (above about 1,200 metres) in a few locations in southwestern Virginia, western North Carolina and eastern Tennessee, including the Great Smoky Mountains. It is named for the Scottish botanist John Fraser, who collected it in the late 18th century.

Description

The Fraser fir is a small-to-medium evergreen conifer, typically 10–15 metres tall, with a narrow conical crown, dark green needles with silvery bands beneath, and upright purple cones whose bracts protrude and curve back. It is closely related to the balsam fir of the north and often grows together with red spruce in the southern Appalachian spruce–fir forest.

Uses and threats

The species is one of the most popular Christmas trees in North America, prized for its fragrance, symmetrical shape, strong branches and excellent needle retention; millions are grown on plantations in North Carolina, and Fraser firs are frequently chosen as the official White House Christmas tree. Wild populations, however, have been devastated by the balsam woolly adelgid, an introduced insect pest that has killed the great majority of mature wild trees since the 1950s, compounded by air pollution and climate stress on the mountaintop "sky island" habitat. The species is listed as endangered.
