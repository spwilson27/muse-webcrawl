Interior of British Columbia

The British Columbia Interior, popularly referred to as the BC Interior or simply the Interior, is a geographic region of the Canadian province of British Columbia. It broadly comprises the land between the Coast Mountains to the west and the Rocky Mountains to the east, covering most of the province's land area.

Geography and climate

The Interior is a vast plateau and mountain landscape drained by the Fraser, Thompson, Columbia, and Peace river systems. Its climate is markedly more continental than the coast's: summers are hot and dry in the southern valleys such as the Okanagan and Thompson, while winters are long and cold, especially in the northern Cariboo and Peace River Country.

Communities and economy

Major centres include Kamloops, Kelowna, Prince George, Vernon, Penticton, Williams Lake, and Cranbrook. Forestry, mining, ranching, agriculture, wine production in the Okanagan, and tourism underpin the regional economy.
