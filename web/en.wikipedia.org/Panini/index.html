Panini

This article is about several topics sharing the name Panini; the term most commonly refers to the ancient Sanskrit grammarian, the Italian pressed sandwich, and the Italian collectibles company.

Pāṇini, the grammarian

Pāṇini (fl. 4th century BCE, though dates proposed range from the 6th to the 4th century BCE) was an ancient Indian Sanskrit grammarian from Pushkalavati, in Gandhara. His Aṣṭādhyāyī ("Eight Chapters"), a set of roughly 4,000 aphoristic rules (sūtras), provides a complete generative grammar of Sanskrit and is regarded as one of the greatest achievements of ancient linguistics. The work's rule system, including its use of metarules, markers, and ordered rule application, has been compared to modern formal systems and influenced Western linguistics through figures such as Leonard Bloomfield and Ferdinand de Saussure.

Pāṇini's Aṣṭādhyāyī describes Sanskrit grammar in about 4,000 terse rules.

Panini, the sandwich

A panini (Italian for "small bread", plural panini) is a sandwich made with Italian bread such as ciabatta or focaccia, typically filled with deli meats, cheese, and vegetables, and served toasted in a sandwich press, which flattens it and leaves characteristic grill marks. Panini became widely popular in cafes in the United States and elsewhere from the 1980s onward.

Panini, the company

Panini is an Italian company founded in Modena in 1961 by the Panini brothers, famous for its collectible sticker albums — beginning with football (soccer) collections, and most notably the FIFA World Cup sticker albums produced since 1970 — as well as trading cards and comics publishing through Panini Comics. The group became the world's largest publisher of collectible stickers and trading cards.
