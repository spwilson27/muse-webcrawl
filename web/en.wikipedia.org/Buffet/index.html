A buffet is a system of serving meals in which food is placed in a public area where diners serve themselves. It is a popular method for feeding a large number of people with minimal staff, and is offered in restaurants, hotels, and at social events such as weddings and parties. Buffets usually have some or mostly hot dishes, and the term is a meal where the diner pays a fixed price and may help themselves to as much food as they wish in a single sitting — the "all-you-can-eat" format.

Etymology and history

The word "buffet" comes from the French name for the sideboard furniture on which food was served. The buffet format has roots in the Swedish brännvinsbord and smörgåsbord traditions of the 16th century, where guests gathered around tables of bread, butter, cheese, and spirits before dinner. The modern restaurant buffet was popularized in the United States in the 1940s, notably in Las Vegas, where casino operator Herb McDonald is credited with introducing a 24-hour all-you-can-eat buffet in 1946 to keep gamblers on the premises.

Types

Buffets range from simple cold spreads to elaborate multi-station services with live cooking. Common forms include breakfast buffets in hotels, salad bars, dessert buffets, and ethnic formats such as Brazilian churrascaria service, Indian buffet lunches, and Chinese buffets in North America. Catering buffets may be staffed, with servers portioning dishes, or fully self-service.

Economics and etiquette

Because diners serve themselves, buffets economize on labour while food costs are managed through menu design and the physical layout of stations. Custom at an all-you-can-eat buffet generally prohibits taking food away from the premises, and many establishments charge for excessive waste.
