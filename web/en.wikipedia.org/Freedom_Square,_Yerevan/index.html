Freedom Square (Armenian: Ազատության հրապարակ, Azatutyan hraparak) is a square in the Kentron district of Yerevan, Armenia. It is located in front of the Yerevan Opera Theatre and is bounded by Mashtots Avenue, Sarmen, Byron, and Baghramyan streets.

History

The square was constructed as part of Alexander Tamanian's general plan for Yerevan. During the Soviet era it was known as Opera Square or Theatre Square and featured a statue of Alexander Spendiaryan. After Armenian independence it was renamed Freedom Square.

Demonstrations

The square has been the traditional centre of political demonstrations in Armenia. It was a focal point of the 1988 Karabakh movement rallies, the protests following the disputed 2008 presidential election, the 2011 opposition rallies, and the 2018 Velvet Revolution, when large crowds gathered there during the protests that brought Nikol Pashinyan to power.

Surroundings

The square is flanked by the Opera and Ballet Theatre building, completed to Tamanian's design, and by statues of composer Aram Khachaturian and writer Hovhannes Tumanyan in the adjoining park areas.
