Cyprinidae is the family of freshwater fish commonly known as carps or the carp family, including the common carp, grass carp, silver carp, bighead carp, koi, goldfish, and many other species often called minnows. It is the largest and most diverse family of freshwater fish, and the largest family of vertebrates in general, with more than 3,000 species in more than 300 genera.

Distribution and biology

Cyprinids are native to North America, Europe, Asia and Africa, and have been widely introduced elsewhere. They are stomachless fish with toothless jaws; food is processed by pharyngeal teeth working against a chewing pad, and sound and pressure senses are enhanced by the Weberian apparatus linking the swim bladder to the inner ear. Most species are egg-scatterers of fresh waters, ranging from tiny rasboras of a centimetre to the giant barb of Southeast Asia, which may reach 3 metres and 300 kilograms.

Importance to humans

Carp have been farmed in China for millennia; the common carp and the Chinese major carps dominate world freshwater aquaculture, while goldfish and koi — both domesticated cyprinids — underpin the ornamental fish trade. Other members include food and sport fish such as barbel, roach, tench and chub, and aquarium staples like danios and barbs. The zebrafish (Danio rerio) is a major model organism in biology. Conversely, invasive carp species cause serious ecological damage in North America and Australia.
