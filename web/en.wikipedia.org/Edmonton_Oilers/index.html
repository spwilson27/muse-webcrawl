The Edmonton Oilers are a professional ice hockey team based in Edmonton, Alberta. The Oilers compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Pacific Division of the Western Conference. They were founded in 1972 by Dr. Charles "Chuck" Allard and Dr. Bill Hunter, as the Alberta Oilers, being one of the twelve founding franchises of the major professional World Hockey Association (WHA).

Dynasty years

After joining the NHL in 1979, the Oilers built one of the greatest teams in hockey history around Wayne Gretzky, Mark Messier, Jari Kurri, Paul Coffey, and Grant Fuhr under coach Glen Sather. They won the Stanley Cup in 1984, 1985, 1987, and 1988, playing a fast, high-scoring style. Gretzky's shocking trade to Los Angeles in 1988 is considered a national trauma in Canada. The team won a fifth Cup in 1990 without Gretzky.

Later years

Small-market struggles followed; the Oilers reached the 2006 Final as an eighth seed, then endured a long playoff drought ("the Decade of Darkness"). The arrival of Connor McDavid, first overall pick in 2015, and Leon Draisaitl restored competitiveness; the team reached the Stanley Cup Final again in 2024, losing to Florida in seven games. The Oilers play at Rogers Place, opened in 2016 as the anchor of the Ice District.
