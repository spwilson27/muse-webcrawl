River Kelvin

Length35 km (22 mi)
SourceNear Banton, North Lanarkshire
MouthRiver Clyde at Glasgow

The River Kelvin is a river in central Scotland, about 35 kilometres (22 miles) long, rising near the village of Banton in North Lanarkshire and flowing west and south-west through East Dunbartonshire to join the River Clyde in Glasgow at Yorkhill. Within Glasgow it runs through Kelvingrove Park and past the University of Glasgow and the Botanic Gardens.

Name and associations

The river gave its name to the Glasgow district of Kelvinside, the Kelvin Hall arena, and — most famously — to the physicist William Thomson, who took the title Baron Kelvin from the river that flowed past his university; his name in turn became attached to the kelvin, the SI unit of temperature.

History and wildlife

The Kelvin once powered mills along its banks and was heavily polluted by industry, but water quality has recovered markedly; it now supports salmon, sea trout, otters and dippers. The Kelvin Walkway follows much of its Glasgow course, linking Kelvingrove Park with the Forth and Clyde Canal at Maryhill.
