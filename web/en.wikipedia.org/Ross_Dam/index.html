Ross Dam

Ross Dam
Location: Skagit River, Washington, USA
Type: Concrete thin-arch
Height: 540 ft (165 m)
Opened: 1949
Reservoir: Ross Lake

Ross Dam is a 540-foot (165 m) high concrete thin-arch dam across the Skagit River in Whatcom County, Washington, near the border with British Columbia, Canada. Completed in 1949, it is the highest of the three dams of Seattle City Light's Skagit River Hydroelectric Project.

Construction

The dam was built in three stages between 1937 and 1949, creating Ross Lake, a reservoir extending about 23 miles (37 km), crossing into British Columbia. It is named for James D. Ross, the superintendent of Seattle City Light who championed the Skagit project. The powerhouse at its base generates hydroelectric power for the city of Seattle.

Setting

Ross Lake lies within Ross Lake National Recreation Area, part of the North Cascades National Park complex. Plans in the 1970s to raise the dam ("High Ross") were abandoned following controversy and a treaty with British Columbia.
