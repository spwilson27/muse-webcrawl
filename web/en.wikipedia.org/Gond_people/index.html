The Gond (also Gondi or Koitur) are an ethnic group of central India and one of the largest tribal (Adivasi) communities in the country, numbering roughly 13 million in the 2011 census. They are concentrated in the states of Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, with significant populations in Maharashtra, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana — a region historically known as Gondwana.

History

Between the 14th and 18th centuries, Gond dynasties ruled substantial kingdoms in central India, including Garha-Mandla, Deogarh, Chanda and Kherla. The Garha-Mandla queen Rani Durgavati, who died resisting Mughal expansion in 1564, remains a celebrated figure. Gond rajas built forts, palaces and irrigation tanks before their kingdoms were absorbed by the Marathas and then the British.

Language and culture

The traditional language, Gondi, belongs to the Dravidian family, though many Gonds now speak Hindi, Marathi or Telugu as a first language. Gond society is organised in clans with totemic emblems. Traditional religion centres on clan and village deities such as Persa Pen, alongside increasing Hindu influence. Gond art — once ritual wall and floor painting — has achieved international recognition through artists such as Jangarh Singh Shyam, whose work launched a contemporary Gond painting movement.

Present day

The Gonds are listed as a Scheduled Tribe, with reserved places in education and legislatures, but remain among India's poorer communities, dependent largely on farming and forest produce and affected by displacement from mining and dam projects.
