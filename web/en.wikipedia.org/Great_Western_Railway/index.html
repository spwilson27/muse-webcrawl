Great Western Railway

Operated: 1833–1947

Headquarters: London Paddington

Chief engineer: Isambard Kingdom Brunel

Gauge: 7 ft ¼ in broad gauge (to 1892)

Successor: Western Region of British Railways

The Great Western Railway (GWR) was a British railway company that linked London with the southwest and west of England, the West Midlands and most of Wales. It was founded in 1833, received its enabling Act of Parliament in 1835 and ran its first trains in 1838. Widely known as "God's Wonderful Railway", it was the only one of the "Big Four" railway companies created at the 1923 Grouping to retain its original identity, and it survived until nationalisation in 1948.

Brunel and the broad gauge

The company's first chief engineer was the 27-year-old Isambard Kingdom Brunel, who designed a route of gentle gradients from London Paddington to Bristol and chose a broad track gauge of 7 ft ¼ in (2,140 mm), which he argued would give smoother, faster running. The line to Bristol, including Box Tunnel and the Maidenhead Railway Bridge, opened in 1841. The resulting "gauge war" with the standard-gauge companies ended with Parliament deciding for standard gauge; the GWR converted its network progressively, the last broad-gauge trains running in 1892.

Expansion and character

Through construction, leasing and amalgamation the GWR grew to serve Cornwall, South and West Wales, the West Midlands and the Thames Valley, building landmarks such as the Royal Albert Bridge over the Tamar (1859) and the Severn Tunnel (1886). It developed the resort traffic of the "Cornish Riviera", standardised time across its system (helping establish Greenwich Mean Time nationally), and built much of its rolling stock at Swindon Works. Under locomotive superintendents such as George Jackson Churchward and Charles Collett, its green-liveried locomotives, including the Castle and King classes, were among the most admired in Britain.

Nationalisation and legacy

The GWR became the Western Region of British Railways on 1 January 1948. The name was revived in 2015 when the FirstGroup franchise operating the historic main lines was rebranded Great Western Railway. Brunel's main line is now proposed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and preserved GWR stock runs on many heritage railways.
