Osama bin Laden

Osama bin Laden (10 March 1957 – 2 May 2011) was a Saudi-born militant who founded the pan-Islamic militant organisation al-Qaeda. He is best known as the mastermind behind the September 11 attacks of 2001, in which hijacked airliners were flown into the World Trade Center in New York City and the Pentagon, killing nearly 3,000 people.

Early life

Bin Laden was born in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, into the wealthy bin Laden family; his father, Mohammed bin Awad bin Laden, founded a major construction conglomerate. He studied economics and business administration at King Abdulaziz University in Jeddah.

Afghanistan and the founding of al-Qaeda

Following the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979, bin Laden travelled there and helped fund and organise Arab volunteer fighters, the so-called Afghan Arabs. In 1988 he founded al-Qaeda ("the base"). After the Soviet withdrawal he returned to Saudi Arabia, but was expelled in 1991 for criticising the kingdom's alliance with the United States during the Gulf War. He lived in Sudan until 1996, then moved to Afghanistan under Taliban rule.

Attacks and manhunt

Al-Qaeda was implicated in the 1993 World Trade Center bombing, the 1998 US embassy bombings in Kenya and Tanzania, the 2000 attack on USS Cole, and the September 11 attacks. After 9/11 the United States invaded Afghanistan and bin Laden became the object of a decade-long manhunt, topping the FBI's most-wanted list.

Death

On 2 May 2011, US Navy SEALs raided bin Laden's compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan, and killed him. His body was buried at sea. Ayman al-Zawahiri succeeded him as leader of al-Qaeda.
