Bike sharing

A bicycle-sharing system, bike share program, public bike share, or public bike scheme is a shared transport service where bicycles are available for shared use by individuals for a fee. Bike share schemes can be docked, where bikes are returned to fixed stations, or dockless, where bikes can be left within a defined area.

History

The first widely known scheme was Amsterdam's White Bicycles (Witte Fietsen) in 1965, which failed through theft and damage. Coin-deposit systems appeared in Denmark in the 1990s, and smartcard-based schemes such as Paris's Vélib' (2007) and London's cycle hire (2010) proved the model at scale. From 2016, venture-funded dockless companies such as Mobike and Ofo expanded rapidly in China and worldwide, followed by consolidation and regulation.

Operation

Users typically unlock bicycles via membership cards, smartphone apps, or QR codes, and pay per trip or by subscription. Operators rebalance fleets to match demand and maintain the bicycles. Many systems integrate with public transport as a first- and last-mile solution.

Impacts and issues

Studies credit bike share with modest shifts from car and taxi trips, health benefits, and improved access to transit. Dockless expansion produced problems with sidewalk clutter and vandalism, and several operators failed financially. E-bike share variants have grown rapidly since the late 2010s.
