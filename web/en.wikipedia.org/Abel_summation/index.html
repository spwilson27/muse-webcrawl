Abel summation

Field: Mathematical analysis

Named after: Niels Henrik Abel

Abel summation may refer to two related ideas in mathematical analysis named after Niels Henrik Abel: the summation by parts formula, and a method of assigning sums to certain divergent series.

Summation by parts

The Abel summation formula is the discrete analogue of integration by parts. If A(x) is the partial-sum function of a sequence a(n), then the sum of a(n)f(n) over a range can be rewritten as a boundary term involving A(x)f(x) minus an integral (or sum) involving A and the derivative (or difference) of f. The formula is a standard tool in analytic number theory, used for example to estimate sums involving the Möbius and von Mangoldt functions.

Abel summation of series

As a summability method, Abel summation assigns to a series the limit, as x approaches 1 from below, of the power series formed from its terms, when that limit exists. Every convergent series is Abel summable to its ordinary sum, and some divergent series — such as Grandi's series 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 + ⋯, which is assigned the value 1/2 — are Abel summable as well. The method is closely related to Cesàro summation and to Abel's theorem on power series.
