Osage Nation

Capital: Pawhuska, Oklahoma
Government: Tribal nation
Language: Osage, English
Recognition: Federally recognized tribe

The Osage Nation (Osage: Ni-U-Kon-Ska, "Children of the Middle Waters") is a federally recognized Native American tribe in Oklahoma. The Osage are a Midwestern Siouan-speaking people who historically occupied much of present-day Missouri, Arkansas, Kansas, and Oklahoma. Their government is headquartered in Pawhuska, Oklahoma, within the Osage Nation Reservation, which is coterminous with Osage County — the largest county in Oklahoma by area.

History

The Osage were a powerful and prosperous people in the 17th and 18th centuries, controlling trade along the Missouri and Arkansas rivers. Through a series of treaties in the 19th century they ceded most of their land to the United States and were eventually removed to Indian Territory. Under the Osage Allotment Act of 1906, the reservation's surface land was allotted to individual members, but the tribe retained the subsurface mineral estate in common. Oil discovered beneath the reservation made the Osage, per capita, among the wealthiest people in the world in the 1920s. That wealth attracted exploitation and the notorious Osage murders of the 1920s, in which dozens of Osage were killed for their headrights; the case became one of the first major investigations of the young FBI.

Government and culture

The Osage Nation adopted a constitutional government in 2006, with an elected principal chief, assistant principal chief, and a congress. The tribe operates casinos, ranches, and cultural programs, and has undertaken major efforts to revitalize the Osage language, including an immersion school and the development of the Osage script (created in 2006 and added to Unicode). In 2016 the Nation purchased the historic Ted Turner ranch lands in the Flint Hills — the Osage Ranch — expanding its land base for bison ranching and conservation.
