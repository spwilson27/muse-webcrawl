Japan (Japanese: 日本, Nippon or Nihon, formally 日本国, Nippon-koku) is an island country in East Asia. It is situated in the northwest Pacific Ocean and is bordered on the west by the Sea of Japan, extending from the Sea of Okhotsk in the north toward the East China Sea, Philippine Sea, and Taiwan in the south. The Japanese archipelago consists of more than 14,000 islands, of which the five largest are Honshu, Hokkaido, Kyushu, Shikoku, and Okinawa.

Japan is the world's eleventh most populous country and one of the most densely populated and urbanized. About three-quarters of the country's terrain is mountainous. Tokyo, the capital and largest city, anchors the Greater Tokyo Area, the most populous metropolitan area in the world.

History

The first known inhabitants of the archipelago were the Jōmon people. Japan's recorded history begins with brief mentions in Chinese chronicles of the first century CE. The imperial court consolidated power over the centuries, and by the twelfth century the country was effectively ruled by feudal military shoguns. After a period of civil war, Japan was unified under the Tokugawa shogunate, which pursued a policy of isolation (sakoku) for more than two centuries. The arrival of Commodore Matthew Perry's American fleet in 1853 forced Japan open; the Meiji Restoration of 1868 launched rapid modernization.

Japan expanded into an empire, annexing Korea and Taiwan and invading China and Southeast Asia during the Second World War. Following defeat in 1945 and occupation by the Allied powers, Japan adopted a pacifist constitution in 1947 and experienced decades of rapid economic growth, becoming one of the world's largest economies.

Government and politics

Japan is a unitary state and constitutional monarchy in which the Emperor is the ceremonial head of state and executive power rests with the Prime Minister and the elected National Diet. The country is divided into 47 prefectures. Japan maintains the Japan Self-Defense Forces under the postwar constitution's Article 9, which renounces war.

Culture

Japanese culture draws on indigenous Shinto traditions, Buddhism introduced from the mainland, and extensive borrowing from China and later the West. Japan is known for its arts (ukiyo-e, calligraphy, ikebana, tea ceremony), cuisine (see Japanese cuisine), literature, cinema, manga and anime, and technological innovation.
