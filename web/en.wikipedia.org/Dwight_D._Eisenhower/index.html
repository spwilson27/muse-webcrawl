Dwight David "Ike" Eisenhower (born David Dwight Eisenhower; October 14, 1890 – March 28, 1969) was an American military officer and statesman who served as the 34th president of the United States from 1953 to 1961. During World War II, he was Supreme Commander of the Allied Expeditionary Force in Europe and achieved the five-star rank of General of the Army.

Eisenhower planned and supervised two of the most consequential military campaigns of 1942–45: Operation Torch in the North Africa campaign in 1942–1943 and the invasion of Normandy in 1944. He then served as president of Columbia University and as the first supreme commander of NATO before winning the Republican nomination for president in 1952.

Early life and education

Eisenhower was born in Denison, Texas, and raised in Abilene, Kansas, in a large family of mostly Pennsylvania Dutch ancestry. He graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1915, in the class famously described as "the class the stars fell on," and served in a variety of staff and command posts during the interwar period, including under General Douglas MacArthur in the Philippines.

World War II

After the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor, Eisenhower rose rapidly. He commanded Allied forces in Operation Torch, the invasion of French North Africa, and went on to direct the invasions of Sicily and Italy. In December 1943 he was appointed Supreme Allied Commander in Europe, and on June 6, 1944, his forces launched the Normandy landings (D-Day), the largest seaborne invasion in history. He accepted the unconditional surrender of Nazi Germany in May 1945.

Presidency (1953–1961)

As president, Eisenhower ended the Korean War, maintained pressure on the Soviet Union during the Cold War, and presided over a period of economic prosperity. Domestically he continued New Deal programs, expanded Social Security, and launched the Interstate Highway System in 1956. He enforced the desegregation of schools in Little Rock, Arkansas, in 1957 by deploying federal troops. His farewell address of January 17, 1961, warned the nation about the influence of the "military–industrial complex."

Later life and death

Eisenhower retired to his farm in Gettysburg, Pennsylvania. He died of congestive heart failure on March 28, 1969, and was buried in Abilene, Kansas. Historians generally rank him among the upper tier of American presidents.
