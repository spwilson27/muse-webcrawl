Expanded universe

Tie-in novels, comics and games expand the setting of a fictional franchise beyond its core works.

The term expanded universe, sometimes called an extended universe, is used in media franchises to describe the extension of a fictional universe into media other than the original work — for example, novels, comic books, video games, films or television series that add stories and background to a franchise whose original form was a movie series or a book.

Usage

The term became prominent with Star Wars, whose officially licensed novels and comics were marketed as the Star Wars Expanded Universe for decades; in 2014, following Disney's acquisition of Lucasfilm, most of that material was rebranded "Legends" and removed from the official canon to clear the way for new sequel films. Similar extended universes exist for Star Trek, Doctor Who, Alien and many superhero franchises.

Canon questions

Expanded-universe material typically occupies a lower tier of canonicity than the core works: rights holders may consult or contradict it, and fans often debate what "counts". Franchise managers therefore use continuity databases and licensing review to keep tie-ins broadly consistent with the main storyline.
