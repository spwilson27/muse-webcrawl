Eye patch

An eye patch (or eyepatch) is a small patch that is worn in front of one eye. It may be a cloth patch attached around the head by a string, or an adhesive bandage. It is often worn by people to cover a lost, blind, or injured eye, but it also has several medical uses in children, and is famously associated in popular culture with pirates.

Medical uses

Patching is the mainstay of treatment for amblyopia ("lazy eye") in children: the stronger eye is covered for prescribed hours each day, forcing the brain to use the weaker eye and develop its vision. Eye patches are also used to protect an eye after surgery or injury, to manage double vision (diplopia), and in orthoptic exercises. Adhesive orthopedic patches with colourful designs are widely used for children.

Sailors and pirates

The eye patch is a stereotypical attribute of pirates in fiction, an image popularized by Robert Louis Stevenson's Treasure Island and later films. A commonly repeated explanation — that sailors kept one eye dark-adapted for going below deck — is a plausible but undocumented theory. Other uses

Marksmen and pilots have used patches or translucent occluders to aid aiming and reduce eye strain. In the Second World War, the British "patches" were issued to some troops to preserve night vision in one eye during flares. Eye patches are also worn as a fashion accessory or costume item.
