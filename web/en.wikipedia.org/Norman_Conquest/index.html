Norman Conquest

The Norman Conquest (1066–1075) was the invasion and occupation of England by an army of Norman, Breton, Flemish and French soldiers led by William, Duke of Normandy, later William the Conqueror. It began after the death of Edward the Confessor triggered a succession crisis.

Battle of Hastings

On 14 October 1066 William defeated King Harold Godwinson, who was killed in battle, shortly after Harold had repelled a Norwegian invasion at Stamford Bridge. William was crowned on Christmas Day 1066.

Consequences

The English aristocracy was largely replaced by Normans, castles were built across the realm, Norman French reshaped the English language, and the Domesday Book (1086) surveyed the kingdom. Resistance, including in the North (the Harrying of the North, 1069–70), was harshly suppressed.
