Jordan normal form

In linear algebra, a Jordan normal form, also known as a Jordan canonical form, is an upper triangular matrix of a particular form called a Jordan matrix representing a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space with respect to some basis. Such a matrix has each non-zero off-diagonal entry equal to 1, each such entry placed immediately above and to the right of the diagonal, and with identical diagonal entries to the left and below them.

Overview

Every square matrix A over an algebraically closed field (such as the complex numbers) is similar to a Jordan matrix J, i.e. there exists an invertible matrix P with P−1AP = J. The Jordan matrix is block diagonal with Jordan blocks on the diagonal, and it is unique up to permutation of the blocks. A matrix is diagonalizable if and only if all its Jordan blocks are of size 1.

Applications

The Jordan normal form classifies matrices up to similarity, determines minimal and characteristic polynomial relationships, and simplifies computations of matrix functions — powers, exponentials, and solutions of linear differential equations y′ = Ay. Its proof relies on the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain, which also yields the rational canonical form valid over any field.
