Tavern

A tavern is a place of business where people gather to drink alcoholic beverages and be served food, and historically, where travellers could find lodging. The word derives from the Latin taberna, a shed or shop, and taverns have existed since antiquity, including the tabernae of Roman towns.

History

Taverns flourished in medieval and early modern Europe as centres of social life, news, and commerce, and in colonial America they served as meeting places, post offices, and venues for political debate — several American revolutionary organisations met in taverns, and many early legislatures and courts convened in them. In Britain the tavern was distinguished from the alehouse and the inn, the latter formally providing stabling and accommodation; in practice the terms often overlapped, and from the 18th century the public house ("pub") became the dominant institution.

Modern usage

Today "tavern" survives in many jurisdictions as a licensing category for bars, and in the names of restaurants and pubs evoking a historic atmosphere. Themed and fantasy taverns are also a staple setting of literature and role-playing games, where they traditionally serve as meeting points for adventurers.
