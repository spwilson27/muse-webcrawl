Neutron moderator

A neutron moderator is a medium that reduces the speed of fast neutrons, ideally without capturing any, turning them into thermal neutrons capable of sustaining a nuclear chain reaction involving uranium-235 or a similar fissile nuclide.

Moderation

Neutrons lose energy through elastic collisions with atomic nuclei. The energy loss per collision is greatest for light nuclei, so the most effective moderators contain light elements such as hydrogen, deuterium, beryllium or carbon. Neutrons are typically slowed from energies of around 2 MeV to thermal energies of about 0.025 eV.

Materials

Common moderators include ordinary (light) water, heavy water (deuterium oxide) and graphite. Heavy water absorbs very few neutrons, permitting the use of natural uranium fuel, as in CANDU reactors; graphite was used in early reactors such as Chicago Pile-1 and in the RBMK and Magnox designs.

Safety considerations

The moderation properties of a reactor core affect its temperature and void coefficients, and thus the stability and safety of the chain reaction.
