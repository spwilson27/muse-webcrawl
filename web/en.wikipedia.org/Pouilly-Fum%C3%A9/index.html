Pouilly-Fumé

Pouilly-Fumé

Country: France

Region: Loire Valley (Centre-Val de Loire)

Appellation status: AOC since 1937

Grape: Sauvignon blanc

Style: Dry white, flinty, smoky

Pouilly-Fumé is an appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) wine produced around the village of Pouilly-sur-Loire in the upper Loire Valley of France. It is made exclusively from the Sauvignon blanc grape, locally called Blanc Fumé — a name that, together with the flinty local soils (silex), gives the wine its name and its reputation for a distinctive smoky, gunflint aroma (fumé meaning "smoked").

Terroir

The vineyards lie on the right bank of the Loire, opposite Sancerre, on a mosaic of Kimmeridgian marl, limestone and flint clays. Each soil is said to leave its mark: marl giving richness, limestone freshness and silex the prized smoky character. The wines are typically crisp and dry, with notes of citrus, green fruit, gooseberry and wet stone.

History and naming disputes

The appellation was formalised in 1937. Because American producers long used "Pouilly-Fumé" generically, the name became caught up in international labelling disputes; United States rules now restrict the term, and California versions labelled as such have largely disappeared. It should not be confused with Pouilly-Fuissé, a Chardonnay wine from the Mâconnais in Burgundy.
