Biological indicator

Biological indicators are test systems that contain viable microorganisms with a defined resistance to a specific sterilization process. They help determine whether the necessary conditions were met to kill a specified number of microorganisms, providing a direct measure of sterilization efficacy rather than merely recording physical parameters such as temperature and pressure.

Use in sterilization

For steam sterilization (autoclaving), indicators typically contain spores of Geobacillus stearothermophilus; for ethylene oxide and dry heat, spores of Bacillus atrophaeus are used. After a cycle, the indicator is incubated: if no growth occurs, the spores were killed and the process is considered effective. Bacterial spores are chosen because they are far more resistant to sterilization than ordinary vegetative microbes.

Self-contained indicators

Modern self-contained biological indicators combine a spore strip with growth medium in a single vial that is crushed or activated after processing, giving results in hours to days depending on the system. They are widely used in hospitals, dentistry, pharmaceutical manufacturing and food processing to validate and routinely monitor sterilizers.
