The Man in the High Castle (1962) is an alternate history novel by American writer Philip K. Dick. Set in 1962, fifteen years after an alternative ending to the Second World War in which the Axis powers won, it depicts a United States partitioned between the Greater German Reich in the east and the Japanese Pacific States in the west, with a neutral buffer zone in the Rocky Mountains. The novel won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 1963.

Premise and plot

In the novel's backstory, President Franklin D. Roosevelt was assassinated in 1933, prolonging the Great Depression and American isolationism; the Axis won the war, destroyed the Soviet Union, enslaved and exterminated much of Africa, and by 1962 the German Reich and Imperial Japan are cold-war rivals, with Germany far ahead technologically, having drained the Mediterranean and begun colonising the Moon, Mars and Venus.

Interweaving storylines follow characters in Japanese-occupied San Francisco and the neutral zone: Frank Frink, a Jewish metalworker who forges fake antique "Americana" and later makes original jewellery; his estranged wife Juliana, who travels to Colorado seeking Hawthorne Abendsen, the "Man in the High Castle," author of a banned novel-within-the-novel, The Grasshopper Lies Heavy, which imagines an Allied victory; antiques dealer Robert Childan, torn between deference to his Japanese clients and resentment of them; Japanese trade official Nobusuke Tagomi, who uses a genuine Colt revolver to kill German agents; and "Mr. Baynes," a Swedish industrialist who is in fact a German intelligence officer come to warn the Japanese of a planned German nuclear strike, Operation Dandelion. Throughout, characters consult the I Ching, as Dick himself did in plotting the novel.

The Grasshopper Lies Heavy

Abendsen's novel imagines a world in which Roosevelt survived and the Allies won — but not our world: in it, Britain retains its empire and eventually outlasts the United States to dominate the postwar order. When Juliana reaches Abendsen, he admits the I Ching guided his writing and that the oracle reveals the "inner truth" that Japan and Germany actually lost the war. The device makes the book a meditation on the nature of historical reality and authenticity, mirrored by the novel's forged antiques and a piece of modern jewellery that gives Tagomi a vision of our San Francisco.

Reception and adaptations

The novel is widely regarded as Dick's masterpiece and a landmark of alternate-history fiction; it brought him mainstream recognition beyond the science fiction field. Dick drafted but never completed a sequel. Amazon Studios adapted the novel as a television series, The Man in the High Castle (2015–2019), created by Frank Spotnitz with Ridley Scott as an executive producer, expanding its world and plot across four seasons.
