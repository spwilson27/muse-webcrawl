Blade Runner is a 1982 American science fiction film directed by Ridley Scott from a screenplay by Hampton Fancher and David Peoples. Loosely based on Philip K. Dick's 1968 novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?, it stars Harrison Ford as Rick Deckard, a burnt-out police detective in a dystopian Los Angeles of 2019 who reluctantly agrees to hunt down four escaped replicants — bioengineered humanoids — who have returned to Earth to extend their four-year lifespans.

Plot

In November 2019, former "blade runner" Rick Deckard is detained by officer Gaff and brought to his ex-superior Bryant, who informs him that four Nexus-6 replicants — Roy Batty, Leon, Zhora and Pris — have hijacked a ship, killed its crew and returned to Earth illegally. Deckard is ordered to "retire" them. At the Tyrell Corporation he meets Eldon Tyrell and his assistant Rachael, whom the Voigt-Kampff empathy test reveals to be an experimental replicant unaware of her nature. Deckard tracks and retires Zhora and Leon, is assigned to kill Rachael, and finally confronts Pris and Roy at the Bradbury Building. Roy, facing his built-in death, spares Deckard's life and delivers a dying speech about memories that will be "lost in time, like tears in rain," before expiring. Deckard flees the city with Rachael.

Production

Fancher's screenplay, developed from 1977 under the title Dangerous Days, focused on environmental and corporate themes. Scott joined the project in 1980; David Peoples rewrote the script, contributing the term "replicant." Production designer Lawrence G. Paull and visual futurist Syd Mead created the film's dense, "retrofitted" cityscape, with special photographic effects supervised by Douglas Trumbull. Filming in 1981 was famously difficult, and studio-mandated changes added a voice-over narration and an upbeat ending to early releases.

Versions and reception

Initial critical reaction was mixed and the film underperformed at the box office, overshadowed by other 1982 releases. Several versions circulate: the 1982 theatrical cut, the 1992 Director's Cut (removing the narration and happy ending and restoring the unicorn dream, which fuels the enduring debate over whether Deckard is himself a replicant), and Scott's 2007 Final Cut, the only version over which he had complete control. Vangelis's score, Jordan Cronenweth's cinematography and the production design have been widely praised.

Legacy

Blade Runner is now regarded as one of the greatest science fiction films ever made and a defining work of the cyberpunk genre and neo-noir. It was selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry in 1993, and its imagery has influenced generations of films, anime, video games and urban design. A sequel, Blade Runner 2049, directed by Denis Villeneuve, was released in 2017.
