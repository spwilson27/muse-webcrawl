Perkele

Ukko, the Finnish god of thunder, with whose name Perkele was once associated.

Perkele is a word in the Finnish language meaning originally a god of thunder, and in later use a name for the Devil. It is also a common Finnish profanity, often considered the best-known Finnish swear word.

Etymology and mythology

The word is thought to derive from a Baltic source related to Lithuanian Perkūnas, the thunder god; cognates include Latvian Pērkons and Slavic Perun. In older Finnish usage, perkele could be an epithet or alternate name of Ukko, the supreme god and god of thunder in Finnish mythology. With the Christianisation of Finland, the name was transferred to the Devil, and older religious vocabulary records perkeleet as evil spirits.

As profanity

As a swear word, perkele is famously said with a trilled, strongly emphasised "r". It is used to express frustration, anger, emphatic determination, or — in the spirit of sisu — grit. Finnish swearing culture, and this word in particular, has become an internationally recognised stereotype of Finnishness; it has been borrowed into Swedish slang as perkele and featured on merchandise, in heavy metal culture, and in humorous "teach yourself Finnish" material.
