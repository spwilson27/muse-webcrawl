Monsoon of India

The monsoon of India is the seasonal reversal of winds that brings the great majority of the subcontinent's rainfall. The southwest monsoon typically arrives over Kerala around the beginning of June and withdraws by October; a weaker northeast monsoon brings winter rain mainly to the southeast coast.

Mechanism

Intense summer heating of the Asian landmass creates low pressure that draws moist air from the Indian Ocean. The flow splits into an Arabian Sea branch, which drenches the Western Ghats, and a Bay of Bengal branch that moves up the Gangetic plain and against the Himalayas.

Importance

The monsoon delivers roughly three-quarters of India's annual rainfall and is vital to agriculture, reservoir levels, and hydroelectric power. Weak monsoons cause drought, while exceptionally strong ones cause destructive flooding in cities such as Mumbai and Chennai.
