Begoniaceae is a family of flowering plants comprising two genera: Begonia, one of the largest genera of flowering plants with over 2,000 described species, and the monotypic Hawaiian genus Hillebrandia. Members are mostly herbaceous plants of tropical and subtropical regions, widely grown as ornamentals.

Description

Begonias typically have asymmetric leaves (giving the family its association with the French botanist Michel Bégon), unisexual flowers, and fleshy stems. Male and female flowers appear on the same plant; female flowers have a distinctive winged ovary that develops into a dry capsule.

Distribution and cultivation

The family is pantropical, with centres of diversity in South America, Africa and Southeast Asia. Thousands of hybrids and cultivars are grown for their colourful foliage and flowers, including tuberous begonias, rex begonias and wax begonias, used as houseplants, bedding plants and hanging-basket subjects.
