The National Council Licensure Examination (NCLEX) is a standardized examination that graduates of nursing programs must pass to obtain a nursing license in the United States and Canada. It is administered by the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN) and delivered at Pearson VUE test centers.

Versions

The exam has two forms: the NCLEX-RN, for candidates seeking licensure as registered nurses, and the NCLEX-PN, for candidates seeking licensure as licensed practical or vocational nurses. Both test the knowledge, skills, and abilities considered essential for safe and effective entry-level nursing practice.

Format

The NCLEX is a computerized adaptive test: the difficulty of each question depends on the candidate's performance on previous items. Under the Next Generation NCLEX (NGN), launched in April 2023, the exam includes case-study items measuring clinical judgment. The RN exam ranges from 85 to 150 items, and candidates are graded pass/fail rather than by a numerical score.

Content areas

Questions are organized around client needs categories, including safe and effective care environment, health promotion and maintenance, psychosocial integrity, and physiological integrity, the last of which carries the largest share of items.
