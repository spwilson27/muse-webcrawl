Liliʻuokalani (born Lydia Liliʻu Loloku Walania Kamakaʻeha; 2 September 1838 – 11 November 1917) was the only queen regnant and the last sovereign monarch of the Hawaiian Kingdom, reigning from 1891 until the overthrow of the kingdom on 17 January 1893.

Early life

Born in Honolulu to high chiefs Kapaʻakea and Keohokālole, she was educated at the Royal School, became an accomplished musician and composer, and married the American-born John Owen Dominis in 1862. She travelled widely, attended Queen Victoria's Golden Jubilee in 1887, and served as regent during her brother King Kalākaua's world tour. She composed more than 160 songs, including the beloved "Aloha ʻOe".

Reign and overthrow

Succeeding Kalākaua on his death in 1891, Liliʻuokalani sought to restore royal authority curtailed by the 1887 "Bayonet Constitution" and to promulgate a new constitution. On 17 January 1893 a committee of American and European businessmen, supported by the US minister and the landing of troops from USS Boston, deposed her and proclaimed a provisional government. She surrendered under protest to the "superior force of the United States of America", hoping Washington would restore her.

Later life

President Grover Cleveland's investigation (the Blount Report) condemned the overthrow, but the provisional government refused reinstatement and declared the Republic of Hawaii. After a failed counter-rebellion in 1895, Liliʻuokalani was tried, imprisoned for months in ʻIolani Palace, and formally abdicated. Hawaii was annexed by the United States in 1898. She spent her remaining years in Honolulu, wrote her memoir Hawaii's Story by Hawaii's Queen (1898), and left her estate in trust for orphaned and destitute children. In 1993 the US Congress passed the Apology Resolution acknowledging American complicity in the overthrow.
