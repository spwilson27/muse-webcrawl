Mantella

Mantella is a genus of small, brightly coloured frogs in the family Mantellidae, endemic to Madagascar. The sixteen or so recognised species are mostly terrestrial and diurnal, and several are popular in the pet trade.

Description

Mantellas are small frogs, generally 18–31 millimetres long, with smooth skin and vivid warning coloration in combinations of black, orange, yellow, green, blue and red. Like the unrelated poison dart frogs of South America, they sequester alkaloid toxins in their skin from the mites and ants they eat; in captivity, on a diet lacking these prey, they lose their toxicity.

Conservation

Many species have tiny ranges in eastern Madagascar's rainforests and are threatened by habitat loss, collection for trade, and chytrid fungus. The golden mantella (Mantella aurantiaca) is listed as endangered.
