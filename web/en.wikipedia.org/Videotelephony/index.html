Videotelephony

Videotelephony is the two-way or multipoint reception and transmission of audio and video signals by people in different locations for real-time communication. A videophone is a telephone with a video camera and display capable of simultaneous video and audio communication. Videotelephony is the underlying technology of videoconferencing, though the latter term usually implies group meetings in business or organisational settings.

History

The idea of seeing the person one is speaking to predates practical television: concept videophones appeared in nineteenth-century speculation and in the 1878 cartoon of Edison's "telephonoscope". Bell Telephone Laboratories conducted an early two-way video experiment in 1927 and demonstrated a one-way system between New York and Washington, D.C. In Germany, the Reichspost operated a public videophone service between Berlin and other cities from 1936 to 1940.

AT&T's Picturephone, unveiled at the 1964 World's Fair, was the most ambitious early commercial attempt; a Mod II service launched in 1970 but attracted few subscribers because of its cost and the need for both parties to own the equipment. Dedicated videophones for the public switched telephone network, standardised under ITU-T H.324, appeared in the 1990s, while 3G mobile networks enabled handsets such as those used with the UMTS video calling standard.

Modern usage

With smartphones and broadband, videotelephony became a mass-market feature: Apple's FaceTime (2010), Skype video calling, and video features in WhatsApp, Messenger and similar apps made personal video calls routine. Videotelephony is especially valued by deaf and hard-of-hearing communities, for whom sign language communication over video relay services and direct video calls replaced text-based telecommunications devices.

Technology

A videotelephony system digitises and compresses audio and video with codecs, packetises the streams for network transmission, and reconstructs them at the far end with minimal latency. Quality depends on bandwidth, codec efficiency, network jitter, and the camera and display. Standards families include the ITU-T H.32x series and IETF protocols such as SIP and WebRTC.
