Arundel is a market town and civil parish in the Arun District of West Sussex, England. It lies on the River Arun at the edge of the South Downs and is dominated by Arundel Castle, the seat of the Dukes of Norfolk, and by the Roman Catholic Arundel Cathedral.

History

The town grew up around the castle founded after the Norman Conquest, held successively by the d'Aubigny and FitzAlan families before passing by marriage to the Howard family, Dukes of Norfolk. The castle was damaged during the English Civil War and extensively restored in the nineteenth century. The cathedral, dedicated to Our Lady and St Philip Howard, was built in the 1870s.

Geography and landmarks

Arundel sits where the River Arun cuts through the chalk of the South Downs. Landmarks include the castle and its gardens, the cathedral, the parish church of St Nicholas, the Arundel Museum, and the nearby Wildfowl and Wetlands Trust reserve. The town retains a steep high street of Georgian and Victorian buildings.

Transport

Arundel railway station lies on the Arun Valley line, with services to London Victoria, Chichester and Brighton. The A27 trunk road passes south of the town.
