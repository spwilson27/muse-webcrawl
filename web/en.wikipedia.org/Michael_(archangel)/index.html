Michael (archangel)

Michael, also called Saint Michael the Archangel, is an archangel in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. His name, from the Hebrew Mīkhāʼēl, means "Who is like God?" — traditionally understood as a rhetorical question implying that no one is like God. In Catholic tradition he is the leader of the heavenly host and the patron saint of soldiers, police officers, and paramedics.

In scripture

In the Book of Daniel, Michael is a "great prince" who protects the people of Israel. In the New Testament, the Epistle of Jude references a dispute between Michael and the devil over the body of Moses, and the Book of Revelation depicts Michael leading heaven's armies against the dragon (Satan) and casting him out of heaven. In Islam, Mīkāl (Mika'il) is one of the four archangels, associated with providing nourishment and rain.

Veneration

Michael's feast day, Michaelmas (29 September), is shared in Western Christianity with the archangels Gabriel and Raphael. Famous sanctuaries dedicated to him include Mont Saint-Michel in France, St Michael's Mount in Cornwall, and the Sanctuary of Monte Sant'Angelo in Italy, a major medieval pilgrimage site. Traditions credit Michael with weighing souls and escorting the faithful to heaven, and with appearing at the hour of death.
