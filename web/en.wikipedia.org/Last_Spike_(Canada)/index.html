Last Spike (Canada)

Donald Smith drives the Last Spike at Craigellachie, British Columbia, 7 November 1885

The Last Spike was the final and ceremonial railway spike driven into the Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR) track by company director Donald Smith on the morning of 7 November 1885. The ceremony marked the completion of the transcontinental CPR and was held at Craigellachie, near Eagle Pass in the Monashee Mountains of British Columbia.

Background

The construction of a transcontinental railway was a condition of British Columbia joining Canadian Confederation in 1871. The Canadian Pacific Railway, incorporated in 1881, built the line across the prairies and through the Rocky and Selkirk mountains under difficult and expensive conditions.

The ceremony

The spike was a plain iron spike, identical to the others used in construction. Donald Smith, later Lord Strathcona, drove it with an iron maul. The first blow bent the spike, and a second spike was used to complete the ceremony. The bent spike was removed and later fashioned into souvenirs; the replacement spike remained in the track.

Legacy

The driving of the Last Spike became an iconic moment in Canadian history, symbolizing national unity and the physical linking of the country from coast to coast. The famous photograph of the ceremony is among the best-known images in Canadian history. The first transcontinental train reached Port Moody the following summer, in July 1886.
