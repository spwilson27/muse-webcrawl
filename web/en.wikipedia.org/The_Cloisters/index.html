The Cloisters

The Cloisters, officially the Met Cloisters, is a museum in Fort Tryon Park in Washington Heights, Manhattan, New York City, specializing in European medieval art and architecture, with a focus on the Romanesque and Gothic periods. Governed by the Metropolitan Museum of Art, it contains a large collection of medieval artworks shown in architectural settings salvaged from European monasteries and abbeys.

Building and collection

The museum building, designed by Charles Collens, opened in 1938. It incorporates elements from five medieval French cloisters — Saint-Michel-de-Cuxa, Saint-Guilhem-le-Désert, Bonnefont-en-Comminges, Trie-en-Bigorre and Froville — which were dismantled, shipped from France and reassembled in New York, largely from the collection assembled by the sculptor George Grey Barnard and purchased for the Met by John D. Rockefeller Jr., who also funded the park and preserved the view across the Hudson. The collection of some 5,000 works includes the Unicorn Tapestries, the Robert Campin Mérode Altarpiece, illuminated manuscripts such as the Belles Heures of Jean de France, and Romanesque sculpture, metalwork and stained glass. The cloister gardens are planted according to medieval herbals.
