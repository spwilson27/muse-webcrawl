Legislature

A legislature is a deliberative assembly with the authority to make laws for a political entity such as a country or state. Legislatures form a central part of most systems of government, typically balanced against executive and judicial branches.

Structure

Legislatures may be unicameral, with a single chamber, or bicameral, with two — commonly a lower house apportioned by population and an upper house representing regions or acting as a revising body. Members are usually elected, though some upper houses include appointed or hereditary members.

Functions

Beyond enacting statutes, legislatures approve budgets and taxation, oversee the executive through questions and committees, ratify treaties in some systems, and may impeach officials or confirm appointments.

Names

National legislatures go by many names: parliament, congress, assembly, diet or council, each carrying local constitutional history. The word itself derives from the Latin legis lator, a proposer of law.
