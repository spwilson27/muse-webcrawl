Teungku Hasan Muhammad di Tiro (25 September 1925 – 3 June 2010) was an Acehnese political leader and businessman who founded the Free Aceh Movement (Gerakan Aceh Merdeka, GAM) and proclaimed the independence of Aceh from Indonesia on 4 December 1976. He led the movement from exile in Sweden for nearly three decades, returning to Aceh in 2008, two years after the Helsinki peace agreement ended the conflict.

Background

Di Tiro was born in Pidie Regency, Aceh, into a prominent family; he was a descendant of Teungku Chik di Tiro, a nineteenth-century Acehnese resistance hero of the Aceh War against the Dutch. He studied at the Islamic institute of Daud Beureu'eh and later in Yogyakarta and Jakarta, where he worked in journalism and for Indonesia's ministry of information. In the early 1950s he went to the United States on a Colombo Plan fellowship and earned a doctorate-level education at Columbia University in New York, while working at the Indonesian delegation to the United Nations.

Business and the independence claim

Di Tiro built an international business career in construction and oil services. He argued, in works such as The Price of Freedom: The Unfinished Diary of Tengku Hasan di Tiro, that Aceh — a sovereign sultanate for centuries — had been illegally transferred to Indonesia by the Dutch in 1949, and that Acehnese resources, especially natural gas from the Arun field, were exploited by Jakarta. In 1974 he returned to Aceh and bid for a pipeline contract, which he lost; his sense of grievance fed into his political campaign.

Free Aceh Movement

On 4 December 1976 di Tiro issued a declaration of independence at Mount Halimun in Pidie and organised GAM as a guerrilla movement. Indonesian military offensives forced him to flee in 1979; he eventually settled in Sweden, taking Swedish citizenship, and directed the movement from Stockholm while armed conflict ebbed and surged in Aceh, notably after 1989 and again from 1999. The insurgency and counter-insurgency cost an estimated 15,000 lives.

Peace and return

After the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami devastated Aceh, GAM and the Indonesian government signed the Helsinki Memorandum of Understanding on 15 August 2005, granting Aceh wide autonomy in exchange for disarmament. Di Tiro initially expressed doubts but returned to Aceh on 11 October 2008 to an emotional welcome. He died of complications from multiple illnesses in Banda Aceh on 3 June 2010 and was buried in the family plot in Meureue, Indrapuri, beside his forebears.
