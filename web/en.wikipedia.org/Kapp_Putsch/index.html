Kapp Putsch

Kapp Putsch
Date: 13–17 March 1920
Location: Berlin, Weimar Germany
Leaders: Wolfgang Kapp, Walther von Lüttwitz
Result: Putsch defeated by general strike

The Kapp Putsch, also known as the Kapp–Lüttwitz Putsch, was a coup attempt against the German Weimar Republic in March 1920. Right-wing forces led by Wolfgang Kapp and General Walther von Lüttwitz seized Berlin and proclaimed a new government, aiming to undo the results of the German Revolution of 1918–19.

Course of events

The putsch was triggered when the government ordered the disbanding of Freikorps units, notably the Marinebrigade Ehrhardt, which instead marched on Berlin. The legitimate government fled to Stuttgart. The putschists failed to gain the support of the civil service or the Reichswehr leadership.

Defeat

Trade unions called a general strike that paralysed the capital, and after four days the putsch collapsed. Kapp fled to Sweden. The event exposed the fragility of the young republic and the disloyalty of much of its military. See also Weimar Republic.
