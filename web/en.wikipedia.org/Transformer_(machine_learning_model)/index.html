A transformer is a deep learning architecture developed by researchers at Google and the University of Toronto in 2017, introduced in the paper "Attention Is All You Need". It relies entirely on attention mechanisms to draw global dependencies between input and output, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions, and it has become the foundation of most modern large language models.

Architecture

The original transformer has an encoder–decoder structure. Each layer combines multi-head self-attention — in which every position attends to every other through query, key, and value projections — with a position-wise feed-forward network, residual connections, and layer normalization. Because attention is order-insensitive, positional encodings are added to the input embeddings.

Variants

Encoder-only models such as BERT are used for understanding tasks; decoder-only models such as the GPT family generate text autoregressively; encoder–decoder models such as T5 handle sequence-to-sequence tasks. Vision Transformers apply the same design to image patches.

Impact

The architecture's parallelizability lets it scale to hundreds of billions of parameters trained on massive corpora. Self-attention has quadratic cost in sequence length, which has motivated research into efficient attention and alternative architectures. Transformers underpin systems including ChatGPT, machine translation services, and protein-structure predictors' components.
