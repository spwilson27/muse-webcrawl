Breakup of the Bell System

The breakup of the Bell System was the divestiture of the American Telephone and Telegraph Company (AT&T), effective 1 January 1984, under an antitrust consent decree with the United States Department of Justice. AT&T's local telephone operations were split into seven independent Regional Bell Operating Companies, nicknamed the "Baby Bells".

Background

The Bell System had held a near-monopoly over telephone service in the United States for most of the 20th century. The Justice Department filed suit in 1974 (United States v. AT&T) alleging that AT&T used monopoly profits from local service to subsidize its long-distance and equipment businesses.

The decree

The 1982 Modification of Final Judgment, overseen by Judge Harold Greene, required AT&T to divest its 22 local Bell Operating Companies, regrouped into seven regional holding companies: Ameritech, Bell Atlantic, BellSouth, NYNEX, Pacific Telesis, Southwestern Bell, and US West. AT&T kept long-distance service, Western Electric, and Bell Labs.

Aftermath

The breakup intensified competition in long distance and customer equipment. Through later mergers — especially after the Telecommunications Act of 1996 — several Baby Bells recombined; today's AT&T Inc. descends from Southwestern Bell (SBC), and Verizon from Bell Atlantic and NYNEX.
