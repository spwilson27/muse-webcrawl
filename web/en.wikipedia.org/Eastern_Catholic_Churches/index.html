Eastern Catholic Churches

The Eastern Catholic Churches or Oriental Catholic Churches are 23 Eastern Christian autonomous (sui iuris) particular churches of the Catholic Church. They are in full communion with the Pope in Rome while retaining their own liturgical rites, theological emphases, canon law and hierarchies.

History

Several Eastern Catholic churches arose through unions with Rome from the 16th century onward, such as the Union of Brest (1596), which formed the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church, and the Union of Uzhhorod (1646). Others, like the Maronite Church, claim an ancient and unbroken communion with Rome.

Organisation

The largest bodies are patriarchal churches headed by patriarchs (for example the Melkite Greek Catholic Church); others are major archiepiscopal or metropolitan churches. Their law is codified in the Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches (1990).

Practice

Eastern Catholic parishes typically celebrate the Divine Liturgy, permit a married priesthood (in most churches), administer chrismation and communion to infants, and venerate icons — practices closely paralleling Eastern Orthodoxy, from which they differ chiefly in communion with Rome.
