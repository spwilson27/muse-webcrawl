Jura (French pronunciation: [ʒyʁa]) is a department in the Bourgogne-Franche-Comté region of eastern France. It is named after the Jura Mountains, which occupy much of its eastern territory along the border with Switzerland. Its prefecture is Lons-le-Saunier, and its largest city is Dole, a former capital of the historic County of Burgundy.

History

The department was created during the French Revolution on 4 March 1790, in application of the law of 22 December 1789, from part of the former province of Franche-Comté. The territory had been contested for centuries between the Holy Roman Empire, the Duchy of Burgundy, and France before becoming definitively French in 1678 by the Treaty of Nijmegen. During the nineteenth century the department industrialised around watchmaking, eyeglass manufacture, and toy making in towns such as Morez and Saint-Claude.

Geography

Jura is bordered by the departments of Doubs, Haute-Saône, Côte-d'Or, Saône-et-Loire, and Ain, and by the Swiss canton of Vaud. The western part is a lowland plain (the Bresse jurassienne) rising eastward through a wine-growing foothill zone known as the Revermont to the forested plateaus and peaks of the Haut-Jura. The highest point is the Crêt Pela at 1,495 metres. Lakes such as Chalain and Vouglans, and the Hérisson waterfalls, are notable natural attractions.

Economy and culture

The department is known for its wines, including the distinctive vin jaune and vin de paille of Château-Chalon and Arbois, and for Comté cheese, produced from raw cow's milk in village cooperatives. Tourism centres on the regional natural park of the Haut-Jura, cross-country skiing, and the salt works of Salins-les-Bains and Lons-le-Saunier. Dole was the birthplace of Louis Pasteur, whose house there is now a museum.
