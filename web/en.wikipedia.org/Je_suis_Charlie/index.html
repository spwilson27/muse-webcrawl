Je suis Charlie

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: "Je suis Charlie" placards at a solidarity march, January 2015]

"Je suis Charlie" (French for "I am Charlie") is a slogan and symbol created by French art director Joachim Roncin and adopted by supporters of freedom of speech and the press after the January 7, 2015 shooting at the Paris offices of the satirical newspaper Charlie Hebdo, in which twelve people were killed.

Origin and spread

Roncin posted the image — white text on a black background in the style of the magazine's logo — on Twitter within an hour of the attack. The phrase and the hashtag #JeSuisCharlie spread worldwide within hours, appearing on placards, vigils, front pages, and landmarks. On January 11, 2015, millions marched in Paris and other French cities in the "Republican marches", joined by dozens of world leaders, in one of the largest public demonstrations in French history.

Meaning and debate

The slogan expressed solidarity with the victims and defiance of attempts to silence satire by violence. It also sparked debate: critics used counter-slogans such as "Je ne suis pas Charlie" to distance themselves from the magazine's content while condemning the murders, and commentators raised questions about consistency in defending offensive speech. The phrase has since been adapted widely (for example "Je suis Paris" after the November 2015 attacks) and remains an emblem of solidarity after attacks on the press and free expression.
