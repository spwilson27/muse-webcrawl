The Vishnu Purana (Sanskrit: विष्णुपुराण, Viṣṇupurāṇa) is one of the eighteen Mahapuranas, a genre of ancient and medieval texts of Hinduism. It is an important Pancharatra text in the Vaishnavism literature corpus. It is considered one of the most important of the Puranas, and has been extolled as the "Purana-ratna" — the jewel of the Puranas.

The Vishnu Purana is among the shorter Puranic texts, with about 7,000 extant verses in the surviving manuscripts, although tradition attributes 23,000 verses to it. It is primarily centered around the Hindu god Vishnu and his avatars such as Krishna, but it asserts that no god is above another and all are interdependent realities.

Contents

The text is structured as a dialogue between the sage Parashara and his disciple Maitreya. It covers cosmogony, the genealogies of gods, sages and kings, the cycles of time (yugas and manvantaras), earth geography, and the legends of devotees such as Dhruva and Prahlada. The fifth book is largely devoted to the life of Krishna.

Date and editions

Scholars have proposed dates ranging from the 1st century BCE to the early centuries CE for its earliest layers, with the extant form generally placed in the Gupta period. H. H. Wilson published the first English translation in 1840, based on Sanskrit manuscripts he collected.

Influence

The Vishnu Purana's famous statement that the goddess Lakshmi and other deities are inseparable aspects of the divine, and its treatment of bhakti, influenced later Vaishnava theology. Its account of the churning of the ocean of milk and the story of Prahlada remain widely retold in Hindu tradition.
