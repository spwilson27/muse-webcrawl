Makhtesh Ramon

LocationNegev desert, Israel
Lengthabout 40 km (25 mi)
Width2–10 km (1.2–6.2 mi)
Depthabout 500 m (1,600 ft)

Makhtesh Ramon (Hebrew: מכתש רמון) is a geological feature of Israel's Negev desert. It is the world's largest makhtesh — a steep-walled, heart-shaped erosion cirque formed by water eroding through layers of rock — measuring about 40 km long, 2–10 km wide and some 500 m deep.

Geology

Unlike impact craters or volcanic calderas, a makhtesh forms when a ridge of hard rock is breached and softer layers beneath are washed away. Makhtesh Ramon exposes rocks and fossils up to about 220 million years old, and contains the world's only known exposures of certain Triassic marine deposits in the region.

Tourism

The town of Mitzpe Ramon sits on the northern rim, and a visitor centre overlooks the crater. The area is part of a nature reserve popular for hiking, stargazing, and viewing wildlife such as Nubian ibex.
