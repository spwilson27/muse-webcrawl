Táin Bó Cúailnge

The Táin Bó Cúailnge ("The Cattle Raid of Cooley") is an epic tale of early Irish literature, often called the national epic of Ireland. It forms the centrepiece of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology and survives in manuscripts such as the Book of Dun Cow (c. 1100) and the Book of Leinster (c. 1160).

Plot

Queen Medb of Connacht, determined that her wealth should equal her husband Ailill's, launches a great cattle raid to seize the prize bull Donn Cúailnge from Ulster. Because the warriors of Ulster are incapacitated by a curse, the seventeen-year-old hero Cú Chulainn defends the province almost single-handedly, fighting a series of single combats at fords, including the tragic duel with his foster-brother Ferdiad.

Significance

The Táin preserves a vivid picture of pre-Christian Irish society, kingship, warfare and honour, and remains one of the most studied works of medieval Celtic literature.
