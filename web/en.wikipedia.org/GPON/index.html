GPON (Gigabit-capable Passive Optical Network) is a point-to-multipoint fibre access standard defined by the ITU-T G.984 series of recommendations. It is one of the most widely deployed technologies for fibre-to-the-home (FTTH) broadband, used by telecommunications operators worldwide to deliver internet, television, and voice services over a shared optical fibre infrastructure.

Architecture

A GPON network connects an optical line terminal (OLT) at the operator's central office to optical network terminals (ONTs) at customers' premises through passive optical splitters, which require no power in the field. A single feeder fibre can serve up to 64 (occasionally 128) subscribers over distances of about 20 km. Downstream traffic is broadcast to all terminals and encrypted; upstream traffic is shared using time-division multiple access coordinated by dynamic bandwidth allocation.

Line rates and encapsulation

GPON provides 2.488 Gbit/s downstream and 1.244 Gbit/s upstream. Data is carried in GPON Encapsulation Method (GEM) frames, which can transport Ethernet and native TDM traffic. Wavelengths of 1490 nm (downstream) and 1310 nm (upstream) are used, with an optional 1550 nm overlay for analogue video.

Evolution

GPON succeeded earlier ATM-based BPON. Its 10-gigabit successors are XG-PON and the symmetric XGS-PON (ITU-T G.9807.1), which can coexist on the same fibre plant through wavelength multiplexing; 25G and 50G PON generations have followed.
