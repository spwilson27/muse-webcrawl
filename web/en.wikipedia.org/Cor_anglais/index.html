Cor anglais

The cor anglais (French for "English horn") is a double-reed woodwind instrument in the oboe family. Pitched in F, a perfect fifth below the oboe, it is about half again as long, with a distinctive pear-shaped (bulb) bell and a curved metal mouthpiece tube called a bocal.

Sound and repertoire

Its tone is mellower and more melancholic than the oboe's, and composers often use it for pastoral or sorrowful solos. Famous passages include the largo of Dvořák's "New World" Symphony, the third movement of Berlioz's Symphonie fantastique, Wagner's Tristan und Isolde, and Rodrigo's Concierto de Aranjuez.

Name

Despite its name, the instrument is neither English nor a horn; the name may derive from a corruption of cor anglé ("angled horn"), referring to the bent shape of its ancestor, the oboe da caccia.
