House of Lords Act 1999

The House of Lords Act 1999 (c. 34) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom, given royal assent on 11 November 1999. The Act reformed the House of Lords by removing the automatic right of most hereditary peers to sit and vote in the House — a right they had held for centuries.

Provisions

The Act removed all but 92 hereditary peers: 90 elected by the hereditary peers themselves, plus the Earl Marshal and the Lord Great Chamberlain, who retained their seats by virtue of their ceremonial offices. Vacancies among the elected hereditary peers were subsequently filled by by-elections.

Context

The Act was a flagship constitutional reform of Tony Blair's Labour government, elected in 1997. It was intended as a first stage of Lords reform; a promised second stage, involving a substantially or wholly elected chamber, was debated for years but not enacted. The Act's compromise on the 92 peers, negotiated with the Conservative leader in the Lords, Viscount Cranborne — the Weatherill amendment — enabled its passage.
