Burma Campaign

Burma Campaign
Date: January 1942 – July 1945
Location: British Burma (present-day Myanmar)
Result: Allied victory

The Burma Campaign was a series of battles fought in the British colony of Burma (present-day Myanmar) as part of the South-East Asian theatre of the Second World War. It primarily involved forces of the British Empire and China, with support from the United States, against the Imperial Japanese Army, aided by Thai forces and Burmese and Indian nationalist collaborators.

Japanese conquest, 1942

Japan invaded Burma in January 1942, seeking to close the Burma Road supply route to China and to shield its conquests in Malaya and the Dutch East Indies. Rangoon fell in March 1942, and British, Indian and Chinese forces were driven out in the longest retreat in British military history.

Stalemate and turning points, 1943–1944

Allied offensives in the Arakan failed in 1942–43, while Orde Wingate's Chindits conducted long-range penetration operations behind Japanese lines. In 1944 Japan launched Operation U-Go, invading India, but was decisively defeated at the battles of Imphal and Kohima — among the worst defeats suffered by the Japanese Army on land.

Allied reconquest, 1944–1945

Under General William Slim, the British Fourteenth Army advanced through central Burma, recapturing Mandalay and Rangoon in 1945. Chinese and American forces reopened a land route to China (the Ledo Road). Japanese forces in Burma surrendered in 1945.
