Barakah

In Islam, barakah (Arabic: بَرَكَة, "blessing") is a blessing power or divine grace believed to flow from God to particular persons, places, times, objects, and actions, bringing increase, protection, and benefit. The word derives from the Semitic root b-r-k, shared with the Hebrew berakhah. It is a central concept in Muslim devotional life and especially in Sufism.

Sources of barakah

Muslims believe barakah originates with God alone and is granted through the Quran — itself described as blessed — through the example and relics of the Prophet Muhammad, and through sacred times such as Ramadan and the Night of Decree (Laylat al-Qadr), and sacred places such as Mecca, Medina, and Jerusalem. Righteous persons, especially saints (awliya) in the Sufi tradition, are believed to carry barakah that can benefit others, which is why the tombs of saints became centres of pilgrimage (ziyara) across the Muslim world.

Practice

Believers seek barakah through prayer, recitation of the Quran, charity, righteous conduct, and visiting blessed places and persons. Everyday expressions such as barakallahu feek ("may God bless you") reflect the concept's place in ordinary speech. In Sufi orders, spiritual lineage (silsila) is understood as a channel transmitting the founder's barakah to successors. Some reform movements have criticised popular practices associated with barakah — such as seeking blessing from tombs or objects — as improper innovation, making the concept a subject of ongoing theological debate.
