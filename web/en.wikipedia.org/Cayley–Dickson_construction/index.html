Cayley–Dickson construction

In mathematics, the Cayley–Dickson construction, named after Arthur Cayley and Leonard Eugene Dickson, produces a sequence of algebras over the real numbers, each with twice the dimension of the previous one. Starting from the real numbers, it successively generates the complex numbers, the quaternions, the octonions, the sedenions, and beyond.

Definition

Given an algebra A with an involution (conjugation) a ↦ a*, the doubled algebra consists of ordered pairs (a, b) of elements of A, with multiplication defined by (a, b)(c, d) = (ac − d*b, da + bc*) and conjugation (a, b)* = (a*, −b). Each application doubles the dimension.

Loss of properties

Each step of the construction sacrifices an algebraic property. The complex numbers lose the self-conjugacy of the reals; the quaternions lose commutativity of multiplication; the octonions lose associativity (retaining only alternativity); and the sedenions lose the division-algebra property, acquiring zero divisors. Algebras beyond the sedenions (trigintaduonions and higher) lose still more structure.

Significance

The construction explains the special place of dimensions 1, 2, 4, and 8 in mathematics: by Hurwitz's theorem, the reals, complex numbers, quaternions, and octonions are the only normed division algebras over the reals.
