Mexican Independence Day

Mexican Independence Day (Día de la Independencia) is celebrated on 16 September, commemorating the start of the Mexican War of Independence in 1810. It marks the anniversary of the Grito de Dolores ("Cry of Dolores"), the call to arms issued by priest Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla in the town of Dolores on the morning of 16 September 1810.

Celebrations

Festivities begin on the night of 15 September, when the President of Mexico, from the balcony of the National Palace in Mexico City, rings the same bell Hidalgo rang and recites a version of the Grito, ending with shouts of "¡Viva México!" Fireworks, parades, concerts, and traditional food accompany the holiday, and a military parade takes place in the capital on 16 September. Similar ceremonies are performed by governors and mayors throughout the country.

History

Hidalgo's uprising began an eleven-year war that ended with the entry of the Army of the Three Guarantees into Mexico City in 1821 and the Declaration of Independence of the Mexican Empire. Notably, the holiday is far more widely celebrated in Mexico than Cinco de Mayo (5 May), which commemorates the 1862 Battle of Puebla and is more prominent in the United States.
