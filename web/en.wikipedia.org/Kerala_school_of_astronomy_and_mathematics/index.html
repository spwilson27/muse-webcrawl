Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics

The Yuktibhāṣā (c. 1530) contains proofs of the Kerala school's infinite series.

The Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics was a school of mathematics and astronomy founded by Madhava of Sangamagrama in Kerala, India, which flourished between the 14th and 16th centuries. Its members included Parameshvara, Nilakantha Somayaji, Jyeshthadeva, Achyuta Pisharati and others, centred largely on the region around the Bharathappuzha river.

Achievements

The school's most celebrated results are infinite series expansions for the trigonometric functions and for π, obtained some two centuries before equivalent work in Europe. Madhava derived series for sine, cosine and arctangent, including the series now called the Madhava–Leibniz series for π/4, and computed π to 11 decimal places. Nilakantha's Tantrasamgraha (1501) presented a planetary model in which Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn orbit the Sun while the Sun orbits the Earth — geometrically equivalent to the later Tychonic system. Jyeshthadeva's Yuktibhāṣā, written in Malayalam, supplied derivations and proofs (yukti) for these results, an unusually explicit record of mathematical reasoning for its time.

Astronomy

Parameshvara conducted a long programme of eclipse observations over some 55 years and proposed corrections (the drigganita system) to bring computed positions into agreement with observation. The school also refined trigonometric tables, developed methods for computing planetary positions, and treated problems of spherical astronomy.

Transmission debate

Historians have debated whether Kerala results reached Europe through Jesuit missionaries and traders on the Malabar coast in the 16th century, possibly influencing the later European development of calculus. The idea, raised by Charles Whish as early as 1835 and revived by modern scholars, remains contested; no direct documentary chain of transmission has been established, and the mainstream view treats the European calculus as an independent development while fully recognising the Kerala school's priority for the series results.
