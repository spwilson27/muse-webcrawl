Right lymphatic duct

The right lymphatic duct is an important lymphatic vessel that drains the right upper quadrant of the body. It forms variously from the convergence of the right jugular, subclavian, and bronchomediastinal lymph trunks, and it empties into the venous circulation at the junction of the right internal jugular vein and right subclavian vein (the right venous angle).

Structure

The duct is short, about 1.25 cm long, and courses along the medial border of the scalenus anterior muscle at the root of the neck. In many individuals the three trunks that feed it open independently into the great veins rather than joining a single duct. It drains lymph from the right side of the head and neck, the right upper limb, the right side of the thorax (including the right lung, the right side of the heart, and part of the liver's convex surface), and the right breast.

Relation to the thoracic duct

The rest of the body — everything not drained by the right lymphatic duct — returns its lymph to the venous system through the much larger thoracic duct, which empties at the left venous angle. Together the two ducts return several litres of lymph per day to the bloodstream, maintaining fluid balance and transporting absorbed fats and immune cells.

Clinical significance

Injury to the duct during neck surgery can cause a lymph leak (chylous fistula), though this is less common and usually less severe than injury to the thoracic duct on the left.
