George Westinghouse Jr. (October 6, 1846 – March 12, 1914) was an American entrepreneur and engineer based in Pennsylvania who created the railway air brake and was a pioneer of the electrical industry, being one of Thomas Edison's main rivals in the early implementation of the American electricity system.

George Westinghouse, inventor of the air brake and champion of alternating current.

Air brake and railways

Westinghouse patented the railway air brake in 1869, allowing a locomotive engineer to apply the brakes on every car of a train simultaneously with compressed air. The invention dramatically improved railway safety and made longer, faster trains practical. He founded the Westinghouse Air Brake Company and went on to develop railway signalling and switching systems, eventually holding more than 300 patents.

The war of the currents

In the 1880s Westinghouse championed alternating current (AC) against Edison's direct current (DC). Acquiring rights to Nikola Tesla's polyphase AC patents, his Westinghouse Electric company illuminated the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago in 1893 and built the Adams Power Plant at Niagara Falls, which began transmitting power to Buffalo in 1896. AC's victory in the "war of the currents" established the standard for electric power distribution worldwide.
