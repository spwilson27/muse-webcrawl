Muddy Waters

McKinley Morganfield (April 4, 1913 – April 30, 1983), known professionally as Muddy Waters, was an American blues singer and musician who was an important figure in the post-World War II blues scene, and is often cited as the "father of modern Chicago blues".

Career

Raised on Stovall Plantation near Clarksdale, Mississippi, Morganfield was recorded in 1941 by Alan Lomax for the Library of Congress. He moved to Chicago in 1943, switched to electric guitar, and assembled a pioneering combo whose amplified sound defined Chicago blues. For Chess Records he recorded classics including "I'm Your Hoochie Coochie Man", "Mannish Boy", "Rollin' Stone" (which gave the Rolling Stones their name) and "Got My Mojo Working".

Legacy

Waters's band launched the careers of Little Walter, Howlin' Wolf, Otis Spann and Pinetop Perkins, and his music deeply influenced British blues-rock. He won six Grammy Awards and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987.
