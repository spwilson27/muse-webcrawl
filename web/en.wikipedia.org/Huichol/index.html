Huichol (Wixárika)
RegionsSierra Madre Occidental: Jalisco, Nayarit, Durango, Zacatecas (Mexico)
LanguageHuichol (Wixarika), Spanish
ReligionTraditional shamanic religion; some Catholic influence

The Huichol or Wixárika are an indigenous people of Mexico living in the Sierra Madre Occidental range in the states of Jalisco, Nayarit, Durango, and Zacatecas. Numbering roughly 50,000, they are renowned for retaining much of their pre-Columbian language, spirituality, and art, having resisted Spanish conquest in their mountain strongholds.

Religion and pilgrimage

Huichol religion centres on a pantheon including the deer god Kauyumari, maize mother, and the sun, and on shamanic practice led by mara'akame. Each year pilgrims travel to Wirikuta, a sacred desert region in San Luis Potosí, to hunt the peyote cactus (híkuri), which is consumed sacramentally and associated with the deer. Peyote is a protected sacrament for the Wixárika, and the pilgrimage route has been defended against mining concessions and other threats.

Art

Huichol art is internationally famous: yarn paintings (nierakate) pressed into beeswax depict mythological scenes in brilliant synthetic colours, and beadwork (chaquira) covers wooden animal figures, masks, bowls, and skulls. These commercial forms developed in the twentieth century from traditional votive offerings such as prayer bowls and arrows.

Society

Huichol communities govern themselves through a hierarchy of civil and religious offices and communal assemblies centred on ceremonial tukipa temple districts. Maize agriculture, cattle raising, wage labour migration, and the sale of art sustain the communities, while language loss and land disputes remain ongoing concerns.
