World religions

Symbols of the major world religions.

World religions is a category used in the study of religion to demarcate the five — and in some listings the twelve — largest and most internationally widespread religious movements. The conventional "big five" are Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, and Judaism, though expanded lists add traditions such as Chinese folk religion, Sikhism, Jainism, the Baháʼí Faith, Shinto, Taoism, and Zoroastrianism.

Origins of the category

The classification emerged in nineteenth-century European scholarship alongside colonial expansion and the founding of comparative religion as an academic discipline. Scholars such as Tomoko Masuzawa, in The Invention of World Religions (2005), have argued that the category historically privileged traditions resembling Christianity — scriptural, doctrinal, and missionary — while marginalizing indigenous, oral, and localized religions.

Classifications

Religions are commonly grouped as Abrahamic (Judaism, Christianity, Islam, and smaller offshoots), Dharmic or Indian (Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism), and East Asian or Taoic (Taoism, Confucianism, Shinto), alongside indigenous and folk traditions worldwide.
