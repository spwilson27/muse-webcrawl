Sexual abstinence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sexual abstinence or sexual restraint is the practice of refraining from some or all aspects of sexual activity for medical, psychological, legal, social, philosophical, moral, or religious reasons. The abstinence described in this article usually means refraining from vaginal, anal, and oral sex, though definitions vary by context. A person who practises lifelong abstinence from sex is often termed celibate.

Religious and cultural practice

Many religions teach chastity outside marriage and prescribe periods of abstinence: Lent and other fasts in Christianity, Ramadan in Islam, Yom Kippur and the laws of niddah in Judaism, and monastic celibacy in Christianity, Buddhism, Jainism, and Hinduism, where brahmacharya is one of the traditional stages and disciplines of life. Historically, abstinence has also been recommended in athletics and military training on the theory that it preserves strength.

Abstinence-only sex education

Abstinence-only-until-marriage programmes promote abstinence as the expected standard for school-age adolescents and do not teach about contraception except in terms of failure rates. Such programmes received substantial United States federal funding from the 1980s onward. Major reviews by medical and public-health bodies — including a congressionally commissioned evaluation of Title V programmes and reviews by the Society for Adolescent Medicine — have found no evidence that abstinence-only education delays sexual debut or reduces pregnancy or sexually transmitted infection rates, whereas comprehensive sex education that also stresses abstinence has better outcomes.

Effectiveness

Complete abstinence from penetrative sex is fully effective against pregnancy and substantially reduces transmission of sexually transmitted infections, although infections spread by skin-to-skin contact are not wholly prevented, and effectiveness in practice depends on adherence. Some abstainers engage in outercourse or other non-penetrative activity, which lowers but does not eliminate risk.
