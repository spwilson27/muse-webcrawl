Washington wine

Washington wine is wine produced from grape varieties grown in the U.S. state of Washington. Washington is the second-largest premium wine producer in the United States, after California, with over 1,000 wineries and about 60,000 acres of wine grapes.

Geography

Nearly all production is in the rain shadow east of the Cascade Range, in the Columbia Valley AVA and its sub-appellations, including Yakima Valley, Walla Walla Valley, Red Mountain, Horse Heaven Hills and Wahluke Slope. The region's northern latitude gives long summer daylight hours, while large day-night temperature swings preserve acidity. The Missoula Floods deposited the soils on which many vineyards are planted.

Varieties and history

Leading varieties include Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah, Chardonnay and Riesling. Commercial winegrowing began in the mid-20th century with the founding of wineries such as Chateau Ste. Michelle (1967); expansion accelerated from the 1970s onwards. Washington wines are noted for combining ripe fruit with fresh acidity.
