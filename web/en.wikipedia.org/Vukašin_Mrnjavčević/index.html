Vukašin Mrnjavčević (Serbian Cyrillic: Вукашин Мрњавчевић; died 1371) was a Serbian magnate who rose from courtier to king during the decline of the Serbian Empire. Crowned co-ruler by Emperor Stefan Uroš V in 1365, he ruled the western part of the empire from Prilep, and died fighting the Ottoman Turks at the Battle of Maritsa.

Rise to power

Vukašin first appears as a župan (count) at Prilep under Emperor Stefan Dušan. Under the weak Uroš V he advanced rapidly — despot in 1364, then king in 1365 — while his brother Jovan Uglješa governed Serres in the east. The brothers' elevation over the old Nemanjić aristocracy reflected, and accelerated, the empire's fragmentation into regional lordships.

Battle of Maritsa and death

In 1371 Vukašin and Uglješa marched east to confront the Ottomans and, if possible, recover Adrianople. On 26 September 1371 their army was surprised and annihilated at Chernomen on the Maritsa river. Both brothers were killed. The defeat opened Macedonia and much of the Balkans to Ottoman expansion; with Uroš V's death that December, the Serbian Empire effectively ended.

Legacy

Vukašin's son Marko Mrnjavčević succeeded as king but ruled as an Ottoman vassal; in Serbian epic poetry Marko was transformed into the great hero Kraljević Marko, while Vukašin himself is often portrayed ambivalently or negatively in the folk tradition.
