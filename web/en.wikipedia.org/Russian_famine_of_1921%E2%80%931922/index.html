The Russian famine of 1921–1922, also known as the Povolzhye famine, was a severe famine in the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic that began early in spring 1921 and lasted into 1922. It is estimated to have killed around five million people, primarily in the Volga and Ural river regions.

Causes

The famine resulted from a combination of drought, the disruption of the Russian Civil War, and the Bolshevik policy of prodrazvyorstka (forced grain requisitioning), which reduced peasants' incentive and reserves for sowing. War communism, transport collapse and epidemics of typhus and cholera compounded the disaster.

Relief efforts

The Soviet government appealed for international aid in 1921. The American Relief Administration, directed by Herbert Hoover, mounted a major operation feeding millions daily, alongside efforts by Fridtjof Nansen and other organisations. Relief helped end the worst of the crisis by 1922–1923, although the episode left deep demographic and social scars.
