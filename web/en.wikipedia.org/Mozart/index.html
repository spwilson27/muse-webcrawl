Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

Born27 January 1756, Salzburg
Died5 December 1791 (aged 35), Vienna
OccupationComposer, pianist

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (27 January 1756 – 5 December 1791) was a prolific and influential composer of the Classical period. Despite his brief life, his rapid pace of composition and proficiency from an early age resulted in more than 800 works across virtually every genre of his time. Many of these compositions are acknowledged as pinnacles of the symphonic, concertante, chamber, operatic, and choral repertoires. Mozart is widely regarded as one of the greatest composers in the history of Western music.

Born in Salzburg, in the Holy Roman Empire, Mozart showed prodigious ability from his earliest childhood. At age five he was already competent on keyboard and violin and composed his first works; he and his sister Nannerl were exhibited as child prodigies on tours of European courts organized by their father, Leopold Mozart. At 17, Mozart was employed as a court musician in Salzburg, but growing restlessness led him to travel in search of a better post. He settled in Vienna in 1781, leaving the service of Archbishop Colloredo, and there achieved fame — but little financial security — as a pianist and composer.

Works

Mozart's output includes 41 symphonies (among them the "Haffner", "Linz", "Prague", and the final "Jupiter"), 27 piano concertos, concertos for violin, clarinet, horn, and flute, 18 masses, and the unfinished Requiem in D minor, K. 626. His operas — Idomeneo, Die Entführung aus dem Serail, The Marriage of Figaro, Don Giovanni, Così fan tutte, La clemenza di Tito, and The Magic Flute — remain staples of the repertoire. He also composed dozens of string quartets and quintets, piano sonatas, serenades such as Eine kleine Nachtmusik, and the Clarinet Quintet.

Death and legacy

During his final years in Vienna, Mozart composed much of his best-known music while his finances and health declined. He died on 5 December 1791, aged 35, of an illness that remains uncertain; he was buried in a common grave in St. Marx Cemetery, in accordance with Viennese custom of the time. His widow Constanze promoted his works, and his reputation grew steadily after his death. His music is characterized by clarity, balance, and melodic beauty combined with profound emotional depth, and influenced composers from Beethoven onward.
