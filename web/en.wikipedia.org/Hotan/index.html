Hotan

Hotan (Uyghur: خوتەن; Chinese: 和田, Hétián, formerly Khotan) is a prefecture-level city in southwestern Xinjiang, China, and the seat of Hotan Prefecture. An oasis on the southern route of the Silk Road, it sits between the Kunlun Mountains and the Taklamakan Desert, fed by the White Jade (Yurungkash) and Black Jade (Karakash) rivers.

History

The Kingdom of Khotan was an ancient Iranian Saka Buddhist kingdom (c. 1st century BC – 1006 AD) whose language, Khotanese Saka, is known from manuscripts recovered by Aurel Stein at nearby sites such as Dandan Oilik. Legend credits Khotan with receiving silkworm eggs smuggled in a princess's headdress from China. It was conquered by the Karakhanids in 1006, later ruled within Mongol and Qing spheres, and was a centre of 19th-century revolts.

Economy

Hotan remains synonymous with white "mutton-fat" nephrite jade gathered from riverbeds, and produces carpets, atlas silk, and dried fruits. It lies on the southern Taklamakan highway-and-rail loop linking Kashgar to the east.
