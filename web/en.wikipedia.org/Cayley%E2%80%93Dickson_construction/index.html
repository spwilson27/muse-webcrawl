Cayley–Dickson construction

The Cayley–Dickson construction, named after Arthur Cayley and Leonard Eugene Dickson, is a process that produces a sequence of algebras over the real numbers, each with double the dimension of the previous one. Starting from the real numbers it successively yields the complex numbers (dimension 2), the quaternions (dimension 4), the octonions (dimension 8), the sedenions (dimension 16), and so on.

Construction

Given an algebra A with a conjugation operation, the new algebra consists of ordered pairs (a, b) of elements of A, with multiplication defined by (a, b)(c, d) = (ac − d*b, da + bc*), where * denotes conjugation. Each step preserves the norm in the sense that the new algebra has a multiplicative quadratic form as long as the previous one does.

Loss of properties

Each successive algebra loses structural properties: the complex numbers lose the self-conjugacy of the reals; the quaternions lose commutativity; the octonions lose associativity (retaining alternativity); and the sedenions lose even alternativity and the division property, acquiring zero divisors. By Hurwitz's theorem, the reals, complexes, quaternions, and octonions are the only normed division algebras over ℝ.
