Valira

Lengthabout 44 km (in Andorra)
SourcePyrenees, Encamp parish

The Valira (Catalan: Riu Valira) is the principal river of Andorra, a small landlocked country in the Pyrenees between France and Spain. Nearly the entire territory of Andorra drains into the Valira and its tributaries, and the river flows south through the capital, Andorra la Vella, before crossing into Spain and joining the Segre, a tributary of the Ebro.

Course

The river is formed by the confluence of two main branches. The Valira del Nord (Valira de la Massana) descends from the north-west of the country through La Massana and Ordino, while the Valira d'Orient (Valira de Encamp) flows from the east through Encamp and Canillo. They meet at Escaldes-Engordany, and the united Valira — also called the Gran Valira — passes through Andorra la Vella and Santa Coloma before leaving Andorra at the Spanish border. It then flows a short distance through Catalonia to join the Segre at La Seu d'Urgell.

Geography and use

The Valira valley is the main axis of settlement in Andorra: most of the country's population lives along its banks. The river's waters have historically powered mills and today support small hydroelectric installations, notably at FEDA's Encamp plant. The Valira is subject to strong seasonal variation, with high flows in spring from snowmelt, and it has flooded on several recorded occasions, most severely in 1982. Its valley floors are bordered by the steep glacial troughs and cirques typical of the central Pyrenees.
