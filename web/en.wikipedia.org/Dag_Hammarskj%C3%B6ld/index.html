Dag Hammarskjöld

Dag Hammarskjöld
Born: 29 July 1905, Jönköping, Sweden
Died: 18 September 1961, Ndola, Northern Rhodesia
Office: 2nd Secretary-General of the United Nations (1953–1961)

Dag Hjalmar Agne Carl Hammarskjöld (29 July 1905 – 18 September 1961) was a Swedish economist and diplomat who served as the second Secretary-General of the United Nations from April 1953 until his death in a plane crash in 1961.

Tenure

Hammarskjöld developed UN peacekeeping, notably during the Suez Crisis of 1956, and pursued quiet preventive diplomacy. During the Congo Crisis he sought a ceasefire in Katanga.

Death

He died when his aircraft crashed near Ndola in Northern Rhodesia (now Zambia) en route to negotiations. The circumstances remain disputed, and inquiries have been reopened several times. He was posthumously awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1961.

Writings

His spiritual diary, Markings (Vägmärken), was published posthumously in 1963 and became a bestseller.
