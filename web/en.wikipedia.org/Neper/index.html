Neper

The neper (symbol: Np) is a logarithmic unit for ratios of measurements of physical field and power quantities, such as gain and loss of electronic signals. The unit's name is derived from the name of John Napier, the inventor of logarithms. As is the case for the decibel and bel, the neper is a unit in the International System of Quantities (ISQ). It is not part of the International System of Units (SI), but it is accepted for use alongside the SI.

Definition

The neper expresses the ratio of two quantities as a natural logarithm of that ratio. For two values x and x0 of the same kind, the ratio expressed in nepers is L = ln(x / x0). One neper corresponds to a field-quantity ratio of e ≈ 2.71828, and a power-quantity ratio of e2 ≈ 7.389.

The neper is related to the decibel by the conversion 1 Np = 20 / ln(10) dB ≈ 8.6859 dB. Conversely, 1 dB ≈ 0.11513 Np.

Usage

The neper is used less often than the decibel in practical engineering, but it arises naturally in theoretical treatments where the natural logarithm is the mathematically natural choice, for example in describing attenuation, propagation constants in electromagnetic theory, and absorption in optics. The neper is a coherent derived unit in the sense that its definition involves no numerical factor.
