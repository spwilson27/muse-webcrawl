Data cap

A data cap, also known as a bandwidth cap, is the maximum amount of data that an Internet service provider (ISP) or mobile network operator allows a subscriber to transfer over a fixed period, typically a month. Exceeding the cap may result in throttled speeds, overage charges, or suspension of service.

Rationale and criticism

Providers justify caps as a means of managing network congestion, recovering infrastructure costs, and price-differentiating heavy users. Critics — including consumer advocates and net-neutrality supporters — argue that caps on modern fixed networks are primarily a business tool that discourages use of competing streaming services and penalises cord-cutting, noting that the marginal cost of data is low. Some providers exempt their own services from caps ("zero-rating"), a practice restricted in some jurisdictions.

Usage

Caps are common on mobile data plans worldwide and on fixed broadband in some markets. "Unlimited" plans frequently remain subject to fair-use policies that deprioritise heavy users after a threshold. During the COVID-19 pandemic several major ISPs temporarily suspended caps as work and schooling moved online.
