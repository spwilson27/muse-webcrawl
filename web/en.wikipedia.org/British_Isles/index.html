British Isles

The British Isles are an archipelago in the North Atlantic Ocean off the north-western coast of continental Europe, consisting of the islands of Great Britain, Ireland, and over 6,000 smaller islands, together covering about 315,000 square kilometres (122,000 sq mi). Politically, the archipelago is divided between two sovereign states: the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland, along with the Crown Dependencies of the Isle of Man and the Channel Islands (the latter geographically nearer to France).

Geography

Great Britain, at 209,331 km², is the largest island in Europe and ninth largest in the world; Ireland is the second largest of the group. The terrain ranges from the Scottish Highlands, home to Ben Nevis, the archipelago's highest point at 1,345 metres (4,413 ft), to the low-lying fens of East Anglia. The climate is temperate maritime, moderated by the North Atlantic Drift, with mild winters and cool summers for its latitude. The islands were connected to mainland Europe by the land bridge of Doggerland until it was submerged by rising seas after the last ice age.

The term

"British Isles" derives ultimately from the Pretannike of Greek geographers, but the term is politically contentious in Ireland, where it is seen as implying British sovereignty over the whole archipelago; the Irish government does not use it, and official Anglo-Irish documents generally refer to "these islands" or "Britain and Ireland."
