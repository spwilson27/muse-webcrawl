Bungalow

A bungalow is a small single-storey house, often with a low-pitched roof, wide veranda, and overhanging eaves. The term derives from the Hindi bangla, referring to houses in Bengal adapted by British colonists in India.

Bungalows spread to Britain in the late 19th century and became hugely popular in North America in the early 20th century, especially the Craftsman bungalow promoted by Gustav Stickley and sold as kits by Sears. They defined the streetcar suburbs of cities such as Los Angeles, Chicago, and Toronto.

In British usage 'bungalow' denotes any detached single-storey house; dormer bungalows have rooms in the roof. The style remains associated with modest, efficient, ground-level living.
