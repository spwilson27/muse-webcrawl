Maranello

Maranello (Modenese: Maranèl) is a town and comune in the province of Modena, Emilia-Romagna, northern Italy, 18 km south of Modena, with a population of about 17,500. It is known worldwide as the home of Ferrari and the Formula One team Scuderia Ferrari, whose factory and headquarters have been located there since the 1940s.

Ferrari

Enzo Ferrari moved his operations from Modena to Maranello in 1943, purchasing land there and building the factory that still produces Ferrari road cars. Adjacent to the works are the Fiorano Circuit, Ferrari's private test track opened in 1972, the Museo Ferrari museum — one of the most visited museums in Emilia-Romagna — and restaurants and hotels serving the steady stream of enthusiasts. The town's identity is bound up with the company: its bell tower rings the church bells when Scuderia Ferrari wins a Formula One Grand Prix.

Geography and history

Maranello lies at the foot of the Apennines along the Via Giardini, a road built in the 18th century by the Este dukes of Modena. The area has been inhabited since the Bronze Age; the medieval castle of Maranello, whose remains stand on a hill above the town, guarded the route into the mountains. Today, besides Ferrari, the comune hosts the Ferrari-themed museum, the library designed by Arata Isozaki and the public auditorium named after Enzo Ferrari.

Sister cities

Maranello is twinned with Oviedo, Spain, among others, and maintains friendly relations with several towns associated with motorsport.
