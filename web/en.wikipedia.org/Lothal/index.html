Lothal

Lothal
Location: Gujarat, India
Culture: Indus Valley Civilisation
Period: c. 2400–1600 BCE
Excavated: 1954–1963, Archaeological Survey of India (S. R. Rao)
[Image placeholder: The ancient dockyard basin at Lothal]

Lothal was one of the southernmost sites of the Indus Valley Civilisation, located in the modern state of Gujarat, India. Settled around 2400 BCE, it grew into an important port city trading beads, gems and ornaments with Mesopotamia and other Harappan centres.

The dockyard

Lothal is most famous for its enormous brick-lined basin, interpreted by excavator S. R. Rao as the world's earliest dock: a trapezoidal structure of burnt bricks, roughly 214 by 36 metres, connected to the Sabarmati river and the Gulf of Khambhat. Some scholars dispute the identification, suggesting an irrigation reservoir instead. A warehouse on a high platform stood beside the basin.

Town and crafts

The town was laid out on a grid with a citadel and lower town, drainage systems, and workshops for bead-making in carnelian and steatite. Lothal's seals, weights and burials show strong Harappan character with local traits. The city declined after repeated flooding around 1900 BCE and was finally abandoned about 1600 BCE.
