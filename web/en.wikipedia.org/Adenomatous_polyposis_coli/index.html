Adenomatous polyposis coli

Adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) is a protein in humans encoded by the APC gene, a tumour suppressor gene located on chromosome 5. The APC protein acts as a negative regulator of the Wnt signalling pathway by promoting the destruction of beta-catenin.

Function

APC forms part of a destruction complex with axin, GSK-3 beta and casein kinase 1 that targets beta-catenin for degradation. When APC is lost, beta-catenin accumulates and drives transcription of genes promoting cell proliferation.

Clinical significance

Inherited mutations in APC cause familial adenomatous polyposis, in which hundreds to thousands of polyps develop in the colon, almost inevitably progressing to colorectal cancer if untreated. Somatic APC mutations are among the earliest events in most sporadic colorectal cancers.
