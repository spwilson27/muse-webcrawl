Astra (satellite)

Astra satellites
Operator: SES
First launch: Astra 1A, 11 December 1988
Main orbital positions: 19.2°E, 28.2°E, 23.5°E, 5°E, 31.5°E

Astra is the brand name for a number of geostationary communication satellites, both individually and as a fleet, that are owned and operated by SES, a global operator based in Betzdorf, Luxembourg. The Astra satellites, which carry television and radio channels across Europe, were among the first direct-to-home satellite systems and helped establish satellite television as a mass medium in Europe.

History

The first Astra satellite, Astra 1A, was launched in December 1988 by an Ariane 4 rocket from Kourou, French Guiana. Positioned at 19.2 degrees east, it initially carried 16 transponders and provided services such as Sky Television to the United Kingdom and channels to continental Europe. The system expanded with further satellites co-located at the same orbital slots, an approach that let viewers receive many channels with a single dish.

Co-location became a hallmark of the Astra system: multiple satellites share one orbital position, providing backup capacity and allowing large numbers of channels to be broadcast to a fixed dish. By the early 1990s Astra served millions of homes, and the position at 28.2 degrees east was developed principally for the United Kingdom and Ireland, later used by Sky and the free-to-air Freesat platform.

Services

Astra satellites broadcast hundreds of television channels, including free-to-air and encrypted services, high-definition and ultra-high-definition channels, and radio stations. The fleet also provides broadband-by-satellite services and occasional-use feeds for news gathering.
