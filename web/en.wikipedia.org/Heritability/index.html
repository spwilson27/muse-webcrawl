Heritability

Heritability is a statistic used in the fields of breeding and genetics that estimates the degree of variation in a phenotypic trait in a population that is due to genetic variation between individuals in that population. In other words, the concept of heritability describes how much of the differences between people (or other organisms) in a given characteristic are attributable to genetic differences, as opposed to differences in environment.

Definitions

Broad-sense heritability, H², is the ratio of total genetic variance to total phenotypic variance (H² = VG/VP), where genetic variance includes additive, dominance and epistatic effects. Narrow-sense heritability, h², is the ratio of additive genetic variance to phenotypic variance (h² = VA/VP); it is the quantity that predicts the response of a trait to selection and underlies the breeder's equation, R = h²S. Values range from 0 (variation entirely environmental) to 1 (variation entirely genetic).

Estimation

Heritability is estimated by comparing trait resemblance among relatives: parent–offspring regression, comparisons of monozygotic and dizygotic twins, adoption studies, and, increasingly, genome-wide association data, in which DNA markers are used to measure genetic relatedness directly (SNP heritability). Animal and plant breeders estimate it from designed experiments and use it to predict gains from artificial selection for traits such as milk yield, growth rate or disease resistance.

Interpretation and misconceptions

Heritability is a property of a population in a particular environment, not of a trait in the abstract: a trait can be highly heritable in one setting and weakly heritable in another if environmental variation differs. High heritability does not mean a trait is unchangeable (height, for example, is highly heritable yet average heights rose markedly with improved nutrition), nor does it say anything about the cause of differences between populations. Because heritability concerns variation rather than the trait's value in any individual, it is frequently misinterpreted in public debate, and careful exposition stresses these limits.
