Calabar (also referred to as Callabar, Calabari, Calbari and Kalabar) is the capital city of Cross River State in south-eastern Nigeria. The city sits on a hill overlooking the Calabar River and the Cross River estuary, near the Gulf of Guinea. It was originally named Akwa Akpa in the Efik language.

History

Calabar was an important port in the Atlantic slave trade: from the 17th to the 19th century a large share of enslaved Africans shipped to the Americas passed through its estuary, carried by Efik traders. After the trade's decline, the city became a centre for palm oil exports. In the colonial period Calabar served as the capital of the Oil Rivers Protectorate and later of the Southern Nigeria Protectorate until 1906, when the administration moved to Lagos. It is also noted for the work of the Scottish missionary Mary Slessor, who campaigned against the killing of twins in the region.

Modern city

Today Calabar is known as one of Nigeria's major tourism destinations, hosting the month-long Calabar Carnival each December, often described as "Africa's biggest street party", and the Tinapa Resort. The University of Calabar and the Cross River University of Technology are located in the city, and the Calabar sea port serves trade in Nigeria's South-South region.
