The Polish Underground State (Polish: Polskie Państwo Podziemne) was a set of clandestine underground structures created in German- and Soviet-occupied Poland during the Second World War, loyal to the Polish government-in-exile in London. It comprised military, political, judicial, educational and cultural institutions — a complete secret state functioning under occupation, unique among the occupied countries of Europe.

Structure

The political arm was the Government Delegation for Poland (Delegatura Rządu na Kraj), headed by the Government Delegate, who held the rank of deputy prime minister. From 1944, a Council of National Unity acted as an underground parliament representing the major political parties. The military arm was the Home Army (Armia Krajowa), formed in February 1942 from the Union of Armed Struggle, which by 1944 numbered some 350,000–400,000 sworn members, making it one of the largest resistance movements in Europe.

Activities

The Underground State ran secret courts that tried collaborators and informers; an extensive clandestine education system, including underground universities attended by thousands of students; a large underground press of over a thousand periodicals; welfare services; and intelligence networks that supplied the Western Allies with valuable information, including on the V-1 and V-2 rockets. Its emissaries, such as Jan Karski, carried reports on the Holocaust to the West, and its Directorate of Civil Resistance organized non-violent opposition.

Warsaw Uprising and dissolution

The Underground State's military bid culminated in the Warsaw Uprising of August–October 1944, part of Operation Tempest, intended to liberate the capital before the arrival of the Red Army. After the uprising's failure and the Soviet installation of the rival Lublin Committee, the Home Army was disbanded in January 1945 and the Council of National Unity dissolved itself on 1 July 1945, following the arrest of sixteen underground leaders by the NKVD — the Trial of the Sixteen in Moscow. Many former members were persecuted under the postwar communist regime.
