Alexander Tamanian

Alexander Tamanian (4 March 1878 – 20 February 1936) was a Russian-Armenian neoclassical architect, best known for his city master plan of Yerevan, the capital of Armenia, and for designing many of the capital's landmark buildings. He is regarded as the founder of modern Armenian architecture.

Early life and career

Tamanian was born in Yekaterinodar (now Krasnodar), Russia, to a family from Karabakh. He graduated from the St Petersburg Academy of Arts in 1904 and built a successful career in imperial Russia, designing estates and buildings including the Kochubey mansion at Tsarskoye Selo and work on the village hospital and station at Prozorovo.

Work in Armenia

In 1919 Tamanian moved to Armenia and led the reconstruction of the new republic's capital. His 1924 master plan for Yerevan laid out a radial-concentric garden city for about 150,000 inhabitants, centred on what became Republic Square and the Opera Theatre district, with broad avenues oriented toward Mount Ararat. Among his major works are the Yerevan Opera Theatre (Freedom Theatre), the Government House on Republic Square, the university observatory and the Sevan–Yerevan hydroelectric stations.

Legacy

Although many of Tamanian's projects were modified or left unfinished after his death in Yerevan in 1936, the essential structure of his plan still defines central Yerevan today. A statue of Tamanian stands near the Cascade, and the Tamanian Museum-Institute in Yerevan preserves his archive.
