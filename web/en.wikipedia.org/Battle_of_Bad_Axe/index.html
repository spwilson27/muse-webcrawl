Battle of Bad Axe

Date1–2 August 1832
LocationMouth of the Bad Axe River, present-day Vernon County, Wisconsin
ResultUnited States victory; end of the Black Hawk War
BelligerentsUnited States vs. Sauk and Fox "British Band"
CasualtiesAt least 150 Native Americans killed; 5 US soldiers killed

The Battle of Bad Axe, also known as the Bad Axe Massacre, was a battle and massacre that occurred on 1–2 August 1832, between United States Army troops and Sauk and Fox Native Americans at the mouth of the Bad Axe River in what is now Wisconsin. The engagement marked the end of the Black Hawk War, in which the Sauk leader Black Hawk had led a band of about 1,000 men, women, and children across the Mississippi River into Illinois in defiance of removal treaties.

Background

After defeats and a punishing retreat northward through Wisconsin Territory — including the fight at Wisconsin Heights on 21 July — Black Hawk's starving band reached the Mississippi hoping to cross to safety in Iowa. Black Hawk attempted to surrender to the steamboat Warrior under a white flag on 1 August, but the exchange miscarried and the steamboat opened fire with cannon and small arms.

The battle

On 2 August, US regulars and militia under Generals Henry Atkinson and Milton Alexander attacked the band on the east bank of the Mississippi. Many Sauk and Fox noncombatants tried to flee across the river on rafts or by swimming; hundreds were shot in the water or killed on the banks, while Dakota Sioux warriors allied with the United States attacked survivors on the western shore. Estimates of Native American deaths range from 150 to more than 300. Black Hawk escaped temporarily but surrendered later that month.

Aftermath

The battle ended organized Native resistance in the Old Northwest for the region then being settled. Black Hawk was imprisoned, later released, and dictated his autobiography — the first published Native American autobiography in the United States. Historians today frequently describe the engagement as a massacre because of the killing of large numbers of women, children, and surrendering fighters.
