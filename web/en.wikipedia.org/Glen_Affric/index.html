LocationHighland, Scotland
DesignationNational Nature Reserve; National Scenic Area

Glen Affric (Scottish Gaelic: Gleann Afraig) is a glen south-west of the village of Cannich in the Highland region of Scotland, some 15 miles (24 km) west of Loch Ness. It is frequently described as one of the most beautiful glens in Scotland. The glen contains Loch Affric and Loch Beinn a' Mheadhoin, the River Affric, and one of the largest remnants of the ancient Caledonian pine forest.

Landscape and wildlife

The native Scots pine woodland of Glen Affric, together with birch, juniper, and other species, forms part of a major conservation and rewilding effort led by Forestry and Land Scotland and the charity Trees for Life, which works to restore the forest and its wildlife. Red deer, golden eagles, ospreys, pine martens, and Scottish crossbills are among the species found in the glen. Guisachan, at the glen's eastern end, was the ancestral home of the golden retriever breed, developed on the estate of Dudley Marjoribanks, 1st Baron Tweedmouth, in the 19th century.

Protection

Glen Affric is designated a National Nature Reserve and a National Scenic Area, and much of it is protected as a Site of Special Scientific Interest and Special Area of Conservation.
