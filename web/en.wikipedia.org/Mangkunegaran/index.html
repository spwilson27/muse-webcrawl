Mangkunegaran

The Mangkunegaran is a Javanese princely state (praja) in Surakarta, Central Java, Indonesia. It was established in 1757 by Raden Mas Said — later Mangkunegara I — after his long rebellion against the Susuhunan of Surakarta and the Dutch East India Company, and was settled by the Treaty of Salatiga (1757), which granted him territory carved from the Surakarta Sunanate.

History

Unlike the hereditary monarchies of Surakarta and Yogyakarta, the Mangkunegaran ruler was formally chosen from the founder's descendants and held the rank of prince rather than king; the state maintained its own army, the Legiun Mangkunegaran. Under Mangkunegara IV (r. 1853–1881) the principality prospered through sugar estates and became a notable patron of Javanese arts, including gamelan and dance; Mangkunegara VII built the palace's celebrated pendopo and founded the RRI radio predecessor. In 1946, during the Indonesian revolution, the principality was incorporated into the Republic of Indonesia, though its palace (Pura Mangkunegaran) remains a functioning court and cultural centre headed by its prince.

Palace and culture

The Mangkunegaran Palace in Surakarta, begun in 1757 and expanded over the 19th century, blends Javanese and European styles and houses a museum of court heirlooms. The court is renowned for its classical dances (such as Bedhaya and Gambyong in the Mangkunegaran style) and its gamelan ensembles.
