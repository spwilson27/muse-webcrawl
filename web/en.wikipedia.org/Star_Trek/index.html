Star Trek

The USS Enterprise, the iconic starship of the Star Trek franchise.

Star Trek is an American science fiction media franchise created by Gene Roddenberry. It began with the television series Star Trek (later called The Original Series), which debuted in 1966 and aired for three seasons on NBC, following the voyages of the starship USS Enterprise and its crew, led by Captain James T. Kirk, First Officer Spock, and Dr. Leonard McCoy.

The franchise

The franchise expanded with an animated series, a film series beginning in 1979, and the sequel series The Next Generation (1987–1994), followed by Deep Space Nine, Voyager, and Enterprise. A reboot film series began in 2009, and streaming-era series such as Discovery, Picard, and Strange New Worlds premiered from 2017 onward. Star Trek has inspired books, comics, games, and a devoted fan culture.

Themes and legacy

Star Trek is known for its optimistic vision of the future, its diverse casts, and its exploration of social, political, and philosophical issues through allegory. Its depiction of advanced technology such as communicators and tablet computers is often credited with inspiring real-world inventions, and its influence on popular culture and the space program is widely recognized — NASA named its first Space Shuttle Enterprise after a fan campaign.
