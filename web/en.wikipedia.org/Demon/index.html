Demon

A demon is a supernatural being, typically considered malevolent, found in religions, folklore and literature across many cultures. The word derives from the Greek daimōn, which originally denoted a spirit or divine power — sometimes benevolent, like the guiding spirit of Socrates — before acquiring its sinister sense in later Jewish and Christian thought.

In religions

In ancient Mesopotamia and Egypt, demons such as Pazuzu and the Mesopotamian udug were blamed for disease and misfortune and were countered with amulets and exorcisms. Zoroastrianism opposed the daevas to the good ahuras. In Judaism, beings such as the shedim and the demon Asmodeus appear in post-biblical literature, while the New Testament depicts Jesus casting out demons that possess and torment people. Christian theology, systematised by writers such as Augustine and Aquinas, generally treated demons as fallen angels serving Satan. Islam recognises the shayatin, demons descended from or led by Iblis, alongside the morally ambivalent jinn. Hindu and Buddhist traditions include beings such as asuras and rakshasas and the tempter Mara.

Cultural legacy

Medieval and early modern grimoires catalogued demons in elaborate hierarchies, while witchcraft panics linked demons to pacts and possession. Today demons are fixtures of horror fiction, film and games, and the term is used metaphorically — Maxwell's demon in physics, or the "demons" of addiction and mental illness.
