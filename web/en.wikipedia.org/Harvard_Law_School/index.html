Harvard Law School (HLS) is the law school of Harvard University in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Founded in 1817, it is the oldest continuously operating law school in the United States and one of the most prestigious in the world. It is home to the largest academic law library in the world and to the Harvard Law Review, and its graduates include a large share of the nation's judges, senators, governors and law-firm partners.

History

The school was founded in 1817, funded by a bequest from Isaac Royall Jr. — a slaveholder whose family crest long appeared on the school's shield before its retirement in 2016 following protests over the Royall family's slave-owning history. Under its early professors Joseph Story and Simon Greenleaf, HLS became the first university law school of national standing. As dean from 1870 to 1895, Christopher Columbus Langdell revolutionised legal education, introducing the case method — the study of law through appellate opinions, interrogated in class by the Socratic method — which became the standard pedagogy of American law schools. The Harvard Law Review was founded by students in 1887.

Programmes and campus

HLS enrolls about 560 students in each JD class — far larger than its peer schools — divided in the first year into sections of about 80 with which students take most classes. It also offers the LLM and the research doctorate SJD, plus joint degrees across Harvard. The campus on the north side of Harvard Yard centres on Langdell Hall (1907), whose library holds some 1.7 million volumes, and the Wasserstein Hall, Caspersen Student Center and Clinical Wing complex (2011). Student organisations publish more than a dozen law journals, and the school's clinics and the student-run Harvard Legal Aid Bureau (1913) provide extensive practical training.

People

Alumni include US presidents Rutherford B. Hayes and Barack Obama, First Lady Michelle Obama, numerous Supreme Court justices — among them Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. studied at the school, and Elena Kagan, who served as its dean (2003–2009) before joining the Court — and Chief Justice John Roberts. Its faculty has included figures such as Felix Frankfurter, Laurence Tribe, Cass Sunstein and Elizabeth Warren.
