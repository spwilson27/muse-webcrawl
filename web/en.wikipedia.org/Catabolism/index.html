Catabolism

Catabolism (from Greek κάτω kato, "downward", and βάλλειν ballein, "to throw") is the set of metabolic pathways that breaks down molecules into smaller units that are either oxidized to release energy, or used in other anabolic reactions. Catabolism is the breaking-down aspect of metabolism, whereas anabolism is the building-up aspect.

Overview

Large molecules such as polysaccharides, lipids, nucleic acids, and proteins are broken down into smaller units such as monosaccharides, fatty acids, nucleotides, and amino acids, respectively. Cells then use the energy released by catabolic reactions — often captured as adenosine triphosphate (ATP) — to drive anabolic reactions, maintain temperature, and power movement.

Stages

Catabolism is often described in three stages: (1) digestion, in which large organic molecules are broken down outside or inside cells; (2) the release of energy as smaller molecules are converted to key intermediates such as acetyl-CoA; and (3) the oxidation of acetyl groups in the citric acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation, producing most of the cell's ATP.

Catabolic hormones

Catabolic processes are promoted by hormones including cortisol, glucagon, and adrenaline, which mobilize nutrient stores. Catabolic states occur during starvation, illness, and intense exercise; excessive tissue catabolism leads to muscle wasting.
