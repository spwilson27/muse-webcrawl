Brand (play)

AuthorHenrik Ibsen
Published1866
GenreVerse tragedy
LanguageNorwegian (Dano-Norwegian)

Brand is a verse tragedy in five acts by the Norwegian playwright Henrik Ibsen, published in 1866. Written while Ibsen was living in Italy, it was his first major success and established his reputation across Scandinavia, though it was conceived as a dramatic poem for reading rather than immediate stage performance.

Plot

Brand, a priest of uncompromising idealism, demands "all or nothing" from himself and everyone around him. Returning to his home village in the Norwegian fjords, he refuses to soften his principles even when doing so costs the lives of his mother, his child, and his wife Agnes. He builds a new church but finally rejects it as too small for his vision, leading his congregation toward the mountains, where he is abandoned and dies in an avalanche.

Themes and reception

The play interrogates the collision between absolute idealism and human frailty, faith and fanaticism; Brand can be read as a hero of integrity or as a warning against inhuman rigidity. Its success allowed Ibsen financial independence, and its philosophical intensity anticipates his later drama, including its companion piece Peer Gynt (1867), whose protagonist is Brand's opposite.
