Voltmeter
Measures: Electric potential difference
Unit: Volt (V)
Connection: In parallel with the component

A voltmeter is an instrument used for measuring the electric potential difference between two points in an electric circuit. It is connected in parallel with the component being measured and is designed to have a very high internal resistance so that it draws negligible current.

Analog voltmeters

Analog voltmeters use a moving-coil galvanometer in series with a large resistance; the deflection of a pointer across a graduated scale indicates the voltage. The sensitivity of such instruments is specified in ohms per volt of full-scale range.

Digital voltmeters

Digital voltmeters (DVMs) convert the input voltage to a digital value, typically with an analog-to-digital converter using dual-slope integration, and display the result numerically. They form the measurement core of the digital multimeter and offer high accuracy and input impedance.

History

Early voltmeters were developed in the late nineteenth century, building on galvanometer designs by figures such as Edward Weston, whose instruments set early standards for electrical measurement.
