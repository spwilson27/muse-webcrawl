Scientific management

Scientific management is a theory of management that analyzes and synthesizes workflows, with the main objective of improving economic efficiency, especially labor productivity. It was one of the earliest attempts to apply science to the engineering of processes and to management. Its development began with Frederick Winslow Taylor in the 1880s and 1890s within the manufacturing industries, and its most influential statement was Taylor's book The Principles of Scientific Management (1911). The approach is also known as Taylorism.

Principles

Taylor proposed four principles: (1) replace rule-of-thumb work methods with methods based on scientific study of tasks, using time-and-motion studies; (2) scientifically select, train, and develop each worker rather than leaving them to train themselves; (3) cooperate with workers to ensure the scientifically developed methods are followed; and (4) divide work nearly equally between managers and workers, with managers planning the work and workers executing it. Piece-rate incentive pay was used to reward workers who met the standards.

Contributors and influence

Other contributors included Frank and Lillian Gilbreth, who developed motion study; Henry Gantt, creator of the Gantt chart; and Henry Ford, whose moving assembly line applied related ideas on a vast scale. Scientific management influenced industrial engineering, operations management, and mass production worldwide in the early 20th century, and was adopted and debated in countries from France to the Soviet Union.

Criticism

Critics, including labor unions, argued that Taylorism deskilled work, treated workers as machines, and intensified labor; a strike at the Watertown Arsenal in 1911 led to congressional hearings and a temporary ban on stopwatch studies in U.S. government arsenals. Later management thought, including the human relations movement that followed the Hawthorne studies, reacted against its neglect of social and psychological factors, though many of its techniques persist in modern operations management.
