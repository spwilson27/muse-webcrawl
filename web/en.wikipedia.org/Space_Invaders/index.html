Space Invaders is a 1978 shoot 'em up arcade video game developed by Taito and designed by Tomohiro Nishikado. It was one of the earliest shooting games and is widely credited with helping to launch the golden age of arcade video games and expanding the video game industry from a novelty into a global phenomenon.

Gameplay

The player controls a laser cannon that moves horizontally along the bottom of the screen, firing at five rows of descending aliens. The invaders move side to side and drop toward Earth, speeding up as their numbers thin — an accidental effect of the hardware rendering fewer sprites that Nishikado kept because it created a natural difficulty curve. Four destructible shields protect the cannon, a periodic "mystery ship" crosses the top for bonus points, and the game ends if the aliens reach the bottom or the player loses all lives.

Impact

The game was a sensation in Japan, where it reputedly caused a shortage of 100-yen coins, and the Atari 2600 version of 1980 was the first officially licensed arcade conversion and the console's first "killer app", quadrupling sales. Its pixel-art invader — the "Space Invader crab" — became an enduring pop-culture icon, and the game influenced generations of designers, setting templates for shooter gameplay, high-score tables and increasing difficulty.
