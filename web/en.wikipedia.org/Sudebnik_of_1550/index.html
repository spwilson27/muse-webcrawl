Sudebnik of 1550

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A page from a manuscript of the Sudebnik of 1550.

The Sudebnik of 1550 (Russian: Судебник 1550 года), also known as the Sudebnik of Ivan IV or the Tsar's Sudebnik, is a law code of the Tsardom of Russia promulgated in 1550 under Ivan IV (Ivan the Terrible). It revised and expanded the earlier Sudebnik of 1497, containing 100 articles.

Contents

The code systematised court procedure, fees and punishments, enlarged the role of elected local representatives (such as the tselovalniki and elders) in trials alongside state judges, and restricted the judicial powers and income abuses of the viceroys (namestniki). It confirmed and tightened rules binding peasants: the right to move between landlords was confined to the week around St. George's Day in autumn, and the exit fee (pozhiloe) was increased — steps in the enserfment of the Russian peasantry.

Significance

The Sudebnik formed part of the reform programme of Ivan's early reign associated with the Chosen Council and the first Zemsky Sobor of 1549. It remained the core of Russian law until superseded by the Sobornoye Ulozheniye of 1649.
