Teleplay

A teleplay is a screenplay or script used in the production of a scripted television program or series. Teleplays can be original works or adaptations of existing material, and are written in a format specific to television production, which differs from the screenplay format used for theatrical films.

Format

Teleplays typically divide an episode into acts separated by commercial breaks — historically four acts for an hour-long drama, with additional teaser and tag scenes. Sitcom teleplays, especially multi-camera productions, use a distinct double-spaced format with dialogue centered and production notes for live studio taping.

Terminology

The term was coined in the early years of television drama, when anthology series broadcast live plays from New York. Famous early teleplays include Paddy Chayefsky's Marty and Rod Serling's Patterns, both later adapted as films. The Writers Guild of America credits distinguish "teleplay by" from "story by", reflecting the division of labour in television writing rooms.
