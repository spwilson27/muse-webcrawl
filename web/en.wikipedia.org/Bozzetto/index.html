Bozzetto

TypePreparatory sculptural model
MediumClay, terracotta, wax, or wood
PeriodEspecially Renaissance and Baroque

A bozzetto (Italian for "small sketch") is a small-scale preliminary model made by a sculptor in preparation for a larger work, usually executed quickly in clay, terracotta, wax, or wood. Bozzetti allowed artists to explore composition and pose in three dimensions before committing to marble or bronze.

Use in practice

During the Renaissance and Baroque periods, sculptors presented bozzetti to patrons for approval before beginning the full-size work, and used them as guides in the workshop. Because they were working tools rather than finished pieces, surviving examples were long undervalued; many were discarded once the final sculpture was complete.

Collecting and study

Today bozzetti are prized by museums and scholars for the immediacy of the artist's handling and the insight they give into creative process. The rapid, sketch-like modelling of artists such as Gian Lorenzo Bernini is often more freely expressive in the bozzetto than in the polished final work. Related terms include the modello, a more finished presentation model, and the maquette.
