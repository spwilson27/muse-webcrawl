Yang Liwei (Chinese: 杨利伟; born 21 June 1965) is a Chinese major general and taikonaut who, on 15 October 2003, became the first person sent into space by the Chinese space programme aboard Shenzhou 5. The flight made China the third country to independently launch a human into orbit, after the Soviet Union and the United States.

Early life and career

Yang was born in Suizhong County, Liaoning. He joined the People's Liberation Army Air Force in 1983, qualified as a fighter pilot, and logged some 1,350 hours of flight time. In 1998 he was selected for the first group of Chinese taikonauts.

Shenzhou 5

Launched from Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center on a Long March 2F rocket, Shenzhou 5 completed 14 orbits in 21 hours 23 minutes before landing in Inner Mongolia on 16 October 2003. Yang carried Chinese and United Nations flags and reported significant vibration during ascent, a detail he later discussed candidly. He was celebrated nationwide as a hero on his return.

Later role

Yang did not fly again but became a senior figure in China's human spaceflight administration, serving as deputy director of the China Manned Space Agency and promoting the programme publicly, including its space station and lunar ambitions.
