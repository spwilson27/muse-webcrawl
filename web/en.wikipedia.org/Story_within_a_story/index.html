Story within a story

A story within a story, also called an embedded narrative, is a literary device in which a character within a story tells another story, or the narrative itself introduces a second narrative level. The outer narrative is termed the frame story when it exists mainly to provide a setting for the inner tales.

History and examples

The device is ancient: in the Odyssey, Odysseus narrates his own wanderings to the Phaeacians, and the Sanskrit Panchatantra nests animal fables inside successive frames. The most famous frame story is One Thousand and One Nights, in which Scheherazade tells the king a new tale each night to postpone her execution, with tales frequently containing further stories inside them. Chaucer's Canterbury Tales uses a pilgrimage as its frame, and the framing can recur many levels deep in works such as the nested narrators of Frankenstein.

Functions

Embedded narratives can provide exposition, mirror or contrast the themes of the outer story, create suspense through delay, or lend authority by presenting the inner tale as a document or testimony. In film, the device appears in works structured as flashbacks narrated by a character, such as The Princess Bride, itself framed as a grandfather reading a book aloud.
