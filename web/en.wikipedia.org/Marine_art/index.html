Marine art

Media: Painting, drawing, printmaking
Themes: Ships, the sea, naval battles, coasts

Marine art or maritime art is a form of figurative art — principally painting, but also drawing and prints — that portrays or draws its main inspiration from the sea. It traditionally focuses on ships and sailing, and flourished especially in the Netherlands in the 17th century and in Britain in the 18th and 19th centuries.

History

Ships appear in art from ancient times, but marine painting became a distinct specialisation in the Dutch Golden Age, with artists such as Jan van Goyen and Willem van de Velde the Younger. In Britain, J. M. W. Turner made the sea a central subject of Romantic painting.

Ship portraits

A related genre is the ship portrait, a precise depiction of a particular vessel, often commissioned by its owner or captain. Marine art remains a recognised specialism, with societies such as the Royal Society of Marine Artists in the United Kingdom.
