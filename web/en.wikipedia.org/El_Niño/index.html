El Niño
PhenomenonWarm phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO)
RegionCentral and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean
RecurrenceEvery 2–7 years, lasting 9–12 months
CounterpartLa Niña (cool phase)
EffectsAltered rainfall, droughts, floods, fisheries collapse, global temperature rise

El Niño (Spanish for "the Child", originally referring to the Christ Child, because the warming often peaked near Christmas off Peru) is the warm phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO), a coupled ocean–atmosphere phenomenon in the tropical Pacific. During an El Niño, trade winds weaken, warm surface water spreads eastward across the equatorial Pacific, and the usual upwelling of cold, nutrient-rich water off South America is suppressed.

Mechanism

In normal conditions, easterly trade winds pile warm water in the western Pacific and sustain cold upwelling in the east. In El Niño years this circulation falters: sea-surface temperatures in the central and eastern Pacific rise by 0.5 °C or more above average, deep atmospheric convection shifts east with the warm water, and the Southern Oscillation — the seesaw in air pressure between Tahiti and Darwin — reverses. The opposite phase, with strengthened trades and unusual cooling, is called La Niña.

Impacts

El Niño events rearrange rainfall worldwide: torrential rain and flooding strike Peru, Ecuador and the southern United States, while drought afflicts Indonesia, Australia, India and southern Africa. Peruvian anchoveta fisheries collapse when upwelling fails, coral reefs bleach in the warmed ocean, and global average temperatures typically spike in the year following an event. The very strong El Niños of 1982–83, 1997–98 and 2015–16 each caused tens of billions of dollars in damage and thousands of deaths.

Forecasting

Networks of moored buoys (the TAO/TRITON array), satellites and coupled climate models now allow forecasters to anticipate events several months ahead, enabling governments to prepare for droughts and floods. Research continues into how climate change affects ENSO's frequency and intensity.
