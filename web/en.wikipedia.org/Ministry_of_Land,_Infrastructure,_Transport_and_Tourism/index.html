The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (国土交通省, Kokudō-kōtsū-shō), abbreviated MLIT, is a ministry of the Government of Japan responsible for land use and planning, infrastructure, transport, tourism, meteorology, and maritime safety. It is one of Japan's largest ministries by budget and personnel, overseeing a large share of public works spending.

MLIT was created on 6 January 2001 as part of the central government reform under Prime Minister Ryutaro Hashimoto, by merging the former Ministries of Transport and Construction with the Hokkaido Development Agency and the National Land Agency. Its portfolio covers roads, railways, aviation, ports, housing, river and coast management, disaster prevention, and the promotion of tourism through the Japan Tourism Agency.

Organization

External agencies under MLIT include the Japan Meteorological Agency, the Japan Coast Guard, the Japan Transport Safety Board (which investigates transport accidents), and the Japan Tourism Agency. The ministry is headed by the Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, a member of the Cabinet of Japan.
