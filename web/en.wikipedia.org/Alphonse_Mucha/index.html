Alphonse Mucha

Alfons Maria Mucha (24 July 1860 – 14 July 1939), known internationally as Alphonse Mucha, was a Czech painter, illustrator, and graphic artist, best known for his distinctly stylized theatrical posters, particularly those of Sarah Bernhardt, and regarded as one of the defining figures of the Art Nouveau movement.

Mucha's posters feature sinuous lines and halo-like circles around idealized women.

Career

Mucha worked as a decorative painter in Moravia and Vienna before studying in Munich and, from 1887, in Paris. His breakthrough came at Christmas 1894 when he designed, at very short notice, a poster for Sarah Bernhardt's play Gismonda: its elongated format, muted pastel palette, and mosaic-like halo behind the actress's head caused a sensation and won him a six-year contract with Bernhardt. The "Mucha style" — beautiful women in flowing robes surrounded by flowers and intricate patterns — came to define the visual language of Art Nouveau and was applied to posters, advertisements, menus, and decorative panels.

The Slav Epic

After several years lecturing and working in the United States, Mucha returned home and devoted nearly two decades (1910–1928) to The Slav Epic, a cycle of twenty monumental canvases depicting the history and mythology of the Slavic peoples, financed by the American philanthropist Charles Richard Crane. He also designed Czechoslovakia's first postage stamps and banknotes after independence in 1918. Arrested and interrogated by the Gestapo after the German occupation of Prague in 1939, he died of pneumonia shortly after his release.
