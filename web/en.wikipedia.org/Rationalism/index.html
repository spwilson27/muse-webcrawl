In philosophy, rationalism is the epistemological view that reason is the chief source and test of knowledge, or that reality itself has an inherently logical structure. The classic rationalists of the 17th century — René Descartes, Baruch Spinoza, and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz — held that, at least in principle, all knowledge can be gained through purely a priori means, especially mathematical and metaphysical truths, and that the mind possesses innate ideas or innate structures that experience alone cannot supply.

Background

Descartes sought certain foundations for knowledge, arriving at the cogito ("I think, therefore I am") and building a deductive system grounded in clear and distinct ideas guaranteed by a non-deceiving God. Spinoza's Ethics, written in geometrical order, deduced a monistic metaphysics in which God and Nature are one substance. Leibniz distinguished truths of reason (necessary, knowable a priori) from truths of fact (contingent), and proposed the principle of sufficient reason and a metaphysics of monads. Ancient antecedents include Plato's theory of Forms and the mathematical orientation of Pythagoras.

Rationalism versus empiricism

Rationalism is traditionally contrasted with empiricism — the view, associated with John Locke, George Berkeley, and David Hume, that all knowledge ultimately derives from sense experience and that the mind begins as a blank slate (tabula rasa). Immanuel Kant attempted a synthesis: knowledge begins with experience but is structured by a priori forms of intuition (space and time) and categories of the understanding. The distinction remains a standard framing device in histories of early modern philosophy, though scholars note the historical figures' positions are more nuanced.

Modern senses

Beyond epistemology, "rationalism" can denote a commitment to reason and evidence over tradition, authority, or faith (as in Enlightenment rationalism), an architectural movement favouring functional form, and, in international relations theory, the English School tradition associated with Martin Wight.
