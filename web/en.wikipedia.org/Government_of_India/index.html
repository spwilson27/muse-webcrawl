Government of India

The Government of India (Hindi: Bhārat Sarkār) is the central government of the Republic of India, a federal union comprising 28 states and 8 union territories. It is governed under the Constitution of India, adopted in 1950, which establishes a parliamentary system based on the Westminster model, with executive, legislative, and judicial branches at both the union and state levels.

Branches

The executive consists of the President of India, who is head of state and formally vested with executive power, the Vice President, and the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister, who exercises effective executive authority and is accountable to the Lok Sabha. The legislature, Parliament, is bicameral: the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) and the Lok Sabha (House of the People). The judiciary is headed by the Supreme Court of India, with high courts in the states; it has the power of judicial review over legislation.

Federal structure

The Seventh Schedule of the constitution distributes powers between the union and the states through the Union, State, and Concurrent Lists. Residual powers lie with the union. Finance is shared through the Finance Commission and, since 2017, the Goods and Services Tax Council. The civil services — notably the Indian Administrative Service — staff the permanent administration at both levels, and the Election Commission conducts elections to Parliament and state legislatures.
