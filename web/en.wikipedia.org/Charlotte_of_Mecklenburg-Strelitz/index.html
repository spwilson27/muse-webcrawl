Biography

Charlotte of Mecklenburg-Strelitz (Sophia Charlotte; 19 May 1744 – 17 November 1818) was Queen of Great Britain and Ireland and later Queen of the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland and Electress (later Queen) of Hanover as the wife of King George III, from their marriage on 8 September 1761 until her death in 1818. The couple had 15 children, 13 of whom survived to adulthood.

Early life and marriage

Charlotte was the youngest daughter of Duke Charles Louis Frederick of Mecklenburg-Strelitz and Princess Elizabeth Albertine of Saxe-Hildburghausen. She grew up at Mirow and received an education strong in botany, natural history, languages and music. When George III came to the throne in 1760, the 17-year-old Charlotte was selected as his bride. She arrived in London on 7 September 1761 and married the king the following day at the Chapel Royal, St James's Palace; her coronation followed on 22 September.

Queen consort

Charlotte was a patron of the arts and sciences. She supported the composer Johann Christian Bach, aided the expansion of Kew Gardens, and helped to found hospitals for expectant mothers, one of which survives as Queen Charlotte's and Chelsea Hospital. An amateur botanist, she took a keen interest in the work of Sir Joseph Banks. Strelitzia, the bird-of-paradise flower genus, was named in her honour.

From 1811 the king's mental illness made Charlotte his guardian under the regency of their son, the Prince of Wales. She died at Kew Palace on 17 November 1818 with her eldest son at her side, having been queen consort for 57 years and 70 days — the longest tenure of any British queen consort.
