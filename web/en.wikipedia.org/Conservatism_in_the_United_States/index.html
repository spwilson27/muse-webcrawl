Conservatism in the United States is a political philosophy and movement based on a belief in limited government, gun rights, individualism, traditionalism, republicanism, and limited federal governmental power in relation to U.S. states. Conservative social and political values have often overlapped with classical liberal ideas, and American conservatism is typically associated with the Republican Party.

History

Scholars trace American conservative themes to the Federalists, the Whigs, and nineteenth-century business-oriented politics, though the self-conscious modern movement emerged after the Second World War. Russell Kirk's The Conservative Mind (1953) gave the movement an intellectual genealogy, and William F. Buckley Jr.'s National Review (founded 1955) fused traditionalism, free-market economics, and anti-communism into a single coalition — a synthesis Frank Meyer called "fusionism."

Barry Goldwater's 1964 presidential campaign, though defeated, realigned the Republican Party toward the movement. Ronald Reagan's election in 1980 marked its ascendancy: tax cuts, deregulation, increased military spending, and confrontational Cold War policy defined the Reagan era. Later phases included the congressional majorities of 1994, the presidency of George W. Bush, the Tea Party movement, and the populist turn associated with Donald Trump.

Beliefs

American conservatives typically support lower taxes, free markets, deregulation, gun rights, restrictions on abortion, school choice, and a strong military. Social conservatives emphasize traditional family structures and religious values, while libertarian-leaning conservatives emphasize individual liberty and minimal state intervention. Tensions between these factions — and between free-trade globalism and economic nationalism — recur throughout the movement's history.

Media and institutions

Conservative ideas are promoted through think tanks such as the Heritage Foundation and the American Enterprise Institute, legal organizations such as the Federalist Society, and a large media ecosystem spanning talk radio, cable news, and online outlets.
