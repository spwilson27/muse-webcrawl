Milan

Milan (Italian: Milano) is a city in northern Italy, capital of Lombardy, and the second-most populous city in Italy after Rome, with about 1.4 million residents in the city proper and over 3 million in the metropolitan area. Milan is Italy's financial and industrial centre, a global capital of fashion and design, and home to the Italian stock exchange (Borsa Italiana).

History

Founded by the Celtic Insubres around 590 BC and conquered by Rome in 222 BC, Mediolanum became one of the capitals of the Western Roman Empire; Constantine issued the Edict of Milan (313) here, granting toleration to Christianity. In the Middle Ages Milan grew into a powerful city-state under the Visconti and Sforza dynasties, who built the Duomo and Sforza Castle and hosted Leonardo da Vinci at court. The city later passed under Spanish, Austrian, and Napoleonic rule before Italian unification in 1859. Milan was heavily bombed in the Second World War and rebuilt as Italy's economic engine during the post-war "economic miracle".

Culture and landmarks

The Milan Cathedral (Duomo), one of the largest Gothic churches in the world, dominates the central piazza, beside the glass-vaulted Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II arcade (1865–77). The opera house La Scala (1778) is among the world's most famous. Santa Maria delle Grazie houses Leonardo's The Last Supper. Milan Fashion Week and the Salone del Mobile furniture fair anchor the city's fashion and design industries.

Economy and sport

Milan is the seat of major banks, publishers, and luxury brands, and hosts Italy's principal financial markets. The city is home to two top football clubs, AC Milan and Inter, who share the San Siro stadium. Milan hosted Expo 2015 and is scheduled to co-host the 2026 Winter Olympics with Cortina d'Ampezzo.
