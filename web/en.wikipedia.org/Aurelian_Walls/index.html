Aurelian Walls

Location: Rome, Italy
Built: AD 271–275
Length: about 19 km (original circuit)

The Aurelian Walls are a line of city walls built between AD 271 and 275 in Rome during the reign of the emperor Aurelian. They replaced and extended beyond the earlier Servian Wall as the city had long outgrown it.

Construction

The circuit ran about 19 kilometres, incorporating existing buildings such as the Pyramid of Cestius and the Amphitheatrum Castrense to speed construction. Emperor Honorius later roughly doubled the height of the walls around AD 401–403.

Later history

The walls remained Rome's principal defence until 1870, when Bersaglieri breached them near Porta Pia during the capture of Rome. Long stretches, gates and towers survive today.
