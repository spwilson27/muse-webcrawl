RIAA equalization


Placeholder image: RIAA equalization

RIAA equalization is a specification for the recording and playback of phonograph records, established by the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA) in 1954. It defines a standard pre-emphasis curve applied during recording and a complementary de-emphasis curve applied on playback.

How it works

During recording, low frequencies are attenuated and high frequencies are boosted. This allows narrower groove excursions for bass, letting more music fit on a disc, while raising treble above surface noise. On playback the curve is inverted: bass is restored and treble reduced, which also lowers hiss and rumble.

Curve

The curve is defined by three time constants: 3180 µs (bass turnover), 318 µs (treble plateau) and 75 µs (high-frequency rolloff). Nearly all vinyl records made since the late 1950s, and every phono preamplifier, follow the RIAA standard, which displaced the many competing pre-1955 curves such as Columbia LP and Decca ffrr.
