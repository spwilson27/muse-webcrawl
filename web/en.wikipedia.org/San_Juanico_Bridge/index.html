The San Juanico Bridge (Filipino: Tulay ng San Juanico), officially the President Ferdinand E. Marcos Bridge, spans the San Juanico Strait in the Philippines, connecting the city of Tacloban on the island of Leyte with the municipality of Santa Rita on the island of Samar. At 2.16 kilometres (1.34 mi), it was the longest bridge in the Philippines at the time of its completion and remains among the country's iconic engineering landmarks.

Construction

Construction began in 1969 under the administration of President Ferdinand Marcos, with Japanese financing and engineering assistance channelled through the Japan International Cooperation framework. The bridge, a steel truss arch structure with a main span rising above the narrowest and deepest part of the strait, was inaugurated on 2 July 1973. Its silhouette, rising in an arc on the Leyte side and levelling toward Samar, is often described as tracing the letters "L" and "S" for Leyte and Samar.

Significance

The bridge carries the Pan-Philippine Highway (Asian Highway 26), providing the only permanent road link between Leyte and Samar and a key connection between Luzon-bound ferry traffic and Eastern Visayas. It suffered minor damage from Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda) in November 2013, which devastated Tacloban, and was quickly reopened to serve relief operations.

A second San Juanico crossing has been proposed to meet growing traffic demand.
