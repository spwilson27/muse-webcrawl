Anabasis (Xenophon)

The Anabasis (Greek: Ἀνάβασις, "an expedition up-country" or "going up") is the best-known work of the Athenian soldier and historian Xenophon, written around 370 BC. It recounts the expedition of Greek mercenaries, the Ten Thousand, hired by Cyrus the Younger in his revolt against his brother, the Persian king Artaxerxes II, in 401 BC — and their long fighting retreat to the Black Sea after Cyrus was killed.

Summary

Cyrus marched from Sardis deep into Mesopotamia, where at the Battle of Cunaxa (401 BC) the Greek hoplites routed the troops facing them — but Cyrus himself was slain, leaving the expedition purposeless. When the Greek generals were treacherously seized and executed by the satrap Tissaphernes under flag of truce, the mercenaries elected new leaders, among them Xenophon, and fought their way north through hostile peoples including the Carduchians and through the winter snows of Armenia. Famously, on reaching Mount Theches and sighting the Black Sea, the soldiers cried "Thálatta! Thálatta!" — "The sea! The sea!" From Trapezus they proceeded along the coast back to Greek territory.

Legacy

The Anabasis demonstrated the vulnerability of the Persian Empire to a Greek army and helped inspire Alexander the Great's later conquest. Its clear prose made it a standard first text for students of Greek for centuries. Xenophon refers to himself in the third person throughout the work.
