Musicology

Musicology (from Greek mousikē, "music", and -logia, "study") is the scholarly study of music. It encompasses the history, theory, and cultural contexts of music, and applies methods from history, anthropology, sociology, psychology, and the sciences.

Branches

  • Historical musicology studies the history of Western art music, composers, works, and performance practices, often through source studies and critical editions.
  • Ethnomusicology studies music in its cultural contexts worldwide, historically emphasizing non-Western and folk traditions, using fieldwork methods.
  • Systematic musicology includes music theory, acoustics, psychology of music, music aesthetics, and music sociology.
  • New musicology, emerging in the 1980s, brought cultural studies, gender studies, and critical theory to bear on the musical canon.

History

The discipline took institutional form in nineteenth-century Germany, where scholars such as Guido Adler set out its scope. The twentieth century saw the founding of professional societies — including the American Musicological Society (1934) — and major reference works such as The New Grove Dictionary of Music and Musicians.
