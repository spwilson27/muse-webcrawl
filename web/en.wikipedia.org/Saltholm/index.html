Saltholm

Saltholm is a Danish island in the Øresund (the Sound), the strait between Denmark and Sweden. It lies east of the Danish island of Amager in the municipality of Tårnby and covers an area of about 16 square kilometres, making it Denmark's 21st largest island, though its highest point is only a few metres above sea level.

Geography and nature

The island is a flat limestone plateau covered with salt meadow and used for centuries as grazing land for cattle and sheep from Amager. It is an important breeding and resting site for wildfowl, including large colonies of eider ducks, swans, geese and waders, and much of the island is a protected nature reserve with restricted public access during the breeding season.

History

Saltholm has been used since the Middle Ages for grazing, limestone quarrying and fishing. In the early 18th century it served as a quarantine station during plague outbreaks. During the First World War, Denmark fortified the island with coastal artillery (the Barakkebatteriet) to guard the approaches to Copenhagen. Saltholm was considered in the 1970s as the site for a new Copenhagen airport, but the plan was abandoned. Today only a handful of people live on the island year-round, and there is no regular ferry service; access is by private boat.
