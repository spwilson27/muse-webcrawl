Santiago de Cuba is the second-largest city in Cuba and the capital of Santiago de Cuba Province, located on a bay on the island's southeastern coast beneath the Sierra Maestra mountains. Founded by the Spanish conquistador Diego Velázquez in 1515, it served as Cuba's first capital until 1553 and remains a major port and cultural centre, celebrated as the birthplace of son cubano and of the Cuban Revolution's opening act.

History

Santiago was a base for expeditions to the mainland, including Hernán Cortés's conquest of Mexico. In 1898, during the Spanish–American War, the Spanish fleet was destroyed in the Battle of Santiago de Cuba and American forces captured the city. On 26 July 1953 Fidel Castro led the failed attack on the Moncada Barracks in Santiago, the event that launched the Cuban Revolution; Castro proclaimed victory in the city on 1 January 1959. Landmarks include the Castillo de San Pedro de la Roca, a 17th-century fortress guarding the bay that is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Culture

The city is renowned for its Afro-Cuban culture and music — son, trova, and the July Carnival, one of Cuba's largest festivals. It is the burial place of José Martí, Antonio Maceo, and Fidel Castro at Santa Ifigenia Cemetery, and home to the Bacardí rum dynasty's original distillery and museum.
