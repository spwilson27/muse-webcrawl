Johannes Vermeer (October 1632 – December 1675) was a Dutch Baroque painter who specialized in domestic interior scenes of middle-class life. He is now considered one of the greatest painters of the Dutch Golden Age. Working slowly and with great care, using expensive pigments such as ultramarine and lead-tin yellow, he produced a small oeuvre of about 37 paintings, including Girl with a Pearl Earring, The Milkmaid and View of Delft.

Life

Vermeer spent his whole life in Delft, where his father kept an inn and dealt in art. In 1653 he married Catharina Bolnes, of a wealthy Catholic family, and joined the Guild of Saint Luke. His principal patron was Pieter van Ruijven, who bought much of his output. Vermeer's career suffered in the disaster year of 1672, when the art market collapsed after the French invasion of the Netherlands.

Work and technique

Most of Vermeer's paintings show quiet rooms lit from a window at the left — women reading letters, making music, pouring milk — rendered with a luminous, almost photographic clarity of light. Scholars have long debated whether he used optical devices such as the camera obscura; characteristic effects such as soft discs of light have fuelled the theory. His drawings of colour and stillness give the scenes their distinctive serenity.

Death and rediscovery

Vermeer died suddenly in 1675, leaving his widow with eleven children and heavy debts; she blamed the stress of the collapsed market. His reputation faded for two centuries until the French critic Théophile Thoré-Bürger championed him in the 1860s. In the 20th century his small body of work became the target of famous forgeries by Han van Meegeren.
