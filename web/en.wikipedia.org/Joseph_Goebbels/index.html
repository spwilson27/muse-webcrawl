Paul Joseph Goebbels (October 29, 1897 – May 1, 1945) was a Nazi politician and philologist who was Adolf Hitler's Reich Minister of Propaganda from 1933 to 1945. One of Hitler's closest associates and most devoted followers, he was known for his skills in public speaking and his deeply virulent antisemitism, which was evident in his publicly voiced views. He advocateded progressively harsher discrimination, including the extermination of the Jews in the Holocaust.

Early life and rise

Goebbels, who had a deformed right foot, earned a doctorate in philology from Heidelberg in 1921 and worked as a journalist and frustrated novelist before joining the Nazi Party in 1924. As Gauleiter of Berlin from 1926 and the party's chief propagandist from 1930, he pioneered modern political marketing: mass rallies, radio, posters and the relentless repetition of slogans.

Propaganda minister

In power, Goebbels controlled Germany's press, radio, film and theatre through the Reich Chamber of Culture, staged the 1933 book burnings, helped orchestrate the Kristallnacht pogrom of November 1938, and produced antisemitic films such as Jud Süß (1940). During the war he sustained home-front morale, delivering the "Total War" speech in February 1943 after the defeat at Stalingrad, and in 1944 became Reich Plenipotentiary for Total War.

Death

Named Chancellor in Hitler's political testament, Goebbels served one day. On May 1, 1945, he and his wife Magda poisoned their six children and took their own lives in the garden of the Reich Chancellery as Soviet troops closed in on the Führerbunker.
