Oxymoron

An oxymoron (from Greek oxys, "sharp/keen", and moros, "dull/stupid" — itself an oxymoron, "sharp dullness") is a figure of speech in which two contradictory terms appear together for rhetorical effect, as in "cruel kindness", "deafening silence", "living death", or "bittersweet".

Usage

Oxymorons are used in literature and rhetoric to create dramatic effect, compress paradox into a phrase, or reveal a deeper truth in apparent contradiction. Shakespeare abounds in them: Romeo exclaims "O brawling love! O loving hate!" Other common examples include "jumbo shrimp", "act naturally", and "original copy". A distinction is sometimes drawn between deliberate rhetorical oxymorons and accidental contradictions in terms, which comedians exploit in phrases like "military intelligence".

Related figures

The oxymoron is closely related to the paradox, in which a whole statement appears self-contradictory yet may express a truth, and to antithesis, which juxtaposes contrasting ideas in balanced clauses. Unlike a contradiction in terms used in logic, the rhetorical oxymoron is not a fallacy but a deliberate stylistic device.
