Richard Posner

Richard Allen Posner (born 11 January 1939) is an American legal scholar and retired federal judge who served on the US Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit from 1981 to 2017. A leading figure in the law and economics movement, he has been among the most cited legal scholars of the twentieth century.

Career

Posner graduated from Yale and Harvard Law School, clerked for Justice William J. Brennan Jr., and taught at Stanford and the University of Chicago Law School, where he helped found the Journal of Legal Studies. President Ronald Reagan appointed him to the Seventh Circuit in 1981; he served as chief judge from 1993 to 2000 and retired in 2017.

Scholarship

His books include Economic Analysis of Law (1973), The Economics of Justice, Sex and Reason, and How Judges Think (2008). Posner championed applying economic reasoning — efficiency, incentives, and cost-benefit analysis — to areas from antitrust to torts, and later became a prominent critic of aspects of the legal profession and of his own earlier positions.

Influence

Posner's opinions were known for their clarity and pragmatic style, and his extrajudicial writing made him a public intellectual on topics from intelligence reform to the financial crisis of 2008.
