History of animation

The history of animation traces the art of creating the illusion of movement through sequential images, from prehistoric cave art and nineteenth-century optical toys to hand-drawn cartoons, stop motion, and today's computer-generated imagery.

Early devices and pioneers

The nineteenth century produced a series of devices exploiting persistence of vision: the phenakistiscope (1832), the zoetrope (1830s–60s) and Émile Reynaud's praxinoscope and Théâtre Optique, which projected hand-drawn animated stories as early as 1892. With cinema, J. Stuart Blackton's Humorous Phases of Funny Faces (1906) and Émile Cohl's Fantasmagorie (1908) founded drawn animation, while Winsor McCay's Gertie the Dinosaur (1914) showed its expressive potential. Ladislas Starevich pioneered puppet stop motion, and Lotte Reiniger made the oldest surviving animated feature, The Adventures of Prince Achmed (1926).

The studio era

In the 1920s and 1930s animation industrialised: cel animation, pioneered by Earl Hurd and John Bray, allowed division of labour; Walt Disney's Steamboat Willie (1928) synchronised sound, and Flowers and Trees (1932) used Technicolor. Disney's Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937) was the first American animated feature, followed by a "golden age" of Disney, Warner Bros. (Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck), MGM (Tom and Jerry) and Fleischer Studios. In Japan, anime developed its own tradition; in Britain and Eastern Europe, distinctive schools flourished.

Television and limited animation

From the 1950s, television economics encouraged limited animation — Hanna-Barbera's The Flintstones (1960) being a landmark prime-time series. UPA's stylised modernism influenced design worldwide. Feature animation diversified: stop-motion masters such as Jiří Trnka and later Aardman Animations, and Japanese features culminating in Studio Ghibli (founded 1985).

Computer animation

Experiments in computer animation date to the 1960s (Ivan Sutherland, John Whitney). Pixar's Toy Story (1995) was the first fully computer-animated feature and inaugurated the CGI era that came to dominate commercial animation, while hand-drawn and stop-motion work continued artistically. Motion capture, real-time rendering and web animation further transformed the field in the twenty-first century.
