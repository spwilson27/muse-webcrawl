Cunobelinus

Illustration: Cunobelinus
Cunobelinus
Reign: c. AD 9 – c. 41
Tribe: Catuvellauni / Trinovantes
Capital: Camulodunum (Colchester)

Cunobelinus (died c. AD 41) was a king in pre-Roman Britain, ruling the Catuvellauni and Trinovantes from about AD 9 until about 41. From his capital at Camulodunum he became the most powerful ruler in Britain, controlling territory across the south-east.

Reign

Known from his coins—stamped CVNOBELINVS—and from references in Suetonius and Cassius Dio, Cunobelinus expanded his influence over Kent and the middle Thames, displaced rivals, and traded with Roman Gaul. Suetonius called him "king of the Britons".

Legacy

His sons included Caratacus and Togodumnus, who resisted the Roman invasion of AD 43. In medieval Welsh tradition he became Cynfelyn, and through Geoffrey of Monmouth's retelling he entered English literature as Shakespeare's Cymbeline.
