Palestine (region)

Palestine is a geographic region in West Asia, situated between the Mediterranean Sea and the Jordan River and various adjoining lands. The name derives from the ancient Philistines and has been used since Greek and Roman times for the area roughly encompassing modern Israel, the Palestinian territories, and parts of Jordan, Lebanon, and Syria.

Geography

The region includes the coastal plain along the Mediterranean, the central hill country (Judea and Samaria), the Jordan Valley and Dead Sea depression, and the Galilee in the north. Major cities include Jerusalem, Gaza, Hebron, Nablus, Haifa, and Jaffa.

History

Palestine was ruled successively by Egyptians, Israelites, Assyrians, Babylonians, Persians, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, Arab caliphates, Crusaders, Mamluks, and the Ottoman Empire (1516–1917). After the First World War it came under the British Mandate for Palestine. In 1948 the State of Israel was established and surrounding areas came under Jordanian and Egyptian administration; Israel occupied the West Bank and Gaza Strip in 1967.

Modern usage

Today "Palestine" also refers to the State of Palestine, recognised by a majority of UN member states, comprising the West Bank and Gaza Strip, where the Palestinian Authority exercises limited self-rule.
