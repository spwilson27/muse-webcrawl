Mono no aware

Mono no aware (物の哀れ), literally "the pathos of things," is a Japanese aesthetic concept describing a gentle, wistful sensitivity to the impermanence of things — an awareness that beauty is heightened because it does not last.

Origin

The term was developed in the Edo period by the kokugaku scholar Motoori Norinaga in his studies of The Tale of Genji, where he identified mono no aware as the novel's central emotional and aesthetic principle: deep feeling in response to the world, especially its transience.

Examples

The classic example is the cherry blossom, cherished in Japan precisely because its bloom is brief. The concept recurs in later Japanese literature and cinema, informing quiet contemplation of seasonal change, aging, and loss.
