Coma

A coma is a deep state of prolonged unconsciousness in which a person cannot be awakened, fails to respond normally to painful stimuli, light, or sound, lacks a normal sleep–wake cycle, and does not initiate voluntary actions. It results from severe injury or disturbance to the brain's arousal systems.

Causes

Causes include traumatic brain injury, stroke, brain tumours, drug or alcohol intoxication, metabolic disorders such as diabetic ketoacidosis and liver failure, infections, and cardiac arrest with hypoxic injury.

Assessment

Depth of coma is commonly measured with the Glasgow Coma Scale, scoring eye, verbal, and motor responses from 3 to 15. Pupil reactions, brainstem reflexes, neuroimaging, and EEG help determine cause and prognosis. A coma rarely lasts more than a few weeks; outcomes range from full recovery to vegetative state, minimally conscious state, or death.

Treatment

Emergency care stabilises airway, breathing, and circulation, treats reversible causes such as hypoglycaemia, and manages intracranial pressure. The term also has astronomical and optical meanings: the coma of a comet and the lens aberration coma.
