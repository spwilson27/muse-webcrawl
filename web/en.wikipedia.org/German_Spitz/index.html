German Spitz

The German Spitz (German: Deutscher Spitz) is a breed of spitz-type dog from Germany. It is recognised by the Fédération Cynologique Internationale in five size varieties, from the large Wolfspitz (internationally known as the Keeshond) down to the Toy Spitz, the Pomeranian. The larger German Spitz varieties historically worked as watchdogs and companions on farms and in towns, valued for their alertness and loud bark.

History

Spitz-type dogs have been kept in Central Europe for centuries; stone-age remains of similar dogs have been found in peat bogs. German Spitz dogs became popular companions in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Queen Charlotte, wife of George III of Britain, brought Pomeranians from Germany, and the smaller varieties became fashionable in England. The Keeshond developed as the Dutch form of the Wolfspitz, serving as a watchdog on Rhine barges.

Characteristics

All German Spitz varieties share the classic spitz outline: a wedge-shaped head, pricked ears, a profuse double coat standing away from the body, and a tail curled tightly over the back. Colours include black, white, brown, orange, grey-shaded (wolf-grey), and parti-colour depending on variety. They are lively, attentive, and devoted to their families, though they can be vocal and wary of strangers.

Varieties

The FCI standard divides the breed by height at the withers: the Wolfspitz/Keeshond (around 49 cm), Giant Spitz (around 46 cm), Medium Spitz (around 34 cm), Miniature Spitz (around 26 cm), and Toy Spitz or Pomeranian (around 20 cm).
