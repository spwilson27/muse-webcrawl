Movement for Socialism (Bolivia)

The Movement for Socialism (Spanish: Movimiento al Socialismo, MAS; full name Movement for Socialism – Political Instrument for the Sovereignty of the Peoples, MAS–IPSP) is a Bolivian political party founded in 1998. It grew out of the coca-growers' movement and allied peasant, indigenous and labour organisations, and has been Bolivia's dominant party for most of the period since 2006.

Rise to power

Under the leadership of Evo Morales, a cocalero union leader from the Chapare, MAS surged in the 2002 elections and won the presidency outright in 2005 with about 54 percent of the vote, making Morales Bolivia's first indigenous president. The party framed itself not as a conventional party but as the "political instrument" of social movements, supporting its programme through the 2009 constitution that refounded Bolivia as a plurinational state, nationalised hydrocarbons, and expanded indigenous rights.

In government and crisis

MAS governments oversaw strong growth fuelled by gas exports, sharp reductions in poverty, and large infrastructure and social programmes, alongside recurring controversy over judicial independence, media pressure and moves to extend presidential terms. Morales resigned in November 2019 amid protests and disputed election-fraud allegations, in what supporters call a coup and opponents a popular uprising. After an interim government, MAS returned to power in the 2020 elections under Luis Arce, though the party later fractured into rival factions around Arce and Morales.

Ideology

MAS combines socialist economics, indigenous communitarianism (suma qamaña, "living well") and resource nationalism, and is generally classed as left-wing populist and indigenist.
