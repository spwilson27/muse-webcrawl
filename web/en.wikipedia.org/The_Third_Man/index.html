The Third Man is a 1949 British film noir directed by Carol Reed, written by Graham Greene, and starring Joseph Cotten, Alida Valli, Orson Welles, and Trevor Howard. Set in post-World War II Vienna, the film follows American pulp writer Holly Martins, who arrives in the occupied city to find his friend Harry Lime supposedly dead — and discovers he was involved in a deadly penicillin racket. The film is celebrated for its atmospheric use of bombed-out Vienna, Robert Krasker's tilted ("Dutch angle") cinematography, and Anton Karas's zither score, whose "Third Man Theme" became an international hit.

Plot

Martins, offered a job by his old friend Lime, learns Lime was killed by a car. A British officer, Major Calloway, tells him Lime stole penicillin from military hospitals, diluted it, and sold it on the black market, causing deaths and madness among patients, including children. Pursuing contradictory witness accounts — was there a "third man" at the scene of Lime's death? — Martins discovers Lime alive, hiding in the Russian zone. Their confrontation on the Wiener Riesenrad Ferris wheel features Lime's famous improvised "cuckoo clock" speech. In a final chase through Vienna's sewers, Martins shoots the fleeing Lime at Calloway's tacit urging. Lime's actress girlfriend Anna, who still loves him, walks past Martins without a word in the celebrated closing long take.

Production

Greene wrote a novella as preparation, published in 1950. The film was co-produced by Alexander Korda's London Films and David O. Selznick. Welles wrote much of his own dialogue, including the cuckoo clock speech. The film won the Grand Prix at Cannes and the Academy Award for Best Cinematography (Black-and-White).

Legacy

In 1999 the British Film Institute voted The Third Man the greatest British film of all time. It is consistently ranked among the finest films ever made.
