100 metres

The 100 metres, or 100-metre dash, is a sprint race in track and field competitions. The shortest common outdoor running distance, it is one of the most popular and prestigious events in athletics. It has been contested at the Summer Olympic Games since 1896 for men and since 1928 for women. The winner of the Olympic 100 metres is often unofficially styled the "fastest man or woman on Earth".

The race

The race is run on a straight section of a standard 400-metre track, beginning from starting blocks. Sprinters typically reach their top speed between 50 and 60 metres into the race; reaction time at the start is critical, and a false start — a reaction faster than 0.1 seconds — results in disqualification. Elite male sprinters complete the distance in under ten seconds.

World records

The men's world record is 9.58 seconds, set by Usain Bolt of Jamaica at the 2009 World Championships in Berlin. Bolt also holds the Olympic record of 9.63 seconds, set at London 2012. The women's world record is 10.49 seconds, set by Florence Griffith Joyner of the United States in 1988 at Indianapolis.

History

The 100 metres originated from the 100-yard dash contested in the English-speaking world in the 19th century. The event was included in the first modern Olympics in Athens in 1896, won by Tom Burke of the United States. Fully automatic timing replaced hand timing for record purposes in 1977. Jim Hines became the first man to break ten seconds electronically in 1968, running 9.95 at the Mexico City Olympics.

Notable sprinters

Past champions include Jesse Owens, Carl Lewis, Maurice Greene, and Usain Bolt in the men's event, and Wyomia Tyus, Florence Griffith Joyner, Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce, and Elaine Thompson-Herah in the women's event.
