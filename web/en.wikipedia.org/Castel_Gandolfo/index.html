Castel Gandolfo

Castel Gandolfo (Latin: Castrum Gandulphi) is a town located 25 km (16 mi) southeast of Rome, in the Italian region of Lazio. Occupying a height overlooking Lake Albano, it is considered one of Italy's most scenic towns and is included in the list of I borghi più belli d'Italia ("The most beautiful villages of Italy").

Castel Gandolfo is best known worldwide as the site of the Papal Palace of Castel Gandolfo, the summer residence of the pope since the 17th century.

History

Archaeological finds indicate that the site was inhabited in prehistoric times and was later associated with the ancient city of Alba Longa. In the Middle Ages the area passed through the hands of several noble families, including the Gandolfi family, from whom the town takes its name, and later the Savelli family. Pope Urban VIII was the first pope to use the palace as a summer residence in the 1620s, and his successors expanded the residence over the following centuries.

The palace and its gardens were granted extraterritorial status under the Lateran Treaty of 1929. Pope Francis, who rarely used the residence himself, opened the palace and gardens to the public as a museum in 2016.

Main sights

The principal sights include the Papal Palace and its Barberini Gardens, the collegiate church of St. Thomas of Villanova designed by Gian Lorenzo Bernini, and the picturesque old town overlooking the volcanic crater lake. The town also hosted the rowing and canoeing events of the 1960 Summer Olympics on Lake Albano.

Geography

Castel Gandolfo lies in the Alban Hills, part of the Castelli Romani group of towns southeast of Rome. Lake Albano, a deep volcanic crater lake, lies directly below the town.
