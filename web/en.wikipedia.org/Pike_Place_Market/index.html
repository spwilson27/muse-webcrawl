Pike Place Market

Pike Place Market is a public market in Seattle, Washington, United States, overlooking the Elliott Bay waterfront on Puget Sound. Opened on August 17, 1907, it is one of the oldest continuously operated public farmers' markets in the country and Seattle's most popular tourist destination, drawing more than ten million visitors a year.

Features

The market spans several levels on a hillside and includes farmers' stalls, fishmongers — famous for the fish-throwing staff of the Pike Place Fish Market — flower sellers, craft vendors, restaurants and specialty shops. The original Starbucks store, opened in 1971, is located at 1912 Pike Place. Other landmarks include the "Public Market Center" sign and clock, Rachel the Piggy Bank (a bronze pig mascot), and the Gum Wall in Post Alley.

History

The market was founded so that consumers could buy produce directly from farmers, cutting out middlemen who had driven up prices. Threatened with demolition for urban renewal in the 1960s, it was saved by a citizen initiative in 1971 that created the Pike Place Market Preservation and Development Authority to own and manage it.
