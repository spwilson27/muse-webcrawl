Vernacular architecture

Vernacular architecture is building done outside professional design traditions, using local materials, knowledge and techniques passed down through generations, and shaped by climate, culture and available resources.

Characteristics

Vernacular buildings respond directly to local conditions: thick earthen walls in hot deserts, steeply pitched roofs in snowy regions, stilt houses in flood-prone areas. They are often built by their users or local craftspeople without architects.

Examples

Examples include igloos, yurts, adobe houses of the American Southwest and Sahel, joglo houses of Central Java, Alpine chalets, and Mediterranean whitewashed villages.

Study and influence

Bernard Rudofsky's 1964 exhibition and book Architecture Without Architects brought vernacular building to wide attention. Scholars such as Paul Oliver documented it globally, and architects including Geoffrey Bawa drew on it for climate-responsive modern design, closely related to passive solar design.
