Shane (film)

Shane is a 1953 American Western film directed and produced by George Stevens and starring Alan Ladd.

Shane is a 1953 American Western film directed and produced by George Stevens, from a screenplay by A. B. Guthrie Jr., adapted from Jack Schaefer's 1949 novel of the same name. Alan Ladd stars as the title character, a laconic but skilled gunfighter with a mysterious past who takes a job on the Wyoming homestead of Joe Starrett (Van Heflin) and becomes embroiled in the conflict between homesteaders and a ruthless cattle baron intent on driving them off the range.

Plot

Shane, a weary gunfighter, rides into an isolated Wyoming valley and takes work with the Starrett family. When cattle rancher Rufus Ryker brings in the hired killer Jack Wilson to intimidate the settlers, Shane — watched adoringly by young Joey Starrett — must take up his gun one final time. After the climactic shootout, the wounded Shane rides away into the mountains as Joey famously calls after him: "Shane! Shane! Come back!"

Reception and legacy

The film won the Academy Award for Best Cinematography (Color) for Loyal Griggs and was nominated for five other Oscars. It is routinely ranked among the greatest Westerns ever made, appears on the American Film Institute's lists, and was selected for the United States National Film Registry in 1993. Its closing scene is one of the most quoted in American cinema.
