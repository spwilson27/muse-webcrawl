Shape-memory alloy

A shape-memory alloy (SMA) is an alloy that can be deformed when cold but returns to its pre-deformed ("remembered") shape when heated. This shape memory effect arises from a reversible, diffusionless phase transformation between a high-temperature phase (austenite) and a low-temperature phase (martensite). The best-known and most commercially important SMA is nitinol, a near-equiatomic nickel–titanium alloy discovered at the US Naval Ordnance Laboratory in the early 1960s.

How it works

Below its transformation temperature, the alloy is in the martensite phase, whose crystal structure accommodates deformation by twinning rather than by irreversible slip, so the material can be bent or stretched substantially without permanent damage. On heating, the martensite transforms back to the more ordered austenite structure, and the atoms return to their original arrangement — restoring the memorised shape and generating considerable force. In the two-way effect, achieved by "training", the material remembers a shape in both hot and cold states. Above the transformation temperature, nitinol also exhibits superelasticity: it can sustain strains of several percent and spring back completely on unloading, because stress itself induces martensite that reverts when the stress is removed.

History

The effect was first observed in gold–cadmium alloys in 1932 and in copper–zinc in 1938, but remained a curiosity until William J. Buehler and Frederick Wang at the Naval Ordnance Laboratory characterised nickel–titanium in 1962–63; the name "nitinol" combines Nickel, Titanium and Naval Ordnance Laboratory.

Applications

SMAs are used as actuators and couplings in aerospace, in temperature-responsive valves and fire-safety devices, in eyeglass frames and mobile-phone antennas (exploiting superelasticity), and extensively in medicine: self-expanding vascular stents, orthodontic archwires, guidewires and surgical instruments. Limitations include cost, fatigue under very many cycles, and a relatively low energy efficiency when used as heat engines or actuators.
