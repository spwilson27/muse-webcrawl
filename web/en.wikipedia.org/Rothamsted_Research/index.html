Rothamsted Research, previously known as the Institute of Arable Crops Research and the Rothamsted Experimental Station, is one of the oldest agricultural research institutions in the world. Located at Harpenden in Hertfordshire, England, it was founded in 1843 by John Bennet Lawes on his inherited estate, Rothamsted Manor, to investigate the effects of inorganic and organic fertilizers on crop yields.

The classical experiments

With chemist Joseph Henry Gilbert, Lawes began the "classical experiments" — long-term field trials that continue to this day and are among the longest-running scientific experiments in the world. They include the Broadbalk winter wheat experiment (begun 1843), the Park Grass hay meadow experiment (1856), which is the oldest ecological experiment on Earth, and long-term trials on barley, potatoes, and crop rotations. Samples of soil and grain from these experiments have been archived since the 1840s, forming a unique resource used today to study everything from fertilizer history to radioactive fallout, antibiotic resistance genes, and DNA of historical plant pathogens.

Statistical science

Rothamsted played a foundational role in modern statistics. R. A. Fisher worked at Rothamsted from 1919 to 1933, where, analyzing the station's mountain of long-term field data, he developed the analysis of variance (ANOVA), the principles of experimental design — including randomization and blocking — and much of the theory of statistical inference set out in Statistical Methods for Research Workers (1925). His successors, including Frank Yates and John Nelder, continued this tradition, producing the Genstat statistical software.

Modern research

Today Rothamsted Research, a charitable company principally funded by the UK Biotechnology and Biological Sciences Research Council, works on sustainable agriculture: crop protection, soil health, nutrient cycling, agroecology, and the reduction of agriculture's environmental footprint. It also operates the North Wyke farm platform in Devon for grassland research. Notable episodes in its history include the development of pyrethroid insecticides in the 1960s–70s and controversy in 2012 over a field trial of aphid-resistant genetically modified wheat, which proceeded under heavy security after threats from anti-GM activists.
