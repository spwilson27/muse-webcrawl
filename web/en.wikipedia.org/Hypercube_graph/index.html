Hypercube graph

In graph theory, the hypercube graph Qn is the graph formed from the vertices and edges of an n-dimensional hypercube. It has 2n vertices, each representing a binary string of length n, with two vertices joined by an edge whenever their strings differ in exactly one bit position. The graph is n-regular, has n·2n−1 edges, and is bipartite.

Properties

The distance between two vertices of Qn equals the Hamming distance between their strings, so the diameter of the graph is n. Hypercube graphs are Hamiltonian for n ≥ 2, vertex-transitive, and are the Cartesian products of n copies of the complete graph K2. Gray codes correspond to Hamiltonian cycles or paths in the hypercube graph.

Applications

Hypercube graphs model the interconnection topology of parallel computers: each processor corresponds to a vertex and communication links to edges, giving a network with low diameter and high symmetry. They also arise in coding theory, where vertices are codewords and edges join words at Hamming distance one.
