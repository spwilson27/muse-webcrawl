Allen M. Sumner-class destroyer

The Allen M. Sumner class was a group of 58 destroyers built by the United States during World War II. The class was an improvement on the preceding Fletcher class, trading a single 5-inch gun mount for twin mounts and a wider beam for greater stability.

Allen M. Sumner class
Builders: various U.S. shipyards
Operators: United States Navy and others
Built: 1943–1945
In commission: 1943–1970s
Completed: 58
Displacement: about 2,200 tons (standard)

Design

The ships mounted six 5-inch/38-caliber guns in three twin turrets, powerful anti-aircraft batteries, and ten 21-inch torpedo tubes. The class lead ship was named for Allen Melancthon Sumner, a United States Marine Corps captain killed in World War I. Twelve ships were completed as destroyer minelayers, and the design served as the basis for the subsequent Gearing class.

Service

Sumner-class destroyers served in the Pacific in the final years of World War II, including picket duty off Okinawa, and many served through the Korean and Vietnam wars. Several were transferred to allied navies after U.S. service.
